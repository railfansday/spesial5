EV Share Jumps to 68%(!) and Small BEVs Rise to Power — China EV Sales Report for October

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

High gas prices and a never-ending wave of new BEVs have allowed October to reach 68% EV market share, a new record!

This record achievement is thanks to a significant ICE (internal combustion engine) crash. The overall market dropped 27% year over year (YoY), to around 1.6 million sales. Plugins were down 14% YoY, but pure ICE sales were much worse off, crashing 44% YoY!

Adding 45% BEV share to PHEVs (23% share) meant that in October a record 68% of all cars sold in China had a plug! If this trend continues in the future, the Chinese market will be fully electrified by 2030, and will be 100% BEV before 2035!

This great result pulled the 2026 share up, to 59%, already 5% above the full year result of 2025. BEVs on their own were up to 40% (versus 33% in 2025).

At this pace, I expect the final number for 2026 EV share in China to be above 60%, with BEVs alone north of the 40% mark. And when the largest global automotive market gets this electrified … then the ICE industry is in serious trouble.

The ICE extinction is also visible in the overall ranking. The overall top 10 was fully made by EVs. Again.

Looking at the best sellers in several size categories, the ICE extinction is also quite visible. Although, once again, the C-segment/compact category is the exception, with the only ICE representative showing up there — the Toyota RAV4, in 2nd, behind the leader Nevo Q05 and just ahead of the BYD Dolphin.

Having a quick look at the five size categories, the highlight is the B-segment/subcompact category, which is the hottest segment right now, with three of the top four top sellers in October belonging to this category.

Considering that 56% of the new SUVs launched this year were bigger than 5 meters/197 inches, there is a clear disconnect between what people want (small hatchbacks/crossovers) and what OEMs offer (large SUVs).

Another highlight is the fact that Xiaomi had a gold plus silver win in the full size category, with the BYD Great Tang closing out the podium while the midsize category podium was 100% BYD Group.

Here’s more info and commentary on October’s top selling electric models:

A BYD Dolphin for BYD Seagull money ($10,000). This was Geely’s internal memo to describe the formula for the Geely Xingyuan when developing its star model. With an interesting name — Xingyuan translates as “wishing upon a star” — it seems that Geely had its wish granted. However, the Geely model will have a tough adversary soon, as the new generation Seagull will land with only one goal — to steal the Xingyuan’s best seller status. But while the new Seagull didn’t land, the small Geely scored a record performance in October, with its 50,190 registrations representing a 14% increase YoY. Besides the new BYD Seagull, Geely’s hatchback now has to deal with Leapmotor’s B-segment contenders, the A10 and A05; the new-generation Wuling Bingo; Chery’s new QQ3; and let’s not forget about BYD’s Yuan Up, too.

BYD’s midsize SUV continues to transition into the new generation. Thanks to the ramp-up of the new Ultra body, BYD’s star player was 2nd, scoring 30,772 registrations. Sales were up by 6% compared to the same month last year. The new Ultra generation features lidar and 1,500 kW DC charging, and these two features aren’t even the most impressive aspects of the model! That would be the price. It starts at 152,000 yuan (or $22,000) with the 76 kWh battery, and it goes up to 180,000 yuan (or $26,000) with the 83 kWh battery. For comparison, the Tesla Model Y starts in China at 259,000 yuan ($38,250)…. Expect the Song to experience a second youth from here onwards, and while it is impossible to displace the Tesla Model Y from the runner-up spot, the last place on the podium is doable this year. And maybe higher up in 2027?

BYD’s small crossover continues to surprise, thanks to recently improved specs. BYD’s team player rose to 3rd in October, scoring 28,514 registrations, which was a 44% increase YoY and a new record score. Despite not being the most exciting vehicle to look at, or drive, the truth is that it is surfing the wave of small, affordable EVs better than most, scoring high in value for money with prices starting at $11,000 and higher end versions having BYD’s driver-assistance system. With small vehicles currently having a moment in China, expect the small crossover to show up in the top 5 more regularly.

Things continue to go well for the startup brand, with its new baby A10 now established as the star player in an already strong lineup. Thanks to 27,247 registrations, the small crossover managed another top 5 presence. The model has the usual value-for-money focus of the brand, and a low, low price of 66,000 yuan ($10,000). On top of that, however, the A10 offers something close to a distinct personality, as the design eschews the white product standard design of Leapmotor for something more personal, mostly thanks to the front and back lights and a floating roof effect. With B-segment BEVs now being the hottest category in China, Leapmotor is well prepared to benefit from the subcompact rising tide, thanks to the recent arrival of the A10 small crossover and its sibling, the A05 small hatchback. Talk about perfect timing….

After the SU7 and YU7, Xiaomi has another success on its hands. This big fat SUV — or shall I say SUVs, as the lineup is broken down between the 7-seat N90 and the 5-seat N70 — scored 27,132 registrations in only its second month on the market, pulling it immediately into the overall top 5. Even with the number of competitors growing every passing day, Xiaomi’s new land yachts have been selling like hot cakes, while drying up sales of the competition. Proof of that, is the fact that there were only two full size models in October’s top 20, with the other being … the Xiaomi SU7.

Looking at the rest of the best seller table, there were a number of models hitting record scores, starting with the #8 Nevo Q05 (20,022 units) continuing its success story. The #10 Bestune Xiaoma (17,887 units) benefitted from a recent refresh to score a surprise record score. Could this mean that small city EVs are (finally) seeing the light at the end of the tunnel?

As for the second half of the table, the highlight is the ramp-up of the Xpeng L03, with the attractive looking crossover, which kind of reminds of a mini-Lotus or small Polestar, reaching 13th thanks to 15,653 sales. This EV looks to be the startup’s star model thanks to a competitive price (124,000 RMB) that is half the price of the Tesla Model Y!

Other models with record performances were the #14 MG 4 (15,503 units), with the new generation proving to be much more popular and adaptable to local tastes than the previous one, and the #17 Chery QQ3 EV, which continued its slow climb in the ranking thanks to 14,041 sales, which made it the 5th B-segment model in the top 20.

Still in the top 20, we should also highlight the best selling foreign model, the Tesla Model Y VW ID.Aura T6, the new China-made (and China-developed) German crossover that has allowed Volkswagen to beat the Texans for the first time in years (the Tesla Model Y was down 60% YoY, to 7,807 units, while the Model 3 was down 85%, to 1,010 units). The Aura T6, VW’s take on the Model Y formula, rose to the 20th position in its first full month on the market!

A promising sign, or just hype? Only time will tell, but at least for now, VW seems to be getting some traction on the EV side of the Chinese market, a point also highlighted by the fact that it wasn’t just the Aura T6 getting decent sales. The also new VW ID.Era 5S (think Chinese VW Jetta) got 7,100 registrations in October. Is there hope after all?

Outside the top 20, the main event was the ramp-up of Nevo’s new baby, the Q06, which is looking to replicate the Q05’s success in the category above. With some 10,000 registrations in only its second month, it looks like it could join the top 20 soon. The same can be said about the Geely Galaxy TT, the make’s answer to the Xiaomi SU7, which had around 10,500 deliveries in October. Then there’s also the BYD Yuan Plus, which, after a long cross through the desert, seems to be picking up steam again. It scored 11,658 registrations last month, a new year best.

Looking at the 2026 ranking, the podium remained the same, and unless something unexpected happens, it should stay as it is until the end of the year, with the small Geely repeating its 2025 win. That also means the Tesla Model Y will return to silver after getting bronze in 2025, and the BYD Song will get back onto the podium after a one-year absence.

Below the podium positions, though, there was some news. The BYD Yuan Up and Leapmotor A10 are surfing the B-segment wave and both climbed a position, to 5th and 6th, respectively.

If the BYD Yuan Up continues on its current record streak, then the small crossover could even aim to surpass the #4 FCB Ti7 before the year ends.

Still, with three subcompacts currently in the top 6 (Geely Xingyuan, BYD Yuan Up, and Leapmotor A10), small is hot right now, in China.

Elsewhere, the BYD Sealion 05 was up two positions, to 16th, while the Wuling Mini EV is hoping to bring tiny city cars back into the spotlight, and in October, it jumped two positions, ending the month in 13th….

Looking at the overall manufacturer ranking, it is a sea of red. Everyone is losing sales — big time. The exception is #5 Leapmotor (+31% YoY), while #8 Xiaomi (+3% YoY) and #10 Xpeng (+0%) are just hanging on.

So, now, not only are foreign OEMs suffering (Nissan was down 70% in October, Honda was down 51%), but even domestic ones are being affected by the EV shift.

Some may be asking, “But what about #9 Nevo and its 125% growth rate?” Well, it isn’t so much that they are winning sales, but they are effectively replacing the sales of their mother brand, Changan, which in October was only 19th after a 60% drop in sales YoY.

Looking at the auto brand ranking, BYD is firmly in the leadership position (17.2%), while runner-up Geely held to its 7.1% market share.

Rising 3rd placed Leapmotor (6.7%, up 0.2%) is fast approaching Geely. It wouldn’t be surprising if the startup brand became the new silver medalist in the final days of the year.

One thing is for certain though — Leapmotor will win its first medal this year. The only question is the material that it will be made of….

Speaking of position changes, Tesla had a horrible month (4.4%, down from 4.9% in September), which allowed Wuling (4.6%) to climb one position in October, into 4th. At the end of the year, expect Tesla to keep the #4 position, as its December peak should help it surpass Wuling.

Looking at OEMs/automotive groups/alliances, BYD is leading with 21.9% share of the market, up 0.1% in October.

Meanwhile, Geely remained in second with 11.8% share. The multinational conglomerate has the runner-up spot secured.

#3 SAIC was stable (8%) and remained firm in the last place on the podium, while #4 Leapmotor (6.7%, up 0.2%) remained on the rise. Will we see the startup challenge SAIC in 2027 for the 3rd spot?
