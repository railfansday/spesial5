Parrott fires Republic of Ireland into Nations League play-offs

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Republic of Ireland vs Kosovo. UEFA Nations League Group B3.

The Republic of Ireland have finished as runners-up in Nations League Group B3; Troy Parrott scored two penalties to send them into the promotion play-offs; the win has also propelled the Euro 2028 co-hosts into a coveted pot-two seeding for next month's qualifying draw

Troy Parrott fired the Republic of Ireland into a Nations League play-off berth with two penalties in their 3-0 victory over 10-man Kosovo at the Aviva Stadium in Dublin.

The result ensured the Republic finished runners-up in group B3, and also propelled the Euro 2028 co-hosts into a coveted pot-two seeding for next month's qualifying draw for that competition.

Tuesday night's contest turned on its head in the 26th minute, when Kreshnik Hajrizi was sent off for handling the ball off the line, and Parrott took advantage with a top-corner finish.

The Real Betis striker doubled the Republic's lead from the spot five minutes after the restart, and Jack Moylan added a 75th-minute cushion to keep alive the hosts' hopes of booking a place in League A for the first time through the March play-off.

Heimir Hallgrimsson's lone change from Saturday's defeat to group winners Austria was the insertion of John Egan into the line-up, replacing injured captain Nathan Collins.

It was a contest that lacked fluidity in the opening 10 minutes, before the Republic found a foothold and Finn Azaz looked to pick out Parrott, but was blocked.

Kosovo then twice missed chances to take the lead. Lindon Emerllahu fired just wide in the 17th minute, and Caoimhin Kelleher was called into action for the first time a minute later to deny Vedat Muriqi.

What had been a largely uneventful encounter suddenly took a dramatic twist in the 26th minute, a red-card incident sparked by Moylan's cross, glanced on by Liam Scales to Parrott.

His effort - clearly goalbound - was cleared off the line by Hajrizi, who extended his arm like a goalkeeper, and was immediately shown a red card by Latvian referee Edgars Malcevs.

Parrott stepped up and gave Arijanet Muric no chance when he rifled into the top right corner to set the Republic on their way.

It remained just a one-goal advantage at the break despite a handful of chances falling the Republic's way, while the visitors created one when Kelleher denied Ilir Krasniqi with 10 minutes remaining to ensure there would be no first-half equaliser.

There was a flurry of Republic chances immediately after the second half kicked off before Parrott added his second penalty.

Muric had just denied Jayson Molumby's effort from range and Parrott was rushing forward to meet the loose ball when he was taken down by the Kosovo keeper.

This time he dove in the right direction, but failed to stop Parrott from doubling the hosts' lead.

The Republic were less successful from open play, unable to put the game away from a string of subsequent chances despite finding themselves in plenty of space, and should have added a third before the hour.

Moylan's persistence finally paid off with 15 minutes remaining, nodding home substitute Liam Kitching's cross in off the post.

The Republic nearly added a late fourth when Adam Idah stung the palms of Muric with two minutes left.

Republic of Ireland striker Troy Parrott speaking to RTÉ about the 2028 Euros:

"I've been thinking about it since I was a kid, I think everyone has.

"Look, it's not a guarantee that we are going to qualify for that tournament, so I think tonight was very important to get into pot two so that we have a better draw.

"We'll be going into the qualifiers now, trying to make sure that we qualify for the tournament.

"A tournament that's one of the host nations being Ireland, without Ireland it would be heartbreaking for all of us, so I think it's a mission now to make sure that we're there.

"It was disappointing that we didn't win the group because that's what we all wanted to do at the start.

"There's been so many nearly matches where we've nearly won, we've played really well and haven't won, and I think tonight we went out there with the mindset that no matter what we have to win.

"Obviously the red card helped us, but I have no doubt in my mind that group of players who were out on that pitch would have won that game."

Republic of Ireland boss Heimir Hallgrimsson speaking at his post-match press conference:

"Everybody, and especially us, likes to play the top teams. To compare yourself with the best teams, that's one thing.

"Second is those teams will expose your weaknesses, and that helps you in your growth to play these teams, so we can continue our growth.

"We're already looking forward to March. Tough teams in the pot. Whoever we draw is going to be a tough two games, and it's good for this team at this time to play these matches instead of friendlies.

"We're really excited for March, and looking forward to meeting the players again."
