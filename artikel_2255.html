Spurs have one Preston North End transfer clause that already looks doomed

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Preston North End's 12-season stay in the Championship is in serious jeopardy based on their early season form.

The Lilywhites were long-term stayers in the second tier the last time they were here, keeping themselves in from 2000 until 2011 with some near promotion misses along the way, before dropping into League One.

Since 2015, PNE have been back in the Championship, mostly being a mid-table team, albeit with some brief flirtations with the top six and also the drop zone, and there was hope over the summer that a switch in transfer business would unlock a new level for them.

North End are usually quite frugal with their transfer budget, cherry-picking some younh, high-quality Premier League loanees and opting to recruit permanent players on cheaper fees or free transfers.

There was much evidence of that last summer when Alfie Devine, Lewis Dobbin, Daniel Jebbison and Harrison Armstrong arrived on loan, whilst they saved paying fees until January of last season, when Odel Offiah, Andrew Moran and Callum Lang all arrived at Deepdale.

For whatever reason though, that tact changed this past summer, with around £15 million spent on new faces and over £5 million recouped on Milutin Osmajic and Ben Whiteman's sales.

And £6 million of that windfall spent was making the loan signing of Devine a permanent one, which came as a surprise to many North End fans, given the interest surrounding his services.

Celtic, Southampton and Wolves were among the clubs that were keen on taking Devine from Spurs, but even after the initial deadline was missed to seal a deal with the playmaker, PNE were able to convince the 22-year-old that Deepdale was the best place for his future.

Devine was once the youngest player and scorer for Tottenham when he featured in an FA Cup clash with Marine in 2021, but he had to go out on several loans to develop, with stints taken in at Port Vale, Plymouth Argyle and Belgian outfit Westerlo before ending up at PNE.

And with Tottenham being a big-spending club looking to turn around their fortunes under Roberto De Zerbi, there was no room for Devine in his midfield for 2026-27, leading to the decision to let him move on permanently for a decent fee, much like they did with Will Lankshear and Ash Phillips when selling the pair to Middlesbrough.

Spurs hold extra clauses when it comes to Lankshear and Phillips, which means they have a vested interest in their futures and will want Boro in the Premier League, as well as matching rights on the pair, which means they are given the chance to match any accepted bid that comes in for their services - it's not quite like that with Devine, but there's still a sell-on clause in there.

The Daily Mail reported at the time of the deal that Tottenham had negotiated a 20 per cent sell-on clause with PNE for Devine, which is based on any profit the Lancashire outfit end up making on Devine - as it stands though, that could be in jeopardy.

The reason why Devine was so in-demand during the summer was his form for North End, with eight goals scored from midfield and six assists registered.

But he's showed nothing like that kind of ability for PNE in the 2026-27 season, scoring just one goal and notching one assist, with the Lilywhites looking like they're in big relegation trouble already.

Ahead of their 15th match of the Championship season against Southampton, North End have just five points on the board and are nine points from safety, which whilst not confirming relegation by any stretch of the imagination, they'll need to go some way to overturn the form they've showed so far.

Paul Heckingbottom's early sacking and club icon Graham Alexander coming into replace him hasn't helped results whatsoever, and Devine is yet to show what he's really capable of - with the general consensus of the fanbase being that he's missing the link-up with now-Southampton forward Dobbin that he shared last season.

Undoubtedly there's a lack of quality in the North End squad compared to last season, and that's showing on the pitch - Alexander didn't even turn to Devine in their 2-0 loss to Stoke City when he was sat on the bench, and he had to wait until the second half earlier this week against Sheffield United to come on, assisting Callum Lang's consolation goal in a 3-2 loss.

With the current trajectory that PNE find themselves going in, Devine will likely not be playing in League One next season if PNE find themselves there, but they won't be getting their £6 million back based on this season's performances, no matter how much potential the former Wigan Athletic youth prospect has.

It would also mean that Spurs do not get any more money for Devine despite their 20 per cent sell-on clause, which they will find a shame, as they would've been banking on Devine continuing to prosper at Deepdale - unlike their clauses for Lankshear and Phillips, Devine's clause is one that they can perhaps almost forget about.
