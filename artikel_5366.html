Linq Raises $90 Million in New Funding Led by Andreessen Horowitz to Build the Infrastructure Platform for AI Agents

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Every business and every person will have an agent. With more than 1 billion messages processed, Linq is building the infrastructure to put them to work.

SAN FRANCISCO, Nov. 18, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Linq, the infrastructure platform for AI agents, today announced $90 million in new funding led by Andreessen Horowitz, with continued participation from TQ Ventures, Mucker Capital, and Abstract, and new participation from Anthos and Left Lane Capital. Ben Horowitz will join its board of directors.

The internet gave every business a website. The iPhone gave every business an app. Now AI is giving every business, and every person, an agent. Cloudflare reports that more than half of internet traffic is no longer human, with AI agent requests on its network up more than 1,700% in a year. These are not simple, customer-service chatbots: these agents book, buy, and make plans by communicating with people and using software on their behalf.

Messaging was Linq's first product and remains a core part of its growing AI infrastructure platform. As the provider for DoorDash, Town, Tomo, and hundreds of others, Linq has already processed more than 1 billion messages. DoorDash uses Linq to help customers find food, build a cart, and check out in a single thread. And now, as an Apple Messaging Service Provider, Linq can help more enterprises launch and scale customer-facing agents on the same channel.

"The best AI experiences fit naturally into people's lives," said Aryan Shah, Head of AI research at DoorDash. "Linq gives us the messaging infrastructure to bring ordering into a familiar conversation, so customers can go from deciding what they want to placing an order in one thread."

"Agents will communicate and get work done just like people do. Linq gives agents the infrastructure to do both at scale," said Ben Horowitz, cofounder and general partner at Andreessen Horowitz.

Messaging is the beginning of a larger shift: people will describe what they need, and agents will get it done. Linq's computer use offering gives agents access to physical computing infrastructure to interact with software and complete tasks. HD Calling for Agents adds voice calling to the platform. The funding will support continued investment in messaging and the expansion of Linq's product lineup through its own software, hardware infrastructure, and strategic partnerships.

"Clicking through five screens to make a payment will become two words to an agent: 'pay rent,'" said Elliott Potter, CEO of Linq. "We help developers build customer-facing agents that use human tools to do human work."

Companies can learn more at https://linqapp.com/
