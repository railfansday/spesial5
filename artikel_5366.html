FHFA maps out FICO 10T rollout with broad eligibility by late 2028

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

FHFA has approved the FICO 10T credit score model for Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. It has also outlined a multiyear road map to phase it in while retiring Classic FICO by late 2028.  AI Summary

The Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA) has approved the FICO 10T credit score model for use by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. It also released an implementation road map that would make FICO 10T broadly eligible and retire Classic FICO by September 2028.

The staged rollout, announced Tuesday, mirrors the VantageScore 4.0 timeline and is meant to give lenders, mortgage insurers and loan origination system providers time to analyze historic FICO 10T data, rework pricing and risk models, and update technology before a full transition, according to the agency.“The timeline below prepares lenders, mortgage insurers and loan origination system providers for the final step in credit score modernization: lender choice between FICO 10T and VantageScore 4.0,” the agency said. At the Federal Housing Administration (FHA), VantageScore 4.0 and FICO 10T are expected to go live alongside the existing Classic FICO in January.

Bob Broeksmit, president and CEO of the Mortgage Bankers Association (MBA), issued a statement of support following the announcement.

“We welcome this milestone as it moves us closer to the industry’s and FHFA’s goal of transitioning to true competition with two modernized credit score models,” Broeksmit said. “We appreciate FHFA’s desire to provide a clear and transparent roadmap for this process. Given the industry’s experience with the just-completed adoption of VantageScore 4.0, MBA will work with FHFA and industry stakeholders to expedite the implementation timeline responsibly.

“MBA and its members will continue to engage with FHFA to establish meaningful competition for both credit scoring and credit reporting. Continued reforms to end the tri-merge requirement will further improve our credit reporting system and lower costs for borrowers. Today’s announcement is a critical next step.”

The FHFA initially validated both FICO 10T and VantageScore 4.0 for use at the government-sponsored (GSEs) enterprises in October 2022. In July 2026, Fannie and Freddie released historical performance data for FICO 10T along with additional VantageScore 4.0 data, a key prerequisite for lenders and investors to back-test new score models and recalibrate credit policy.A FICO 10T pilot is expected to begin around December 2027. In this “launch and refine” phase, lenders will deliver a subset of loans underwritten with FICO 10T while FHFA, the GSEs and industry stakeholders monitor data quality, pricing and delivery performance.

Based on pilot results, FHFA expects upfront pricing grids and private mortgage insurer eligibility requirements, or PMIERs, to be updated to reflect the new score model. That step will be critical for secondary market execution and risk transfer structures that currently rely on Classic FICO.

A limited FICO 10T rollout is targeted for April 2028, followed by a scale-up and transition phase that leads to broad eligibility around September 2028. During this final stage, FHFA plans to confirm industry readiness, fully implement Uniform Loan Delivery Dataset (ULDD) Phase 5, expand FICO 10T use across GSE channels and retire Classic FICO.

FHFA emphasized that dates after November 2026 are targets and are subject to change.

In October, Fair Isaac Corp. said it will hold its wholesale mortgage price at $10 for a bundle of its credit scores for lenders and brokers enrolled in its Preferred Program. The $10 price is available through participating tri-merge credit report resellers to lenders that commit to FICO under the program. It covers both Classic FICO, which has been the mortgage standard for nearly three decades, and FICO Score 10T, the company’s trended-data model.

FICO is also preparing a new subscription-based pricing model for mortgage credit scores in 2027, sources told HousingWire.

VantageScore 4.0, by comparison, has been offered at 99 cents per score, or at no additional charge when purchased alongside a FICO score through Equifax, Experian and TransUnion, which jointly own VantageScore.

Bank of America Global Research, using data from Recursion, found that more than 170 lenders now pull VantageScore 4.0. Despite that breadth of use, only a small share of conventional loans are actually priced with VantageScore 4.0. In October, 20% of issuance reported a VantageScore 4.0, yet only about 8% of total issuance used the score model for pricing, according to the research.
