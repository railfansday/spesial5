Disney Increases Dividend By 33%. Here’s What’s Driving the Shift.

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Here’s how much of Disney’s cash the new dividend will consume.

Disney (DIS +2.29%) paid shareholders $1.50 per share in dividends during its 2026 fiscal year. For fiscal 2027, it just set the dividend at $2.00, a 33% raise. That's a hefty hike for a company that's also spending heavily on content and new experiences across its parks and resorts, while also repurchasing a boatload of its shares.

Here's a look at what's driving the shift toward returning more capital to shareholders.

Disney declared a $2.00-per-share dividend for fiscal 2027, to be paid in two installments (January and July). That's a 33% increase from the $1.50 it paid in its 2026 fiscal year. At its new rate, Disney's dividend yield will rise to around 1.7% based on its recent share price. That makes Disney a more attractive dividend stock, as the S&P 500 yields around 1.1%.

The raise is a continuation of Disney's dividend rebuild following the pandemic-driven pause in payments. The entertainment, experiences, and sports giant suspended its dividend in 2020 to preserve its financial strength and fund its streaming investments. It reinstated its semi-annual dividend in late 2023 at $0.30 per share, and has steadily raised its payment each year.

Disney is coming off a strong fiscal year. Its revenue rose 8% to $102.3 billion, while its total segment operating income increased 12% to $19.7 billion. Earnings per share were up 18% after adjustments to $7.02 per share. Meanwhile, cash flow was strong at $19.3 billion (up 6%), while its free cash flow rose 5% to $10.6 billion.

The company's entertainment segment was the biggest growth driver, with revenue rising 10% to $46.6 billion and operating income rising 16% to $5.4 billion. Experiences had a record year, delivering 9% revenue growth to $39.6 billion and 12% operating income growth to $11.2 billion. That helped offset slower growth in sports (5% revenue growth and 7% operating income growth).

Disney expects to deliver double-digit growth in its adjusted earnings per share for fiscal 2027. That suggests it will earn at least $7.72 per share this year. With its new payout set at $2.00 per share, Disney's dividend payout ratio is around 26% of its earnings.

However, the dividend is only part of the company's capital allocation strategy. The company plans to invest $9 billion to continue to drive growth across its experiences this year. Despite that heavy capital spending, Disney expects to generate around $11 billion in free cash flow this fiscal year. That's still plenty to cover the 33% increase in its dividend payment. Disney's dividend cost the company about $2.6 billion last year, up from the $1.8 billion paid out in fiscal 2025. The raise implies Disney will pay out around $3.5 billion in dividends during its 2027 fiscal year, depending on the impact of share repurchases on its outstanding shares.

However, Disney also plans to repurchase another $9 billion of its shares this year. Add that to the dividend, and total cash returns to shareholders will be around $12.5 billion, which exceeds its anticipated free cash flow by about $1.5 billion. That means Disney will utilize its balance sheet to help fund its 2027 capital allocation strategy.

With $6.7 billion of cash on its balance sheet (against $44.5 billion of debt), it has ample capacity to fund the shortfall. The company also outspent its cash flow on capital spending and cash returns to shareholders in fiscal 2026. It's part of Disney's strategic shift to use its strong A-rated balance sheet to return more cash to shareholders even as it invests heavily in new experiences across its parks and resorts.

Disney is coming off a strong year and expects to deliver double-digit earnings-per-share growth again this fiscal year. That's supporting its shift to pay out more of its cash as dividends. While the company's total capital spending and capital return plans will again exceed its anticipated free cash flow this year, it can easily cover the shortfall with its fortress balance sheet. That puts its newly raised dividend on a rock-solid foundation. Meanwhile, if this year doesn't go as planned, Disney could simply reduce its share repurchases to preserve more financial flexibility.
