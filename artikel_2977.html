Fantasy Basketball Weekend Streamers: Kevin Huerter to step up with Duncan Robinson sidelined

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

With Duncan Robinson set to miss at least a week with a quad injury, Kevin Huerter has slid into the starting unit as the floor spacer in his place. The first two starts for Huerter were fairly quiet, but he had a really strong four-game stretch before that. At least for their two games this weekend, with one of those coming against a Hawks team that is currently second in the league in pace, Huerter should continue to get the nod.

His role has expanded significantly over Detroit's last six games, and he has averaged 14.5 points, 3.3 rebounds, 3.8 assists, 1.0 steal, 1.7 blocks and 1.3 triples per game. He isn't a high-volume shooter, though it wouldn't be the worst thing if he became one, at least while Robinson is out. However, not many shooters can provide as much value in other categories as Huerter does.

The Hawks, Celtics, Bulls, Mavericks, Nuggets, Pistons, Pacers, Pelicans, Knicks, Thunder, Magic, Kings, Jazz and Wizards all play two games this weekend, so prioritize those teams when deciding what players to stream.

It might be getting colder outside, but the Pelicans have been having a Poole Party recently. He's returned to the rotation since Dejounte Murray requested a trade, and he has scored in double figures in five straight games. He scored a season-high 22 points in their last game and has at least five dimes in four straight games. Poole hasn't been as fantasy relevant in New Orleans, and his place in the rotation has been inconsistent at times. Right now, he's playing a lot and playing well.

This is a risky play since Flemings is listed as questionable with a lumbar strain after missing the Hawks' last two games. If that's too big of a risk for you, try Aaron Wiggins, who started Atlanta's last game with Flemings out. CJ McCollum is set to miss a month with a calf injury, which means more minutes for Flemings (and Wiggins). Flemings is definitely a long-term option that is still rostered in too few leagues.

Okoro has been starting for Chicago since Caleb Wilson fractured his hand, and the last few games have actually been solid in the box score. He has averaged 12.3 points, 4.8 rebounds, 3.0 assists, 1.5 steals and 2.3 triples per game over Chicago's last four games. Okoro has never been a strong fantasy option, which is why you need to strike while the iron's hot. This probably isn't going to last long.

Mikal Bridges' early-season struggles are well documented, which has opened the door for Shamet to play a larger role, and he has taken advantage. He has scored in double figures in five of his last six games, including a season-high 23 points against Toronto last weekend. Shamet is a solid rotation piece, though he really only provides buckets from beyond the arc consistently. They also play two teams that rank in the top 10 in the NBA in pace, which should mean extra shots for Shamet.

Martin has started eight of the Mavs' last nine games, and while he didn't produce much at first, he has been excellent over the last three, where he has averaged 11.7 points, 6.0 rebounds, 3.7 assists, 1.7 steals and 2.3 triples per game while shooting 61.9 percent from the floor. Injuries have prevented Martin from making much of an impact in fantasy basketball in recent years, but he's finally healthy and playing well.

Oubre has been in the starting lineup for Indiana's last four games, which has led to him averaging 15.8 points, 4.8 rebounds, 1.8 steals and 1.3 triples per game during this stretch. He should be a solid option as long as he continues to start, which should continue until Tyrese Haliburton returns. It was a slow start to the season for him, but Oubre is starting to pick up the production.

Williams has been in and out of the starting lineup for OKC recently, and while he hasn't done much scoring, he has been able to provide some steals recently. He isn't a high-upside play, but he has gotten steady minutes and contributed decent defensive stats while remaining efficient. There's no reason to consider Williams as a long-term option.

Bamba has started three straight games for the Jazz, and he has been the player that the Magic hoped they were taking with the No. 6 overall pick back in 2018. During this stretch, he has averaged 15.7 points, 10.3 rebounds, 1.0 steal, 1.3 blocks and 2.3 triples per game. Until Jusuf Nurkić returns, Bamba should continue to start, or at least play significant minutes. There hasn't been much clarity surrounding Nurk's return as of late, so while Bamba should be a good play this weekend, he may be worth keeping around longer than that.

Prioritize Bamba, but Hayes was still solid enough as a reserve to be picked up. He had been starting, but in his return from a two-game absence, he played 25 minutes off the bench and contributed six points, seven rebounds, two assists, two steals and two blocks. There is a chance that Hayes takes the starting spot back from Bamba, but he should at least split the minutes down low with him.
