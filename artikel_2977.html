Warren Buffett's Successor, Greg Abel, Overhauled Berkshire's Portfolio for a 3rd Straight Quarter, Reducing 6 Stocks and Piling Into a Hot Tech IPO

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

This isn’t your grandparents’ Berkshire Hathaway any longer.

The trillion-dollar company Warren Buffett built, Berkshire Hathaway (BRKA -0.25%)(BRKB -0.01%), entered the year in uncharted territory for the first time in well over half a century. Following the Oracle of Omaha's retirement as CEO at the end of last year, Greg Abel was handed the reins to Berkshire's day-to-day operations and its closely watched $364 billion investment portfolio.

Abel wasted little time making his mark. During the first quarter, Berkshire's new boss jettisoned 16 stocks and gobbled up shares of Google parent Alphabet (GOOGL -4.07%)(GOOG -3.85%). This transformation has continued into the third quarter, with Abel reducing his company's stake in six stocks and piling into a hot artificial intelligence (AI) initial public offering (IPO), Bending Spoons (BSP -0.24%).

Some of this selling activity is arguably benign. For instance, the minor selling in DaVita likely offsets the company's ongoing share buybacks. Likewise, Nucor is a relatively small position.

But the ninth consecutive quarter of selling in Bank of America definitely stands out. BofA was notably absent from Warren Buffett's and Greg Abel's lists of "indefinite" holdings or companies that can compound over decades. Though Bank of America is well-positioned to take advantage of a rising interest-rate environment, it's not the bargain it was when Buffett initially bought $5 billion of preferred stock in August 2011.

The selling in VeriSign, another longtime Buffett holding, is also noteworthy and likely indicative of valuation concerns. Though VeriSign operates a virtual monopoly in .com and .net domain registration, its trailing 12-month price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of 33.2 is 17% above its trailing five-year average.

While Abel nearly doubled Berkshire's stake in Lennar (LEN +0.21%)(LENB -0.11%) and increased his company's stake in Delta Air Lines (DAL +0.41%) by 12% in the third quarter, it's his purchase of AI- and tech-driven merger and acquisition company Bending Spoons that pops.

Berkshire Hathaway acquired nearly 20.7 million shares of Bending Spoons at its July IPO and added another $330 million, via a private placement at $33 per share, in mid-October.

The likely lure for Berkshire's new boss is Bending Spoons' low-cost, high-margin operating model that seeks to acquire established tech-driven businesses and make them more efficient. Using its own software and AI, Bending Spoons can turn these established brands into high-margin, subscription-driven businesses. Some of its top acquisitions include AOL and Vimeo.

Bending Spoons is also expected to deliver exceptional growth. While Wall Street analyst estimates remain fluid and are dependent on the company executing new deals, Bending Spoons is currently projected to grow its sales and per-share profit by 47% and 37%, respectively, in 2027.

AI and tech stocks are on the menu in a way that was never observed under Warren Buffett.
