Palantir vs. Snowflake: The AI Sell-Off Is Exposing a Critical Difference for 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Palantir and Snowflake continue to deliver impressive growth, but soaring AI expectations and renewed market volatility are putting their valuations under scrutiny.

The artificial intelligence (AI) boom has fueled remarkable growth across the technology sector, but the recent sell-off has Wall Street reconsidering how much that growth is worth. For investors looking toward 2027, the pullback presents an opportunity to reassess two leading enterprise AI players: Palantir Technologies (PLTR +3.48%) and Snowflake (SNOW +2.35%).

Both of these companies are benefiting from growing AI adoption, but their distinct business models, financial performance, and valuations present nuanced investment opportunities.

Palantir develops software platforms that help government agencies and businesses integrate complex datasets, improve decision-making, and deploy artificial intelligence. Its Gotham, Foundry, and Artificial Intelligence Platform (AIP) products serve customers ranging from the U.S. military to commercial enterprises.

In fiscal 2025, Palantir generated $4.5 billion in revenue, an increase of 56% year over year. Net income reached approximately $1.6 billion, representing a profit margin of 36.5%. More recent results demonstrate accelerating growth. In the third quarter of 2026, revenue surged 96% to $2.3 billion, while U.S. commercial revenue climbed 148%. Notably, U.S. commercial revenue surpassed U.S. government revenue for the first time, highlighting the company's expanding presence in the private sector.

At the end of 2025, the company carried virtually no debt, with a current ratio of approximately 7.1x. Free cash flow reached $2.1 billion during the year, providing substantial flexibility to fund continued expansion. However, stock-based compensation totaled approximately $684 million, representing 32% of operating cash flow, which could introduce potential shareholder dilution despite the company's strong cash generation.

Snowflake operates a cloud-based data platform that enables businesses to store, manage, and analyze information across multiple cloud environments. Its consumption-based pricing model allows customers to pay for the computing resources they use, while partnerships with Amazon (AMZN -0.35%), Microsoft (MSFT -0.99%), and Alphabet (GOOGL +0.78%) (GOOG +0.78%) support its global reach. As businesses increasingly incorporate artificial intelligence into their operations, Snowflake's platform plays an important role in making enterprise data accessible for AI applications.In fiscal 2026, Snowflake generated approximately $4.7 billion in revenue, an increase of 29% year over year. Still, it was unable to turn a profit and reported a net loss of $1.3 billion, representing a negative profit margin of roughly 28%. However, more recent results demonstrate continued growth. In the second quarter of fiscal 2027, revenue increased 35% to $1.55 billion, while product revenue climbed 37%.

At the end of fiscal 2026, its debt-to-equity ratio stood near 1.2x, while its current ratio was approximately 1.3x. The company generated $1.1 billion in free cash flow, but stock-based compensation totaled $1.6 billion, exceeding operating cash flow.

One of the key risks for Palantir comes from its reliance on large government and enterprise contracts, where lengthy sales cycles and customer concentration can create revenue uncertainty. In addition, its involvement in sensitive defense and surveillance operations also introduces regulatory and reputational risks.

Top risks for Snowflake include intense competition and a consumption-based revenue model that makes growth sensitive to customer spending. Not to mention, ever-growing cybersecurity threats introduce operational and regulatory risks.

Snowflake offers a lower P/S ratio, measuring price against sales over the past twelve months, while Palantir is cheaper relative to future earnings estimates via its Forward P/E.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

From a valuation standpoint, neither stock is cheap. Palantir trades at 103 times forward earnings, while Snowflake commands an even higher multiple of 131 times. Snowflake is considerably cheaper on a price-to-sales basis, at 18.9 times sales compared with Palantir's 54.5 times, but its continued losses and path toward sustained GAAP profitability are less certain.The key difference is that Palantir has demonstrated a stronger ability to turn growing AI adoption into profits. Its latest quarterly revenue surged 96%, driven by accelerating commercial demand, while its strong balance sheet and minimal debt provide considerable financial flexibility.Looking ahead to 2027, Palantir is my preferred buy, although its valuation gives me pause. Investors are paying a substantial premium for future growth, leaving little room for disappointment if the company falls short of expectations. Ultimately, both stocks carry lofty valuations, and the recent sell-off doesn't automatically make either a bargain.
