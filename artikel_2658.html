Week 12 Fantasy Football Fades & Busts: Jonathan Taylor, Garrett Wilson among players worth avoiding

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Welcome to the 12th of 17 weekly articles spotlighting the fantasy football fades of the week! I am your host, Matt Okada, and I'll be bringing you half a dozen potential busts each and every Friday, from now until fantasy championships.

As a note, just because a player earns a "fade" or "bust" designation doesn't automatically mean they should be benched — it depends on the rest of your roster or the options on your waiver wire. But you can expect them to fall short of expectations (when I get them right). I include a "What to Do" section for every player to help with that.

Belated Happy Thanksgiving, Happy Holidays and enjoy your weekend! Without further ado, here is my list for Week 12 of the 2026 season.

After starting the year extremely hot, Bryce Young has cooled significantly over the last five weeks. And that cooldown started in Week 7 with a home game against the Buccaneers, who tagged him for two interceptions and a forced fumble, and held him to 163 yards and just one touchdown. His 7.42 fantasy points from that game remain his lowest output of the season and the outlook isn't much better in the Week 12 rematch.

Young will now be traveling into Raymond James Stadium, where the Bucs have allowed just 11.5 points per game to opposing QBs this year, in prime time, where Young has averaged just 12.56 fantasy points per game in five career contests. No matter which poison you pick, and there are several options, the situation is not great for Young heading into Tampa Bay this Monday night. With zero teams on bye, there are plenty of preferable streamers available. Go with one of those guys.

What to do ❓ You should be able to bench Young for alternative options like Kirk Cousins, Deshaun Watson, J.J. McCarthy or even Jacoby Brissett.

Look, I can already hear the objections. Pollard has scored double-digit fantasy points in four straight games and averaged 23.3 PPG over that span. That includes the game two weeks ago, when he scored 19.9 against Jacksonville. So why would we fade him in the rematch? Well, let's dig into that stretch a little. He has scored fewer points in each successive game over that month, culminating in just 11.0 against the Cowboys last week, due to zero catches and no touchdown. And in that Jaguars game in particular, he managed just 33 rushing yards on nine carries, but salvaged his day with a career-high eight catches, one of which went for a 32-yard, fourth-quarter touchdown. That single play alone accounted for nearly 50% of his fantasy points on the day. The Jags remain the toughest run defense in the league — allowing the fewest rushing yards per game to running backs — and I don't foresee the same pass-catching parachute saving Pollard in this one. That makes him a major risk with a single-digit floor.

What to do ❓ Pollard is still a fringe RB2 on the merits of volume, but he's closer to the range of Zach Charbonnet or Chris Rodriguez Jr. (if Bhayshul Tuten is out) than he is to the top 15.

Admittedly, you'd need some impressive depth at running back to actually bench Jonathan Taylor ... but it's also been the right decision more often than not lately. Amidst the Colts' midseason fire sale, Taylor has lost significant volume and struggled for fantasy. He's logged fewer than seven fantasy points in three of his last four games, with zero touchdowns and two fumbles lost over that span.

From Weeks 1-7, he averaged 23.7 opportunities per game (third in the league) ... but from Weeks 8-11, he dropped to just 16.8 per game. He averaged just 9.1 points per game against the extremely soft Cowboys and Dolphins run defenses in Weeks 9 and 10 and draws a much tougher matchup with the Giants this Sunday. New York is allowing the sixth-fewest fantasy points per game to running backs, with just four rushing touchdowns on the season (tied with the Jaguars for third-fewest).

I don't foresee the move to Anthony Richardson Sr. helping this offense much — they've averaged 21.9 points per game in his 19 starts — and he's a threat to vulture a goal-line touchdown from Taylor as well. At the very least, the former RB1 is a fade in DFS ... but he's also worth avoiding in redraft lineups if you have options.

What to do ❓ I'd put Taylor in the same range as Pollard ... while he's tough to sit, he's a major risk for single-digit fantasy points and is far closer to RB25 (or lower) than RB15.

Yeah, we've got some big names in this week's article. Similar to Pollard and Taylor at running back, Wilson has long been a good bet for "must-start" volume ... but things have changed a bit over the second half of this season. He's seen fewer than seven targets in three of the last five games, logged fewer than 45 receiving yards in all three of those contests and hasn't scored a touchdown since Week 5.

As a result, he's averaging just 7.8 fantasy points per game since Week 6 ... and his season-low of just 3.6 points came against the Miami Dolphins in Week 7. Wilson caught just two of three targets for 26 yards in that game. And it wasn't all that shocking: no defense has faced fewer targets per game to wide receivers than Miami (14.0) and they've allowed the fourth-fewest fantasy points per game to the position as a result. Geno Smith being active didn't help much last week — Wilson logged just three catches for 39 yards — and the prevalence of the run game and growing contributions from Adonai Mitchell have made Wilson far less reliable than he once was. With a floor in the low single digits and a realistic ceiling in the low teens, he should be actively avoided in typical leagues.

What to do ❓ Wilson is a legitimate sit candidate, decently outside WR2 range in my book. I'd rather look to lesser names with better opportunities like KC Concepcion, Carnell Tate, Stefon Diggs and Antonio Williams or Ladd McConkey.

Keenan Allen had a strong first game with the 49ers, catching five of seven targets for 61 yards against the lackluster Cowboys defense. Then, he had an expectedly down game against the elite Vikings defense, with just three catches for 38 yards. Unfortunately, his outlook against the Seahawks this Sunday is much more likely to emulate the former than the latter.

Seattle is one of very few teams allowing fewer fantasy points per game to wide receivers ... in fact, the Seahawks are No. 1 in that metric, allowing an absurdly low 16.9 PPG. Only one wideout has topped 11 fantasy points against Seattle since Week 6 (Luther Burden III), and only three have done so all year (also Mike Evans and Terry McLaurin). With Brock Purdy playing like he has been, he'll probably hit a big pass or two, and throw a touchdown or two, but those plays are much more likely to go to Evans, George Kittle, Christian McCaffrey and even Deebo Samuel Sr. Allen is likely to be the odd-man out in a tough matchup and should be benched in all formats.

What to do ❓ Allen isn't even a good FLEX option this week, do whatever you can to bench him in Week 12 (and stow him away for better matchups in the playoffs).

Kyle Pitts Sr. had a strong stretch in the middle of the season that convinced fantasy managers that he was a reliable TE1 once again. It was a mirage. Over the last three weeks, he's totaled just 10.4 fantasy points, and even when he scored 10.7 back in Week 7 (thanks to a touchdown), he managed just 22 receiving yards. Outside of that trio of games from Weeks 5-7, he's logged fewer than five catches, 50 or fewer yards and zero touchdowns in every other week this season.

And if the inconsistency wasn't enough to turn you away, the matchup with the Vikings should be. They've allowed the second-fewest fantasy points per game to the position (7.8) and had held every tight end they'd faced to single-digit fantasy points until Kittle scored a pair of touchdowns last week (on just three catches and 34 yards, by the way). Barring an extremely lucky touchdown, Pitts will be a massive bust once again in Week 12. Not the kind of bet you want to make amid a playoff push.

What to do ❓ Sit Pitts for just about any viable streaming option. That list includes guys like Chig Okonkwo, Mike Gesicki, Brenton Strange and possibly even Dalton Schultz (if healthy) or Darren Waller.
