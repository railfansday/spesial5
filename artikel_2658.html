5 Bears Takes: Grading The First Half and Dissecting The NFC North

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Following a fast and furious nine games, the Chicago Bears enjoyed their bye over the weekend. In the process, they watched two divisional foes beat each other up and got a live look at their Week 11 opponent as the Panthers beat them at home. The playoff picture continues to take shape, and the Bears will start practice early next week as they gear up for a second-half run. We'll cover all of that, and more in a special installment of 5 Bears Takes.

1. Grading out the offense over the first nine games of the season.

Before the season, head coach Ben Johnson spoke about wanting to set the NFL's scoring record. While some took those words and ran with them, the Bears were well on pace after a Week 1 that produced 59 points on the road. Since that point, the offense has yet to break the 40-point mark again, but they've eclipsed the 30-point mark four separate times. All of which were with starting quarterback Caleb Williams under center.

Through nine games, the offense ranks within the Top 5 in yards per game (383), Top 10 in points per game (26.8), 11th in EPA/play, and 10th in success rate. In total, their numbers are right around where they sat for the majority of last season, with one major caveat: They missed their starting quarterback for a third of the season (so far). They've also faced four of the Top 10 defenses in the league through that stretch.

Overall, there's still plenty of meat left on the bone when looking at this unit's production and what it could (or should) be. With better health at the quarterback position and no clunkers in the second half of the season, the Bears have a real opportunity to finish as a Top 5 offense. Part of that will depend on health at key positions like running back and receiver, but the offensive line will play a key part, too. We've seen multiple flashes of dominance, but there's still plenty to clean up, especially when it comes to penalties and self-inflicted mistakes. With a better-than-expected defense, they've been able to control most games, but for them to take the next step and be scarier down the stretch, the offense must be more consistent.

2. Grading out the defense over the first nine games of the season.

Coming into the season, the defense felt like the Achilles' heel of what could be a Super Bowl contender. During the offseason, the front office did very little to improve the defensive line, and through the first month and a half of the season, it seemed clear the interior defensive line wouldn't be good enough. Despite what their depth chart has looked like on paper, they've performed better than expected, even before the acquisition of defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons.

Through nine games, the defense ranks 1st in third-down percentage (22.8%), Top 5 in fourth-down percentage (30.8%), Top 10 in EPA/play, yards per game, and sacks, and gives up just 20.4 points per game. It's also worth mentioning that they were 9th in pressure percentage coming into Week 10. With Simmons in the mix on the interior, the biggest hole this group has is at cornerback. The hope is that with a healthy Malik Muhammad, either Jaylon Johnson or Tyrique Stevenson can step into the spot opposite the rookie and provide consistency and stability. With any luck, they'll get Coby Bryant back at some point in December. It's worth pointing out how well Cam Lewis has played since taking over the starting spot opposite first-round rookie Dillon Thieneman.

With the divisional defenses starting to shake out, this group must continue its upward trajectory. Getting back Dayo Odeyingbo should help the defensive line, but Austin Booker, Montez Sweat, and even Grady Jarrett have been noticeably better since the arrival of Simmons. Many expected the Bears to make another move or two before the deadline, but with nothing materializing, they're stuck with what they have. Similar to the offense, health will be key down the stretch, but overall, the team should feel pretty good about where they stand. The only real concern heading into the second half is how well their cornerbacks can perform against top offenses.

3. Dissecting the NFC North after another hectic week that saw the Bears drop from first to third place.

With the Bears' bye week, they were the only team in the division that was not in play on Sunday. The Detroit Lions took their crack at the piñata that has become the New England Patriots in the early-morning slot, and outside of the opening drive, it wasn't much of a game. The Patriots lost quarterback Drake Maye early, and, to put it nicely, backup quarterback Tommy DeVito was ineffective. For as impressive as the Lions were, they lost another star defender when Aidan Hutchinson went down with what appeared to be a serious knee injury following a sack of Maye on the first drive. The play (and subsequent celebration) took two of the most impactful players out of the game before it could really even get going. Although the Lions have been playing much better defense as of late, losing their top two leaders, Jack Campbell and Hutchinson, will wear on them as the season goes on.

During the next time slot, the Packers hosted the Kirk Cousins-led Vikings. What looked like a good matchup on paper turned into a season sweep for Minnesota, following a 30-10 drubbing. The Vikings' defense continues to be one of the best overall units in the league, and with Cousins under center, the offense looked more cohesive, even if it wasn't a perfectly played game. Cousins has plenty of familiarity with the offense, so it's fair to assume that he'll continue to hit the ground running. For the Packers, it's hard to know whether Sunday's 20-point loss was simply a bad day at the office, or a sign of things to come. Green Bay has shown clear holes all season, and although they've looked considerably better over the last month, there are still ways to attack them.

All in all, the division once again had another winning week. With the Lions and Vikings both winning, there's a three-way tie at the top with all three teams sitting at 7-2. The Packers will go on an abbreviated bye week before going to Los Angeles to face a red-hot Rams team on Thanksgiving Eve. Ultimately, their bye week will amount to the same number of days off as it would if they played on Thursday night and then played a Monday night game the following week.

It's clear that all four teams are playoff-caliber, but it remains to be seen if all four can make the playoffs come January. With Sunday's results, the Packers have now fallen a game and a half behind everyone else, while sporting a 1-3 record within the division. Even more troubling is that their 4-4 conference record won't help them much in tie-breakers once more games are played. Heading into Week 11, all four teams fill out the seven teams that would comprise the NFC playoff picture, but with plenty of head-to-heads remaining (both in the division and the conference), this is still a long way from a resolution.

Barring a surprise, the Packers will find themselves a full two games back of first place. It's just a matter of how many teams will be sitting at 8-2 by the conclusion of next Sunday. It's also worth noting that Green Bay and Minnesota have just two divisional games remaining, while Detroit has three, and Chicago still has four.

4. Taking a deeper dive into the NFL playoff picture: The AFC is taking shape, while the NFC is still a nine-team race.

As we turn the page on Week 10, the playoff picture is truly taking shape in both conferences. The elite teams seem clear, as do the handful of teams that are all but out of the playoff picture. There aren't many middling teams in either conference, but there are more in the AFC than the NFC through 10 weeks. Taking a deeper dive into each conference, each race is becoming clear.

On the outside looking in: New England Patriots (5-5), Baltimore Ravens (4-5), Las Vegas Raiders (4-6)

The top of the AFC is loaded with top-tier teams. The Bills are tied with the 49ers for the best record in the league at (8-1) and have a favorable schedule down the stretch. In the AFC West, the Chiefs hold the tie-breaker over the Broncos, but with both games already in the books, it'll come down to whoever has the best divisional record. The loser of the West should take the fifth seed. The Jaguars and Bengals are both also sitting at 7-2, and an argument could be made for either team being right up there with the Bills. Barring a surprise with Jacoby Brissett under center, the AFC North looks like a two-team race, with the loser grabbing the sixth seed. The seventh seed will likely come down to the Browns and Patriots, but the Ravens have an outside shot if they can field a better offense. A few weeks ago, I would have taken the Patriots to earn that final Wild Card spot, but with their injuries and how bad they have played over their last two games, it feels like a true toss-up between the two. The Browns are a younger team and maybe aren't as talented as the Patriots, but sometimes the season's context matters. Houston's loss on Sunday against the Browns all but shut down their slim playoff hopes.

Flipping over to the NFC, it's a similar story, with a handful of clear "top teams." Yet, unlike the AFC, there's going to be a division winner (or two) who will finish with a worse record than most (if not all of) the Wild Card teams. While the 49ers currently hold the No. 1 seed, they'll see a handful of playoff-caliber teams over their final eight games. Here's a look at the current NFC playoff picture:

On the outside looking in: Seattle Seahawks (6-4), Los Angeles Rams (6-4), Dallas Cowboys (4-6)

While many had the 49ers as a potential playoff team, it's hard to imagine that anyone had them with a commanding two-game lead in a division with the defending Super Bowl Champions and this year's overwhelming Super Bowl favorites. Yet, here we are. Not only are the 49ers 8-1, but they are 3-0 within the division. Ultimately, they are likely to win most tie-breaking scenarios within the division, but they still have games against Minnesota, Kansas City, Seattle, and Los Angeles. The NFC North is a gauntlet, and that doesn't appear to change any time soon. There are still plenty of head-to-head matchups that will help shape the final standings, but for now the Lions hold the tie-breaker over both Minnesota and Chicago due to their divisional record (and head-to-head). I'm still not convinced all four will make the playoffs, but right now it seems like a good bet they'll send at least three. The Packers vs. Rams on Thanksgiving Eve will be big for tie-breaking purposes, as both teams come off bye weeks. The NFC East and NFC South are both disasters. We'll see if the Giants can maintain a record above .500, but the winner of the South seems less likely to hold that honor. Ultimately, the last three playoff spots will come from the North and West. The Cowboys have an outside chance, but at this point, that's likely more a divisional conversation than anything to do with the Wild Card.

Barring some great surprises, it could be argued that at least 10 of the 14 playoff teams are already "set". We'll see how the back end of each Wild Card race settles, but outside of that, the NFC South is the only division that is really up for grabs and could add or subtract someone from the overall playoff picture.

5. A second-half look ahead: With eight games remaining, what's the magic number to not only make the playoffs but secure a second consecutive NFC North title?

As highlighted above, the Bears head into their final eight games of the season with the hardest remaining schedule in the league based on current records. For reference, the .630 winning percentage is almost .200 points higher than the winning percentage they have faced in their current schedule. It's also worth mentioning that this includes a three-win Saints team on Sunday and a one-win Dolphins team in Week 14.

At 7-2, Chicago's current playoff probability sits in the high 80s, according to The Athletic and ESPN. They hold a game and a half lead on the trio of the Packers, Rams, and Seahawks, while already holding the head-to-head tiebreaker over Seattle.

Ultimately, 11 wins should get the Bears into the playoffs, but it's highly unlikely they'll outright win the division like they did last year. While they sit at 5-2 in the conference right now, their only two losses have come within the division. To make matters worse, they are the only team in the NFC North without a divisional win, after losses to the Vikings in Week 1 and the Packers in Week 5. The good news is that they still have four divisional games remaining. The bad news is that three of the four matchups will come over the final three games of the season.

Assuming they take care of business and beat the only two teams currently under .500 that they'll play, that would leave them at nine wins. Add in two divisional wins, and that leaves them at 2-4 within the division. Thus, the chances of 11 wins being enough to earn another NFC North title seem highly unlikely.

Finishing at 11-6 would mean a 4-4 finish, which in the grand scheme of things wouldn't be a terrible finish considering their strength of schedule. Matching their win total from a season ago with a harder schedule could be seen as a win, but it would also likely lock them into a Wild Card spot. While some look at potential matchups as the fifth or sixth seed and see the winners of the NFC East and NFC South, that's just one round of the playoffs. Having to win three games on the road to get to the Super Bowl is not impossible, but it's highly unlikely.

It's clear that the Bears are one of the better teams in football, but their remaining schedule will make it hard to finish with the type of record that will ultimately reflect how good they are as a team. With that being said, a 3-1 finish in the division, coupled with winning against the Saints and Dolphins, might be good enough to get the job done.

While 4-4 would be "acceptable," the baseline over their final eight games should be a 5-3 record. Sure, it won't be easy, but 2-0 against below .500 teams and 3-1 in the division would get it done. If they go 2-2 within the division, a split between AFC juggernauts could also make sense. The Bears likely won't be favored in all of their games, but that doesn't mean they won't have more than a fighting chance in each one. Some matchups are more favorable than others, but as we've learned with this team so far in 2026, they can beat anyone. 12-5 might be enough to win the division, but a 13-4 record might be good enough to not only lock down the North but also push them into the No. 1 seed, depending on how San Francisco looks down the stretch.

Overall, it's safe to say that six out of the final eight games will have a playoff-like atmosphere, and although they'll only have three standalone games in this stretch, I would not be surprised to see the Jaguars or Lions game flexed before Week 18. No matter how it shakes out, this team is in great shape not only for the division, but also to be primed for a Super Bowl run. They'll have plenty of stiff competition in front of them, which will help once January rolls around. Embrace the difficulty, and just know that most of these games will feel like standalone playoff games over the next eight weeks.
