Week 12 NFL odds, picks, predictions: Our best bets for Chiefs-Bills, Sunday's games

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Week 12 of the NFL season has a smorgasbord of terrific matchups, beginning on Wednesday night when the Los Angeles Rams host the Green Bay Packers.

What will Thanksgiving Day and the rest of the weekend have in store for bettors?

Our NFL handicapping team of Matt Jacob, Jeremy Pond and Matt Russell combine to give their best bets for Week 12 of the NFL season.

Jacob: In the season opener — a 59-37 wipeout of Carolina — Bears quarterback Caleb Williams fled the pocket 10 times and gained 65 yards. The following week, in slick conditions, Williams turned five carries into 42 yards. But on the last of those carries, he went down in a heap with a non-contact hamstring injury.

Although the injury wasn't as serious as initially feared — and as Williams' immediate reaction suggested — the 2022 Heisman Trophy winner sat out the next three games. Since returning, he hasn't scrambled upfield very much — and when he has, the results have been paltry. We're talking 20 carries for 49 yards across five games.

The per-game rushing yardage totals: 3 (at Atlanta), 18 (vs. New England), 1 (at Seattle), 16 (vs. Tampa Bay) and 11 (vs. New Orleans).

Now, on a short week, Williams heads to Detroit to face an improved defense that has limited its last six opponents to an average of 75.8 rushing yards.

Granted, three mobile quarterbacks have had success on the ground against the Lions. Those QBs: Josh Allen (14 carries, 69 yards in Week 2); Kyler Murray (four carries, 35 yards in Week 8); and Baker Mayfield (six carries, season-high 36 yards last week). However, no other QB has rushed for as many as 20 yards against Detroit.

To repeat: Williams hasn't hit that number in five consecutive games. That streak will reach six on Turkey Day.

Bet: Caleb Williams under 22.5 rushing yards

Jacob: Breaking news: Patrick Mahomes is on a heater. The Chiefs' franchise quarterback has racked up 1,235 passing yards in his last four games.

Mahomes easily blew past his passing yards prop in three of those contests — torching the Broncos, Falcons and Cardinals for 321, 348 and 324 yards, respectively. And his 242 passing yards against the Jets missed the oddsmakers' projection by just 2.5 yards.

This week, Mahomes faces an old nemesis that he's toyed with over the years. In six regular season games against Buffalo, Mahomes has averaged 258.7 passing yards, reaching at least 250 air yards four times (all in the last five meetings). Toss in four postseason games, and that average jumps to 271.5.

The Bills enter this matchup ranked 19th in passing yards allowed. That ranking looks worse considering the QBs the Bills have defended in the last four games: Tyler Huntley, Joe Fagnano, Carson Wentz, Max Brosmer, Geno Smith, Cade Klubnik and Kyle McCord.

In other words, seven QBs who will never be confused with Patrick Mahomes.

So of course, after all that build up, I'm … fading Mahomes in Buffalo. The reason? Weather. The Thanksgiving Night forecast in Orchard Park, N.Y., calls for near-freezing temps, steady rain and gusty winds — not exactly ideal for downfield passing.

Keep this in mind, too: Mahomes has done most of his damage against the Bills at home. In four visits to Buffalo (including one postseason contest), he's thrown for 196, 215, 225 and 250 yards — that's 221.5 per game.

Pond: If there is a team entering Week 12 with a major chip on its shoulder, it's Jacksonville.

The Jaguars are coming off a gutting 23-18 road loss against the New York Giants that featured a controversial call negating a Parker Washington TD midway through the fourth quarter that everyone was talking about during and after the contest.

The Jaguars cruised to a 40-23 triumph just two weeks ago against Tennessee (now 1-9) in a game where quarterback Trevor Lawrence threw for three touchdowns and a season-high 352 yards. Jacksonville will be looking to take last week's frustrations out on the AFC South cellar-dwelling Titans.

Lawrence gets another crack at an opponent Dak Prescott just torched for 325 yards on 28-for-33 passing attempts and four touchdowns.

Lawrence has thrown for at least three touchdowns in his last five games and I think he'll have just as much success in the rematch facing a team that has given up a stunning 134 points in its last three games.

Bet: Trevor Lawrence over 2.5 passing TDs (+122, FanDuel)

Russell: We've got a couple of general principles in play with regards to a bet that doesn't sound like a good idea, but probably is:

Anytime a team goes from a sizable home underdog (Giants +6 vs. Jaguars) to a sizable road favorite, it probably means there's been an overreaction to one recent result.

If an awful QB is replaced, downgrading that team because they're without that quarterback is often a mistake.

The last time that J.J. McCarthy and the Giants were road favorites, they trailed the Eagles in Joe Milton's first start before Jameis Winston stepped in for a touchdown drive that helped New York cover a point spread shorter than this one.

The Giants got out to a big lead last week against Jacksonville, on the strength of a pass rush that rattled Trevor Lawrence just enough in front of a raucous Giants' home crowd.

The Colts have finally unplugged the Daniel Jones era, after season-long metrics worse than only Justin Herbert, and have turned to Anthony Richardson.

The 2023 fourth-overall pick hasn't started since the 2024 season — when his passing game needed work — but he excelled on the ground, running for 499 yards and eight touchdowns. That element alone should provide the Colts offense with a different look and a game plan that should combine an already solid run game with Jonathan Taylor, the use of Richardson's legs to avoid the Giants' pass rush nad extend drives, and increased efficiency in the red zone.

If the Colts, coming off a mini-bye from playing last Thursday, can get healthier on the offensive line and at cornerback, they may be live for an upset.

Pond: Inclement weather will likely be a factor when San Francisco, the current favorite to win the Super Bowl, hosts struggling Seattle in a must-win game for the reigning champion Seahawks.

The latest forecast shows a 85% chance of rain in Santa Clara, Ca., with temperatures hovering in the low-to-mid 60s. Another factor Mother Nature could throw into the mix is some wind, which is currently forecasted to be gusting at 15 miles per hour at Levi's Stadium.

Put that all together and we're probably going to see both franchises use their balanced, versatile ground games more in this NFC West clash.

And while the focus for bettors will likely lean toward Christian McCaffrey to have a big game, I think his backfield partner provides plenty of value in his rushing-yardage prop.

Kaelon Black has been a fabulous complement to McCaffrey and the 49ers offense, rushing for 364 yards (currently best among rookies) and two touchdowns. San Francisco has done a great job preserving McCaffrey's legs and keeping him healthy, largely in part to Black's success.

Black has cleared his prop number in four of his last five outings, rushing for at least 34 yards in every game during that stretch. I think we'll see more of Black (40 combined carries in his last four games) and that will get him past his yardage total.

I'm also backing Zach Charbonnet to find the end zone. Following a Nov. 2 loss to Chicago where the Seattle running back had six carries for 23 yards in his first game of the season, he ripped off a combined 36 carries for 171 yards in victories against Arizona and Las Vegas.

Charbonnet doesn't have a touchdown yet, but I think this affair sets up perfectly for him to cash for his anytime TD backers. With the aforementioned weather conditions likely meaning more rushing opportunities, expect a heavy red-zone workload for Charbonnet to lead to touchdown glory.

Bets: Kaelon Black over 19.5 rushing yards (-114) and Zach Charbonnet anytime touchdown (+120) at FanDuel
