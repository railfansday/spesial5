Not extending Ja’Quan McMillian would be a mistake

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The news breaking of the $67M contract extension for cornerback Riley Moss was well-received by most fans. As it should, he's been the primary focus of targets with Pat Surtain II opposite him and he has held up as well as any number one guy on any defense. He probably could have gotten close to $20M a year and Denver got him for $16.75. A very good deal.

That said, it would still be a mistake for the Denver Broncos to let fellow cornerback Ja'Quan McMillian walk in free agency. McMillian is just a playmaker and you cannot put a price tag on that or find any easy replacement. They will have same issue if/when Marvin Mims Jr. walks, although he has been a bit quiet this year so far on the playmaker side of things.

We asked fans a month ago who they felt the Broncos should keep if they only kept one and the vote was quite literally one-sided:

The funny part about that is even those of us who voted McMillian would also love to see Riley Moss around too. He has been a very good outside cornerback, but McMillian has made the big plays in key moments. Those monster plays stick with you when you think about who has made a bigger impact.

Hopefully Denver finds a way to keep both and gets McMillian locked down long-term ahead of free agency next year.

Broncos vs. Raiders: 3 Things to Watch in Week 11

The Denver Broncos host the Las Vegas Raiders on Sunday. Here are three storylines I'll be watching closely in their Week 11 matchup.

How the Denver Broncos can beat the Las Vegas Raiders

In order for the Denver Broncos to defeat the Las Vegas Raiders on Sunday, here are three things they need to accomplish.

Broncos looking to start second half of season on a high note

The Denver Broncos are back to business this week and will host the Las Vegas Raiders on Sunday. Despite a 4-6 record, Head Coach Sean Payton knows they'll be a tough team to beat.

On if he can tell what the bye week did for the team physically

"It's hard to tell. I think you never can control obviously your bye. I'm sure there are a handful of players that may not have been able to play had we have played last Sunday that will have a chance of playing this Sunday. I thought we had good work. It was a good full practice longer than two weeks ago. Yes, it went well."

On if he knows Raiders Head Coach Klint Kubiak

"I know his father [former Broncos Head Coach Gary Kubiak]. I know he was here. [He's] one of the guys I spent a lot of time with. I have a ton of respect for their family and what he's doing. There are a lot of ties just to that organization. I think already you can see it on tape what they're wanting to build and the early success this year. You go through Kansas City, Seattle, Buffalo, these are all teams that are in the thick of it, and they played all those teams to one-score games."

On if the Raiders' offense has changed with a new starting quarterback

"There's a system they believe in though, and you still saw it in the game they played last week. So they're going to do a good job with the run game, the play-action off of it. Fortunately we've had a week at least to see tape on the new quarterback. You have to defend the offense and then obviously the people in it."

On what he likes about what the team is doing right now

"It's a little bit more than halfway in the season, and there's a lot that still needs to be done. I feel the same way about our team. We knew going in there was a challenge to the start of the season based on the opponents. You get so micro-focused on just the next game and the things that these guys do well. They return kicks well. They punt it well. They force fumbles better than anyone in the league. They're really good offensively, red zone. They'll possess the ball if you allow them to. They do a really good job of the time of possession. The takeaways have been noticeable. I don't know if that answers your question, but rarely do you really take a bigger picture look. You're aware of where your team's at, but then you're also very mindful of the things that we need to continue to improve on."

On how the offensive line can block better

"There are times in a game where there are going to be some looks that are tougher looks. There were some opportunities against Carolina where we could have been a little bit better, a little cleaner."

On how taking the whole bye week off benefits the team and his conversations with Chiefs Head Coach Andy Reid about it

"Remember it wasn't like we split the atom when he gave me that answer. It's a long season. I think guys get away mentally, and physically, the guys that were or currently are injured are here. It's good to have everyone back."

On Raiders QB Fernando Mendoza's first start last week

"It's a tough start obviously when you play the Super Bowl champion. I remember his college tape well. All of us that would go through the draft process. Obviously he's a winner. The things I see really are just from afar, but you see the quick release and all the things you're looking for in that position. I've heard nothing but great things from people that have been around him."

On if the evaluation of quarterbacks in the draft process is the same when there's not need at that position

"It is. We're not going to have a private workout, so maybe to some degree it isn't also. Fair question. I just remember what it was the year [QB] Bo [Nix] came out. The tape evaluation, the grades, the reading of the player, that's very much the same, the time you spent. You don't really try to hurry through position groups. You really try to put grades and discuss players, and you have plenty of time to do that."

On if the notion that rookie quarterbacks struggle in their first few starts is overblown

"There have been great debuts. You could go back and find an argument probably for a lot of [players], and a lot of it may be circumstantial relative to the surrounding cast. This clearly is a different team than we've played the last couple of years. That's the part where you see [Head Coach] Klint [Kubiak]'s imprint, the people that are there coaching from top down."

On if WR Courtland Sutton's ability to win a release in the short red zone has improved since he arrived

On if he looks for the ability to release when grading receivers before the draft

"I think you're looking for stop start, you're looking for transition. I like bigger receivers in our league period. That doesn't mean entirely, but the target radius and all those things are quarterback friendly with him."

On if Lions DE Aidan Hutchinson's injury during a celebration is a teaching moment

Two good players got hurt on the same play, and I think that there are a lot of other things that I would put on tape on a Friday, Saturday that are [teaching moments]. That, I wouldn't put that on."

I kind of speak on his behalf, we sign him there. We're together there. We counted the years once. There was a clip where he was somewhere else, then at Miami and then back coaching. He's always been gritty, tough, dependable, reliable—all the traits that you admire as a player, you see now as a coach, as a communicator. Very quickly I think that team has kind of taken on his personality, and it's been a real good fit for that city. He's real authentic."

On if he ever watches the TV copy when reviewing film

"Believe it or not, in our cut-ups, you can set this setting—mine is set right now—to go wide copy, end-zone copy, end-zone copy, TV copy. So if I want to see the TV copy or hear the audio of something, I can. I usually click through it, but sometimes if there's something that I… If I see an opposing quarterback maybe look like he audibled now that we put microphones everywhere, we have to change them… So I'll go to the TV copy to see if I heard was he changing a play or not, that kind of thing."

On if he pays attention to the TV analysts while watching

"Not really. I know that there are a lot of games televised. The only big difference for me is years and years in the NFC, there was more FOX. Then here, much more CBS. I would say that's the noticeable difference."

On if he saw anything while reviewing film that he wants QB Bo Nix to improve on

"I think the one thing that's changed… Fair question. The old bye week was the collection of… We get such updated information, it's now do you want now's information the week you're not playing because you had it last… In other words, it's much more available, quicker each week. There'll be a self-scout we pay attention to. I like how he's playing, progressing. We keep looking at that."

On being on the ManningCast this week and the reaction from his teammates

"It was fun. They (Pro Football Hall of Fame QB Peyton Manning and former Giants QB Eli Manning) do a really good job. Obviously, I've known those guys for a while. They always have great attitudes and are great guys to talk to. They are extremely funny and they have great personalities. At the same time, they are the Mannings, so they have great football minds, too. It's fun to get on and talk with those guys. It was a great environment and easy conversation. It was all good."

On how he feels about the offense trending in the right direction

"I think even if we were playing great that we would feel really good. I think with the numbers and stats, we will always want more. That's the thing about stats is that they are never good enough. For us, it's always about the win column and being efficient and helping our team by playing complimentary football. Overall during the season, we've done that. When we've needed to score, we've scored. When we've needed a stop, we get a stop and then our special teams are playing really well. Overall as a team, we are playing well and complimentary off of each other. We just need to finish strong. We haven't really done much yet, so we have to keep going."

On if there is an added sense of urgency coming off the bye week entering the second part of the year

"You're refreshed and you get going, so you have a second wind. I think there is something to that. The first part of the season is really tough coming out of the gate after training camp, so you get to the bye and that is always a point to regroup and go from there. I think we've put ourselves in a spot to now make the push and really get going. There is an element to the peak of the season. We're almost to that point and almost to the end in December and January, where the games get really important. Not that they weren't already, but those have playoff implications and then you run into the playoffs. You want to be confident going into those. Right now is the stretch as a team that we can't let off and really continue to press."

On if he believes the offense has gotten into a groove the last few weeks

"We have to keep progressing and everyone on the team will tell you that there is room for growth. I'm not too worried about the things that come with the wins. It's all about, 'Did I put my team in a spot to win the game?'"

On his connection with WR Courtland Sutton

"No. 1, he does a great job of using his physicality down in the red zone. He's a big body, so that helps. When the field gets tighter, he is able to go up for the jump balls and he is able to create space. Then at the end of the day when the game is on the line and it's red zone time, he just knows that is his area and where he is strongest at… When I get to him and he's open, that's always the best part of the play. I know he's going to come down with the catch in the end."

On if his mentality of peaking at the right moment helps him get through the season

"Absolutely. In the NFL, there are so many weeks that you have to put your head down and just keep working. One week could look completely different and one week doesn't tell the story for the next week. For me, it's a marathon and not a sprint. You can't wear yourself out early. You don't want to peak too soon. It's just sort of the game within the game of the season. It's finding ways to get better so that when that moment comes and the playoffs are here and when you have to win the football game, you're not going home early because you missed out on your peak. Coming off the bye, we feel fresh, we feel good and ready for this next stretch."

On the environment at Empower Field at Mile High for opposing offenses and quarterbacks

"We have a pretty tough environment for opposing quarterbacks. It's never easy going on the road against our crowd and obviously against our defense. That's going to be big-time this weekend, just affecting the quarterback and communicating will be hard as well. It's always tough going against our crowd, which is an advantage for us."

On the challenge of playing against Raiders TE Brock Bowers

"Some people think that he's a tight end, I think he's a receiver the way they utilize him in the route tree. I feel like his route tree and his yards per target is very telling. They utilize him in a multitude of ways that you have to be keyed in about. It's all about honing in on the details and understanding how they're trying to use him."

On what he sees from the Raiders offense this season under Head Coach Klint Kubiak

"It's a different offense, like more of that West Coast offensive feel. They want to get started on the run game, and that leads to the play-action and stuff like that. It opens up their offense even more. They utilize a whole lot of personnel, 13, 12, 11, whatever how you look at it. It's going to be a test for us just to get lined up in matching personnel."

On what he sees from the Raiders this season

"Someone asked me this weekend about divisional games. I tell you all the time, every game weighs as heavy if it's the next one, but divisional games obviously carry a little bit more weight in the overall standings of things. The one thing that I've learned, noticed and have witnessed myself is it doesn't matter what a team's record is when you're talking about divisional games because they're going to show up and play hard no matter what. The Raiders have always been that team. No matter what their record is, they always show up and play the Broncos really hard. I wouldn't expect them to do anything different. To answer your question, on film you can see. You watch the games, and they're in a lot of these games against really good teams. Record may not show it, but that's a really good team that plays really hard. Going back to [Raiders Head] Coach [Klint [Kubiak], they have a really good leader over there. I wouldn't expect them to come in and do anything else than to give us their best."

On the wide receiver group's mentality and how guys have stepped up

"I think we got to see it at different parts of the season so far in different games, if a guy goes down here, if a guy goes down there. The thing that I appreciate and I love about our room is the next-man-up mentality. You never want to see your brother go down, but whenever someone does go down, we still have to go out there and perform. To see guys go out there and not only know what to do, but to go out there and perform at a high level… Some of the plays that [WR] Troy [Franklin] executed in the game, he didn't even practice throughout the week. He just went out there, and he knew the job assignment, and he went out there and made plays. 'Marv' (WR Marvin Mims Jr.) as well. Some of the things that 'Marv' was doing were some of the plays that were for [WR] Jaylen [Waddle] and Jaylen got hurt. 'Marv' stepped up and knew what to do. Troy stepped up and knew what to do. [WR] Pat Bryant had a really good game. I think our room—and I say it all the time—our room is very deep. We have a lot of talent in our room. A lot of people may not know a lot of names in there, but their names will continue to get buzzed around the league because whenever it's time for them to step up and go out there and perform, they do that. I appreciate that for our room because it allows us to be able to be more versatile."

On tying Ring of Fame WR Demaryius Thomas for the fifth-most starts at wide receiver in team history

"It's humbling. Anytime someone tells me my name getting buzzed around anything that he [Ring of Fame WR Demaryius Thomas] has done, it's truly humbling and heartwarming because I know how much he means to this franchise, and organization and to this city. To be able to have that many games under my belt, which honestly shocked me. I had no clue before literally I came up here on the podium that it's going to be 125 games. I was like, 'Daggum.' I didn't know that. You go into each season, you want to be healthy. You want to do your best but then also you're chasing a Super Bowl. You don't think of how many games, this amount of games, that amount of games. It stacks up. It's truly an honor. I'm truly humbled to be able to have that milestone be crossed and for it to be around the same time as my brother being here as well. I'm grateful. Definitely grateful."
