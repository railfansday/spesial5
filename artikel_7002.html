A Jewel-Toned Chicago Home Inspired by Pakistani Culture

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Kate Haynes of Noble Studio brought bold personality to her client’s family home.

A room painted in rich eggplant isn’t exactly a subtle introduction. But in this Wilmette, Illinois, home, that’s precisely the point. Just beyond the foyer, a dining room drenched in Farrow & Ball’s Brinjal, complete with a glossy lacquered ceiling, offers the first sign that the designer at the wheel had little interest in playing it safe. And, as Kate Haynes of Noble Studio quickly discovered, neither did her client.

The pair connected over Instagram as construction on the family’s 6,500-square-foot home neared completion. With the architectural details and hard finishes already in place, Haynes was tasked with giving the new build some personality. Luckily, the homeowner, a former graphic designer, had plenty to spare. “We talked about incorporating deeply saturated colors along with a lot of personal touches,” recalls Haynes. “She is one of the warmest, most delightful people, and her home totally reflects her personality.”

What Haynes created for the family is a masterclass in exuberance, informed in part by the homeowner’s Pakistani heritage. Rather than lean into overt cultural references, Haynes borrowed from the richness of Mughal architecture and the bold, jewel-toned palettes of South Asia, weaving both together throughout the five-bedroom home. In the daughter’s bedroom, a scalloped headboard recalls a multifoil arch; elsewhere, vintage rugs lend the new construction a sense of history. “We wanted to have that original depth that can only come from a textile that has seen some life and years,” Haynes explains.

There’s plenty of levity, too: a hanging chair in the blue reading room, a rock wall in the playroom, and a custom circular banquette that rivals the best restaurant in town. Each detail speaks to a family that takes its pleasures seriously, whether that’s gathering with friends for dinner or curling up with a book. “She was just a person who looks for things to be delighted by,” Haynes says of her client. Consider the house a reflection of that philosophy.

An expansive crane mural sets a serene, transportive mood in the primary suite, where “subtle eastern influence and massive graphic appeal” establish an earthy palette, says Haynes. The blush pink upholstered bed is flanked by khaki green Lawson-Fenning nightstands, while a rustic vintage Indian teak bench adds a timeworn counterpoint to the room'så more refined furnishings.

Cornflower blue wainscoting and patterned Penny Morrison wallpaper establish a cheerful backdrop in the daughter’s bedroom, where saffron yellow accents tie the space into the rest of the home’s palette. “The homeowner had seen an inspirational image with geometric wainscoting topped with a patterned wallpaper, and it really resonated with her, so she asked us to run with that idea,” adds Haynes.

“We wanted to create serene escapes in each of the two guest rooms that we worked on,” explains Haynes of this enveloping escape, swathed in a taupey pink. “We loved the Penny Morrison block printed graphic fabric that is on the drapes—the coppery brown is so rich against the quieter wall.”

“Our client is an avid reader, and she had wanted to create a dreamy blue space with a hanging chair where she can eventually cultivate plants that will become a living, breathing part of this space,” says Haynes. A Murphy bed is built into the bookshelves for additional sleeping space when the family has a full house, while the graphic sofa plays into the client’s adventurous side.

Designed to keep the couple’s daughter entertained through Chicago’s long winters, the playroom is equal parts imagination and movement. A modular foam sofa can be reconfigured into a fort or gymnastics landing pad, while a custom climbing wall encourages active play. Haynes’s favorite detail? A hand-crocheted saffron yellow swing that brings the home’s recurring signature hue into its most playful space.

Drama was on the menu in the dining room, where walls painted in Farrow & Ball’s Brinjal meet a matching lacquered ceiling. Getting the glossy aubergine finish just right took several attempts, but the result was worth the trouble.

In the home’s soaring living room, Haynes tempered the airy architecture with a collection of tactile, earthy furnishings. Seafoam sofas dressed in block-printed pillows mingle with woven armchairs, a pair of travertine coffee tables, and a vintage African stool. The mix lends visual weight to the expansive space without sacrificing the comfortable, family-friendly atmosphere.

A deep archway visible from the front door offered the perfect opportunity for a little surprise. Haynes tucked a pair of custom banquettes around a circular dining table, upholstering the snug seating in a saffron-and-blue block print sourced from Etsy. The palette echoes the nearby reading room, while the clever configuration makes everyday meals feel wonderfully intimate.

Founded originally in 2018 as Kate Haynes Interiors and reborn in 2023 as Noble Studio, Kate Haynes has been honing a fascination with spaces, architecture, and art since childhood. Her company has always been about beautiful spaces (naturally), but the rebirth as Noble Studio comes with a sense of mature intentionality to create quiet luxury for clients. Kate brings to her team a relentless commitment to the intangible result of joy present in the aesthetics and flow of a place, no matter what style it wears.
