Is Micron Stock a Buy After HPE Said Memory Will Stay Scarce Into 2028?

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

A big memory buyer and a bearish bank are now at least a year apart on when the shortage ends.

By Daniel Sparks – Nov 26, 2026 at 8:17AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Micron Technology ›

Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE +1.57%) told investors on Nov. 23 that it still can't get all the memory chips it wants. And it doesn't think that'll change soon.

"We expect supply of memory, NAND, and many other critical components to remain constrained to at least the second half of calendar 2028," CEO Antonio Neri said on the company's earnings call that evening.

That seems like good news for Micron Technology (MU -4.53%), which makes DRAM (the memory computers use while they work) and NAND flash storage chips. But the memory maker's shares slid anyway. Micron closed at $707.70 on Nov. 23, just before the call. As of this writing on Nov. 25, the stock traded near $550, less than half the $1,213.56 it closed at on June 25, its record.

Meanwhile, BNP Paribas downgraded the shares, saying memory supply might catch up to demand a year sooner than it had thought.

The tech firm said in March that DRAM and NAND make up over half the cost of the parts in a traditional server. And its inventory more than doubled in fiscal 2026, from around $6.4 billion on Oct. 31, 2025, to $11.8 billion by July 31 and $14.1 billion by Oct. 31, 2026.

HPE's timeline seems to have stretched, too. In March, Neri said he expected high DRAM and NAND prices to "persist well into 2027."

CFO Marie Myers said the $14.1 billion inventory reflected higher costs for commodity parts like memory, purchases for customer orders, and what she called "strategic buys." She added that HPE's commodity costs climbed by significant double-digit percentages in 2026, and she expects smaller increases ahead. But smaller increases are still increases, not the price drops a glut would cause.

BNP Paribas thinks one could. In a note reported on Nov. 24, it cut its rating on Micron to neutral from outperform. It now says DRAM and NAND supply and demand might approach balance starting in mid-2027 and drive average selling prices down. It flagged risks to big cloud companies' data center plans for 2028 and new factory capacity coming online.

Micron's stock dropped 11% that day, and again on Nov. 25, when research firm TrendForce said NAND flash might move into oversupply in 2027. TrendForce still expects DRAM to stay tight through 2027, and DRAM made up about three-quarters of Micron's revenue in fiscal 2026.

Part of the new factory capacity BNP mentioned is Micron's own. Its first new fab in Idaho should start making wafers in mid-2027, and a facility in Taiwan should begin meaningful shipments around then too. But Micron says output at a new fab ramps up over a few quarters. And its other planned fabs, including a second one in Idaho and a new NAND factory in Singapore, aren't set to start output until the second half of 2028 or later.

"[W]e do not have line of sight to when supply and demand will return to balance," management wrote about DRAM in Micron's prepared remarks for its Sept. 30 earnings call.

HPE's 2028 date matches Micron's building schedule. BNP's earlier date seems to rely more on demand weakening.

Micron's fiscal 2028 ends on Aug. 31, 2028, so HPE's outlook covers nearly all of the year. If the outlook holds, Micron could spend most of fiscal 2028 selling into a market still short on chips. If BNP is right, prices could be dropping for most of it.

The stock price looks closer to the second case.

At roughly $550, Micron trades at a price-to-earnings ratio of about 7.4 on its fiscal 2026 results. Based on the average analyst estimate for fiscal 2028, the price is below 3 times earnings.

Even BNP sees Micron's earnings per share bottoming at $86 in 2029, over the $74.33 per share Micron earned in fiscal 2026 under generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP). The stock looks cheap even on that bearish estimate.

One buyer's view could be wrong, of course. But I think HPE's outlook undercuts the glut worries, especially for DRAM, Micron's largest business. A buyer still stocking up at higher prices is tough to square with a stock priced for falling memory prices by mid-2027.

I'd consider buying some Micron shares here, in a position small enough to ride out more days like Nov. 24. If HPE starts pulling that 2028 date forward, like BNP just did with its own, I'd rethink that.
