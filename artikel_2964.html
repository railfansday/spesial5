Nets’ comeback bid falls short against LeBron James-less 76ers

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Few NBA teams can rival the 76ers in terms of star power these days, even when LeBron James is out of their lineup.

The Nets have a few intriguing present and future pieces on their roster, but they definitely are not among that select group of teams.

James was out of uniform for the tail end of back-to-back games, but the Nets comeback bid from a 24-point hole and an ugly first-half defensive performance ultimately fell short in a 135-123 loss Monday night to the reloaded Sixers at Barclays Center.

Philadelphia 76ers center Joel Embiid (21) fights for the ball with Brooklyn Nets forward Julius Randle (30) in New York, Monday, Nov. 16, 2026. AP Photo/Yuki Iwamura

"We found the energy, we made the run, but we fell short," head coach Jordi Fernández said. "At the end of the day, especially against a team like that, you want to end up playing like this from the beginning.

"So I can say that I'm proud of the second half that we played. Now, our challenge is trying to put four quarters together."

Joel Embiid recorded a season-high 37 points, Jaylen Brown finished with 31 points and 13 rebounds, and Tyrese Maxey added 24 as the Sixers improved to 12-3. They are now 4-0 in games played without James, the four-time league MVP and the NBA's all-time leading scorer.

Julius Randle matched his season-high 33 points, Michael Porter Jr. contributed 25, and Nolan Traore tied his career best with 23 for the Nets (3-10).

Jordi Fernández ditched the supersize lineup he used in recent games, going back to Traore at the point while shifting Egor Dëmin back to shooting guard as Danny Wolf returned to a reserve role.

Porter was back among the starting unit after sitting out Sunday's loss in Memphis for personal reasons, replacing Noah Clowney.

Dëmin and Mikel Brown Jr., the team's past two lottery picks, remained on separate units. The linchpins of the organization's rebuilding plan came into the game having totaled just 18 minutes on the court together this season, with Dëmin missing six games due to a bone bruise and Brown subjected to a minutes restriction early in his rookie campaign.

Anfernee Simons buried the first five points of the second quarter to stretch a 33-26 lead to 12.

Philadelphia 76ers center Joel Embiid (21) shoots over Brooklyn Nets center Day'Ron Sharpe (20) during an NBA game in New York, Monday, Nov. 16, 2026. AP Photo/Yuki Iwamura

The Sixers dominated boards early, building a 19-8 edge as the lead ballooned to 46-28 on a three-point play by Dean Wade barely two minutes into the period.

The Nets trailed by as many as 24 before halftime following a wide-open Maxey trey with a little over five minutes remaining.

The Sixers led 78-59 at intermission, just three off the Nets franchise record for points allowed in the first half set by the Knicks with 81 in 2023.

"We were getting scored on every single possession," Dëmin said. "That's not something we're proud of."

The Nets' 13-2 spurt helped them climb back to within 10 thanks to an and-one by Traore plus a Dëmin 3-pointer midway through the third quarter.

Brooklyn Nets forward Julius Randle drives past Philadelphia 76ers center Joel Embiid and guard Jaylen Brown on Monday, Nov. 16, 2026, in Brooklyn, N.Y. JASON SZENES FOR NEW YORK POST

Traore's weaving layup, a 3-pointer by Clowney and a bucket by Porter cut the lead to six with under three minutes left in the period. But Jabari Walker rattled in a 3-pointer at the buzzer for a 10-point Philly cushion, 105-95, entering the final quarter.

Two more long-range buckets by Wolf and Randle cut the Nets deficit back to five in the opening minutes of the fourth.

Dëmin knocked down another triple to eventually pull them within four with 1:14 to play, but Embiid responded with his second 3-pointer as the Sixers closed out the game.

"We were a more connected group in the second half on both ends of the floor," Randle said. "Maybe we should punch first. I think in the second half, we punched first and set the tone.

"I think we have to start every game like that, punching first."
