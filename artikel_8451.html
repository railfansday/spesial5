Investing - Theory, News &amp; General • TIPS ladder placement (taxable vs trad vs Roth)?

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

I understand how a TIPS ladder works, its pros/cons, stuff like how to compute breakeven assumptions about CPI, how to build one, and so on.

The thing I am struggling with however is where to put it.

The standard answer sees to be "in a traditional IRA" which at least on the surface (if not deeper) makes sense because of the phantom interest, etc.

But the thing I wonder/worry about is that if I'm going to be spending the cash spit out by the ladder each year (which is kinda the point of it!) then putting it in a trad IRA means I'm locking in having to make real $X distributions every year for years. Which means at some level I'm losing control of my taxable income (or less strongly, putting a pretty high floor on my taxable income).

Now, once I'm 75 (I'm 59 now) I'll be having to take RMDs anyways, so pulling ladder-funded living expenses from the trad IRA will be partially/fully satisfying those RMDs so at least part of the ladder may as well live in a trad IRA.

But if I'm looking to retire before Medicare age I have to deal with ACA and the cliff (though who knows what might be in place by Jan 2029 and how that might affect health insurance in general) and pulling out the cash thrown off by the ladder out of the trad IRA plus income from taxable accounts may well put me over the ACA cliff.

So that gets me to wondering if the early years of the ladder should be in a Roth IRA with later years in a trad IRA. Or maybe some rungs should even be in a taxable account?

It all seems horribly complex and potentially quite sensitive to various assumptions.

So I'm wondering if anyone has some good pointers to readings and papers that address this whole question.
