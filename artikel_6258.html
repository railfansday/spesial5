I Tested SwitchBot’s Tiny Robot Vacuum, and It Reaches Under Couches Where Bigger Bots Can’t

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

It stands just 3.6 inches tall, picks up pet hair and cat litter, and docks in a station that’s nice enough to leave out.

I’ve had a SwitchBot robot vac running laps around my home’s lower level for more than a year, so I figured the K11+ Pro wouldn’t hold many surprises. The robot itself is the same compact cleaner I already know from the SwitchBot K10+ Pro Combo, minus the handheld attachments that come with the Combo. Then I unpacked the docking station.

It takes up less floor space than a sheet of printer paper, has a creamy finish with a faux wood top, and looks more like home decor than a cleaning appliance. It’s the perfect way to upgrade the form and function of our guest suite—adding a cute cleaning tool without taking up too much space or making the room feel like a utility closet.

SwitchBot calls the K11+ Pro the world’s smallest robot vacuum, and that diminutive size is the whole appeal. Most robot vacuums stop at the edge of the couch or bed. This one disappears underneath furniture and comes back out with the pet hair, lint, and stray cat litter that I can rarely reach with an upright vacuum or cordless stick vac.

After weeks of frequent cleaning, I’ve come to appreciate this little robot for exactly what it is. It’s not a do-everything flagship, and it doesn’t try to be one. It’s one of the easiest robot vacuums to live with—and its price makes it even easier to like.

I ran the K11+ Pro on hardwood, laminate, tile, and low-pile carpeting, and I sent it under every sofa, bed, and dresser it could fit under. My house supplies plenty of test material, including pet hair, tracked cat litter, crumbs, and lint.

I tested the mapping, no-go zones, mopping pads, and voice prompts, and I watched how it maneuvered around pet toys, shoes, blankets, cords, and my weird little cat who likes to get in the way of every robot vacuum. Since I’ve used the K10+ Pro for more than a year, I also had a long-term reference point for how this robot holds up day to day.

At 3.6 inches tall and just under 10 inches wide, the K11+ Pro fits under furniture that blocks most other cleaning robots. It slides beneath my couch and beds without getting wedged, and it cleans hidden zones just as well as the open floor.

Corners usually give round robots trouble, but SwitchBot’s Anti-Tangle Twin Side Brush helps solve this problem. The brush extends to sweep debris out of corners and along walls, so the K11+ Pro gets closer to full edge-to-edge coverage than I expected from a robot this size.

The smaller footprint pays off in another way, too. The robot weighs about five pounds, so I can carry it to another room, set it down, and let it work. That makes it handy for a single room, a home office, or a spot that needs a quick cleanup.

With up to 150 minutes of runtime, it can handle my home’s entire lower level before heading back to the dock. When I set it to do just our guest suite, it’s back on the charger long before it runs low on battery.

I didn’t expect a whole lot of cleaning power from a robot this compact, but 12,000 Pa of suction gives the K11+ Pro more pull than its size suggests. I watched it collect pet hair, lint, cat litter, and crumbs from hard floors and the surface of low-pile carpet without leaving trails behind. It’s a solid fit for pet-friendly homes that mostly have hard floors and area rugs.

The rubber roller brush resists tangles. I never had to cut hair off the roller itself, though hair can build up at the ends of the brush. SwitchBot includes a combination cleaning and hair-cutting tool, which handles that in a few seconds.

Thicker carpet is a different story. Like every robot vacuum I’ve tested, the K11+ Pro lifts debris from the top of the carpet but doesn’t reach down to the pad. For deep carpet cleaning, I still rely on a full-size machine like the Dyson Ball Animal 3, since upright and canister vacuums reach deeper into thicker piles.

The K11+ Pro’s docking station is where this model separates itself from the K10+ Pro. It takes up even less room, and the extra-large four-liter dust bag fills the whole upper cabinet.

SwitchBot says a bag can last up to 90 days, and based on how slowly mine has filled, I believe it. Lifting off the top to check the bag takes a second, and the dock comes with a bag preloaded, so I had it running within minutes of unboxing.

The one catch here is noise. The robot runs at a comfortable volume while it cleans, but the self-emptying cycle gets loud for such a small machine. It lasts only a few seconds, though, and I just schedule runs for times when I don’t need a quiet house.

The K11+ Pro uses LDS LiDAR to map rooms and plan efficient cleaning routes, and side sensors help it steer around chair and table legs. Drop sensors keep it away from stairs, and it climbs thresholds up to 1.7 cm, which includes every transition strip in my house.

I named rooms on the map and set no-go zones in a few minutes. It steered around pet toys, blankets, and shoes, and kept cleaning even while one of my cats stalked it from room to room. It will swallow cords, though, so check under and behind furniture before a run.

It also lacks the headlight many costlier robots use to spot obstacles and stay visible in dark rooms. LiDAR units don't need light to map or run, so I didn’t notice any trouble with navigation, but it’s worth knowing if having a lighted vacuum matters to you.

The K11+ Pro comes with premoistened mopping pads you attach to the bottom of the unit by hand, and they work for light cleanup on hard floors. But think of it as making passes with a Swiffer rather than a powerful scrub. The pads pick up dust and light spills, and the no-go zones come in handy for keeping the damp pad off rugs and carpet.

If you need deep mopping, you’ll want a different tool for the job. A wet-dry vacuum like the Dreame H15 Pro Heat or G10 Pro tackles sticky spills and dried-on messes, and a robot with a self-cleaning roller mop, such as the Eufy Omni E35, handles hands-off scrubbing.

Setup was a breeze. I just followed the quick-start guide: turn on the switch under the top cover, open the app, and connect it to my WiFi. I already had the SwitchBot app loaded on my phone, and it connected right away.

I’ve always found the SwitchBot app easy to use. Starting a full cleaning run, picking a single room to tackle, or drawing a no-go zone takes a couple of taps. The robot also talks, announcing what it’s doing and when it needs something, which saves me from checking the app.

If you already run a smart home, it’ll fit right in. The K11+ Pro works with Alexa, Google Home, Apple Home, and Home Assistant without an extra hub.

I love the K11+ Pro, and that comes down to expectations. It’s not a full-size robot vacuum and mop with every bell and whistle. If you want a robot that scrubs floors and washes its own mop, a model like the Eureka J15 Pro Ultra fits that role. The K11+ Pro does something different. It squeezes into tight spaces, cleans under and around furniture, and keeps hard floors and low-pile rugs tidy with very little help from me.

While it’s not the top choice as the main vacuum in a larger home, it’s perfect for apartments, smaller homes, individual rooms, and anyone who wants a dependable robot vacuum for pet hair on hard floors. Pair it with a lightweight stick like the Levoit Aero or another cordless stick vacuum for quick spot cleanups, and you’ve got most of your floors covered.
