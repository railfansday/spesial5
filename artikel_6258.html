American Airlines Officially Discontinues Boeing 777-300ER Flagship First Sales

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

American Airlines stopped selling Flagship First on its Boeing 777-300ER fleet for travel on Thursday, ending the last long-haul international first-class product offered by a US airline. The cutoff removes eight seats from every aircraft in the 20-jet fleet. American disclosed the date on September 2, the day its first retrofitted 777-300ER entered revenue service between New York-JFK and Buenos Aires. Business class is now the highest cabin customers can buy on the type.

Flagship Suites will replace both former premium cabins. The retrofitted layout holds 70 lie-flat suites in a 1-2-1 arrangement, along with premium economy recliners and Main Cabin Extra seats, raising total capacity from 304 to 330. The aircraft, first delivered in 2012, was American’s only widebody with separate first and business cabins, and it serves long-haul routes from Miami, Dallas/Fort Worth, Los Angeles, New York-JFK, and Charlotte to cities including London, Tokyo, São Paulo, and Sydney.

Before the conversion, each aircraft carried eight Flagship First seats, 52 business seats, and 28 premium economy seats. The first class cabin included private airport check-in and a dedicated fine dining experience at select airports. Under the new layout, all 70 passengers in the forward cabin receive identical service, including priority check-in, security, boarding, and baggage handling. Eight of the suites carry the Flagship Suite Preferred label, with up to 19% more bed space and 42% more living area than the standard suite, and Afar reports that they inherit features once reserved for first class.

The wider refresh benefits other passengers as well. Every seat now has a personal entertainment screen, a feature the old main cabin lacked, and AAdvantage members receive free WiFi sponsored by AT&T, which was previously sold separately. Bluetooth connectivity, 4K screens, and power are standard throughout. American also expanded Flagship Dining access to all Flagship customers, and lounge access remains available where offered.

American is the last of the three largest network carriers to drop a separate international first class, following United and Delta, which gave up the cabin years ago. According to Fast Company & Inc., all three have judged a standalone first cabin no longer worth the floor space it occupies. The move has been tied to carriers chasing higher-spending travelers, according to KSL.

The seat math supports that reading. The Points Guy counted 60 lie-flat seats before the retrofit and 70 after, and premium economy grows from 28 to 44 seats. American says the result is more premium seats than any other US carrier operates on a single aircraft. United, the only other US operator of the 777-300ER, retrofitted its fleet in 2019 to hold up to 60 business class seats, leaving American with the larger premium cabin on the type.

The paradigm shift in premium air travel at AA.

Transcontinental Flagship First follows on March 28, 2027, with the cutoff tied to more Airbus A321XLR aircraft entering the fleet. The A321 Transcon subfleet, which serves only Boston, Los Angeles, New York, Orange County, and San Francisco, currently offers 10 first class and 20 business seats, and American has been converting those jets to a standard domestic first class layout. The A321XLR, by comparison, carries 20 Flagship Suites with 155 total seats.

On the widebody side, American plans to retrofit all 20 777-300ERs by the end of 2027. The first aircraft, registered N718AN, spent eight months out of service in Hong Kong for its interior work, an indication of the scale of the program. The sources reviewed do not say how aircraft awaiting retrofit will be configured or sold after November 19, so travelers booking in the near term may want to confirm the cabin on their specific flight. Flagship Suites already fly on the 787-9 and the A321XLR, so the 777-300ER conversion carries the brand across three fleets. Chief Commercial Officer Nat Pieper called the aircraft “the next evolution of premium travel at American”.
