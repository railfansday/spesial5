Random: GTA 6 Hides An Adorable Mario & Luigi Easter Egg In Its Massive World

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

After 13 long years, Grand Theft Auto 6 is finally out and breaking records left, right, and centre. While folks who only own a Switch or Switch 2 can't get in on the action, we can at least appreciate a good Easter Egg when we see one.

Case in point, the game contains a pretty blatant nod to Mario and Luigi if you know where to look. As highlighted by TheGamingRevolution on YouTube, heading to Lummox County in the north of Leonida and locating a ruined site in the countryside will yield a pair of hats that look remarkably similar to the attire made famous by Nintendo's brotherly mascots.

Essentially, at the top of the ruins, you'll notice a near-invisible block floating just above the main structure. Reaching the top and then jumping into the block will cause it to burst, revealing two hats: one is red with the letter 'J' adorned for Jason, and the other green with the letter 'L' for Lucia.

It's a lovely little nod to Nintendo, and one that we suspect most people will never see. It clearly showcases Rockstar Games' appreciation for Nintendo and its games, and it's a stark contrast to the sheer stupidity we've seen recently with so-called 'fans' review-bombing games over on Metacritic.

In case you missed it, GTA 6 has found itself being review-bombed by folks claiming it to be 'woke' while lamenting the lack of 60fps. Some people, however, seemingly believe that Nintendo fans are to blame for the low scores, and so have taken it upon themselves to do the same to Zelda: Ocarina of Time for Switch 2, lowering its once record-breaking user score of 9.6 down to 8.3.

@Lizuka The recent Xbox "glitch" has shown that the game isn't as CPU-intensive as initially thought. It basically unlocks the game's frame rate, but keeps the game logic to 30fps, so while an Xbox Series X could be running it at 45-50fps, everything is faster like it's sped up at ~1.5x.

The Series S using this glitch has a minor improvement, though looking upwards allows it to increase the frame rate because it's not having to render as much. So in this particular case, the GPU is the bottleneck. The game forces a 720p native render before upscaling to 1080p, and there is no dynamic resolution scaling to lessen the load. The idea was that a Witcher 3 solution could be used by shifting CPU tasks to the GPU. With seeing the CPU utilization not being as heavy as once thought, it makes it that much more valid to use this trick, especially on simulations that don't need immediately reactions.

A hypothetical Switch 2 version would not need to render at 720p. They could render as low as 540p if needed (docked), reducing GPU and RAM bandwidth utilization, and then upscale to 1080p with DLSS. Use of dedicated RT cores will help as well because they are not only more complete, but can run concurrently with rendering. Series S uses RDNA 2, which digs into the GPU shader and TMU resources, cutting into the render budget because it can't be don concurrently.

Honestly, Series S tends to get the lazy treatment from developers, but Rockstar is not one of those. If they are willing to put in the time to craft a Series S version to look as good as it does, then they should be capable of making a good Switch 2 version.
