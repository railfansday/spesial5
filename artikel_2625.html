Australia defeat frustrates Wales head coach Tandy

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Wales head coach Steve Tandy said he was frustrated as his side failed to back up their epic win over New Zealand, narrowly losing to Australia.

The Wallabies edged the Nations Championship Test 29-26 in Cardiff, a week after a first Wales victory over the All Blacks since 1953 had led to wild celebrations.

Taniela Tupou, Brandon Paenga-Amosa, Max Jorgensen and Len Ikitau secured a try-bonus point for Australia as Ryan Lonergan and Carter Gordon added nine points with the boot.

Wales also scored four tries, through Dewi Lake's double, Blair Murray and Ryan Elias, with Sam Costelow landing three conversions.

"The overarching feeling is the frustration of losing," said Tandy.

"We were disappointed. We probably weren't as accurate as we could have been. We were held up three times and could have gone ahead on the scoreboard."

Tandy added: "Maybe we needed a little bit of composure but there were enough opportunities to come away with the win.

"I know it's not the result anyone wanted but the supporters were amazing again."

Despite the loss, and that frustration, the Wales head coach praised his side's attitude in attempting to follow up their historic win against the All Blacks.

"Fair play to the boys, after such a monumental game last week with the emotional energy, we stuck in the game and scored four tries," he said.

"So there are a lot of positives in the performance to take as well."

Australia, having soaked up fierce first-half pressure, produced some fine attacking rugby after the break to prevent Wales from winning three matches in a row for the first time since the 2023 World Cup.

"It's sport, you have major highs and then lows, and that's the game we play," said skipper Lake.

"Our frustration comes from the fact that we probably weren't at our best.

"We created opportunities during large parts of the game, but there were little inaccuracies at the wrong times."

Lake, however, said he could not fault the effort of the squad.

"We spoke about backing up last weekend, and we didn't back it up with a result but I think we backed it up with effort and energy," said the captain.

"We had opportunities to win that game, but unfortunately it wasn't to be for us today.

"We'll fix those accuracy issues and we'll be ready to go again next week."

Wales will face Japan at the Allianz Stadium at Twickenham on Saturday after both sides finished fifth in the two Nations Championship tables - north and south.

It will be a fifth time Wales and Japan have met in 16 months with Tandy's side defeating Eddie Jones' team 28-21 in Cardiff just two weeks ago.

Wales have an injury doubt over scrum-half Tomos Williams who has a tight groin, with Tandy expected to make a number changes to freshen up the side.

Australia head coach Les Kiss was happy to have broken a four-match losing sequence which included recent defeats against Scotland and England.

"I am not only proud but also happy for these boys," said Kiss.

"The work they've been putting in over the last four weeks has been good and they just haven't been getting a reward

"Hats off to Wales. They beat the All Blacks here last week but didn't seem to lack any verve or energy.

"That didn't take it out of them and actually created more energy and confidence in them."

Captain Harry Wilson said it was a special to end the Wallabies poor sequence.

"When you have four losses on the bounce, you question a few things," said the back-row forward.

"Rugby is such a results-driven game and we hadn't been getting the results even though I felt like we weren't far off last week

"To beat a team who just had defeated the All Blacks, in front of 65,000 Wales supporters where the atmosphere was crazy, was special."

The Wallabies will now face Ireland on Friday in the contest for fifth place in Twickenham (20:10 GMT) after both sides finished third in their respective pools.
