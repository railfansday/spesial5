AI debt markets shudder as Nvidia's top 'banker' returns

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

AI credit markets are shuddering again with Jensen Huang back as CEO of Nvidia.

Fears about the massive debt being taken on to fund the artificial intelligence buildout climbed Tuesday as Wall Street weighed what Huang's return to Nvidia means for its bankrolling of the AI boom.

Huang is considered one of the top financiers, or bankers, in the AI industry, spearheading deals with neoclouds like CoreWeave and other firms to buy chips, backed in some cases by Nvidia guarantees. His return to Nvidia has revived prospects for the company to continue "circular financing" of AI chips and data centers — raising concerns among credit investors.

CoreWeave, which has become the poster child of AI debt concerns, saw its stock sink 13% and the yield on its 9.75% bond due in 2031 top 17%. Bond yields move inversely to prices.

At the same time, the spread on the neocloud company's five-year credit default swaps jumped 8.5% to over 1,200 basis points. Credit default swaps are insurance against nonpayment and are considered a barometer of perceived risks. Rising CDS spreads mean investors view the debt as riskier.

Huang was reinstated as CEO on Monday after stepping down on Nov. 12 amid allegations of multiple affairs, including one with an alleged Chinese spy. His reinstatement on Monday was cheered by investors, who pushed stocks higher, yields lower and spreads on CDS tighter. However, stocks retreated, bond yields rose and CDS spreads widened on Tuesday.

Ben Emons, founder and chief investment officer at FedWatch Advisors, called it a waning of the "Jensen effect."

The moves suggest "markets see government intervention as 'moral suasion' that carries credit and interest-rate risks," he said.

President Donald Trump told CNBC on Monday he "convinced" the board to bring Huang back.

CoreWeave wasn't the only name seeing heightened concerns in CDS spreads. Oracle's five-year CDS spread jumped 7% to 355.68. Even highly rated hyperscalers Meta Platforms and Amazon.com saw spreads widen by more than 2%. Nvidia's CDS added 3.4%.

Larry Holzenthaler, senior portfolio manager for fixed income at Catalyst Funds, believes the drama that unfolded with Huang's return to Nvidia won't necessarily sit well with credit investors.

"A lot of old school credit people are going to look at what kind of materialized there, the administration's involvement in putting him back in, and I think a lot of credit participants are going to step back and tiptoe away from that a bit," he said. "Credit market investors typically don't like that type of drama or uncertainty."

While Nvidia has commitments across the AI ecosystem, it is the neoclouds that have been under the most scrutiny. Many of the companies, which rent out AI computing power and cloud capacity, are highly leveraged and have relied on Nvidia financing to reduce their exposure to the bond market.

With so much riding on Nvidia's funding, Huang's next move is being closely watched.

During his brief absence, chief financial officer Colette Kress said during the company's earnings call that Nvidia would be "rigorously evaluating future investments" and will remain disciplined in how it commits capital.

That suggested Nvidia was going to be more judicious with financing, says Paul Meeks, head of technology research at Freedom Capital Markets.

"That concern should be put to rest with Jensen [Huang] back," he said.

Investor Steve Eisman, of "Big Short" fame and host of "The Real Eisman Playbook" podcast, believes Huang feels emboldened now that he's back in charge.

"[H]e'll double down on the circular financing," Eisman predicted on CNBC's "Squawk Box" Tuesday.

After Huang's departure, Eisman believed that the ecosystem the CEO created by pouring money into AI companies would likely unwind and leave some of the companies in "big trouble."

"Now that he's back, they're not," he said.

Others are not so certain the level of funding will continue.

"Given scrutiny of disclosure around financing and guarantees … we do not expect Mr. Huang's return to bring a quick reversion to broader customer financing," Stifel analyst Ruben Roy said in a note Monday.

Nvidia said on its earnings call that it has $118 billion in guarantees, as of Oct. 25, to back AI projects.

Concerns about the level of debt to fund the AI infrastructure buildout had already taken hold before Huang's departure and reinstatement.

"These issues have not disappeared just because the White House pressured the weak board to reinstate Jensen Huang," said economist David Rosenberg, founder and president of Rosenberg Research.

John Lloyd, global head of multi-sector and corporate credit at Janus Henderson Investors, believes having Huang back at the helm "reaffirms his existing go-to-market strategy." However, the credit market is still taking a cautious look at the neoclouds as it waits for more clarity on long-term supply/demand dynamics.

Still, Lloyd is cautiously positive on CoreWeave, which he owns in portfolios he manages, and noted all the data point to a "pretty healthy environment right now." That includes strong residual values on graphics processing units and spot compute rates, he noted.

"The company is doing everything in its power to execute," he said. "They don't need the high yield market at this time."

In the meantime, investigations into Nvidia and Huang are ramping up. Huang is expected to appear before three House committees next week. Nvidia is conducting an independent investigation into his activities, and California Attorney General Rob Bonta has launched a probe. At the federal level, President Trump said Monday that U.S. Attorney General Todd Blanche "has confirmed that the U.S. Government does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct."

"There's other shoes to drop," Rosenberg said. "I don't think that the book has been closed on this file."
