Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has just resigned, but you already knew that. What you may not know is how removing the AI kingmaker will impact you?

The consensus seems to be unanimous amongst the many experts we've talked to: things will stay the same in the short term for consumers, but the long-term is going to look very different.

Could a more financially-minded Interim CEO take the choice to pivot harder to data centers? What happens to the AI bubble now the de facto leader at the top has gone after admitting to an affair with a suspected Chinese spy, and then being caught in another?

While there's a salacious story here that's fit for a Netflix drama, I'm more focused on that. Let's find out together live. But first, some context.

In 2025, US intelligence officials briefed Donald Trump regarding Jensen Huang’s close relationship with Alice Huang (no relation). Huang is an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based data center company. US officials began watching her under the suspicion she’s smuggling Nvidia’s AI chips into China.

Spring 2026: A memo drafted by U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio circulates among top policymakers in the Trump administration — explicitly stating that the Megaspeed executive is a spy for China’s Ministry of State Security.

In May 2026, despite these concerns being circulated regarding Huang’s relationship with a suspected Chinese asset, he was invited to join Trump’s official delegation during a diplomatic trip to China.

At September’s White House State Dinner, Jensen Huang and his wife, Lori Huang, attended the dinner hosted by Trump for Chinese President Xi Jinping. The Huangs are seated at the top table alongside Trump, Xi and then-Apple CEO Tim Cook.

In October, the intelligence became public. The New York Times published a report detailing the 2025 intelligence briefings, putting these initial allegations into the public sphere.

November 2026, a second extramarital affair came to light following another New York Times article claiming Huang engaged in a relationship with Nvidia employee Kimberly Powell. Following a discussion with the board, Jensen Huang resigned as CEO.

Nvidia's board has 11 members, including Huang, that are meeting today to consider whether to dismiss Jensen from his position following an internal investigation launched on November 8.

The ten other board members are independent and have the power to fire the CEO on the grounds of breaking the company's ethical standards. We are expecting a result to be announced later today on the verdict they've reached — even if that verdict is a further delay.

Quite possibly. According to an exclusive report from the Wall Street Journal, Nvidia's board "is preparing to oust CEO Jensen Huang" and that it would leave "one of the world’s most valuable companies in need of a new leader."

However, there's no immediate successor who could replace the leather jacket-wearing CEO who has become the face of the current AI arms race.

Dan Ives, a tech analyst at Wedbush Securities described Huang as the "central nervous system" of the U.S. technology ecosystem. Ives went as far as calling him "irreplaceable" and warned that losing him would disrupt not just Nvidia, but the broader tech ecosystem. According to Ives, should Huang leave Nvidia, his immediate exit could trigger a 25%–30% stock drop.

This saga hit critical mass when The New York Times published a report detailing a long timeline of Huang’s alleged affair with Alice Huang, a suspected Chinese spy.

Following this report, Jensen admitted to an affair in an internal email to his staff, and while he didn’t outright admit it was with Alice Huang, the fact he spent part of his statement talking about “national security concerns,” and that he’s “proud and grateful to be an American,” seems to connect the dots.

Nvidia operates a famously flat structure with Jensen Huang actively involved in the company's routine operations. That said, one analyst told me his departure today is unlikely to torpedo the company's current AI focus.

"A prolonged leadership crisis could also disrupt product development or investment, but I think Jensen Huang's departure is unlikely to have any immediate effect on the price or availability of Nvidia products," said Louise Wooldridge, Senior Research Manager, Games, Ampere Analysis.

"There is also the possibility that, if Nvidia's competitive position is weakened by Huang leaving, other chipmakers (like AMD) could gain an advantage, and this stronger competition could improve the landscape for consumers.

"Overall, though, I think the commercial incentives pushing Nvidia towards AI will remain, regardless of the leadership situation, and so it is more likely that little will change in the long run."

Jensen Huang has announced his resignation as Nvidia's CEO.

Nvidia has confirmed that, following Huang's departure, Colette Kress, current Executive Vice President and Chief Financial Officer will step into the role of Interim Chief Executive Officer.

"Kress will continue to serve as Executive Vice President and Chief Financial Officer while serving as Interim Chief Executive Officer until a permanent successor has been named," Nvidia said.

"The Board is initiating a search for a permanent Chief Executive Officer and will consider internal and external candidates."

In a press release announcing Huang's departure, Colette Kress, Interim CEO and CFO of Nvidia said: "I am committed to building on the extraordinary foundation Jensen created over the last 33 years.”

“We will continue advancing the work that has made Nvidia a leader in accelerated computing and AI, and execute on the opportunities ahead. Our focus remains on serving our customers, partners, developers and shareholders.”

The press release ended by saying the Nvidia Board and the company "thank Jensen for his immeasurable contributions to the company and the industry."

The market isn't going to officially open until 9:30 am, but Nvidia shares have already fallen 10% in U.S. premarket trading after Huang's exit was announced.

Nvidia is one of the "Magnificent Seven" tech stocks, along with the likes of Apple, Amazon and Alphabet, that collectively make up over 30% of the S&P 500. A lot of standard 401(k) plans are heavily weighted in index funds that are tied to the performance of these companies. So today's news could have a knock-on effect for a lot of people.

So, who is the new Interim CEO of Nvidia? Colette Kress joined the company in September 2013 and is the executive vice president and chief financial officer. She has previously served nearly 25 years in a range of finance roles at major technology companies, including 13 years at Microsoft.

“Colette has helped lead NVIDIA through a period of extraordinary growth and has a deep understanding of our business, customers, operations and financial position,” said Harvey Jones, the newly-appointed chair of the Nvidia Board of Directors.

“She is supported by an experienced leadership team that has been instrumental in executing Nvidia’s strategy and advancing its technology roadmap. The Board has full confidence in Colette and the leadership team, and we expect the company’s operations, customer commitments and product roadmap to continue without interruption.”

As U.S. markets open for the day, Nvidia's stock price is jumping up and down. I saw it open at 13% down before recovering somewhat to 8% down at the time of this writing but it's set to be a turbulent day for Wall Street.

Nvidia is the lynchpin of the current AI boom and demand for its chips has seen the company become one of the most valuable companies in the world. Today's news isn't going to change that but with investor confidence in AI expenditure starting to waver, we could see some long-term fallout further down the line.

"In the near-term, I think Nvidia will continue to perform as usual. The fundamentals won't change for CY 2027 and potentially CY 2028. A lot of these deals and supply chain are already locked up, Ryuta Makino, software and semiconductor analyst at Gabelli Funds, told Tom's Guide.

"The longer-term, post Feynman is where I would watch for. So 2029 and 2030 are years that may be impacted more broadly. There is potential that this will lower AI infrastructure spend in the long-term given that Nvidia will likely lose market share.

"This can also mean that lower efficiency gains on these servers equals lower AI efficiency and therefore the entire ecosystem will be worse overall. I am still assessing the impact but there will definitely be a ripple."

While this is going on, we're keeping an eye on Nvidia's stock price as the market absorbs the news. Opening at 13% down — wiping more than half a trillion dollars off the company's market value — it now appears to be sitting at 6% down.

Nvidia is set to report its latest financial results next Tuesday.

So now we know the news — the world knows the news and the memes have been splendid. But the question on everyone's lips is a simple one: what happens now?

The AI bubble was starting to show cracks. Does this pop it? That's what we're going to analyze and see what happens.

Because you've got an equal amount of people saying "it's over," as saying "this won't change anything."

Kieren Jessop, Principal Analyst in Omidia's PC team has told Tom's Guide that "nothing will change for consumers next month," but warns that "I'd be more cautious about the next two to three years."

"Consumer is now a small share of Nvidia’s revenue, but strategically it’s the on-ramp: PC users and gamers are how a generation of developers got onto the platform.

Local AI inference on PCs is a growing workload, led by businesses that want sensitive data kept in-house, more predictable costs than per-use cloud pricing and lower latency, with consumers likely to follow later."

In Jessop's own words, Huang "treated that link as strategic," while an interim CEO from finance may be more "likely to focus on data center customers," which would mean "fewer new consumer initiatives."

"The exposed areas are the follow-through: Nvidia’s first Windows-on-Arm PC chip only reached laptops last month and still has software compatibility work outstanding, and the next GeForce generation has reportedly slipped to 2028.

Founders have come back before, Steve Jobs being the obvious example, Altman the more recent example, but whether Huang does is unknowable at this stage. Either way, the risk isn’t that Nvidia products get worse; it’s that fewer ambitious ones get launched while the question is open."

As we're hearing from experts, nothing will change in the short-term. But panic is setting in among PC builders on socials that Nvidia might take this leadership vacuum as an excuse to abandon GeForce entirely.

Because the numbers unfortunately back-up a pivot if someone financially-minded was to take a look. Gaming makes up 7.4% of Nvidia's total revenue.

Once they became the picks and shovels company for the AI gold rush, it was no longer a gaming company; it became a data center company.

And as PC Gamer points out, "Nvidia would be foolish to put more focus on gaming hardware while the AI cash cow continues to provide."

While he became the AI kingmaker, Jensen Huang was also integral to the growth of PC gaming — no matter how you swing it. Now he's out, and with an Interim CEO in town, this could change.

We're starting to see reactions from Washington, and they all seem to be unanimous: strong chip export controls are needed — not just on the physical chips but on access to remote cloud compute.

And if that happens, the global total addressable market (TAM) of the silicon providers gets locked down, and the sudden drop would impact the big three memory makers (SK Hynix, Micron, Samsung).

My prediction if this happens: Wafer capacity is rebalanced in the face of this reduced capacity, and the valuation of AI changes significantly. That doesn't mean immediately lower prices on consumer PC hardware, as a lot of these costs are locked in. But the money has to come from somewhere.
