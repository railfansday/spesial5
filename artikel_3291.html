Tidal Is the First Streaming Service to Ban AI Music

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Tidal has announced an ambitious plan to ban all music wholly generated by artificial intelligence.

On Dec. 9, the streaming service will start rejecting uploads of all music made with AI, and removing any that are currently on the platform. Tidal head Robert Anderson stated that all AI tracks will be gone by the beginning of 2027.

The new policy will apply only to music made completely with AI tools like Suno, Lyria, and Udio. Tidal will continue to allow tracks made with the assistance of AI tools, “but not the sole outputs” of them.

As Anderson noted on X, this decision makes Tidal the first digital streaming platform to ban AI music, and he pointedly encouraged others in the industry to “follow suit.” The move comes after Tidal began labeling and demonetizing AI-generated tracks over the summer.

The proliferation of AI music on streaming platforms has caused numerous headaches for artists and labels over the past few years. While much of it goes unnoticed — Tidal estimated 96 percent of the AI songs it’s already flagged have garnered zero streams over the last 90 days — tracks that do catch on can divert streams and royalties from working artists.

There are also fraud concerns, whether it’s bots streaming AI-generated music, or scammers uploading AI tracks under the names of actual artists. Tidal said it’s already taken down over 65,000 such tracks “flagged for suspected artist-name impersonation.

“Wholly AI-generated music intended to mislead listeners and impersonate artists is another serious problem that has only increased in scale: It diverts streams and royalties away from the artists, and confuses fans,” Tidal said in its statement.

Speaking to the scale of the AI music problem, Tidal estimated that there are 27 to 29 million AI tracks on its platform now (about six percent of its total catalog). Additionally, the company said 43 percent of all uploads from the past 90 days were flagged as wholly AI-generated. While Tidal’s reject-and-remove policy is a novel step in the effort to combat AI slop, effective enforcement could pose some challenges. Earlier this year, DistroKid, the largest music distribution platform by volume, accidentally removed a bunch of real music, made by real artists, while doing mass takedowns in response to a copyright infringement lawsuit filed by Universal Music Group. (DistroKid apologized for the accidental takedowns, and has denied UMG’s accusations.)

Tidal has, however, at least been able to test out its detection tools thanks to its efforts to flag and demonetize AI music over the past few months. Working with Ircam Amplify, a Tidal spokesperson said, “The vast majority of appeals we have received have been reaffirmed upon closer review, which means the detection success rate is very high. Along with using these tools to determine whether a song is made with AI, Tidal said would ban distributors from “delivering these tracks to us” and would “reject delivers if their metadata says they were generated solely using AI tools.”

Tidal said its AI policy “was designed to provide a great experience for listeners, while protecting the authenticity and livelihoods of artists and rightsholders. Tidal will always fight for artists to get paid more. Everything in this update comes back to that. If you make music, you should be able to put it on Tidal knowing it won’t be buried under tracks no one asked for, or copied by someone pretending to be you. We’ll keep sharing what we find and we’ll keep acting on it.”
