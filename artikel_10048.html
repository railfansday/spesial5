Techie rigged off-spec fan near flammable liquid and an exposed motherboard, then left it to run for days

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Nobody died, money got made, and the maintenance crew didn’t care

If you've made mistakes at work that threatened to torch your career, The Register has just the column for you. We call it Who, Me?, we print one every Monday, and you're reading this week's instalment!

Today, meet a reader we'll Regomize as "Axl" who once worked at a petrol station (aka a gas station for US readers).

One Friday afternoon, in the shadow of a holiday weekend, the PC that drove the station's pumps and point of sale stopped working, leaving it unable to operate.

Axl called the manufacturer and got the very unwelcome news that a technician could visit on Tuesday at the earliest.

As the petrol station was in a spot that would attract a few weekend tourists, the potential losses were substantial, and Axl felt he needed to act.

He knew the main computer lived inside a large metal box affixed to a wall, an arrangement that meant electronics and flammable vapors were always kept well apart. The machine's power switch was accessible, so Axl turned it off and on again – and watched the machine come back to life, before shutting itself down five minutes later.

That behavior was a strong hint that something was overheating but confirming that theory was not easy because the metal case included a lock (see previous remarks about preventing fires) and Axl didn't have they key.

Axl inspected the hinges on the box, found they were held on with screws and was able to remove the lid and access the PC.

"Aside from the considerable amount of dust and hair inside, I could see that the CPU fan was not turning," he wrote. "Either it had failed or was not receiving the control signal to cool."

The service station had an office out the back. Axl liberated a desk fan, pointed it at the PC, and watched it come alive again.

An hour later, the PC was still working, so Axl decided he needed a fix that would last the weekend.

"I drove to a local electrical shop and purchased a high speed bathroom extraction fan which I rigged up inside the case, then closed it up best I could with tape and cardboard," he told The Register.

That rickety rig kept fuel flowing for three days.

"On Tuesday afternoon, the support engineer arrived and I explained how I kept us in business over the weekend," Axl wrote. "He just shrugged his shoulders, smiled and changed the failed fan. I didn't even get the 'It's our kit, leave it alone' lecture."

He did get the satisfaction of a job well done.

"No one died from an exploding petrol station and the tourists were happy they could go home," he wrote.
