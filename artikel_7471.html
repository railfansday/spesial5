North Carolina Return Preparers Sentenced to Prison in Pandemic-Relief Fraud Scheme

Two North Carolina women were sentenced to prison for conspiring to prepare false tax returns claiming fraudulent refunds based on COVID-19 tax credits.

“Whitnee Leach and Jeannie Negron helped turn a tax preparation business into a vehicle for filing false returns and seeking millions of dollars in fraudulent COVID-19 tax credit refunds,” said Assistant Attorney General Colin M. McDonald of the Justice Department’s National Fraud Enforcement Division. “These credits were created to support struggling businesses during the pandemic, not to generate fraudulent payouts for tax preparers and their clients. Today’s sentencings demonstrate that the Fraud Division will continue to hold accountable those who exploit taxpayer-funded relief programs and abuse the trust placed in tax professionals.”

“Millions of taxpayer dollars were put at risk by a scheme that exploited COVID-19 relief programs for personal profit,” said U.S. Attorney W. Ellis Boyle for the Eastern District of North Carolina. “These sentences reflect the serious consequences that await those who manipulate federal programs and attempt to steal from the American people. Our office will continue working with our law enforcement partners to investigate and prosecute those who engage in fraud against the United States. We are paying attention. We will find you. We will prosecute and convict you. You will go to federal prison. Cheaters. Never. Win.”

Whitnee Leach was sentenced to 43 months in prison. According to court documents and statements made in court, Leach was the office manager of a tax return preparation business with locations in Lumberton and Hope Mills, North Carolina. Jeannie Negron, who was sentenced to six months in prison for her role in the scheme, was a return preparer who worked at the business. The business was owned and operated by Nejlai Mitchell. From approximately April 2022 through May 2023, Leach, Mitchell and six employees, including Negron, filed false tax returns that sought fraudulent refunds based on the paid sick and family leave credit, a credit passed by Congress to aid struggling businesses during the COVID-19 global pandemic. The conspiracy resulted in a total of approximately $23.9 million in fraudulent tax refund claims, more than $13 million of which the IRS paid out.

In addition to the prison sentence, Leach was also ordered to pay $13,890,697 in restitution and serve three years of supervised release. Negron was ordered to pay $73,514 in restitution and serve one year of supervised release.

Mitchell and five of the return preparers were previously sentenced for their roles in the scheme. On Oct. 1, Mitchell was sentenced to 60 months in prison. On Sept. 3, Tiffany Moody was sentenced to 26 months in prison. On July 8, Shaneen Ray was sentenced to 18 months in prison, Eyoubo McBurney was sentenced to 18 months in prison, and Sylvia Swindell was sentenced to 16 months in prison. On July 9, Katrena Stanback was sentenced to six months in prison.

Leach and Mitchell both pleaded guilty to conspiracy to defraud the IRS and helping clients file false tax returns. The remaining six defendants all pleaded guilty to helping clients file false tax returns.

Assistant Attorney General Colin M. McDonald of the Justice Department’s National Fraud Enforcement Division and U.S. Attorney W. Ellis Boyle for the Eastern District of North Carolina made the announcement.

IRS Criminal Investigation investigated the case.

Trial Attorney Caroline Pearson of the Fraud Division’s Tax Section and Assistant U.S. Attorney Ethan Ontjes for the Eastern District of North Carolina prosecuted the case.

On April 7, the Department of Justice announced the creation of the Fraud Division. The Fraud Division is laser-focused on investigating and prosecuting those who commit fraud against the American people. The Department’s work to combat fraud supports President Trump’s Task Force to Eliminate Fraud, a whole-of-government effort chaired by Vice President J.D. Vance to eliminate fraud, waste, and abuse within Federal benefit programs.
