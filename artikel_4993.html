The 25 Best Places to Live in the US (2026-2027)

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Each year U.S. News & World Report releases a list of the best places to live in the US. While typically these top-rated locales include a number of big players, like Miami and New York, in the most recent list, suburbs reign supreme and major metropolises are notably absent. To determine the best cities in the country, U.S. News considers a multitude of factors, including quality of life, the local job market, value for your money, and the place’s desirability. And while we agree that all these factors are important in determining where to live in America—which is also ranked on AD’s list of best countries to live in—we’d also throw access to art and design into the mix. That’s why we used the U.S. News list as a jumping-off point—zeroing in on the best places for design lovers among them.

Before you read on, a little more about the methodology:

Carmel, Indiana, earned the No. 1 spot on the U.S. News list thanks in large part to a healthy job market. In this suburb about 20 miles north of Indianapolis, unemployment is lower than the national average, and the median household income is higher: $144,615, compared with $83,181. Plus, Carmel packs on the Midwestern charm, with friendly residents, a walkable downtown, and safe neighborhoods. The schools are excellent here as well. Carmel High School landed on the U.S. News Best High Schools list. And though homes in Carmel are pricier than the national average ($477,625 compared with $359,870), you get plenty for the money.

U.S. News determines quality of life based on how satisfied residents are with their daily lives, considering factors such as crime rates, quality of education, quality and availability of health care, and commute times. Tamarac, Florida, ranked No. 1 for quality of life, with Palo Alto, California; Doral, Florida; and Bethesda, Maryland (included on our list below), right behind it. For those who want quality of life outside of the US, consider our list of the best countries for Americans to move to.

On a separate U.S. News list of The Most Affordable Places to Live in the US, Eagle Pass, Texas, took top honors. Broken Arrow, Oklahoma, landed in the top 20 (at spot 12) and is included on our list below. But within the Best Places to Live list, U.S. News examined each city’s median annual household income compared with home prices in the area. And as you’ll see below, good design and affordable real estate aren’t mutually exclusive. Average housing costs in Ankeny, Iowa; Pearland, Texas; Broken Arrow, Oklahoma; Sioux Falls, South Dakota; El Paso, Texas; Appleton, Wisconsin; Farmington Hills, Michigan; Oklahoma City, Oklahoma; and Cedar Rapids, Iowa, are below the national average, and all of these places have landed on our design-centric list.

We chose our top 25 from the U.S. News list based on access to museums, art, cultural institutions, and top-notch architecture. We also considered which cities best fuel creative energy and make the best places to live for designers, artists, and innovators.

Indiana is a highly celebrated state, claiming the first two spots on the U.S. News list with Carmel and Fishers. Texas also makes a number of appearances in the top 10, thanks to Flower Mound, Leander, Frisco, and Sugar Land. But in a separate U.S. News list of the Best States, the top honor goes to Utah, which earns high marks for its fiscal stability, infrastructure, and excellent education.

The Carmel Arts and Design District is at the heart of the suburb.

Anyone who regularly reads best-places-to-live lists will instantly recognize Carmel—this Indianapolis suburb is always a favorite. It has superb schools, low crime, and a thriving job market. It also has a buzzing Arts and Design District, with galleries, studios, locally owned shops, and public art and sculptures. Plus, the district is a major hub on the Indiana Glass Trail, a collection of artists and studios dedicated to the region’s rich history in glassmaking that stretches across eight counties in the Hoosier State.

A train station in Fishers that was later turned into a pedestrian walkway

Coming in just behind Carmel, Fishers ranks second for places to live in Indiana and second in the national ranking, according to U.S. News. And back in 2017, Money Magazine named it the best place to live in the country. Located about 30 minutes north of Indianapolis, Fishers consistently earns accolades for its safety, top-notch education system, and excellent outdoor attractions, including 100 miles of nature trails, 24 parks, and even an outdoor music venue—the Nickel Plate District Amphitheater. The city also packs a punch in terms of cultural offerings, most notably through The Fishers Art Center, where locals can take pottery, painting, and drawing classes, and explore contemporary art exhibitions.

As the name suggests, Flower Mound is rich in natural beauty, with nearly 1,000 acres of parkland; over 75 miles of trails for hiking, biking, and horseback riding; as well as proximity to two lakes: Lake Grapevine and Lake Lewisville. The town gets its name from the wildflowers—including Texas bluebonnets, pansies, and wild foxglove—that blanket its elevated town center. Flower Mound also has top-tier schools and easy access to major metropolitan areas. Dallas is just 28 miles southeast of town, and Fort Worth is 33 miles southwest. All of the above, plus high ranks in health care and a strong economy, helped Flower Mound earn the No. 1 spot on Livability’s list of Best Places to Live in 2025.

Ankeny is a fast-growing suburb of Des Moines.

The top spot to live in the Hawkeye State, according to U.S. News, is Ankeny, a suburb of Des Moines that began as a farming community—it’s even home to a 455-acre John Deere factory that’s been in operation since 1947. But these days, there’s more to Ankeny than just soybeans, corn, and tractors. The rapidly growing suburb has its fair share of cultural attractions too, including museums such as the Ankeny Area Historical Society Museum, the Iowa Aviation Heritage Museum, and the Ankeny Art Center. Both kids and adults can take classes in pottery, drawing, watercolors, and acrylics at the art center. That is, of course, when they’re not busy cycling the town’s 25-mile High Trestle Trail, which is an art attraction in itself. At night, Blue LED fixtures light up a bridge on the trail, creating a mesmerizing portal-like display. The glowing bridge was made possible by a public art grant, illustrating Ankeny’s commitment to culture in addition to first-rate schools, public parks, and a high standard of living.

Johns Creek, Georgia, is a suburb of Atlanta.

This Atlanta suburb landed in the top five on the U.S. News list (and in the top spot in last year’s ranking) thanks in large part to a healthy job market and a median household income of $163,653 (compared with the national median household income of $79,466). Additionally, the average commute time in Johns Creek is only 26 minutes. That proximity to the city also means access to Atlanta museums such as the Museum of Contemporary Art of Georgia and the High Museum of Art—the Southeast’s largest museum for visual art and features architecture by both Richard Meier and Renzo Piano. In Johns Creek itself, The Art Center is home to exhibits, classes, and workshops. Plus, public art—including various murals and sculptures—can be found in parks and along pedestrian trails around town. The town also has its own orchestra, the Johns Creek Symphony Orchestra.

Hoover Metropolitan Stadium in Hoover, Alabama

Alabama’s top place to live earns high marks for its affordability, with a lower cost of living compared with other similarly sized metro areas. The town, just 20 minutes south of downtown Birmingham, also has a great appreciation for preserving its architecture and parkland. In 1997, the city purchased the historic Aldridge estate, turning it into the Aldridge Gardens, a botanical garden that’s open to the public and offers yoga classes, art exhibits, and group tours of its remarkable hydrangea beds. The city also bestowed a Hoover Historical Society Marker upon its Park Avenue Historical District, a neighborhood with architecture that spans from the late 19th century to just after World War II. The district is home to the 1923 Bluff Park Elementary School, the 1889 Overseer’s House, and the 1910 Hale-Joseph House.

Like Hoover, Alabama, Rochester Hills has a low cost of living when you compare housing costs (an average of $388,231 vs. a national average of $359,870) and the median household income ($119,938 vs $83,181 nationally). This family-friendly town, located about 25 miles north of downtown Detroit, is also celebrated for its low crime rates, top-notch schools, and accessible health care—Henry Ford Rochester Hospital is here. The town also offers plenty to inspire art and design fans, including galleries like Rochester Fine Art Market, Subjectively Objective, and the Oakland University Art Gallery, which is in the neighboring city of Rochester.

This Austin suburb offers proximity to the state capital at a more affordable price. Here, the average housing cost is $495,712, compared with $581,402 in Austin, which is only a 30- to 45-minute drive away. Plus, the CapMetro Rail gives commuters a direct route to downtown on public transportation. Design-wise, Leander offers Texas Hill Country charm, more modern architecture, and even a master-planned community of midcentury-inspired homes. Starlight Village, where construction began about 10 years ago, is a community of homes that look like they could’ve been plucked straight from 1950s Palm Springs—butterfly roofs, orange doors, wood-paneled walls and all.

Located within North Carolina’s Research Triangle—the region that quite literally forms a triangle between Raleigh, Durham, and Chapel Hill, and their research-heavy universities (Duke, UNC-Chapel Hill, and NC State)—Cary is a desirable home for anyone interested in science and tech. The city is also ripe for residents wanting to live somewhere that embraces progressive urban design and planning. The town has even adopted a long-term plan called “Imagine Cary,” a comprehensive vision to guide growth through 2040 and create a sustainable urban-suburban lifestyle. Areas of focus under the plan include transportation, housing, shopping, and environmental initiatives. In addition to looking toward the future, the people of Cary actively preserve the past through notable restoration projects including the Page-Walker Arts & History Center, a former 19th-century hotel that’s now a hub for the arts, and the Nancy Jones House, a residence that dates back to 1803.

The Kresge Foundation headquarters in Troy, Michigan

If it was good enough for legendary architect Minoru Yamasaki, who designed the original World Trade Center, the city of Troy deserves a spot on our list. Yamasaki and his family lived in Troy for 25 years, and he operated his architecture firm, Yamasaki and Associates, out of the Detroit suburb. Locally, his firm designed their office building, the Columbia Center, and the Temple Beth El, a synagogue in nearby Bloomfield Hills. Beyond its architectural history, Troy is celebrated for its strong economy (global consumer intelligence company JD Power is headquartered here), excellent education system, and outdoor recreational offerings—the town has more than 1,000 acres of parkland.

Buffalo Creek Forest Preserve in Buffalo Grove, Illinois

The No. 1 place to live in the Land of Lincoln offers all the benefits of the ’burbs—great schools, low crime rates, and plenty of green space—with proximity to one of the country’s top cities for architecture: Chicago. Buffalo Grove is only about 30 miles northwest of the Windy City, giving its residents easy access to architectural marvels like the Frank Lloyd Wright–designed Robie House and the Wrigley Building, as well as world-class institutions such as the Art Institute of Chicago and the National Museum of Mexican Art. In Buffalo Grove itself, The Raupp Museum tells the history of the city, including an exhibit about Potawatomi life in the 1830s and a replica of an early-20th-century general store.

The Delores Fenwick Nature Center in Pearland, Texas

Affordable housing (the median housing cost is $319,753 compared to a national median of $370,489) and a low unemployment rate (3.6% compared to a national average of 4.5%) helped Pearland nab a spot in the top five on the U.S. News list. The city of 123,986 earns a place on our list because of its dedication to creative living. In 2024, it rolled out a Cultural Arts Master Plan aimed at fostering more cultural events and activities, developing a community arts and cultural center, providing more opportunities for children to participate in the arts, and creating a more vibrant downtown. Add to that, Pearland is only minutes from Houston, home to an Arts District with over 300 studio spaces for creatives of all kinds.

We like Broken Arrow—the fast-growing Tulsa suburb with a history tied to the Muscogee Creek Nation—for its buzzy arts and entertainment center, the Rose District (the name is a nod to the rosebushes that dotted the area’s original landscaping). The district is home to multiple art galleries, a performing arts center, a pavilion for a weekly farmers market, and The Museum Broken Arrow. A hub for local history, the museum gives visitors the chance to experience the town’s Main Street as it would have looked in the early 20th century, plus step inside a Civil War–era cabin. Meanwhile, U.S. News appreciates Broken Arrow for its job market, which is better than the national average, and its affordable housing—the median home value here is $251,667.

Lake Sammamish is a highlight of the Seattle suburb.

The ’burbs often get a bad rap for being nothing more than a series of strip malls, but that couldn’t be further from the truth in Sammamish—one of Seattle’s most desirable suburbs. Located about 30 minutes east of the city, Sammamish offers the best of both worlds: access to the metropolis’s tech-driven job market and jaw-dropping natural beauty. Lush forests, tranquil lakes, and the peaks of the Cascade Mountain Range are all baked into the scenery here. As such, boating, fishing, hiking, and cycling are popular activities among residents, as are visits to nearby Lake Sammamish State Park. Add to that, the city is celebrated for its excellent public schools, low crime rate, quality health care facilities, and culturally diverse community.

Called the “Technology City of the South,” Alpharetta is a hub for major corporations like ADP, Bakkt, and Aptean, and offers plenty of employment opportunities—some even say it has twice as many jobs as residents. The average household income is also higher here: $153,928 compared with $83,181 nationally. On the design front, Alpharetta is celebrated for its diverse residential styles, including Greek Revival, Queen Anne, and Craftsman homes in its historic downtown, as well as French country and European-inspired homes around Lake Windward.

Arlington Heights is located just northwest of Chicago.

Rumors abound that this northwest Windy City suburb might be the future home of the Chicago Bears’ stadium, a major perk for the area’s football fans. Other advantages of living in this city (technically an incorporated village) with a population of roughly 75,000 include access to public transportation (it’s only about 30 minutes to downtown Chicago by train), excellent public schools, a median housing cost that’s only slightly higher than the national average, and a super charming, walkable downtown. Arlington Heights is also home to the Metropolis Performing Arts Centre, where locals can take improv or acting classes as well as catch a show. And of course, with easy access to Chicago, fans of the performing arts are only minutes from the Chicago Theatre, the Auditorium Theatre, and the Lyric Opera House.

Like many of the top cities on this year’s U.S. News list, Bethesda offers the comforts of suburban living with access to a major city—in this case, Washington, DC. But that doesn’t mean locals have to commute for culture. Bethesda has plenty going on in terms of arts and entertainment. For music lovers, there’s the Strathmore, a 1,976-seat theater that regularly hosts the National Philharmonic Orchestra; and the Bethesda Theater, home to concerts as well as comedy shows. Meanwhile theater enthusiasts flock to Round House, and fans of the visual arts find plenty of eye candy in the city’s Arts & Entertainment District, with its galleries and public art.

Outdoor recreation is a strong attraction in Sioux Falls.

Catch the Big Sioux River cascading over pink quartzite rock at the 123-acre Falls Park; explore the world’s largest annual exhibit of public sculptures at SculptureWalk; and hit the powder at Great Bear Ski Valley—it’s all part of the lifestyle in Sioux Falls, South Dakota’s largest city. Living here also means affordable housing—with the median home cost at $315,767, compared to the national average of $370,489—plus no state income tax. The city’s low unemployment rate and excellent public safety are other reasons Sioux City landed in the top 50 on the U.S. News list. But, of course, the real draw here is the area’s natural beauty. In addition to the waterfalls mentioned above, the city is home to a 45-mile system of biking trails that connects various parks; this includes a 19-mile loop along the Big Sioux River.

Round Rock is within easy grasp of Austin but also boasts its own thriving cultural scene.

For those itching to live in Austin (ranked #164 on the U.S. News list) but not thrilled about the capital city’s high costs, Round Rock may be the place to be. Only about 30 minutes north of Austin, Round Rock has a median home value of $446,967, compared to $581,104 in the big city, according to U.S. News. But planting roots in this suburb doesn’t mean you have to totally sacrifice culture. Round Rock has a spirited arts scene, including public murals and sculptures in its downtown, as well as a brand-new creative hub called the City Arts Center. Opened in January 2026, the center is located in downtown’s historic Griffith Building, providing 44,000 square feet and two-story-high cathedral ceilings for art exhibitions, film screenings, and live music.

An aerial view of El Paso, Texas, and Juarez, Mexico

Affordable housing isn’t El Paso’s only draw, but it’s a big one. The average house costs $196,914 here, compared with a national average of $359,870. And for roughly 45% less, you get nearly 300 days of sunshine and the largest state park in an urban setting. Franklin Mountains State Park is almost 27,000 acres of desert terrain, including over 100 miles of trails for hiking and biking. Architecturally, you’ll find everything from adobe to Art Deco styles. The city’s Mission of Guadalupe dates all the way back to the 1650s, and the Socorro Mission was built in 1840. Prolific architect Henry Trost rolled into town in 1903, and he and his firm would eventually design more than 300 buildings in El Paso, adding Prairie, Spanish Colonial, Art Deco, Gothic, and Byzantine styles to the area.

For a luxury apartment in a historic high-rise, you might think you have to head to a major city, like New York or Chicago. But Appleton, about 30 miles southwest of Green Bay, offers architecturally unique apartment living. Its neo-Gothic Zuelke Building, constructed in 1931 and the city’s first high-rise, has been converted into luxury apartments, with the building’s original brass elevators and pink marble floors restored. Nearby, the Historic Fox River Mills Apartments are spread across three restored paper mills. The brick buildings, all of which are on the National Register of Historic Places, date from 1883 through 1915. And it’s not just the apartments that are appealing to design lovers—downtown Appleton is a hub for creativity. In addition to live music, the Trout Museum of Art, and the Fox Cities Performing Arts Center, the area is home to Creative Downtown Appleton, Inc., an organization that actively funds public art and urban design improvements.

Snowcapped Mount Rainier in Bellevue, Washington

Located directly across Lake Washington from Seattle, Bellevue earns high praise for its booming economy. Major corporations, including Microsoft, Amazon, T-Mobile, and Expedia Group, have offices here, and the average household income is $172,076, compared with a national average of $83,181. Of course it’s a good thing that wages run higher here, as the average home value is a staggering $1,304,230. But if you can swing the high cost, you’ll also get top-tier schools, an exceptionally safe and clean city, and extraordinary outdoor experiences. Take, for example, Bellevue Downtown Park, a 21-acre oasis in the heart of the city that features a 240-foot-wide waterfall and views of Mount Rainier in the distance.

Farmington Hills is about 20 miles northwest of downtown Detroit.

This Detroit suburb has Michigan’s largest dedicated municipal Cultural Arts Division, which coordinates year-round programs, including classes, concerts, and exhibitions. Farmington Hills is also home to The Hawk—an award-winning 270,000-square-foot recreation and arts venue that includes the Hawk Theatre, where Grammy-celebrated musicians have performed; several 2D and 3D art studios; gallery spaces that showcase local, regional, and national talent; and the Hawk Makerspace, an immersive shared creative and technical studio. The city’s Cultural Art Division also plans and juries Art on the Grand, an annual fair held in neighboring Farmington each June. The event features paintings, ceramics, and photography for sale, plus art demos and live music.

Though the median household income here is just under the national average, both the unemployment rate and average housing cost are also below the national average. In terms of cultural appeal, Oklahoma’s largest city and its capital has a number of museums, including the Oklahoma City Museum of Art and performing arts venues that host ballets, operas, and musical theater productions. Plus, OKC’s Paseo Arts District, a neighborhood just north of downtown, has 80 working artists and 20 studios that take part in an art walk the first Friday of every month, sharing their latest collections.

In the Hawkeye State, Cedar Rapids has a reputation as an artistic hub. In the 1920s and ’30s, American Regionalist painter Grant Wood, best known for his work American Gothic, lived here. Residents can enjoy his creations at various locations around town, including at the Cedar Rapids Museum of Art, home to the world’s largest collection of work by Wood; at the Veterans Memorial Building, which has a Wood-designed 24-foot-tall stained-glass window; and at the Grant Wood Studio, the artist’s former home and workspace, now owned and operated by the Cedar Rapids Museum of Art. Add to that, a number of Wood’s paintings can be found hanging in the town’s elementary and high schools, giving local kids regular access to celebrated art.

Job opportunities in tech, education, and health care abound in Austin, as do activities for the design-loving crowd. Museums in town include The Contemporary Austin, the Blanton Museum of Art, and the Mexic-Arte Museum. The last in that list is located in Austin’s bustling Warehouse District, where old warehouses have been ingeniously redesigned to host restaurants, bars, and shops. Twice a year, the arts nonprofit Big Medium coordinates studio tours, offering a peek behind the creative curtains of nearly 500 local artists and makers.
