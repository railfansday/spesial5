'Steps' Ending Explained: Director Reveals the True Meaning Behind Cinderella Adaptation

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

An unexpected finale reshapes who belongs in Netflix's latest hit

Netflix’s newest animated sensation, directed by Alyce Tzue (Soar) and John Ripa (Raya and the Last Dragon), dares to ask: what if Cinderella’s ugly stepsisters weren’t so ugly and bad after all, but were instead besieged by a Western world that reacted to their East Asian heritage with distrust and unease?

The answer is Steps, a film that turned out to be far more distinctive than the concept of fairy-tale revisionism it seemed to hint at. The relationships among Lilith (Ali Wong), Margot (Stephanie Hsu), the aforementioned ugly stepsisters, and Cinderella (Amanda Seyfried) propel the movie from great to “must-see.”

Its ending, however, might catch a few people off guard. Not because it’s particularly confusing, but because it takes the story of Lilith and Cinderella​​​​​​​ in a surprising direction.

The main plot of Steps centers on Lilith and Cinderella’s efforts to undo the harm​​​​​​​ their ex-friend, the bratty and insecure Priscilla (Nikki Glaser), inflicted on the kingdom of Alouette. All they need to do is get the glass slipper on Cinderella’s foot, and they’ll be transported to the night of the ball before Priscilla stole the fairy godmother’s (Bette Midler) magic wand.

Lilith and Cinderella manage to do so, but instead of returning to the night of the ball, Cinderella chooses to go further back—to the day she silently decided to shun Lilith and Margot in favor of Priscilla and the other Alouette residents who looked down on them.

By going back to that time and protecting her sisters, Cinderella changes everything. Lilith and Margot can embrace their East Asian heritage, and so can the people of Alouette. Fairy-tale creatures​​​​​​​ like trolls, previously seen as dangerous and unable to enter the kingdom, become accepted. There’s even a touching moment when we see​​​​​​​ two men dancing and holding one another on the dance floor, which, while not revolutionary, is still wonderful to see​​​​​​​ in a children’s movie.

At a press junket for Steps, Polygon asked the directors about the importance of that ending and what it meant to show a happier world where people embrace diversity and inclusion.

“[Alouette] is a very small box of a kingdom in terms of its standards. So I love that, not only all of our characters, but the entire world changes from this one kind moment between Lilith and Cinderella,” Ripa explained.

Before Lilith and Cinderella’s rekindling changed the kingdom for good, the people of Alouette were conformists; no one liked change​​​​​​​. However, in showing the acceptance of trolls and non-heteronormative pairings, Tzue and Ripa wanted to highlight the importance of everyone getting their happy ending.

“We wanted to make sure that the idea of inclusion and belonging extends to everybody,” Ripa told Polygon. “It was one of the points of the film: everybody deserves to be a part of the fairytale.”

Steps is now available to watch exclusively on Netflix​​​​​​​.
