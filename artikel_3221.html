Here's the Average Social Security Benefit at Age 62

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Claiming Social Security at 62 is the earliest option, but the check you receive may look very different from what you expect.

By Kailey Hagen, CFP® – Nov 20, 2026 at 6:00AM EST

The average 62-year-old collected $1,424.40 per month as of December 2025, the most recent data available from the Social Security Administration (SSA). Age 62 is the earliest you can claim Social Security, but it comes with a significant trade-off in the size of your check.

If your full retirement age (FRA) is 67, claiming at 62 means a permanent 30% reduction in your monthly benefit. Full retirement age ranges from 66 to 67, depending on your birth year, and it's 67 for everyone born in 1960 or later.

The SSA calculates your benefit based on an average of your wages across your 35 highest-earning years. Those earlier wages are adjusted for inflation and run through the primary insurance amount (PIA) formula to find your baseline check. Then, if you claim at 62, the government applies an early claiming penalty of up to 30% to your PIA.

Social Security was designed to replace about 40% of your pre-retirement income. That share is well short of what most retirees need, so many rely on savings or other income sources to cover their costs.

Gender differences in work history also impact these averages. The average benefit for men at age 62 is $1,572.83, while women receive $1,285.50, a gap of about $287.33 per month according to the SSA data from December 2025.

Claiming at 62 provides immediate cash flow, making it a popular choice for many who have stopped working. However, the trade-off is a much smaller check than you'd receive if you waited until a later age.

The average benefit for 70-year-olds is $2,274.68, which is $850.28 per month higher than the age-62 average. That amounts to about $10,203.36 per year in additional income for those who wait for the higher payout.

You tend to receive roughly the same total over your lifetime regardless of when you claim, if you live an average lifespan. At age 65, men are expected to live about 18 more years, and women about 21 more years.

The 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) that will take effect in 2027 adds about $51 per month to that age-62 average. These adjustments are designed to help your benefits keep pace with inflation, though they can vary widely from year to year.

Seeing the $1,424.40 average helps you benchmark your own planning, but your specific check will ultimately depend on your career earnings and when you choose to file.

Claiming at 62 could actually give you a larger lifetime benefit if you have a short life expectancy. Those with chronic health issues or a terminal illness might prefer to claim as soon as possible rather than taking the chance that they pass away before they're able to apply.

Claiming Social Security at 62 can also make sense if you are unable to work and don't have enough personal savings to cover your expenses on your own. Even if you wind up with a smaller lifetime benefit, that's preferable to going into debt.

Married individuals whose spouse significantly outearned them throughout their career are typically better off claiming early, too. This might enable their spouse to delay Social Security until they qualify for bigger benefits. Then, when the higher earner applies, the lower-earning spouse can switch to a spousal benefit if that's worth more than their own retirement benefit.
