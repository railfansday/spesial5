3 Data Center Power Stocks Down More Than a Third From Their Records to Buy Now and Hold Through 2030

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Revenue for one of them more than doubled last quarter. So why are all three stocks so far below their peaks?

By Daniel Sparks – Nov 21, 2026 at 3:43AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Bloom Energy ›

On Tuesday, Nov. 17, Nvidia said its data center revenue grew 110% year over year to $107.6 billion in its fiscal third quarter. Its largest customers aren't letting up, either. Alphabet expects capital spending of $195 billion to $205 billion this year, and its CFO said the number should grow significantly in 2027.

But several stocks linked to powering these data centers have sold off hard. As I write, Bloom Energy (BE -15.30%) is about 50% below its June 22 record close, Vertiv (VRT -10.14%) is about 48% below its May 14 record close, and Vistra (VST -7.17%) is about 41% below its record close in September 2025.

I think all three are worth buying at today's prices and holding through 2030. But each gets paid at a different stage of a data center's life, which might matter if spending slows.

Bloom builds fuel cell systems that produce electricity right where a customer needs it. This means a data center doesn't have to wait on the local grid to start running.

Showing how fast this demand has arrived, Bloom's revenue surged 138% year over year in the third quarter to around $1.24 billion, up from about $1.07 billion in the second quarter. Non-GAAP (adjusted) operating income grew about sixfold to $286 million. And management lifted its 2026 revenue outlook for the third time this year, to $4.4 billion to $4.6 billion -- about 122% growth at the midpoint.

On Nov. 5, NTT DATA agreed to deploy 900 megawatts of Bloom's onsite power at data centers worldwide, with shipments starting in 2027.

That said, Bloom is arguably the most exposed of the three to a slowdown. New equipment sales made up about 87% of its third-quarter revenue, so a pause in data center construction might show up in its results quickly.

And at around $173 as of this writing, the stock trades at a price-to-earnings ratio of about 32 using analysts' 2027 estimates.

Vertiv builds the power and cooling gear that keeps a data center's servers running, such as liquid cooling systems.

Its growth picked up sharply this year. Net sales growth surged from 24% year over year in the second quarter to 61% in the third, when sales hit around $4.3 billion. The adjusted operating margin widened from around 20.8% in the first quarter to 22.6% and then 25.2%, and adjusted earnings per share climbed 78% to $2.21.

"We expect continued significant above market organic sales growth in 2027," CEO Giordano Albertazzi said on the call after Vertiv's third-quarter results.

But investors will have less to go by from here. Organic orders more than doubled year over year in the third quarter, though management said on the same call that it won't share order data anymore.

Still, Vertiv trades at around 20 times expected 2027 earnings, well under Bloom.

Vistra produces power from a fleet with natural gas and nuclear plants. Amazon agreed, through Amazon Web Services, to buy up to 1,200 megawatts from Vistra's Comanche Peak nuclear plant in Texas for 20 years. And Meta Platforms signed 20-year contracts for over 2,600 megawatts from three Vistra nuclear plants.

Plus, the business is speeding up. Vistra's ongoing operations adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization) grew around 20% year over year in the first quarter, 31% in the second, and 50% in the third, to about $2.4 billion. Management sees $7.7 billion to $8.5 billion in 2027, up from $7.1 billion to $7.5 billion this year (helped by Cogentrix Energy, the gas plant owner it bought in October). As of Oct. 30, it had already hedged around 98% of its expected 2027 generation.

What's more, Vistra is the cheapest of the three, at about 13 times its expected 2027 earnings per share.

All three rely on data center construction staying strong. If this spending slows, I'd expect Bloom to feel it first. Vistra, with most of 2027 hedged and 20-year nuclear contracts, would probably feel it least. And the stock prices already line up that way, at around 32, 20, and 13 times their 2027 earnings estimates, respectively.

But for now, the spending is still climbing, and I'd consider buying all three today and holding them through 2030. I'd just expect Bloom to be the bumpiest of the three to own.
