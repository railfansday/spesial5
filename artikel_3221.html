EU leaders meet in Dublin for the bloc’s first AI summit

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Leaders at the ninth European Political Community summit in Dublin on 12 November 2026.

European Union leaders gather in Dublin on Friday for their first-ever summit devoted to artificial intelligence, debating how the bloc can catch up with the United States and China.

The summit caps a week of marathon diplomacy in Ireland. On Thursday, Dublin’s Convention Centre hosted about 50 leaders from across the continent for the ninth summit of the European Political Community, themed around a strong, secure, and connected Europe, which Taoiseach Micheál Martin called “the biggest gathering of international leaders that Ireland has ever hosted”.

European Council president António Costa called that meeting because, as he wrote in his invitation letter, “a first dedicated discussion, at leaders’ level, is now needed” on AI’s opportunities and its transformative effect on Europe’s economies.

Two outside voices will brief the 27: Mistral chief executive Arthur Mensch, the standard-bearer for European AI sovereignty, and Nobel laureate Geoffrey Hinton, the field’s most prominent worrier.

The agenda is competitiveness first: funding, red tape, and guardrails. The sharpest exhibit is the bloc’s own flagship. The plan to build seven AI gigafactories, data centres housing advanced processors, cloud, and connectivity under one roof, has yet to fully take off, snagged on the fight between member states over competitiveness spending in the 2028–2034 EU budget.

The Commission’s InvestAI programme promised to mobilise €200B, including €20B for gigafactories; delays and funding stumbles have been piling up for months.

The meeting lands after a year of incidents involving rogue AI agents, and a week in which Nvidia lost its CEO, leaving the industry without its laitmotive. Safety, once a Brussels speciality exported through safety pledges, is suddenly everyone’s agenda item.

Also, Ukraine was added to the programme after US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner flew into Dublin to meet President Volodymyr Zelenskiy, who was already in town for the EPC, the envoys fresh from talks with Vladimir Putin in Moscow.

Per a senior EU official, leaders are likely to discuss Europe’s role in peace negotiations and the security guarantees the bloc could offer in a ceasefire.

Europe called a meeting to plan its technological independence, and geopolitics walked in the door anyway. The Commission’s Apply AI Summit follows in Brussels on 17 November; what the leaders actually commit to today will show whether Dublin was a strategy session or a group photo.
