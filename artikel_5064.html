Is Ethereum the “worst asset of all time,” amid estimated $8.83 billion BitMine loss ?

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

A dashboard shared by trader Flood estimates an unrealized loss of about $9 billion, but applies August purchase costs to November holdings.

Trader Flood, who posts as @ThinkingUSD, called Ethereum a “contender for the worst asset of all time” on Nov. 13, sharing a dashboard that put BitMine’s unrealized loss at about $9 billion.

The image marked the Ethereum treasury company 43% underwater. Its holdings match BitMine’s November disclosure, while its average-cost field is dated August. Applying that older average to the later balance approximately reproduces the dashboard’s displayed cost, leaving its loss figure dependent on that assumption.

Flood cited money lost to hacks, development time spent on inadequate tools and retail losses from betting on Ethereum’s price. He supplied no figures for those categories or comparative data establishing an all-time ranking. His criticism targeted Ethereum, with the BitMine dashboard providing a specific example of treasury exposure.

The image Flood shared listed 6,070,092 ETH, a $3,380 average cost, a $20.52 billion total cost basis and an $11.69 billion market value. Its displayed unrealized loss was $8,829,920,029.

The average-cost field is labeled as the filed average for Aug. 31, 2026. BitMine’s annual accounts reported 5,901,837 ETH at that date, with a cost basis of about $19.95 billion and a fair value of about $14.56 billion. Dividing the cost by the quantity gives approximately $3,380 per ETH.

Those accounts were filed on Nov. 10, but the holdings and cost figures describe the August balance.

BitMine’s Nov. 9 release reported a larger holding of 6,070,092 ETH as of Nov. 8 at 2 p.m. ET. Multiplying that quantity by the rounded August average produces roughly the $20.52 billion cost basis displayed on Flood’s dashboard.

That calculation extrapolates across two reporting dates. It reproduces the rounded total without confirming the dashboard’s underlying methodology or establishing what the expanded ETH holding actually cost. The November release supplies no updated aggregate cost basis.

The dashboard’s $8.83 billion loss estimate is therefore an unrealized mark against an assumed cost. It provides no evidence that BitMine sold those holdings and realized the loss in cash.
