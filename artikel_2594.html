2026-27 NBA Rookies Report Card 2.0, Grades Nov. 25: Two players get an A: Cameron Boozer, Yaxel Landeborg

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

No one is going to confuse me with a Harvard professor, but it feels like "grade inflation" has hit Rookie Report Cards 2.0. Turns out, this highly touted draft class is largely living up to its billing, with many of these players not just showing promise (if not efficiency yet), but also contributing to winning. There are a lot of good grades, and anything below a B feels a bit harsh this early in the season (and their careers). With that intro, here's our second round of Rookie Report Cards.

2026-27 Stats: 16.6 points, 9.2 rebounds, 5.3 assists, 41.7% shooting

What Boozer is doing with Memphis is what he has done at every stop on his basketball journey: The stats are impressive, but they don't begin to tell the whole story. Boozer simply contributes to winning, doing whatever the team needs on a given night, and he has been completely at ease as the face of the franchise since he walked in the door. He has been everything the Grizzlies hoped for when they drafted him.

Boozer has been a big part of Memphis' fast start, as has their depth — and that depth will be put to the test for the next month with Boozer out due to a hamstring strain. Injuries suck.

2026-27 Stats: 19.2 points, 7.4 rebounds, 3.6 assists, 45.2% shooting

Heading into last June's draft, the league-wide consensus was that the top four picks were in a class of their own, and everyone else was a tier below. Lendeborg has blown that logic up early this season and should be considered a frontrunner for Rookie of the Year. He ranks near the top of this rookie class in pretty much every statistical category — points (second), rebounds (second), blocks (fourth) — and his lowest is sixth in assists. He's shooting 36.7% from 3-point range and has been incredibly efficient for a rookie.

Things have been tougher for Lendeborg since Stephen Curry went out and he became the guy at the top of opponent scouting reports — asking him to lead the offense and be the focus of the defense is a lot for a rookie. That said, Lendeborg has still played well and is exactly the guy the Warriors needed.

2026-27 Stats: 7.9 points, 2.7 rebounds, 4.2 assists, 48.8% shooting

It took just nine games for Flemmings to replace veteran CJ McCollum in the starting lineup in Atlanta, a sign of how good he's looked to start the season — and how good he's looked in practices to coach Quin Snyder and the staff. He has just returned from missing a few games with a back issue.

He is a two-way player who brings physicality and defends well. In Summer League, his decision-making off the pick-and-roll showed promise — and he has carried that over to the regular season, especially since moving into the starting lineup. What was unimpressive at Summer League was his shooting, but he has found his form to start the season, and while the volume is low, he is knocking down 40% of his 3s so far this season. Atlanta may just have the point guard they were seeking.

2026-27 Stats: 11.4 points, 5.7 rebounds, 2.5 assists, 45.5% shooting

I am higher on Burries play so far than many others, and this early-season grade might be optimistic, but I like what I see. Maybe nobody is noticing because few people are watching Milwaukee right now, but once Taylor Jenkins moved Burries back to the bench a couple of games ago, it seemed to unlock something, he's playing much more free and much more efficiently. On the season, he has a below-average 52.4 true shooting percentage as a starter, but that jumps to an impressive 63 as a reserve.

There is legitimate concern about his 3-point shot (now up to 31.3% for the season), but just watching him play, Burries is a reason for hope for a Bucks fanbase that needs something to get behind at the start of a rebuilding process.

Acuff's running mate at Arkansas, who fell to the No. 34 pick, looks like the steal of the second round. This grade is based partly on the expectations vs. production the Cavaliers have gotten from him — he's started three games and is getting 18+ minutes a night, something impressive for any rookie.

Thomas is a shooter, knocking down 40.7% on 3-pointers. We should have seen this coming when he led Summer League in scoring, averaging 27.3 points per game. He's got room to grow — he isn't much of a playmaker yet and isn't driving and drawing a lot of fouls — but he is knocking down shots in a league where that is a highly prized skill.

2026-27 Stats: 16.5 points, 6.9 rebounds, 2.8 assists, 42.2% shooting

It's a little hard to grade Dybantsa because it feels like he's being limited by his environment. This man is a strong, physical driver who has to wait his turn as the third guy in the pecking order behind Anthony Davis (great start to the season) and Trae Young (um... not as great a start to the season, let's say). Plus, Alex Sarr is a quality young big, but he's not spacing the floor or creating driving lanes. Still, we see Dybantsa's undeniable talent shine through at moments.

Dybantsa seems to be breaking through of late, with three double-doubles in his last four games and 19.8 points and 10.3 rebounds in those contests. The Wizards need to build on that and feature Dybantsa more and let him grow and learn through some mistakes. His grade is this low only because a left ankle sprain delayed his start to the season and, with that, his development is maybe a little behind where it could be, but he's starting to really demand more time on the ball and more opportunities.

2026-27 Stats: 15.9 points, 4.5 rebounds, 4 assists, 40.1% shooting

Kind of like Dybantsa in Washington, the challenge with Peterson in Utah has been fit. You can try to label Peterson and Utah's other young guard, Keyonte George, as point and shooting guards (in whatever order you wish), the reality is they are both scoring guards who want to attack. Then, take Lauri Markkanen and Jaren Jackson Jr. out of the picture in Utah due to injuries and it's just hard to get a read on Peterson at times.

That said, Peterson is improving — he had a stretch of five 20+ point games a few weeks ago — and more importantly, he's getting to the rim more and not settling for midrange jumpers as he did earlier in the season. He is at his best when he is an aggressive, downhill attacker reading the defense, and the Jazz need to encourage more of that and less of him pulling up or taking floaters. Let him be himself, working through the growing pains.

2026-27 Stats: 24.6 points, 3.4 rebounds, 7.2 assists, 40.3% shooting

While some high draft picks are having to stand in line to get touches and opportunities, that is not the case with Acuff — he is at the front of the line for the all-you-can-eat buffet of shots in Sacramento. He's third in the league in shot attempts per game. He actually leads the NBA in average time of possession — he has the ball for 7.2 minutes per game.

The Kings' record this season is what you get when you turn the keys to the offense over to a 6'2" rookie guard, and no doubt his efficiency and ability to lift up teammates must get better. What you have to like is his relentlessness, and he shows he knows how to read the pick-and-roll. There is a lot to build on, but Acuff also has a long way to go. As do the Kings.

2026-27 Stats: 10 points, 7.1 rebounds, 1.9 assists, 64.9% shooting

Thrust into a larger role with Chet Holmgren out, what you have to like about Mara is that he plays within himself — he knows what he does well, where he can impact games, and he stays in that box. He's not going to space the floor (which is why the double-big lineups with him and Isaiah Hartenstein can have issues). What he can do is protect the paint, set screens and score around the rim. His defense has been fantastic, and the Thunder have needed that as they battle through a rash of injuries.

Caleb Wilson, Grade: Incomplete (B- in games he played)

2026-27 Stats: 18.9 points, 6.2 rebounds, 1.9 assists, 42% shooting

He's only played in 10 games due to a fractured hand, which is a setback in his development and the reason for the incomplete grade (something that will change later in the season). It's also a freak injury beyond his control, a fluke.

If we base the grade on the 10 games he played, you see a player with immense talent — maybe the highest ceiling of any player in this class — who has already put together an impressive highlight reel.nBut he is so raw and making so many rookie mistakes — turnovers, missed 3s that he should pass on — it's hard to grade him. Do we grade him on the potential? Then it's an "A," no question. However, if we're judging him as a rookie player, as I have done, the grade drops, maybe B- is too harsh, but it's where I landed. For now. I expect that by the end of the season his grade will be much higher. He will figure it out.

2026-27: 11.2 points, 3.5 rebounds, 2.9 assists, 34.4% shooting

Part of the challenge in grading Wagler is that he comes off the bench at a stacked position for the team. Wagler is in the same space as veterans Derrick Jones Jr., Kris Dunn and Max Strus, and Tyronn Lue needs to showcase those veterans for potential trades. Lue has admitted he needs to find Wagler more minutes.

There are a lot of things Wagler does well, he can knock down 3s (he's been impressive as a shooter of late after a slow start), and he knows how to get to the free throw line. The concern is the 34% shooting on two-pointers, a lot of them from floater range. All of that should improve, and his game will come around if he gets enough minutes on the court.

This grade may be a little harsh for Johnson, who had injuries slowing him in training camp and is just 20 years old (Johnson is more on Cooper Flagg's timeline than the 24-year-old Lendeborg). My questions are: Did the Mavericks draft the third-best player on coach Dusty May's championship Michigan team, and would Mara have been a better fit in Dallas?

That said, he has struggled with fouls on the defensive end, and is not converting enough of his offensive opportunities. It's early in the season and his career, but Johnson has a lot of work to do.

Others receiving consideration: Mikel Brown Jr., Brooklyn (injuries and a minutes limit have held him back so far, but he shows real promise); Trevon Brazile, Denver (the best defensive rookie in this class, already a plus defender); Christian Anderson, Charlotte; Allen Graves, Toronto; Bennett Stirtz, Oklahoma City.
