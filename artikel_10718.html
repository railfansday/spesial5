New app punishes you and your friends for spending too much time on your phone

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

A new app called Island is taking a different approach to reducing screen time, turning your phone usage into a farming simulator where spending too much time on your device can have consequences for you and your friends.

Unlike traditional screen time apps that block access to certain applications after a set period, Island encourages users to put their phones down by rewarding them for meeting daily limits.

The app, which launched in October 2026, lets users build and manage a virtual farm, with their progress directly tied to how well they control their screen time. However, those who struggle to stick to their limits could end up affecting their friends’ progress as well.

Developed by entrepreneurs Ashish Selvaraj and Ethan Pronev, Island uses a farming simulator to encourage users to spend less time on their devices without physically restricting access to their apps.

Users establish daily screen time limits and earn rewards for sticking to them. Meeting these goals improves an in-game “credit score,” which increases farm production and unlocks new crops, animals, and other content.

Rather than locking users out of their phones when they reach their limits, Island sends reminders, including a warning when they have 15 minutes remaining.

The app also introduces a social element that allows friends to visit each other’s islands, compete in group challenges, and hold one another accountable for their screen time habits.

One feature even allows two friends to link their islands together, meaning that if either person exceeds their daily screen time limit, both players’ farms will suffer the consequences.

Speaking to TechCrunch, Selvaraj explained that the team wanted to expand on the concept of existing productivity apps that reward users for staying focused.

Similar applications, including Hank Green’s Focus Friend, have previously used virtual companions to encourage people to spend less time on distracting apps.

However, Island takes the concept further by introducing a farming simulator with shared consequences, allowing users to experience setbacks without “having to torture your pet,” according to Selvaraj.

The developers say their approach has already shown promising results following early testing and the app’s October launch.

According to data shared by the startup, 82% of users stop using their phones when they reach their established screen time limits, while others put their devices down after receiving the 15-minute warning.

Island also reportedly has a 49% retention rate after 30 days, meaning nearly half of users are still active on the app on their 30th day after installation.

The app is currently free to use, although an optional $39.99 annual subscription allows users to establish individual screen time limits for different applications rather than relying on a single daily limit.
