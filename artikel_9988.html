China has announced it'll be redesigning its electricity grid to make it renewables friendly. The problem has been that renewables aren't good at load balancing - in fact they're disruptive to the smooth running of a traditional monolithic grid.

However, China thinks it can bring sufficient compute to balance 10s of thousands of suburb and factory sized mini-grids. Each mini-grid will take around 60% of its energy from local renewables supported by batteries (including ad hoc connected EV batteries). The new mini grids will have thousands of interconnects within the grid, dozens on interconnects to neighbouring grids and then to a new backbone transmission system - all two-way. The backbone will draw on the traditional generating sources but supplemented by seven new UHVDC corridors running from the west to the east.

They're saying it'll all be operational by 2030. I've got potholes older than that.
