Two Major Developers Explain Why They Like And Dislike Making Games For Xbox Series S

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

"I wish hardware like the 'Xbox Series S' wouldn't be released (laughs)"

Over the years, we've heard all kinds of stories from all kinds of developers about whether they either like or dislike making games for the Xbox Series S, and former Tekken boss Katsuhiro Harada falls into the "dislike" category.

In a recent panel discussion that was transcribed by the folks at InvenGlobal, Harada noticed that a senior Xbox official was in the audience, and joked that he wanted to let them know how much he isn't a fan of the Xbox Series S.

"Since a senior Xbox official is actually in attendance here today, I wish hardware like the 'Xbox Series S' wouldn't be released (laughs)."

He then went on to explain that although the low memory is "an issue" of the Xbox Series S, the hardest part is that it's actually so similar to the Series X, but with "awkwardly lower performance". Harada also pointed out that players direct their complaints to the developers rather than Microsoft if they don't like the visuals and/or performance.

"Low memory is an issue, but the hardest part is that it's a device so similar to Series X while having awkwardly lower performance [...] Furthermore, in markets like the US, there are more Series S users than Series X users, so we can't just cut corners. Yet after launch, Series S users direct their complaints at us developers rather than Microsoft, asking "Why is texture resolution lower only on Series S?"

Finally, he also noted that developing for the Nintendo Switch 2 is actually easier in some ways because it's a "distinct device for Nintendo fans", whereas the Xbox Series S is akin to "having to do extra work for a slightly weaker child" compared to putting in your "absolute best output" for the Xbox Series X.

Despite what it might seem like, a lot of this conversation seemed to be very lighthearted and full of laughs, and when Cyberpunk 2077's Paweł Sasko stepped in to highlight the benefits of the Xbox Series S including its affordable price and the ability to better optimise for low-spec platforms, Harada admitted that he made it sound a lot more attractive.

In fact, it was so convincing that Harada jokingly worried that it might lead to a similar Project Helix alternative!

Director Paweł: "Harada speaks a lot of truth. Players don't fully understand the technical context behind why fewer characters appear on screen or why visuals are downgraded. However, intensely optimizing for lower-spec platforms definitely brings the benefit of making the game run significantly better across all platforms—including PS5, high-end PCs, and handhelds like Steam Deck and ROG Ally.

And the biggest advantage of Series S is that its affordable price makes it accessible. Not every gamer can buy expensive high-end hardware, so from a developer's perspective, even if the strict constraints are painful, there is a silver lining in expanding market penetration to deliver the game to more players."

CEO Harada: "Director Paweł packaged Series S so gracefully that the Microsoft representative in the audience keeps nodding along. I'm worried they'll feel reassured and release a successor like 'Helix S' for the next-gen Xbox as well (laughs)."

It's best not to take any of this too seriously, but it does at least highlight the differing opinions throughout the industry about whether the Xbox Series S was a good or bad idea in hindsight.

From a consumer perspective at least, we reckon it was a fantastic idea. The Xbox Series S has continually managed to deliver incredible-looking games over the past six years, and in a time where console prices are constantly on the up, the Series S at least provides a somewhat affordable current-gen hardware option - at least if you find a good deal.
