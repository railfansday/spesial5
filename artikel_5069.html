VanEck is dropping Gemini from nearly $1 billion in Bitcoin, Ethereum and Solana ETFs

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Coinbase will remain the funds' custodian as VanEck ends its custody, clearing and staking arrangements with Gemini.

VanEck is ending its relationship with Gemini across three spot crypto ETFs, leaving Coinbase Custody as its remaining named custodian.

The asset manager disclosed in Nov. 19 regulatory filings that it had notified Gemini of plans to terminate its custody and clearing agreements for its Bitcoin ETF (HODL), Ethereum ETF (ETHV) and Solana ETF (VSOL).

The agreements are scheduled to expire in February 2027, and Gemini will continue to provide services until the termination takes effect. The decision also ends a separate arrangement under which Gemini facilitates Solana staking for VSOL.

The filings did not disclose the reasons for the termination or how much cryptocurrency VanEck currently holds with Gemini.

The three funds have attracted approximately $1.15 billion in cumulative net inflows since their respective launches and collectively managed $934.2 million in net assets as of Nov. 24, according to SoSoValue data.

VanEck's Bitcoin ETF accounted for the bulk of that exposure, with $977.1 million in cumulative net inflows and $848.2 million in net assets. Its Ethereum and Solana funds recorded cumulative net inflows of $151.6 million and $19.2 million, respectively.

VanEck's decision extends beyond asset safekeeping. Gemini also provides clearing infrastructure that helps the funds settle cryptocurrency transactions associated with ETF share creations and redemptions.

Under the existing arrangements, VanEck's funds can use Gemini Clearing to complete trades negotiated with liquidity providers that are also Gemini customers.

The service has supported cash-based orders, which require the funds to purchase or sell cryptocurrencies, and in-kind transactions involving the direct transfer of digital assets.

The scheduled February 2027 exit affects two services across VanEck’s HODL, ETHV and VSOL.

Coinbase Custody stays with all three funds. The filings name no successor to Gemini’s trade clearing service.

Coinbase will continue serving the Bitcoin and Ethereum funds under their existing prime broker agreements, while the Solana fund also maintains a separate custody agreement with the company.

VanEck did not identify a successor to Gemini's clearing services or provide a timetable for any cryptocurrency transfers between the custodians.

VanEck's decision contrasts with arrangements at other major US crypto ETF issuers that have established relationships with multiple digital asset custodians.

BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust ETF (IBIT) uses Coinbase Custody as its custodian and has appointed Anchorage Digital Bank as an additional provider. Its latest quarterly report said the fund had no immediate plans to transfer Bitcoin to Anchorage.

BlackRock also maintains flexibility in its trading arrangements. The fund can execute Bitcoin transactions directly with third-party counterparties or through Coinbase Prime when processing cash-based creation and redemption orders.

Meanwhile, the Fidelity Wise Origin Bitcoin Fund maintains its primary custody and trade execution relationship with Fidelity Digital Assets, an affiliate of its sponsor. Fidelity has also appointed BitGo Bank & Trust as an additional custodian, although it reported no immediate plans to transfer Bitcoin to the company.

These arrangements allow BlackRock and Fidelity to retain additional custody providers without necessarily distributing their existing Bitcoin holdings across them.

VanEck's termination of Gemini's agreements moves in the opposite direction across three separate crypto investment products, increasing the importance of its remaining relationship with Coinbase.

Its September quarterly reports describe transactions conducted directly with liquidity providers, while earlier prospectuses also allowed alternative execution arrangements for certain trades if settlement failed.

Those existing arrangements could accommodate transactions previously handled through Gemini Clearing without VanEck appointing another dedicated clearing provider.

With the termination scheduled for February, VanEck's subsequent regulatory disclosures could reveal whether it intends to consolidate more of its trading infrastructure with Coinbase or establish new arrangements with other institutional service providers.
