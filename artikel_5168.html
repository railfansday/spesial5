An Ethereum ICO whale turned an initial $31,000 investment into $87.21 million.

The full investment generated an estimated 2,804x return across multiple sales.

Ethereum has fallen 27% over the past month to around $1,641 amid heavy selling pressure.

Crypto never fails to pay off its long-term holders. An Ethereum investor who put around $31,000 into ETH during the 2015 ICO has finally emptied the wallet, turning an early investment into $87.21 million in realized proceeds.

The wallet’s final sale of 45,000 ETH for $75.35 million closes a decade-long investment that began when each token cost just $0.31.

The investor acquired 100,000 ETH at roughly $0.31 each during Ethereum’s 2015 ICO for roughly $31,000. The wallet later sold 50,000 ETH in 2016 at around $5.47 per token and another 5,000 ETH in late October 2026 for about $2,316 each.

More than 11 years later, on November 26, the whale wallet sold its remaining 45,000 ETH for about $75.35 million, or roughly $1,674 per ETH.

Across all the sales, the original $31,000 investment generated approximately $87.21 million, a return of about 2,804 times the initial amount.

The final sell-off was also split into multiple transfers.

On-chain records show repeated 900 ETH movements worth about $1.53 million each, along with 600 ETH transfers worth roughly $1.02 million.

The activity points to more than one reason for old wallets becoming active, from taking profits and securing liquidity to managing long-held crypto wealth.

The timing is notable because large holders have been making very different moves around ETH.

Lookonchain reports that a separate whale sold 1,107 BTC worth about $86.76 million on Hyperliquid and used the proceeds to buy 34,422 ETH worth roughly $86.5 million, immediately staking the full position.

Other ICO-era wallets have also started moving long-dormant ETH through exchanges, OTC desks, and swaps

The whale activity comes as Ethereum faces a continuous price drop, with ETH falling to $1,641 and losing 27% over the past month.

Selling pressure has also increased as U.S. spot Ethereum ETFs recorded around $1.51 billion in outflows in November, marking eight consecutive sessions of withdrawals.
