Nvidia's board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO, president and board director.

Shares reportedly rebounded from around $125 to an intraday high of $166.50 on Monday.

Meanwhile, the $150 and $130 levels could return as support if the stock loses momentum.

Nvidia’s leadership drama has taken another sharp turn. Jensen Huang reinstated as CEO is the headline after the board unanimously approved his return, sending Nvidia shares sharply higher following Friday’s sell-off.

The board said it received input from senior US government officials and believes his reinstatement poses no national security risk. However, an independent investigation will continue.

The board said its decision followed discussions and meetings with Huang. It also confirmed that the investigation into recent foreign-intelligence-related reports would proceed, with Huang committed to cooperating.

Huang said he was honored to return and lead the company. Board chair Harvey Jones described the decision as being in the best interests of shareholders, employees and partners.

The market reaction was dramatic. Nvidia shares reportedly fell 14% on Friday, closing at $125.53 after breaking below $150 support on November 19 and $130 the following day.

Monday’s trading brought a sharp reversal. The stock reportedly rebounded from around $125 to an intraday high of $166.50 as news of Huang’s return spread.

That puts the leadership announcement at the centre of the latest price recovery. But one strong session doesn’t erase the recent volatility.

If buying momentum continues, $180 and $200 are potential upside targets identified in the chart analysis. Neither is guaranteed.

A reversal could bring $150 and $130 back into focus as support levels. The Nvidia stock rebound now faces a simple test: whether buyers can sustain the recovery.

Jensen Huang reinstated as CEO has given the stock a powerful headline catalyst, but the ongoing investigation and those key price levels leave plenty of uncertainty.
