Standard Chartered Expands Prime Brokerage Partnership With 24X to Cover BTC and ETH

Institutional investors will be able to access Bitcoin and Ether trading through an expanded relationship between 24X and Standard Chartered. In a November 10 announcement, 24X Bermuda said the banking group will extend its prime-brokerage role from foreign-exchange products to digital assets on 24X’s multi-asset technology platform.

TL;DR: 24X says Standard Chartered is expanding its prime-brokerage relationship to cover BTC and ETH. The arrangement is aimed at institutional counterparties accessing crypto and FX through one execution infrastructure. It changes the trading and counterparty workflow for eligible participants; it is not a retail exchange rollout.

The company’s stated model allows an institutional participant to face Standard Chartered as its central counterparty while using 24X for execution, matching, liquidity configuration and straight-through processing. That separation matters. The participant’s commercial relationship and credit exposure are handled differently from the technology that matches orders.

Traditional foreign exchange and spot cryptocurrency trading do not have identical market structures or risk profiles. FX markets have established bank relationships, while crypto markets operate around the clock and rely on a patchwork of exchanges, brokers and custody providers. An integrated platform can simplify some operational tasks, although it still requires clients to understand the product-specific terms.

The release says 24X already offered FX, non-deliverable forwards and spot services. BTC and ETH are the additional digital-asset instruments named in the expanded partnership. It would be misleading to claim the agreement immediately covers every token, derivative or jurisdiction.

A recognized bank acting as a central relationship may appeal to institutions that want more familiar onboarding, credit arrangements and oversight. It does not remove Bitcoin volatility, market liquidity problems or the possibility of disruptions in the surrounding infrastructure. Nor should the word ‘prime’ be used as a shortcut for saying all trades are insured or guaranteed.

Institutions will need to evaluate exposure limits, settlement timing, custody arrangements, collateral terms and what happens during market stress. The distinction between an execution venue and the organization responsible for a trading obligation is especially important for crypto, where the history of market failures has made counterparty concentration a persistent concern.

NewsBTC previously looked at Ripple Prime’s effort to bring traditional markets into 24-hour trading workflows. That is a distinct product and operator, but it reflects the same strategic demand: customers increasingly want to manage digital and conventional exposures without jumping between disconnected systems.

The announcement establishes the partnership expansion, but it does not provide a full ledger of live client trades, volumes or fee terms. Commercial success depends on whether eligible counterparties find the combined trading and brokerage model attractive enough to move business from existing venues.

As tokenized securities and digital assets enter more established markets, investors may become less focused on whether a blockchain is present and more concerned with the legal machinery behind settlement. Our report on the NYSE’s tokenized securities infrastructure partners describes why those arrangements matter. For 24X, the next proof point will be active institutional adoption rather than the breadth of the announcement alone.
