LayerZero's Zero mainnet launch and new institutional validators could strengthen ecosystem adoption and investor interest in the ZRO price.

ZRO remains in a descending channel, with weakening OBV highlighting selling pressure and the need to defend the demand zone.

LayerZero’s native token, ZRO, is approaching a crucial milestone as it trades near $1.10, up approximately 1.12% in the past 24 hours. The upcoming November 30 launch of its Zero mainnet has renewed investor attention, with new validators and trading platforms joining the ecosystem ahead of the rollout.

However, ZRO’s price continues to struggle within a prolonged descending channel, raising questions about whether this major launch can finally reverse its bearish trajectory. With the token hovering near its demand zone and the launch just days away, investors are watching whether the November 30 event can trigger a meaningful recovery or expose ZRO to further downside.

With the November 30 Zero mainnet launch approaching, LayerZero is adding more established names to its ecosystem. On November 25, the protocol announced that Kakao Pay, Samsung Card and ZAN would join Zero as genesis validators. Their involvement will help support the network’s initial operations, including consensus and proof verification. Kakao Pay is also exploring the possibility of using Zero’s infrastructure for a Korean won-backed stablecoin ecosystem, although no launch has been confirmed.

LayerZero is also expanding its cross-chain capabilities. On November 12, Flare announced its integration with the protocol as a Decentralized Verifier Network (DVN). This will allow Flare to help verify messages transferred between blockchains, initially supporting Flare and Ethereum. The integration is expected to go live in early December.

Another development worth watching is ZRO staking, which opened on November 23. Token holders can now stake their holdings to support Zero’s initial validator network. However, there is a catch: staked tokens will remain locked until March 31, 2027, with an additional withdrawal cooldown of up to seven days. Together, these developments show that LayerZero is building out its infrastructure and bringing more participants into the ecosystem ahead of launch.

ZRO continues to face selling pressure on the weekly chart, with the token trading around $1.109 after declining 7.12% during the week. The latest pullback follows an unsuccessful recovery towards $2 to $2.3, suggesting that buyers are struggling to sustain upward momentum. More importantly, ZRO remains confined within a descending parallel channel that has guided its price movement since early 2025.

The On-Balance Volume (OBV) indicator is also showing signs of weakening demand. Its latest reading stands at approximately 389.4 million, after retreating from a recent peak above 500 million. This decline suggests that selling volume has been outweighing buying volume during the latest pullback. The weekly chart also highlights an important demand zone around 0.70–1.10, where ZRO is currently trading. Holding this region could provide a base for another recovery attempt. However, a sustained breakdown below the lower boundary of the descending channel would expose the token to further downside.

On the upside, the descending channel’s upper boundary remains an important hurdle. A breakout would need to be followed by a move above the $2.97 to $3.16 resistance zone, which has previously acted as a significant supply area. Beyond this, the next major order-block region lies between $4.90 and $6.00.

LayerZero’s November 30 Zero mainnet launch could improve market sentiment, supported by new institutional validators and ecosystem integrations. However, ZRO’s weekly chart remains bearish, with declining OBV indicating weakening buying pressure. For now, the mainnet launch is a potential catalyst, but ZRO’s price needs stronger buying volume and a breakout to confirm a trend reversal.
