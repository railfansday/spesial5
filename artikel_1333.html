How much Napoli would save by selling De Bruyne in January

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Reports in Italy over the last few weeks indicate that Kevin De Bruyne could leave Napoli as early as January, which could provide the Partenopei with around €10m to reinvest if they do allow the Belgian superstar to leave midway through the season.

De Bruyne is into the final year of the two-year contract he signed with Napoli after leaving Manchester City at the end of the 2024-25 season. There is an option to extend that agreement by a further 12 months, until the end of the 2027-28 season, but this appears an unlikely scenario at this stage.

Reports earlier this week claimed that Dutch side PSV Eindhoven are interested in pursuing a deal for De Bruyne, potentially as early as January, and that they are willing to offer around €2m to Napoli for the 35-year-old, who will have less than six months remaining on his contract during the next transfer window.

Some outlets claim that Napoli will hold out for a larger transfer fee, while others, including the Corriere dello Sport, still claim that Napoli wish to keep hold of De Bruyne until the end of the season at least.

There have also been suggestions that De Bruyne is keen on the idea of a move to the Netherlands, as he and his family could move into the home they own in the Belgian province of Limburg, which borders the Netherlands.

According to the latest updates from La Gazzetta dello Sport, what is becoming clear is that De Bruyne would need to take a significant pay cut if he is to join PSV during the January transfer window.

The Pink Paper claims that De Bruyne earns a net salary of around €5m per season with Napoli, which costs the club around €10m per season gross. La Gazzetta dello Sport reports that PSV would not ‘seem able to afford’ these figures.

If Napoli do offload De Bruyne in January, though, they will save half a season’s worth of wages, around €5m gross, plus around €2.5m in bonuses that would no longer be valid, as well as a transfer fee of €2m or above, which could provide Napoli with around €10m to reinvest in January.

Peter Young is a Senior Reporter with Football Italia.
