Walters confident he can win trophy in first season with St Helens

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

New St Helens coach Kevin Walters has pinpointed where he believes things may have gone wrong for the club in 2026; St Helens missed the Super League play-offs for the first time in the club's history; Walters put pen to paper on a three-year deal in August

Kevin Walters believes he can win a trophy with St Helens in his first season in charge, as the side's new head coach set out his ambition to restore the team's "winning DNA".

After guiding Australia to their fourth consecutive Rugby League World Cup success earlier this month, the former Brisbane Broncos boss has been tasked with galvanising the Saints following an underwhelming season that saw them slump to seventh in Super League and miss the play-offs for the first time in the club's history.

The 59-year-old, who put pen to paper on a three-year deal with the Saints in August, is confident he can restore the club to where it belongs and added St Helens are "not in as bad a position as some people may think".

Asked by Sky Sports Rugby League's Jenna Brooks whether he can win silverware in his first season, Walters said: "If I say no to that question, I look like a loser. I'm not a loser. I love winning.

"So I'm going to answer that positively and say yeah, I believe we can have great success here.

"It starts early; the pressure is on. I've been at big clubs and I understand the pressure that comes with it. I understand there's a lot of pressure on myself and the club to do well. But I'm confident we can do really well.

"Pressure is a privilege, isn't it? I wouldn't be in the role if I didn't [have that] understanding. There's always pressure in professional sport, particularly in rugby league - in Australia and here in the Super League. It's a big sport in the north.

"Everyone knows about St Helens and their success, and we need to get them back to where they belong."

It was a turbulent season for the 10-time Super League Grand Final winners, who suffered a swathe of injuries to key players, including Matty Lees and Jack Welsby, which contributed to their disappointing form.

St Helens also underwent some major coaching upheavals, with head coach Paul Rowley departing in May, less than a year into his three-year contract, before Eamon O'Carroll took over on an interim basis.

Club chiefs then turned to Walters, one of rugby league's most distinguished coaches. It will mark the first time the 59-year-old has taken up a full-time coaching role since he was sacked by the Brisbane Broncos in September 2024.

Walters, who arrived in the United Kingdom on Monday and has already led a few training sessions with his new team, pinpoints a loss of connection between his players and fans as one of the side's biggest issues.

"It's hard to judge from afar. I've been speaking to people in the club; the connection between the team and the community is one thing that has been lost a little bit," Walters said.

"The Saints fans, from what I've seen and been told, are very passionate about their club. The English fans, across all of the teams, their passion is a little bit different to the Australian fans, who are probably not as vocal in their support, but also in their care for the side.

"I know there were some things that did go wrong; maybe the connection among the players was lost a little bit as well."

Walters, who also spearheaded Melbourne Storm's 2012 NRL Grand Final win, and won back-to-back State of Origin series as Queensland head coach in 2016 and 2017, believes he is the right man to solve that connectivity issue.

"One of my great strengths as a coach is my ability to bring people together for a common cause, and that's what I feel coaching is. Lots of things go into coaching; my ability to bring players and teams together, that's where I feel my big strength is.

"If I can do that here… well, I will do that here and then we'll see where that takes us."

He added: "Without making too many bold predictions, we're going to be hard to beat. That's the mainstay for me. Defensively, we've spoken to our players about getting better in that space.

"The last time Saints won the premiership in 2022, it was based on defence. We have to make sure we have a strong defensive team that's well connected and then when we get the ball, we're going to play some footy.

"The best defensive teams are there where it matters."

Reports have suggested that Tristan Sailor is being eyed up as a potential target for multiple other Super League clubs.

The full-back, who previously played under Walters at the Broncos, joined the Saints in August 2024 and has gone on to make 54 appearances for the club.

While Welsby is favoured to lock down the No 1 jumper for St Helens, Walters insisted Sailor still has a future at the club.

"There's a lot of noise around the whole group and Tristan has been caught up in that," Walters said. "I believe Tristan's got a lot to offer this club still.

"He's been a great player here. I coached him at the Broncos and unfortunately Reece Walsh was parked in front of him in that role at full-back.

"He's come here in a different role, but I feel Tristan... whatever position he plays - he was good playing full-back, centre, five-eighth, he might have played some wing. Mr Versatility but also a very good team person."
