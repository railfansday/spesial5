What went wrong for Alonso's Chelsea at Forest?

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Sky Sports analyses the decisions from Xabi Alonso that contributed to Chelsea's 3-2 defeat to Nottingham Forest; Wesley Fofana started despite his controversial interview, while Levi Colwill was dropped; Xabi Alonso called for fundamentals but Chelsea were outrun

Xabi Alonso called for his Chelsea side to get back to the fundamentals before kick-off - and then watched on as they were outworked and outclassed on their way to a miserable 3-2 defeat at Nottingham Forest.

"Everyone in the club and team, we need to stick together and go through this moment. We have to deal with it and we are responsible," Alonso told Sky Sports after the game, which marked their third successive defeat in the Premier League.

"It is a difficult moment for everyone at the club. For the team and the fans. Disappointed to lose again but we need to keep trying to find solutions.

"We can't stop. We are low in confidence. At this level you need to hold results and not concede so much. That is why we are losing."

Alonso now has as many losses in 15 games across all competitions as he did in 34 for Real Madrid (6). Since March, no side has more defeats in the top-flight than the Blues (14). And their problems are mounting by the day.

Here, Sky Sports analyses the decisions that contributed to the worrying defeat at the City Ground.

Despite the calls from Alonso to strip the game back to basics and get the fundamentals right, Chelsea felt short from minute one.

At the half-time interval, facing a 1-0 deficit and with zero shots on target, Chelsea had ran 3.8km less than Oliver Glasner's side.

The Forest boss was then quick to point out that they had outrun their opponents by 6km after the game. Yes, Chelsea had more possession, but they were also well behind in high-speed runs.

"I've seen one team jogging after the ball and one team sprinting after the ball and in this day and age, you cannot do that," said Jamie Redknapp.

"Distance covered is not always the barometer, but one thing is for sure, high-speed runs will tell you everything."

Starting Wesley Fofana in the same week the player had to apologise for his controversial interview, where he said he can "laugh about" the penalty miss that contributed to their Carabao Cup exit to Liverpool and contemplated retirement during injury spells at the club, was a bold move from Alonso. It did not pay off.

The tone was set early on. Fofana's first touches were heavily booed by the travelling Chelsea fans who had made the 130-mile journey in the cold and rain to watch their side slip to yet another dismal defeat.

The centre-back could only watch on as Igor Jesus volleyed in his stunning opener but was at the centre of attention, for all the wrong reasons, for the second.

After misjudging the ball while battling with former teammate Liam Delap, who caused problems all evening, Fofana rushed to atone for his error and fouled the striker in the penalty area.

Morgan Gibbs-White doubled the lead from the spot and ultimately the comeback, led by substitute Emmanuel Emegha, fell short.

He was cautioned for the challenge and eventually taken off for Levi Colwill, who was dropped by Alonso instead of Fofana, and the fans booed him once again.

"It didn't feel like a game for him tonight. He had a bad week with the podcast," said Jamie Redknapp after the game.

"By playing Fofana tonight, you created that. It was inevitable. He is lacking so much confidence; he needs some time.

"He is having a real difficult run of it at the moment."

After making the decision to drop Colwill, options at the centre of defence were limited with Maxence Lacroix and Reece James both sidelined.

Playing Josh Acheampong at centre-back and giving a rare start to Malo Gusto, an unused substitute on the night, seemed the more obvious choice but Alonso opted to play Moises Caicedo, arguably the best midfielder in the league, in the back four instead.

He was then tasked with dealing with a strike partnership of Delap and Igor Jesus. A difficult task for even the tallest of centre-backs, let alone a makeshift one standing at 5-foot-10.

He gave space to the Brazil striker for the opener and his presence in midfield was missed. He still led the way with 114 passes on the night, but Chelsea needed him in the middle of the park to deal with the likes of James McAtee and Xaver Schlager.

"They have bought players but if they do not sign a centre back, they cannot compete," said Redknapp after the game. He is right.

Alonso admitted that Emegha's performance after scoring twice off the bench was the lone positive for his side to take from their trip to the East Midlands. "For sure, he is having a good impact," he said. "He has done well. That is a positive."

He scored his first goal in the 4-1 defeat to Leeds United last time out but lost his place in the starting line-up on Friday.

Danny Welbeck, 36, was given the nod ahead of him and his performance mirrored the struggles Chelsea had as a unit.

Alonso called on the experience of Jordan Henderson and Welbeck to help weather their current struggles but it only whipped up the storm.

Welbeck was a bystander in the first half and had just seven touches before being replaced at the break, Emegha then scored with his first two to help spark hopes of a comeback.

His header for the second, holding off Jair Cunha, was one of the rare moments Chelsea matched the physicality of their opponents. He has to start moving forward without Joao Pedro available.

Alonso said the only way he knows how to address these issues is to attack them on the training ground. He has just five days to do that before they are put to the test against Crystal Palace, live on Sky Sports, who will be desperate for a win.
