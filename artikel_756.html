Yoro red card: Written reasons for overturned decision revealed

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Leny Yoro had a red card overturned after being sent off in Man Utd's 4-0 loss to Liverpool; Pro Ref chief Howard Webb agreed with the decision, but the FA ruled out the one-game suspension after Man Utd appeals; United used Pascal Strujk's red card for Brighton as part of their evidence

The Football Association has released the written reasons why Leny Yoro's red card for Manchester United at Liverpool was overturned - even though refereeing body Pro Ref said the on-field decision was correct.

Yoro was sent off for bringing down Liverpool striker Alexander Isak as the last defender outside the penalty area, with referee Sam Barrott sending him off for a denial of an obvious goalscoring opportunity (DOGSO), with VAR John Brooks agreeing with the call.

United went on to concede four goals in the heavy defeat to Liverpool - and despite Pro Ref chief Howard Webb indicating that no clear mistake was made, the FA still decided to overturn the red card for an "obvious error" by the officials, as Isak was not in possession of the ball when he was fouled.

In the written reasons, the three-person panel - consisting of three former professional footballers, including one goalkeeper - said Isak had taken a heavy touch "to the extent he had in fact lost control of the ball" - which under the rules, says Yoro should not have been sent off.

"The Commission members were unanimous that, taking the foul committed by Yoro out of the scenario, Isak would not have had an obvious goal-scoring opportunity," the statement read.

"Despite the fact that Isak had reached the ball initially, his touch was heavy to the extent that he had in fact lost control of the ball - that control had, in effect, passed to the advancing MUFC goalkeeper who in the view of the Commission would have been able to gather or smother the ball before AI [Alexander Isak] could get to it and shoot at goal.

"To the mind of the Commission, the referee had made an 'obvious error' in deciding that AI would have had an obvious goal-scoring opportunity."

The written reasons also said United provided extensive 'evidence' to say why Yoro should not have been sent off. As well as providing three separate angles of the incident, they also provided clips of Pascal Struijk's red card for Brighton against Sunderland - which was later overturned.

United's analysis also claimed that Isak was, "at best 1.8m short of reaching the ball". The FA said there was no way of determining that analysis themselves, so it was "viewed with caution and allocated weight accordingly".

In the latest edition of Match Officials Mic'D Up, Pro Ref chief refereeing officer Webb backed the process of his officials although was non-committal when sharing his own verdict on the incident.

"By and large most people would see that as a correct decision with Isak moving quickly on towards that ball," said Webb.

"The alternative view is that Lammens might get there first, but it's a little bit debatable, I guess. Certainly not clearly wrong."

Audio from the officials revealed the VAR Brooks, who backed referee Barrott's decision, determined Isak was "likely" to get on the end of the ball before Lammens. His assistant VAR, Paul Howard, agreed.

This is not the first time this season that a DOGSO red card has created division within the football authorities.

In October, two independent panels disagreed over whether Brighton's Struijk deserved to be sent off at Sunderland, an event which United used in their evidence to get Yoro's red card overturned.

Struijk was dismissed for denying an obvious goal-scoring opportunity in the second half when he was adjudged to have fouled Sunderland striker Wilson Isidor as he tried to latch on to a pass that ran through to goalkeeper Bart Verbruggen.

Neither referee Thomas Bramall nor his assistant gave a foul, but VAR Neil Davies intervened and recommended an on-field review. Bramall watched several replays before changing his decision and dismissing Struijk.

However, Brighton appealed and an independent regulatory commission, which is convened through the FA to consider disciplinary matters, overturned the red card and subsequent one-match ban.

But disagreements over the decision continued after the Key Match Incidents (KMI) panel - another independent panel who determine the validity of refereeing decisions - voted 3-2 that the VAR intervention was correct.

The KMI panel ruled that Struijk committed a "cynical foul clearly impacting the attacker's ability to gain possession of the ball which should have been penalised by the referee".
