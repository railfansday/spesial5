Four first half goals for Hassocks in hammering of Hurst

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Four first half goals away at Billingshurst showed Hassocks have not suffered any lasting damage from their midweek hammering in the Sussex Transport Senior Cup at the hands of Chichester City.

Chi relentlessly stuck six past the Robins on Tuesday night. Hassocks were equally unforgiving on Hurst, pocketing all three points before the half time whistle.

No further goals were added nor needed after the break, allowing the Robins to take their foot of the pedal and James Westlake to rattle through the substitutions ahead of a huge upcoming five weeks which could be season-defining.

Hassocks go to Rusthall next Saturday for their first ever appearance in the third round of the FA Vase. A place in the last 32 of the national competition is up for grabs.

December then brings matches against Roffey, Guernsey, East Grinstead Town, Eastbourne United and Haywards Heath Town. Five games against teams in the top eight of the Southern Combination Premier Division all harbouring hopes of promotion.

"It's going to be busy, it's going to be hard," said James Westlake of those upcoming fixtures. "It is a tough run through until January time really. But those big games in the league are the ones we want to be competing in."

Billingshurst provided a different type of challenge. Several ingredients were there for a banana skin - a pitch already heavy before kick off, opposition fighting relegation and a popular former Robin in the home dugout.

Eddie French expressed his displeasure at the performance of the officials afterwards and it was hard not to have some sympathy for the ex-Hassocks favourite.

Certainly in relation to the bizarre series of decisions which saw the Robins awarded a penalty for their second goal of the afternoon, Nat Guest sent off and Billingshurst left to play for 70 minutes with 10 men.

Hassocks took the lead five minutes prior to that pivotal sequence of events. Playing down the hill, Omar Abdsami exchanged a one-two with Dan Perry to split open the centre of the Hurst defence.

Abdsami still needed to get around covering right back James Bendell. He did that via some quick feet before firing low and hard across Fraser Trigwell and into the far corner.

Trigwell provided another current link between the clubs whilst Hassocks introduced former Hurst midfielder Luke Brodie from their bench in the second half.

Then came the major talking point. Lewis Finney delivered a corner which Dan Turner met with a towering header. The ball was several feet over the line when it struck Guest in the hand.

Remarkably, the officials did not award the goal but instead gave the Robins a spot kick and sent Guest off for deliberate hand ball.

Chaos ensued with the surreal scenario of both Hassocks and Billingshurst players protesting against the same decision for different reasons.

Once order was restored, Finney put away the spot kick despite Trigwell diving the right way. And just to really rub salt into Hurst wounds, the initial corner Turner scored from was a clear goal kick too.

"I would certainly have been frustrated if that went against us with the awarding of the corner to start it all off," Westlake said afterwards.

"If you are a neutral watching the game, you would ask that the goal from Turner is given and it is not a sending off. It was a shame for them but I guess we already had a good foothold and would have been 2-0 up anyway."

Two very quickly became three and there was nothing controversial about the spectacular strike from Finney 120 seconds after his successful penalty.

Liam Hendy played a long ball forward which Perry won in the air. The flick dropped into the path of Finney, who hit a 25 yard dipping volley. Trigwell got a hand to it but could not keep it out.

Hassocks enjoyed near-total domination for the remainder of the first half with the Billingshurst X account posting at the interval: "To say this has been like the Alamo would be harsh on the Alamo as it has been more one-sided than that."

Tall striker Jamie Wilkes lent so far back that he was almost horizontal when putting a shot from a good position into orbit. Trigwell then made an excellent finger tip save diving low at his near post to keep out an Abdsami effort.

Having set up both Abdsami and Finney, Perry added the fourth himself five minutes before half time. Jack Troak started the move down the left and his square pass inside was beautifully back heeled to Finney by Darren Budd.

Finney glided past Jack Griffin and although Trigwell did well to parry away the shot, Perry followed up for a poacher's finish to open his Hassocks account.

Billingshurst will undoubtedly be happy to see the back of the Hitman. Five weeks ago he scored a hat-trick against them for East Grinstead Town with what turned out to be his final goals in a Wasps shirt.

French changed Hurst's shape for the second half and it allowed them to stem the flow of goals without ever really offering much going forward.

Ed Cooper did not have a serious save to make although Griffin and Kieran Britland both should have done better when shooting high and wide respectively.

Britland did at least have the excuse of losing his footing as he pulled the trigger. He was not the only one to slip and slide as the already challenging surface cut up massively the longer the game went on.

Chances at the other end for Hassocks came for Charlie Bonwick-Adams, who put a header straight at Trigwell. Finney guided a Troak cross wide of the front post and substitute Raging Joe Bull did everything right after overlapping Abdsami, receiving a pass and cutting inside only to shoot into an adjacent field.

Trigwell made an outstanding double save when pushing away a Budd piledriver from the edge of the box at full stretch before reacting quick enough to get up and deflect the follow up from Abdsami behind using his feet.

Turner hit a volley diverted behind by a fine block with the final opportunity of the game involving two more replacements. Lewis Croal headed down a cross for Big Alex Fair to shoot directly upwards rather than towards goal.

"We wanted to kick on in the second half but that obviously wasn't meant to be," Westlake added. "The lads have come off disappointed considering we couldn't score more goals. But a clean sheet, four goals and three points are the really important thing today."

Hassocks: Ed Cooper; Charlie Bonwick-Adams, Dan Turner, Liam Hendy, Jack Troak; Jamie Wilkes, Darren Budd; Devon Fender, Lewis Finney, Omar Abdsami, Dan Perry.

Subs: Liam Benson, Lewis Croal, Joe Bull, Luke Brodie, Alex Fair (used).

Starman: Lewis Finney converted a penalty for the second goal, scored a stunning volley for the third and set up Dan Perry for the fourth.
