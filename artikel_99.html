UK-EU reset talks will go ‘down to the wire’ as crunch summit approaches

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Britain makes concessions on youth mobility scheme as negotiations on food and farming go down to the wire

British and European negotiators were closing in on a reset deal on Thursday night as they provisionally agreed to allow 100,000 EU citizens to come to the UK every year under a new mobility scheme.

It is understood the EU dropped its demands at the last minute that EU students would pay home tuition fees if they attended British universities in a last minute concession to get the deal over the line.

The UK had strongly resisted EU text on the table committing it to home fees instead of “significantly reduced” international fees, but a compromise was hammered out before 10pm, with a “supplementary agreement” to work out the details within the next six months.

As talks went into the night, EU sources suggested both sides were on track for an agreement on Friday.

The EU-UK reset talks went down “down to the wire”, sources said, as both sides battled over the nature of the UK’s role in shaping future European food and farming rules.

But on the eve of a summit in Brussels, the EU was “going into a landing zone” on the question of a British voice in shaping the rules under a food and drink agreement the government hopes to unveil on Friday, an EU source said.

There also seemed to be a compromise by France after a dispute among member states over how far Britain could be involved in shaping policies related to the food and drink deal.

The UK had demanded a “voting” role on some law-making in relation to food standards but France heavily opposed, and London eventually conceded on a point many think was unrealistic from the start.

Instead, the UK will be consulted on new food laws, but the details on the level of involvement remain to be seen.

Earlier on Thursday the UK made concessions to increase the quota of EU citizens who could take part in a new youth mobility scheme, agreeing a cap of 100,000, instead of 50,000-80,0000 as previously sought.

It was also agreed to raise the age cap from 30 to 31.

The number of young British people allowed to go to the EU under the scheme will be matched but could be higher, with some countries, including Germany, being prepared to take unlimited numbers each year.

With less than 24 hours to go before Andy Burnham was due in Brussels for the summit with EU leaders, the two sides still needed to resolve some sticking points.

Speaking in general terms about the disagreements, another EU diplomat said: “It will go down to the wire. Our feeling is that the Brits have to move if they want a deal.”

A spokesperson for the British prime minister also described the talks as close, saying if an agreement was reached, the government would bring forward legislation, the cutting costs bill, to parliament next week.

At the long-awaited summit, the EU and UK hope to sign three agreements, which are expected to add 0.4% to British GDP, including the highly prized food and drink deal that was a Labour manifesto commitment.

The deal, expected to come into force on 1 March 2028, requires the UK to align with EU rules on sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) standards, making Britain a rule taker.

Asked about Britain’s status as a rule taker, No 10 said the Labour manifesto had aimed to build closer ties with the EU, adding: “We were obviously elected on a manifesto that promised to reset the relationship and seek to deepen ties with our European friends.”

According to EU sources, the UK has been pushing to have a formal role in EU committees involved in implementing existing and setting new SPS rules.

An EU diplomat said: “They want to be able to shape decisions, not only implementation, but future decisions. That has always been a red line.”

Under the SPS proposal, the UK will agree to dynamically align with EU laws and will have a “voice” but “not be in the room” when laws are drafted.

A second diplomat from a country sympathetic to the British position said the European Commission had proposed an “extremely restrictive consultation mechanism” for the UK.

In contrast, the EU diplomat thought it was reasonable for a British representative to take part in the implementation of SPS legislation affecting the UK.

“Of course the UK cannot vote in the council as a non-member state, but we feel that we need to be pragmatic about coordination and implementation,” they said.

The issue proved difficult to resolve because it divided member states. France was perceived by other member states to be the main barrier to greater UK involvement in EU working groups on food and drink laws. “They are super principled on that point,” said a second EU diplomat.

The French government does not agree that it has been the main block, arguing that only a few member states wanted to accommodate the UK.

The issue raises the tensions at the heart of the UK’s status as a rule taker, and would be many times magnified were Britain to choose to enter the single market as a non-EU member state.

The precise details of how the UK would be consulted on future food and farming rules remain unclear.

Burnham’s spokesperson suggested there was a UK-EU consensus that there would be no further incremental reset summits beyond the one on Friday until a full negotiation commenced on a longer-term, more stable relationship, such as rejoining the single market.

“After the summit, we will begin those discussions with our European partners about the realistic options for our longer-term relationship,” he said.

The UK has prioritised seeking commitments on Britain’s position relating to the EU’s Made in Europe legislation. The trade defence policy will include tariffs on Chinese electric vehicles but will also affect British manufacturing. The sides are believed to be close to an agreement to language at the summit that would point towards UK inclusion, but nothing can be formalised until the EU passes its own legislation.

Burnham’s spokesperson said: “[The UK is] engaging constructively with the EU on that, and we’ve been clear about the challenges it could represent for British industries. We have deeply integrated supply chains with Europe, and we want to keep working together to boost growth and open up trade.

“The UK and Europe have shared economic challenges, and we believe shared challenges are best met with a collective approach that is in Britain’s best interests, and in Europe’s too.”
