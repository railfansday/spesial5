‘I never executed Jesse Baird’: Beau Lamarre-Condon re-enacts shooting when giving evidence in NSW trial

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Former police officer has pleaded not guilty to murdering Baird and Luke Davies in Paddington in early 2024

Warning: This article contains graphic content

Beau Lamarre-Condon has told a Sydney court he did not “execute” Jesse Baird as he acted out the way he allegedly shot the 26-year-old.

The former police officer is accused of fatally shooting Baird and his new partner, Luke Davies, with his police service weapon in Baird’s Paddington home in Sydney on 19 February 2024.

He has pleaded not guilty to two counts of murder and one charge of aggravated break and enter.

Giving evidence under cross-examination on Monday, Lamarre-Condon reiterated that Baird was holding a knife while the pair struggled in the house.

Lamarre-Condon said he told the other man to drop the knife before aiming his gun at Baird’s shoulder in an attempt to disarm him.

After this shot, Lamarre-Condon’s finger remained on the trigger as both men continued to struggle, he told the New South Wales supreme court jury.

Baird then tripped and the pair fell, which led the officer to pull the trigger again, Lamarre-Condon told the court.

“That was my fault, it was unintentional,” he said on Monday.

He said the shot could have happened either during the fall or as the men impacted the ground.

Lamarre-Condon said he thought the bullet grazed the side of Baird’s head and did not know it had gone through his right temple.

The crown prosecutor Brett Hatfield SC asked whether Lamarre-Condon had “executed” Baird. Lamarre-Condon told the jury: “I never executed Jesse Baird.”

He said he believed the first shot had gone through Baird’s right shoulder.

Lamarre-Condon told the court he laid Baird on his back in the recovery position and did not look towards the shoulder area, instead searching for signs of life in his face.

Hatfield asked in court: “Did you notice there was a massive gun shot wound through [Baird’s] mouth?”

The former officer responded it was “hard to know” due to the amount of blood.

Asked if it was obvious that Baird was dead, Lamarre-Condon said: “No, I knew he was unresponsive, I just didn’t know he was dead at that point.”

Lamarre-Condon told the court he then began dialling triple zero and tried to check Baird’s pulse and get him to squeeze his hand.

The officer had entered Baird’s home dressed as an UberEats driver in a motorcycle helmet and hi-vis jacket to try retrieve his belongings from the TV presenter without anyone in the home noticing him, Lamarre-Condon told the court last week.

Hatfield on Monday asked whether Lamarre-Condon was still wearing his helmet while attempting to help Baird.

Lamarre-Condon said he did not need to take the helmet off as he was not attempting to perform CPR.

Asked if he had ever spoken to someone on the phone while wearing a motorcycle helmet, Lamarre-Condon said he could put the device on speaker phone.

The former officer told the court he then saw Davies standing in front of Baird’s bedroom door holding the firearm. Lamarre-Condon told the jury Davies said: “I’m going to fucking shoot you.”

Lamarre-Condon then got into a struggle with Davies, he told the court, grabbing his left wrist while holding the phone in his other hand as it dialled the emergency hotline.

He said on Monday that he had “trained for close to seven years” with the police to disarm people. He told the court they were taught to push the gun upwards.

“I saw his left index finger pull down on the trigger towards himself,” Lamarre-Condon told the jury.

Hatfield suggested Lamarre-Condon shot Davies through the bedroom door after hearing noise from within the bedroom. The accused denied this and claimed his ears were ringing from the first two shots which meant he could not hear Davies.

Earlier, Lamarre-Condon reenacted firing the first shot at Baird to the court after explaining Baird’s knife was next to his throat, which led him to push the other man back with his right arm.

The crown prosecutor asked Lamarre-Condon to stand up and demonstrate, telling the accused: “Put your rosary beads down while you do that.”

The officer reenacted the struggle for the jury, with Hatfield describing his movements.

“[The accused] with his left arm out at shoulder height, demonstrated pushing Jesse Baird’s right hand with the knife away from him, so that the left arm was fully extended,” the prosecutor told the jury.

“[Lamarre-Condon] gestured pulling the pistol around the area around his belt … then held it up at around chest or shoulder height and demonstrated with a bent elbow firing a shot towards the right shoulder of Jesse Baird.”

Lamarre-Condon told the court he “discharged a shot” and said he was aiming towards Baird’s right shoulder.

“My arm did extend … I don’t know to what extent … there was constant movement in this struggle,” the accused told the court. “That’s as clear as I can be. This happened very, very quick.”

Hatfield grilled the officer on the aftermath of the alleged murders, when Lamarre-Condon told a friend he had shot Baird.

He told the court he had become accustomed to “dissociating and detaching” during his time as a police officer.

“Just because you see me on camera without a reaction doesn’t mean I wasn’t crying,” Lamarre-Condon told the court.

“Jesse was someone I did care about, so I was very emotional … I’m not laughing on camera, I’m not having a good old time.”

He later told the crown it was “very interesting” the prosecution had shown the jury “30 minutes of a car sitting still” but not the entire CCTV footage of him and his friend speaking in a Sushi Train the day after the alleged murders.

“I’m not smiling, I’m not licking my fingers - I’m freaking out,” Lamarre-Condon said.

The trial before Justice Natalie Adams continues.
