Bitcoin price trades in a critical zone, as a surge could open the path toward $64K, while a breakdown risks $58K.

Heavy long liquidations and declining open interest reflect leverage unwinding, but persistent selling across spot and futures markets suggests buyers remain unconvinced.

The concerns over AI spending have shaken the global markets, as reflected during Anthropic’s IPO, which was a success but failed to elevate tech-based stocks. The growing uncertainty across the US & Asian markets heavily impacted the Bitcoin price, which marked lows around $61,290. Although the price slightly recovered to $61,700, the $60,000 support is now back in focus, with $58,000 appearing to be an important range to hold. The coming sessions could be extremely important in determining whether BTC price stages a relief rebound or extends its decline.

Bitcoin’s daily chart shows a clear shift in price structure, with the token forming lower highs and lows since its October peak. The decline has intensified in the recent sessions, pushing the price close to $61,000. Currently, the price has entered a crucial support zone that offered a strong base in June 2026 and propelled the levels beyond $88,000. However, the technicals remained largely bullish then, while fear of an extended correction looms over the BTC price rally.

The momentum indicators also reflect a sell-off, with the RSI trading well below the lower threshold. On the other hand, the CMF remains below the average levels, indicating that money flow remains tilted towards selling pressure rather than accumulation. Therefore, the price range between $61,600 and $62,000 remains the immediate level to watch, as a breakdown could expose the rally towards the lower support at $58,000.

Bitcoin’s decline has been accompanied by a noticeable drop in aggregated open interest, which currently stands at approximately $22.06 billion. This suggests the traders are closing positions as the market weakens, potentially reducing some of the leverage that had built up.

The funding rate is also negative, pointing to a bearish tilt in derivatives positioning. Meanwhile, both spot and futures CVD remain negative, with spot CVD at -176.413K and futures CVD at -393.75K. Moreover, $195 million in BTC positions were liquidated over the past 24 hours, including $155 million in longs.

The decline in open interest, negative funding rate, heavy long liquidations, and negative spot and futures CVD collectively point to a market still dominated by bearish sentiment and selling pressure. While falling open interest and long liquidations suggest that leveraged positions are being flushed out, the negative CVD readings indicate that buying demand has yet to show a convincing recovery. Bitcoin may be seeing a leverage reset, but confirmation of a reversal would require stronger spot buying and a sustained price recovery.

The BTC price remains at a critical juncture as price hovers around the $61,000 to $62,000 zone. The daily RSI has dropped into deeply oversold territory, but weak money flow and negative spot and futures CVD suggest buyers have yet to establish control. Meanwhile, falling open interest and heavy long liquidations indicate that leverage is being flushed out, although this alone does not confirm a reversal.

A sustained reclaim of $62,000 could open the door toward $64,000, while continued weakness could expose Bitcoin to a further decline toward $58,000. For now, the reaction around $62,000 will be crucial in determining whether BTC price attempts a short-term recovery or extends its downtrend.
