Rangers avoid title blip as late own goal breaks Motherwell resistance and Celtic kept within eyeline

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

In the crisp chill of a late November evening in North Lanarkshire, Rangers required a late deflection to prise three vital Scottish Premiership points from the grasp of a resolute and industrious Motherwell side.

With the contest seemingly bound to end 1-1, a potentially significant blip for the visitors in terms of their title aspirations, Ryan Naderi’s 88th-minute effort struck the unfortunate Martin Moormann and looped past goalkeeper Alex Paulsen, inflicting the cruellest of blows on the Steelmen.

There seemed an almost ritualistic rhythm to Rangers’ recent visits to Fir Park: waves of possession crashing against a disciplined claret-and-amber breakwater, accompanied by the persistent nagging doubt that dominance without ruthlessness invites punishment.

Derek McInnes’s side were living out that exact cautionary tale on their latest visit before Naderi popped up with a crucial late winner.

For the opening 15 minutes, this contest threatened to become a procession. Rangers monopolised the ball and the opening goal arrived after just eight minutes. Motherwell’s defensive structure momentarily frayed under early pressure, allowing centre-back Emmanuel Fernandez to rise unmarked and head home Kim Min-Su’s corner with disarming simplicity.

At that juncture, Motherwell appeared to be unravelling. Within six minutes of falling behind, Alfred Johansson was forced into an early reshuffle when winger Ibrahim Said limped off injured in visible distress to be replaced by Regan Charles-Cook.

Rangers sensed blood. Naderi probed tirelessly, Djeidi Gassama repeatedly cut inside off the left flank to test the Steelmen defence, and Motherwell goalkeeper Alex Paulsen was called upon to produce a string of top-class saves to prevent the visitors from establishing an unassailable cushion.

However, Scottish football rarely rewards aesthetic superiority alone. Having weathered the initial storm without conceding a second, the hosts summoned an equaliser from their first corner of the evening in the 21st minute. Emmanuel Longelo rolled a clever short pass to Tom Sparrow, whose curling delivery into the penalty area found Jake Girdwood-Reich entirely unmarked. The Australian defender needed no second invitation, sweeping a composed finish past Ivor Pandur to restore parity against the run of play.

That subtly altered the psychological temperature inside Fir Park. While Rangers continued to dictate proceedings, racking up nearly 20 attempts on goal, their lack of cutting edge in the final third was glaring. Before the interval, Paulsen defied the visitors once more with a superb reflex stop from Nico Raskin in the 41st minute.

McInnes’ men pressed forward with renewed urgency after a stern half-time team talk. Seven minutes into the second half, Dan Neil drifted into a pocket of space on the edge of the area and drilled a low effort that whistled agonisingly past the post.

Visible frustration began to creep into Rangers’ ranks passing the hour mark as a combination of misplaced passes and poor finishing drew audible groans from the packed away end.

Sensing his team’s grip on the contest loosening, McInnes turned to his bench in the 69th minute, introducing summer signing Kevin Kelsy in place of Gassama to add a greater physical presence in attack alongside Naderi. There was little immediate improvement. Badredine Bouanani raced down the right flank and cut the ball back for Neil, whose shot sailed high and wide.

Motherwell were looking increasingly comfortable and Tawanda Maswanhise tested Pandur from distance as the home side sensed an opportunity to claim something more substantial than a point.

Findlay Curtis was introduced for Rangers and almost made an immediate impact, delivering an excellent cross towards the back post which Naderi was unable to turn home from close range.

Then came another extraordinary intervention from Paulsen. Curtis' effort appeared to have beaten the keeper, who could only palm the ball into the path of Sterling. However, Paulsen recovered magnificently to divert the right-back’s follow-up onto the crossbar. It was a remarkable piece of goalkeeping and, by this stage, it seemed entirely possible that the home side would clinch a share of the spoils.

With two minutes remaining, Rangers finally found the breakthrough their territorial dominance had threatened to deliver. Naderi got his effort away, but Moormann intervened and inadvertently diverted the ball beyond his own goalkeeper. The unfortunate centre-back could do little about the decisive deflection, but the consequences were devastating for Motherwell.

The travelling Rangers support erupted, relief and celebration combining in equal measure. For McInnes, it was a precious late reward for a side that had made unnecessarily hard work of a match they might have settled long before the closing stages.

The victory extends Rangers' unbeaten run in all competitions to 13 matches and ensures they remain in touch with Celtic, moving back within four points of the league leaders with a game in hand.
