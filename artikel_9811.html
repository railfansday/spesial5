Why You Go Quiet the Moment Someone Asks About You

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Posted on Nov 4 • Originally published at Medium

You are good at keeping a conversation going. You ask questions. You listen. People tell you about their jobs, their kids, their weekend.

Then they turn it around. "So what do you do?" "What about you, any plans this weekend?"

And you give a one line answer, shrug, and flip it right back to them as fast as you can.

Later you wonder why the conversation never really went anywhere.

Your brain treats attention like a threat

There is a part of your brain called the reticular activating system, the RAS. It filters what you notice out of everything going on around you. If enough past moments of being the center of attention went badly, it learned a rule: attention on you means danger.

So the moment the spotlight swings your way, the filter starts scanning. Are they bored? Did that sound weird? Am I talking too long? Your mind is so busy watching for judgment that there is no room left to actually answer the question.

Asking questions feels safe because the attention stays on them. That is why you are great at it.

Why preparing better answers does not fix it

A lot of people try to solve this by rehearsing. They come up with a smooth answer to "what do you do" and a couple of stories ready to go.

It helps a little. But the filter does not care how polished your answer is. It cares whether being looked at has been safe before. You cannot script your way past something your brain learned through experience. You have to give it new experiences.

Start smaller than you think you need to.

Move up only when your current step feels boring. Boring is the sign your brain stopped treating attention as a threat.

After the conversation, skip the replay of whether they seemed interested. Ask one question: did I share one true thing? If yes, you won. That is the only scoreboard that changes the filter.

I used to be the guy who once spent four hours walking around Chicago trying to work up the nerve to talk to one stranger. What changed it was not a better script. It was small reps, every day, in the right order.
