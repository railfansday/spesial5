Organic Search Is Shrinking, While Email Builds a More Durable Audience

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Posted on Nov 4 • Originally published at blog.geosearchleads.com

Organic discovery is becoming a less dependable single source of website visits in the data examined by Search Engine Land. At the same time, email is growing in both absolute visits and year-over-year performance, giving publishers, brands, and ecommerce sites a stronger reason to invest in audiences they can reach without relying entirely on a search results page.

In Kevin Indig's Search Engine Land analysis of Similarweb data, email visits rose from January through August 2026 compared with the same period in 2025. The analysis also found that direct and organic traffic declined over a longer two-year comparison. The evidence does not mean search has stopped mattering. It does suggest that visibility in search and AI-generated answers should increasingly support a broader audience-development model rather than stand alone.

The analysis covered roughly 1,896 to 1,897 domains, depending on the comparison. It is therefore an observed sample, not a measure of every site or all web traffic.

For January through August 2026 versus the same months in 2025, email visits increased 16.3% in the United States and 17.0% in France. Paid search also grew in the reported data, by 20.8% in the U.S. and 20.3% in France, although those figures require important attribution context.

Direct remained the largest channel in the analysis, but it was shrinking. Direct visits were down 2.7% year over year in the U.S. and 3.1% in France for January through August. From August 2024 to August 2026, direct visits declined 3.4% in the U.S. and 5.3% in France. Organic visits fell more sharply over that latter period: 13.2% in the U.S. and 23.4% in France.

These are not proof that every website is losing organic traffic or that email will outperform every other channel. They are a useful directional signal from the domains studied, particularly for teams whose acquisition plans still assume organic traffic will be the primary scalable source of returning attention.

One of the most valuable parts of the analysis is its warning about routing and attribution. Paid-search estimates can change depending on how visits through Google's googleadservices.com ad-tracking domain are categorized.

When that domain was included, reported paid-search growth was 13.5% in the U.S. and 10.7% in France. When it was excluded, paid-search growth rose to 21.0% in the U.S. and 15.2% in France. In those comparisons, email growth was reported at 20.5% in the U.S. and 17.6% in France.

The discrepancy is a reminder that channel labels are not always clean descriptions of user behavior. Redirects, tracking domains, analytics configuration, consent choices, apps, and users moving between channels can all influence what is credited to organic, paid, direct, referral, or email.

For marketers, the practical lesson is not to disregard channel reports. It is to avoid treating a single channel line item as a complete causal explanation. Compare analytics platforms and tagging rules, document changes in tracking, and evaluate acquisition alongside downstream engagement and repeat visits.

Organic search, Google AI Overviews, AI Mode, ChatGPT Search, and other answer engines can still introduce people to a brand. But discovery is different from a durable audience relationship. A citation, ranking, or mention may generate awareness without creating a reliable path back to the site.

Direct traffic deserves similar attention, even though it declined in the reported data. Direct is a mixed category, not a pure measure of brand loyalty. It can include untagged campaigns and other traffic that analytics cannot classify. Still, rising repeat visits, bookmarked access, and branded return behavior are generally useful indicators that a site is building recognition beyond one-time search clicks.

The appropriate response is diversification, not abandoning SEO or treating email as a replacement for search. Search content can create the demand that makes subscriptions and repeat visits possible. The goal is to make the channels reinforce each other.

Start by reviewing which high-value organic landing pages attract new visitors but fail to create a next step. Add relevant, low-friction subscription opportunities that match the page's topic or user need. A newsletter about the subject, a product-update list, or a recurring research digest can be more useful than a generic sign-up request.

Next, separate discovery metrics from relationship metrics. Track organic entrances, AI referral traffic where it is identifiable, email-driven sessions, returning users, branded search interest, and conversions. Do not assume a fall in last-click organic traffic means search content has no role in subsequent email sign-ups or direct returns.

Finally, audit attribution before changing budgets. The googleadservices.com finding illustrates how much channel interpretation can depend on classification. Maintain consistent UTM conventions, review redirect behavior, and annotate major tracking or consent changes when comparing periods.

If AI search reduces clicks for some informational queries, this approach becomes even more relevant. The content that earns visibility should also demonstrate enough expertise and usefulness that the people who do click choose to establish a direct relationship.

Does this analysis show that organic search is declining for every website?

No. The analysis reports trends across a sample of roughly 1,896 to 1,897 domains in the U.S. and France. Performance can differ significantly by site, market, content type, and measurement setup.

Why did paid-search growth change when googleadservices.com was included or excluded?

Google's ad-tracking domain can affect how visits are routed and categorized in traffic data. The analysis found materially different paid-search growth rates under the two treatments, showing that attribution rules affect interpretation.

Is direct traffic a reliable measure of brand loyalty?

Not by itself. Direct traffic can include bookmarked visits and people intentionally returning to a brand, but it can also include traffic that analytics could not attribute to another source. It is best evaluated alongside branded search, email engagement, and returning-user behavior.

Should businesses replace SEO with email marketing?

No. The data supports diversifying acquisition and building owned audience relationships, not replacing search. SEO and GEO can support discovery, while email provides a way to maintain contact with interested visitors.

The Similarweb findings point to a practical shift in priorities: organic visibility remains valuable, but it is less safe as the sole foundation for audience growth. Sites that connect search and AI discovery to permission-based email relationships, repeat visits, and disciplined attribution will be better positioned to understand and reduce their dependence on any one traffic source.
