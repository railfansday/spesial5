Slay the Spire 2: 10 Best Fool Cards, Ranked

Finally, the Slay the Spire 2 community has been blessed with a new character, and this one packs some serious firepower! The Fool is the newest addition to the Spire, and they’ve got their sights set on bringing down the Architect once and for all.

It's time to turn up the heat! Slay the Spire 2's changes to the format make it more difficult than ever.

But just who is this mysterious Fool, and what powers do they wield? Well, there are quite a few powerful gimmicks in this deck, the most broken of which are the Wah Cho Ki cards. These are melodies, kind of like the Defect orbs that, when played, help you trigger other powerful cards.

Of course, you also have your bow and arrow cards that can deal serious burst damage, the Stretch mechanic, which is great for blocking, and the Sadness mechanic, which can help you prolong your turns.

But which of these cards are the most powerful for a deck, and which ones should you always be on the lookout for? Let’s dive right in!

Encore is a fun one, massively helping you extend your turns and get the most out of a tricky hand all for the great cost of 0 energy!

Once played, you gain one energy and draw two cards. However, you can only play one more card for the rest of the turn. Realistically, you should play this when you've exhausted your energy and want to eke out a little extra block or damage.

However, there are a couple more ways to play this. You can use it for a big card, or extend your energy for an X-cost card like Repeat Shot. Furthermore, if you manage to clone this card, Encore actually stacks on top of itself, replacing the effect every time you play it. Playing multiple copies of this leads to some ridiculous turns.

I put these two cards together because they serve a pretty similar purpose, and well, it’s probably not worth having both in your deck unless it’s specifically set up for that.

Both of these cards do a huge amount of single-target damage using your arrow. Sky Shot is easier to get, as it’s just a rare card, and it has Innate, giving you a ridiculously strong first turn and often ending fights before they even begin.

Divine Shot, on the other hand, is an upgrade of Foolish Shot and can be found by picking the Archaic Tooth Relic from Orobas. This also does a huge amount of damage, costs 0 energy, and even has Retain, so you can use it on the turn it's most effective. While these cards pack a punch, it is always worth being cautious about how many Bow cards you have in your deck. If you don’t have a spare arrow, it’s basically a curse!

Sadness, for the most part, is generally a pretty bad thing in STS 2. It clogs up your hand and is a bit of an eyesore. But that doesn’t mean it’s useless. If you can turn this Sadness into Joy with a card like Punch of Joy or Ecstasy, it can become a blessing.

Now, on a card like Brighten Up, we have an engine with an upside and a downside, but if you have the required pieces, Brighten Up can be doubly beneficial!

When played, this card gives you 2 Sadness and 2 Joy, or 3 Joy when upgraded. For one energy, you’re basically getting three more energy and three free draws, making it an incredible card for extending your turns and ending fights without taking damage. If you have a way to get rid of that Sadness, then this card is unreal.

Motif, at first glance, might seem a little weak to some. two energy is a lot to spend on a card that doesn’t do anything immediately. However, upgrading this halves its cost, making it way more playable.

Motif draws you an extra card every time you gain a melody, so that you can draw up to three extra cards per turn. The thing is, when you have a small deck and not much energy generation, this often doesn’t help much.

Which, of course, means that Motif should be a card picked up in the latter half of your run, when you have more resources available. When you start getting longer turns, and your deck has more powerful individual cards, Motif suddenly becomes extremely potent.

The thing is, a lot of cards in Slay the Spire only become good at a certain point in time, or when paired with something else to allow a certain combo. You know what’s better than that? Something that’s cracked right out of the box!

Cho Firm is one of those cards, and one that’s pretty easy to overlook. So long as you have an arrow available, which is most of the time if you’re playing a melodic-based build, you’ll gain double the block from this card. Generally, this’ll give you 12 block for one energy; however, there are plenty of ways to enchant this, and whatever you gain from the first block will double.

And of course, if this wasn’t powerful enough, you’ll also gain a Cho melody, activating cards like Song Path, Motif and Wah Blast. For a common card, this one is a must-pick for every deck.

I mentioned Wah Blast in the previous entry for good reason. This card is an absolute monster! For starters, playing this card normally deals 14 (18) damage to all enemies. That’s already decent.

However, it gets even better when you pair this with other melody cards. If you have a Cho or Ki melody, playing something like Cho Firm will make this attack hit twice! That’s 28 (36) damage across the board with a single attack and very little set-up.

And of course, like Cho Firm before it, this activates any card that wants more Wah melodies, like Violent Chord, Motif or Rhythm Strike. Under the right conditions, paired with Violent Chord, this card can wipe out entire multi-enemy combats in a single turn. No more losing Max HP from those pesky biting books in Act 3!

If you happen to come across Darv in Act 2 or 3 and see the Dusty Tome on offer, well, drop everything, because things are about to get absurd!

Picking up the Dusty Tome gives you the Lost Lute, a Power card that plays the top card from your deck every time you gain a Melody. Do I have to explain how ridiculous that effect is? Getting multiple melodies in a single turn is pretty easy, and this Power can even play you more melodies, meaning more cards!

At three energy, this is a pretty expensive card, but unlike the other Form cards, upgrading it reduces its cost to two, making it much easier to slot in. While it might be tough to get this card to work in the 2nd act, it really shines in Act 3, and if you manage to cheat it out with something like Jewelled Mask, you can win entire runs with this card alone.

And speaking of broken three-cost cards, we have Mythic Form, which honestly might be the most powerful Form card, beating out Echo Form and Void Form for the title.

At the start of your turn, you’ll get a random Tarot card added to your hand. But just what are these Tarot cards, and what do they do? Well, Tarot cards are stupidly broken: they all have 0 energy and can do anything from giving you seven energy to instantly killing anything under 30 HP, dealing damage based on what floor you’re on, and giving you an extra turn, among others!

Similar to Lost Lute, the only tough thing about this card is finding time to play it. As soon as you start gaining the effects from this card, you just begin to steamroll! But for a form card, this feels pretty fitting! Let’s just hope it doesn’t get nerfed too hard!

If you enjoyed using Wah Blast, meet its older, more powerful brother, Wah Symphony!

Wah Symphony deals six damage to all enemies… sounds pretty bad, right? Well, this card gains an extra four (six) damage for every other card containing Wah Cho or Ki in your deck. Now, suddenly, simply by owning other good cards that you’re going to be using all the time, this card skyrockets in power.

It’s pretty normal for this card to deal 40+ damage to every enemy every time you use it, and of course, if you have something like Bag of Marbles, it can often end fights on Turn 1. And as usual, I have to mention it also procs cards that benefit from Wah, making it even more powerful. This card is wildly strong!

Let me introduce you to the ultimate card in the Fool’s portfolio. Yep, with a name like Wah Cho Ki, I’m sure you can already imagine why this card is pretty broken… but I’ll break it down.

For starters, for one energy, this card deals 9 damage, gives you 9 block and draws a card. Upgraded, this goes to 12 damage and two cards! Already, these are ridiculous numbers for a single energy, giving you a bit of everything!

Of course the real potential of this card comes from the synergies it allows. And those synergies are… well, all of them! Yep, this single card gives you every melody, so any other card that requires even one is immediately fulfilled. So if you have Song Path, Violent Chord and Motif, this card basically doubles in power.

It sounds simple on paper, but when you think about how much this card actually does, you’ll be terrified when Mega Crit comes in with the nerf hammer!
