Windows is the operating system that everyone thinks of first when they hear the term OS. The thing is, not all versions of Windows were created equal. Windows 11 is great, particularly for gaming, but if you really want to harness all of your PC’s power and all of its RAM, you really should be using Windows 11 Pro.

Windows 11 Pro supports PCs with up to 2TB of RAM. That’s almost 16 times more than the standard Home version of Windows 11 can handle. It also boasts a Remote Desktop feature that allows you to log in and control your gaming PC hub from any other device in the house. An incredibly useful feature for gamers who hop between devices based on what they’re playing and where they prefer to play it.

VPNs are becoming more and more commonly used by just about everyone. If you have a gaming PC but you don’t have a VPN, you’re doing yourself a disservice. You should have one for a multitude of reasons, and if you’re going to get one, then you may as well make it the industry standard, NordVPN.

VPNs can protect gamers from DDoS attacks, whether they be the kind that target entire games and studios, which are unfortunately becoming more common, or the kind that focus on you, courtesy of a disgruntled rival you may have bested in an online lobby overreacting to their defeat. A VPN also means you can bypass regional restrictions and grab games that might not be available in your part of the world.

As for why NordVPN, the gold standard offers military-grade encryption that offers better security and protection than just about any rival VPN on the market. It also offers two additional security protocols so that even the actions between your PC and the VPN itself can be masked.

Have you ever been playing a game on your PC that should look and run a lot better than it does? Or been sitting with a new game in your virtual cart, wondering if your setup will be able to run it at its best? Well, there’s software for that. A program that will test everything about your PC and its capabilities that checks back in with you to let you know what it can handle, or why it can’t handle what you expect from it.

That program is called 3DMark. 3DMark can check in on all sorts of internal details and potential issues with your PC that let you know where you might need to upgrade, or what needs to be fixed. It will automatically let you know what your PC’s ideal benchmark is, estimate how games will perform based on those results, and monitor important variables such as CPU temperature, speed, and framerate.
