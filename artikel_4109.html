Half-Life: Alyx Was Officially Announced 7 Years Ago Today

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Valve has a penchant for making the biggest, boldest announcements, with the fanfare of a local bus route confirming cancellations. "We're excited to unveil Half-Life: Alyx, our flagship VR game, this Thursday." That's how the first Half-Life game in over a decade was announced.

No trailer, not even a screenshot, a tweet. And it worked. I vividly remember seeing that post while scrolling my feed and almost leaping out of my chair. The long-rumored HLVR was real. It hadn't been canceled without a peep from Valve like so many iterations before it. The dry streak had ended and Half-Life was back.

I hate to make you feel old, and remind you of the brutal passage of time, but that announcement was 7 years ago today—over half the length between Episode Two and Alyx.

So, where's Half-Life 3? The powder keg felt as though it was finally going to blow last October, and then last November, and then last December, and then... poof. It was a dud. No news, no Half-Life 3, and silence from Valve. Mike Shapiro's cryptic 2025 new year's tease led to nothing. The HLX leaks and confident reports from everyone, not just the usual leakers, fell flat. And now, fans are wary of jumping on the hopium train again lest they fall into a Citadel core of disappointment.

By all accounts, Valve did intend to unveil HLX, widely believed to be Half-Life 3, but delayed its plans because something was "not up to snuff." Whatever that something was, we don't know, but it was enough that timelines got completely pushed back. Fans are again convinced that Geoff Keighley, posting his usual anniversary celebrations, is teasing HLX at The Game Awards, but given Valve's unceremonious Twitter announcements in the past, that's incredibly unlikely. What we do know is that Half-Life 3 hasn't been canceled, as an employee outright mentioned HLX during a livestream.

Although it doesn't completely change the game, Half-Life: Alyx sets a new standard for quality that will unquestionably make a huge impact on VR.

Mike Morasky said during the stream that "HLX has its own version" of Deadlock's HDR system, to which his coworker muttered, "nice work." Half-Life 3 hasn't gone the way of... well, Half-Life 3. It still exists, and dataminers continue to find evidence that it's being worked on. Considering it was an internal delay and not an outright cancelation and reboot, we probably won't be waiting another 7 years for Valve to announce it, even if 2026 ends without a whiff of confirmation.

In late October, reputable Valve leaker Tyler McVicker, who held his ground on 'HLVR' right up until Alyx was confirmed on November 18, 2019, uncovered all sorts of new strings—Hammer map editor enhancements, Combine gunships with bomb targets, and even a surge of activity surrounding the game. Gabe Follower claimed just last week that Valve had added new rendering functionality to Counter-Strike 2, including parallax occlusion mapping and frost effects. These were used for the new winter-themed Cobblestone, but could easily be doubled up on for HLX, given that Episode Two's cliffhanger ends with Gordon Freeman set for an expedition to the Arctic in search of the Borealis.

So, it's been 7 years since Alyx was announced, and 6 years since Alyx launched. To answer the question, 'Where is Half-Life 3?' It's being worked on behind closed doors in complete secrecy, with the occasional slip-up from devs on livestreams confirming its existence, while dataminers rip out whatever references they can from the innards of its development. It's real. It hasn't been canceled. That could always change, but for now, there's reason to be optimistic. Hopefully, one day soon, we'll have an announcement that we can look back on in 7 years' time just like this.
