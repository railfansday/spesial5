Why these candylike gumdrops have converted me into a creatine lover

The supplement landscape has never been murkier, more confusing, or more overwhelming. While I have a pretty extensive wellness regimen, it feels like there are a million and one powders, pills, and gummies I should be adding to my routine. One supplement that keeps coming into the spotlight is creatine.

I first heard about creatine from my 62-year-old mother, who waxed poetic about the supplement. Then, the influencer of all influencers, Hailey Bieber, posted on Instagram a few weeks later a creatine gummy from Arrae. I thought: That’s it. I’m taking the creatine plunge.

“Creatine works like an energy recycler,” says Roxanne Pero, a Dallas-based obstetrician and gynecologist and part of O Positiv’s Medical Advisory Board. “Every cell in the body needs energy, and that energy comes from a molecule called ATP, or adenosine triphosphate. Creatine helps the body regenerate ATP more quickly, especially when energy runs low, and especially in tissues that use a lot of it, like muscle and the brain.”

As someone who works out five times a week, often in heated classes that push my body to depletion, I figured I was the perfect candidate for creatine. But it’s actually one of those supplements anyone can benefit from; it kicks in at moments when our cells need more energy—not just during strenuous exercise, but also during sleep deprivation and stress, which everyone experiences at some point. It’s even recommended for perimenopause, menopause, and postpartum.

“Active women in their 20s and 30s may benefit from improved training performance, while women approaching menopause or experiencing it may be interested in supporting muscle strength as part of a broader strategy that includes resistance training and adequate protein,” says Rachel Brief, a registered dietitian and wellness advisor at the Lanby.

Recently, the supplement has gained traction among women in their 40s and above. “As a woman enters menopause and postmenopause, the decrease in estrogen contributes to age-related muscle and strength loss,” says Shilpa Raut, the vice president of R&D at Cymbiotika, who holds a master’s degree and a PhD in pharmaceutical sciences. Creatine can help women maintain muscle mass, support steady metabolic health, and improve cognitive resilience, which often declines with age.

If you’re wondering why everyone isn’t already taking this magic supplement, well, I was too. Creatine has long been associated with gym bros, and it has a bad reputation for causing bloating, which is not a risk I’m willing to take as a girl with a sensitive gut. I also wasn’t excited about consuming a chalky white powder. But Arrae’s Tone gummies tout all of the benefits without the bloat, and they come in a candylike form. “Creatine had always been marketed to men, because no one had ever presented it in a way that felt like it was made for women,” Arrae’s cofounder Siffat Haider tells me. “Our customers adopted it immediately, and that demand is why we kept building on the flavors.”

The one caveat with creatine is that you need to drink water to get the full benefits. “Creatine draws water into muscle cells, and that is part of how it works,” says Michael Fredericson, a sports-medicine physiatrist, professor at Stanford University, and Elysium Scientific Advisory Board member. If you’re dehydrated, creatine will pull your body’s existing water supply into your muscles, causing dehydration and hindering normal bodily functions.

I’m now two months into my experiment with Arrae Tone. Whenever I tell people that I started taking creatine, they ask me the same question: “Does it actually work?” My verdict: I don’t plan to stop taking it any time soon. Experts say creatine is especially beneficial as a supplement to strength-training workouts, so I was excited to see how it amplified my workouts. Even a few weeks into taking creatine, I noticed more definition in my abdominals and arms, areas where I’ve historically struggled to see muscle.

The Arrae gummies have no chalky taste and are easy to swallow. I take a lot of different powders and pills, so having a delicious gummy is a nice change of pace. Plus, the dosage is only 3.75 to 5 grams (depending on whether you take three or four gummies), which is on the lower end of the spectrum and has been proven to be effective without causing the dreaded bloating. And it’s creatine monohydrate, which is the gold standard of creatine, Brief explains. Still, they’re not my only form of creatine. On days when I’d rather add a powder to a smoothie or my water to boost hydration, I love the Elysium Health powders; they’re only 5 grams, so they’re easy on my stomach, and they come in delicious fruity flavors that taste like nostalgic lemonade.
