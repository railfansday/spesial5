The United Nations announces it is developing the “Global Information Integrity Exchange (GIIEx)” to provide solutions to “a global information crisis.”

Hours upon hours of the 81st UN General Assembly was filmed and made publicly available on the UN website; however, video from the September 24 “Information Integrity Solutions Dialogue” where the announcement was made, has not seen the light of day.

It wasn’t until October 2 that the UN put out a press release announcing the results of the session and the development of the GIIEx.

“The UN is developing the Global Information Integrity Exchange (GIIEx), which will connect practitioners, researchers, policymakers and other stakeholders working on solutions to information threats”

The “Information Integrity Soultions Dialogue” was co-hosted by the government of Brazil, the UN Secretariat, the UN Development Program (UNDP), and UNESCO.

Speaking at the dialogue, UN Under-Secretary-General for Global Communications Melissa “We Own The Science” Fleming took aim at bad actors like big tech companies and advertisers for contributing to “a global information crisis.”

“Today, a handful of companies still dominate and shape our global information architecture,” said Fleming, adding, “their priorities remain the same: engagement, profit. Always placed above the public interest.

“Today, information defenders – civil society, journalists, scientists and others – are still underfunded and under attack.”

“In short, we are deep into a global information crisis. The crisis has grown but so has our resolve”

Melissa Fleming, UN Information Integrity Soultions Dialogue, September 2026

This year’s Information Integrity Solutions Dialogue was a continuation of the UN’s Global Principles for Information Integrity and Global Digital Compact, which were both launched in 2024.

Prior to that, the UN established a “voluntary UN Code of Conduct for Information Integrity on Digital Platforms” for member states and other “stakeholders” to follow.

This “voluntary UN Code of Conduct” is replete with policies aimed at silencing dissenting voices on digital platforms under the guise of mitigating “mis- and disinformation,” which is conveniently lumped-in with hate speech.

After all, you can’t build a digital control grid without first establishing a solid technological foundation with a basic internet connection.
