Hands-on: The Apple HomeView is gorgeous, costs $299, and has no App Store

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

The Apple HomeView finally arrives on doorsteps (and in Apple Stores) on Friday, and I think this gorgeous little smart display is about to become a common sight in homes around the world. I've been testing the HomeView ahead of its release, and I would definitely recommend it to homeowners, parents, and anyone looking for Christmas gift ideas.

From the moment I saw the HomeView up close, I've had a premonition: Apple is going to sell a million of these things. And after adding it to my own home, I'm even more confident in that prediction.

As a product reviewer, you get a sixth sense for this. The last time I felt this way about a product? Back in March, when Apple debuted the MacBook Neo, which proved to be extremely popular.

For someone like me, who lives in a one-bedroom New York City apartment and tries not to think about whether or not I'll ever be able to afford a down payment, the Apple HomeView is an awesomely unnecessary sidekick. Personally, I'd buy an iPad stand before a HomeView, but YMMV. However, for my parents, my big sister, and my better-off friends who do own their own homes, I would definitely recommend this device.

For upper-income homeowners, the HomeView is a beautiful digital butler for the modern smart abode. (Side note: Apple products have always been high-end, but after the latest price increases, it feels more and more like a luxury brand to me.) If you plan on investing in Apple's other new smart home gadgets — the new Apple TV 4K, HomePod Mini 2, new home security cameras, and video doorbells — I'd even say the HomeView is a necessity.

Thanks to the Apple Home app, you can use the HomeView to adjust smart thermostats, view live security camera footage, dim the lights, and turn connected devices on or off. An intercom function lets you use connected speakers to send messages around the house. For this type of user, the wall-mounted model is ideal, though the mount is sold separately and brings the total to $378.99. For those of you who can afford all those smart home gadgets, why not get a HomeView for your entryway and your kitchen? I suspect many wealthy homeowners will do exactly that.

The HomeView doesn't just act as a smart home hub. You can also listen to Apple Music, watch Apple TV, FaceTime with family, browse for recipes in Safari, and check your Apple calendar. The App Store is conspicuously missing, so Safari is your only option for services like YouTube, Gmail, TikTok, and Disney+. (The HomeView runs on its own custom OS that Apple calls HomeView software.) That lack of apps is the biggest problem with the HomeView so far.

But if you're already a happy little pea in the iOS pod, you can get a lot done with Apple's own apps and widgets, and I think the HomeView justifies its $299 price for the right user.

After Apple announced the HomeView, I said on Mashable's Instagram that this was a 10/10 product design. I might revise that down to 9.5/10, as it does have larger bezels than most modern displays. But I'm still enamored with the design.

The HomeView actually comes in two models: one with a domed speaker base and an adjustable hinge for the display, and a wall-mounted version. Whether you set it on your kitchen counter or mount it in your entryway, it looks beautiful. For this review in progress, I'm going to focus on the speaker version I tested, which I also think will have a wider appeal.

The HomeView has a premium design, with a recycled aluminum body and a stainless steel adjustable hinge for the display. It has perforations on the bottom of the display and speaker base for audio, and a camera and sensor in the upper bezel. The 7-inch Liquid Retina display looks clear and bright whether you're catching up on the latest Silo cliffhanger on Apple TV or admiring the lava lamp-inspired home screen. The HomeView defaults to a swirling abstract clock face that responds to light and movement. The shapes on the display will darken in low light and track your movements. Like the opening/closing animation on the iPhone Duo, it's the kind of subtle design flex that reminds you Apple does consumer tech design better than anyone. You can also cycle through a variety of widgets and clock faces at your leisure.

Apple's new smart display may lack third-party apps, but Apple taught it some pretty cool tricks. The HomeView recognizes up to six people in your household by face and voice (if they have a connected iPad or iPhone running iOS 27.2). So, when you step up to the HomeView, it instantly pulls up your messages, your calendar, and your preferences. But if your husband or wife walks up to it, they'll see their own information, or personal context, as Apple calls it.

The HomeView also has a custom recipe experience, which recognizes a recipe on Safari and breaks it down into simple lists of ingredients and steps. (I'm not sure how recipe writers will feel about this feature, but it's undeniably useful.) And if you don't have a smart thermostat, the HomeView has its own temperature and humidity sensors.

The HomeView also makes good use of the new Siri AI beta. When you say, "Hey, Siri...," the new Siri logo drops from the top bezel with a nifty teardrop animation. But if you've tried the new Siri AI, then you know it's still not done cooking. Siri sometimes makes stupid mistakes, though in general, the combination of broad world knowledge and personal context results in useful answers to my questions.

Siri AI has been improving with each OS update, and hopefully that continues to be true.

Like most new Apple products, the HomeView has a 12MP Center Stage camera for video calls. It works well enough for FaceTime calls, which is good, because that's its main job.

I did find the speakers a little disappointing. Because the HomeView is more speaker-shaped than a MacBook, I expected more from it. The new MacBook Pro PROLED has much better speakers, for instance. I found the bass disappointing, not in terms of volume, but in depth and mix.

The speaker system isn't bad exactly. It's just not quite as good as I expected. But for jamming out to music while you work or cook, it's just like the Center Stage camera: good enough.

Colors: Silver, Starlight, Rose Pink, and Space Gray

Display Panel: 7-inch Liquid Retina display

Display Brightness: Up to 500 nits (0.3 nits minimum brightness)

Power: Wired USB-C 30W power adapter (wall-mounted model comes with 20W power adapter)

Connectivity: WiFi 6E, Bluetooth 6.0, Thread

Audio: 2 full-range drivers, 1 down-firing woofer, and 2 passive radiators (wall-mounted model has two custom full-range drivers)

Sensors: Temperature, humidity, ambient light, and accelerometer

System Requirements: Setup requires an iPhone running iOS 27.2 or an iPad running iPadOS 27.2

Dimensions: 5.73 inches × 6.82 inches × 5.13 inches (wall-mounted model is 5.73 × 5.73 × 0.39 inches)

Weight: 2.1 pounds (wall-mounted model weighs 0.63 pounds)
