The Blue Tsunami Made Waves in Ways I Didn’t Expect, but I’m More Than Glad to See It

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Sure, some elections touched upon the second-worst idea in American politics. But we have to take the wins when we can.

(Permanent Musical Accompaniment to This Post)

Being our semi-regular weekly survey of what’s goin’ down in the several states where, as we know, the real work of governmentin’ goes on, and where guns and clubs followed him down, all because his face was brown, better get away from Oxford town.

I’ve been busy. Did anything happen out in the states?

Anyway, the good folks at Bolts tracked an interesting rivulet of the blue wave that touches upon the second-worst idea in American politics: an elected judiciary. It seems that the momentum last week swept into state supreme court chambers.

Montana politics never cease to amaze me. The state voted for Bill Clinton twice but has gone red ever since. It had a long and proud tradition of campaign-finance legislation until Citizens United turned the table. Like all timber and mining states, Montana has a long—and fairly unique—history of labor activity. Butte became known as the “Gibraltar of Unionism.” In 1914, two factions of the powerful Western Federation of Miners went to war with each other. (The dissidents blew up the union hall in Butte.) The recent election shifted things around within that state tradition.

Similar relief spread all over the Mountain West.

And it seems possible that there’s more to come.

Meanwhile, Kansans continued to bedevil conservatives.

Speaking of Ohio, Justice Jennifer Brunner is the only Democrat on the court right now. And she has been on the right side of some very bad decisions.

As a karmic reward, Brunner will be joined by fellow Democrat Marilyn Zayas, who beat an incumbent Republican.

We have seen the importance of state supreme courts in this dismal time. Many of them threw sand in the gears of Republican gerrymandering schemes. The effect of the Democratic gains in the states is going to be deep and vast. And Deb Haaland, one of the blog’s favorite people, is the new governor of New Mexico.

And we conclude, as is our custom, in the great state of Oklahoma, whence Blog Official Escapee Friedman of the Algarve brings us a tale we can all cheer, given the events in Mississippi and elsewhere last Tuesday. From the Oklahoma Eagle:

She lived long enough to see it all. God save the good work.

This really is your democracy, America. Cherish it.
