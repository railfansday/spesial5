Coherent parity oscillations in coupled minimal Kitaev chains

Nature (2026) Cite this article

Majorana zero modes provide a route to fault-tolerant qubits by encoding information non-locally in fermion parity. Their noise sensitivity is expected to decrease exponentially with separation between Majorana zero modes, a suppression known as topological protection1. Kitaev chains in quantum-dot–superconductor arrays can host Majorana zero modes at their ends1,2, even in two-site chains3. These minimal-chain modes, known as poor man’s Majoranas, retain characteristic Majorana properties, including near-zero energy and equal electron–hole character, but lack topological protection3,4,5. A key challenge is moving from identifying these modes in electrical transport to coherent qubit control. Here we demonstrate coherent time-resolved rotations between parity states encoded by four poor man’s Majoranas in two coupled minimal Kitaev chains3,6,7,8. Total fermion parity conservation separates the system into global even- and odd-parity manifolds. We observe coherent parity oscillations in both, with frequencies matching within 5% at the poor man’s Majorana sweet spot, consistent with largely isolated Majoranas6. The oscillation frequency, rotation axis and coherence depend systematically on inter-chain coupling and quantum-dot detunings, in agreement with our model for short chains. Our results establish coherent control of a qubit encoded in the fermion parity of Majorana zero modes, realized using minimal Kitaev chains. They lay the groundwork for universal qubit operations, comprehensive coherence characterization and probing protection in longer chains.

For fast ramps (≲5 ns), parity mixing is observed between the chain and the dot. We attribute this to a diabatic excitation of the coupled system, which rapidly decoheres before the final measurement. In the main text, we use ramp times of 25 ns to avoid these excitations. For pulse amplitudes ≲11.5 mV, no parity mixing is observed, which we use to roughly identify when QDC passes through resonance. f, Schematic of the configuration for the measurements in c-e. g-j, Same as c-f, but for the right chain, with the QDs of the left chain off-resonance. Readout characterization using time traces of the reflected resonator signal \(\Delta {S}_{{\rm{M}}}^{{\rm{11}}}\), measured for three different amounts of detuning with the coupler QD kept fixed far from resonance. Thresholds used for single-shot state identification are obtained by fitting a Gaussian mixture model. The detuning configuration in (e-g) is used for qubit readout. a, Schematic representation of the readout configuration with both chains on resonance. b, Time trace of \(\Delta {S}_{{\rm{M}}}^{{\rm{11}}}\) and corresponding histogram for the readout configuration in a. Residual coupling between the chains results in inter-chain hopping that is faster than the readout time of 500 μs, preventing readout in a single shot. The two quantum capacitance signals corresponding to the \(| ee\rangle \) and \(| oo\rangle \) states are partially averaged which makes them difficult to distinguish from the \(| oe\rangle \) and \(| eo\rangle \) states. c, As in a, but with both chains detuned from resonance. d, Time trace of \(\Delta {S}_{{\rm{M}}}^{{\rm{11}}}\) and corresponding histogram for the readout configuration in c. The slight detuning suppresses inter-chain hopping, though it remains too fast for high-fidelity single-shot readout. Two distinctive regions can be observed: When the system is in the global even state, rapid jumps between \(| oo\rangle \) and \(| ee\rangle \) are visible. When the system is poisoned, it enters the global odd manifold. Transitions between \(| oe\rangle \) and \(| eo\rangle \) are not resolved because they produce similar readout signals. e, As in c but with both chains detuned further from resonance. f, Time trace of \(\Delta {S}_{{\rm{M}}}^{{\rm{11}}}\) and corresponding histogram for the readout configuration in e. The two computational states \(| ee\rangle \) and \(| oo\rangle \) correspond to the red and blue signals, and can now clearly be resolved. The green signal contains both leakage states of the odd global parity manifold (\(| eo\rangle \) and \(| oe\rangle \)). Due to the antisymmetric detuning where the ground state of one dot is full and the other is empty, the \(| oo\rangle \) state is energetically favored over the \(| ee\rangle \) state, resulting in a skewed population between the two. Transitions are mainly observed between the even and odd global manifolds. The signal-to-noise ratios between the leakage distribution and the \(| ee\rangle \) and \(| oo\rangle \) distributions are 2.88 ± 0.28 and 2.76 ± 0.09, respectively. Integrating the fitted Gaussian distributions across the assignment thresholds gives false-leakage probabilities of 0.45% for \(| ee\rangle \) and 0.25% for \(| oo\rangle \), showing that finite readout SNR accounts for only a small fraction of the observed leakage. g, Dwell time histogram of the three observed states, indicating the difference in lifetimes. The shortest measured state lifetime in this readout configuration is 8.3 ms, corresponding to a probability of approximately 5.8% for a state transition during the 500 μs integration window. The trace is acquired with the coupler QD kept far from resonance, showing that state switching on this timescale is already present in the readout configuration. These transitions include both residual inter-chain hopping and quasiparticle-poisoning events and can produce time-averaged signals that are assigned as leakage for pulsed measurements.

a, Map of p(oo∣oo) (left) and p(oe∣oe) (right) as a function of the left and right hybrid-gate voltages (VLH and VRH). The diagonal and antidiagonal features of enhanced parity mixing correspond to degeneracies between the qubit basis states. The points labeled c-k indicate the VLH,VRH values used in the remaining panels. b, Schematic representation of the four configurations measured at each point: left- and right-chain pulsed charge-stability diagrams in the global even and odd parity manifolds. c-k, Pulsed charge-stability diagrams measured for the left and right chains in the even (top) and odd (bottom) parity manifolds. Unlike conventional charge-stability diagrams referenced to the Fermi energy in a normal lead, here each chain provides the energy reference for the other. As such, the measured features reflect the difference between the excitation energies of the two individual chains. Crossings are therefore observed simultaneously for the left and right chains when the splittings of the two chains compensate each other, corresponding to the qubit degeneracy condition. Consequently, the appearance of a crossing in a single parity manifold does not imply that the corresponding chain is tuned to its sweet spot. Indeed, crossing-like features are observed in only one manifold in panels c, e, i and k. The orientation of the avoided crossings depends on the parity manifold: in the even manifold, the left and right chains exhibit the same orientation, whereas in the odd manifold the orientation is reversed, reflecting the sign difference ε12 ± ε34 between the two manifolds. Only near the sweet spot (panel g) are crossings observed for both chains in both parity manifolds simultaneously, providing a unique signature that both chains are individually tuned to their sweet spots.

The value of VC is indicated above each panel. The device configuration is the same as in Extended Data Fig. 8. a-h, The superconducting phase difference between the chains is set to φ ≈ 0. As VC is made more negative, the coupler QD is first brought closer to resonance and parity mixing along the degeneracy lines increases. Eventually, when the coupler QD is near or below resonance (around VC = − 9 mV), the final state is dictated by the ground state of the chain in the relevant sector of the charge-stability diagram irrespective of the initial state. We note that the chain appears to move from CAR-like at low pulse amplitudes (tR < ΔR, a) across a sweet spot (tR ≈ ΔR, b-e) to ECT-like at high pulse amplitudes (tR > ΔR, f-h), which we attribute to uncompensated cross-capacitance between VC and VRH. i-n, The superconducting phase difference between the chains is set to φ ≈ π. The main difference is that the visibility of QDR2 is suppressed, as predicted by theory (Supplementary Note 4).
