Emotional Mauricio Pochettino gets perfect sendoff in his US farewell... before boarding jet for new life as Manchester United manager

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

By ALEX RASKIN, US SPORTS NEWS EDITOR and CHRIS WHEELER, NORTHERN SPORTS WRITER

Published: 21:06 EST, 17 November 2026 | Updated: 00:56 EST, 18 November 2026

Mauricio Pochettino received a near-perfect sendoff as US men's national team head coach on Tuesday in Cincinnati, where Manchester United's next manager watched his now-former players beat Haiti before bidding an emotional farewell to his adopted country.

Christian Pulisic, Pochettino's brightest pupil over the last two seasons, rose to the occasion by scoring on a gravity-defying bicycle kick in the 25th minute and 18-year-old prospect Julian Hall followed in the 43rd with a goal of his own.

But for all of the excitement on the field – where Pochettino helped rebuild the American attack with a high-intensity style – all eyes were on the manager as he embraced his team one last time.

With the 2-0 win (8-0 aggregate), the USMNT now advances to the CONCACAF Nations League semifinals, but that's no longer Pochettino's concern. Instead, former American fullback and current under-23 manager Steve Cherundolo is expected to take over on an interim basis, while Pochettino turns his attention to an 18th-place United squad that has just one win in the English Premier League through 10 matches this season.

'When you finish the game, you start in your mind to think about different things,' Pochettino said after the win. 'Of course, it's tough because it's emotional. Because I really love these guys, and I love the players. I love the staff. I love the fans and everything.'

Following the win, Pochettino was scheduled to fly from northern Kentucky to Manchester aboard a private plane chartered by his new club. The quick escape offered the chance to avoid major airlines following Friday's chaotic nationwide shutdown, and perhaps a few hours' sleep for a 54-year-old starting his new job on Wednesday at Carrington Training Centre.

And Pochettino didn't plan on traveling alone.

Outgoing USMNT first-team coach Miguel D'Agostino, strength and conditioning coach Silvia Tuya and analyst Alec Scott were expected to join Pochettino aboard the flight to Manchester, where assistant Jesus Perez, goalkeeper coach Toni Jimenez as well as Pochettino's son and fitness coach Sebastiano have already begun preparing for Sunday's matchup with Liverpool at Anfield.

The quick turnaround means Pochettino will be able to work with the players as they begin reporting back from international duty. Furthermore, he is expected to speak to club media before conducting his pre-match press conference around lunchtime on Friday.

The Argentine national is replacing Michael Carrick at Old Trafford on a two-and-a-half year contract after United agreed to pay more than £10million ($13m) to buy him and his backroom staff out of their contracts with the US Soccer Federation.

Before boarding his flight, Pochettino not only waved to his adoring fans, but took a photo with staff and players on the pitch in Cincinnati.

'It's impossible not to cry, but that is because we care about the people and the people care about us,' he told reporters. 'When that happens, it's normal to show your emotions, no? Because we are human. We are no robots.'

Pochettino had been on something of a farewell tour in the US, where he embraced the local sports scene over his brief tenure. In between overseeing his final games with the USMNT, Pochettino was spotted at the Cincinnati Bengals' win over the visiting Pittsburgh Steelers on Sunday.

The manager, sporting his blue USSF coat, was captured standing on the sidelines with members of his staff ahead before kickoff, where he mingled with Bengals special teamers such as kicker Evan McPherson, long snapper William Wagner and punter Ryan Rehkow.

A night earlier, Pochettino guided the US to a 6-0 win over Haiti in Miami to give his team a safe cushion for his farewell on Tuesday in Cincinnati.

Pochettino broke down in tears as he announced his decision last week, while admitting he couldn't turn down the 'dream' job with Manchester United.

Flanked by US Soccer Federation bosses at the team's headquarters in Georgia, an emotional Pochettino wiped away tears as he thanked the players and staff.

'I haven't prepared a speech, I'm talking with my heart,' he said. 'It's difficult for me to talk now because I am a very emotional guy.

'I think it was one of the most difficult decisions of our career, but you have to move forward.

'Not too many opportunities can happen in your career with a side like Manchester United. That is difficult to turn down when it arrives.

'In some part it's a very sad decision, in another part we are professional and we are trying to make our dreams come true. We are excited for that step forward.'

United will pay the US Soccer Federation more than $13.2 million (£10m) in compensation for Pochettino and his backroom staff.

'I never said I wanted to go back in the Premier League,' Pochettino, a former Tottenham, Chelsea and Southampton boss, told reporters last week. 'All of this was so quick because we arrived here last Friday and on Sunday afternoon this type of situation started to appear.'

Pochettino put together a 23-12-2 record with the US, while 78 players earned caps for the US over his tenure. Remarkably, nine teenagers made their USMNT debuts for Pochettino, including Hall, who scored on Tuesday.

But the Americans failed to crack into the sport's upper echelon during Pochettino's brief tenure in the US. In his lone World Cup appearance with the team last summer, Pochettino guided the USMNT to the Round of 16, where the Americans fell to Belgium, 4-1.

That result was an improvement on 2018, when the US failed to qualify, but not on 2022, when then-coach Gregg Berhalter's similarly team fell short of the quarters with a 3-1 loss to the Netherlands.

Despite this, Pochettino's American players have remained largely supportive of his tenure, including Berhalter's son Sebastian, who said the outgoing coach helped him 'develop as a player and as a person.'

Prior to Tuesday's win, Pochettino thanked American fans for the support he received over his two years with the national team.

'Thank you so much from the bottom of my heart for all the fans, for all the people that was supporting us in the last two years,' Pochettino said in a video posted on social media. 'Special, great memories of the World Cup and everything that we lived together. Always in my heart, always one of you. All the best and I wish everything good for the future.'

Potential candidates to replace Pochettino in the US include former Barcelona, Bayern Munich and Manchester City manager Pep Guardiola, ex-England manager Gareth Southgate and some lesser-known candidates like Cherundolo, who now guides the United States U-23 team.

'For us to win, we need to be great at a lot of things, and we need someone who cares about youth development, cares about our youth national team programs, our relationships with our clubs and the success of the federation more broadly,' US Soccer CEO JT Batson told TNT Sports on Tuesday. 'We know that is a key part of our success, and it's also a key part of how we've been able to attract great people because they get to do big things.'
