Nets can’t finish comeback vs LeBron-less Sixers, lose 135-123

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

I went to watch my New York Giants host the Washington Commanders at MetLife this past Thursday. They gave up a game-winning score with less than a minute to go. Last night, as a proud Syracuse alum, I went to watch the Orange face St. John's at Madison Square Garden. They ended up losing by 17 after leading by three at half.

Desperately hoping to see a winning performance, I knew the Barclays Center was the place for me to go on Monday evening!

Yes, I'm being sarcastic … a bit cynical, too. As expected, the Nets lost tonight to the Philadelphia 76ers. However, they did battle, so if you haven't soured on morale victories yet, read on.

With Michael Porter Jr. returning from a one-game absence due to personal reasons, the Nets first five in game No. 13 featured MPJ alongside Nolan Traoré, Egor Dëmin, Julius Randle, and Day'Ron Sharpe. The Sixers began with Jaylen Brown, Tyrese Maxey, VJ Edgecombe, Joel Embiid, and Dean Wade. LeBron James sat this one out to rest his back on the back-end of a back-to-back.

Like Joseph Quinn and Chris Evans, Michael Porter Jr. and Julius Randle had the hot hand at different times tonight, but both did simultaneously in the early going. The pair of vets combined for 13 of Brooklyn's first 19 points to keep the Nets in it as Joel Embiid mismatched his way to 13 opening quarter points. They were also helped by Keon Ellis, who returned to the rotation after playing just seven minutes of garbage time vs Memphis on Saturday. Eager to stay out of the dump, he went 2-of-2 from deep in the period with two blocks and a steal.

But even as Ellis helped the Nets post 46/43 splits in the first, they still trailed 33-26 after it. At the other end, whatever the Sixers weren't getting on their first try, they were on their second or third. Philly outscored the Nets in second chance points during the opening frame 10-3. Jaylen Brown, still haunting us just in a new rival's uniform, pulled down three offensive boards to help out on that front. He also finished the game with 31 points, 13 total rebounds, five assists, a steal, and a block.

Nolan Traoré chose to take the blame for the weak defensive start postgame.

"Don't be passive in the first minutes of the game," he said. "I've got to be the one, the one pushing, the one putting pressure on them, and not the inverse. So yeah, that's it."

The Nets did improve on the offensive glass in the second, not because they boxed out better or went with a bigger lineup, but because their opportunities to defend their own glass vanished. The 76ers began the second shooting 9-of-11 from the field, eventually going 14-of-21 by the end of the period and 4-of-7 from deep.

Brooklyn, who shot a less impressive 12-of-26 from the field and 0-of-6 from deep, looked out of position both on defense and just as Philly's opponent in a competitive setting. Whenever the Sixers weren't swinging the ball around to stretch Brooklyn out and get favorable looks, they were hitting off isolation takes that just put a sweltering and oppressive spotlight on Brooklyn's lack of comparable talent.

In fairness, these are the "super" Sixers, even without King James. Remember when the Nets had a team like that? That was fun.

Anyway, Philly led 78-57 at the break. Randle led the team by a wide margin at that point, pitching in 18 points on 7-11 shooting. With eight points in the second, Traoré worked his way to 10 points and two assists on 3-6 shooting. However, the other young guards struggled in what was arguably their worst collective game so far. Mikel Brown Jr. and Egor Dëmin combined for zero points on 0-6 shooting and two turnovers at half. Brown only made one basket the whole game, going 1-7 from the field, but did drop five assists with only one turnover.

Dëmin's first points of the night didn't come until the 6:25 point of the game, and although tardy, their timing was appreciated. After Randle kept Brooklyn hanging around with another eight points in the opening half of the third, Porter Jr. and Day'Ron Sharpe teamed up for a block on Embiid that sprung a transition opportunity, leading to a Dëmin three that cut the deficit to 10.

And after that, Michael Porter Jr. picked up the baton. He got 12 of his 25 points for the game in the third to bring Brooklyn even closer. A large percentage of his makes came via the dribble-handoff sequences Brooklyn often runs for him, but the biggest was a straight up heat check, which made it a six point game with about a minute to go…

After those six blanks in the second, the Nets had gone 5-7 from three in the third. They also bounced back on the glass to win by a 13-6 margin in the period.

"I think we just played harder," Traoré said. "We grabbed more rebounds, even if it wasn't enough, but it was just like a team effort to get stops together and then run."

Traoré enjoyed another solid night, contributing with a career-high 23 points alongside eight assists, two steals and just two turnovers while shooting 7-12 from the field and 2-4 from deep. Randle agreed with his assessment that the Nets played harder in the second half.

"Just punch first," he said. "Like in the second half, we punched first, and set the tone. I think we have to start every game like that punch first approach."

Randle was right about that and just about everything tonight. He had his best game as a Net, finishing with 33 points, six rebounds, an assists, two steals, and a block while shooting 13-20 from the field and 2-4 from deep.

The Sixers went two for one down the stretch of the third, with the latter offensive possession generating a Jabari Walker three at the buzzer to put them back up 10 entering the fourth. But the Nets were far from discouraged. Danny Wolf got the scoring started there with a drive-and-kick three from Noah Clowney. Julius Randle added on in transition shortly after that got it down to five with 9:54 to go.

But down the stretch, Philly started spamming the Joel Embiid x Tyrese Maxey pick-and-roll, which was as calming for the Sixers as it was effective in producing points. Bit by bit, Philly inched further ahead to lead 122-111 with 2:56 remaining.

But while quiet for most of the game, Egor Dëmin did have something to say before Philly could ice things. After the Sixers had slowly but surely rebuilt a double digit lead, Dëmin quickly and impulsively brought it back to two after sneaking inside for a finish at the rim before trotting down on the next possession and sinking a pull-up triple with 1:14 to play.

It felt a bit like the Orlando game last year, only this time, the visiting team's All-Star hit the big three to essentially end it a few seconds earlier.

The next time down the floor, Embiid splashed a corner three, which pushed the lead back up to five with 52 seconds remaining. In a monster game, the Philly big man finished with 37 points, five rebounds, an assists, a steal, and two blocks while shooting 13-22 from the field. Dëmin finished tonight with nine points, four assists, and three rebounds while shooting 3-10 from the field and committing five turnovers. He and Brown Jr. were still a combined of 12 points on 4-of-17 in 44 minutes.

"I'm just trying to do the best for the team, whatever's in front of me," Dëmin said. "I don't know. I'm just trying to enjoy the game, go out there and hoop with my friends, and some days are like that, some are different, and hopefully I can get more of the good days then bad days, so kind of trying to approach it with this kind of attitutde."

To stave off the comeback, Philly only need to take care of business at the line. Edgecombe and Maxey handled all the paperwork, going 7-8 from the line as the clock ran out.

It wasn't just finisher move from Philly, but another lesson for the Nets in what they need to patch up. With Brooklyn's offensive renaissance in the second half, they ended up shooting better from deep for the game by nearly five percentage points. They shot worse from the field by just over one. However, they left 12 points at the line, shooting 22-34 for the game.

"I don't want to go to the players and go, 'yeah, make free throws,' and then you go there and you're like nervous," Fernández said. "You know, obviously, I don't want to shoot 64%. If we shoot better, we probably have a bigger chance, but I know our guys are good shooters, and they're going to make free throws. We're going to concentrate and make sure we believe that all of them are gonna go in."

Final: Philadelphia 76ers 135, Brooklyn Nets 123

Julius Randle collided with Jaylen Brown in the fourth quarter took a shot to the face. He stayed in the game, but didn't mince words about it postgame. Nevertheless, we highly doubt he'll miss any time.

"It feels terrible, to be honest," Randle said. "But I'm fine. It just went to track on me, he turned, and didn't see me. Just kind of collided, but it's fine."

Julius Randle tied his season high with 33 points. It was his second 30-point game this season.

Day'Ron Sharpe recorded five assists in his sixth straight game, extending the longest such streak in Nets history.

Nolan Traore tied his career high with 23 points. It was also his high for the season thus far.

The Nets play the Chicago Bulls Wednesday night at Barclays Center, starting at 7:30 p.m. EST. It will be Nic Claxton's first return to Brooklyn after the three team June trade that sent him and a second rounder (Isaiah Evans) to Chi-Town and Julius Randle and a first (Joshua Jefferson.)
