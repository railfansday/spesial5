Magnite vs. Pinterest: Which Media Stock Is a Better Buy in 2026?

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

One operates the ad-tech backbone for streaming; the other has built a massive online platform. Their financial profiles and risk exposures also tell very different stories.

As digital advertising shifts toward streaming and social commerce, choosing between Magnite (MGNI -0.47%) and Pinterest (PINS -4.19%) requires a close look at where marketing dollars are moving today.

Magnite serves as the technical backbone for publishers looking to sell ad space, while Pinterest provides a visual search environment where users actively look for inspiration and products. Comparing these two companies helps investors understand the different ways to play the ongoing evolution of the digital ad market.

Magnite provides a sell-side advertising platform for publishers and media owners in the media stocks landscape. As the largest independent player in this space, it helps content creators monetize their inventory across connected television, mobile, and desktop. The company maintains critical partnerships with providers like LG Ad Solutions and Telly, allowing programmatic buyers, including The Trade Desk, to access premium video inventory.

In FY 2025, Magnite reported revenue of nearly $714 million, representing a growth rate of approximately 6.9% compared to the previous year. The company achieved a net income of roughly $144.6 million during this period. This is an increase from the net income of approximately $22.8 million reported in FY 2024, reflecting an expansion in its net margin to roughly 20.3%.

Looking further into the financials, Magnite has total cash of $392.8 million and total debt of $411.9 million. Its operating cash flow for the 12-month trailing period is nearly $300 million.

Pinterest operates a visual discovery and shopping platform that now serves approximately 661 million monthly active users. Unlike traditional social media, the platform focuses on curation, making it highly attractive to advertisers in the retail and consumer packaged goods sectors. By integrating shopping features directly into its pins, the company has transformed from a simple image board into a performance-driven marketing channel.

In FY 2025, revenue reached approximately $4.2 billion, which was an increase of nearly 15.8% over the previous year. The company reported a net income of roughly $416.9 million for the year. This resulted in a net margin of approximately 9.9%, which followed a net margin of roughly 51% in FY 2024 when the company benefited from significant tax adjustments and valuation changes.

For a quick glimpse at the financials, Pinterest has $1.4 billion in total cash and $1.2 billion in total debt. Its operating cash flow for the 12-month trailing period is $1.3 billion.

Pinterest faces significant competition for user attention and advertising budgets from Amazon, Meta Platforms, Alphabet, and smaller rivals like Snap (SNAP -2.55%) and Reddit (RDDT +1.38%). The company is currently involved in a securities class action lawsuit related to previous advertising disclosures. Furthermore, Pinterest remains vulnerable to changes in mobile operating systems that limit tracking capabilities, which can make it harder for advertisers to measure the effectiveness of their campaigns.

Pinterest appears to be more of a value, according to traditional metrics.

Pinterest has built up an impressive monthly user base. And there is something to be said for building your own network and controlling that user base. But Pinterest is caught between being a social media site and an advertiser, whereas Magnite's value is more straightforward. Also, between the two, Magnite is more of a growth stock, whereas Pinterest is seeking more stability, with its stock price down 30% over the last 12 months. In comparison, the Magnite stock price is up 81.3% over the same period.

Analysts also have a more favorable outlook on Magnite: of the 18 analysts who rate Magnite, 89% rate it a buy, with 11% rating it a hold. In comparison, the view is more split on Pinterest. Of the 42 analysts who rate PINS, 50% rate it a buy and 50% a hold.

As a growth stock, Magnite could be hit particularly hard if there were a slowdown in the economy. And heading into 2027, we're seeing concerns about an artificial intelligence bubble and mixed signals about the economy's strength. Still, between the two, I would rather own Magnite as a long-term position because of its sales growth opportunities.
