A Bear Market Could Be Coming. Here's My Exact Investing Strategy to Prepare for It.

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Much depends on your age and your financial situation.

By Selena Maranjian – Nov 19, 2026 at 8:27AM EST

Could a bear market be around the corner, happening this year or next? Absolutely -- because stock market crashes and corrections, and, often, ensuing recessions, simply do happen every few years. Arguably, Wall Street is due for one. Check out the recent returns of the S&P 500 (^GSPC +0.76%) index of America's 500 largest companies:

With six of the past seven full years featuring double-digit gains, a pullback in the near future would not be surprising. Other indicators, such as the Buffett indicator and the cyclically adjusted price-to-earnings (CAPE) ratio, also favor a bear market of some sort.

The first way to prepare for a bear market is simply to expect it -- and to understand that it's extremely likely to end and be followed, eventually, by a market recovery. Check out some data from The Hartford Funds:

All that said, there are different ways for different people to prepare for a bear market.

For older folks who may be living on a fixed income or will soon be getting by without a regular salary, it's probably best to be cautious. It's true that many bear markets end by the time your car needs its next oil change, but some can last several years. So to be on the safe side, don't keep any money that you expect to need within the next five years (or 10, to be more conservative) in the stock market. You don't want to have to sell to generate living expenses when your portfolio is temporarily smaller.

You might put your short-term money in less volatile places, such as certificates of deposit (CDs), money market accounts, bonds, or high-yield savings accounts. Then you can draw on those investments as needed for living expenses.

If you're still young -- or youngish -- you can view market downturns more opportunistically. Like older folks, don't keep any short-term money in the stock market. You don't want to have to sell at a suboptimal time to generate, say, a down payment for a home or college tuition for your kid.

As you anticipate a coming bear market, you might also try to stockpile some dollars -- as you look forward to a market pullback. Yes, it will hurt to see a portfolio worth, say, $400,000 become one worth, say, $300,000 or even $250,000 (for a while), but when most or all of your holdings are suddenly at depressed prices, the stocks of lots of great companies will be too. It's a terrific time to go shopping!

Warren Buffett explained it all well in his 1997 letter to shareholders:

If you expect to be a net saver during the next five years, should you hope for a higher or lower stock market during that period? Many investors get this one wrong. Even though they are going to be net buyers of stocks for many years to come, they are elated when stock prices rise and depressed when they fall. [...] This reaction makes no sense. Only those who will be sellers of equities in the near future should be happy at seeing stocks rise. Prospective purchasers should much prefer sinking prices.

Another way to prepare for a bear market -- whatever your age -- is to have a good emergency fund. It should be able to support you for at least three to six months, even if you lose your job. Job losses can happen more often in bear markets and recessions, after all.

Consider holding some defensive stocks in your portfolio to balance any growth stocks. Defensive companies are ones whose offerings are needed regardless of the economic condition. Think shampoos, utilities, food, and so on. Know that when the market pulls back, high-flying growth stocks often fall harder.

So don't worry too much about the possibility of a stock market crash. Instead, just prepare for one.
