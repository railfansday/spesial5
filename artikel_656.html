Ella Langley Breaks a CMA Awards Record by Winning Six in One Night, Including Entertainer of the Year: The Complete List of Winners

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Ella Langley won nearly every award she was up for, including the top prize, as well as female vocalist and album, single and song of the year. Stephen Wilson Jr. was a surprise to many in being named best new artist. Taylor Swift sang her nominated song and presented a lifetime achievement award to Brooks & Dunn.

Senior Music Writer and Chief Music Critic

In a near-sweep that surprised no one, Ella Langley won six trophies at the CMA Awards, including the top prize, entertainer of the year. That’s an all-time record for the most awards ever won in a year by one person.

The “Choosin’ Texas” singer so swept the field that the show’s host, Lainey Wilson, came out with a broom during her final acceptance speech and pretended to be cleaning up the stage.

“I watched these awards since i was a little kid, and the awards i cared most about were song of the year and entertainer of the year,” Langley said at the close. Ultimately, after five turns at the podium (the sixth award was announced pre-telecast), she said, “I’ve run out of things to say. I’m at a loss for words.” But, she finally added, “I could quit tomorrow and have the best year of my life.”

Langley came in to the show with a leading nine nominations. It was only possible for her to win seven, since she was nominated against herself in two categories (single and song of the year, where “Choosin’ Texas” had “Be Her” as competition). She wound up winning almost everything she could have, with the lone exception of a nomination she shared for musical event of the year. Her sweep of the other six possible categories included wins for best female vocalist; album of the year for “Dandelion”; and, for her record-breaking “Choosin’ Texas,” a triple crown of single of the year, song of the year and music video of the year. Langley was joined on stage for all her wins by fellow country superstar Miranda Lambert, who also drew a big haul of honors, as an executive producer of the “Dandelion” album and co-writer and co-producer of “Choosin’ Texas.” Their time together on stage was stretched even further as they did a fiery duet of “Snakeskin Boots,” from Lambert’s new album, “Crisco.” That was one of two all-female-powered duets Lambert did during the evening; the other found her joining Kacey Musgraves on a spicy “Horses and Divorces,” an undisguised story about those two singers making up after a long estrangement. Langley got in a solo turn apart from the Lambert duet, performing “Loving Life Again,” her latest song to become a country airplay No. 1.

In fact, Langley’s collaborators on her album and single — including Lambert, frequent songwriting partner Joybeth Taylor and producer Ben West — were the only other honorees to win more than one CMA Award this year.

Among the early wins was an upset, but not a complete shock to industry insiders — a win for Stephen Wilson Jr. as new artist of the year. Wilson thanked his wife, Sixpence None the Richer singer Leigh Nash, among others. Noting just how unusual his career course had been, Wilson said, “I left my job as a scientist. I’d never sung a song in my life for anybody. A lot can happen in 12 years.”

Among repeat CMAs winners, Chris Stapleton won male vocalist of the year, the Red Clay Strays won as vocal group of the year for the second straight year, and Brooks & Dunn won vocal duo.

Taylor Swift did not need to win to be more responsible for additional tune-in beyond the norm than any other artist who appeared on the telecast. She lost her lone nomination, for “I Knew It, I Knew You,” as single of the year, to “Choosin’ Texas,” in what was surely a long-foreseen inevitability even to Swift. That hardly kept her from being an enthusiastic participant, singing the “Toy Story 5” song in a mash-up with “Our Song,” uniting her first country radio No. 1, of 20 years back, with her most recent. Keith Urban joined her on ganjo for the performance, which began with a choreographed backstage verse in which she interacted with stars like Lainey Wilson, Brad Paisley, Little Big Town, Sugarland and veteran Bill Anderson. Later, she provided a spirited introduction to her friends Brooks & Dunn as they received a lifetime achievement award.

Performers besides Swift included both Wilsons (Lainey and Stephen), Cody Johnson, Miranda Lambert, Tim McGraw, Kacey Musgraves, the Red Clay Strays, Zach Top, Tucker Wetmore, Luke Combs, Megan Moroney, Mumford & Sons, Chris Stapleton and Stephen Wilson Jr. Additionally, Brothers Osborne, Luke Bryan, Halestorm, Hardy and Marcus King took part in a tribute to Brooks & Dunn. A salute to Dolly Parton kicked off the program, with Vince Gill and Wilson dueting on “I Will Always Love You.”.

Wilson, who was awarded entertainer of the year last year for a second time, hosted for the third year in a row. She surprised the audience by having former co-host Brad Paisley out to join her for the monologue, doing a medley of musical parodies, as Paisley used to do with Carrie Underwood when they shepherded the show. Besides the award for “Choosin’ Texas” for best video, an additional award was announced prior to broadcast: musical event of the year for “McArthur,” by Hardy, Eric Church, Tim McGraw and Morgan Wallen. The trophy for musician of the year was given during an early commercial break and went to guitarist Charlie Worsham, who later got an on-air recognition, as is customary at the show.
