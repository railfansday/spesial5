Dragon Pose Is the Essential Recovery Stretch For Tight Hips and Back

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

The yin yoga pose we love to hate, Dragon Pose, is a smart recovery tool for cyclists to improve hip extension. Because cycling involves repetitive hip flexion at the top of the pedal stroke, the primary hip flexor muscle (psoas major) can tighten and become compressed over time, making it harder to access a powerful position off the bike.

The low torso angle on a tri-specific bike provides aerodynamic benefits, but also requires greater hip flexion. In the aero position, the hip flexors, which not only flex the hip but also stabilize the pelvis and lower back, repeatedly contract without fully extending, shortening the muscles and potentially leading to low back pain and stiffness.

Dragon Pose is useful for triathletes who spend a lot of time folded over the handlebars during indoor training through the winter. It can also help you during the warmer months by restoring hip extension and preparing you for the longer stride that running off the bike demands. On the run, you repeatedly load one leg as the hip extends behind the body. Dragon Pose simulates that position, so you step off the bike feeling ready to hit your running stride.

Add Dragon Pose to your recovery routine. Practice this pose after a training ride or race to stretch the hip flexors and improve hip mobility.

Keep in mind that multiple bike fit factors affect hip angle and comfort. This guide provides five elements of a proper bike fit for smoother pedaling and to keep your position within your comfortable hip flexion range.

Improving hip extension can help you feel less compressed on the bike, move more easily through transitions, and ease low-back discomfort.
