How to Master the Run/Walk Method: Train for a 5K &amp; 10K

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Learn how to tackle the approachable distances with the power of walk breaks.

You’ve completed your Magic Mile time trials, dialed in your pacing, and tracked your progress with your run/walk workouts. Now, it’s time to hit the starting line!

The 5K and 10K make for perfect race distances for beginner and veteran run/walkers alike. Whether you’re shooting to cross your first finish line or earn that big PR, you can train for and race toward your goals with the run/walk method powering every step.

The Runner’s World guide, How to Master the Run/Walk Method, helps you get to the finish line and cover two of the most popular and approachable distances. Pair our chapter with one of our dedicated training plans to prepare for your first or fastest 5K or 10K yet.

For beginners, a 5K or 10K serves as a great introduction to using the run/walk training strategy for race day. It’s an opportunity to get accustomed to pacing, structure your intervals, and follow a dedicated run/walk training plan. Once you’ve nailed your 5K/10K training block, you may find it easier to bump up to longer distances and chase after new race goals.

But even if you’re not a racing newcomer, a shorter, approachable distance can be an ideal place to start with run/walk. You might even race faster with the run/walk method than you did previously when running the entire distance.

The shorter the distance, the shorter the walk break will likely be, especially if you’re shooting for a PR. Use your data from your Magic Mile test to determine your goal pace for race day.
