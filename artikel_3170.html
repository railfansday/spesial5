Seanergy Maritime (SHIP) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Record nine-month profits meet a fleet expansion that keeps growing.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

Seanergy Maritime Holdings Corp. (SHIP +1.04%) reported peak profitability for the first nine months of the year and announced an expansion of its fleet renewal program. Management reported the acquisition of four additional vessels since the second quarter, bringing the total investment program to 12 ships. The company indicated that funding for these commitments is supported by substantial cash reserves and staggered payment schedules through 2029. Fleet performance in the third quarter was driven by a significant increase in time-charter equivalent rates, with high coverage levels already secured for the fourth quarter. The company continued its multiyear streak of dividend payments while maintaining a net loan-to-value ratio of 28%.

Stamatios Tsantanis: Thank you, operator, and welcome, everyone. Seanergy delivered another highly profitable quarter, and our nine-month net income reached a record $61.9 million. In the third quarter, our fleet earned approximately $34,500 on a time-charter equivalent basis, up 46% year-over-year, and adjusted EBITDA was $40.8 million. For the first nine months, adjusted EBITDA more than doubled to $110.5 million. Adjusted earnings per share rose to $3.21, up from $0.60 last year. These results show the earnings power of our pure-play Capesize platform. They also allow us to increase our shareholder returns and renew our fleet at the same time from a position of strength.

Our Board of Directors has raised the quarterly cash dividend by 14% to $0.40 per share, which is double the first quarter level. This is our 20th consecutive quarterly dividend, 5 years without interruption through good and bad market conditions. Cumulative cash dividends now reach about $3.59 per share, and including buybacks, we have returned approximately $117 million to our shareholders. Now to fleet investments, which have been our main focus since our last call.

Since our second-quarter results, we have added four more high-quality Capesize and Newcastlemax vessels for a total of about $264 million: a modern 2019-built Japanese Capesize, the Midnight ship, joining our fleet in January 2027; a scrubber-fitted Newcastlemax newbuilding at Hengli Heavy Industry for delivery in the first quarter of 2029; a scrubber-fitted Capesize newbuilding at a first-class Japanese shipyard for delivery in the second half of 2029; and a 2014-built Japanese Newcastlemax for delivery in the second half of 2029. Our fleet investment program now stands at 12 Capesize and Newcastlemax vessels consisting of 9 newbuildings and 3 modern Japanese-built secondhand vessels.

That is about $855 million of investment and 2.3 million deadweight tons of new capacity, growing our fleet's current capacity by more than 50% over the next few years. We expect the contribution of the new acquisitions to our NAV, EBITDA, and cash flow to be very, very significant. Stavros, further down, will take you through the funding of this CAPEX. Our first Capesize newbuilding at Hengli is now expected in January 2027, in a couple of months from now, about 4 months ahead of schedule. Together with the Midnight ship, that means two modern Capesizes will join our fleet at the start of next year, adding to our earnings from early '27.

At the same time, we continue to sell older vessels at the right time. We have agreed to sell the 2009-built Friendship, our oldest vessel, to United Maritime for $33 million with delivery in January of 2027. The deal was negotiated and approved by a special committee of independent directors. Delivery is expected in the first quarter of 2027, as I just said, generating approximately $20 million of net liquidity and a book gain of about $12 million, and Seanergy will continue to manage the vessel while at United Maritime.

Regarding the employment of the new ships, four out of the six vessels joining our fleet by the end of '28, we have secured or we are close to securing multi-year charters with first-class charterers. The structure is the same each time: a floor rate above the vessels' cash break-even from day one plus profit sharing above a certain set level. That means that downside protection from delivery is secured and upside when the market is strong will also benefit our shareholders. On the commercial side, our index-linked charters give us direct exposure to a strong market, and our conversion options let us lock in rates when the market makes sense.

For the fourth quarter, we have fixed about 86% of our operating days at close to $33,000 per day on a time-charter equivalent basis. Based on the current FFA curve, we expect fourth-quarter time-charter equivalent at about $33,300 per day. That puts '26 on track to be a record profitable year for Seanergy. We have also started to lock in good rates for '27. We have converted four vessels so far to fixed rates, and that excludes the newbuildings, at an average of about $35,000 a day, while the rest of the fleet keeps its index-linked exposure. We might also consider some additional fixtures in the near future.

Finally, our existing charters continue to be renewed with the same charterers, a clear sign of demand for our well-maintained and fuel-efficient vessels. With that, I will now pass the call to Stavros for the financial review, and I will come back to this call to discuss the market fundamentals.

Net loan-to-value ratio, including the outstanding bond principal and after deducting cash reserves as at the end of the quarter, stood at 28%, leaving us with financial flexibility as we continue to deploy capital.

At quarter end, we had invested $109 million in our fleet renewal program, and to date, this figure has increased to approximately $127 million or 15% of the program's CAPEX. We have already paid approximately $127 million towards the program, primarily from own resources. We have committed bilateral financing arrangements for five vessels.

We have the proceeds of our EUR 100 million unsecured bond, and we entered the quarter with approximately $168 million of liquidity. These sources are complemented by operating cash flow and proceeds from selective disposals as the renewal program progresses. Importantly, we believe this remaining amount can be financed at moderate leverage levels, allowing us to complete our fleet renewal program while preserving balance sheet strength and financial flexibility. As Stamatios mentioned earlier, the multi-year employment secured or being finalized for the first wave of newbuilding deliveries also strengthens the financial profile of the program.

The contractual floor rates are expected to cover the vessels' cash break-evens from commencement, providing visibility around operating cash generation and debt service, while the market-linked components preserve upside. From a financing perspective, this materially reduces the cash flow risk associated with the initial delivery phase. So the key financial message is pretty straightforward. We enter the next phase of the investment program with substantial liquidity, strong operating cash generation, diversified funding sources, and a staggered payment profile. Based on our current assumptions, we expect to execute the program while maintaining prudent leverage and continuing to preserve capacity for shareholder distributions. I will now turn the call back to Stamatios for a discussion of the Capesize market outlook, and broader industry fundamentals. Stamatios, please go ahead.

Stamatios Tsantanis: Thank you, Stavros. '26 has been a very strong year for the Capesize market so far, and it supports our pure-play focus. Year to date, the Baltic Capesize Index, the BCI, for 180,000-deadweight vessels has averaged at about $33,500 a day. The third quarter averaged about $41,400 a day, and the index peaked at about $50,000 a day in early September, its highest level since '21. Ton-mile demand keeps growing and accelerating from 3.7% in '24 to 5.5% in '26. But the main reason for the strong market is the low vessel supply. For three years in a row, the effective fleet growth has been below the ton-mile demand growth, and this year it is below 1.6%.

If we add more dry-docking days, congestion, and other inefficiencies, effective fleet growth this year is actually below 1%. On the supply side, the Capesize order book stands at about 400 vessels. And that includes, of course, options, Newcastlemaxes, and bigger ships altogether, against a fleet of about 2,000 vessels altogether. At the same time, about 190 vessels are already 20 years or older, and another 830 ships, about 40% of today's fleet, will reach 20 years of age in the coming years. In addition, we expect a large part of the fleet to struggle to meet efficiency, environmental, and charter requirements in the near term.

About 99 newbuildings, again, Capesize, Newcastlemax, and all that, are due in '27, or more than 4% nominal fleet growth. But only 28 of them are due in the first half of the year, which is about the same as the first half of '26. So we see a positive supply picture for the next several years. The market needs only modest demand growth to stay very strong. On the demand side, growing long-haul iron ore and bauxite exports from the Atlantic have been a key driver for Capesizes. At the same time, lower Indonesian coal exports, mostly carried on smaller ships, have moved more coal cargoes into Capesizes.

Looking ahead, the Simandou iron ore project in West Africa is ramping up faster than expected, which supports Capesize demand for years to come. In China, domestic iron ore output is down about 5% year-over-year. Despite a small uptick in October, the trend is clear: less local iron ore and more high-quality imports from Brazil, West Africa, and Australia. We expect this to continue as long as ample seaborne supply keeps iron ore prices below what most Chinese mines need to make money. Bauxite exports from West Africa rose about 21% from an already increased previous year, and bauxite was the biggest Capesize demand driver for the first and third quarters.

With the rainy season ending, we expect strong volumes again in the fourth quarter. Growth may slow from the very fast pace of the past three years, but China's alumina refineries keep using more imported bauxite. So these long-haul cargoes remain a very solid support. And because the West Africa rainy season runs opposite to Brazil, first-quarter exports are now stronger than in the past. Over the longer term, global demand for aluminum and steel keeps growing, driven by exports of finished goods, logistics networks, next-generation telecoms, data centers, and electric vehicles, and of course, increased energy needs.

The Capesize market may see some volatility in '27, especially given the uncertainty around Chinese commodity demand, but the structural drivers of ton-mile growth remain in place, and we remain very positive on the long-term outlook. We believe our long-term charters of the newbuildings, our index-linked exposure, and our proven hedging program set us very well ahead for the years to come. To conclude, '26 so far has shown the earnings power and cash generation of Seanergy's fully focused platform. We expect to enter '27 with strong liquidity, our first new vessels about to deliver, more earnings visibility, and a proven commitment to shareholder returns.

Our focus now is simple: deliver the program we have built and turn it into long-term value per share. With that, I will now turn the call over to the operator for any questions you may have. Operator, please take the call. Thank you.
