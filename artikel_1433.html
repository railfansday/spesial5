Forget Spain: James Trafford made the greatest save of his life at Port Vale

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

James Trafford produced one of the greatest saves from an England goalkeeper in living memory – but it wasn’t his best ever…

The Leeds United stopper preserved a huge win for the Three Lions with a 92nd-minute double save to deny Fermin Lopez then, most impressively, Aymeric Laporte.

It capped a fine performance and the 24-year-old further endeared himself to the nation in his post-match interview with ITV.

“Which one?” he replied to Gabriel Clarke when asked about his incredible save.

“Yeah obviously, I can’t actually remember to be fair,” Trafford said. “I think Yamal cut in short… it happened so quickly, and that’s what we train everyday, every week for, the split-second decisions. I’m just happy to help the team.”

When asked him if this was his best save, Trafford highlighted another favourite in less salubrious surroundings.

“I made an incredible one away at Port Vale, when I was at Bolton. That will forever be my best save!”

When you look back at that stop, it is hard to argue with Trafford’s take.

It came three games into his first full season as a senior no.1, out on loan at Bolton from Manchester City in August 2022 after the then teenager had spent the second half of 2021/22 with the Trotters.

At Vale Park, Bolton were on the ropes, down to 10 men since the first half as the hosts peppered Trafford’s goal with 29 attempts.

They thought they had finally broken the young keeper’s resistance late on when Bolton switched off to allow a corner to be played short. When the cross came, it was met inside the six-yard box by Ellis Harrison, who directed his header back in the direction Trafford had travelled from.

This would have been a brilliant save even without accounting for the double set.

Trafford first planted his feet expecting Vale’s Brad Walker to make the first contact but the ball was too high for the Vale substitute, instead reaching Harrison at the far stick.

Shuffling, setting, stepping again and setting once more, all within split seconds, was technically high level. Shifting his weight back to his left, pushing off the outside foot while sweeping the inside leg at such speed to get down to shovel Harrison’s header around the post was Champions League goalkeeping in League One.

Trafford fought the urge to celebrate – sadly, there was no Bolton equivalent of Ezri Konsa to really enjoy the moment – but Harrison’s disbelief illustrates the significance of the save.

The striker had set off to celebrate before reality dawned, while other Vale players slumped to the ground, realising the inevitability of Trafford’s clean sheet.
