The amount of time it takes to develop games has been growing steadily longer for decades, and Atlus' Persona series is no exception to this rule. While Persona 4 Golden and Persona 5 were separated by four years (long considered standard for game development) it's been seven years since Persona 5 Royal and Persona 6 still doesn’t have a release date—though we did get Metaphor: ReFantazio in between.

Nonetheless, it's an issue that P-Studio is very much aware of, and it was recently addressed by general producer Kazuhisa Wada. In an interview with Sega Samania (via Eurogamer) Wada stressed that the wait for whatever comes after Persona 6 will be shorter.

"Persona 6 is not the end," Wada explained. "We have been working to make sure that people won't have to wait as long for the next game as they have previously. In order for Persona to grow as an IP, we consider how to live up to people's expectations in terms of creativity and development every day."

Now, we're probably not looking at a dramatic reduction in development time. Wada emphasised that "large-scale RPGs like the Personal series can take five years or more to develop," adding that. "There can be a long time between numbered entries." Yet even if the average timeline was five years, that would be a considerable improvement.

Wada doesn't explain how Atlus plans to speed up development of its games. But there's always one question when developers begin talking about making games quicker—does it involve generative AI? Wada expressed interest in the tech several years ago, stating that it might be useful for "early concept and idea formation" and that the Atlus art team had been "talking about wanting to implement AI." That said, there's been little, if any talk about AI from Atlus since, and there's no evidence at present that Persona 6 uses the tech.

In fact, Atlus hasn't said much about Persona 6 at all since the game was announced earlier this year, though there are rumours that we'll find out more at The Game Awards in a few weeks' time. As for Wada, while he didn't comment on Persona 6 in detail, he did say that Atlus is "creating something better than anything we've made before, and that is hugely satisfying."
