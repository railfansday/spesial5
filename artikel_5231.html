US Could ‘100%’ Lose Crypto Race To China, Digital Chamber CEO Warns

The crypto industry’s run of friendly treatment in Washington looks set to end. Cody Carbone, chief executive of The Digital Chamber, warned that a Democrat-controlled Congress will be “very hostile” to digital assets. Broad market structure legislation such as the CLARITY Act is now unlikely to pass.

Lawmakers are instead expected to focus on investigating crypto companies with ties to President Donald Trump. The political shift comes as Bitcoin trades at its lowest level since July.

Carbone said in an interview that the industry is unlikely to get the broad legislation it had pushed for. The CLARITY Act, meant to set clear rules for how digital assets are regulated in the US, has been left in limbo.

Two longtime critics of the industry are set to gain influence over financial policy: Senator Elizabeth Warren in the Senate and Representative Maxine Waters in the House.

For crypto firms, the agenda in Washington is moving from passing rules to defending against scrutiny.

Trump’s Crypto Ties Move Into the Spotlight

The industry is preparing for sweeping inquiries into crypto businesses connected to the President. Carbone expects Warren and Waters to press hard on Trump’s links to digital assets.

The Digital Chamber’s plan is to counter those investigations by shifting the debate to the technology itself. Carbone wants supportive lawmakers to steer hearings toward topics such as tokenization, so the conversation is about substance rather than politics.

The strategy depends on finding allies inside the incoming majority. Carbone admitted that pro-crypto Democrats are “few and far between,” but said their numbers are growing.

Many of the newly elected Democrats are young and focused on technology. According to Carbone, several have told the industry they are “crypto curious.”

The Digital Chamber plans to target moderates and junior committee members. Carbone said no single Democrat can take on Warren alone, but a larger group could. The aim is to build enough support within the party to push back against the more hostile voices.

Opposition to data centers poses another major threat, Carbone said. Local resistance to energy-heavy facilities hits both crypto mining and artificial intelligence.

Carbone said voters made their concerns clear in the November election and have a right to be skeptical. Still, the industry argues that bans and moratoriums are the wrong response.

The Digital Chamber plans to show lawmakers how data centers can bring jobs and revenue to struggling districts. Carbone described blockchain, digital assets and AI as closely linked industries, and framed leadership in all three as a matter of national competitiveness.

The big warning was about America’s global position. Asked whether the US could lose its lead in the sector within the next two years, Carbone answered and said “100%.”

Developers in crypto and AI are already moving to friendlier places such as the UAE and Singapore. Carbone pointed to China, which holds the most blockchain patents worldwide, as the country most likely to take the lead if the US falls behind.

“If we don’t lead, other adversaries will,” Carbone said, arguing that the US must set the global rules before its rivals do.
