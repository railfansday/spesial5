New champion Antonelli tops Brazil practice

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

F1's new world champion sets the pace from its outgoing one on the first day in Brazil; Max Verstappen third fastest but Ferraris off pace on short runs; Rain forecast over weekend; watch final practice at 2.30pm ahead Qualifying at 6pm live on Sky Sports F1 on Saturday

Kimi Antonelli completed an impressive first day back on track as F1's new world champion to set the pace in second practice at the Sao Paulo Grand Prix.

Five days after becoming the sport's youngest title winner in Mexico, Antonelli followed up his second-fastest time from first practice with the quickest of the more representative second session around the undulating Interlagos lap.

Antonelli was the only driver to lap below 71 seconds with his benchmark time of 1:10.763 proving two tenths of a second quicker than nearest challengers Lando Norris, the P1 pacesetter, and Max Verstappen.

"I think Antonelli has just found a better rhythm around here than anyone else," said Sky Sports F1's Karun Chandhok.

"Antonelli is driving without the baggage of the championship sitting on his shoulders and has found an incredible rhythm."

But with fastest and second-fastest times of his own from Friday, Norris can also be satisfied with his day after McLaren introduced an upgrade to their MCL40 cars. The outgoing world champion, last year's winner in Sao Paulo, appeared quick on the longer runs too.

George Russell was close behind Norris and Verstappen to finish fourth fastest in the second Mercedes. He carries a five-place grid penalty for a fresh engine into the weekend. Oscar Piastri was fifth in the other McLaren.

But it appeared a more difficult first day on track for Ferrari, particularly on the short runs.

Following the first-lap clash that cost the team a likely victory in Mexico, and prompted a Thursday evening meeting between the drivers and team principal Frederic Vasseur to clear the air, Lewis Hamilton was sixth fastest with Charles Leclerc seventh.

Leclerc returned to the track after missing Thursday's media day and then Friday's first session after flying back from Monaco following the birth of his first child, Bianca, earlier this week.

Audi's Nico Hulkenberg was among the experienced drivers to sit out the opening session for rookies and reliability problems meant he ended the day without a lap on the board at all.

A technical problem had already stopped British youngster Freddie Slater after just seven laps while he was in the car in the first session and, while the team worked to repair it for the second hour, an electrical problem kept it sidelined for the rest of the day.

On the plus side, Gabriel Bortoleto was eighth quickest in the sole-running Audi ahead of his home grand prix. Red Bull's Isack Hadjar was ninth with Haas' Oliver Bearman 10th.

Bortoleto is one of two Brazilians in the field this weekend after Haas replaced Esteban Ocon - who will not race for the team again - with Rafael Camara, the 21-year-old making his F1 debut on home soil.

Camara's first day in the car was largely incident-free, finishing 16th fastest, half a second behind Bearman, although he did get in Leclerc's way on the fast uphill run to the start-finish line and was summoned to the stewards after the second session.

Since a run of two wins either side of F1's August summer break - followed by a victory that got away through Virtual Safety Car misfortune in Madrid - McLaren have endured a complicated run of races.

They start this weekend 17 points behind third-placed Red Bull in the standings but Norris sees early optimism following their latest round of car updates.

"I think FP1 we seemed quite easily on the pace," he said.

"FP2 we struggled a little bit more to match the Mercedes but it's been a pretty good start to the weekend and we have continued the trend we had in Mexico," said Norris.

"All in all, a positive day. Today was also a day where we kind of work through the upgrades and see if they work as they should, and they do. We have seemed to gain performance.

"Nothing has changed from a driving point of view. Still tricky. But it's maybe brought a little bit of lap time, which is what we want."

Antonelli, meanwhile, agreed with suggestions he was able to drive in a more relaxed manner with the title now won.

"Yes definitely. I'm trying to enjoy it more," he said. "Definitely not thinking about the championship right now.

"I'm enjoying it. I think it was an enjoyable day. Not easy out there. Conditions are tricky but also the grip is quite low.

"Overall, the degradation seems to be high in the race [runs], so it's interesting. Not easy to find the right car balance, but I think we did a good step between FP1 and FP2 and we just need to work on tomorrow."

Despite Interlagos being increasingly enveloped by low, misty cloud during the course of the second session, Friday's running was dry throughout.

However, the threat of rain showers - which are often heavy and unpredictable in Sao Paulo - looms over the remainder of the race weekend.

"Tomorrow will be very, very wet!" predicted Norris. "I would bring your rain jacket and a hat."

Antonelli added: "I mean it's so uncertain. We will try to be ready for anything.

"We just need to be prepared, be dynamic because we know how unpredictable the weather can be.

"We just try to see where we can improve the car, the driving and will see tomorrow."

And on the fight for pole, Antonelli said: "I think it will be very close. McLaren look strong, Red Bull look strong and we know Ferrari in qualifying can find a bit extra.

"We will all be very close to each other and it will be a very tight qualifying."
