The dangerous gamble Man United face in bid to escape a brutal reality

Man United’s squad building has become a vicious cycle, writes Miguel Delaney, with Mauricio Pochettino already facing a crunch decision to salvage a nightmare season

After wrestling with one season-defining dilemma for weeks, the Manchester United hierarchy might immediately face another following their latest humiliation at the hands of bitter rivals Liverpool. Do they spend the £150m they can just about get away with in a difficult January window, affecting future planning, or actually risk a future that is almost unimaginable: relegation.

The weight of that decision is only made heavier by a much more difficult decision facing Mauricio Pochettino.

What system does he go with? What approach can actually amplify the abilities of these players? What formation can get them out of this mess, and just steady things?

Ideally, Pochettino would play his high-pressing system in a 4-3-3… but we’re a long way from ideals. We're into brutal realities. This squad just isn’t ready for that, and the manager faces yet another dilemma in how trying to prepare them for such an approach might bring a brutal learning period that only worsens an already dismal situation. They don’t have that luxury right now.

Can Pochettino do something similar to what Antonio Conte did with Chelsea in 2016-17 and tailor a formation to fit, or is that even possible? One insider tells the Independent about yet another contradiction in its strange construction. They were bemoaning the quality of the centre-halves, one of the few areas where the squad is overstocked, but pointed to how the latter almost dictates that you have to lean on them.

The urgency of the situation almost warrants Pochettino just going to a back five in a bid to grind out a few badly-needed wins.

In other words, break glass for emergency.

But then that’s where they are, an emergency; an extreme situation that has incrementally been arrived at.

It’s a bit like bankruptcy, an analogy that is by this point at bit too fitting: gradually then all at once.

The state of the squad is a manifestation of all of that.

As recently as five years ago, United were paying out one of the highest wage bills in football. Not all of that expenditure may have been worth it but the sheer expense offered a certain insulation.

That insulation has frayed to the point of vanishing.

The club’s financial situation, as they seek to service so much debt, has influenced and combined with the team’s lack of success to force their level - in quality and spending power - further and further down.

It didn’t help that, throughout that time, the squad was constantly dragged this way and that depending on the demands of different managers.

So they are left with this, and a situation where no one in European football agrees with chief executive Omar Berrada’s comments that they have “made the squad fundamentally better”.

The chief executive of another recent Champions League club instead tells the Independent that United have “an incredibly poorly constructed squad”.

Another experienced football figure says it is “damning” on INEOS’ running of football operations, especially since their supposedly enlightened decisions seem to have made things worse.

This is almost the greater frustration with this leadership. It is as if they didn’t quite grasp what they were taking over.

Because, in a certain context, some of the signings obviously make sense. United specifically wanted to get away from this problem of buying players to certain managerial approaches, so instead went for “adaptability” in talent like Matheus Cunha and Bryan Mbuemo.

But that adaptability only works properly if you know what you are adapting from; if there is a core idea that such signings can work around. United’s most major recent additions instead look like cosmetic additions, rather than the surgery this squad really needs.

Again, they point to that core that has been there for so long, almost the opposite of Chelsea’s problems.

It is not that they are individually bad players, despite increasing and understandable excoriation from the stands.

It is that they have all been there too long, carrying too much “scar tissue” - something said a lot about Tottenham Hotspur last season.

Five current squad members, for example, played in the 5-0 home defeat to Liverpool in October 2021: Bruno Fernandes, Marcus Rashford, Luke Shaw, Harry Maguire and Diogo Dalot.

That defeat was seen as a historic nadir, which played a significant part in Ole Gunnar Solskjaer being dispensed with.

Such an embarrassment has instead become relatively routine. United have suffered another three thrashings to their biggest rivals since: 4-0, 7-0 and, on Sunday, 4-0.

That is a lot to carry. You almost could see it in the latest, the weight of all of this just bearing down as yet another huge match was spiralling towards humiliation. The facial expressions said enough.

One insider is adamant that they don’t just need a clear-out, but to actually go and sign 10 players in one window.

The club are at least thinking about January, although that hasn’t necessarily filled industry figures with much confidence. Certain inquiries have instead raised eyebrows, as United seem to be going for some players they don’t appear to greatly need.

A further potential issue is the strategy.

Various sources say the club splits targets into two main categories: “value” signings, who can deepen the squad and grow, and “superstars”.

That again makes a certain logic in the right context, but not this one. One internal view is that the starting bracket of the superstars is a mere £70m.

That’s what Liverpool were paying for Virgil van Dijk almost a decade ago.

Now, in the words of one source, it just about gets you a Nick Woltemade or a Viktor Gyokeres.

And then, many of those moves take you deeper and deeper into this same cycle: spend worse, get worse, need to spend again, on a more restricted budget.

There’s this time the danger this long-term cycle spins out of control, and maybe into relegation.

It’s why Pochettino, for now, looks like he has to forget his ideals. He just has to get a hold on this, and lock everything down.

And yet that’s just one of many big decisions this club still has to take.
