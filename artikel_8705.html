The 2026 Amateur Olympia brought international bodybuilding talent to Las Vegas, where competitors battled for overall titles and the opportunity to earn IFBB Pro League status during Olympia Weekend.

The next generation of professional bodybuilding talent took center stage September 22–23 at the 2026 Amateur Olympia Las Vegas. Held at the Pearl Theater inside the Palms Casino Resort during Joe Weider’s Olympia Fitness & Performance Weekend, the competition brought together amateur athletes from around the world, all competing for championship titles and the opportunity to earn an IFBB Pro League card.

As one of the premier amateur bodybuilding competitions associated with Olympia Weekend, the Amateur Olympia provides aspiring professionals with an opportunity to compete on an international stage while taking an important step toward the professional ranks.

This year’s competition featured standout performances across men’s and women’s divisions, including Men’s Bodybuilding, Classic Physique, Men’s Physique, Women’s Bodybuilding, Women’s Physique, Figure, Bikini, Wellness, and Fit Model.

For the athletes who earned overall victories, the weekend represented more than a championship. It marked the beginning of a new chapter in their competitive bodybuilding careers.

One of the weekend’s standout performances came from Lithuania’s Albertas Krinickis, who captured the Men’s Bodybuilding overall championship. At just 20 years old, Krinickis demonstrated impressive muscular development and conditioning in the super heavyweight division, ultimately securing his IFBB Pro League card.

Having previously competed in Classic Physique, his transition to Men’s Bodybuilding and success at the Amateur Olympia made his victory particularly noteworthy.

In an Instagram post following the competition, Krinickis reflected on just how much his physique had changed over the previous year.

“From 12th place in Classic Physique to an Open Bodybuilding Overall Champion!” he wrote, highlighting the progress he had made in a relatively short period.

The transformation was also reflected in his competition weight. During a post-show interview, Krinickis discussed moving from approximately 200 pounds at his previous competition to 240 pounds onstage in Las Vegas.

“Last year I competed at around 200 pounds, while for the Amateur Olympia I stepped onstage at 240 pounds. The work done on mass and structure paid off every bit of effort,” Krinickis said in comments translated from the post-show interview.

The victory established Krinickis as one of the young names to watch as he begins his professional bodybuilding career.

Germany’s Rene Hildebrand claimed the Men’s Classic Physique overall title, while Peru’s Edward Alonso Guillen Alva secured the Men’s Physique overall championship. Both athletes earned professional status through their respective victories.

The men’s division highlighted the international depth of amateur bodybuilding, with competitors representing different countries and bringing a range of physiques and competitive backgrounds to the Olympia stage.

The women’s divisions delivered an equally competitive showcase, with athletes earning championship honors across several categories.

Haley May of the United States captured the Women’s Bodybuilding overall title, while Australia’s Nicole Beeforth won the Women’s Physique overall championship and earned her IFBB Pro League card.

Beeforth’s victory was particularly significant for Australian bodybuilding. According to the GMV International Museum of Bodybuilding, she became the first Australian woman to earn an IFBB Pro League card at the Las Vegas Amateur Olympia.

In Figure, Kerri Ferns of the United States claimed the overall victory. Nicole Grumblatt took top honors in Bikini, while Ana Carolina De La Mora won the Wellness overall championship.

Alissa Christine rounded out the women’s overall champions by capturing the Fit Model title.

The variety of women’s divisions reflects the continued growth of competitive bodybuilding and physique sports, providing athletes with different competitive pathways based on muscular development, conditioning, symmetry, and presentation.

The Amateur Olympia serves an important role in the bodybuilding industry by providing athletes with a pathway toward professional competition.

Earning an IFBB Pro League card is a significant milestone for amateur competitors. It gives qualified athletes the opportunity to enter professional-level contests and begin pursuing the next stage of their careers.

For many competitors, the opportunity to earn professional status during Olympia Weekend makes the accomplishment particularly meaningful. The event connects emerging bodybuilding talent with the sport’s most established athletes, industry professionals, and fans.

The international field also reinforces bodybuilding’s continued global growth. Athletes travel to Las Vegas from across the world to compete, bringing different training backgrounds and competitive experiences to one of the sport’s most recognized stages.

According to Cayden Riley, Producer of Amateur Olympia Las Vegas, the quality of competition and the athlete experience remain central to the event’s continued development.

“Every year, the level of competition continues to rise. We have incredible athletes coming from around the world, and the energy they bring to the Amateur Olympia is something special. Jake Wood and Dan Solomon have always emphasized putting the athletes first, and their leadership continues to inspire us to create an unforgettable experience for every competitor who steps on the Olympia stage,” Riley said.

Riley also emphasized the significance of helping athletes reach the professional level.

“Our goal is to give these athletes an unforgettable experience and a stage worthy of the Olympia name. Seeing them earn their IFBB Pro cards and realize their dreams is what makes this event so rewarding,” he added.

For the overall champions of the 2026 Amateur Olympia Las Vegas, earning professional status marks the beginning of a new competitive opportunity.

The transition from amateur to professional bodybuilding brings a different level of competition, with athletes facing established IFBB professionals and working toward opportunities to qualify for major professional events.

While each athlete’s competitive path will be different, the Amateur Olympia provides a platform for emerging talent to introduce themselves to the broader bodybuilding community.

The 2026 competition once again demonstrated the depth of international bodybuilding talent and the importance of developing opportunities for athletes working toward professional status.

For these newly crowned champions, Las Vegas was more than the site of a major amateur competition. It was the place where their professional bodybuilding journeys began.
