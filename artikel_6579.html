The Bride Wore Jacquemus—and Went Barefoot—for Her New York City Wedding on a Vintage Yacht

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Photographer Anna Watt spent her summers at her family’s home in Montauk growing up. She had just finished her freshman year of college in 2017 when she met Tommy Powell, a second-generation commercial fisherman, at the family-owned bar and restaurant, The Montauket. And what began as a summer fling on Long Island would eventually turn into a nearly decade-long love story. “Over the course of almost 10 years, we always found our way back to each other,” Anna says.

“I introduced myself, but that wasn’t the beginning of ‘us’ just yet,” Tommy recalls. “It took running into her a few more times before I finally convinced her to go on a date with me. Persistence is key!”

“I remember going back to college that fall and telling a friend, ‘I have a feeling I’m going to marry the boy I met this summer,’” Anna says.

As a commercial fisherman, Tommy spends 10 days at sea followed by 10 days on land, a schedule that has shaped the couple’s relationship. Whenever he takes an extended break from fishing, the two make a point of traveling together. In October 2024, they planned a trip to France and Portugal, with surfing and some much-needed time together on the agenda. “When we landed in Paris, I asked what the plan was for the weekend, and Tommy quickly replied with ‘We’re going on a boat tomorrow!’” Anna remembers. “For someone who is typically very go-with-the-flow, I knew what this meant!”

On October 27, 2024, Tommy proposed beneath the Eiffel Tower aboard a small boat on the Seine. (Anna jokes that they like to have all their relationship milestones happen on boats.) “I like to think Anna had no clue it was happening,” Tommy says. “But that wasn’t exactly the reality of the day. That morning, she thought we had no plans, and when I told her we were going on a boat, I think she immediately knew something was up. It was perfect, though—out on the water, with the Eiffel Tower lit up just after sunset.”

Nearly two years later, on September 18, 2026, the couple exchanged vows aboard the Mariner III on the Hudson River in New York City, with 85 of their closest friends and family in attendance. For Anna, who works as a photographer, the wedding was an opportunity to bring her deeply personal vision to life. “I have a very creative type B brain,” Anna says. “I had a very specific vision and designed every detail of the day, down to the silverware, so intentionally.”

Event planner Alex Reiss helped translate Anna’s ideas into reality—including one rather ambitious undertaking. When Anna couldn’t find rental furniture she loved, she enlisted her friend Dewi, an interior designer in Bali, to help her custom-design and import all of the furniture for the boat. “It was such a fun project,” Anna says. “I learned so much, and it ended up being one of my favorite parts of the process!”

The bride’s fashion journey took an equally unexpected turn. “I actually had a completely custom wedding dress made and ready to go,” Anna explains. “But then I saw the Jacquemus spring 2026 runway images and did a complete 180.” After falling for one particular look, she began calling the Jacquemus boutique in SoHo weekly, hoping to track it down. “Finally, this past spring, a sales associate I had been messaging called me and said they had received one,” Anna remembers. “I immediately ran to SoHo with my friends Chris and Hanna to try it on and, somehow, it fit perfectly. Not a single alteration was needed!” It went home with her that day.

A traditional veil didn’t feel quite right with the dress, so Anna commissioned a custom scrunchie to match its trim instead. She kept her jewelry equally understated. “I’ve always been pretty minimal when it comes to jewelry,” she says. “So I wanted to keep everything really simple.” For her something borrowed, she wore a vintage Greek charm bracelet belonging to her mother, while her friend Sophie Thorner, founder of Vice Versa, created custom stud earrings to complement her engagement ring. Another friend, Erin Pollard of Underwater Weaving, made a basket to hold her ceremony flowers, as well as a miniature blue basket for her “something blue.” Her something old was the garter her mother had worn at her own wedding, and for her second look, Anna turned to Laura Tønder of Kettel Atelier, who created a one-of-a-kind dress entirely from vintage crochet and doilies.

Anna made one last-minute adjustment to the bridal look, thanks to the Hudson River itself. “When we hopped onto the boat to hide before guests started arriving, I realized how rocky it was and decided I wanted to ditch my shoes for the ceremony,” Anna remembers. “It just felt right, and I didn’t end up putting my Chloé flats on until we left the boat for the after-party!”

Tommy, meanwhile, chose a navy linen suit by Ralph Lauren Purple Label, paired with white Vans. “I wanted something classic and timeless that still felt right for the setting and the occasion,” Tommy says. “Anna looked absolutely incredible, and seeing her for the first time that day is something I’ll never forget.”

On the wedding day, Anna’s father escorted her down the aisle on the boat’s deck to “Going to California” by Led Zeppelin. “I didn’t have an ounce of nerves,” she says. “I don’t think Tommy did either. We opted to exchange letters the morning of rather than vows as we wanted the ceremony to be light-hearted and fun!”

With the Hudson River and New York City skyline as their backdrop, the couple exchanged vows in a ceremony that was short, sweet, and entirely their own. “I felt so lucky,” Tommy says. “I was marrying my best friend in such a unique place, surrounded by all of the people we love. It was one of those moments where I really tried to take everything in because I knew how special it was.”

“It didn’t feel real!” Anna adds. “When we walked up to the top deck, it was so sunny and bright. Seeing everyone we love all in one place was a crazy feeling I will never forget.”

After the ceremony, guests headed downstairs for cocktail hour, where dirty martinis and French cigarettes set the tone. “The captain of the boat took us right past the Statue of Liberty, which was so insanely cool to see!” Dinner followed upstairs, where custom paper lamps illuminated two long tables. Rather than sit at a sweetheart table, the newlyweds took their places among their guests, right in the middle of the celebration. A family-style meal was served as the boat cruised along the Hudson.

“When I walked up and saw what an amazing job our planner Alex had done, I was blown away,” Anna says. “It truly felt like a once-in-a-lifetime experience having dinner under the George Washington Bridge!”

For Tommy, seeing Anna’s vision come together was one of the most meaningful parts of the weekend. “It was incredibly special, down to every little detail,” he says. “I couldn’t have been more proud of her, of us, and of the incredible job our wedding planner, Alex, did bringing everything together.”

Looking back now, the newlyweds agree they would do it all over again in a heartbeat. “We couldn’t be happier with how the entire night came together,” Tommy says. “It was everything we hoped it would be. It really felt like us.”

“It was perfection!” Anna adds. “I wouldn’t change a thing.”
