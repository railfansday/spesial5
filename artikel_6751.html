Tapestry Revenues Hit 12% Growth Despite Kate Spade Dip

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Tapestry attributed its 12% sales increase to continued momentum from its core Coach consumer, Gen Z — of the 2.6 million new customers welcomed by the brand in Q1, 35% were from the cohort. Coach’s strong performance, however, was offset by a continued decline at Kate Spade.

The company updated its full-year guidance for fiscal 2027 to revenues of $8.5-8.6 billion, an improvement on the previous forecast of $8.4-8.5 billion. “Our first quarter marked a powerful start to the year, with revenue, margin, and earnings growth ahead of expectations,” said CEO Joanne Crevoiserat on Thursday’s call. “We delivered broad-based growth across regions, gaining share and expanding the market.”

Coach sales climbed 16% this quarter, generating revenues of $1.66 billion. Meanwhile, Kate Spade’s fell 10% to $223 million, as the business continues to streamline the brand’s operations, closing unproductive stores, reducing promotions and investing in marketing to build brand relevance — evidenced by Kate Spade’s Fall/Winter 2026 campaign featuring brand ambassador Tyla. The brand also intends to capitalize on its new executive creative director, Jonathan Saunders, who assumed post in August.

“What we need to see is higher customer acquisition [at Kate Spade] to drive long-term growth,” said Crevoiserat. “We’re building our handbag assortment with that in mind.” In the quarter, handbags drove 500,000 new customers to the brand.

Across Coach and Kate Spade, the core leather goods category fueled growth, attracting consumers through accessible pricing. At Coach, handbag AUR — the average selling price of an item over a period — increased by a low double-digit percentage; a focus on bags ranging between $200-$500 will continue. Footwear also supported growth, up more than 20% in the quarter thanks to demand for Coach’s Margot pump, as well as traction in sneakers.

By region, Greater China led the way, with revenues up 39% in Q1. “Other Asia” — including Australia, South Korea, Malaysia, Singapore, and other countries mostly in Asia — followed at 23%, with Europe close behind at 22%. “Other” — representing mostly royalties earned from licensing and sales in the Middle East — was up 11%, followed by North America and Japan, both up 4%.

For Coach CEO Todd Kahn, Japan’s return to growth was a compelling example that the brand’s strategy had paid off. “We worked hard the last year and a half to reset that market and focus on a younger consumer and relevant marketing,” he said. “It worked, and that’s what gives us confidence that we can continue to replicate this strategy over and over again across the world.”

Kahn added that Coach’s North America revenues continue to grow, while Gen Z remains a core focus for Coach — and Kate Space. “When we win with Gen Z, we tend to win with every other cohort as well,” said Kahn.
