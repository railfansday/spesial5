‘The Traitors’ Star Ben McDonnell on ‘New Blood’ Set Secrets and Alan Cumming: “You Can Never Break Character”

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The gay cowboy from the first-ever all-civilian season that aired on NBC shares his own secrets — like being an actor and never breaking character — and what it's really like to film on the Emmy-winning Scottish castle set.

[The following story contains spoilers through the 10th episode of The Traitors: New Blood.]

Ben McDonnell had just finished bingeing every episode of The Traitors when he got the call to join the fifth season of the Peacock and NBC Emmy-winning reality competition. The barrel racer living on a ranch in Texas with his husband, where he hosts his weekly comedy podcast It’s Ben a Week, accepted the gig, which he considers one of his biggest challenges yet, and quickly found himself heading to the Scottish castle to live with strangers and host Alan Cumming. McDonnell became the King of One Liners and the fan- and cast-favorite gay cowboy — until his elimination in the penultimate episode that aired last week. “Every morning I would wake up freaking out [about getting eliminated]. And for some reason, the day I got murdered, I was so calm,” McDonnell says when speaking to The Hollywood Reporter after his exit. McDonnell went onto the show assuming his personality would make him a Traitor, but being a Faithful sent him away from the series with something more valuable than the $250,000 prize money. “I didn’t think they were going to love me for me,” he says of his castmates. “And that part was healing in an unexpected way, when you didn’t even know you were broken to be healed.”

Below, when opening up to THR, McDonnell reveals a surprise for fans and his castmates (he’s actually a trained actor), talks about the hardest parts about filming, like giving up his phone (“People are going to think I’m going to rehab,” he remembers thinking) and why Cumming is a national treasure who inspired the name of McDonnell’s new piglet, Alan Cunningham.

As a trained actor who left L.A. for a Texas ranch, how did Hollywood pull you back for this show?

This experience was truly one of the most exciting acting experiences of my life. When you’re on a set, you show up to play a character, but then when the director says “cut,” you go back to being Ben. When you’re playing Traitors, the director never says cut. You are in character at all times. Even when the cameras are not rolling, you still have to play whatever character you’re showing up as. If you’re having lunch, you can never break character. You have to play it up the whole time.

I have a pretty large and loud personality, so I really had to tone it back, which was challenging because I want to be the center of attention. I wanna go be loud, but that’s immediately going to put a target on my back. I also didn’t tell anyone that I was an actor. I really leaned in to, “I’m this gay cowboy,” which is not a lie. I really am the gay cowboy. It’s just that the gay cowboy is more of a hobby than my career.

The producers hired me to play a game about lying and deceit. But the show is non-union, so there are definitely people there who aren’t trying to pursue an acting or Hollywood career. For instance, Abby L. didn’t even have Instagram before she got on the show. She’s an astrophysicist, so she must be a fan of the show and then applied. There are people from all different walks of life, which was amazing.

I went to BravoCon and saw this Traitors thing, and I remember asking my friend, “What is Traitors?” She was like, “You don’t watch Traitors?” I had never seen it. She’s like, “You have to watch. It’s the best show.” I was like, whatever. Then in Texas, we got an insane ice storm last January and here in Texas, when you get an ice storm, you’re stuck at home because no one knows how to drive.

AJ, my husband, came out one day like, “What are you watching?” I’m like, “Let’s just watch this Traitors thing.” I turn on one episode — instantly hooked. I binged every season of the U.K. show, every season of the U.S. show. Then two weeks later I got the phone call: “Would you be interested in The Traitors?” I was like, “I just binged it. This is crazy.”

Next thing you know, I’m at a Scottish castle with Alan Cumming, playing Clue, basically, in the middle of the Scottish Highlands.

Were you pursuing acting at the time? Or do you feel like it was lucky timing?

Acting has always been my main goal. As an actor, I never thought I would do reality TV. For a long time, I was opposed. I’ve had other offers in life for a reality show idea, and I’ve always been like, “You can’t do reality and be taken seriously as an actor.” I do feel there has been a shift. Like, 10 years ago when I was like, “I can’t be gay and be an actor because I’m going to be put in this box where you’re just the gay actor.” That is totally not the case anymore. You can be gay and play straight roles, you can be straight and play gay roles. It’s not as scary as it used to be.

So same thing with reality. You you look at people like our Ariana Madix from Vanderpump Rules. She’s on Broadway. She’s on an NBC show. She’s doing all sorts of stuff. There are so many different reality stars who are now pursuing scripted. So I guess it was not quite as scary, but scripted television and movies have always been number one.

I know you were hoping to be a Traitor. What was your strategy going in? And did your strategy change once you found out you were a Faithful?

I had only thought about being a Traitor. It was so set in my mind.. There was absolutely no question of me not being a Traitor. Stupidly, that did not even enter my brain. Preparing for the show, flying over there in the castle. At no point was I like, “What am I going to do if I’m a Faithful?”

So when I didn’t feel the tap on my shoulder, I remember being like, “Do I need to ask someone if didn’t feel the tap? Like, for sure, I’m a Traitor.” And when I was a Faithful, I laid in bed that night and was like, “Well, now what?”

If you’re a Traitor, it’s strategy all the way. You need to plan. You need to think 10 steps ahead. If you’re a Faithful, it’s a popularity contest. We are back in high school, and you need the Traitors to think you trust them so they want to keep you around, and you need the Faithful to like you so that they don’t banish you. You can watch every season of every Traitors in every different country. What you think would work with one group of people may not work with the next. You have no idea how different people will react.

Personally, I think my training at The Groundlings and with improv really helped me roll with the punches. You had to go with it. I really didn’t try to overthink it. Once I was a Faithful, I’m like, “Just be honest.” And it got me quite far.

This is a huge thing to keep secret from family, friends, work. What was that like for you? Did you tell anyone?

AJ is the only person I told. When you go to Scotland to film, you have no phone. No internet. No connection to the outside world. I was gone during my birthday. It’s very overwhelming.

I actually thought I was going to miss not having my phone. It was so nice and relaxing to not have a phone. The only thing that would drive me nuts is wondering, “Who’s trying to contact me?” I was like, “People are going to think I’m going to rehab.” (Laughs.)

I’m very active on social media. I post on my story all the time. The fact that I’ve just gone completely quiet? Also, I had to record my podcast, It’s Ben a Week, in advance. I had to get all the social media clips. So my podcast Instagram posted a clip every single day. But I was like, “What if I’m causing drama and I’m not even responding?” You have no control.

Let’s go inside your elimination. You were very calm. What did you mean when you said, “I knew it was time” before you opened the envelope and found out you were murdered?

I’m one of the calmest people I know. Nothing riles me up that much. I’m always like, “It’s fine.” That’s my line. This show caused so much anxiety! I’ve never had anxiety. I’m like, “Can someone get me a Xanax, please? (Laughs.) Because I think I need one.”

So every morning I would wake up freaking out. And for some reason, the day I got murdered, I was so calm. That morning I actually started packing my things. I knew there was something coming. That fur coat that I wore? I brought that to wear either at the finale or when I’m murdered. I even talked about with the stylist about saving that coat, and that day, I brought the coat with me.

When I opened the door, I saw the envelope and there was a little hint of me that was like, “Maybe I’m being recruited [to be a Traitor,]” which I was like, “What a dream.” And then I was like, I don’t think so. That’s when I was like, it was time. I was ready to go.

What’s the exit process like? Do you leave right after that?

You do more confessionals. You’re there for a whole other day doing stuff. But you don’t get to say goodbye to anyone. Shooting the show is such an immersive experience that you have to remind yourself no one is dying. They don’t waste money on budget or any of the production value. That funeral scene, for example, when we were in the graveyard and there was smoke and fog and you see a gravestone with your name on it? You’re watching your friends get into coffins in the ground. It feels real. Then the exits are abrupt; no goodbyes. It’s like, boom, gone.

Did you make it further than you thought, or did you think you could win?

I definitely made it farther than I thought. Every day, when I’d walk into breakfast, I’d do a sigh of relief. There are so many steps to get there, I did not want to be the first one eliminated. Then we’re halfway through it, and by the time you can see the end of the tunnel. It’s like, “Let’s just finish it.”

But if I’m being really honest, after the feast and hearing some of the other people’s stories, like Abby B and Shane and some of the other contestants opening up, I was like, “I actually think there are other people who deserve the money more than me.” I truly feel like if I would have won the money, I may have had guilt.

I would love to have an extra $250,000. But I think there are other people where it can change their life.

Are you still going to “haunt” Katie, or are you over it?

No, I’m still haunting her. (Laughs.) She’s great, and I don’t blame her. Obviously, it’s part of the game. But I was onto her for a while.

You opened up about yourself — what it was like growing up gay in Wisconsin and parts about your family life. What about this process lends you to open up in the way that you did?

I’m not a very emotional person. Not many people have ever seen me cry. Everything I do in life is with humor, whether that’s deflecting pain or hurt. So, those emotions you saw were very real and raw. Growing up as a gay kid in a small town in Wisconsin, being bullied in school for being gay, and then having a super traumatic experience with my stepdad. He literally held a gun to my head when he found out I was gay.

I’ve kind of struggled with male relationships. I’m much more comfortable with women, and then with gay men, it’s like, “Is this friendship? Does someone want more here?” I do have male friends. I wanted to clear that up. But I would say 99 percent of my friends are female. So going into the castle, I had zero expectation of making any friends. I’m like, “I’m just here to play this game; get in, get out.” And I made such good friends with so many people on the cast.

In my interviews leading up to it, I had said, “I’m going to be the girl’s gay best friend. I’m the social butterfly. And the guys will see me as an asset because I am a 6-foot-3 Sasquatch that lives on a ranch. I can do all these challenges. They’re going to love me for that.” But I didn’t think they were going to love me for me. And that part was healing in an unexpected way, when you didn’t even know you were broken to be healed.

In that moment, I said I feel like I’ve already won, which I also think is another reason why I was so at peace leaving. I had a great experience. I had so much fun. I got to come play Clue with all these fun people in the Scottish Highlands and Alan Cumming, a national treasure. It checked every box for me.

From a filming standpoint, behind the scenes, going from viewer to being a part of it. What is something we don’t realize about the show, the filming schedule, about Alan?

You could watch TV. You get Netflix, Hulu, Amazon, Disney. I was trying so hard to get cocktails in the castle. You’re only allowed a certain amount of alcoholic drinks, and I just wanted a drink to take the edge off. I always drink a Whitney Houston, which is a vodka and Diet Coke. They would give you a drink made and then one time, they brought it to me, and the shot was tiny. I switched to red wine. I would take the wine, and pour it into my coffee cups, and I was hiding it in the closet. Then when you had a day off, I would drink the wine hiding in the closet. (Laughs.)

But I think the thing people would be surprised to know is that Alan sits in those round tables the whole time. They are really long. You see five minutes. They’re sometimes two hours long. He sits there the entire time, and he never breaks character.

The one time we got a glimpse of him breaking character is when he told me he wanted a pig. And that’s when I go, “Pigs are the new dogs.” We actually have a brand new piglet we just got, and his name is Alan. I sent him pictures and he said, “Oh my God, did you name that pig after me?” I go, “Of course. His name is Alan Cunningham.” He was dying.

Also as an actor to watch him? He’s an icon. I would love to be an actor like Alan Cumming, where you can do so many different things and play so many different characters and have such a long career. What an inspiration. It was almost like I was doing a movie with Alan Cumming.

What’s something about the format you’d change?

I would make it longer because I really think this cast of people was so interesting and dynamic. People came from all different backgrounds. I really don’t think you got to see how cool and special everyone was and is, and the relationships. Tamika and I were ride-or-die besties from day one, and no one even knew we were friends. You don’t see any of it. So I wish people got to see more — and maybe a little more airtime for Ben!

Is there something about you that you want people to know that we didn’t get to see?

A lot of my friends, or people who know me, said, “You are so quiet and calm, and flying under the radar,” and that is so not who I am. I’m loud, energetic. Usually people would say I’m life of the party. So I’m definitely much larger than life than I was shown on the show. But I also think you got to see a more sensitive, nice side of me on the show.

You’ve been called King of the One Liners. Which one are you most proud of?

“Shut up, Jay” was an iconic line. I think “pigs are the new dog,” and also comparing that Victor and Cher will live forever.

Is there anything you want to say in response to things you’ve read from your castmates who’ve been doing interviews?

(Laughs.) There are some castmates out there who have had some strong opinions. I think that in the climate we live in, gay people have had it hard enough. I don’t understand why another gay guy is out there trying to bash people or tear people down. We really should be building each other up. I don’t see the point in that.

How do you feel about reality shows now? Would you do another one?

Scripted will always be my front and center, and the ultimate goal. But I’ve never been one to say no. So I’m not closing any doors. I think I could do a little Dancing With the Stars.

You have your podcast, It’s Ben a Week, which is the most appropriate title for this moment. It’s a weekly comedy, pop culture show. Will you have Traitors stars on, now that you can finally talk about the show?

Yes, now that I’m able to. That was really hard for me. It’s Ben a Week really has been my baby for years. I’m a one-man band. I book my own guests, edit my own shows. I do all my own social media. I spoke about this on a recent episode: It’s been really hard to not to talk about it. It’s almost like I couldn’t be honest these last few months. Traitors has taken over my entire world, and I wasn’t able to talk about it. Every week I’m coming on talking about how it’s been a week, but I can’t really talk about what my week has been. I’m excited that now I can speak on it and share my experience.

I’m opening the invitation to any of the castmembers who want to come on. I know a lot of them will because we’re good friends, but the invitation is open to all of the castmembers. I also have some other cast from previous seasons who will be coming on , so you’re going to hear from some other Faithfuls and Traitors.

I love comedy podcasts and casual conversations, which is something I try to do. I want to sit in my car and feel like we’re all hanging out together. I want people to feel like they’re hanging out with their friend Ben. I love Las Culturistas with Matt Rogers and Bowen Yang. I love Long Winded with Gabby Windey. She’s been on The Traitors. The fact that she can just sit by herself and talk for an hour and how she rambles and how smart she is and quick witted, I love her. It’s the funniest thing. I also listen to, Sorry, We’re Cyrus, which is Brandi and Tish Cyrus. I listen to Chelsea Handler. And, of course, I listen to Call Her Daddy because she gets some really, really cool guests.

What’s your dream acting role? I hear Taylor Sheridan lives in your neighborhood.

Taylor lives right down the street. He’s filming so many shows. I’m pretty sure he has a new partnership with NBC that’s starting up the next couple years. … I can literally ride my horse to set. Taylor, you have an actor in your backyard who has his own horses he can ride to set. I don’t even need a stunt double!

I love comedies like Schitt’s Creek. A David in Schitt’s Creek. I love Grace and Frankie. I would love one of those shows that has a cultlike following. I think everyone wants that!

Half of me loves the comedy, but then I can put that cowboy hat on. I just did a movie that comes out next year, called Cowboy. It’s a gay cowboy movie where I play the straight cowboy. On Traitors, I’ve been portrayed as this gay cowboy, but I also can play the straight cowboy.

The next batch of Traitors will be back to celebrities. Any advice for them now that you’ve been through it?

Celebrities have it so much harder, because everyone knows everyone. I’m a Real Housewives fan, so when I’m watching The Traitors, I’m always rooting for the Housewives. But even if you don’t watch Housewives, you know Lisa Rinna’s reputation. She can’t go on the show and try to lie or be something else. I had the opportunity where I could change my personality a little bit or be what I wanted everyone to believe.

But I guess, try to be yourself and try to make friends.

What about your season — who are you rooting for to win on Thursday? Where are you watching the finale?

I’ll be watching the finale in New York at Club Cumming, Alan’s club. I have my alliance with Clyde and Abby B, so I really would hope for them to win. And of course, my sweaty boy, Victor. I’m excited to see the finale and who is going to win, or what group. Faithful or Traitor?

I tried, and they were like, “That’s a hard no.” (Laughs.) I did get to take some of the wardrobe. You bring your own wardrobe, but if you wear some of their stuff, you get to keep it. I did get some really cool pants!

The two-part Traitors finale releases Thursday at 8 p.m. on Peacock; all episodes stream on Peacock.
