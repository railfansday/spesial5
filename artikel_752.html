Here’s Where to Find Tickets to Celine Dion’s 2027 Paris Residency Online

Celine Dion made a triumphant return to the stage this fall, as the singer kicked off her 26-date Paris residency at the Plenitude Arena in Nanterre. Dion finished her first run of shows with more than $125 million and 539,000 tickets sold across 16 dates in September and October, and the overwhelming demand has led to ten new dates announced for 2027.

Tickets to Dion’s Paris 2027 shows were initially released through Ticketmaster, though most dates are now almost sold out. You can check the Ticketmaster France website to find resale tickets or to see if new seats have been released (which sometimes happens closer to show date). The Paris residency marks the “I’m Alive” singer’s first run of shows since announcing her diagnosis with Stiff Person Syndrome in 2022, which ultimately led to her cancelling a North American and European tour. Now on the road to recovery, Dion is staying in one place as fans flock to Paris to catch the diva on stage.

I was in attendance for one of the shows in October, and the Canadian singer delighted a joyous, sold out crowd with a setlist of both French and English-language songs comprising some of her biggest hits, along with personal favorites. Despite her health setbacks, she was in full spirits and showed little sign of decline, belting out songs with ease while moving freely around the stage (and running backstage for no fewer than six costume changes throughout the show).

Dion stayed at the Le Royal Monceau – Raffles Paris while she was in town, holding court at the iconic luxury property near the Arc de Triomphe and Champs-Élysées. The French-Canadian singer was seen posing for photos and interacting with fans who have been lining the street in front of the hotel each day in the hopes of catching a glimpse of the star. Le Royal Monceau has also played host to everyone from Michael Jackson to Madonna over the years.

As for the residency, while the concerts are being billed as Dion’s big return to the stage, the singer did perform at the 2024 Paris Olympics, where she delivered a soaring rendition of Édith Piaf’s “Hymne A L’Amour” from atop the Eiffel Tower.
