CISAC’s International Standard Musical Work Codes System Hits 100 Million Songs Milestone

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The system, which assigned its first code to ABBA's "Dancing Queen" 30 years ago, has been instrumental in helping songwriters get paid.

CISAC (the International Confederation of Societies of Authors and Composers) says its International Standard Musical Work Codes system — the tracking mechanism it created to identify and track individual songs — has hit the 100 million songs milestone.

“Behind every code is a song, a story, and the people who created it, CISAC’s director of business and technology said in a statement. “CISAC’s priority is to strengthen the connection between accurate data and fair remuneration for the creators the system serves.”

The first work assigned an ISWC code, or T-000.000.001.0, was ABBA’s “Dancing Queen” 30 years ago.

The code is an essential ingredient in ensuring songwriters get paid, especially in today’s world, where multiple collection societies around the globe track activity for each song, many of which have multiple songwriters. Each code provides a unique, permanent reference for a musical work, connecting it to information about its authors and composers, the announcement says, adding it has become key in sharing musical works data across the global network of song collection management organizations, also known as performance rights organizations in the U.S. Today, more than 100 societies around the globe are using the new system.

CISAC adds that close to 30 publishers are allocating ISWCs by themselves and validating those ISWCs against the database. It says the major digital service providers (DSPs) and data companies have started to query the ISWC system, resulting in more than 20 million queries per month.

CISAC notes that it completed a “major modernization of the ISWC system in 2020… in order to improve speed, accuracy and efficiency of code assignment,” facilitating song activity and the subsequent responsibility of paying royalties to the rightful parties.

“Founded in Paris in 1926, [CISAC] continues to develop the international cooperation, standards and systems that help creators benefit from the use of their works as technology evolves,” added the organization in a statement.
