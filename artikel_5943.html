House unveils 2027 calendar

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The dawn sun lights the sky behind the U.S. Capitol dome in Washington in October. (Bill Clark/CQ Roll Call)

House Democrats unveiled a 2027 legislative calendar Friday that includes 33 voting weeks, as the incoming majority party looks to pass an ambitious agenda following massive midterm election wins.

According to the calendar released by soon-to-be House Majority Leader Katherine M. Clark, the chamber would convene for the first session of the 120th Congress with a rare Sunday gathering on Jan. 3. The first votes of the year would take place that week.

“House Democrats are hitting the ground running on January 3,” the Massachusetts Democrat said in a statement. “This calendar reflects the scale and urgency of our work ahead. Americans voted for a Majority that will move decisively to lower costs, protect healthcare, and hold the crooks accountable. Whether we’re working in DC or in our districts, that’s exactly what we’re going to do.”

In most months, the House will be in session for three weeks, with the exception of the annual August recess. That will be followed by four consecutive weeks of voting in September.

The calendar also includes a two-week break for Easter in March, while the Fourth of July falls on a Sunday.

The 33-week schedule suggests a jam-packed year for the House, following a year in which the chamber saw dozens of canceled session days because of floor impasses and a record-long partial government shutdown. The struggles of the outgoing Republican majority has even bled into the lame-duck session, with absences by dozens of lawmakers forcing outgoing Speaker Mike Johnson, R-La., to send a letter to members Friday urging them to “remain fully engaged” in the closing weeks of the 119th Congress.

With the calendar released, House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries of New York has already outlined some of his top priorities for his presumptive speakership, including an ambitious health care agenda, immigration legislation and aggressive oversight of the Trump administration.

But even after flipping both chambers of Congress, Democrats face the uphill battle of working with President Donald Trump if they want their agenda signed into law.

Trump emphasized his “extreme” veto power during a news conference Friday, but he also said he thinks Jeffries and Senate Democratic leader Charles E. Schumer, D-N.Y., are “smart.”

“They're going to send things my way, and I'm going to veto them, and then they're going to send more things, and I'm going to veto them,” he said. “I think after a month or so, they're going to send things, and I'm going to say, ‘I'm going to veto unless you want to do this and this and this.’"

The Senate calendar for next year has not yet been released.
