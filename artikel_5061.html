Elon Musk Joins 160,000 Backing Petition to Bring Jensen Huang Back to Nvidia

Elon Musk has thrown his support behind a campaign to reinstate Jensen Huang as Nvidia's CEO, as the ousted chipmaker co-founder reportedly weighs a comeback just days after resigning.

"A petition is underway to reinstate Jensen and I'm signing it too," the Tesla and SpaceX CEO tweeted on Friday. Musk had previously said it would be "an honor" to hire Huang.

The "Stand with Jensen" open letter calls on Nvidia's board to bring Huang back while an independent investigation establishes the facts. It has drawn more than 160,000 signatures, including Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney and Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan, CNBC reported, though the site notes that signatories' affiliations are self-reported and unverified. More than 7,500 signatories identified themselves as Nvidia employees, The Guardian reported, a figure it said was equivalent to 18% of the company's workforce.

The campaign follows internal calls from Nvidia staff for Huang's return.

Huang has discussed the possibility of returning with confidants, the Financial Times reported, citing multiple people familiar with the matter. One ally told the newspaper they were assembling a "war room" of Silicon Valley executives to lobby for his reinstatement and would seek support from the White House, adding that the effort had Huang's backing.

Even supporters acknowledged the obstacles. "Having an affair with a subordinate is a fireable offence," one person involved in the effort told the FT.

Two major Nvidia shareholders, Altimeter Capital's Brad Gerstner and Atreides Management's Gavin Baker, urged the board in an open letter on Friday to leave "all options on the table, including the potential return of Jensen Huang."

Huang resigned as Nvidia's president, CEO and board member on Thursday, capping a week of reports of affairs with Alice Huang, a former executive at Nvidia customer Megaspeed whom U.S. officials suspect of spying for China, and with an Nvidia executive who reported to him. Chief financial officer Colette Kress is serving as interim CEO.

The campaign faces resistance in Washington. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on Sunday that Nvidia should look outside the company for its next CEO. "They need a dramatic shake-up," he told Fox News.

Bessent said on Sunday Morning Futures that questions over Nvidia's compliance with U.S. export restrictions were the bigger issue, and that the company should be judged on whether it had "intentionally ignored the warning signs" of shell companies in Singapore. He said he wanted answers "in the next 10 days from the board and from the outside counsel." Huang also faces congressional inquiries into Nvidia's chip exports to China, as well as a proposed shareholder class action.

Nvidia's board has approached Arm CEO Rene Haas as a potential successor, the FT has reported. "We are in the process of selecting a search firm for the NVIDIA CEO role. We have not formally engaged any potential candidates," an Nvidia spokesperson told CNBC.

Analysts are divided on Huang's chances. "We see no chance Jensen comes back to Nvidia as CEO," Dan Ives of Yorkville Ives & Co. said in a note cited by CNBC, while Seaport Research Partners' Jay Goldberg said Huang could stage "a second act" if he gets through his legal troubles. Former White House communications director Anthony Scaramucci was blunter: "The FBI doesn't take petitions," he tweeted.

Nvidia reports quarterly earnings on Tuesday, the first under Kress.
