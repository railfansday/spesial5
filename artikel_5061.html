7 Pendant Lights to Perfect Your Overhead Lighting Scheme (2026)

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Sure, recessed lighting serves a functional purpose, but pendant lights are where things get interesting. A quick sweep through any home tour, and you’ll recognize the strategically placed Louis Poulsen above the kitchen island, an Isamu Noguchi in the entryway, and a Herman Miller saucer pendant hovering over the living room that will steal the show. Below are seven pendant light fixtures that set the mood instead of harshening the vibe. We rounded up the best pendant lights for various design personalities, including a vintage-inspired showpiece and a few variations of low-key ambient lighting. Among them are most of the different types of pendant lights you’re likely to come across, with picks for modern minimalists, Belle Époque enthusiasts, and everyone in between.

First developed in 1958, the PH 5 is a classic for a reason. Renowned Danish lighting manufacturer Louis Poulsen designed the iconic pendant with a three-shade system to equally distribute light. While the pendant works for that functional purpose, it also doubles as an unconventional art piece. Choose from a number of monochromatic and multitone colorways, including this steel blue.

Look familiar? That’s because this large pendant, inspired by Noguchi’s Akaris, hangs in many an AD home tour, including above the dining room table in this Japandi-inspired Bay Area home and in the bedroom of this South African–inspired craft home, for example. There are a few reasons why this style of paper lighting is so popular: It goes with everything; it’s affordable; and it adds a sense of levity to the room with its ethereal shape and its soft, warm glow. As lantern pendants go, this option from Design Within Reach is about as low-commitment as they come. It works anywhere you want ambient lighting rather than a spotlight.

Designer pendant lights can cost upwards of $500, but you can pay a fraction of that with finds from Article, a direct-to-consumer furniture store that sells high-quality wares at more palatable prices. That’s certainly the case with the Aino pendant, with matte colorways and a simple angular silhouette that illuminates whatever you’re chef-ing up in your Scandi-style kitchen. The metal cone shade and braided fabric cord make it an easy pick for task lighting over a counter, and if you’re working with a tighter kitchen space, there’s also a mini pendant version for $99 that ships with an LED bulb.

This three-tiered ceiling light from West Elm adds visual texture to blank space, making it a welcome addition to transitional areas in need of personality. A piece like this can also help to add structure to an open plan where the kitchen and dining rooms collide. This piece comes in 26-inch and 32-inch sizes (the larger is a proper large pendant light that can hold its own over a dining table), as well as in hardwired and plug-in options, making it one of the few statement hanging lights you can install without calling an electrician.

Any cold kitchen space dominated by stone and steel fixtures could use a touch of handmade whimsy. Hanging this handwoven rattan pendant over a kitchen island promises to warm up the whole room. Usually, two or three pendant lights hang over a kitchen island; this single shade provides a cozy, welcoming alternative, softening the light rather than spotlighting the countertop, so your mise en place won’t feel so interrogated.

This midcentury Herman Miller pendant, aptly named a saucer for its elliptical shape, is prized by commerce writer Julia Harrison for its retro, Mad Men–esque appeal. Despite the resemblance to our Hay pick above, Miller wasn’t riffing on Noguchi with this pendant: He wanted a Swedish silk-covered pendant he couldn’t justify buying, so he made his own using a sprayed plastic skin originally developed for mothballing Navy ships (whereas the orbital Hay version uses rice paper). The vertical ribbing gives it a shape that seems to be almost expanding as it hangs. Its white shade and chrome hardware make it easily adaptable to many (if not most) design styles.

This lighting fixture brings to mind 10-foot ceilings, Flemish tapestries, and an echoey marble entryway. If you’re lucky enough to have all of the above, this is exactly the sort of pendant your space needs. If not, luckily, the fixture carries that whole vibe on its own, with its cream glass bowl, brass detailing, and a sculpted metal tassel that adds a bit of movement. A vintage-inspired piece like this transforms a more humble hallway into something dignified and scenic.
