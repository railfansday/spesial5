Four years ago (oh my god) strategy RPG Symphony of War: The Nephilim Saga rather impressed our reviewer Jon Bolding. He wrote that "novel tactical mechanics make this indie a contender for the best strategy RPG this year".

Well, buckle up, 2027, because Symphony of War 2 has broken cover with a promise to release next year, and it's coming courtesy of the strategy sommeliers at publisher Hooded Horse, which I take as a very good sign.

SOW2 looks set to continue the Fire-Emblem-ish framework of its predecessor, tasking you with recruiting a gaggle of heroes to wage war with (and possibly compose symphonies, who's to say?) in "a world filled with political intrigue, betrayal, and tense, large-scale tactical battles". Which is a lot like our world, really.

It all looks very nice indeed. In the announcement trailer, we get glimpses of its squad-based combat and anime cutscenes, and I am officially into both. The art is poppy and distinctive, at some point an age-of-sail warship transforms into some kind of German WW1 destroyer, and the overworld triggers the regions in my brain devoted to thinking about Heroes of Might and Magic. Check, check, and check.

"Anime cutscenes!?" I hear you cry. "That sounds like the kind of thing that bespeaks a dense tapestry of lore I'll need to get to grips with first!" Well, apparently not. I am informed by Symphony of War 2's Steam page that the devs have no expectation you know what a "Nephilim Saga" even is before you pick this game up. "Symphony of War 2 is a standalone experience set several generations after the events of the first game—it can be enjoyed without having played Symphony of War: The Nephilim Saga". The game's tale of a fallen empire trying to un-fall can be enjoyed single-serve.
