Binance Says It Won Court Order to Recover Frozen Hack Funds

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Binance says it has secured a court order from Abu Dhabi Global Market to help it recover crypto frozen after the record $6.3 billion hack last month, Reuters reported on Wednesday. The exchange has not said how much of the money the order covers.

Reuters described Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM) as Binance’s main financial regulator. Binance deputy general counsel Dugan Bliss confirmed the order in a statement but gave no details on its terms. Neither the company nor Reuters said which assets it applies to.

“We will continue using every lawful tool available to trace, freeze and recover stolen assets, support our partners in returning funds where possible, and keep those assets out of the hands of the attackers,” Bliss said.

Binance has said about $2.85 billion of the stolen tokens were frozen or intercepted after the Oct. 29 attack, according to its own incident report. That is roughly 45% of the $6.34 billion it says was affected.

Most of it sits with stablecoin issuers. Tether said it froze more than $1.5 billion in USDT within 30 minutes of the attack, and Circle said it froze about $1.1 billion in USDC in coordination with law enforcement. Circle added that it does not decide who owns frozen assets, a question it left to law enforcement and the courts.

The Justice Department has separately obtained court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of the stolen crypto. It is part of more than $2.7 billion that Justice says government and private-sector efforts have frozen. Criminal Division head A. Tysen Duva said the department helped “immediately halt about half of this historic breach.” Reuters did not say how the ADGM order fits with the U.S. action.

Binance says the attack began months earlier, with a tampered software installer that compromised one engineer in June. In September, another engineer was approached by a fake recruiter and persuaded to run a bogus interview assignment that installed malware, the company said.

From there, the attackers reached an internal wallet-signing service and drained Binance hot wallets across multiple blockchains on Oct. 29. The thefts included 233.46 million XRP, about 970,500 SOL and 18,270 BTC, according to Binance.

The FBI has attributed the theft to North Korea’s TraderTraitor group, and Binance has said the same. Pyongyang denies involvement. Blockchain analysts said the attacker tried to outrun the freezes by swapping stablecoins into ether over about 20 minutes, but the issuers moved faster.

That split matters for bitcoin. Tether and Circle can block their own tokens, but bitcoin has no issuer. The stolen BTC is not covered by those freezes and can be stopped only if it reaches an exchange or service that blocks it.

Binance has already said it replaced the affected assets with company funds, using part of its SAFU emergency fund and its corporate treasury. Withdrawals resumed in phases earlier this month, and the exchange aims to reopen all remaining networks and assets by the end of this week, Reuters reported.

On Wednesday, Binance’s Asia-Pacific head SB Seker told Reuters at the Singapore FinTech Festival that the company plans to expand into five Asian markets in 2027 and does not need to raise new funds. He said talks are under way with five or six markets, three of them at a mature stage, and details are due in the first quarter.

Seker said the cost of replacing the stolen tokens was “statistically” not a large amount for the company. “In terms of the impact to us, we can take the impact and we can weather it without any change to the way we operate,” he said.
