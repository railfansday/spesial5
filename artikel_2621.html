Nolan Traore emerging as unlikely star among Nets’ first-round picks

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Among seven first-round picks over the past two years, including two in the lottery, few expected Nolan Traore to emerge as the most productive offensively for the Nets this season.

The 19th overall selection in 2025 continues to earn playing time — and the trust of Brooklyn coach Jordi Fernández — with 16.6 points per game over seven November appearances, including 23 (which tied a career high) with four assists and two steals in Monday's home loss to the 76ers.

"I think [it's] the pace that he's playing with, but I also like his voice. His voice is growing," Fernández said about Traore after the game. "He's creating to score, but also he is creating for his teammates. And you see it.

Nolan Traore drives to the basket during the Nets' loss to the 76ers on Nov. 16, 2026, at Barclays Center. NBAE via Getty Images

"I think he had four assists to two turnovers, which is a pretty good ratio. And everything else. He shot the 3 well. He scored at the rim. He's played very well, and I think you can see the growth, and that's contagious. It's his speed and the way he plays … He can make winning plays."

Of course, the Nets haven't won often — they are 3-10 entering Wednesday's home game against the Bulls — but the 20-year-old Traore has earned a spot in Fernández's starting lineup in eight of his 12 appearances this season.

The second-year point guard out of France also is connecting on 42.5 percent of his attempts from 3-point range, up significantly from his .318 percentage from deep in 56 appearances as a rookie.

"I'm just trying to take the shots that the game gives me. I guess it goes well for now and I hope it's gonna keep going," Traore said, before being asked about Fernández's assertion that he is "using his voice" more this season.

"Yeah, I think so," Traore added. "I'm comfortable in the team and in the league, in general, so hopefully it's easier for me to give advice or just tell the guys what we can do better. So yeah, I've noticed it."

Meanwhile, it's impossible not to notice that 6-foot-9 combo guard Egor Dëmin — the eighth overall pick in 2025 — has struggled to find any consistency in his game.

Nolan Traore looks to pass during the Nets' loss to the Knicks on Nov. 2, 2026, at Madison Square Garden. AP Photo/Frank Franklin II

Though this year's lottery pick Mikel Brown Jr. (sixth overall) has shown flashes with 11.0 points in 22 minutes per game off the bench, he has been subjected to a strict minutes restriction in his debut campaign.

The 6-3 Traore similarly has dealt with playing-time limitations since joining the organization.

He also spent 13 games last season playing for the Long Island Nets of the G League.

Nolan Traore dribbles upcourt during the Nets' blowout loss to the Mavericks on Nov. 8, 2026, in Dallas. AP Photo/LM Otero

"He just plays to his strengths," said Julius Randle, who scored 33 points in Monday's loss. "He uses his speed to get downhill and does a good job of getting into the paint, at will.

"From there, he either finishes or finds guys. So he's just doing a good job of playing to his strengths and using his speed."

Traore, who sat out a game last week in New Orleans with a calf injury, matched his season high by logging 30 minutes against the Sixers. But he also sat on the bench for a 10-minute stretch bridging the third and fourth quarters.

"As you guys know, these guys have some sort of restrictions, and obviously I'm not going to tell you what the minutes are," Fernández said. "But we're doing what's best for their bodies, and sometimes it's just like you gotta manage the game.

"There's no excuses here, but I think that Mikel made a couple of shots, [we] let him run, and then I made sure that Nolan was able to finish, and I think that we competed."
