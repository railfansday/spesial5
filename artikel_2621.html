Are the Chiefs ready for the gauntlet ahead of them?

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Time flies in the NFL, and as the days get colder, the games become more important.

This week, the Kansas City Chiefs defeated the Arizona Cardinals 34-7, for their third consecutive win. The tie puts them in a gridlock with the Denver Broncos for the AFC West lead, but the Broncos hold a slight edge because they beat the Chiefs in Week 8.

The Chiefs have lost only two games so far this season, but only one team they've played this year would be in the playoffs if the season ended today: the Broncos.

Although the season series is split due to the Chiefs' Week 1 victory at Arrowhead Stadium, the Broncos have an easier path to the postseason.

The Chiefs are gearing up for an absolute gauntlet of a regular-season finish that will see them take on not only the best of the AFC, but other legit Super Bowl contenders.

Three consecutive road games — the first two on Thursdays — will see the Chiefs take on Buffalo Thanksgiving night, the Los Angeles Rams a week later, and cap the road trip against the Cincinnati Bengals. Once returning home, the team will play the New England Patriots on "Monday Night Football," before a mega clash against the Super Bowl front-running San Francisco 49ers.

The Chiefs will finish their season against the Los Angeles Chargers and Las Vegas Raiders, but the next five weeks will define the season's finish.

The next five weeks will also define how multiple players and members of the organization are viewed.

I tweeted a few thoughts earlier this week, but wanted to expand on a few stakeholders with the most riding on the rest of the season.

George Karlaftis has been good this season. In fact, he has been very good, and perhaps the Chiefs' best defensive player. With eight sacks and 10 passes defended, he has been among the most productive defensive ends in football this season.

This is his fifth season in the NFL, and the best of his career so far. We have seen big stretches from him in the past, and big moments in the playoffs, but starting now, he has a chance to break out of the very good category and enter the elite conversation, and for the season to end the way the Chiefs need it to, they will need him to do so.

Although Chris Jones has been heating up the last few weeks, there is a visual decline in his game, even if he still can create game-breaking plays.

Earlier in his career, Karlaftis was fine being "good" because that was all that was needed when Jones was wrecking games week in and week out.

The roles are now reversed to a degree, but with an elite group of quarterbacks down the pipe, Karlaftis must finish plays with sacks. He must find ways to create an impact on his own and disrupt plays before they can get going. This is what will move him from good to elite.

Pressures are good, but sacks are great, and against great players like Josh Allen, Matt Stafford, and Joe Burrow, you must finish plays with sacks, or they will make you pay.

This duo has been one of the season's most entertaining storylines in Kansas City so far, and the Chiefs may have found one of the best cornerback duos in the NFL for years to come.

While the pair still need time to develop, they are preparing to face the best competition they have seen so far in their careers.

Quarterbacks who can fit balls into the tightest windows imaginable, manipulate the field with their eyes and legs, and match up against some of the NFL's best wide receivers.

They will also play critical roles in run defense, with three of the NFL's top-ten rushing attacks slated through the five-game stretch.

Brett Veach gambled by trading Trent McDuffie this offseason, but so far the gamble has paid off. Delane and Williams have been spectacular in man coverage this season, and if the pair shows up in crunch time, they might find themselves in the All-Pro conversation.

The Chiefs have built the best running back room in the NFL, and when Kenneth Walker comes back healthy, they should use both him and Emmet Johnson to wear teams down.

This has the potential to be the catalyst for an offensive explosion in KC, and with a makeshift offensive line, running the ball will be key to not only keep defenses honest, but also help the passing game.

Both runners play with relentless effort, and their elusive style can gas out defenses over the course of the game.

It's not lightning and thunder per se, but more of a terrible twosome, where you don't know where they will cut until they are already at linebacker level, and every play can go to the house.

This season has been rough for Andy Reid and company, but they have a chance to right the ship.

Seattle and Denver were eye-opening moments for the Chiefs offense, and their response has been good even if they are playing inferior opponents. Reid's key will be continuing to evolve week by week and not falling back on old habits.

Against Seattle, and the end of the game against Denver, were some of the most embarrassing moments of the Mahomes era.

The short yardage may still be a serious question mark, but adding Josh Downs boosts the Chiefs' WRs to average, and this is where Mahomes can do the rest.

I suspect Rice and Downs can become a solid tandem, and with Travis Kelce potentially having his best season in the last four years, the answers should be there for Mahomes.
