Pokemon TCG Now Has Pikachu Cards Made Out Of Gold

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Pokemon released a 30th anniversary TCG expansion in September that was pretty special for several reasons. Chief among them is the 30 special Pikachu cards trainers have been tasked with collecting, and just to make them a little more special, Pokemon has now revealed alternate versions of all 30 cards that have been crafted using pure gold.

Before you fly out of the front door and buy up every last 30th anniversary booster pack you can find, these gold cards aren’t available to the public, and there’s no sign as of yet that they ever will be. The weight of them alone, compared to your average Pokemon card, would make finding them in booster packs and ETBs far too easy.

From popular promos, to terrifying trainers, these are the most expensive cards from the Scarlet & Violet era.

The 30 Pikachu cards are part of the 999 Gold Card Collection, and they will be on display at the Pokemon Card Art Illustration Exhibition in Shanghai, China. The 999 in the collection’s name comes from the fact that each of the cards is made of 99.9% pure gold. That means each of them weighs about 30g, due to the fact that they’re made out of a precious metal instead of standard gold.

By comparison, a typical Pokemon card weighs about 1.7g, so these special gold cards are about 17 to 18 times heavier than a normal card. As I said, that would make finding them out in the wild, if they were ever released, incredibly easy. Well, easy if you were lucky enough to happen upon a store selling a booster pack with one of the cards inside, as that pack would be significantly heavier than the rest.

Based on the amount of gold and that gold’s purity, used to make the cards, each one is worth around $3,765. That means there’s almost $113,000 worth of gold in all 30. Ironically, though, if these cards were to ever go on sale, the fact that they’re made of pure gold would almost certainly not be what leads them to fetch an extortionate price at auction.

These cards are all presumably one of a kind, and it’s that rarity which would really drive up their value. The record for the most expensive Pokemon card ever sold was broken in February of this year when Logan Paul sold his Pikachu Illustrator for $16.492 million, and that doesn’t have a single ounce of gold in it. It’s simply an incredibly rare card in impeccable condition that every TCG collector in the world wishes was a part of their collection.

Maybe, like the Pikachu Illustrator that was only ever given to contest winners in the 1990s, some of these pure gold cards will appear in auction houses three decades from now and sell for astronomical amounts we can’t even begin to imagine. For now, just focus on trying to find all 30 of the regular versions, and keep half an eye on 2027, when we’re going to be getting our first-ever three-set expansion to kick off the next Pokemon TCG era after Mega Evolution ends.
