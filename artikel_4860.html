YOU ALREADY KNOW you need to brush at least twice a day, no matter what toothpaste or mouthwash you use. But technique matters just as much. Even for me—a grooming editor who writes about this stuff—it can feel like a chore. In the morning, I brush before coffee when I’m barely awake. At night, I’m wiped out from the day or drifting off for less wholesome reasons. That’s why I love electric toothbrushes: they simplify brushing and deliver a better clean by doing most of the work for you.

Unlike manual toothbrushes, electric toothbrushes use vibrations and automated head movement to remove plaque and debris. Many models include smart programs for whitening or gum care, interchangeable heads for sensitive or deep cleaning, and built-in timers. Some of the latest options even feature AI that tracks your brushing and suggests improvements.

But the main selling point of electric toothbrushes is their ability to clean more thoroughly and efficiently than manual options, using power and vibrations no hand can match. Top-tier electric toothbrushes can be pricey, but they’re an investment in your smile. It’s the closest you can get to a dentist-level clean at home.

For the past two years, the Oral-B Series 10 has been listed at the top of this list. It remains the best overall electric toothbrush; however, it’s currently sold out everywhere due to this demand. The model that immediately preceded it, the iO Series 9, is the next best thing. It features many of the same features and power, just with a less high-tech charging mount (which may be a benefit for some who prefer something lighter and less complicated).

Series 9 is similarly a premium electric toothbrush packed with advanced features designed to elevate your oral care routine. Just like the 10, there are seven customizable modes—including Daily Clean, Sensitive, Super Sensitive, Intense, Gum Care, Tongue Clean, and Whitening—this brush adapts to your needs, whether you have sensitive gums or want a deep clean. Its oscillating, vibrating round brush head provides a powerful yet gentle clean, and the built-in pressure sensor ensures you're brushing with the right amount of force, lighting up red (too strong), blue (too weak), or green (just right) based on your pressure.

The Series 9 pairs with a companion app to track your brushing in real time across 16 zones of the mouth, offering instant feedback and a visual map of missed areas. I’m not a huge tech junkie, especially when it comes to something like brushing my teeth, since I just want to get it over with quickly. But this is one of the few apps that genuinely adds value, with accurate tracking, clear visuals, and brushing reminders.

Even if you skip the app, the built-in display shows your selected mode and a timer, though the latter is easier to read in the app. That being said, it’s nearly impossible to achieve a 100 percent clean according to the app (I usually aim for between 80 and 90) without brushing for five or more minutes, which is not recommended because it can damage enamel. If you’re a perfectionist, this will prove annoying.

In terms of design, the Series 9 is sleek and solid, and comes with a sturdy travel case. It charges in just three hours (significantly faster than most others on this list) and comes with four replacement heads, which should cover your first year of use if you replace them every three months, as dentists advise.

Interchangeable brush heads, specifically designed for tasks like whitening or tongue cleaning, also help you achieve optimal results with minimal effort. Similar to the Series 9, it also has app-enabled smart modes that provide personalized feedback on your brushing habits and overall progress. Plus, the charging travel case makes it easy to travel while protecting your investment.

As with manual toothbrushes, electric toothbrush heads need to be replaced every few months. Because the latter tend to be more expensive, this makes owning electric models more expensive. I recommend buying the heads in bulk when available, as this can make for pretty substantial savings. Electric toothbrushes have more nooks and crevices than their manual counterparts, which means they require a little extra daily maintenance.

This means carefully rinsing them after every use to stave off funky toothpaste and spit residue. It’s also worth actually removing the brush head from time to time to wipe the attachment point where the head meets the handle. A cotton swab or even your old toothbrush is usually enough to do the trick.

It’s tempting to just toss old toothbrushes and toothbrush heads in the trash once they’ve outlived their usefulness. But they’re actually perfect for detailed cleaning work that requires moderately stiff bristles to clean out tight spaces. Think: Shower heads, hiking boots, bathroom grout, your dishwasher filter, bird feeders—you get the idea.
