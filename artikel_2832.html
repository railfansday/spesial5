Cooper Flagg was as good as the Warriors starters were bad in blowout loss in Dallas

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

There's a famous German optical illusion known as the "Old Woman, Young Woman Ambiguous Figure." From one perspective, it looks like a young, vibrant woman. From another, it looks like an old crone. That's kind of like looking at the Warriors with and without Steph Curry.

Or just looking at the starting lineup versus the reserves. The Warriors' starters fell behind 21-9 before Steve Kerr made his first substitutions of the game. After halftime, the Dallas Mavericks started the third quarter with a 16-4 run against those same starters. That's a 24-point deficit in about 10 minutes, and it essentially decided the outcome quite with 20 minutes left to play in a 116-94 Mavs win.

I was tempted to start typing gibberish for the first 10 percent of this recap just to best capture the feeling of watching the Warriors right now. And then the sub-headlines, phot captions, and tagging would be incredible, the recap equivalent of Will Richard coming off the bench to score 20 points and collect four steals to finish +8 in 27 minutes, meaning the Warriors were -30 with him on the bench. For the record, I do not have a fully-torn UCL in my thumb

Sophomore sensation Cooper Flagg scored 33 points on just 15 shots, going 11-for-15 making all eight free throws. He also had nine assists and five rebounds in one of the most impressive performances we've ever seen from a guy who wears a t-shirt under his jersey. PJ Washington scored 21 points, made five threes, and blocked three shots. He and Flagg were both +32.

Kyrie Irving tormented the Warriors but what else is new? He had three steals, like Flagg, scored 21 points, and had five dimes, playing 29 minutes.

After the Warriors dug themselves into a 25-9 hole, the reserves got them back into the game by closing the quarter on an 11-4 run, highlighted by two threes from Gary Payton II. The bench simply played with dramatically more energy than their counterpoints in the starting lineup, as evidenced by the backups grabbing 10 of the team's 14 steals — De'Anthony Melton had three of them.

Richard and Gui Santos were displaying so much hustling that Jennifer Lopez is in talks to play Santos in the film version. During the second quarter, Richard and Draymond Green combined on a steal, but Santos got stripped on the subsequent fast break. Seconds later, Richard knocked the ball away again, and Santos dove on the floor to get the loose ball, flinging it forward for a Richards layup. Richard also took in a steal for a slam all by himself.

How long did it take Warriors announcer Bob Fitzgerald to begin complaining about the Mavericks' three-point shooting success, based on a small sample size? Just over eight minutes of game action, when Dallas was 4-for-8 from deep. Oddly, Fitz brought this up after two Mavs misses had dropped them to 4-for-8 from deep.

Fitzgerald returned to this topic four minutes into the second quarter after Cooper Flagg made a three to beat the shot clock while being closely guarded by Will Richard. "I mean, if that's going in, you gotta be kidding me!" Fitz declared. After the Warriors forced a turnover, not by Flagg, Fitzgerald continued, "Will Cooper Flagg miss a shot tonight?…That three had NO chance of going in, and went in!" (Flagg was 5-for-5 at the time)

Flagg did eventually miss, but he still had 22 points on 7-for-9 shooting at halftime. The Mavericks as a team were 10-for-22 behind the arc before halftime, launching a few shots FROM PLANO!

Dallas led 62-49 at the break, thanks to going 10-for-10 from the free-throw line while the Warriors were 1-for-5, and making four more threes than the Dubs. The Mavericks' big run to start the second half basically ended the game, no matter how well the Warriors backups fought.

How can you tell? The NBCS Warriors X account didn't post a single highlight about the final 30 minutes of the game. The official team account didn't post any footage after halftime, taking the same commitment to social media that Draymond Green brings to a game when Curry is out. Green did make a three-pointer, but it was the only shot he attempted. Is it possible he's quiet quitting the Warriors?

Yaxel Lendeborg shot 6-for-18, which may force another dramatic change to his appearance. Shooting sleeves? Neck tattoo? Wearing a t-shirt under his jersey? All we know is that the Warriors have one more chance not to get swept on this road trip, Thursday in Memphis, but we fear that they'll be serenaded by Three Six Mafia instead.

In a nice tribute to Don Nelson, the Mavericks left an empty seat on the bench to honor their late coach. If he were still with us, we're pretty sure Nellie would have left midway through the third quarter to go play poker and drink scotch.
