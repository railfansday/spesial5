What everyone said about Lions, Jeffery Simmons false start controversy

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The CBS broadcast for the Detroit Lions vs. Chicago Bears Thanksgiving Day classic jumped all over an interesting controversy involving back-to-back false starts from Lions center Juice Scruggs in the second quarter. The two penalties set the Lions behind the sticks, and they punted two plays later.

But when the broadcast came back from commercial, they showed a replay that appears to show Bears defensive lineman Jeffery Simmons yelling out something loud that immediately resulted in Scruggs' false-start flinches.

Let's break down the controversy by looking at the rulebook and hearing everyone's perspective about the situation via comments after the game.

Simulating a snap count—or trying to disrupt the offensive line verbally—is an unsportsmanlike conduct infraction that carries a 15-yard penalty. Here is the exact wording of the rule, which you can find in Rule 12, Section 3, Article 1, subarticle i:

There shall be no unsportsmanlike conduct. This applies to any act which is contrary to thegenerally understood principles of sportsmanship. Such acts specifically include, among others:

(i) Using acts or words by the defensive team that are designed to disconcert an offensive team at the snap. An official must blow his whistle immediately to stop play.

So if Simmons was indeed yelling or screaming to intentionally get the Lions to jump, that should carry a 15-yard penalty in Detroit's favor.

J.J. Watt was the one to point this out on the broadcast, calling it out as a penalty. But he also described why it's so easy to get away with as a defensive lineman.

"He barks a little bit or he says something right here, and that's when Scruggs moves. It's a little defensive line trick. You normally can't ever get caught doing it, because people can't see you. But I think he is getting a little reason for Scruggs to be dropping back."

Simmons claims he was just calling a defensive alert to his teammate during those plays, and not trying to simulate the snap count.

"If I say, 'Run,' I'm telling my defense, run. Think I care to tell them what they about to do? I'm talking to my guys. So all the crying that they're doing, that's a them problem, not me."

Mahogany was the most outspoken of the group, blatantly calling out Simmons for the infraction—while also admitting it's still on the offensive line not to false start.

"They're simulating the snap count. They're using − they find out the snap count of the quarterback and they use it, and that's it. They use the words. So like I said, no bitching and complaining, we self-inflicted those wounds and we can't false start."

Campbell clearly wasn't in the mood to talk about the controversy, initially shutting down the first question he got about it. When pressed, he said he had an idea of why the false starts were happening. But when asked why, he shut it down again.

"No, I'm not going to share it. So, we'll be good on that. We'll be good. We've got to clean it all up and make sure that we just take care of our business. That's all."

Goff was far more direct, accusing Simmons of indeed simulating the snap count. However, the Lions quarterback downplayed the impact, noting it happened only once or twice and that the team needs to handle it better.

"I only think he did it really once that was really egregious to me – where they yell. It's a really hard thing to officiate because at what point is he simulating the snap count? At what point is he just talking to his guys? And I understand it happens all across the league, this isn't the first time it happened. But yeah, I'm just trying to make sure that they know that I'm not allowed to simulate snap count with my hands. He shouldn't be allowed to do it with his voice. And that wasn't the difference in the game. But yeah, that was early, and I don't believe it happened more than once or twice."

Later adding, "We got to be able to handle that a little bit better than we did."
