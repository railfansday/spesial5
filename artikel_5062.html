CoinShares unloads $19.3M in ETH and SOL, yet keeps crypto risk

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

CoinShares sold $19.3 million in Ethereum and Solana to fund potential buybacks as crypto prices continue to shape its earnings.

CoinShares Chief Executive Jean-Marie Mognetti said on Nov. 18 that October’s sale of the firm’s entire corporate treasury Ethereum position and half its treasury Solana freed up $19.3 million for potential share repurchases.

The company’s third-quarter earnings show how its shareholders remain exposed to crypto prices through its remaining holdings and the fees it charges on investment products.

CoinShares raised $16.6 million from the Ethereum sale and $2.7 million from Solana. Together, they amounted to about 48% of the board’s $40 million repurchase authorization.

The board approved the program on Oct. 22, and CoinShares announced it on Oct. 27, with a run time until the next annual meeting, expected in the second quarter of 2027. Purchases depend on market conditions, blackout periods, and trading-volume limits.

Before the sale, CoinShares held $43.2 million in treasury digital assets, including 235 Bitcoin worth $19.6 million, as of Sept. 30, according to its interim financial statements.

The treasury also held $17.6 million in Ethereum and $5.9 million in Solana at that date. The October disclosure identified sales of Ethereum and Solana, with half the Solana position retained.

The filing separately lists $4.1 billion of digital assets held for operations as of Sept. 30, including $880.5 million of Ethereum and $407.8 million of Solana. Some of those assets back exchange-traded products or hedge its obligations to product holders.

Assets under management climbed from $5.5 billion at the June close to $7.4 billion at the September close.

The $125 million in quarterly net inflows accounted for about 7% of the increase, with price appreciation adding roughly $1.75 billion, based on CoinShares’ first-half and nine-month figures. At its CoinShares Physical platform alone, prices added $938.4 million and net inflows added $77.8 million.

CoinShares reported that management fees held at $19.1 million in both quarters. The fees accrue on daily assets, and the late-quarter rally lifted the September closing balance more than the average fee base.

The total also includes seed assets that pay no fees, with CoinShares reporting $6.4 billion in fee-paying assets at quarter-end.

The asset manager had $358.1 million of digital assets on loan as of Sept. 30, after accounting for expected credit losses, up from $69.8 million at the end of 2025. Some loans are unsecured, so the firm can lose money if a borrower defaults.

Management said it shifted Ethereum from staking into lending during the third quarter, and the loan book earned $4.3 million in interest that quarter, alongside $2.8 million in staking revenue.

Stronger lending and trading lifted capital markets segment revenue and gains to $17.8 million from $4.9 million in the second quarter.

The third-quarter segment figure comprised $8.6 million in revenue and $9.2 million in operating gains. CoinShares reported $27.7 million in consolidated revenue, including asset management fees.

An unrealized $9.6 million gain came from the gap between the value of cash-redeemable XBT certificates and the assets CoinShares holds to hedge them. The gain was reflected in the company’s $28.4 million of operating income and excluded from the $17.8 million segment figure.

A $14.4 million treasury revaluation gain was recorded below operating income and helped lift net income to $39.6 million.

CoinShares said its expanded monthly tracker adds lending, staking and treasury figures to the existing product assets and flows data.

That gives investors a way to track what the company holds and what it puts to work, while measuring buyback execution separately by the shares the company reports purchasing.
