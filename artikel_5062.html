The portfolio risk Toobit’s growth chief wants AI to flag before a trade

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Yasser Elmansy describes how leverage can magnify losses when several tokens move together and why the trader should make the final call.

Holding a basket of different tokens often feels like diversification, right up until a market downturn drags them all down at once.

Toobit’s chief growth officer, Yasser Elmansy, told CryptoSlate he’d like an AI assistant to warn traders when a new position adds to a risk they already carry.

For Elmansy, the goal isn’t to hand trading over to bots, but to give retail users better visibility before they click buy.

Trading once required a phone call to a broker; the internet moved it to computers, and smartphones put market access in people’s pockets.

“The internet changed how we execute decisions; AI could change how we prepare for and make them,” he says.

Elmansy imagines an assistant that, with a trader’s permission, could review holdings, open positions, leverage, and trading history. Suppose the trader owns several tokens that tend to move together. The assistant could explain how a single downturn might affect all those positions and how leverage can magnify a loss.

The trader would still set objectives and risk limits, authorize access, and approve consequential actions.

“I’d rather see AI help someone make a better-informed decision than simply push them to trade more.”

Models can misread information or produce convincing but wrong explanations, he warns, and in a leveraged market, small errors can be costly. If many automated strategies react to the same signals, they can worsen volatility.

“Speed isn’t always an advantage if everyone responds in the same way,” he says.

He wants leverage controls and clear ways to pause or stop automation.

That emphasis on informed participation carries over into how Elmansy approaches growth at Toobit. He helps choose partners among trading communities and creators and discusses deal terms. Rather than chasing raw follower counts, he looks at how many people actively trade, how long they stay involved, and how much trading takes place.

He says Toobit already uses those criteria to assess prospective partners.

“I don’t want to attach a specific growth figure to that work without verified reporting,” he says.

The chief growth officer role is recent, he says, so he “wouldn’t claim to have transformed the business in that capacity already.” What has changed is the scope of his thinking.

“To me, sustainable growth isn’t only about why users arrive; it’s also about why they stay,” he adds.

Elmansy’s focus on building durable systems and questioning standard playbooks comes from two decades of learning business and crypto from the ground up.

His first venture started in sixth grade, when he told his brother and a relative he was working in advertising and both asked to join.

“Rather than admitting I didn’t actually have a business, I decided to give them an assignment,” he says.

Armed with a print catalog from a shop his father’s accounting firm used, Elmansy learned that 100 business cards cost about three pounds to produce. He sent his recruits to nearby businesses, including a supermarket, to sell them for 20 pounds.

“To my complete surprise, they both came back with customers and money,” he recalls.

He placed the orders, delivered the cards, and started again. Websites and Facebook ads followed. For years he found the clients and did the work himself, until the business hit a wall because he couldn't scale alone. He convinced a backer to help fund an office and staff, and launched his first marketing agency in 2013, concluding that growth eventually requires trusting people and building structure.

He remembers keeping pictures of Bill Gates and Mark Zuckerberg on that first office wall. He wanted to build his own technology company.

“I wasn’t interested in technology simply as a tool for marketing,” he says.

By 2019, the agency had around 40 employees. When the pandemic hit, businesses cut marketing budgets, so he scaled back and spent late 2020 studying decentralized exchanges and liquidity pools.

After watching an OKX webinar, he contacted the exchange’s recruitment team. He lacked the business development background it wanted, but pitched his marketing and DeFi knowledge and got a chance.

When he joined, his manager mentioned a trading-volume target of $100,000.

“I genuinely didn’t know whether that was large or small,” he says.

What Elmansy did know was influencer marketing. At OKX, he says, he challenged assumptions about what the exchange should pay its partners and argued for backing deals he believed in. He spent nearly three and a half years at OKX, then worked at Pionex, before he joined Toobit in January 2026 as head of global business development.

Looking ahead, Elmansy thinks crypto will reach more people when sending money gets simpler. Today, he says, users still have to think about wallets, private keys, networks, and fees “before they can do something as basic as moving value.” He wants users to understand the risks they take even as the mechanics get easier.

Over the next year, Elmansy says he wants to expand Toobit’s reach and bring the same scrutiny to marketing and product decisions. Getting people to sign up is one part of the job. He wants to understand what keeps them.
