8 Mistakes Everyone Made When Playing The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Remake

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Many of us grew up with the original Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time and were excited when The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Remake was announced. We had years upon years to play the original title, and it has honed our skills for the remake.

Of course, that is what everyone thought, and then when each of us started the remake we all started making numerous mistakes. Maybe you completely forgot about the sprint button, or maybe you completely forgot that you can manually jump now.

There are a bunch of mistakes that are easy to make when you have the muscle memory of the original game baked into your being.

The original Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time is known for not having a dedicated jump button. Actually many of the early Zelda titles didn’t have a dedicated jump button. Of course, this was changed in the remake, and Nintendo made sure that everyone was aware of that.

When the remake was finally shown off, Nintendo made sure to emphasize that you could manually jump instead of relying on hitting a ledge. Despite this, I know I am not the only one who walked off a ledge.

Even though Nintendo told everyone about the jump button, we all ran to a ledge and muscle memory took over, so instead of jumping, we all just fell off into the water around Kokiri Forest.

Autosave has been around in games for a long time, but it didn’t always exist. Back in the day, you needed to manually save all of your games or you lost your data when things went south.

I know multiple people who gave up playing Ocarina of Time for a bit because they forgot to save. Now, you don’t need to worry about it, as there is an autosave feature in the remake.

I can’t be the only one who relied entirely on the autosave. In fact, I didn’t manually save for the first few hours of playing the game, and it came back to bite me later on. Make sure you always save your game and don’t just assume the autosave has your back.

There are some classic Rupee exploits we used to perform in the original Ocarina of Time, with one of the most obvious being the drawbridge jump. When you climbed the chains attached to the drawbridge, you could make some easy money by jumping off along the wall.

This would get you red Rupees, which are worth 20 Rupees, and that went a long way towards purchasing some important items. However, these exploits don’t work anymore.

The remake has removed the exploits from days past, and now we must rely on new exploits to gather a bunch of Rupees in a quick manner, but that doesn’t mean we all didn’t try it anyway.

After we beat the original Ocarina of Time, it became a habit to want to beat it as fast as possible, and thus, the insane number of speedrun attempts started, making Ocarina of Time one of the most replayed games in history.

If you were someone who tried to speedrun the game over and over again, you might have tried to perform one of the speedrunning tactics only to find that they just don’t work anymore. Nintendo didn’t even purposefully leave any in as a tribute.

However, that hasn’t stopped gamers from figuring out many of the remake’s glitches. Now you can skip straight to the Shadow Temple after leaving Kokiri Forest.

The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Remake has finally been released, and fans have already discovered several game-breaking glitches.

After you play through a game enough, you remember the various details, like where a key is supposed to be, or the layout of a dungeon. There are many people who remember the layouts of every dungeon in Ocarina of Time like the back of their hand.

All this muscle memory doesn’t help in the remake, as the dungeons have been redesigned. Some of the rooms are different, and there are even some new areas to explore that weren’t in the original.

It has made the remake feel like a brand new Zelda title rather than the same old game everyone is used to, but the fact remains that you probably thought a room would be in a specific place and ended up running into a wall.

Redeads were terrifying when in the original Ocarina of Time. Their lifeless bodies freaked out many kids back in the day, and Nintendo managed to keep that same feeling alive in the remake.

Many people, like myself, underestimated the newer version of the Redeads. Nintendo made them much quicker this time around, and it is very easy to get caught in their screams.

I went into the game thinking I would easily take them down, and I do now, but at first, I was grabbed, and it took me off guard. They are much more menacing this time around and shouldn’t be taken lightly.

Motion controls can be the bane of anyone’s existence if they aren’t prepared for them. Back in the day, Nintendo didn’t have motion controls in games, meaning those who grew up with the original Ocarina of Time didn’t need to worry about them.

However, since the Nintendo Switch 2 has motion controls, Nintendo decided to turn them on for the start of the Ocarina of Time Remake. If you didn’t know this, it became very apparent when playing the slingshot mini-game.

As soon as I started the mini-game, something felt off, and I quickly noticed that my aim was being controlled by my movement. I am not the only one who didn’t realize motion controls were automatically turned on, and we all quickly turned them off.

Remember, if you want to use your slingshot, you need to be a kid. If you want to use your bow, you need to be an adult. This is a very common mistake that was made in the original, and now it is common to make in the remake.

Child Link and adult Link have two different inventories. Some items that work for you as a kid, don’t work when you are an adult and vice-versa. This can be a bit annoying, especially since an adult definitely knows how to use a boomerang.

Anyway, it is a mistake that we have all made, as it is easy to play as one version of Link for a while, switch to the other version, and then forget what items you have access to.
