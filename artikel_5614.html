Can New Gout Drugs Improve on Existing Treatments?

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

— Expert weighs promise of two investigational treatments against current options

New data presented at the American College of Rheumatology annual meeting highlighted investigational treatments for gout, including the URAT1 inhibitor pozdeutinurad and the interleukin (IL)-1 beta inhibitor SSGJ-613.

In this exclusive MedPage Today video, Austin Wheeler, MD, of the University of Nebraska Medical Center in Omaha, discusses the potential of these emerging therapies and the unanswered questions that could determine their place in clinical practice.

The following is a transcript of his remarks:

REDUCE-2 is with pozdeutinurad, which is a URAT1 inhibitor, which is kind of a novel mechanism for serum urate reduction. And so there's a lot of interest in this as a potential target. It looked like it did pretty well in the trial that they presented. They showed some nice data of serum urate reduction. They got about 79% of their patients to a goal serum urate, which this was against placebo. So it looks like it works in terms of lowering serum urate levels in gout.

I think it will need to be seen what clinical implication that really has. We know that if we use treat-to-target approaches with allopurinol and febuxostat [Uloric], the STOP Gout study, for example, showed that we can get about 80% of patients to a goal serum urate with those medications. And so I think time will tell, we'll need to see how this stacks up against a treat-to-target approach with our xanthine oxidase inhibitors to really know how this is going to change the landscape of gout treatment.

And then the other study was an IL-1 inhibitor, and so they were looking at how does this perform against placebo, and it looks like it performs as you would expect an IL-1 inhibitor to perform in gout. They did make the statement that it potentially has less of an infection risk than other IL-1 inhibitors. I haven't seen any actual data that shows the frequency of adverse events from an infection standpoint stacked up against our other IL-1 inhibitors.

And so I think that's one where potentially that could be something that makes its way into our treatment options at some point. But we do already have canakinumab [Ilaris] as a long-acting IL-1, we have anakinra [Kineret] as an IL-1. I think until we see more data on is there truly less of an infection risk or not, it's going to be hard to know exactly how that one plays in as well.

I would imagine that at some point they're planning on taking this outside of China. I haven't heard anything personally about plans to do that, but definitely that's something that I think we would need to see. The big things that I'd be looking for would be how does it perform outside of that population and then how does it perform when we have more direct comparisons against the IL-1 inhibitors that we already have available? Is there truly something novel about it that would cause us to reach for that as opposed to canakinumab as a long-acting IL-1?
