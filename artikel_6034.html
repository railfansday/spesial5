The AI Agent Is Travel’s Ad Blocker

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Travel companies have built profitable businesses around persuading customers to spend more. AI agents working for travelers may be harder to persuade.

Travel companies make money from impulse purchases. A traveler sees a better seat, a room upgrade, or another offer at checkout and decides to spend more. An AI agent booking on that traveler's behalf doesn't buy on impulse. That’s the concern MCR Hotels CEO Tyler Morse raised at our Skift Commerce Summit earlier this month, and it’s a risk to the upsells that drive a large share of many travel companies’ profits.

Wall Street is focused elsewhere. In a November 15 report, analysts at Citizens tested two AI booking agents, Instinct and Meta's Muse. They found the experience “underwhelming” but saw significant long-term risks for online travel agencies, ranking Expedia as the most exposed, followed by Booking Holdings, with Airbnb the most insulated.

The analysts focused on who would capture the booking and how much commission they could charge. Morgan Stanley's October analysis examined similar questions.

AI booking agents may not behave like the impulse buyers that travel companies have built their margins around, and at least one hotel owner wants to block them outright. At Skift Commerce Summit, MCR Hotels CEO Tyler Morse said an agent following a traveler's instructions is less likely to add something simply because an offer looks appealing, and said he wants to stop AI agents from booking his hotels. The same day, United Airlines announced restrictions on unauthorized personal AI agents, citing customer security and regulatory risks rather than lost sales. Wall Street has been looking at a different question: a November 15 Citizens report tested two agents, Instinct and Meta's Muse, called the experience "underwhelming," and ranked Expedia as the most exposed online travel agency, followed by Booking Holdings, with Airbnb the most insulated. The money at stake is large. IdeaWorksCompany estimated airlines would generate $157 billion in ancillary revenue in 2025, nearly 16% of total revenue, up from $67.4 billion and 9.1% in 2016, and Expedia generated $206 million in advertising revenue in the third quarter of 2026 while Airbnb CEO Brian Chesky has spoken of a $1 billion advertising opportunity. When Skift asked Instinct whether sponsored placement influenced its recommendations, the agent replied: "Placement isn't a signal I use." AI bookings remain tiny — Booking Holdings says large language model referrals account for well under 1% of its room nights — and agents can sell too, with Wyndham saying its own AI phone agent books rooms at rates averaging 15% above published rates.
