As luxury Amazon trips grow in popularity, operators are having to go further to deliver what they sell: remoteness. Gateway cities, including Brazil’s Manaus, are sprawling, and lodges that once felt remote no longer are.

As luxury Amazon trips grow in popularity, tourism is adding pressure to the very thing it’s sold on: remote, authentic experiences.

Tour operators increasingly define luxury as access to remote locations and unique experiences. As the industry grows, the gateways to these experiences, and especially to the Amazon in Brazil, are becoming more urbanized — threatening the very thing travelers are there to experience.

One notable pressure point is waterways. CroisiEurope will debut luxury cruises from Brazil to Colombia next year, and Carnival Corporation’s Seabourn announced a new cruise featuring Amazon river sailing for 2028. Lindblad also chartered a 42-guest ship to expand its presence in the Amazon for this year, doubling its capacity, and Ponant bought a majority stake in Aqua Expeditions, which runs Peruvian river cruises.

Luxury Amazon travel is expanding fast, and the gateways travelers pass through to reach the rainforest are becoming more urbanized. CroisiEurope will debut luxury cruises from Brazil to Colombia next year, Carnival Corporation's Seabourn announced an Amazon river sailing for 2028, Lindblad chartered a 42-guest ship that doubles its Amazon capacity this year, and Ponant bought a majority stake in Peruvian river cruise operator Aqua Expeditions. Amazonastur, the tourism agency in Brazil's Amazonas state, expects 17,000 visitors including crew across the 2026-2027 cruise season, two-and-a-half times the previous season. G Adventures' VP of product Yves Marceau said lodges outside Manaus that used to be remote are now reachable by car, with light pollution and less wildlife, so operators have to go further out — and that costs. Intrepid Travel's Peru general manager Fernando Rodriguez said higher value, lower volume models can work when managed responsibly, pointing to small group travel, community-owned businesses, and lodges accessed by boat rather than road. Marceau's warning for operators building Amazon product: partner with lodges that are "way out there," because "when you build tours, you have to futureproof."
