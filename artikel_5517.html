Emirates president said the Iran war has again derailed his retirement plans

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Clark, 76, was considering stepping away after record profit, while Emirates' first-half profit fell 66% as the war disrupted flights

Tim Clark, president of Emirates, the airline unit of Emirates Group, said the Iran war derailed his plan to step away from the carrier, according to Bloomberg.

Clark, 76, said he had begun thinking about leaving once the airline booked its highest profit ever, according to Bloomberg. “I thought now is the time and, bingo, the war started,” he said during the yearly economic summit held in Berlin and run by the newspaper Süddeutsche Zeitung. “So here we are again.”

Global events have now interfered twice with his retirement plans, Clark said. The first came with the outbreak of the global pandemic in 2020.

Clark, a British citizen who holds a knighthood, has been working for the carrier ever since the 1980s. He is credited with helping turn Dubai into an international connections hub, according to Bloomberg. Emirates is the world's largest long-distance airline, and the industry watches its succession plan closely.

Clark did not discuss that plan, according to Bloomberg. Potential candidates include Adnan Kazim, the airline’s chief commercial officer, and Adel Ahmed Al Redha, its chief operations officer. Ghaith Al-Ghaith, head of Emirates’ regional partner flydubai, is also named as a possible successor.

Missile attacks and airspace closures tied to the war, along with higher oil prices, have cut into Emirates' earnings, according to Bloomberg.

Emirates last week reported a 66% drop in profit for the six months through Sept. 30. The company said regional conflict disrupted its operations and raised jet fuel prices. Profit before tax was AED 3.7 billion ($1.0 billion), compared with a record AED 11.4 billion ($3.1 billion) in the same period a year earlier.

The Iran war began Feb. 28 and led to the closure of the Strait of Hormuz. Drones targeted Dubai International Airport in March, and the airport temporarily halted operations. Emirates called off thousands of flights.

Emirates carried 21.8 million passengers in the half, down 22% from a year earlier. At Sept. 30, the airline was operating 91% of its pre-disruption network.
