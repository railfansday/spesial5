Pro-housing coalition presses Senate on BAAJA permitting reforms

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

In a letter co-led by Inclusive Abundance Action, a coalition of pro-housing groups argued that BAAJA would streamline NEPA and historic preservation reviews, allow qualified state or Tribal reviews to substitute for federal review, and expand categorical exclusions and set firmer deadlines, with potential savings of about $425,000 per project.   AI Summary

A coalition of national housing organizations, homebuilders and 58 local pro-housing groups is urging Senate leaders to pass the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (BAAJA), arguing its permitting reforms could sharply cut timelines and costs for federally supported housing.

In a letter to key Senate committee leaders, co-led by Inclusive Abundance Action and summarized in a public statement Monday, the coalition backs the housing provisions in BAAJA (S. 5653), the Senate’s bipartisan permitting reform package.

The groups say the bill could “transform how quickly federally supported housing gets built,” even though most public attention on BAAJA has focused on its energy and infrastructure impacts rather than its potential to accelerate affordable and multifamily projects.

Today, federally assisted affordable and multifamily housing typically faces National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) reviews and National Historic Preservation Act (NHPA) reviews that do not apply to most private-market projects. Affordable housing developers report delays of a year or more to clear environmental review and up to three months just to document that a project is exempt, according to the letter.
