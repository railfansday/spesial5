Is Derby's season already damaged beyond repair?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Shane Duffy said Derby's heads dropped in their defeat by Wolves [Shutterstock]

After Derby played Bolton at Pride Park just under three weeks ago, I spoke about how their second-half performance suggested boss John Eustace had a group of players at his disposal who looked like they were playing for him.

Coming back after the international break to face both Southampton and Wolves away from home was never going to be easy.

Despite the lack of expectation to pick up points, a demand to show some signs of encouragement seemed fair, and was perhaps the bare minimum to ask of Eustace and the players.

Seeing the gulf in class between both sides at Molineux on Tuesday was actually quite frightening. At times, it looked less like a Championship fixture and more like a League One side coming up against Premier League opposition in a cup tie.

The decision to play five at the back is understandable when results are poor and confidence is low but a system is only as effective as the personnel available to execute it, and Derby do not appear to have the right players to make it work.

Rhian Brewster is not a defender, and when you have someone like Jack Robinson sat on your bench, it rather begs the question, 'why was Brewster Eustace's first choice to play at left wing-back?'

Ryan Nyambe on the other side provides the defensive quality, but does not have enough in the tank to consistently provide the attacking threat required from a wing-back, despite continuing to give all he had.

For much of the season, Derby have also looked unfit. Questions have to be asked about pre-season, particularly when you consider the level of opposition Derby faced.

Did coming up against the likes of Alfreton Town, Rotherham United, Burton Albion and Mansfield Town actually provide adequate preparation for a gruelling Championship season?

It appears to be a squad of players with good careers behind them, plenty of experience, balanced with some promising young talent. On paper there is more than enough ability to pull away from the relegation zone. Yet collectively, something looks badly wrong.

Eustace instilled an incredibly healthy 'fighters not victims' mentality when he initially took charge, forcing the players not to feel sorry for themselves. But that mentality seems to have long gone, and last night the contrast in both benches only reinforced that for me.

Jordan James came on for Wolves and was heavily involved in their second and third goals. On the other hand, Bobby Clark came on for County and looked defeated.

Shane Duffy's post-match comments on BBC Radio Derby about heads dropping were eye-opening. The Irishman has only been at the club for just over a month, yet already seems to be witnessing the same problems that we as supporters have been concerned about for much longer.

We cannot rely solely on Patrick Agyemang to come back and provide all the answers, or expect one or two individuals to drag the team out of trouble.

But at the moment, who is actually going to want to come to Derby in January? What is the attraction in joining a struggling side who sit rock bottom of the Championship?

Is now finally the time for the board to pull the plug? Or, perhaps more worryingly, we are already at the point where Derby's season is damaged beyond repair.
