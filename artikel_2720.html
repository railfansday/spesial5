How do the Steelers stack up in the AFC playoff picture?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Following an embarrassing performance against the rival Bengals in primetime, it's safe to say that the energy in the Steelers fanbase has once again returned to biblical levels of doom and gloom. And it's hard to blame anyone for feeling that way. After putting together their most well-rounded and physically imposing victory of their own against the Browns before the bye, it's been a bitter dose of reality in the days since the team's 38-18 loss to Cincinnati, in which most well-seasoned Steelers fans could tell the game was all but over after a first quarter in which Pittsburgh possessed the ball for barely a minute.

Before the season, this column predicted that the Steelers would win roughly 10 games, make the playoffs, and potentially win one postseason game if they landed a favorable matchup. And while the Steelers certainly did not feel like a serious team at any point during Sunday Night Football, it's worth pointing out that they are still on track to make the postseason.

Looking at the AFC playoff picture on the NFL's website, the Steelers have a projected 73% chance of making the playoffs. The field for the AFC seems narrowed and set, barring any insane collapses and/or turnarounds. Of the nine teams currently outside of playoff positioning, six of them have three wins or fewer. Two of the remaining three teams are likely without their starting quarterback for the rest of the regular season at minimum (Patriots, Ravens), and the third just turned to a rookie quarterback with a tough schedule remaining (Raiders).

As flawed as this Steelers roster may be, it is still more likely than not that they'll be playing in the Wild Card round. So while the timing might feel off based on the collective bad vibes we're feeling, Sunday's loss makes the following conversation feel that more relevant.

We ranked the top eight non-Steelers team in the AFC playoff picture/bubble based on the criteria: Who could Pittsburgh realistically beat in a playoff game?

Current AFC No. 1 seed; >99% playoff chance per NFL.comRB: Is this finally the Bills' year? Buffalo's struggles to get over the hump to the Super Bowl have been well-documented, but Josh Allen and co.'s ability to win in the Wild Card round (6-1, including a 2024 victory over Pittsburgh) has not been a problem.

The Bills are a juggernaut on offense, ranking first in points per game, fourth in yards, and third in EPA. What always flies under the radar is how Buffalo can win in a number of ways, with the league's second-ranked rushing offense led by James Cook, along with a 13th-ranked passing offense led by perpetual MVP candidate Josh Allen. (The Bills would rank much higher if they threw more, currently third in yards per pass play and fifth in EPA per pass).

However, the Bills still have some proving to do despite a near-perfect 8-1 record, having given up 30-plus points four times already this season en route to a 28th-ranked passing defense. Still the Bills rank middle of the pack in points per game allowed (15) and top 10 against the run (eighth). They have an underrated front and have allowed just 28 points over the last three games, although it has been against the Jets, Vikings and Ravens.

Buffalo fits in that tier of teams where it's hard to imagine Pittsburgh keeping up if a game turned into a shootout. And the Bills' defense's strength being against the run just makes things tougher for a Steelers team that needs to get the ground game going to succeed.

Current AFC No. 3 seed; >99% playoff chance per NFL.com

RP: The Jaguars were my preseason pick to represent the AFC in the Super Bowl, and that feeling has only increased for me since then. With respect to Josh Allen and the Bills, the team from Duval gets my vote for most difficult matchup, largely because I believe them to be the most well-balanced team in the conference this year.

Whether you're a fan of modern analytics or more traditional stats, this Jaguars club favors well. They are ranked fourth in both offensive EPA per play (+0.10) and opponent EPA allowed per play (-0.18). They rank first in point differential (+125). They score the fourth-most points per game (29.9) while allowing the fourth-fewest (16.0). And they are both ninth in total yards per game gained (358.9) and allowed (319.2).

On offense, this team motions receivers at the NFL's fourth-highest rate (75%) to create favorable matchups, and then they're bombs away with the highest frequency of passes traveling over 10 air yards (43%). On defense, the Jaguars live in base defense (4-3) at a higher rate (52%) than all but three teams. They don't blitz a ton, but still generate a high rate of pressure. Long story short, the Jaguars have the type of offense that can give the Pittsburgh secondary fits, while still winning enough on the ground. On defense, the Jaguars rely on a base defensive look that can counterpunch the Steelers' running game while still smothering the passing game. We'll dive into this team in further detail when these teams play in Week 14, but suffice it to say, this is a scary matchup for Pittsburgh.

Current AFC No. 2 seed; 97% playoff chance per NFL.com.

RB: The Chiefs are largely back after a disastrous 2025, currently ranking as the AFC's No. 2 seed with a 7-2 record. That's scary news for the rest of the NFL, as the head coach/quarterback combination of Andy Reid and Patrick Mahomes has far more postseason experience and success than the rest of the field combined.

The Chiefs' offense, top 10 in both points and yards this season, has Mahomes averaging eight yards per attempt for the first time since 2022, even if he still isn't pushing the ball down the field much. And the addition of Kenneth Walker III, now an Offensive Player of the Year frontrunner, has given the Chiefs an elite running back for the first time in the Mahomes era. Don't discount the recent trade for Josh Downs, either, which provided a much-needed reinforcement to KC's group of pass catchers.

The Chiefs' defense is once again competent, in the upper half of the league against the run and pass and top six in points and EPA per play allowed. Kansas City is blitzing less than in past seasons (a 17th-ranked 30.6% this year) but coordinator Steve Spagnuolo remains one of the league's most innovative minds when it comes to pressure, and he's not afraid to use man coverage, either — not a great matchup for Pittsburgh.

The one saving grace of facing the Chiefs for the Steelers is that a wide receiver room of Rashee Rice, Downs, and Xavier Worthy isn't as terrifying for a reeling Pittsburgh secondary as most of the other teams on this list. But this is not a team or coaching staff you want to be facing in January.

Current AFC No. 4 seed; 98% playoff chance per NFL.com

RP: We don't need to drag this one on too long; the wounds are still fresh. As long as Joe Burrow, Ja'Marr Chase, and Tee Higgins are healthy, this matchup is a coin flip for the Steelers on the best of days, and a complete mismatch on their worst. Joe Burrow is turning in an MVP-level performance this season, despite every team knowing exactly how the Bengals plan to attack them. He can pick apart zone coverages, and the All-Worldly duo of Chase and Higgins can win matchups against man coverage more often than they lose. If secondary weapons like Chase Brown and Mike Gesicki bring their A-games to a contest, it's all the worse for the opposing defense.

The Bengals defense is still streaky. The defensive line is currently its strength, but the linebackers are gettable in both phases, and the secondary lives and dies by the pass rush's performance. Bryan Cook and Jordan Battle are an above-average safety tandem this year, but the cornerbacks have been up and down. The Bengals' defense is also pretty terrible against the run, with the sixth-worst EPA allowed per rush in the league. As such, they've had some bad performances, even recently. It was only a couple of weeks ago that a putrid Tennessee offense hung 36 on this unit, and an abysmal Dolphins squad managed 24 points against the Bengals. Heck, even the Steelers managed 30 points in their first meeting.

If an opposing offense comes to play, especially with a strong running game, beating the Bengals in a shootout is more realistic than you might feel following Sunday's loss. But do the Steelers have that level of discipline, let alone consistency? That's the part that leaves me unsure, especially after the Mason McCormick injury.

Current AFC No. 5 seed; 97% playoff chance per NFL.com

RB: The Broncos are a tough team to gauge. On one hand, they're 7-2 with a top-10 scoring defense in one of the league's tougher first-half schedules. On the other hand, they have a bottom-six offense and defense in terms of yards, and a quarterback in Bo Nix who most analysts agree is a few tiers below Mahomes, Allen, Lawrence, Burrow and CJ Stroud. Saying a team "just wins" isn't always a great argument for postseason success.

Creating the fourth-highest pressure rate in the league (39.7%), the Broncos defense comes with a dangerous front (Zach Allen, Nik Bonitto, Jonathon Cooper) and a secondary with a lot of talent when healthy (Patrick Surtain II, Talanoa Hufanga, Ja'Quan McMillian). Good news for the Steelers is you can run on Denver, with the Broncos giving up the sixth-highest yards per rush in the NFL this year (4.5).

On offense, the Broncos have a good foundation with one of the top offensive lines in the league and an experienced play caller in Sean Payton. However, the Broncos' 26th-ranked rushing offense has struggled to find consistency (although the emergence of Jonah Coleman is promising), while the trade for Jaylen Waddle has failed to provide a spark in a 25th-ranked passing offense. Nix can run and has the arm to uncork deep shots, but continues to heavily rely on short, outside the numbers throws. He has the second-lowest air yards per attempt of qualified passers in 2026.

To me, this feels like the first team on the list where Steelers fans might have some confidence in a playoff matchup. Facing this team and defense in Denver isn't an enviable task, but the Broncos offense is the least intimidating on this ranking so far. Still, Denver is tied for allowing the lowest sack rate in the league this season, with the quick passing game nullifying some of the dangers of opposing pass rushes. We'll get to see how these teams match up on Black Friday.

Current AFC No. 11 seed (currently out of playoffs); 23% playoff chance per NFL.com

RP: It's a bit funny that the Texans rank this high. With just three wins on the season, and coming off an embarrassing loss to the Browns in which they blew a 17-0 lead, it's mostly a nod to how bad of a matchup this feels like it would be if Houston were miraculously able to turn the ship around and sneak into the playoffs. As things stand, for the Texans to get in, it would likely require a brutal collapse in the second half of the season from either the Steelers or Browns, or an even more devastating collapse from the Jaguars, Chiefs, Bengals or Broncos.

Still, this is a team on paper that should give the Steelers problems, and they did so just a season ago in the playoffs. While the Texans defense has not lived up to expectations this season, they still have a fearsome pass rush led by Will Anderson Jr. and Danielle Hunter, a feisty secondary, and a stingy run defense. On the other side of the ball, C.J. Stroud has been one of the hottest quarterbacks this season despite the team's record. He's top-seven in both passing yards and touchdowns, and his three interceptions are the fewest among quarterbacks with at least 300 dropbacks this season. Receiver Nico Collins has the exact size and skill set that has bullied this secondary this season, and the Texans have an interesting stable of secondary receivers to prevent defenses from focusing solely on him.

They may be highly unlikely to make the playoffs at this point, but they aren't a team I'd be uber confident the Steelers could beat in January. Like Jacksonville, we'll dive into Houston in further detail in a few weeks when these teams play.

Current AFC No. 7 seed; 56% playoff chance per NFL.com

RB: Like with the Bengals, the Steelers have split their series with Cleveland this season, with the most recent matchup being a blowout. However, in this case, that game went in favor of the Steelers, who defeated the Browns 27-6 in Week 8 in what has been Pittsburgh's strongest game on both sides of the ball so far this season.

The Browns rank in the bottom half of the league in both yards and points per game on offense, but still have a bevy of exciting young playmakers and a quarterback in Deshaun Watson who has risen to the moment in some big games this season for Cleveland, which is shockingly 6-4 entering a Week 11 bye.

On defense, the Browns rank in the middle of the NFL in terms of yards and points, but with a bottom-10 rushing defense that shouldn't surprise Steelers fans. Still, Cleveland has a sixth-ranked passing defense that lives heavily in man coverage, something that bit the Steelers the first time the two teams met this season.

The Browns' potential on offense and ability to shut down the Steelers' passing game — not to mention that they're a division rival — still makes this a somewhat worrisome potential matchup for Pittsburgh in the playoffs. But it's still not every day you get to play a team in the postseason that you've already beaten by 21 points earlier in the year.

Current AFC No. 10 seed (currently out of playoffs); 15% playoff chance per NFL.com

RP: This almost feels disrespectful. Truthfully, this might just be a case of out of sight, out of mind for now. But the Ravens have also been struggling lately, including a loss on Monday Night Football to the previously winless Chargers. The case here is simple enough to make. This team isn't the same without starting quarterback Lamar Jackson, and it's looking unlikely that he'll return from injury quickly enough to save Baltimore's season.

If they can gut out some tough wins with the recently acquired Jacoby Brissett at quarterback, and then beat this Steelers team twice, Baltimore might sneak in. But their remaining schedule isn't an easy one: Carolina, Houston, Tampa Bay, Pittsburgh, Cleveland, Cincinnati, Pittsburgh. At 4-6, they have little margin for error, and they haven't scored more than 20 points in a game since Oct. 11.

I'm not one to count the Ravens out until they're officially out of things, but also looking at the rest of the AFC playoff hopefuls, it's hard to say Baltimore without Lamar isn't the best possible matchup. Still, it'd be an unlikely one, as they're most likely to steal a spot from a fellow AFC North team. I don't see Cincinnati falling out of the race if they remain healthy. And even if the Steelers rebound and somehow win the AFC North, they'd most likely be the fourth seed and would play the fifth. The current fifth seed, Denver, is 7-2. Hard to see this Ravens squad getting that high, so this is likely a moot point.

Also on playoff bubble: Patriots (5-5; No. 8 seed; 36% playoff chance); Raiders (4-6; No. 9 seed; 17% playoff chance).
