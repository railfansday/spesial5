Raiders’ special teams can ill afford adding to the team’s overall woes

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Considering the team's defense ranks dead last in points allowed and yielding the second-worst amount of yards allowed, the Las Vegas Raiders special teams can ill affording adding to the overall woes.

Field position comes at a premium in the NFL and for the Silver & Black, a disadvantage of that nature puts Rob Leonard's defense in dire straits. Which makes the pair of times Raiders punter AJ Cole III's boots were blocked by the Denver Broncos in the Week 11 38-24 loss a cause for concern.

"I have to watch the tape," Las Vegas head coach Klint Kubiak said when asked about the punt protection in the postgame press conference.

"Two punts were huge. They were big," Denver head coach Sean Payton said after the game. "One of them I know was a rush. When you look at what that does field position-wise and just say it's a 40-yard swing, it's almost like a turnover."

Even a day later, when the NFL statisticians officially marked the two punts as deflected and not blocked — due to the ball crossing the line of scrimmage — a sour taste remains in the Raiders special teams room.

The first deflection occurred in the opening quarter when Denver's Sai'Vion Jones bull rushed up the middle and changed the trajectory of Cole's boot with his forearm. The ball eventually settled two yards past the line of scrimmage. The second occurred a quarter later with Dondrea Tillman tipping Cole's punt limiting the boot to just 27 yards.

It was indeed a forgettable Mile High outing for not only Cole, but special teams coordinator Joe DeCamillis.

Shoring up the blocking and protection assignments on punts is a must as the Raiders' Week 12 matchup with the Cleveland Browns was flexed to the Sunday night primetime slot instead of the standard afternoon affair it was originally was.

Everything gets amplified under the lights in front of a national audience and repeating the lack of protection which resulted in Cole's normally booming punts ricocheting off a defender's appendage isn't ideal.

With 40 punts on the season, Cole is ranked 12th amongst his punting brethren in the league. The 2,029 yards his kicking foot has amassed ranks ninth while his 50.7 yards per punt average is third-best in the NFL. Cole's 43.8 net yards per punt — average that subtracts return and touchback yards — is seventh-best as the league average on average yards per boot and net yards per punt are 47.7 and 42.2, respectively.

The 31-year-old punter has four touchbacks which puts him at fifth-most and with 15 of his boots landing inside or being downed in the opponent's 20-yard line puts him right in the middle at 16th overall. That's the area where Cole is under the league average of 39.9 percent of kicks pinning opponents deep with his being 37.5 percent.

In comparison, the Philadelphia Eagles' Braden Mann leads the league in punts (53) and yardage (2,566) and he also owns the longest boot of the season with an 86-yard blast. The New York Giants' Jordan Stout tops the yards per punt average at 51.7 — the Kansas City Chiefs' Matt Araiza is second at 51.0 — while the G-Men's boot specialist is atop the league with a 44.7 net yards average per boot. Mann also leads the NFL with 24 of his 53 punts pinning opponents within their own 20-yard line.

Place kicking-wise, Matt Gay has certainly rekindled the accurate and dependable ways with DeCamillis in Las Vegas, much like the duo had during their respective time with the Los Angeles Rams.

As noted in the tweet above, Gay's two best seasons in his eight seasons in the NFL were with DeCamillis in Los Angeles. The initial skepticism of Gay being a productive replacement for long time kicker Daniel Carlson have been certainly allayed.

At 22-of-24 on field goals this season, the 32-year-old kicker has the sixth-best accuracy percentage at 91.7 percent. Gay's long of 59 yards gives him the sixth-longest boot amongst his contemporaries and going 25-for-25 on extra points gives him the third-best percentage in that department.

Gay's misses this season were from 49 yards and 51 yards out — both in the month of October. The first occurred in Week 5 against the New England Patriots (Gay finish 1-for-2 on field goals that game) and the second a Week 7 miss against the Rams (he finished 3-for-4 that day).

With the 22 field goals and 25 extra points made so far during the Raiders' 2026 campaign, Gay (unsurprisingly) leads the team in points scored at 91.

For comparison's sake, there's only one kicker who hasn't missed a field goal attempt this season: The Tampa Bay Buccaneers' Chase McLaughlin who is 22-for-22. A total of 12 kickers are perfect on extra points heading into Week 12 action. The NFL average for field goal percentage is 84.7 percent while it's 95.5 percent on extra points.

Return game-wise, Las Vegas boasts one of the top kick returners in running back Dylan Laube.

The 27-year-old reserve tailback is one of two return men in the league to house a kickoff. The 95-yard burst Laube had against the New York Jets in the Raiders' Week 8 38-33 victory was the first end zone visit on a kick return this season. Baltimore Ravens wide receiver Devontez Walker housed a kickoff (86-yard gallop) in Week 11.

Laube currently ranks second in average yards per return at 29.9. He trails Buffalo Bills running back Ray Davis (30.1 average) in that department. In terms of total kick return yards, Laube is third at a tally of 956. Tennessee Titans wideout Chimere Dike and Cincinnati Bengals wide receiver Dohnte Meyers are ahead at 1,112 and 968 yards, respectively.

The Raiders punt return game hasn't been as explosive.

Rookie wide receiver Malik Benson has seven returns for 94 yards while veteran wideout Deven Thompkins has one for 18 yards and Laube has two for 13 yards. Las Vegas is more apt to signal fair catch than return punts.
