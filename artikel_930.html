Pietersen fires England up ahead of ODI decider in Australia

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Gus Atkinson says it was "nice" to see Australia fans leave early as England levelled three-match ODI series with emphatic victory at Adelaide Oval on Sunday; Atkinson took 4-28 as hosts rolled for 220; Wednesday's final fixture in Hobart (3.30am start UK time) now winner-takes-all

Kevin Pietersen has fired England up ahead of the ODI series decider against Australia in Hobart on Wednesday, according to fast bowler Gus Atkinson.

England batting great Pietersen is now a white-ball mentor with the side in a role that will run up to and including the 50-over World Cup in Africa in late 2027.

The 46-year-old will not be with the team for the winner-takes-all ODI in Tasmania, having flown to India for a YouTube conference, but gave his players some words to ponder before departing.

"KP said that winning in Australia is the best thing you can have as an England cricketer," Atkinson told reporters, with Pietersen having been part of the touring side to win the Ashes down under in 2010-11 under the captaincy of Sir Andrew Strauss.

Atkinson impressed as England levelled the ODI series in Adelaide on Sunday, bagging 4-28 as Australia were rolled for 220 chasing 356.

Three of his wickets came inside the first seven overs as the home side slumped to 29-4 during a 135-run hammering.

The Surrey quick said, after the local fans drifted away long before their team's defeat was complete, that it was nice (to see their supporters leaving).

"I said to Harry Brook at mid-off, 'listen, it's so quiet now'. It was really nice. We copped it a bit during the [2025-26] Ashes, so to come here and win was great.

England bounced back emphatically from their seven-wicket drubbing in Perth two days earlier, posting 355-6 - their highest ODI score in Australia - thanks largely to Tom Banton's 121 from 99 balls before dismissing their hosts in 44.3 overs.

Atkinson added: "The first game didn't go to plan but to come back and have the series on the line in Hobart is very exciting. If we can come away with it, that would be fantastic."

Banton's century was his sixth fifty-plus score in nine innings (two hundreds, four half-centuries) since replacing the injured Jacob Bethell at the top of the ODI batting order, during which time the Somerset man has hit 540 runs at an average of 60.

The 28-year-old said, when asked whether he felt comfortable in his spot: "I guess so, but I'm not thinking too much about it - that's for other people to do.

"I'm just trying to do as well as I can and score as many runs as I can for England. That question, and the answer, is for the people with higher power. I'm just focusing on the game."

Australia are likely to bring young pace prospect Mahli Beardman back into their XI for the Hobart decider (3.30am UK time on Wednesday).

The 21-year-old took 6-24 at Optus Stadium last week but was then rested in Adelaide, with Riley Meredith coming into the side in his place.

ODIs 3.30am UK time, with T20s 8.15am UK time
