Meet the Grammys 2027 Best New Artist Nominees: Ella Langley and More

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Ella Langley, Sienna Spiro and more stars were nominated for Best New Artist at the 2027 Grammy Awards. Keep reading for all the musicians competing in the category.

The nominations for the 2027 Grammy Awards are officially in—and it’s safe to say they’re pitch perfect.

Though the Recording Academy announced all the nominees for the event on Nov. 16, one category in particular had music lovers everywhere holding their breath. After all, the event’s Best New Artist category is known to catapult budding stars into full-blown music legends.

As for who will be competing for the honor this time around?

Well, Ella Langley and Angine De Poitrine are just two of the artists being recognized for making sound waves this year. Meanwhile, Geese, Slayyter and Sienna Spiro also made the cut.

And rounding out the category, the noms of Don Toliver, Remi Wolf and The Womack Sisters proved they also always strike the right chord.

But no matter who takes home the award, they’ll be joining the likes of music royalty.

Take, for instance, The Beatles, who may have brought the sun to the 1965 Grammys, but left with something even brighter: the award for Best New Artist. Then there was Lauryn Hill who doo wopped her way to the top when she received the honor in 1999.

And who could forget Amy Winehouse’s 2008 win where she proved she certainly was good at her craft?

And joining those ranks could be Langley, whose “Choosin’ Texas” surpassed Mariah Carey’s “All I Want For Christmas Is You” with the longest run at the No. 1 spot on the Billboard Hot 100 list earlier this year.

While Carey’s holiday hit has long held the record at 22 weeks, Langley’s breakup song hit 23 weeks in the top spot this past September, an achievement the 27-year-old still can’t quite wrap her head around.

“It’s changing my life,” Langley said in a Sept. 22 Instagram video. “It’s changing the lives of the writers of this song. It’s changing the lives of my family, and it is truly growing an atmosphere for something I’m going to be able to do the rest of my life.”

It’s no surprise that Spiro is willing to die on the hill that music is her whole life. In fact, she began writing her own songs at age 10, drawing inspiration from music legends she heard growing up.

“I’ve been singing since I could talk, like doing nursery rhymes,” she told Interview Magazine in March. “When I was super young, Frank Sinatra, Nina [Simone], Etta James, Dinah Washington, Barbara Streisand, Al Green—all the greats were played in my house.”

“That was my pop music,” she added, “and I used to try and emulate the way they would sing.”

And that’s exactly what she’s done. After all, the British singer’s 2025 single “Die on This Hill” garnered global attention before her debut album Visitor earned her first UK number-one single with the track "Great Expectation.”

As she put it, “This is all I ever want to do.”

The Indie rock band first took flight in 2016 when childhood friends Cameron Winter, Emily Green, Max Bassin and Dominic Digesu formed the group in Brooklyn, New York. (Foster Hudson was also a member of the group from 2019 to 2023)

Getting their footing as a band in a Fort Greene basement studio they called "The Nest,” they officially established their place in the musical pecking order in 2021, when they released their debut album Projector.

Since then, they released two more albums—3D Country and Getting Killed—and even made their Saturday Night Live debut in February 2026.

But even with their soaring success, they’re making sure to keep both feet on the ground.

"None of us are changing as people,” the band told the BBC in January. “We all have the same mindset and are just super excited that other people like what we like.”

Langley may be a breakout country star today, but the 27-year-old once thought about choosin’ a different career path.

Hailing from Alabama, Langley dropped out of Auburn University at age 20, where she was pursuing a degree in forestry, to move to Nashville to make her dream of becoming a country music star come true.

After her song “If You Have To” went viral on TikTok, she debuted at the Grand Ole Opry in 2023 and signed her first record deal. And the following year, she released two chart-topping hits with Riley Green: “Don’t Mind If I Do” and “You Look Like You Love Me.”

But it was her 2025 hit “Choosin’ Texas,” which dethroned Mariah Carey’s “All I Want for Christmas” for the longest-running No. 1 single in Billboard Hot 100 history, that changed her life.

“I’m really excited to just experience a moment like this in my life,” she said of the achievement on Instagram Sept. 22. “Just thank you so much. ‘Choosin’ T’… Wow, that’s the one, huh?”

Slayyyter (born Catherine Grace Garner) will be wearing her brand new Chanels to the 2027 Grammy Awards. After dropping out of the University of Missouri to pursue music in 2016, she began recording music in her mom’s closet.

But her career took off after a preview of her 2019 single “Mine” went viral on social media, which caught the attention of a talent manger. Later that year, she released her self-titled debut mixtape.

Then, 10 years after she started her music career, she found even more success with hits like “Dance…” and “Brand New Chanels.”

“That people have responded to the music in this way means I can have more creative freedom to make what I want to make,” she told The Hollywood Reporter of her 2026 album WOR$T GIRL IN AMERICA in October. “This whole project has just been about that and doing things yourself and in the way you want to do them.”

Though the powerful trio—daughters of legendary married duo Linda Womack and Cecil Womack and nieces to Bobby Womack—made their recording debut in 1993, they made a splash on the R&B scene when they released their self-titled debut album in August.

“Our brand of soul, we got straight from the family tree,” BG Womack recently told People. “[We’ll be] keeping the word out that they were here, and they made this great music that should never be forgotten.”

Don Toliver (born Caleb Toliver) has been in the music game for quite some time, first experimenting with his sounds as a teenager using a PlayStation Eye camera over YouTube instrumentals.

Though the 32-year-old released his independent mixtape Donny Womack in 2018, his career really took off after hopping on fellow Houston-native Travis Scott’s “Can’t Stay” that same year.

Shortly after the feature on Scott’s Astroworld album, he signed with the “Sicko Mode” hitmaker’s Cactus Jack Records in a joint venture with Atlantic Records, which skyrocketed Toliver’s success to new heights following the release of his hit single "No Idea.”

But even with five studio albums under his belt—the most recent being Octane released in January 2026—his sound is ever evolving.

“All of my favorite things collide on this album. It’s an extension of me being a motorhead and loving all of that s--t. I love cars, boats, jets, all of it,” Toliver told Billboard in March. “It’s me fleshing out my passions and things I grew up loving and giving it to the world through my eyes.”

The former American Idol contestant probably will cry after earning a nod in the category this year, considering she dropped her third studio album Mud in October, exactly seven years after releasing her solo debut.

For the “I Won’t Cry” artist, her latest body of work stands out as her personal yet.

“I wanted to write songs about the way I was feeling, and I needed to heal,” the California native told NME. “I wanted to do that surrounded by people that felt like home to me, because I’m happiest when I’m with my friends, playing guitar and singing together. Luckily, a lot of my friends are also incredible musicians."

Hailing from Quebec, the experimental rock duo—known for sporting black and white polka dot costumes from head-to-toe—has been one to watch, as they’ve made waves by combining experimental rock with performance art and microtonal music.

And though their career has been ascending since earlier this year, the group—formed in 2019—are just focused on hitting the right notes naturally.

“We haven't cracked any code, man,” Khn de Poitrine explained to CBC in July. “We just have been doing this for a very long time, and it just happened that this one thing speaks to a lot of people, but we are not masterminds.”
