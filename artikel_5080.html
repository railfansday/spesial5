Why Women Over 50 Should Add This Underrated Carry to Their Strength Routine

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

“Many people think core training means crunches or planks, but one of the most functional ways to strengthen the core is by learning to resist movement,” says personal trainer Alice Jordan.

Her go-to exercise for doing exactly that? The kettlebell suitcase carry.

“Holding a kettlebell in one hand and walking with good posture forces the deep abdominal muscles, obliques, and spinal stabilizers to work continuously to keep your body upright,” explains Jordan, who specializes in strength training during perimenopause.

“For women over 50, this is particularly valuable because it develops balance, posture and stability while also improving grip strength, which is an important marker of healthy aging,” notes Jordan.

Indeed, one landmark study published in The Lancet found that lower grip strength was consistently associated with a higher risk of early death—even after accounting for age, physical activity, smoking, body size, and other cardiovascular risk factors. Notably, the researchers reported that grip strength predicted somebody’s risk of dying better than systolic blood pressure.

The single-arm kettlebell suitcase carry, adds Jordan, “also translates directly into everyday activities such as carrying shopping, lifting luggage, or playing with grandchildren, making it a highly functional exercise.”

Below, Jordan explains how to do the exercise effectively and safely.

“Aim to include the exercise 2-3 times per week as part of a strength training program. It’s particularly effective when performed after your main strength exercises or as part of your core training,” adds Jordan.

As for how heavy a weight you should use for this exercise, Jordan recommends “choosing a kettlebell that feels challenging while still allowing you to maintain perfect posture throughout the walk.”

“You should be able to resist leaning to one side, keep your shoulders level and your core engaged from start to finish,” she adds.

“As a general guide, beginners might start with around 15-20 lb, while those with more strength training experience may use 20-30 lb or heavier, depending on their ability.”
