Netflix’s 2-Season R-Rated Fantasy Series Is A Masterclass In Worldbuilding

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Vanessa Piña has been writing as a journalist for over two years. As a senior writer, she focuses on timely coverage of the latest anime releases and popular comic franchises.

Worldbuilding is an essential part of any fantasy series, as a well-defined setting helps viewers understand a society's magical elements, supernatural creatures, and physical laws, allowing them to become immersed in the story. Striking the right balance is, in itself, a difficult task, since although some entries in the genre offer a detailed exposition of their settings, few series manage to integrate their fantastical elements in an original way. However, one underrated Netflix series has one of streaming's most unconventional and twisted magic worlds.

Dorohedoro is what happens when the darker elements of fantasy storytelling are pushed to a whole new level, resulting in an extremely quirky and fascinating journey. Rated TV-MA on Netflix, which makes sense, given how explosively violent it is, Dorohedoro doesn’t use disturbing imagery merely for shock value; instead, its gore serves to illustrate the social structure and the internal logic of its universe.

Released in 2020 by MAPPA, the studio behind hit series such as Jujutsu Kaisen and Attack on Titan, Dorohedoro features one of the most distinctive worlds in anime, characterized by an industrial aesthetic and a chaotic society that can only be brought to the screen through animation. The series is based on the seinen manga by Q Hayashida, which is aimed at adult readers and widely considered a masterpiece within this demographic, making it an essential work for any fan of dark fantasy.

Scrolling through Netflix's catalog, it is easy to find fantasy series that focus on hard worldbuilding, which prioritizes establishing complex rules to explain their magical elements. In this regard, Dorohedoro is one of the few series that successfully employs soft worldbuilding by avoiding exhausting lore dumps and over-explaining its mechanics, instead throwing viewers in a disordered dystopian setting that aligns with the story's tone.

The series follows Caiman, a man whose head has been magically transformed into that of a lizard and lost his memory. Armed only with a human head, which mysteriously sits inside his mouth and serves as a means of identification, and accompanied by his friend Nikaido, Caiman is set to find the sorcerer who cursed him, seeking both revenge and the truth about his identity. That way, Dorohedoro creates a unique atmosphere where viewers are just as confused as Caiman, using each bit of information provided to gradually piece together how the world and the magic of the sorcerers function.

That said, what makes Dorohedoro truly brilliant is the stark contrast between its two settings: the Sorcerers' World and The Hole, the latter being the desolate place where Caiman and Nikaido reside and where sorcerers ruthlessly harm its inhabitants to practice magic. The result is a world that feels alive in every respect, from the dynamics between those who live in The Hole and the sorcerers to the characters' side jobs outside acting as sorcerer hunters and the creative ways the series illustrates the consequences of using magic, as seen in the annual event "The Night of the Living Dead."

Beyond its world-building and the intriguing mystery surrounding Caiman’s true identity, the charm of Dorohedoro is that it's a rather touching series. Even with its body horror, brutal killings, and abundance of gore, the anime is heartwarming at its core, incorporating dark comedy and lighthearted moments of camaraderie, showing the small joys in life such as sharing a meal. This makes the 23 episodes of Dorohedoro, spread across two seasons, particularly easy to binge-watch.

The best part is that Dorohedoro still has plenty of story left to tell. With the anime having adapted around 80 chapters, it has reached roughly the halfway point of the manga. Fortunately, Dorohedoro Season 3 has already been confirmed.

Fans of Chainsaw Man and Berserk absolutely need to check out this dark fantasy masterpiece anime that just got renewed for a third season.
