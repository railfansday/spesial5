Robert Pattinson As James Bond? Baz Bamigboye Has Some Thoughts

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

A wise movie-world person of my acquaintance who has resided in these British isles long enough to understand the customs of the country shook their head when they heard that Robert Pattinson had been officially cast to play Ian Fleming’s James Bond in the 007 reboot that Denis Villeneuve is in pre-production to direct.

They intoned: “Bond is mysterious. Bond creates the stars. The stars don’t create Bond,” then added for good measure, “For f*ck’s sake!”

While I don’t think it’s the right thing for reinventing Bond, my sense is that Pattinson will be good, though I don’t think it’s hardly fresh to cast a 40-year-old movie star. Watch on Deadline

The Twilight movies catapulted Pattinson to superstardom, then he spent years shunning the limelight they brought him, becoming for a while David Cronenberg’s darling in Cosmopolis and Map of the Stars.

Then he shot Robert Eggers’ sublime The Lighthouse in 2019 opposite Willem Dafoe, and that was the moment I saw him in, forgive me, a different light, and began taking him seriously as an actor.

Christopher Nolan took him under his wing for Tenet and this year’s The Odyssey — which he stole, along with Samantha Morton and John Leguizamo. Those three provided the heat in that epic. I made myself sick popping popcorn (so bad for my delicate teeth) when they weren’t on the ginormous Imax screen.

Pattinson was at an FYC screening of Primetime on Sunday night, but he scooted away like a bat out of …you know, because he had an early call for The Batman: Part II. He’s great as the Caped Crusader, but I feel Batman baggage will color how we see him as Bond.

Fleming described his creation as a “blunt instrument,” and I just can’t see how that will come across here.

Robert Wade and Neil Purvis, who wrote for several Bond pictures under the reign of Barbara Broccoli and Michael G. Wilson, took a different tack. When I spoke to them Monday night in London they told Deadline that they reckon R Patz — as Pattinson fans call him — is “a terrific choice, very exciting.”

They suggested, correctly in my ever-so-humble view, that the classically trained Daniel Craig, last of 007’s parish, “is a hard act to follow in terms of chops.”

They added: “But Robert has what it takes to — we liked the sly wit he brought to the spy in Tenet, and with this decision it’s clear Denis wants to keep Bond cool.”

See, while I agree with much of what they said, I’m kinda shaken but not at all stirred by Pattinson’s casting.

Perhaps it was misdirection, and probably partly because I wanted it to be true, but I brought into this notion that Bond 26 would be cast with a relative unknown. Remember that Craig was not a big name when Broccoli and Wilson chose him for Bond. Sure, he was well admired and well known to London theatregoers, and we all marveled at his performances in the likes of Our Friends in the North when it hit BBC screens in 1996 and movies such as The Mother, Sylvia, Munich and Layer Cake. But I’d argue that he wasn’t then a bona fide star; rather, he was on the cusp of becoming one.

Let’s not forget that director Martin Campbell loathed the idea of casting him as Bond in Casino Royale. He and others had hated Craig’s Bond screen-test.

Campbell wanted to go in a different direction, but word started filtering out from the set of Matthew Vaughn’s Layer Cake that, hey, Craig’s holding this movie up. A rough cut was screened for them, and Broccoli’s eyes lit up. By the way, she’d always admired his stage work, and with Campbell’s agreement, a second screen test was arranged — and, well, we know the rest.

I checked in with Vaughn, The Kingsman filmmaker, and he just laughed over the phone.

“When I last checked Bond is dead. Long live The Kingsman,” he said before politely putting the phone down on me.

And, clearly, none of the other names who underwent screen tests was right — and from what I can fathom, Amazon and all concerned panicked. The panic was at such a level that Villeneuve asked to see what Pattinson could do with the From Russia with Love test that all Bond contenders must do.

Passing that test, where actors have to perform a scene from the 1963 film that starred Sean Connery, is bloody hard to pull off. It’s a very unnatural environment doing it from that movie.

Look, that’s my take. Congrats to Pattinson. I know Villeneuve, who has long dreamed of making a Bond movie, has the cinematic vision to prove me totally wrong about R Patz. After all, he cast him in the new Dune — can’t wait to see that!

So, for now, I’ll hold off from putting the Curse of Baz upon Bond 26.
