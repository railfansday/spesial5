Salesforce Pays $5.66B for Braze, Buying the Rival That Outran Marketing Cloud

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

On Tuesday, October 20, 2026, Salesforce signed a definitive agreement to acquire Braze, the New York customer engagement platform, for about $5.66 billion in enterprise value. Braze Class A holders get $47.50 in cash per share, a roughly 58% premium to the prior close of $30.04. The deal is expected to close in Salesforce's fiscal 2028, pending Braze shareholder and regulatory approval, with "no anticipated change to Salesforce's FY27 guidance."

This is no sleepy tuck-in. Salesforce is buying the real-time, event-driven engagement layer that Marketing Cloud has long been accused of lacking, and removing a competitor that was winning brands that outgrew or fled its ecosystem. The market reaction captured the tension: Braze rose 51% and Salesforce fell 3% as investors weighed the cash against the logic.

Marketing Cloud has been a soft spot. Specialized rivals and the rise of mobile-first, high-volume consumer apps left Salesforce playing catch-up, and Braze was built for exactly that world. Salesforce itself describes Braze as "optimized for high-volume, real-time B2C engagement."

"Every interaction is an opportunity to wow your customers," said Patrick Stokes, Salesforce's President of Applications and Marketing. "Braze was built to turn live customer behavior into personalized engagement at scale." The plan is to fold Braze's real-time engine and AI decisioning into all four layers of Salesforce's architecture: Data 360, Customer 360, Agentforce and SIForce.

The deal fits a pattern. Salesforce bought Fin, formerly Intercom, for about $3.6 billion earlier in 2026 to bolster agentic customer service, and closed its roughly $8 billion Informatica deal in 2025 for data. Reuters framed Braze as "the latest bet by Salesforce to accelerate its AI transition" as investors worry AI could erode demand for traditional software. Both companies tout an "open, composable approach" that should ease integration in theory. Salesforce's integration history, from Slack onward, suggests theory and practice do not always align.

At $47.50, Salesforce is paying just over six times Braze's projected annual sales. Evercore ISI's Kirk Materne said the size of the premium "seems a bit odd unless it was competitive", while granting that "the deal logic makes sense." Piper Sandler's Billy Fitzsimmons downgraded Braze to Neutral, lifted his target to $47.50, and framed the deal as Salesforce removing a growing competitor to Marketing Cloud.

The downgrades piled up as analysts moved to the deal price: BTIG, Piper Sandler, Wells Fargo and Oppenheimer all cut ratings, and Loop Capital followed. Jefferies argued the acquisition "re-energizes Marketing & Commerce Cloud". Rival Klaviyo jumped more than 12% as the market priced in either more consolidation or validation of the category.

Braze serves about 2,770 customers, including Canva, Dairy Queen, Equinox, Instacart, Urban Outfitters and Venmo. For years, a core Braze sales motion was positioning itself as the modern alternative to Marketing Cloud: real-time, mobile-native, lighter. RepVue's Adam Little noted that Braze reps ranked Salesforce as one of the competitors they faced most often in head-to-head deals. Customers who chose Braze to escape the Salesforce orbit now face being pulled back in.

Longtime Marketing Cloud users may read the deal as Salesforce conceding that its own platform never fully adapted to the real-time, mobile-first era. Markup AI's Holly Enneking put the open question this way: "whether Salesforce can preserve the focus that made Braze competitive."

A fiscal 2028 close gives both companies time, and gives competitors time to respond. Integration is the real test. The open, composable architecture is a good sign, but cultural fit, product roadmaps and keeping Braze's engineers will matter more than press-release language about "wow."

For the wider software market, the deal adds a data point to the AI consolidation thesis: specialized platforms that own a critical workflow layer stay valuable even when public multiples compress. Salesforce's stock slide also shows investors no longer write blank checks for AI-adjacent purchases. The premium has to show up in growth, margins or both.

Braze customers will watch for signals on product independence, pricing and roadmap continuity. Marketing Cloud customers will watch to see whether Braze's technology lands in the product they use daily. And the rest of the MarTech landscape will recalibrate: if the "better Salesforce" can be bought, the incentives for the next generation of engagement platforms just changed.

The deal is logical. Whether it is wise will show up in the product that ships two years from now, not in today's stock chart.
