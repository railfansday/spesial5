TikTok Shop Is Changing the Relationship Between Brands and Content Creators

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Image:-By Aidan Castillo, VP of Client Success at MediaLabs

The traditional consensus around influencer marketing primarily treated it as a means of generating awareness. Pay creators to introduce products to their audiences, hoping that attention eventually translates into sales.

Influencer marketing is now becoming a bottom-of-funnel channel, and TikTok Shop is substantially driving that evolution.

By integrating commerce directly into the content experience, the platform has made it possible for creators to influence purchasing decisions and facilitate transactions without directing consumers to external websites.

For brands, the implications extend well beyond another digital sales channel.

In my role as VP of Client Success at MediaLabs, I work with consumer brands developing their TikTok Shop strategies and the creators responsible for executing them.

One of the most significant changes I've observed is the growing importance of creators who aren't necessarily traditional influencers.

Historically, a creator's commercial value was closely tied to the size of their audience. Brands paid premiums for reach, even when the relationship between impressions and actual purchases was difficult to measure.

The idea of genuinely social commerce introduces a different set of considerations.

A creator with a relatively modest following can produce a video that directly generates substantial sales. Conversely, an established influencer with millions of followers isn't necessarily as readily equipped to sell a singular product directly.

The distinction matters because content performance and commercial performance are not interchangeable.

This changes how brands approach creator partnerships.

Rather than concentrating their budgets on a handful of expensive sponsorships, companies can work with larger networks of creators, test different approaches and identify which content consistently generates purchases.

At MediaLabs, our network includes more than 3,000 creators producing content for consumer brands.

During the first quarter of 2026, creators across our network generated approximately $19.35 million in gross merchandise value for participating brands. We also launched 29 creator retainer campaigns during that period.

These figures illustrate the scale at which creator-led commerce can operate, but they also point to an important operational challenge.

Managing a few influencer relationships is relatively straightforward. Coordinating hundreds of creators, maintaining content quality, evaluating performance and ensuring brands have consistent support requires considerably more infrastructure.

Training, creator selection, campaign management and ongoing communication become as important as the content itself.

There's also a meaningful change taking place on the creator side.

TikTok Shop has created opportunities for individuals to generate income through product recommendations without first establishing themselves as recognizable internet personalities.

That doesn't mean earning a sustainable income is easy. Competition is substantial, content performance varies, and creators must continually adapt to changing consumer preferences.

But the commercial opportunity is no longer reserved exclusively for people with enormous audiences.

As social commerce develops, I expect brands to place greater emphasis on measurable sales performance, repeatable creator partnerships and the systems required to manage them.

The companies that understand this distinction will be better positioned to evaluate TikTok Shop as a serious component of their broader e-commerce operations, rather than simply another outlet for influencer marketing.
