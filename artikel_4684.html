5 Ways to Feel Happier Today

Your body and brain work together to shape how you feel. Although happiness is influenced by many factors, certain brain chemicals play important roles in pleasure, motivation, connection, and well-being. Four of these are dopamine, serotonin, oxytocin, and endorphins.

Here are five healthy actions you can take today that can trigger the brain's feel-good chemistry and help you feel happier:

Doing something new engages your mind in an entirely different way from how it works when you’re doing the same old activities day after day. Your brain loves a challenge.

When you’re feeling low, pick something you’ve never done before that sounds like fun, and give it a go. Bake a cake. Take a yoga class. Go on a hike. Put up a tent—and then sleep in it. Create a work of art. Do a headstand. Rearrange your room.

Novelty can stimulate the release of dopamine, a chemical involved in motivation and reward, which may be one reason new experiences feel energizing. It doesn’t even matter if you aren’t successful (like if your cake comes out salty or your tent collapses). The happiness may even come from getting out of your comfort zone.

One of the easiest shortcuts to feeling happier is helping someone else feel good. Give a genuine and unexpected compliment. Shovel snow off a neighbor’s sidewalk without being asked. Help an elderly person across the street or set up their cellphone. Plan a meal for your family and prepare it. Say thank you, sincerely and often.

When you do something kind for another person, you strengthen your connection with them. Such positive social interactions can engage your brain's reward systems, including those involving dopamine, and may also involve oxytocin, which plays an important role in social bonding.

Helping someone else can therefore feel good for both of you.

When two people shake hands or share a welcomed hug, a connection is made that’s more than skin-deep. In each of their brains, oxytocin can be released, which helps create a bond between them and feelings of comfort and safety.

If you don’t have anyone with whom you want to hug or hold hands at the moment, you can experience the effects of oxytocin by nuzzling a dog or petting a cat. In fact, when you cuddle your pets, their brains release oxytocin so they feel safe and happy, too.

When you step outside, your entire world changes. The light, the temperature, the sounds you hear, the colors, even the qualities of the air perk up your senses. Each of these gives your brain a boost and can improve your mood.

An instant happiness boost can come from sunshine. Natural light helps regulate your body's internal clock and can influence mood-regulating brain systems, including those involving serotonin. For some people, a light therapy lamp (not a tanning lamp) can also help, particularly during months when there is less daylight.

Exercise has the potential to trigger many feel-good chemicals. You don’t have to be an athlete. You don’t have to take a class. You just need to find a physical outlet that feels right to your body and then make time for it.

A few possibilities: yoga; jumping jacks; a competitive or not-so-competitive sports team; hiking in beautiful places.

As an extra bonus, when you move your body, some of the chemicals that can be released are endorphins, which ease pain and stress.

You don't have to wait for happiness to find you. By doing new things, being kind to others, connecting with people and pets, spending time outdoors, and moving your body, you can encourage positive feelings. These activities won't make every problem disappear, but they can give you energy and better perspective to help deal with your troubles.
