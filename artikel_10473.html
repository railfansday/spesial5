Nvidia stock has plunged 17.5% in 5 days. A new lawsuit adds to the chaos

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Late Monday afternoon, a shareholder filed a lawsuit against Nvidia and its CEO amid the fallout of the Chinese spy scandal that has engulfed Silicon Valley and, more recently, Washington, D.C. The suit accuses the company and CEO Jensen Huang of misleading and deceiving investors about the scandal, which may have been an act of corporate espionage and an attempt by China to access Nvidia’s technology.

As more has come to light, the lawsuit says that Jensen Huang was “directly or indirectly involved in drafting, producing, reviewing and/or disseminating her false and misleading statements and information” regarding Huang’s relationship with Alice Huang, “a Megaspeed executive and suspected Chinese spy, who is suspected of seeking to help smuggle Nvidia products into China.” (Though they share the same surname, Jensen and Alice are not related.)

The lawsuit was filed by Mark Kistenmacher, who claims in the filing that he and other investors bought Nvidia shares at “artificially inflated” prices—something they may not have done had they known the full story. According to the complaint, “As a result of Defendants’ wrongful acts and omissions, and the precipitous decline in the market value of the Company’s securities, Plaintiff and other Class members have suffered significant losses and damages.”

In effect, shareholders believe that they were misled by the company, which knew there was something damaging waiting in the wings and didn’t disclose it. Kistenmacher is seeking unspecified damages as a result of the lawsuit, and a jury trial. 

Fast Company has reached out to Nvidia for comment.

Nvidia shares were down around 6% on Tuesday as of 11 a.m. ET, and are down 17.5% over the past five days. In all, that amounts to a loss of almost $700 billion, though the company remains valued at over $4 trillion.

On November 8, Jensen Huang published an open letter on Nvidia’s website, offering an apology to his family and the company and admitting to the affair, while also noting that the company’s board of directors was also investigating the matter. 

“I support the investigation and will cooperate fully. I love our country and our company. I am committed to protecting Nvidia and supporting America’s national security,” he wrote. “I am proud and grateful to be an American.”
