Artifact Spotlight: A Morning Suit That Made History

The formality of the Supreme Court is unmistakable, from the marble columns to the burgundy velvet curtains, every detail reinforces the gravity of the institution. But few traditions capture this sense of dignity quite like the morning suit worn by the Solicitor General when arguing before the nation's highest court.

We are proud to spotlight a significant addition to our collection: a morning suit worn by Solicitor General Noel J. Francisco during his arguments on behalf of the United States government before the Supreme Court. This elegant ensemble represents far more than formal attire, it embodies a living tradition that connects today's legal practice to centuries of courtroom history.

Until the 1970s, morning dress was required of all attorneys appearing before the Supreme Court. The traditional outfit consists of a black or charcoal tailcoat, striped trousers, a waistcoat, and a wing-collar shirt, attire that originated in 19th-century formal daywear and gradually became reserved for the most ceremonial occasions.

While the Court relaxed this requirement for most attorneys decades ago, the Office of the Solicitor General has largely maintained the tradition. Male Solicitors General and deputies continue to wear morning dress when arguing before the Court. The tradition evolved in 2009 when Elena Kagan became the first female Solicitor General and, after consulting with the justices, chose to wear dark suits instead of morning coats. Following her precedent, the office established a policy making morning dress optional for female attorneys while male lawyers continue the tradition. This adaptation demonstrates how customs can evolve while still symbolizing formality and profound respect for the Court and the weighty constitutional questions at stake.

Often called "the Tenth Justice," the Solicitor General occupies a distinctive position in American government. This office determines which cases the federal government will ask the Supreme Court to review, represents the United States in cases before the Court, and serves as a bridge between the executive and judicial branches.

The morning suit serves as a visual reminder of this special relationship. When the Solicitor General stands before the justices in traditional dress, it acknowledges the Court's unique authority and the solemnity of the moment, moments that often shape the course of American law and society.

Noel J. Francisco served as Solicitor General from 2017 to 2020, arguing numerous significant cases during his tenure. The morning suit he wore during those arguments witnessed constitutional debates, legal interpretations, and decisions that will influence American jurisprudence for generations.

By preserving this artifact, we document not just a piece of clothing, but a tangible connection to our legal heritage. It reminds us that the law is built on traditions, some written in constitutions and statutes, others carried forward through customs like the morning suit that honor the past while serving the present.

The next time you see the Solicitor General arguing before the Supreme Court, take a moment to appreciate the morning suit. It is a quiet but powerful symbol of continuity, respect, and the enduring traditions that help anchor our system of justice.
