Pay Student Loan Interest While in School – Start Repayment Owing Only What You Borrowed

Unsubsidized federal loans and most private student loans charge interest from the day the money is paid out, even while you’re in class. Pay that interest as it builds, in small payments to your loan servicer, and repayment starts from what you borrowed, not from a balance that grew all through school.

Federal student loans for undergraduates require no payments until six months after you leave school, which makes the interest easy to ignore. The Education Department covers it only on subsidized loans, which go to undergraduates with financial need.

Subsidized and unsubsidized loans carry the same fixed rate, 6.52% on new undergraduate loans, according to Federal Student Aid. Say a student borrows $5,500 at the start of each of four years at a round 6.5%, and her first bill comes six months after the fourth year ends. Her interest runs about $30 a month in the first year and about $119 a month by the fourth. Left unpaid, it adds up to about $4,300, so she starts repayment owing about $26,300 on the $22,000 she borrowed.

She owes that $4,300 either way, and carrying it into repayment costs more. Most private loans add unpaid interest to the principal, the amount you’re charged interest on, when repayment starts, the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) says. From then on, it builds interest of its own. Federal loans don’t, but every payment covers unpaid interest before principal, so her $22,000 wouldn’t start shrinking until the $4,300 was paid.

Find your servicer, the company that handles your federal loans’ billing, in your StudentAid.gov account, where one is listed once your first loan is paid out. Set up a monthly payment that matches the interest, or send one payment a term from part-time pay.

A private loan’s lender takes the same payments, since any student loan can be paid early without fees or penalties, the CFPB notes. Even a partial payment cuts the interest you’d carry into repayment, and it’s an easy way for a parent to help.

After graduation, aim any extra payments at principal so they don’t just push back your next due date.

Until then, ask your servicer for the monthly interest on each unsubsidized or private loan and pay that, about $5.40 for every $1,000 borrowed at 6.5%.
