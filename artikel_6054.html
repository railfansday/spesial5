Standing Athwart Pumpkin Tyranny

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Through October and November, this mediocre flavor and scent are in everything everywhere all the time.

A display of Trader Joe’s pumpkin and fall-themed products at a grocery store in Lafayette, Calif., September 25, 2026.

A pumpkin spice latte is on the menu at every coffee shop. Sometimes, the drink is served in a pumpkin-shaped mug. To lure in customers for brunch, quaint cafes serving pumpkin pancakes put a chalkboard sign on the sidewalk saying, “COME IN FOR GOURD VIBRATIONS!” Each bakery has pumpkin scones, pumpkin pie, pumpkin banana bread, and pumpkin coffee cake. An attempt to order an old fashioned will be met with an invitation from the demoralized bartender, “Do you want to try our pumpkin spice old fashioned?” The pen at the bank is topped with a rubber pumpkin; this way, customers know that all the money is kept safe. Little plastic pumpkins are in the windows displays at clothing shops, or beside the cashiers (who work in front of pumpkin-flavored nicotine patches). Squashes are piled high in cardboard bins by the entrance of every grocery store. I reach for what I think is my favorite chai tea blend on the shelves — but alas! It is now pumpkin chai with an orange label.

Through October and November, pumpkin is in everything everywhere all the time. Anything edible is infused, swirled, drizzled, and dusted with pumpkin. You cannot escape the flavor: pumpkin cereal, pumpkin protein powder, pumpkin bagels with pumpkin cream cheese. The Asian aisle has frozen Kung Pao-mpkin Dumplings. Dog treats are flavored like a pumpkin spice latte because everyone must participate, no exceptions. Longstanding favorites are replaced, so French fries are now pumpkin fries. That peculiar squash always sneaks its way into a dish, and so the stuffed raviolis have a little bit of pumpkin. Despite its omnipresence, the pumpkin surprises you: The waiter arrives at the table with a salad that is topped — just for this week — with toasted pumpkin seeds. (Of course, the utensils are wrapped in a flimsy brown napkin dotted with little orange pumpkins.) Even things we thought were reliably mint, like gum and the dentist’s fluoride, are pumpkin spice.
