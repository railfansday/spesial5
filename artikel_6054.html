Republicans hold lone Alaska House seat despite losing Senate, governor races

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

‘Fox News @ Night’ anchor Trace Gallagher reports on Democrat Mary Peltola defeating GOP nominee Dan Sullivan in the Alaska U.S. Senate race, flipping the ninth GOP-held seat.

Rep. Nick Begich, R-Alaska, won re-election to Alaska’s lone House seat Wednesday, surviving a Democratic wave that flipped both the Senate seat and the governor’s mansion.

Begich, who was endorsed by President Donald Trump, defeated independent challenger Bill Hill, a commercial fisherman and retired teacher backed by the Alaska Democratic Party, following the state’s ranked-choice voting tabulation.

Begich’s victory, called at 9:27 p.m. ET on Wednesday – more than two weeks after Election Day – stood in sharp contrast to the rest of Alaska’s top-of-the-ticket results, where voters handed Democrats major breakthroughs despite Trump winning the state by 13% in 2024.

Rep. Nick Begich, R-Alaska, continues to show political strength in the state that has flipped Democratic in this midterm election cycle. (Nathaniel Wilder/Bloomberg via Getty Images)

The race was among the final House contests called nationally after the Nov. 3 general election, offering a rare bright spot for Republicans in a midterm cycle that saw Democrats seize control of both chambers of Congress. Democrats thus far hold a 257-172 majority in the House, having flipped 46 seats to just three for Republicans. There are three races that remain too close to call.

"This process requires engagement, and the perspectives brought forward during the campaign added value for Alaskans as they made important decisions about their vote in this race," Begich said in a statement after his victory.

Hill, who campaigned on lowering costs, curbing big money in politics and pushing back against billionaires, said he called Begich to concede and congratulate him.

"I wished him well as he continues representing Alaska in Congress, because all Alaskans have a stake in his success," Hill said in a statement.

ALASKA DEM PELTOLA DEFEATS SULLIVAN, FLIPPING 9TH GOP SENATE SEAT AFTER 'DECOY DAN' UPROAR

One day before Begich’s race was called, Democrat Mary Peltola unseated second-term Sen. Dan Sullivan, R-Alaska. Peltola — who previously represented Alaska in the U.S. House before losing to Begich in 2024 — became the first Alaska native elected to the Senate and the first Democrat to win a Senate race in the state in nearly two decades. Her victory cemented a 56-44 Democratic Senate majority, the party's largest since 2009.

"As a lifelong Alaskan who has dedicated my career to serving our state, there is no greater honor than having Alaskans’ support in our fight to take on corporate greed, lower costs, and put Alaska first," Peltola wrote on X.

Democrats also secured the Alaska governor’s office for the first time in nearly 30 years, with Democrat Jonathan S. Kreiss-Tomkins winning the open-seat race to succeed term-limited Republican Gov. Mike Dunleavy.

Throughout the House campaign, Begich pitched himself as an effective advocate aligned with Trump’s push to expand oil, gas and resource development in Alaska. But the final stretch of the race was overshadowed by controversy surrounding Democrat Eric Hafner, a perennial candidate with no ties to Alaska who is currently serving a sentence in a federal prison in New York following a 2022 conviction for threatening to kill judges, police officers and others.

Hafner, whose state election booklet biography made no mention of his incarceration, captured nearly 8% of the first-round vote. In the closing weeks of the race, Hill’s campaign distributed stickers and yard signs branding Hafner a "fake ‘Democrat,’" while state party officials spent thousands of dollars on text messages to clarify that Hill was the party’s endorsed candidate after mysterious, unsourced texts circulated promoting Hafner.

Hafner’s mother, Carol "Kitty" Hafner — who also lacks ties to Alaska and finished sixth in the state's August Senate primary — dismissed claims that her son was recruited as a spoiler, writing in an email to The Associated Press that his runs have been "entirely independent of any influencers" and adding, "Tell President Trump to Pardon Eric Hafner!"

Controversy over alleged ballot spoilers also marked the Senate race, where independent candidate Dan J. Sullivan Jr. drew suspicion due to sharing a name with the Republican incumbent. Following reports that members of Peltola’s team helped recruit Sullivan Jr. into the race, the U.S. Justice Department opened a criminal investigation and Gov. Dunleavy ordered a state inquiry. Peltola has repeatedly denied any involvement.

"I personally know that I am innocent, so I don’t spend a lot of time hand-wringing" about it, Peltola said Wednesday.

The Associated Press and Reuters contributed to this report.
