FedEx Jet Runs Off Miami Runway 80 Days After Amazon Air Crash Killed 5 There

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

FedEx flight 910 from Memphis ran off the end of runway 26R at Miami airport Wednesday night and stopped in the grass just inside the fence at NW 67th Avenue. From across the street, the nose of the MD-11 lined up with the traffic light. Both people aboard got out safely. Nobody on the ground was hurt. Eighty days earlier, a Boeing 767 flying for Amazon Air crossed that same avenue after landing on a different runway. Five people in a van died. Here’s how close the FedEx jet came to the street on Thanksgiving eve, in a photo shared by aviation watchdog JonNYC. What A Miami Controller Reported Before FedEx Landed Reporter Anna Giaritelli says a Miami air traffic controller sent her a text before the overrun, while the controller was off duty. They described aircraft headed for Miami and Fort Lauderdale running out of routes around the weather, landlines going down between facilities and more than 10 reports of severe turbulence. “This was absolutely the most dangerous operation I’ve seen.” Read the controller’s message and see the regional weather radar. The FAA’s 6 p.m. operations plan shows thunderstorms made Atlantic routes unusable. Miami Center wanted departures routed down the middle of Florida, and controllers had to leave extra space between planes they handed off to Havana because of a communications problem. By 7:43 p.m., the FAA said the Havana equipment problem was resolved. One Boeing 767 crew reported severe turbulence at 8,000 feet about 39 nautical miles south of Miami at 4:24 p.m. At the airport there was light rain, seven miles of visibility and a south wind at 6 knots gusting to 18. Twelve minutes after Fedex landed, the airport reported thunderstorms to the northeast and east. Watch the roadside video of the two aircraft. I was supposed to land in Miami at about the same time this incident occurred.

Approximately one hour prior to landing, I began preparing for my approach to runway 26R. Buried deep in the NOTAMS were the runway condition codes of 3/3/3. Miami does not publish runway condition codes in their digital ATIS like most big airports.

I determined runway 26R was too short for landing (737 MAX-8). I set up for runway 27 instead (based on surface winds and runway condition code).
