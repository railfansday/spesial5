Knicks’ Karl-Anthony Towns acting like ‘second coach’ sign of fixed Mike Brown partnership

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

TORONTO — A once rocky partnership has evolved into a trusting and collaborative relationship between Karl-Anthony Towns and Mike Brown.

Towns revealed Tuesday that Brown has been embracing his suggestions "like I'm a second coach," which is a residual benefit of their success in the 2026 postseason.

"It's more familiarity with the system and building that trust with Mike where he can trust me with things I see and say, which started in the playoffs," Towns said before scoring 16 points and grabbing 16 rebounds in the Knicks' 111-102 win over the Raptors on Wednesday night. "So building off that this year. Having more input and him hearing me out."

Towns said he and the coach collaborate "a lot" during games.

"Him trusting me like a second coach. Me and him are always coaching with each other in the game, looking at game plans and plays and maybe ideologies and things I see that can help our team win, especially in close games," Towns said. "Working with him and coaching on the floor. It's been great. It's been fun."

Karl-Anthony Towns looks to make a play as Scottie Barnes defends during the Knicks' 111-102 win over the Raptors on Nov. 17, 2026, in Toronto. NBAE via Getty Images

It wasn't always so harmonious between the coach and All-Star center. When Brown took over before last season, Towns' production plummeted and it was clear the player-coach dynamic was off-kilter.

As Towns said multiple times during last season's struggles, "The system is different." Towns also declared during the 2025 training camp that he didn't know his role. It got messier by midseason, when Brown called out Towns in January for not recovering on defense after falling on a layup attempt.

"When you fall down, you've got to get up and got to sprint down the floor," Brown said at the time. "And even when you're the last guy down the floor, you've got to get down there just in case there's a long rebound. There was no urgency."

But then Brown placed Towns as an offensive hub in the playoffs, and the Knicks raised a banner because, among other reasons, the adjustment worked spectacularly.

This season, Towns is off to the best start on the roster with averages of 21.6 points and 13.6 rebounds entering Tuesday's game against the Raptors. He was shooting an efficient 53.4 percent from the field, and 39.7 percent from beyond the arc — both dramatic improvements from last season.

Not coincidentally, he's in a better place with his head coach.

"We just grew. We've grown with each other," Towns said. "And obviously there's trust that has to be established and both of us trust each other a lot. And the last playoff run showed it. So just building off that trust and the relationship that was built in the playoffs and having it make our team better this year."

Brown agreed they're in a better place and he's given KAT ownership of the adjustments.

"It's just part of being around each other," Brown said. "You hope your relationship with everybody grows. That's not gonna always happen but you hope it does. KAT's got a really good feel of how to play the game. Obviously he's a perennial All-Star. So those things you've always got to take into account when it comes to trying to listen or try to adapt more to what their style of play is.

"As a head coach — come in, it's your first year, and you have an idea what you want to do. Sometimes it doesn't fit. Offensively, or defensively. And that's when you've got to pivot. And so we kept pivoting and pivoting and building until we found a nice little groove. We feel pretty good about each other right now."
