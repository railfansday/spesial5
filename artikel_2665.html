NBA power rankings: How far will slumping Thunder, Celtics fall?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

As the NBA calendar churns on, Thanksgiving will give fans the chance to catch their breath — and, perhaps, catch some football.

No games will be played Thursday, Nov. 26, though NBA Cup action is starting to ramp up.

Another thing ramping up are the stakes, as certain contenders are facing adversity and losing skids. Chief among them are the Oklahoma City Thunder, who have been besieged by injuries and lost six of their last eight. In the East, it's the Boston Celtics who tumbled on a four-game losing streak, though they snapped it Tuesday, Nov. 24 against the Wizards. How far will both those teams fall?

Here are the Week 5 USA TODAY Sports NBA power rankings:

Movement from previous rankings in parentheses

Tyler Herro has embraced efficiency and has been on a scoring flash. Milwaukee's first win of November (against the Raptors) was a nice boost.

With all their injuries, they've had six different players lead the team in scoring over their last six games. That said, points are still too tough to come by.

A gritty win against the Celtics in which the Kings held Boston to just 13 points in the third quarter was a glimpse of what this roster can do when playing well. The problem? That doesn't happen anywhere near enough.

The offense has moments when it struggles to make baskets, but no team allows more points per 100 possessions than Brooklyn does, a staggering 120.3.

The Clippers rank second in the NBA in 3-point percentage (38.1%) but are middle of the pack in attempts (37.8 per game; tied for 15th).

Losers of 11 of their last 12, the Hornets have serious offensive issues and rank last in scoring, averaging just 100.6 points per game.

New Orleans isn't much better and has lost 10 of 11. The Pels also rank dead last in 3-point attempts per game (30.3).

Scottie Barnes has been stellar, but this team simply won't compete as long as Kawhi Leonard is sidelined. Toronto has lost seven of eight.

The Bulls have been far better as of late, with wins over the last 10 days against three teams in the top 15 of this list: the Hawks, Timberwolves and Cavaliers.

In their last game, they beat the injured Thunder (Shai Gilgeous-Alexander did play, however). That was a positive, but the Pacers offense cannot afford to be without Tyrese Haliburton. The good news? He practiced with Indiana's G League squad.

Anthony Davis has been superb and has stayed healthy, but Washington's other offensive weapons haven't been as consistent. Still, three wins in their last four is solid for the Wizards.

Franz Wagner was keeping Orlando competitive, but it has become clear, as teams have started to scout the Magic, that they miss Paolo Banchero (ankle). Even when he returns, however, he'll need reps to figure out how he jells with Wagner in new coach Sean Sweeney's system.

Since Stephen Curry (oblique) went down, the offensive production simply hasn't been there to sustain competitive play. Golden State ranks last in points per game (94.5) over the last six games.

Even though he hasn't been out long, and even though his production has dipped this season, the Cavs have regressed on defense and miss the scoring of Evan Mobley (toe).

After a slow start in which the offense lagged, Phoenix has won eight of its last 10. The Suns are doing a nice job of sharing the ball and limiting turnovers.

The other night against the Heat, the Thunder dressed just nine players because of injury. Their 10th place standing in the West is the lowest they've been since April 9 … of 2023.

They let the Mavericks clamp them up to go on a 17-0 run Tuesday night to close out the first half and begin the second, leading to a 21-point deficit. Low effort on transition defense compounded everything.

This young team has had some solid wins this season. And, with his blend of scoring and play-making, Cooper Flagg is well on his way to his first (of many) All-Star appearances.

It's clear Deni Avdija and Damian Lillard are the offensive catalysts. But since Ja Morant (hamstring) went out, no player has emerged as a third option.

The Grizz have continued to be one of the top surprises this season, but their star rookie, No. 3 overall pick Cameron Boozer, missing a month may set them back a touch.

Very quietly, the Hawks have been building a terrific season. They're the only team to average more than 30 assists per game, they rank fourth in second-chance points per game (16.9) and they've been playing cohesive and collaborative team defense.

Live by the 3, die by it; during Boston's recent four-game losing streak, which it snapped Tuesday night against the Wizards, the Celtics shot just 28.3% from beyond the arc.

They own the NBA's longest winning streak (seven games), have incorporated more on-ball screening and pick-and-rolls and an encouraging sign was that the offense rolled against a very depleted Thunder squad while Giannis Antetokounmpo was out.

After a slow start, the defending champs have settled in. They're leading the league in rebound percentage (53.8%). Karl-Anthony Towns leads the NBA in rebounding (13.9 per game) and hasn't had a single game in which he didn't reach double figures in the stat.

One of the more positive signs for Detroit has been its defense, which ranks fourth in rating (108.3) and tied for second in blocks per game (5.8) — all without Jalen Duren.

Houston has dropped two of its last four, both against solid opponents, but the Rockets have embraced efficiency, ranking third in true shooting (59.9%) and effective field goal percentage (56.0%).

Ayo Dosunmu has been a revelation for the Timberwolves with Anthony Edwards (knee) out, and his steady scoring has kept the offense afloat.

With Autin Reaves (heel) out, things have been shakier lately, though just one loss in their last 12 shows this team has staying power. But L.A. could use a Reaves return.

Joel Embiid's illness has come at a tough time, as he had just started to find his offensive dominance, scoring 29.7 points over his last three.

Even without Dylan Harper (hip), the Spurs are thriving, and Victor Wembanyama is playing at an MVP level. San Antonio also leads the NBA in net rating (+12.3).
