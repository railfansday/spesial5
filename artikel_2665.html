Lakers star divulges ‘uncomfortable’ reason for success without Austin Reaves

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

For the first four weeks of the season, the Lakers, for the most part, won their games in similar fashion.

A scorching start from Luka Doncic en route to a 30-point (and oftentimes, 40-point) performance.

Austin Reaves, Doncic's backcourt co-star, efficiently filled in the gaps offensively en route to an uber-efficient 25-point game.

Walker Kessler provided the rim pressure the team needed, which in combination with the scoring/playmaking from Doncic and Reaves, opened up scoring opportunities for the players around them.

Luka Doncic #77 and Austin Reaves #15 of the Los Angeles Lakers look on. NBAE via Getty Images

Los Angeles Lakers forward Jarred Vanderbilt playing defense. AP Photo/Kyusung Gong

The recipe not only led to the Lakers winning 12 of their first 14 games, but also powered them to have the league's top-ranked offense.

But then the formula that led them to most of their early-season victories changed before their longest stretch of games at home.

The ingredients changing meant the recipe for success also had to be altered – which the Lakers successfully pulled off during the 3–1 homestand that ended with Monday's victory over the Jazz.

Doncic cooled off from his sizzling start to the season during the homestand, averaging 29.5 points (38.5% shooting, 23.5% on 3-pointers), after averaging 39 points (48.6% shooting, 40.6% on 3s) beforehand.

Utah Jazz forward Kyle Filipowski (2) goes up for a basket as Los Angeles Lakers center Walker Kessler (14) defends. AP Photo/Kyusung Gong

Reaves wasn't available, missing the last five games because of a bruised left heel. The Lakers are hopeful he'll return during Friday's road game against the Spurs.

Which led to the Lakers leaning on their defense to pull out most of their recent wins.

"We have a standard and we have to play to that standard and do what we're supposed to," Collin Sexton said. "When you're playing to a standard, it doesn't matter who's in and who's out. We all have to help. It doesn't matter who's coming into the game, we all have a role to play to do what the person in front of us did."

The Lakers held the Hornets to 114 points in their overtime win Nov. 17, allowed 109 points in the Nov. 18 loss to the Jazz, held the Kings to 104 points in Friday's win before holding the Jazz to 94 points on Monday, which is the fewest amount of points a team has scored against the Lakers this season.

Los Angeles Lakers forward Jarred Vanderbilt (2) vies for the ball against Utah Jazz forward Kyle Filipowski. AP Photo/Kyusung Gong

Their halfcourt defense has been a strength of the Lakers throughout the season.

But they cleaned up some of their transition defensive concerns. They remained disruptive, recording at least 16 turnovers in each game of the homestand. And using more zone defense, sometimes with full-court pressure, gave the Lakers a boost on that end of the floor.

"We have a lot of like disruptors defensively," Jake LaRavia said. "We do a good job at just creating pressure for the other team, whether it's deflections or just ball pressure, and kind of making them uncomfortable in bringing the ball up the court. It's obviously been working for us, so we're going to keep doing it 'til it stops working."

The Lakers' defensive rating improved from No. 17 to No. 10 during the homestand – albeit against inferior opponents that have a combined record of 9–43.

They're going to need Reaves available and improved play from Doncic as the level of competition picks up with the four-game trip post-Thanksgiving that features matchups against the Spurs, Grizzlies and Rockets. Those are three of the four teams closest to the Lakers in the top of the Western Conference standings.

But even though it wasn't always pretty, the Lakers showed they can at least win in other ways outside of what they showed in the first few weeks of the season.

"Austin is a very, very important piece to this team, and so seeing how we compete without him, not at our full strength, that's good," Kessler said. "It builds resiliency. Because [you're] not going to be always [at] your full strength. Things happen, it's a long season, so it's encouraging to see."
