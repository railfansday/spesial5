Nets sorely miss Michael Porter Jr. in beatdown by Grizzlies

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

MEMPHIS, Tenn. — The Nets came into Saturday with every advantage. And they flushed them all with an embarrassing 129-110 non-effort against the Grizzlies before 15,103 at FedExForum.

The Nets were the fresher, more rested team. They were coming off a huge win and a solid practice, while Memphis was coming off a back-to-back. And while they were missing Michael Porter Jr., the Grizzlies were without Ty Jerome, one of the biggest breakout stories in basketball.

And the Nets never competed. Not for a moment.

Brooklyn Nets center Day'Ron Sharpe (20) handles the ball against Memphis Grizzlies forward Cameron Boozer (27) on Saturday, Nov. 14, 2026, in Memphis, Tenn. AP Photo/Brandon Dill

"It's frustrating they played harder than us," admitted Jordi Fernández.

They were down by 18 in a first quarter that saw them give up 9-for-11 shooting from deep. They fell behind by as much as 26.

And when it was done, they knew they'd turned in a stinker.

"We cut it to seven at some point and you should've been in the game, but we're not mature enough," said a frustrated Fernández. "We played against a team that played on the second [night] of a back-to-back with the same travel that we had and they played the right way, played really hard. We're not there yet."

Sitting on the bench as a late scratch for personal reasons, Porter saw his Nets stumble badly right out of the gate, already trailing 35-17 with 2:47 left in the first quarter after a Cam Spencer 3-pointer.

After a sloppy turnover by Egor Dëmin, the Nets called a timeout. The young guard got chastised by Fernández and spoken to by Julius Randle.

Brooklyn Nets forward Julius Randle (30) shoots against the Memphis Grizzlies during an NBA basketball game Saturday, Nov. 14, 2026, in Memphis, Tenn. AP Photo/Brandon Dill

"I was just trying to tell, it's on us that we had bad organization getting the ball in," Randle said. "But in those moments, instead of turning the ball over and having a live turnover, just take the five-second [violation] so we can just get back and get our defense set. So, on us. Just trying not to compound it."

"Yeah, they've got to grow fast," Fernández said of young guards Dëmin, Nolan Traore and Mikel Brown Jr. "Being 20 is not an excuse. … I'm frustrated or I get mad because I know they're very good and they're better than this. And when I just don't see a connected group is when it hurts because we make those pushes and cut it to 10, cut it to seven. It's just frustrating."

The Nets had clawed to within 85-77 on Randle's 3 with 5:12 left in the third quarter, but they got no closer.

A Randle turnover killed the brittle Brooklyn momentum, and it quickly swung back the other way. The Nets coughed up a 16-1 run, with a Taylor Hendricks dunk putting them in a 101-78 hole with 40 seconds left in the third.

Brooklyn Nets head coach Jordi Fernandez gestures from the sideline during an NBA basketball game against the Memphis Grizzlies in Memphis, Tenn., Nov. 14, 2026. AP Photo/Brandon Dill

The Nets fell behind by 26 after a Javon Small 3-pointer with 7:08 to play.

"We weren't committed as well on defense, and they played harder than us. That was really it," Day'Ron Sharpe said. "I just felt like we weren't connected, weren't trusting one another, and we've just got to get better at that and we got to keep focused on just staying consistent in our energy."

Randle finished with 22 points but was a minus-18. Sharpe had 16 points and 11 boards. But the Nets allowed 52.9 percent shooting and 42.9 percent from deep.

Brooklyn Nets guard Nolan Traore (10) reacts in the second half of an NBA basketball game against the Memphis Grizzlies in Memphis, Tenn., Saturday, Nov. 14, 2026. AP Photo/Brandon Dill

Memphis' Cedric Coward had a game-high 23 points, while Hendricks added 22 for a Grizzlies team that didn't even have Jerome.

It was a galling way to end the four-game road swing.

"It was things that we can control," Randle said. "But we didn't get enough stops. They made like nine 3s in the first quarter. It's tough when a team gets it going that early. We didn't punch first. We didn't come out with a sense of urgency. And they took advantage of that.

"Frustrating because we want to see our habits translate, and they didn't [Saturday]. So we've got to be better."
