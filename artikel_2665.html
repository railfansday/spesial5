‘Kudos & Wet Willies’ review: Defense rules as Giants defeat Jacksonville

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

EAST RUTHERFORD, N.J. — The New York Giants didn't do it the easy way, but they did manage to get a huge victory over the Jacksonville Jaguars on Sunday. Let's get to our 'Kudos and Wet Willies' review.

The defense as a whole — The Giants turned away the Jaguars again and again on Sunday, refusing to let a missed field, a Cam Skattebo fumble, and an inconsistent day from quarterback J.J. McCarthy stand in the way of a signature victory for the Giants as they improved to 6-3-1, and moved a step closer to their first NFC East title since 2011.

The Giants pressured a flustered Jacksonville quarterback Trevor Lawrence into his worst game of the season. Lawrence went 25 of 47 for 301 passing yards, but threw two interceptions, was sacked three times, hit 11 times, adn was chased endlessly by Giants pass rushers.

The Giants got interceptions from Deonte Banks and Paulson Adebo. They had 14 passes defensed. They had a key fourth-down stop on fourth-and-1 at the Giants' 11-yard line late in the third quarter after a Cam Skattebo fumble had given the Jaguars the ball at the New York 31-yard line.

Abdul Carter — Flush with the excitement of a great performance in a huge victory for the Giants, Carter called himself "the best in the world."

Carter has a long way to go before he can prove that, but he might well have been the best pass rusher in the NFL on Sunday. Carter said he played "real angry" after seeing some online criticism over the fact that he entered the game with just 2.0 sacks.

Lawrence ended up feeling Carter's anger in the second-year edge defender's best game as a pro. Carter had two sacks, five quarterback hits, and a career-high 12 pressures, per NextGen Stats. This was the first multi-sack game of Carter's career.

Deonte Banks — Banks set up the Giants' first points with an interception that put the ball at the Jacksonville 31-yard line. He also had three passes defensed, including one on a desperation throw into the end zone by Lawrence on a fourth-quarter pass from the 12-yard line.

Paulson Adebo — The forgotten veteran cornerback was forced into action when the Giants lost starter Greg Newsome II to an ankle injury after just four snaps. Adebo was terrific.

Adebo intercepted a Lawrence pass with 11:08 left in the game, putting the Giants at the Jacksonville 28-yard line and setting up a Dominic Zvada field goal. Adebo also had four passes defensed. Per Pro Football Focus, he gave up just one completion in eight targets while spending much of his day chasing Jacksonville's Parker Washington.

Per PFF, his passer rating against was 0.0. Adebo got a game ball from John Harbaugh.

D.J. Reader — The big nose tackle's job is to stuff the run. He certainly did that Sunday with a tackle for loss and, per PFF, three stops. Somebody forgot to tell him, though, that nose tackles not named Dexter Lawrence aren't supposed to be impact pass rushers. Reader had a sack, two quarterback hits, and five total pressures. He continued to play at a Pro Bowl level.

Malik Nabers — I am honestly not sure about this one, but Nabers did have seven receptions for 55 yards and a touchdown. Through 29 career games, Nabers has caught 192 passes, the third-most through that many games in NFL history behind only Odell Beckham Jr. (199) and Puka Nacua (194).

Xavier Gipson — The Giants' new return man averaged 25.0 yards on five kickoff returns, including a 36-yarder, and had a 27-yard punt return. The Giants said they signed Gipson off the Philadelphia Eagles' practice squad because they needed "a spark" in the return game. Gipson gave them one.

Dru Phillips — Yeah, yeah. Phillips gave up seven completions on nine targets. Don't care. I care about his three passes defensed, especially on fourth-and-1 from the Giants' 11-yard line in the third quarter.

Jordan Stout — The Giants' punter averaged 60.3 yards on four punts, with a net of 54.5. The shortest punt Stout had was 57 yards. The amazing thing is that Stout manages to hit the ball that far while still getting enough hang time on his punts to give the Giants a chance to cover them.

Zaire Barnes and Tyler Nubin — Barnes had four special teams tackles, added to the league-leading 14 he had entering the game. Nubin has not sulked about losing his starting safety job to Ar'Darius Washington. He had three special teams tackles.

Second half offense — The Giants' offense seemed out of sorts after Cam Skattebo's fumble late in the third quarter with the Giants seemingly driving for what could have been a score that would have effectively put the game out of reach.

After that the Giants got just one first down over their next four possessions, netting -20 yards of offense along the way. They played oddly for a team trying to run the clock and protect a lead. Over those four drives, they ran the ball only one time. Those four possessions took a total of just 3:44 off the clock, and continued to give Jacksonville opportunities to get back into the game.

Micah McFadden — This was a rough night for McFadden. He made six tackles, and missed same number. Missed tackles have always been a bug-a-boo for McFadden, and this might have been his worst-ever game in that department. He missed a tackle in the backfield on a fourth-and-1 that went for a 21-yard run, and had a handful of chances to nab Lawrence in the backfield that he could not convert.

Cam Skattebo — Skattebo had a costly third-quarter fumble. He had just 34 yards on 14 carries, an average of 2.4 yards per attempt. After his third quarter fumble, the Giants seemed determined NOT to hand him the ball. Still, when the Giants needed a first down to ice the game, Skattebo delivered with two runs for 10 yards and a game-clinching first down at the two-minute mark.

Skattebo also delivered a trademark backflip, a move he said he had retired, after the victory-sealing first down.

"When energy calls for it, it calls for it," Skattebo said. "I do whatever I can to bring the energy for the team. I play this game with my heart and soul. When that happens it's second nature. It's hard to hold back from."

J.J. McCarthy — This was not McCarthy's steadiest game throwing the ball. He went just 18 of 38 (47.4%) for 173 yards and a touchdown.

McCarthy overshot a wide open Darnell Mooney on a couple of deep throws. He missed Mooney on a deep out where he was open. He failed to see an open Malik Nabers for what could have been a long touchdown.

Yet, he made game-winning plays. His 8-yard touchdown pass to Nabers was a nice touch throw. He made a terrific 19-yard throw to Malachi Fields for a critical first down inside the three-minute mark. McCarthy used his legs to escape the pocket and pick up some key first downs.

His play of the game, though, might have been his touchdown-saving tackle of Antonio Johnson after the Skattebo fumble.

Dominic Zvada — The rollercoaster ride with the Giants' strong-legged rookie placekicker continued on Sunday. He made field goals of 53 and 54 yards — from the right hash mark where all six of his misses had occurred this season. Then, Zvada missed a chip shot 32-yarder from that right hash early in the third quarter before adding a 43-yarder from the left hash mark for three critical points with 10:03 to play.

The Giants continue to express faith in Zvada, but it can't be a surprise to anyone if the Giants work out placekickers some time soon.
