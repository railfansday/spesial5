BMW Classic Pulls the Wraps off a Secret 1997 M7 Prototype

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Along with a six-speed manual, this one-off features a 400-hp 4.9-liter V-8 from the E39 M5.

At the end of Raiders of the Lost Ark, a nameless government employee wheels the crated-up Ark of the Covenant into a vast and anonymous warehouse, forever to be lost again. You have to imagine this is like what going to work in BMW’s mechanical archives is like, where hidden wonders wait patiently to see the light of day again.

Well, here’s something to melt your face right off: the secret prototype M7 BMW never made. Powered by the 4.9-liter V-8 that would go on to make the E39 M5 such a legend, it's a manual-transmission 1997 short-wheelbase E38 7-series, a total wolf in sheep’s clothing.

Rated at the same 400 horsepower as the later M5 would have, this car was only just revealed by BMW’s Classic division. As a prototype, it’s quite the what-might-have-been story, although BMW probably wasn’t all that serious about producing a full M version of its luxury flagship. Instead, the V-12-powered 750iL that Pierce Brosnan pilots remotely from the back seat in 1997's Tomorrow Never Dies provided plenty of drama.

However, the idea of a roomy limousine-class sedan with M5 power certainly gets the imagination going. BMW financed a series of films in the early 2000s featuring Clive Owen as a nameless driver, collected together as The Hire, and the one Guy Ritchie directed featured Owen ferrying Madonna around at breakneck speed in an M5. This M7 prototype would have worked just as well.

Besides the powertrain, a big part of this prototype’s appeal is just how understated it is. BMW’s M division doesn’t really do subtlety much anymore—customers want their carbon-fiber accents and M badges—but this black sedan would have flown completely under the radar in 1997. Engineers out testing it on the unrestricted autobahns must have had a heck of a time.

Sadly, like the E31-chassis, V-12-powered M8 prototype, the M7 would never make its way to a production version. Perhaps, though, that makes it all the more appealing, a secret treasure that’s been kept out of view, now finally breaking cover again. Dr. Jones might argue that it belongs in a museum. We’d sure like to slide behind the wheel and see if this one-off M7 will hit its stated top speed of 155 mph.
