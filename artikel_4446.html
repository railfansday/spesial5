Laid-Off Id Software Employees Form their Own Studio, Hidden Horses

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Daniel is a lifelong gamer with a love for various genres and gameplay styles who enjoys exploring games and playing from various angles.

2026 has been home to a swath of large-scale layoffs throughout the gaming industry, mostly in the western sector. A statistically-significant portion of these layoffs have come from Microsoft, which has been systematically pruning many of its subsidiaries, including some of its highest-profile studios like id Software and Bethesda. Obviously, this is terrible for the many, many developers who worked for those studios, but some of those same developers have decided to turn this pinch into a chance.

Xbox cut 3,200 jobs. The developers who lost theirs explain what that actually means for the games that were supposed to come next.

Today, a coalition of developers who were specifically laid off from id Software following their work on Doom: The Dark Ages announced the formation of their new independent studio: Hidden Horses. If that name is a reference to something, I don’t know what it is, but what this studio may lack in readily-apparent branding, it more than makes up for in the crew of talented, highly-experienced developers it consists of.

According to Hidden Horses’s newly-created website, this independent studio wants to make games “like it was done in the 90’s,” with an emphasis on carefully hand-crafted gaming experiences tailored by longtime industry veterans. The studio’s first project is already in progress, with plans to bring it to life via crowdfunding in the near future.

While these industry vets have lost the ample funding that comes with a major backer like Microsoft, they have, in turn, gained substantial freedom in how they pursue their projects. This is one of the stated benefits of the studio’s formation, as outlined on the “How We Work” section of the Hidden Horses website.

“The company was created as a developer run and owned studio,” the page reads. “This allows us to pursue fun and creativity in our games, unencumbered by short-term thinking or chasing market trends.”

They have gained substantial freedom in how they pursue their projects.

The team considers this structure optimal because helps them make games right, rather than make them quickly, creating new and interesting gameplay concepts rather than trying to flip half-baked products for a quick buck. The team is firmly of the belief that, despite changes in the industry and players, there is still very much a demand for the kinds of carefully-designed games that they’re accustomed to creating.

After being cut off by its previous publisher, Romero Games has secured a fresh bout of funding and refocused with a smaller dev team.

The page also notes that the studio will be run democratically, with input from every member of the team on all important business decisions, all to ensure nobody gets left out in the cold. “We do this not only because we think it is fair, but because we think it is a better way to run a business.”

Despite having just formed, Hidden Horses already has irons in the fire. On the “Our First Project” page of the website, the studio outlines its plans for its very first game development project. This game, titled Danger Pay, will be a “high-octane single-player FPS” set in a malevolent, futuristic game show in a manner reminiscent of Smash TV and The Running Man.

“Our game will focus on classic FPS gameplay, awesome weapons, crazy enemies, and will be highly optimized.”

Of course, financial backing for an entirely new game project doesn’t come out of nowhere, which is why the plan for this first project is to fund it via Kickstarter. A page for Danger Pay has already been created, though there’s no word yet on when the campaign will actually begin.

“We believe that the community around the type of games we have helped develop is robust, and we invite all supporters to join us in trying to create something special,” the Kickstarter description reads. “This will include the typical things needed to start a business and finance a game's development - licenses, hardware, healthcare, etc. Our goal will be based around the timeline of our first project and associated costs.”
