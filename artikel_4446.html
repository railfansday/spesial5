Ori Creator Is Very Unhappy With How Nintendo Handled Zelda: Ocarina of Time Remake

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Covering the video games industry since 2017, with experience in news, articles, and reviews. Currently a writer at DualShockers and gaming magazines.

We all know that few games have left as lasting an impression on the gaming industry as The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time. Even decades after its original release, the Nintendo classic continues to inspire developers and entire generations of players.

And its recently released remake has already brought plenty of attention back to Hyrule, with many critics and players praising its beautiful new presentation and sharing emotional reactions to revisiting one of their favorite adventures – no wonder it’s already one of the highest-rated games ever!

However, not everyone is completely satisfied with Nintendo's approach. Thomas Mahler, best known as the creator of the acclaimed Ori franchise, has shared a rather lengthy criticism of the remake on X (formerly Twitter), explaining why he believes the developer missed an opportunity to make the experience much better from a game design perspective.

Mahler recently shared his thoughts on social media after playing the Ocarina of Time remake alongside his son, who was immediately impressed by its new art direction. The developer even mentioned that his son was so amazed by the visuals that he tried touching the screen to grab the Kokiri Sword when he first obtained it, which is pretty cute!

The remake's dev team included younger folks, several of whom never played the original Ocarina of Time, who provided a much-needed modern view of things.

Unfortunately, Mahler himself wasn't nearly as impressed with the experience. In his post, he curiously explained that he has always been somewhat disappointed by the original Ocarina of Time, particularly when comparing it to A Link to the Past. For him, the Nintendo 64 adventure feels emptier and more confusing, and the problem now is that the remake hasn't addressed those issues.

Thomas Mahler, best known as the creator of the acclaimed Ori franchise, has shared a rather lengthy criticism of the remake, explaining why he believes the developer missed an opportunity to make the experience much better from a game design perspective.

His biggest complaint revolves around Hyrule Field, which he considers one of the game's weakest areas. Mahler believes its relatively empty design was understandable back in 1998, considering the Nintendo 64's technical limitations. However, with modern hardware and the technology available today, he feels Nintendo could have done much more than simply improve its appearance. “Sure, Hyrule Field looks prettier now, but it's still just as empty and uninteresting as it was back then,” Mahler explained.

Mahler believes the remake could have taken a similar approach to A Link to the Past by introducing more activities, hidden areas, and interactions throughout Hyrule Field. He also suggested that acquiring new dungeon items should encourage players to revisit previously explored locations and discover paths or secrets they couldn't access before.

Instead, he feels Nintendo preserved an outdated design that could have benefited from a more substantial overhaul. He even argued that the game would deserve a 7.5 or 8 out of 10 today, rather than the perfect scores some reviewers have awarded it.

Eiji Aonuma and the development team for the Ocarina of Time remake discussed how one of the original's infamous dungeons is being improved.

Naturally, Mahler's comments didn't go unnoticed, and plenty of Zelda fans pushed back against his opinion. Some questioned whether changing the original game's structure would undermine what made Ocarina of Time so special in the first place.

The developer wasn't particularly convinced by those arguments, however, and continued defending his position in the replies. “Let's be a bunch of bozos instead who praise 1998 game design and act like it's the end all be all even though Nintendo themselves would never ever design a shit overworld like that for their next mainline entry," he responded.

It's worth noting that Mahler did acknowledge the remake's impressive visuals, and his criticism is primarily directed at its level design rather than its presentation. Still, his comments highlight an interesting discussion about how much developers should change when revisiting beloved classics.

After all, preserving what made a game special and improving the elements that haven't aged quite as well aren't necessarily mutually exclusive. And while plenty of players are clearly enjoying their return to Hyrule, Mahler's reaction shows that even a legendary adventure like Ocarina of Time won't be everyone's cup of tea.

The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time is one of the most beloved games of all time, but these remakes might be just as meaningful to fans.
