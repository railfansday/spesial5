Keep Your Network Warm Before You Need a Job – Help First, Ask Later

Stay in touch with your professional network while you need nothing from it, so asking about a job later isn’t a cold call. Check in with each contact every few months without asking for anything, and help first with an introduction or a job lead, since people remember who helped them.

Reaching out only when you need a job means every note you send includes a request. After three years of silence, a former coworker can tell from the timing why your note arrived.

The same request lands differently with a contact who heard from you twice last year. To them, it’s one more note from someone they already know. If one of those notes passed along a job lead or an introduction, helping you also feels like returning a favor.

That’s why the time to keep in touch is while you’re employed and need nothing. Notes without a request are what make a later request easy to say yes to.

Start a list of 30 to 50 people who could point you to a job someday, such as former managers, past coworkers, classmates, and clients, and write down the date you last talked to each. A spreadsheet is enough. Then build three habits around it.

A two-line note congratulating a former manager on her new role takes a minute to write. A year later, asking her about openings at her company is the next note in a conversation you’ve kept going.

Block 15 minutes on your calendar every Friday and send three notes. At that pace, everyone on a 40-name list hears from you about every three months.
