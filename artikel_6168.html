The Centurion Card from American Express has a reputation that few other cards can match. Its invitation-only application process, substantial fees and long list of perks have made the Amex “Black Card” a status symbol.

But you don't need a Centurion invitation to get better treatment at a hotel, airport lounge access or have someone help with a dinner reservation. Plenty of cards offer those benefits, often for a fraction of the cost.

Some Centurion perks remain difficult to replicate. Others are available with cards you might already have — and, in a few cases, another card can get you a higher level of status.

Here's what to know about the U.S. Centurion Card and the other ways to get its most useful benefits.

The information for the Centurion Card has been collected independently by The Points Guy. The card details on this page have not been reviewed or provided by the card issuer.

There are personal and business versions of the Centurion Card. Both are invitation-only.

Amex doesn't publish a spending threshold that guarantees an invitation. Reports of the amounts required vary widely, and meeting a rumored minimum doesn't mean you'll be invited to apply.

If you're interested, you can contact Amex to ask about consideration. Just don't build your spending strategy around getting an invitation: There isn't a published qualification path you can follow as you would for airline or hotel elite status.

The has an $895 annual fee (see rates and fees). That's a substantial expense, but the Centurion Card costs considerably more.

The Centurion Card carries a $10,000 initiation fee and a $5,000 annual fee, bringing the first-year tab to $15,000 between the two.

At that price, the card needs to do considerably more than earn rewards. Whether it makes sense will depend on how much you use its services and benefits and how much you'd otherwise pay for them.

The Centurion Card has some impressive benefits, but its base earning rate of 1 Membership Rewards point per dollar spent isn't a reason to get it. The business version has additional earning opportunities, including a 1.5-point rate on eligible large purchases.

Other options for earning: You'll generally get better Membership Rewards point-earning potential in bonus categories with the or the .

For example, the Platinum earns 5 points per dollar spent on flights booked directly with airlines or through American Express Travel® (on up to $500,000 of those purchases per calendar year, then 1 point per dollar spent).

And if you aren't loyal to Membership Rewards, the (see rates and fees) earns 4 Chase Ultimate Rewards points per dollar spent on flights and hotels booked directly and 3 points per dollar spent on dining. The Capital One Venture X Rewards Credit Card earns at least 2 miles per dollar spent on all purchases.

Along with being a status marker itself, the Centurion Card offers complimentary elite status with several loyalty programs.

Marriott Bonvoy Gold elite status includes a 25% bonus on eligible base points, a welcome gift (at participating brands), space-available room upgrades and 2 p.m. late checkout (when available). It doesn't include complimentary breakfast or lounge access.

Other ways to get it: The and (with a $895 annual fee; see rates and fees) offer Gold status (enrollment required).

Hilton Honors Diamond status includes a 100% bonus on eligible base points, executive lounge access (at properties with an eligible lounge) and space-available upgrades (that can include suites). Breakfast or food-and-beverage benefits vary by brand and region.

Although Diamond was once Hilton's highest elite tier, the program introduced Diamond Reserve in 2026. Still, Diamond remains valuable for frequent Hilton guests, particularly those staying at full-service properties.

Other ways to get it: You can get Diamond automatically by holding the , which has a $550 annual fee (see rates and fees).

The Centurion Card offers up to $369 in Clear+ statement credits per calendar year. At standard prices of $219 for the main membership and $125 per additional adult, that fully covers a member and one other adult. Clear expedites identity verification at participating airport security checkpoints and event venues like stadiums.

Other ways to get it: The additional member coverage is unique to the Centurion Card. The , and also offer up to $219 in statement credits for Clear+ memberships (enrollment required; subject to auto-renewal).

Centurion cardmembers can enroll for a complimentary Equinox Destination Access membership, which includes an Equinox+ digital subscription.

Other ways to get it: The offers up to $300 in Equinox statement credits per calendar year for eligible U.S. club memberships or an Equinox+ subscription (enrollment required; subject to auto-renewal). It can cover part of your costs, but it doesn't replace a full club membership.

The Centurion Card offers an application fee credit of $120 for Global Entry or up to $85 for TSA PreCheck (every four or 4½ years, respectively).

Other ways to get it: A Global Entry/TSA PreCheck credit is a fixture on many travel rewards cards, making this one of the easier Centurion benefits to replicate.

The personal Centurion Card offers up to $200 in annual Uber Cash for eligible U.S. rides and orders (issued as $15 monthly with an extra $20 in December). It also offers up to $120 in annual statement credits toward an eligible Uber One membership (subject to auto-renewal).

Other ways to get it: The personal provides the same benefits, while the offers $10 in monthly Uber Cash. (Uber Cash will be deposited into one Uber account when you add an eligible card as a payment method and redeem with any Amex card.)

Amex Fine Hotels + Resorts bookings include daily breakfast for two, a $100 property credit toward eligible expenses, guaranteed 4 p.m. checkout and a room upgrade (when available).

Centurion enhancements at participating properties include an additional $200 credit (on stays of at least two consecutive nights; book by phone through Centurion services).

Other ways to get it: The and provide access to the standard FHR program.

There are also comparable hotel programs outside Amex. The and offer The Edit; the Venture X and Capital One Venture X Business offer the Premier Collection; and those with the (see rates and fees) and (see rates and fees) can book The Reserve by Citi Travel.

Eligible bookings can include breakfast for two, a $100 property credit and space-available room upgrades. Their rules differ — particularly around late checkout — so compare the benefits and total price before booking.

One of the Centurion Card's most appealing services is its concierge, which can help with requests such as restaurant reservations, event tickets and travel arrangements. How much that's worth depends on how often you use it and the assistance you receive.

Here’s what the original Points Guy himself, Brian Kelly, has to say about Ray, his Centurion concierge:

I’d guess that Ray saves me more than $20,000 a year. From knowing my preferred seat on a plane based on the layout of the cabin to advising hotels of my arrival and departure time, Ray is a jack of all trades. He’s arranged a tour in Beijing for me and my parents to visit the Forbidden City, Tiananmen Square and the Great Wall of China. He’s gotten me theater tickets, great concert seats (front row for Celine Dion) and hard-to-get restaurant reservations. Ray even takes on particularly cumbersome tasks, like locating my headphones when I lost them 10,000 miles from home.

Other ways to get it: While not quite as premium as the Centurion concierge, select Amex cardmembers can access an American Express Concierge for assistance purchasing event tickets, making reservations, booking travel and fulfilling shopping requests.

This perk is available on the , the and the .

Visa Infinite cards such as the and Venture X also provide concierge assistance.

Centurion† assistance at participating airports requires a first- or business-class ticket paid with the card through Amex Travel (and a request at least 74 hours ahead). Availability applies; expedited immigration isn't guaranteed.

Other ways to get it: You can purchase airport meet-and-greet services separately. That can be worth comparing if you need assistance on a particular trip, although you'll need to pay for the service yourself.

Centurion cardmembers receive access to Amex’s Global Lounge Collection, including Centurion Lounges, Priority Pass lounges (enrollment required), Escape Lounges, Plaza Premium lounges, Lufthansa lounges and Delta Sky Clubs.

Guest rules depend on the lounge. At U.S. Centurion Lounges and select international locations, Centurion cardmembers can bring their immediate family — a spouse or domestic partner and children under 18 — or up to two guests without charge. Delta Sky Club guests generally cost $50 each.

Centurion cardmembers also have access to reserved seating or dedicated spaces at select Centurion Lounges. Some locations go further: Tokyo Haneda Airport (HND) has a Centurion-only room with its own beverage station.

Other ways to get it: The and are the closest alternatives for access to the same lounge networks (enrollment may be required), with stricter visit and guest rules. Each provides 10 Delta Sky Club Visits per Medallion Year on eligible travel, compared with Centurion's unlimited eligible visits. Platinum cardmembers generally pay for Centurion Lounge guests unless they meet the $75,000 annual spending requirement for complimentary guest access.

The Delta Reserve personal and business cards offer 15 Sky Club Visits per Medallion Year and Centurion Lounge access on eligible Delta itineraries. Both those cards and the Amex Platinum cards can unlock unlimited Sky Club visits after $75,000 in eligible calendar-year purchases.

For lounge access outside Amex, the and offer Sapphire and Priority Pass lounge access, while the offers Priority Pass and four Admirals Club passes per calendar year. The Venture X and Venture X Business offer Capital One Lounges and Landings plus Priority Pass. None of these cards provides Centurion Lounge access.

The Centurion Card includes a PS membership and two complimentary Salon visits with a guest per calendar year (booked through Centurion services; after enrollment). The Salon is a shared lounge experience, distinct from a private suite.

PS operates at Dallas Fort Worth International Airport (DFW), Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport (ATL), Los Angeles International Airport (LAX) and Miami International Airport (MIA).

Other ways to get it: You can book PS without a special credit card or a membership. Nonmember rates start at $1,295 per person for the Salon and $4,950 one-way for a private suite for up to four travelers (excluding taxes and fees).

Centurion cardmembers enjoy the highest level of travel and shopping protections American Express has to offer, including protection against trip delay and cancellation** and baggage delays*** as well as extended warranty*** and purchase protection.***

Other ways to get it: The and offer several overlapping protections; similar protections can be found on many cards across Amex’s portfolio.

Centurion cardmembers receive access to dining opportunities and special events. However, several useful reservation benefits are available with less exclusive cards.

The Centurion Card combines some hard-to-replicate services with benefits you can get elsewhere.

If Hilton Diamond status is what matters to you, the is a straightforward alternative. If you want Marriott status, the actually provides a higher tier. And for lounge access and hotel booking perks, the and cover much of the same ground.

Ultimately, the Centurion Card's biggest differentiators are its exclusive experiences and personalized services. If those aren't priorities, you may be able to get the benefits you value most from a far less expensive card.

Start with the benefits you'll use, then work out which card provides them at a cost you can justify. You might not need an invitation to make your next trip more comfortable.
