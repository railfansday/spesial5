Marilyn Monroe and Arthur Miller’s Tragic Love Story

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

“I think you’re the saddest girl I ever met.”

“You’re the first man that ever said that. I’m usually told how happy I am.”

This exchange, from The Misfits, written by Arthur Miller, shows Marilyn Monroe drawing closer to something other than the “dumb blonde” persona for which Hollywood praised her.

After all, the actor—who was born in Los Angeles in 1926 and died in the City of Angels in 1962 at the age of 36—wanted to be taken seriously as a performer. She never fully identified with the image Hollywood created for her. She spent her limited free time enjoying uncharacteristic pastimes, as evidenced by one of her most famous photographs, which depicts her reading James Joyce’s Ulysses while donning a striped tank top, black shorts, and a deep, melancholic gaze.

Marilyn Monroe reads a book in her room at the Hotel Ambassador in midtown Manhattan, March 1955.

“A sex symbol becomes a thing. I just hate to be a thing,” said Monroe to Life magazine in 1962, reflecting on her strained relationship with Hollywood.

Miller, who was born in New York in 1915 and passed away in 2005, initially believed he would be a good partner for Monroe, whose life was marked by great successes, but also abandonment and her unfortunate inability to have children—one of the most painful issues for Monroe.

Monroe and Miller first met in 1950, when he was in Los Angeles with director Elia Kazan. It was Kazan who introduced them.

Monroe and Miller began talking, and he emphasized her potential to become a dramatic actor on New York stages. He struck a chord.

In the mid-1950s, the star left Hollywood and moved to New York to study, attending The Actors Studio and working with Lee Strasberg. She met with Miller again in the Big Apple, and in 1955, they began dating seriously, joining two seemingly distant worlds.

Marilyn Monroe and playwright Arthur Miller play with his dog Hugo,at Miller’s summer home on June 25, 1956.

Monroe felt respected by him, unlike the way she felt in Hollywood, which she once called “an overcrowded brothel, a merry-go-round with beds for horses,” in her unfinished memoir, My Story.

Miller, who had won the Pulitzer Prize in 1949 for Death of a Salesman, was captivated by her and ended his failing marriage.

The subsequent marriage between Monroe and Miller—her third marriage, his second—took place on June 29, 1956, in White Plains, New York. A few days later, a private Jewish ceremony was also held, following the actor’s conversion to Judaism.

The first major rift in their marriage stemmed from ongoing tension between public and private life. Miller wanted a more secluded, domestic life; Monroe wanted a family, but she was not willing to give up the career she had so painstakingly built.

For the 1957 film The Prince and the Showgirl—directed by and starring Laurence Olivier, with Monroe as the female lead and coproducer—the actor and her husband moved to England, where tensions rose.

Arthur Miller and Marilyn Monroe after their wedding.

Monroe found some of Miller’s notes, in which he expressed disappointment with their marriage and admitted to sometimes feeling embarrassed around his friends in the cultural world.

It was one of the hardest moments in their relationship: Monroe discovered that the man who seemed to recognize her talent and intelligence also viewed her through the very same lens from which she was trying to free herself.

In her own notebooks, she reflected on her fear of becoming “someone’s wife” and losing her independence.

Miller would later say that he wrote very little during the years of his marriage.

Monroe, meanwhile, was struggling to become a mother. During her marriage, she experienced a miscarriage, an ectopic pregnancy, and another spontaneous termination of pregnancy.

On the set of The Prince and the Showgirl, her difficulties increased: She often arrived late, struggled to work consistently, and her relationship with Laurence Olivier became strained.

In the background, Miller faced a trial, as he had refused to name Communist Party members and sympathizers. He was convicted of contempt of Congress in 1957, but the conviction was overturned the following year.

Marilyn Monroe and her former husband Arthur Miller as they leave Doctors Hospital on August 10, 1957, after she recovered from a 10-day hospital stay following an ectopic pregnancy.

Despite Monroe publicly expressing her support for her husband, the cracks in their marriage turned into chasms. Arguments, a lack of trust, disillusionment, drugs, alcohol, all of it became too difficult to contain.

The point of no return was another feature film, The Misfits, written by Miller for Monroe.

The desert setting where it was filmed didn’t help. Amid hellish temperatures, Monroe and Miller were no longer on speaking terms, even though they were staying at the same hotel. Intermediaries were needed just to get them to communicate.

It was during this shoot that Miller met the photographer Inge Morath, whom he would later marry in February 1962.

They divorced shortly after The Misfits was released in theaters.

After The Misfits, Monroe went through one of the most difficult periods of her life. In 1961 she was admitted to the Payne Whitney Psychiatric Clinic in New York. Joe DiMaggio intervened to have her transferred to another facility.

Marilyn Monroe’s photo shoot at Hollywood agent Johnny Hyde’s backyard in Beverly Hills, California, 1957.

Before her death, Monroe nevertheless continued to work, seek roles, and imagine a life different from the one others intended for her.

In a 1988 interview that aired on the Italian network Rai, journalist and writer Enzo Biagi asked Miller: “How do you want to remember Marilyn Monroe?”

He replied: “She made everyone believe she was very happy, but I didn’t believe it. She was very depressed, but she had the extraordinary talent of being able to mask her state of mind. She wore a mask because she was suffering too much. She was completely unable to cope with her depression, but she had been an abused child. She was looking for someone to save her.”
