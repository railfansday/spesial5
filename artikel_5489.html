InterScripts and Children's Nebraska Earn 2026 CHIME Collaboration Award for AdaptCare

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

CHANTILLY, Va., Nov. 19, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- InterScripts, Inc., a healthcare IT services and software company, today announced that it has received the 2026 CHIME Collaboration Award together with Children's Nebraska from the College of Healthcare Information Management Executives (CHIME). The award was presented at the CHIME26 Fall Forum, held November 11â14 at the Gaylord Rockies Resort & Convention Center in Aurora, Colorado, and recognizes the two organizations' joint development of AdaptCare, a patient care platform that gives emergency responders instant access to a child's individualized care plan.

The CHIME Collaboration Award is presented jointly to a CHIME member organization and a CHIME Foundation firm in recognition of a collaborative effort that delivers value to the healthcare IT community, both in the quality of the partnership and in the significance of the work itself. It is one of the few industry honors that recognizes a health system and its technology partner together rather than separately.

Built With Clinicians for the Children Who Need It Most

AdaptCare grew out of Children's Nebraska's Project Austin program, which develops individualized emergency care plans for children with medical complexity so emergency responders and emergency department teams understand each child's unique medical and communication needs before providing care. Working directly with the program's nurse founders, InterScripts designed and built a secure mobile and web platform that integrates with the hospital's electronic health record and delivers each child's care plan, communication needs, and treatment precautions to first responders at the point of care, even when a parent or guardian is not present.

The platform provides responders with important information about a child's individual needs, including communication preferences, sensory sensitivities, medical conditions and medications that may affect emergency treatment. Because EMTs, firefighters, and other responders are typically not credentialed at the hospital, AdaptCare closes a long-standing information gap between the health system and the first responders who reach a child first.

Children's Nebraska has reported that the program has contributed to a 27% reduction in hospital admissions, a 50% decrease in overall hospital length of stay, and a 55% reduction in cost across organizational services for enrolled patients. InterScripts now hosts, supports, and continues to develop AdaptCare, with Children's Nebraska as its founding clinical partner, and is making the platform available to additional children's hospitals and health systems.
