15 key moments in Jensen Huang's path from Computex to resignation

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Fifteen dates track former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's rising favor in Washington beside reported warnings about Alice Huang, up to the Nov. 9 fallout

When the White House and a handful of chief executives decide between themselves which American artificial intelligence chips may be sold in China, an executive's private life no longer stays private. In that arrangement, an executive who is close to the president can win sales for his company. The people close to that executive also gain a possible route to the officials who set policy. Few executives hold more of that access than Jensen Huang, who co-founded Nvidia $NVDA and still runs it. His judgment about the people around him matters to investors in a company that analysts expect to bring in nearly $694 billion in sales in fiscal 2028, according to FactSet estimates reported by CNBC. Any question about who stood close to Huang, and why, is also a question about how advanced chips reach China.

The story follows two tracks that may have run at the same time. In public, Washington showed Huang more and more favor. Inside the government, officials reportedly worried about a businesswoman tied to an Nvidia customer. The gravest claim against her is that she worked for Chinese intelligence. VnExpress reports that the claim appears in a classified U.S. intelligence memo and that the Chinese Embassy denies it. No criminal charge against her has been publicly reported, according to Yahoo News. Every mention of spying in this story is therefore an allegation rather than a finding, even though officials took the concern seriously enough to put it in writing. That uncertainty does not make the story smaller. It moves the focus away from what Huang did and toward what officials knew, and when they knew it. Neither question has a settled public answer, so the dates of each honor and each reported warning matter.

ABC News traced how Huang's relationship with Alice Huang, who is no relation, led to his downfall. His path took him to a White House state dinner, onto Air Force One on the way to Beijing, to a crowded Taipei restaurant during a tech expo, and to a presidential science medal. Huang's rising standing in Washington grew alongside reported warnings about the woman at his side, and how those two records fit together remains unresolved. How officials explain that overlap will shape how far Washington trusts the chip industry's leaders on China from here on.

At Computex, the technology trade show in Taipei, Nvidia $NVDA's chief executive spends his days among partners and buyers, and one more meeting there draws little notice. ABC News reports that Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang was with Alice Huang in Taipei during Computex on June 7, 2024. It was the second time the two had met, according to Korea JoongAng Daily, so they already knew each other when they met at the trade show. Reuters obtained a photo of the two together. That image made an ordinary trade-show meeting into a fixed point that anyone could go back to, and everything that follows on this timeline is read against it.

Alice Huang had a business reason to be near Nvidia's chief. She first met him as a representative of Megaspeed, a company based in Singapore, according to Korea JoongAng Daily. For the head of the world's leading AI chipmaker, time with buyers at an industry event is part of the work. "Meetings with customers are part of a CEO's job," Nvidia spokesperson John Rizzo said. Taken on its own, the Taipei encounter shows nothing more than a chief executive spending time with a customer's representative at a trade show.

Her ties to Megaspeed began before Taipei. Singapore's company registry listed her as a Megaspeed director from March to November 2023, according to Korea JoongAng Daily, though her broader career has not been independently confirmed. The company itself soon drew federal attention when the Commerce Department opened an investigation into Megaspeed in late 2024. Within months of the trade show, the business that had brought her into Huang's circle was under scrutiny in Washington.

The photo from Taipei never changed. What changed was what officials came to suspect about the woman beside him.

Jensen Huang went to the White House as an executive whose sales were bound up with rules the new president had the power to undo. He met President Donald Trump there on Jan. 31, 2025, according to ABC News. CNBC reports that Trump could reverse the diffusion rules on AI chip exports, so the visit put Huang in front of a man able to change the terms of Nvidia $NVDA's sales. From that point on, Huang's personal standing with Trump had commercial value for Nvidia, and the company had reason to protect it.

China was likely to come up from the very first meeting. People familiar with the plans expected the two men to discuss China's DeepSeek, according to CNBC, and the diffusion rules were expected to take effect in 2025. The meeting therefore took place while those export limits were still pending, with a Chinese AI rival expected to be a topic. The question of where American AI chips can be sold, which would later turn Huang's private life into a public matter, was already part of his dealings with the president on this date.

Nothing in public at this stage pointed to trouble in Huang's personal life. He appeared as a chief executive with growing ties to a new administration and a direct line to the president on the policy his company cared about most. That clean start is why what followed reads as a sharp reversal rather than a slow decline. The more Nvidia's interests depended on Huang having Trump's attention, the further any later question about the people around him would reach beyond one company.

Jensen Huang was filmed with Alice Huang during the busiest week of Taiwan's tech calendar, at a dinner he hosted rather than at a hidden meeting. The dinner took place in Taipei during the Computex tech expo, ABC News reports, and Huang himself was the host, according to VnExpress. That put the evening on Nvidia $NVDA's business calendar, with the company's chief at the head of the table. Cameras caught the two leaving the restaurant, and they reportedly got into the same car, a public exit at one of the most watched moments of the industry's year. A chief executive with this much riding on his judgment kept no distance between this relationship and his company's most visible week.

The dinner matters beyond gossip because of where Alice Huang had worked. Megaspeed International, a data center company based in Singapore, had employed her as a senior executive, according to VnExpress. The man beside her that night was Nvidia's co-founder and CEO. Data center companies buy the kind of hardware Nvidia sells, so Megaspeed did business in Nvidia's own market rather than in some unrelated industry. Any board would have to weigh a close personal tie between people on either side of the chip market, even with no espionage question attached.

Taiwanese broadcaster TVBS captured video of the evening, but accounts of the dinner reached the public only in November 2026, more than a year later. AFPTV obtained CTI footage of the dinner on Nov. 9, 2026, according to VnExpress. What the two said at the table has not been reported, so the night shows that they were close and nothing more. It shows no exchange of business or secrets.

Every meeting and honor that followed came after cameras had already recorded this evening.

In the fall of 2025, the suspicion that Alice Huang was a spy reportedly reached the president himself, and he took no action at the time, according to ABC News. From that point, the government was on notice. Before, people could treat the worry about the woman close to Jensen Huang as talk circulating around Washington. Now the president had heard it, and the White House took no step against Huang in response. Leaving things as they were was itself a decision. Trump left his ties to an executive who had his ear on chip policy toward China unchanged while a serious question about Alice Huang remained unanswered.

The warning that reached Trump was a belief held by sources, not an established finding that any spying took place. Officials faced a suspicion, with no evidence of a crime before them. That leaves two ways to understand the inaction. A president could reasonably decide that an unproven belief did not justify cutting off one of the country's most important executives. He could also have set aside a security concern to protect a relationship that was valuable to his administration. Nothing on the public record shows which reading is correct. Either way, the central question changes here. People no longer need to ask whether anyone raised the concern. They need to ask what was done with it once it reached the top.

From this season on, every new sign of favor toward Huang has to be read against a warning that, by the sources' account, the president had already heard. That is why this quiet season, in which no decision was announced, is among the most important in the story.

Nvidia $NVDA collected its clearest reward from its ties to the White House on this date. President Donald Trump personally cleared Nvidia's H200 chip for sale in China, according to CNBC, so the company won access to that market from the president rather than through an agency ruling. ABC News reports that the U.S. government would take 25% of those sales. Even with that cut, Nvidia's stock gained about 2% in trading after the market closed. From then on, Washington held a direct financial stake in Nvidia's China business, and the company's interests and the government's were tied together.

Trump made the promise before the rules were written, while the Commerce Department was still finalizing the details, according to CNBC. The concession had a ceiling. The H200 outranks the H20, but Nvidia makes better chips than either. The deal's main safeguard was a requirement that only approved customers, vetted by the Commerce Department, could buy the chip. That safeguard depended on judging who the buyers were and who stood behind them, and the president announced it after a briefing about Huang in the fall of 2025. It remains an open question whether that briefing had any bearing on how the customer rules were drawn up.

Nvidia answered the president as a company, through a spokesman rather than its founder, and presented the bargain as a national interest. "Offering H200 to approved commercial customers, vetted by the Department of Commerce, strikes a thoughtful balance that is great for America," an Nvidia spokesman said in a statement to CNBC. Rival chipmakers, AMD $AMD and Intel $INTC among them, were due to get identical terms. That made the deal a rule for the industry's China trade rather than a personal favor to Huang.

A deal this valuable to Nvidia raised the cost of any doubt about the people near its chief.

Huang's access to Washington became an official role on this date, when he took a formal seat advising the president on science and technology. Trump appointed him to the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology, according to ABC News. The White House lists him among the council's first appointments, announced March 25, 2026, which puts Huang among the people chosen to define the council from its start. The new role gives his private associations a new weight. An outside executive's associations are a problem for his company, but an adviser's associations are also a question for the administration that appointed him.

The seat placed Huang among the best-known founders and chief executives in the technology industry. He joined as Nvidia $NVDA's chief executive, and the White House names Zuckerberg, Ellison, Su, Dell $DELL, Brin and Andreessen among the other appointees and says the council can hold up to 24 members. The council had 13 members after its first appointments, so Huang was among those picked first while the rest of the roster was still being filled in public. That visibility cut both ways. On a council with seats still open, any later change in a founding member's standing would be hard to miss.

Huang's appointment was an honor, not a disclosure. It records the day he joined the council rather than any briefing, so it says nothing about what anyone in government knew at the time about the woman close to him. It shows only that the administration chose to elevate him. Whatever officials were saying about the people around Huang, the White House was moving him closer to the president, and how those two facts fit together remains the open question from here on.

On April 28, 2026, Jensen Huang appeared in public as a settled family man. He attended as a guest with his wife, Lori, and as Nvidia $NVDA's CEO, so anyone watching saw the head of the company and one half of a long marriage at the same moment. That marriage had lasted more than 40 years, according to ABC News, and the partnership on display had been part of his whole adult life. Huang's appearance that day gave outsiders no visible reason for doubt, even though warnings about the people around him were reportedly already circulating inside the government.

A marriage of four decades helped make Huang look like the steadiest kind of executive. In Washington, personal standing with the powerful counts as an asset, and that image of steadiness made him an easy person to bring close to the president and to the officials shaping chip policy toward China. His public image combined a long marriage with his record at Nvidia, and that combination raised the stakes for him. The more his standing drew on his family life, the further it could fall once questions about the people around him reached the public. A founder who looks this settled gives critics little reason to ask about his private associations, and he leaves himself more exposed if those questions arrive anyway.

Every later date on this timeline should be measured against the image of a long and steady marriage that Huang showed on April 28.

By spring 2026, the worry about the woman at Huang's side had become a written allegation that named a specific spy service. A memo that season accused Alice Huang of ties to China's Ministry of State Security, according to CNBC. The public knows of the memo only through earlier New York Times reporting described by ABC News, and no government has released it. The person named in it was a Megaspeed International executive with a business identity of her own, so the suspicion could not be dismissed as gossip about a private companion. The memo still records what officials were told to believe, not a proven case.

The warning reportedly stayed inside the government for months before news reports brought it into public view. Even now, people familiar with the memo who spoke to ABC News are the only basis for its existence, and Washington has acknowledged nothing. By their account, officials had the warning in writing by spring. Through those same months, Huang kept his job and his standing in Washington. A written warning about the woman close to him and the government's continued favor toward him existed at the same time.

A suspected state-security operative close to the head of the company whose chips sit at the center of export decisions on China is a policy problem, not a private one. The memo cannot settle whether Alice Huang worked for Chinese intelligence, and nothing in it amounts to a court finding. It can show that officials had been told she might, and roughly when they were told.

From this spring on, every honor Washington gave Huang has to be read against a warning his government reportedly already held.

Jensen Huang was a late addition to the president's trip to China. Yahoo News reports that his name was missing when the delegation was first drawn up. ABC News reports that he joined the entourage in Alaska on May 12, 2026, and Trump then told his followers on Truth Social that Huang was flying with him on Air Force One. That post put the Nvidia $NVDA chief's presence on the public record. The head of the company whose chips sit at the center of the China export question was flying with the president to China itself.

That same plane is where the warning about the woman close to Huang reportedly reached the president. Yahoo News reports that, by officials' account, Secretary of State Marco Rubio raised the subject of Alice Huang with Trump during the flight. A briefing from the secretary of state made the warning a cabinet-level matter. Trump has denied hearing about it at that time. "No, I didn't hear about it then," he said. The State Department, whose chief reportedly gave the briefing, declined to comment. That leaves the president's denial against the officials' account, with nothing on the record to settle which is true.

The timing matters beyond the affair because of who Alice Huang is. She is a Chinese national associated with Megaspeed, a Singapore-based company, according to Yahoo News. Huang reached Beijing the next day, May 13, 2026, as part of the president's delegation. If the warning was given on board, Trump heard it about a Chinese national close to Nvidia's chief while he was bringing that chief along on his visit to China.

The open question from this date is whether anyone in charge knew enough to ask why Huang was on the plane.

Trump gave Huang his most symbolic sign of favor yet when he included him at an evening built around China's own leader. President Donald Trump hosted a White House state dinner for Chinese President Xi Jinping on Sept. 24, 2026, according to ABC News. CNBC reports that it was Xi's first U.S. state visit since the Obama presidency, which made the evening a rare high point in relations between the two countries. Huang attended as Nvidia $NVDA's CEO. That gave the chipmaker at the center of the fight over which chips can be sold to China a place at the summit. The allegations about his relationship with Alice Huang did not become public until Oct. 29, so he sat at Xi's dinner more than a month before they surfaced.

The guest list showed which businesses the administration wanted in the room for its dealings with Beijing. CNBC reports that a person briefed on the preparations was unaware of any invitations to Chinese companies, even though business leaders had been expected to attend. Not every American AI company had a seat either, since the AI developer Anthropic was not on the guest list. Huang's place at the dinner was a choice the White House made, not a courtesy that every major technology company received.

The government put Huang in the room with China's leader at the height of its diplomacy with Beijing. By then, warnings about the woman close to him had reportedly already reached top officials, and those warnings did not cost him the seat. The administration's favor toward Huang continued in the most public setting it could offer.

Once the allegations surfaced, the question became what the White House knew when it seated him.

Washington's most public show of trust in Huang came as an honor from the government itself, not as a deal or a private meeting. Huang received the National Medal for Science and Technology and Innovation from President Donald Trump on Oct. 8, 2026, according to ABC News. With that medal, the government put national prestige behind the Nvidia $NVDA chief, on top of the president's personal goodwill.

The award came months after Secretary of State Marco Rubio reportedly warned Trump on May 12 about the woman at Huang's side. The disputed point is when the president actually learned of the allegations. Trump's account of that differs from New York Times reporting that he had been briefed, according to CNBC. If the reports are accurate, the president gave a national honor to a man he had already been warned about. Whatever Trump took from those briefings, the ceremony raises the question of whether anyone in the White House checked a public honor against a private security briefing.

Trump presented the medal in person, so critics now treat the ceremony as a test of his judgment as much as Huang's. "Trump has been busy trying to defend Jensen Huang when he ought to be defending our national security. It's that simple, it's obvious," Sen. Charles Schumer, D-N.Y., the Senate minority leader, said, as reported by CNBC. The charge comes from the opposition party, but the question behind it has nothing to do with party. That question is whether an honor this public was ever weighed against what the government's own security officials were reportedly saying about the people close to its recipient.

Every answer about what officials knew, in the weeks that follow, will be measured against this ceremony.

On Oct. 29, 2026, Jensen Huang's relationship with Alice Huang became public, and it turned one executive's private judgment into a question about chip policy. ABC News reports that the first public account that day described the two as personally close. Her job is what made the tie a concern. Alice Huang was an executive at Megaspeed, a chip buyer that had earlier been suspected of helping evade U.S. chip export controls, according to CNBC. A relationship that connects the head of Nvidia $NVDA to a company suspected of helping evade those rules is a policy question, whatever it means in private.

The reported worry centered on the president himself. The fear was Huang's direct line to Trump, according to New York Times reporting described by CNBC. That put an executive of a company suspected of evading export controls one personal step from the president, through the chief of the company whose chips those controls cover. The access Washington had given Huang could now serve someone else as a way to reach Trump. The open question therefore moves from Huang's conduct to what officials understood about the people around him while they kept him close.

Nvidia defended a customer and left the relationship unaddressed. CNBC reports that an Nvidia spokesperson said the claims that the regional cloud provider had diverted chips had been looked into closely and shown to be false the previous year. That answer did not mention the CEO's tie to an executive at the same firm. The company also leaned on trust in Washington. "We're confident that if the U.S. government believes that any person is a threat to American industry, it would let us know," the spokesperson said. In effect, Nvidia was saying it had received no warning.

That confidence puts the next question to the government: whether it had concerns about the people around Huang, and whether it ever passed them on.

By the end of October, officials in three governments reportedly shared a worry about the woman close to Huang. Intelligence officials in the United States, Singapore and Taiwan had grounds to suspect that Alice Huang could be a spy, according to Wall Street Journal reporting described by ABC News. When officials in three governments reach a similar view, critics cannot easily dismiss it as one administration's politics or one agency's guess. Even so, the concern is still a suspicion and not a proven case, and no one has publicly shown that she worked for Chinese intelligence.

A concern held in three countries makes the question of timing harder to avoid. Congress and the public will want to know how long American officials had been watching her while the White House kept honoring Huang. When officials in Singapore and Taiwan first formed their view has not been made public. That gap leaves the question open, and it settles nothing in Huang's favor or against him. For an executive whose business with China depended on his access to the president, a doubt shared by officials in three countries was no longer a matter of his private life.

It had become a test of the judgment Washington had been trusting on chips.

Nvidia $NVDA answered the allegations against its founder in two separate parts on the same day. The company's board opened an independent investigation on Nov. 8, ABC News reports, as Huang sent his own message to staff. CNBC reports that Huang admitted in that email that he had been unfaithful to his wife. With that admission, Huang addressed the personal side of the story in his own words. The investigation would decide whether the relationship had put anything at risk, and the board, not Huang, would deliver that answer.

The directors addressed the security question in terms that neither accused Huang nor cleared him. "While the Board has no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised, the Board's inquiry will examine all available information," Nvidia's board said in a statement, as reported by CNBC. The board offered a reassurance and promised a full review in the same sentence, so the outcome remains open. For anyone weighing what this means for chip sales to China, the central question remains unanswered. Nobody at Nvidia had yet said whether anything leaked.

Handling the private matter and the security matter separately made one result possible: Huang could keep his job while the inquiry ran. He remained CEO and was committed to continuing in the role, according to CNBC. The investigation would therefore run under the executive it was examining.

Nvidia tied Huang's future to an inquiry it had only just opened, and it could keep that position only as long as the two questions stayed apart.

President Trump stood behind Huang within a day of his confession. On Sunday, the day Huang told his staff in a memo that he had been unfaithful to his wife, Trump praised him as an honorable man, according to CNBC. Huang's answer to the gravest charge reached the public through Trump after the two men spoke by phone the next day, according to ABC News. "He said he had absolutely no idea that the person in question was a spy," Trump said at a press briefing. That denial came secondhand, in the president's words rather than Huang's. It covers only what Huang knew about the woman at his side, not what the government knew while it honored him.

Trump's backing carried less weight that Monday because his party had just lost control of Congress. Trump's relayed denial does not reach that question.

Investors reacted to the election result as well as to the affair. They drove Nvidia $NVDA shares down 12% over the two days that followed the vote, according to CNBC. For shareholders, the new Congress may matter as much as Huang's private conduct. Huang also has a public appearance he cannot easily avoid, a keynote at a Washington conference on Dec. 1, in the city where the new majority is preparing its questions.

Huang still had the president on his side, but Trump cannot answer the questions that matter most for him.
