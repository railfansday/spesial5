Masked ICE Agents Disrupt Alaska Airlines Boarding—Crew Too Shaken To Continue The Flight

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

An airport is almost the ideal place to arrest someone. The government has everyone’s travel information in advance, checks their identity, screens them for weapons, and knows where they’re going to be. There are cameras everywhere, and myriad law enforcement agencies on site.

But those systems were built to protect aviation and travelers. Repurposing them for routine immigration ambushes creates a distraction from safety.

The confrontation happened around 8 a.m. on November 21 at Seattle’s gate N9. The Port of Seattle says it involved Immigration and Customs Enforcement and two passengers, a father and daughter.

There were roughly 50 people pressed against the jet bridge walls during a confrontation lasting more than 10 minutes. A pilot left and two flight attendants asked to be replaced.

His original post is here. The flight, scheduled for 8:10 a.m., departed nearly three hours late.

Watch the jet bridge video shared by KUOW reporter Kate Stone.

An airport is a chokepoint, and the government has already done much of the work of locating and identifying a traveler.

There’s an identity check and weapons screening. Travelers funnel through controlled checkpoints and then present themselves at an assigned gate. That makes them easier to find and reduces some risks to arresting officers.

There’s already substantial law enforcement. Seattle has its own Port police department, with about 120 commissioned officers across its airport and maritime responsibilities, alongside federal agencies operating at the airport. There’s DEA, FBI, TSA, CBP, local police and more.

I’ve covered how passenger data collected for aviation security is being used to locate people for immigration arrests. There’s now a data sharing agreement between TSA and ICE. This is helpful to ICE, but adds no benefit to aviation security. A system justified by protecting passengers from terrorists and weapons is being repurposed to produce immigration arrests. That pulls crew and airport staff into confrontations, disrupts flights, and undermines trust in the security process.

A crowded jet bridge is an especially bad place to create a struggle. Passengers have little room to move. Crew attention gets diverted from boarding and safety duties. Here flight attendants were too shaken to work afterward. There was no immediate threat to justify this intervention.

In the video, a woman says, “We have a work permit.” Work authorization and lawful immigration status are separate issues. A person can have permission to work while an immigration case remains unresolved. While work permits have historically been issued pending adjudication of status, this administration acted on the status issue alone.

But that doesn’t justify a jet bridge ambush. If someone has a valid work permit, the government has already dealt with them through a formal process and authorized their employment. Someone traveling openly under their own identity on a domestic ticket isn’t demonstrating an intent to disappear.

Absent a specific danger or concrete reason to expect flight from the legal process, there’s a much stronger case for arranging an interview or pursuing the case through ordinary procedures than for a physical confrontation among boarding passengers. The government’s choice to treat they’ve even authorized to work as an emergency is patently ridiculous.

What stands out in Seattle is the reported combination of a struggle in a packed jet bridge, passengers being deplaned, crew members needing replacements, and an airline and airport publicly questioning whether agreed procedures were even being followed.

The Port of Seattle explains that federal agencies have broad airport access. It says its police don’t assist civil immigration enforcement.

We are concerned by this morning’s law enforcement action during the boarding of Alaska Flight 1306. We recognize how distressing this incident has been for employees and guests.

The airline also says it received no advance notice and is examining what happened and whether proper protocols were followed. Its statement continues:

We will work with the Port of Seattle and government authorities to ensure the collaborative standards and practices that have kept our air system safe and secure for decades are respected moving forward.

We are outraged about this morning’s incident involving ICE and two passengers as travelers boarded AS 1306 to Los Angeles. Immigration enforcement actions at SEA are conducted without the Port’s participation or knowledge. The Port has continually asked, and local federal immigration authorities have previously agreed to protocols at SEA, and we are in conversation with DHS on whether any of these protocols were breached. However, the Port remains committed to the safety of all of our travelers and staff and airline team. This incident was not only distressing for the affected family but the passengers around them, and Port officials continue to engage federal officials to understand more about what occurred.
