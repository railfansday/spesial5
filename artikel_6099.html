Qatar Airways Adds 1st Ever Airbus A321LR Routes: See Map Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Qatar Airways has not received its first Airbus A321LR yet. The latest information is that it'll be delivered at the end of 2026, which is just a few weeks away. Given this, the oneworld member has disclosed its first two routes on the new variant.

Each week, airlines from around the world submit schedule changes to OAG and Cirium Diio. In the latest update, Qatar Airways disclosed that its first two A321LR routes are due to be from Doha to both Vienna and Zurich, but things could change.

While subject to the exact date on which the initial airframe arrives in Doha and entry into service procedures, the first passenger-carrying A321LR service is due to take place on January 4, 2027. Running daily, the Gulf giant initially plans to fly the 160-seat variant to Vienna.

On January 4, the A321LR will be on QR185 from Doha and QR186 back home. Thereafter, it is entirely scheduled on QR189 to Austria and QR190 back, as outlined below. It'll replace the 132-seat and far less fuel-efficient A320ceo on those specific flight numbers on the route, which covers 2,144 nautical miles (3,971 km).

Then there's Zurich. The new variant in the airline's fleet is due to debut in Switzerland on January 16, with a daily service. As such, the airline anticipates at least two aircraft deliveries by then. Replacing the 283-seat A350-900, it'll help the airline to right-size capacity with demand. This approach should improve fare, yield, and load factor performance. It'll be the first time Qatar Airways has flown a narrowbody to Zurich since 2013.

After a long-drawn-out battle, Qatar Airways confirmed its order for 50 A321neos — including ten A321LRs — in 2019. The LR will be the carrier's first narrowbody equipment with the latest cabins and lie-flat beds (a so-called mini-QSuite). Etihad Airways' A321LRs, which have two seats in first class and 14 in business, entered service in August 2025.

Looking at the share of premium seats among different operators is a bit unfair as it is not a like-for-like comparison. Nonetheless, if premium economy is stripped out, Qatar Airways' 10% is similar to other full-service operators, such as Gulf Air (just under 10%), Aer Lingus (just under 9%) and TAP Air Portugal (just over 9%). It is a notable increase on ITA Airways (a bit over 7%), which uses the same platform as the Gulf giant.

Let's narrow the focus to January to June 2027. In this period, many Gulf airlines will fly narrowbody equipment to Europe. There have been intriguing developments recently. For example, Saudia has applied for slots at Edinburgh, and seemingly intends to use the 110-seat A320ceo. Elsewhere, SalamAir just began Muscat-Baghdad-Copenhagen service on the A320neo, replacing Oman Air's Boeing 737 MAX 8s. Etihad started flying to Luxembourg last month.

From Doha To These European Destinations (January-June 2027)*

Ankara, Antalya, Athens, Belgrade, Bodrum, Bucharest, Budapest, Istanbul Airport, Istanbul Sabiha Gökçen, Larnaca, Malta, Mykonos, Sarajevo, Trabzon, Vienna, Zagreb

In the first half of 2027, Qatar Airways plans a total of 1,587 departures on narrowbody equipment from Doha to Europe (including Turkey, as IATA includes the nation within that region). Such aircraft will account for 11% of the carrier's offering to the continent, which is a substantially smaller proportion than Gulf Air (73%) and Etihad (35%). Qatar Airways flew the A321ceo to Europe until 2022, a year after the last A319 operation. Between 2014 and 2016, the A319 even flew to London Heathrow with all-business seats.
