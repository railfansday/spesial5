Emirates Pokes Fun At US’ Aviation Disaster From Ground Stop & Microsoft Outage

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Dubai-based Emirates has taken a poke at the US aviation disaster that unfolded this week, posting on its social media channels that 'the skies are always open'. The original post, which included a lighthearted nod to the Microsoft Outlook and cloud outage, was then followed up with a 'was it something we said?'

This comes as, on Friday, a rare nationwide ground stop was issued by the US Federal Aviation Administration after an L3Harris router malfunction stopped the FAA's air traffic control network in its tracks, forcing thousands of aircraft to be grounded and preventing flights from departing to, from, or within the US.

The post, on X, has racked up 613,000 views at the time of writing and continues to increase. The Middle Eastern airline has shared this across other social media channels, but has only reached 2,400 likes on Facebook.

Due to the four-hour delay in US flight operations, this had a ripple effect on airlines around the world, grounding US-bound services. This affected several Emirates flights, from Dubai to destinations across America. While flights already airborne were permitted to land, those still at the gate were forced to wait until final clearance was provided, and services resumed at 8:15 PM EST on Friday.

Emirates services were eventually able to operate to the US, with slight delays on some flights. There were no blanket cancellations or major disruptions to US-bound services. In the US, thousands of flights were canceled on Friday and Saturday, with delays expected to linger in the coming days as local airlines return to normal operations and clear the passenger backlog.

Experienced by airlines around the globe, flight departures were marginally delayed until clearance was given for services to resume. This had no major impact on non-UAE flight operations, and neither Emirates nor neighboring Etihad Airways or Qatar Airways saw any major displacement of aircraft or crew, unlike the thousands of canceled flights and crew timeouts experienced in the US.

Simple Flying reached out to Emirates for comment on the Microsoft Outage and ground stop; however, an airline spokesperson could not immediately be reached.

A ground stop of this magnitude in the US has not been seen in more than a quarter century. The last time the FAA ordered a nationwide halt to all flight departures was briefly in January 2023, due to a malfunction in its Notice to Air Missions system (NOTAM). The last major ground stop was in 2001, following the September 11 terrorist attacks.

"Your email might be down, but the skies are always open" was a light-hearted jab at the Microsoft Outlook and cloud outage that disrupted millions of users of Microsoft cloud-based services and software suites worldwide. This affected Microsoft 365, Outlook, Teams and Copilot users.

The multi-region technical failure knocked out key collaboration and email tools for millions of users across 74 countries. Microsoft confirmed this was not a cyber attack, security breach, or AI malfunction, but instead stemmed from a technical issue that was introduced during a network capacity upgrade. Microsoft's services were fully restored later on Thursday, but many were left in the lurch without access to these vital software systems. The global outage indirectly affected Emirates by disrupting the airline's corporate communications and internal office and email tools, while also hampering customer support channels, as confirmed by Gulf Business.

Emirates is a large international airline that operates a robust network of international flights from its home at Dubai International Airport (DXB). The airline operates a fleet of Airbus A350s, A380s, and Boeing 777s. It is the world's largest operator of the A380 and 777, and handles more than 3,600 flights per week, in excess of 500 per day, to and from the Middle East, and several unique fifth-freedom routes.
