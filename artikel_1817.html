Scott Parker's baptism of fire: West Ham fight back from three goals down to earn draw at Cardiff in new manager's first match in charge

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Published: 18:32 EST, 4 November 2026 | Updated: 03:18 EST, 5 November 2026

Scott Parker's West Ham launched a thrilling comeback from three goals down to draw 3-3 at Cardiff in his first game in charge.

Jack Moylan’s first hat-trick in English football had Cardiff in cruise control at half-time and the travelling fans were furious in the away end.

But the Hammers – inspired by captain Jarrod Bowen – fought back in incredible fashion as Divine Mukasa, Arne Engels and Max Kilman netted to secure a point for the new boss.

Parker was confirmed as Hammers manager on Wednesday morning – just 24 hours after Nuno Espirito Santo was sacked.

Nuno was axed after a run of poor performances and the club opted to bring in a familiar face, with Parker having made 129 appearances for them as a player between 2007-2011.

It was a tough watch for the former England midfielder initially as Moylan had Cardiff 3-0 ahead inside 39 minutes.

Scott Parker took charge of his first West Ham game after Nuno Espirito Santo's sacking

Max Kilman is seen scoring the dramatic equaliser to make it 3-3 against Cardiff

Moylan scored a hat-trick on his Republic of Ireland debut against Grenada in May, but this was his first club treble since scoring three for Shelbourne in May 2023.

The draw extends Cardiff’s unbeaten home league run to 10 games over the last two seasons, while West Ham occupy the final play-off place in eighth.

Nuno’s 13-month tenure had ended after the Hammers had won just twice in six matches and lost successive home games to Southampton and West Brom.

Taty Castellanos had hinted at better times ahead, forcing Nathan Trott into early action after Bowen’s effort had deflected to him.

But the Hammers were behind after Alex Robertson found Moylan in the 11th minute. Moylan advanced to find the top corner of Mads Hermansen’s net with a ferocious strike from 20 yards.

Hermansen tipped aside Calum Scanlon’s cross and Will Fish headed over as Cardiff probed. There was no reprieve for West Ham when Perry Ng curled a ball over a static defence after 36 minutes.

Moylan cleverly slowed down for Morato to bundle him over, a clear penalty.

Cardiff had missed their last two spot-kicks, but this time Moylan was entrusted with the responsibility and sent Hermansen the wrong way.

Angry Hammers fans responded with cries of ‘you’re not fit to wear the shirt’, and many of them appeared to be making a swift departure three minutes later.

Jarrod Bowen was a key man for the Hammers as they staged a remarkable recovery

Joel Bagan’s corner cleared Fish at the near post, but Moylan came in behind him to head home his sixth goal in five games, and his 10th of the season.

Parker sent on Mohamadou Kante and Joel Piroe for Soungoutou Magassa and Castellanos at half-time and there was instantly more conviction from the Hammers.

Bowen floated a delicious pass through after 58 minutes, and Mukasa lifted the ball over Trott and in off a post.

The deficit was reduced further seven minutes later when Piroe flicked on Bowen’s pass and Engels slid home his first Hammers goal.

Kante nearly toe poked the ball past Trott before Kilman touched home Bowen’s brilliant cross, and West Ham almost won it through substitutes Ezra Mayers and Tomas Soucek.
