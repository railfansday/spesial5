This clever tool fixes the biggest blind spot of Google Photos

Photo processing, reinvented

The tool I want to share with you today has an extra-rare distinction of (a) enhancing an app many of us already know and love, and (b) being a newly expanded version of something you may have actually heard about before.

➜ It’s called Batch​, and the reason it might sound familiar is that we talked about it in these very same Cool Tools quarters​ a few months back.

At the time, the app had just launched with the simple sole purpose of letting you apply the same edits to a bunch of images in your Google Photos collection at once—in a way that’s incredibly practical and oddly impossible with Photos by itself.

☝️ This week, Batch is adding a bunch of extra features that make it an even more useful and indispensable Google Photos power-up.

⌚ They’ll take you a few minutes to explore and start using.

Batch, in case you don’t recall, is a completely browser-extension-driven tool that adds extra features into Photos without ever accessing any of your data or uploading your images to any server. It works entirely in your own browser, on any computer you’re using, for complete privacy protection—and it looks and acts just like part of Photos itself, as added buttons and options within the standard Photos website​.

Now, in addition to the bulk editing business, Batch can do a bunch of other gap-filling Photos feats—most notably using AI (again, only locally, on your own device) to look through an entire album of images, group similar shots, and then objectively rate the image quality and expressions of everyone pictured to help you find the best 25, 100, or however many you want from the bunch.

You can use Batch’s “Pick for me” default approach, or customize the way it works to your own exact preferences. 

From there, Batch shows you exactly which images it selected for you and why, and you can either accept its suggestions or fine-tune and adjust as you see fit. 

Then, it’s another couple of clicks to use Batch’s original Photos-integrated tools for enhancing all the images you’ve selected (or any other series of images in your collection) in the same simple way—something Photos itself has never made possible. 

You can even save the exact set of edits you like as a custom preset for future use, if you really want to get fancy. Everything Batch does is always easily undoable, too, in case you ever change your mind.

Batch is free for up to 25 bulk edits per month. Its more advanced features, including the best shot selection, require a $7-per-month or $55-per-year “Plus” plan.

If you take as many photos as I do and find yourself spending far too much time trying to go through ’em all, it might just be a Photos upgrade worth making. And even if you don’t make that leap, at its free level, there’s an awful lot to like and put to good use.

  • Batch works entirely in your browser​, within the Google Photos website, via its desktop Chrome extension​ (which can also be installed in any other Chromium-compatible browser).
  • It’s free to use on a limited level, with the full set of features requiring a $7 monthly or $55 annual plan.
  • The tool doesn’t store or see any of your photos or personal info, and everything it does happens locally on your own device.
