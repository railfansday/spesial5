Rethinking the Scruffing Debate: Dr. Karyn and Nicole discuss how complete anti-scruffing rules miss the mark on practical care. They explain that learning safe restraint acts as harm reduction, letting you handle fast tasks like pulling out a thorn or trimming claws without having to resort to full sedation.

Test Sensitivity First: Before going in for a hold, Dr. Karyn recommends checking the neck area with gentle pets and tickles. If a cat flinches or pulls away (like her cat, Zelda), you should never try to scruff them.

Skip the "Pinch": The most common mistake people make is grabbing a tiny pinch of skin, which just annoys the cat and provokes a fight. Proper restraint needs a full, firm hand on the scruff so the cat feels secure and can't wriggle free.

Never Pick Them Up by the Scruff: The hosts emphasize that while a hand on the scruff helps keep a cat grounded, you should never lift or carry an adult cat this way.
