U2 Performs Posthumous Duet With Late Dolly Parton at NFL Game

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

U2 paid tribute to the late Dolly Parton with a posthumous duet at the NFL’s Thanksgiving Eve game.

The Irish rockers projected archival footage and audio of the country music icon for a hybrid live performance of their collaborative track “Torn” during halftime of the Green Bay Packers and Los Angeles Rams’ Wednesday, November 25, game at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, California.

In between their ’80s hits “Sunday Bloody Sunday” and “Where the Streets Have No Name,” U2 joined a worldwide Netflix audience in celebrating the life and legacy of Parton, who died at age 80 in August following a brief battle with cancer.

“Torn” is thought to be the final song Parton recorded, having worked on the track with Bono in Nashville only two months before her death. The gripping ballad posits the hope of reconciliation in a family divided over their differences.

“I can’t deny this passion and desire / But I know it has to burn me standing this close to the fire,” Parton sings. “What am I supposed to do? And how am I supposed to choose? / I love ’em both, and I just feel so torn.”

The track was officially released on U2’s 16th studio album, Carnaval de Luz, earlier this month. Parton shares a writing credit on “Torn” with U2 and Snow Patrol’s Johnny McDaid.

“When she got ill, she got out of her bed to sing this song,” Bono, 66, told Rolling Stone in August. “And you can hear that. And I can’t even listen to it now.”

The musician revealed that he initially reached out to Parton about potentially collaborating on a completely separate project.

“She said, ‘That’s a little too Irish for me,’” he recalled. “But she said, ‘I got this song. It’s called ‘Torn,’ and do you wanna see where you can take it somewhere else, maybe?’”

U2 producer Garret “Jacknife” Lee told the outlet that Dolly “loved” the finished version of “Torn,” adding that she was “so proud of her vocal and the recording.”

“She was thrilled with it,” Lee, 59, noted. “We were very nervous that she might not approve or would be uncomfortable with it, but she loved it.”

Bono remembered being blown away that Parton delivered such a powerful and haunting vocal performance considering her declining health at the time.

“She sang her heart out, and I cannot believe that’s an 80-year-old woman,” Bono told Rolling Stone. “It’s very moving to have had these intimacies with such a figure. The fact that they’re not there now, in any way you can call back, other than through her songs, is very sobering.”

Before the release of “Torn,” Parton’s most recent studio album, Rockstar, debuted in November 2023. That record featured rock collaborations with surviving Beatles Paul McCartney and Ringo Starr, Judas Priest frontman Rob Halford and even a cover of her goddaughter Miley Cyrus‘ hit “Wrecking Ball.”
