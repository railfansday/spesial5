Parker Tells West Ham Players An Uncomfortable Championship Truth

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

West Ham United manager Scott Parker has identified defensive positioning, transitions and set pieces as areas he intends to improve following the international break. Speaking to talkSPORT this morning, the Hammers boss explained why his team must become more disciplined without sacrificing their attacking ambition.

West Ham are the Championship’s highest scorers, but their defensive record has repeatedly undermined their attacking performances. Parker acknowledged that committing players forward can leave his side exposed, particularly when possession changes hands, and stressed the importance of what his players do behind the ball.

Personally, I’ve always thought there were some relatively easy wins for Parker in terms of fixing West Ham’s defensive problems. We’re scoring plenty of goals; we just need to sort the defence out. And if there’s one thing Parker has demonstrated during his managerial career, it’s that he knows how to organise a team defensively.

During his time at Burnley, Parker oversaw one of the most remarkable defensive campaigns in English football history.

His side conceded just 16 goals across 46 Championship matches during the 2024/25 season, setting a new record and keeping 30 clean sheets on their way to promotion.

Speaking in his talkSPORT interview, Parker discussed the importance of positioning when West Ham commit players forward, explaining that transitions and set pieces are particularly dangerous in the Championship.

And it’s interesting to hear him targeting transitions because that was exactly how West Ham conceded the opening goal against Blackburn Rovers in our last game.

Parker has informed West Ham players that they are now Championship players whether they like it or not

But perhaps the most interesting part of Parker’s interview was his frank assessment of the mentality required from players who have dropped out of the Premier League.

He pointed out that the demands of the Championship are completely different, with players needing to accept the physical and psychological challenges of the division.

Parker then delivered a particularly pointed observation.

“If there’s a player here at this present moment in time playing in this division, it’s because that’s where he is.”

It was a way of saying that irrespective of where you come from, it’s where you are that counts.

And no doubt there are players in the West Ham squad who consider themselves to be Premier League, European or international quality.

However, as of right now, they are Championship players, and Parker is quite right to point that out.

The new gaffer has been promoted from this division three times, so I suspect he knows rather more about what is required than some of the players who have spent most of their careers in the Premier League.

Of course, the international break has given Parker a welcome opportunity to work with his players, although it’s important to remember that ten were away representing their countries.

Nevertheless, he will have learned considerably more about those who remained at Rush Green.

In particular, players such as full-backs Ezra Mayers and Joël Veltman have had the opportunity to work with the new manager, while a good number of West Ham’s midfielders have also remained at the club.

That should have allowed Parker to concentrate on some of the defensive positioning and tactical habits he discussed during the interview.

And let’s not forget that he’ll also have Thursday and Friday’s training sessions to work with his returning internationals ahead of Preston.

That is considerably more preparation time than he enjoyed before his opening two matches against Cardiff and Blackburn.
