Scott Parker could reverse a West Ham decision that angered Nuno's bosses

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

A new era is underway at West Ham United following the appointment of Scott Parker as the club's new head coach.

After spending four years at the club as a player, Parker returned to West Ham as head coach when he replaced Nuno Espirito Santo last week, but it has not been the start to his tenure at the London Stadium that he would have hoped.

Parker's reign at West Ham got off to a nightmare start as his side found themselves 3-0 down at half time in his first game in charge at Cardiff City last Wednesday night, but they produced a stunning second half comeback to draw the game 3-3.

If Parker was hoping that the Hammers would carry that momentum into their next match, that certainly did not prove to be the case as they slumped to a disappointing 2-1 defeat at Blackburn Rovers on Saturday.

West Ham head into the November international break sitting in ninth in the Championship table, one point outside the play-off places and six points from the automatic promotion spots, and Parker will be keen to pick up his first victory at the third attempt when bottom side Preston North End visit the London Stadium next week.

Given his previous connection to the club, and the fact that he has won promotion from the Championship on three occasions with Fulham, Bournemouth and Burnley, it is easy to see why the Hammers turned to Parker after parting company with Nuno, and the 46-year-old may have an early opportunity to impress his new bosses.

While West Ham were widely expected to win the Championship title this season, there was plenty of shock at the club's brutal decision to sack Nuno last week, despite sitting just four points from the automatic promotion places at the time of his exit.

Back-to-back home defeats to two fellow promotion hopefuls in Southampton and West Bromwich Albion were the final straw for the Hammers' hierarchy, but it seems they may have had growing doubts about Nuno for some time, including some of his selection calls.

West Ham signed midfielder Edson Alvarez from Ajax for a fee of around £35 million in August 2023, but after he spent last season on loan with Turkish giants Fenerbahçe, many assumed he would leave the London Stadium this summer following the club's relegation.

It was, therefore, something of a surprise when Alvarez failed to score a move away before the end of the summer window, but despite remaining in East London, he was almost entirely overlooked by Nuno, making just one EFL Cup appearance and being left out of all of his Championship matchday squads.

Another midfielder who found himself out in the cold under Nuno was Soungoutou Magassa, who made 26 appearances in his debut campaing with the Hammers last season after arriving from French outfit Monaco for a fee of around £17.3 million.

Magassa did not make a single league or cup appearance under Nuno prior to his depature last week, but, unlike Alvarez, he was included in a handful of matchday squads in the Championship.

It seems that Nuno's bizarre selection calls involving Alvarez and Magassa did not go down well with the West Ham hierarchy, with The Guardian revealing that his reluctance to use the latter in particular had caused "disquiet" at the club, both because his absence meant that he was being "devalued" and a vulnerable defence was "going without enough protection".

While West Ham are the top scorers in the Championship, having netted 37 goals so far this season, they have also conceded 23 goals, which is the most of any side in the top half of the table other than 12th-placed Norwich City.

Under-fire goalkeeper Mads Hermansen and the Hammers' fragile backline have come under plenty of scrutiny in recent weeks, but there is no doubt that Nuno's refusal to play a holding midfielder significantly contributed to his side's defensive woes and his own personal downfall.

Nuno may have understandably been disappointed that Alvarez and Magassa both sought exits from the London Stadium this summer, but after both were still present at the club when the transfer window closed, he should have made use of the pair.

Alvarez has spent much of his career at the top level of European football, while he made four appearances for Mexico at this summer's World Cup, including in England's famous 3-2 victory at the Azteca in July, so the 29-year-old would surely be capable of doing a job in the Championship.

Magassa struggled to make an impact at times during his first year at West Ham last season, but he was far from the only player to underperform as the club were relegated from the Premier League, and it is difficult not to feel that a player of his potential could also have a role to play in the second tier.

Hammers fans have long been calling for Alvarez and Magassa to be given a chance to solve their side's midfield problems, and with the club's hierarchy seemingly sharing that view, Parker should focus on reintegrating the duo over the international break.
