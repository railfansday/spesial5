Celtic support group issues 'have a look' call over Dermot Desmond's stake

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Celtic support group Celtic Supporters Limited has called for the Takeover Panel to examine issues surrounding Dermot Desmond’s shareholding in the club.

The group wants the body to look at the circumstances around Desmond’s stake and whether questions over influence and control merit further examination.

Speaking on the Let Me Be Frank podcast, Celtic Supporters Limited chairman Duncan Smillie stressed the support group was not accusing Desmond of wrongdoing, but believes there are grounds for the Takeover Panel to look at the matter.

Smillie said: “If a single shareholder takes their stake in a company above 30 per cent... they are obliged to make an offer for all the shares in a company. That happened to Dave King at Rangers.

“Dermot, in 2005... bought his shares pretty much at a third of the market price... took his shareholding up to 34 per cent or 35 per cent. That then triggers the Takeover Panel. One of the main things you have to guarantee is that you will not try to exert control on that club.

“We at CSL are not making an accusation; we’re not saying he’s done something he shouldn’t have done.

“All we’re asking the Takeover Panel is to have a look at this, because there’s some anecdotal and some real evidence that he has exerted control over the club.”

The intervention comes as Celtic Supporters Limited prepares to raise wider concerns over the club’s shareholding structure and the number of supporters who have become disconnected from their shares.

CSL director Brian McLaughlin said: “There are 37,000 shareholders in Celtic PLC, and many of them have become disconnected from their shares over the years... died, moved house, whatever it may be.

“They were given a piece of paper in 1994. That piece of paper could be anywhere, in the loft, in the bin, you know.

“Our AGM [resolution] is quite simply tasking Celtic... to go to the company that is the registrar of the shareholding... and say: reunite all our shareholders with their shares. Put the power that they’ve got back in their hands.”

The support group believes reconnecting shareholders with their stakes is important to restoring the balance between supporter and corporate ownership.

McLaughlin added: “When Fergus [McCann] sold his shares, it was always about having a balance between the fan shareholding and the corporate shareholding.

“And that balance is gone. It's corporate shareholding now. Between Desmond and Lindsell Train, they're well above 50 per cent.”

Smillie believes the number of disconnected shareholders can also affect how influential a major shareholder is in practical terms.

He said: “When you’ve got 100 per cent of your shares out there, and you’re a principal shareholder like Dermot Desmond is... let’s say you’ve got 35 per cent for argument's sake. If you’ve got 35 per cent of 100 per cent, that’s a genuine 35 per cent.

“But if there’s a whole chunk of those missing and you actually have 35 per cent of 80 per cent or 85 per cent, you start to have an even more dominant position.

“So it’s maybe not in his personal interest... I think the club like that everybody’s detached.”

Celtic Supporters Limited will now seek to put the share-register issue firmly on the agenda ahead of Celtic’s upcoming AGM.
