Mastercard vs. S&P Global: Which Financial Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

One operates a scalable 45% net-margin payments network; the other relies on capital-market demand. Their valuations tell starkly different stories.

Should investors bet on the expansion of global digital payments or the steady demand for essential financial intelligence? Deciding between Mastercard (MA -1.66%) and S&P Global (SPGI -1.92%) involves choosing between two market leaders.

Mastercard maintains a massive electronic network that facilitates commerce across hundreds of currencies and territories. S&P Global provides the data analytics and credit ratings that corporations and investors rely on for transparency. Both companies occupy dominant positions, yet they play very different roles in the modern financial system.

In its latest annual report, filed for the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, Mastercard described its role as a technology company connecting a vast ecosystem. It provides virtual card technology and payment solutions for financial institutions, merchants, and governments. Key partners include issuing and acquiring banks, which manage the direct relationships with account holders worldwide.

In fiscal 2025, revenue reached nearly $32.8 billion, representing growth of approximately 16.4% over the previous year. Net income was close to $15 billion, demonstrating the company's ability to turn high volumes into profit. This resulted in a net margin of roughly 45.6%, highlighting the efficiency inherent in its scalable network business model.

As of Mastercard's December 2025 balance sheet, the current ratio -- which measures the ability to cover short-term debts with current assets -- is approximately 1. The debt-to-equity ratio of nearly 2.5 compares total debt, including short-term and long-term liabilities, to the value of shareholder equity. Free cash flow, which equals cash from operations minus capital expenditures, was roughly $16.4 billion.

S&P Global provides credit ratings, benchmarks, and data analytics to the global capital and commodity markets. As a prominent name among financial stocks, the company offers benchmarks that investors use to track performance and risk. In its latest annual report, filed for fiscal 2025, it noted its mobility segment provides specialized data to automotive manufacturers and suppliers.

For fiscal 2025, the company reported revenue of nearly $15.3 billion, an increase of approximately 7.9% from the prior year. Net income for the year reached $4.5 billion, supporting a net margin of roughly 29.2%. While Mastercard's growth is steady, it remains sensitive to the total volume of debt issuance in global markets.

According to its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.5, indicating the company uses less debt relative to its equity. The current ratio is roughly 0.8. Free cash flow for fiscal 2025 was nearly $5.5 billion.

Mastercard faces intense competition from traditional networks like Visa (V -1.20%) and American Express (AXP -0.76%), alongside fintechs and government-backed digital infrastructure. It also deals with legal risks regarding interchange fees and regulations on cross-border payments. The company must defend against cybersecurity threats that could disrupt its transaction switching systems or compromise proprietary data.

S&P Global is heavily dependent on the health of capital markets, as a slowdown in debt issuance reduces demand for its ratings business. It also faces risks from the planned separation of its mobility business and the need for constant innovation in artificial intelligence. The company competes with other global data and analytics providers while managing complex international regulations and legal oversight.

S&P Global trades at a lower forward P/E than its peer, while the P/S ratio for Mastercard reflects its significantly higher net margin.

I view these companies as bets on two areas: consumers and markets. Mastercard benefits when people are spending more freely, because it helps boost the fees the payments giant charges financial institutions. If consumer spending slows, Mastercard could be affected, but spending won't disappear altogether.

S&P Global relies very heavily on the markets. Participants in the global capital and commodities markets turn to the company for its credit ratings, benchmarks, and data analytics. That reliance puts S&P Global in a tight spot if there's a slowdown in any of the segments it serves. And market downturns can persist -- sometimes not recovering for years. Additionally, S&P Global's net margin isn't nearly as high as Mastercard's.

Over the past decade, Mastercard has outperformed S&P Global by more than 200 percentage points. Of course, we can't know whether that pattern will continue, but that chart speaks for itself. I think Mastercard is the more attractive stock here.
