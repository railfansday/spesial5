SHT Days 18, 19, 20: Plugging along SOBO, some SOLO

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Yep, today was a nasty weather day. Having made my decision to back off miles a bit, Bill and I decided to sightsee more by car, take a short day-hike, and make a stop at another brewery.

We drove north of Grand Marais on Highway 61 (a scenic drive on its own) to Judge Magney State Park. I wanted Bill to see Devil’s Kettle Falls. I had been here last year, on that fateful day of my knee injury. Here’s a link to my final post on that day from last year- https://thetrek.co/sht-day-7-journey-to-the-center-of-the-earth/ I wanted to see it again as well, to remember it on better terms.

We piled on some layers, including our rain jackets, and hiked up the mile from the trailhead. The falls were just as cool as I remember, one half disappearing into oblivion, and the other side cascading down toward Lake Superior. Then again were the stairs, though they were much easier to tackle this time around, with a functioning knee and no backpack. We took our pictures and admired the falls together before returning to the trailhead.

We decided to while away the afternoon at Voyageur Brewing Company in Grand Marais. It was pretty busy. It seems everyone else in the area had the same idea to escape the weather. Families with children were even here, parents playing games with their kids at the tables.

The brewery in Grand Marais. I’ve been here before, on a trip to Isle Royale National Park.

We had a few drinks and appetizers, and enjoyed some local, northern Minnesota grub. I made use of the Wi-Fi to help my parents with an issue back at home, and eventually we headed back to camp.

Sampling the local fare: smoked whitefish from just a few feet away in Lake Superior.

I forgot to mention that we had moved to a new campground at this point. I had made a reservation well in advance for Cascade State Park, and we got ourselves packed up and moved there last night. I had reserved this campsite assuming I’d be moving along northward on the SHT, and while I was no longer doing that exactly, we absolutely loved Cascade State Park and it was a great decision to stay here for the remainder of our vacation.

Today the weather was marginally better than yesterday, but I was itching to get back on trail again. I planned to move SOBO on trail from here on, to close the gap between Judge Magney State Park and the Highway 1 trailhead near Tettegouche State Park.

This meant that today I would be re-doing a few miles from Judge Magney south toward Grand Marais. There was no way around it, as last year I bailed out on a private road. No matter, it’s just a few miles, and if my memory serves me correctly, with a properly working knee the miles should be easy.

Bill wanted to hike with me today as the trail makes its one and only trip to the beach of Lake Superior in this section. We shuttled one vehicle to the trailhead on County Road 14, and together headed farther north back to Judge Magney. The first couple of miles truly were easy, and memories from last year were flooding back. Bill couldn’t believe I hiked this far on a bum knee. He remembers how much pain I was in when I came home last year.

I loved the vibrant green moss on the trail today.

The low-lying vegetation was the star of the show this morning.

Eventually we popped out of the trees and crossed Highway 61. Just on the other side was the beach! There was Lake Superior in all her glory. She was angry today though, splashing cold waves and mist into the gray skies. We had lunch on some rocks on the shoreline. We had the brilliant idea to heat up some chicken noodle soup and bring it in a thermos today. Man, did that hit the spot on a day like today!

And there she is! Lake Superior! I’ve been seeing it in the distance for 200 miles now, and finally get to walk next to it.

Reaching to touch the water, just to say I did. There’s no way any more than a hand is getting in there today. Lake Superior is freezing cold all year round.

Our cozy lunch spot, enjoying hot soup and watching the waves.

Today was not a good day to enjoy the lake, as it was so cold and windy, but it did make for some amazing waves. We enjoyed watching and hearing them roar as we had lunch, and walked along the beach portion of the SHT for the next 1.5 miles. To be honest, this beach walk was the most difficult mileage of the day. The loose pebbles made it hard for our footsteps to make purchase, and I was happy to return to firm ground.

Retreating inland to the quiet and relative warmth of the woods, we continued on. We briefly climbed up away from the lake and highway as we made our way to the Kadunce River. We crossed the river on a nice bridge and finished out the last few miles to retrieve our vehicle. Today was 11 total miles from the large trailhead at Judge Magney to the tiny trailhead off Cook County Road 14.

Today, under better weather conditions, we were going to finish the miles to the town of Grand Marais.  We parked a vehicle at the large Pincushion Trailhead just outside of Grand Marais and together drove north back to County Road 14. Together we hiked south back to Pincushion.

We crossed a couple of damaged bridges to start our day, and the first four miles were incredibly rooty and rocky. I was actually glad Bill got to experience this, as roots and rocks were pretty normal for this trail. He’s had fairly easy terrain on his day hikes with me so far. Now he knows what it’s usually like out here!

The bridge is out over Cliff Creek. I did not even attempt to cross this one since the creek is so low and I could just hop across it.

Another bridge out over Durfee Creek. Despite the massive tree on top, the bridge looked pretty structurally sound and we both gingerly walked across it anyway.

We gently climbed for a few miles up and over Wildflower Hill before coming right back down alongside Woods Creek. We had lunch at the Woods Creek Campsite before entering a wonderful forest of giant pines after the Lindskog Road Trailhead. It was as if we entered a new country. The scenery in here was incredible. Combined with the views of the Devil Track River, and this afternoon’s hiking was truly amazing.

The brand new, just finished days ago, bridge over the Devil Track River.

Catching a glimpse of falls through the trees.

I still had gas left in the tank, so to speak, so after we reached Pincushion, I decided to tackle some additional mileage while the getting was good. I had Bill shuttle me over to where the trail crosses Old Ski Hill Road and I hiked BACK (NOBO) to Pincushion Trailhead again. This way I could take advantage of going downhill (great decision!), and Bill could run to the grocery store in town for dinner provisions. Just as I was crossing the Gunflint Trail, which is a bit of a misnomer as it is a major paved highway out of Grand Marais, Bill happened to drive by on his way back to Pincushion! What timing! He honked at me as I waved back, stunned. In short order, I finished those extra two miles and we called it a day. 14 miles in total today, 12 with Bill and 2 bonus miles solo.
