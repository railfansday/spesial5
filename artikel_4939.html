Movie2K Defendant Could Hand Over 40,000 Bitcoin to End Trial—and Stay a Billionaire

The alleged operator of defunct movie piracy site Movie2K could walk away from his trial in Germany and remain a billionaire, under a deal offered by the judge in the case.

The presiding judge at the Leipzig Regional Court has offered to drop tax evasion and money laundering charges against the 43-year-old main defendant if he hands over 40,000 BTC, public broadcaster MDR reported last week. He would also have to pay €38 million ($42 million) in compensation for potential victims, according to MDR.

Saxony's Office of the Public Prosecutor General confirmed the offer to news agency dpa, tech outlet heise reported on Tuesday. The 40,000 BTC would be worth around €2.36 billion ($2.6 billion) at current prices, according to heise.

But prosecutors believe the defendant holds a total of about 56,000 BTC. If he accepts, he could keep around 16,000 BTC, worth roughly €940 million ($1 billion) today and more than €1 billion when the offer was made.

A co-defendant has been offered a deal to hand over 600 BTC and pay €17 million ($19 million), and his lawyer said she could accept it, according to MDR. The main defendant's lawyers are considering the offer. The trial resumes on December 3 and could end before Christmas.

Movie2K offered hundreds of thousands of pirated films before shutting down in 2013. Its operators made money from advertising and subscription traps, and invested much of it in Bitcoin when it traded far below today's prices.

The copyright violations are time-barred, so the trial, which began in February, has focused on money laundering and tax evasion. The judge has reportedly expressed doubts about proving some of the charges, in part because legally and illegally acquired Bitcoin may have been mixed in the defendant's accounts, heise reported.

The main defendant was arrested abroad in 2023, and in early 2024 handed over nearly 50,000 BTC. Authorities in Saxony sold the coins later that year, raising about €2.64 billion ($2.9 billion), which is held in a state judicial treasury account. The money still belongs to the defendant until there is a final ruling, according to heise. Germany's government was left holding less than $300 million in Bitcoin after the sales.

Blockchain analytics firm Arkham said last year that German authorities had failed to seize some 45,000 BTC linked to the site.

The offer comes days after German lawmakers approved new powers allowing customs officials to seize assets, including crypto, of doubtful origin without first opening a criminal case. Under that law, owners of significant assets would have to show they were lawfully obtained.
