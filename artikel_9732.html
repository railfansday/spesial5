AI sovereignty means your team controls the parts of an AI system that someone else could otherwise read, change, or switch off. It covers your data, applicable laws, the model version, access to the model, and what you’re allowed to do with its outputs. It’s not exactly the same thing as simply choosing an EU region, and it doesn’t mean you have to host models yourself.

TL;DR: Sovereignty = control + a tested way out. Check five layers: data, jurisdiction, model version, access, and outputs.

Definition: AI sovereignty is a team's ability to control where its AI workloads run, who can access its data and models, which model versions it uses. It also means being able to switch to an alternative without rebuilding the product.

Tech Monitor describes it as keeping control of critical workloads while having credible alternatives to outside providers. Acadia quotes Ben Van Roo, who refers to sovereignty as "a set of exits" rather than owning everything. I agree with both. You don’t need to own the data center. You need to be able to leave.

The post notes that, in most stacks, this adds up to four or five vendor accounts.

As you can see, choosing a sovereignty level is the easy part. Running it is where things get tough: GPU nodes, model upgrades, mirrored weights, evals, and someone on call when a worker pod won’t start. That’s a lot of engineering time for something that’s probably not your product's main feature.
