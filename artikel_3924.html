Rockstar has hung onto its staff with, uh, 34 notable exceptions

When you purchase through links on our site, we may earn an affiliate commission. Here’s how it works.

With GTA 6, Rockstar has created a game few other developers could even dream of competing with. That's partly down to the game's lengthy development cycle and massive budget, but you can also thank Rockstar's willingness to keep its veteran employees around when so many other studios are willing to lay off their most experienced developers.

21% of Rockstar, representing about 1,000 employees, have been at the company for more than 10 years, a spokesperson tells Bloomberg. Admittedly, that tenure only covers two major new releases – Red Dead Redemption 2 and GTA 6 – but developers with a decade or more of experience at a single studio are becoming a rare breed.

Nintendo offers one of the most notable counter-examples, where the same five developers who worked on the original Super Mario Bros. were still in the credits of Super Mario Bros. Wonder nearly 40 years later. Rockstar hasn't been around for 40 years, but many of the names you'll find in the credits have been there since the PS2 era, when GTA fever had just started to reach its absurd highs.

Other publishers, meanwhile, are laying off even their most experienced staff by the bucketload. Devs like Fallout's Tim Cain are warning of the consequences of cutting off institutional knowledge even as Microsoft cuts off developers who'd been making megahits like The Elder Scrolls for decades. How can The Elder Scrolls 6: Sentinel live up to expectations when the people who know how to make Elder Scrolls games are constantly being laid off?

Clearly, Rockstar has been more willing to hang onto its veteran staff – unless, of course, we're talking about the 34 union-associated employees it fired in 2025. Disputes over those firings and the union itself are still ongoing, and ugly allegations about continued crunch at Rockstar are coming up as a result.

On top of all that, GTA 6 devs are "worried about getting laid off" even after "working on the biggest game of all time," and it's clear that there's no true insulation from the threat of layoffs when an executive decides your name is on the wrong side of a spreadsheet. Those fears remain even as GTA 6 itself stands as a monolithic example of what a group of experienced developers are truly capable of.
