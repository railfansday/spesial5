It might've mirrored Valve's own Steam Machine

Xbox Project Helix, the next (mysterious) console underway from Microsoft, has been in the limelight recently for its support of PC games – support that was reportedly far more fleshed out during the hardware's initial conception.

Jason Ronald, vice president of Xbox's gaming devices and ecosystem team, recently said that Project Helix is "a console that is also capable of playing games from your PC," as "it's really designed to be the ultimate gaming machine." That sounds pretty dang capable already, but apparently, it was supposed to feature even more PC integration – think full-on free online multiplayer access, Steam, and the like.

Anonymous sources tell Windows Central as much, alleging that the original plan – one operating underneath a different codename – for the console was for it to essentially be a gaming computer that can also run Xbox games… on top of PC titles on storefronts like Valve's own Steam. Think the ASUS ROG Xbox Ally, for example, albeit not handheld. I'm reminded of the Steam Machine, personally, although that notably doesn't run on Windows.

The supposed predecessor to Project Helix would work with a gamepad-centered Xbox-style full-screen interface (like Steam's Big Picture Mode, for example), allowing users to exit out to a normal Windows desktop and run other programs such as Steam.

Furthermore, the report states the console would've been free to play online – no extra subscriptions or fees like Sony's PlayStation Plus service required to engage in multiplayer games.

That would mean no Xbox Game Pass paywall, as a PC wouldn't… well, work with a paywall at all, really. This wouldn't have been a cheap decision for Microsoft, and that paired with a move toward another hardware strategy, changes in management, and more, likely contributed to a reshuffle of plans – if this intriguing computer-esque console ever truly was on the table, that is. It does all beg the question: what will Project Helix look like?

I suppose we won't know until it's finally here. Let's just hope it doesn't cost as much as $1,800 – one rumored price point I can't quite get out of my head. For now, I think I'll just stick to my good ol' actual PC.
