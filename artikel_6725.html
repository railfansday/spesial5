A Guide to the Best Canada Goose Jackets

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Every product was carefully curated by an Esquire editor. We may earn a commission from these links. Here’s how we test products and why you should trust us.

High performance or high fashion? There’s a pick for each.

Since 1957, Canada Goose has been crushing the luxury outerwear game. And if its decades of history tell you one thing, it’s that Canada Goose isn’t going anywhere anytime soon. In fact, one of the best decisions that you can make is investing in a piece or two (if you haven’t already) because it’s a purchase that’s going to pay off. The high-quality designs are durable, practical, and stand at a timeless intersection where fashion meets function.

The biggest issue we have with Canada Goose is that there are too many options to choose from. You’re probably already aware that the brand has more than just its hefty parkas. Sure, it’s got those too, of course, but we also love its bombers, flannels, and fleeces. Canada Goose brand has even been venturing into the high-fashion space, with its creative director Haider Ackermann debuting collaborations with the NBA’s Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, CNCPTS, and KidSuper, among others.To help you find the right fit for you, we did a full breakdown for you. Below, find our official guide to the six best Canada Goose jackets. You’re not going to regret getting any of these.

This bomber jacket has some history to it. Originally designed over 25 years ago, this iteration of the jacket fuses modern functionality with classic bomber detailing, like a double placket, pen pocket, and ribbed waist. The archive-inspired satin gives it a glossy finish, but it’s still subtle enough that it will go with pretty much just about anything. Wear it with some black jeans, combat or Chelsea boots, and you’ve got a badass fit.

Every man should own a bomber jacket, so if for some reason the Seguin Aviator didn’t resonate with you, maybe this one will. Inspired by the brand’s 2004 Air Force parka, this bomber adds a warm fleece for insulation—as well as style points. With five pockets (two welt pockets at the chest, two lower pockets with coin-snaps, one zippered sleeve pocket), it masters practicality.

You can never go wrong with a traditional puffer. It’s actually more of a prerequisite than anything else, especially if you live somewhere with an actual winter. Canada Goose’s puffer is top-of-the-line, as it’s ultra-lightweight, super soft, and durable. So many puffer jackets weigh you down, but this one doesn’t. Its features are also endless: There are backpack straps, recessed rib-knit cuffs that add comfort and lock in heat, interior drawcord to customize fit, two exterior fleece-lined handwarmer pockets with zipper closures, three interior pockets, two mesh drop-in pockets, one security pocket—and more.

If you want a streamlined parka that doesn’t have too much going on on the outside—but still has a ton of cool features inside—the Langford is the one for you. It has a sleek silhouette that’s slightly longer than a puffer, and it’s even warmer than a puffer, too. With 625 Fill Power down inside, it can protect you in temperatures down to -13°F, and it will look great while doing it. It also has an adjustable down-filled hood, lined chin guard, two-way zipper underneath snap closures, and fleece-lined upper hand pockets so you can really keep the cold out and turn up the warmth.

The Expedition Parka is Canada Goose’s original—and most iconic—extreme-weather jacket. Initially developed for scientists working in Antarctica, this jacket is made to keep you warm in the most extreme of environments. It does that—and then some—with its elasticized snow skirt for added protection, interior drawcord at the waist to customize fit and retain warmth, and double-entry pockets for easy access. We could elaborate even more here, but you can just check out our love letter to the iconic parka for all the details.

But this version is even cooler than the original: It’s part of the Snow Goose line, which is basically creative director Haider Ackermann’s passion project. It features some more fashion-forward designs, stuff that’s harder to wear. However, this version of the Expedition Parka keeps the classic shape, but it comes in two seasonal colors, so you’ll stand out from everyone else wearing the same model, but won’t feel like you’re on the runway. It’s a win-win.

We know this puffer jacket looks like a sweater, but that’s kind of the point: It has the fun design of a winter sweater and the warmth of a down jacket—and when you add in the soft feel of the exterior from the wool-alpaca blend, you’re getting a lot of good stuff. It doesn’t look too bulky, but it can keep you warm down to -4°F, if you can believe it. It’s a good alternative to the more classic polyester or nylon puffer jackets; just be careful about taking it out in heavy rain or snow, and keep in mind that the exterior material requires more delicate handling than the usual puffer jacket material.
