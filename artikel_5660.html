TVA touts $1.7B data center deal under new large-load tariff

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The Tennessee Valley Authority is set to receive $1.7 billion in aggregate payments from an unnamed data center customer under its new tariff for data centers above 5 MW, the federal utility said in a Nov. 12 U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission filing.

“Capacity commitment charges under the agreement are based on a rate of approximately $1.5 million per MW of requested capacity above 5 MW,” the filing said. “This rate reflects TVA's current cohort-based pricing methodology, which may be revised for future customer cohorts.”

The new tariff took effect Oct. 1 and will be phased in through 2029, the filing said. That phase-in period will apply to existing customers to allow them to retain their existing rate structure for a period of time, the Nashville Scene reported in August.

TVA CEO Mike Skaggs said during a Nov. 13 earnings call that the change to data center rates has supported the utility’s investments.

Those investments include extending the lives of the utility’s existing nuclear units and “helping expand the nuclear fuel and supply hub in the East Tennessee area,” he said.

The utility recently received the first small modular reactor construction permit from the U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission for a BWRX-300 reactor at the Clinch River Site in Oak Ridge, “although TVA has not yet decided whether to construct a reactor at the site,” its filing said.

Skaggs came out of retirement to serve as interim CEO following the retirement of former CEO Don Moul and was instated as TVA’s permanent CEO following a Nov. 10 board meeting, TVA’s vice president of financial planning and investor relations Clifton Lowry said on the call.

TVA’s filing also noted that in August, the utility’s board approved 1.5 GW of new natural gas generation, “targeted to be online in 2032.”

As of Sept. 30, TVA said it “had spent $463 million on long-lead-time equipment associated with previously approved projects, which will now be utilized in connection with the project. TVA could spend up to an additional $3.1 billion on this project.”

That previously approved project is TVA’s defunct Middle Tennessee Energy Solution project.

In August, TVA said its approval of the new 1.5 GW gas facility “closes out” the previous project, “and all funding has been transferred to the New Gas Facility 2032.”

TVA’s President and CFO Tom Rice said that the utility is in a “strong financial position” to executive its $13 billion capital investment plan over the next three years. That investment comes on top of the more than $23 billion in investments since 2020, Rice said.

However, TVA’s filing notes that it “continues to experience supply chain pressures resulting from inflation, tariffs and other trade restrictions, material constraints, and labor availability.”

“These factors have contributed to project delays, limited availability of critical materials, and increased costs for both materials and labor,” the utility said. “TVA anticipates that inflationary and tariff pressures will persist into 2027. Although these challenges have been managed with limited disruption to business operations thus far, continued or escalating pressures could result in more substantial operational impacts and increased pressure on power rates.”
