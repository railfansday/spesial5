How Travel Credit Cards Are Becoming Full-Trip Companions: Watch Video

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Travel credit cards are moving well beyond points and booking. At Skift Global Forum, Capital One Travel’s Sarah Moore said the opportunity lies in remaining useful throughout the trip by combining context, experiences, technology, and trust to build deeper relationships with travelers.

This sponsored content was created in collaboration with a Skift partner.

Travel credit cards have long competed on points, perks, and booking benefits. For Capital One Travel, the next phase is about extending the relationship further into the trip and finding more ways to stay useful after a customer books.

The company sees an opportunity to connect more parts of the travel journey, from planning and airport support to destination experiences. That approach draws on Capital One’s technology, booking data, travel products, and existing relationship with cardholders as their financial partner.

At Skift Global Forum in New York, Sarah Moore, head of Capital One Travel, discussed how the company is trying to become what she called a “travel companion” for its cardholders, with a role that extends before, during, and after the trip.

The Travel Card’s Next Act: From Earning Points to Erasing Friction

Capital One entered the travel business relatively recently, launching the Capital One Travel booking platform in 2021. Moore said arriving later gave the company an opportunity to look at what already existed and build around the expectations of a modern traveler.

That meant taking a technology-first approach, but also starting with the customer rather than simply recreating the traditional credit card travel model.

“We were able to really see what was out there in the marketplace and understand what the modern traveler was looking for,” Moore said.

That distinction matters as the boundaries between financial services, travel booking, loyalty, and trip planning continue to blur. A travel card may still sit in a traveler’s wallet, but the relationship increasingly extends into the rest of the trip.

For years, booking has been treated as the finish line for many travel companies. Moore sees it more as a starting point.

Capital One Travel wants to remain with customers after the booking transaction, whether that means helping a traveler navigate the airport by surfacing lounge information, providing flight updates, or helping them decide what to do once they reach their destination.

Being useful when something goes wrong can be particularly important, giving a company an opportunity to demonstrate the value of the relationship.

“Knowing when to step up is what builds trust,” Moore said.

For Capital One Travel, those moments can help turn a single booking into a longer-term customer relationship.

Moore also emphasized the importance of collaboration across the travel industry to better support travelers throughout their journeys. No single company can manage every aspect of the travel experience today, making partnerships across the broader ecosystem essential to delivering consistent support to customers throughout their journeys.

Delivering that kind of support also requires a different approach to personalization.

Travel brands have spent years collecting customer data, but Moore said the most useful form of personalization is contextual. The question is not simply who someone is, but what kind of trip they are taking right now.

A traveler taking a weekend trip with a spouse, for example, may want something entirely different from the same traveler on vacation with children.

Moore used her own family as an example. A trip with her husband might call for a relaxing stay at one of Capital One Travel’s Premier Collection hotels. Traveling with her children could mean booking a four-bedroom vacation rental, also available on Capital One Travel, with many homes offering the same luxury benefits found in the Premier and Lifestyle hotel collections. Even within the same family, different travelers may want completely different activities.

That same thinking extends to what travelers actually do once they arrive.

Moore said in a survey conducted by Capital One Travel, more than 70% of travelers are looking at some type of event or live experience, from sports and dining to other forms of cultural immersion. For some travelers, those experiences are increasingly shaping the trip itself rather than simply being added after the flight and hotel are booked.

“Travel is not just a flight and a hotel,” Moore said. “It’s what you’re actually doing when you land and what you’re engaging in when you’re there.”

Capital One has responded by building experiences into its broader cardholder proposition. Moore pointed to partnerships with Major League Baseball and touring artists, as well as more intimate experiences tied to food and culture that can be booked through activities on Capital One Travel.

One example is a culinary–driven trip led by chef José Andrés in his hometown of Asturias, Spain, exclusively for cardholders, giving them a more distinctive and immersive way to experience the destination.

Taken together, those efforts point to a broader shift in what a travel credit card can become. Moore said Capital One is focused on becoming a travel companion that supports cardholders from booking through servicing and the trip itself.

The company launched a dedicated Capital One Travel app within the past year, an example Moore cited of meeting travelers in the channels where they increasingly expect to manage their trips.

AI could push that evolution further. Travelers are increasingly finding inspiration, researching destinations, and making decisions through new interfaces. Moore said Capital One Travel will need to meet them in those places while maintaining the value and trust that come with its role as their financial partner.

That trust also creates an opportunity to use the information Capital One already has more effectively. Transaction data can provide signals about how some customers travel and what they may be looking for next, provided it is used to make the experience easier and more relevant.

For Moore, that is ultimately what “frictionless” travel means: understanding what a traveler needs and making it simple for them to get it.

This shift has implications beyond credit cards. Travel companies across the industry are trying to extend their relationship with customers beyond a single transaction. Doing that effectively requires more than adding another loyalty benefit or digital touchpoint.

It means understanding the context of each trip, being useful when travelers actually need help, and creating access that enhances the experience. It also requires accepting that the traveler, not the brand, is ultimately in control of the relationship.

For Capital One Travel, the opportunity is to make the card and the platform behind it more relevant throughout the journey. If that works, the booking becomes less of an endpoint and more of one moment in a much longer relationship.
