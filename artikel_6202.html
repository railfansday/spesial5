United MileagePlus has four published elite tiers: Premier Silver, Premier Gold, Premier Platinum and Premier 1K. Benefits and qualification requirements vary by tier and are subject to change, so review United’s current program rules.

You earn Premier status by collecting Premier qualifying points (PQPs) and meeting United’s other requirements, including taking a minimum number of United-operated flights each calendar year. United has announced that beginning in 2027, PQPs will become the sole qualifying metric.

PQPs are earned solely for the purpose of qualifying for elite status, and they are separate from MileagePlus miles. You cannot redeem PQPs for award flights. How you earn them depends on factors such as the airline operating the flight and how you book, and earning rules are subject to change.

United cobranded credit cards do not provide elite status on their own. Eligible card spending may earn PQPs, up to limits that vary by card. Earning rates, caps and offers are subject to the issuer’s terms and can change.

Having elite status with an airline can make a significant difference in your travel experience. Shorter lines, faster service, complimentary upgrades, international lounge access and other benefits can easily make stressful trips more seamless. But these perks don't come without a cost, and United MileagePlus overhauled how members earn status in 2026.

This guide will evaluate United Airlines Premier status and break down the different status tiers, how to earn them and whether their perks are worth the effort for United flyers.

One requirement that is not changing: You must take at least four flights operated by United or United Express in a calendar year to qualify for any status tier.

When you reach any elite status tier, it is valid for the remainder of the calendar year, the entirety of the following year, and the first month of the year after. For instance, if you reach status requirements anytime in 2026, you will hold the status through Jan. 31, 2028. So, the sooner you earn elite status, the longer you can enjoy the benefits.

Now, let's dive into how to earn PQPs (and, through the end of 2026, PQFs).

The classic way to earn PQPs is via your spending on flights. The earning rate depends on which airline operates the flight and whether you book it with cash or miles. PQPs are awarded to the passenger, not the person who buys the ticket.

Earn 1 PQP for every 6 award miles on MileagePlus partner airlines, up to a maximum of 500 PQPs per flight in economy and premium economy and 1,000 PQPs per flight in first and business class.

You can also earn PQPs through spending on United's cobranded credit cards. None of these cards provide complimentary elite status, but you’ll earn PQPs on everyday purchases (up to a certain amount), and some cards offer PQPs as part of the welcome bonus.

On top of the various metrics for earning miles and status, United MileagePlus awards something called PlusPoints, a separate currency available only to Platinum and 1K members.

PlusPoints were originally used for seat upgrades, though these have become harder to get in recent years as demand has skyrocketed. That’s why United introduced several new ways to redeem your PlusPoints, including for bonus miles, PQPs, TravelBank cash and gifting Premier status to other MileagePlus members.

Elite status can certainly make travel more comfortable and convenient. If you regularly travel with United or its partner airlines and/or spend significantly on a cobranded credit card, you may find it relatively easy to earn Premier status. And it can be a savvy way to maximize your travel.

Plus, this means you’ll also be earning plenty of United miles, which you can redeem for United award flights — all while enjoying your elite status benefits.

However, it’s not worth pursuing status when you can’t enjoy the benefits you’ve worked hard to earn. Be sure to consider United’s route map, its presence at your home airport and how easy it is to get to your desired destinations. If United and its partner airlines don’t fly where you live or where you want to travel, you won’t get much use from Premier status. You may be better off pursuing status with a different program, such as Delta SkyMiles or American AAdvantage.

Finally, remember that Premier status does not provide domestic lounge access when traveling with United. If this benefit is important to you, consider applying for the , which provides United Club access and perks such as two free checked bags and Premier Access travel services.
