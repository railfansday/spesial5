A common framework makes it easier for operators to benchmark against one another and their suppliers, but the burden still falls on operators to collect and crunch the numbers. The best way to get tour operators to calculate their emissions would be releasing a tool to do it for them.

In 2026, hotels tend to report emissions on a per-room basis, trains report per passenger, and a bus company reports its emissions for the whole vehicle. From these fragmented data points, a package tour operator has to fold all of those numbers into one to understand its climate impact.

Two tour operator associations have developed a website they hope can help solve this problem. The idea is to simplify emissions calculations for package tour operators by bringing resources into one place.

The site, called TEMPO, is the work of the European Tourism Association (ETOA) and United States Tour Operators Association (USTOA). It points to free methodologies operators can use to measure emissions and to free calculation tools for each part of a tour, as well as identifying when one doesn’t exist.

The European Tourism Association (ETOA) and the United States Tour Operators Association (USTOA) have launched a website meant to simplify emissions measurement for package tour operators, who currently have to reconcile hotel data reported per room, rail data per passenger, and bus data per vehicle into a single number. The site breaks a tour into components — flights, hotels, road transport, activities, meals, and sea travel — points to free methodologies and calculation tools for each, and flags where no tool exists. It offers two reporting routes: a quick estimate for operators with little time or budget, and a more rigorous version requiring precise data. Operators including Avanti Destinations, EF Education First, and the Globus Family of Brands helped shape the effort, which is supported by Travalyst, whose members include Booking.com, Expedia, and Google. ETOA's Tim Fairhurst said the idea came from members who wanted an easier way to measure and compare, and he hopes consistent methods will show operators where the most polluting parts of an itinerary sit. Supplier data remains the open question: Intrepid Travel's Susanne Etti told a COP31 session that small suppliers often lack the capital, technology, or expertise to measure or cut emissions alone. Separately, UN Tourism revised its Glasgow Declaration guidelines on Wednesday so signatories can submit emissions reports they already produce elsewhere.
