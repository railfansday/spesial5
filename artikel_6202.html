Air Canada doesn’t need another lie-flat seat. It needs to run on time, and its own operations chief admits it isn’t there yet.

As the airline industry pours money into premium, Air Canada argues it got there first. The carrier is “the original premium airline in North America,” its chief operations officer, Mark Nasr, said at the Skift Aviation Forum in New York City on Thursday.

“We were the first with lie-flat seats, all-aisle access, a lot of investments in concierge services, food and beverage,” Nasr said. “The last major thing that we need to accomplish to round out the entire picture is to get a higher level of consistent reliability.”

Air Canada's chief operations officer, Mark Nasr, used the Skift Aviation Forum in New York to argue the carrier was the first premium airline in North America — citing lie-flat seats, all-aisle access, and investments in concierge services and food and beverage — and said the missing piece now is reliability. Nasr said Air Canada's on-time performance sits in the mid-70s and should be in the low 80s, which he said would make it best of breed in North America. He pointed to staffing levels, more training, more decision-making freedom for employees, and schedules with enough buffer for weather and airport construction as the levers. After what Nasr called the largest snowstorm in Toronto's recorded history in January, the airline started making cancellation decisions earlier and now aims to rebook customers from the point of origin rather than at the hub. Air Canada also rolled out its first large-scale AI agent tool to all contact center staff, which Nasr said is meant to improve service without adding cost rather than cut front-line jobs. Nasr, who spent nearly a decade in the carrier's loyalty business, said loyalty is a multiplier on a base, and that the program can't do its full job if the base is weak.
