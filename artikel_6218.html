Rove Launches Shopping Offers Of Up To 25 Miles Per Dollar—Why Brands Will Pay So Much To Win Your Business

Rove is launching personalized shopping offers of up to 25 miles per dollar on November 20. Connect your card accounts, activate an offer, and the program can give you a higher earning rate based on your shopping history.

If you buy from Nike, Adidas may be willing to spend real money to win your next purchase. That’s the example I reported from Rove’s presentation at the oneworld Loyalty Summit in September. CEO Max Morganroth described targeted merchant commissions reaching 40%. This puts that pitch in front of consumers, with an established advertising company helping supply the offers.

Rove says members will find personalized offers in the ‘For You’ section of its website and app after connecting their card accounts. Enhanced offers will display the standard earning rate alongside the higher rate.

They’re also expanding in-store earning to hundreds of participating physical retailers. Activate an eligible offer before buying, pay with the connected card, and the purchase gets tracked when the charge clears. Rove says miles will typically post within seven days.

These are selected offers reaching up to 25x, and Rove tells me merchants can change from month to month, and the same merchant might offer one person 5x and another 20x depending on whom it wants to reach.

Rove began rolling out in-store shopping alongside its app, dining program and instant gift card rewards in August. The new pieces are the personalized enhanced rates and broader retail coverage.

A shopping portal generally earns a commission for sending a merchant a sale. Better targeting can make that referral worth more.

Someone who regularly spends money in a category, but buys from a competitor, is a prospect worth reaching. So is someone who used to shop at a store and stopped. A merchant might pay to bring that person back, get an occasional customer to make another visit, or encourage a larger purchase.

The best prospect isn’t necessarily the biggest spender. It’s the person whose spending the offer can profitably change. Adidas gains little from paying a committed Adidas customer a huge rebate on shoes they were already going to buy. And it doesn’t much from showing a Nike loyalist an offer they’ll never use.

Rewards provider Valuedynamx describes a retailer campaign that gave new shoppers stronger incentives, lapsed shoppers offers to bring them back, and loyal customers smaller rewards. Its case study reports a 20.2% incremental sales lift among lapsed customers and 13.3% among new ones.

Targeting also lets a brand offer a substantial rebate without cutting the public price for everyone. It can spend more to win a customer it wants while avoiding an expensive storewide sale.

Cardlytics announced its Rove partnership on October 6, giving Rove access to its offers program. Rove brings an audience motivated by travel rewards. Cardlytics brings merchant campaigns and purchase-based targeting.

Rove’s program terms name Cardlytics and Plaid. Members can connect eligible debit or credit accounts through Plaid, and Cardlytics uses account transactions for targeting, reporting and measurement. Rove tells me its partners process the last 24 months of transaction history so merchants can target offers. Rove itself says it neither stores nor interacts with that history.

That history can reveal which merchants you use, how recently you’ve shopped, how often you return and how much you spend.

The policy also says Rove’s browser extension matches participating stores locally and doesn’t transmit your browsing history.

For the Cardlytics offers, personal identifiers are removed, but permit aggregated marketing insights, transaction-level validation and licensing transaction data to partners.
