Air France A350 Nearly Collides Midair With American A321 After Wrong Turn At LAX

Two aircraft belonging to American Airlines and Air France came within 400 feet (121 meters) of vertical separation as both jets departed from Los Angeles International Airport (LAX). Quick actions by air traffic control and the pilots onboard averted a close call that could have had catastrophic consequences.

The two planes were departing from California's busiest airport in the early hours of Saturday morning. Air France flight 28 was heading for Papeete (PPT), while American flight 1660 was flying to Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport (DFW). The two jets — which collectively could have been carrying more than 500 passengers — came dangerously close to each other shortly after takeoff, before controllers intervened to prevent a potential collision.

The Air France Airbus A350-900, operating as AF28, had been cleared to take off from Los Angeles via the SUMMR2 procedure. SUMMR2, according to the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), is a set route that tells departing aircraft which direction to fly after takeoff. In this case, planes taking off from Runways 24L/R and 25L/R must initially climb straight ahead before following their assigned route.

At the same time, the American jet, destined for Texas, was told by air traffic control to stay at 2,000 feet, to allow the Air France widebody to turn and then head to FL270. However, an unauthorized turn by the Air France aircraft brought the two jets onto conflicting flight paths.

Simple Flying reached out to Air France for comment on the incident on Sunday; however, an airline spokesperson could not immediately be reached.

According to TurbineTraveler on X, the Air France plane made an unauthorized turn left shortly after takeoff, rather than continuing straight as instructed. This placed the A350 directly in the trajectory of the departing American jet. This led the two planes to come within dangerous proximity of each other, known as a loss of separation event.

The controller quickly spotted the developing conflict and instructed both aircraft to turn away from each other. The controller then questioned the Air France crew about the deviation from its departure route. Both the American and Air France flights were then able to continue safely to their intended destinations with no reported injuries or harm to anyone on board. A similar incident occurred in Los Angeles in 2025, when an American and ITA Airways jet almost collided midair after a pilot deviation from the busy airport.

Two aircraft lost separation while climbing out of LAX.

As one of the busiest airports in the US and the world, Los Angeles International Airport has carefully controlled departure routes to keep aircraft safely separated. The SUMMR2 procedure directs westbound departures out over the Pacific Ocean, helping keep them clear of other departing and arriving traffic.

SUMMR flights often transition to long-haul or coastal routes, including to destinations in Hawaii, Asia, and the north, like San Francisco. Instead of following this procedure, the Air France pilot made a sharp left turn shortly after departure, which intersected the path of the American jet that had departed from Runway 25R. Fortunately, the departure controller identified the conflict, and their swift actions resulted in the two planes being able to quickly change course and avoid a midair collision.
