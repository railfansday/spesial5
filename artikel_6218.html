KLM Forced Into 'Painful' €1B Financial Overhaul

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

KLM has announced a widespread restructuring and consolidation program as it looks to improve its earnings in what is an increasingly challenging operational environment. This year, more than most, has seen airlines around the world come to terms with various external pressures, such as the airspace closures and fuel price hikes related to the ongoing geopolitical instability in the Middle East.

With this in mind, KLM is looking to improve its annual earnings by €1 billion (€1 = $1.12). Such a boost, it says, "is needed to invest in the fleet, customer experience, and KLM's people." However, this will come at a cost, with both its network and workforce consolidating to save money. Most notably, thousands of employee positions are being reorganized, with the total workforce set to drop by around 15%.

In a statement released earlier today by the Dutch flag carrier and SkyTeam member, KLM explained that it has made good financial progress in recent years, with existing efforts having already resulted in "structural improvements of more than €750 million." Having got its pilot and maintenance staff shortage under control, the airline has outsourced catering and maintenance ops and trimmed its office work.

However, with external pressures mounting, these efforts, alone, are not enough to provide long-term financial security for the airline. With this in mind, KLM has now announced further measures that it says will "strengthen its position and keep the Netherlands connected to the world." It found that delaying such actions "would further limit KLM's strategic options," with President and CEO Marjan Rintel saying that:

"The environment we operate in is becoming increasingly complex, with global instability, high fuel prices, and growing pressure on the industry. That’s why, at this pivotal moment for KLM, we’re taking far-reaching, sometimes painful measures."

KLM wants to improve its annual earnings by €1 billion to a total of €1.4 billion. This growth will come at a human cost, with 5,000 positions being reorganized as KLM cuts its workforce by 15%. Of these 5,000 positions, 1,000 will be lost through "structural productivity improvements," while the remainder in catering, ground services, and customer contact have been or will be outsourced or transferred.

The airline says that, via "natural attrition, voluntary arrangements, and transfers to other positions," it hopes that it will be able to limit the overall impact of this reorganization. President and CEO Marjan Rintel says that this difficult choice is the right one, adding that "doing nothing would ultimately have even more painful consequences, both for jobs and for our country's connections to the rest of the world."

Consolidation is also expected at the network level, particularly in terms of European routes. KLM believes that it can restore regional and short-haul profitability by focusing its capacity "on routes that contribute most to passenger connectivity and the nonstop network from Schiphol." On the fleet side, KLM Cityhopper will play a more important role, with its smaller jets allowing the airline to better align capacity.

The regional airline is increasing capacity in the E195-E2’s economy cabin by adding four additional seats.

Elsewhere in its operations, KLM believes that increased operational efficiency is also set to make a big difference as it targets earnings increases. In ground services alone, it has identified three key pillars on this front. Firstly, passenger services will rely more heavily on digital technology, allowing flyers to have more control over their bookings while employees turn their attention to hospitality.

Secondly, KLM will look to work more closely with external partners when it comes to apron and baggage services. On this front, the Dutch flag carrier says that such providers "can invest more and at a faster pace, while delivering services more efficiently and at a lower cost." Third and finally, technology and external collaboration will be used to streamline the Hub Control Center at Amsterdam Schiphol (AMS).
