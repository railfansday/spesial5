Your Student Loan Offer Is a Maximum – Accept Only What You Need

The federal loans in your college aid offer are what you’re eligible to borrow, not what you need. Subtract grants, scholarships, savings, and earnings from your real costs, and borrow only what’s left. Take subsidized loans first, since the government pays their interest while you’re in school. Lower or decline the rest through your school’s aid office.

Schools set the loan amount at their cost of attendance, an estimate of a year’s tuition, fees, housing, food, books, transportation, and personal costs, minus the other aid you receive, up to a federal limit. Your savings, summer pay, and family help don’t lower the total.

The school’s housing and food figures are usually averages that can run higher or lower than what you’ll actually spend. For a dependent student, the federal limit is $5,500 the first year, no more than $3,500 of it subsidized, and $7,500 a year from the third year on.

Unsubsidized loans build interest from the day they’re paid out, at a fixed 6.52% on undergraduate federal loans first paid out this school year.

To figure what you need, start with the school’s cost of attendance and replace its housing and food figures with your own. Subtract grants, scholarships, savings, what you’ll earn, and what your family will pay, then cover that gap with subsidized loans first, unsubsidized next, and private or parent loans only for anything still left.

Say a dependent freshman on track to graduate in four years gets an offer of $3,000 in subsidized loans and $2,500 unsubsidized, and grants and summer earnings leave a $3,000 gap. Paid out half each semester at 6.52%, the unneeded $2,500 would build about $650 in interest before repayment starts six months after graduation. Accepting only the $3,000 subsidized loan keeps the $2,500 and its interest off the books.

To take less, enter a lower amount in your school’s aid portal, or ask the financial aid office to reduce or cancel the loan before the money is paid out.

Money already paid out can still go back. The Education Department treats loan money returned within 120 days of being paid out as canceled, with no interest or loan fee owed on it. Ask the aid office to send it back, or return it through your loan servicer, the company that manages your federal loans and is listed in your StudentAid.gov account.
