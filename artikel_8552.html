Investing $10,000 – Fill a Roth IRA, Then Buy a Broad Index Fund

With $10,000 to invest, use accounts with tax breaks before a regular one. Take any 401(k) match you’re missing, pay off high-interest debt, and top up your emergency fund. Then fill this year’s Roth IRA, and put the rest in a broad, low-cost index fund in a brokerage account.

The account you choose matters more than the fund you buy.

Accounts with tax breaks have yearly limits you can’t make up later, while a brokerage account takes any amount, any time. Work down this list until the money runs out.

Say you get the match, owe nothing on cards, and have a full emergency fund. If you’re under 50, this year’s limit, $7,500, goes in the Roth and the rest in a total-market index fund.

Invest it all at once, since Vanguard’s research found a lump sum beat splitting it into three monthly purchases about two-thirds of the time from 1976 to 2022. If a drop right after would rattle you, dollar-cost averaging, spreading it over a few months, is fine too.

Skip hot stocks, and don’t pay an advisor 1% a year to manage it. At an assumed 7% yearly return, that fee leaves you 6%, so $10,000 grows to about $57,400 in 30 years instead of $76,100.

Buy the index fund the day each deposit arrives, since the money sits in cash until you choose an investment.
