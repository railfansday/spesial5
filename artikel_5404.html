Mars CFO: $1.4 billion U.S. manufacturing investment aims to deliver ‘durable growth’

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Good morning. Mars Inc., maker of M&M’s, Snickers and Pedigree, plans to invest an estimated $1.4 billion in U.S. manufacturing and operations in 2027, its largest single-year investment.

“It is fundamentally a growth investment,” Mars Inc. CFO Claus Aagaard told me. “In the near term, we are putting capital behind high-demand, high-growth brands and businesses where additional capacity can unlock growth.”

Longer term, Mars is modernizing its network with automation, high-speed lines and standardized systems “so we can scale efficiently and build a stronger, more resilient supply chain,” Aagaard explained.

Following its combination with Kellanova, Mars is now a more than $65 billion family-owned business, based on combined 2025 net sales, according to the company. In the Q&A below, Aagaard discusses the rationale for the investment in manufacturing, how Mars will measure its return and what it means for the communities where it operates.

Why is 2027 the right time for Mars’s largest single-year investment?

We have invested more than $9 billion in U.S. manufacturing and operations over the last decade, and while this $1.4 billion commitment is the largest we’ve invested in a single year, it is part of our long-term approach. Demand across our business is high, and American manufacturing is very strong.

Advances in smart factory automation, energy-efficiency technology and digital tools mean that retrofitting or building facilities today can deliver a higher return than it did a decade ago. Right now, we’re seeing a compelling opportunity to not only invest in meeting growing demand, but to unlock capabilities and ensure operational excellence for generations to come.

How is the $1.4 billion divided, and how do you measure the return?

The $1.4 billion investment in 2027 and the $9 billion that has been committed over the past decade, supports manufacturing and operations across our snacking, food and pet businesses, with capital allocated where we see clear opportunities to expand capacity, modernize facilities and strengthen resilience.

For example, we’ll spend more than $34 million to open four new Mars Veterinary Health hospitals in 2027, expand access to emergency and specialty veterinary care, bring innovative medical capabilities to growing communities, and support the development of highly skilled veterinary professionals.

We have invested $60 million in Salt Lake City to add two Trü Frü production lines to our recently opened Nature’s Bakery facility, unlocking capacity for 17 million frozen products annually. And, we have spent $49 million in Columbus, Ohio, to bring Temptations Lickable Treats manufacturing to the U.S., with a target of 400 million pouches annually.We measure return across several dimensions, including our ability to meet consumer demand, improve efficiency and productivity, strengthen supply chain resilience, support profitable growth, and expand access to high-quality veterinary care.

Why does keeping 94% of U.S. products made domestically matter to Mars?

Mars has operated in the U.S. for 115 years—it’s our largest market, and we’re globally headquartered here. The U.S. is critical for Mars. Making 94% of the products we sell in the U.S. domestically gives us an important foundation for long-term growth. A strong local manufacturing footprint puts production closer to consumers, creates greater supply-chain resilience, and allows us to expand capacity as demand grows. It also means that when we invest in growth, those investments can translate directly into facilities, capabilities, and jobs in the communities where we operate.

What does this investment mean for the communities where Mars operates?

We have operated in many U.S. communities for generations, so these investments are about more than adding capacity. They modernize and strengthen facilities, support skilled jobs, and create additional economic activity locally.

This month, our facility in Waco, Texas is celebrating 50 years of operations. Mars has steadily expanded and modernized the Waco site, which produces Snickers, Starburst, and Skittles, to meet growing consumer demand while advancing environmental sustainability. Mars has invested $165 million in the facility since 2024, with recent capital projects heavily focused on enhancing energy efficiency and operational excellence.

Today, the site is powered by nearly 900 dedicated associates, but the milestone speaks to the dedication of generations of associates and longstanding partnerships we share with the Central Texas community.

What would success look like for this investment a few years from now?

Success means we have converted this capital into durable growth and a stronger operating network. We should be able to serve more consumers and pet owners, scale high-growth brands, operate more efficiently, and have greater resilience across our supply chain.

EY-Parthenon’s latest Deal Barometer points to a normalization, not a retreat, in U.S. dealmaking in 2027. After three consecutive years of double-digit growth, deal volume is forecast to rise 2%, keeping activity roughly 22% above pre-pandemic levels.

Corporate M&A is expected to hold steady, while private equity deal volume is projected to increase 4% as sponsors face pressure to deploy capital and return cash to investors. The outlook suggests a still-active but more selective deal market, rather than another year of rapid expansion.

As companies introduce AI products and charge customers based on usage, finance teams face a growing challenge: making sure customer usage, billing, and revenue records all line up. Workday and Zuora are expanding their partnership to address this challenge with the Zuora Monetization Suite for Workday, the companies announced. The suite connects Zuora Billing and Zuora Revenue with Workday Financial Management and Accounting Center.

The offering aims to reduce the time finance teams spend manually reconciling financial records and help companies adopt AI, usage-based, and hybrid pricing models while maintaining financial accuracy and control.
