Omar Berrada hits back at criticism over Manchester United’s transfer strategy

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Manchester United’s CEO Omar Berrada has hit back at criticism of the club’s transfer strategy last summer and insists a ‘disciplined’ approach is required.

United spent £155 million on signing three midfielders in Youri Tielemans, Andrey Santos and Carlos Baleba in the summer transfer window.

However, Mauricio Pochettino arrives at Old Trafford this week without a recognised striker as Benjamin Sesko is out for the remainder of the year with a shin problem, while Luke Shaw has missed United’s last seven games due to injury.

United supporters have criticised the club’s decision to only strengthen Michael Carrick’s midfield in the summer but Berrada is adamant the squad is ‘fundamentally better’ after their signings.

‘You have to take it in the context of the last few years,’ Berrada told Sky Sports.

‘The summer of 2025, I think the recruitment team was widely praised with the four players that were signed. And they did very well. They helped the team achieve Champions League qualification.

‘When we look at this summer, we went in knowing what we needed to do. Of course, there have been different dynamics at play. It’s probably been the summer or the transfer window where there’s been the highest inflation in transfer fees.

‘And we’ve said publicly and we’ve said consistently in the last few years that we need to stay disciplined. We need to take the right decisions. We need to buy the right players for the right value at the right time in terms of what the squad needs.

‘And when we look at the summer transfer window of 2026, I know we’re receiving a lot of criticism for it but we’re actually happy with the players that we bought.

‘We think that Carlos Baleba, Youri Tielemans, Andrey Santos and Karl Darlow, and let’s not forget that we’ve also welcomed back Marcus Rashford, who had a very good season on loan at Barcelona – these are five additions that we think have made the squad fundamentally better.

‘It’s true that we lost Casemiro, who is a top player and gave a lot to the club. We also lost Manuel Ugarte to, unfortunately, an ACL injury. So our focus was very much about straightening the midfield.

‘When we started the transfer window, we really only had Kobbie Mainoo as our midfielder. So we wanted to reinforce that, and we did it.’

Berrada also addressed United’s decision not to sign a new left-sided defender after the club made an enquiry for Newcastle United’s Lewis Hall in the summer.

‘When you look at the other areas of the pitch, and there’s a lot of discussion about the back line,’ Berrada said.

‘But we did come into it thinking we have depth. We have nine players that can play in the back line, in the back four positions.

‘So that’s why we looked primarily at investing in the midfield. But we were always looking for opportunities. I think it’s been widely reported that we were looking at potentially the left-back position as a reinforcement.

‘I think what we’ve seen in the back line is we’ve had injuries, it’s taken Matthijs de Ligt longer than we would want it to get back. Unfortunately, Luke Shaw suffered a muscle injury that meant he was out for five or six weeks.

‘So could we have signed an additional defender to straighten the depth and the quality? Yes, but we have to find the right players and you have to find the right players at the right value.

‘There’s no point in us spending £40 million, £50m for players that we’re not convinced are going to add value to the team.

‘So it’s always about being disciplined and finding the right opportunities and getting the right players that are going to take us forward.

‘When I talk about being disciplined, I talk about not overspending on players that don’t necessarily add quality to the team.

‘Because when you sign a player that doesn’t work out for Man Utd, it becomes very difficult to move that player on or to get the sale we want. And that can set us back.

‘So when I talk about being disciplined, I’m not talking about necessarily not spending to get the top players. We have the ambition to win, to win the Premier League, to win the Champions League, to win the domestic cups. And for that, we need top talent. So when we find the right opportunities, we will spend. But what we don’t want to be doing is spending just for the sake of it.’
