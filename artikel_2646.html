Nets put up a dud vs the Bulls, lose 137-107

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

It wasn't just a fitting, but a necessary night for the Brooklyn Nets to celebrate their ABA roots. The present looks bleak, and the future murky at best. At this point, it feels like we have no choice but to seek refuge in the past.

Prior to tonight's game, Brooklyn hosted a panel featuring hoop legends Julius Erving and Brian Taylor, who spoke on the franchise's first two ABA Championships in 1974 and 1976. They also gave out ABA-inspired merch, hosted a dunk show at halftime, and payed homage to each championship team's other players in a variety of ways.

I have no memory of the ABA, so the nostalgia didn't appeal to me, but that made the whole ordeal even more impressive from my point of view. The organization used every opportunity it had to celebrate this group of men, who's legacies could've easily been washed away as the team changed leagues, cities, and names over the years. In their smiles and body language, you could tell it meant a lot to them, and that was pretty damn cool.

They weren't the only Nets of yore in the house. For the first time in his seven year career, Nic Claxton played in the Barclays Center as a visitor this evening. A member of the Chicago Bulls since Brooklyn shipped him out over the summer in a three-team deal to bring in Julius Randle, Clax was happy to be back, and spoke positively about the organization at shootaround a few hours before tipoff.

"I got the fam here. I feel like I also got fam that's here working for the Nets, so you know, it'll be good," he said. "No ill will at all. It's all good vibes. I don't have nothing negative to say about anybody in this organization in Brooklyn. I'm grateful for my time here. I had a beautiful time here. Cultivated a lot of great relationships, so it's all love."

Usually, nobody likes getting traded or cut. But after an abundance of rocky, or at best ominous exits from presumed franchise cornerstones over the past few years, that's refreshing to hear.

Brooklyn's on-court product tonight was anything but. In wasn't just stale, but rotten, and the kind of thing that makes you question every decision made since committing to a rebuild. Julius Randle called it one of the worst games he's ever been a part of.

Both teams, sitting in the Eastern Conference's basement with 3-10 records, looked the part early on. They combined for five turnovers in the game's first three minutes. Chicago got six points off theirs while Brooklyn got just a deuce, so the Bulls began the game up 12-6.

Brooklyn's defense was equally uninspiring. Chicago benefitted form an abundance of open looks as Egor Dëmin consistency got beat off the dribble the top of the key, leading to easy drive-and-kick actions. The Nets also allowed seven offensive rebounds in the first, leading to 18 second chance points.

So, the Bulls led by even more at the end of the period, up by a 37-21 score. And then five minutes later, they were up 55-32. Brooklyn stopped giving up every other rebound in their own end, but kept defending poorly on perimeter, allowing the Chicago to open the period 4-5 from downtown. They ended up going 15-29 in the first half, hitting a franchise record. Coming into the game tonight, no NBA shot worse from three than the Bulls.

"Everything was uncontested, and it's happened before," Fernández said. "We've addressed it. I think that I remember the Boston game. We had the effort to go and contest shots and so forth. And sometimes I don't know, like we forget, and we have to be reminded again."

Nine of those threes came from the corner, where Brooklyn has been especially vulnerable so far this year. Only the Los Angeles Clippers and Sacramento Kings are allowing their opponents to shoot a higher clip form there this year. Postgame, Randle said it all starts on the interior, however.

"Not sure, not sure," he said. "But I mean, they were living in our painting at will tonight, getting into the paint and spraying the basketball. So I'm not sure exactly why the corner three was open, but I felt like we didn't stop them at all in the paint tonight."

Brooklyn trailed at the break 76-51. Among Brooklyn's prospects, Nolan Traoré was once again a candle on dark, desolate night. He had 11 points at half and two dimes while shooting 5-8 from the field. Outside of he and Julius Randle, all other Nets combined to shoot 9-23 from the field.

It was Brooklyn's third straight game going into the half down more than 20 points. When asked about instructing his team to start better, Fernández made it clear his message hasn't been getting through.

"I can try to say it in Spanish, because in English they don't understand," he said. "So, I'll keep trying and find whatever language works, but yeah, effort was not good."

At the break, when the Nets brought out all their ABA champs, Erving shared a few words, and at the end of his speech, he encouraged the Nets to make comeback.

Dëmin seemed to take it upon himself to reduce the deficit in the third, putting up 11 of the team's first 14 shots in the period, which drew mixed results. Either attacking off screens or occasionally catching flat footed defenders in transition, he got the rim four times and finished on three of them. He was equally aggressive, but far less accurate from deep though, going 1-6 from downtown in the frame.

"Within the game, I saw opportunities, and I took them," Dëmin said.

The Nets were still down 30+ once the bench group checked in, and Chicago's reserves proceeded to outscore Brooklyn's 20-9 in the third. As that period trickled into the fourth, the effort went from poor to nonexistent, as the Nets also gave up another 10 second chance points and got outscored 24-14 on the interior.

"We were not a competitive group," Fernández said. "We were not a connected group. We were not a selfless group. So I saw selfishness. I saw pointing fingers, and you know, and I saw guys that didn't always play hard."

The fourth quarter, or the open-to-public scrimmage vs Chicago, saw Brown Jr. put in 12 points and drop two assists while shooting 3-4 from the field and 2-3 from deep. He finished with an 15/4/1 line.

"It's 82 games," Brown said. "We have to mark it off and move on from it, but obviously learn from it as well. We'll watch film to tomorrow, see some things that we have to fix, but I mean, you can't each effort. You know everybody says that."

If you were to have anyone put lipstick on a pig with a strong performance in garbage time, your top pick is probably the best choice. But in terms of positives, that's about it, and frankly feels like charitable thing to mention anyway. The defense never found its way, and after eight minutes of run in the second half, playing Porter Jr. and Randle wasn't worth it anymore. MPJ. finished with just five points while shooting 1-7 from the field. Randle finished with 17 points on 5-10 shooting.

"That was embarrassing," Randle said. "Been in the league for 13 years, and that's one of the worst games I've ever been a part of. Like, you lose by 30 sometimes. It's just a bad night, but it's how we did it. That just wasn't cool."

The Nets will host the Orlando Magic on Friday, who've won four of their last five, but will need to face themselves first as well. I don't expect either to be easy.

"I think when you win, you find ways to win. When you lose, you find excuses. Seems like a lot of excuses," Randle said. "So, you know, for me, I got to go back into work. I got to go back into the film. I got to figure out how to look myself in the mirror tonight too, like everybody else, and figure out what I can do better. Not point the finger or anything. Like I got to figure out as a man, you know, pride that has played this game since a kid and love it, and has given a lot to me, I got to figure out how I can help the team and be better for the team. So, you know, I put it on me."

Final: Chicago Bulls 137, Brooklyn Nets 107

The Nets will host the Orlando Magic before getting back on the road on Friday evening. At the time of writing, Orlando has won four straight after beginning their season 2-7 with Franz Wagner playing at an All-Star pace. It'll be his first time NBA game with his brother, Mo, as an adversary rather than a teammate. Hopefully the brotherly love prevails and he takes it easy on all of us. This one tips off at 7:30 p.m. EST.
