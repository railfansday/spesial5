Vanguard is extending proxy voting choice to all its U.S. equity funds

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The Investor Choice expansion covers $8.2 trillion in assets, including the world's largest mutual fund, and starts next year

Vanguard Group is expanding its Investor Choice proxy voting program to all of its U.S. equity funds, according to Bloomberg. The funds hold $8.2 trillion in assets, and the change takes effect next year.

Investors in the funds will get to decide which voting approach is applied to their stakes, according to Bloomberg. The expansion covers the Vanguard Total Stock Market Index Fund, which Bloomberg describes as the world’s largest mutual fund.

Clients who sign up can choose from proxy voting policies that fit their views, among them backing company management or emphasizing environmental, social and governance goals, according to Bloomberg. That choice sets the way their slice of the fund is voted at shareholder meetings of publicly traded companies, on matters such as director elections, compensation for executives and central ESG issues. In most cases, the pass-through system stops short of giving a single investor control over how their stake is cast on each separate ballot question.

Vanguard has offered Investor Choice to clients since 2023, according to Bloomberg. The latest update doubles the amount of assets the program covers.

John Galloway, Vanguard's global head of investor engagement, said the program can help address any worry that a fund company's votes lean toward a particular bias, according to Bloomberg. He added that the firm engages with policymakers.

"We have over 50 million individual investors in the U.S., and tens of millions more internationally; those individuals have a wide range of perspectives," Galloway told Bloomberg. He said participation in the program shows investor views fall across the spectrum, in what he called a normal distribution.

Vanguard joins other large U.S. asset managers, including BlackRock $BLK and State Street $STT, that have introduced proxy voting programs, according to Bloomberg. The firms argue that these programs widen investor options and capture the variety of views among their clients. The growth of pass-through voting has coincided with greater U.S. political scrutiny of how far money managers back proposals tied to ESG goals.
