The U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved a new breast cancer drug from Roche, teeing up the launch of a medicine that the company has said could become its top-selling product ever, STAT says. The approval for the drug, known as giredestrant, was one of the most important regulatory decisions Roche had been awaiting. It gives the company another foothold in breast cancer treatments, a field in which it has a storied history with the drug Herceptin through its Genentech subsidiary, though in recent years it has fallen behind rivals in oncology. Analysts broadly see giredestrant becoming a multibillion-dollar blockbuster if Roche wins approval in other forms of breast cancer.

New results from a closely watched clinical trial showed that weight loss drugs are effective for children as young as 6, though some doctors say they are concerned about the prospect of prescribing these medications to such young patients, The New York Times writes. The study, presented at the medical conference Obesity Week, examined children ages 6 to 11 who were taking semaglutide, the compound in Ozempic and Wegovy. Most children in the trial had severe obesity, meaning they had a body mass index of 35 or higher. After 68 weeks, nearly 40% of those who took the drug no longer had obesity, compared with none of the patients on a placebo.
