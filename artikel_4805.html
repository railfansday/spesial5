Here’s what happened in crypto today

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Need to know what happened in crypto today? Here is the latest news on daily trends and events impacting Bitcoin price, blockchain, DeFi, Web3 and crypto regulation.

Today in crypto, Coinkite disclosed how a software error left Coldcard users’ Bitcoin recovery phrases vulnerable to hackers, South Korea moved to tighten oversight of stablecoin issuers and crypto exchanges, and 42 Macro warned that persistent inflation and further monetary tightening could put additional pressure on Bitcoin.

Bitcoin hardware wallet maker Coinkite has detailed how a security flaw in its Coldcard devices went undetected for more than five years despite several independent security reviews, including those using artificial intelligence (AI).

In a Nov. 23 postmortem report, Coinkite said that while its security checks confirmed the wallets had the correct system for generating secure recovery phrases, the company never verified whether the wallets were actually using it. As a result, a software error caused the wallets to use a weaker system instead, making the recovery phrases easier for hackers to guess.

A Bitcoin hardware wallet is a physical device that stores private keys offline, while a recovery phrase is a series of words that allows users to restore access to their funds if the device is lost or damaged.

Coinkite said at least 15 independent security reviews and research efforts failed to catch the flaw before the thefts began. It added that the weaker system went undetected because the recovery phrases looked normal and could still pass some security tests.

The flaw allowed hackers to steal at least 1,789 Bitcoin (BTC), worth around $114.7 million at the time of the thefts, Galaxy Research reported in August. Coinkite said it fixed the flaw on July 31 and introduced further security measures in August.

South Korea’s Financial Services Commission (FSC) unveiled a draft bill Tuesday that would initially limit issuance of won-denominated stablecoins to groups majority-owned by banks and generally cap shareholders’ stakes in crypto exchanges at 20%.

Banks would have to own more than half of a stablecoin issuer collectively, though a fintech or technology company could be its largest individual shareholder, the FSC said in its announcement.

Domestic stablecoin issuers would need FSC authorization and at least 5 billion won (about $3.7 million) in equity capital. They would also have to hold reserves covering at least 100% of their outstanding tokens and redeem them at face value without delay.

Exchanges would need dealing, brokerage and custody licenses and would have to meet capital and operating requirements. Shareholders’ stakes would be capped at 20%, or 34% with FSC approval.

The draft also seeks to lift South Korea’s 2017 ban on domestic initial coin offerings.

Darius Dale, founder and CEO of 42 Macro, says his firm has trimmed its Bitcoin exposure and is waiting for signs that the sell-off has run its course before it considers buying again.

“We’ve already de-risked out of Bitcoin,” Dale said in an interview with Cointelegraph last week. “Our stress levels are low. We’re just waiting to buy.”

Dale’s comments came just days before Bitcoin fell to a two-year low around $55,100 on Saturday, raising questions about whether Bitcoin has finally found its bottom this cycle.

However, Dale warned that persistently high inflation could prompt further Fed tightening that could drain the liquidity that supports Bitcoin. The US Federal Reserve is widely expected to increase rates by 25 basis points at its next meeting on Dec. 9.

“Bitcoin doesn’t generate earnings. Bitcoin needs liquidity to go up,” he said.

Bitcoin was trading at around $64,000 at the time of the interview. Dale said it could fall another 30% to 40% if the S&P 500’s decline from its Oct. 6 peak reaches 20% by Christmas, which would put it at around $38,400. The S&P500 is currently down 7.4% from that peak.
