Week 11 power rankings: Rams inch closer to top-10 heading into bye

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The Los Angeles Rams are riding into their bye week on a four-game winning streak. Granted, those wins were against some lowly teams with a gauntlet of games in the second half of the year, but the Rams now have a week to assess their roster and make plans for a playoff push.

L.A.'s biggest issue is their health. Davante Adams and Terrence Ferguson might return after the bye week, but Puka Nacua, Coleman Shelton and Steve Avila probably won't. While these absences didn't tank the Rams in Week 10 against the Arizona Cardinals, not having them for the hardest stretch of their schedule could be detrimental.

Even still, experts moved the Rams up slightly as a borderline top-10 team. Most had the Rams between Nos. 11 and 12, while Yahoo Sports and Sports Illustrated both ranked L.A. No. 10.

Here is a roundup of the NFL power rankings heading into Week 11.

Who knew that fifth-year RB Ronnie Rivers was their closer? The Rams have certainly been disappointing to date, but give them credit for winning games they need a bit unconventionally.

With all the injuries the Rams have dealt with on offense, they've had to lean on their running back tandem of Corum and Kyren Williams. On Sunday, Corum ran for 100 yards and a touchdown on 17 carries and finished with a career-high 20 touches. It was his sixth straight game with a touchdown. Corum has been a key part of the Rams' rushing success during a four-game winning streak in which they've outrushed their opponents 653-175 (plus-478). According to ESPN Research, that is the team's largest rushing yards differential over a four-game span since 1984, the year Hall of Famer Eric Dickerson ran for an NFL-record 2,105 yards.

Don't look now, but the supposed Dream Team has won four straight to salvage some respectability out of their season and maybe, just maybe, turn them into a threat again. The way the Rams are hemorrhaging players, it might be too little, too late. They will be without their two best wideouts, Davante Adams and Puka Nacua, for a while, and they've got the Packers, Chiefs, 49ers, Cowboys and Seahawks lined up when they come out of their bye week. Never mind what they did to the Cardinals in Week 10. They're just not an offensive juggernaut anymore. They'll need their defense to come up big if they want to stay in the playoff chase.

They dominated the Cardinals without Puka Nacua, which is a big deal. The run game came alive, which they will need.

The Los Angeles Rams won their first game since placing Puka Nacua on injured reserve with a knee injury. They did so in a convincing fashion and showed that they have enough offensive talent to field a functional offense while Nacua and Davante Adams are sidelined.

However, there's a big difference between having a capable offense and being a top-tier Super Bowl threat. Los Angeles could still be the latter, and it is on a four-game winning streak, but it is about to face a higher degree of difficulty.

After their Week 11 bye, the Rams will face a brutal stretch that includes games against the Green Bay Packers, Kansas City Chiefs, San Francisco 49ers, Dallas Cowboys, and Seattle Seahawks (twice).

The Rams have won four in a row. They're still more likely than not to miss the playoffs — 44.2% chance to make it, via DVOA — because they have by far the NFL's toughest remaining schedule. But the Rams played well, albeit against a bad Cardinals team using rookie quarterback Carson Beck in his first career start. The Rams won 34-6 by running it 46 times for 202 yards. Konata Mumpfield provided some hope at receiver with Puka Nacua and Davante Adams out, with three catches for 81 yards. L.A.'s defense limited Arizona to six first downs.

It's still a hard path to the playoffs because of the schedule and injuries, but the Rams are hanging around.

The Rams pivoted from 13 personnel to 20 personnel (two running backs, no tight ends). As I wrote in the offseason, it's not about following Sean McVay and doing what he does. It's about doing what no one else is prepared to stop.
