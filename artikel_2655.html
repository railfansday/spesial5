Takeaways from UH's win at West Virginia: Cougars bounce back, Keisean Henderson arrives

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

MORGANTOWN, W.Va. - Five takeaways from No. 20 Houston's 34-31 victory over West Virginia on Saturday:

How would the Cougars respond after a disappointing performance in a 42-32 loss at Colorado?

The Cougars showed plenty of fight and, while the defense still showed some cracks and the offense sputtered at times, it was a Big 12 road win. UH also remains in position to reach the 10-win mark in consecutive seasons for the first time in program history.

"We had to flush it out and come back stronger," running back Makhi Hughes said. "That's what we did."

All the talk has been about the Keisean Henderson era beginning in 2027. Well, that's still the case, but UH fans got a sneak peek when the true freshman made his first career start in place of injured fifth-year senior Conner Weigman. Henderson had some impressive moments (his 68-yard touchdown pass to Amare Thomas on the third play of the game) but it was what he didn't do that should be praised.

The heralded freshman knew when to throw the ball away. He knew when not to try and do too much. He trusted his pass protection and had enough patience to stay in the pocket long enough to see things develop. He also showed his ability to run, including an 18-yard keeper.

His game management down the stretch resembled that of a veteran, not a first-year quarterback who had been limited to 17 snaps all season. The future is bright for Henderson.

UH's offensive line, a Joe Moore Award semifinalist for the best in the nation, gave Keisean Henderson plenty of time to work. Henderson averaged 3.19 seconds to throw on all dropbacks, according to Pro Football Focus. Only one of Henderson's two sacks was credited to the offensive line. "We got a couple of blitzes and got a little pressure on him, but outside of that, it was way too much time to throw the ball," West Virginia coach Rich Rodriguez said. We'll give the line a pass for the three false starts.

That seems to be what the UH defense has seen in the past month. It was another heavy dose of runs Saturday as the Cougars allowed a season-high 329 yards to the Mountaineers. That followed the 249 yards allowed by UH in a double-digit loss at Colorado. In the first seven games this season, UH was among the best in the nation, allowing an average 82.6 yards per game. In the last four games, UH is allowing 241.5 points per game. But as one UH staffer said leaving the field, "We made the stops when it mattered."

On its final five drives, West Virginia missed a field goal and had four punts. After allowing a touchdown on the opening drive of the second half, UH did not allow any points over the final 27 ½ minutes.

Jonathan Dimas has been money all season, connecting on 13 of his 15 field goal attempts, an 86.7% success rate.

Dimas delivered a 23-yard field goal as time expired and had a 34-yarder late in the third quarter. His 54-yard try just before halftime had the distance but hit the left upright. It would have been a school record.

A week earlier, Dimas had a career-high 52-yard field goal at Colorado.

"Tremendous season," coach Willie Fritz said.
