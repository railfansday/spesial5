Washington WBB Remains Idle in Top 25 Ahead of Coconut Hoops

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The Washington Huskies stood pat at No. 16 in the latest AP Top 25 poll this week, coming off consecutive 50-plus-point victories at Alaska Airlines Arena against Stanford and Cal Poly, with another unique test coming after Thanksgiving.

The Huskies (6-0) are off to the third-best start since Tina Langley took over as head coach back in 2021-22, with a cross-country trip next up to play in the 14th-annual Coconut Hoops event in Fort Myers, Florida.

UW opens the two-game tournament against Oklahoma State at 1:30 p.m. (10:30 a.m. PST) Friday at Alico Arena on the campus of Florida Gulf Coast University, followed by a first-ever meeting with UMass on Sunday at 1 p.m. (10 a.m. PST) to conclude the cross-country trip.

Of the two games, the Cowgirls (7-1) offer the best test of the season for the Huskies as their first opponent that has been ranked inside the top 25. Fifth-year coach Jacie Hoyt's squad came into the 2026-27 season ranked No. 23 and climbed as high as No. 19 before the team dropped to No. 25 following a 64-62 overtime loss at Tulsa.

Oklahoma State fell completely out of the AP Top 25 this week despite recording a 3-0 record last week, earning 39 votes to lead seven schools in the others receiving votes section, placing the Cowgirls as the de facto No. 26 team in the country.

Liv McGill, a two-year starter at Florida and one of the top players in the transfer portal this past offseason, comes into Friday's non-conference matchup with UW as the Cowgirls' leading scorer, averaging 20.4 points, good for second among players in the Big 12 Conference behind former Oklahoma State and current Utah guard Lena Girardi (22) and slightly above the 19.2-point average third-year Huskies' guard Avery Howell has put up through the first six games in 2026-27.

Although the two Power Four guards are among the top 40 scorers in the nation this season, both have a significantly different style of play. Howell has blossomed into UW's go-to shot maker from deep, averaging 4.2 made shots from behind the arc—second in the country behind VCU veteran Macy Spencer—over the first six games, with at least two three-pointers in each of the first six games.

McGill does her damage in the midrange, using a combination of jumpers, runners and layups to score 54-of-100 shots from inside 21 feet, with just 7-of-18 shots from three-point territory for the Cowgirls. That puts more pressure on the UW shooter to continue her career-best clip behind the arc on Friday.

"We're a team that is working on our consistency," Langley said after UW's win over Cal Poly. "I think everyone in the country is. It's kind of like a battle for who can get there first. I was really ragging on them after the Stanford game in the way that we have learned to communicate.

"It's a work in progress, still, because we're also knowing each other and the systems. But as we continue to grow that communication skill I think you'll see this team--it's growth trajectory is really high."

The opening game of the Coconut Hoops tournament in Fort Myers will ultimately come down to the battle of the bigs between Huskies' center/forward Tilda Trygger and ex-Iowa State star Audi Crooks, a three-time All-Big 12 First Team selection that sits at No. 14 in the country shooting 64.5 percent from the floor to begin her Oklahoma State career.

Trygger, a 6-foot-6 transfer from North Carolina State who proved to be one of the top front-court threats in her last season in the ACC in 2025-26, has acclimated well to the UW women's hoops program 10.7 points and 7.3 rebounds per game alongside second-year forward Brynn McGaughy and a trio of guards, fourth-year Sayvia Sellers and both third-year standouts Devin Coppinger and Howell.

If Langley can get multiple double-digit scoring contributors coupled with a strong presence in the paint from either or both McGaughy and Trygger, the Huskies should be able to continue their unbeaten run against the Cowgirls and improve to 7-0 going into the second game of the three-day tournament against UMass.
