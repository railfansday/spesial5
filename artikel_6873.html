Front Office: Radhi Devlukia

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

The host of the A Really Good Cry podcast turned to designer Jess Cooney to create a home office-meets-studio made for deep conversations.

Radhi Devlukia isn’t afraid of tears. On her podcast, A Really Good Cry, the whole point is to get personal, and to do so with guests as varied as supermodel Miranda Kerr, dermatologists, hospice nurses, personal finance advisors—and Devlukia’s husband, fellow podcaster Jay Shetty.

Now, two years into the show, Devlukia, a best-selling author, cook, and founder of beverage company Juni, is unveiling a new studio and office space located within her and Shetty’s Los Angeles house. Devlukia turned to designer Jess Cooney, who has a namesake Berkshires-based interiors firm, to craft a space designed to support these really good cries. Cooney had never tackled a podcast studio before but was ready for the challenge, which included building a room that could both serve as a set—technical components, artificial light, and all—as well as a welcoming home office.

From the start, Cooney knew what the space would not include. “We wanted to move away from the tropes usually seen in podcast studios, like styled bookshelves or cluttered backgrounds that detract from the people being interviewed, and also anything logo or merch heavy,” she says. “And toward something that felt warmer and more inviting.”

Instead, she took cues from spaces like fashion designer Bella Freud’s London apartment, Jean Cocteau’s Villa Santo Sospir in the Côte d’Azur, and Paloma Elsesser’s home by Gregory Rockwell. The resulting office, says Cooney, “has this incredible womb-like, feminine atmosphere that cocoons and holds you.”

Below, Devlukia takes our Front Office survey, giving a behind-the-scenes look at her new home base.

Radhi Devlukia: The hanging art piece in the middle of the window, Kissed by Katie Burdon.

When you need to lock in and power through work, what’s your setup?

Doors open, facing the window, instrumental music playing. I also move areas a lot during work hours to stay focused, so sometimes I’m at the desk, sometimes on the swivel chairs, sometimes on the floor by the window!

I have articles printed about my grandma and grandad on my canvas memory wall. They taught at schools in Africa in the 1970s and there were articles written about how much they were doing for the community. I also have vintage ceramics—I love the mix of old and new.

It really depends. If there are two of us, we’re on the swivel chairs. If there are a few of us, we’re around the marble table. We love keeping things casual at the office so sometimes people sit on the cozy rug or wherever feels comfortable.

What was the best meeting you’ve had there?

We have had so many creative meetings in this office. It’s such a calming and flowing environment. The whole rebrand of the podcast was ideated in this new office, which I’m obsessed with now.

What would you change about it if you could?

Maybe just having the ability to shoot with the blinds open.

What’s the funniest thing that happened there?

My team loves to prank me, so there’s been a lot of laughter in our office!
