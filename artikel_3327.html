Superman Debuts Fiery New Power No Other Kryptonian Has

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Most superheroes use just one superpower to battle the forces of evil, and there are a decent number of them who are fortunate enough to have several powers. Then there are the powerful DC characters like Superman, who has one of the most stacked lineup of abilities. Since being introduced in 1938’s Action Comics #1, Clark Kent has had super strength, super speed, and durability, and other mainstays like flight, heat vision, x-ray vision, and freeze breath were added in the following years.

Even in the modern era, DC Comics continues to add new powers to Superman’s arsenal, even if they only stick around for a little bit. At the start of the Superman Unlimited series in 2025, Clark gained a golden form that rendered him immune from Kryptonite for 200 seconds, but at the cost of draining the solar energy in his body. While that power was burnt out at the end of the Kingdom of Zod crossover event, Superman has a new one to take its place that also isn’t shared by other Kryptonians.

Warning: SPOILERS for Superman #44 are ahead!

Superman #43 kicked off the “Night Shift” story arc with the Man of Steel being zapped with some kind of “dark energy” while he was battling shadow monsters in Metropolis. It's clarified in Superman #44 that this was energy from a corrupted Black Lantern power ring, and the result was Superman being horribly burned by sunlight. Mercy Graves subsequently ran a test on the Kryptonian superhero while he wore a containment suit, and that’s when he first displayed the ability to emit flame from his body.

Unfortunately, this power is more of a curse than a gift. The tradeoff is that Superman’s cells are no longer absorbing sunlight. Meaning, along with the pain he’s suffering, he’ll lose his powers once the sunlight already absorbed in his body is exhausted. Clark won’t be restored to normal until he and his allies, including Marilyn Moonlight and Silver Banshee, uncover the mystery of the Dead Zone within Metropolis. This will in turn lead to the return of Nekron, the personification of death, and his Black Lantern army.

“Night Shift” isn’t Superman’s first time being affected by Black Lantern energy. In Blackest Night #5, Clark was turned into a Black Lantern alongside other superheroes who had died and been resurrected. They were later freed from Nekron’s control when Hal Jordan turned them into White Lanterns. In that same crossover event, the corpse of the Earth-2 Superman was reanimated as a Black Lantern, but it was eventually destroyed by the Justice Society.

Superman has seen many iterations over time, with many versions eclipsing his original in strength, and some of the best are featured here.

While Superman grapples with the vampire-like problem of bursting into flame whenever he’s hit with sunlight, another member of the Superman Family will also soon gain a new power. This will happen to Jon Kent, who’s now going by Tomorrow Man, in January’s Superman Unlimited #21. However, in Jon’s case, it sounds like it will be a true power-up rather than a burden like what’s been thrust on his father.

The Superman Unlimited #21 solicitation teases that the issue will revolve around Clark and Jon’s various vision-based powers, which will be put to the test when something mysterious threatens National City. The events that unfold will somehow result in Jon gaining a power that Clark doesn’t have, though it’s not specifically clarified if this will also be vision-based or something different. Either way, the solicitation indicates that this will be a permanent addition to Jon’s arsenal, whereas Superman won’t have to deal with being an involuntary Human Torch for too long.

Superman #44 is now available from DC Comics.
