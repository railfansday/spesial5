Beyond commission splits: How wellness benefits give brokerages a recruiting edge

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Brokerages are expanding benefits aimed at agent well-being, from access to group-style PPO options to coaching on stress and resilience. Executives frame the programs as foundational support as market conditions remain uncertain heading into 2027.  AI Summary

By many estimations, real estate professionals are facing the most challenging housing market in recent memory in 2027. Unlike in the past, many will be doing so with access to more resources than ever designed to ensure they are feeling healthy, at the peak of their game and ready to tackle whatever the 2027 housing market throws at them.

This is because over the past year, several brokerages announced a variety of wellness programs and health insurance opportunities they are now offering to agents, who as independent contractors, typically do not have access to things like insurance through their brokerage.

Since the start of 2026, ENRG Realty, Compass International Holdings, Equity Union Real Estate and John R. Wood Properties have announced the launch of health insurance or wellness benefits initiatives.

“We were founded on Maslow Hierarchy of Needs, so health insurance fits right in with some of the core basic values that we need to provide to our agents so they can be successful and thrive,” Erinn Nobel, the co-founder and president of ENRG Realty and a HousingWire Woman of Influence award winner, said. “In order for our agents to function at a high level, they can’t be sitting at home worried about an illness or being able to pay their bills because they don’t have health coverage. It is a foundational offering and a benefit for us to be able to provide it for our agents.”

Nobel said that while some Realtor boards or associations offer health insurance, there is often just one policy option, something she experienced first hand after moving to Alaska from Washington state.

“It was great to be able to access insurance through our board, but there was only one plan, so you couldn’t shop around,” Nobel said. “It just didn’t sit well with me that my options were so limited.”

A similar experience with challenges accessing insurance for his family is also what inspired Harma Hartouni, the CEO of Equity Union Real Estate, a RealTrends Verified brokerage, to begin providing his agents with access to group-style health coverage options, including a nationwide PPO network, through a partnership with Trident BPO.

When he approached the executive team about launching this initiative, Hartouni said they were a bit divided as to whether or not they should do this.

“Some thought the agents wouldn’t care, but some of our managers thought it was great,” Hartouni said. “Ultimately, our agents are our clients, and I knew that if these people were not healthy or not in a clear mindset in this challenging market, they would not function well.”

In the current uncertain economic environment, Hartouni added that the offering has also provided many agents, who previously had access to insurance through a spouse who lost their job, a level of peace of mind that they couldn’t previously provide.

“The consistent feedback from agents is that they are saving money and are able to see better doctors,” Hartouni said.

Nobel said she’s heard similar feedback from her agents. After rolling out the program, Nobel said she and her team received countless messages from agents saying thank you. Beyond better serving existing agents, Nobel said the program also helped ENRG Realty with agent recruitment.

“It has become an attraction magnet for us. Savvy agents that have been in the industry for a while are starting to really compare brokerages and look at what benefits they get from each brokerage and the different values they provide,” Nobel said. “If an agent already has access to health insurance through a spouse or partner, then this may not convey the same value as it would to a self-employed single mom who would love to have access to health care.”

Hartouni has also noticed the agent recruitment benefits of offering health insurance.

“The smart broker is looking for producing agents that are in the middle of their career, where they still have plenty of years left in the business to learn and grow, but have already begun to establish themselves,” he said. “But these agents often have families to think about and how can we help them have one less thing to worry about? We can provide them with access to health insurance.”

While John R. Wood Properties is not providing agents with access to health insurance, the brokerage recently announced it was providing its agents with access to individualized coaching focused on their personal well-being and professional development, through a partnership with HatchPath. Through the program, agents can gain access to coaching support across a wide range of areas including nutrition, stress management, mental resilience, leadership, career development and major life transitions.

“People are just so burn[ed] out in general and everybody is on edge from personal challenges, financial challenges, family challenges. I just thought what better way to support our people than to give them access to coaching,” Corey McCloskey, the president of John R. Wood Properties, a RealTrends Verified brokerage, said. “I think a lot of us who run businesses have realized that you bring your whole self to work. You can’t just turn off what’s happening in your personal life when you walk into your office — it affects you whether you realize it or not.”

While still in the early stages of the rollout, McCloskey said she has seen her agents and staff use HatchPath coaching for help with everything from financial planning to fitness and overall physical wellness.

Although brokerages are continuing to face challenging housing market conditions and ever-tightening profit margins, these executives believe it’s important for brokerages to support their agents beyond just professional development.

“If we are encouraging people to be entrepreneurs, we have to support them and provide them some sort of value,” Nobel said. “Anytime we can offer a benefit that has real value to agents, we really look to do that because ultimately that is what we as a brokerage are supposed to do — provide value for our agents.”

Nobel added that she would encourage more brokerages to consider providing agents with access to health insurance.

“This is something that we need to be offering our agents in the world right now,” she said. “Offering insurance is what is best for our industry.”

McCloskey, a HousingWire Woman of Influence, shares a similar view.

“Knowing that not only us, but other companies are supporting agents as whole people, I think that will only serve to make our industry better,” she said. “The younger leaders coming into the industry have seen the toll this industry can take on someone who doesn’t take care of themselves. They want to do things differently, and I think we have an opportunity now that we’ve never had before where we can offer these sorts of things to people and really tell them how important they are to us.”
