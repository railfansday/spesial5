Day 20/21 – An Unexpected Party

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Route: Pennine Way thru-hike – Kirk Yetholm, out and back

After all the trains and buses, and pleasant days wandering around towns and cities in the northeastern UK, I arrived in Kirk Yetholm.  The Border Hotel, the official end of the Pennine Way was just as warm and cozy as I imagined.  It was with a certain amount of melancholy that I found myself standing on the village green of this tiny settlement in Scotland.  Signs posted at the terminus of the trail allowed me to reminisce the points along my own journey.

Per tradition, the folks at the pub offered me a free half pint to commemorate my arrival.  I declined.  I had not earned that reward.

My room was lovely and comfortable with a view of the garden.  I had made this reservation months ago.  My two boxes had arrived a couple of weeks earlier and were waiting for me in my room.  In hindsight, I should have left the extra clothes at home.  Everything in those two boxes now needed to fit in my pack.  This included a large duffel that I use to protect my backpack when checked for the flight.

The rest of the day passed quietly.  Tomorrow, Graeme and Rachel would arrive at the end of their Pennine Way walk.

The next morning, after breakfast, I decided I could at least have a look around.  With my ankle brace and the last 10 days of recovery, I could comfortably walk 3 or 4 miles.  A walk around town revealed that Kirk Yetholm has no shops or cafes.  It is literally the hotel, a church, and the nearby houses and pastures.  Residents use the bus to Kelso in order to shop.  There was nothing left but the trail.

In total, I had walked 117.5 miles on the Pennine Way.  If I walked another 3 miles, I could round out my achievement to 120 miles.  And so, off I went.  Skies were grey, but there didn’t look to be any rain.  I headed out of town, south on the Pennine Way, just me and my hiking boots.  No pack.

The road out of the village climbs steeply, past sheep and pony pastures.  Then it descends to a fork: the “high road” and the “low road”.  The lower path continued on the paved road. The high road headed into the open hills.  My spirit needed the high road.  I turned left, crossed a small stream, and headed upwards.

As I climbed, the wind grew stronger.  It was literally pushing me up the slope!  By the time I reached the top of the rise at Green Humbleton, I had to lean into the wind to remain standing.  But I didn’t care.  This is where I wanted to be.  This hilltop was just about 2 miles from Kirk Yetholm.  This would do.  I turned around to head back.

Afterwards, I spent some time relaxing in my room before heading to the pub for a cider.  I was about halfway through my beverage when Graeme and Rachel walked in, followed by a large entourage!  Although they live in the US, they are originally from the UK with lots of friends remaining in their home country.  And all those friends joined them for the last section of the Pennine.

It was such a festive evening.  Graeme and Rachel were celebrated by the landlord of the inn, who stopped by to tell us the history of the place and share his favorite jokes.  The official register, going back to 1968, was signed and certificates were handed out.  They had done it!  I was so happy for them.

The next few hours were full of food, drink, and fellowship.  Everyone shared stories.  The landlord eventually sent over a round of Limoncello!  We were there past closing, but it was finally time to say farewell.  Both to new friends and to the Pennine Way.  Tomorrow, I would backtrack my way via buses and trains to Edinburgh where I would await my flight home.

I went to see a podiatrist as soon as I returned home.  The first thing he told me was, “ligaments take longer to heal than bones.”

Exactly one month before I started the Pennine Way, I was making the long, rocky descent from Furnace Mountain in Shenandoah National Park.  I had been wilderness backpacking for three days.  With less than three miles left in my trip, I rolled my left ankle—badly!  I took a long break and did my best at some trailside first aid.

That injury came back to haunt me.  The doctor explained that my ankle had not yet fully healed, even though I had no pain  It eventually gave out due to overuse.  It took me 100 miles to hit that threshold, but once it gave out, there was nothing to do but secure it in a brace and get off the trail.  The good news is that I can expect a full recovery.  As of yesterday, he said I can run and hike again as long as I wear my brace for the next month and don’t experience any pain.

I’ve decided to write one last post, consolidating my lessons-learned and talking about overall costs.  If that sounds fun or useful, join me for that final post.  And thank you one and all for the kind comments and encouragement as I pursued my Pennine Way dreams.
