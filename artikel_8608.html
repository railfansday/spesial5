A Cheaper City Weekend – Stay Near a Train Line and Do the Pass Math

On a city weekend, book a room near a train line rather than downtown, ride transit instead of paying to park, and plan your days around sights that are free for visitors. Buy an attractions pass only if the places you’ll see would cost more at the gate.

The easy version of a city weekend, a downtown hotel with the car in its garage and a ticket to every big attraction, is also the priciest. In the Loop, Chicago’s downtown, the Palmer House Hilton charges $57 a night just to self-park.

Good transit means you need neither a car nor a downtown room. A hotel near a train or bus stop a few miles out often costs less than one downtown, and so can a hostel’s private room.

Two other savings need a check before you count on them. A museum’s free day may be for local residents only, and an attractions pass saves money only on places you’d have paid to see anyway.

In Chicago, a CTA 3-day pass costs $15 and covers 72 hours of buses and trains, including the Blue Line ride from O’Hare that costs $5 without a pass. Buy it from a vending machine in the O’Hare station. A taxi from O’Hare to downtown averages $50, the airport says. Stay near a Blue Line stop such as Logan Square, and the same train takes you to the Loop.

The Art Institute, the Field Museum, and the Shedd Aquarium save their free days for Illinois residents. Lincoln Park Zoo and the National Museum of Mexican Art are free to everyone, and Chicago Greeter volunteers lead free hour-long walks from the Chicago Cultural Center.

Chicago CityPASS costs $144 per adult for five attractions over nine days, with upgrades such as faster entry at several of them. See just three, such as the Skydeck, the Art Institute, and the Field Museum, and general admission without the upgrades starts at $94 for all three, $50 less than the pass.
