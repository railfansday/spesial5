British Airways Visa Card Review: Is It Worth The $95 Annual Fee?

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The British Airways Visa Signature® Card is one of Chase’s co-branded airline credit cards, and British Airways Club is certainly a popular frequent flyer program, given the overall value of the Avios ecosystem, plus the number of destinations in the United States that British Airways serves.

Who should be applying for the British Airways Visa, and under what circumstances is it worth spending money on it? Let’s take a look at what you need to know about this card, and what the best alternatives are.

If you’re considering the British Airways Visa Signature Card, its welcome bonus, application rules, and annual fee are all probably top of mind. So let’s start by taking a look at these important details.

The British Airways Visa currently offers the following welcome bonus: Earn 75,000 Avios after you spend $5,000 on purchases within the first three months of account opening.

Based on my current points and miles valuations, which put Avios at 1.2 cents apiece, this current bonus is worth about $900. That’s a pretty solid welcome bonus, especially for a card with a moderate annual fee.

The British Airways Visa has a $95 annual fee. This is not waived for the first year, and there’s no additional fee to add authorized users.

British Airways’ credit card has no foreign transaction fees, so it’s potentially a great card to use for purchases abroad.

The welcome bonus on the British Airways Visa isn’t available to those who currently have the card, or those who have received a new cardmember bonus on the card in the past 24 months. You are eligible for this card if you’ve had the Iberia Visa Signature® Card and/or Aer Lingus Visa Signature® Card, despite the similarities between the cards.

Chase’s other general policies on card approvals apply as well, including the 5/24, whereby you generally won’t be approved for a Chase card if you’ve opened five or more credit cards in the last 24 months.

The British Airways Visa Card has no foreign transaction fees, but does the rewards structure warrant putting spending on the card? It might, but only thanks to additional bonuses offered by the card, which incentivize spending.

British Airways is known for adding large taxes and surcharges to some of its own award flights, so this is worth keeping in mind.

Personally I find Avios to be one of the most useful frequent flyer programs for redeeming points, thanks to the number of redemptions this opens up.

Often one of the big challenges people have with points is figuring out how to pool them since you typically need enough points in a single account for a redemption. A cool feature of the British Airways Club program is that you can form household accounts. This allows you to pool the Avios you earn with up to six other people registered at the same address as you.

This is especially awesome in the context of a credit card welcome bonus, since you can pool a ton of Avios in a single account if you have two people get the card in a household, etc.

There are some benefits to the British Airways Visa Card that could alone justify having this card. For some people, it will be worth spending money on the card to unlock some perks, while for others just having the card and not spending money on it offers sufficient benefits. Let’s look at what those benefits are, and how they work.

One of the best benefits of the British Airways Signature Visa is that it offers a 10% discount on British Airways flights starting in the United States. To take advantage of this, just book through ba.com/chase10, and use your card to pay while using promotion code BACHASE10.

British Airways now also offers Reward Flight Saver from the United States, allowing you to redeem more Avios and pay lower surcharges. Getting a further statement credit of up to $200 on that is pretty great.

One of the potentially great perks of the British Airways Visa is that you can earn a Travel Together Ticket when you spend $30,000 on the card in a calendar year.

This companion ticket will be deposited in your British Airways Club account four to six weeks after you’ve completed the required spending.

With this certificate, you can book a British Airways award ticket at the full cost of Avios (plus taxes, fees, and surcharges), and then for the second passenger you don’t have to pay any Avios, but rather just have to pay all the fees.

This also offers a lot of flexibility — you can also use the voucher as a solo traveler and get 50% off the Avios required for a ticket. Your ticket can originate anywhere in the world, and you can even travel on Aer Lingus or Iberia.

This can represent a fantastic deal for first and business class travel, given how much those tickets would otherwise cost. This isn’t for everyone, though it has the potential to represent a good value, and I know it’s a feature that many value.

The British Airways Visa is one of the best co-branded airline credit cards out there, in my opinion. The card offers a solid welcome bonus, and it can be worth spending money on the card to earn a companion ticket.

Beyond that, the card can be worth having for the discounts offered on both paid tickets (up to 10% off) or award tickets (up to $200 in statement credits), which can cover the annual fee over and over. That said, the card would be even stronger if it offered a free checked bag and lounge access as benefits — though I’d expect the annual to increase if those were added, especially the latter perk.

When it comes to earning British Airways Avios long term, personally I’d much rather earn Amex, Bilt, Capital One, and Chase points, and transfer them to British Airways. Not only does this allow you to earn points at a much faster rate, but it also gives you a lot more flexibility.
