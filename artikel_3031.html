Breakfast News: Summit’s FDA Setback Isn’t the End

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Summit falls on cancer drug approval setback, Nvidia headlines the week’s earnings, and more…

Shares of Summit Therapeutics (SMMT -6.20%) plunged more than 20% in pre-market trading after the company announced on Nov. 15 that the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) issued a Complete Response Letter (CRL).

A CRL means regulators have finished reviewing a drug application and can’t approve it in its current form. This rejection halts the immediate U.S. commercial launch of the company’s flagship cancer drug, ivonescimab, in a targeted lung cancer setting.

The sell-off looks heavier than the problem warrants, since it ties to one statistical requirement in one clinical trial, not a flaw in the drug.

Summit had sought FDA approval to use ivonescimab alongside standard chemotherapy for patients with a specific type of advanced lung cancer known as EGFR-mutated non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), where tumors kept growing after initial targeted therapies stopped working.

Unlike older treatments that perform only one task, ivonescimab is a "bispecific antibody," engineered to attack cancer in two ways at once: it takes the brakes off the body's immune system so immune cells can find and destroy tumor cells, while simultaneously cutting off the blood supply that tumors need to grow.

The FDA said Summit did not provide substantial evidence of effectiveness for this patient group. The sticking point was overall survival (OS), the gold standard measure in cancer research of how long patients live after starting treatment. Simply slowing down the cancer's growth was not enough to justify approval.

Summit’s application relied on results from a large, international late-stage study called the HARMONi trial. To win approval, the company tracked two main goals: progression-free survival (PFS), which measures how long a patient lives without their cancer worsening, and OS, which measures total lifespan.

The drug easily succeeded on the first goal, reducing the risk of the disease worsening or causing death by 43% compared to chemotherapy alone. On OS, the data narrowly missed the strict mathematical threshold regulators required to prove the benefit was not due to chance.

Summit submitted updated follow-up data showing a 24% lower risk of death and a clean, manageable side-effect profile. The FDA held firm, since regulators generally require companies to hit their pre-agreed statistical targets on the first try.

This isn’t the end of the road for Summit Therapeutics. "Second-line" treatment (meaning therapies used only after a patient's first treatment fails) represents only a fraction of ivonescimab’s total commercial value. Most of the drug's blockbuster potential lies in "first-line" settings.

The company has 17 ongoing late-stage clinical trials, including major studies like HARMONi-3, HARMONi-7, and HARMONi-GI3 targeting broader lung and gastrointestinal cancers. Summit is now focused on generating the definitive survival data needed to win over regulators in larger markets. It has a development partner Akeso (AKESF -3.38%), a $2 billion investment from pharmaceutical giant AstraZeneca (AZN +1.17%), and nearly $630 million in cash reserves, so there’s ample money to continue research.
