16 Space-Based Technologies That Could Solve Business Problems

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Advances in satellite technology, connectivity and data analysis are creating new ways for businesses to address challenges here on Earth. From monitoring infrastructure and tracking environmental risks to improving communications in remote locations, space-based capabilities are becoming increasingly practical across industries.

For businesses, the challenge isn’t simply gaining access to these tools—it’s identifying where they can improve decisions, boost efficiency and strengthen resilience. Below, members of Forbes Technology Council highlight space-based capabilities that are becoming practical for business use and explain the problems they could help solve.

Satellite radar can detect subtle ground movement around railways, pipelines and industrial sites, giving businesses early warning of infrastructure risks across vast networks. Paired with ground inspections, it helps operators target maintenance before damage escalates. The payoff: fewer costly shutdowns, smarter use of maintenance budgets, and better protection for people, assets and revenue. Its real value is turning unseen risks into actionable priorities. - Shelli Brunswick, SB Global LLC

Space-based weather and climate intelligence is becoming practical enough to shape operational decisions across industries. Hyper-local forecasts, severe weather tracking and climate risk modeling now draw on satellite data at resolutions that support utility grid planning, agriculture, insurance underwriting, logistics routing and renewable energy forecasting, leading to meaningful business and financial impact. - Nitin Agarwal, Luminace

Satellite-based methane detection is becoming a practical business capability. Advanced orbital sensors can identify emissions at individual industrial facilities, enabling companies to detect costly leaks without extensive ground inspections. Integrating this intelligence with enterprise analytics transforms environmental monitoring into actionable insights that reduce operational losses and strengthen regulatory compliance. - Gouri Sankar Dash, Tata Consultancy Services

On-orbit AI processing is becoming practical enough to change how businesses use satellite data. Instead of sending massive amounts of raw imagery to Earth for analysis, satellites can process data in orbit and transmit relevant insights. That can reduce latency and bandwidth while enabling faster responses to disasters, infrastructure issues and other events. - Karan Alang, Versa Networks Inc.

Satellite-based Earth observation is becoming a powerful business tool these days. Using frequent imagery and AI analytics, companies can monitor crop health, detect supply-chain disruptions, assess natural-disaster damage and evaluate infrastructure risks. What once required costly, time-consuming field inspections can increasingly be done remotely, helping businesses make faster, data-driven decisions and build resilience. - Deepali Kaushik, NBCUniversal

Satellite-based flood monitoring can help companies protect construction sites and supply routes. Combine updated imagery with site and road data to identify exposure and prioritize inspections or rerouting. The value comes from turning that information into an operational decision, with people on the ground confirming conditions. - Shahen Minasyan, Twin Peaks Inc. (TPI)

Satellite-based maritime connectivity is finally becoming practical enough to keep ships connected far from shore. Having worked aboard cruise ships, I know how quickly isolation complicates operations. Reliable links can support live equipment diagnostics and faster medical consultations. Start with one critical shipboard system. Space technology earns its keep when it solves problems at sea. - Joel Frenette, TravelFun.ai

Space-based data centers are an exciting emerging capability. Companies like TakeMe2Space are exploring how computing infrastructure in orbit could address some of Earth’s data center challenges. While technical and economic hurdles remain, this approach could create new possibilities for processing data in space and reducing dependence on resource-intensive ground infrastructure. - Prashanthi Kolluru, KloudPortal Technology Solutions Pvt Ltd.

There’s satellite-verified proof of origin for supply chains. New rules on deforestation and forced labor require companies to prove where raw materials such as cocoa, palm oil or timber came from, down to the plot. Linking satellite imagery of those plots to supplier records and purchase orders in the ERP turns a paper declaration into evidence an auditor or customs officer can check. - Hari Sonnenahalli, NTT Data Business Solutions

Satellite broadband is now practical. It gives remote sites like mines, ships, farms and disaster zones low-latency links to the cloud where fiber can’t reach. Those edge locations can stream Internet of Things data, run cloud-based analytics, and stay online when local networks fail, so remote operations become connected and data-driven. - Srinivas Chippagiri, Salesforce Inc.

High-precision satellite positioning is becoming practical for construction, agriculture and autonomous operations. With real-time correction services, centimeter-level positioning can help businesses coordinate equipment, automate physical work and reduce costly errors. Location is becoming an operational signal, not just a point on a map. - Vibhor Kumar, EDB Postgres AI

Direct satellite connection to ordinary phones—which allows workers to stay reachable outside cell coverage—used to mean buying satellite hardware and training people to carry it, so most crews didn’t leverage it. Now a standard phone can send a message through a satellite from a dead zone. For the utilities, logging, mining and construction industries, that means a lone worker in a canyon can reach someone, and a supervisor always knows where the crew is. - Kriti Faujdar, Yelp

There’s satellite-based asset tracking for remote operations. Low-orbit connectivity now makes it affordable to monitor equipment, shipments and field crews in places cellular networks never reached. For businesses with assets at job sites, rail lines or rural facilities, that turns blind spots into live data on location, condition and usage, which means fewer lost assets and faster response. - Steven Singer, Arbill

Satellite monitoring of construction progress can help lenders decide when to release project funds. Imagery can verify whether a road, plant or housing phase has advanced as reported, flag stalled work, and direct inspectors to locations that need a closer look. That gives credit teams a cross-check between site visits, reducing delays in drawdowns without treating pixels as proof of workmanship. Each alert can be tied to human inspection and a documented payment decision. - Jagadish Gokavarapu, Wissen Infotech

Low-Earth-orbit satellite internet’s lower latency and falling costs now support video visits, remote monitoring and image transfer in rural clinics that fiber never reached. Hospitals are also adding it as backup connectivity when storms or cable cuts take down terrestrial lines. For health systems, it closes access gaps and keeps clinical systems reachable during disasters. - Will Conaway, Tuxedo Cat Consulting

Satellite monitoring of harmful algal blooms is becoming practical for aquaculture and water-dependent businesses. Ocean color sensors can identify changes in water conditions that may indicate developing blooms. Fish farms and coastal operators can use these alerts to prioritize water testing and take protective action when needed. This helps reduce unexpected losses and supports better management of marine operations. - Salice Thomas, Wipro Limited
