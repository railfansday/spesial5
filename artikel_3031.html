Three Things I’m Looking at in the New York Jets vs Los Angeles Chargers Game

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The New York Jets are headed to the City of Angels for a football game where stadium seats cost less than a cheeseburger in that stadium. A rare Sunday where I get to wait until 4pm to be utterly disappointed instead of the classic 1:00 devastation. I figured the Jets hadn't been to SoFi too often, but I was shocked to learn that they haven't been there since two trips in 2020.

The first trip should sound fairly familiar: a Week 11 contest between two terrible teams that had only two combined wins. The Jets employed an old journeyman QB with no tangible long-term future with the team, and the Chargers had Justin Herbert. Had the Chargers not won this past Monday, this would've been the exact description of both Jets vs. Chargers games at SoFi Stadium.

The second trip was much more memorable: the 0-13 Jets faced a talented 9-4 Rams team and shocked the world by finally winning their first game of the season. We all remember this as the game that shifted the Jets' odds of having the first overall pick when every poor fanbase was looking to 'Tank For Trevor'. The Jets won the following week and finished 2-14, securing the second overall pick and failing to properly tank.

For those of you who value tanking so much, the Jets have an opportunity to repeat history by winning a game in SoFi Stadium and dropping further down the draft board. But don't fret: if history repeats the first trip to SoFi, it would mean the Chargers winning a shootout against the Jets, and you get what you want.

For what it's worth, I hate the toxicity of tank culture. For 16 years, the Jets have been losing time and time again, getting high draft picks, and the team isn't any better. How about we root for wins? Especially this year, with the Colts having released Daniel Jones and the Jets having two additional first-round picks, why do you care so much whether the Jets' specific pick is 6th or 11th? The flexibility to move anywhere on the board will be there regardless.

We keep saying late wins don't matter, we keep saying let's get the highest pick, isn't that the definition of insanity?! We're literally yearning for the same thing every year and expecting a different result. Clearly, the high draft picks alone aren't enough to save this team. Especially when they miss (Zach Wilson, Mekhi Becton, Olu Fashanu?).

Anyway, let's get back to this game. Regardless of which outcome you're rooting for, let's discuss what's worth looking at:

Can someone tell the New York Jets that the FAA has resumed flights? They must still think they're grounded. In each of the last two games, the Jets have scored exactly zero touchdowns and four field goals for a total of 12 points in each game. With all the firepower at their disposable, this is a pretty inexcusable and mind-numbing stat. We talked last week about specifically scoring in the red zone, but now they can't even throw a TD bomb from outside the red zone. Their last touchdown was a 21-yard run by Braelon Allen in the 3rd quarter against the Raiders.

Someone on social media said it's Breece Hall's fault they haven't scored a touchdown in the two games they've played since his return. Seeing how Braelon only scored two touchdowns in his five-game break out, I can't co-sign that claim, but perhaps decreasing his workload to just eight carries per game is hurting the offense overall. Allen's still averaging a healthy 4.5 yards per carry with Breece back. Hall is averaging 3.9 yards per carry and has had 26 carries to Braelon's 16. It's definitely interesting to consider what the rushing totals would be if it were Braelon who had 26 carries and Breece who had 16.

While the Chiefs defense has been good, they had allowed a TD in six of the seven games they played before the Jets, despite facing bottom-feeders like the Dolphins, Raiders, and Colts during that time. The Bills had allowed seven of eight, with the exception being Joe Fagnano's first (and perhaps only) NFL start ever. Sure those are two good teams, but neither defensive unit is giving the '85 Bears a run for their money.

How about this hypothesis: Geno Smith isn't doing enough to get this offense going. We mentioned last week how three Jets receivers sit atop the league-wide ranks for average separation score. Geno is not getting people open, that's a combination of the receivers themselves and Frank Reich. Geno's job is to hit people when they're open, or at least not completely covered and he's failing to do that.

A quick look at Geno's numbers from game-to-game tell the story. He's been afraid to throw interceptions, so instead he makes a safer throw or holds onto the ball too long and takes a bad sack. After a three-week stretch where he aired out the ball more freely but threw an interception in each game, it seems apparent that his fear has returned. He stopped throwing picks, but had a huge uptick in sacks (again).

In these past two weeks of safe Geno, he is throwing 58.5% completion, 0 touchdowns, 0 interceptions, 6 sacks in 10 quarters, and a paltry 5.9 yards per attempt. To compare that to the previous 8 games, he was throwing 69% completion, 12 touchdowns, 3 interceptions, 23 sacks in 32 quarters and 8 yards per attempt. The issue is that now he's not hitting those "safe throws" either – and the offense as a whole is suffering.

You could see pretty clearly the stark difference of Cade's time in the game. He moved the ball down the field, was absolutely fearless, and while he was much less accurate than Geno he was still able to make plays due to his willingness to trust his playmakers to make plays – which they did. Geno is the more accurate option, he obviously is better at pre-snap recognition at this point in their careers, but he needs to find Cade's level of fearlessness if the offense is going to take flight again.

This week is the latest in a weekly tradition of telling you guys to buckle up for a thrashing by the opposing team's running back. It was looking like the Jets would finally get a break from facing a star running back, as Omarion Hampton was struggling(!) the first 8 weeks of the season. In his first seven games, he was only averaging 44 rushing yards per game on 3.5 yards per carry and three touchdowns in that span. Terrible numbers.

In the last two games, however, he must've remembered he was a first round pick last year because now he's averaging 81.5 yards per game on 4.7 yards per carry and one touchdown in that span. He has been explosive and a huge part of the reason why a team that was only averaging 14.1 points per game with 0 wins is now averaging 22.5 over it's last two and split 1-1.

What can the Jets do to stop him? Smart money would say 'nothing', and bet the over on Hampton's yards. FanDuel currently has his rushing yards at 62.5, are they kidding? The Jets are on a streak of allowing 80+ rushing yards to the opposing RB1, and almost had bonafide scrub Corey Kiner reach 62.5 (he had 57) in just 11 carries in garbage time in Foxborough. I won't go back and keep talking about Kyle Monangai's performance, let's keep it to the last five games:

Why include that last column? To make it clear that for this ineptitude, game script is irrelevant. The Jets could be down multiple scores, up multiple scores; it could be a one-score game. The only constant is the opposing leading rusher dominating our hopeless group. Our hopeless group is now 30th in rushing yards allowed per game, facing a team that's surprisingly 10th on the year despite being winless until Monday. Their game plan is clear: Run it early and run it often.

Let's make money together: Hampton over 62.5 yards + Hampton touchdown. If you're a risk-taker like me, you can alt Hampton's rush yards to 80+, add Herbert 150+ pass yards, and add Geno +175 pass yards. Let's turn $20 into $100 to dry our tears if we don't get our way on Sunday.

I'm super curious to know how many people reading this article have ever watched an episode of The Dukes of Hazzard. It was a great show whose influence on pop culture still resonates today in several ways. Unfortunately for us, I'm fairly confident the phrase "a great show" will never describe the Jets' defense this year. Despite offseason improvements at every position, the team has been absolutely horrid at stopping other teams when it's not raining.

They allow 15 points per rain game and 28.3 points per game in clear skies. That 28.3 number would rank 2nd worst in the NFL if it were the full-season average. Perhaps this utter failure is why Aaron Glenn, who famously said defensive play-calling is his superpower, decided to give up play-calling to a guy who had never called plays in the NFL before. Yes, Brian Duker, the first-time defensive coordinator who was hired due to familiarity with Glenn, was suddenly calling the shots against arguably the best team in the NFL.

I can write an entirely separate piece on what that means for the big picture of the Aaron Glenn Error and how that reads as a symbolic waving of the white flag, but instead I'll focus on this game. Is he calling the plays for this game? If so, how does the defense respond?

The team finally caught their second interception in 27 games, and maybe they can thank their playcaller, Brian Duker, for that. This Sunday, they face a quarterback who threw an interception in every single game until this past Monday. Can Duker get Herbert feeling jittery? We also saw a brief Will McDonald sighting last week after weeks of silence. With him wanting a bigger role and guaranteed to remain a Jet this season, can Duker get more out of him than Glenn did? I will be watching with bated breath. Let's go Jets!
