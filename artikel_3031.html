Finally, General Motors Embraces Fixing 1 of Its Few Weaknesses

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

General Motors may have been reluctant to embrace hybrids after EVs sputtered in the U.S. market, but now it seems to be changing its tune.

By Daniel Miller – Nov 24, 2026 at 10:00AM EST

10 stocks we like better than General Motors ›

General Motors (NASDAQ:GM) investors have heard plenty about the company's electric future, and also the billions in charges it (and many other auto companies) took by pumping the brakes when electric vehicles (EVs) took off more slowly than anticipated in the U.S.

Then a funny thing happened: Demand for hybrids took off, and it's fair to say that GM wasn't ready to seize the opportunity.

That appears to be changing. Now, GM is preparing to bring more hybrids into its lineup. That could help it compete for customers who want better fuel economy without making the jump to an all-electric vehicle.

In October, GM confirmed plans to prepare its Lansing Delta Township plant for future hybrid production. The factory currently builds the Chevrolet Traverse, Buick Enclave, and GMC Acadia, although GM hasn't officially announced hybrid nameplates or launch timing. Reuters separately reported plans for hybrid versions of those three-row SUVs, citing people familiar with the matter.

For investors, the potential benefit is more than simply adding another powertrain to the next presentation slide. A hybrid could help GM retain a buyer who likes its vehicles but would otherwise visit a competitor for better fuel economy.

Toyota Motor already competes for those shoppers, and initially was one of the only automakers telling the industry to consider hybrids before going all in on EVs. Its U.S. Grand Highlander Hybrid sales reached 65,201 during the first nine months of 2026, up 34.3% year over year. That doesn't establish how many sales GM missed, but it emphasizes a segment where GM could offer its existing customers a reason to stay within its brands.

The surprise is that hybrids don't necessarily require shareholders to accept worse vehicle economics, as you might assume. Rival Ford Motor Company has already offered a compelling reason to think otherwise.

Two years ago, at a Bernstein conference, Ford CEO Jim Farley noted: "Many of our hybrids in the U.S. are now more profitable than their non-hybrid equivalent."

Farley explained that customer pricing had begun covering the additional hybrid cost, something Ford hadn't achieved a year earlier. Obviously, adding a battery and electric motor adds expense. But if buyers value the fuel savings or additional capabilities enough to pay for them, the vehicle economics can absolutely work for automakers.

That changes the discussion from reluctantly offering hybrids to pursuing products customers want – which, in theory, should help protect market share without big losses generally associated with all-electric vehicles. To be fair, Farley's statement describes Ford's experience at that time, not a guarantee of GM's successful drive into more hybrid offerings. GM still has to deliver competitive products at the right cost and lure in the customers.

GM isn't starting from a business where every gasoline vehicle needs rescuing, and in some cases it may have valid reasons for avoiding additional hybrid options. During its October earnings call, CEO Mary Barra said Equinox production was already fully utilized without a hybrid.

Chevrolet Equinox. Image source: General Motors.

The opportunity is filling gaps in customer choice while earning enough to justify development and production costs. Investors would be wise to keep updated on its hybrid plans. It may need to expedite its development process to seize the opportunity before potential customers choose other brands, or before current customers leave for better options.

GM doesn't need to win a debate about the perfect powertrain, or produce a hybrid option for every nameplate. It needs to sell vehicles that customers want at prices that leave something worthwhile for shareholders. Over the past decade, GM has done that at nearly every opportunity. Don't be shocked if it executes similarly with its upcoming hybrids.
