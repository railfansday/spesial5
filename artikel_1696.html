Andriy Shevchenko hits back at explosive Mykhailo Mudryk claims in blame game over doping case: Ukraine chief accuses £89m star's camp of asking official to falsely take blame for positive drugs test

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

By LUKE AUGUSTUS, ASSISTANT SPORTS EDITOR

Published: 16:00 EST, 19 November 2026 | Updated: 18:30 EST, 19 November 2026

Andriy Shevchenko has accused Mykhailo Mudryk's representatives of trying to persuade the Ukrainian FA to find someone to falsely take the blame for the winger's failed drugs test.

Mudryk, now on loan at Tottenham from Chelsea, was suspended for 20 months between November 2024 and July 2026 after testing positive for banned substance meldonium in October 2024.

In an interview, Mudryk blamed the Ukrainian FA (UAF), claiming the substance entered his body while on international duty.

'This drug could only have got into my body where I was, and that's Ukraine,' he said.

But Shevchenko, president of the UAF, described the accusations as 'baseless slander'. Shevchenko added: 'His representatives repeatedly approached the UAF with a proposal to find an employee who would falsely take responsibility for the meldonium found in his doping sample,' he told Tribuna. 'Such a proposal contradicts the principles of our work.'

Shevchenko said investigations had failed to establish how the substance entered Mudryk's system. He added that national team staff complied with Chelsea's request not to give Mudryk medication, and that two of his personal specialists were allowed at training camps.

Mykhailo Mudryk (left) has been accused of 'baseless slander' by Ukraine FA (UAF) president Andriy Shevchenko after he blamed his failed drugs test on the national team's setup

'If Mykhailo has evidence to back up his words, let him provide it,' Shevchenko said, demanding a public retraction and apology otherwise.

Mudryk's representatives have been approached for comment.

The 25-year-old was initially handed a four-year ban after being charged by the Football Association for failing a doping test. He was able to return when a seismic resolution was struck after changes to the 'scientific reporting standards'.

In a bombshell two-hour interview with Ukrainian journalist Dmitry Gordon via his YouTube channel, Mudryk laid the blame for his situation with the Ukraine national team - something Shevchenko vehemently denies.

'Loud statements have been made about the UAF and the Ukrainian national team. This is baseless slander against our people, who do their work honestly and professionally,' the UAF president, who also played for Chelsea during his time, said in a statement to The Athletic.

'After Mykhailo became a Chelsea player, the club sent a letter to the UAF requesting that he not be given any medication during national team training camps,' he continued.

'The national team staff complied with this request. Head coach Serhiy Rebrov also approved for Mykhailo to have two of his personal specialists work with him at the training camps. These were the exact people who monitored his regimen and recovery, and helped him with his daily routine.

Mudryk has won 28 caps for Ukraine but is yet to represent them since his ban was overturned

'The UAF completed all necessary checks in this case. We provided Mudryk's representatives with the information they requested and remained in contact with those working on the case.

'I know the people who have worked with the national team for many years. I trust them, because throughout all this time there have been no incidents in the team that could make anyone doubt their professionalism. If Mykhailo has evidence to back up his words, let him provide it. If no such evidence exists, I expect him to publicly retract his accusations and apologise to the people whose honesty and professionalism he has called into question.'

In his interview, Mudryk - who is yet to add to his 28 caps for his country since returning from his ban - laid the blame at Ukraine's door.

'I had never heard of a drug like meldonium, never seen it, never come across it in my life. I only learned about it when they told me the news. I definitely have ideas. It's a drug you can only get in Eastern Europe,' the winger said.

'This drug could only have got into my body where I was, and that's Ukraine. There are no such drugs in England. It supposedly stimulates your heart and helps you, as far as I understand, handle physical load better.

'I was at home and Chelsea's management came to see me and they said we needed to talk. Then they say you've returned a positive doping test and now you're facing a ban.

'You can't believe it's true. You can't believe this is happening to you. If I'd ever taken anything anywhere, I'd have understood that this conversation might happen one day.

He is currently on loan at Tottenham from Chelsea but has only played once so far this season

'At least I'd have understood it was possible. But when you spend a huge pile of money on watching your diet, on a nutritionist, on a chef. When you spend that much money every year on all these things, that's the last thing you expect. Because it speaks for itself: that you take things very seriously, like nutrition, everything that goes into your body, you watch it very closely.

'Otherwise there'd be no need to spend money on it, because you could manage without it. They tell you this news, and you sit there, and you kind of listen to them but don't hear them.

'At some point you just go into a trance. It feels like you're falling in a dream off a skyscraper. You're flying down, you feel yourself flying down, and only in a dream do you wake up when you hit the ground, but in real life you don't wake up, you just keep falling. It hits you very hard mentally. At that moment the tears came later.'

Mudryk, who joined Chelsea for £89million from Shakhtar Donetsk, has only made one substitute appearance for Tottenham so far this season due to an ankle injury.
