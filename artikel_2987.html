Stock Market Today, Nov. 17: Grab Holdings Shares Slip Amid Broader Stock Market Sell-Off

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

On Nov. 17, 2026, Grab's stock fell as investors weighed macroeconomic factors affecting the company.

Grab Holdings (GRAB -0.94%), a Southeast Asian super-app provider, closed at $3.18, down 0.93%, on Tuesday, Nov. 17. The decline was part of a broader U.S. stock market drop, as investors remained concerned about elevated inflation, rising interest rates, and growing government regulation toward the artificial intelligence sector. This week, the U.S. Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security released new restrictions meant to curb China's access to advanced AI chips.

Trading volume reached 101.8 million shares, coming in about 53% above its three-month average of 66.6 million shares. Grab Holdings IPO'd in 2020 and has fallen 73% since going public.

The S&P 500 (^GSPC -1.03%) closed at 7,341, down 1.03%, while the Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC -0.74%) ended at 24,328, down 0.74%. Among internet marketplaces and platform-based mobility, delivery, and fintech services, Uber Technologies (UBER -1.69%) closed at $68.81, down 1.69%, and DoorDash (DASH -2.87%) finished at $164.91, down 2.87%.

On Tuesday, Nov. 17, Grab's annual general meeting (AGM) notice for December 14 kept the stock in focus as investors watched for any updates on capital allocation. This is particularly noteworthy after the company's Nov. 9 announcement of a partnership with SoftBank Group to develop artificial intelligence infrastructure in Malaysia, expanding Grab's role in Southeast Asia's AI buildout.

The move raises Wall Street concerns over capex spending amid rising global fuel costs and how that would impact Grab's delivery business. That said, the company appears to be on a roll.

Two weeks ago, Grab reported third-quarter revenue rose 27% and raised 2026 guidance, which helped frame the stock's stronger fundamental backdrop. The company has also executed share buybacks, and committed to repurchasing $500 million worth of shares over the next 12 months, after already repurchasing $400 million across September and October of this year.

Earlier in 2026, Grab agreed to acquire a controlling 60% equity interest in Atome Financial, adding to its financial services push, and investors are watching whether that strategy lifts future earnings power.
