The Nasdaq Composite Is in a Correction, but These 3 Dividend Stocks Are Near 52-Week Highs. Here's Why They're Still Worth Buying Right Now.

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Johnson & Johnson, Visa, and Coca-Cola have held up during the market's recent turbulence.

By Thomas Niel – Nov 26, 2026 at 4:13PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Johnson & Johnson ›

After slumping throughout November, major indexes like the S&P 500 staged something of a comeback on Monday. Tuesday and Wednesday were a bit more chaotic, but the indexes have held onto most of Monday’s gains so far. Only time will tell whether this week’s action was a dead cat bounce or if it marks the end of the latest stock market pullback. Still, given the recent surge in stocks making new highs, it may be time for investors to consider going defensive.

Across multiple sectors, defensive dividend payers have continued to perform steadily. While each one declined during the sell-off, these three stocks remain near their 52-week highs: Johnson & Johnson (JNJ -0.91%), Coca-Cola (KO -0.03%), and Visa (V +1.83%).

With 64 consecutive years of dividend growth, Johnson & Johnson became one of a small number of Dividend Kings — companies with payout-hiking streaks of 50 years or more — over a decade ago. Yielding around 2% at current prices, this healthcare stock has averaged mid-single-digit annual dividend growth over the past decade.

J&J's latest dividend increase may have only been 3.1%, but the company's ongoing pivot toward a higher-growth business model points to stronger payouts and gains down the road. In particular, the company has pivoted toward building a pipeline of specialty drugs. As Goldman Sachs managing director Asad Haider discussed in an interview with Barron's last month, J&J’s recently released drugs include the immunology treatment Tremfya and the psoriasis treatment Icotyde.

Also, per Haider, promising Johnson & Johnson drug candidates include the bladder cancer treatment Inlexa. And recently approved immunology drug Imaavy could generate up to $5 billion in sales. J&J may trade for around 23 times forward earnings, a big premium to other pharmaceutical stocks, but if its growth pivot persists, the stock could sustain or even grow this valuation.

Coca-Cola is another Dividend King, with a dividend-growth streak also spanning 64 years. At its current share price, Coca-Cola, one of the world’s largest consumer staples companies, has a forward dividend yield of around 2.2%. Its dividend growth has averaged in the mid-single digits in recent years. Still, its strong stock market performance has been driven by its ongoing growth resurgence.

For Q3, the global beverage company reported 7% revenue growth, 12% operating earnings growth, and 8% earnings-per-share (EPS) growth. With this representing improved growth relative to recent years, it's no surprise that Coca-Cola stock has rallied by nearly 40% so far in 2026.

Of course, with this big rally has come a big jump in the stock's valuation. Shares now trade at a forward earnings multiple in the high 20s, on the upper end of the stock's historic valuation range. Yet while this blue chip dividend stock has become pricier as of late, it may have more room to run. Recently, TD Cowen analyst Robert Moskow reiterated his buy rating on Coca-Cola, setting a $112-per-share price target, which is nearly 15% above present price levels. In his research note, the analyst primarily cited the company's deep economic moat and its growth prospects as reasons it could sustain and expand its premium valuation.

Visa may have a forward dividend yield of just 0.75%. Still, with nearly 20 years of consecutive dividend growth, as well as annual payout growth averaging in the mid-double digits during this time frame, I would consider it one of the blue chip dividend stocks.

Visa's latest annual dividend increase came in at 16%, with its quarterly payout rising to $0.78 per share. Two factors suggest that Visa can sustain above-average levels of dividend growth for years to come. First, the stock continues to have a low payout ratio of around 21%.

Second, as the company continues to benefit from the "digitalization of payments" secular growth trend, its top- and bottom-line growth remains steady. For the fiscal quarter and year ending Sept. 30, the company reported net revenue growth of 13% and 15%, respectively, with quarterly and annual non-GAAP (adjusted) earnings per share rising by 17% and 16%, respectively.
