StoneCo: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Simplifying its product lineup to cut merchant churn, StoneCo is betting deeper banking ties will matter more than fee growth for long-term holders.

On its third quarter of 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 12, 2026, StoneCo (STNE +2.59%) reduced its 2026 profitability guidance as persistent high interest rates squeezed small merchants across Brazil. Macroeconomic friction forces the payments processor to pivot toward guaranteed credit lines and tighter expense control to protect long-term returns. Three things management said stand out.

Persistent inflation and elevated borrowing costs continue to strain small businesses, making future financial modeling unpredictable. Withdrawing medium-term targets allows the company to adapt to volatile macroeconomic shifts without anchoring to outdated projections.

Expanding credit to small merchants during an economic slowdown increases delinquency risks. Directing loan volume into government-guaranteed lines protects the balance sheet while preserving steady interest income.

Rapid product expansion created operational friction that to higher merchant churn. Streamlining pricing tiers and restructuring sales incentives prioritize long-term customer retention over short-term transaction fees.

High borrowing costs and softening merchant sales continue to exert pressure on profitability, prompting leadership to tighten credit underwriting and focus on strict expense discipline. StoneCo relies on a growing deposit base and deep distribution networks to protect its market position during the economic slowdown. Executive leadership is closely monitoring credit model performance, merchant transaction growth, and operational efficiency across all business lines.

Long-term expansion hinges on transitioning simple payment-processing merchants into fully integrated banking clients. Management expects that deepening these layers of relationships will build a durable competitive moat across Brazil.
