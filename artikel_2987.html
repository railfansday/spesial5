Now what? Colts should have traded Daniel Jones when they had the chance

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Here's the Daniel Jones timeline for the past eight months — and stick with us, because it's a wild ride.

The Colts signed Jones to the largest two-year contract in NFL history despite coming off Achilles surgery a few months earlier.

The Colts kept Jones as their starter, without any new competition, this season.

The Colts continued to put Jones as QB1 while he struggled, throwing interceptions in 10 of his 11 starts.

The Colts reportedly listened to trade offers for their starting QB prior to the Nov. 10 trade deadline before ultimately passing.

The Colts started Jones in Thursday's blowout loss to the Texans.

Whew. Caught up? Good. Now consider: Cutting Jones will cost the team nearly $15 million in dead salary-cap money, plus another $35 million for next year.

Friday's move was nothing short of a stunner, if only from a financial standpoint. From a football standpoint, the Colts couldn't justify keeping him as the starter? Cutting him now, knowing what a hit they'd take? It almost feels personal at this point.

Colts head coach Shane Steichen clearly had enough of him Thursday night. The speculation on why Steichen hadn't gone to Anthony Richardson Sr. sooner was that the coach might have trusted Richardson even less.

Steichen might also be in self-preservation mode, trying to hold together a ship that was partially stripped before the trade deadline and already in rough shape before that. But assuming Steichen didn't reach his breaking point during Thursday's game, why did the Colts wait to get rid of him?

And not only for nothing. It actually cost the Colts money and flexibility to release him now. It doesn't get much uglier than that.

Daniel Jones' run with the Colts ends after 24 starts in which Indianapolis went 11-13.

There have been credible reports that multiple teams inquired about Jones' availability before the trade deadline, including the Ravens and Vikings. Others might also have checked in on him. The Colts held firm. The Ravens got Jacoby Brissett and the Vikings landed Kirk Cousins.

This feels like a major missed opportunity. By releasing Jones, the Colts got nothing and made their situation worse. Even with the potential return of a Day 3 draft pick and a slightly better impact on their salary cap, the Colts passed.

It should be noted that the Vikings feel great about Cousins, and it's possible that their interest in Jones was more of a fallback option. The Ravens? Brissett's numbers over the past two seasons have been slightly better, so it's fair to say that they might have preferred the QB they ended up with.

But any potential interest in Jones should have been met with a take-him-off-our-hands type of offer — the kind a team couldn't turn down. Someone is going to sign him here in the coming days, and given all the QB injuries around the league, it wouldn't be a stunner to see Jones start a game at some point this season.

The Colts were more than willing to part with other assets prior to the deadline, including DeForest Buckner, Quenton Nelson and Josh Downs. They listened to offers for Jones and Jonathan Taylor but ended up keeping both. Taylor had 11 carries for 21 yards and four catches for six yards on Thursday. Jones struggled and was cut. Little of this makes sense.

When general manager Chris Ballard was fired midseason and replaced by interim GM Ed Dodds, it felt like an opportunity for Dodds to earn the full-time job by starting the process of replenishing the Colts' draft pool. They've done that, but they've also thrown away large chunks of meat still on the bone.

Dodds was dealt a tough hand (one he helped put together, for what it's worth), but it's arguable he has played it poorly — unless Jones' release was mandated from on high. That's possible. If it's true, the Colts' situation is even uglier than it appears.

Since Andrew Luck's shocking retirement in August 2019, the Colts have started 12 different quarterbacks. Jones' 24 starts were the most from that group in that span, but it's highly likely some new names are added to the list next season. This franchise is starting to become synonymous with QB instability, and after two decades of having Peyton Manning and Luck, that's a bitter pill to swallow.

The remaining options on the roster this season are Richardson and Riley Leonard. The Colts don't appear to have great belief in either one. They declined the fifth-year option on Richardson's rookie deal, making him a free agent this spring, and have been reluctant to give him much of a shot prior to now. Leonard was last seen in last year's Week 18 start and hasn't played a snap in 2026.

The assumption is that Richardson will play elsewhere next season. He certainly has a chance to rewrite his story in Indy as Jones' replacement, but whatever he does down the stretch will likely serve as an audition for his next stop. Leonard could be back in 2027, but it's unlikely he'd be anything higher than a backup to start next season.

The Colts could have waited until the offseason and made Jones a post-June 1 cut, with far less of a salary-cap footprint. The question of whether to keep Jones is settled, but the problems are only starting.

The Sauce Gardner trade cost them next year's first-round draft pick, and though the pre-deadline trades netted them some Day 2 and 3 ammunition over the next two years, it still makes acquiring a quarterback tougher come springtime.

The Colts can still move into the bottom of Round 1 for a quarterback in next spring's draft. But will there be a viable starting candidate available in that range? What was thought to be a banner QB crop in the 2027 NFL Draft is turning into more of a grab-bag situation, depending on which underclassmen declare.

Perhaps an unexpected veteran or two could hit the market. The veteran route feels more likely now, at least from an immediate-starter perspective. But will there be starting-quality options? That hope feels dubious now. Kyler Murray, Geno Smith and Deshaun Watson are the caliber of names that could be options. Do any of them elevate this team or give the Colts much more than a bridge option?

You can forget about trading for Lamar Jackson. The Colts don't have the assets to compete. That holds true for any other potential starting QB who could be available via trade.

The pathways to salvation appear quite limited. The head coach appears to be on thin ice. The Colts sunk major money into Taylor and Alec Pierce this offseason but have little to show for it.

In short, the whole situation is a hot mess.
