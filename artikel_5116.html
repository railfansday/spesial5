Bitcoin (BTC) Has Never Been This Correlated With Gold

Bitcoin's 90-day correlation with gold has reached its highest level on record, according to Galaxy Research data. The measure peaked at 0.56 on Oct. 22 and broke the previous record of 0.50, which Galaxy dated to Nov. 6, 2020.

Alex Thorn, head of firmwide research at Galaxy Digital, posted about the record on X on Friday. "Bitcoin isn't digital gold? Bitcoin reaches highest correlation to gold of all time," he wrote.

Thorn shared two Galaxy Research charts together with the post. According to the charts, the 90-day correlation was at 0.54 on Nov. 23, and the 30-day correlation stood at 0.59 on the same day.

In the first half of 2026, the 90-day correlation stayed near 0.1. It started to rise in July and climbed above 0.50 by early October, according to Galaxy's data. Between 2020 and 2025, the average reading was 0.11.

On Jan. 20, Galaxy CEO Mike Novogratz wrote on X that "the gold price is telling us we are losing reserve currency status at an accelerating rate." "$BTC is disappointing as it is still being met with selling," he added.

Thorn wrote about Bitcoin and gold on X in February. "Bitcoin's failure to trade like gold as part of 'the debasement trade' since Sep. '25 damaged its narrative with new entrants," he wrote. "When bitcoiners said 'digital gold,' they were describing its fundamental properties, not that it's high beta to gold today," he added.

On Wednesday, Galaxy published a report that withdrew its Oct. 26 call on the Bitcoin bear market. "We withdraw our call that the bear market ended in June," the report said. Thorn shared it on X with the comment, "As it turns out, the Bitcoin bear market is definitely not over."

According to the report, Bitcoin closed the week ended Nov. 22 at $61,104 after seven straight weekly losses. Thorn wrote in the report that the level to watch was $58,525, Bitcoin's closing low from June 30. According to Thorn, a daily close below that level would mark "the start of a move to a materially lower low."

"The clock says this bear is overdue to end; the depth says it has further to fall," the report said. On Nov. 22, the bear market was 412 days old, and earlier ones lasted 163 to 406 days from high to low, according to Galaxy.

Bitcoin was down 53% from its October 2025 record at that point. In past bear markets, it lost 77% to 93%. If the June low breaks, Galaxy sees possible new lows at about $48,500 and $28,200.

Galaxy tracks 13 bottom signals, and two of them have been met so far. According to the report, U.S. spot Bitcoin ETPs held about 1.24 million BTC, down 71,339 BTC since Oct. 4.

On Nov. 17, SkyBridge Capital founder Anthony Scaramucci asked Novogratz at the Digital Assets Forum in New York whether Bitcoin could return to $90,000 by the end of 2026. "Unlikely now," Novogratz said. "Less likely than it was. You need something to happen," he added.
