Happy Fails

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

I set off on my final 4 day backpacking stint, what surprises does the trail have in store for me?

Spirits were high when I returned to Pen Mar Park in Maryland around 7:00AM. I felt prepared to finish off my double distance Appalachian Trail hike over the next 4 days. I said my traditional goodbye to my van and approached the pavilion perched above the incredible vista. I pictured myself stumbling back here on Sunday, exasperated but filled with a sense of having completed a once-in-a-lifetime endeavor. I turned south to assess the trail. I’d want a good idea of what I was signing up for. It didn’t take long before I found myself in a bit of a boulder field. That wasn’t ideal. I was supposed to leave the rocks behind in Pennsylvania, this section of trail was supposed to be relatively easy. After about 2 miles I began a climb up toward the High Rock overlook. The climb was no gimme either – plenty of uneven rocks, slippery leaves, and a decent grade. I would at least be going down this slope on my 4 state challenge attempt in a few days, but that was almost worse in a way. I’d have hiked 42 miles by this point, dealing with slippery downhill terrain seemed like a recipe for disaster. My resolve remained strong though, I’d find a way, just as I had over the 4,300 miles up to this point.

Back at Pen Mar Park for the first time since July.

Once I finished the climb I wandered around some of the rocks offering views West. Visitors have been quite active with graffiti, hardly a rock was left its original color. I continued along the short High Rock loop trail through the small parking lot and up to the main view point. I had the spectacular 180 degree view to myself. I realized the potential to hedge my plans a bit. If I wasn’t on pace to make it to the Pen Mar Park by sunset, I could watch it up here instead. Then I could take my time on the final 3 miles, relying on my headlamp again if needed. That felt like a fair compromise if the situation called for it.

I continued south, enjoying the warm, clear day. The trail conditions improved, but were still less friendly than I expected. There are far easier sections of the AT than what I had been traveling through. I crossed Raven Rock Road, making note that I’d need to be back here by about 3:00PM on Sunday if I wanted to mark sunset at Pen Mar Park since it was about 6 miles north of the crossing. I stopped for water at a creek about a mile later, which is notable only because I had all but stopped filtering water along the trail. I had no trouble waiting until I was at camp, unless there happened to be potable water available along the trail. The trail continued to feature more elevation change than ideal. I was going to be in for a tougher day than I had been picturing. I pressed on, feeling like I needed to prove to myself that today’s 22 miles wouldn’t feel so bad by the end of the day and than I’d feel better.

That crossover never arrived. I climbed up South Mountain without much hassle, but still wasn’t finding the going easy. I realized I was actually cutting it quite tight to get to the Washington Monument State Park by sunset. I tried calling them to confirm that I could arrive and camp after dark, but the call dropped before I could get a response. I checked the weather forecast to see if Sunday was looking any better in terms of cloudiness and what I saw just about stopped me in my tracks. About an inch and a half of rain was now expected to hit the entire area. The chance of rain was around 60-80% for nearly the entire day. What now? My margins were already incredibly fine assuming things went generally well. Now I had found the trail to be more challenging, my diet had been turned inside out, and I was going to have to do all this in the pouring rain?

My head fell as I continued along the mountain wondering if I even had a choice. I walked to the Black Rock Cliffs, noticing a tempting looking tent site. If this was an ordinary stint I might concoct an itinerary change so that I could camp here with a view of the sunset, but I shook the thought off and continued toward Washington Monument State Park.

I stopped by the Ensign Cowall shelter on my way south.

On I wandered, re-checking the forecast hoping to see something different. Was I thinking straight? It was Thursday and I was being thrown off by a forecast for Sunday. I considered my options. If I pushed the 4 state challenge to Monday I would run out of food, but I could fix that by buying town food in Harper’s Ferry, I’d need to spend Sunday somewhere anyway. I looked at hostels but realized that this would completely solve my problem. To do the challenge on Monday, I’d need to be at the Virginia/West Virginia state line Sunday afternoon so I could pitch my tent and get some sleep right before beginning the attempt. The rain on Sunday meant that Monday was essentially out too. I looked up at a sign for a side trail to Annapolis Rock. I didn’t have time for a side quest, even if it was only a quarter mile off the trail. Even so, I read through the description on FarOut. A large campsite is located near the overlook…two privies…a spring nearby. My progress came to a halt. Ahead of me lay a mix of certainties and uncertainties. Rain, pain, and darkness were certain. Success was very much uncertain. Behind me was a nice sunset with two privies at my disposal.

I turned around and into the Annapolis Rock campsite. The view was spectacular, sunset would indeed be incredible. I found the spring, gushing water even now past the mid point of November. I walked through the entire campsite before winding up at the first campsite. Technically it was a group campsite but since I hadn’t seen anyone I figured it was probably safe to claim it and give myself a little room to spread out. My only complaint was the tent pads which seemed to have been constructed exactly 6 inches too short to pitch even a one person backpacking tent.

It was hardly 3:00PM. I felt guilty for not hiking through even second of daylight. I settled myself by acknowledging that I still had plenty of lee-way built into my shorter itineraries the next two days if the forecast improved and I wanted to get back on track for the challenge. After a restful podcast session in my tent I ventured back over to the overlook. The photos speak for themselves which is handy because I was left speechless. Suffice to say, this was the kind of sunset that made you feel like you’d make the correct decision. I enjoyed myself finding some creative positions amongst the rocks (after doing thorough copperhead) to take pictures from. I must have gotten too excited because I tripped over a rock, leaving a nice scratch in my phone screen. Under other circumstances I might have kicked myself, but I was too awestruck to care much.
