Serie A preview: AC Milan vs. Frosinone – Team news, opposition insight, stats and prediction

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Milan will return to action on Sunday evening, hoping to pick up where they left off before the international break with a strong performance against Frosinone.

If we look at the league, it has been a very decent start to the season for Ruben Amorim and his men. However, they are yet to win three games on the trot, so tomorrow’s encounter is a fantastic chance for them to really show their worth.

It won’t be easy, though. Amorim said during his press conference that it’s hard to identify the movement patterns of Frosinone, who certainly have the ability to make life difficult for the Rossoneri.

Game date: Sunday, November 22 Kick-off time: 18:00 (CET) Venue: San Siro Referee: Luca Pairetto (VAR: Pezzuto)

Some good news arrived on Saturday morning as Strahinja Pavlovic was deemed fit enough to be called up for the game. It’s not certain whether he will start or not, but Amorim at least said that he’s happy to have the Serbian and ‘his fire’ back.

A few days ago, it was also confirmed that Mike Maignan will be at the manager’s disposal despite the injury scare with France. Of course, it’s not all perfect on the injury front as Matteo Gabbia, Fikayo Tomori, Ruben Loftus-Cheek, Alexis Saelemaekers and Samuel Chukwueze are still sidelined.

Anyway, moving on to the predicted starting eleven, it was initially thought that Amorim would field the same one as we saw against Genoa. However, he made it very clear during the presser that Musah won’t play as a left-back, so there will indeed be some changes.

According to the latest reports, the American will still start but in midfield instead, allowing Adrien Rabiot to have a slightly more advanced role. It also means that Davide Bartesaghi will come into the eleven, currently ahead of Pervis Estupinan in the race.

Alphadjo Cisse has been in red-hot form lately but it seems Amorim wants to manage expectations a little, with the youngster tipped to start on the bench. Christian Pulisic should thus have a role on the flank as opposed to in the centre, although one would imagine everything will be a bit fluid.

It’s no surprise to see that Goncalo Ramos should start up front, but of course, he will need to get going with the goalscoring sooner rather than later. Francesco Camarda is knocking on the door, as is Cisse who has been tested in that role before.

Predicted Milan XI (4-3-3): Maignan; Gila, De Winter, Diawara, Bartesaghi; Musah, Modric, Rabiot; Moreira, Ramos, Pulisic.

Predicted bench: Torriani, P. Terracciano, F. Terracciano, Pavlovic, Vladimirov, Estupinan, Jashari, Comotto, Hutchinson, Cisse, Camarda.

Injuries & suspensions: Gabbia, Tomori, Loftus-Cheek, Saelemaekers and Chukwueze.

Frosinone have definitely been one of the surprises of the season so far. After a very strong Serie B campaign with 81 points and only three games lost, they secured direct promotion to the top tier (finishing second) and have been making the most of the adventure.

They are currently on 20 points after eleven games, winning six, drawing two and losing three, which has put them in sixth place. That’s very strong for a newcomer and for a coach who is only in his second season with the club, especially considering the upset against Como.

It’s the 56-year-old’s second attempt at Serie A after coaching Cremonese for half a season in 2022-23. He didn’t manage a single win then, so you can definitely say that the performance so far has been great revenge for him.

So, what is behind this rise? As pointed out by Gazzetta dello Sport, the direct style of play has been key for Frosinone. As opposed to Milan, who want to see a lot of the ball, they are willing to have less possession and break on the counter.

It doesn’t mean that they sit back, though. In fact, it has been the opposite as they are the highest-pressing team in the league. The strategy thus seems to be to force mistakes out of the opponents, while also making sure that they are direct in possession.

While collective effort deserves most of the praise, it has definitely helped that the likes of Giacomo Calo, Giorgi Kvernadze and Antonio Raimondo – among others – have performed at a very high level. They aren’t exactly big-name players, so a lot of them are having their breakout seasons.

Moving on to actual team news, the situation up front is not ideal for Frosinone as their top scorer, the aforementioned Antonio Raimondo, is out injured. Birigilea, who is on two goals, is set to replace him in the No.9 position.

The Milan academy product Sebastiano Desplanches, who left the club four years ago, will be on the bench. He’s yet to make an appearance this season, with Alvini preferring Lorenzo Palmisani instead.

Predicted Frosinone XI (4-2-3-1): Palmisani; Oyono, Calvani, Monterisi, Terzic; Calo, Masini; Ghedjemis; Schmid, Kvernadze; Birigilea.

Predicted bench: Desplanches, Pisseri, Cittadini, Tchato, Amey, Akpoguma, Fayed, El Azzouzi, Cichella, Hasa, Fini, Zerbin, Bobcek.

Injuries & suspensions: Bracaglia, Grillitsch and Raimondo.

This fixture has only been played six times, but even with such a small sample size, it’s clear that Milan are the favourites with four wins and two draws so far. They have won the last three as well, spread across the 2023-24 and 2018-19 seasons.

That said, the most recent encounter was a dramatic one, if you like. Olivier Giroud opened the scoring for Milan away from home but then Frosinone scored twice. Thanks to goals from Matteo Gabbia and Luka Jovic, the Rossoneri were able to turn it back in their favour.

The last time at San Siro, meanwhile, it was a great 3-1 win as Jovic, Pulisic and Tomori found the back of the net. Most of you will remember the American’s goal, as it was Maignan who assisted him with a fantastic pass.

➤ Milan are unbeaten in all six previous meetings against Frosinone in Serie A (W4 D2): among the teams against which they have never lost in the competition, the Rossoneri have played more games only against Pisa (14 – six also against Crotone and Lecco).

➤ Milan have scored at least three goals in four of their six Serie A matches against Frosinone, producing 15 goals overall, an average of 2.5 per game. Only against Pescara (2.9) have they recorded a higher average, among the opponents they have faced on at least five occasions (2.5 also against Casale and Messina).

➤ Frosinone have won six games this season, drawing two and losing three. Only three newly promoted teams in the last 40 years have recorded more wins in their first 12 Serie A games of the season: Bari in 1994/95 (seven), Chievo in 2001/02 (seven), and Hellas Verona in 2013/14 (seven).

➤ Milan have won four of their last five league games (L1), scoring at least two goals in each, after failing to win three of their previous four (D3). The last time they scored more than one goal in consecutive Serie A matches was between September 2020 and January 2021, when they managed 15 games.

➤ Although Frosinone are unbeaten in three of their five away games this season (W1 D2), they have won just two of their last 27 away games in Serie A (including previous participations), with 10 draws and 15 defeats.

➤ Milan have failed to win four of their last seven Serie A games played after the international break (D2 L2). On the other hand, Frosinone are unbeaten in three of their last five top-flight matches immediately following Italy matches (W2 D1).

➤ Frosinone have attempted the most counterattacks from advantageous situations in Serie A this season: 22, almost double the number of any other team. On the other hand, Milan are the only team that haven’t allowed a single counterattack when trailing in the current campaign.

➤ Only Juventus (79) have attempted more long-range shots than Frosinone (77) this season. On the other hand, Milan are the team that have attempted the fewest shots from outside the area (30) and the one that have conceded the most shots from outside the area in relation to the total conceded, 44%.

➤ Adrien Rabiot has earned the most points for his team from direct goal contributions this season, having scored 10 goals and assisted four times. In the most recent matchday, he became the first Milan midfielder to be involved in at least one goal in six consecutive league appearances since Kaká in 2004 (who managed seven).

➤ Only Matías Soulé (80) has been involved in more sequences ending with a shot than Giacomo Calò (76) in this Serie A. The Frosinone player has scored five goals in the current campaign and only one midfielder has scored more goals in the first 12 matchdays in the top flight for a newly promoted team in the three points for a win era: Hidetoshi Nakata with Perugia in 1998/99 (six).

Since Frosinone like to play on the counterattacks, one player who I think could be crucial for Milan is Musah. As he’s set to start in midfield and not at left-back, the feeling is that his main role will be to prevent the opponents from having too much open space on the counter.

Further up the pitch, I’m excited to see Moreira. He got himself a goal in Belgium’s win over Italy and he has looked a lot better on the right flank ever since the derby performance. He can definitely make the difference with his skill.

As for Frosinone, it’s obvious that Calo is the man whom Milan will want to stop. He likes to dictate the play and if he’s allowed too much time on the ball, identifying his teammates in good positions, then there could be trouble for the Rossoneri.

It will also be interesting to see if Birigilea can fill the shoes of Raimondo. He bagged a brace against Lecce but in the last two games, he failed to find the back of the net. He will surely be keen to get back on target.

A lot of people are expecting this to be a high-scoring affair and that’s a very reasonable take, given how both teams play. That said, I think this could be one where Milan actually get their defence in order and don’t concede, even if that might be a somewhat optimistic prediction.
