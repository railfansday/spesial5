Vietnamese Coffee Martini

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

You get dessert and a cocktail all in one with this Vietnamese coffee martini, which adds sweetened condensed milk to the classic espresso martini for a rich, frothy sip.

Whipping up a Vietnamese coffee martini after dinner means two things: You don’t have to make dessert, and you aren’t going to sleep anytime soon. This cocktail is next-level sweet and caffeinated, and it makes your regular espresso martini look like child’s play. Sure, this cocktail shares some of the same elements (vodka, coffee liqueur and espresso), but it includes sweetened condensed milk for a rich, luxurious texture, signature caramel sweetness and super-frothy finish.

Vietnamese coffee is typically made with dark-roasted robusta beans, which taste bolder and more chocolatey than the arabica beans in most coffee blends. In traditional preparations, the grounds drip slowly through a small metal filter called a phin, straight into a glass with a layer of sweetened condensed milk waiting at the bottom. Fresh milk was hard to come by and didn’t keep well in Vietnam’s heat, so canned milk became the go-to. It’s almost as thick as syrup and tastes a little like melted caramel. Shaken into a cocktail, it lands somewhere closer to a creamy Baileys espresso martini than a classic one, only richer.

Fill a cocktail shaker three-fourths full with ice. Add the vodka, espresso, sweetened condensed milk and Kahlua. Cover the shaker and shake hard until condensation forms on the outside, 10 to 15 seconds.

Strain the cocktail into a glass and top with the coffee beans. Serve immediately, while the foam is at its fluffiest.

Editor’s Tip: Chill your martini glass or coupe for 10 to 15 minutes before you start mixing. A frosty glass keeps the cocktail colder longer and helps the foam hold its shape.

Enjoy a Vietnamese coffee espresso martini right after you shake it, since the foam starts to deflate as it sits. If you’ve mixed up a little extra, store the unshaken mixture of vodka, coffee, condensed milk and Kahlua in a sealed jar in the refrigerator for up to two days. Give it a good stir, then shake it over fresh ice right before serving to revive the froth.

Making Vietnamese coffee martinis ahead of time is a smart move for hosting. Brew and chill the espresso or cold brew up to a day ahead, then mix the cocktail without ice. Refrigerate the batched cocktail until guests arrive. When it’s go time, shake each drink to order with fresh ice so every glass gets that signature foamy top.

For a dairy-free Vietnamese coffee martini, swap the regular sweetened condensed milk for sweetened condensed coconut milk. You should be able to find it in most grocery stores near the baking aisle. It shakes up just as creamy and sweet, with a subtle toasted coconut flavor that plays beautifully with the coffee. Kahlua is dairy-free, so the rest of the recipe stays the same.

Vietnamese coffee is usually made with robusta beans, which have nearly twice the caffeine of the arabica beans used in most espresso blends. Ounce for ounce, phin-brewed Vietnamese coffee can rival a shot of espresso, which is why it stands up so well to sweet condensed milk.

The foam comes from the oils in freshly brewed coffee, so espresso gives you the most dramatic froth, while cold brew and instant coffee create a slightly thinner layer. The condensed milk helps, too, adding body that traps air bubbles as you shake. Use plenty of ice, shake like you mean it and strain right away into a chilled glass so the foam settles on top in a smooth, creamy cap.
