Plant-Based Milk Alternatives May Not Give You Enough Iodine. Here’s Why That Matters

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

Replacing dairy with plant-based alternatives (PBAs), even partially, could substantially lower iodine intake, especially if those alternatives aren’t fortified, according to a recent study published in the Lancet Planetary Health.

The United Kingdom-based researchers found that only 12% of UK plant-based milk, yogurt, and cheese products analyzed were fortified with iodine. This compares with 61% for calcium and 46% for vitamin B12.

In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) provides guidance on the nutritional content of PBAs, but fortification with iodine is voluntary.

The study found that replacing as little as 20% of dairy intake with plant-based alternatives could increase the risk of inadequate iodine intake, particularly among adolescent girls and women of reproductive age.

“People often worry about a decline in calcium intake when they replace some of their dairy with plant-based alternatives. As a result, iodine is frequently overlooked. This study serves as a valuable reminder of iodine’s significance as a nutrient,” said Victoria Finn, MD, board certified endocrinologist at Medical Offices of Manhattan, who wasn’t involved in the study.

The study included analysis of UK plant-based milk, cheese, and yogurt products, as well as dietary data from 3,500 people.

The researchers used statistical modeling to predict how swapping dairy for PBAs might affect iodine and other nutrients.

The people who are most vulnerable to iodine inadequacy are adolescent females and people at reproductive age, especially those who are pregnant or who plan to become pregnant.

The researchers estimated that replacing 20% to 35% of dairy intake with PBAs would increase the proportion of people with inadequate iodine intake:

When the researchers modeled the replacement of all dairy products with PBAs, the proportion of adolescent females with inadequate iodine intake rose to 80%. Among females ages 19 to 49, that proportion remained relatively stable as replacement rates increased.

It is important to keep in mind that the study used dietary modeling rather than direct measurement of iodine status.

Iodine is important for thyroid hormone production, metabolism, and both fetal and child brain development.

“Your thyroid gland requires iodine to produce thyroid hormones, which play a crucial role in regulating metabolism and supporting normal growth and development,” said Finn.

She added that if you consistently consume too little iodine over an extended period, the thyroid gland may enlarge and eventually struggle to produce enough hormones. This condition is known as hypothyroidism, which can lead to various symptoms such as lethargy, sleepiness, weight gain, dry skin, and more.

“Hypothyroidism can be particularly dangerous during pregnancy, as the baby’s brain, nervous system, and organs rely heavily on the mother’s thyroid hormone during the early stages of development,” Finn added.

In the United Kingdom, dairy is a major iodine source due to cattle feed supplementation, not because dairy is naturally high in iodine.

In the United States, dairy is also an important source of iodine for the same reasons.

However, the risk profile for iodine deficiency in the U.S. may differ from the U.K., as iodized salt is also a significant source of iodine and is widely available in the U.S., but not in the U.K.

According to the National Institutes of Health (NIH), the recommended daily intake of iodine depends on your age.

Plant-based or plant-forward diets have grown in popularity, in part due to the health benefits they offer. For example, consuming less meat can reduce your risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes, obesity, and high cholesterol.

However, as this study suggests, it may be possible to reduce your iodine intake when transitioning to a plant-based diet. There are ways you can protect your iodine intake when choosing a more plant-forward diet, though.

Check the labels on plant-based dairy alternatives to find those fortified with iodine. You can also read the nutrition label. Iodine may be listed as potassium iodide or potassium iodate.

Seafood, eggs, and iodized salt are all good sources of iodine.

You can also discuss iodine supplements with your healthcare professional.

“Iodine supplements should be taken with caution, especially without checking your thyroid levels,” said Finn. “Additionally, be cautious with kelp supplements, as their iodine content can vary, and excessive iodine intake can disrupt thyroid function.”
