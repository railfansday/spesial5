Poulet Basquaise

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Rich tomatoes, stewed onions and peppers, and tender bone-in chicken make this Spanish- and French-inspired poulet Basquaise recipe extremely flavorful while still being surprisingly easy to make. Savory and rustic, it’s a stew that’s ideal for making ahead and enjoying all week long. The key ingredient? Piment d’Espelette—the regional spice known for its peppery, smoky essence. If you’re looking to master a classic dish or simply want a reliable, cozy weeknight dinner, then this recipe won’t let you down.

Heat a large skillet with oil and season the chicken with salt. Working in batches, add the chicken pieces and sear until the skin is golden in color, which should take 5 to 7 minutes. Remove the chicken to a heatproof plate.

Then reduce the heat and add the onion, bell peppers, garlic, piment d’Espelette, and salt. You’ll want to stir this once in a while so it doesn’t stick, but also let the vegetables soften. This should take 8 to 10 minutes.

At this point, increase the heat again and add the wine. Scrape up any bits on the bottom and allow those peppers and onions to absorb the wine. The liquid should reduce.

Add the tomatoes and broth and bring to a simmer. Be careful of splattering sauce and adjust the heat as needed. Place the chicken back into the skillet, skin side up, and cook until the chicken is tender and nearly falling off the bone. The sauce should also be thicker and fragrant. This should take about 30 to 40 minutes.

Stir in the chorizo and cook until it’s warmed through. Top it off with thyme and serve!

Let cool and store in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 5 days. It’s certainly a great meal-prep dish. Make this on a Sunday night for lunch Monday and Tuesday.
