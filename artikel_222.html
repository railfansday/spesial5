One of the items on the agenda for UN diplomats meeting in Geneva this week was working towards rules regarding the use of autonomous weapons, or so-called “killer robots.” Eventually, representatives left the meetings agreeing to “start discussing options” after a largely fruitless week of negotiations.

Before the negotiations got underway, the International Committee of the Red Cross, the guardian of the Geneva Conventions, said it was urgent for legally binding rules to be drafted within a year. That call was backed by the U.N. secretary-general and by Pope Leo.

The compromise reached was that countries would instead have two years to propose regulations. Negotiations between countries on which regulations to adopt and how to enforce them will likely drag on well beyond that. Additionally, room was left for some of those yet-to-be-adopted regulations to remain “optional.”

Humanitarian organizations and some countries see a need to urgently adopt legally binding rules as semi-autonomous weapons and drones have been responsible for a significant number of civilian deaths in conflicts around the globe, including in Ukraine, Gaza, and Sudan.

According to NPR, Russia, Israel, and the US proved to be the primary obstructions:

On day one, Russia delayed proceedings by objecting to the meeting’s agenda. Then the United States proposed a five-year window to discuss nonbinding, optional guidelines for autonomous weapons.
