Whats new to streaming this week? (Nov. 6, 2026)

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Don't be overwhelmed or waste an hour scrolling through your services to determine what to watch. We've got your back, whatever your mood. Mashable offers watch guides for all of the above, broken down by genre: comedy, thriller, horror, documentary, and animation, among others. But if you're seeking something brand new (or just new to streaming), we've got you covered there, too.

In 2005, the missing persons case of Natalee Holloway became national news when the 18-year-old from Tennessee vanished in Aruba. She was last seen with Dutchman Joran van der Sloot, who was an early person of interest and over the years has told conflicting stories of what came next.

Netflix's newest true crime doc series reexamines the case, offering interviews with authorities and Holloway's mother and friends, as well as previously unreleased footage. Directors Dani Sloane and Matthew Galkin teamed with Story Syndicate (Maternal Instinct, Titan: The Oceangate Disaster) for this three-part documentary series about a mother's quest to getting justice for her daughter. — Kristy Puchko, Entertainment Editor

How to watch: Taken: Natalee Holloway is now streaming on Netflix.

Look, I love a Netflix Christmas movie, but to be honest, this one looks outstandingly average. And I love dog movies. If you saw the 2017 Dennis Quaid-fronted drama A Dog’s Purpose or its sequel, 2019's A Dog's Journey, this is pretty much the same deal but set at Christmas. And that makes sense since all three movies (as well as A Dog's Way Home) are based on novels by W. Bruce Cameron, who has screenwriting credits on each movie. The rumply basset hound and pristine corgi puppy who are seemingly tasked with bringing a family together in yuletide unity are cute, but the trailer is not. — Shannon Connellan, Senior Editor

Starring: Dennis Quaid, Milo Ventimiglia, Brooke Lena Johnson, Thomas Haden Church, Mary Steenburgen, Kathleen Rose Perkins, Jennifer Tilly, Ethan Loomis, and Marissa Jaret Winokur

How to watch: A Dog's Perfect Christmas is now streaming on Netflix.

Psychic children abducted by the government and held in a brutal secure facility? It's a classic Stephen King formula, and The Institute — based on the horror author's novel of the same name — did well enough to bag itself a second outing. While the first season focused on new recruit Luke Ellis (Joe Freeman) surviving in his strange new prison, Season 2 looks set to widen its scope, with the main cast struggling to find evidence that'll expose the facility's corrupt practices. — Sam Haysom, General Assignment Editor, UK

Starring: Joe Freeman, Ben Barnes, Mary-Louise Parker, Alfie Allen, Simone Miller, Fionn Laird, Arlen So, Robert Joy, and Hannah Galway

How to watch: The Institute Season 2 premieres Nov. 8 at 9 p.m. ET on MGM+.

I'm sorry, but isn't The 12 Dates of Christmas already a movie? The cheeseball 2011 Groundhog Day scenario starring Amy Smart as a Scroogey woman stuck in a time loop of dates to teach her a lesson about being nicer to people? Well, this isn't that.

Hulu's identically named film reunites Pretty Little Liars stars Lucy Hale and Ian Harding in another romantic scenario, this time much less problematic than that show's student-teacher relationship. Will I be settling in to see Hale's character determined to thwart her own singlehood by going on 12 dates in 12 days at the busiest time of the year? As sure as a partridge in a pear tree. — S.C.

How to watch: The 12 Dates of Christmas premieres Nov. 6 on Hulu.

Vermont's finest return in Super Troopers 3, a madcap sequel from comedy troupe Broken Lizard. The film sees Farva (Kevin Heffernan) about to marry Thorny's (Jay Chandrasekhar) sister Sarita (Hannah Simone). Of course, all hell breaks loose thanks to a rowdy bachelor party and a new drug known as "Canadian Crystal." Can the troopers save this wedding (and make way for a new sequel in the process)? You'll have to stream to find out. — Belen Edwards, Entertainment Reporter

Starring: Jay Chandrasekhar, Kevin Heffernan, Steve Lemme, Paul Soter, Erik Stolhanske, Hannah Simone, Nat Faxon, Chace Crawford, Andrew Dismukes, Marisa Coughlan, Jon Rudnitsky, Lisa Gilroy, Sakina Jaffrey, Iqbal Theba, Lynda Carter, and Brian Cox

How to watch: Super Troopers 3 is now streaming on Hulu.

Almost anything can be scary in the right circumstances. That, at least, seems to be what writer/director Yûta Shimotsu is going for in New Group, a horror comedy largely centered around human pyramids. These start to pop at Ai's (Anna Yamada) new high school, and it soon becomes clear that the phenomenon is more disturbing than simple gymnastics. — S.H.

Starring: Anna Yamada, Yuzu Aoki, Arisa Sasaki, and Pierre Taki

How to watch: New Group debuts on Shudder on Nov. 6.

Andrew Garfield says "no kings" in The Uprising, the latest from director Paul Greengrass (The Bourne Supremacy). The film tells the story of England's 1381 Peasants' Revolt, with Garfield playing a fictionalized farmer at the center of it all.

While The Uprising's tale of standing up to tyranny is timely, critics found the film's execution lacking. As Mashable Entertainment Editor Kristy Puchko wrote in her review, "Greengrass gives us a bland, muddy slog with a black-and-white hero and villain, and an ending that is sure to satisfy no one."* — B.E.

Starring: Andrew Garfield, Jamie Bell, Stephen Dillane, Tom Hollander, Cosmo Jarvis, Thomasin McKenzie, Jonny Lee Miller, Woody Norman, and Katherine Waterston

How to watch: The Uprising premieres Nov. 6 on Peacock.

Romantic drama Charlie Harper hits digital this week — and no, it is not about someone named Charlie Harper, but rather about the love story between Charlie (Nick Robinson) and Harper (Emilia Jones). The film uses a nonlinear approach to tell the story of this pair's relationship, from their sweetest moments to their most tumultuous. Critics didn't fall completely in love with Charlie Harper, but if you're looking for some onscreen romance, it's definitely worth a shot. — B.E.

How to watch: Charlie Harper is now available on digital.

Yes, there is a new Korean romantic epic streaming this week, and yes, it does come with a heap of buzz from this year's Busan International Film Festival. Based on the hugely popular Naver WEBTOON, The Remarried Empress sees a royal relationship in peril, that of the Eastern Empire's Empress Navier (Hometown Cha-Cha-Cha's Shin Min-a) and Emperor Sovieshu (Kingdom's Ju Ji-hoon). When the emperor's affections stray elsewhere, the empress makes one hell of a deal: She wants to remarry. And not just anyone; she's got her eye on Heinrey, the rival Western Kingdom's dreamy crown prince (Law and the City's Lee Jong-suk). The very future of the kingdoms are at stake! — S.C.

Starring: Shin Min-a, Ju Ji-hoon, Lee Jong-suk, and Lee Se-young

How to watch: The Remarried Empress is now streaming on Disney+ and Hulu.

If you're after a steampunk anime western from Yasuke creator LeSean Thomas, Netflix's Bass X Machina will fit your wanted poster. In the six-episode animated series, Oscar-nominated Causeway star Brian Tyree Henry voices Bass Reeves, a character based on the real American frontier icon who was one of the first Black U.S. Deputy Marshals west of the Mississippi. Expect a world of ruthless outlaws, desert machines, and otherworldly forces as one man tries to protect his family and deliver justice. — S.C.

Starring: Brian Tyree Henry, Janelle Monáe, Tati Gabrielle, Cree Summer, Chaske Spencer, Currie Graham, and Starletta DuPois

How to watch: Bass X Machina is now streaming on Netflix.

Owen Wilson's sports comedy Stick made a sweet splash in its first season, telling a story of golf redemption and found family. Now, Wilson returns as ex-golfer Pryce Cahill, who's coaching 17-year-old golf prodigy Santi Wheeler (Peter Dager). As Santi continues his pro golfing career, Pryce makes the game-changing decision to mount a comeback and try to become the oldest golfer to win a major championship. To accomplish that feat, he'll need some help, and Apple has recruited Pluribus Emmy winner Rhea Seehorn for the job. She plays Coach Amanda, a "swing doctor" who might just take Pryce back to his glory days. — B.E.

Starring: Owen Wilson, Peter Dager, Marc Maron, Mariana Treviño, Lilli Kay, Judy Greer, Timothy Olyphant, Rhea Seehorn, John Higgins, Billy Zane, Rex Linn, Sasha Alexander, and Missi Pyle

How to watch: The first two episodes of Stick Season 2 are now streaming on AppleTV, with new episodes every Wednesday.

Thirty years after Marian Keyes' first Walsh sisters book was published, the BBC finally brought the lives of Rachel, Anna, Claire, Maggie, and Helen Walsh to UK screens in February. Now, The Walsh Sisters is streaming in the U.S. through Roku. From Stefanie Preissner (Can't Cope, Won't Cope) and Kefi Chadwick (Rivals) comes a deeply moving adaptation that weaves several Keyes' books together — and boasts an incredible cast. As I wrote in my review, "A raw, authentic portrayal of sisterhood, addiction, grief, and mental health, The Walsh Sisters feels well overdue on our screens." — S.C.

Starring: Caroline Menton, Danielle Galligan, Stefanie Preissner, Máiread Tyers, and Louisa Harland

How to watch: The Walsh Sisters is now streaming on Roku.

An ancient serpentine predator lies in slumber, deep in the Amazon, in this Anaconda-style creature survival horror from director Mike P. Nelson (V/H/S/85; Silent Night, Deadly Night).

Beware Boiúna sees a group on a medical mission into the South American rainforest hunted by a mythical semiaquatic reptile who's recently been rudely awakened. Kiana Madeira (Fear Street), Jessica Rothe (Happy Death Day), and Logan Marshall-Green (Upgrade) disturb the slumber of Boiúna, whose bite is way worse than her non-existent bark. Written by Nelson with his Wrong Turn collaborator Alan B. McElroy and Doom: Annihilation's Tony Giglio, this film feels meant to be bravely watched in cinemas behind an enormous popcorn bucket — but now you can watch it from the comfort of your sofa.* — S.C.

Starring: Kiana Madeira, Jessica Rothe, and Logan Marshall-Green

How to watch: Beware Boiúna is now available on digital.

Keira Knightley and Ben Whishaw return for spy shenanigans in Season 2 of Black Doves, and the stakes (and suspense) have never been higher.

Helen (Knightley) is still giving up England's secrets to the Black Doves, even as her husband, Wallace (Andrew Buchan), gets ready to take on the role of Prime Minister. Meanwhile, triggerman Sam (Whishaw) is stuck taking smaller hit jobs. However, the two reunite when a sinister plot to undermine Reed's (Sarah Lancashire) position in the Black Doves emerges. It's not long before the two are sucked back into a web of espionage, violence, and most importantly of all, friendship.* — B.E.

Starring: Keira Knightley, Ben Whishaw, Sarah Lancashire, Andrew Buchan, Kathryn Hunter, Ella Lily Hyland, Gabrielle Creevy, Agnes O’Casey, and Molly Chesworth

How to watch: Black Doves Season 2 is now streaming on Netflix.

Director Georgia Oakley (Blue Jean) and screenwriter Diana Reid adapt Jane Austen's 1811 novel about two very different sisters seeking a match in Regency-era England.

After the death of their father, the rigorously rational Elinor Dashwood (Daisy Edgar-Jones) and her impulsive younger sister Marianne (Esmé Creed-Miles) are eager to find husbands. But they're looking for something more rare than financial security. They're looking for love in a world of infuriatingly righteous gentlemen and unfortunately sexy rogues.* — K.P.

Starring: Daisy Edgar-Jones, Esmé Creed-Miles, Caitríona Balfe, Bodhi Rae Breathnach, Frank Dillane, Herbert Nordrum, George MacKay, and Fiona Shaw

How to watch: Sense and Sensibility is now available for rental or purchase on digital.

Before Lance Oppenheim blew our minds with You Can See Everything, he dropped Primetime, a docudrama exploring Chris Hansen's problematic news special series To Catch a Predator.

Robert Pattinson is chilling and uncomfortably funny in the lead role of Hansen, reimagining the real-life newsman as a creepy clown with a dangerous blind ambition. And while Oppenheim and a talented cast make a movie that is thought-provoking, this critic hoped it'd go harder. As I wrote in my review, "I was left wishing Primetime had the guts to follow the dark path this heightened Hansen charted to somewhere deranged, perhaps providing a more satisfying ending than real life (and reality TV) has. Chase those midnight movie vibes that Pattinson has coursing through his final act into something damning and daring! Or, don't, I guess.

"While Primetime is exhilarating and disturbing thanks to its sensational cast, a flimsy conclusion has this film end with a whimper instead of a bang." — K.P.

Starring: Robert Pattinson, Merritt Wever, Skyler Gisondo, and Phoebe Bridgers

How to watch: Primetime is now available on digital.

Director John Wilson, of HBO's How To with John Wilson, makes his feature directorial debut with documentary The History of Concrete. Don't be fooled by the seemingly dry title. The History of Concrete is an absurd deep dive into the gray material that surrounds us, one that involves everything from Hallmark movies to gum-busting machines. If you enjoyed How To with John Wilson or want another meta-documentary after seeing You Can See Everything, this should scratch that itch. — B.E.

How to watch: The History of Concrete is now available on digital.

Alex Gibney's Musk documentary had a blink-and-you'll-miss-it theatrical run, being pulled from cinemas after just two weeks. But if you didn't get chance to see the nearly four-hour doc — which charts the rise to power of the world's richest man, featuring interviews with family members and former colleagues — you can catch it on digital this week. Mashable's Matt Binder described it as "brutal and accurate" in his review for Mashable. It struggled at the box office, but had its marketing campaign hampered due to ads being restricted on multiple social networks. — S.H.

How to watch: Musk is now available on digital.

Jess McKenna and Zach Reino have made their mark on Dropout with improvised musical numbers on Play It By Ear and Game Changer, including the finale "Last Talent Standing." Now, they're bringing their song-writing skills to the comedy streamer's first scripted series in seven years, MP3.

Seemingly inspired by the TRL days of MTV, MP3 focuses on a TV show where host Dane Cartel (Jeremy Culhane) offers up music videos alongside his new co-hosts Chia (Aparna Nancherla) and Dr. Muse-Tina (Skye Townsend). This setup allows Dropout's wide ensemble of comedians to pop up for kooky cameos, singing and dancing in a variety of bizarre music videos. Plus, based on the trailer, this show shares a universe with Very Important People. So, watch out for the epic return of Princess Emily. — K.P.

Starring: Jeremy Culhane, Paul F. Tompkins, Zach Reino, Aparna Nancherla, Skye Townsend, and Jess McKenna

How to watch: MP3 is now streaming on Dropout.

Teachers deserve better. Our underpaid educators put up with everything from disruptive students to micromanaging parents. And in Jonatan Etzler's dark comedy thriller Bad Apples, one (Saoirse Ronan) takes matters into her own hands. Written by Jess O'Kane as an adaptation of Rasmus Andersson's novel De Oönskade, the film sees Ronan as Somerset-based primary school teacher Maria. She loves teaching, despite the underfunded education system testing her daily. When troubled student Danny (newcomer Eddie Waller) constantly disrupts her class, Maria makes some choices.

As I wrote in my review, "With Ronan's always-impeccable talents supported by a sublime young cast, Bad Apples leans into unethical impulses, all the while exposing home truths about the under-resourced education system and social disdain toward children with behavioural, emotional, and social difficulties. Plus, it's really funny."* — S.C.

Starring: Saoirse Ronan, Eddie Waller, Jacob Anderson, Rakie Ayola, Robert Emms, Sean Gilder, and Kerry Howard

How to watch: Bad Apples is now available on digital.

One of 2025's best TV surprises was Canadian comedy North of North, a small town sitcom set in the Arctic Circle. Young Inuk mother Siaja (Anna Lambe) decides to blow up her unhappy marriage and start fresh, embarking on a warm, moving journey of self-discovery. That journey continues in Season 2, which will see Siaja try to keep leveling up her life, professionally and personally, even if it means making some big mistakes in the process. Wherever your new road takes you, Siaja, we can't wait to keep supporting you!* — B.E.

Starring: Anna Lambe, Maika Harper, Braeden Clarke, Jay Ryan, Mary Lynn Rajskub, Zorga Qaunaq, Bailey Poching, Nutaaq Doreen Simmonds, Keira Belle Cooper, and Bruce McCulloch

How to watch: North of North Season 2 is now streaming on Netflix.

If you're on the hunt for surgically precise joke writing and an impeccable ensemble, I've got one name for you: Reggie. Dinkins.

Created by 30 Rock's Robert Carlock and Sam Means, The Fall and Rise of Reggie Dinkins cemented itself as one of the year's best comedies when it premiered this January. Now, it's back and bound to deliver more riotous one-liners and pop culture references. The series follows disgraced New York Jets star Reggie Dinkins (Tracy Morgan) and documentarian Arthur Tobin (Daniel Radcliffe), who's making a film about Reggie's life. Morgan and Radcliffe are a duo to die for, and Erika Alexander and Bobby Moynihan are riots in their supporting roles. Buy into the ridiculousness and have the best time.* — B.E.

Starring: Tracy Morgan, Daniel Radcliffe, Erika Alexander, Bobby Moynihan, Precious Way, and Jalyn Hall

How to watch: The first episode of The Fall and Rise of Reggie Dinkins Season 2 is now streaming on Peacock. New episodes premiere Mondays at 8:30 p.m. ET on NBC, with episodes available on Peacock the next day.

Good news, everyone! You can watch Weapons director Zach Cregger's Resident Evil reboot at home this week. The adaptation of Capcom's popular horror game franchise was co-written by Cregger with John Wick: Chapter 4's Shay Hatten, with horror and action through the roof. Except this time the Resident Evil chaos is happening to a regular Raccoon City resident (Austin Abrams) who just can't seem to figure out those damn locks. — S.C.

Starring: Austin Abrams, Zach Cherry, Kali Reis, and Paul Walter Hauser

How to watch: Resident Evil is now available on digital.

Leo Reich's new comedy It Gets Worse set the internet ablaze when Channel 4 released an NSFW clip involving Andrew Scott as a therapist. And while that's certainly a great hook, especially for everyone who's still not over Scott's role in Fleabag, it's far from the best thing about this beautifully messy series.

Reich stars as 24-year-old Ethan, who's going through the catastrophic double whammy of a quarter life crisis and eviction from the London apartment he shares with his two best friends from university, Abi (Emily Fairn) and Sam (Olive Gray). As the three try to find a new place, they split up across the city, where they each face a harrowing question familiar to anyone in their early 20s: What the hell am I going to do with my life? To find answers (if there are any), Reich spins a tale of fraught friendships, awkward hookups, and hard truths sure to make any young 20-something at a crossroads feel seen. — B.E.

Starring: Leo Reich, Emily Fairn, Olive Gray, Sean Delaney, Dane Williams, India Mullen, John Tothill, Andrew Scott, Lena Dunham, and Amelia Dimoldenberg

How to watch: All episodes of It Gets Worse premiere Nov. 6 on Paramount+.
