The Aaron Rodgers, Steelers breakup is the weirdest NFL story in a long time

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Of course Aaron Rodgers' time with the Steelers — and possibly his entire NFL career — would end in a midweek episode of The Pat McAfee Show.

It all started with a bizarrely-timed team announcement from the Steelers. At 11 p.m. EST on Tuesday, team spokesman Burt Lauten released a statement saying that backup quarterback Mason Rudolph would start against the Broncos in Week 12, and that Rodgers wouldn't just be benched, but inactive.

Sure, Rodgers wasn't exactly playing at the level of an un-benchable quarterback, but everything about the move screamed that there was something else brewing behind the scenes. Why was the statement from Lauten and not head coach Mike McCarthy? More importantly, why was it late on a Tuesday night before the Steelers played the Denver Broncos on a short week Friday — and in a game with sizable implications in the AFC playoff picture?

Sure enough, the other shoe dropped the next day, with Rodgers joining The Pat McAfee Show — where he's filmed many a controversial segment over the last few seasons — on Wednesday to share his thoughts in a half-hour segment. And the future Hall of Fame quarterback didn't hold back in a series of stories about alleged dysfunction under McCarthy's leadership that would result in the team waiving Rodgers hours later.

While the story is still unfolding, both Rodgers, and later McCarthy, spoke of an ultimatum between the two that came to a head with the quarterback's release on Wednesday.

"This has been months of conversation leading up to the last few days," Rodgers said of his benching, adding that he found out about the decision not from the team, but from a phone conversation with teammate Pat Freiermuth.

Steelers head coach Mike McCarthy later disputed Rodgers' claims, saying the team tried to reach out to the veteran quarterback several times. NFL insider Ian Rapoport reported that the Steelers' organization is supporting McCarthy with no plans of making an "immediate" coaching change following Tuesday and Wednesday's events.

McCarthy said he's still the right person to lead the Steelers. "I know how to win. And that's the facts," he told the media in a Wednesday press conference.

Rodgers told McAfee that he had talked "for a long time (in) the last few days" with McCarthy, later confirming that the two had met as late as 6:15 p.m. Tuesday without the topic of benching ever coming up. Rodgers said he still hadn't heard from the team by midday Wednesday; he also said he had been taking starting reps through Tuesday. Rapoport reported that players were "surprised" by the benching.

Then, Rodgers pivoted, saying he wanted to talk through his last week to explain "some of the issues I've been dealing with that ultimately led to where we're at now."

Rodgers started with the Steelers' Wednesday quarterback meeting before the Week 11 loss to the Eagles, saying that McCarthy was late and then had fallen asleep within 10 minutes of entering the meeting. Then, he woke himself up and promptly fell asleep again, later waking and leaving the room "like nothing happened."

Rodgers claimed that he and Steelers general manager Omar Khan have joked about McCarthy sleeping in meetings. McCarthy did not directly deny the claims of sleeping in meetings in his Wednesday press conference.

"Thursday, (McCarthy) misses an offense meeting. Friday, he doesn't come to the walkthrough," Rodgers said, later adding that missed meetings have been a problem since training camp. "Saturday, he misses the morning meeting, and we don't have a team meeting Saturday night for whatever reason."

Rodgers went on to accuse McCarthy of "not wanting to get to know the defense," despite prompting to get to know the players better. "(McCarthy) had already lost a lot of those guys," Rodgers said.

Rodgers said that "the nail in the coffin" was the 63-year-old head coach breaking down the team on "1, 2, 3, Packers" at halftime against the Eagles amid "weird rambling."

"We're all looking around, like 'What the f—-, what's going on? Where's his head at?'" Rodgers said. "We've had the conversations for months now — it began as a concerned friend, about someone who is unhealthy. If you're sleeping in meetings, you're unhealthy. … I could go week by week throughout the season on stuff that's going on. This guy is calling the plays!"

McCarthy confirmed to the media on Wednesday that he mistakenly did say "Packers" at halftime against the Eagles in an "embarrassing moment."

Rodgers recalled saying that the team needed a change on Monday, adding that "really, the entire team" agreed with the sentiment. "Everybody knows what's going on," he said.

Then, on Tuesday, Rodgers said that McCarthy was behind on watching film and had only viewed clips. "There's already a first- and second- and third-down install at this point," Rodgers said, later saying that it was a repeat issue.

"You're unwell," Rodgers said of McCarthy. "What's best for the team is you give it up to somebody and you take care of yourself and work on your health."

Rodgers indicated that he had wanted McCarthy to continue acting as head coach but have quarterback coach Tom Arth call the plays. "We don't have to tell anybody," Rodgers recalled.

NFL Network's Mike Garafolo reported early Wednesday that the team had been considering the move.

Steelers insider Gerry Dulac later reported that Rodgers said he wanted to call plays on Tuesday, telling the team to release him if not. "They called his bluff and released him," Dulac posted.

"This has been a conversation for weeks and weeks and weeks," Rodgers said. "And every time I bring these things up, (McCarthy) just denies it."

In his press conference Wednesday, McCarthy said it was his decision to bench Rodgers, saying, "There was an ultimatum made, clearly outside of (Rodgers') job description." The coach declined to "get into specifics."

Rodgers repeatedly said that others on the team and staff would agree with his claims. PennLive Steelers beat reporter Nick Farabaugh posted Wednesday that players hadn't corroborated Rodgers' statements on the record, but many in the organization have "backed up the claims Rodgers said about McCarthy."

ESPN's Brooke Pryor also wrote that "multiple league sources corroborated Aaron Rodgers' account of Mike McCarthy's actions – sleeping in meetings, breaking down the huddle with '1,2,3 Packers' (to ESPN)."

"I don't think (McCarthy) knows the plan," Rodgers said. "He learns the plan during the week."

While Rodgers spent much of the segment with McAfee airing his grievances with McCarthy, the quarterback did say that he played a role in bringing the veteran coach, who at one point coached Rodgers in Green Bay, to the Steelers.

"I did vouch for (McCarthy) to Omar," Rodgers said of the Steelers' head coaching interview process ahead of the 2026 season. "I'm not a victim; I'm accountable to this."

Rodgers claimed that McCarthy still blames him for getting fired from the Packers in 2018, something McCarthy denied in his Wednesday press conference.

Rodgers added that McCarthy's alleged issues have occurred throughout the latter's time with the Cowboys and Packers. He said he had thought McCarthy would be different in 2026 knowing it was his quarterback's last year in the NFL.

Why haven't more things come to light? Rodgers said players and coaches worry about job security. "I can (tell the truth) because what are the repercussions for me?" the veteran quarterback said. "I don't care about getting released. I don't care about making more money."

Rodgers said that everyone in the Steelers building is aware of McCarthy's issues, but Khan and team owner Art Rooney II aren't listening enough or asking the players to verify.

McCarthy said Wednesday that he met with the team's leadership council, and "a lot came out of" his conversations with the players. "Probably should've had more dialogue early in the process when I first arrived (in Pittsburgh)," he said, saying there were changes to consider.

McCarthy said he was focusing on keeping the focus on playing the Broncos on Friday, acknowledging that the timing of Rodgers' release made things difficult for the players.

Pittsburgh now faces a difficult path forward. The team's starting quarterback is suddenly off the roster after an unprecedented in-season blowup with the head coach. And Rodgers claims he's speaking for the entire team when noting McCarthy's alleged behavior of sleeping through meetings and being out of touch with game planning.

A team loaded with veteran leadership and currently sitting in the AFC playoff picture past midseason is now enduring one of the most dramatic stretches for any organization in recent NFL history.

And up next? A dangerous row of opponents in the Denver Broncos, Houston Texans and Jacksonville Jaguars.

"We're 6-4 and in the mix for the playoffs," Rodgers told McAfee on Wednesday. "Not how I thought it would end."
