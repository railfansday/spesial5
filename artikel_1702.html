Cherki magic helping Maresca to step out of Guardiola shadow

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Rumours of Manchester City’s supposed demise without Pep Guardiola appear to be much exaggerated.

They swept past Crystal Palace at Selhurst Park with a style of play under new manager Enzo Maresca that was just as swashbuckling as under his predecessor, with Rayan Cherki playing the role of chief architect. The Frenchman’s two goals included a captivating mazy dribble, which will long be remembered by those who had the fortune to see it.

City created enough space for themselves through clever movement. With Cherki zigging one way and Phil Foden zagging the other, they created even more by dribbling past a mesmerised Palace back three that became a back five at times and still could not cope.

Erling Haaland also scored twice. Even with the creative and unpredictable talents of those behind in a four-man attacking City midfield, Haaland remains irreplaceable with his ability to convert chances.

However, this night belonged to Cherki. Guardiola chided him publicly after he set up a City goal with a “rabona” cross last season. The thrust of Guardiola’s comments were that Cherki was not Lionel Messi. Of course not, but he possesses a genius all of his own, which Maresca would surely be foolish to rein in.

The jaw-dropping splendour of Cherki’s first goal drew a collective intake of breath at Selhurst Park.

Collecting a Foden pass, he attacked the Palace area on a diagonal path, slaloming between Jaydee Canvot, Takehiro Tomiyasu and Chris Richards, before having the spatial awareness and technique to flick his left foot at the ball to send it rolling on a perpendicular trajectory past Dean Henderson.

An insight into Cherki’s mindset came after the match.

He said: “My performance is s---. In the first half, we didn’t find the space, I played bad. I need to show them that I want to play, I want to score. Next time I want to give the ball around more.”

Forget the words, Maresca cannot ignore the actions of a player he left on the bench against Bournemouth.

Sterner tests will come for City than against a Palace side missing key players who may or may not leave south London in the remaining days of the transfer window.

Whether Maresca will trust his forward players to express themselves as freely against their rivals for the Premier League title remains to be seen, but with Elliot Anderson providing a one-man shield behind them, City could not be stopped.

Maresca said: “For sure Rayan is happy tonight. His performance was very good and not just for his two goals. He has to press off the ball and he was in charge of [marking] Adam Wharton, so it was a complete game by him.”

Only for the first three minutes did Palace hint at a different outcome but, after shots from Eddie Nketiah and Daichi Kamada were diverted away for corners, City took control.

They took the lead in the 17th minute when the otherwise quiet Antoine Semenyo flighted a cross over the heads of everyone except the shorn locks of Haaland, whose determined header looped over Henderson and dropped in the far corner.

The only negative of Foden’s performance came in front of goal, with a ballooned shot and a mistimed header.

After Cherki’s solo masterpiece had made it 2-0, Yeremy Pino seized the next moment for his own with a stunning free-kick that flicked the crossbar, then the back of City keeper Gianluigi Donnarumma before finally dropping over the line.

His shot at the end of a darting run flicked the ankle of Tomiyasu to send it just out of Henderson’s reach.

Foden then set off on a swerving run that set up Haaland to lash home his second goal of the game and make it 4-1.

The fear for Palace is that they cannot keep selling their best players and expecting to replicate the success on the pitch that has brought them three trophies in the past 15 months.

Leicester City offer a shuddering example of that. From Premier League champions to League One in 10 seasons.

Pierre Sage, the new Palace manager, will welcome the certainty that the closure of the transfer window will bring, particularly over the futures of forwards Ismaïla Sarr and Jean-Philippe Mateta, as well as wing-back Daniel Muñoz.

The one player he cannot afford to lose is Wharton, who matched the City midfield on his own at times in the first half.

Sage said: “I think the score is heavy. We stayed in the game for 65 minutes and created some chances. We know we are in for a tough season, but we have created a lot of opportunities in the first two games.

“I could speak about the transfer window, but I don’t want to find an excuse. We just have to be sure that in four days’ time, the team will be better than today.”

“It has been a short pre-season. Rayan [Cherki], five weeks ago was at the World Cup. Game by game, we will be back, just need a few games. We played well, it is a new manager but we have been playing together, with the addition of a few new players. We know what to do and have to trust the system.”

“We have to keep on playing together, find out where we are best and how to play. We have so many good players and we have to fit everyone in. He has to keep going because we know how good Phil is, it is about showing it week in week out. This is the right step forward for everyone. We have to build on this.”

The second weekend of the Premier League season kicks off under the lights in South London as Crystal Palace host Manchester City at Selhurst Park. Manchester City won both fixtures 3-0 last season and they are in fact unbeaten in their last 11 Premier league away games against Crystal Palace, who have won just two of the 22 top flight meetings.

Crystal Palace experienced defeat on the opening weekend, falling to a 2-0 loss at Everton last Saturday, meaning the Pierre Sage era started with a defeat. Tonight is Sage’s first home game in charge of the club.

“I think it will be a wonderful moment, but I prefer to have a wonderful moment with good news at the end of the game,” Sage said. “That is why I do not think about me in this situation. The most important thing is the team and the result for sure, because we prepare all the week for that, to make them [the fans] happy. They will be happy to see us, but I think they would prefer to see us win. I will give my life to make them happy. I cannot do more. When you are in this stadium [Selhurst Park], you feel the history. You feel that there is something heavy weighing on you. It is a great responsibility that I have been entrusted with. We cannot wait to see this stadium in action.”

Sage has confirmed that Ismaila Sarr, who has been linked with a move away from the club this summer, and Chadi Riad will miss out tonight due to injury, as will striker Jean-Philippe Mateta. Palace have confirmed the addition of Axel Disasi on loan from Chelsea and the French defender could be involved tonight.

Manchester City appeared to be heading towards defeat in Enzo Maresca’s first league game in charge of the club last weekend. They were 1-0 down at home against Bournemouth in the closing stages before goals from Marc Guehi and Josko Gvardiol gave them a come-from-behind win.

It has been a busy week in the transfer window for Manchester City. Both Savio (formerly Savinho) and Omar Marmoush have left for Tottenham, joining a long list of departures from the Etihad this summer. Moroccan midfielder Ayyoub Bouaddi has arrived in an £85m deal from Lille and has become the most expensive teenager in Premier League history. Maresca has confirmed that 18-year-old Bouaddi is ready to start.

“He [Bouaddi] is ready,” Maresca said. “He was working with his former club so he is fit. We will see how long he needs in terms of adaptation but he is ready to go. He is quite calm. He does not look 18, he already looks older, which is important. But in this case, we need to go slowly because he is very young, he has come from a different country so there is no rush for him. He is going to help us now and in the future. He is very mature for being 18.”
