South Korea's KOSPI plunged nearly 5% intraday, extending last week's sell-off as global AI-stock weakness spread across Asian markets.

Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix lead losses, with foreign investors turning sellers as semiconductor stocks face renewed pressure.

South Korea prepares a 10.76 trillion won market-stabilization fund, as authorities seek to contain panic selling and market volatility.

South Korea’s stock market is facing renewed turmoil as the global artificial intelligence sell-off spills into Asia, intensifying Black Monday fears among investors. The KOSPI fell nearly 5% intraday on Monday after suffering a 5.21% decline in the previous session, while semiconductor heavyweights Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix came under renewed selling pressure. The latest decline reflects growing doubts over AI valuations, massive technology spending and the sustainability of the semiconductor rally.

With foreign investors retreating and authorities preparing emergency stabilization measures, investors are watching whether the sell-off remains concentrated in technology stocks or spreads across the broader market.

South Korea’s benchmark KOSPI extended its losses on Monday, falling as much as approximately 5% amid heavy selling in technology and semiconductor stocks. The index had already plunged 5.21% on Friday to 4,869.96, slipping below the psychologically important 5,000 level and marking its lowest close in around 10 months.

By late Monday morning, the index was down 4.33% at 4,659.33, showing that selling pressure remained elevated after the opening decline. The KOSPI’s latest slide has erased much of its earlier advance, bringing the benchmark back toward levels last seen in January.  The weakness has also extended to South Korea’s smaller-cap market, with the KOSDAQ falling sharply as investors reduced exposure across risk-sensitive equities.

The semiconductor sector remains at the center of the market rout, with South Korea’s biggest technology companies bearing the brunt of the selling.

Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix declined as investors reassessed the outlook for AI-related chip demand and the industry’s massive capital-investment cycle. Both companies had benefited from the AI boom, but the same concentration in semiconductor exposure is now amplifying market losses.

Foreign investors also shifted to net selling, adding pressure to large-cap stocks and weakening the broader index.

The latest KOSPI crash is closely linked to Wall Street’s technology-stock sell-off, which intensified concerns about whether AI valuations have moved too far ahead of earnings expectations.

US stocks suffered a sharp decline on Friday. The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.28%, while the S&P 500 lost 3.7%. Semiconductor stocks experienced particularly heavy selling, with the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index plunging 10.73%. Investors are increasingly questioning the scale of AI infrastructure spending, the potential returns from data-center investments and whether chip demand can sustain current valuation levels.

Concerns over semiconductor export restrictions and technology-sector risks have added another layer of uncertainty. Because South Korea’s equity market is heavily weighted toward semiconductor companies, the global chip sell-off has translated into disproportionate pressure on the KOSPI.

South Korea’s latest decline highlights how quickly the AI-driven correction is spreading across global markets. Semiconductor-heavy economies are particularly exposed as investors unwind positions in companies that previously powered the market rally.

The immediate outlook will depend on whether US technology stocks stabilize, foreign selling eases and confidence returns to the AI investment narrative.

For now, Black Monday fears are intensifying across Asia, with South Korea’s KOSPI emerging as one of the clearest indicators of the pressure facing global AI and semiconductor stocks. A sustained recovery in chipmakers could help stabilize markets, but continued selling would risk extending the correction beyond technology shares.
