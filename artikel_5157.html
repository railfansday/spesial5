A US judge acquitted two brothers accused of causing $25 million in losses to Ethereum trading bots.

The Ultrasound relay released transaction data without checking two zeroed block-header fields.

Prosecutors failed to prove the material misrepresentation required for wire fraud.

The Ethereum MEV exploit case has ended in acquittal after a US federal judge cleared two brothers accused of causing approximately $25 million in losses to automated trading bots. The deciding detail? The relay released transaction data without checking two critical fields in a block header.

US District Judge Jessica Clarke acquitted Anton and James Peraire-Bueno on November 16, 2026, ruling that prosecutors failed to prove the material misrepresentation required for wire fraud. The decision closes the government’s first criminal prosecution centred on an Ethereum MEV exploit.

The alleged operation took place in April 2023. Prosecutors said the brothers used eight bait transactions to attract sandwich-trading bots, which profit by placing trades around other users’ orders.

When their validator received a proposed block through the Ultrasound relay, it signed a block header with two critical fields set to zero. Those values described a block that couldn’t be valid.

But the relay didn’t check them. It released the block’s transaction contents anyway, allowing the brothers to construct a different block and replace the bait transactions with trades that worked against the bots.

The operation caused approximately $25 million in bot losses in around 12 seconds.

The judge found that prosecutors hadn’t shown the disputed fields influenced the relay’s decision. Without that connection, the alleged false information couldn’t establish the material misrepresentation required for the wire fraud case.

Clarke also rejected the argument that bait transactions promised bots a particular execution order. Ethereum‘s public mempool doesn’t guarantee that pending transactions will be included or executed in a specific sequence.

The brothers were indicted in May 2024. Their first trial ended in a mistrial in November 2025 after jurors failed to reach a unanimous verdict.

The November 16 acquittal covered wire fraud, conspiracy to commit wire fraud and conspiracy to commit money laundering.

It doesn’t make every MEV strategy or exploit legal. But this Ethereum MEV exploit ruling draws a clear distinction between questionable trading conduct and the specific deception prosecutors must prove in a criminal fraud case.
