NYSE Taps Avalanche to Power Tokenized Stock Trading as Wall Street Embraces Blockchain Infrastructure

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Home » Exchanges » NYSE Taps Avalanche to Power Tokenized Stock Trading as Wall Street Embraces Blockchain Infrastructure

NYSE has selected Avalanche technology to support settlement for its planned tokenized securities platform, bringing round-the-clock stock trading and faster settlement closer to reality, pending regulatory approval.

The New York Stock Exchange is mixing together blockchain technology and traditional finance. It selected Avalanche to power the settlement infrastructure of its upcoming tokenized securities platform. The decision is part of a growing effort to bring crypto trading and its benefits to traditional finance.

Announced on November 9, 2026, the partnership will see Avalanche provide the core ledger technology for the clearing firm supporting the NYSE-affiliated digital trading venue. One of the goals is to enable round-the-clock trading, as well as clearing and settlement of tokenized securities, all of which still require regulatory changes.

NYSE has organized a yearlong test, and afterwards Avalanche emerged as a favorite provider and one that could meet its needs and provide a seamless inclusion of blockchain traders into the fold of the stock exchange account holders.

The platform is designed to support equities issued across multiple blockchain networks, meaning Avalanche will not necessarily host every tokenized stock traded through the venue. The technology will therefore mostly be used to record and settle transactions.

NYSE Head of Digital Issuance Dennis Lee said that the focus was on speed, reliability, and predictable costs. Ava Labs CEO Emin Gün Sirer also highlighted the importance of dedicated infrastructure.

The crypto infrastructure industry is huge, and there’s a growing list of cryptocurrency exchanges, including those reviewed by CryptoManiaks, to choose from. Platforms made for transfers and smart contracts automating payments have also grown and now include thousands of providers. NYSE chose Avalanche due to some of its unique characteristics.

Avalanche’s architecture allows businesses to create dedicated blockchain networks with rules tailored to their operational and regulatory requirements. It creates a flexibility that traditional financial institutions are looking for.

Stock exchanges handle countless transactions, and stability is the most important feature they are looking for. Another important feature is atomic settlement. It allows the transfer of securities and corresponding payment to occur as part of the same transaction. Therefore, there’s no chance one side of a trade is completed while the other remains outstanding.

NYSE also plans to operate a custom blockchain built using Avalanche technology rather than relying directly on Avalanche’s public network.

The decision reflects a broader effort to tokenize traditional markets and get the best of both worlds. That way, crypto companies won’t dominate the space, but can combine their features with those of the stock market.

Nasdaq is among the NYSE’s most prominent competitors. It, too, has developed tokenized stock infrastructure and is pursuing partnerships that could help distribute blockchain-based securities. This is done in cooperation with Kraken and in a way that NYSE is responding to the demands of a competitive market.

The Depository Trust & Clearing Corporation (DTCC), a central institution in US securities settlements, also has plans to introduce tokenization. It plans to use blockchain to support securities custody and transfers.

NYSE has also expanded its list of partners. It now includes Broadridge Financial Solutions, Computershare, Equiniti, and Superstate. These developments show that financial institutions are fighting to establish the underlying infrastructure that will determine how digital securities are traded, transferred, and settled.

What Tokenized Stocks Could Mean for Investors

Tokenized stocks could change how investors are accessing traditional financial markets. The possibility of trading outside of business hours is the first thing investors have been looking for, alongside faster settlements. The NYSE’s broader platform plans include stablecoin-based funding, fractional share trading, and immediate settlement.

Ownership will also change. The NYSE has indicated that its platform will support tokenized shares that are fungible with traditionally issued securities, as well as securities originally issued in digital form. Shareholders will continue to have the same rights as traditional owners, meaning they’ll receive dividends and take part in corporate governance.

All of these benefits aren’t regulated, and before investors can take advantage of them, traditional regulatory bodies need to act and establish rules and guidelines for the new features.

Despite the institutional interest, the NYSE’s Avalanche-powered project still faces several challenges before it can become a functioning alternative to conventional securities infrastructure. The platform needs to demonstrate that it can maintain the safeguards expected of a major US stock exchange.

There’s also a distinction between Avalanche’s institutional adoption and the potential benefits for its native AVAX token. It’s not clear if the cooperation with NYSE will generate demand for AVAX. The project will depend on its ability to attract issuers, brokers, and investors while delivering reliable settlement.
