United Airlines said Wednesday it is restricting access for third-party AI agents, citing regulatory and consumer safety concerns.

The restrictions will cover United’s own digital platforms and access through online travel agencies and aggregators.

"United is taking steps to restrict access — directly within our digital platforms and indirectly via travel agencies and aggregators — to prohibit use by unauthorized, third-party personal AI agents or assistants," United said.

United Airlines said Wednesday it is restricting access for third-party personal AI agents, covering both its own digital platforms and indirect access through online travel agencies and aggregators. United cited consumer safety and regulatory concerns, saying shared account credentials can expose sensitive information and that AI tools could trigger unintended purchases, account changes, or MileagePlus redemptions. The carrier also said automated AI purchases could run afoul of Federal Aviation Administration and Department of Transportation rules, pointing to check-in, which requires a person to confirm their bags don't contain banned substances such as explosives. United is the first U.S. airline to explicitly state it will block these agents, and it said it would evaluate the policy as personal AI assistants evolve while updating its Digital Trust Center to explain the restrictions. The move lands as consumer AI assistants expand into booking: Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg called travel a "top use case" for Muse, OpenAI acquired AI travel agent Odessia on Tuesday, and Southwest launched a ChatGPT plug-in for flight search. Delta CEO Ed Bastian said he is "cautious" about AI agents in booking, and United CEO Scott Kirby said at a Chicago event, according to The Wall Street Journal, "I don't want to create another middleman."
