Netflix, Amazon, and Fox are expected to bid for 2030 World Cup U.S. rights as Disney sits out

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Free daily briefing on global business news.

YouTube is expected to join the bidding for the U.S. rights, and FIFA plans to choose a winner before the end of the year

Netflix $NFLX, Amazon $AMZN, YouTube and Fox were widely anticipated to put in bids ahead of Thursday's cutoff for American rights to the 2030 men's World Cup, while Disney $DIS will stay out of the race, according to CNBC.

Fox and NBCUniversal's Telemundo hold the current rights, according to CNBC. FIFA would rather hand a single buyer the rights in both languages for the 2030 tournament, which Spain, Portugal and Morocco will host. Media companies can bid on the 2034 World Cup in Saudi Arabia as well.

Media executives expect the winning bid to land around $1.5 billion or higher, according to CNBC. The figure could differ if a single company ends up buying the rights to both the 2030 and 2034 tournaments.

Next week, FIFA executives intend to come to the U.S. to sit down with bidders, review their proposals and possibly gather final offers, according to CNBC. FIFA intends to pick the winning bidder prior to year's end. A FIFA spokesperson would not comment, saying the tender process is confidential.

Disney's ESPN spent recent weeks crunching its finances and found it could not afford $1.5 billion while also setting aside funds for its NHL, MLB, English Premier League and Champions League agreements, according to CNBC. Those deals will likely come up for renewal in 2027. ESPN weighed teaming up with another company on an offer but decided against it. ESPN Chairman Jimmy Pitaro had expressed interest in World Cup rights earlier this year.

NBCUniversal has not decided on a bid, which could put Telemundo's hold on the games at risk, according to CNBC. The company has already committed billions to the NBA and must also hold funds for MLB, perhaps the Premier League, and the NFL, whose talks are probably coming in 2028. Skydance and Apple $AAPL are sitting out.

Executives at media companies were budgeting between $1.5 billion and $2 billion per tournament for the 2030 and 2034 World Cups as of July. For the 2026 tournament, Fox paid $485 million for English-language rights and Telemundo paid $600 million for Spanish-language rights.

Netflix secured exclusive rights to stream all 104 matches of the 2030 World Cup in Spain and Andorra in October, the company said. Financial terms were not disclosed.
