Rhode Island Hospital asks Fifth Circuit to axe DOJ subpoena for trans patient records

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

The hospital argues the subpoena was issued for the improper purpose of pressuring it to end gender-affirming care.

He also argued the Justice Department lacked a legitimate purpose for investigating the hospital’s provision of gender-affirming care, saying its real goal was to use a burdensome subpoena to pressure the hospital into stopping such treatment since the feds reached settlements with at least six other hospitals requiring them to do just that.

Justice Department attorney John Bailey said the government is lawfully investigating potential violations of the Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act. While it is legal for doctors in Rhode Island to prescribe puberty-blocking drugs and cross-sex hormones for the treatment of gender dysphoria â a use for which they have not been approved by the FDA â it is not legal for drug manufacturers and distributors to promote such off-label use. Bailey insisted the Justice Department is seeking evidence from Rhode Island Hospital showing such improper promotion by manufacturers and distributors.

But U.S. Circuit Judge Irma Ramirez questioned why the government is seeking patient records if it’s targeting drugmakers.

“If you are investigating whether the manufacturers are misbranding the drugs, then why not start with the manufacturers or limit the scope of the subpoena to communications to the hospital or to the doctors about these allegedly misbranded drugs?” the Joe Biden appointee asked. “Why ask for patient files and personnel files?”

Although the hospital is in Rhode Island, the government filed its petition for enforcement in the Fort Worth division of the Northern District of Texas, where the two active federal judges are both deeply conservative.

Bailey told the Fifth Circuit panel that the hospital is producing responsive records, with patient information anonymized, for the Texas federal court to hold privately while the two appeals are resolved.

Duncan said the hospital had created a “procedural conundrum” through parallel proceedings, and Engelhardt called its actions a “sharp practice” that potentially raises ethical concerns.

But Fitzgerald said the case in Rhode Island was started by the Child Advocate, and he argued the hospital was justified in joining that challenge to the subpoena because O’Connor’s order was void for lack of due process.
