Rosa has the backing of the opposition Movement for Democracy, which governed for a decade before losing May’s parliamentary election. Women have held senior Cabinet positions, but the Atlantic island nation off West Africa has never had a woman president or prime minister.

The archipelagic country in the Atlantic Ocean, off the coast of West Africa, is battling with challenges such as a high unemployment rate and cost of living.

“I see many promises that are not fulfilled. I am tired, but I am still voting,” Aderito Cardoso, a 44-year-old resident of Villa Nova in the capital city of Praia, said after casting her vote.

Under Cape Verde’s parliamentary system, the prime minister directs policy and holds most executive power. The directly elected president’s role is largely ceremonial, although the office carries the power to veto legislation.

The country of about half a million people has a record of competitive elections and peaceful transfers of power that stands out in the region. Nearly 77,000 citizens living abroad are registered to vote, accounting for almost a fifth of the electorate.
