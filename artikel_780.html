To the ‘Moon’: Decades-Old Feist Indie Favorite Goes Viral for the Second Time in the Past Year

If you weren’t old enough to catch Feist‘s piano-pounding indie staple “My Moon, My Man” — or its block-rocking Boys Noize remix — back in 2007, chances are still pretty good you’ve been exposed to it by now. First, the song was used in a memorable opening montage sequence in the fourth episode of Canadian TV phenomenon Heated Rivalry‘s first season in late 2025, resulting in a huge streaming bump for the song that has mostly lasted through the 2026 calendar year. Now, it’s gone doubly viral, thanks to TikTok and FINNEAS.

The latter Grammy-nominated singer-songwriter covered the song for an Amazon Music original release back in September, in advance of Feist’s induction into the Canadian Hall of Fame. His version of the song has taken off as part of a couples TikTok trend with the “aaaaa”s from “my moon, my maaaaaaan” traveling from one slide in the post to the next. (Though according to reports, the trend is going just as viral for the people doing it wrong as those following the formula correctly.) It’s a complicated chain of events, as nearly every viral story is in 2026.

The result of it, however, is that Feist’s original “My Moon, My Man” — which never really stopped going viral from its Heated Rivalry bump nearly a year ago, still pulling in millions of streams a week — has now jumped up yet another level on streaming. For the seven-day period from Nov. 10-16, the song racked up over 4.3 million official on-demand U.S. streams, up 61% from the 2.7 million it notched the seven day period before that (Nov. 3-9), according to Luminate.

Meanwhile, the FINNEAS “Moon” remains an Amazon exclusive, so its streaming totals are more modest — but those have also seen a considerable uptick, notching 68,000 streams over that same Nov. 10-16 period, up 98% from the just-over-34,000 streams it collected the prior period. - ANDREW UNTERBERGER

From RAYE and kwn to Jordan Adetunji, Odeal and Sasha Keable, the UK R&B scene is one of the hottest enclaves in mainstream music — and Season 25 is making his case to be added to that list with his viral hit “Shotta’s.”

Season 25 debuted a snippet of the Tobiswag and Katzy-produced song in an Aug. 29 TikTok that has since garnered over 1.2 million views. Listeners immediately latched onto the song, citing elements such as its Partynextdoor-reminiscent production and lyrics that some described as “misogynistic.” “Look at how she treatin’, son, I know you’s a tender man/ When you call, she do not come, that is not your ho,” he sings at the top of the first verse. He officially dropped the song on Sept. 25, and it quickly became a favorite amongst TikTok influencers, fan cam creators and dancers. On Nov. 12, Billboard Hot 100-topping rapper Swae Lee brought Season 25 out for a surprise performance of “Shotta’s” at his London show.

According to Luminate, the song has explored by over 705% in streaming activity over the past five weeks. During the week leading up to his surprise appearance at Swae Lee’s show (Nov. 6-12), “Shotta’s” pulled in 1.83 million official on-demand U.S. streams, up 53% from the week prior (Oct. 20-Nov. 5), when it logged 1.19 million official streams. On TikTok, the official “Shotta’s” sound plays in over 3.4 million posts.

Last week (Nov. 12), Music Business Worldwide reported that Season 25 signed a deal with London-based indie label Isekai Records and New York-based broke Records off the success of “Shotta’s.” — KYLE DENIS

If you’re of a certain age, you’ll remember Gucci Mane and Lloyd’s “Fly S—t” collaboration from the ATL rapper’s 2013 Trap God 2 mixtape. 13 years later, TikTok has introduced the smooth hip-hop-meets-R&B joint to a new generation.

Most of the love is due to Lloy’d sultry hook, with that specific part of the song soundtracking a few disparate trends. Some users have used it for a dance challenge; members of the historically Black fraternity Kappa Alpha Psi have rallied around it to showcase their strolls (their Halloween posts were particularly popular), and others have hilariously pointed out that Lloyd’s voice sounds a bit like Steve from Family Guy. Searches for the song on TikTok were also driven by some users having difficultyfinding the song on Apple Music.

During the week of Oct. 23-29, the song pulled just 16,200 official U.S. streams. The following week (Oct. 30-Nov. 5), that figure leapt a whopping 1,296% to over 227,000 official streams. By Nov. 6-12, streams for “Fly S—t” jumped another 255% to over 806,00 official streams. That’s good enough for a two-week increase of 4,858%!

Trap God 2 tapped out at No. 20 on Top Rap Albums back in 2013, but maybe this “Fly S—t” resurgence could help it rewrite history. – KD
