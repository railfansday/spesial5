Khawaja 'hurt' by 'personal attacks' from Cummins in sandpaper denials

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Usman Khawaja hopes there is collective responsibility for the Sandpapergate scandal in time and says current captain Pat Cummins still has the "opportunity of power"; former batter says Cameron Bancroft feels like a "different person" after finally telling his side of ball-tampering saga

Usman Khawaja has told the Sky Sports Cricket Podcast that he was "hurt" by Pat Cummins' "personal attacks" on him as the Australia Test captain explosively disputed his claims that every member of the side was aware of the use of sandpaper in 2018.

Khawaja made the allegations in his autobiography Sabr and they have since been backed up by David Warner and Cameron Bancroft - two of the players, along with then-skipper Steve Smith, who were slapped with bans following the ball-tampering plot in South Africa.

Cummins, part of the Australia bowling attack on that tour eight years ago, insisted he was not privy to the scheme when he and fellow quicks Josh Hazlewood and Mitchell Starc, as well as spinner Nathan Lyon, released a joint-statement in 2021.

He then categorially denied knowledge earlier this month in a 68-minute press conference in which he accused Khawaja of being "casual" with people's lives.

Khawaja said in a chat with Michael Atherton and Nasser Hussain: "It was the most surprised I've been for a long time. I wasn't expecting that [from Cummins] whatsoever.

"I thought Pat had this really great opportunity to come out and bring everyone together. He's such a great leader. I've said in the book that I love the person he is, love the leader he is, so I thought if anyone can bring us together, it's Pat.

"I had hoped that he would - I text him before, trying do anything I could to make it easier, to do something to bring everyone in - but I realised that wasn't going to happen when he first started talking, so that was really sad.

"Then obviously the personal attacks were sad. I'm not going to lie about that, they hurt from a friend, someone you really respect. That wasn't very Pat Cummins-like, very different to the Pat Cummins I know, and it's hard for me to judge him on that.

"I know Pat, I know his family. They're beautiful, so it was really hard to reconcile the Pat I knew and the Pat that was attacking me personally rather than attacking what I'm saying. That was by far one of the toughest days I've ever experienced.

"I'll caveat that by saying that I have nothing but love for Pat. I don't judge people on certain instances or whatnot. That's not how I live my life. That's not how I function.

"Right now, are we talking? No, not really, but I've still got a lot of hope in my heart that at some stage if there's anyone that can bring everyone together, it's Pat. He's got the opportunity of power, whether it's now or later.

"But I think there was a perfect opportunity there if he took a softer route. He took a real harsh route and I think because of that everything just blew up after that like even more."

Khawaja wanted the team to take collective responsibility for the sandpaper plot at the time but says he was talked out of it by Smith as only the skipper, Warner and Bancroft - the player caught on camera looking to manipulate the ball - were suspended.

The 39-year-old, who retired from international cricket following Australia's 4-1 Ashes thrashing of England last winter, is hopeful "accountability" will eventually come.

He said: "For me it was about the team, Cricket Australia, coming together and saying, 'look, we didn't live up to our standards, we didn't live up to our values and three guys suffered for that'.

"I think there is a way to collectively take this still.

"Will everyone like agree on this? I can't see it happening now the way it's gotten, but for me the middle ground is the collective responsibility and accountability as a team.

"We win together, we lose together, we get caught for ball tampering, we take it together. I think that's achievable, but I can't speak for everyone.

"It doesn't feel there is an ending right now and that sucks for everyone."

Khawaja explained that he became extremely close to Bancroft on the 2019 Ashes tour of England when the latter returned to the side for the first time since 'Sandpapergate' and played what, as things currently stand, are his final two Test matches.

Bancroft's detail-filled, 4,000-word-plus account of the sandpaper deception dropped in the West Australian newspaper on the day of Australia's third ODI against in Hobart, adding even more intrigue to a story that has rocked, and gripped, cricket.

Khawaja said of his relationship with Bancroft: "In the 2019 Ashes, we both played, we got both got dropped and we would play Uno pretty much every day. If I kind of knew him at the start, I knew him really well by the end because we talked about everything.

"I remember asking him about the whole sandpaper incident once, too, He was still working his way through it at the time, trying to figure out how he's dealing with this.

"In his mind I assume he was thinking, 'I'll play 50 more games, become a legend and then I'll earn the respect back of the people'. Unfortunately it didn't work out that way for him and I think that's the thing that's really played on his mind throughout this time.

"I've kept in contact with him periodically. I remember being sat on the bus with him maybe four years ago and I brought this topic up again, asking why he didn't come out and talk about this. He said 'I can't'. He said it to me a few weeks ago actually."

Khawaja continued of Bancroft: "He got in touch with me because he was really struggling. I talked to him on the phone for an hour and a half and told him what I was planning to write in my book.

"That was about halfway during that conversation. The second half of that conversation, I could feel his energy change, because the weight and burden of trying to carry this was eating him up. In his mind, he was thinking, 'no, I'm not the only one here'.

"The first day it came out, he sent me a text saying, 'Uzzy, all I want to say is you've done the right thing and I appreciate this more than you can ever imagine'. That that was one of the things that kept me going because everyone was nailing me that first day.

"We do have a good relationship and a good rapport. He is one of the guys throughout the last two weeks who has been grateful and I needed that, I'm going to be honest, because at times I was like, 'have I done the right thing? Why am I doing this?'

"He finally got to put out a statement. I talked to him afterwards and he's chill, he's laughing, he's joking like he hasn't joked with me in eight years. I can tell that he's a different person.

"If that's the silver lining, it makes it worth it. I can never quite fathom what he went through in those eight years."
