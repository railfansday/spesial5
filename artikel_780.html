RAYE & Rosalía Lead 2027 Grammy Nominations as Women Make History

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Also, for the first time in Grammy history, seven artists are nominated for album, record and song of the year.

RAYE is the leading artist in the 2027 Grammy nominations, with 11 nods, tying Beyoncé’s record set two years ago for the most nods by a woman in one year. But the story goes way beyond RAYE.

For the first time in Grammy history, the six artists with the most nominations are all women. RAYE is followed by Rosalía with nine nods, and then Phoebe Bridgers, Miranda Lambert, Ella Langley and Olivia Rodrigo with seven each. (For the record, the male artists with the most nominations are Bruno Mars and Leon Thomas, with six each.)

Seven of the album of the year nominees are by women artists, which matches the record set in 2024, when the nominees included six female solo artists and one all-female group (boygenius). Three of the same women were nominated in both years: Bridgers (as part of boygenius then; solo now), Rodrigo and Taylor Swift.

Also, for the first time in Grammy history, seven artists are nominated for album, record and song of the year: Olivia Dean, Langley, Mars, RAYE, Rodrigo, Rosalía and Swift. Langley is also nominated for best new artist. She’s the first country artist to be nominated in each of the four highest-profile categories since Bobbie Gentry in 1968.

This is the fifth time Swift has been nominated for album, record and song of the year in the same year, which sets a new record. It’s the third time that Mars and Rodrigo have achieved this feat.

Rosalía becomes the second Latin artist to be nominated for album, record and song of the year in the same year. Bad Bunny achieved the feat last year with his album Debí Tirar Más Fotos and single “DTMF.” Rosalía is nominated for record and song of the year for “Berghain,” a collab with Björk and Yves Tumor. That collab dramatizes the increased globalization of pop music. Rosalía is from Spain. Her featured artists are from Iceland and the U.S., respectively. The song is performed in German, Spanish and English.

Mars and Swift are each nominated with different songs in record and song of the year. Mars made it with “I Just Might” in record and “Risk It All” in song. Swift is competing with “The Fate of Ophelia” in record and “I Knew It, I Knew You” in song.

Swift’s The Life of a Showgirl is her eighth album to receive an album of the year nod – more than anyone else in Grammy history for strictly solo albums. Moreover, it’s her fifth consecutive studio album to be nominated (not counting Taylor’s Version re-recordings). She’s only the second artist (following Kendrick Lamar) to be nominated for album of the year with five consecutive studio albums.

In song of the year, Swift and her collaborator Jack Antonoff are nominated for their Disney tune “I Knew It, I Knew You.” It’s the ninth nomination in the category for the frequent collaborators, extending their shared record as the songwriters with the most nominations in the category. The only disappointment for Swift is that The Life of a Showgirl was passed over for a nod for best pop vocal album. It’s the second time she has received an album of the year nod for an album that failed to receive a nod in a genre album category. The same fate befell Evermore.

“I Just Might” is Mars’ eighth record of the year nod as an artist. Only Beyoncé has had more (nine). (In addition, Mars was nominated as a producer of Cee Lo Green’s “F**k You (Forget You)”).

Langley’s “Choosin’ Texas” is the first song by a core country artist performing a country song to receive a record of the year nod since Lady A won for “Need You Now” in 2011. Langley’s Dandelion is the first album by a core country artist to land an album of the year nod since Kacey Musgraves’ Golden Hour, which won in 2019.

Tame Impala’s “Dracula” (featuring JENNIE) is up for record of the year. This is the second year in a row that a member of Blackpink has been nominated in that high-profile category. ROSÉ was nominated last year for her Bruno Mars collab, “APT.” (Oddly, Blackpink has yet to receive a Grammy nomination.)

Ariana Grande receives four nominations, including her first song of the year nod for “hate that i made you love me,” and her record seventh nod for best pop vocal album for Petal. She surpasses Swift and Kelly Clarkson, who have each received six nods in that category. (Swift would have kept pace with Grande if Showgirl had been nominated.)

Beyoncé becomes the first artist to receive 100 career Grammy nominations as “Morning Dew (Donk)” is nominated for best R&B solo performance. She is followed on the Grammy nominations leaderboard by Jay-Z (89 nods) and Paul McCartney, whose nominations total advances to 86 this year.

The late Dolly Parton receives three nominations, the most she has amassed in any one year since 1982, when her smash “9 to 5” led to four nominations. Parton has two nominations for best country duo/group performance for “Light of a Clear Blue Morning” (featuring Lainey Wilson, Miley Cyrus, Queen Latifah and Reba McEntire) and Zac Brown Band’s “Butterfly” (on which she is featured). She’s also nominated for best audio book, narration, and storytelling recording. (More on that category below.)

Three veteran artists make a strong showing in the nominations. Madonna receives four nods, the most she has received in any one year since 1999, when she nabbed five. Her latest album Confessions II is nominated for best dance electronic album. The Rolling Stones and McCartney each receive three nods. The Stones are up for best rock album for Foreign Tongues. McCartney is up for best traditional pop vocal album for The Boys of Dungeon Lane.

Lambert, nominated as a producer on Langley’s single and album, is nominated for producer of the year, non-classical. Lambert is the first woman to be nominated in that category since Alissia two years ago. The other nominees in the category are Dernst “D Mile” Emile II (his fourth nod) and Tony Berg, Noah Goldstein and Mike Sabath (their first in each case).

Amy Allen and Edgar Barrera are nominated for songwriter of the year, non-classical for the fourth time (out of the five years the category has been presented). RAYE and Theron Thomas are each nominated for the second time in the category. Joybeth Taylor, a collaborator on “Choosin’ Texas,” is nominated for the first time.

In addition to dominating the Big Four categories, women completely own several categories. All five nominees for best dance pop recording (Zara Larsson, Madonna, RAYE, Slayyyter and Tinashe) are women, as are all five nominees for best R&B solo performance (Beyoncé, Kehlani, Victoria Monét, Jill Scott and Tems) and best contemporary country album (Kelsea Ballerini, Carter Faith, Ella Langley, Megan Moroney and Kacey Musgraves).

The Wizard of Oz at Sphere is nominated for best compilation soundtrack for visual media. It’s not the first time an album from a Las Vegas stage production has been nominated in this category. In 2008, The Beatles’ Love won the award. Love was a theatrical production by Cirque du Soleil which combined the music of The Beatles with a lavish stage performance. The show played at a specially-built theatre at the Mirage on the Las Vegas strip.

The Broadway cast album from Schmigadoon! is nominated for best musical theater album, five years after Schmigadoon! Episode 1, from the property’s initial incarnation as an Apple+ TV series, was nominated for best compilation soundtrack for visual media.

Two former Grammy winners for best new artist (Olivia Dean and Olivia Rodrigo) are nominated for album of the year, as are four artists who were nominated for best new artist but lost. They are: Rosalía (who lost best new artist to Billie Eilish), Phoebe Bridgers (lost to Megan Thee Stallion), RAYE (lost to Chappell Roan), and Taylor Swift (lost to Amy Winehouse).

Elton John’s Billboard 200-topping 1975 album Captain Fantastic & the Brown Dirt Cowboy was nominated for both album of the year and best pop vocal performance, male back in the day. This year, a 50th Anniversary Edition of the album is nominated for best immersive audio album.

The most competitive category? It may just be best audio book, narration, and storytelling recording. Voters must choose between a beloved star who has faced physical hardship with dignity and humor (Michael J. Fox), a widely admired former First Lady (Michelle Obama), a former Beatle (Paul McCartney), two stars who died this year (country legend Dolly Parton and actor/singer Tim Curry) and a hot rapper/record producer (Lil Jon). Tough one.
