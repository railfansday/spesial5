JetBlue Cancels All Flights On 35 Routes: See Full List Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

JetBlue has removed a considerable 35 routes from its network, of which 22 are domestic and 13 are international. They all operated at some point in the first ten months of 2026, but no flights are scheduled in the future.

In winter 2026/2027, JetBlue's schedule submission to Cirium Diio data shows an average of 919 daily departures (double for total movements). Compared to the prior winter, 7% more activity is scheduled. By far the main winner is Fort Lauderdale, with departures up by a staggering 58%, influenced by the end of Spirit. In contrast, Newark flights are down by 32%.

Analyzing the evolving carrier's entire network identified that the following markets no longer exist. The examined period only includes January to October 2026, with any routes that will end in November 2026 or thereafter not mentioned. They'll be explored separately another time.

No JetBlue Flights Now Exist On These Routes

New York JFK to Ponce (in the US territory of Puerto Rico)

Boston to Dallas/Fort Worth, Bozeman to Fort Lauderdale (limited flights for spring break), Denver to Fort Lauderdale (limited flights for spring break), New York JFK to Tampa, Reno to Fort Lauderdale (limited flights for spring break), Salt Lake City to Fort Lauderdale (limited flights for spring break)

Portland to Fort Lauderdale (limited flights for spring break), Providence to Fort Myers

Manchester to Fort Lauderdale, Manchester to Fort Myers

Newark to Fort Myers, Newark to Las Vegas

Hartford to Tampa, Manchester to Orlando, Newark to Tampa

Boston to Daytona Beach, New York JFK to Daytona Beach, New York JFK to Vero Beach

Newark to Los Angeles, New York JFK to Chicago O'Hare, New York JFK to Nashville

Despite the big overall growth at Fort Lauderdale, the South Florida airport has seen the most eliminated domestic routes. However, this is primarily because most only operated very briefly for spring break. The cuts included Reno, from which no flights to Fort Lauderdale (or Florida generally) previously existed. Given the US Department of Transportation (DOT) shows a load factor of 60.1%, it is hardly surprising that JetBlue isn't returning.

As is usually the case, some of the eliminated markets related directly to JetBlue pulling out of certain cities entirely. The last flights to Manchester, New Hampshire, existed in July 2026, while the airline's final service to Daytona Beach ended two months later. JetBlue only returned to the Florida city in December 2025, having previously flown there until January 2019.

All the following markets had nonstop flights with JetBlue at some point between January and October this year, but no service is now scheduled. The cuts contribute to JetBlue's overall international flights falling by 2% this winter. In contrast, the carrier's domestic activity has risen by 11% compared to last winter. JetBlue has now pulled out of Antigua, Belize City, Bonaire, and Tulum.

No JetBlue Flights Now Exist On These Routes

New York JFK to Belize City, New York JFK to Guatemala City, New York JFK to San Pedro Sula

Fort Lauderdale to Grand Cayman, Tampa to Punta Cana (Breeze entered this market in March this year)

Newark to Antigua, Newark to Cancun, Newark to Punta Cana, Newark to Santo Domingo, Orlando to San Jose

Like the experience of other airlines, service to the Mexican tourist gateway of Tulum did not last long. JetBlue flew there for less than two years, running between June 2024 and March 2026. Only New York JFK was served. DOT data shows that 91,226 round-trip passengers were carried, but just 70.4% of seats were filled. However, at least the airline tried, with the aircraft redeployed to where it may perform better.

DOT data provides passenger traffic and load factor information. This source was used to explore JetBlue's entire network in the 12 months to July 2026. Six routes met the criteria of having at least 5,000 round-trip passengers but a load factor not exceeding 50.0%.

They are Boston to Martha's Vineyard (9,333 passengers; 45.8%), Boston to Steamboat Springs (6,529 passengers; 49.8%), Boston to Presque Isle (42,119 passengers; 43.5%), Fort Lauderdale to Orlando (26,664 passengers; 48.3%), New York JFK to Hartford (53,383 passengers; 46.5%), and New York JFK to Providence (47,073 passengers; 41.1%).

The very short hops from the Big Apple are arguably for slot-sitting purposes, although connectivity via JFK will be important. Like the others, especially those with decent traffic, they may also all play an important network role even if they do not make a profit by themselves. But given how empty they are, for how long will they realistically last?
