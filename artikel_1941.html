Portsmouth v West Ham Preview: Predicted XI As Parker Searches For First Win

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Portsmouth v West Ham United EFL Championship, Fratton Park, Tuesday 24 November 2026, 7.45pm GMT

This is a crucial match for West Ham and Scott Parker, who has probably endured the shortest honeymoon period of any Hammers manager in recent memory. His first three games have produced a rousing comeback against Cardiff City, a forgettable defeat at Blackburn Rovers and Saturday’s frustrating 1-1 draw with Preston North End.

West Ham dominated possession and registered 30 attempts against Preston, yet never really looked clinical enough to score the winner. Indeed, had the referee awarded another 20 minutes of injury time, I’m still not convinced we would have found a second goal.

Parker is still waiting for his first victory as West Ham head coach, and Tuesday’s trip to Fratton Park represents another difficult assignment. Portsmouth have lost just once in eight Championship matches and sit only one point and two places behind the tenth-placed Hammers.

Ordinarily, one might expect Parker to do some tinkering after failing to beat the team at the bottom of the division. However, I suspect he won’t make sweeping changes because he was very pleased with the way his team performed against Preston.

Edson Alvarez – likely to become key in Parker’s plans this season

Parker said he got almost everything he wanted from the performance apart from the result, while describing Finlay Herrick and Edson Álvarez as “brilliant” in his post-match assessment. I’m not quite sure I would agree with that overall verdict, although the return of Álvarez was unquestionably a bonus.

The Mexican looked decent on his return to the team and provided some much-needed protection in front of the back four. Parker has probably made it clear that, for the remainder of this season, West Ham will play with a recognised holding midfielder.

Without wishing to sound like a scratched record, I still believe Jarrod Bowen needs to be used more centrally. Parker appeared to have been given the keys to unlocking West Ham’s attacking potential against Cardiff, but unfortunately he seems to have kept them in his back pocket ever since.

Bowen remains our most dangerous attacking player, yet watching him pinned to the right flank against Preston was depressing. West Ham lacked magic, invention and somebody willing to take responsibility in the areas where matches are won.

Mohamadou Kanté can consider himself unlucky not to have featured at all against Preston because he has been one of West Ham’s better players this season. There is an argument for playing him in the attacking midfield position at Portsmouth, where his pace and direct running could give Parker’s team something different.

However, I don’t think Parker will throw the baby out with the bathwater. He has already spoken about remaining stable and consistent, so I expect Divine Mukasa to retain his place behind Joël Piroe.

Piroe should continue up front, as much because of the lack of convincing alternatives as anything else. Manor Solomon could return on the left, with Parker perhaps deciding that his experience is better suited to a difficult night at Fratton Park.

I suspect there will be a change at left-back, with Ollie Scarles coming into the team. Max Kilman is available again after completing his one-match suspension and should immediately return alongside Konstantinos Mavropanos.

According to the club’s official team news, Alphonse Areola and Kyle Walker-Peters continue to be assessed. Unless Walker-Peters is unexpectedly declared fit, Joël Veltman should remain at right-back.

Portsmouth possess a worse attack than West Ham but a better defence, so this is unlikely to be an open game. They are only two places and one point behind us, while their recent results suggest Parker’s team will have to work extremely hard for anything at Fratton Park.

West Ham’s failure to convert possession into goals remains the obvious concern. However, if Parker can keep things tight defensively and finally find a way to make one of our attacking opportunities count, I believe the Hammers can edge it.
