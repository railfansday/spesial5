Crawley Town have a 'long hard fight' on their hands, says Kevin Blackwell as he reveals one of the big factors that made him stay

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The 67-year-old was confirmed as boss for the remainder of the 2026/27 campaign on Thursday (November 19) after a month as interim head coach following the sacking of Colin Kazim-Richards in October.

Reds are currently bottom of League Two and out of every cup competition and the the former Leeds United boss knows it will be a tough job getting his side up the table – but he believes they can do it.

"Obviously we're disappointed with the cup results, there's no doubt about that, but the league form is what I've come to do and try and sort out,” he said.

“If the players just give me what they've given me in training, then I know we'll be okay.

"We've got a long hard fight on our hands and that's why everybody's pulling together. As I said, the ownership where the club needs to be going and they've expressed that they want to bring new staff in, which we've already started and we're going to hopefully bring someone else in.

“We're looking at new recruitment, the whole lot, getting the club back on its feet again. So, yes, it's an exciting time, but a tough time.”

The first thing on Blackwell's mind will be how to get his side to stat winning in the league and he laid out how they start to do that. He said: “I think the first thing you do is, if you're in a hole, stop digging. I think clearly, now as the club's stopped digging, we've now got to get out of the hole. But if that support is there, and we know it is, and the dynamics of the owners, they know what they want for the club.

As I said, if you look around the place, this club is in decent shape, but we've now got to match the ambition of the owners on the pitch. That's my job now and the players'. We've got to support the owner with what they've done for us and say thank you.

“The only way we can do that is in the league.”

Blackwell said he would not have believed he would be appointed long-term when he was first around the club but highlighted the players have had a big say on what has happened. “The thing that's impressed me now is the attitude of the players. I've enjoyed coming in training and that's half a job, is if you enjoy doing your job,” he said.

“I love football, but there's been times even in my career where you think, I've got to go in today and face the players and whatever. They've been a joy to come in and they've worked really hard. Some of them have struggled, but they've still given it a go and that's the sort of attitude I'm looking for.

“We must have that never-say-die attitude, because if we don't have that, then we are in trouble. But so far, the players have really responded and I'm really pleased and that's really one of the big factors as to why I've decided to stay.”

In the coming weeks, Kevin will be making further appointments to his coaching team, following the recent arrival of Jordan Tait.

A statement from ownership will follow shortly.
