Expedia's Muse connection is live: CEO Ariane Gorin noted it may even have happened during the OTA's earnings call this week. So Skift took it for a spin to see what it can and cannot do.

Meta’s Muse agent can now book Expedia hotels, about six weeks after the companies announced the integration. But there are limits.

While discussing third-quarter results last Wednesday, Expedia Group CEO Ariane Gorin teased the release, saying the connection was going live in the U.S., “potentially while we’re on this call today.”

In a follow-up, Expedia confirmed to Skift what the integration can and can’t do: It covers natural-language search and booking, but not cancellations or rebooking.

"Travelers can describe their destination, dates, and hotel preferences in their own words, and Muse can surface relevant hotel options from Expedia," a spokesperson said. "Because Expedia is the merchant of record, changes can be made through Expedia’s customer support, just like when a booking is made directly with us."

Meta's Muse AI agent can now book Expedia hotels in the U.S., roughly six weeks after the two companies announced the integration, but the connection is narrower than the announcement suggested. Expedia confirmed to Skift that Muse handles natural-language search and booking while cancellations and changes still route through Expedia customer support, since Expedia is the merchant of record. In a limited Skift test, Muse said it can only book properties with upfront "pay-now" pricing; pay-at-property listings require the agent to use its built-in browser on Expedia's site. Payment runs through Stripe Link, Meta's default option, even when a traveler links an Expedia account — though linking an account unlocks member pricing, which cut one listing's cost by more than $100 versus guest mode. Expedia is adding agentic channels quickly, including ChatGPT, Claude, and Google's AI Mode in the third quarter, and is packaging its technology as a B2B "template" with Qantas as the first partner. The volume isn't there yet: Expedia said it still isn't seeing meaningful traffic from AI, and Booking Holdings CFO Ewout Steenbergen said large language model referrals account for "significantly less than 1%" of Booking.com room nights. CEO Ariane Gorin said the integrations are valuable now mainly as a way to learn how agents make decisions and what tech they require, and that the small volume is high-intent traffic.
