American Airlines and Citi are working on a $695 AAdvantage Executive card for small businesses. It’s been an odd omission. The $695 United Club Business Card already earns 1.5 miles per dollar on everyday spending. Delta Air Lines has its $650 SkyMiles Reserve Business card. American’s Admirals Club card has been a consumer product.

Doctor of Credit shares some card designs that Citi is surveying. One looks a lot like the consumer Citi® / AAdvantage® Executive World Legend Mastercard® [See rates and fees] I’ve carried for a long time. However, the other would earn: 1.5 miles and 1.5 Loyalty Points per dollar on everyday purchases. Put $100,000 of bills on one employee’s card in a qualification year and the business would earn 150,000 redeemable miles. The employee would earn 150,000 Loyalty Points, enough for Platinum Pro, and the owner would earn 100,000 Loyalty Points, enough for Platinum. In January I wrote about Citi testing a $150 fee for the regular business card and the idea of replacing its $99 companion certificate with a flight discount. Citi went ahead with the fee and kept the certificate. So not everything being tested happens, and I’d put the strongest prediction on a card like this coming at a $695 annual fee with Admirals Club membership, realizing that the rest are potential options under consideration (and in some cases even controls that aren’t going to make the final product). Credit: Doctor of Credit What The Lounge Membership Gets A Business Both versions give the primary cardmember Admirals Club membership with up to two guests. The personal card also admits immediate family, which matters when there are four (or more) of you. Employee cards carry no additional annual fee. The company can buy lounge access for the people who travel, at $175 total for the first three and $175 apiece after that. So that’s basically the same as the consumer card, except that the annual fee plus lounge is optional rather. American charges $1,200 to $1,400 for full membership, depending on status. And the clubs are getting better. I prefer the newer American lounges to what United and Delta are building. My home club in Austin is slated to double in size and get an outdoor terrace. Someone who wants the lounge may already have gotten the Citi® / AAdvantage® Executive World Legend Mastercard®. But Citi’s going to want the smaller group of customers who want big company expenses on a business account. Some Ideas Being Surveyed For The Card Both versions shared from this survey would earn 4 miles per dollar on American Airlines and 12 on eligible hotel and car bookings through American. Both come with Group 4 boarding, priority check-in and screening, Omni Champion and Avis President’s Club status, up to $100 for inflight or club purchases, and $15 in Lyft credit after three eligible rides in a month. One largely copies the personal Executive card. It earns one mile and one Loyalty Point per dollar on ordinary spending, plus 10,000 Loyalty Points at each of four balances (50,000, 90,000, 165,000 and 240,000). The other awards 1.5 Loyalty Points on every eligible dollar the cardholder spends. It drops the four milestones, along with the two $250 American Airlines Vacations credits and two $100 inKind dining credits. Instead there’s $20 a month toward Melio or Xero and half off an annual Fox One and Wall Street Journal bundle (meh). It adds Sixt to the $120 Avis and Budget credit and covers a second domestic checked bag. But its two Omni free nights interest me. On the personal Executive card you enroll and complete a qualifying stay of at least two consecutive nights booked directly with Omni to earn one free night. If this didn’t require a stay to earn that would be exceptionally valuable (I’d probably redeem them in New York or worst case if I didn’t otherwise use them, down the street at Barton Creek Resort in Austin). I like the potential add-on of rewards for $100,000 in spending, since the consumer version doesn’t really give a strong incentive to actually spend money on the card (it’s a great one to get and keep, but spending is really only incentivized by the Loyalty Points). Here they’re surveying a choice of 2,500 Loyalty Points, two Flagship Lounge passes or a $200 flight discount. American sells the passes for $150 apiece, though I’d value them as an upgrade from the Admirals Club I can already visit. It’s probably best to just take the flight discount unless the points pushed someone over a status or reward threshold. The $200 adds just 0.2% across $100,000. But for someone already at $96,000 spend it’s worthwhile for the next $4,000. Would I Keep Two $695 American Airlines Cards? I have the regular Citi® / AAdvantage BusinessTM World Elite Mastercard® [See rates and fees], and there’s a reason to spend on it even at one mile per dollar. Through December 31, 2028, eligible purchases on an employee’s card earn a Loyalty Point for the employee and another for the primary cardmember. Both survey designs call that a promotion. On the more interesting version of the premium business card, the extra half Loyalty Point goes to the person whose name is on the card. If employees pay the bills, the owner’s rate on those purchases stays at one. An owner who earns all four personal Executive bonuses from flying could give up 40,000 Loyalty Points by replacing the personal card with that one. The regular business card’s fee goes from $99 to $150 on January 24, 2027. At $100,000 of ordinary spending, E would cost $545 more and earn 50,000 more redeemable miles. That’s 1.09 cents per extra mile before valuing Omni nights or other benefits. If the alternative is a 2% cash back card with no annual fee, though, you’re giving up $2,000 and paying $695. You’d need nearly 1.8 cents apiece from the 150,000 miles to cover both with miles alone. Status remains the reason for spending on the card. A big initial bonus could make either version worth getting for a year. I’d keep my personal Executive and look at replacing my regular business card with this new one.
