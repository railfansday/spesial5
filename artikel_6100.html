As merger talk with JetBlue has made a comeback, United is shutting it down, with CEO Scott Kirby still set on a mega-merger with American.

United Airlines threw cold water on the possibility of a JetBlue acquisition, even as industry chatter of a deal grows following JetBlue’s bond selloff in October.

“As far as acquiring other airlines, you’ve got to look at the whole enterprise value, not just the equity value,” said United chief financial officer Mike Leskinen at the Wings Club in New York on Thursday when asked about JetBlue. “And the carrier we spoke of has a very large debt balance.”

The question asked whether JetBlue could provide United better connectivity to regions like the Caribbean and Latin America. United Airlines again pushed back on the idea of buying JetBlue, with CFO Mike Leskinen pointing to JetBlue's debt load as the obstacle. Speaking at the Wings Club in New York, Leskinen said any acquirer has to look at enterprise value rather than equity value, and that "the carrier we spoke of has a very large debt balance." JetBlue carries nearly $10 billion in debt, and a bond selloff in October — driven by that debt and higher fuel prices — fueled speculation about a Chapter 11 filing. JetBlue executives have rejected that reading: CEO Joanna Geraghty said there is a "significant disconnect" between the bond market narrative and the carrier's results, and the airline reaffirmed targets of operating profitability in 2027 and $1 in earnings per share with positive free cash flow by 2028. It ended the third quarter with $2.2 billion in liquidity and $7 billion in unencumbered assets. Leskinen also said United's Dulles and Houston hubs already cover the Caribbean and Latin America, though some routes are better served from Miami, American's hub. United's stated consolidation interest remains elsewhere: CEO Scott Kirby, who called a JetBlue merger "idiotic" in May, said this week he is more convinced than ever that a merger with American could win regulatory approval, with divestitures.
