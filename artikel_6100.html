The AAdvantage program is revered among points and miles enthusiasts for its cheap award tickets, valuable partner redemptions and extensive domestic route map.

Whether you're an American Airlines enthusiast or a traveler who flies often with its partners, it may be worth considering (see rates and fees).

Citi offers the $350-per-year card as an alternative for those who want more perks than the (see rates and fees) provides but don't want to pay for a full Admirals Club membership.

Let's dive into the six reasons why you might want to add it to your wallet.

New cardholders can earn 60,000 bonus miles after spending $4,000 on purchases in the first three months from account opening.

According to TPG's November 2026 valuations, this welcome offer is worth $870.

You can likely get even more value from it by redeeming your miles for lie-flat business-class seats on American's partners, such as Finnair or Cathay Pacific.

For example, it's possible to book a one-way lie-flat seat in Finnair business class to Helsinki for 57,500 miles (plus taxes and fees).

Welcome bonuses are, of course, the best way to earn a large number of rewards at once. After you've earned the 's welcome bonus, you can still earn additional AAdvantage miles at the following rates:

Unless you have a card that earns more rewards per dollar spent on flights booked directly with the airline, such as the , the AAdvantage Globe is a great bet for your American Airlines purchases.

This is also a solid pick for your wallet if you want to rack up AAdvantage miles specifically, since its earning rates are reasonable across American Airlines purchases, dining and transit.

AAdvantage miles can go a long way with the right redemptions. For example, I recently redeemed 7,500 AAdvantage miles plus $11 in taxes and fees for a one-way flight from Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport (PHX) to Portland International Airport (PDX).

This ticket would've cost $364 had I paid in cash, meaning I received 4.7 cents per mile in value after accounting for the award ticket's taxes and fees. That's more than triple our valuation of AA miles.

You'll receive four Admirals Club passes each calendar year with the .

If you fly American, Alaska Airlines or another Oneworld carrier often enough to value occasional lounge access but not enough to justify a full membership, the Globe could be a great fit.

However, if you're looking for a full Admirals Club membership, check out the (see rates and fees) instead.

A day pass for an Admirals Club is $79, so you can almost cover the cost of this card's annual fee by using all four.

And there are additional ways to get outsized value out of the Globe's $350 annual fee, like day-of-travel benefits and statement credits.

With the , you'll receive perks to make your experience flying American smoother. These benefits include receiving your first checked bag free and Group 5 priority boarding (out of nine total boarding groups).

When flying domestically, you and up to eight companions on the same itinerary can receive your first checked bag free. Checked bags start at $45 each way, so you could recoup this card's annual fee if you and three family members (on the same itinerary) each check a bag on a domestic round-trip flight.

I receive priority boarding from my AAdvantage Platinum Select; I enjoy being able to board early enough to significantly lower the odds of needing to gate-check my carry-on.

You'll also receive an American Airlines Companion Certificate starting in your second cardmembership year when you renew your account. It lets you add one traveler to a qualifying round-trip domestic Main Cabin itinerary for $99 plus taxes and fees. Depending on the cash fare, that could save you a ton of money.

Beyond Admirals Club access and day-of-travel benefits, the offers a few statement credits to help elevate the value you get out of your card.

For starters, you can receive an up to $100 Splurge credit every year for purchases at up to two eligible brands of your choice (1stDibs, eligible AAdvantage Hotels bookings, Future Personal Training and Live Nation; exclusions apply).

You can earn up to $30 back in statement credits for every eligible Turo ride you complete (up to $240 each year). You're also eligible for up to $100 back each year in statement credits for inflight purchases on American Airlines flights.

On top of that, the card offers up to $120 in statement credits every four years toward a Global Entry or TSA PreCheck application fee.

Of course, these statement credits are only valuable if you already use these merchants. If you'd need to go out of your way to use these credits and rarely make inflight purchases, you'll want to make sure you can get enough out of this card's other perks to justify its annual fee.

With the , you'll receive 1 Loyalty Point for every dollar spent. That can go a long way toward boosting your progress toward AAdvantage elite status, especially if you plan on putting a lot of spending on your card.

Plus, you'll receive a 5,000 Loyalty Point bonus after every four qualifying American Airlines flight segments (up to 15,000 Loyalty Points each status qualification year). AAdvantage Gold requires 40,000 Loyalty Points in a qualification year, so if you maximize this perk, you'll be almost halfway there.

The is a solid airline card for anyone who flies American Airlines at least a few times each year. As long as you typically check bags with American, can use at least some of the Admirals Club passes and see value in the Globe's statement credits, I view this card as pretty much a guaranteed boost to your wallet.

The current welcome offer can give your AAdvantage balance a major boost. With the 60,000 miles you earn, you could easily take multiple domestic round-trips or book that business-class flight you've been dreaming of, like a seat in Alaska Airlines' new long-haul business class.
