A Latte Every Workday Costs Over $1,000 a Year – Brew Most Cups at Home

A coffee shop latte costs about $5, and buying one most workdays adds up to more than $1,000 a year. At home, the same drink costs about 70 cents. Make most cups yourself with a setup that pays for itself within two months, and keep a café visit or two a week as the treat.

That $5 is the typical price of a 12-ounce latte at independent coffee shops, according to the fall 2026 Coffee Price Index by Fresh Cup. Buy one five workdays a week for 50 weeks and that’s $1,250 a year, before tax and tip.

Making the latte yourself costs a fraction of that. Ground coffee averaged $9.25 a pound in October 2026, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. At the weakest strength the National Coffee Association suggests for a French press, a 12-ounce mug takes about 22 grams of coffee, roughly 45 cents’ worth. Brew those 22 grams with less water for a strong base, add 8 ounces of whole milk at the bureau’s average of $4.10 a gallon, and a latte costs about 70 cents.

Pricier beans don’t close the gap. At $15 a pound, that latte costs about $1, still about $4 less than the café’s $5. Plain drip has a gap too, $3.25 for 12 ounces in those shops against about 45 cents at home.

The switch lasts only if you like the home cup, so spend on beans you enjoy before you spend on gear. A drip coffee maker or French press handles plain coffee. For lattes, pair a handheld milk frother with a moka pot, a stovetop brewer that makes strong, espresso-style coffee. Add a travel mug for the commute, plus a grinder if you buy whole beans, and keep the total near $100. At about $4.30 saved per homemade latte, that setup pays for itself in about 23 cups, six to eight weeks at three or four homemade lattes a week.

Judge any upgrade the same way. A $500 espresso machine takes about 117 homemade lattes to break even.

Keep the café for the visits you’d miss most. Brew at home three or four days a week, buy a latte on the others, and the year’s coffee costs about $390 to $610 instead of $1,250.
