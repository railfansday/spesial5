Choose a no-annual-fee credit card by the spending you already do. Take the Wells Fargo Active Cash for a flat 2% if you won’t track categories, the Blue Cash Everyday if groceries and gas lead your bills, or the VentureOne if you’d rather earn travel miles than cash.

With no annual fee to earn back, a card’s rewards are profit from the first purchase, as long as you pay the bill in full. A card with a fee beats a no-fee card only when its extra rewards or credits outearn the fee on spending you already do, which is how to judge whether an annual fee is worth paying.

The cards below are grouped by how they pay, so start with the group that pays most where your money already goes. If you’ll spend abroad, a card with no foreign transaction fee matters more than the rewards rate, since a 3% fee costs more than a flat 2% card earns. Frequent flyers and loyal hotel guests may do better with an airline card or a hotel card from the brand they already use.
