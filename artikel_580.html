‘Percy Jackson and the Olympians’ Star Leah Sava Jeffries on Her Idea for Annabeth’s Gray Contacts and the Big Fan Surprise Coming in the Season 3 Finale: A ‘Dream Came True’

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

SPOILER ALERT: The following story contains plot details from “I Learn How to Grow Zombies,” Season 3, Episode 3 of “Percy Jackson and the Olympians,” now streaming on Disney+.

Seventeen-year-old Leah Sava Jeffries carries the weight of the world on her shoulders in Season 3 of “Percy Jackson and the Olympians” — literally. The story of Atlas, a Titan burdened by the unbearable task of holding up the sky, is one of the many Greek myths that serves as the basis for a Season 3 storyline. The Season 3 premiere saw Jeffries’ Annabeth kidnapped by a human-lion-scorpion hybrid monster called a manticore and taken across the country from Percy (Walker Scobell) and Grover (Aryan Simhadri). Annabeth faces her own struggles while her friends work to rescue her, which is new for the show; the first two seasons followed the trio going on epic quests together. With Annabeth separated for much of “The Titan’s Curse” — the third book in Rick Riordan’s “Percy Jackson” series, which Season 3 is based on — Jeffries’ main scene partners now are Charlie Bushnell, who plays Luke, and Dafne Keen, who plays Artemis.

That’s not the only way Season 3 is wading into uncharted waters. The first-ever screen interpretation of “Percy Jackson” was a pair of movies in the early 2010s based on Riordan’s first and second books. But Jeffries is keenly aware of the fact that “The Titan’s Curse” has never been adapted.

“It’s the first time anyone has ever seen it live action instead of just reading it in the book,” she says. “I want to make sure that I’m still delivering how the description was as much as I can, but then also still giving my own little twist with it.”While dedicated readers of the “Percy Jackson” books love to see Easter eggs in the TV show, Jeffries never wants the show to be for those fans alone. She points out many viewers hadn’t read the books until Seasons 1 and 2 came out and aims to perform for that audience too — especially as someone who hadn’t even been born yet when the original novel was released in 2005, she points out.

“Coming into these roles in the beginning was a tough thing because [the books] had such a big fan base already, and so many people that already knew about them before me,” Jeffries says. “It was a lot to learn from, but I’m so excited that I was able to get this role and embody the character as much as I can.”

Jeffries says she has learned a lot from Bushnell, 22, whom she acted opposite in several key scenes in Episodes 2 and 3 this season. She recalls him checking in after fight scenes or making sure he didn’t accidentally hit her during a take.

“He’s such a role model for people. I learned so much stuff from him. He’s just such a great person to work with as well as a scene partner,” Jeffries says. “You could just tell he loves doing what he does, and that’s such a big thing to see because even before the show has come out, with criticism, sometimes I still get in my head. But when I watch him working, his confidence and the way he goes about things, it just makes me have a brighter day, so I’m very grateful to work with him.”

In Episode 2, Annabeth discovers that Luke has been subjected to the punishment that once burdened. Atlas, the Greek Titan: holding up the sky. She decides to takes over him, and when the weight hits her shoulders, she lets out a guttural yell that Jeffries says was hard to find — and sounds different than she remembers. “The crazy thing is that it sounds so much more high-pitched on the show. I promise you, I have videos when I had a deeper scream and it was grittier, but I think I just lost my voice at that time,” she says, adding that the outburst didn’t come naturally to her: “I’m not a screaming person. Someone can ask me right now. like, ‘Hey, scream!’ I cannot just open my mouth and be like, ‘Ahhh!’ Like, I can’t.”

So Jeffries devised a trick. If she needs to scream on camera, she pushes against something or strengthens her core with a quick sit-up beforehand. “I will growl before, and then go,” she says. “I have to know that my voice will hit it.”

In the show, when a character is carrying the weight of the sky, an eerie, gray haze spreads across their eyes — achieved with color contacts. That visual detail was actually Jeffries’ idea.

She says she told Daphne Olive, one of the “Percy Jackson” writers, that using contacts would give more weight to the scene. “The grayness puts the strain on your eyes. It shows that you’re in this different world and this different dreaming life,” Jeffries says. Eventually, Jeffries got approval from the production to move forward with her pitch.

In Episode 3, still stuck in the gray haze, Annabeth has a vision of herself holding onto Percy and Thalia (Tamara Smart) as they dangle over a cliff. It’s an emotional sequence — Annabeth has tears in her eyes — but Jeffries mostly recalls how awkward it was to film. On set, she was hanging off of a practically built cliff and was fitted with wires, “because obviously, they don’t want me to fall,” she says. And though Jeffries had Percy and Thalia on her mind, she didn’t have Scobell and Smart to look at. “Whenever you saw my face, they actually weren’t even there, and I wasn’t talking to them,” she says. “I was actually holding two weights while hanging over the top, which is the funniest thing ever. Everyone was staring at me. When I looked down — I always try to do my best, but — I was so nervous.”

At the suggestion of her acting coach, Jeffries took some quiet time to concentrate before her more emotional scenes. “It was hard because I love making jokes,” she says, “but the scene came out so beautifully. I just loved it.”

Percy and Annabeth don’t share many moments together during Season 3, with the former going on a lengthy journey to rescue the latter. But Jeffries and Scobell spent a lot of time working together on Seasons 1 and 2, so when thinking about how to develop their relationship this season, Jeffries says they just went for it.

“It’s crazy because we really didn’t [talk], but in a good way,” she says. “I like the fact that we had our own assumptions of what it would be like, and then just see how we were going to play it on the actual day.”

For their scenes together, the pair would just follow their instincts without a discussion for a few takes, then put their heads together to see what else they could build. “We just knew that if we were to leave it to ourselves, then it would be better when they say action,” Jeffries adds. “We were going to learn from there.”

From Jeffries’ perspective, the best is yet to come. She says she’s especially excited to see her work with Bushnell in Episode 5 — ”The improv we did was genuinely crazy,” she says — and teases that a big surprise is coming in the Season 3 finale.

The last episode “is something that literally means so much to me because I feel like so many people have asked for it,” she says. “I know so many people who wanted to see it, and that dream came true.”
