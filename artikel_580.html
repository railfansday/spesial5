Chiwetel Ejiofor on How Stephen Frears’ ‘Dirty Pretty Things’ Led Him to Fall in Love With Film

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Chiwetel Ejiofor started his film career by doing something that many would consider as the absolute peak: he made a film with Steven Spielberg.

“Amistad” was his very first film and, understandably, he was afraid of peaking too soon.

“I was definitely worried about it at the time,” he said at Torino Film Festival. “You feel that there are stages to how you develop as an actor and the opportunities you get, and you think they might follow a very linear path. But of course, my first film role was with Steven Spielberg. I realized then, very early on, that it’s a deeply unpredictable profession. You can either feel disquiet or anxiety about that, or you have to go with it.”

He added: “The opportunities can be sporadic or come from different places and angles that you didn’t imagine. Part of the challenge, but also the exciting thing about being an actor, is having the capacity and ability to roll with it, to travel on the journey of it and see where it takes you.”

The British actor came to Italy to receive the Stella della Mole award for lifetime achievement.

He admitted that, for the longest time, he didn’t see himself as a film actor.

“Even though I had done the Steven Spielberg film, I still thought of myself primarily as a theater actor. I worked with the National Theatre in Great Britain, in the West End and with many off-West End theater companies,” he said. In 2008, he won the Laurence Olivier Award for best actor for “Othello.”

It took Stephen Frears’ thriller “Dirty Pretty Things” – which screened at Torino Film Festival on Thursday – to finally change his mind. Frears also received an award at the festival this week.

“I think it was around that time when I started to fall in love with film as well, with film acting and the rhythm and poetry of cinema. A lot of that had to do with Stephen Frears and Chris Menges, who was the cinematographer on that film. It became something that I started to pursue.”

For a while, he was able to “balance the two very well.”

“It was when I became more involved in writing and directing that it started taking up a lot of my time outside of acting in films. Historically, this would have been the time I spent on stage. I ended up writing or directing my own films.”

Ejiofor has directed two feature films: “Rob Peace” and “The Boy Who Harnessed the Wind.” But his return to theater is imminent.

“It’s still something that I’m very passionate about. Finding the time to do as much theater as I want can be challenging, but it’s something I’m in the midst of conversations about.”

He hopes to be involved in another theater production later on next year. In the meantime, he’s happy to reflect on some of the highlights of his career, which includes Oscar-winning “12 Years a Slave,” directed by Steve McQueen, also shown at the Italian fest.

In the film, he played Solomon Northup, a free black man who was kidnapped in 1841 and sold into slavery in Louisiana.

“I hadn’t heard of his story at all. When I was sent the script, I was incredibly moved and shocked by it. Then I read Northup’s own memoir. I thought it was an extraordinary story, an extraordinary tale,” he said before the screening, praising the story that captured a “very tragic period of absence of humanity.”

“It felt very profound to me. I was just deeply moved. Steve McQueen is a phenomenal filmmaker, incredibly passionate, detailed and thorough, with a wonderful vision and cinematic heart. It was incredibly gratifying to see the film being received the way that it was, globally and by the Academy.”

He noted: “At its heart, it was always a story about human respect and human dignity.”
