Crypto has an AI sector of its own, and from the outside it looks like one trade. Up close, the projects in it do very different jobs, and a comparison only makes sense once you know which job each one does.

Part of the sector already earns real revenue. Venice, a private AI app, became a unicorn in July 2026 with a $65 million Series A, and its CEO, Erik Voorhees, told TechCrunch that the company is profitable, with annualized run-rate revenues of over $70 million. People pay for private AI.

The map goes layer by layer, and each project is described from its own site.

The base layer connects idle graphics cards with the people who need them.

Most of what this layer sells is time on hardware, though Akash and io.net now also sell access to open models through APIs. For teams weighing a rented GPU against their own hardware, Lightchain AI has published a comparison of self-hosting an LLM and a worker network.

Here the network itself decides whose work is best, and pays for it.

This layer competes with ChatGPT on the user’s side of the screen.

Lightchain is building an agents layer of its own, Lightchain Agents, with a waitlist open now.

This is where Lightchain AI sits. It is a layer-1 blockchain built to run AI. Its blog keeps a plain list of what is live on the network today.

The sector will keep trading as one theme. It works more like a stack, where each layer needs the others: GPUs to run on, models worth running, apps people want, agents that act, and a record that shows what happened. Lightchain’s bet is on that last layer: the more AI runs on open networks, and the more agents pay one another, the more a public record of who served each answer matters.

Lightchain AI is a layer-1 blockchain for AI. Independent operators stake LCAI and serve open models such as gpt-oss, Qwen3, and Llama from their own GPUs. The chain assigns each job, holds the fee in escrow, and keeps a record of every job that anyone can look up. Products live today include Lightchain Chat, Lightchain Desktop (beta), Lightchain Studio, the Lightchain Bridge, and the Lightscan explorer. The LCAI DUNA, a Wyoming association, is the project’s legal entity.
