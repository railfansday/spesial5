$20 XRP Demand Exposes Ripple’s Legal Boundaries

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Home » Trendsetter News » $20 XRP Demand Exposes Ripple’s Legal Boundaries

An open letter urging Ripple to push XRP to $15–$20 has raised concerns about the legal implications of explicit price support.

A demand that Ripple should drive XRP price to $15 – $20 could undermine the legal argument holders spent years defending, wealth focused commentator Kamilah Stevenson warns in her video.

Her concern is that an explicit price-support pledge could strengthen claims that buyers expect profits from Ripple’s efforts.

The open letter, posted anonymously on X on November 1, addresses Ripple executives Brad Garlinghouse and David Schwartz. It accuses the company of prioritizing RLUSD and other businesses over XRP and calls for market action to lift the token’s price.

Dr. Stevenson cites attorney Bill Morgan’s objection: the letter asks Ripple to provide the very support that holders argued they had not relied on when buying XRP. “XRP holders bought a digital asset, not shares of Ripple,” she says, describing pushback against the letter.

That distinction matters under the Howey test, which examines whether an arrangement involves an investment of money in a common enterprise with an expectation of profits derived from others’ efforts.

In July 2023, Judge Analisa Torres distinguished between Ripple’s institutional sales and its programmatic exchange sales.

About $728 million in institutional sales constituted investment contracts; the programmatic sales at issue did not, with the court emphasizing the circumstances of those transactions, including buyers’ lack of knowledge about whom they were paying.

The ruling did not give every future XRP transaction blanket protection. Equally, one holder’s letter does not change XRP’s legal treatment: Dr. Stevenson’s warning concerns what Ripple might promise or do in response.

The YouTube video recalls the trading restrictions that followed the SEC’s December 2020 lawsuit. Coinbase suspended XRP trading in January 2021 and restored it after the July 2023 ruling, leaving U.S. customers without that trading venue for roughly two and a half years.

Dr. Kamilah Stevenson argues that Ripple should instead build reasons to use XRP, pointing to payments and its acceptance as collateral at Ripple Prime. Those activities are different from promising holders a particular XRP price.
