This Ancient Meteorite Hints at Mars' Warm, Wet Past

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Mars is the subject of an ongoing, decades-long effort to understand its past. Its potential ancient habitability drives that effort. More than 20 missions have been sent to its surface (though many didn't survive the entry and landing). Two are operating there currently, Curiosity and Perserverance, and multiple orbiters scan the planet's surface from above.

Clues to Mars' past are in its rock, and the more ancient a piece of rock, the deeper the clues it can contain about the planet's ancient past. Visiting Mars with rovers is only one way to access the planet's rock. Fortunately, some chunks of Mars have been sent to Earth. Violent impacts have sent chunks of Mars rock into space, where they've eventually found their way to Earth as meteorites.

A Martian meteorite named Black Beauty set the standard for ancient meteorites from Mars when it was found in 2011. Most meteorites are found in deserts or in snowy/icy areas simply because they're more easily seen, and Black Beauty (Northwest Africa 7034, or NWA 7034) was found by nomads in the Western Sahara. Portions of it are about 4.43 billion years old, and Mars is about 4.5, maybe 4.6 billion years old. So Black Beauty is extremely ancient.

The Sahara Desert has yielded other ancient Martian meteorites, including Teghaza 001 (Teg 001), found in Mali in 2022. New research uncovered some unusual properties in Teg 001, and they're presented in new research.

Ancient meteorites are rare finds and they're extremely valuable scientifically.

"Martian meteorites provide a record of the planetâs geological history," the authors explain in their research. "However, most are geologically young samples that are not representative of Marsâ surface, which is dominated by ancient rocks."

Most meteorites are young just because of selection effects. Meteorites are launched into space by impacts, and that favours young rocks, which are stronger. So meteorites tend to come from younger, basaltic volcanic rocks. More ancient parts of Mars crust are weaker because they're fractured and brecciated by impacts. Some ancient rocks are also sedimentary or clay-bearing rock. These rocks are weaker and much less likely to survive an impact that can launch them into space.

Teghaza is one of the rare ancient Martian meteorites.

"We describe the Martian meteorite Teghaza 001 (Teg 001), a slowly cooled igneous rock that formed at least 4.1 billion years ago," the authors write. "Its composition, mineralogy, and texture are distinct from other Martian meteorites." Distinct from other Martian meteorites, but also similar to likewise ancient Black Beauty.

"I looked at it microscopically and found domains within it that had high levels of manganese oxide, a compound being looked for on Mars by the rovers as an indicator of environments conducive to life.â

MSL Curiosity found rock fractures in Gale Crater that were filled with material rich in MnO. It suggests, but doesn't prove, that the planet's atmosphere once had free oxygen and oxygen-rich groundwater. That's still being debated.

Lead author Yang Liu and other Caltech scientists used a dating technique to find the age of tiny zircon crystals in Teg 001. Zircon crystals are important finding the age of ancient rocks because they can survive geological processes. They also contain trace amounts of elements with long half-lives. This is how Liu determined that Teg 001 is 4.1 billion years old. "We conducted ten measurements of U and Pb isotopes, in six shocked zircon grains with different morphologies, to determine their U-Pb and Pb-Pb ages," the authors explain.

Mars' early, potentially more Earth-like time period is called the Pre-Noachian period. Planetary scientists struggle to paint a clear picture of the Pre-Noachian because it was so long ago, and geologica processes and weathering have erased much of the ancient. Some research shows that Mars may have had global oceans during this period, a stunning difference from the frigid, dessicated planet it is today.

It's possible that billions of years ago, Mars had a global ocean, as shown in this illustration. That could explain the aqueous alteration in Teg001. Image Credit: By Ittiz - Own work, CC BY-SA 3.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?curid=7861829

It's possible that Mars still has some simple, extant life. If it does, that life is hiding somewhere underground where the last of the planet's ancient water might be sequestered, away from the harsh radiation on the surface. But if the emerging picture of an ancient, warm and wet Mars is true, that would be the Pre-Noachian.

âIf there was ever abundant life on Mars, on the surface, not sequestered away in some subsurface, that would be the time that you would be looking at, the Pre-Noachian period,â said Agee. âIt's the very earliest time, and that was when maybe Mars still had a magnetic field, and it was shielded from radiation, and there were surface waters. There was a denser atmosphere. If primitive life was abundant in Mars. That is the time that is most likely for it to have happened.â

Most Martian meteorites are much younger than Teg001, only a few hundred million years old. It likely came from a place no rover has visited, possibly from Mars' southern highlands. Remote sensing shows that that ancient region has a similar composition to Teg001.
