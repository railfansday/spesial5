From Caramelized Amber to Bombshell Blowouts, These Are Fall’s Biggest Hair Trends

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Hairstylists share the buzziest fall hair trends their clients can’t get enough of.

When dried leaves in varying hues of orange, red, and yellow crunch under our feet, it’s basically a bat signal that the best season is underway. Fall sits right in the middle of summer’s balmy rays and winter’s biting chill, with a refreshing briskness that won’t necessarily expedite reversion for curly hair going straight and won’t dry out freshly bleached and/or dyed strands. The options are endless in this kind of weather, which can make decision-making feel a bit overwhelming. Do you try a new color? A bob cut? Maybe a pixie? According to the hottest hair trends of the season, you can do it all.

The transition from summer to fall is an important one for your hair. “Hair can be a little more compromised or porous based on the environment, from swimming pools and sun in the summer. Going from fall to winter, hair treatments to encourage hydration and strength are the easiest way to get your hair back on track,” says Eric Marshall Wright, stylist and owner of Eric Marshall Salon in Nashville, Tennessee. With a proper hair routine at home, your hair becomes strong enough to withstand your next hair transformation—that’s where fall’s biggest hair trends come in.

If you’re itching for a new hairdo, we polled several hairstylists, including Wright, to see what they predict will be the most head-turning hairstyles of the season, straight from their very own salons. Whether you’re a balayage connoisseur or tempted to do a bob, allow this list of trends to inspire you ahead of your next appointment.

Here’s a subtle take on the popular balayage technique that works with your hair color, creating a soft gradient with lowlights and highlights. “These highlights are soft, tone-on-tone highlights with dimension—think of them as a soft whisper in an overstimulated world. They look underdone instead of overdone; it’s a delicate hair color that’s sustainable and grounded. The look is effortlessly cool, glossy, and glazed for a finish that’s organically imperfect,” says Leslie Shores, color specialist at Eric Marshall Salon. This shade is perfect for “anyone who prefers a timeless, sustainable color. It’s achieved with no bleach, lightener, balayage, or ammonia-free demi-permanent hair color. Ask your colorist for soft tone-on-tone color.”

Sometimes the road to blonde is far too intimidating from dark brown or black. Shores’ answer to that gives you a lift without veering too much into blonde territory. “Cocoa brondette,” she says, is “a rich brunette with soft ribbons of deep indulgent brown shades.” The color is reminiscent of a rich cup of hot cocoa, with swirls of light brown throughout. “This shade looks best on someone with an olive skin tone, and can be achieved with demi-permanent or permanent hair color or with balayage. Ask your colorist for an all-over color with ammonia-free color around a level seven, six, or five; they can also paint soft ribbons of balayage.”

While most of the world is obsessed with bobs and pixies, another percentage of the population still favors long locks. Dimitris Giannetos, celebrity hairstylist and Great Lengths ambassador, says long hair isn’t going anywhere. “Hair can take a long minute to grow, so I have the help of Great Lengths, especially the Linea Wefts, to achieve the perfect, lived-in long hair,” he shares. His favorite hair trend this season? “CEO blowout,” which looks polished “but secure enough to get a bit messy.” This looks even better when paired with a “quiet, lived-in color, with more wispy, secret highlights versus an obvious balayage,” he adds. Your tools to achieve this are simple: “I use three different shades of the Great Lengths Line Wefts. They have such a fine spine, which makes them easy to blend and fully match your client’s color with an extra twist. They are super flat on the scalp, so they blend together perfectly without unwanted volume on the sides. I always recommend razoring the wefts at the end to create the perfect blend,” Giannetos says.

Wright concurs, adding that layers can elevate long styles that “looks effortless and moves.” Placement matters when it comes to layering hair, depending on the style you wear most often. “Layers can be as short as collarbone so you can easily put it up, but this is more about wearing it down and long-styled. Think hippie boho or 60s and long like Pocahontas. The cut is accomplished by point cutting and dry cutting techniques to make it soft and lived-in, not overly aggressive but effortless layers,” Wright shares.

If you want a louder balayage, Shores encourages you to opt for “rich shades of buttery gold and copper throughout the hair that look warm, rich, cozy, and earthy.” The gradient of hues might immediately scream summer, but it offers a toasted finish that “adds exotic warmth, depth, and dimension to the hair that feels like golden hour. Ask your colorist to paint big balayage pieces mainly in the front of the hair to match the volume and layers. These can be foiled or painted on. It looks best on brunettes and someone with thicker hair with layers.”

Already blonde? Shores suggests “bright, airy white-blonde balayage highlights that are airy and dreamy placed perfectly, as they carry a lightness to them that make them look ethereal and serene.” This look works great for those who are already “bright platinum without the constant touch up. [It’s also] perfect also for grey blending. This look can be achieved by using bleach balayage or foilyage. Ask your colorist to use bleach balayage or foilyage to get as light as possible!” she adds.

“Thanks to Off Campus and [actress] Ella Bright, we’re gravitating towards this shoulder-length bob that is worn flipped, sleek, curled, or blown out,” Wright says. Pictured here, this bob is marked by an asymmetrical blunt cut and soft waves throughout. “The cut needs to have facial shape and neck width taken into consideration, as those will cause different results, as well as the natural density and hair diameter,” Wright adds.

With summer’s sticky weather behind us, fall’s coolness means those bangs you’ve been dying to get won’t get too stuck to your forehead. “Being in a humid environment, they can devastate you in the summer, as the humidity makes them fussy. A fringe or bang can make you look styled and undone at the same time, meaning you can just style your bangs and pull your hair up to look chic. I recommend a fuller style rather than wispy so they look intentional. Keep hairline, cowlicks, and recession in mind, as bangs can have a mind of their own; style them first, as they can be difficult to dry on their own. For a softer, fuller look, ask your stylist to point-cut the ends for softness,” Wright explains.

Davines This is a Dry Texturizing Finishing Spray

“For long, high-volume natural textures, go with Davines Dry Texturize. This enhances volume on the mid-shaft through ends, especially for those who like texture and volume on their ends. Clipping [the hair] back [is an easy way] to give high volume to a half-up/half-down look and secure it with a claw clip.”—Eric Marshall Wright

“Those with sleek or textured bobs, I personally would recommend the Davines Shimmering Mist, as it creates high shine with zero hold. This could really emphasize the texture of the bob whether it’s straight or blown out or done with waves.”—EMW

Davines Hair Refresher, Dry Cleansing Shampoo

“As far as bangs go, I highly recommend the Davines Hair Refresher because bangs can be tricky and you sometimes need to rewash them. It’s nice to have something that is powder base to refresh them, that way you’re not re-styling them or rewetting them—you’re just refreshing them.”—EMW
