The 7 Airlines Flying Airbus A321XLRs On Both Short & Long-Haul Routes

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

In evolutionary biology, conversation is often focused on 'the' thing features evolved for. For example, bird wings evolved for flying. In reality, birds will use them for whatever they can. Penguins use them for swimming, while chickens use them for changing direction while running and shading their chicks.

Aircraft are somewhat similar. They may be optimized for a specific mission, but that doesn't mean they will be used for those missions by the airline all the time. In Vietnam, VietJet sometimes flies its widebody A330s on ultra-short domestic routes from Ho Chi Minh City to Nha Trang half empty. VietJet apparently does this because the time slot is available in the A330's international schedule.

The Airbus A321XLR is optimized for longer, but thinner routes. On shorter routes, its increased weight from its extra tanks and strengthened landing gear imposes a penalty. Even so, some airlines will operate them on short routes, partly because they are available. Let's examine some of the airlines using the XLR on both short and longer-haul routes.

Aer Lingus is the flag carrier of Ireland and has a fleet of six Airbus A321XLRs. These are configured with 16 business class seats and 168 economy class seats for a total of 184 seats. This is identical to its complementary fleet of eight A321LRs. In July 2026, Aer Lingus flew more than 300 flights longer than 3,100 miles (5,000 kilometers) and more than 200 routes shorter than 1,250 miles (2,000 kilometers) with its XLRs.

Those shorter routes are European hops when the aircraft are not flying their longer transatlantic trips. An example of a long Aer Lingus XLR flight is Dublin to Nashville, which comes with a scheduled flight time of nine hours and 20 minutes. Meanwhile, during the first week of November, the XLR (registered EI-XLU) has flown hops such as Dublin to London (48 minutes) and Dublin to Barcelona (one hour and 50 minutes).

Flightradar24 shows EI-XLU's longer recent flights include Nashville (Tennessee), Cleveland (Ohio), Toronto (Canada), Indianapolis (Indiana), and New York. Being located at the far western edge of Europe, Aer Lingus has the advantage of being able to reach more destinations in the US with the XLR. The XLR is an excellent aircraft for thinner transatlantic missions, but it is also ok to use on short hops, accepting a modest penalty.

Iberia was the launch customer for the Airbus A321XLR in late 2024, and today it operates a fleet of seven examples with an eighth on order. These are configured with 14 business class seats and 168 economy seats, totaling 182 seats. Spain (excluding Russia) leads Europe with the most domestic flights, driven by the need to connect the mainland with the Canary Islands and Balearic Islands.

From its base in Madrid, Iberia flies its A321XLRs to transatlantic destinations in the United States such as Boston, Newark, Washington DC, and Canada's Toronto. Having a more Spanish and Latin focus than carriers like the UK or Ireland, it also flies them to the Dominican Republic (Santo Domingo) and Puerto Rico (San Juan) in the Caribbean (both Spanish-speaking).

In South America, its XLRs fly as far as Recife and Fortaleza in Brazil. Recent flight tracking data for the XLR registered EC-OOJ reveals it is mostly flying longer transatlantic routes. However, it has also flown European hops to Zurich and Paris (both one hour and 30 minutes) as well as Venice (two hours).

Qantas will have one of the highest passenger capacities onboard its Airbus A321XLRs.

Air Canada is one of the XLR's newest operators, with the national Canadian airline flying the aircraft since June. Now the airline has a growing fleet of five to six in service. Air Canada's official website says it has six in inventory, while Planespotters.net still records five delivered, of which one is currently grounded. That aircraft is brand new and working up.

Regardless, Air Canada is in the process of building up one of the world's largest XLR fleets, with around 24 of its 30-strong order still to be delivered. Air Canada is using the aircraft on a range of longer routes, including transatlantic flights to Europe. C-GXLR was the first XLR delivered to Air Canada. In recent weeks, its longest flight was Toronto to Copenhagen (up to eight hours),

Other longer routes include Montreal to Toulouse (about seven hours), as well as Montreal to Lisbon, Montreal to Porto, and other European destinations. It is also used extensively on medium (three- to five-hour) flights to various destinations in the United States and western Canada such as Los Angeles and Calgary. Ultra short hops include Ottawa to Toronto (39 minutes) and Montreal to Toronto (53 minutes).

Qantas is another of the leading Airbus A321XLR customers, with a total of 48 on order or delivered. The Flying Kangaroo is currently taking delivery of its XLRs, with seven now in inventory. These are ideally suited to some Pacific routes where the range is vast, but the routes are thin. The fact that Qantas ordered 20 more XLRs in August 2025 suggests it has many Asia-Pacific routes in mind.

However, few have been announced. The XLR registered VH-OGA was the first delivered to Qantas in April 2025. Confusingly, its flight history over the last month shows it has only been used on domestic routes. The shortest route is the one-hour flight between Sydney and Melbourne, while the longest flight is the domestic transcontinental flight from Sydney to Perth, which is less than five hours.

Qantas is introducing the type progressively and assigning it to domestic trunk routes before expanding internationally. Upcoming international routes include Perth to India (expected), Adelaide to Singapore, and Brisbane to Manila. While Qantas has not announced all of its upcoming Pacific routes, interesting destinations include Fiji, Samoa, the Cook Islands, and Tahiti.

American Airlines now has 11 of its 40 XLRs ordered in inventory. As with European carriers, American is assigning these to transatlantic routes. They also have the range to reach many leisure and thinner destinations in the Caribbean, Central America, and South America.

The first XLR American took delivery of is N300NY. Its most recent flight history is dominated by transcontinental routes flying from Boston or New York to Los Angeles or San Francisco, taking around five hours. It is also flying transatlantic routes from New York to Barcelona (up to eight hours) and New York to Edinburgh (up to six hours).

While short hops appear less common in American's fleet, there are some. One XLR (N301NY) has flown hops like Boston to Philadelphia (one hour). As with other airlines, the American XLR fleet is working up and is yet to take on many of its intended routes. South America is identified as a future market with potential American routes including Miami to Porto Alegre, Brasilia, Belo Horizonte, and others.

The Airbus321XLR will change the mid-sized market and allow new destinations for airlines.

United Airlines is another major airline busy inducting its new XLR fleet and gearing up for transatlantic and other routes. It has recently suggested that the XLR lacks the range to serve some deeper European routes, such as those to Greece. This is somewhat confusing, as it is well within the advertised range and other airlines fly comparable distances.

United has not provided a detailed breakdown of its calculations, including payload, required margins, routing, etc., to understand the assertion. It has just received the first three of its 50-strong order, leaving 47 remaining on the order book. These are now starting to replace its aging fleet of 757-200s.

With the first delivered in May, United's XLR remains a fleet in its infancy. Flight records show they have yet to fly longer transatlantic routes to Western Europe, although they are coming. Mostly, the flights have been transcontinental, around four to five hours. One short hop in the record is San Diego to Denver, a flight time of less than two hours.

IndiGo is the leading low-cost carrier of India and is one of the world's fastest-rising carriers. It boasts a fleet of 425 aircraft in service and has 881 more on order. While delivered, that will make it one of the largest airlines in the world in the 2030s. The airline's fleet is overwhelmingly A320 family narrowbodies, although it has 60 A350 widebodies on order.

IndiGo has 175 A321neos in service plus four new XLRs. It has the largest order for the XLR, totalling 69 examples. This is made up of the 40 original firm orders publicly confirmed by IndiGo and 29 additional aircraft reflected in industry fleet databases. Once again, the fleet is in its infancy and is working up.

With that being said, IndiGo seems much faster at deploying them on its longer-haul routes than other airlines. Flight history for VT-NLA shows it has been flying between Delhi and Athens (over eight hours), Mumbai and Istanbul (over seven hours), Mumbai and Amsterdam (up to nine hours and 30 minutes). One example of a short hop is the two-hour route between Delhi and Mumbai.
