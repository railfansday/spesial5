How to dream more: 6 tips to increase how often you dream

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Clinically reviewed by Dr. Chris Mosunic, PhD, RD, CDCES, MBA

We all know that one person who says, “You’ll never believe what I dreamt last night!” Many people who dream can become obsessed with the stories their minds show them at night. Some people believe their dreams provide insights into their subconscious, while others say they inspire creativity. And those who aren’t into the deep dream interpretation simply like the fantasy world that their minds create when they’re asleep.

If you’re fascinated by your dreams for any of these reasons, you might want to learn how to dream more and make your dreams more vivid. And while you can’t control everything—like the inevitable falling nightmare everyone has had at least once—you can do things to dream more frequently and to make those dreams easier to recall. Here are a few simple techniques and lifestyle changes you might want to try to make the most of the time you spend asleep.

When you sleep better, you’re more likely to enter the rapid eye movement (REM) part of your sleep cycle, when most dreaming occurs. This sleep cycle may increase the chances of having more frequent and vivid dreams, so here are a few tips to encourage deeper sleep.

Go to bed and wake up at the same time every day to help regulate your sleep cycle and increase the likelihood of dreaming. To get enough REM sleep, aim for 7–9 hours of sleep each night.

Read, take a warm bath, or practice mindfulness to ease into rest and create an environment for dreaming. Avoid screens for at least an hour before bed, as the blue light can disrupt your body’s natural sleep-wake cycle. If you must use devices, try a blue light filter or dim your screen.

To improve sleep quality and increase your chances of dreaming, keep your bedroom dark, quiet, and cool. The ideal temperature is about 60–67 degrees Fahrenheit (or 15–19 degrees Celsius). Use blackout curtains to block light, earplugs or a white noise machine to drown out noise, and invest in a comfortable mattress and pillows to support your posture.

Lastly, keep your bedroom tidy and use it only for sleep and relaxation if you can. This can help you create a mental association between your bedroom and restful sleep.

Heavy meals, caffeine, or alcohol before bed can disrupt sleep and decrease dream frequency. Opt for light snacks if you're hungry, and avoid caffeine or alcohol close to bedtime.

Drink water throughout the day to support sleep quality, but try to limit water intake about an hour before bed to prevent trips to the bathroom in the middle of the night.

During your wind down routine, you can sip teas like chamomile or valerian root. These are thought to have calming effects that may promote relaxation and improve sleep quality, which may lead to more vivid dreams.

Sleep is divided into several stages that cycle throughout the night, typically lasting about 90 minutes per cycle.

Stage 1 (light sleep): This is a transitional phase where you drift in and out of sleep.

Stage 2 (deeper light sleep): Your body temperature drops and your heart rate begins to slow.

Stage 3 (deep sleep): Known as slow-wave sleep, this stage promotes physical recovery and health.

REM sleep: Rapid eye movement (REM) sleep, where your brain activity increases and your eyes move rapidly under your eyelids, is when the most vivid dreams occur.

Melatonin is a hormone your body naturally produces that helps to regulate your sleep-wake cycles. Levels rise in the evening to help you feel sleepy and decrease in the morning to help you wake up. Some people take melatonin supplements to help with sleep issues like insomnia, jet lag, or other sleep disorders, but it may also affect your dreams.

Some studies suggest that melatonin may enhance REM sleep. This might lead to more frequent and vivid dreams, and some people who take melatonin report more intense and memorable dreams.

Melatonin may also help with dream recall by enhancing overall sleep quality.

The effect of melatonin on dreams can vary from person to person. Remember to always consult a medical professional before you take any supplement, as they can interact with other medications or simply not be right for you.

People like remembering their dreams for a number of reasons. Perhaps it sparks their curiosity about what they could be feeling on a subconscious level or could provide them with an escape from reality. Whatever your reason for wanting to remember your dreams may be, dream recall can become easier and more natural with time and practice.

Keep a dream journal: Write down your dreams as soon as you wake up, even if you can only remember bits and pieces of them. Over time, this can train your brain to pay more attention to your dreams. Review your dream journal during the day to help reinforce your memory and increase your chances of remembering future dreams.

Set an intention: Before you go to sleep, reinforce your desire to remember your dreams. Repeating a phrase like "I will remember my dreams" may increase your chances of recalling them when you wake up.

Stay still upon waking: Moving too quickly when you wake up can cause you to forget your dreams, so before you get out of bed, lie still for a few moments and try to focus on your dream. Sometimes a single image or emotion can trigger the memory of the entire dream.

Reflect on your day: Before bed, think about your day and journal about it. This can help create a connection between your waking and dreaming life and associate both with memories and self-reflection. Thus, this practice may make it easier to remember your dreams.

Practice mindfulness: Regular mindfulness and meditation practice can improve your overall awareness and attention, including your ability to remember dreams. Meditate before bed to clear your mind and prepare for a restful night’s sleep. These six tips for meditating in bed can help you get started.

Wake up naturally: Waking up suddenly can startle you into thinking intensely about the here and now, making it harder to remember dreams, so try to avoid using an alarm clock if you can. Pay attention to your body's natural sleep cycles and try to go to bed early enough to wake up naturally. If you need an alarm, set one that wakes you gently, perhaps with soft sounds or a sunrise effect.

Vivid dreams can be more engaging and memorable than other forms of dreaming that are less detailed.

Before falling asleep, visualize a recent dream or imagine a scenario you’d like to dream about in as much detail as possible. This can help stimulate your mind and lead to more vivid dreams.

Experiment with different sleeping positions to see if they influence the vividness of your dreams.

Get natural light during the day and reduce light exposure before bed to help regulate your sleep cycle and improve the quality of your REM sleep. Improving your sleep cycles and natural circadian rhythm may lead to more vivid dreams.

Creative activities like drawing, writing, or playing music can stimulate your imagination and enhance your dreams.

Meditate before bed to help calm your mind and prepare for a restful night’s sleep. This may increase the chances of having vivid dreams. Some people use supplements like galantamine, choline, or vitamin B6 to try to enhance dream vividness. Consult a healthcare provider before using any supplements, as they can have side effects and interactions with other medications you’re already taking.

Once you’re remembering your dreams most mornings, you might get curious about steering them. That’s lucid dreaming: knowing you’re dreaming while it’s happening, which can let you look around, change the scene, or just enjoy the ride.

It takes practice, and it doesn’t happen for everyone. The good news is that things you may already be doing, like keeping a dream journal and setting an intention before bed, are also how many people start learning it.

Certain foods and nutrients may influence your dreaming patterns. For example, foods containing tryptophan, like turkey and dairy products, can promote sleepiness and may impact the quality of your dreams. Foods rich in vitamin B6, like bananas, chickpeas, fish, and fortified cereals, may help with recall.

Eat a balanced diet with a variety of nutrients to support your overall sleep health, and avoid heavy meals and caffeine close to bedtime, as they can disrupt sleep and reduce dream frequency.

Several factors can affect your ability to remember dreams.

Poor sleep quality, such as disruptions in your sleep cycle, can hinder dream recall.

Stress or anxiety can interfere with sleep and make it harder to remember your dreams.

Sleep disorders like insomnia, sleep apnea, and restless leg syndrome disrupt sleep and impact dream recall.

Some medications, particularly those that affect the central nervous system, can interfere with dreaming.

Alcohol and drugs can alter sleep patterns and reduce the likelihood of remembering dreams.

A lack of interest in dreams can mean the brain doesn’t focus on retaining them.

Sleep disorders can significantly impact dreaming. Insomnia, which makes it difficult to fall or stay asleep, can reduce the amount of REM sleep you get, leading to fewer dreams and poorer dream recall.

Sleep apnea causes repeated interruptions in breathing during sleep, disrupting REM sleep cycles and reducing the frequency and vividness of dreams. Treatments like CPAP therapy for sleep apnea may improve sleep quality and dreaming.

Restless leg syndrome can also interfere with falling and staying asleep, which can impact dream frequency and recall.

Narcolepsy is marked by excessive daytime sleepiness and fragmented nighttime sleep. It may lead to vivid (and sometimes bizarre) dreams or dream-like hallucinations.

Generally, there are few risks to trying to dream more. Supplements like melatonin or galantamine may cause side effects or interact with other medications, so talk to your doctor before taking them.

Paying more attention to your dreams can sometimes lead to more vivid nightmares, especially if you have underlying anxiety or stress.

Understanding your dreams may reveal hidden thoughts, fears, and desires, and can help process emotions, particularly those you may not fully address during waking life. This may help in emotional healing.

Dreams can also offer creative solutions to problems or provide new perspectives for challenges you face in waking life.
