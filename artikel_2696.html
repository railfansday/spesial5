Colts reportedly shutting down WR Alec Pierce for remainder of 2026 season

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The Indianapolis Colts are reportedly shutting down wide receiver Alec Pierce for the remainder of the 2026 season, prioritizing his long-term health as the organization prepares for another rebuilding period, according to ESPN's Ian Rapoport.

Pierce has been sidelined since aggravating a left heel injury during Indianapolis' Week 2 loss to the Kansas City Chiefs, the same heel he underwent surgery on during the offseason. The 26-year-old receiver missed nearly all of training camp before returning to practice in late August, only to suffer another setback shortly after the regular season began. He has been on injured reserve since September, with head coach Shane Steichen repeatedly declining to provide a definitive timetable for his return.

According to Rapoport, Pierce has been working extensively throughout his rehabilitation and had been pushing to return for the final few games of the season. However, the Colts are reportedly unwilling to risk another setback, instead focusing on having him fully healthy for the beginning of the 2027 campaign. While Pierce had hoped to return before the end of the year, the organization's decision effectively brings his disappointing 2026 season to an end after just two appearances.

The news comes during what has become an increasingly difficult season for Indianapolis, which has fallen to 3-8 following Thursday's loss to the Houston Texans. With the Colts already having traded several established veterans, including wide receivers Keenan Allen and Josh Downs, the organization has little incentive to rush one of its most important offensive players back onto the field during a season that is unlikely to produce a playoff appearance.

Pierce signed a four-year, $114 million extension during the offseason following a breakout 2025 campaign in which he recorded 47 receptions for 1,003 yards and six touchdowns, leading the NFL in yards per reception for the second consecutive season. Expectations were understandably high entering 2026, with Pierce expected to become one of the central pieces of Indianapolis' offense. Instead, he finishes the season with just five receptions for 72 yards, a frustrating outcome for a player who had established himself as one of the league's most dangerous vertical threats.

His absence also leaves the Colts with an extremely inexperienced receiving corps over the remainder of the season. Laquon Treadwell, Ashton Dulin, Anthony Gould, Nick Westbrook-Ikhine and rookie Deion Burks are among the remaining options, placing additional pressure on the younger players to contribute as Indianapolis evaluates its roster for the future. With Anthony Richardson now back under center following the release of Daniel Jones, having Pierce available would have provided a much-needed downfield threat, although protecting his long-term health is clearly the more important consideration.

From Indianapolis' perspective, the decision makes plenty of sense. Pierce is under contract through 2029 and remains one of the few established offensive players who could realistically contribute to the franchise's next competitive team. With significant changes expected during the offseason and a new general manager set to take over, ensuring Pierce returns fully healthy should be a much greater priority than attempting to squeeze a few additional games out of him during an increasingly disappointing season.

After an injury-plagued year that never truly got started, the Colts will now hope that a full offseason of recovery allows Pierce to return to the level that earned him one of the NFL's largest wide receiver contracts.
