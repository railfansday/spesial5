Keeping a ZKTeco fingerprint door in sync with gym billing: on-site connector vs ADMS push

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Most gyms that use fingerprint entry already own a ZKTeco terminal. The hard part of gym software isn't the scanner. It's keeping the scanner's idea of "who may enter" in step with the billing system's idea of "who has paid".

This is how we approached it in FitZone Pro, the gym management app I build, and the trade-offs between the two ways a cloud app can talk to these devices.

A ZKTeco terminal keeps its own user list and fingerprint templates on the device, and decides at the door whether to open. The membership and billing data lives in the cloud. If the two drift apart, either an unpaid member walks in, or a paid member is stuck outside. The second one is worse.

So the rule we work to: the billing record is the source of truth, and the terminal's user list is a cache of "people allowed in right now".

Many ZKTeco terminals speak a binary protocol over the local network (TCP port 4370 on most models). A cloud server usually can't reach that port, because the device sits behind the gym's router. So a small agent runs on a Windows computer inside the gym, talks to the terminal over the LAN, and relays to the cloud.

The non-negotiable requirement: it starts with Windows and reconnects by itself. Nobody at a gym front desk is going to restart a service.

Terminals that support ADMS ("push" mode) make HTTP requests to a server address you configure: they upload attendance logs and poll for pending commands such as adding or removing a user. No computer at the gym.

FitZone Pro supports both. Each branch has one machine, and that machine feeds that branch's attendance.

We use two mechanisms, because either one alone fails.

The reason for the second one: a plan expiring is time-based, not event-based. Nothing "happens" in your system at midnight when a monthly plan runs out, so if you only react to events, expired members keep walking in.

The terminal keeps the last list it was given. So paid members still get in during an outage, scans stored on the device sync once the connection comes back, and the desk can check people in by hand in the meantime.

That is a deliberate choice: people who were allowed at the last sync stay allowed, rather than locking everyone out because the router is down.
