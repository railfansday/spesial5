Chainflip, a decentralized cross-chain swap protocol, has faced another security scare after nearly $4.86 million in crypto was drained from its Ethereum vault in a single transaction. The attacker moved the stolen funds through Tornado Cash within just nine minutes, forcing the protocol to halt deposits, withdrawals, and swaps.

While its team investigates how the funds were taken.

On-chain investigations by Bitquery show that the attacker targeted Chainflip’s cryptographic key system rather than exploiting a bug in its smart contracts.

The attacker first obtained a valid Schnorr signature from the Ethereum Vault’s Threshold Signature Scheme (TSS) aggregate key. This allowed the attacker to pass the vault’s normal signature checks.

On November 21, 2026, a newly funded wallet (0x78fa) called the Vault.transferBatch function. The transaction passed verification and drained all the vault’s available funds in a single move.

The attacker then moved quickly to hide the stolen funds. Within nine minutes, the exploiter swapped the 3,249,631.41 USDC and 758,141.33 USDT for 2,909 ETH (worth around $4.86 million), then sent it to Tornado Cash in 38 separate batches.

Chainflip has not yet confirmed the $4.86 million loss. Instead, the protocol said it was aware of a potential exploit affecting its vaults and moved into full safe mode as a precaution.

We’re aware of a potential exploit affecting Chainflip vaults.As a precaution, the protocol is being put into full safe mode. Deposits, withdrawals, swaps and quoting are disabled.We’re investigating and will share updates here as we learn more.

“Deposits, withdrawals, swaps, and quoting are disabled; we’re investigating and will share updates here as we learn more.”

This is Chainflip’s second major attack in just over two months. On September 12, 2026, the protocol lost $736,442 in a separate exploit targeting its TRON integration. That attack was caused by a software flaw in how Chainflip processed TRON transaction data, while the November 21 attack targeted its Ethereum Vault’s cryptographic signing key.

After the September attack, Chainflip paused TRON swaps and used its surplus reserves to fully reimburse affected liquidity providers in USDC.

The protocol has yet to confirm whether it will follow the same recovery plan for the latest $4.86 million loss.
