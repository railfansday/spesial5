Torreense 1-2 Celtic: Camilo Duran springs from the bench to get Bhoys out of jail again

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

By JOHN MCGARRY, SCOTTISH CHIEF SPORTS FEATURE WRITER

Published: 17:40 EST, 26 November 2026 | Updated: 18:11 EST, 26 November 2026

Some 90 miles north of the scene of the club’s crowning moment, Celtic ensured that their latest return to Portugal did not tarnish the legacy of the Lisbon Lions.

Sleep would not have come easily to Martin O’Neill ahead of this awkward assignment in the small city of Leiria.

Almost 60 years ago, the Northern Irishman watched Jock Stein’s legendary side defeat Inter Milan on a black-and-white television while in boarding school.

For the current side to fail to see off a second division side in Torreense would have been embarrassing enough at the best of times. For it to have happened in this season of all seasons would have added to the sense of shame.

In a game where a victory was really the only acceptable outcome, O’Neill’s men toiled for the longest time yet got there in the end.

Yet again, Camilo Duran was the toast of the visiting supporters as he claimed his 15th goal of the season with six minutes remaining.

Celtic's performance certainly wasn’t pretty. But after a diabolical start to this Europa League campaign, the Parkhead men are back in the saddle.

Camilo Duran celebrates after his 84th minute header saved Celtic's skins in Portugal

Now with back-to-back victories against Besiktas and Torreense, O’Neill’s men have more than a puncher’s chance of reaching the play-off round again.

One win from their remaining games with Omonia, Marseille and Union Saint-Gilloise might get them there. Four points certainly will. It goes without saying, however, that they will have to produce much better than they did here.

After a tough six-week spell when the team looked lost, though, O’Neill has taken it to a better place. For just the third time this season, Celtic have chalked up four straight wins.

Although O’Neill had stressed that he still wanted to make an appreciable impact on Europe, there was an air of compromise about his line-up ahead of Sunday’s trip to Tynecastle.

Duran was initially stationed on the bench with Kasper Hogh, no goals in his past 16 games, tasked with leading the line. It’s fair to say the Dane did not enhance his reputation.

With Cameron Carter-Vickers nursing a strain, Dane Murray was stripped although the pair were swapped at the interval.

Jota, unsurprisingly, started in his homeland after being drafted into the squad as a replacement for the injured Mika Baur. By some distance, the winger was Celtic’s best player on the night.

The Colombian heads the ball home after a superb cross by fellow sub James Forrest

Built for Euro 2004, the Estadio Dr Magalhaes Pessoa in Leiria made for a strange backdrop to a match of such significance.

With Torreense forced by UEFA to play 60 miles from their own tiny ground, Celtic supporters outnumbered the Portuguese fans by two-to-one.

The fast start would have met with O’Neill’s approval. Liam Scales flicked on a corner which Adriel, Torreense’s Brazilian keeper, flapped at. Yang Hyun-jun’s goal-bound shot was blocked.

Alex Oxlade-Chamberlain then played a measured pass down the left to release Jota. The cross was attacked by Yang, but knocked clear. Amid the chaos, Hogh took a boot to the head which required treatment.

Jota was already relishing the task of tormenting Jonathan Mutombo. When he faced the right-back up on nine minutes, he eased past him in a blur of skill and pace.

The cross which followed was menacing. Stood at the far post, Javi Vazquez, under pressure from Yang, tried to usher the ball round the post and got it all wrong. He lashed the ball into his own net. Celtic had the early breakthrough they craved so badly.

It's relief for Martin O'Neill after Duran sprung from the bench to seal a 2-1 victory

With Torreense then compelled to come out, everything was in Celtic’s favour. Provided they maintained the tempo of the match and their quality on the ball, it had the makings of a comfortable night. So much for that theory.

For reasons only they could explain, Celtic became sloppy with the ball and lethargic without it.

Torreense served a warning that the game wasn’t done when Sam Johnstone had to boot away a free-kick. It wasn’t heeded.

Arriving on 19 minutes, the nature of the leveller was shocking from O’Neill’s perspective. Alex Alfaro was given far too much time on the edge of the box to take aim and fire. The ball bounced back off the bar.

Kieran Tierney panicked and sliced a clearance. As Dany Jean pounced, Colby Donovan put in a half-hearted tackle. The Haiti international smashed the ball home from an angle.

Could Celtic quickly respond? Not if they were depending on Hogh. The Dane earned a break of the ball and couldn’t pick out Jota.

Hogh’s ball retention started to go from bad to worse. Yang, who’d begun the game brightly, was equally as culpable of that failing.

Time and again Celtic looked to Jota to provide a moment of magic. He danced away from Manu Pozo on the flank and fired a rocket towards goal. Adriel beat it away.

Another terrific delivery from the winger presented Hogh with another chance to end his goal drought. Again, he lacked any conviction and the keeper made a routine save.

Camilo Duran goes over to celebrate in front of the fans who made the trip to Portugal

Torreense were not without their moments. Scales was forced to clear the ball off the line after Volnei Feltes had enjoyed a free header following a foul.

Hogh did well to pick the pocket of Stopira in the right channel. The Cape Verde international breathed a sigh of relief as the forward again failed to steer the ball away from Adriel’s arms as a raft of visiting players were poised to claim a tap-in.

Another excellent delivery from Jota saw Luke McCowan win the ball at the back post. On this occasion, it was hard to fault Hogh for not connecting with the headed ball back across goal.

The second half simply had to be much better from Celtic. Jota was gifted a chance to claim a second when Stopira passed the ball straight to him. His chip didn’t have quite enough to beat the keeper.

Jota dribbled his way to the line and centred. Oxlade-Chamberlain let the ball run to Tierney. The full-back lashed a shot which Feltes stood up tall to block.

Donovan’s weak strike was the cue for O’Neill to ring the changes, with Duran and Oliver Sorensen replacing Hogh and Oxlade-Chamberlain.

Before either substitute had enjoyed a touch of the ball, the impressive Jean fired into Johnstone’s side-net. This was not going to plan.

Duran is hailed by team-mates Donovan and Forrest after his late rescue act for Celtic

O’Neill’s men were mighty fortunate not to fall behind with 20 minutes left. Scales completely misjudged a long ball. Carter-Vickers didn’t provide cover. Musa Drammeh, the former Hearts forward, ought to have done better to fire his shot beyond the far post.

With Jota off after tiring, it all got rather desperate. Within the space of 60 seconds, Yang let a simple pass roll under his foot before Sorensen ran the ball out of play.

It was to be a combination of an old stager and the man of the moment which saved the day, however.

With six minutes remaining, substitute James Forrest bobbed and weaved his way down the right and sent in a cross which you’d seen a thousand times. Duran attacked it and thumped home a clinical header.

Played in by Forrest, McCowan was prevented from claiming a late third by the keeper’s legs. Relief was the overriding emotion at the end.
