Solo Health offers a different approach to health coverage designed specifically for entrepreneurs, freelancers, and self-employed business owners. Instead of purchasing a traditional individual health insurance policy, eligible business owners establish a self-funded major medical plan through their business.

But how does Solo Health work? Is the coverage comprehensive? And could it actually save you money? In this Solo Health review, we’ll break down the costs, benefits, potential drawbacks, and important details you should understand before enrolling.

Solo Health offers several key medical benefits designed to protect members from both routine healthcare expenses and larger medical bills.

Here’s a closer look at what is included.

Solo Health covers eligible preventive healthcare services at 100%, with no deductible required.

This can be particularly helpful for self-employed individuals who want to stay on top of their health without paying the full cost of every preventive appointment. However, diagnostic tests or treatments performed during a preventive visit may be billed differently.

Solo Health covers primary care physicians and medical specialists. Members can access participating providers through the MultiPlan PHCS network, now associated with Claritev.

You generally don’t need a referral to see a specialist. For most non-preventive visits, you’ll pay the applicable cost until you meet your deductible. After you meet your deductible, the plan covers eligible medical services at 100%, subject to plan terms.

One of the most important reasons to have health coverage is protection against unexpected medical emergencies. A serious accident, hospitalization, or surgery can result in tens of thousands of dollars in medical bills.

According to the company, emergency services are processed as in-network regardless of which hospital provides the care.

Certain planned hospital admissions and surgeries require prior authorization.

Solo Health covers eligible mental health and substance use treatment. This can include outpatient therapy and other qualifying services. However, limits apply.

For example, Solo Health limits outpatient mental health therapy to 40 visits per covered individual each year. Some mental health treatments may also require prior authorization. If you regularly use mental health services, review these limitations before enrolling.

Solo Health includes prescription benefits administered through FairosRx. Preventive medications in Tier 1 are covered at $0. For other covered medications, members generally pay the discounted cost until they meet their deductible.

Afterward, prescription copays apply based on the medication’s tier. Some specialty medications can still involve high out-of-pocket costs.

Solo Health also lists maternity and newborn care among its covered benefits. This can include eligible prenatal services, childbirth, and medical care for newborns.

However, anyone planning to start or expand a family should review the plan’s maternity provisions, eligibility requirements, and applicable exclusions before enrolling. Don’t assume every pregnancy-related service will automatically qualify for payment.

One of the biggest questions when considering any health coverage option is how much you’ll pay. Solo Health offers three plan designs, each with a different deductible.

The main difference between these plans is how much you must pay toward eligible medical expenses before the plan pays most covered costs at 100%.

All three plans offer the same core coverage and provider network. The deductible you select influences your monthly payment. Generally speaking, plans with lower deductibles cost more per month, while plans with higher deductibles cost less.

Your actual monthly cost depends on factors such as your age, location, household composition, and eligibility. Because pricing is individualized, the best way to determine your cost is to request a quote directly from Solo Health.

Let’s say you choose the Solo Health plan with a $5,000 individual deductible. You would generally pay the applicable cost of eligible non-preventive healthcare services until you’ve spent $5,000 toward your deductible.

After that, the plan pays 100% of eligible medical expenses for the remainder of the plan year. However, prescription copays and potential out-of-network charges can still apply.

This is why it’s important to understand the difference between your deductible and your total possible healthcare expenses.

Solo Health advertises its deductible as the out-of-pocket maximum for eligible medical care.

This structure can make medical expenses easier to plan for.

However, the advertised maximum may not cover every expense you could incur.

Prescription copays, excluded services, and charges above the plan’s out-of-network reimbursement amount may result in additional costs.

When comparing plans, consider both your monthly payments and what you could reasonably afford if you had a major medical event.

A Health Savings Account (HSA) can be a valuable tool for managing healthcare expenses. HSAs can offer tax advantages when you contribute, invest, and use the funds for qualified medical expenses.

According to Solo Health’s published FAQ, the V2500 and V5000 plan designs are HSA-eligible, while the V10000 plan is not currently listed as HSA-eligible. However, HSA eligibility depends on federal tax requirements, not simply a plan’s marketing designation.

Before contributing, confirm that your specific coverage qualifies as an HSA-compatible high-deductible health plan under current IRS rules. This matters because deductible amounts alone don’t establish HSA eligibility.

Solo Health uses reference-based pricing for out-of-network services. This means the plan may reimburse a benchmarked amount rather than the provider’s full billed charge.

Solo Health offers a different structure that may appeal to qualifying business owners. However, these products are not interchangeable. For someone with ongoing medical needs, ACA-compliant coverage may provide greater predictability around eligibility and consumer protections.

For an eligible entrepreneur, Solo Health may be worth comparing based on its benefits, provider access, and total annual costs.

Solo Health offers an interesting alternative for self-employed individuals seeking major medical coverage outside the traditional individual insurance market. Its biggest advantages include multiple deductible options, access to a nationwide PPO network, preventive care benefits, and coverage for significant medical expenses.

For qualifying entrepreneurs, the business-based structure may also provide financial and tax-planning opportunities. However, there are important trade-offs. Applicants must meet health eligibility requirements, higher deductibles can create substantial out-of-pocket costs, and out-of-network billing can become complicated.

I recommend comparing Solo Health with at least two other coverage options before deciding. Look beyond the monthly payment and calculate what you might spend over an entire year, including deductibles, prescription costs, and potential out-of-network expenses.

The goal isn’t simply to find the cheapest health plan. It’s to find coverage that protects your health and your finances when you need it most. Eligible self-employed business owners may want to explore Solo Health.
