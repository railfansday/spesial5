Price your next vacation, divide the cost by the months until you leave, and set up an automatic transfer for that amount into a high-yield savings account used only for the trip. Send windfalls like a tax refund or a work bonus there too, to reach the full cost sooner.

That way you come home owing nothing. A trip charged to a credit card can leave a balance you’re still paying off, with interest, months later.

The monthly amount should come from the trip’s price and date, not from whatever is left at the end of the month. Moved automatically into an account that holds nothing else, that money becomes a sinking fund, savings set aside for one cost you know is coming. You can’t spend it by mistake, and the balance shows how close you are.

Price the trip line by line, adding up flights, lodging, food, and activities, then divide the total by the months until you leave. Leaving interest out, a $3,600 trip a year away takes $300 a month.

Open a high-yield savings account just for the trip. Some banks also let you split one savings account into named buckets, and a bucket named for the trip works the same way. Schedule the transfer for the day after payday, so the money moves before you can spend it. If you’d rather earn airline miles than cash interest, a Bask Mileage Savings Account pays American Airlines AAdvantage miles on your balance.

Then speed it up. Send tax refunds and work bonuses straight to the trip account, move your credit card cash back there as it posts, and add what you make selling things you don’t use or from a short-term side gig. With a $700 tax refund added a few months in, the account holds $3,700 after 10 transfers instead of 12, enough to cover the $3,600 trip.

Open the account and schedule the first transfer this week. Every month you wait leaves fewer transfers to cover the same price, so each one has to be bigger.
