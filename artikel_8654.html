Before you pay for a savings app, automate your savings for free. Schedule a recurring transfer from checking to savings for the day after each payday, or split your direct deposit. An app is worth its monthly fee only if it gets you to save more than a free transfer would.

A payday transfer moves your savings out of checking before you have a chance to spend them. That’s why automating your finances works better than saving whatever is left at the end of the month.

Set up the transfer in your bank’s app or online banking to repeat on your pay schedule. Time it for the day after payday, since a transfer that runs before your paycheck arrives can overdraw checking and cost you an overdraft fee. Most banks charge nothing to move money between your own accounts, but check your account terms once to be sure.

If your employer offers it, split your direct deposit so a set amount of each paycheck goes straight to savings. Send either one to a high-yield savings account.

Paid apps add three features a plain transfer lacks. Round-ups save the difference between each card purchase and the next whole dollar. Rules move money when something you choose happens, such as $5 every time you buy coffee. Algorithms track your income and spending and move small amounts to savings whenever they calculate you can afford the transfer. Qapital, which offers round-ups and rules, charges $3 to $12 a month.

Say you’re paid every two weeks and make 60 card purchases a month, each rounding up by about 50 cents on average. A $100 transfer each payday saves $2,600 a year and costs nothing. Round-ups would add about $30 a month, and a $3 to $12 monthly fee takes 10% to 40% of that. Raising your payday transfer by $15 instead would save $32.50 a month, more than the round-ups’ $30, with no fee. So an app is worth its price only if you wouldn’t raise the transfer on your own.

Any app you choose should keep your money at an FDIC-insured bank.

Open your bank’s app and schedule the first transfer for the day after your next payday.
