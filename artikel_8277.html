Scarpa Golden Gate LT Review: Moderate Stack Is So Back

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Trail shoes with a moderate stack are becoming harder to find. New trail models either have inches of soft, bouncy foam underfoot (sometimes with a carbon plate), or they are minimalist models that let your feet feel every pebble. Fortunately, a few that balance protection and ground feel still exist. I’ve just discovered one: the Scarpa Golden Gate LT.

Scarpa is an Italian mountain brand based in the Dolomites. Specializing in mountain climbing footwear, elite mountaineers historically wore the brand’s lightweight mountain shoes to cross rugged terrain. Scarpa’s trail running shoes reflect that heritage; they’re known for their rugged protection and firm, surefooted ride. But many trail runners who don’t live in places like the Alps or the White Mountains don’t need an indestructible mountain running boot. Enter Scarpa’s new trail shoe line-up.

The Golden Gate LT is Scarpa’s lightweight “door-to-trail” shoe meant for a broader trail running audience who want a single functional trainer on runs that bridge varied terrain, from asphalt to gravel to rocky singletrack. I put the shoe’s prowess to the test across Los Angeles’s urban streets, steep horse trails, and sandy beaches.

Weight: 10.5oz (men’s 9, women’s 10) Stack Height: 30 – 24 mm Heel-to-Toe Drop: 6 mm

+ Handles road-to-trail transitions smoothly + Stable base + Excellent traction – Fit too narrow for wide feet – Slightly sluggish on quick turnover

The Golden Gate LT may be a departure from Scarpa’s roots in many ways, but not in style: I received a hot orange-white colorway that immediately reminded me of the Orange Crush soda can and blends right in with Scarpa’s line up of mountain running shoes and ski boots. Though the flashy look isn’t entirely my style, I’m just glad it’s not a monster truck of a trail shoe.

Most of my test runs were on the hilly, paved roads of my neighborhood. Cruising through the familiar streets, I was happy to find that while cushioned, my landings weren’t squishy at all. Instead, I noticed a small bounciness in the midsole — like I was running on a shallow trampoline. That’s likely because Scarpa blended 20% PEBAX into the EVA compound midsole to provide extra responsiveness for what I call the “road” side of the hybrid shoe’s performance.

Taking the shoes uphill, the rebound was still there but didn’t do the heavy lifting for me. That changed, however, when I picked up the pace. With some downhill momentum, my stride lengthened, and any feeling of a firm trail shoe was completely gone. At quicker paces, the bounce takes over and helps maintain speed, feeling like an uptempo road shoe. Normally I don’t like shoes with much cushioning, but the upper’s gentle TPU-reinforced support wrapped my feet so nicely that I felt confident running faster and farther.

Later, I took them out for a long run mostly on trails, connected by some roads, to see how they held up on different surfaces. On the trails, the midsole bounce was less noticeable, which I appreciated, as bouncing over unstable surfaces doesn’t make sense to me.

Compared to other trail models I’ve worn, the lugs are far more flexible, providing amazing traction across sand, dirt, and rocks. The flexibility of the outsole kept my stride smooth and stable on uneven trails and helped the shoe maintain its beginning-of-the-run feeling late into a 14-mile long run. Overall, the outsole’s efficiency felt more alive on the trails than on paved surfaces.

If you’re crazy, like me, and sprint down steep trails, you’ll appreciate the moderate, 24 mm stack under the forefoot, making toe-offs effortless, helping short, quick strides remain stable, and ensuring that tight cornering is no problem. The 30 mm heel stack, however, which contributed to the smooth cushioning on the road, could sometimes feel a bit sluggish on steep trail descents. I prefer 4 mm-drop shoes to keep my steps quick and agile and maximize the thrill of heading downhill with my hair on fire. But if you’re more sensible and take it easy on descents, then the 6 mm drop shouldn’t be a problem at all.

Back on the roads toward the end of my long run, I felt the same road-shoe ride I get on my neighborhood runs. Though my legs were more tired after battling the dusty, hilly terrain, the flexibility of the upper and the outsole encouraged me to keep my pace until the end of the run.

Durability seems mixed so far: After testing the shoe for a month of road and trail runs, the outsole has progressively felt more flexible under my feet and can take on rocks and tough objects on the trails. The edges of the lugs have retained their sharp definition from when it was new, while the upper and overall cushioning still feel just as comfortable. The midsole bounce, however, is much gentler than when it was new (for better or worse), and the bright Orange Crush splash has since faded.

Quality moderate-stack trail shoes are becoming harder to find. If your favorite model lost its way in the “space-age arms race of trail super shoes,” as our editor Cade Michael aptly put it, and you don’t like feeling every rock beneath your feet through a thin minimalist sole, this one’s for you.

Runners with narrow or medium-narrow feet who prefer a tight fit should appreciate this shoe. The heel and midfoot’s gentle padding hug my narrow feet quite tightly, and the toe box has some room for toe splay, though it’s not wide — it’s right in the middle. This close-fitting shape is useful for tight cornering and platform stability, but if you have wide feet and need breathing space for your toes, the M2C H1, an Altra, or a Topo trail shoe might be a better fit.

So, is this the lower-stacked hybrid shoe that we’ve been waiting for? With its dual personality, the Golden Gate LT certainly handles roads and trails like it’s built for both. You can also totally wear this exclusively as a trail shoe for runs of any length. Though difficult trails with demanding terrain may not be its greatest strength, this shoe provides a comfortable, energetic ride on all other terrain, achieving exactly what it’s designed for.
