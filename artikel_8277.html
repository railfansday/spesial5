The Best Holiday Gifts for Every Type of Climber in Your Life

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The holiday season is meant to be a time to relax, be with friends and family, and, ideally, get some climbing in. Don’t let the obligation of holiday shopping take up too much of your time—the editors at Climbing have determined what we believe are the best gifts for the climber in your life, whether a gym, sport, trad, or ice climber, a burgeoning mountaineer, an all-arounder, or someone who hasn’t tried climbing yet, but is eager to get started.

Whether you’re crimping sharp Smith Rock incuts, jamming perfect Indian Creek splitters, or bearing down on your gym’s fresh new set, you are, at some point, going to tear up your skin. Fear not! Rhino Skin Solutions makes a purpose-built skin balm to restore your digits as efficiently as possible. Split+ is a roll-on cream that comes in two sizes (5oz and 3.4oz) and uses beeswax, vitamin E, arnica, dragon’s blood, and tea tree oil, offering a blend of occlusive (sealing) and antibacterial properties.

Ocun’s new Jett S climbing shoes are as comfortable as they are affordable, and best suited for the gym. They excel at precision placements and perform well on steep terrain, hooking, and scumming, on everything from 5.13 tech to the Tension Board 2 at 40° overhanging.

Last year, Petzl updated the Connect Adjust to make a good item great: they slimmed down the dynamic tether from 9.5mm to 9.0mm, making it noticeable lighter and easier to extend/contract. The belay-loop attachment point is now a sewn Dyneema webbing, rather than the thick rope itself, and as a result feels lower profile on the harness. The Connect Adjust also now features a pinhole to attach accessory cord to; if weighting the Connect, clip a carabiner into the accessory-cord loop and it will easily disengage.

Edelrid’s Ohmega brake assistant is a crucial belay tool for partnerships where the climber weighs significantly more than the belayer. By adding friction, the device makes the belayer “heavier,” reducing the size of whips and keeping the belayer on the ground during a fall. We recommend any climbing duo with a notable weight difference to invest in one

The Friend Camming Device is one of the most legendary and trusted pieces of climbing equipment. Newly updated for 2026, the Friends are a tiny bit lighter than the previous model, but, most importantly, they come with a new, seamless “Magic Cord” sling. The bartack-free sling enables smooth extension so you can enjoy your leads without rope drag.

The Free Wall is the ultimate quiver-of-one backpack for rock climbers, made from a super-tough recycled nylon that our testers hauled for thousands of feet this summer. Unlike a traditional big-wall haulbag, with its simple, monolithic compartment, the Free Wall Pack is designed with on-the-wall access in mind: two internal pockets, two gear loops, and a large external pocket on top of the lid.

Apparently Scarpa’s Veloce is designed for beginner and intermediate climbers who spend all of their time at the gym—but our experienced field tester didn’t get the memo. After a quick break-in period, they became his go-to shoes inside and out, where he clipped the chains on the two hardest sport routes he’s sent in the last decade.

Black Diamond’s Magnetron has the smoothest operation of any triple-action carabiner we’ve ever encountered; a true one-handed operation that provides immediate security upon letting go of the gate. Its pear shape is ideal for belaying and rappelling, and it has a surprisingly narrow nose, making it easy to clip into chain anchors and busy masterpoints.

These insulated rubber gloves were originally designed for fishers, but nowadays you’re equally likely to see them on ice climbers, especially on steep water ice in the Pacific Northwest. The Temres 282-02 is the only fully waterproof glove we’ve ever worn, and they climb decently well thanks to a supple, dexterous rubber, and a wool liner that doesn’t migrate much within.

Climbing tape isn’t just for hardcore climbers nursing a busted pulley or gobie—it’s a needed part of any decent first aid kit, repair kit, and can help you fashion a makeshift stickclip in a pinch. (BYOStick). Tension’s climbing tape is some of the stickiest we’ve used, performing well in both sweaty and freezing environments. The Tri-Pack includes a one-inch roll ideal for larger body parts like wrists and ankles and for making tape gloves should you forget yours at home, the half-inch roll works well for large blisters and flappers, and the 0.3-inch roll is preordained to fit individual digits.

What gift could be better than continued access to America’s best rock climbing? Gift your climbing partner a membership to Access Fund, the largest climbing advocacy movement in the country, which tirelessly protects and conserves historic areas—and opens new ones. They’ll even get a free t-shirt.

The daughter of Climbing’s editor in chief, Maya Silver, has worn the Black Diamond Momentum kids’ climbing shoe for the past four years. Once she finally outgrew it this last summer, the decision was easy to order her another pair. Like the adult Momentum shoe, the Momentum kids is super comfortable thanks to its neutral last, easy to get on/off with a simple Velcro closure, and offers sufficient performance for most little crushers.
