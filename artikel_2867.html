Week 6 Fantasy Basketball Week Ahead: Nuggets pick up two big wins; Dylan Harper and Jaylen Brown injured

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

With Week 6 on the horizon, fantasy managers have another day off to account for, as the entire NBA takes Thanksgiving Day off. While the holiday hasn't wreaked havoc on many team schedules, a few may not be the best options as managers craft their rosters for the week. Week 5 delivered a few more historic performances (thank you, Victor Wembanyama and James Harden), while Chicago, Philadelphia and San Antonio lost starters, and the Warriors were hit hard by Stephen Curry's absence.

- Nuggets pick up big wins over Rockets, Celtics: Denver's surprising November 16 loss to the Suns dropped their record to 7-5, which certainly isn't bad, but the result was one that few expected. David Adelman's team rebounded nicely, picking up home wins over the Celtics and Rockets ahead of their Week 5 finale in Chicago on Sunday. While Nikola Jokić and Jamal Murray led the way offensively as expected, Aaron Gordon and DeMar DeRozan's improved play stood out. In those victories, Gordon totaled 31 points (11-of-19 FGs, 8-of-11 FTs), 11 rebounds and four assists. While the three-point shooting remains an issue, Gordon's overall efficiency and production improved, a welcome development.

As for DeRozan, he's struggled some to fit in with his new team. However, he came up big against the Celtics, including a run early in the fourth quarter in which he got the Nuggets into the penalty by himself. Denver outscored Boston 15-2 at the foul line in the fourth, which was much needed, as the team was a plus-4 (25-21) for the quarter in a game they would win by four points. Against Boston and Houston, DeRozan totaled 33 points (9-of-22 FGs, 14-of-15 FTs), four rebounds and four assists. Neither Gordon nor DeRozan posted elite numbers in the wins over Houston and Boston. However, both showed signs of turning things around after an uneven start to the season.

- Spurs lose Dylan Harper for 1-3 weeks: Not only did the Spurs' 12-game winning streak end on November 17 against the Pistons, but they also lost a key player. Harper was injured late in the fourth quarter when he slipped while attempting to catch a pass, suffering a hip strain that's expected to sideline him anywhere from one to three weeks.

As has been the case when the Spurs were down another perimeter player this season, Julian Champagnie has returned to the starting lineup. In wins over the Clippers and Pelicans, he totaled 29 points (11-of-24 FGs), seven rebounds, four assists, one block and seven three-pointers. Champagnie stands to be most valuable to fantasy managers needing three-point production.

Also of note from those two games without Harper was Stephon Castle's play. While he committed eight turnovers against the Clippers, the third-year guard totaled 40 points (14-of-24 FGs, 5-of-5 FTs), nine rebounds, 18 assists, four steals, seven three-pointers and 10 turnovers. Although his field-goal percentage has decreased a few points (43.7 compared to 47.1 in 2025-26), Castle has kept the turnovers in check this season, with his average down to 2.4 per game (from 3.2 tpg last season)

- 76ers lose Jaylen Brown (hamstring): After playing 33 minutes in the 76ers' November 16 win over the Nets, Brown was diagnosed with a strained left hamstring that sidelined him for Philadelphia's final two games of Week 5. He was supposed to be re-evaluated ahead of Saturday's game against the Bucks, but the team released no news. Brown, who did some individual work at Friday's practice, is day-to-day until the 76ers provide a firm timeline. Kentavious Caldwell-Pope started the two games Brown missed, recording totals of 19 points (7-of-11 FGs), nine rebounds, three assists, two steals and five three-pointers. The veteran guard is a decent option for deep-league managers needing three-point production, with steals being a bonus.

Also, VJ Edgecombe played 43 and 38 minutes in the two games Philadelphia played without Brown to end Week 5, recording totals of 40 points (13-of-27 FGs, 9-of-9 FTs), 11 rebounds, 12 assists, four steals, one block and five three-pointers. While his shot attempts have increased slightly in Brown's absence, Edgecombe's increased playing time stands out and likely raises the second-year guard's fantasy ceiling in the short term.

- Raptors' injury woes continue as Barrett, CMB go down: Already playing without Kawhi Leonard, whose knee injury has kept him out since October 23, Toronto lost RJ Barrett (hamstring) and Collin Murray-Boyles (concussion protocol) during Week 5. Barrett will be re-evaluated in two weeks, while Murray-Boyles' timeline depends on how soon he's cleared to resume full activity. Those injuries have freed up opportunities for multiple players, including Jamison Battle and first-round pick Allen Graves.

In games against the Wizards and Pistons, Battle totaled 29 points (11-of-21 FGs), 10 rebounds, one steal and seven three-pointers. While he started against Washington and played 33 minutes, Battle was used in a reserve role in the November 20 win over Detroit, playing 20 minutes. As for Graves, he had a career night against Detroit, finishing with 20 points (6-of-9 FGs, 6-of-6 FTs), 10 rebounds, one assist, three steals, one block and two three-pointers. While he came off the bench for his lone season at Santa Clara, Graves' advanced numbers were a big reason he was selected in the first round. Like Murray-Boyles, he has the tools needed to be a reliable category-league option for fantasy managers.

- Anthony Edwards (knee) reveals that he has no return timeline: Sidelined since the final week of October after undergoing an arthroscopic procedure on his right knee, Edwards met with the media ahead of the Timberwolves' November 19 loss to the Knicks. The Timberwolves star said he doesn't have a firm return timeline, so it could go longer than the initially reported two-month timeline. Ayo Dosunmu has been the replacement in the starting lineup, and he's performed well, averaging 19.7 points, 4.5 rebounds, 3.2 assists, 1.6 steals and 2.4 three-pointers in 13 starts.

Jaden McDaniels is enjoying the best individual season of his NBA career, while LaMelo Ball and Jonathan Kuminga have fit in well with his new team. Since losing Edwards, the Timberwolves have gone 9-4 and looked like a team more than capable of holding down the fort until their star returns. The one concern for Minnesota may be Rudy Gobert, who has continued to struggle offensively. His scoring average of 7.6 points per game is the lowest since his rookie season (2013-14).

- Austin Reaves (heel) should return...but when?: Reaves has missed the Lakers' last four games with a left heel contusion, but he could be back "soon." Ahead of Friday's win over the Kings, Lakers head coach JJ Redick said that he hoped to get Reaves back within the next two games. If Reaves can't play on Monday against the Jazz, the next possible return day would be the day after Thanksgiving against the Spurs in San Antonio. Quentin Grimes has performed well as the replacement in the starting lineup, hitting 10 three-pointers and dropping 47 points in an overtime win over the Hornets, but the Lakers also need Reaves back to ease some of the pressure on Luka Dončić as a playmaker.

- Stephen Curry (oblique) to be re-evaluated: Injured on November 11, Curry was given a re-evaluation timeline of two weeks, which falls in the middle of Week 6. Obviously, he isn't guaranteed to return to the court immediately, but the Warriors desperately need their star guard back. Not only has Golden State lost all four games that Curry's missed, but they also haven't been competitive. During this five-game losing streak, which began with the game against the Rockets in which Curry was injured, the Warriors have led for a total of 46 seconds and have four wire-to-wire defeats.

While Yaxel Lendeborg has done what he can in Curry's absence, making him the primary scoring option puts him in an incredibly difficult spot. Lendeborg's efficiency has suffered, while Brandin Podziemski has scored 15 points or less in each of the Warriors' last four games.

- Evan Mobley is expected to be re-evaluated: Mobley has been out since the Cavaliers' November 11 win over the Knicks with a toe injury, and on November 16 it was reported that he would be re-evaluated in 7-10 days. Given the timeline, he should be re-evaluated early in Week 6, with Cleveland playing three games next week. They'll play road games against the Bulls (Monday) and Knicks (Wednesday) before hosting the Heat on Sunday. Tyrese Proctor initially moved into the starting lineup after Peyton Watson was sidelined for the November 14 win over the Mavericks, and he's remained in that role since Watson returned.

In four starts, Proctor averaged 13.5 points, 4.0 rebounds, 3.8 assists and 2.3 three-pointers per game, shooting 47.7 percent from the field and 60 percent from the foul line. It should be noted that the first game of that stretch, the win over Dallas, was played without Cleveland's entire starting five. Proctor's production has decreased game by game, but Monday's matchup with the Bulls could be a good one for him.

- Norman Powell (hamstring) to be re-evaluated during Week 6: Powell has been diagnosed with a "mild" strain of his right hamstring and is expected to be re-evaluated during Week 6. The Bulls open their Week 6 at home against the Cavaliers on Monday, so that game can likely be ruled out. Chicago finishes the week with games against the Hornets (Wednesday) and the Pacers (Saturday), so fantasy managers may not get anything out of Powell.

With Isaac Okoro already in the starting lineup due to Caleb Wilson's broken hand, Buddy Hield could be the player who steps in for Powell. The veteran guard has scored in double figures in four of his last five games, and he's made at least two three-pointers in five straight games. Hield doesn't qualify as a must-add player, but he's worth watching in the Bulls' Week 5 finale against the Nuggets on November 22.

- Atlanta, Dallas and Washington have two-game weeks: The Hawks, Mavericks and Wizards play two games in Week 6. However, while Dallas and Washington will each play a game before Thanksgiving, both of Atlanta's games will be played after the holiday. The Hawks have been without Kingston Flemings (back) and CJ McCollum (calf) recently, with the latter not expected to be re-evaluated for another three weeks. Aaron Wiggins has moved into the starting lineup, while the playmaking responsibilities have been shared among Nickeil Alexander-Walker, Dyson Daniels and Jalen Johnson. Hopefully, the time off is enough for Flemings to return if he can't play in Atlanta's Week 5 finale against the Pistons on November 22.
