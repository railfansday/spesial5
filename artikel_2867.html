The Largest IPO in Stock Market History Is Here. What It Means for Your Portfolio

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

You may be investing in the company soon, like it or not.

By Stefon Walters – Nov 25, 2026 at 11:43AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Anthropic ›

In June, Space Exploration Technologies (SpaceX) set the record for the largest initial public offering (IPO) in stock market history, raising $75 billion. Five months later, on Nov. 19, artificial intelligence lab Anthropic (ANT -1.74%) beat SpaceX's record, raising $81.3 billion at a $1.5 trillion valuation.

There was a lot of hype leading up to Anthropic's IPO, especially as it beat ChatGPT's creator, OpenAI, to the punch and gave us our first look under the hood of a stand-alone AI lab. With shares now trading, here's what it could mean for your portfolio.

Anthropic -- founded just five years ago by former OpenAI employees -- became an AI powerhouse because of its popular Claude models. At first, Claude was like the typical chatbot. Then came Claude Code, which took its business to a whole new level. While OpenAI focused more on consumers, Anthropic focused more on enterprise customers.

The company doesn't technically report earnings by enterprise and consumer segments, but it noted around 250 customers spent at least $10 million in the past 12 months, 1,900 spent at least $1 million, and more than 11,900 spent $100,000. Those numbers show it isn't your everyday person spending money; it's businesses.

Like many AI giants right now, Anthropic is a tale of two extremes. On one hand, its revenue growth is as impressive as you could want, going from $1.38 billion in last year's third quarter (Q3) to $16.96 billion this year. On the other hand, its spending is massive and keeps growing by the quarter. In Q3, its operating expenses totaled $17.52 billion, up nearly 292% from last year.

ANT Operating Income (Quarterly) data by YCharts

Much of the spending is on compute power and other infrastructure, so it's a means to an end, but it will be interesting to see how forgiving investors are of its continued losses. And with $900 billion in spending commitments ahead, there's no telling when that may be.

A common question about blockbuster IPOs is whether the stock will be included in popular index funds such as those tracking the S&P 500 or Nasdaq Composite. The short answer is yes, but likely not in the way many people think.

Most major indexes are weighted by market capitalization, so larger companies account for more of the index than smaller ones. With a $1.19 trillion market value, Anthropic is the world's 14th-largest public company (as of Nov. 23), meaning it would normally carry a lot of weight in an index. However, most major indexes go by a company's float (shares available to trade on the open market).

Right now, Anthropic's float is small, so its initial weight will be small. That said, you'll automatically invest in Anthropic if you invest in common ETFs like an S&P 500 ETF, a Nasdaq-100 ETF, or a total market ETF.

To be included in the S&P 500, Anthropic will need to trade for at least 12 full months, have a public float of at least 10%, and be profitable (by GAAP standards) for the four quarters leading up to its inclusion.

The Nasdaq-100 updated its rules this year (thanks to SpaceX's IPO) to allow mega IPOs to be "fast-tracked" into the index. You'll likely see Anthropic in these funds in mid-to-late December. Morningstar also updated its fast-track rules, so Anthropic should soon be eligible to enter the popular Vanguard Morningstar Total Stock Market ETF once it has at least a 10% float.

Anthropic is a good company, no doubt. Unfortunately, good companies don't always make good investments if they're bought at the wrong time and the wrong price.

In Anthropic's case, timing isn't on its side, with overall sentiment toward AI stocks down and bubble fears rising. The market hasn't been kind to the major hyperscalers that bring in billions in profits, so it's hard to see Anthropic getting a pass early in its trading days.

If you're interested in investing in Anthropic, I would wait it out a bit and let the dust settle. With more earnings under its belt, investors will be able to see its revenue and spending trajectory and make a more informed investing decision.

There's no need to rush and invest right now and expose yourself to inevitable high volatility that typically doesn't work in mega IPOs' favor in the short time after going public.
