Bitcoin's regulation bull case is played out. What could drive crypto gains moving forward

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Crypto investors are hunting for the next positive catalyst after U.S. crypto regulation — a longtime market driver — fizzled in 2026.

Bitcoin traded around $61,500 on Friday, a more than 50% decline from its all-time high above $126,000 reached in October 2025. The broader digital asset market has struggled after the Clarity Act, a crypto market structure bill, failed to advance in the Senate in September.

Investors now see Wall Street's embrace of blockchain technology — particularly tokenization — as the next trend to give bitcoin a boost.

"Every single traditional firm is now looking at digital assets," BitGo CEO Mike Belshe told CNBC last week in an exclusive interview from the fintech-focused Cantor Convergence conference in Miami Beach. That includes BlackRock, the world's largest asset manager.

He pointed to statements from the asset management giant's CEO, Larry Fink, who said the next major evolution in global finance is the creation of digital representations of every stock, bond, fund and real-world asset onto a shared blockchain ledger.

"I think the biggest catalyst we're seeing right now is actually very much in motion and it's happening, which is capital markets are moving onchain," eToro CEO Yoni Assia told CNBC in an exclusive interview from the conference. He pointed to the SpaceX IPO as an example, which had derivatives trading on eToro before the IPO.

"First day of trading, we distributed it to our retail clients, and it started trading 24/7, and that's all because capital markets are now copying crypto markets and are sitting on the same rails," Assia said. "Crypto markets have always been 24/7, they never sleep, and now what we're seeing is that capital markets are copying crypto markets using the same technology so stocks are moving onchain, they're being tokenized."

Assia said that supportive regulation in the U.S. from agencies, including the Securities and Exchange Commission and the Commodity Futures Trading Commission, is also helping adoption of crypto.

He pointed to the CFTC earlier this month clearing the way for crypto-style perpetual futures tied to individual stocks to trade on regulated U.S. exchanges for the first time. Assia also highlighted the SEC's move to issue an order in September, creating a regulatory pathway for certain trading venues to trade tokenized representations of publicly traded U.S. stocks.

"Blockchain technology is now starting to power what's happening in capital markets," Assia told CNBC. "That is the one of the biggest changes we're going to see in capital markets for many, many years."

"We're talking about hundreds of trillions of dollars that are going to be tokenized and move onchain and at the same time, I do believe that will support prices of bitcoin and other blockchains as well," he continued.

The industry is also watching whether inflation will ease and geopolitical tensions will subside in the new year, which could lower broader macro risk currently hampering the industry.

Last month, Strategy's Michael Saylor said he thinks bitcoin's next bull market is already underway.

ARK Invest Chief Investment Officer Cathie Wood told CNBC the investment management firm's $1.5 million bull case price target for bitcoin by 2030 still stands. She also said that it wouldn't surprise her if bitcoin reached its all-time high again before the end of 2027.

"Our macro forecast has inflation breaking down considerably," Wood told CNBC in an exclusive interview at Cantor Convergence. "I think the elections have increased our confidence there because the war [in Iran], one way or the other, is going to end."

She added that "bitcoin has weathered so much bad news," including the $6.3 billion Binance hack and the "distraction" of AI which, "many people would say is sucking the oxygen away from bitcoin."

The maturation of the artificial intelligence trade could provide an opportunity for bitcoin to find new inflows.

Wood said that ARK Invest believes bitcoin has found its low and stressed that cybersecurity is "top of mind right now," which could provide yet another opportunity for crypto markets to rebound.

"I think if the Bitcoin and crypto world coalesces and makes investors feel that they've got their arms around security and actually understand that there might be more security issues in the traditional financial system, I think we're going to see a wave of support," Wood said.
