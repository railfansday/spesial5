Lions, Bucs injury designations: Cade Mays ruled OUT

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The Detroit Lions (7-2) and the Tampa Bay Buccaneers (3-6) have declared their injury designations ahead of their Week 11 matchup at Ford Field.

The Lions held a walkthrough on Friday, their second of the week, and coach Dan Campbell noted that this lighter schedule was designed to allow the players to get some proper rest in their current schedule—after returning home from Germany, the Lions had to quickly adjust back to the Michigan time zone for this Sunday, and then play on short rest next week, as they play again on Thursday (Thanksgiving).

"This is a stretch we're in right now, and you've got to make sure that they're sharp enough; the sword is sharp enough to do what it needs to do, but we can't dull this blade over this three-week period," Campbell said on Friday. "And so yeah, we've kind of pulled back a little bit and the mental has gone way up and that's where the focus has got to be, making sure we're locked in on our responsibility, our job. It's important."

While there are no real surprises for those following this week's injury reports, Let's take a look at what we know.

Note: Any changes from the most recent injury report will be bolded.

Mays will miss his third game in a row, and with the Lions' next game on Thursday, they will likely only hold walk-through practices, so he won't have a chance to fully test his ankle before the Bears come to town. With that in mind, it wouldn't be surprising to see him miss the Thanksgiving game as well, and possibly target a return in Week 13 at Atlanta, if he is healthy enough.

With Mays sidelined, the Lions will continue to roll with Juice Scruggs at center. Scruggs has been terrific this season, though he'll have his hands full this week lining up across from Bucs' NT Vita Vea.

CB D.J. Reed (hip) — won't rush back, unlikely to play vs. Bucs

Vaki has played through his ankle injury the last two games and is expected to be available again this Sunday, barring any setbacks between this report and game time.

Lacy is expected to be a big part of the Lions game plan this week, given his prowess for stopping the run. Fortunately, the Lions plan to play Lacy's backup, Ahmed Hassanein, this week, so they'll have support if Lacy needs to take some reps off.

Bachie's status isn't totally clear right now, but limited practices all week are a good sign he could contribute on special teams.

Reed said earlier in the week that he won't rush back from this injury like he did last season because he doesn't want to be a "hindrance" to his teammates, who are playing at a high level right now. He plans to take his time, listen to trainers, and return when everything lines up. That could mean he plays this week, but Thanksgiving seems more likely.

Dorsey required attention from the training staff last Sunday, but he's been able to practice in limited fashion this week and looks like he'll have a chance to play against the Bucs.

Rakestraw has gotten in limited practices this week, so it appears he'll also have a chance to play on Sunday.

Joseph is set to play his first game since Week 5 of the 2025 season, and the All-Pro safety insists that he's ready, won't need to knock off any rust, and is still the same player he's always been. Campbell echoed that sentiment at his Friday press conference, noting that he's shown his previous range level in practices, and he's encouraged Kerby to just let the game come to him and not try to do too much.

"I know he's very eager, as you can imagine," Campbell explained. "It's been a long time. He's put in a ton of work to get back to this point; it's just play within the system. Don't try to do too much. Let the game come to you. Just play the safety position, play the call, trust the guys next to you. And don't try to go outside of that right now because I know it's been a long time. So that'll be it. And every rep that he gets, he'll settle in; he'll be just fine."
