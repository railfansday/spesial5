Here's What a $1,000 Investment in Kraken Robotics Will Be Worth by the End of 2027 if Undersea Drone Orders Keep Doubling

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Down 70% from its high, would a doubling of drone revenue in 2027 be enough to drive a big rebound for Kraken Robotics stock?

By Keith Noonan – Nov 18, 2026 at 7:15PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Kraken Robotics ›

Following strong gains in 2025 and an impressive run-up through the first three months of this year, the tide has turned for Kraken Robotics (KRKNF -3.67%) stock. The company's share price has been hit with strong bearish pressure, and it now trades down 40% over the last year and 70% from the lifetime high that it reached in March.

The company specializes in subsea and maritime technologies, and the batteries and sensor systems it sells for underwater drones are central to its stock story -- but the market appears to have become far less optimistic about how much these products can drive growth. With that in mind, what would it mean for $1,000 invested in Kraken stock at today's prices if undersea drone orders double in 2027?

Before diving more specifically into what a doubling of Kraken's underwater drone component sales in 2027 would mean for the company's stock, it's worth detailing some of the factors that have caused the company's share price to pull back so sharply.

There are several catalysts at play here, and understanding the dynamics will help model the business's growth opportunities in the category in relation to moves for its stock.

In addition to these catalysts, Kraken continues to rely heavily on stock-based compensation to fund its operations. This allows the company to operate without burning through cash, but the creation of new stock dilutes shareholders' positions.

Kraken's subsea drone business focuses on selling batteries and sensors to companies rather than building completed drones itself. In addition to this business, the company also sells towed synthetic aperture sonar (SAS) systems, other subsea sensors, and software. Its business is also significantly more diversified following the acquisition of Covelya. Still, the subsea drone market appears to be Kraken's most important near-term growth driver.

So, what would a doubling of the company's subsea drone revenue in 2027 mean for its valuation? It might not be enough to spur gains in the stock because many investors are banking on even higher growth.

While Kraken is attracting interest from other players in the drone space, Anduril is currently its most important customer by a wide margin. Anduril is the next-gen defense tech company responsible for the Dive-LD and Dive-XL subsea drones and the Ghost Shark drones built with Australia on the Dive-XL platform.

Anduril is working to ramp up production of these drones in 2027, and that could cause Kraken's drone business to more than double if it retains its component wins. For example, Anduril expects that production of Dive-LD drones will increase from roughly 50 this year to its full production rate of roughly 200 by the end of next year. Production for the Dive-XL drones, which are larger and use more batteries, should also see big scaling next year.

So what would a doubling of the company's drone market mean for a $1,000 investment in Kraken stock? Assuming other important dynamics that affect its share price remain relatively constant, I think a doubling of drone-related revenue could mean the stock trades in roughly the same range as now. There may be moderate upside, depending on how the market reacts to the lifting of uncertainty. Big sell-offs this year already seem to bake in softer growth, and a doubling of drone revenue next year would seemingly reflect a ramp that's slower than previously anticipated.
