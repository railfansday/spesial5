Johnson & Johnson vs. Pfizer: Which Healthcare Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Johnson & Johnson's drug-and-device product mix faces off against Pfizer's high-stakes oncology and obesity bets, with a valuation gap that may sway your choice.

By Erin Kennedy – Updated Nov 22, 2026 at 8:05AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Johnson & Johnson ›

Investors seeking long-term stability often turn to the healthcare sector, yet choosing between two industry titans requires a close look at their diverging growth strategies. Is Johnson & Johnson (JNJ +1.25%) or Pfizer (PFE -0.51%) the better buy for 2027?

While both companies are foundational pieces in many retirement portfolios, they are moving in different directions. Johnson & Johnson relies on its dual-engine model of drug development and medical devices, while Pfizer is aggressively pivoting toward oncology and weight-loss treatments to secure its next era of growth.

Johnson & Johnson operates through two primary segments, innovative medicine and medtech, reaching patients in nearly every corner of the globe. Its medtech division is a significant player among medical device stocks, providing critical solutions for surgery, orthopedics, and vision. J&J distributes its products directly to wholesalers and hospitals, bolstered by partnerships with companies like Genmab (GMAB -1.46%) and AbbVie (ABBV +0.94%).

In the 12 months ended Sept. 27, 2026, revenue reached nearly $99.3 billion. This represented growth of approximately 7.8% compared to the prior 12-month period. During this same time frame, the company reported net income of roughly $16.1 billion.

As of the December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was approximately 0.6. This ratio measures total debt relative to shareholders' equity, indicating the extent to which the business relies on borrowed capital. The current ratio is nearly 1, meaning the company has sufficient short-term assets to cover its immediate liabilities, and free cash flow in the fiscal year ended Dec. 28, 2025, reached close to $19.7 billion.

Pfizer is a biopharmaceutical leader focused on discovering and manufacturing vaccines and medicines for patients in approximately 200 countries. To drive future growth, the company is investing heavily in its oncology and obesity pipelines through major acquisitions such as Seagen. In the weight-loss space, it is developing oral treatments to compete with established names like Eli Lilly (LLY -0.30%).

In the 12 months ended Sept. 27, 2026, revenue reached close to $63.6 billion, an increase of approximately 1.3% over the previous 12-month period. For this time frame, the business generated net income of nearly $4 billion.

As of the December 2025 balance sheet, Pfizer reported a debt-to-equity ratio of roughly 0.8. Its current ratio is approximately 1.2, suggesting a comfortable cushion for meeting short-term obligations, while free cash flow in the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, was close to $9.1 billion.

Johnson & Johnson faces significant litigation risks, most notably regarding a $5.5 billion settlement offer recently proposed to resolve thousands of lawsuits related to its talc products. The business also deals with pricing pressure from government healthcare programs, which impact major revenue drivers like Darzalex and Stelara. Additionally, J&J faces the loss of patent exclusivity for Stelara and operational risks associated with the planned separation of its orthopedics business.

Pfizer contends with substantial legal and regulatory risks, including litigation related to its Depo-Provera contraceptive and recent settlements regarding its smoking cessation drug, Chantix. The company is also navigating revenue impacts from the Inflation Reduction Act, which targets high-expenditure drugs like Eliquis and Ibrance for government-negotiated pricing. Furthermore, it faces competitive erosion from generic versions of its products and the challenge of successfully integrating large acquisitions like Seagen to revitalize its oncology portfolio.

Pfizer looks significantly cheaper than Johnson & Johnson based on its forward P/E and P/S ratio, reflecting the market's cautious outlook on its future earnings estimates.

It feels a bit dicey to be picking stocks amid the market's recent meltdown. That said, long-term investors know it's not especially productive to worry about what happens in a given day, week, or even month. That's why I prefer looking at longer time frames. Both healthcare giants are trailing the S&P 500's (^GSPC -3.70%) return, and Pfizer is actually sitting on a loss. Past performance is no indication of future results, of course, but it's not a good look for Pfizer.

Though I'd prefer to see stronger growth from J&J, I think it's the more attractive stock. It's a mature, established company that has increased its dividend annually for more than 60 years. Pfizer cannot say the same.

"But wait," you say. "Pfizer's dividend yields more." True, but dividend yield and share price have an inverse relationship: as a stock falls, its yield rises. On an absolute basis, J&J's dividend payout is roughly three times larger.

I doubt we'll see especially impressive revenue growth from J&J in the near future, but I think the stock can provide steady ballast for your portfolio. Johnson & Johnson gets my vote for 2027 and beyond.
