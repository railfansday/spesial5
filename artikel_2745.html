Knee surgery complicates Puka Nacua's future

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

On Friday, Rams coach Sean McVay said receiver Puka Nacua will miss “a couple months.” Nacua is reportedly expected to miss a couple months more than that.

Via ESPN, the recovery time following surgery to repair the MCL tear is four to five months. If accurate, that ends Nacua’s 2026 season.

The problem becomes 2027. Nacua is in the last year of his rookie contract. He hasn’t gotten a second deal, due in large part to the off-field issues that recently resulted in a three-game suspension under the Personal Conduct Policy and that culminated in an offseason stay at an in-patient holistic rehab facility after he was “drowning myself” in alcohol.

Based on the ESPN report, Nacua won’t be back to 100 percent before free agency starts. The Rams will have to decide whether to sign him to a new deal, to apply the franchise tag (or the transition tag), or to let him become a free agent.

The injury makes it very difficult for Nacua to land in the Jaxon Smith-Njigba ballpark ($42.15 million per year), even though Nacua’s skills and abilities justify that type of deal.

The situation becomes a prime example of the extent to which off-field issues can complicate a player’s financial interests. Without the New Year’s Eve incident that resulted in a lawsuit and, eventually, a diversion program and, inevitably, a suspension under the Personal Conduct Policy, Nacua would have had his second contract by now. Or, at a minimum, he would have had greater standing to hold out (or hold in) until he got paid what he deserves.

Instead, Nacua continued to carry the injury risk into the final year of his rookie contract. And the injury risk became reality 13 days ago, when McVay dialed up a running play to Nacua on third and 11 while trying to run out the clock in a 20-13 game against the Commanders.

It’s a win for the Rams, who may be able to keep Nacua for a lot less than it would cost to give him a market-level contract. However the Rams play the situation, Nacua’s knee injury will make it easier, and cheaper, to re-sign him.

Ultimately, Nacua may have to prove in 2027 that he is back to full strength. And he may have to get through a full season unscathed to unlock the long-term deal his performances earned — but his off-field complications squandered.

Given the way he plays, it’s hardly a given that Nacua will emerge from the 2027 season with the kind of performance, and health, that will finally get him the contract he should have gotten by now.
