Jaylen Brown explains apparent snub of former teammate Jayson Tatum: 'I wasn't paying attention'

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Two days before playing his former team for the first time since being traded this summer, Philadelphia 76ers star Jaylen Brown told reporters he had "nothing to talk about" with ex-teammate Jayson Tatum.

Apparently he meant it — and it might have had something to do with lamb chops. Figuratively speaking.

Before Tuesday's game at Philadelphia's Xfinity Mobile Arena, Tatum approached Brown and gave him a tap on the arm and a few on the chest. Brown wouldn't even look in the direction of the player with whom he won an NBA championship, made two NBA Finals appearances and played in five Eastern Conference championship series during nine seasons as Celtics teammates.

After the game, a 123-101 Celtics win, Brown implied to reporters that he didn't notice Tatum's attempt to interact with him.

"I'm just focused on coming out and doing what's best for my team," Brown said when asked about the apparent snub. "Everything else was just — to be honest, I wasn't paying attention. But credit to Jayson, he played well tonight. Credit to the Celtics, they executed, they shot the hell out of the ball, and they were the better team tonight."

Tatum also dismissed the pregame non-encounter as no big deal.

"No headlines about anything like that," Tatum told reporters. "I was just getting ready for the game, ready for jump ball, go out there and compete, have some fun and try to get a win."

Still, things have been a bit frosty between the two players in the aftermath of a July 6 trade that sent Brown to Philadelphia — after spending his first 10 NBA seasons in Boston — in exchange for Paul George, two first-round draft picks and two second-round picks.

Celtics teammates Jayson Tatum and Jaylen Brown share a laugh during a 2025 game against the New York Knicks in Boston. (Maddie Meyer / Getty Images)

In October, Bleacher Report posted a clip on Instagram of Tatum saying he was "caught off guard" by the trade. Brown responded in the comment section of that post with a blue hat emoji, which is often used to represent the word "cap" when being used as slang for "lie."

Asked about the situation during an Oct. 23 news conference, Tatum insisted "there's no beef" between the two players.

"I think he's right. There's no beef," Brown told reporters. "Jason comes from a wonderful family. We've accomplished some great things. He's a hell of a basketball player. He's got a great PR team. There's no beef but, you know, it might be a little lamb chops and some gravy, you know what I mean? He should stay ready at all times."

Asked why he would say that, Brown responded: "Just, you know, that's what it is. No beef. It should never ever be that. It's just basketball. We're both blessed, but it might be a little lamb chop. We'll be all right."

Brown was then asked if he had been suggesting that Tatum was being untruthful about the trade situation.

"Hey, look, like I said, it's no beef," Brown said. "The PR and all that type stuff is it is what it is, but I think we're just both focused on what's in front of us."

George was the leading scorer in Tuesday's game, with 36 points, while Tatum added 21 points, six rebounds and two blocked shots. Brown netted 13 points, his lowest output in 11 games with the 76ers.

"I was looking forward to this," Brown said after the game. "I was looking forward to playing well, but you know, s— happens. You just get ready for the next one, I guess."
