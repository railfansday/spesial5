From Brock Purdy's masterclass to WR's hissy fit, what stood out in NFL Week 11 | Monday Morning JB

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Brock Purdy knew they were coming. From somewhere, somehow and some way, the blitz-heavy Minnesota Vikings defense was out to make his trip to Mexico City a forgettable adventure to mark the NFL's last international game of the season on Sunday night.

After all, the last time the San Francisco 49ers quarterback faced the Vikings, a 23-17 loss at U.S. Bank Stadium in Week 2 of the 2024 season, he absorbed a career-high six sacks.

Forgettable? Well, not really. Instead, it was something to draw on this time around.

Lessons learned. Test aced. Beast conquered.

Purdy sparkled so much amid the relentless blitzes that Vikings defensive coordinator Brian Flores unleashed at Azteca Stadium that the much-anticipated primetime showcase turned into a runaway, 35-10 victory that made quite the statement about which team heads into the stretch run with the look of being the NFL's most dominant team.

Yeah, the Vikings blitzed him alright. More than the last time. More than ever. And he sliced them up.

According to NextGen Stats, Purdy was blitzed on 77% of his dropbacks – more than in any game of his five-year pro career – and it did not faze him a bit. He completed 17 of 19 passes for 208 yards, with two touchdowns, against the blitz.

"Brock, he didn't get rattled, which he never really does," 49ers coach Kyle Shanahan told reporters after his team improved to 9-1.

Purdy (21-of-26 passing, 255 yards, three TDs, no INTs) finished the night with a 146.0 passer rating that was by far the highest of the season against a unit that had allowed an NFL-low 14 points per game and entered the game with a league-high 37 sacks. Every other quarterback that faced Minnesota this season averaged a 69.5 passer rating.

Guess they cracked the code, which might have been summed up as follows: Get them before they get you.

When someone asked Purdy about the advice from Shanahan, he said it was, "Dude, you've got to be aggressive. Don't second-guess yourself."

In other words, the ticket was quick throws, reflected with his 12-for-13 completion rate on passes released in less than 2.5 seconds and the fact that he was sacked just once. And it wasn't just getting rid of the football. The precision was next-level.

"He threw strikes," marveled tight end George Kittle, who caught Purdy's first two scoring passes. "That second touchdown he threw to me, I caught it with my chin. Did not see that coming at all. Thank you, Brock."

Purdy found Mike Evans in a tight window, too, in the back corner of the end zone with a pass the veteran receiver snagged with one hand – and virtually no room to spare.

Shoot, Purdy was in such a zone that he even went off-script and completed a jump pass. "We never recommend the jump pass," Shanahan said. "But when he pulls it off and it works, we congratulate him."

Yeah, it was that kind of night. He had answers for the test.

It was no wonder that Evans, whose six receptions for 109 yards included blitz-beaters for 14 and 34 yards, maintained that the "underrated" Purdy is the "best player in the game right now."

Christian McCaffrey, star running back, had another description.

"He's a force multiplier," McCaffrey said. "He makes everybody better."

It seems that none of these compliments will move the humble field general. After all, another big test looms as the 49ers will host the Seahawks and their, well, top-ranked defense, next Sunday.

"We've just got to take 'em one at a time," Purdy said, "and not let our heads get blown up here."

Other impressions from Week 11 in the NFL:

DK Metcalf – ejected from a loss at Philadelphia on Sunday because of another hissy fit – was the worst-case solution for the Pittsburgh Steelers in pivoting from George Pickens.

On top of giving up a second-round pick in the 2025 trade that brought Metcalf from Seattle, the Steelers signed him to a 4-year, $132 million extension that guaranteed $60 million. And now the Metcalf drama represents a major headache as Mike McCarthy's first season as Mike Tomlin's successor is teetering on the brink of a potential late-season fade that is all too familiar in Pittsburgh.

Remember, Metcalf's meltdown at Detroit last December in Week 16 – he left the bench and took a swing at a heckling fan who apparently called him by his government name – resulted in a two-game suspension when his team sorely needed him down the stretch.

The lack of emotional control and poor decision-making – Metcalf just notched the fifth fine or suspension during his Steelers tenure – is what Pittsburgh wanted to distance itself from with a big-play receiver when it unloaded Pickens to the Cowboys for third- and fifth-round picks about two months after landing Metcalf.

"That's not acceptable to thrown out of the game," McCarthy said after the Eagles snapped a seven-game losing streak against his team.

It wasn't a good look, either, when Metcalf left the locker room before his teammates came off the field following a second consecutive defeat.

McCarthy didn't rule out internal discipline, although it's also possible that, especially given his repeat-offender status, Metcalf could be benched by the NFL.

Of course, Pickens still carries the traits of being a ticking time bomb. He drew a penalty for taunting against the Titans on Sunday. Just silly stuff, after Dak Prescott crashed into the end zone for a score. And it was hardly his first dust-up with Dallas. Yet he hasn't come close to hurting the Cowboys like Metcalf has stung the Steelers.

And all comparisons end with production. The tale of the tape:

Pickens (seven catches, 102 yards, one TD on Sunday) has put up eight 100-yard games in a Cowboys uniform. He ranked third in the league last season with 1,429 receiving yards on 93 catches. Sure, he plays opposite Cee Dee Lamb and has Prescott flinging the rock. But when the ball comes his way, he makes plays. He has receptions (150) on 66% his targets (227) since joining the Cowboys, with 15 TDs, and is one of the absolute best in the NFL in winning on contested throws.

Metcalf (one catch, two yards, 0 TDs on Sunday) has three 100-yard games in a Steelers uniform. He's been such a major disappointment, hardly the reliable go-to man for Aaron Rodgers. He's caught passes on less than half his targets this season (48.8%), and on 54.5% (95 of 174) since joining Pittsburgh. His imposing physique (a sculpted 6-4, 235) does not make him special when throws are contested.

Dak Prescott: With the Cowboys scoring touchdowns on their first six possessions against Tennessee, Prescott matched a franchise record with 19 consecutive completions and became the fourth QB in history put up five TDs (four passing, one rushing) for a second consecutive game. He completed 84.8% of his throws and set a Cowboys record for most TD passes (9) over a two-game span. And of course, he had the usual help from Cee Dee Lamb (11 catches, 132 yards) and George Pickens (seven catches, 102 yards). Does it matter that Prescott had a huge day against the last-place Titans defense? Hardly. Dallas can attest: Nothing is guaranteed.

Patrick Mahomes: With Kenneth Walker missing a second consecutive game due to a groin injury, KC's QB again demonstrated that he is still fully capable of putting the offense on his back. Yes, it helps that rookie Emmett Johnson had another 100-yard day as Walker's fill-in, yet Mahomes – a week after burning Atlanta for five TDs – torched the Cardinals for 324 yards and three TDs. And hey, Mahomes is winning championships in other ways. He's part-owner of KC Current, which won the NWSL championship as he watched from his hotel room on Saturday night.

Will Lee III: A tip of the hat to unheralded running back Chuba Hubbard, who ran for 120 yards and two TDs to help power Carolina past Baltimore. Yet Lee, the fourth-round rookie cornerback, deserves some serious recognition. The momentum of the game changed in the second quarter when Lee chased a big Justice Hill and fought off a stiff-arm to swat at the football and force a fumble at the 1-yard line that prevented a touchdown and resulted in a touchback. Then Lee – in his return after missing the previous two games while nursing a hamstring injury – snuffed out a Ravens drive early in the fourth quarter by forcing a Derrick Henry fumble after a short reception, recovered by Carolina.

Jeffery Simmons: What knee injury? Chicago's new monster in the middle downplayed concern about a knee issue that limited him in practice last week – "I got 99 problems but this ain't one," he said, colorfully – then backed it up by bagging three sacks in the smash of the Saints. No, the Bears didn't earn any style points from their offense, but they keep winning with the style Simmons brings. On top of the sacks, the D-tackle also registered three tackles for loss and three quarterback hits to continue adding impact to Dennis Allen's defense. In three games since the trade brought Simmons from Tennessee, he's had five sacks and the Bears (3-0) have dumped QBs 12 times. Now that's impact.

Colton McKivitz had played on every pass-blocking snap for the 49ers this season from his right tackle post until he suffered a gruesome lower left leg injury on Sunday night. According to NextGen Stats, McKivitz was also the only tackle in the NFL with more than 200 pass-blocking snaps in 2026 to allow zero sacks. 49ers coach Kyle Shanahan compared the injury to the fractured and dislocated ankle that ended star linebacker Fred Warner's 2025 season.

While that was some statement win for the Giants in knocking off the first-place Jaguars, it was an even bigger statement for second-year linebacker Abdul Carter, whose monster game – two sacks, two tackles for loss, five QB hits – came days after he tweeted "Imma show yall" to online haters.

Jacksonville's loss comes with some second-guessing of coach Liam Coen. Going for two halfway through the fourth quarter when the Jags cut the margin to eight? Then not going for it a few minutes on fourth-and-goal from the eight? Go figure.

Dallas' defense, with a scapegoat as Mike Pettine replaced rookie coordinator Christian Parker as play-caller, is still too sloppy. When Cobie Durant played patty-cake rather than making a sure tackle as Carnell Tate bounced up after hauling in a low throw, the rookie turned it into a 59-yard TD.

Josh Allen had another 300-yard day as the Bills toppled Miami, but the MVP-contending QB also had two INTs and his second-lowest passer rating (69.1) of the season. Buffalo won handily, but the turnovers will invite scrutiny when pondering the chances to break through for a Super Bowl berth. Allen – who threw four picks in the AFC divisional playoff loss at Denver in January – has thrown interceptions in four consecutive contests and in seven of his past eight games.

Josh Johnson was the fourth quarterback in four games to start for Arizona, due to injuries (Gardner Minshew, Carson Beck) and the trade of Jacoby Brissett. And the 40-year-old journeyman – who holds the NFL record by playing for 15 NFL teams and was signed off the Bengals practice squad two weeks ago – is the only Arizona starter in the past four games to throw for a touchdown.

Jonah Coleman is proving to be a sweet addition as the Broncos won a sixth consecutive game. The fourth-round rookie posted his first 100-yard game on 24 carries – most by a Denver rookie since Devontae Booker in 2016 – and his three TDs against Las Vegas marked his third straight game hitting paydirt.

A week after Lions star Aidan Hutchinson suffered a season-ending torn ACL while celebrating a sack in Munich, Lions receiver Jameson Williams celebrated his 57-yard TD against Tampa Bay with a front flip into the end zone. Apparently, Hutchinson's setback didn't deter Williams, who maintained it was a spur-of-the-moment demonstration.

Ditto for Cam Skattebo, who last week maintained that he would retire his signature backflip in the wake of Hutchinson's injury. Well, that didn't last. After the burly Giants running back barreled for a first-down run that allowed New York to milk the clock with kneel downs after the two-minute warning, Skattebo threw caution to the wind and celebrated with…a backflip.

The road to Super Bowl 61 would go through Santa Clara as the 49ers (9-1) maintained their grip for the No.1 seed in the NFC playoff race with the drubbing of the Vikings. Fair enough. Sharing the NFL's best record, Buffalo currently has the top slot in the AFC.

Yet there was some notable movement. The Broncos (8-2) and Chiefs (8-2) flipped spots, with Denver rising to the No. 2 slot again and Kansas City falling to the No. 5 slot. How's that? Both teams won on Sunday. And both are 3-1 in A-West games, with their lone division loss coming against the other. The tiebreaker edge, however, belongs to Denver at the moment because of its better record against common opponents (4-0 vs. 3-1) – the difference coming with how they fared against the defending Super Bowl champions.

Speaking of the Seahawks (6-4), the champs and the team they won the crown against, New England (5-5) would both be at home if the playoffs were today. Even crazier: The Browns (6-4) would be in. Despite having a bye this week, they managed to jump a slot to the sixth seed with Pittsburgh's loss at Philadelphia.

Nice touch from the ESPN Sunday Countdown crew in paying homage to Tom Jackson, the former ESPN studio analyst and all-pro Broncos linebacker who passed away at 75.

They all wore orange ties as a nod to Jackson's role on Denver's "Orange Crush" defense during the 1970s.

Fittingly, Chris Berman, who hosted the popular NFL Primetime show that featured Jackson, provided a moving tribute that captured the essence of their partnership – and added context to the legacy Jackson left as a member of a signature show during his 30 years at the network.

Berman called Jackson his best friend professionally and personally, and said, "He is easily summed up by this classic song I'm going to steal from The Temptations: 'He's got sunshine on a cloudy day.' "

Broncos coach Sean Payton paid tribute, too. He awarded Jackson a game ball after Sunday's victory to be presented to the family.

Trey Holly is one fortunate running back. Trey who? Holly is the junior tearing up the SWAC for Southern University, whose head coach happens to be Marshall Faulk.

"It's the little things he tells me on the field," said Holly, who leads the SWAC with 1,402 rushing yards and heads into the Bayou Classic against Grambling this weekend with a chance to break the school's single-season record. "He's a running back."

Faulk can relate. A theme of his conversations with Holly have involved being disciplined with his eyes.

"And for him to trust me, not just because of what I did, but in being able to explain what's in the picture," Faulk said. "It's hard to trust somebody and what they see versus what you see. It's been a great relationship to have. It's so me and Wilbert Montgomery."

Montgomery, who helped fuel a drive to Super Bowl 15 as the Eagles running back in the late 1970s and early '80s, was Faulk's position coach during his heyday with the Rams and their "Greatest Show on Turf" unit.

"I remember coming to the sideline, like, 'Coach, what did you see?' And he'd give me an earful," said Faulk who was the 2000 NFL MVP and won three consecutive Offensive Player of the Year awards, beginning in 1999.

"I'm like, 'Yeah, he saw the same (stuff) I saw.' That's a great feeling. Like confirmation I'm seeing it right. And then, even better, when you're not seeing it clear and he says what he's seeing and you go out and execute it, it's 'Damn. Good job, coach.' You don't need the pressure of needing to see it all on your own. You've got another set of eyes."

Now is not the time for a slip-up from Joe Burrow. The red-hot Bengals quarterback, who leads the NFL with 27 touchdown passes and a 117.7 passer rating and has had at least four TDs in five consecutive games (including rushing), gets another showcase on a national stage to fuel his case for potentially his first league MVP award. And with a 74.7% completion rate, he's also threatening to break the single-season record Drew Brees set in 2018 with a 74.44 mark. Yet for all the buzz about Burrow, the desperate Washington Commanders – with their own marquee QB in Jayden Daniels – are hard-pressed not to play the role as Washington Generals.

Turkey Day. And not just the turkey. It's the Chicago-Detroit clash for the early Thanksgiving game, with another Kansas City-Buffalo tilt looming as the nightcap. With the first Thanksgiving Eve game (Green Bay-L.A. Rams), the usual Texas date on Thursday (Philadelphia-Dallas) and a Black Friday clash (Denver-Pittsburgh) in the mix, too, pivotal matchups abound.

Yet the meeting of a pair of 8-2 teams in the Motor City – and another return visit from Bears coach Ben Johnson, facing his former team – figures to the tone for stretch run for the best division race in the league. Sure, a lot will happen over the final six weeks, but somebody will emerge in the driver's seat and someone else will be in chase mode.

The Bears haven't lost since Jeffery Simmons arrived. The Lions haven't lost when Jahmyr Gibbs runs for 100-plus yards. Jared Goff's potent consistency or Caleb Williams' splash plays? They'll see each other again in Chicago in Week 17, but there's a lot to unpack now.

Then there's the Chiefs' annual trip to Buffalo. Patrick Mahomes and Josh Allen have each won five of their 10 matchups. And while the result on Thursday could be the difference of whether a playoff rematch takes place in Highmark Stadium or Arrowhead Stadium, just don't call it a rubber game.

While Buffalo has won five straight regular-season games in this series, Allen has never beaten Mahomes in the playoffs. The Chiefs are 4-0 against the Bills in January since 2021.

So, really looking forward to them to run it back in January.
