Geno Smith, Garrett Wilson add context to Jets red zone inefficiency

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

There's something particularly frustrating about watching an offense move the football, only to have everything fall apart when the end zone is finally within reach. The New York Jets have become all too familiar with that experience, and the numbers suggest their struggles are getting worse rather than better.

New York ranks last in the NFL in red-zone touchdown conversion rate at 37.93%. That figure drops to an alarming 22.22% over the Jets' last three games, leaving an offense already struggling to generate points with even fewer opportunities to make its possessions count.

The Jets haven't scored a touchdown from anywhere on the field in their last nine quarters of action. While that drought extends well beyond the red zone, their inability to capitalize when scoring opportunities present themselves remains particularly difficult to overlook.

Quarterback Geno Smith addressed the team's red-zone struggles Wednesday, pointing to the relationship between an effective rushing attack and an efficient passing game. With less field to defend, opposing defenses can crowd throwing lanes and make every yard more difficult to gain.

"It starts with the run game. You have to run the ball down there... From there, the passing game has to be efficient... For us, it's always execution, always just, what can we do to be better?" Smith said.

Smith's assessment speaks to the challenges of operating in confined space. Establishing the run forces defenses to respect multiple possibilities, but that advantage means little if the Jets cannot execute when the opportunity arrives. The offense needs more than a promising drive; it needs the discipline and precision to finish one.

Wide receiver Garrett Wilson isn't placing the responsibility entirely on the offensive scheme or the quarterback. He acknowledged his own shortcomings Wednesday, offering a candid assessment of his production when the Jets have approached the goal line.

Wilson has been targeted eight times in the red zone this season but has recorded just one reception. That's a troubling return for one of New York's most important offensive weapons, particularly in situations where the margin between a touchdown and another wasted opportunity can be remarkably small.

"Not a great percentage on my part of making a play when the ball comes my way. No, I'm not happy with my production down there," Wilson said.

Wilson's willingness to accept responsibility is noteworthy because the Jets' problems cannot be attributed to one player or one position group. Smith wants better execution, and Wilson recognizes that he must make more of his opportunities. Both assessments point toward a collective failure that has become increasingly costly. Wilson shared more detail about the extra work that's being put in to improve.

The Jets can discuss adjustments, execution and accountability throughout the week, but none of those conversations will change what happens on Sundays by themselves. For Smith, Wilson and the rest of the offense, the next meaningful response must come when the field gets shorter and the stakes of every snap become greater.
