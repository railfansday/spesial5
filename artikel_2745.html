Eye of the Tiger: Pivotal position matchups for LSU vs. Tennessee

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Although the College Football Playoff wishes have sailed for LSU, there's still very much at stake in this game. Sitting at #9 with an 8-2 record, Tennessee is in a great spot to make the 12-team playoff for the second time under Josh Huepel. Each of their losses were by just a field goal. The first came at the end of September to then #1 Texas, then on the road at South Carolina. They have won 5 of their last 6, which is impressive considering they made a quarterback change 5 weeks ago.

The Volunteers entered 2026 with a massive question mark at the QB position. They felt strongly about last year's starter, Joey Aguilar, being able to get another year of eligibility, but he didn't. They then turned their attention to none other than…Sam Leavitt. We know how that story went, though. Lane Kiffin flew to Knoxville to meet Leavitt in the airport and convince him to commit to LSU and the rest is history.

True freshman Faizon Brandon started the first 5 games of the season for Tennessee, but it was never pretty. So, Huepel turned to redshirt freshman George MacIntyre and he's gone 4-1 as the starter.

LSU heads to Tennessee with a disappointing 6-4 record after a crushing collapse at home against Texas last week. Their season is effectively over and they are playing for pride for these final two regular season games.

We'll get our first peek at what Lane Kiffin's coaching looks like when things are going poorly. Will the Tigers come out fired up and looking to play spoiler? Or will they cower and lose their third straight?

We'll find out starting at 2:30 pm on Saturday in Neyland Stadium and here are three key matchups to look out for:

This one is pretty broad, but things are at an all time low for Leavitt. He was criticized heavily in the media for leaving the field prior to the alma mater as the team's leader, there were anti-Leavitt chants by students as they exited the stadium, and when given the ability to clear the air about some of when speaking publicly on Monday, he avoided it.

Now, he heads to Knoxville, the place he left high and dry to come back down to Baton Rouge. The Tennessee defense is ranked 3rd in total defense, 1st in scoring defense, and 1st against the pass. They sack the quarterback as much as anyone in the conference other than LSU, sitting just one short of the Tigers. It's a tough matchup, in a tough environment, in a tough time for Leavitt.

LSU offensive line vs. Tennessee defensive line

As I just mentioned, the Volunteers can get after the quarterback. They've racked up 39 sacks in 10 games. While 7 of those did come from Tyree Weathersby Sr, who is out for the season, they have plenty of other dudes that can get it done.

Xavier Gilliam and Mariyon Dye have 6 and 5 sacks, respectively, on the defensive line. Although he's listed as a linebacker, Arion Carter can come off of the edge and has 4 sacks of his own.Sam Leavitt was clearly hobbled last week against Texas. It looked to be his right leg/ankle, which is different from the left hamstring that he was successfully playing through earlier in the season. He looked hesitant to run and became scared to throw the ball after his third interception, putting his offensive line in a tough spot where they had to protect while he scrambled around in the backfield. That led to a few holding penalties when he exited the pocket before they expected him to.

Where this somehow gets even more concerning is when LSU tries to run the ball. As good as Dilin Jones is, he's had to make a lot of his magic on his own by making defenders miss. With Leavitt potentially compromised and the Tigers being in a hostile environment, they badly need to find a rushing attack to keep the Jim Knowles defense honest.

Blake Baker's run defense has been excellent all season. Despite missing Whit Weeks since week 4, they've been able to limit opposing running backs on a consistent basis. They've mostly been able to limit opposing offenses, in general, all season, but even when the other team has been able to move the ball, it's typically not on the ground. They're ranked #2 in the SEC in rush defense, allowing just 86.1 yards per game. Opponents are averaging just 2.73 yards per carry.

This week presents a true challenge, though. Allow me to introduce you to DeSean Bishop, the SEC's leading rusher. Wearing #18 for the Volunteers, Bishop has rushed for 1,163 yards with 13 touchdowns. He's averaging 5.1 yards per carry and has very seldom been slowed down. The only team that has limited him was Texas, who held him to 34 yards rushing in week 4.

Bishop has surpassed the century mark in rushing yards in 6 out of 10 games this season. In 2 of the games that he didn't, he finished with 97 yards. The only outliers are that game against Texas and their week 3 game against Kennesaw State, in which he had 9 carries for 42 yards and a touchdown before they took him out of the blowout and let then backup Daune Morris run for 116 yards.

The bottom line is, Tennessee can run the ball. Their offensive line is playing like one of the best in the country. They've only thrown the ball for over 200 yards twice the entire season. Brandon did it in the season opener against Furman and MacIntyre did it in his first start at Arkansas. Aside from that, the Volunteers have gotten to 8-2 and #9 in the country by running Bishop and playing defense.
