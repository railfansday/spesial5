How Yin Yoga Bananasana Can Transform Your Swimming

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Swimmers spend plenty of time working on stroke mechanics and building upper-body strength, but mobility of the side body, particularly the intercostal muscles between the ribs, can affect how freely you can breathe, especially as fatigue sets in.

Bananasana, a gentle yin yoga pose, stretches the side body. A supported side-lying pose, with blocks under the ribs and head, creates a more targeted stretch that can help expand your ribs for three-dimensional breathing.

While Bananasana offers a relatively subtle lateral stretch of the obliques, intercostal muscles, and IT band, a supported side-lying variation increases the sensation and may help ease fascial tightness.

Fascia is a network of connective tissue that surrounds and connects muscles, nerves, bones, and organs. When fascia’s layers glide well, the body moves freely. However, training load, injury, prolonged postures, and other factors can contribute to stiffness or reduced mobility. Restriction in the rib cage and side body can make a full, expansive breath feel harder to achieve.

Give your shoulders and lats attention, but don’t overlook your ribs and side body. Add these stretches to your regular mobility routine to build breath awareness and find more ease and efficiency in the water.
