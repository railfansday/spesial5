Whether you’re starting your fitness journey or you’re a weightlifting junkie, there are several supplements that should be staples in your stack. As a certified personal trainer and nutrition coach, I’d put a high-quality whey protein powder and one of the best creatines at the top.

Not far behind would be another supplement that can help you achieve your muscle-building and weight-loss goals: pre-workout.

However, just like not all barbells are created equal, neither are supps. The strongest pre-workouts stand out from the pack because they are formulated with research-backed doses of four key ingredients — caffeine (at least 200 milligrams), L-citrulline (at least 4,000 milligrams), beta-alanine (at least 3,200 milligrams), and betaine (at least 2,500 milligrams) — for physical and mental performance enhancement.

To save you the trouble of sipping on something that won’t give you an effective muscle pump, our team of expert product testers tried more than 80 pre-workouts and reported which supplements delivered the most focus, power, and tingles. Then, with some guidance from a few other experts, our squad of CPTs, CNCs, CrossFit enthusiasts, and dedicated weightlifters narrowed down the list to the eight strongest pre-workouts so you can select the perfect match for your goals and preferences.

For bodybuilders, a “pump” is a sign of a successful training session. Increased blood flow to the muscles makes them feel fuller and appear larger and more vascular during and after a workout. A pump also promotes muscle growth by delivering essential nutrients and oxygen to the muscles as blood flow increases. While you can achieve a pump through high-rep sets, drop sets, and supersets, high-stim pre-workouts like Gorilla Mode are specially designed to improve your pump.

Gorilla Mode contains several pump-enhancing ingredients in each scoop, including 5,000 milligrams of pure L-citrulline (more per scoop than any other pre-workout on our list) and 2,000 milligrams of Hydro Prime glycerol powder. L-citrulline works to widen blood vessels, increasing blood flow to the muscles. Glycerol powder is rapidly absorbed by the muscles and draws in water, which can cause muscle cell swelling.

If you’re caffeine-sensitive or budget-conscious, this intense pre-workout may not be the best for you. Although one serving only contains 200 milligrams of caffeine, you will need to take two servings to get research-backed doses of some key ingredients, including creatine monohydrate (only 2.5 gram per serving). For this reason, we docked a point off the formulation, giving Gorilla Mode 4 out of 5 stars in this category.

A strong pre-workout contains a combination of performance-enhancing ingredients in research-backed dosages. “Usually, the strength of a pre-workout is based upon the amount of stimulants it contains. High doses of caffeine (300+ milligrams) are usually a quick identifier of the pre-workout’s strength,” registered dietitian Alex Oskian says.

However, the potency of a pre-workout goes beyond the caffeine content. Other active ingredients like L-citrulline, beta-alanine, and betaine anhydrous can impact energy, focus muscular endurance, and power output.

“Stronger via the amount of caffeine isn’t always better. If the goal is a bigger pump in the gym, other ingredients like L-citrulline may be better,” says Alex. “Another common ingredient that can help a pre-workout feel stronger is beta-alanine, which may cause a tingling or ‘pins and needles’ sensation on the skin.”

It’s also important to remember that “strong” is a subjective term. For example, I can handle a full scoop of RedCon1 Total War without a problem, but I have friends who get too many tingles from a single serving.

“Everyone’s body reacts differently to various compounds, so what works for one person might not be best for another,” says Dr. Christopher Mohr, Ph.D., RD.

A strong, well-dosed pre-workout supplement may help you train harder by boosting your energy, focus, and endurance during intense workouts.

“There are a few things that are really helpful when you are looking for a great gym session: energy to do the work, mental clarity to help focus on what you are doing (not just to work the muscle, but to prevent injury), and a great workout,” registered dietitian Alex Oskian says. “Using a pre-workout may just help you achieve all of these.”

Let’s explore the potential benefits of taking a strong pre-workout before you start your training session.

Caffeine is one of the most popular stimulants used in pre-workout supplements to enhance energy levels and improve focus. Almost all of the pre-workouts on our list use caffeine and L-theanine. Studies have shown when paired with caffeine, L-theanine may improve cognitive performance.

“Most pre-workouts contain the stimulant caffeine in higher dosages. L-theanine helps counteract the unwanted effects of caffeine supplementation: jittery feelings and a post-caffeine crash,” Alex says. “You might see less mental fatigue, improved reaction time, and fewer headaches while regularly supplementing with L-theanine.”

I like taking a pre-workout to improve my focus because it helps intensify my workout and keep me #lockedin. Several pre-workouts on our list contain Alpha-GPC and tyrosine to give you that zoned-in feeling. Research suggests Alpha-GPC may improve cognitive function, and tyrosine may increase dopamine levels and cognition. One study found L-tyrosine helped improve people’s memory while they worked on a demanding task.

A pre-workout may help you squeeze out a few more reps as you work to build strength and muscle. Two ingredients commonly used in pre-workout, beta-alanine and betaine anhydrous, may help reduce fatigue and support muscular endurance.

“Research suggests betaine anhydrous can improve muscle strength and power via increased total work capacity and improved protein synthesis within the body. Simply put, you can do more work, which leads to increased strength and muscle growth,” says Alex.

Another common ingredient, taurine, may improve your performance and power.

Registered dietitian Alex Oskian says not every pre-workout is created equal, so it’s important to pay attention to what’s on the label. “Supplements are not regulated by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA). This means the supplement might not contain all the ingredients you’re looking for and may even contain unwanted ingredients,” she says. Alex recommends looking for supplements that undergo third-party testing.

If you choose a high-stim pre-workout, be mindful of your caffeine intake from other sources like coffee, tea, or soda so that you don’t go over the daily recommended value of 400 milligrams. This will help prevent potential negative side effects like jitters, anxiety, or trouble falling asleep.

“Consuming caffeine at the end of your day may cause sleep disturbances, which only hurts performance in the long run,” says Oskian.

No matter which pre-workout you choose, it’s important to follow the recommended intake guidelines. Remember, pre-workouts should not be taken more than once per day.

“Pre-workout supplements are generally safe for healthy adults, but they can have some potential side effects,” says Dr. Raj Dasgupta, MD, FACP, FCCP, FAASM. “It is important to start with a low dose and talk to your doctor before taking them, especially if you have any underlying health conditions.”

Many strong pre-workouts incorporate a variety of active ingredients. When choosing a pre-workout supplement, consider your goals. For example, a bodybuilder may prefer a pre-workout powder that’s highly caffeinated and contains ingredients such as L-citrulline, betaine anhydrous, and creatine to help improve performance. On the other hand, an endurance athlete may want a pre-workout that has less caffeine but includes beta-alanine, BCAAs, and mineral electrolytes.

Whenever possible, I choose supplements that are tested by a third party for purity and have transparent labeling. Athletes who undergo drug testing should look for certifications from Informed Choice and NSF, which ensure the product does not include any banned substances.

Although we test all of the products we recommend, it’s important to check out customer reviews, especially from people with similar fitness goals and preferences. They can provide valuable insights into the product’s overall effectiveness, potential side effects, and the most popular flavors.

“Recommended brands and products are ones that are highly rated by users and contain ingredients that have been backed by scientific research,” says Dr. Raj Dasgupta.

“Taste plays a big role in pre-workout selection. If you don’t like a particular flavor, then you likely won’t drink the pre-workout no matter its benefits,” Oskian says. It’s also important to consider the use of artificial flavors, colors, and sweeteners.

The price of a pre-workout doesn’t necessarily reflect its effectiveness. Understanding the ingredients list and dosage levels is important to ensure you’re paying for a high-quality supplement and not just expensive marketing.

There are a few potential red flags you want to avoid when picking out a pre-workout.

I like to avoid supplements made using proprietary blends. These blends are combinations of ingredients, but the individual doses are not fully disclosed. This lack of transparency makes it impossible to assess the effectiveness and safety of the supplement.

Many fitness enthusiasts and athletes prefer all-natural ingredients and products free from unnecessary fillers or additives. “If sugar alcohols and artificial sweeteners wreak havoc on your stomach, make sure those aren’t on the ingredient list,” registered dietitian Alex Oskian says.

Athletes who compete in sanctioned organizations should make sure the supplements they use are third-party tested and certified banned substance-free.

If you have big fitness goals, choosing a strong pre-workout formulated with safe and effective doses of science-backed ingredients is worth it to deliver performance-enhancing effects. Keep in mind that even the strongest pre-workouts on the market won’t make you run faster, lift heavier, or exercise longer on their own. These supplements are designed to help you put in the work at the gym to make gains and enhance your performance over time.

Ultimately, though, a strong pre-workout is only part of the equation to becoming the strongest version of yourself. At the end of the day, you have to put in the work, follow a sound nutrition plan, and prioritize rest and recovery to crush your fat-burning and muscle-building goals.
