TJX Beat Its Own Sales Forecast With 4% Comparable Growth, but Management Is Bracing for a Slower Holiday Quarter.

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

It's forecasting 2% to 3% growth in "comps" for the current quarter.

By Eric Volkman – Updated Nov 19, 2026 at 6:56AM EST

One of the more stock-boosting quarterly reports from retailers this earnings season was published by TJX Companies (TJX -0.69%) on Wednesday. In the third quarter of fiscal 2027, the company, known mainly for its TJ Maxx and Marshalls discount retail chains, posted comparable sales growth higher than it had forecast. Along with improved guidance and other encouraging developments, this excited investors about its future, driving the company's shares up more than 8% that day.

Can it sustain that rise? It's forecasting comparable sales growth for the all-important holiday season lower than the actual third-quarter result, after all. Here's a brief look at whether that makes the stock a buy regardless.

Those "comps" were 4% across all TJX's brands and geographies, which was reflected in a nearly 6% year-over-year increase in net sales, to $15.95 billion. GAAP net income climbed 7% to $1.54 billion, for earnings per share (EPS) of $1.39. However, $0.05 of that per-share figure was attributable to tariff refunds from the federal government (the duties it levied in 2025 were nixed in a Supreme Court decision this past February, hence the rebates). When adjusted for this, EPS was $1.34.

That was in line with the consensus analyst estimate. TJX beat the average pundit projection of $15.82 billion for net sales, but not by much.

There were heartening developments elsewhere in the earnings release. One was the broad improvement in comps, breaking down into 8% growth in the company's international reporting unit, 6% for TJX Canada, and 5% for the HomeGoods brand. On a more downbeat note, the flagship Marmaxx unit (the company's TJ Maxx and Marshalls operations in the U.S.) only inched 2% higher.

The company now expects full-year comps to rise by 4%; previously it proffered a range of 3% to 4%. It also raised the lower end of its adjusted EPS estimate range to $5.17 per share from $5.15. It kept the ceiling intact, at $5.20.

Although TJX's guidance for the current holiday quarter is below its full-year outlook, at 2% to 3%, management asserts that it's well-primed for the shopping season. In the conference call discussing its third-quarter performance, CEO Ernie Herrman provided some color about the two distinct marketing pushes that will support its leading brands -- Never Uniform, Always You for TJ Maxx, and Holiday Hustlers for Marshalls.

"We are targeting both new and existing shoppers across a wide consumer demographic of varying ages and income levels, and leaning into digital channels that align with today's consumer media habits," Herrman said. "We feel great about our social media strategies and believe they will drive high engagement."

Judging by the 30-second spots for both campaigns I viewed, they do a decent job flagging the strengths of each brand. TJ Maxx, with its proportionally larger selection for female shoppers, sharply targets women with Never Uniform, Always You. Holiday Hustlers emphasizes the deals on quality merchandise that Marshalls is theoretically capable of engineering.

The Wednesday pop feels like something of a relief rally to me, as TJX's shares have slumped this year in the wake of less inspiring quarters. There were numerous things to like about the company's third-quarter performance, sure, but I’d be concerned that the comparable-sales growth guidance for the holiday frame is notably below the full-year estimate. It's also well underneath the 5% TJX achieved in the same period in both fiscal 2025 and 2026.

Meanwhile, on valuations, the company's stock is pricier than non-discount retailers that, in my view, have performed better lately. Its 22-plus forward P/E feels high compared to the levels for those peers, which are currently hovering in the low- to mid-teens in the run-up to shopping season.

While I don't think TJX is doing badly, to me it's not enough of an outperformer for its stock to be compelling. I also think there are better bargains elsewhere in the broader retail sector right now.
