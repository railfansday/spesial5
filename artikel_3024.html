Zscaler Stock Surged on CrowdStrike's $43.9 Billion Buyout. Here's How Much Shareholders Will Actually Get

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

CrowdStrike is paying for Zscaler in its own stock, and that stock fell almost 10% the day the deal was announced.

By Jonathan Ponciano – Nov 23, 2026 at 3:58PM EST

10 stocks we like better than CrowdStrike ›

CrowdStrike (CRWD -8.92%) announced on Monday that it will buy Zscaler (ZS +17.67%) in an all-stock deal valued at about $43.9 billion. The press release put the offer at $260.41 per Zscaler share, a 34% premium to Friday's close.

But Zscaler holders won't receive $260.41 in cash. They will receive 1.135 CrowdStrike shares for each Zscaler share, a fixed ratio that rises and falls with CrowdStrike's price. The $260.41 figure comes from CrowdStrike's Friday close of $229.44.

By 3:30 p.m. ET Monday, CrowdStrike had dropped 9.6% to $207.45. At that price, 1.135 shares are worth about $235.45, roughly $25 less than the headline number. Zscaler traded at $227.45, up 16.8% on the day but still $8 below the deal's current value.

CrowdStrike reported its third-quarter results alongside the deal. Revenue rose 28% to $1.58 billion. Net new annual recurring revenue reached a record $419 million, and total annual recurring revenue grew 27% to $6.26 billion.

The stock fell anyway, losing about $22 billion in market value, which is half the price CrowdStrike agreed to pay. Paying in stock means CrowdStrike's current shareholders give up part of the company: they will own about 84.1% of the combined business, and Zscaler's holders 15.9%.

CrowdStrike says the deal will add about $1 billion in annual revenue within 30 months of closing and cut about $500 million in yearly costs within 18 to 24 months. It plans to sell its Falcon products to about 8,000 Zscaler customers that don't use CrowdStrike today.

The merger filing needs approval from Zscaler shareholders and antitrust regulators. The companies expect to close in the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal 2028, which runs from August 2027 to January 2028. Either side can walk away if the deal hasn't closed by Nov. 23, 2027, a date that moves to Feb. 23, 2028, if only antitrust or foreign-investment reviews remain.

Founder and CEO Jay Chaudhry and his affiliates, who hold about 34% of Zscaler's shares, have agreed to vote in favor of the deal. Zscaler would owe CrowdStrike a $1.4 billion fee if it walked away for a better offer.

A 3.5% gap for a wait of a year or more is small, which suggests traders expect the deal to close. It also means Zscaler's stock now mostly tracks CrowdStrike's. Anyone buying Zscaler at $227 is effectively betting on CrowdStrike's price in late 2027 among cybersecurity stocks, and that price has already fallen 26% from its 52-week closing high of $278.86 on Oct. 6.

Zscaler holders who want to own the combined company can hold on. Those who bought Zscaler for its own business should know that, if the deal closes, they will end up owning CrowdStrike stock.
