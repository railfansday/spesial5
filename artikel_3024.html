Boeing vs. Space Exploration Technologies: Which Industrials Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Boeing's $89.5 billion in revenue and Starlink's 33% growth frame a tough choice, but the cash flow and valuation gaps tell a sharper story.

By Jake Lerch – Updated Nov 24, 2026 at 10:08AM EST

Investors are increasingly weighing the established legacy of Boeing (BA -0.09%) against the high-growth trajectory of Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -5.96%). Both companies are vying for dominance in an evolving aerospace landscape.

Boeing provides a diverse mix of commercial airplanes and defense hardware, while Space Exploration Technologies focuses on reusable rocket technology and global satellite internet. This comparison looks at their financials and risk profiles to help you decide between them.

Boeing remains a dominant force among defense stocks, providing critical infrastructure to government agencies and global airlines. Its defense segment relies on major contracts with the United States Department of Defense and NASA. Foreign military sales to non-U.S. nations accounted for 46% of total revenue in 2025, and this high concentration in government-related contracts adds a layer of risk to the business.

Reliable recent quarterly figures for at least one of these companies aren't available, so this comparison uses each company's latest full fiscal year. In FY 2025, revenue reached nearly $89.5 billion, representing a 34.5% increase compared to the previous year. Net income for the period was approximately $2.2 billion, resulting in a net margin of 2.5%.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is 10.0x, a metric that compares total debt to shareholder equity. The current ratio, which measures the ratio of liquid assets to short-term debts, is approximately 1.2x. Free cash flow, representing cash from operations minus capital expenditures, was nearly $(1.9) billion. Note that stock-based compensation accounted for roughly 40% of operating cash flow, inflating reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Space Exploration Technologies designs launch vehicles, spacecraft, and the Starlink satellite broadband system. The company serves a diverse market including launch services for commercial satellites and crewed spaceflight. Its rapid deployment of satellite infrastructure aims to provide high-speed internet to users globally.

During FY 2025, the company generated revenue of approximately $18.7 billion, representing a 33.2% year-over-year increase. Despite this growth, it reported a net loss of nearly $4.9 billion for the same period, resulting in a negative net margin of 26.4%. This compares to a net income of roughly $791.0 million in the prior fiscal year.

The December 2025 balance sheet shows a debt-to-equity ratio of nearly 0.6x. Its current ratio is approximately 1.4x, suggesting the company has sufficient short-term assets to meet its obligations. Free cash flow was nearly negative $14.0 billion, and note that stock-based compensation accounted for roughly 28.7% of operating cash flow, inflating reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Boeing faces significant operational risks related to complex manufacturing and supply chain constraints, particularly following its acquisition of Spirit AeroSystems. Cost overruns on fixed-price contracts have led to substantial reach-forward losses in both defense and commercial segments. Furthermore, ongoing FAA regulatory oversight of quality control and safety systems creates uncertainty about future production rates.

Space Exploration Technologies operates in a high-risk environment where launch failures or technical setbacks could disrupt its satellite deployment and mission schedules. The company competes with established giants like Airbus (EADSF +2.56%) and Lockheed Martin (LMT +2.55%) in the global aerospace market. Rapid expansion of the Starlink network also requires continuous heavy capital investment and the navigation of complex international regulatory frameworks.

Boeing appears to have a more conservative valuation based on its Forward P/E relative to the high multiple assigned to Space Exploration Technologies.

When comparing Boeing and SpaceX, investors should consider a few key factors. Let's have a look at them and see what that tells us about each stock.

Firstly, we should consider revenue growth. SpaceX is growing by leaps and bounds. In its most recent quarter, the company reported year-over-year revenue growth of 258%. Boeing, by contrast, has delivered average year-over-year revenue growth of about 10% over the last three years. That's certainly decent growth, but it pales in comparison to SpaceX's exceptional growth -- advantage SpaceX.

Another factor to consider is each company's profitability. Neither company has delivered consistent profits in recent quarters. However, SpaceX's operating margin did hit 2.2% in its most recent quarter. Boeing's, on the other hand, remained slightly underwater at -0.1%. While both companies hope to improve their margins going forward, SpaceX holds a slight advantage at this stage.

Lastly, there's valuation. Since both companies have not delivered consistent profits, we should examine their price-to-sales (P/S) ratios. SpaceX's P/S ratio is 54.1x, while Boeing's is 1.4x. In other words, SpaceX stock is massively more expensive on a price-to-sales basis. There are reasons why -- SpaceX's incredible revenue growth being the most significant -- but for those who place a high emphasis on valuation, Boeing has a big edge.

To sum up, both of these industrial stocks have their strengths. Boeing stock is extremely affordable, with a P/S multiple of only 1.4x. However, my choice in this head-to-head matchup is SpaceX. The company's explosive growth and its improving margins are what tip the scales in its favor.
