26-year-old steps up to prove his value in absence of influential Blackburn Rovers man: Opinion

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Blackburn Rovers have maintained solid form since the international break, drawing with Burnley before a midweek win over Watford. They’ve had to deal with adversity ahead of those matches too, notably seeing head coach Tony Mowbray step back from his duties for health reasons.

Injury to Sam Morsy also presented a dilemma for Rovers. Since moving to Ewood Park in the summer, the 35-year-old has emerged as a leader and formed a fine partnership with Kristi Montgomery in the middle of the park.

However, on hand to step up and soften the blow of Morsy’s absence has been Moussa Baradji. After some challenging moments in his debut campaign, the 26-year-old midfielder has delivered when needed and proven his value in the Rovers squad.

Baradji joined Blackburn Rovers on an initial loan last summer, and he had to deal with injury in his first season. His impact was limited as a result, and when he was available, fans weren’t too sold on his performances.

Despite this, the Frenchman was signed permanently in the summer. When injury hit and Baradji missed the first six games of the new Championship season, many of the Rovers faithful feared a similar campaign would pan out.

However, Baradji has now played in all of the last 10 Championship games – four have come from the start, two when covering for Montgomery during his injury absence, and two while Morsy has been out. The last two appearances have been the best of the lot, battling hard to help Rovers to a point against Burnley before another well-rounded display in the mature midweek win over Watford.

Baradji has brought power in and out of possession in Morsy’s absence. He isn’t afraid to get stuck in and use his stature to muscle opponents off the ball, and his physical attributes, paired with slick footwork, make him a good ball carrier as well.

The midfielder’s per 90 stats highlight the impression he has made when called upon. As per FotMob‘s dataset, Baradji is averaging 1.30 successful dribbles per 90 (top 9% of Championship midfielders), 9.88 duels won (top 1%), 7.64 defensive contributions (top 8%) and a 75% long-ball accuracy rate (top 4%).

Baradji’s signing came with some reservations, as a deal was initially shelved when his medical raised an injury problem. However, the numbers he posted in a competitive Swiss Super League for a relegated Yverdon Sport side showed exactly why Blackburn Rovers were interested.

And, his recent performances have started to show that he can be a hit in the Championship too. Baradji pairs power and skill to offer a real force in possession, and his work rate and physicality have seen him offer the strong presence needed in the middle in Morsy’s absence.

Morsy will be backed to reclaim his place when fit. But, at 35, he isn’t a long-term option. Baradji has shown he is ready to stake a claim for a long-term spot in the Rovers team.
