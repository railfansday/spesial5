Midterms provide clarity on data center development: Vistra CEO

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

“As we’ve said for some time, load interconnect queues were overstated across the country and especially in ERCOT, driven by speculation from developers,” Vistra CEO James Burke said.

The midterm elections — with data centers a key issue in many races — helped provide clarity around the environment for data center development, James Burke, Vistra president and CEO, said on an earnings call earlier this month.

“Before the elections, in many markets, we were seeing concerns about data centers in the way that the public was reacting to the pace of development, who had control over those decisions, what impact could it have on electric prices, water, all of that,” Burke said on the Nov. 6 call. “Moving past this election is a positive just in terms of being able to understand what's at stake, and now how do we move forward?”

So far, the key messages from Texas Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa have been “very similar to some of the key messages that Governor Abbott had expressed and … consistent with messages we've been expressing, which is there has to be some guardrails on this development,” Burke said.

Vistra wants tougher requirements for data center interconnection, including higher collateral requirements and take-or-pay agreements for transmission charges, according to Burke.

“As we've said for some time, load interconnect queues were overstated across the country and especially in [the Electric Reliability Council of Texas footprint], driven by speculation from developers,” Burke said.

Even with the midterm elections and regulatory actions around data centers, through 2030 Vistra expects load to grow by 4% to 6% a year in ERCOT and by 2% to 3% annually in the PJM Interconnection footprint, according to Burke.

Despite Vistra’s expectations for load growth in ERCOT, power prices there have been trending lower due to some crypto mining facilities coming offline so they can convert into data centers, and the state’s data center audit and permitting pause, Burke said.

“We do not see this as a long-term impact,” he said, noting that ERCOT hit a 91 GW peak demand record this year.

Vistra’s fleet has grown to about 50 GW with its Oct. 30 Cogentrix acquisition, Burke said.

The fleet includes combined cycle and peaking power plants that are running more often, according to Burke.

“As renewable penetration continues throughout the country, you're seeing these assets get called on more,” Burke said in response to an analyst question about potentially selling off parts of the fleet. “So we've been bullish on dispatchable. We've been bullish on flexibility … So as a long-term owner-operator, we like the fleet.”
