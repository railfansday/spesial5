Robert Pattinson and Suki Waterhouse are fast becoming the it couple of the moment (not least of all because the man formerly known as Edward Cullen is now Bond, James Bond). So you'd best believe they're setting trends left, right, and center – even in the world of indoor gardening.

And while we'd expect the pair to have a suitably impressive garden, it turns out their houseplant of choice is considerably more interesting than your average monstera or spider plant. In fact, it's a miniature tree with a rather sophisticated reputation: the not-so-humble bonsai!

Yes, with the couple's busy schedules taking them between different homes and locations, it makes sense that they're all about the idea of bringing greenery indoors. After all, houseplants offer a lovely way to make a space feel personal, even when you're not necessarily putting down roots for the long term. And if you're going to invest in an indoor plant, then why not make it something a little more unusual?

Speaking to the Perfectly Imperfect Newsletter in September 2024, Suki Waterhouse revealed that she developed a rather unexpected obsession with bonsai trees during the final month of her pregnancy.

'When I was preggers in the last month and didn’t really feel like seeing anyone, I got obsessed with getting a bonsai tree,' she says. 'I found a number on Google and pulled up to a house in East Hollywood and a lady had a bunch of them locked up in her back garden. Felt super cool and mysterious. Some of them she’d had for 30 years. Fell in love with this guy and have become obsessed with pruning.'

The leaves on this bonsai fold up at night or in subdued light, and it wakes up when we do!

It's a wonderfully specific plant obsession, and one we can absolutely get behind. Especially as there's something rather magical about a tiny tree that's been carefully cultivated for decade; there's a reason that bonsai is as much an art form as it is a gardening hobby.

Of course, Suki Waterhouse and Robert Pattinson aren't the only ones who appreciate the appeal of the beautiful bonsai tree. In fact, there's been something of a growing appreciation for the slower, more mindful side of gardening, and bonsai? Why, that fits the philosophy rather beautifully.

More importantly? Well, despite the fact that bonsai trees have a reputation for being (whisper it) high-maintenance, they're not as intimidating as their appearance might suggest. The trick is choosing the right one – so look for a variety suited to indoor conditions and make a note of its basic requirements. And, as Suki herself pointed out, a little regular pruning and thoughtful watering can go a long way.

If Suki Waterhouse and Robert Pattinson have inspired you to introduce a little bonsai magic into your own home, there are a few varieties worth considering...

If you're new to bonsai, ficus is arguably the best place to start. These tropical trees are considerably more forgiving than many traditional bonsai varieties and adapt well to indoor conditions. Their glossy leaves and interesting roots make them particularly striking, too.

Give something like this Small Ficus Live Bonsai Tree from Brussell's Bonsai a bright position with plenty of indirect light, keep it away from cold draughts and water when the top layer of compost begins to dry.

If Suki Waterhouse and Robert Pattinson have got you in the mood for something a little more unusual, consider the Hawaiian umbrella tree (Schefflera arboricola).

This bonsai's distinctive leaves and naturally branching growth make it a particularly attractive choice for an indoor plant.

It's also relatively tolerant of indoor humidity levels and can cope with slightly less-than-perfect light conditions, although a bright spot will always help it thrive. Take care to allow the soil to dry slightly between waterings, and prune regularly to maintain its shape.

A lovely option for anyone who tends to be a little forgetful with watering, jade bonsai (Crassula ovata) is a succulent that enjoys plenty of bright light and needs much less frequent watering than tropical bonsai varieties.

Pick up something like this Live Jade from Brussell's Bonsai, let the compost dry out thoroughly between waterings, and make sure it's planted in a free-draining bonsai mix to prevent its roots from sitting in excess moisture.

Of course, even the most beginner-friendly bonsai isn't quite a plant you can forget about. Even Robert Pattinson and Suki Waterhouse will tell you, light, watering and occasional pruning are all important, and it's worth remembering that not every bonsai is suited to indoor living.

As a general rule of thumb, tropical and subtropical varieties are generally the best choice for growing inside; most traditional temperate trees need to experience the changing seasons outdoors.

Still, there's something rather lovely about the idea of tending to a little tree that could stay with you for decades. And if Suki Waterhouse's experience is anything to go by, it might just become your next very absorbing, very cool, very mysterious hobby.
