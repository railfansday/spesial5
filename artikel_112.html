Irishman charged with destroying US military aircraft with axe

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The military plane, seen above, was effectively destroyed by the vandalism

US officials have charged an Irishman with destroying a $60m (Â£45m) American military aircraft after he allegedly sneaked onto the tarmac of an airport in Ireland.

Daithi O'Corrain, 48, is accused of doing extensive research before he allegedly used chemicals and various tools - including an axe - to damage a US Air National Guard C-130 Hercules parked at Shannon Airport near Limerick.

American prosecutors have charged him with destruction of government property, which carries a maximum sentence of 10 years in prison.

O'Corrain is not currently in US custody, according to the Associated Press news agency, and it is unclear whether he has a lawyer who could speak on his behalf.

In filings unsealed by prosecutors in New York on Tuesday, agents from the FBI argued that the act jeopardised US national security. They say the plane was parked at the airport while en route to another country on a US military assignment.

"As alleged, Daithi O'Corrain carried out a deliberate, planned attack against a US military aircraft, causing tens of millions of dollars in damage and jeopardizing critical military operations," prosecutors said in a court filing.

"This was not an act of spontaneous vandalism," said FBI Assistant Director in Charge James Barnacle Jr, explaining O'Corrain "spent months researching the aircraft, acquiring tools and materials, and preparing to maximize the destruction he could inflict."

Officials say that the suspect planned extensively for the 11 April 2026 raid, and brought tools with him for the demolition, which took place over several hours.

He entered the plane by breaking a locking mechanism, officials say, then used straps to close the doors before covering the windows.

"O'Corrain then proceeded to inflict severe damage to the interior of the Aircraft by, among other things, cutting wire bundles and hydraulic lines within the Aircraft, crimping a liquid oxygen line within the Aircraft, destroying every gauge in the Aircraft's cockpit, and puncturing the Aircraft's air ducts," the US indictment says.

He also used gallium, a substance that can erode aluminum and steel, ball bearings which he poured in the fuel tank, and an axe which he used to puncture the exterior of the plane.

He was caught by airport crew, and filmed standing on top of the plane. He was arrested at the scene, and charged by Ireland's national police with causing criminal damages.

O'Corrain seen standing on the rear of the aircraft in a handout from the US Department of Justice

The US charges say that he had planned well, because he avoided cutting a line that would have risked causing massive explosion.

According to the Limerick Post, external, O'Corrain is from Cork City. Supporters have described him as an anti-war activist.

Shannon Airport is a key hub for US military personnel, and is frequently the scene of anti-war protests. It was used by President Donald Trump in September for his visit to Ireland.

US prosecutors note that there have been several breaches there in recent years by protesters, including in May 2025 when three people drove a van through a perimeter fence, and then again later that month when a US airplane was sprayed with paint, making it temporarily inoperable.
