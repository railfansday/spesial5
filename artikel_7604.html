AppLovin Is Back To Smashing Earnings, But Investors Are Slow To Buy

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

AppLovin is growing at a blistering pace, but Wall Street investors remain cautious about the business.

The mobile ad tech company earned just shy of $2.2 billion in Q3 this year, per the company’s earnings report after-hours on Wednesday. Of that, $1.44 billion fell straight to net income (which is to say, profit). That is an “industry-leading” profit margin of about 66%, said CFO Matthew Stumpf.

It’s the type of number ad tech companies haven’t seen since Rocket Fuel hit the market. Which, admittedly, isn’t such fantastic company, considering Rocket Fuel’s disastrous downfall. But that association – not with Rocket Fuel, but with third-party ad tech – is part of the challenge for AppLovin.

Co-Founder and CEO Adam Foroughi addressed the education and mind-shifting among media buyers (and, ahem, among its investors) that needs to happen for its consumer ad business to accelerate.

“People have to go, ‘This is a bigger ad tech company, that’s sort-of-independent ad tech without a first-party site, [and] bigger than anything we’ve ever seen before,’” Foroughi told investors.

There is a great deal of “headroom for growth,” as Foroughi put it multiple times throughout the call, when it comes to the consumer ad business. But filling that headroom is going to take a lot of time.

Right now, AppLovin’s consumer ads segment is growing fast – more than 100% year over year, Foroughi said, though revenue for that segment is not disclosed. That business was only formally launched out of beta last year, so the revenue is still relatively low. The business will compound over time, he said, as advertisers figure out the platform and run incrementality attribution studies to feel confident with the ROAS.

Foroughi pointed to the changes Apple is making to iOS and mobile web inventory as another reason for advertisers to get on board with AppLovin’s platform.

“A lot of what’s called, I guess, traditionally, ‘open web,’” Foroughi said, is actually just a cheap reach play. That mentality is not like AppLovin’s ad system, which tracks and values impressions through to the point of conversion, he said. A healthy platform with strong ROAS may very well see CPM increases, despite being antithetical to the cheap reach mindset. But the mobile gaming space is “much, much bigger” now, he added, and the web is becoming less trackable.

“We think a lot of those dollars will shift into the app space,” he said.

Then there’s AppLovin’s creative knowledge gap.

Advertisers in the consumer segment have mostly not purchased ads in mobile games before, nor are they familiar with the particulars of how AppLovin’s media platform works, according to Fouroughi.  Mobile game advertisers all use the platform already, and they have specific creative that, say, offers a mini-game or test gameplay to prompt a download. Ecommerce advertisers on social media can’t just port over their creatives.

For one thing, he said that social platforms are all about super-quick videos with a hook delivered in a second or two. But AppLovin’s gaming audience is watching a full-screen ad for 30-60 seconds. It practically never happens on a social feed.

“There’s a learning process here that has to happen so that the media buyers really understand the framework,” Foroughi said.

Eventually, agencies and advertisers will figure it out and approach AppLovin as an always-on platform with a standardized approach to creative.

“There will be playbooks for our platform,” he prophesied.

AppLovin laid out an ambitious marker on its earnings call.

“We believe this business can compound at roughly 30% annually over the long term,” Foroughi said of the ad tech business.

The current earnings report has ad revenue up by 55% and a profit margin that, as Foroughi put it, “is probably second to none.”

But 30% growth over the long term is bold, considering the multibillion-dollar scale of AppLovin’s ad business. And investors are still wary.

Some advertisers purportedly anticipate price hikes on AppLovin, since there are now gaming and consumer advertisers bidding for the same impressions, Stephen Ju of UBS Investment Bank noted.

Matthew Cost of Morgan Stanley wondered when the “law of large numbers” would catch up with AppLovin, and the company could no longer project 20%-30% growth. He also asked Foroughi whether the headroom he promised would come from CTV. Foroughi didn’t think AppLovin would see “accretive” numbers on CTV anytime soon. But there is a pilot program running right now for AppLovin CTV ads.

Shweta Khajuria, of Wolfe Research, put it most directly: “As an ad tech company for investors who are questioning the visibility of your business model and how much confidence you have in the durability of your top-line growth, is there something else incremental that you would consider sharing that would give more confidence in terms of the way your product works?”

For one thing, Foroughi said, AppLovin has delivered strong earnings that exceed investor expectations in 15 of the past 16 quarterly reports. No other large public tech company has its combination of scale, growth rate and profit margin.

Still, the consumer business is too nascent to offer confident predictive benchmarks around its growth or revenue totals, he said. “You sort of have to make your own guesses.”

AppLovin’s earnings can’t just be hand-waved away, nor is its margin vulnerable to any sudden collapse, as happened when Rocket Fuel disclosed a take rate above 60%.

AppLovin is an actual economic force in the mobile economy.

“That ad spend is creating GDP,” Foroughi said. “It’s creating new transactions that otherwise wouldn’t have been there.”
