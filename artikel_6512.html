Taco Bell Made A Hot Dog With A Chalupa Bun &amp; Fans Are Horrified

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

“Imagine getting a glizzy from Taco Bell 😭”

Taco Bell is no stranger to controversial menu items, but one new viral dish might be the wildest yet.

Last week, the Tex-Mex chain launched the first-ever Live Más Open, a star-studded golf tournament hosted by Kevin Hart. The guest list for the nine-hole competition included pro athletes, television stars, musicians, and more. However, the appearance that’s generating the most buzz isn’t a celebrity—it’s a Taco Bell hot dog.

Attendees were served a lineup of event-exclusive dishes that we've never seen at the Bell before, like lemon pepper ranch-dusted chicken nuggets and pimento cheese Quesaritos. Taco Bell fans, though, have the most FOMO for the Fire Piña Hot Dog.

According to FoodBeast, the dish featured a hot dog served with the chain’s signature chalupa flatbread instead of a traditional bun. Taco Bell loaded the hot dog with grilled pineapple, cotija, pickled red onion, cilantro, and two different sauces. The exact sauces haven't been confirmed, although one attendee suspected they might be sour cream and Taco Bell Fire Sauce.

Bailey Fink from Allrecipes attended the Live Más Open and tasted the hot dog for herself. She called it “easily one of the best hot dogs I’ve ever had” from a fast-food restaurant and compared the flavor profile to a traditional Colombian hot dog (minus the potato chips).

Social media users who didn't get the chance to try the Fire Piña Hot Dog, though, don't seem too convinced. “The words Taco Bell and hot dog should not be in the same sentence,” one Instagram user wrote. Another added, “If you order a hot dog from Taco Bell, you need to be on watch list…”

But if you take a look at Taco Bell's origin story, introducing hot dogs would actually bring the chain full circle. Founder Glen Bell's early restaurants served hamburgers and hot dogs before he shifted his attention to tacos. He eventually opened Taco Tia, which preceded the first Taco Bell restaurant in Downey, California.

Unfortunately, though, it appears Taco Bell won't be adding hot dogs to the menu anytime soon. All the dishes served at the Live Más Open were made exclusively for the event, and the chain currently has no confirmed plans to roll them out nationwide.

But don't despair: Part of the golf tournament prize is the opportunity to help choose a discontinued menu item to bring back for a limited time next year. So while a Taco Bell hot dog likely isn't in our immediate future, we can still look forward to the return of an old favorite.
