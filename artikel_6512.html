Brave New Visions Creativity as Rebellion

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

PhotoVogue is proud to unveil the shortlist of 100 photographers and video makers selected for Brave New Visions: Creativity as Rebellion, our Global Open Call dedicated to the power of creativity to challenge the status quo, question established narratives, and imagine alternative futures. With 95,758 submissions from 9,160 artists across 155 regions, the response to this year's Open Call was extraordinary. But beyond the numbers, what moved us most was the imagination, urgency, and creative courage of the work we received. We live in a world saturated with images. More images are produced and consumed than ever before, yet more images do not necessarily mean more understanding, more imagination, or more freedom. What matters is not simply creating something new, but finding a way to make us see, feel, or question something we might otherwise have overlooked. Perhaps today, one of the most rebellious things an artist can do is refuse to give us another image we already know. Rebellion does not always announce itself loudly. It can be political and confrontational, but it can also be intimate, poetic, playful, uncomfortable, or deeply personal. It can take the form of tenderness, humor, beauty, or an unexpected way of looking at something familiar. What makes it rebellious is the freedom to question what we have been taught to accept and to imagine what might exist beyond it. This year's Open Call brought together an extraordinary range of ideas, emotions, and visual languages. Some works confront urgent social and political realities. Others explore identity, memory, belonging, desire, or the complexities of human experience. What connects the 100 selected artists is not a shared aesthetic or a single definition of rebellion, but a willingness to take risks, experiment, and follow their own vision. Images do more than represent the world. They influence how we understand it, whose stories we recognize, and what we believe is possible. The artists presented here remind us that creativity can do more than reflect reality. It can challenge the assumptions through which we see it and make space for futures we have not yet imagined.

For centuries, tbourida, the Moroccan equestrian tradition reenacting ancient Arab military manoeuvres, was an exclusively male domain. Today, women are mounting their horses, leading their own troupes and rewriting the rules of a centuries-old practice. Known as farisat, these riders are challenging the patriarchal structures that have kept them out of the arena, claiming a place in Morocco’s cultural heritage. This photograph is part of the project ‘Una Storia Italiana’. Rivas Bao revisits the story of Imane Fadil, an Italian-Moroccan model and aspiring sports journalist who became a key witness in the trial surrounding Silvio Berlusconi’s infamous ‘Bunga Bunga’ parties. After testifying about the exploitation of young women at his private residence, Fadil died in suspicious circumstances in 2019, aged just 34. Combining archival material, photographs taken inside Fadil’s Milan home and AI-generated imagery, Rivas Bao reconstructs the images that were described in court but never visually documented. Reclaiming the visual language of Berlusconian television and propaganda, the project assembles a counter-narrative from the fragments of a national memory still reluctant to confront its own Me Too moment.

Melissa Harris is Editor-at-Large at Aperture Foundation, where she served as Editor-in-Chief of Aperture magazine from 2002 to 2012, earning the publication a National Magazine Award for General Excellence. Her curatorial projects include Mary Ellen Mark: Encounters (2023–2024), A Kind of Language at Fondazione Prada (2025), and a retrospective of Graciela Iturbide’s work at C/O Berlin (2026). She has taught at Yale, Columbia Journalism School, and New York University, where she has been part of the Tisch School of the Arts for twenty years. She is also Creative Director of Magnum Foundation’s ongoing US Dispatches / Time Capsule project.

Writer, Art Curator & Professor, New York University

Nicole R. Fleetwood is a writer, curator, and professor at New York University, as well as a MacArthur Fellow and elected member of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences. She is the author of Marking Time: Art in the Age of Mass Incarceration, which received the 2020 National Book Critics Circle Award, as well as On Racial Icons and Troubling Vision. Her curatorial projects have been presented at institutions including MoMA PS1, Aperture Foundation, the National Underground Railroad Freedom Center, and the Brown Arts Institute. Her work has received support from leading cultural and research institutions, including the Mellon Foundation, Ford Foundation, and New York Public Library’s Cullman Center for Scholars and Writers.

Noelle Théard is a photographer, educator, and the US Head of Photography at The Guardian. Previously, she was Senior Photo Editor at The New Yorker, where she also produced Photo Booth, the magazine’s photography column. Her work focuses on social justice, community engagement, and the intersection of education and visual storytelling. She co-founded FotoKonbit, a nonprofit empowering Haitians to tell their own stories through photography, and has held roles at Magnum Foundation, Parsons, and the Museum of Contemporary Art in Miami. She has also taught African Diaspora and Art History at Florida International University.

Member of the Board of Directors, Friends of Palais de Tokyo

Olivia Anani is a Paris-based curator, writer, and art market expert with a background in Asian Studies and Contemporary Art. She has worked at Christie’s, Sotheby’s, and Phillips, contributing to the sale of major masters and the reevaluation of African artists. Olivia serves on the Board of Friends of Palais de Tokyo and leads its Young Patrons initiative. She supports institutions like the Louvre and is involved in developing a new cultural institution in Benin. Anani has contributed to major monographs and was named among France’s 100 Most Influential Women in Culture in 2021.

Willow Defebaugh is the co-founder and editor-in-chief of Atmos, an award-winning climate and culture magazine that seeks to re-enchant people with our shared humanity and the Earth. She is the author of The Overview, a deep ecology newsletter and book, and host of The Nature Of, a podcast from Atmos. She is a lifelong student of nature and graduated with a degree in creative writing from the University of Michigan. Her work has been featured in Vogue, Harper's Bazaar, Teen Vogue, V Magazine, Interview, i-D, BBC, The Guardian, Them, New York Magazine, and more. She lives in Brooklyn on unceded Lenape territory.
