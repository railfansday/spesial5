LLM feedback improves video physics consistency

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Iteratively feeding generated video back into a large language model slashes physical error scores while keeping visual fidelity intact. The twist is that this loop requires only a lightweight post‑generation step, not a full retraining of the video generator.

Before PhysLoop, creators relied on static prompt conditioning or expensive fine‑tuning to coax motion realism out of diffusion models, and even the strongest baseline, Seedance 2.5, still faltered on basic dynamics. Physical supervision was scarce because gathering dense error signals from raw videos is prohibitively costly.

On the PhysLoop‑Gen benchmark, the loop reaches an 85% task‑success rate, eclipsing Seedance 2.5’s 73% score[1]. The gain stems from the LLM’s ability to flag implausible trajectories and request corrective refinements during generation.

Human evaluators also prefer PhysLoop’s physics: it scores 4.06 on physical plausibility and 4.12 on causal consistency, versus 3.80 for both metrics in the baseline, while its visual realism rating (3.72) trails Seedance 2.5’s 4.29[1]. The modest dip in aesthetic score is offset by a noticeable jump in believable motion.

When tasked with repairing flawed clips, PhysLoop attains an 86.00% success rate, dwarfing the strongest standalone baseline’s 19.33% performance[1]. The repair loop isolates offending frames, queries the LLM for corrective code, and re‑synthesizes only those segments.

The approach does not eliminate visual quality loss; the lower realism rating indicates that LLM‑driven edits can introduce subtle texture artefacts. Moreover, the method hinges on the LLM’s physics knowledge, which may be incomplete for niche domains or longer temporal horizons, leaving scalability and domain‑specific fidelity as open questions[1].

PhysOPSD reaches a similar conclusion from a different angle: executable code used as a privileged physical context serves as an effective means to improve plausibility that “consistently improves physical plausibility across backbones with high sample efficiency, while preserving general video quality” and “smooths knowledge transfer via an intermediate information bottleneck”[2]. This convergent evidence suggests dense, programmatic supervision is the key lever for physics‑aware generation.

If these findings hold, any production pipeline that already deploys a diffusion‑based video model should prepend a lightweight LLM repair stage at inference time rather than retrain from scratch. The released PhysLoop‑Repair benchmark provides an immediate yardstick to validate such post‑hoc improvements.
