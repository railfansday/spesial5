Senators press acting U.S. Navy Secretary on Iran war costs, sailor health in confirmation hearing

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Acting U.S. Navy Secretary Hung Cao's confirmation hearing before the Senate Armed Services Committee led to some heated exchanges as he was grilled about sailors' health and the Iran war.

The Navy changed condition codes on munitions previously classified as expired in order to use them, Cao told senators Tuesday, as lawmakers questioned the conflict's strain on weapons stocks and naval deployments. He also said the military had increased production of key weapons.

"When I was in uniform, we only produced 125 Tomahawks a year. Right now, we're well above 60 every month," he said, adding that the military was working to increase SM-6 missile production and bring in additional ammunition manufacturers.

Cao's confirmation hearing comes as the Republicans, who hold a majority in both the House and Senate, are coming to the end of their time in power in both houses. The Democratic Party flipped many seats in the midterm elections to win a comfortable majority in the next session starting in January. President Donald Trump's nominees for offices, and his legislative agenda, will face much tougher challenges then.

Cao said the Navy was accelerating work on unmanned surface vessels to relieve the 17 destroyers supporting the blockade of Iran's ships, in hopes of reducing the strain on the Fifth Fleet. But Senators still pointed to the war's effect on the Navy's resources.

"For the first time in many years, the United States has no forward-deployed carrier in the Pacific at this moment," Sen. Jack Reed (D-R.I.) noted.

Cao acknowledged that the war was "stressing our naval resources." However, he argued that the demonstration of combined American military capabilities had "really set China back on their heels," a characterization Sen. Mazie Hirono (D-Hawaii) disputed.

Lawmakers also pressed Cao on the war's financial costs, with Cao appearing to agree with Sen. Elizabeth Warren's (D-Mass.) estimate of approximately $32.5 million a day in costs to maintain the blockade on Iranian ports.

The demands of prolonged deployments have raised concerns about sailors' access to food and basic supplies, as Cao told senators that the destroyer USS Gonzalez had a week of rations remaining.

"Usually, I don't like them to go below two weeks of supplies," he said, while insisting the provisions were adequate and ships had never run out of food.

When Sen. Mark Kelly (D-Ariz.) asked about reports of sailors losing 45 pounds, Cao stated that the food preferences might not be to certain sailors' liking.

He likened it to his children opening the refrigerator door and saying "there's nothing to eat here."

"Losing 45 pounds is more serious than just not liking the food. Our sailors are being asked to serve thousands of miles from home for months at a time. We have a responsibility to make sure they have what they need to do their jobs and stay healthy," Kelly posted on X.

Senators also questioned Cao about eight suicide attempts across the USS Abraham Lincoln carrier strike group during its deployment. Cao defended the Navy's mental health support, describing the incidents as "eight suicide mitigations" because personnel had intervened.

Sen. Kirsten Gillibrand (D-N.Y.) called the term "mitigations" an "insult," saying they should be called suicide attempts.

The questioning followed an earlier acknowledgment by the top U.S. commander in the Middle East of mental health concerns aboard the Lincoln.

Cao pledged that the entire Department of the Navy would achieve a clean financial audit by the end of the fiscal year. The Pentagon has had nine consecutive failures to obtain a clean department-wide audit opinion, according to Reuters.

"We owe the American public to tell them exactly where their money is," he said.
