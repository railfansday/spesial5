James can still take over ‘any given moment,' and Sixers trust he'll pick right spots

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

James can still take over 'any given moment,' and Sixers trust he'll pick right spots originally appeared on NBC Sports Philadelphia

LeBron James isn't oblivious to the calendar and the clock.

He knows he'll turn 42 years old next month, he's ancient by NBA standards and decline is inevitable.

James also knows who he is and what he still can do, whatever the norms are for players of a certain age.

"I think his battle against Father Time is one of the most impressive things he's done over the course of his entire career," Heat head coach Erik Spoelstra said Wednesday before Miami's loss to the Sixers. "We all know and everybody always says that Father Time always wins. But right now he's giving Father Time hell."

James went on to record his first triple-double with the 15-4 Sixers, posting 25 points, 12 assists and 10 rebounds. The four-time MVP is at 126 regular-season triple-doubles and counting in his career.

Only Russell Westbrook, Nikola Jokic, Oscar Robertson and Magic Johnson have more in NBA history. James is 17 above Philadelphia native and Sixers legend Wilt Chamberlain.

With Joel Embiid sidelined by illness for a second consecutive game and Jaylen Brown ruled out at halftime with left hamstring tightness, James' do-it-all performance was timely. Tyrese Maxey's 30-point, six-assist outing was essential, too.

"I'm just trying to play the game the right way," James said. "I don't think my job is to dominate the game every night for this ball club. We have a Finals MVP (Brown), we have a regular-season MVP (Embiid), we have an up-and-coming superstar at point guard, we've got a young fella in VJ (Edgecombe) who's up and coming as well.

"My dues are paid with what I've done. If they need me to dust off the cape from time to time, then I can still accomplish that. But that's not my job. … But if they need it, I can do it at any given moment."

James' night featured a flurry of very deep three-pointers in the second quarter.

He started the season 10 for 46 beyond the arc (21.7 percent), but his percentage has risen to 31.6 with two sweet-shooting games in a row.

"The work has been put in and I'm not ever worried about anything as far as my game," he said. "The difference between the start of the season and now is that my legs are feeling better, my ramp-up is feeling better.

"I'm about to be 42 years old in a month. S—, it takes a little bit longer for the ramp-up, but I've been feeling pretty good as of late. I don't ever second-guess myself … because I know the work that I put in."

While James' rebounding was perhaps the least spectacular aspect of his game, Sixers head coach Nick Nurse appreciated his work on the boards.

James has remained a statistically solid defensive rebounder into his 40s and he's one reason why the Sixers have made a major improvement in that department. According to Cleaning the Glass, the Sixers' opponents have grabbed 28.4 percent of their own misses, which ranks sixth in the NBA. The team ranked 27th last season.

Asked about James' cape comments, Maxey shouted out, "Dust it off then!" with a grin.

Maxey's not sure exactly when James will dramatically elevate his game, but he trusts the 22-time All-Star to pick the right spots.

"I don't know," Maxey said. "Early in the game, he was telling me he was tired. I said, 'Well, we don't have time for you to be tired.'

"But he does a good job of just managing the game. He knows when he needs to score, he knows when he needs to pass. We're going to need a lot from him. I know he's seasoned, but we expect a lot of him and he does that."
