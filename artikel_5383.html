Bitcoin Overnight Rally Roundtripped: Saylor and Strategy Didn’t Flinch

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Bitcoin price rocketed to the moon and back overnight; its rally disappeared almost as quickly as it arrived. BTC briefly jumped above $64,000 before surrendering its gains and slipping back toward $61,000. Crypto liquidity is still thin even with the boost from Nvidia news.

Michael Saylor, however, isn’t backing away from Bitcoin. Strategy disclosed another 457 BTC purchase worth $29.6 million last week, taking its total holdings to 851,305 BTC. The company paid an average of $64,898 per coin, leaving its purchase underwater after Bitcoin’s sharp retreat.

Strategy continues accumulating while the market struggles to sustain even a relief rally. Was this just another crypto dead cat bounce driven by macro news?

Strategy has acquired 457 $BTC and repurchased $212M of $STRC. We increased our Digital Credit Securities Repurchase Program by $1B and reallocated $1B from our USD Reserve to USD Cash. As of 11/22/26, we hold 851,305 BTC and $4.8B of USD Assets. $MSTR https://t.co/jqhYrehyTl

Strategy’s latest buying came during a brutal stretch for Bitcoin. BTC briefly plunged toward $55,000 over the weekend, reaching its lowest level in more than two years. The subsequent recovery lifted prices above $60,000, but buyers haven’t managed to turn that rebound into a convincing reversal.

Saylor had already hinted at another purchase with his familiar Bitcoin accumulation tracker. His post, captioned “The dots aren’t done,” signaled that. The latest filing confirmed the purchase, but it also exposed the mounting gap between Strategy’s average cost and the market price.

Strategy is buying Bitcoin through a difficult market. Its latest purchases were funded partly through stock sales, while the company also spent heavily on preferred-share repurchases. That capital allocation matters as Strategy balances Bitcoin accumulation against its financing obligations.

Bitcoin’s overnight surge briefly pushed its price above $64,000, but the recovery quickly collapsed. The move followed a dramatic turnaround in technology stocks, yet Bitcoin failed to hold the momentum. BTC slipped back toward $61,000, erasing much of its intraday progress.

The market’s weakness goes beyond one failed rally. Bitcoin has faced heavy ETF withdrawals, elevated volatility, and growing pressure from macroeconomic uncertainty. Money is also watching upcoming US inflation data, which could shift expectations for interest rates and risk appetite.

Bitcoin’s rebound last night was accompanied by falling open interest. This could mean that some of the move came from markets closing short positions and not fresh leverage entering the market. That leaves bulls needing stronger spot demand to establish a more durable recovery.

Bitcoin now faces a difficult test around the $62,000–$64,000 region. Reclaiming that zone would give buyers a chance to challenge higher resistance. Failure to recover it could leave BTC vulnerable to another retest of the weekend lows.

Saylor’s buying offers a bullish counterpoint, yet Strategy can keep accumulating while Bitcoin continues falling. That makes the next purchase less interesting than the price action that follows it.
