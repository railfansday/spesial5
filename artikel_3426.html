There's a chasm between critics and audiences that occasionally becomes wider and more noticeable. For instance, several of Dwayne Johnson's recent movies — Red One, Black Adam, and even the Moana live-action remake — have all earned solid audience scores on Rotten Tomatoes despite having been poorly reviewed. The reverse of this phenomenon is equally prevalent. Just as a star can get their army of fans to rally behind a critically panned film, fans can also organize a mob to take down any film or show that goes against their values and beliefs.

This act of targeted hate designed to affect a film or show's popularity rating on an aggregator website has come to be known as review bombing. Typically, such campaigns are orchestrated by disgruntled fans who object to the most basic progressive ideas, such as inclusivity and diversity. This is one of the reasons why titles that have been most affected by review-bombing tend to feature actors or filmmakers from minority groups. Take, for example, Star Wars: Episode VIII — The Last Jedi, the series The Acolyte, and HBO's The Last of Us.

One of the biggest examples of audiences outright rejecting a major new project and making their dislike felt online is The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power. The mega-budget streaming series recently returned with a third season on Prime Video, and while the reviews have been mostly solid, the fan reaction has been predictably unpleasant.

The Rings of Power premiered in 2022 amid reports that Prime Video invested $250 million before the show's release and committed $1 billion over five seasons. The first season of the show clocked 150 million views, according to Amazon, but reports suggested that the series struggled to retain audiences for the duration of its eight-episode run. The second season saw similar issues. The third season is getting an unusual rollout, beginning with four episodes out of the gate, with two more the next week and the final two the week after that.

The Rings of Power holds an overall 83% critics' score on the aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes, but its audience score is languishing at 53%. The first season was most affected, with a 38% audience score based on more than 25,000 ratings. The third season, on the other hand, holds a 68% audience score. Viewers have been comparing the show to Peter Jackson's landmark film series, while also questioning some of the creative decisions and casting choices.
