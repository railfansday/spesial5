15 Movies To Watch if You Love 'MobLand'

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Guy Ritchie has long been the go-to Guy when it comes to British crime movies. Ever since his 1998 feature-length debut, Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels, it has been clear that this British director has a keen eye for the wickedly violent and the devilishly funny. With the premiere of the second season of The Gentlemen and Prime Video's Young Sherlock, there's no better time to be a Ritchie fan.

This is especially true when considering the unquestionable success of his latest foray into the world of crime, MobLand (the series was created by Ronan Bennett, with Ritchie attached as a director). With the stars aligning to take on Ritchie's big-budget caper, including Tom Hardy, Pierce Brosnan, Helen Mirren, and Paddy Considine, this modern-day series has received plenty of high praise thus far, including a strong 83% approval rating on review aggregator Rotten Tomatoes. For fans craving more movies like MobLand, several films deliver the same beats. With all that in mind, here's a look at the best movies to watch if you love MobLand.

There's no better place to start when recommending a movie following MobLand than 2019's The Gentlemen, not to be confused with Ritchie's series of the same name. Directed by Ritchie, the film follows Matthew McConaughey's American expat who, while trying to find the highest bidder for his marijuana empire, ends up sparking all-out criminal chaos.

Featuring a star-studded ensemble worthy of rivaling MobLand's — including the aforementioned McConaughey, Charlie Hunnam, Jeremy Strong, Hugh Grant, and more — The Gentlemen is a fast-paced, stylish run through the best of Ritchie. Effortlessly funny and often brutal, this is a winding crime web spun to perfection.

Mobland's brilliance lies in its distinct mix of witty charm and hard-hitting crime action, making the overall experience an absolute blast to watch, a strength shared by the underrated 2000s crime comedy Smokin' Aces. The film follows a magician turned mafia informant on the run after the mob gets word of his betrayal, placing a $1 million bounty on him as well as looking to take him for their own personal reasons.

The initial setup is about as classic as crime comedies get, seeing various personalities with ties to the mob converging and facing off in an attempt to make it on a high bounty. However, as the intricacies of these crimes and an array of shocking twists come to light, it becomes largely apparent that the film is hiding a much larger web of lies, deception, and madness between intersecting parties. Similar to Mobland, the film has an absolute slam-dunk cast to help elevate its story, featuring the likes of Ben Affleck, Ray Liotta, Jeremy Piven, Ryan Reynolds, Alicia Keys, and Jason Bateman. —Robert Lee

For those who like Hardy in MobLand, Legend is the next port of call. The man behind fixer Harry Da Souza takes on not one but two of the most infamous criminals in British history, portraying both of the Kray twins in this journey through their lavish, tragic lives in 1960s London.

Perhaps Hardy's best performance to date, his ability to switch between both twins, offering evolving and nuanced perspectives on both their lives, is nothing short of remarkable. Add to that a stellar supporting cast, a neat script, and several action scenes that live long in the memory, and a recipe for British crime movie success is born.

Mobland doesn't simply follow a singular mob family, but instead the infighting and squabbles that come up as a result of conflicting mob families, a facet that has consistently found strength within crime storytelling. Lucky Number Slevin takes this classic archetype and provides the engaging dark comedy of taking a completely unrelated third party, throwing him in the middle of this conflict, but having completely mistaken him for a friend who is in deep with the mob.

It covers a similarly dark comedy tone that Mobland expertly utilizes, with Lucky Number Slevin getting a lot of comedic mileage out of not just the inherent miscommunication of its concept, but all of the different moving pieces and wild characters that end up intertwined in this dilemma. The film also features a standout cast, including Josh Hartnett, Morgan Freeman, Bruce Willis, Lucy Liu, Ben Kingsley, and Stanley Tucci. —Robert Lee

Brosnan's patriarch, Conrad Harrigan, is one of the standout characters in MobLand, with Die Another Day the best next stop for the actor at his suave and stylish best. The movie follows Brosnan as the iconic James Bond as he attempts to bring down an international criminal enterprise that plans to build a world-threatening space weapon.

Brosnan is still as effortlessly cool as ever in MobLand, but rewind 23 years to 2002, and his portrayal of 007 marks the actor's peak. An action-packed Bond entry that also marked the end of an era for the series, Die Another Day might usher in an even greater period for the franchise, but let that take nothing away from just how impressive it is on its own.

While Mobland was undeniably the standout gangster story worth watching in 2025, one other gangster film from the year that parallels its strengths in distinct ways is Caught Stealing, the most recent film from Darren Aronofsky. Similar to Lucky Number Slevin, the film follows an unaffiliated third party who finds himself at the center of an ongoing feud between a bunch of different criminal groups. From Russian mobsters to Hasidic brothers to corrupt police, all of the different moving pieces prove to make the film that much more of a striking watch.

Leading star Austin Butler is one of the key selling points of the film; he exceptionally navigates the chaos and death that constantly follow him both as a result of these criminals and the guilt of his past haunting him. The aesthetic of '90s New York also does wonders for the feel and look of the film, harkening back to iconic New York crime movies of the '90s like A Bronx Tale and Carlito's Way. —Robert Lee

For many, Snatch is Ritchie's magnum opus. Featuring a cast that includes a young Stephen Graham and Brad Pitt at the peak of his powers, Snatch follows the theft of a priceless diamond and the many invested criminal minds attempting to beat the competition and track it down.

Snatch feels like Ritchie let loose. Packed from start to finish with iconic one-liners, unapologetically violent action, and even a terrifying Irish action from Pitt for good measure, Snatch is an enigmatic, bloody imprint on Ritchie's wonderful legacy. MobLand might show Ritchie at his most mature, but Snatch will showcase him at his best.

Your nemesis does not want to beat you. He wants to know you completely, and he is patient enough to wait for it. The frightening part is how good the conversation is.

Your nemesis is not after money, territory or revenge. He is after a demonstration, and you are the person he has chosen to prove his point on.

Your nemesis respects you, which sounds flattering until you work out what it means. He chose you because nobody else was worth his time.

Your nemesis has no personal interest in you whatsoever, and that is precisely what makes him unsurvivable. You are not a rival. You are a stop on the way.

While this crime comedy certainly leans more toward comedy than crime in its tone, the overwhelming amount of violence and dark humor present in Seven Psychopaths helps the film never stray away from its roots as an unrestrained story of crime. The setup is about as goofy as a crime story gets, with a screenwriter getting into hot water with the Los Angeles criminal underworld after his friends kidnap a gangster's beloved Shih Tzu. However, the pain and carnage that this kidnapping sets in motion is very real, emphasized by the immaculate directing of Martin McDonagh.

This strange blend of killers, blackmail, and extravagant personalities at times feels like a distillation of classic gangster films down into an absurdist satire on crime culture, Hollywood, and everything in between. The film was largely celebrated when it first released, but has largely been lost in the shadows due to McDonagh's career being elevated to Academy prestige in his subsequent films. Still, there is a charm and brilliance within Seven Psychopaths that makes it still worth watching almost 15 years later. —Robert Lee

MobLand boasts an extremely talented cast, with one of the more underrated performances in the series coming from Considine. Back in 2004, Considine once again put in a highly underrated supporting turn as Phil in Paweł Pawlikowski's My Summer of Love. Two young women from either end of the class spectrum end up entwined in a burning romance across a hot British summer, only for the reality of their situation to rear its ugly head.

Considine's performance as the reformed criminal Phil in My Summer of Love underpins almost every emotional beat in the story. Perhaps underrated due to this movie marking the breakout of one Emily Blunt, experiencing Considine's skin-crawling turn with knowledge of his impeccable career that followed certainly makes this a worthy follow-up to MobLand.

MobLand is seen as this glorious collaboration between English filmmaking talent, most notably with director Guy Ritchie and A-list leading star Tom Hardy. However, this actually isn't the first time that these two have collaborated on a creative work, as Hardy appears as one of many icons of English cinema in RocknRolla, one of Ritchie's most often forgotten crime comedy films. The film sees various members of London's criminal underworld pursuing their share of a lucrative fortune brought about by a Russian mobster's real estate scam.

Hardy's role in the film isn't front and center, as it is largely balanced with its prominent cast of other English talent, including Gerard Butler, Mark Strong, Idris Elba, and Thandiwe Newton. Still, even in this side role, released 17 years before they would collaborate again in MobLand, Hardy fits into Ritchie's signature style of directing perfectly. It certainly also helps that MobLand and RocknRolla are both crime stories that act as a workaround for exceptional English ensembles. —Robert Lee
