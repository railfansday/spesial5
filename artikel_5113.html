Bitcoin, XRP Hit by $875 Million Squeeze After Nvidia Euphoria Fades: Main Crypto News This Morning

On Tuesday morning, the crypto market gave back almost all of the gains it made after Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as its CEO on Monday. According to CoinMarketCap data, the price of Bitcoin stood at around $60,000 on Tuesday morning, and on Monday evening, Bitcoin was trading as high as $64,372. XRP was back at $0.93 on Tuesday morning after the cryptocurrency reached $1.026 on Monday.

The price of Ethereum was between $1,680 and $1,695 on Tuesday morning, according to CoinMarketCap. In addition, the Crypto Fear and Greed Index stood at 10 on Tuesday, and the index showed extreme fear in the market.

U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs had $569.7 million in net outflows on Monday, Nov. 23, SoSoValue data shows. Spot XRP ETFs in the U.S. also saw outflows on Monday, and the total amount of these outflows was $2.5 million.

On Monday, Nvidia's board voted to bring Jensen Huang back as CEO after input from senior levels of the U.S. government, according to the company's press release. Nvidia shares closed 31.5% higher at $165.05, and the company added $954.3 billion in market value in one session. That is more than twice the previous one-day record of $450 billion that Microsoft set in July.

The S&P 500 gained 4.73% on Monday, and the Nasdaq 100 gained 6.86%. Bitcoin followed the rally and rose to $64,372 on Monday evening, and XRP reached $1.026.

Nvidia shares were down 4.1% at $158.03 in premarket trading on Tuesday, and Nasdaq 100 futures lost 1.62% on Tuesday morning. The VIX rose 8.72% to 28.93 at the same time.

Anthropic shares traded below the $83 IPO price on Tuesday. Earlier, all seven co-founders of the AI company filed to sell a combined $1.96 billion worth of Anthropic stock, or about $280 million each, and there was also news about internal safety problems at Anthropic.

According to CoinGlass data, 146,356 traders were liquidated over the past 24 hours, and the total amount of liquidations reached $875 million.

Short sellers were hit first during Monday's rally, and short positions accounted for $473 million of the total. The largest single liquidation was a $17.83 million BTC-USD position on Hyperliquid. On Tuesday morning, it was the turn of long positions. Over the past 12 hours, longs accounted for $168.85 million out of $207.37 million in liquidations.

Bitcoin dropped as low as $59,366 and was trading at around $60,300 on Tuesday morning, down 1.5% over 24 hours. Bitcoin liquidations amounted to $372.68 million. In addition, open interest in Bitcoin futures rose 1.31% to $39.49 billion as the price was going down.

XRP followed Bitcoin and fell from $1.026 to $0.9238 before it stabilized at around $0.93. XRP liquidations reached $14.05 million over 24 hours, and 3,350 traders were affected. The Coinbase premium for XRP was -0.13%, and the premium for XRP in South Korea was 1.56%.

BitMEX co-founder Arthur Hayes also commented on the Nvidia rally. According to Hayes, Nvidia's rise is the worst-case scenario for crypto portfolios. Hayes said that crypto needs a crash in U.S. stocks that would force the Fed to start printing money again.

The U.S. Department of Justice has filed a forfeiture complaint against more than $1.1 billion in USDC stablecoins. The funds were stolen from Binance by North Korean hackers on Oct. 29, and Circle helped the authorities with the case. According to the Department of Justice, North Korean hackers stole more than $6 billion from Binance in the attack. Earlier in November, the department said that U.S. authorities and private companies had frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen funds.

The Ethereum Foundation also addressed the threat of artificial intelligence. According to the foundation, AI is a more urgent threat to the network than quantum computers. In a blog post, the EF developers said that AI finds vulnerabilities in code faster than auditors can check them, and they also said that AI could break mathematical algorithms that have been used for decades.

EF researcher Justin Drake also spoke about Ethereum's move to hash-based cryptography at Devcon 8 in Mumbai. Drake said that he hoped to complete the transition by December 2028. Earlier, the Ethereum Foundation set December 2029 as the deadline for quantum resistance in its roadmap.

There was other news from regulators and banks as well:

According to Grayscale, the current bear market for Bitcoin has lasted 411 days, and it is the longest one in history. Still, Grayscale called it the mildest one. Bitcoin fell 56% from its October 2025 peak of $124,824 to last week's low of $55,254, according to Grayscale.

Galaxy Research put the drawdown at 53%. According to Galaxy, Bitcoin lost 85%, 84% and 77% in the bear markets of 2013–2015, 2017–2018 and 2021–2022, respectively.

The market capitalization of altcoins excluding Bitcoin and Ethereum dropped to $630.71 billion on Tuesday.

According to CoinGlass data, large buy orders for Bitcoin on OKX and Binance were placed between $59,137 and $59,366 on Tuesday, and $30.3 million worth of long positions could be liquidated in the same range. A large sell order for Bitcoin was placed on Hyperliquid at $60,682, and $64.4 million worth of short positions could be liquidated near $63,996.

The max pain price for Bitcoin options expiring on Nov. 25 stood at $61,000, and the notional value of these options was $642.3 million.

For XRP, liquidations data shows a buy order on Gate at $0.9240 and $577,200 worth of long positions that could be liquidated at $0.9187. Sell orders for XRP on Gate and Hyperliquid were placed between $0.9340 and $0.9444, and $1.4 million worth of short positions could be liquidated at $0.9887.

Two-year U.S. inflation swaps fell to 2.09% ahead of the PCE report, the lowest level for the swaps since September 2024.
