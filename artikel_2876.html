Intuitive Machines vs. Redwire: Which Space Infrastructure Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Intuitive Machines leans heavily on NASA contracts for its lunar missions, while Redwire's business is anchored by national security work, a very different risk profile.

By Brendan Coffey – Updated Nov 23, 2026 at 11:41AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Intuitive Machines ›

The race to establish a permanent presence on the moon is creating significant opportunities for space infrastructure companies. Investors are looking at Intuitive Machines (LUNR +11.17%) and Redwire (RDW +14.51%) to lead this new frontier.

Intuitive Machines focuses on lunar surface access and data communication services primarily for civil government agencies. Redwire offers a broader range of hardware and autonomous systems used across national security, commercial, and scientific space missions.

Intuitive Machines provides space infrastructure including spacecraft, lunar delivery services, and data transmission networks. It primarily serves NASA through programs like the Commercial Lunar Payload Services initiative and the Near Space Network. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business, as NASA historically accounts for a significant majority of its revenue. Its work includes participating in the growth of the defense stock market by supporting the Space Development Agency.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached approximately $707 million, representing growth of nearly 221% compared to the prior period. This sharp increase in revenue reflects the scaling of its mission operations and recent business acquisitions. Despite this growth, the company reported a net loss of roughly $128 million for those same twelve months.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the company maintained a current ratio of nearly 5.0x, which measures its ability to cover short-term liabilities with short-term assets. Its debt-to-equity ratio was approximately -0.5x. This figure indicates that total liabilities exceed shareholder equity. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was nearly negative $56.0 million, the cash remaining after the company funds its operations and capital equipment.

Redwire operates as an integrated space technology company that builds infrastructure and autonomous systems for a wide variety of customers. Its business is heavily weighted toward national security, which accounted for approximately 47% of its revenue in its latest annual report. The company also serves major civil agencies like NASA and the European Space Agency, while expanding its commercial footprint in satellite manufacturing. Redwire serves diverse defense clients including the U.S. Army and the U.S. Space Force.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached about $431.9 million, indicating growth of roughly 46% over the previous twelve-month period. This upward trend highlights the steady demand for its space platforms and multi-domain operations. However, the company experienced a net loss of approximately $226.1 million during this period.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, Redwire reported a debt-to-equity ratio of nearly 0.2x, meaning its total debt is approximately 20% of its equity. Its current ratio stood at roughly 1.6x, showing it has more current assets than current liabilities. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was approximately negative $190.8 million. Free cash flow is a measure of the actual cash a business generates after accounting for capital expenditures.

Intuitive Machines faces risks related to its heavy dependence on U.S. government contracts, which can be subject to unexpected funding changes or terminations. The company also requires significant capital to maintain its complex spaceflight operations and integrate the recent Lanteris acquisition. Any failure in its lunar missions could result in substantial financial losses and damage its reputation among commercial and civil partners.

Redwire must navigate the risk that its technology could become obsolete in a rapidly changing aerospace market. In its latest annual report, the company identified material weaknesses in its internal control over financial reporting. It also faces risks from geopolitical instability affecting its European operations and must compete with larger industry players like Lockheed Martin Corp. (LMT +1.07%) and Northrop Grumman Corp. (NOC +1.10%).

Based on the P/S ratio, Intuitive Machines currently trades at a lower valuation multiple than Redwire. The P/S ratio measures a company's market value against its sales over the past twelve months.

Valuation metrics include those sourced from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from those of other data providers.

Intuitive Machines has been posting superb results in fiscal 2026, most recently reporting sequential quarterly growth of 31% to $269 million in the third quarter, reported in early November. Management says they have an order backlog of $1.9 billion, up $1.2 billion from the end of 2025. NASA is moving toward steady access-to-space flights and deliveries, too, which bodes well for the company's longer-term sales. Revenue for fiscal 2026 is expected to more than quadruple to $935 million, though net loss is expected to widen to about $100 million. Analysts expect the business to turn a profit for the first time in 2029.

In April, Redwire was selected as one of 14 vendors (out of a total of 32 bids) on the Space Systems Command $1.8 billion 10-year Andromeda Indefinite Delivery Indefinite Quantity, or IDIQ contract. That's a project to replace aging GPS satellites and upgrade the U.S. space infrastructure to counter emerging threats. The IDIQ win is something management feels moves Redwire up the food chain with the Department of Defense. It could mean significantly more revenue, since U.S. Space Systems Command provided a notice of its intent to raise the total shared ceiling for the Andromeda IDIQ to more than $6 billion to meet increased demand.

In the near term, Wall Street projects Redwire to post fiscal 2026 revenue of around $463 million, representing about 38% growth over the prior year. In addition, the business has an order backlog of $620 million. The business is still expected to post net losses for the foreseeable future, but they are trending in the right direction.

These are both exciting companies contributing to the build-out of U.S. space infrastructure. Intuitive Machines' larger order backlog is appealing, offering investors a more stable pathway for the next year. At a lower price-to-sales ratio compared to Redwire, Intuitive Machines is the better choice.
