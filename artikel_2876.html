Good Morning, Illini Nation: Finding diamonds in the rough

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Western Kentucky coach Hank Plona has seen some of the same players on the recruiting trail that have, perhaps unexpectedly, wound up in Champaign. The way the Illinois coaching staff evaluates, Plona told reporters earlier this week, is notable.

The big get, of course, was Keaton Wagler. The unheralded guard out of Shawnee, Kan., wound up a top five pick after just a single season with the Illini. Before that?

"I watched Keaton Wagler play against (current Western Kentucky guard) Armelo Boone in some back gym in Georgia," Plona said. "(Wagler's AAU coach Victor Williams) said nobody is on him. I said, 'We can't get that kid. That kid is unbelievable,' and he said, 'Nobody is recruiting him.' Sure enough, a month later he's going to Illinois."

Ethan Brown is another player Plona said he remembered watching in high school. Whether the 6-foot-4 guard plays against Western Kentucky remains to be seen after surgery to repair his broken nose Wednesday, but what the Rolla, Mo., native has done for Illinois this season has impressed the Hilltoppers' coach.

"I don't think he's considered on whatever list of players, but when you watch him play he reminds me of Curtis Jones, a kid I coached at Indian Hills, who was in the NBA last year," Plona said. Jones went on to play at Buffalo and Iowa State before playing 10 games for the Denver Nuggets last season while also suiting up in the G League.

"Ethan Brown throws the ball up the floor to the first open guy so fast," Plona continued. "If he sees it, that pass is gone. Forget his percentages. The really good guards, you see it in rebounding, you see it in steals and you see it in assists. They're so in the moment making the right play all the time. That's clearly what Ethan Brown does in addition to being able to shoot the ball.

"I saw a little bit where people were surprised at the beginning of the year when he was starting. I know Coach Underwood is going to start the best players and play the best players and trust the best players and for sure trust them at the point. (Brown's) willingness to pass the ball and just his vision and feel and presence is very, very impressive to watch."

Quentin Coleman had a higher profile coming out of Principia (Mo.) — a five-star background that's shifted into lottery pick projections. The way he's given Illinois a potent 1-2 with Andrej Stojakovic in terms of ability to put pressure on the rim has also stood out to Plona.

"They're patient," the WKU coach said. "They're so physical, and they draw contact. It's hard to figure out. It's, 'Oh, keep them out of the paint.' Yeah? How? They get to their spots. That's an NBA phrase. The best players in the NBA get to their spots. I would say Stojakovic and Coleman get to their spots. They get to where they want on the floor. Their pace, their handle and their timing is all very, very professional."
