Rapid Recap: Trail Blazers 124, Bucks 114

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

In a game with plenty of highs and lows, the Milwaukee Bucks lost to the Portland Trail Blazers 124-114. On the second night of their back-to-back, the Bucks came out lethargic, but frankly, so did the Blazers. Portland gave Milwaukee plenty of chances to blow the game open, but they refused to take them, and in the end, Portland's vets knew what to do when the game was on the line. On the positive side, Nate Ament dropped a career-high 18 points on 6/11 shooting, while the Blazers were led by old friend Damian Lillard, who scored a season-high 33.

Early on, this looked like a game where the Bucks were on the second night of a back-to-back and the Blazers were deep into a road trip, with intensity lacking on both sides. Tyler Herro looked rough, losing the ball and even missing free throws. Thankfully, Nate Ament was the spark, scoring seven of the first nine points as the Bucks took an early lead. Without Deni Avdija, the Blazers looked considerably less dangerous offensively but still stayed close thanks to their defence as they kept forcing turnovers. Of course, it didn't help that the Bucks, yet again, couldn't make a free throw to save themselves, going 2/7 (!) in the opening quarter. And to top it all off, Gary Trent Jr. went for one of his signature steal attempts on the final play and missed, leaving Scoot Henderson open for three to give his team a 24-21 lead—really disappointing stuff.

It was more of the same bad Bucks basketball to open the second: more turnovers, missed free throws, and bad fouls as the Blazers quickly upped their lead to double digits, 32-22. Thank goodness for Nate Ament, though, who poured in a quick five points—paired with triples from Tyler Herro and Kel'el Ware—to reduce Milwaukee's deficit to just one at the six-minute mark as they went on a 12-4 run, down 40-39. Both teams traded leads for the next few minutes, with Brayden Burries really starting to get in his bag, nailing a catch-and-shoot three and following it up with a smooth deceleration move in the lane for the and-one. The Bucks finally played with some verve as Kasparas Jakucionis began to make his mark, nailing a transition three and a put-back as the home side took a 55-47 lead with 3:30 to play. Of course, Scoot Henderson, who was shooting 20.0% from three coming into the game, went 4/4 in the first half as Portland closed on a 15-3 run to take a 60-58 lead into halftime.

The Bucks started the third with great energy on both ends. They opened with a perfectly run post-entry play where Herro got the ball off a screen for a three; then, a few plays later, Burries stole the ball, and Herro drained another three—this time from the corner—Bucks up 68-63 after two minutes. Portland brought Jrue Holiday in to stem the tide, and, like clockwork, the Blazers responded with a 15-2 extended run. Yep. HOWEVER, the Bucks didn't go quietly as the second unit came in and offered a real spark, cutting the deficit back to three with a few minutes remaining. Crucial late plays from Jakucionis and Rollins put the Blazers up 96-92 after three.

After playing his first minutes of the season last night and making a great impression, Caris LeVert opened the fourth with an elite defensive play, stealing the ball from Dame and converting on the and-one. A few plays later came the sequence of the night: Rollins flipped it to Ware for the baseline poster, then Rollins ripped Dame in the backcourt for the lefty finish, then Burries stonewalled Henderson in the backcourt to force the eight-second violation—Fiserv was rocking! The Blazers would fight back to extend their lead to six, but the Bucks answered with an 8-0 spurt made entirely off of buckets from Ware and Burries to take a 108-106 lead with six minutes to play! But of course, Jrue Holiday—who had made big shots all night—dropped consecutive buckets to give Portland back the edge, leading 112-108. At the three-minute mark, after buckets from Ament and Herro, it was a one-point game, but poor backcourt composure allowed Portland extend their lead to seven, which was enough in the end. Damn.

The Bucks won the possession game by a huge margin (91-77), but they just couldn't convert enough open shots. They were particularly horrid in the first quarter, shooting 8/21.

Trade Kel'el Ware. Trade Myles Turner. Trade Jericho Sims. Pete Nance is the only big you will ever need. Team-high +8 and one of just two Bucks with a positive rating.
