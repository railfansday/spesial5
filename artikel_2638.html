Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, OKC Thunder snap three-game skid with convincing win over Kings

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

SACRAMENTO, CA — Keegan Murray drilled a 3-pointer during the third quarter, signaling the start of a traditional celebration inside Golden 1 Center.

"Keeeeegan," the public address announcer hollered.

"Muuuuurray," the Sacramento Kings crowd chanted back.

But the celebration didn't last long. It ended on the very next play, as Shai Gilgeous-Alexander shook his defender with a stepback before connecting on a triple of his own.

That silenced everyone inside the arena. There's no better feeling when on the road. And it must've hit even harder for Gilgeous-Alexander and the OKC Thunder, who've been surrounded by a lot of outside noise lately.

OKC entered Sunday's game against Sacramento on a three-game skid. And while some of that could be overlooked due to the fact that it's short-handed, there was no excusing its recent loss to the struggling Los Angeles Clippers.

But OKC got back on track with a 125-103 win. A much-needed victory to finish a four-game road trip, which is the longest road trip of the season for the Thunder (9-5).

Gilgeous-Alexander led OKC with 37 points, two assists and two rebounds in just 29 minutes on 13-for-20 shooting from the field (4 for 5 from deep). Aday Mara also recorded 16 points, 13 rebounds and three assists in 25 minutes on 8-for-11 shooting from the field.

Ajay Mitchell looked just fine when he walked onto the Golden 1 Center floor on Sunday night.

There was no hitch in his stride after missing the last two games due to a sprained left ankle. And when the ball tipped up, there was nothing that kept him from hitting the ground running either.

Mitchell came out of the gates hot by hitting a triple for OKC's first points of the night. Then he showcased his mid-range game by sinking a 10-foot floater. Then he flashed his facilitating skills by finding Gilgeous-Alexander for an easy bucket. Then he displayed his defensive skills by intercepting a pass.

It only took five minutes of game time for Mitchell to show just how valuable he is to OKC. The 24-year-old guard does it all, and it was all missed during his brief absence.

Mitchell continued to play well throughout the night. He finished with 14 points, five assists, three rebounds and two steals in 27 minutes on 6-for-14 shooting from the field (1 for 4 from deep).

It's the public address announcer's job to hype up everything the home team does, just like it's his job to downplay everything the road team does.

The opposition's starting lineup is said swiftly, as if the tipoff time isn't already late. And when the game does start, every bucket is announced just as blandly.

But the Kings' public address announcer made an exception midway through the first quarter of Sunday's game against the Thunder. He tapped into the same electric energy for Nique Clifford checking in that he does with Sacramento's players.

He was a Sacramento player as of last week, after all.

After being traded to OKC in exchange for two second-round picks last Sunday, Clifford made his return to Sacramento exactly one week later. And he received plenty of love, not just from the public address announcer but his former teammates and the Kings fans in attendance.

Clifford contributed nine points, three rebounds and one steal in 23 minutes on 3-for-4 shooting from the field.

Sunday's game wasn't just a return for Clifford. It was also a return for Jared McCain, who's from Sacramento. And he had to wait a lot longer than one week to come back home.

Despite being in his third season, McCain hadn't yet played an NBA game in Sacramento. He was supposed to do so as a rookie with the Philadelphia 76ers, but he underwent season-ending surgery to repair a torn meniscus in his left knee about two weeks before their road game against the Kings.

McCain then made a full recovery as a sophomore last season, and Philadelphia was scheduled to travel to Sacramento on March 19. But he was then traded to the Thunder, which had already played its lone road game against the Kings, on Feb. 4.

McCain finally got his homecoming game on Sunday, though. And while he missed all four field goal attempts during the first quarter, he didn't mind having to wait a bit longer to hit his first Sacramento shot. It came at the 9:46 mark of the second quarter, as he lost his defender with a stepback before sinking a 14-footer.

McCain ultimately recorded 12 points, two steals and one assist in 30 minutes on 3-for-12 shooting from the field (1 for 8 from deep). Not a huge night on the stat sheet, but a huge night nonetheless for the 22-year-old guard and those close to him who attended the game.
