NASCAR Cup Series viewership hits all-time low in 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The 2026 NASCAR Cup Series season averaged 2.42 million viewers, down 2% from 2025 and setting a new all-time low for the series.

NASCAR's premier division wrapped up its 36-race points-paying schedule at Homestead-Miami on Sunday, with Kyle Larson capturing his third Cup Series championship. The title race, however, had been decided by the end of Stage 1. The race averaged 2.47 million viewers on NBC, down 8% from last year's season finale at Phoenix and marking the least-watched Cup Series season finale on record.

For perspective, the last time the Cup Series held its season finale at Homestead-Miami, in 2019, the race averaged 3.74 million viewers on NBC. That's a substantial decline over just seven years.

The 10-race Chase, which returned for the first time since 2013, averaged 1.79 million viewers across USA Network (seven races) and NBC (three races), down 4% from last year's playoffs (1.87 million). Five Chase races posted year-over-year increases, mostly at the start of the postseason, while the other five declined. Several double-digit drops ultimately outweighed the gains.

NASCAR secured a significant increase in rights fees through its current media rights deals, which began in 2025. However, that came alongside more fragmentation in the sport's distribution, with most Cup Series races now airing on cable television. The cable portion of the 2026 Cup Series schedule, consisting of 24 races (including the All-Star race), averaged 1.94 million viewers, down 5% from 2025 (2.03 million).

On the broadcast side, which contained 10 races, including The Clash, viewership averaged 3.57 million, down 4% from 2025 (3.73 million). One additional race aired on broadcast television compared to last season, thanks to the spring Kansas race moving from FS1 to Fox.

Turning to streaming, which included the five-race summer stretch on Prime Video, the Cup Series averaged 2.14 million viewers, up 2% from 2025 (2.10 million). Prime Video, which has seen significant viewership gains with its NFL and NBA coverage as deals with those leagues progress, provided one of the few year-over-year bright spots for NASCAR's overall audience.

One noticeable trend across the various platforms was the relatively modest year-over-year declines for the Cup Series. While individually small, those declines add up. Still, despite several double-digit drops in recent weeks, the series finished the season down just 2% overall. That provides some encouragement heading into 2027, although NASCAR will have plenty of work to do if it hopes to reverse the longer-term viewership trend.

NASCAR CEO Steve O'Donnell said publicly last month that he planned to meet with Skydance Sports president David Berson to discuss moving races currently allocated to TNT Sports to the CBS broadcast network. Such a move could provide a meaningful audience boost for a portion of the schedule currently airing on cable. The 2026 calendar saw the premier division go 125 days without a race airing on broadcast television, a stretch that ran from April through August.

On a network-by-network basis, Fox averaged 4.56 million viewers across five points-paying races, down 7% year over year. FS1 averaged 2.30 million (-7%), Prime Video averaged 2.29 million (+6%), TNT averaged 2.12 million (+4%), USA Network averaged 1.60 million (-4%), and NBC averaged 2.64 million (-1%).

Despite the year-over-year declines, NASCAR Communications noted significant gains among younger demographics across NBC and USA Network, something the sport has long worked to improve.

Adults 35-49 increased 27% across the Chase, with growth on both USA Network (+25%) and NBC (+30%). Additionally, NBC's three Chase races averaged 455,000 viewers in the Adults 18-49 demographic (+23%) and 115,000 among Adults 25-34 (+25%).

The Homestead-Miami championship race also saw notable demographic growth, averaging 476,000 Adults 18-49 (+20%), including 187,000 Adults 18-34 (+47%) and 122,000 Adults 25-34 (+36%).

Beyond television viewership, NASCAR also recorded positive gains at the track. Trackside sales increased 20% year over year, reaching their highest levels in more than a decade. Specifically, Talladega's fall race recorded its highest trackside sales since 2014.

Rounding out the season, NASCAR's three other marquee series — the O'Reilly Auto Parts Series, Craftsman Truck Series, and ARCA Menards Series — all recorded year-over-year viewership increases.

The NASCAR O'Reilly Auto Parts Series averaged 1.04 million viewers, up 1% from 2025. The series also recorded a 13% increase in the Adults 18-49 demographic and finished the season with its most-watched championship race since 2019, as Homestead-Miami drew 1.18 million viewers on The CW.

The NASCAR Craftsman Truck Series averaged 540,000 viewers, up 12% year over year. The series saw particularly significant gains among younger demographics, with P18-24 up 88% and P18-34 up 50%. Races airing on FS1 specifically were up 21% from 2025.

Lastly, the ARCA Menards Series averaged 242,000 viewers, up 24% year over year.

Overall, it's a mixed bag for NASCAR heading into 2027. The Cup Series continues to see viewership declines, though it finally recorded some significant growth among younger demographics, while the other national touring series saw encouraging year-over-year gains.
