Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The Los Angeles Chargers and Baltimore Ravens will close out Week 10 on "Monday Night Football," with kickoff set for 8:15 p.m. ET at M&T Bank Stadium.

The Chargers are the only team in the league still without a win at this point in the season, as the offense continues to struggle under offensive coordinator Mike McDaniel. Two-time Pro Bowl quarterback Justin Herbert has struggled this year, with a career-low 70.4 NFL passer rating, having thrown for 1,591 passing yards, 6 touchdowns and 11 interceptions this season. To make matters worse for Herbert this week, top wide receiver Ladd McConkey was ruled out with a back issue ahead of kickoff.

The Ravens are better positioned at 4-5, but there is a slim margin for error in a competitive AFC North, especially without starting quarterback Lamar Jackson, who is expected to miss 6-10 weeks after undergoing thumb surgery last week. After a disastrous start for undrafted free agent rookie quarterback Joe Fagnano, the Ravens acquired veteran Jacoby Brissett in a trade with the Arizona Cardinals. Baltimore now turns to Brissett in hopes of keeping its season alive until Jackson is in a position to return.

The anthem's in the books, and it's almost time for kickoff in Baltimore between two of the most disappointing teams in the NFL.

Los Angeles Chargers head coach Jim Harbaugh told reporters Thursday that the Daiyan Henley trade fell through after the deadline because the Denver Broncos used a "loophole" to base the linebacker's physical on whether the Chargers would take less compensation.

Henley was supposed to go to Denver, with L.A. receiving a 2027 fourth-round pick and a 2028 fifth-round pick in return.

That exchange was agreed upon before last week's NFL trade deadline, but, like any trade, it was pending a physical. While the Broncos didn't provide a rationale for why the transaction wasn't finalized in their Wednesday night statement, NFL Network's Ian Rapoport reported Thursday morning that Denver viewed the "failed" physical as the reason for nixing the trade.

According to Harbaugh, the calculus behind the scrapped deal wasn't as simple as dissent between two medical staffs.

"Here's what I know from talking to Joe," Harbaugh said, via Alex Insdorf of "Guilty As Charged Podcast," referencing Chargers general manager Joe Hortiz.

"The Broncos based a pass-fail on a medical grade, based on whether we would take less compensation, and we wouldn't, and we didn't. And in my opinion, it's one of those loopholes that the league should really look into because it sets a bad precedent, continues to set a bad precedent."

The Baltimore Ravens would have been excused for packing it up this season and not trading off draft picks for a long shot on a discarded quarterback dragging them to the playoffs.

But the Ravens haven't quit on the season. They traded a 2027 fourth-round pick and a 2028 seventh-round pick to the Arizona Cardinals for Jacoby Brissett and a 2027 fifth-round pick. Brissett will make his first start with the Ravens, who have quarterbacks Lamar Jackson and Tyler Huntley on injured reserve, on Monday night.

The Ravens are 4-5 and technically still in the AFC wild-card race. The dream for the Ravens is for Brissett, who started eight games for the Cardinals this season before being traded, to keep the team in the race until Jackson can return. The team said Jackson is out 6-to-10 weeks with a dislocated right thumb. If Jackson returns at the shortest end of that timeline, he could be back in Week 16. That would give Baltimore three regular-season games and the playoffs, though there's a lot of work to do to be relevant when Jackson returns.

Brissett is the key figure in Monday night's game. It's rare for quarterbacks who can step in and start to be available, but the Cardinals were ready for a change. Brissett was benched in a game against the Cowboys, Gardner Minshew II led a comeback win and Brissett was demoted to third string behind Minshew (who suffered a season-ending injury in Week 9) and rookie Carson Beck.

Part of why it's hard to imagine Brissett being good enough to keep the Ravens relevant is that he was benched by a 3-7 team, and the Cardinals were 4-16 in his starts over the past two seasons. But he was better than any other realistic option, and can at least deliver the ball to playmakers like Zay Flowers and make teams respect the passing game, which can open things up for Derrick Henry.
