Canadiens Highlights: Larkin Earns Point In Loss Versus Kings

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The Montreal Canadiens hosted the Los Angeles Kings on Monday night, marking the debut of newly-acquired forward Dylan Larkin.

The former Detroit Red Wings captain was used on the second line, alongside Alex Newhook and Alexandre Texier, whereas Nick Suzuki anchored the first trio, with Cole Caufield and Juraj Slafkovsky.

The Habs controlled the high-danger scoring chances by a wide margin, and Larkin did earn a point, but the Kings emerged from the fray with a 2-1 win.

Earlier in the season, Canadiens fans chanted, "We want Larkin!" as the Red Wings visited the Bell Centre. Following the trade, things came full circle, with the Bell Centre-faithful proclaiming, "We got Larkin!", as a thank you towards general manager Kent Hughes.

Fans have good reason to be excited, as Larkin should immediately improve two significant areas of weakness.

Larkin enjoyed a warm welcome from the fans, at the very least. His actual introduction to Canadiens hockey involved a great Alex Laferriere goal midway through the first period, followed by yet another Kings goal a few moments later. Thankfully for the Habs, the second goal was reviewed, and deemed to be offside.

The Kings seemingly scored a second-period goal, which ended up being goaltender interference, saving the Canadiens from yet another one-goal deficit.

And that's when Montreal's luck ran out. The Kings eventually found the back of the net for the second time of the game, as Arber Xhekaj and Adam Engstrom got mixed up in their own zone, allowing Trevor Moore to beat Jakub Dobes rather easily. The two goals by the Kings were enough to secure a 2-1 win.

It didn't take long for the newest member of the Canadiens to make an impact, as Larkin registered his first assist with the Habs when Alex Newhook scored his seventh goal of the season, via an excellent Alexandre Texier pass.

It may not seem like Larkin actually touched the puck, but if you slow it down frame-by-frame, you'll note that it barely glanced off Larkin's skate prior to landing on Texier's stick. Regardless, it was the type of play fans want to see with more frequency from the Canadiens, especially when they have a poor start to the game.

As we discussed earlier in the season, Juraj Slafkovsky is off to a rather slow start to the year. That's perfectly normal for him, as he tends to be a second-half player, and his 5v5 production is similar to what we saw in 2025-26, but there are a few red flags in his game that are impossible to ignore.

Notably, his powerplay production has collapsed, though part of that is tied to the system, which is currently non-functional.

But his lack of puck protection is evident, and has led to a considerable uptick in giveaways, both at 5v5 and on the powerplay.
