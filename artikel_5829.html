New York state is short electric resources beginning 2033: ISO

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Let Utility Dive's free newsletter keep you informed, straight from your inbox.

New York will face an approximately 225 MW statewide resource deficiency, with the need for power resources growing to 1,850 MW by 2036, the state’s grid operator said Wednesday.

New York ISO’s 2026 Reliability Needs Assessment reveals the state’s increasing reliance on emergency operating procedures to maintain grid stability, highlighting the imminent resource shortfall. The ISO said it expects to call on demand response, voltage reductions, operating reserves, load curtailment and other measures, approximately 3.5 days next year, with that total growing to 21 days by 2036.

“New York’s reliability needs are no longer a distant concern,” Zach Smith, senior vice president of system and resource planning, said in a statement. “New generation and transmission resources take years to plan, permit, and build, so the reliability needs identified in this report must be addressed today.”

Along with the statewide resource deficiency, the report also identified local reliability needs. New York City is projected to be deficient by approximately 400 MW in 2032, growing to approximately 1,850 MW by 2036, while the Lower Hudson Valley deficiency grows from approximately 130 MW in 2032 to approximately 1,950 MW in 2036.

“While transmission investments can help address local reliability needs ... they cannot resolve the statewide resource shortfall,” the ISO said. “A combination of new generation, repowered resources, energy storage, dependable demand reductions, flexible large loads, and additional transmission will be required to maintain reliability over the next decade.”

NYISO expects demand growth of about 1,000 MW from manufacturing facilities and 1,700 MW from data centers will contribute to the shortfall. “The age and availability” of the state’s generation fleet is also a growing reliability concern, the grid operator said.

Approximately 4,600 MW of fossil generation will be more than 70 years old by 2036, according to the report. The assessment “accounts for this risk by projecting approximately 2,600 MW of generation as unavailable by 2036,” it said.

The ISO said it will continue to monitor the situation quarterly, reporting on the issue through its short-term reliability processes.

Once the 2026 RNA is approved by the NYISO board of directors, the grid operator said it will request updates to the base case “that may reduce or eliminate the identified reliability needs,” including changes in the status of generation and transmission projects, demand forecasts, demand response resources, large load assumptions and other system developments.

“If reliability needs remain after those updates, the NYISO will solicit and evaluate market-based solutions, regulated backstop solutions, and alternative regulated solutions” through the development of a 10-year reliability plan, the operator said.
