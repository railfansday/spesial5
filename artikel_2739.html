Bucks vs. Nets Player Grades: Turner, Burries, and rest of team come down to earth in dreadful showing

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

In what might have been the worst Bucks game I've watched in some time—which is saying something—the Milwaukee Bucks lost to the 3-13 Brooklyn Nets 104-97. It really felt like the Bucks got the monkey off their backs against the Raptors and came into this game without a care in the world. They didn't play as a team, nor did they play with any passion. The worst part is that there aren't going to be many games this season where the Bucks have a genuine 50-50 chance of winning, and for Milwaukee to put up that kind of performance against a Brooklyn team that really tried its hardest to lose was really disappointing. The game also brings the Bucks to 0-3 in the NBA Cup. Read our full summary of the game here and catch a six-minute audio recap on the Bucks+ podcast, Bucks In Six Minutes, below.

37 minutes, 28 points, 3 rebounds, 5 assists, 3 steals, 5 turnovers, 10/20 FG, 3/9 3P, 5/5 FT, 0

I mean, Herro was just about the team's only source of creation, so I do want to credit him for that. Overall, though, this wasn't all that impressive of a game. I didn't think Herro was as willing to get his teammates involved as he had been, settling for a fair few bad shots and dominating the ball too much; granted, he would have had a few more assists if anyone could make a shot, but alas. And then the turnovers, man; most of them felt like killer live-ball giveaways.

18 minutes, 2 points, 3 rebounds, 3 assists, 1 steal, 0 turnovers, 0/7 FG, 0/4 3P, -7

Oof. Really did not like Rollins' game. He made some nice reads to open the third, but overall, most of his shots came after he dribbled the air out of the ball for 10-14 seconds, then settled for step-back threes or wild drives at the rim. It was almost as if Rollins was trying to force his way into a groove, rather than playing instinctively and letting opportunities come to him. Moreover, we can usually rely on Rollins' defence being solid, but even that part of his game felt off; there was one possession in the third where the Bucks had finally taken the lead, were in a groove, and Rollins was guarding someone on the right wing; he was in perfect position to contain the ball, but he lunged for the steal, didn't get it, and his man went right past him, leading to the open three. Absolute coach killer.

31 minutes, 13 points, 3 rebounds, 2 assists, 1 turnover, 5/11 FG, 3/7 3P, -12

Not too many complaints about Kuzma's game. He was just about Milwaukee's only credible defender on Randle, and overall, I thought he did a solid job making the Nets veteran work. Also made some solid reads to get into the paint and then spray out to shooters.

34 minutes, 21 points, 4 rebounds, 4 assists, 2 steals, 9/15 FG, 1/5 3P, 2/4 FT, +5

Jaquez was solid in the middle game and a key part of the Bucks' best runs. He finished well around the rim and got his teammates involved. Still, can't go 1/5 from three when it feels like most of them are wide open. Just can't happen.

25 minutes, 3 points, 6 rebounds, 2 assists, 1 steal, 3 blocks, 3 turnovers, 1/2 FG, 1/2 3P, -1

Obviously, people will look at the scoring numbers, but I would say Turner's lack of production was a byproduct of the Bucks' lack of ball movement. Against the Raptors, the ball was moving, and that's how he got his opportunities; against the Nets, everything was stagnant and slow, so Turner shot just twice. The big man still impacted the game in other ways, but he also had a few backbreaking live-ball turnovers. Overall, it wasn't a good game for Turner by any means, but I thought other factors also contributed to him not playing well.

22 minutes, 13 points, 14 rebounds, 2 assists, 2 blocks, 1 turnover, 6/10 FG, 1/2 3P, -6

On balance, Ware was probably one of Milwaukee's better players. He created extra opportunities, with five of his 14 rebounds coming on the offensive glass, and he would have scored more points if just a few of those tip-ins had gone in. Also, continues to rack up blocks and be a presence around the rim (on both ends). I will say that the Nets were happy to play much higher in their pick-and-roll coverage with Ware in the game because they didn't trust his decision-making with a numbers advantage; he has to improve in that facet of the game.

21 minutes, 3 points, 3 rebounds, 3 assists, 2 turnovers, 1/6 FG, 0/2 3P, 1/2 FT, -4

Burries ended his hot streak with a stinker. He couldn't find the same creases he had been finding (which is weird against one of the league's worst defensive teams), and he also didn't impact in other areas the way he usually does. Burries also made a really odd turnover in the fourth quarter: he caught the ball with no awareness that MPJ was right there and got it ripped away; part of that was on Ware for passing him the ball there, but Burries needs to have better awareness.

21 minutes, 8 points, 3 rebounds, 1 turnover, 4 PF, 3/6 FG, 2/5 3P, 0

I mean, Ament got most of his looks off catch-and-shoot opportunities, but he also got handed a few grenades as the shot clock was winding down. The rook was the third defender (behind Kuzma and Jaquez) on Randle, and despite a major weight disadvantage, he was decent in that matchup; Ament clearly read the scout, forcing him to his right hand and into help as much as possible—good stuff. He had a few possessions where he rotated late from the weak side in help, though.

12 minutes, 4 points, 4 rebounds, 3 assists, 2/7 FG, 0/5 3P, +2

Weird minutes for Jakucionis, who played three minutes in the first half and then was thrown into the fire at the end without really getting a feel for the game. He missed all his threes, but most were the right shots to take (the one bad one being his corner three, where he had Herro wide open for the extra pass up top). Also got baited into a bad foul late as the Bucks were making a charge.

11 minutes, 2 points, 2 assists, 1/2 FG, -6

I don't know. I guess LeVert was fine in his minutes. As I'll get to below, my issue was with how much he played.

This was the first game where I truly felt Jenkins played a key role in the team losing, mainly because of rotations. He opened the second quarter with a Rollins-Green-LeVert-Kuzma-Turner lineup that had a -78.6 net rating in 3:18, with Rollins—who, news flash, has been struggling—being the only creator (and boy did he try to create!). Then, to start the fourth quarter, Jenkins threw out a Jakucionis-Burries-LeVert-Ament-Ware lineup that had a -40.0 net rating in 2:38. One downside to starting all the veterans—a unit that, to their credit, played well together in this one—is that you can eventually get to lineups like these. Granted, I get that with all of this you're robbing Peter to pay Paul to some degree: if you take one guy from one lineup to help another, that original lineup will lose something. Even so, Jenkins can strike a much better balance than he did last night.

DNP-CD: Jericho Sims, Ousmane Dieng, Bogoljub Markovic, Gary Trent Jr.

Inactive: Pete Nance, Cormac Ryan, Kam Jones,

Even the Bucks can get high on their own supply and, dare I say it, disrespect their opponent.

Jenkins: "We abandoned what was working for us in the first half. We didn't have rhythm to our offence. I guess we got bored with the stuff that was working well for us."

Kuzma: "I think we were just thinking for ourselves… How can I get myself going instead of how can I get the team going? That's the story of our season right now."

I can't stress enough how much the Nets tried to give this game to the Bucks. They shot 41.6% from the field and 26.3% from three. Michael Porter Jr. went 4/14, for goodness' sake; you have to win when that happens. It felt like the team entered this game thinking they'd get a win; they were playing a team just as bad, and another win would come easy while they all played for their own numbers.

The Bucks had 14 turnovers for the game, and nine came in the last quarter.

Assistant coach Brad Jones noted at halftime that all 47 of the Bucks' first-half points came after touching the paint in that possession—every one.

I don't get the LeVert minutes sometimes. Like, if he stands out in his first stint, sure, throw him out there for another; but if his first stint doesn't stand out in any way, why put him back in? Surely you'd rather give those minutes to Dieng or even a guard. Sometimes the rotations can feel incredibly random.
