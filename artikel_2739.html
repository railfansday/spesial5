Jets’ Breece Hall Gets Major Trade Prediction Ahead of NFL Deadline

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

The New York Jets face a decision about their backfield as the NFL trade deadline approaches, with Breece Hall emerging as a potential trade candidate. A growing role for younger running back Braelon Allen has raised questions about whether the team should move its established starter and prioritize its longer-term rebuilding plans.

Allen has delivered an impressive stretch during Hall's absence, giving the struggling offense a productive option and creating a new challenge for the coaching staff. In a standout performance, Allen rushed for 147 yards on 21 carries and scored on a 21-yard run. His production has made it increasingly difficult to overlook the possibility that he can handle a larger role.

The opportunity has also highlighted the difference in cost and workload between the two backs. Allen is playing on an inexpensive contract and has accumulated relatively few NFL touches, while the veteran has handled more than 1,000 career touches and dealt with injuries, including a torn ACL in 2022. Allen has recorded 504 rushing yards across his past five starts, a span that underscores his value to the offense.

The Jets, however, have maintained that their established starter remains part of their plans. Coach Aaron Glenn has indicated that the role will not change when the veteran returns, while offensive coordinator Frank Reich has described the presence of two capable backs as a positive situation. That stance suggests the organization is not actively preparing to move him, despite the outside speculation.

Oct 26, 2025; Cincinnati, Ohio, USA; New York Jets running back Breece Hall (20) celebrates the win against the Cincinnati Bengals at Paycor Stadium. Mandatory Credit: Joseph Maiorana-Imagn Images

Hall is projected to be traded to a new team before the NFL trade deadline this year. This is detailed more in an analysis by Tyler Sullivan for CBS Sports.

Hall fits the bill as a player who is more valuable to them as a trade asset rather than a contributor on the field. That's thanks, in part, to the emergence of fellow running back Braelon Allen.

As Hall has been sidelined with a quad injury, Allen has flourished in the starting role. Over the last five games with Hall on the shelf, Allen has started in each contest and has rushed for 504 yards on a 5.4 yards-per-carry average and has two rushing touchdowns. Allen is also averaging 3.92 yards per carry after contact over this stretch, which is elite. For reference, that's better than Derrick Henry (3.11) and Jahmyr Gibbs (3.39) this season.

A trade could nevertheless make sense if New York receives an attractive offer. The team reportedly turned down a third-round selection from Kansas City last year, and another deal would likely need to provide comparable value. Hall's contract is not necessarily an obstacle, as its structure could allow the Jets to create cap flexibility by moving him.

Allen's recent performances give New York a credible alternative in the backfield, while additional draft capital could help the franchise address broader roster needs. The Jets must decide whether keeping an established playmaker in Hall is more valuable than acquiring future assets. Any move should reflect the return they receive rather than simply making a change before the deadline.
