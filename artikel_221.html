Stripe backer Atomico calls for Europe to own its success stories

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Europe is home to many of the world’s fastest growing companies of all time, veteran VC firm Atomico highlighted in its latest annual State of European Tech report.

The report, for instance, notes that the likes of Black Forest Labs and Microagi have reached $100 million in revenue in months, not years. “I think we can put to bed the idea that there’s a fundamental reason or constraint on the ability to scale a breakout company from here in Europe,” Atomico partner and head of intelligence Tom Wehmeier (pictured) told TechCrunch.

Yet, there’s still more that the VC firm founded by Skype’s Niklas Zennström would like to see — and its call this year is for Europe to “build it” and “scale it,” but also “own it.” That’s a way of wishing these companies will remain European longer. But there’s also a sense that this success hasn’t been acknowledged enough, perhaps obscured by louder calls for improvements.

Wehmeier, the report’s lead author, was quick to opine that “policy, procurement and capital are not moving at the same speed as our founders.” But he said the tech sector should advocate for itself in an inspiring way.

“When it comes to policy, I think the language needs to be, ‘Hey, this is not us complaining. This is us saying here is an opportunity to make the magnitude of impact even greater,’” Wehmeier said. “When we frame it as problems, that becomes harder.”

This reframing may also help European tech improve its image abroad, where complaints around overregulation may have overshadowed Europe’s potential to leverage its deep tech talent and growing pool of repeat entrepreneurs to have even more of success stories.

Plenty could be still done to fully unlock this potential, and Wehmeier said that “the next few weeks are going to be a really important test, with EU Inc.” Also known as the 28th regime, this initiative would create a unified European corporate regime akin to the Delaware C Corp.

If the last stretch of EU Inc’s grassroots campaign achieves it goal, it will become much easier to create and operate businesses across the EU. It would also show that the tech system got itself heard, which could open the door to the kind of endorsement Atomico is hoping to see more of.

Politicians have strong incentives to give the tech sector what it wants. If it is Europe’s second largest sector, biggest contributor to growth, and biggest job creator, “Do we not want more of that?” Wehmeier asked rhetorically. But it is unclear whether it will win them any votes.

To make the answer more straightforward, the tech sector will have to work on having public opinion on its side. For Wehmeier, one key is to “demonstrate that this is translating into value for citizens.”

The report is supporting this endeavor with a mix of high-level data and examples from its portfolio; for instance, it notes that ICEYE’s satellites monitor severe weather, flooding, and other hazards; and Daniel Ek’s Neko Health has now done more than 100,000 body scans, some of which helped identify severe or life-threatening health conditions.

One limit to this argument is that Neko Health’s scans will seem pricey to most Europeans, who largely rely on collective, public health insurance. But Atomico seems to hope that its report will give readers FOMO not just as potential customers, but also as potential shareholders.

There’s more to be done for this to happen. Beyond joining the sector’s push for an EU capital markets union, he hopes more of Europe’s institutional investors will invest in the region’s own tech companies. “We must get to the place where Europe and European beneficiaries are the biggest shareholders in the success that we create,” Wehmeier said.

Before that can happen, the industry must overcome anti-industry beliefs like fears of AI-induced job destruction. But Wehmeier is a believer in the power of proof. “I think that trust is going to be earned through showing results to the public — be that lower energy bills, better healthcare, more homes built for cheaper and faster, and having infrastructure that we can rely on.”
