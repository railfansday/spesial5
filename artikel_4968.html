UniCredit Opens Bitcoin to Italian Savers, Capping Gains at 65%

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

UniCredit began selling a six-year, capital-protected certificate linked to BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin ETP to retail clients in Italy, with gains capped at 65%.

UniCredit on Tuesday began selling a capital-protected certificate that lets retail clients in Italy follow the bitcoin price, a product the bank calls the first investment solution of its kind in the country.

The six-year, euro-denominated certificate will be listed on the Munich stock exchange and is linked to the iShares Bitcoin ETP managed by BlackRock, UniCredit said in a release. The placement period runs from Nov. 24 to Dec. 18, 2026.

The note is open only to UniCredit Italia retail clients with specific characteristics, and only through the bank’s advisory service. It can’t be bought independently or by investors who aren’t UniCredit clients, and the minimum investment is €1,000, Italian newswire Teleborsa reported.

A capital-protected certificate is a structured note. The issuing bank promises to repay the original investment at maturity and pays a return tied to an underlying asset, in this case bitcoin through the BlackRock product. Buyers hold a claim on the bank, not bitcoin itself.

If bitcoin is down at maturity, holders get 100% of their capital back. If it rises, they share in the gain up to a maximum return of 65% on the capital invested. Before maturity, the certificate’s price follows market conditions, so the protection applies only at the end of the six years.

That cap works out to roughly 8.7% a year at most, no matter how far bitcoin climbs. And because the certificate is UniCredit’s own debt, the protection depends on the bank’s ability to pay. Documents for a similar UniCredit note in Germany state that no third-party guarantee covers an issuer default.

“With the launch of this certificate, UniCredit is writing a new page of financial innovation in the country, confirming its market leadership,” said Chicco Di Stasi, Head of Group Investment Product Solutions at UniCredit.

“Diversification is no longer just a prudential rule, but the key to capturing the value of the future,” Di Stasi said, adding that the bank is responding to growing client demand and offering the product to Italian savers “for the very first time.”

Di Stasi said the bank is bringing to Italy “a pioneering model already proven by its success in Germany and Austria.” Those earlier notes are denominated in dollars and track BlackRock’s U.S.-listed iShares Bitcoin Trust instead.

HypoVereinsbank, UniCredit’s German unit, sold a version to private clients in March with a 160% cap, or 60% upside. UniCredit Bank Austria launched its own six-year note in April with a 175% cap, or 75% upside. Italy’s 65% falls between the two.

The bank has moved into bitcoin in steps. It first offered a capital-protected bitcoin product to professional clients only in 2025. In May, it placed a bitcoin and ether certificate for professional clients with a €100,000 minimum.

In September, UniCredit was reported to be weighing crypto custody, including the infrastructure to hold and trade digital assets. Elsewhere in Italy, Banca Sella became the first Italian bank licensed for crypto services under MiCA in May.

UniCredit is one of Europe’s largest banks. It reported €918.3 billion in total assets at Sept. 30 and a record €3.3 billion in third-quarter net profit.

Bitcoin traded near $60,400 on Tuesday, more than 50% below its record above $126,000.
