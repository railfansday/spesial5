Dems pivot from ‘affordability’ pitch to DHS probe threats after midterm wins

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

At a House Homeland Security Committee hearing, Rep. Delia Ramirez, D-Ill., delivers a powerful and emotional statement, questioning if any parent wouldn't go to extreme lengths for their dying child. Ramirez attacks for-profit prison corporations and makes a direct call to abolish ICE and dismantle DHS, citing concerns over inhuman conditions.

Several prominent Democrats who ran on an "affordability" message were largely silent when pressed by Fox News Digital on their sudden pivot to foreshadowing investigations into the Trump administration, including in many cases its immigration enforcement efforts.

Rep. Robert Garcia, D-Calif., who is poised to lead the House Oversight Committee, responded to Democrats' win Tuesday with a statement: "Lawyer Up", despite having pledged to prioritize household costs alongside government accountability.

Garcia told CNN before the election that "Democratic priorities" would be lowering the cost of "gas, groceries, the mortgage, the rent," alongside combating government corruption.

Rep. Robert Garcia, D-Calif., speaks to members of the media July 15, 2026. After the Democrats retook the House, he is set to become the chairman of the House Oversight Committee. (Graeme Sloan/Bloomberg via Getty Images)

In response to a request for comment, a representative for Garcia shared a tweet he posted Wednesday, after his Tuesday "lawyer up" warning, that referenced affordability:

"People can’t afford gas, groceries, and rent because Donald Trump and his family are ripping off the American people and enriching themselves. Taking on corruption is about making life more affordable for working families," Garcia said in the tweet.

Before the election, "Squad" Rep. Delia Ramirez, D-Ill., wrote in a PBS-Chicago voters guide that there is "no question that the rising cost of living is the number one issue" for a "working people’s agenda."

After the election, however, her tone appeared to change:

"Noem, Markwayne, Miller, Homan, Bovino, what’s up? Clear your schedule," Ramirez said.

Several respondents pushed back, including one of her stated targets, former Border Patrol Cmdr. Gregory Bovino.

"Honey, slow your totalitarian roll," Bovino responded on X. "Citizens don’t answer to communists in this country as much as you seem to believe that."

Bovino went on to suggest Ramirez instead relax with a bottle of whiskey from a brand the former immigration official launched upon retirement.

Other respondents also pointed out that Ramirez’ post-election missive didn’t mention her affordability priority:

SCHUMER PREACHES AFFORDABILITY IN DEM MIDTERM PITCH WHILE SABOTAGING GOP FIXES FOR SOARING COSTS

Rep. Delia Ramirez, D-Ill., speaks during a news conference with immigration experts, DACA recipients, and Dreamers. Over the weekend, she attended a conference in Mexico City where she said she was a proud Guatemalan before an American. (Getty Images)

"Notice nothing about how she is going to help her constituents but ‘get Trump’," said one.

"So, you guys never really gave a s--- about affordability," added former Energy Department staffer Matt van Swol.

Rep. Analilia Mejia, D-N.J., who now represents the longtime Republican-held seat of former Rep. Rodney Frelinghuysen, made both affordability and immigration policy part of her campaign message.

"We’re going to toss out subpoenas like Mardi Gras beads," Mejia’s first X post Wednesday read.

Rep. Tony Wied, R-Wis., responded it showed Democrats "have no plans to make life better for you," eliciting a response from Mejia in which she dubbed him "Mr. Weird" and only then mentioned an "economy that works for" Americans.

In April, Rep. Yassamin Ansari, D-Ariz., released a lengthy statement announcing a "New Affordability Agenda" and highlighting her role on the Congressional Progressive Caucus’ "Lowering Costs Task Force."

On Election Night, Ansari posted: "Thank you Mr. President. Our thank-you will come in January with subpoenas." An ensuing tweet called for the impeachment of War Secretary Pete Hegseth.

On Wednesday, Ansari ultimately posted a policy-related pledge to curtail AI.

Sen. Edward Markey, D-Mass., stated during the midterms that his priorities were "groceries, gas prices energy costs" and the like.

However, his post-election missive remarking on how many "new forests" will need planting for "all of the subpoenas we’ll be printing," instead signaled a priority to investigate tech executives like Elon Musk.

"Affordability," quipped former Virginia Del. Nick Freitas, R-Culpeper, in a reply.

Ramirez, Mejia, Markey and Ansari did not respond to requests for comment on their apparent pivots. Garcia’s representative responded by providing his subsequent post on affordability. Both Garcia and Ramirez had previously identified government accountability and immigration oversight, respectively, as priorities, but their immediate post-election statements emphasized investigations rather than the affordability measures they had highlighted on the campaign trail.

DHS appeared to take the newfound priority in stride in comments to Fox News Digital.

"It is unfortunate that Democrats are more interested in political shenanigans than in governing," said DHS’ top attorney James Percival.

"Rest assured, however, that we are well prepared to deal with this nonsense so Secretary Mullin can continue focusing on protecting the homeland," the counsel said.

A Justice Department spokesperson echoed Percival, saying the agency is "not afraid of threats from Democrats" and assured its staff has followed the law.

"It is no surprise that rather than focus on bringing down costs for Americans, Democrats in Congress are picking up where their weaponization efforts left off when they were last in power," the spokesperson said.

Meanwhile, the White House told Fox News Digital that Trump remains willing to work with Democrats on issues they can collaborate on to help "hardworking Americans."

"If Democrats choose to pursue sham investigations and radical-left policies, they are choosing to work against the American people," spokeswoman Lauren Bis said.

Immigration hawks signaled preparations for what Democrats foreshadowed.

The Mass Deportation Coalition, whose leaders have been critical of Biden-era open-border policies and have asserted that the Trump administration has not done enough on the issue in some areas, told Fox News Digital they are prepared to defend officials amid the onslaught.

"We know what’s coming," said Mass Deportation Coalition leader Mike Howell. "A lot of people are going to be cashing-out and charging patriots expensive legal fees to the most vulnerable."

"We have been through this before and are ready to sacrifice for the patriots who are targeted by weaponized harassment."

Former House Oversight Committee counsel and immigration judge Andrew Arthur said Congress has the prerogative to investigate DHS’ work but added that it may not be what Americans are looking for as a priority.

Citing Ramirez’ mention of Border Czar Tom Homan, Arthur noted that the onetime Obama official long held "positive" ratings from Republicans and Democrats.

"He was commonly viewed as the guy who came in and cleaned things up after Noem left, got things a little bit better. So that's probably not going to be a terribly popular thing," he said.

"This isn't really affordability but anything that shows waste in government technically goes to affordability and it all depends on how willing the media is to accept that talking point," said Arthur, now at the Center for Immigration Studies.

Arthur said DHS can largely proceed as usual with its previously established funding while any investigations take place, adding that much of what Mullin and Trump have done already falls under established law that Congress itself passed.

Referencing the Immigration and Nationality Act of 1952, Arthur said that, short of "amending the law itself – which they can’t do if [Trump] vetoes it" Democrats can probe and try to paint the agency in a "bad light" over ICE-involved shootings and similar incidents, but that their larger agenda would be buttressed by the INA.

A 2026 whitepaper by Federal Reserve Bank researchers found elevated "unauthorized" immigration contributed to rising housing costs between 2021 and 2024, estimating that the influx increased home prices by 6.6% and rents by 4.3% in the average metropolitan area.

The researchers attributed the increases to additional housing demand that was not matched by sufficient new construction, lending indirect empirical support to arguments that illegal immigration can have adverse effects on key affordability metrics.
