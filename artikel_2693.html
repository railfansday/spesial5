Cleveland Browns proving that TD passes may be overrated

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The Cleveland Browns continued to befuddle the NFL on Sunday with their latest comeback victory.

After falling behind 17-0 to the Houston Texans, the Browns did what they seem to do almost every week: the defense put a chokehold on the Texans, the offense slowly found its footing, and when Quinshon Judkins crossed the goal line with 53 seconds remaining, the Browns had their sixth victory of the season.

No one can really explain how the Browns are doing it, not even the coaches or the players themselves, but they're doing it because Cleveland has followed the same basic script in each of their six victories.

Chief among the reasons that the Browns find themselves in a hole each week, but also one of the key reasons they have been able to overcome those deficits, has been the play of quarterback Deshaun Watson, who has generally been poor to bad for the first three quarters of each game, only to come alive in the fourth quarter.

Watson has been especially effective in the final two minutes of games, as Pro Football Focus highlighted after Sunday's win, as Watson is:

That last bullet point is an interesting one, given a narrative that has emerged surrounding Watson, as his detractors are eager to point out that he has not thrown a touchdown pass since Week 5 against the New York Jets.

But you know who did throw touchdown passes on Sunday?

Cam Ward had two for the Tennessee Titans and lost

Tyler Shough had one for the New Orleans Saints and lost

Dak Prescott had five for the Dallas Cowboys and lost

Aaron Rodgers had two for the Pittsburgh Steelers, and oh boy did they lose.

Touchdown passes are fun and can make for great highlights, and there is no doubt Watson would love to have back that errant pass to Jerry Jeudy on Sunday that would have been a walk-in touchdown. But they are just one part of the bigger goal: outscore your opponent by any means necessary.

Think back to the three touchdowns the Browns scored on Sunday:

Watson had a six-yard run after hitting Jeudy on a 65-yard pass

KC Concepcion had a six-yard run to finish a drive that included an 18-yard pass to Malachi Corley

Judkins scored the game-winner on a five-yard run to finish a drive that featured a 30-yard completion to Harold Fannin Jr.

Would it have made any difference if any of those scores had come on a pass instead of a run? Unless the NFL changes its rules overnight, a touchdown still counts as six points no matter how it is scored.

Think back to all those times over the years when the Browns have been in a first-and-goal situation only to bumble their way into a turnover or settle for a field goal instead of a touchdown. The type of drive that goes something like a run on first down for minus-2 yards, a sack on second down, then an incompletion on third down that deflates a once-promising drive.

At some point, Watson will throw another touchdown and another nonsensical narrative will die from a lack of oxygen.

As long as the Browns finish more of their drives with touchdowns than field goals, does it really matter if they do it via the run or the pass?
