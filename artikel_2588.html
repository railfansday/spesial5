Cowboys keep pace in division race with near-perfect day on offense

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The Dallas Cowboys scored the most points they ever have with Brian Schottenheimer on staff to win 51-30 against the Tennessee Titans on Sunday. Facing just six third downs on offense, the Cowboys had the same 40 points they had against the 49ers last week by the latter stages of the third quarter. This was the fewest third downs the Cowboys offense has had to line up for in a game since week three of 2015 against the Atlanta Falcons, and it meant by the time they broke 40 points this time they had a three-score lead.

The Cowboys scored touchdowns on their first six drives, and kept the pressure on all the way until the final whistle by kicking a field goal and scoring one more touchdown in the fourth quarter to end the day with zero empty possessions, not counting coming out for one Landry shift kneel down to end the game.

All the talk this week when it came to coaching surrounding this football team was on the defensive side of the ball after 40 points wasn't enough to win a home game for the second year in a row, and the Cowboys made a play-caller change to Mike Pettine going into this Titans game.

That 49ers loss was still one that head coach Brian Schottenheimer ultimately had to wear as the latest loss on his record that squandered a chance to get a momentum-building win streak started. Whatever trickle down effect came from having the more experienced Pettine taking over added defensive responsibilities compared to the first-time coordinator in Christian Parker, Schottenheimer put together his best play-calling game of the season to more than do his part at the start of a week where the Cowboys will play twice.

Let's get to some notes on the Cowboys' fourth win of the season.

For an NFC vs. AFC battle that featured a team from Dallas, TX and visitors from Nashville, TN, making a George Strait reference just feels right. This game was like a hit record on CD from "King George" for the Cowboys top two wide receivers.

For the first time in any game they've appeared in together from last season until now, both George Pickens and CeeDee Lamb had over 100 yards. For the second week in a row, they both had a touchdown, also meaning for the first time they've both had touchdowns in back-to-back games.

After the Cowboys offense had to come off the field in the first half too often against the 49ers with several misses to Lamb, hitting Lamb on the first third-down attempt of the game on the opening drive was an encouraging sign early. Eight plays later, Javonte Williams capped the tone-setting drive with a touchdown, ending a drive that saw Williams, Lamb, Ryan Flournoy, James Mitchell, and Kavonte Turpin all touch the ball, as well as Dak Prescott converting a fourth down with his legs.

The Cowboys showed they could simplify the game on offense when they needed to with the third down comeback route to Lamb, but also get creative to keep piling on points and force the Titans to chase all afternoon. This was evident on the second touchdown drive, when an orbit motion from Lamb to take defenders out of the play pre-snap and huge pulling blocks from Tyler Smith and Cooper Beebe sprung Turpin for another touchdown. The drive after this was all Lamb, as he started it with a 29-yard catch, followed it with a ten-yarder with 15 tacked on for a facemask, and then another big third-down catch on third-and-goal for another touchdown.

It was at this point the Cowboys defense was able to start turning up the pressure even more, something they did while holding the Titans to a field goal on the ensuing drive. The next time the offense touched the ball, it was time for Pickens to get in the painted turf, which he did to push the lead to 27-6 and start the rout.

Pickens' touchdown came one play after the Cowboys threw a screen to the opposite side to Lamb, and then used Lamb in the slot for the touchdown play to draw the middle of field coverage away from Pickens. It was also three plays after starting CB Cor'Dale Flott came off the field with an injury for the Titans, and the Cowboys went after his backup matched up against Pickens.

This is something fans have been begging the offense to be better at for a very long time, and the context of the opponent matters, but they flashed huge potential in their ability to find winning matchups and exploit them here in late November to try and remain a dangerous team down the stretch.

There wasn't really a moment on Sunday where the Cowboys felt like they were truly letting the game slip away, but they also didn't exactly keep a firm stranglehold on it for all 60 minutes against a Titans side that's been pesky all year. One area they would have loved to be cleaner in to remain in firm control was the middle eight, an area all coaches at all levels emphasize.

The Pickens touchdown we just covered came just before the clock hit four minutes remaining in the first half. The defense had a golden opportunity to get off the field quickly, putting the Titans in 2nd-and-12 after a Quinnen Williams tackle for loss on first down. It should have been third and even-longer after a James Houston sack on this play, but a Joey Porter Jr. facemask penalty bailed the Titans out.

Williams would help make up for it again by himself with a sack, but the Cowboys couldn't get off the field on third down in this sequence either. Dallas would hold Tennessee to a field goal as part of a game they kept the Titans to 2-5 scoring touchdowns in the red zone (compared to 6-6 for the offense), but they could have kept all points off the board or even given the ball back to the offense with time to do something.

Coming out of halftime, penalties again hurt the Cowboys defense as Caleb Downs got called for pass interference against fellow first-round rookie Carnell Tate. This was one of a few plays this drive where Cam Ward had plenty of time to throw, mostly on reps coming where Donovan Ezeiruaku was on the field.

Pettine has had no problem shuffling bodies in the front seven, but prior to this Parker already talked about hard conversations with Ezeiruaku about potentially getting less snaps. Ezeiruaku did not make the most of his snaps against the Titans, ending up on the ground on the drive-starting play to Calvin Ridley to start the second half, and James Houston asserted himself for the second week in a row. That could be a key personnel change to watch going forward.

The Cowboys held onto the ball past the middle eight point of the third quarter in response to Ward's sneak TD and did the best thing they could with it, scoring on a Williams swing pass. This helped re-stabilize the game in a big way, as we've seen these types of lateral throws to Williams be drive-killers before, but this time it went for six. Still, when it comes to how this 21-point win potentially translates to how Dallas will play against tougher competition, some attention to detail was still lacking.

The play-action game has been a huge part of the last two weeks of Dak Prescott putting up video game numbers, with a combined completion percentage of 69% (58-84) for 668 yards, nine touchdowns, and one interception. The one moment the Cowboys defense had their weekly obscene play to let Tate go to the ground, get back up and score to make it a two-score game in the second half did not cost them like it did against the Cardinals or 49ers in the other two more recent home losses.

This was because the Cowboys offense again got right back to work, and had big play-action passes to both Lamb and Pickens before Prescott ran it into the endzone himself to make it 41-23. The Cowboys also went back to play-action for Brevyn Spann-Ford's touchdown to break 5o points. This ensured the Cowboys got seven points out of Williams forced fumble recovered by Markquese Bell and nearly returned for a defensive touchdown.

Having the run game working is probably the best way to keep these play-action looks working as well as they are for Dallas, but a lot of credit should go to the interior offensive line as well. Their ability to make runs and passes look the same and get out in space and block has been great, and Williams' strength in pass protection was another huge plus here as well. Even Jashaun Corbin in his first action with the team didn't create a noticeable drop off as either a blocker, runner, or receiver with his limited reps behind Williams as the RB2 on Sunday.

This is a stretch of football the Cowboys must do everything they can to not waste from Prescott, as all phases of the offense are humming as long as #4 is on the field right now.
