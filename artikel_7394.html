How SharkNinja is doubling down on its creator strategy

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The home appliance brand introduced two new creator programs, SharkNinja First Fans and SharkNinja Storytellers, to tap into the quickly growing influencer economy.

SharkNinja CEO Mark Barrocas in 2008 told a retailer that his plan was to build the company “one five-star review at a time.” In Barrocas’ retelling, the retailer’s executives dismissed the idea as foolish because they couldn’t imagine who would write a review of the brand’s products. That was before the rise of influencers.

Over the ensuing 28 years, SharkNinja has become a multibillion-dollar global company, which built its brand through a social-forward, ultra-connected strategy that includes partnerships with celebrities including David Beckham, Courteney Cox and Alix Earle, alongside a host of influencers.

SharkNinja is doubling down on that strategy with two new programs designed to give storytellers, both inside and outside of the company, access and opportunities to develop and create even more content.

The programs, SharkNinja First Fans, for company employees, and SharkNinja Storytellers, for general consumers and creators, are designed to help the company continue to grow and manage its expanding list of creators and partners.

“We felt we were underserving this community by not creating a space where we could have more scaled one-to-ones with them, bring them further into the fold in terms of our product board maps, and incentivize participation,” said Michelle Crossan-Matos, chief brand and experience officer at SharkNinja.

Over 200,000 posts about SharkNinja’s varied household products, which include blenders, vacuums and ice-cream makers, have been made this year, garnering more than 19 billion impressions, according to the company. Last year, it recorded more than $6.4 billion in sales.

The secret sauce is the company’s practice of fostering, nurturing and actively supporting those who want to create content around SharkNinja’s products, according to Crossan-Matos.

“We bring creators all the way upstream with our products, and we’re having them react to and test and make content with some of our products before we go to market. It’s always been core for our strategy,” said Crossan-Matos.

The new programs use leaderboards and rewards to ensure the content keeps coming. For outside creators, the rewards, which can include commissions and perks, are tied to growth and activity. The most active creators will get exclusive first looks at upcoming products and even get a voice in shaping them. The company has created tutorials and other materials, including an artificial intelligence-powered app, to help the internal creators develop compelling narratives.

Though not exactly commonplace, employer-supported creator programs are not unheard of either. Starbucks has its Green Apron Creators, IBM has a Buzz Squad, Gap opened up its creator ecosystem to employees, and Dell has compensated its employees for sharing content on their own channels. The scope of SharkNinja’s creator efforts are notable.

“I don't know if anybody is doing it the exact way that we are,” Crossan-Matos said. “I think to have both [consumer and employee programs] launch as quickly as we did and at this scale … Our appetite is to drive this globally.”

The company can support these programs on a global scale because of its broad product base, Crossan-Matos said. SharkNinja creates products across 41 consumer categories and launches an average of 25 new products every year.

“We're very lucky to have so many products for so many different types of communities globally,” Crossan-Matos said. “Creators just fit so seamlessly into our co-creation and our storytelling. We think [these initiatives] are going to propel more people to tell their own stories in a way that is even more relatable because they're everyday people.”

Keeping an eye on metrics will be key to measuring program success.

“We’ll be looking at content performance, just like we do with everything. We will be looking for what posts are going viral, what things are gaining traction,” Crossan-Matos said. “We expect really high numbers as we expand.”
