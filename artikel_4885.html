Inside Sam Bankman-Fried’s $35 Million Bahamas Crypto HQ

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Four years after the fall of cryptocurrency partners-in-crime Sam Bankman-Fried and Caroline Ellison, their Icarian story is coming to the small screen twice over. A docuseries about the scandal, The Boy Who Stole Millions?, debuts on Prime Video November 30, and Netflix’s drama series The Altruists premiered November 19.

No matter which retelling you tune into, much of the action is sure to take place at Bankman-Fried’s $35 million “crypto frat house” (as CNBC once called it) in The Bahamas, which served as the headquarters of his cryptocurrency exchange FTX at the time of his and Ellison’s arrests for wire fraud and several conspiracy charges in 2022.

The Orchid building at Albany, The Bahamas

Bankman-Fried and Ellison met while working at trading firm Jane Street in Manhattan in 2016, and began an on-again, off-again romantic relationship. Bankman-Fried’s first crypto business was the quantitative trading firm he cofounded in 2017, Alameda Research. He went on to found crypto exchange FTX. Ellison, who first began working at Alameda in 2018, became its co-CEO and ran the firm as FTX’s hedge fund.

Bankman-Fried and Ellison moved to The Bahamas in 2021, reportedly motivated by looser COVID-19 pandemic protocols, and “a proactive stance taken by The Bahamas and its regulatory bodies on cryptocurrencies,” Bankman-Fried told Blockworks.

Bankman-Fried shared his 11,500-square-foot unit with Ellison and eight other FTX and Alameda employees. The apartment has a circular living room, a light-up bar, Italian marble floors, cove lighting, and custom closets. Per a real estate listing obtained by Semafor, Bankman-Fried’s unit had German-engineered windows and doors. Outdoor amenities included a firepit, spa, grill and cooking area, and pool.

The couple attempted to evade culpability for misappropriating $8 billion of FTX customer money, a crime uncovered after Bankman-Fried bought his Bahamas property. Both were convicted of wire fraud and several conspiracy charges. Ellison served 14 months of a 24-month sentence. Bankman-Fried is currently serving a 25-year prison sentence and maintains that he is innocent.

To bring this story to life for The Altruists, production designer Todd Fjelsted re-created the infamous penthouse. Several interiors were shot in Vancouver, but scenes requiring a beach or docks were shot in the Dominican Republic.

Julia Garner as Caroline Ellison in a scene from The Altruists filmed in the Orchid set

Fjelsted and his crew also built miniature replicas of real Albany buildings and a marina complete with a 10-foot remote-control yacht, using forced perspective to shoot them as backdrops. He calls this “the anti-AI set.”

In the dramatization, coworkers trade crypto in a communal office and party on a balcony overlooking the water. An employee in a hot tub bangs a gong for rounds of speed chess played on fancy sets. Inside, gaming is a major pastime, and there’s a baby grand piano on hand, though no one plays it. In real life, it was reported that Bankman-Fried kept a bean bag under the piano for napping.

Alex Lawther as Sam Trabucco and Anthony Boyle as Sam Bankman-Fried in The Altruists

Looking to humanize the leads, Fjelsted observed how “insecurities [and] their feelings of a little bit of an imposter syndrome” informed Sam and Caroline’s design choices or lack thereof. Neutrals, sea colors, and palm tree prints made for a better on-camera look, but in real FBI interior photos, Fjelsted saw bedroom walls with powder blue sponge-painted over navy.

Garner and Boyle as Ellison and Bankman-Fried in The Altruists

Throughout the show’s eight episodes, the penthouse goes from bustling with the excitement of trading to vibrating with panic as the scandal comes crashing down. Eventually, the space is left nearly empty, with just Bankman-Fried and his parents remaining. In 2024, it was reported that Bankman-Fried’s real estate holdings would be sold off in order to pay back his debts, and today, the unit is indeed up for sale.
