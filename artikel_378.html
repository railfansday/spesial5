Best Smart Bed of 2026: Bryte and Tempur-Pedic

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

While they may not be able to levitate or act like a Jetsons-style robot personal assistant (yet), smart beds these days come pretty close to accomplishing both. Like adjustable mattresses, smart beds offer modifiable firmness and the ability to raise and lower the head and foot. These days, though, smart beds offer so much more, integrating features like dual-climate control, sleep tracking, and massage therapies.

The best smart bed is, by far, the Bryte Balance Pro Smart Mattress, and if you’re looking for the most luxurious, it’s the Tempur-Pedic Tempur-ActiveBreeze. Let me break it down for you below.

For more old-school mattresses, check out our guides to the Best Mattresses, the Best Organic Mattresses, and the Best Mattresses for Back Pain.

I’m currently testing the Eight Sleep Pod 6.

Most smart beds on the market use air chambers on each side of the mattress to achieve adjustable firmness. This is fine, but you can definitely feel them beneath you. But that's not the case with Bryte's Balance Pro, which is why it's so great.

Instead of air chambers, this smart bed is designed with coils (Bryte Balancers) and memory foam layers. These coils can be adjusted to specific zones, and if you want extra lumbar support or contouring, you can customize each section through the companion app. There are also 100 firmness levels, which makes it suitable for a variety of sleepers, from belly sleepers to larger-bodied individuals who may find air-chambered beds too soft.

Former WIRED reviewer Julia Forbes’ favorite feature is the massage function, which gently presses the coils against you. The smart bed is controlled exclusively via app, where you can also view your sleep insights. Along with metrics like sleep stages, duration, and heart rate, it also tracks the number of bed rebalances, which is how many times the Bryte Balancers are auto-adjusted throughout the night. There’s also an AI Sleep Concierge that is best for getting tips on how to sleep better.

Bryte Balance Pro ranges from $5,999 for a twin mattress to $6,899 for a California king.

Hot sleepers and overheaters will love Tempur-Pedic's Tempur-ActiveBreeze. There are fans built into the adjustable base that disperse cool or warm air, with three airflow speeds (slow, medium, and fast). The airflow is streamlined from the base through two ports in the bed, and the perforated memory foam layers and coils help promote circulation. According to the company, the dual climate control offers a 30-degree temperature range, which is the largest we’ve come across in testing. Former WIRED reviewer Julia Forbes could feel the air lightly skittering along the surface. On the bright side, even when she piled on her heaviest bedding, she didn’t run hot once.

The Tempur-ActiveBreeze also offers sleep tracking, AI coaching, and a suite of relaxation programs that combine meditative soundscapes and massage therapy to lull you to sleep. Probably most notably, there’s an automatic snore response, which raises your head if it detects a breathing disturbance. The companion app breaks down your sleep data into digestible nightly, monthly, and annual reports.

The Tempur-ActiveBreeze (base included via Tempur-Pedic) ranges from $10,398 for a queen to $19,396 for a split California king.

Personal Comfort’s Rejuvenate is a solid adjustable mattress for side sleepers. It doesn’t offer any additional features or sleep-tracking capabilities, so it doesn’t truly qualify as a smart bed in my book.

Nevertheless, the Rejuvenate is a side-sleeper-friendly option because of its deep memory foam layers that contour to the body. If you want to sink in while still relying on the 45 firmness levels to ensure you're getting the support you need, this is it. Former WIRED reviewer Julia Forbes found a medium-firm 30 to be ideal for a healthy spine alignment when side sleeping.

What About Personal Comfort’s Adjustable Base?

An adjustable base is available as an add-on and helpful if you opt for one of Personal Comfort’s specialty bed sizes, such as the Queen Flex-Head or King Flex-Head. It’s the only way to move the head and foot of the Rejuvenate, as well as implement adjustable lumbar support, pillow tilt, and massage features.

Julia tested the Power Flex 4. It provides a firmer feel right around the midsection, and its lumbar adjustment setting really amps up back support. She did find the massage features to be uncomfortable, though, describing them as “increasingly aggressive vibrations, akin to tectonic plates.”

FYI, Personal Comfort often runs a bundle promotion.

Should I Use a Sleep Tracker With a Smart Bed?

Some smart beds offer contactless sleep tracking, which eliminates the need for an additional wearable. That said, they do carry some limitations. They’re generally not as accurate as contact-based trackers and can overestimate sleep stages and miscalculate duration. They often can’t track the same breadth of physiological measurements as smartwatches and smart rings do. And they can misattribute data from a partner or pet sharing the bed. But they’re convenient, especially if you dislike wearing trackers to bed.

Personally, I still sleep with my Apple Watch Series 12 and/or Oura Ring 5 to bed to compare. But, if I forget to wear either, my smart bed is auto-tracking my slumber in the background.

Do I Need an Adjustable Base to Go With My Smart Bed?

You are very likely going to want an adjustable base to go with your smart bed in order to enjoy everything your smart bed has to offer. This is especially true if you are purchasing one with the expectation of being able to move the top and bottom of the bed into different angles, like “TV watching” and “zero gravity” settings.

What’s the Difference Between an Adjustable Bed and a Smart Bed?

Generally speaking, adjustable beds and smart beds are similar in that they both can raise or lower different sections of your mattress, with a remote control or companion app. Smart beds take it up a notch by incorporating additional features, such as sleep tracking, temperature control, and automated adjustments.

Can a Smart Bed Work for All Sleeping Positions?

Smart beds often come in adjustable firmness levels, and therefore can work for all sleeping positions. However, just like with any other mattress, the fabric technology is a huge deciding factor when it comes to comfort.

For example, the Bryte Balance Pro is a much better choice for stomach sleepers or if you have a heavier body type. Coil support simply does not compare to even the firmest of inflated air chambers, which are usually what make smart beds' firmness levels “adjustable.” On the other hand, the Rejuvenate has a softer feel right off the bat because of its memory foam layers, making it ideal for side sleepers who need pressure relief.

Do I Need Wi-Fi Connectivity for My Smart Bed to Work?

Yes—if the power goes out, your smart bed will likely go offline. Smart beds are factored into smart home technology, meaning they rely on Wi-Fi for full functionality, especially to track sleep data and maintain the firmness level you've chosen.

What Does WIRED Do With the Mattresses After Testing Them?

These smart beds were provided for free as media samples, and we either return them to the company or donate without personal benefit when testing is done. Most of these beds went to nurses, students, families in need, or those with medical conditions that were affecting their sleep.
