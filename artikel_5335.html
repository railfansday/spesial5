SpaceX shares dropped 6.08% in premarket trading on Friday to $130.02, down $8.41 from Thursday’s close of $138.43, slipping below the company’s $135 IPO price from June. The stock fell 3.53% on Thursday as the broader tech selloff spread across space and AI names. Investors are also weighing SpaceX’s heavy spending, including a reported $32 billion debt raise to buy Nvidia chips and $74 billion planned for US data centre projects, along with scrutiny over possible merger talks with Tesla. On the operations side, the FAA is seeking public comment on SpaceX’s plan to launch Starship from Texas up to 456 times a year, following last weekend’s first successful catch of the rocket’s upper stage. The US also raised the combined ceiling on military launch contracts for SpaceX, Blue Origin and United Launch Alliance by $985 million. In a setback, Google picked Blue Origin’s New Glenn rocket for a 2029 orbital AI mission, even though SpaceX launched the project’s first prototype.
