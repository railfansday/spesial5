The Tragic Story Behind <i><em>La Bola Negra,</em></i> One of the Year’s Best Films

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Based on an unfinished play by Federico Garcia Lorca, the movie is already garnering Oscar buzz

It is rare to come across uncompromisingly queer work from LGBTQ+ artists from a century ago and beyond. Many writers did not get to see their work published in their own lifetime. E.M. Forster’s groundbreaking novel Maurice, which he began writing in 1913 and featured a rare-for-the-time happy ending, was not published until a year after his death, in 1971; Emily Dickinson’s romantic stanzas, which many critics believe were dedicated to her sister-in-law, Susan Gilbert Dickinson, weren’t even discovered until long after her passing. But even those queer artists who did see their work out in the world didn’t manage so easily. James Baldwin was told by his agent to burn the manuscript for Giovanni’s Room. Jane Rule was rejected more than 20 times in her efforts to find a publisher for Desert of the Heart.

One of this year’s most critically acclaimed films, La Bola Negra (“the black ball” in English), is based on an unfinished play of the same name written by the Spanish poet and playwright Federico Garcia Lorca in 1936. In the four pages of the story that remain, a wealthy young man is trying to gain entrance to an exclusive casino in Granada. In order to determine his admittance, the members turn to a voting system where they choose to roll a white ball in favor or a black ball in opposition. After they vote, he is handed a black ball; later, when his father asks him why he thinks he was rejected, the boy tells him that it’s because he is gay.

Tragically, Lorca never got the chance to finish what may have been a groundbreaking story. (He was shot dead by nationalist forces after being imprisoned during the beginning of the Spanish Civil War, and his remains have never been found.) But he had detailed his ideas for the drama in a letter to fellow playwright Cipriano Rivas Cherif.

That’s where Javier Calvo and Javier Ambrossi, the directing duo endearingly referred to as “Los Javis,” comes in. Their movie rewrites a bit of history, making it so that Lorca (also a character in the film) manages to complete most of his play, save for the last few pages. The film jumps between three different years—1932, 1937, and 2017—to explore the lives of different gay men across a century who are all loosely connected by the writer’s work. (The cast includes stars like Élite’s Miguel Bernardeau, folk-pop singer Guitarricadelafuente, and Oscar nominee Glenn Close.)

“We don’t have that many movies or books about the history that connects us as a community, the queer community,” co-director Ambrossi told NPR earlier this week. “That’s why we wanted to make it big. We wanted to make it epic, and we wanted to make it bold.”

To try to piece together the story, the directors combed through old letters and archives to try to imagine how Lorca might have ended it. “We were like detectives,” co-director Calvo noted. “It was fun to discover that he would like to talk about his projects in parties.” And these pieces are precisely how the story of La Bola Negra came together (but don’t worry, no spoilers here).

Now, Los Javis are bringing light to this untold story in an effort to preserve queer history and maintain the legacy of someone who couldn’t so freely tell his truth.

“We are all connected by this black ball that we all share, that we pass to each other, every generation—a black ball that is silence, pain, shame, but also it’s a spark of life and desire and love and joy,” Calvo said. “I feel like [this project] made me feel proud of my community and of the brave people that fought before me.”

You can watch La Bola Negra in theaters now. It arrives to Netflix on December 2.
