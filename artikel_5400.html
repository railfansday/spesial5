The seasonal decline in housing inventory is in full force

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Inventory declined to 895,988 as mortgage rates reversed higher to 7.50%. New listings remained fell slightly year over year to 53,162, while weekly pending sales showed 4 weeks of stabilization.  AI Summary

Even with volatility in mortgage rates last week, the seasonal decline in inventory is in full force. Weekly housing demand has continued to stabilize over the last four weeks and the new listing data is not showing any stress in the housing market. We had a lot of crazy things happen last week — in fact I’m writing this article still stuck in Dallas because of the FAA’s nationwide ground stop — but housing data never sleeps, so let’s take a look.

Housing inventory has picked up recently, after mortgage rates went above 6.64% and then shot up toward 7.60%. Inventory growth had been very tame this year, with some weeks negative year over year. As housing inventory returns to normal levels, it takes weaker demand to get stronger growth and rates moving toward 7.60% did that. Still, it’s more about sales taking longer to happen and demand being hit, because new listings data didn’t budge much recently. Now, we are in the full-blown seasonal decline as we get ready to prep for spring 2027.

Inventory growth is running at 7.11% over last year even with easier comps, as last year demand was running toward a 9-month high in December of 2025.

New listings have been in their seasonal decline since June and nothing abnormal with this data line has happened in 2026; in fact, it’s been the best year of new listings since 2022. One concern I had about higher rates is that more people might simply not list their homes — as they did in the second half of 2022 due to higher rates. However, that hasn’t happened; we did see a more noticeable year-over-year decline this last week, but for the most part it’s been a healthy year.

In a normal environment, new listings range from 80,000 to 100,000 per week during peak periods. For context, during the housing bubble years, new listings ranged from 250,000 to 400,000 per week for several years.Here is last week’s new listings data for the past two years:

Typically, about one-third of homes see price reductions before they sell, reflecting the housing market’s dynamic nature. Overall, price-cut percentages this year have been lower than last year. This held true until rates moved above 6.64% and then above 7%. At that point demand fell and the year-over-year price-cut percentage rose 1%.In my 2026 home-price forecast, I called for a national decline of -0.62% for the year. Home-price growth really isn’t going anywhere this year and my -0.62% forecast may be hard to achieve, since most home-price indices show growth between 1% and 2%.However, with higher rates and a rising price-cut percentage, we will be closer to the forecast. Clearly, if rates hadn’t risen above 6.75%, pricing data wouldn’t have shown the recent slowdown.

Last week wasn’t good for mortgage rates or the bond market: we had hawkish Fed statements and hot inflation data. We were getting closer to mortgage rates below 7% for the holidays, but they made a big reversal from 7.17% to 7.50%. In addition, President Trump talked about raising tariffs against Mexico and we still have no deal with Iran. Pretty much everything you needed for rates to go lower went the other way — something I talked about in this episode of the HousingWire Daily podcast with Editor in Chief  Sarah Wheeler.

Mortgage spreads have worsened recently, but they did improve last week. Traditionally, when mortgage rates escalate with the 10-year yield and then make a big reversal — like we just had from a 5.35% 10-year yield top that dropped down toward 4.82% — spreads do get worse to try to compress mortgage rate volatility. However, we had a big reversal higher, and spreads improved some as the 10-year yield rose toward 5.14%.

Historically, mortgage spreads have ranged from 1.60% to 1.80%. Last week, spreads fell to 2.13% from 2.27% a week earlier.

Our pending home sales data provides a week-to-week perspective, though holidays and short-term fluctuations can affect results. This weekly pending sales data typically takes 30-60 days to reflect in sales data.

The key for the last four weeks: housing demand has stabilized, as our weekly pending sales have shown. Now, higher rates most likely will impact this week’s data more than last week, but the last four weeks have shown if we can get rates back below 7% and just have them stick, we can work with this.

Housing demand typically grows when rates get below 6.64% and fades when rates are above 7%, especially if the move in rates is fast, like we saw recently. Still, it was encouraging to see stabilization in the data when rates were trying to get below 7% again. Of course, we are seeing big year-over-year declines in this data line as rates were heading toward 6% last year.

Purchase application data, which looks out 30-90 days, has softened as mortgage rates rose above 6.64% and are now above 7%. We did have 8% week-to-week growth last week as rates tried to get below 7% the week before. I expect the next purchase application data to get hit as rates picked up. This data was very positive year over year when rates were under 6.64%, but not so much when it headed toward 7% and got to 7.60%.
