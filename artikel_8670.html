Living Without a Car Can Save Thousands – If Your Trips Fit the Alternatives

Before you sell your car to save money, price a month of your real trips on transit, rideshare, a bike, or a car-share service, and add a cushion for the rides you didn’t plan. Go car-free only if that total is less than a month of the car’s full cost.

For the average new car, AAA puts that full cost at $12,863 a year, or about $1,072 a month, once the value it loses, loan interest, insurance, fuel, upkeep, and registration are counted. An older, paid-off car costs less, but its insurance, parking, gas, and upkeep keep coming every month.

To check yours, list every trip you make in a typical month and price each one the car-free way, at the transit fare, a rideshare app’s estimate, or the car-share or rental rate. Add a cushion for the rides you didn’t plan, the ones a storm, a sick kid, or a missed last train forces on you. Going car-free pays only if that total comes in under what owning your car costs in a month, insurance and parking included. Two-car households can run the same test on the second car first.

Say a city couple owns a paid-off car worth $9,000 that costs them $700 a month for insurance, parking, gas, and upkeep. Their trips, cushion included, price out at $350 a month on transit, rideshare, and car sharing. Selling saves them $350 a month, or $4,200 a year, and turns the car into $9,000 in cash.

The savings also have to be worth what car-free costs in time and hassle, which your total leaves out. Transit takes longer than driving, rain and snow make biking and waiting harder, trips with kids mean hauling car seats, and an emergency can’t wait for the next bus.

So try going without the car before you sell it. Park it for a month, pay for every trip the car-free way, and compare the receipts with what the car costs you in a month.
