Most phone plans charge less for each line as more lines join one account, so relatives or friends paying for separate plans can each pay less by sharing one. Check the plan’s price at your exact number of lines, and share only with people you trust, since the account owner owes the whole bill.

AT&T’s Value 2.0 unlimited plan costs $50 a month for one line but $30 a line for four, with AutoPay from a bank account and paperless billing.

Two lines cost $45 each and three cost $35 each, all before taxes and fees. So four relatives each paying $50 for a line of their own on that plan spend $200 a month, while the same four on one account pay $120. Together they save $960 a year.

Many prepaid brands, which run on a major carrier’s network for less, cut the price per line too. Total Wireless’s MAX 5G plan, on Verizon’s network, costs $50 for one line, $40 a line for two, about $32 for three, and about $26 for four. Those prices already take off its AutoPay discount and include taxes and fees. Not every plan works this way. Verizon’s Simplicity plan charges a flat $45 a line with Auto Pay and paper-free billing, however many lines you add.

Share with relatives or friends whose payments you’d count on. At AT&T, the account owner is responsible for the whole bill, so an unpaid share can bring a late fee and put every line on the account at risk of suspension. AT&T’s SplitPay can text each person the share for their line, with a link to pay it.

Plan for someone leaving, too. On AT&T, the account owner starts a transfer of billing responsibility, and the person leaving accepts it. That moves the line and its number to that person’s own account. They must pass AT&T’s credit check, and any phone still being paid off on the line must be paid off or transferred first. The lines that stay then pay the higher per-line price of a smaller account.

Price the plan at the exact number of lines you’d have before anyone joins, and write down each person’s share and the day it’s due.
