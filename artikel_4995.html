Here’s what happened in crypto today

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Need to know what happened in crypto today? Here is the latest news on daily trends and events impacting Bitcoin price, blockchain, DeFi, Web3 and crypto regulation.

Today in crypto, prediction markets favored Democrats taking control of Congress as Americans headed to the polls for the US midterm elections, Kraken restricted accounts linked to a suspected $133 million crypto theft in Europe, and North Korea denied involvement in the $6.3 billion Binance hack.

As Americans across the country head to the polls for the US midterm elections Tuesday, prediction market traders have spent months wagering on the outcome. According to an analysis by The Washington Post, nearly $1.9 billion in election-related contracts had traded on Kalshi and Polymarket as of Monday, marking record trading activity for a midterm election cycle.

Markets on both platforms favor Democrats winning control of Congress, with a Democratic sweep priced at 75% on Kalshi and 75.5% on Polymarket as of 4:30 pm UTC. Kalshi traders gave Democrats a 93% chance of taking the House and a 75% chance of winning the Senate, while Polymarket priced those outcomes at 94.5% and 75.5%, respectively.

The vote comes nine days after prediction markets failed to anticipate the outcome of Brazil’s presidential election, with Flávio Bolsonaro favored at roughly 80% the day before incumbent President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won the runoff. Kalshi CEO Tarek Mansour reportedly cautioned that “a 5% probability doesn’t mean it won’t happen,” according to CNBC.

For the crypto industry, a shift in congressional control could reshape the prospects for long-awaited regulatory clarity.

“This wasn’t one mistake or one breach — it was a months-long campaign,” Binance co-CEO Richard Teng said in a Tuesday post on X.

Kraken has restricted accounts linked to a suspected crypto theft of more than $133 million from an unidentified European Union entity and is working with law enforcement to support recovery efforts.

“This incident did not originate on the Kraken platform, and we’re actively investigating reports that some of the assets were subsequently moved through accounts on our platform,” Kraken told Cointelegraph. “We’ve restricted the accounts in question and are working with law enforcement to support recovery efforts.”

Kraken’s statement follows blockchain investigator ZachXBT’s disclosure on Tuesday that he identified another $27 million in withdrawals from centralized exchanges, increasing the suspected losses from the $106 million he reported on Monday. He said some withdrawals occurred after his initial disclosure.

Bitquery reported that the suspected stolen funds were withdrawn from accounts at Kraken, Crypto.com and OKX. It also found that $62.98 million in stablecoins was converted into 27,553 Ether (ETH) within roughly four hours.

Crypto.com told Cointelegraph that it had taken steps to prevent funds associated with the incident from moving through its platform.

OKX acknowledged Cointelegraph’s request for comment and said it was looking into the matter.

Kim Yo Jong, the sister of North Korean leader Kim Jong Un, denied North Korea’s involvement in the $6.3 billion Binance hack and appeared to suggest, without evidence, that the US could have been behind the theft.

In a statement released via the Korean Central News Agency (KCNA) on Tuesday, Kim rejected the FBI’s attribution of the attack to a North Korean-backed hacking group.

“The FBI is trying to brand the DPRK as a ‘cyber criminal state’, claiming that it obtained information that a hacking organization ‘supported’ by the DPRK was involved in the incident,” said Kim. “But the US assertion is an absurd rhetoric and nothing new.”

She described the allegations as a political provocation intended to damage North Korea’s international standing. The FBI also blamed North Korea for the $1.5 billion Bybit hack in February 2025, an accusation Kim also rejected on Tuesday.

“This is not the first time that the U.S. has attempted to spread misunderstanding of the DPRK in the international community, blindly linking the DPRK with the ‘cyber crime’ incidents whenever they occur on an international scale,” said Kim.
