Pink Hair Is Back and Everyone From SZA to Adéla Are Making a Case for It

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Remember when Kylie Jenner was changing her hair color about every other week, Tumblr was overflowing with pastel-haired girlies, and we were all contemplating whether we could pull off pink hair? Well, dust off your 2016 Pinterest boards, bb, because pink hair is officially making a comeback. But this time, it’s not just King Kylie leading the charge. SZA, Adéla, and even Martha Stewart have been putting their own spins on the color, proving that there’s no one way (or age!) to enter your pink hair era. “Since pink is not a natural hair color, you don’t really have to abide by any typical color analysis rules,” says colorist Emily Claire, which means you have free range to do whatever the heck you’d like.

And the range is range-ing. From electric pink to soft rose gold, I promise there’s a rosy hue for you. Personally, I’m loving this return to hair color that’s just fun—and I’m not the only one. “Recently my clients with pink hair have been requesting baby pink and deep berry pink,” says colorist Cara Craig. Plus, Cosmo called out pink as a top fall hair color trend this year. So, whether you’re ready to go full-on Barbie or want to experiment with a subtle rosy tint, there’s a pink hair idea with your name on it. Ahead, we’re breaking it all down, including how to choose your shade, what to ask for at the salon, and how to keep your pink hair looking fresh.

Short answer: Yes. There’s a truly pink hair color for everyone. “I have clients asking for all shades of pink, from blush and rose gold to hot pink to magenta,” says hairstylist Alyssa Chapa.

Pink is surprisingly so versatile, so there are plenty of ways to make it work for you. “In my opinion, pink is a neutral. I think of fashion colors as cousins to classic shades,” says Craig. “So if you are a blonde, consider baby pink. If you are a brunette you’ll want to move to darker pinks and fuchsias. If you want to feel like a redhead then wear rose golds and warm pinks.” She also recommends a violet pink if you prefer keeping things cool-toned.

Wondering about how your skin tone will look with pink hair? “I’m personally of the opinion that there is no right or wrong shade for your skin tone,” says Chapa, but if it’s really important to you, here are her tips for finding the best hair color for your skin tone. “Start by figuring out your undertones,” she says. “I like using the veins on the inside of the wrist; if you have blue or purple veins then you usually have cool undertones, green veins mean warm undertones, and a mixture of both is a neutral undertone.” Then, it’s pretty easy from there: Match your undertones to the shade of pink, which your colorist will be able to help out with (think baby or icy pink for cool tones, peach or salmon pink for warm tones, that kind of thing).

First things first: Bring reference photos to your colorist—we’ve handily included some pics throughout this story for ya. After all, Adéla’s vivid pink and Stewart’s subtle rose gold hair require very different approaches, so you’ll want to make sure you’re specific about what shade you’re after.

“For most people, unless you happen to be naturally blonde, the process would involve lightening your hair to the desired level with bleach, then toning it to the desired pink with a direct dye,” says Claire.

Need some specific pink hair ideas? These are colorist-approved: “One of my favorite ways to wear pink lately is over classic blonde highlights,” says Craig. “I also love a pink double process (e.g. Adéla).” Double process just means a two-step coloring method—your colorist will first bleach/lighten your hair, then apply the pink color. You can also go with chunky highlights, “especially a halo hairline where the pink frames the face,” adds Chapa. “Mixing chunky pink highlights in with shades of red or brown to create a kind of calico effect is also popular online.”

Now for the not-so-fun part: Pink hair doesn’t necessarily equal low-maintenance hair. “No matter the tone, you’re going to have to refresh it in four to six weeks if you want it to stay pink,” says Chapa. If you want to go as low-maintenance as possible, a softer pink is the way to go, notes Craig.

For a more natural-looking grow-out or to keep your hair color easy, “your alternatives are to choose an already muted color like a dusty rose or mauve and let it fade to blonde or brunette, or to invest in an at-home color depositing treatment to maintain your pink until your next salon visit,” Chapa says.

You can help extend your color by washing less frequently, using color-safe shampoo and conditioner, limiting heat styling, and incorporating a color-depositing mask or at-home hair gloss. “I always recommend my clients use a pink-tinted shampoo or conditioner to keep up with the color between appointments at home,” says Claire.
