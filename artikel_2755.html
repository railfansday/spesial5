5 Things I Think I Think About the Miami Dolphins After Week 10

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

If you're looking for a silver lining after the Miami Dolphins' 24-22 loss to the Indianapolis Colts, I have good news: The franchise may have found themselves a new hero, and it's Zane Gonzalez. Here are 5 things I think I think after the head-shaking loss to the Daniel Jones-led Colts.

At Nashville's Nissan Stadium, the New York Jets just won their first Super Bowl in more than 50 years. Gang Green is celebrating: Champagne, laughter, tears. Three years earlier, the Colts had traded away the pick that became the No. 1 selection in the 2027 draft. Now the quarterback chosen with that pick has delivered New York a championship. In the on-air interview, head coach Aaron Glenn hugs his third-year superstar quarterback, yelling joyously to the cameras, "We're only here because of this guy! He put us on his back and carried us to become world champions!"

The words "world champions" echo in the brain of thirty-four-year-old Miami Subs employee Zane Gonzalez as he shuts off the black-and-white television in his parents' basement.

Zane takes in all the Miami Dolphins gear around his room – his prized foam finger from 1984, the 8-inch T.D. stuffy on his pillow – and sighs. "Where did we go wrong, T.D.?" he asks his stuffy. "How did the Jets win the Super Bowl while we're still looking for a playoff win?"

"You're right, it was that 2027 draft. That win over the Colts knocked us out of the running for the top pick in the draft! We should have been the Super Bowl winners this year!" He looks up. "T.D., I know what I must do."

Six months later, after convincing Dolphins owner Stephen Ross to invest $500 million in temporal engineering, Gonzalez steps into a prototype time machine and travels three years into the past.

His mission is simple: Convince the Dolphins to bring him on as placekicker. And then?

Most importantly: Miss against the Colts.

Make sure Miami loses enough games to stay ahead of the Colts in the draft order. Land the future franchise quarterback who would finally deliver the Dolphins' long-awaited Super Bowl title.

Sacrifice a few meaningless wins today and the Dolphins become champions tomorrow.

After all, no one would willingly miss two field goals and an extra point in a game his team lost by two … right?

Zane Gonzalez stands at South Pointe Park Pier, staring out at the Atlantic.

The day before, the Dolphins lost to the Patriots, finishing off a 2-15 season and securing the first overall pick in the upcoming draft, one win ahead of the sad sack Colts.

He turns. A little girl, maybe six years old, stands behind him in an oversized Ricky Williams jersey. She clutches her well-loved T.D. stuffed dolphin under one arm.

The girl looks him up and down. Then spits at his feet.

"Nice kicking, dill weed." Before he can respond, she pivots and marches away.

Gonzalez watches her disappear down the pier.

Two weeks ago, the Dolphins held a Drake Maye-led Patriots offense to 3 points in the first half. Last week, that same defense limited Detroit's high-powered offense to one of its lowest point totals of the season. And while the final score on Sunday will show the Colts scored 20 points, the reality is a little different. After halftime, Indianapolis managed exactly three offensive points. Daniel Jones finished with fewer than 110 passing yards. The Dolphins intercepted him twice, returned one for a touchdown, forced a fumble and repeatedly forced Indianapolis off the field with three-and-outs. By the fourth quarter, the defense was dragging the rest of the team back into the game whether it wanted to come along or not.

The usual suspects were everywhere. Chris Johnson (59-yard pick six) continues to look like one of the steals of the draft. Michael Taaffe (6 tackles, 1 pass defended) once again found ways to impact the game. Chop Robinson (1 sack, 2 QB hits) consistently disrupted the pocket. Jordyn Brooks (9 tackles, 1 pass defended) was all over the field.

The Dolphins spotted Indianapolis seven points, generated almost nothing in the kicking game, and still found themselves with a chance to win in the final minutes because the defense spent the second half strangling the Colts offense. For a unit that has carried this team for much of the season, it was another outstanding performance.

Remember this scene from the greatest movie of all time?

The Dolphins' defense is now the Bluesmobile. The Dolphins entered the game without Zach Sieler. Then Jordan Phillips got hurt, and is out for the year. Then Zeek Biggers got hurt, and he's out for the year. By the end of the afternoon, Miami was essentially down to Kenneth Grant and Keith Cooper as healthy defensive tackles.

In-game, that degree of attrition eventually catches up with you. Without those defensive tackles, the run defense clearly felt the strain. Indianapolis finished with 172 rushing yards as Jonathan Taylor and Seth McGowan consistently found running lanes. The Dolphins simply didn't have their usual flexibility up front – too many snaps played by too few healthy bodies.

Miami's defense was good enough to win Sunday, but it wasn't healthy enough to win Sunday.

Let's get the obvious part out of the way first: Kyle McCord made mistakes. The strip-sack touchdown changed the game. The late sack in the fourth quarter was costly. Neither play can simply be ignored because he's a rookie.

But: The strip-sack wasn't the result of panic – it was a quarterback trying to make the correct read and reacting a second too slowly. The late sack wasn't a quarterback seeing ghosts as much as a young passer processing pressure incorrectly while trying to work deeper into the progression.

And then there's everything else. McCord converted a third-and-13. He made several NFL-caliber throws. He stayed aggressive. And he didn't fall apart after the strip-sack touchdown put seven points on the board for Indianapolis.

Young quarterbacks have gotten rattled by far less. Instead, McCord kept battling and gave Miami a chance to win. If you're looking for reasons to be optimistic, Sunday's mistakes looked fixable.

Back in August, I was so young and naïve, blinded by optimism that 2026 would lead to a glorious 6-11 season. Sure, we're rebuilding, but at least as the youngest team in the league, we would avoid some of those devastating injuries the league's more geriatric squads had to deal with.

Now? Now we have Kyle McCord handing the ball off to Ollie Gordon behind a leakier-than-expected offensive line. Our defensive tackles are so decimated, that we're down to three starters – Kenneth Grant, Keith Cooper and you, dear reader. Go suit up.

The team is still in evaluation mode, and there's still a lot of meaningful development that can and should happen for our first- and second-year starters. But the pipe dream of 6 wins feels especially unattainable now. At 38%, the Dolphins have the best odds to pick first in the 2027 draft, according to the prediction market platform Kalshi. ESPN gives the team a 41% chance, also first.

I'd like to see the Dolphins win against the Jets the Sunday after Thanksgiving. A two-win season isn't great, but if both wins come against the Jets, that can get me through the offseason.
