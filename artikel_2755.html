Nets shake up everything but the outcome in heartbreaking fourth straight loss

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Team meetings. Lineup changes. Whatever it took. Brooklyn tried everything to shake up a three-game losing skid.

The Nets finally showed some heart Friday night. But then they got it broken in a 113-111 loss to the Magic in front of a crowd of 17,067 at Barclays Center.

Franz Wagner hit a go-ahead 14-footer with 2.8 seconds left, and Julius Randle missed a desperation 3-pointer at the buzzer on the other end.

Brooklyn Nets forward Danny Wolf shoots as Orlando Magic center Wendell Carter Jr. defends on Friday, Nov. 20, 2026, at Barclays Center in Brooklyn, N.Y. Robert Sabo for NY Post

Randle finished with a team-high 27 points, and Brooklyn fought. They just couldn't quite close.

After the Nets (3-12) embarrassed themselves in Wednesday's 137-107 non-effort against Chicago, they held a team meeting, with some real talk about what went wrong and how to fix it.

Friday's effort showed it wasn't all just talk.

"No one wants to lose like we did [Wednesday] … so a lot needed to be said. A lot of guys have been doing a lot of talking; we've all been doing a lot of talking," Michael Porter Jr. said. "We've gotta string some games together. We've had spurts of good responses after really bad games, but that has to be a consistent theme. We can't just let that be responses to really bad losses."

Wednesday qualified as a really bad loss, the worst of a disappointing season.

Brooklyn Nets forward Julius Randle (30) goes up for a shot against the Orlando Magic at Barclays Center in Brooklyn, N.Y., Friday, Nov. 20, 2026. Robert Sabo for NY Post

After that, the Nets had a look-in-the-mirror meeting that sources said included not only players, but also coaches.

"Everybody collectively. It was a good conversation, and everybody pitched into what they saw," Porter said. "There was a lot of internal discussion that I'll keep between the team. But just realizing we got to be better and addressing what needed to be addressed."

One thing that coach Jordi Fernández did was shuffle his rotations.

Orlando Magic forward Franz Wagner dunks during the second quarter against the Brooklyn Nets on Friday, Nov. 20, 2026, at Barclays Center in Brooklyn, N.Y. Robert Sabo for NY Post

Egor Dëmin — last year's lottery pick — was pulled out of the starting lineup. He was brought off the bench for the first time since Nov. 5, 2025, his rookie season.

Instead, Fernández went with 6-foot-11 forward Danny Wolf as a jumbo shooting guard. And the move paid immediate dividends.

Wolf poured in 20 points and five rebounds, finishing a team-high plus-15. Rookie Mikel Brown Jr. added a career-high-tying 18 points.

Porter, who admitted not having the right energy in his five-point, 1-of-7 effort Wednesday, bounced back well. His layup off a Day'Ron Sharpe feed gave the Nets a 63-62 edge coming out of the half.

The Nets coughed up 10 unanswered Orlando points, the run capped by Wagner (game-high 35 points).

Brooklyn Nets center Moritz Wagner defends against Orlando Magic forward Franz Wagner during a game Friday, Nov. 20, 2026, at Barclays Center in Brooklyn, N.Y. Robert Sabo for NY Post

The Nets trailed 72-63, and things got worse when starting point guard Nolan Traore limped off with eight minutes left in the third with a hamstring injury. Those are tricky for any player, especially for a twitchy speedster such as Traore.

Brooklyn trailed 89-85 after Wagner found Desmond Bane for a 3-pointer with 1:24 left in the third.

But Wolf drilled a 3-pointer off a Randle feed to cap a 13-4 run and put the Nets ahead 98-93 with 8:36 left to play.

After Brown missed a fadeaway 19-footer with 9 seconds left, Wagner didn't miss the go-ahead bucket on the other end.
