Fantasy Football Week 11 RB Report: Another fumble makes D'Andre Swift tough to trust, plus more backfield splits

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Let's take a second to appreciate the backup running back and the importance of having that insurance on your bench. Emmett Johnson drew another start in place of the injured Kenneth Walker III on Sunday. For the third time in four weeks, the rookie back went for 22+ fantasy points on a hefty 23 carries for 105 yards and a score, plus four catches for 38 yards. Most of that damage was done well before the game clock wound down in the fourth quarter.

If you're a Walker manager who was smart enough to add Johnson back after Week 8, nice work. If you were like me (in one league) and didn't grab Johnson to replace Walker, well … it's been a rough watch the past month or so.

In this piece, we talk a lot about backfield splits and depth running backs, specifically, in an attempt to inform you for future weeks. Let's see what the highlights and box scores show us that we can use for fantasy football going into Week 12 and the Thanksgiving week slate.

It was an ugly week in general for the Bears offense in a spot we thought could produce points, considering the team had an implied total around 30. Instead, we got inconsistency, particularly in the run game despite the return of Kyle Monangai. He had missed the past five games due to turf toe and a thumb injury. This was also an elite matchup for running backs; New Orleans entered Week 11 allowing the sixth-most rushing yards per game and sixth-most rushing TDs this season.

While Swift got to an OK floor for fantasy purposes because of a short TD catch, he could be back in the doghouse after another lost fumble, his third of the season. As a result, we saw carries for fourth-string RB Salvon Ahmed, with Roschon Johnson leaving the game early due to an ankle injury. Monangai also wasn't his usual efficient self, though we're willing to give him a pass coming back after a long absence.

Let's talk about Swift first. In games following a Swift fumble, Swift maintained a heavy workload with 22 touches in Week 3 and 13 in Week 5 (no Caleb Williams). So Swift shouldn't lose his role, but he also hasn't been all that impressive. He doesn't have a carry longer than 15 yards since Week 1 and doesn't have a catch that went for more than 13 yards since Week 2. Ben Johnson could opt to lean more on Monangai, but he and Swift have had a relatively even split of the carries when both are healthy, with Swift being more of a threat in the passing game. Ahmed also didn't look all that bad and could leech a few touches.

The next two weeks aren't ideal, either. The Bears get the Lions on Thanksgiving on the road and the Jaguars in Week 13, two of the worst matchups for RBs in fantasy. It will be tough to count on both Swift and Monangai as anything more than flex options in the short term, and after that we could see the latter take on more work if he can stay healthy. The matchups soften a bit in a key stretch from Weeks 14-16.

Maybe it was the Panthers defense but the Ravens offense looks revived with Jacoby Brissett under center. At least for fantasy purposes. Baltimore scored 33 points and had over 400 yards of offense Sunday in the narrow loss to Carolina. For real life, it was brutal. For fantasy managers, it was a sigh of relief for those with Henry and Zay Flowers.

But we're here to talk about the backfield specifically, where Henry got back to work with his highest total on the ground since Week 5, when he went for over 100 yards rushing. He now has four total TDs in the past three games and a useful nine catches in the past two. A lost fumble put a minor dent in his final total as well.

Despite getting back on track the past two weeks, at least from a fantasy points perspective, there's still cause for concern for Henry managers. And it's in the form of Hill.

The backup RB put together another strong outing, nearly reaching 100 total yards on just 10 touches with a 19-yard TD before halftime. Hill also had a 36-yard long rush in the game that resulted in a fumble just before scoring. All in all, he continues to showcase big-play ability. He entered the game averaging 6.8 yards per carry and had 11.5 per against the Panthers. That's going to keep Hill involved.

Looking ahead, how Baltimore performs down the stretch could play a factor in how this backfield looks for fantasy managers. With six straight losses, sitting at 4-7, the Ravens aren't entirely out of the playoff picture. They still have four division games left (two against the Steelers). But a few more losses could sink Baltimore and that could lead to less work for Henry and more for Hill. We recommended Hill in the early waiver piece going into Week 12.

Henry is still an RB2 for fantasy but there's also the Week 13 bye — not ideal. Hill has shown he can be an RB3/flex with upside. If you have Henry, you need Hill on your bench. If you're looking for a potential league-winner late in the season, Hill has that upside if the chips fall right.

Kamara can't keep getting away with this, can he?

Last week, it was a tilting five touches for 14 total yards and two scores. This week, it's just TWO yards on two touches, but another touchdown. Seven other carries went to various skill position players and QB Tyler Shough. But at least it was a decent showing by Etienne in his second game back after being placed on injured reserve after Week 3.

It wasn't a dominant performance by Etienne but it was good enough for his managers to trust him back in their lineups. Most of his 76 yards came on a 46-yard run, but that's fine; we wanted to see Etienne get back to making big plays, and that was his biggest of the 2026 season. The volume was also a great sign with Audric Estimé a healthy scratch for Week 11; the backfield was all Etienne's.

The larger issue is the Saints offense. It's not performing up to par, like the boat races and shootouts we saw in flashes last season after Shough took over. The O-line isn't great and Shough has regressed a bit. The rest of the fantasy schedule lines up well for points (at CIN, vs. GB, at CAR), which is a plus. But we need to see some progress from Kellen Moore's group overall in order to have faith in Etienne as an RB2.

The back has that potential but he'll need 15-20 touches and a few big plays mixed in. Etienne is going to lose snaps to Kamara and New Orleans likes using the vet in the red zone. This game has us putting Etienne back in the RB2/3 range, while Kamara is only a risky, TD-dependent flex.
