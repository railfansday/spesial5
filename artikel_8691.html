Missing a tax deadline can cost you a penalty, a refund, or a deduction, and the deadlines fall all year, from January’s estimated tax payment to October’s due date for extended returns. Add the dates that fit your income and accounts to your calendar as yearly reminders.

Income with no tax withheld, like freelance pay, can mean four estimated tax payments a year. An IRA or HSA adds an April deadline for last year’s contributions, and an extension moves your return’s due date to October.

Meeting one deadline doesn’t excuse missing another. A freelancer who files April’s return on time but skips the June 15 estimated payment can still owe an underpayment penalty on that payment. Last year’s total tax can tell you how much to pay on each date.

A refund has a deadline too. If you’re owed one and don’t file, the IRS usually gives you three years to claim it, and after that the U.S. Treasury keeps the money.

A date that falls on a weekend or legal holiday moves to the next business day. In 2027, January 31 is a Sunday, so employers and payers have until February 1.

The IRS also postpones deadlines automatically for people in a federally declared disaster area, and in 2020 it moved April 15 to July 15 for everyone because of COVID-19. Whenever a storm, flood, or fire hits your county, check the IRS’s disaster relief page to see whether your dates moved.

Set each date that applies to you to repeat every year in your phone’s calendar, with an alert a week ahead, starting with January 15.
