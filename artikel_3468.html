Snoopy & Woodstock's 10 Greatest Comics In Peanuts History

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Few friendships in comic strip history are as entertaining or heartwarming as the one shared by Snoopy and Woodstock in Peanuts. For nearly 50 years, Charles M. Schulz brought these two beloved characters together for countless hilarious adventures and surprisingly profound moments. From their endearing friendship to their unforgettable comedic antics, Snoopy and Woodstock have created some of the Peanuts' best moments.

Whether they're embarking on another chaotic Beagle Scouts expedition or enjoying one of their iconic, yet unintelligible, conversations atop Snoopy's red doghouse, these two always find a way to make even the simplest moments memorable. With so many comics celebrating their friendship, we're looking back at 10 of Snoopy and Woodstock's greatest strips in Peanuts history.

Snoopy sitting atop his doghouse and typing away on his typewriter as he pursues his dream of becoming a famous novelist is one of the beagle’s most iconic recurring gags, resulting in some of the pup’s most hilarious strips. However, the hilarity is cranked up a notch when Schulz adds Woodstock to the mix.

In this strip, we see Snoopy typing away when Woodstock takes it upon himself to become Snoopy’s editor, suggesting through his nonsensical chirping that Snoopy change his previous prose of “Always remember that beauty is only fur deep” to “feather deep.” Not many people would appreciate unsolicited writing advice, but Snoopy takes it in stride.

Like Snoopy, Woodstock has his own fair share of recurring gags, and among the most beloved is the little bird's love for snowflakes. This recurring gag also often features Snoopy sitting atop his doghouse, silently judging Woodstock's fascination.

The strip above is one of the best examples of this recurring gag, as it shows Woodstock falling in love with two snowflakes, only for Snoopy to give up watching his friend and lie down on his doghouse, wondering, “Can a person really be in love with two different snowflakes at the same time?”

Some of Snoopy and Woodstock’s best moments unsurprisingly take place atop the roof of Snoopy’s doghouse. The comic above, though, is a double whammy, as it also showcases Snoopy and Woodstock sharing food, another recurring activity between the two friends.

Here, Snoopy learns that, unlike himself, Woodstock prefers his ice cream cone with chocolate on the bottom and vanilla on top, the opposite of Snoopy’s own preference. We wholeheartedly agree with Woodstock that this is the correct way to layer ice cream; what about you?

While the two best friends share plenty of peaceful, heartwarming moments, they have also contributed to some of Peanuts' most hilarious comic strips, with many of these funny moments stemming from absurd misunderstandings.

The strip above shows Snoopy discovering that he mistook February 2's Groundhog Day for "Ground Hugs Day." A few panels later, we see Woodstock hugging the ground, making it clear that Snoopy has passed along his hilarious misunderstanding to Woodstock, resulting in the little bird spending the day hugging the ground.

The strip above shows Snoopy napping atop his doghouse when an exhausted Woodstock lands on his mouth and nose. With his breathing now obstructed, Snoopy startles awake as he gasps for air, only for Woodstock to make himself comfortable and fall asleep on Snoopy’s nose. The beagle then thinks, “Must have been a rough flight.”

The fact that Snoopy makes no attempt to remove the little bird from his face is beyond heartwarming and shows just how sweet their relationship is and how deep their bond runs. Woodstock using Snoopy as his personal perch has become one of the strip’s most iconic visuals, and it remains beloved to this day.

Beagle Scout Leader Snoopy is perhaps one of the pup’s most legacy-defining recurring gags, featuring Snoopy leading Woodstock and his fellow bird scouts on camping trips and outdoor adventures. This recurring gag went on to inspire Peanuts book collections and graphic novels, theme park attractions, and even an Apple TV+ animated series titled Camp Snoopy.

That's an incredible legacy for a recurring gag, and this list of the greatest Snoopy and Woodstock comics would be incomplete without mentioning it. The strip above is one of our personal favorite Beagle Scout comics.

The love Snoopy has for Woodstock is perhaps the greatest thing about the duo, and one of the best examples of Snoopy’s ride-or-die energy for his little friend can be found in the strip above. Here, we see Snoopy explaining Thanksgiving to Woodstock, including the tradition of roasting a turkey. However, Woodstock gets scared at the mention of roasting, fearing that he might end up on the menu himself.

Snoopy then reassures his friend with a hug, promising that no one will roast him and that if anyone dares to try, he'll punch them in the nose. Snoopy, of course, acts out his promise, inadvertently punching poor, unlucky Charlie Brown in the nose and adding some hilarity to an otherwise heartwarming moment.

Every Snoopy fan is more than familiar with his Flying Ace persona, one of the beagle’s most iconic recurring alter egos in Peanuts, in which he imagines himself as a daring World War I fighter pilot battling his nemesis, the infamous Red Baron. However, this isn't Snoopy's only sky-riding persona.

Snoopy has also imagined himself as a helicopter, with Woodstock serving as his pilot. The strip above, though, shows Snoopy experiencing some dreaded engine failure. Rather than going down with Snoopy, Woodstock parachutes to safety, leaving the beagle to crash alone and make a hilarious comment about his pilot's lack of loyalty.

Peanuts fans know that while we get to hear Snoopy's dialogue through his thought bubbles, any "dialogue" from Woodstock remains unintelligible, at least to us human readers. Snoopy, on the other hand, understands Woodstock perfectly, leading to some amusing one-sided conversations from the readers' perspective.

In the strip above, we see Snoopy becoming emotional over what Woodstock is telling him, with the beagle commenting, “It's the way he tells it…” as though Woodstock has a profound way with words. The humor comes from the fact that, to the reader, Woodstock's heartfelt speech is nothing more than complete gibberish.

While Snoopy and Woodstock’s interactions are typically defined by hilarity and heartwarming moments, the two also have their fair share of opportunities to impart profound wisdom to readers in an entertaining way. In the strip above, we see Snoopy and Woodstock sitting together, with Snoopy answering the little bird’s off-panel question by saying, “No, our kind doesn't get to vote... We don't have anything to say about what goes on in the world.”

Woodstock then gets up and returns to his egg, humorously implying that he'd rather return to his unborn state than live in a world where he has no say in anything. While the moment is hilarious, it also offers a profound reminder to human readers: we decide what happens in this world, and the consequences of those decisions affect not only us but all other living creatures on the planet. With that great power comes the responsibility to do right by these other creatures.

This strip conveys one of Peanuts' most profound and meaningful messages, delivered through Snoopy and Woodstock.
