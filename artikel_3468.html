Casting Director Francine Maisler on ‘Dune: Part Three’

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

When Denis Villeneuve brought on seasoned casting director Francine Maisler for the first Dune movie, they were starting from scratch. For the ambitious adaptation of Frank Herbert’s 1965 sci-fi novel, they had to cast not only the lead character, Paul Atreides, but dozens of other main characters—powerful warriors, scheming royals, shape-shifting conspirators—to inhabit the world.

Luckily, the pair had already worked together on Villeneuve’s three previous films, Sicario, Blade Runner 2049, and Arrival. “Denis’s vision is my compass,” Maisler tells Vanity Fair. “His passion [for Dune] since he was a teen was all I needed to follow.”

When they were casting the first installment, Maisler was aware that it was possible that these actors might play these characters for more than just one film. “I did have an idea that they would have to have the presence and the acting talent to be able to carry this potentially for three movies,” she says.

Dune (2021), starring Timothée Chalamet as Paul Atreides and Zendaya as Chani, was just the first step. Maisler returned to cast Dune: Part Two (2024), and Dune: Part Three, which will wrap up Villeneuve’s trilogy when it hits theaters on December 18.

Maisler is a titan of casting, having worked on hundreds of films with filmmakers such as Luca Guadagnino, Greta Gerwig, Aaron Sorkin, Paul Greengrass, Adam McKay, and Terrence Malick, and she was nominated for the first casting Oscar in 2026 for her work on Ryan Coogler’s Sinners.

The casting Oscar didn’t exist when she began working on the Dune franchise, but this installment is likely to draw attention from the Academy. Over the three films, the cast has grown into an incredible ensemble, packed with many of today’s biggest movie stars, along with well-known veterans and a few unexpected choices. “One thing about Denis is if ever I bring him a name and he likes it, and I’ll say, ‘Well, do you want to hire that actor?’ He says to me something I think is so poetic,” says Maisler. “He says, ‘Francine, let me get back to you tomorrow. I need to dream about it.’”

Ahead of the release of Dune: Part Three, Maisler looks back at casting the trilogy’s most important roles, including the exciting new additions to the upcoming film—and the possibility of more Dune in the future.

In Herbert’s book, Atreides is a slender 15-year-old with dark hair. “To find a 15-year-old who had that talent to carry this whole movie, we weren’t sure was out there,” remembers Maisler. The name that came up was then 22-year-old Chalamet, whom Maisler had previously cast in Hostiles, Beautiful Boy, The King, and Little Women. Chalamet, who didn’t have to audition but did meet with Villeneuve, would have to play Atreides from a young man who then begins to amass power in the second film and, after a 12-year time jump, is burdened with the choices he’s made waging a galactic war in the third. Though Chalamet hadn’t led a big blockbuster before, Maisler saw the depth of his talent. “I do think Beautiful Boy showed me the depth of Timothée as an actor, as a young man, and that this was the next step in his progression as an actor, that he was able to hold a franchise in his hand,” says Maisler.

There was a worldwide search for Chani, the skilled Fremen warrior from Arrakis who falls in love with Atreides. At the time, Zendaya was in the middle of filming the first season of Euphoria, and her graduation from Disney star to movie star had not yet happened. Zendaya screen tested with Chalamet, which sealed it for Maisler and Villeneuve. “She came in with five other incredibly talented actresses, but the minute you saw her with Timothée in a room, you just knew that there’s something special happening,” she says. “She has such power when she walks in the room, and she’s breathtaking in the most interesting way.”

Of course, Paul Atreides’s parents, Duke Leto Atreides and Lady Jessica, needed to look like Chalamet, but Maisler started with the characters’ essences when casting. Duke Leto Atreides was considered a fair and devoted leader as the noble head of House Atreides. Maisler saw that gravitas in Oscar Isaac. Lady Jessica becomes a force in the story because of her heightened physical and mental control as a trained member of the Bene Gesserit sisterhood. “Rebecca is so powerful, isn’t she?” says Maisler. “She really, I have to say, surprised me. I always knew how talented she was, but she just owned that part.”

Villeneuve and Maisler had worked with Brolin on the 2015 crime thriller Sicario. Maisler, who calls Brolin “one of our greatest actors,” was excited to have him back, this time playing Gurney Halleck, the Warmaster of House Atreides and a mentor to Paul Atreides. The character is described in the book as a warrior-poet, requiring strength and depth. “He’s so charismatic, so masculine,” says Maisler of Brolin. “He’s kind of a throwback from other generations.”

When Momoa’s agent brought up Momoa’s name, Maisler wasn’t quite sure about the idea because at the time he was known for starring in the DC movie franchise as Aquaman. “I was a bit skeptical, I have to admit, because I knew he had this incredible presence on screen, but I didn’t know, truly, his talent, and that’s taught me a lot about being open to people who come from different types of films,” she says. Villeneuve was more open to the idea at the start, and met with him about playing Duncan Idaho, a fierce fighter and protector of Paul Atreides. He returns in the third movie as a ghola—a sort of clone—named Hayt, which demands a more complicated performance from Momoa. “Jason’s an incredible artist, and I’ve come to just love him,” says Maisler. “He’s so funny. I want to put him in a romantic comedy next, because he really is one of the big surprises in the ensemble.”

Taylor-Joy had already risen in stardom by the time she joined the franchise for the second film. Her breakout was in The Witch, and she starred in hit series The Queen's Gambit, which earned her a Golden Globe and a SAG Award. This made it slightly surprising that Alia Atreides, Paul’s volatile sister, makes a blink-and-you’ll-miss-it appearance in the second film. “I give her a lot of credit for not counting lines in the second one,” says Maisler, who says Taylor-Joy signed on for the second because of the potential of the third installment. Indeed, in Dune: Part Three, Alia is a loyal sister and vengeful force who is not afraid to get blood on her hands. Taylor-Joy’s charisma and intensity will jump off the screen, says Maisler. “Actors are very smart, and they know when to trust a director like Denis,” she says. “When he says there is going to be something in the third film and he describes it, he will make it happen.”

A tribal leader of the desert-dwelling Fremen, Stilgar is a seasoned warrior and becomes a devout follower of Paul Atreides as Muad’Dib. The role required a level of intensity. “Oh, I think we all just prayed that Javier would do it,” says Maisler. “I think every actor wants to work with Javier, and he’s one of probably a handful of actors I think can do anything.”

Pugh joined for the second film as well, but like Taylor-Joy, she didn’t have a ton to do. But Princess Irulan, who marries Paul Atreides as a political union, is a key part of the plot in Dune: Part Three, joining a conspiracy against Paul. Maisler first saw Pugh in the 2016 film Lady Macbeth, and then cast her as Amy March in Gerwig’s Little Women. “You saw on that what an intelligent actress she is, and singular,” she says. “She knows no boundaries, and she’s confident and bold.” She says Pugh has always reminded her of Debra Winger, the star of An Officer and a Gentleman (1982), and Terms of Endearment (1983). “She’s just commanding, and she has this energy, and everything she does is fascinating,” says Maisler. “You can’t take your eyes off of her.”

The third installment brings in yet another major star with Pattinson, who plays Scytale, a shape-shifter who is conspiring to dethrone Paul Atreides. Maisler, who also worked with Pattinson in Primetime, which also came out this year, describes him as “one of the versatile actors in his choices.” She had also worked with Pattinson on Mickey 17, which proved that he could play many different versions of a character—a skill that would be required of him in this third Dune movie. “Robert's a tremendous actor, and I think he takes these really bold chances,” says Maisler. As for his recent announcement as the next James Bond, Maisler has faith he and Villeneuve will deliver. “I think the way Denis and Rob created the character in Dune: Part Three together speaks to the surprises that we will see in Bond,” she says.

For Dune: Part Three, Maisler and Villeneuve had to find two actors who looked like twins and also looked like the offspring of Chalamet and Zendaya. They initially hoped to cast real twins, but didn’t find the right pair. In the end, they found their Ghanima and Leto II in Ida Brooke and Nakoa-Wolf Momoa. Brooke, who looks strikingly like Zendaya, is a relative newcomer who previously appeared on Apple’s sci-fi drama Silo (which stars Rebecca Ferguson). And Nakoa-Wolf Momoa, who makes his feature acting debut in Dune: Part Three, is Jason Momoa’s son—though Maisler didn’t know that when she first watched his audition tape. “Then came this tape of this young man who had incredible presence, and—we didn’t ask for this—he added some combat footage to his tape,” she says. Maisler says sometimes actors will call in a favor to get their child an audition, but Wolf, as she calls him, got this on his own. This casting was the most important for the third film because of what might be to come. “It was almost like casting the first one where these two young actors could go on to potentially, if there’s another movie where they’re leads,” says Maisler. There have been no official announcements of any future Dune movies, but for those mourning the end of the Villeneuve trilogy, Maisler is planting a small seed of hope here. The third Dune book, Children of Dune, centers on Leto and Ghanima, which means the two young actors Maisler found may have more story ahead of them.
