What Young Adults Want Parents to Know About Them

Posted November 13, 2026 | Reviewed by Monica Vilhauer Ph.D.

I am a psychologist who helps to run an Intensive Outpatient Program (IOP) for young adults ages 18-26. These young people are facing mental health challenges that temporarily prevent them from enjoying their lives and relationships to the fullest extent they might. They also may be struggling with independence, as well as career or college progression. Here are some of the themes that I have heard consistently over the past few years. I hope it helps you to connect with and support your young adult children.

I do care about my future. If I am taking a leave of absence from college or am struggling with keeping or finding a job, it is not because I do not care. I care very much, actually. However, I am struggling with anxiety, depression, or other mental health concerns that need to be addressed before I can resume my journey.

I may not always like you, but I do love you. We have spent a lot of time together over the years, and it has not always been easy. In fact, we may disagree about a lot of things. However, that does not mean that I do not love you. Yes, I can not like you at times and still love you. Please remember that.

I want you to listen and see me for who I am. My personality and values are very important to me. I may not have everything figured out yet, and I am still learning about myself and the world, but I ask that you respect me for who I am today. Ask questions and be curious about what is important to me. If you ask in an open, loving way, I am happy to let you into my world.

I am frightened of the future, so please do not minimize the state of the world I am inheriting. Let’s be honest, the older generations have not left us with a world that is thriving and well. Selfish politicians, wars, climate change — all of this is stressful to me. So, yes, I have lost faith in “the system.” And can you blame me? Please be sympathetic when I am discouraged about the state of the world.

The world is different now than it was when you were my age (and not in a good way). It is a difficult time to be a young adult. With the introduction of AI and the comparisons on social media, life is particularly stressful for me. Yes, you had your own stressors, but please know that what we are dealing with now is new territory. No one knows where it will lead, which is stressful and scary for me.

Online friends are real friends too. I know you may not understand because the world is different today, but connecting with others online can be meaningful too. Especially while living through the pandemic, we had to get creative in how we meet and interact with others. Sometimes online connection feels safer and easier, actually.

Please, never give up on me. I know I am going through a difficult time. I may be struggling with my next steps academically, professionally, or personally. That does not mean that I have failed or that I will struggle my whole life. I just need some help and support now. I am excited for what is next but may need to heal and regroup first. Please stay in my corner as I prepare for what’s next.
