Scheduling Your Day Like This May Reduce Physical Pain and Lessen Depression

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Here’s why your body and brain love this.

It can be hard to think of anything else when you’re dealing with physical pain and depression. Though treatments for each are very different, new research suggests that a simple lifestyle trick might help ease symptoms of each: Keeping a consistent schedule.

A recent study published in the Journal of Behavioral Medicine makes a solid case for having a routine and dealing with less physical and mental pain. Researchers specifically found that older adults who follow a regular daily schedule tend to have less physical pain and fewer symptoms of depression, even when they have insomnia.

The findings shouldn’t be interpreted to mean putting yourself on a schedule will magically make your pain and depression disappear. Instead, it suggests that having a daily routine could be a complement to the treatments you’re already doing, like physical therapy, talk therapy, and taking an antidepressant. Here’s what doctors recommend keeping in mind—and why they’re on board with the idea of creating and sticking with a regular schedule.

Meet the experts: Eunjin L. Tracy, PhD, lead study author and director of the Sleep, Health, and Relationships Lab at the University of Missouri; Christopher Winter, MD, a sleep medicine specialist, neurologist, and the author of The Sleep Solution: Why Your Sleep Is Broken and How to Fix It; and Beth Malow, MD, a neurologist and chief of the Division of Sleep Disorders at Vanderbilt Health.

For the study, researchers surveyed 67 older adults—37 of those had insomnia and 30 said they slept well. People who said they experienced insomnia were also likely to report that they had more physical pain and higher levels of depression than those who didn’t have insomnia.

But when the researchers dug deeper, they also discovered that participants who stuck with consistent daily schedules reported less pain and fewer symptoms of depression, whether they struggled with insomnia or not.

“The results suggest that greater behavioral-social rhythm regularity is associated with more favorable health outcomes,” the researchers wrote in the study.

The study simply found an association, so it didn’t prove that sticking with a consistent schedule leads to less pain and depression. But experts say there’s likely something here.

“Regular daily routines help keep the body's internal clock synchronized, and that clock influences many systems tied to mood, pain sensitivity, and metabolism,” says Eunjin L. Tracy, PhD, lead study author and director of the Sleep, Health, and Relationships Lab at the University of Missouri.

A consistent schedule can also help support your nervous system, according to Beth Malow, MD, a neurologist and chief of the Division of Sleep Disorders at Vanderbilt Health. “There are many chemicals that are released into our nervous systems during the day and night,” she says. “When we have a consistent schedule, our brains and bodies are more responsive to these chemicals—think of it similarly to taking a prescription medication at the same time every day.” That can lead to less physical pain and a better mood, Dr. Malow says.

An inconsistent schedule can also interfere with activities that tend to combat depression and pain, like exercise, says Christopher Winter, MD, a sleep medicine specialist, neurologist, and the author of The Sleep Solution: Why Your Sleep Is Broken and How to Fix It.

“It's also possible that other factors, such as personality traits like conscientiousness, contribute both to more regular routines and to better health,” Dr. Tracy says.

Ultimately, doctors say more research is needed. “This is a [well] known relationship, although the underlying mechanisms are not fully understood,” Dr. Winter says.

A schedule only works if it’s one you’ll actually follow. Dr. Winter recommends starting with a consistent wake time. “This tends to lend itself to a routine at least with breakfast and coffee, and helps with bedtime,” he says. Try to be consistent with that bedtime, too, adds Dr. Malow. “Even meals can affect our schedule,” she says. “Try to eat at similar times of the day to promote consistency.”

Dr. Winter calls exercise “a daily requirement,” adding that it should be outside if possible to get exposure to sunlight. And, if you can schedule this at a consistent time in your day, even better.

“You don't need to change everything at once,” Dr. Tracy says. “Start with one anchor, keep it consistent, and build from there.”
