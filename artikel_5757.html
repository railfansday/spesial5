'They Don’t Know the Questions to Ask': What We Heard This Week

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

— Quotable quotes heard by MedPage Today's reporters

"They have the best of intentions and want to help, but they don't know the questions to ask." -- Anthea Gray, PsyD, of the Ohio State University Comprehensive Cancer Center in Columbus, discussing the first guidelines for cancer caregivers.

"We're heartened to see a number of people elected who are likely to be public health champions." -- Bruce Mirken, communications co-chair at Defend Public Health, on the 2026 election results.

"[The] degree of success is remarkable given the extent of dose reduction, and it is exciting to see such superb outcomes." -- David Sher, MD, of UT Southwestern Medical Center in Dallas, commenting on long-term trial results for reduced-dose radiation in human papillomavirus-associated oropharyngeal cancer.

"I worry that healthcare increasingly relies on tools like questionnaires or brief telehealth assessments that make context difficult to capture." -- Devina Wadhwa, MD, of Northern Ontario School of Medicine University in Canada, on the need for clinical judgment when distinguishing between normal distress and psychiatric conditions.

"Fluent, confident wording can coexist with omissions, outdated information, and errors." -- Nicole Fischer, MD, MPH, of the University of Pittsburgh, evaluating ChatGPT's answers to patient queries about assisted reproduction technology.

"If not done well, [it] can really change the nature of someone's face." -- Danielle Eytan, MD, of NYU Langone Health in New York City, discussing blepharoplasty.

"This study shows the real-world consequences of vitamin K refusal." -- Elizabeth Wolf, MD, MPH, of Virginia Commonwealth University in Richmond, highlighting the risk of brain hemorrhages in infants diagnosed with vitamin K deficiency.

"Reducing exposure to sources such as contaminated water would be a very reasonable step." -- Alexandra White, PhD, of the National Institute of Environmental Health Sciences, on ways to reduce exposure to "forever chemicals" that were linked to aggressive breast cancers.
