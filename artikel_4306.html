GTA 6 Breaks a 25-Year Tradition, and Rockstar Explains Why

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

You can deal drugs, pay for private dances at strip clubs, and even mow down pedestrians in GTA 6. One old activity, though, isn't making the cut. Players can no longer hire sex workers, ending a feature that's been part of the series since Grand Theft Auto 3 arrived in 2001.

We now know plenty about Grand Theft Auto 6, including the type of relationship Jason and Lucia have.

This wasn't simply an overlooked feature. Rockstar deliberately left it out. Shacknews asked the GTA studio about it, and they confirmed the feature was removed. Rockstar also offered an explanation, and it comes down to the kind of story it wants to tell with Jason and Lucia.

In its response to Shacknews, Rockstar described GTA 6 as a story about a couple on the run. The studio said it had considered which familiar GTA elements would work alongside the characters and the relationship at the center of the game, rather than bringing back every previous feature by default.

Rockstar pointed to systems such as police pursuits, radio stations, robberies, and car theft as examples of the elements it carried forward. Those all fit the criminal adventures Jason and Lucia get caught up in. Hiring sex workers, apparently, didn't fit what the team had in mind.

It's an interesting explanation because Rockstar didn't claim the removal was due to a technical limitation or a ratings requirement. Rockstar framed the removal as a creative decision tied to Jason and Lucia's story.

Anyone who's watched the GTA 6 launch trailer will have seen how much of the marketing revolves around the pair. Rockstar keeps bringing the focus back to Jason and Lucia, even when the trailers show off everything else happening in Leonida. Their story is the reason the two playable protagonists are together in the first place.

Hiring sex workers first became a recognizable open-world interaction in GTA 3, and it remained available across later mainline games, including Vice City, San Andreas, GTA 4, and GTA 5. For some players, it became one of those strange little things they expected to find when wandering around a GTA city at night.

It also brings back one of GTA 3's more infamous controversies. Players could hire a sex worker, kill her afterward, and pick up the money they'd just spent. That drew plenty of criticism at the time, and the mechanic stuck around. It was still possible in GTA 5.

I can't help connecting this to Jason Schreier's 2022 Bloomberg report, which said Rockstar was trying to avoid jokes that punch down at marginalized groups. He suggested GTA 6 might feel quite different from GTA 5, and seeing this particular feature disappear makes me think he might have been onto something. Rockstar says it's about Jason and Lucia, of course, but I wonder if both things played a part.

It was never the reason most people played these games, of course. But GTA has always made a point of filling its streets with activities that aren't strictly necessary for completing missions. The series' reputation comes as much from those distractions as it does from car chases, shootouts, and elaborate heists.

That's why removing a relatively minor interaction is still newsworthy. It has survived several generations of GTA, across different protagonists and settings. Now that Rockstar is putting two characters in a relationship at the heart of its newest game, that particular carryover has finally been dropped.

This doesn't mean GTA 6 has suddenly become a tame version of Grand Theft Auto. The game's nightlife is very much present. Our guide to GTA 6's strip clubs already covers several venues around Leonida, so the absence of one activity shouldn't be confused with the removal of adult entertainment altogether.

There's a distinction between the atmosphere of Vice City and the specific actions Rockstar lets the protagonists take. A setting can still have strip clubs and the more outrageous side of GTA without including every interaction that existed in previous games.

The confirmation is also about a specific action players can take. It doesn't establish whether sex workers appear in the game as background characters, or whether the story mentions them. Rockstar answered a question about the gameplay interaction, not every possible appearance of the subject.

Some fans will probably find the change strange, particularly if they've been playing since the PS2 days. Others might not have noticed the feature was missing at all. Either way, it's one of the clearer examples of Rockstar choosing to leave an old mechanic behind instead of carrying it over simply because it's always been there.

For a series known for giving players countless ways to get distracted, that's a notable decision. And this time, at least, Rockstar has given a pretty straightforward reason for making it.

Grand Theft Auto 6 delivers an enormous open world, a gripping story, a vibrant Vice City and an astonishing range of activities.
