Testing gaming headsets really isn't too difficult. You make sure it's compatible with what it says on the box, and then tell the people if it’s comfortable and if it sounds good. Shit simple until you do it enough to realize a simple truth. Most models from reputable brands are worth their costs, and very few companies have duds in their lineups. That said, there are ways to tell what's going to rise from the crop. Here's some pointers for the next time you shop for headsets.

Comfort — If you can't stay comfortable in them for more than an hour they are going straight in the trash. Even the most uncomfortable among these picks are still in the top 5% of comfortable gaming headphones.

Sound Quality — Like with the pair of headphones you use to listen to music, you want clear sound with plenty of range.

Microphone — If you're gaming online, your microphone needs to cut out unwanted background noise. Some offer noise cancelling, some just have good unidirectional builds. All on this list are great in this department.

Directional Sound — Spatial audio is cool when watching movies, but when playing games this can take your team play to the next level. When you hear an enemy's footsteps, you can know they're behind you at four o'clock, not six. And that makes you an asset. It also makes the taste of victory that much sweeter.

Connection — I will discuss wireless vs. wired shortly, but when it comes to the going wireless there's usually two ways to connect a gaming headset. Bluetooth or via a USB dongle. The latter typically has less latency and is the preferred way to connect.

Are wired or wireless headsets better for gaming?

If you care about latency (aka lag), we are going to recommend wired over wireless. The added hassle of wireless charging and connecting doesn't necessarily make up for the hassle of having a wire. Plus, you don't have to worry about it not working on some devices—which you do with pretty much every wireless device you buy.

Do all gaming headsets work across all platforms?

I'm glad you asked. No! As you may have noticed, there tend to be two kinds of headsets. Those that work with Xbox.. and those that work with everything else. Since Xbox uses its own wireless standard instead of Bluetooth. In practice, this means Xbox gamers need to be extra careful when buying headsets to make sure the one they're getting is compatible. SteelSeries, for example, sells two versions of each headset, one marked for Xbox.

It's actually more complicated than "all headsets that work with PC also work with PlayStation and you have to get ones especially for Xbox," but in many of these cases that is the gist. Although the PlayStation headsets work on PC and Mac but not Nintendo Switch. So, yeah, it's pretty inconsistent. Seriously, just read the list of compatible platforms before you buy a headset.
