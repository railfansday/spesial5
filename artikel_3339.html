Hexed Review: Walton Goggins & Hailee Steinfeld's Magic Makes Up For Disney's Forgettable New Villain

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Disney's Hexed conjures an entertaining, magical adventure with a heartwarming mother-daughter story to overcome its lackluster villain plot.

After nearly a century, Disney is still finding new ways to both repeat and subvert its classic storytelling formulas for princess movies. Though Hexed often falls into familiar traps with the former, the animated film mostly succeeds with the latter, telling a simultaneously heartbreaking and heartwarming mother-daughter story.

Among the greatest strengths of Hexed is its all-star voice cast, bolstered by Hailee Steinfeld as impulsive teen Billie, who finds out she’s a witch and is transported to the magical land of Hexe. Billie embarks on a dangerous adventure alongside her cautious mother and mother’s former best friend as they uncover dark family secrets, offering a fast-paced, humorous, twist-filled take on the classic “Chosen One” story that will keep kids entertained throughout.

After the opening act establishes Billie’s rebellious streak and strain with her mother Alice (Rashida Jones) in Ohio, there’s a lot for the younger crowd to enjoy once the duo enters the portal to Hexe. As Walt Disney Animation’s other flagship movies have excelled at in recent years, Hexed thrives in its visually-striking animation and fantastical worldbuilding.

As Billie, Alice, and Alice’s former animal companion, Beef Roger Crummchuk (Walton Goggins), journey across the realm and evade the villainous Queen Celeste (Jodie Foster) in hopes of fulfilling an ancient prophecy, Hexed travels through what essentially becomes Disney’s revamped take on Wonderland, or an animated version of Oz. The Wizard of Oz and Wicked comparisons are difficult to ignore as Hexed explores its magically suppressed and segregated world, with the film even acknowledging the similarities in a quick “Yellow Brick Road” reference.

From the swamps and waterside sanctuaries to the magical creatures and villages, Hexe becomes a fully-realized, captivating world, following in the footsteps of recent Disney fantasy locations like Encanto’s Casita and Frozen 2’s Enchanted Forest. While still leaving plenty more of Hexe and the larger magical world a mystery for a potential sequel to explore, jumping through so many locations may also be in hopes of expanding with a Disney Parks attraction. Unfortunately, it felt that this focus also came at the cost of some crucial character and story arcs.

The beating heart of Hexed is the arc of the mother-daughter relationship between Billie and Alice, elevated by gripping performances from Hailee Steinfeld and Rashida Jones.

Still, Hexed introduces several lovable new characters to Disney’s growing lineup, supported by engrossing performances by the voice cast. Walton Goggins’ Beef Roger Crummchuk is an absolute standout and the greatest comical presence. A cynical, three-eyed cat with a soft heart, Crummchuk avoids the funny sidekick cliché by serving an integral role in the overarching narrative between Billie and Alice, grounding both by helping them see their own worth and each other’s perspectives.

Of course, the beating heart of Hexed is the arc of the mother-daughter relationship between Billie and Alice, elevated by gripping performances from Hailee Steinfeld and Rashida Jones. At the core of the story is a mother learning that she can protect her daughter while still giving her independence, and a daughter learning to see her mother as her own, layered person, while coming to understand the root of her overwhelming cautiousness. Hexed becomes a coming-of-age story for both Billie and Alice as they contend with the “Chosen Witch” prophecy, giving multiple generations a touching, surprise-filled dynamic.

That said, Hexed struggles to land the punch with the film’s villain, Queen Celeste, despite a compelling vocal performance by Jodie Foster. Her introduction and development are largely glossed over, quickly making her unlikable and only growing to make her more so without much complexity. Though an early twist makes her essential to the emotional crux of the story, Celeste’s weak characterization, plans, and motivations make her one of the more forgettable Disney animated villains.

Unlike the protagonists, Hexed's underdeveloped villains come off as more generalized, poorer versions of familiar characters like the Evil Queen, the Wicked Witch and her Flying Monkeys, and LeFou. The pacing of Hexed is so fast that many of these characters hardly stand out from the various, colorful citizens of Hexe briefly introduced across different locations. Issues with pacing also affect the more emotional moments between Billie, Alice, and Beef, flying through key heart-to-hearts to get back to the action.

While supported by fabulous animation, Hexed also weaves in some underwhelming action scenes that don’t always feel necessary. The problem may stem from its pop soundtrack, with awkward needle drops that weaken the sequences rather than musically and thematically enhancing them. The score itself is quite strong, but the songs feels oddly out of place in the wider context of their scenes, as well as the movie at large. (The highlight of the soundtrack doesn’t arrive until the credits, when Steinfeld herself sings a cover of The Cars song “Magic.”)

Overall, Hexed still finds itself among Disney’s stronger recent animated pursuits outside its Pixar movies. And, while it may have been better suited for a September or October debut to take advantage of the witch-focused content and spooky new creatures, the film offers a touching family story right in time for Thanksgiving. Hopefully, there will be more of Hexed in Disney’s future, with great narrative potential and plenty left to explore around Billie and the world of Hexe.

Hexed releases wide in theaters on Wednesday, November 25.
