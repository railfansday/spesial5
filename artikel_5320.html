North Korea Calls the Binance Hack Claims “Absurd” and Points at the US, as $3.24 Billion in Stolen Crypto Still Waits to Be Laundered

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Bitcoin news: North Korea calls Binance hack claims “absurd” as $3.24B in stolen crypto, mostly BTC and ETH, sits in hacker wallets.

In Bitcoin news on Tuesday, November 3, North Korea rejected US claims that its hackers stole $6.3 billion from Binance, as Kim Yo Jong called the FBI’s attribution “absurd rhetoric” and suggested that Washington itself might be behind the heist.

The statement, carried by the state news agency KCNA, came three days after the FBI named TraderTraitor, a North Korean state hacking unit, as the culprit.

It changes little on the ground. Blockchain firms Chainalysis, TRM Labs and Elliptic had already tied the theft to North Korea, and the money is still moving.

That is why it matters for Bitcoin. Elliptic counts $3.24 billion still sitting in wallets linked to the attackers, mostly in Bitcoin and Ether, and it saw the first stolen BTC go through a mixer on Monday.

BTC slipped 1.8% to $76,729 on the day, back on its 200-day average, and the Crypto Fear & Greed Index dropped from 37 to 26.

So what exactly did North Korea say, and how much of the stolen money could still hit the market?

Half of it is still in the hackers’ hands. Elliptic’s latest count puts the theft at $6.43 billion, slightly above the $6.3 billion most reports cite.

The hackers are moving slower than after Bybit, when they cleared all the stolen ether in ten days. In the first four days here, they used about 12,000 wallets on 11 blockchains, one new wallet every 25 seconds.

Elliptic expects the rest to be turned into Bitcoin over the coming months and cashed out through OTC brokers and exchanges. That is $3.24 billion of potential supply, drip-fed into a market already in fear.

It is sitting right on it. BTC trades at $76,729 on Binance, down 1.8% in 24 hours, after dipping to $76,389 on Tuesday morning. The 200-day EMA (exponential moving average) is at $76,502, and the October 30 post-hack low of $76,326 is just below.

That floor has now held for five days. The bounces haven’t, though: Monday’s push stalled at $78,878, well short of the 50-day EMA at $80,105.
