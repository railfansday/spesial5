Bitcoin ETFs Lose $975M in a Week as Russia Moves to Open Bitcoin to Funds

US spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds (ETFs) lost a net 4,658 BTC, about $296.7 million, in their latest daily update, on-chain tracker Lookonchain reported on Thursday, November 19. Over seven days, the funds shed 15,314 BTC, or $975.5 million. All five of the largest funds posted outflows on both the day and the week.

On the same day, Russia moved the other way. A Bank of Russia official said the central bank plans rule changes that would let investment funds for qualified investors buy Bitcoin directly, with the first products possible in 2027. Retail access to crypto futures is also being weighed, but later.

We compared the week’s ETF selling with the new coins miners produced over the same days. The selling was almost five times larger. So can new buyers like Russia fill a gap this big?

Together, the US funds now hold 1,231,539 BTC, worth about $78.45 billion. The week’s outflows equal about 1.2% of that.

Ethereum ETFs saw the same pattern on a smaller scale. They lost 9,904 ETH, about $17.9 million, on the day. Over the week, outflows reached 65,373 ETH, or $117.8 million. BlackRock’s ETHA accounted for most of it.

Every day, miners create new BTC as a reward for adding blocks to the network. We counted the blocks added in the seven days to Thursday afternoon UTC on mempool.space, a public block explorer: 1,030 blocks at 3.125 BTC each, or about 3,219 new BTC.

This is where Russia’s plan looks small for now. From November 17, ordinary Russian investors can buy BTC through licensed brokers and exchanges, but only up to 300,000 rubles a year per intermediary. That’s about $3,670, or roughly 0.057 BTC at today’s price. It would take about 267,000 Russians buying the full amount to match one week of US ETF selling.

For Bitcoin, Russia is a long-term story. Regulated funds could add steady demand from 2027, but none of it arrives in time to offset this month’s ETF selling.

On the daily chart, Bitcoin trades at $64,037. That is below its 50-day exponential moving average (EMA), an average that gives recent prices more weight, at $75,258, and its 200-day EMA at $75,627.

For Bitcoin, the number to watch is the daily ETF flow. A few days of inflows would ease the pressure on $62,300. Another week like this one, with outflows near five times new supply, would make that support much harder to defend.
