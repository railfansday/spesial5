Beyond Nvidia: Convoy co-founder and former Microsoft leader raise $277M to rethink AI infrastructure

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

by Todd Bishop on Nov 23, 2026 at 4:30 amNovember 23, 2026 at 1:27 pm

Former Microsoft executives Dan Lewis and Kutta Srinivasan have raised $277 million on a bet that the next wave of AI will run on a very different kind of data center.

The outsized seed funding for their new startup, Marlowe, reflects the scale of the challenge they’re taking on. As AI shifts from training models to running them in everyday products, Marlowe designs and operates computing systems for that work. It’s starting with chips from AMD and Etched, expanding beyond the Nvidia GPUs that have fueled the AI boom.

They say tuning the chips, networking and software for specific AI workloads and applications will maximize performance and efficiency, lowering costs as a result.

The new funding values Marlowe at $1.15 billion, according to the company, making it a unicorn out of the gate. Accel led the latest round, joined by a long list of big names in tech and finance, from KKR co-founder Henry Kravis to Uber CEO Dara Khosrowshahi.

Lewis, Marlowe’s CEO, previously co-founded the trucking startup Convoy and later was an enterprise AI leader at Microsoft, where Srinivasan, now Marlowe’s CTO, was a corporate vice president and the company-wide technical lead for AI infrastructure.

“The demand for intelligence and AI is very high,” Lewis said in an interview, when asked how a former freight marketplace CEO ended up in AI computing. “It’s something that should be accessible and affordable for everyone.”

Marlowe, founded in May, is based in both Bellevue, Wash., and Redwood City, Calif., and is approaching 30 employees. Lewis said the two home bases reflect where its customers, partners and talent are concentrated, pointing to Bellevue as an emerging AI hub.

Lewis, a former Amazon general manager, joined Flexport as a technical advisor when the freight company acquired Convoy’s technology after the startup’s abrupt shutdown in 2023. In February 2025, he returned to Microsoft, where he had also worked previously, as a chief product officer focused on enterprise AI, later becoming a corporate vice president.

GeekWire first reported in June that he had left Microsoft to launch a new startup, but he wasn’t ready to share details at the time.

From Microsoft to Marlowe: The founders said the idea for Marlowe grew out of what they saw at Microsoft: demand was surging for inference, running AI models in products and services, but data centers were still being built mainly for training those models in the first place.

“We were always oversold, effectively sold out,” Lewis said.

Since then, demand for inference has grown faster than supply, pushing up costs at the same time that power shortages, permitting and financing are making new data centers harder to build. The company cites a 40% increase in data center construction costs since 2020, which makes every watt more valuable.

The company isn’t using Nvidia chips for now, but it hasn’t ruled them out.

Its first site, built around Etched’s chips, is scheduled to go live in December, the founders said. A second, larger site using AMD’s new Helios systems is on track for the second quarter of 2027.

Both sites are under contract, and a third is under a letter of intent. The company says it has signed three customer agreements but isn’t yet naming the customers or the locations.

Srinivasan said that at the big tech companies where he has worked, chips from vendors other than Nvidia made up a small slice of the fleet. When a new AI model came out, the top engineers focused on getting it running on the Nvidia chips, and the rest got left behind.

“There were literally times when a significant portion of our non-Nvidia fleet was either idle or grossly underused or didn’t support the latest models and workloads,” he said.

That’s the gap Marlowe is trying to close. Its engineers work directly on customers’ workloads to get other chips performing at their full potential, giving those companies a way to reduce their dependence on a single supplier.

Marlowe says gains of 10% to 20% at each layer of the system, from chips and networking to power management and software, add up to two to three times more output per watt and per dollar than conventional GPU clouds, measured in tokens, the chunks of text that AI models process and generate.

Competition and funding: Marlowe isn’t alone in the bet. Gimlet Labs, which raised $300 million at a $3 billion valuation in September, and General Compute, which raised $100 million in October, are also building inference clouds that run on a mix of chips.

CoreWeave, the largest of the so-called neoclouds, rents out computing power built almost entirely on Nvidia chips. Nvidia held about 47 million CoreWeave shares as of Sept. 30, and CoreWeave has taken on about $47 billion in debt to build out its data centers.

Marlowe’s launch also comes during a turbulent stretch for Nvidia. The company’s board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO on Monday, less than two weeks after his resignation.

Much of Marlowe’s funding will go toward chips, networking equipment and the support needed to run the sites, along with hiring, the founders said. By the standards of AI infrastructure, $277 million doesn’t go far.

Rather than chasing giant facilities, Marlowe plans to prove its approach at sites of tens of megawatts and then finance more capacity site by site, Lewis said. He noted that some startups have raised four to five times as much and will deploy a little less computing power on a similar timeline.

The $277 million includes a smaller initial round led by Eclipse, Lewis said. Eclipse founder and CEO Lior Susan is Marlowe’s executive chairman, and Ido Susan, founder and CEO of the data center networking company DriveNets, is on the board. Other backers include Bessemer, Altimeter, Atreides, Journey and Valor.

Angel investors also include Box CEO Aaron Levie and former LinkedIn CEO Ryan Roslansky. Mark Okerstrom, the former Expedia CFO and CEO who later served as Convoy’s president, is also an investor.

Past lives: Srinivasan’s career runs from video games to AI infrastructure. In an earlier stint at Microsoft, he worked on DirectX and games including Halo 3, then spent eight years at Valve, where he led the team that built the first Steam mobile app.

He later served as the lead architect for machine learning training infrastructure at Facebook, and as a technical fellow at LinkedIn before moving to Microsoft’s CoreAI group in 2025.

Marlowe is a very different business from Convoy, the digital freight marketplace Lewis co-founded to match truckers with shippers. Convoy reached a $3.8 billion valuation before shutting down in 2023, amid a freight recession and a pullback in venture funding.

Asked how Marlowe would avoid getting similarly squeezed by forces outside its control in a capital-intensive business, Lewis said the industry is at “the very beginning” of a long-term shift in how people use AI and data.

He also pointed to a mix of long- and short-term contracts and multiple customers, rather than one big customer that could pull out.

Ultimately, Lewis said he believes Marlowe’s edge will come down to having the engineering skills to get the most out of new chips. “You can’t just take a new chip that comes out and plug it in and expect it to produce tokens,” he said. “You have to go do the work.”
