Nutanix CEO announces gradual departure, intent to complete set of storage links

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Rajiv Ramaswami will stay in the chair until new hire lands, then stick around as advisor

Nutanix CEO Rajiv Ramaswami has decided to step down from the job once the company’s board can find his successor.

Ramaswami announced his decision on Tuesday and in conversation with The Register said he doesn’t have another job lined up, isn’t sure what he will do next, fancies sitting on boards or advising startups, but will take a break after Nutanix finds its next leader.

The CEO moved from VMware to Nutanix in 2020 and characterized his time at the company as writing its “second chapter.” The first, he explained, saw Nutanix focus on hyperconverged infrastructure (HCI) and a limited group of workloads, especially desktop virtualization, while struggling to make a profit.

In chapter two, Nutanix redefined HCI as “hybrid cloud infrastructure” by enabling its stack to run on-prem or in public clouds, and by adding support for containers alongside VMs. Ramaswami took credit for stabilizing Nutanix’s finances while leading the expansion of its platform and for keeping net promoter scores high while the company grew.

The CEO added that Nutanix’s decision to end its devotion to virtualized storage and start to support external arrays was also part of chapter two. Ramaswami said he hopes to expand the number of storage vendors Nutanix supports beyond Dell, Everpure, and NetApp, until it works with all major array suppliers.

Chapter 3, he said, will be making Nutanix an AI platform, “by catering to a whole new set of ecosystems.”

Ramaswami told The Register he had been thinking about moving on for some time and raised it with the board in recent weeks. In the letter he wrote to Nutanix staff, the CEO said the company's board has begun the search for a successor and said he will remain at his post until a replacement is found and then “continue to serve as a senior advisor to the company for a period of time.”

News of the CEO’s planned departure came on the same day Nutanix announced its Q1 results, which saw 15 percent revenue growth to $772.4 million. The company added 410 customers in the quarter, the slowest growth for several years. Ramaswami said high hardware costs remain “a headwind” for Nutanix, but said the company continues to win customers seeking an alternative to VMware. He also pointed to the largest new customer win for the quarter coming from a financial services outfit that uses Dell external storage, a significant score because two years ago Nutanix could not have competed for that business.

Nutanix forecast slightly faster growth for the rest of its financial year, suggesting it is not ready to ride AI’s rocket to riches. Investors knocked about ten percent off the company’s share price in after-hours trading, sending it to around $50, roughly double what it was when Ramaswami took over as CEO, but below $79-plus peaks in late 2025. ®
