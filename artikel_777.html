'Triumph over trauma' - new women's boxing promotion begins

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Fight Back Promotions will have its inaugural event on Saturday at London's Ladbroke Hall; Fight Back Promotions is a women's boxing venture that supports a charitable programme which works with women who have been affected by domestic abuse and gender-based violence

A new boxing promotion, created to support women who have experienced domestic abuse and gender-based violence, will have its inaugural event on Saturday at Ladbroke Hall in London.

Fight Back Promotions was founded by Lesley Sackey, a former GB boxer, Shelly Sweeney, a champion and coach herself, and Poppy Mason-Watts, who has a background in social impact story-telling.

Sackey told Sky Sports: "Fight Back Promotions is an all-female boxing platform. It started from a CIC [a Community Interest Company or social enterprise] called Fight Forward.

"Fight Back Promotions was born as an answer and response to giving a space for all women to be able to step into the ring with not only safety but community, encouragement and a way to promote themselves and go on their own journey.

"It's about using boxing as a vehicle to support women who'd experienced domestic abuse."

Twenty-five per cent of the promotion's profits will go back to Fight Forward's charitable work.

"Women's sport is having a moment and there's still so much more to do," Sackey said. "It really seems like there's opportunity and potential to be a global platform.

"Women put on excellent fights, I don't think we need to prove that any more, and they can sell tickets as well.

"A percentage of whatever we make will always go back feeding into the not-for-profit Fight Forward to support other women in tough, challenging spaces."

Sackey formed the concept for Fight Forward, which uses boxing training to support its participants, after leaving an abusive relationship.

"The ring is just a destination, it's really about the journey, it's about the process, it's about the discipline of having to show up for yourself which for so many women in these situations, they've not had the choice or even the opportunity to do something for themselves," she explained.

"It's about discipline, it's about showing up, it's about being connected to your body, it's about being present, resilient. These are all the tools that we teach the women who participate in the sessions. Not all of them are contact. It's not just about contact, it's about community, being around other women who get it.

"Just to put on gloves and see another woman in an empowered position, the ripple effect is huge and it reaches so many women just by seeing that.

"It's triumph over trauma. You don't have to remain in this survivor state."
