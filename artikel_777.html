Tallulah Willis Regrets Sharing "Intimate" Photo of Dad Bruce Willis

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Tallulah Willis detailed her regret over sharing a personal photo of her dad Bruce Willis as he continues to battle a form of dementia: "I can acknowledge that and vow to do better.”

Tallulah Willis is always in her father Bruce Willis’ corner.

One day after the 32-year-old shared a sweet father-daughter photo with The Sixth Sense star, who is currently battling a form of dementia, she explained why she ultimately felt it best to delete the snap.

“I posted a photo of my dad yesterday because we were having a BEAUTIFUL visit,” she wrote on Instagram Nov. 22 alongside a throwback photo with her dad. “I had someone take a super sweet photo of a shared moment between us watching a video of Frankie Valli singing.”

“I was very excited by the sweetness of the moment that I didn't think farther ahead that this was possibly too intimate a photo to show,” she added. “I can acknowledge that and vow to do better.”

In the now-deleted photo shared to her Instagram Nov. 21, Tallulah—who Bruce shares with ex-wife Demi Moore alongside daughters Rumer Willis, 38, and Scout Willis, 35—sat next to the 71-year-old on an outdoor porch swing as they held hands.

While Tallulah wore a red sweater, jeans and sunglasses as she wrapped one arm around the Die Hard star, he wore a gray cardigan and a pale pink shirt.

Though Tallulah decided it best to keep the memory between the pair of them, she acknowledged that she knows how much her updates mean to his fans.

“I know you all love him,” she added. “I love him. My family loves him. And he's still always the absolute hero we know him to be.”

She captioned the post, “I can do better.”

Bruce’s family shared he was diagnosed with FTD—an umbrella term for diseases that affect the frontal and temporal lobes of the brain, which are associated with personality and behavior, per Mayo Clinic—in 2023.

And recently, his wife Emma Heming Willis offered deeper insight into how the Willis family is coping with their new normal.

“It is extremely difficult and painful,” Emma—mom kids Mabel, 14, and Evelyn, 12, with Bruce—told Hello! In an interview published Sept. 7. “It's natural, we all want our loved ones to be healthy, full of life and being the person they always were. But FTD is a devastating illness that steals things from you very slowly. So the reality is that you find yourself constantly in a state of grief.”

But the dark chapter has been made brighter with the Willis women by her side.

“What has been truly beautiful to see throughout this process is how we all pour our hearts into supporting him—how deeply we love him, while completely respecting the way each person chooses to express that love,” she explained. “I think the balance we've found is simply a reflection of who Bruce is at his core; it’s the result of all the love and support he planted over the years coming right back to him now.”
