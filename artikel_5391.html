Another record low for pending home sales, NAR index drops to 63

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

NAR reported the Pending Home Sales Index fell to 63 in October, down 4.7% from September and 16.7% from a year ago. All 4 regions declined, led by the West, while economists see limited near-term rebound with rates elevated.  AI Summary

Pending home sales fell to yet another record low in October, according to data published by the National Association of Realtors (NAR) on Wednesday.

According to the report, NAR’s Pending Home Sales Index fell to a reading of 63 in October, down 4.7% from a month ago and 16.7% a year ago. An index value of 100 is equivalent to the level of contract signings in 2001.

Despite the new record low, NAR’s chief economist is optimistic that things may turn around in November.

“October could mark the low point in contract signings for this housing cycle, as mortgage rates appear to have topped out,” Yun said in a statement. “Two consecutive months of record-low pending contracts point to very sluggish fourth-quarter home sales closings, and annual existing-home sales could be the lowest since the mid-1990s. It is somewhat puzzling that home sales would fall to levels last seen three decades ago, even as the U.S. population has grown by roughly 75 million. Either sizable housing demand is building in the background, or the dream of homeownership is increasingly out of reach for many Americans.”

HousingWire Data, which is more up to date, shows that for the week ending Nov. 13, 2026, 295,621 single family homes were pending in the U.S., down 14.1% from a year ago.

Pending home sales declined in all four major regions on both a year-over-year and month-over-month basis in October, according to NAR data. The West posted both the largest annual and monthly declines, dropping 20.3% from a year ago and 6.9% a month ago to an index value of 45.8. The Northeast (58.5) posted the smallest monthly and annual declines at 0.8% and 11.1%, respectively. Despite falling 18.7% annually and 6.3% from a month ago, pending home sales in the South remained the closest to 2001 levels, with an index reading of 74.5. Meanwhile, pending sales in the Midwest (65.5) fell 2.7% month-over-month and 14.0% from a year ago.

Locally, pending home sells fell annually in all 50 of the largest metro areas analyzed, with the smallest declines occurring inCincinnati, OH-KY-IN (-4.7%), Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos, TX (-9.7%) and Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford, CT (-10.0%) according to data from Realtor.com Economics.

Looking ahead, with the Federal Reserve expected to raise interest rates at their meeting in December, economists believe a near-term rebound in contract signings is unlikely.

“A meaningful rebound looks unlikely any time soon, with further rate hikes by the Federal Reserve likely to keep a floor under rates as it keeps up its fight against inflation,” Sam Williamson, First American’s senior economist, said in a statement. “While those hikes could eventually open the door to lower rates, but it will take convincing evidence that inflation is retreating toward the Fed’s target. In the meantime, life events will keep some households moving, even as high rates discourage move-up and other discretionary moves and give some first-time buyers a little more reason to wait. Higher incomes and slower price growth may restore some buying power, but rates will limit how much of it turns into sales.”
