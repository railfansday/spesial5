3 in 4 Fortune 500 Companies Fail Google's User Experience Benchmarks - Digital Silk

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

NEW YORK, Nov. 19, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Nearly three in four Fortune 500 companies have websites that fall below Google's Core Web Vitals benchmarks, according to a new analysis by Digital Silk. The agency tested the homepage of every company on the Fortune 500 list in Google PageSpeed Insights. Of the 496 sites that returned results, 369 failed the Core Web Vitals assessment on mobile, desktop or both.

Core Web Vitals are three metrics Google uses to measure real-world loading speed, responsiveness and visual stability. A site passes only when all three meet Google's targets, and Google recommends good Core Web Vitals for success in Search.

On mobile, 65% of sites failed the assessment, compared with 51% on desktop. Among the 496 companies with results on both devices, the outcomes split four ways.

Failing on mobile only was nearly three times as common as failing on desktop only. On a phone, the median homepage took about half a second longer to show its main content, and its response time almost doubled, from 74 to 150 milliseconds.

Because Google uses the mobile version of a site for indexing, a homepage that performs well only on desktop is ranked on its weaker version.

Every failed assessment traces back to at least one of three metrics. Here is how often Fortune 500 sites missed each of Google's targets.

Loading speed is also where the typical homepage has the least room to spare. The median mobile LCP was 2.4 seconds, just 0.1 seconds under Google's limit, so one larger image or extra script can push a passing site over. Another 13% of sites already take longer than 4 seconds on mobile, which Google rates as poor.

Many failing sites are also close to passing. Of the 324 sites that failed on mobile, 111 missed only the loading target. Fixing that one metric would lift the share of sites passing on mobile from 35% to 56%.

Layout shift was the only metric that failed more often on desktop, which usually happens when banners, ads or images load without reserved space.

Among the 10 largest companies on the list, only Amazon, Apple and ExxonMobil passed on both mobile and desktop. Walmart, UnitedHealth Group, Alphabet, CVS Health and Cencora failed on both, Berkshire Hathaway failed on mobile and McKesson failed on desktop.

Performance, Accessibility and SEO Scores

PageSpeed Insights also runs a Google Lighthouse lab test that scores each page from 0 to 100. Mobile performance was the weakest category, with a median score of 53. Only 9% of homepages reached 90 or higher, and 45% scored below 50.

Other categories scored higher. On mobile, 32% of sites scored below 90 for accessibility, and 44% scored below 90 for both SEO and best practices.

"These are some of the best-funded companies in the world, and most of their homepages still miss Google's benchmarks on a phone," said Gabriel Shaoolian, Founder and CEO of Digital Silk.

"Most of these sites are one fix away from passing, and that fix is usually loading speed," Shaoolian added.
