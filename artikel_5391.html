Apollo's Europe chief says continent needs an estimated €1.4 trillion a year in investment

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Apollo's Diego De Giorgi links the increase from €800 billion in the Draghi report to spending on defense, power and AI

Diego De Giorgi, head of Europe at Apollo Global Management, said the continent now requires roughly €1.4 trillion ($1.5 trillion) in investment each year, compared with the €800 billion figure former Italian Prime Minister Mario Draghi put forward in his report, according to Bloomberg.

De Giorgi spoke at Bloomberg's Global Credit Forum in London. He linked the higher estimate to Europe's need to catch up on defense, power and AI spending, and said little headway has been made in making the region's capital markets deeper or more integrated, according to Bloomberg. He became Apollo's head of Europe earlier this year and previously served as chief financial officer of Standard Chartered.

"Finding ways of financing, not just the big corporates of Europe, but the supply chains that enable those big corporates to deliver to us more tech sovereignty, more defense capabilities, more next gen manufacturing ability is one of the most important challenges for Europe," De Giorgi told Bloomberg. He said Apollo is "very, very focused on defense."

Apollo has become one of the largest financiers of the global AI build-out, through shorter-term, investment-grade credit, according to Bloomberg. De Giorgi said Apollo generated over $300 billion in new assets last year, with as much as 85% in credit and roughly 80% rated investment grade.

He said higher-for-longer interest rates, together with what he called the most massive capital-spending wave ever seen in AI, are likely to create more openings next year.

A tech sell-off in October and the exit of Nvidia $NVDA boss Jensen Huang have prompted doubts over whether the AI boom can last, according to Bloomberg. De Giorgi said the turmoil reinforces the "need to put layers of safety" in investments, and that the healthy cash generation of the biggest tech firms provides some reassurance.

Apollo's AI financing includes a proposed deal in which it is expected to lead a $40 billion financing for SpaceX's purchase of Nvidia AI chips, which was in preliminary talks as of early October. Apollo has also struck a $7 billion financing arrangement with Elon Musk's xAI for Nvidia chips earmarked for the Colossus 2 data center in Memphis.

Earlier this month, Apollo rolled out a European private-credit platform worth €10 billion, named European Credit Co., whose backers include the Abu Dhabi Investment Council, according to Bloomberg. The platform is launching with funds drawn from Apollo vehicles as well as other contributors, including Athora, Norinchukin Bank, Standard Chartered and Aranda Principal Strategies.
