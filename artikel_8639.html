Federal Home Energy Tax Credits Ended – Check State & Utility Rebates Before You Sign

The federal tax credits that covered up to 30% of the cost of insulation, heat pumps, windows, and home solar ended for work installed after 2025. Before you sign a contract, check what your utility and your state will pay, since many rebates pay only if you apply first or use an approved contractor.

The IRS ties the credits to the year the work was installed, not the year you paid, so a heat pump paid for in December 2025 and installed in January earns no credit. Work installed by the end of 2025 still counts, and if your 2025 return left it out, you can claim it on an amended return with Form 5695 within three years of when you filed.

Many utilities still pay rebates on heat pumps and insulation, and some states offer their own tax credits. Some state energy offices also run one or both federally funded Home Energy Rebates programs. One pays up to $4,000 toward a whole-home upgrade at any income, and more to lower-income households. The other pays up to $14,000 for electric upgrades like heat pumps to households earning up to 150% of their area’s median income, though no longer to replace gas or oil equipment.

The federal credits came at tax time, after the job. A rebate program may need to approve your project and your contractor before the work begins, and it pays only for equipment that meets its efficiency rules.

Search your ZIP code on DSIRE, NC State University’s database of state and utility incentives, and on ENERGY STAR’s Rebate Finder. Then check your utility’s rebates page, and ask your state energy office whether its programs have opened. A home energy audit from your utility can also point you to rebates.

Redo the payback, the years an upgrade’s savings take to cover its cost, counting only rebates you’ve confirmed. Say your utility’s rebate is the same as in 2025, a heat pump would have earned the full $2,000 credit, and it cuts your bills by $500 a year. It now costs you $2,000 more than it would have in 2025, which adds four years to its payback. That extra cost can put the cheap upgrades that pay back fastest ahead of big projects.

Before you sign, ask the contractor which of these programs it’s enrolled in, and get any approval a rebate requires in writing.
