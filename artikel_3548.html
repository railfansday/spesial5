'Calvin and Hobbes' Best Bully Quote Is Still Among the Wisest in Comic Strip History

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

In the comic strip Calvin and Hobbes, Calvin often defines his babysitter Rosalyn as his arch-nemesis. He regularly plots against her and tries to sabotage her relationship with her boyfriend. He even locked her out of the house once while she was babysitting him, which got him into big trouble with his parents.

But really, Calvin’s rivalry with Rosalyn is kind of fun for him. She’s a trusted, responsible person who he likes to antagonize, but Calvin knows that he’s still safe around her and that she’s not actually against him. The best proof of this isthe story in which Calvin and Rosalyn play Calvinball together, and they actually get along.

Calvin’s real enemy in Calvin and Hobbes is Moe, the school bully, a violent moron who often beats up little Calvin.

Moe is a classmate of Calvin’s in the first grade. He’s much bigger than Calvin and looks like he’s older, but he’s also a 6-year-old. The proof of this came in the very first comic strip featuring Moe, when he walks up to Calvin and says, “Okay twinky, let’s have that ball.” Calvin gives the ball up without a fight, then he looks at us and says, “Never argue with a six-year-old who shaves.”

Usually, comic strips featuring Moe show him picking on Calvin without any provocation. Calvin will be minding his own business in the hallway or on the playground when Moe comes along out of nowhere​​​​​​​ and threatens Calvin or takes something away from him, like in this comic strip where he takes a swing from Calvin:

Sometimes Calvin slinks away without a fight, bowing to Moe’s superior size, but many times Calvin can’t help but respond with some witty retort. That’s usually when Moe responds by pounding Calvin, regardless of whether or not he understood what Calvin said.

There are other times, however, when Calvin seems to go out of his way to provoke Moe.​​​​​​​ I’m not saying Calvin deserves to get hit, but there are definitely times where Calvin’s beatings are entirely avoidable.

In addition to being mean, Moe is also very dumb, which Calvin often makes fun of with his snappy comebacks. This comic strip is probably the best example of that, when Calvin comes up to Moe and says, “Moe, I was wondering something. Are your maladjusted antisocial tendencies the product of your berserk pituitary gland?” The two then sit in silence for a panel, and in the final panel​​​​​​​, a befuddled Moe simply says, “What?” Calvin then breaks the fourth wall and says to us, “Isn’t he great, folks? Let’s give him a big hand!” While we don’t see it, I assume this resulted in a pounding for Calvin.

Unlike Rosalyn, who is eventually shown to be a multi-faceted character, Moe is never depicted as anything more than Calvin’s big, dumb bully. However, there are a few times when his threats to Calvin are more creative​​​​​​​ than a straightforward pounding — sometimes they’re even borderline clever.

For example, this comic strip begins with Moe telling Calvin, “It’s gonna cost you 50 cents to be my friend today.” Calvin snaps back, “And what if I don’t want to be your friend today?” and Moe says, “Then the janitor scrapes you off the wall with a spatula.”

Of course, Moe is still being a big dumb jerk here, but I think there’s a hint of intelligence​​​​​​​ behind this threat. I’d even argue that it seems slightly out of character for Moe, as though Calvin and Hobbes creator Bill Watterson had a really good line he wanted to use, so he decided to stretch Moe’s character a little for the sake of the joke (which was totally worth it).

Watterson did this at least one other time with Moe, when he gave him a threat​​​​​​​ that seems somewhat beyond Moe’s brainpower, but it’s entirely forgivable given how funny it is. In this comic strip, which features Moe’s funniest, best-ever line from Calvin and Hobbes, Moe walks up to Calvin and says, “Hey​​​​​​​ Twinky, gimme a quarter.” Calvin snaps back, “What?! Why should I give you my money?!” And Moe gives this genuinely clever, hilarious reply:

“It’s for the ‘Let Calvin Live Through Recess Fund.’”

Man, that’s a funny line and entirely worth a slight betrayal of Moe’s otherwise dim-witted nature.
