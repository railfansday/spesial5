Every MCU Doctor Doom Scene Confirmed So Far, Ranked Before Avengers: Doomsday

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Doctor Doom is coming. In fact, he's already arrived. For all the accusations that Robert Downey Jr.'s villain lacks any of the complicated build that Thanos got towards the end of the Infinity Saga, Marvel Studios has actually done a fair amount of work establishing Doom as a game-changing figure in the MCU.

He's now appeared in as many films as Thanos did before Infinity War, and though the amount of screen time that he's had doesn't compare (thanks to Thanos's bigger role in Guardians of the Galaxy), it's all been about building aura and establishing that Doom is just as big a threat as Thanos was, if not even more so. Given the fact that Marvel Studios had to pivot away from the original Kang Dynasty plan (though there are now suspicions that Doctor Doom was always inevitable at some point), Doom's buildup ahead of Doomsday's release in December has actually been impressive.

But what do we know already about Doom? From the footage that we've seen, both from Doomsday's marketing and his appearances in The Fantastic Four: First Steps, Avengers: Endgame Encore's additional footage, and VisionQuest's post-credit scene, it seems Doom is both a savior for the multiverse and a problem for the heroes. In just a few scenes, including those shown in the Doomsday trailers, he has built an incredible aura and revealed some crucial things about his past and his plan in Avengers: Doomsday. But which of the scenes so far stands out most?

Though he's not shown actually delivering the speech, Avengers: Doomsday's trailer featured a chilling voice-over from a scene in which Doom speaks to what must be the targets of his rage: Earth's heroes who caused incursions. Whether that's everyone who returned back from Thanos' snap, or those who benefited from Loki's involvement in saving doomed multiverse branches is unclear - though it would seem Captain America is doomed no matter what - his short speech is an important tone setter for his arrival: “All of you have lived stolen lives, and now you have to give it back."

The final additional scene added to Avengers: Endgame Encore stole the original Endgame tribute to Tony Stark after his death in the Battle of Earth. Originally, Endgame ended only with a short audio clip of Stark forging the first Iron Man armor in the first movie, as a quiet callback honoring his importance to the first age of the MCU. Endgame Encore repurposed the scene to show Doom forging his own iconic mask.

It is a slightly confusing scene because Doom is already wearing his mask, which may be a trick of editing. Or it may be further evidence to the increasing rumors that Robert Downey Jr. may not be the only Doom in Doomsday. By the way, it's only really a stinger, and because it undermines Endgame's original tribute to Iron Man, it's not a great one.

Now that we've seen more of the marketing for Avengers: Doomsday, his first appearance in the MCU in The Fantastic Four: First Steps post-credit scene seems to be misdirection. We already know that Victor von Doom had a relationship with the Fantastic Four prior to the events of First Steps, and originally, it was assumed that Doom was actually there to kidnap Franklin Reed, but that may be an oversimplification.

A later Doomsday trailer reveals that, by that point, Doom had already undergone the fundamental change that turned him towards villainy, which means it's unlikely that he's on good terms with the Fantastic Four in this scene. That doesn't necessarily mean that he's taking Franklin, though there is, of course, a possibility that he sees Franklin's powers as a means to rebuild the multiverse after he cleans up its collapse. But then, that would be wild speculation beyond what's actually shown in this scene, which is a fairly straightforward teaser that doesn't really add much narratively.

Now, this is what you call aura setting. Pieced together from the second of the Doomsday trailers, we see Doom moodily sitting on his throne in Doomstadt in Latveria. You get the voiceover from Sue Storm, talking about his backstory as well as hinting at the trauma that turned him evil before the collapse of the multiverse, but whether this scene plays out exactly like this in the final movie remains to be seen.

It's more likely that these establishing shots will be in there, but without Sue's voiceover. All the same, we get a great shot of Doom's castle, his magical prowess as he sits in front of a giant pentagram, and hints of the personal trauma that are so key to his backstory.

It's likely that the footage of Doom that we've seen in the trailers is taken from a fairly small amount of the final film. It's very possible that Mr. Incredible's confrontation with Doom comes immediately after the attack on the X Mansion or the confrontation with Thor. All three could well play part in one sequence, but for now, let's explore them as separate entities.

Pedro Pascal's performance here offers real insight into the established relationship between Doom and the leader of Fantastic Four. Sue's voiceover in the throne room scene suggests they are already estranged because of Doom's change in nature, but here, Mister Fantastic seems to show some level of disbelief, maybe even self-delusion, that Victor would even be capable of doing whatever he is reacting to. This is a small flash suggesting that their relationship will actually be a key part of the narrative and shows that Doom's plan goes beyond saving the multiverse.

In a surprise move, Avengers: Endgame Encore's new scenes included the belated post-credits stinger for Spider-Man: Brand New Day, which sees Mark Ruffalo's Bruce Banner in self-imposed isolation in a bunker under the city. As he works on research to control his mutation, his video diary is, of course, interrupted by the off-screen arrival of Doom, who uses his magic to teleport into the bunker and tells Banner that he is here to "collect" him.

Banner Hulks out, the Hulk roars in rage, and all that's left is one of Doom's runes on the floor. It's a great scene that wraps up what comes next for Hulk, as well as confirming him for Avengers: Doomsday, when it was widely suspected that he might sit it out entirely. The idea of Hulk being used as a weapon by Doom, though it repeats one of the MCU's favorite Hulk storylines for the fifth time, is a genuinely compelling twist in Bane's arc. Interestingly, the Russos have since hinted that Hulk is the reason why Banner has been recruited.

As was unfortunately leaked well before we actually got to see it in a Doomsday trailer, one scene - presumably in the X-Men universe as it crumbles - shows Doctor Doom raising the iconic X-Men Sentinels from the earth (presumably remnants of former battles with the X-Men). The giant robotic mutant killers are powered by Doom's magic and are presumably key to defeating the X-Men in their encounter with Doom. Beyond very cool aura farming, this moment clearly leads directly to the shot of Cyclops screaming in anguish as the Sentinels are shown walking behind him through a fiery haze. Just having the Sentinels back in their iconic original form is a huge deal for Marvel fans, and this already feels like one of the movie's best set pieces.

What better way to establish just how powerful Doom is than by pitting him against arguably the most powerful surviving Avenger, at least the one who is active at the moment? Without wanting to get into a debate, that is undoubtedly Chris Hemsworth's Thor.

He was shown in one of the first Doomsday trailers praying to the All-Father to help him defeat an unprecedented enemy, and his briefing of the other Avengers and New Avengers reveals how terrifying Doom is compared to all of his other foes. Most impressively, the scene shows Doom effectively shrugging off the full force of Thor and Stormbreaker's combined might. With very little effort, he resists the mystical axe, is unmoved by Thor's lightning powers, and then blasts him away. It's a shocking display of power and was the first real confirmation of exactly how much of a threat Doom would be, and that's before his familiar recruits were even revealed. This also appears to be the same scene that sees Doom sent flying out of the window of the strange structure.

Perhaps this is an emotionally led choice, but Doctor Doom returning to Mount Wundagore to resurrect Scarlet Witch after her death at the end of Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness is a huge deal. It was enough to inspire a surge in Doomsday ticket sales when it was revealed.

VisionQuest's post-credits scene took a hard swerve away from Vision, Tommy and Billy's reunion, and Vision's hopeful suggestion that maybe he'd be able to say hello to Wanda once more. That gave way to Doom visiting the site of Scarlet Witch's death and calling for her to awaken, while telling her that her powers would become his. There may be too much of a literal interpretation of what he says going on, as it feels more likely that Scartlet Witch is going to be one of his Latverian witches, as opposed to him absorbing her powers. But their alliance, even if it is forced, is a real game changer that gives Doctor Doom a far more powerful base than even Thanos had.

This all, of course, sets up the end of Scarlet Witch's arc and a possible redemption, with the likely reunion of her vision and her sons. It also subtly suggests that Doctor Strange is also working with Doctor Doom, given he was the only person to know where Scarlet Witch died. It's a lot of information for a very short amount of footage, but that's precisely why it feels like the biggest of Doom's scenes so far.

Avengers: Doomsday is scheduled to release globally on December 18.
