In 'The Spot', Claire Danes and Ewan McGregor Unravel Together

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Claire Danes was standing in the operating room, dressed in scrubs and a mask, when a surgeon’s assistant gave her the opposite of a pep talk. “You may think you’re badass, but if you think you’re fainting, you’re fainting, and we can’t have that, so just let me know,” she told Danes.

Danes, luckily, did not faint, though she says she was incredibly aware of her breathing as she watched an intricate brain surgery performed by a team of doctors.

The three-time Emmy winner was observing in preparation for her role in The Spot, a new Hulu series in which she plays a successful neurosurgeon who begins to suspect that she may be responsible for the hit-and-run death of a child. As she tries to investigate the horrific possibility, she and her husband (Ewan McGregor) find themselves falling deeper and deeper into a web of suspicion and dark secrets.

“I’ve always thought of it as an exploration of a marriage through the lens of a crime and maybe exploration of a crime through the lens of a marriage,” says Solomon of The Spot.

“I’m fascinated with this notion of what if your worst-case scenario way in the back of your head started to really look like it was true, and how could you dramatize that?” says writer, creator, and showrunner Ed Solomon.

The bold psychological thriller, which Vanity Fair can exclusively reveal will debut on January 13, follows a woman unraveling at the possibility of her worst fears coming true, and a marriage under enormous strain. There’s been a steady stream of whodunit miniseries in the last decade, but here Solomon, whose past work includes writing The Christophers and Now You See Me, has created a highly watchable, page-turning series that’s more of a “did I do it?”

“I never want it to be pretentious,” says Solomon. “I just want it to be fun and entertaining, but also elevated, respectful of the viewer.”

"I had wanted to work with Claire for years and we just had great fun acting and playing together while making this show,” says McGregor.

Danes was initially drawn to the series because of the portrait of the marriage at the heart of it, and Solomon’s particular gift for weaving the tragic into the comic. “It gets really very dark pretty quickly,” she says, “but it’s absurd too.”

She not only watched two brain surgeries in preparation but also shadowed a surgeon on his rounds, being with patients before they went into surgery and while they were recovering with their families. “I felt horrible that there I was in this room with these people who couldn’t be more vulnerable, and there I was, trying to be discreet in the corner,” she says.

But the experience gave her insight into what kind of person her character Lou Shadwick would be: intense, hypervigilant, and high achieving. “There’s a lot of marathon runners in there, and highly, highly focused people,” she says. “And they carry a lot of responsibility, and I think they compartmentalize very effectively.”

Danes had previously worked with Solomon on the 2023 series Full Circle, which was directed by Steven Soderbergh. When Kate Winslet, who was initially attached to play Lou, exited, Danes stepped in. Solomon needed an actress who could portray a mother and surgeon who is obsessed with doing the right thing and being a good person. “To be able to see someone who has a very, very present and active inner life that when you look, you can see well beyond the eyes and you can see someone who can be basically playing many things at one time, which was really important and interesting to me,” he says.

Danes had never worked with McGregor before, though they both had previously worked with Baz Luhrmann (on Romeo + Juliet and Moulin Rouge, respectively) and she thinks they had met briefly around that time. “We were definitely of a certain time, the ’90s,” she says. “So we share a lot of references, but it was just an immediate rapport.”

Lou is doing what many mothers juggling work and family have done: driving home from work, talking on the phone about dinner while also checking an unending flurry of text messages. It’s at that moment when she thinks she might have accidentally hit a child on a bike and not realized it until she heard about it later. “It’s so effective, this circumstance that he’s drawn for us,” she says of Solomon. “It’s entirely relatable, and I think it’s the fear that we all have experienced and hosted many, many times, if not on a daily basis.”

Michael Patrick Thornton as Pete Porphyry

As she tries to piece together what might have happened, a police officer (Daveed Diggs) and a detective (Michael Patrick Thornton) are doing the same, and begin to suspect Lou. Her husband, Damon, gets involved, making more trouble for both of them.

Lou and Damon met in medical school, but Damon dropped out and is now pursuing medical aid work in the shadow of his wife’s illustrious career. “I really like the idea of a man, who is charming and good-looking and therefore used to feeling very much in control when he walks into a room, having a spouse who is quite significantly on the rise,” says Solomon. “What does that do to your sense of ego if you’re a guy like that?”

Lou begins to see signs that she may be the guilty party.

McGregor says he had wanted to work with Danes for a long time, and was drawn as Damon as a character. “I think the character has an amazing arc throughout the story from where he starts to where he ends,” he says. “He has a very deeply hidden, shameful secret that gets uncovered as the story goes on.”

McGregor brings to the role a kind of playfulness. “I’ve always loved a certain quality of Ewan’s where there’s a maturity and a boyishness happening simultaneously,” says Solomon. “The boyish quality within this character is really important and the ability to cover a sense of wound.”

Set in upstate New York, the new series will take viewers on a wild ride following two people who are putting themselves in an increasingly dire situation and web of lies. “She’s a decent person, and so is her husband, really,” says Danes. “And yet, they allow their moral compass to become increasingly compromised.” Solomon hopes that even as the story gets darker and wilder, it will leave viewers with at least one big question they’ll have to grapple with: “I would hope that they’re asking questions, like what would I do if I were in that situation?”

“In Ewan, you can see the fragility in him,” says Solomon of McGregor.
