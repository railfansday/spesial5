United Airlines just made a big change to flight bookings. Here’s why Delta and American aren’t following

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

United Airlines is banning personal AI assistants from booking and modifying tickets on its system, a change that could see other airlines follow suit.

The Chicago-based carrier updated its contract of carriage on Wednesday, prohibiting the use of AI agents to “make, modify or service a ticket, and/or purchase any service or product.” The airline cited concerns that third-party, AI assistants can expose the sensitive information of passengers and that actions taken by these tools may result in unintended purchases or account changes, it said in a statement. United also noted that automated AI actions could potentially violate Federal Aviation Administration or Department of Transportation regulations—noting, for example, that flight check-in requires passengers to acknowledge that their bags don’t contain any banned items.

The carrier, it seems, may be taking its cue from other industries as it cited that corporate leaders of banks and retail companies have taken similar actions regarding AI agents. “We encourage others to consider measures that preserve the data and security of their customers and strengthen safety,” it said.

As is often the case for the industry, other airlines may follow in United’s path, though none of the other major U.S. carriers have similarly banned AI agents—yet. Just last month, Southwest Airlines announced a plugin partnership with ChatGPT to “offer a simpler, more direct path from inspiration and discovery to booking options” with the airline.

Here’s how the other major carriers are currently approaching AI tools for booking.

DELTA AND AMERICAN: ‘EVALUATING’ AI

United is one of the three largest carriers serving the U.S., along with Delta Air Lines and American Airlines. Both of those airlines indicated to Fast Company that some AI tools continue to have booking privileges on their systems, though they’re both evaluating the landscape.

A spokesperson for Fort Worth, Texas-based American said that because AI-assisted travel shopping and booking are evolving quickly, the airline will continue to evaluate how to best serve customers. 

“Some AI tools are ​able to facilitate the purchase of American Airlines tickets,” the company said in a statement. “At the same time, protecting our ​customers and their experience remains our priority. We actively monitor traffic across our website ​and app and ​can investigate, manage ​or block automated traffic when necessary ​to protect our ​customers and their experience.”

A spokesperson for Delta told Fast Company that if an AI tool can authenticate a passenger’s credentials, then it can complete a booking on their behalf. But the company noted that even if passengers use AI tools, they’re ultimately responsible for overseeing any actions and authorizing any transactions taken by these assistants on their behalf. 

“We currently support certain interactions with AI assistants when they can be appropriately authenticated, while maintaining additional safeguards for sensitive transactions,” the company said in a statement to Fast Company. “We’re continuing to evaluate how new technology, including external AI agents, could enhance the way customers engage with Delta, always with security and the customer experience in mind.”

Representatives for Southwest, JetBlue AirWays, Alaska Airlines, and Frontier Airlines didn’t immediately respond to requests for comment from Fast Company.

Shares of United fell 1.8% on Wednesday while the broader S&P 500 Index rose 1.3%.
