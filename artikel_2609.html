USC caps perfect homestand with big win over Louisiana

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

LOS ANGELES -- It wasn't as flashy as the first two games of No. 18 USC's homestand, but they got the job done nonetheless. The Trojans improved to 6-0 with an 82-56 victory over the Louisiana Ragin' Cajuns on Sunday night at the Galen Center, led once again by the talented backcourt duo of Rodney Rice and Alijah Arenas.

The pair combined to lead USC in scoring, totaling 36 on 50% shooting. Both guards dropped 18, with Rice dishing out a game-high six assists and snagging three rebounds in the process. Meanwhile, Arenas added two assists of his own. Their combined 18-for-21 mark from the free-throw line helped provide a nice padding throughout the evening.

"I thought [Rice] was phenomenal, and so was Alijah. We were +33 with Alijah on the floor...so some really good stuff there in our backcourt for sure," said head coach Eric Musselman.

While the duo has certainly been a success thus far, Musselman admitted that there was uncertainty surrounding how they might produce together on the floor.

"Sometimes when you have returning players, you have a feel. But they never played one second together," he said. "There's a risk that we talked about internally as a staff; we talked about it a little bit with them as well, how to make it work. I don't see a backcourt that's playing better than them..."

Arenas headed into Sunday's contest with his 22 points per game ranked second in Big Ten scoring, fueled by a 57.8% shooting display through the first five games of the season. After going 4-of-9 in the victory, he has now shot at least 44% in every single game.

It was the Ratliff twins, however, that helped kickstart an early run for the Trojans. Adonis opened the scoring with a pair of free throws before collecting a steal a few plays later, which led to a four-point play from Darius. Their six quick points came as part of a 10-0 USC run to begin the contest.

Both continued to add on steadily throughout the night, finishing in double-digit scoring and getting it done on the defensive end as well. Darius finished with a career-high 17 points, adding six rebounds, three assists and a steal. He also earned a game-high four blocks, imposing his will against the driving attack of Louisiana.

Adonis used a 14-point performance to reach double-digit scoring for the third consecutive contest, adding a game-high eight rebounds while also collecting two assists and two steals. It was the first time since the season opener that both reached at least ten points in the same game.

Despite the hot start, the Trojans did not put together as sharp a first half as in the first two games of the homestand. They were outrebounded 16-14 by the Ragin' Cajuns and generated only four points off the bench. Both sides made just 11 field goals each. Louisiana didn't do itself any favors to capitalize, shooting just 3-of-10 from behind the arc in the first 20 minutes and ultimately trailing 42-27 heading into the locker room.

Rice sent USC into halftime on the right note, converting the team's second four-point play of the half on their final offensive possession. His 12 points at the break led all scorers, largely thanks to a 9-of-9 display from the free-throw line.

"I'm the type of player to not force anything," Rice said. "There's going to be some points of the game where I'm ultra aggressive, but I'm only doing what I need to for the team to win and get a big lead, so just staying at the free-throw line, that's the main thing. Staying aggressive."

Louisiana remained in the fight to open the second half after a slam from forward Dani Pounds, as he was fouled by Arenas, creating a three-point play opportunity that cut the deficit to 12. From that point, USC initiated a 10-2 run of its own to go up 55-35, securing their largest lead of the night to that point.

Guard Jamyron Keller led the Ragin' Cajuns in scoring, finishing the evening with 13 points on 5-of-12 shooting. Fellow guard Deion Ware was the only other scorer to reach double digits, posting 10.

It was all gas and no brakes for the Trojans the rest of the way, as Rice, Arenas and both Ratliff brothers continued to add on. By the time the final buzzer sounded, they had accumulated 67 of the squad's total points.

Guard KJ Lewis played 28 minutes, contributing seven of USC's nine bench points. He led the way in the steals department for the Trojans with three.

"Just trying to come in and be an aggressor, enforcer, and give us some energy," Lewis said. "I know steals and long rebounds lead to transition. Those are easy buckets, so just doing whatever I can to make the game easier for my teammates."

Freshman forward Christian Collins, who was seen limping to the locker room just ahead of halftime, did not check back into the game the rest of the way. Musselman revealed it was an ankle injury, but said Collins probably could have played the second half if needed.

USC will now head to Palm Desert for a pair of games next Wednesday and Thursday at Acrisure Arena as part of the Acrisure Series. Wednesday's battle will be against South Carolina (5-0), a program the Trojans are 2-2 against in the all-time series.
