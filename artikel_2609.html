Thanksgiving Day games will impact NFC North, AFC top seed races

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

It's not every year that the NFL ends up with elite matchups on Thanksgiving Day that will have enormous impacts on the league's playoff races. Part of this is due to the Detroit Lions and Dallas Cowboys being involved every year, as the two franchises have been hit-or-miss in terms of their playoff impacts over the past few years, as well as the league often finding random pairs of teams to match up in the night game.

For example, last year's Thanksgiving games ended up having one big game: the Packers' 31-24 win over the Lions, which was an obviously important game heading into the day. The two teams were within a game of the NFC North lead at the time — the Packers coming in at 7-3-1 and the Lions at 7-4 — and Green Bay's win ended up keeping them ahead of Detroit for the NFC's final playoff spot at the end of the season. However, the Cowboys-Chiefs game saw both teams sputtering a bit heading into the game, while the night game between Cincinnati and Baltimore was a bit of a dud.

2024 was even less exciting from a national perspective. The Lions hosted the Bears, but Chicago was 4-7 heading into the holiday and was mired in a five-game losing streak; Cowboys-Giants featured teams with a combined six wins ahead of the game; and Packers-Dolphins was a cross-conference game that had little rivalry juice.

Thanksgiving Day in 2026 is a very different story, however. The power balance in the NFL's best division and the race for the top seed in the AFC are both on the line today, providing an exceptional pair of matchups in the early and prime time games that should keep NFL fans riveted. Two of today's games feature a pair of teams with at least 8 wins each, something that has never happened before, according to my research. In fact, the only other time that Thanksgiving had two games pitting teams with even 7 or more wins each was way back in 2014 with Eagles-Cowboys (both 8-3) and Seahawks-49ers (both 7-4).

It's a perfect year to schedule dinner for around kickoff of the afternoon, ensuring that the football fans in your household can watch an impactful game before food, then eat and get a nap in before grabbing some leftovers to chow down with a big night game.

This is the big one in the NFC North. Like the Packers and Bears a year ago, Chicago and Detroit enter the later stages of this season as the top teams in the league's best division with two games to play against one another in the final handful of weeks. The two teams come into the holiday at 8-2, one game up on the Vikings and two up on the Packers in the NFC North standings. They're also on equal footing in a number of areas — their point differentials are almost equal (+68 for the Lions, +65 for the Bears).

If one team needs this game more than the other right now, it's the Bears. Chicago is 0-2 in the division, while the Lions are 2-0. If the two teams split the season series and tie for the division lead, the tiebreaker will go to the team with the better division record; therefore, Chicago is in a tougher spot and really cannot afford to go a game down and lose the first half of the season series plus yet another divisional game.

The winner of this one will stake a strong claim to the division and will stay within striking distance of the one-loss 49ers for the NFC's top spot overall. The loser can make up the difference in these teams' rematch in Week 17, but there will be work to do before then.

As for the Packers, they would probably benefit more from a Bears win here, since Green Bay has one head-to-head win over Chicago already. The division isn't entirely lost to the Packers yet, but they'll have lots of ground to make up no matter the result of this game.

Here's your mealtime/naptime game. The NFC East is a bit of a mess this season, with the Eagles in particular struggling with a new offensive coordinator and no Jalen Hurts. They come in at 3-7, while the Cowboys continue to ride an explosive offense (and equally explosive defense) to a 5-6 record.

There could be some fun firepower in this game, but it doesn't move the needle in terms of a matchup that has a major impact on the playoffs. Eat up, rest up, and recharge for a big night game.

Here's the dessert for football fans: yet another thrilling matchup between elite quarterbacks. The Bills sit atop the AFC at 9-1 and they are running away with the AFC East. They are the second-highest scoring team in the NFL, and their defense surprisingly ranks in the top ten in scoring. That's largely due to schedule, however; the Bills have allowed fewer than 10 points per game on average over their last four games, but those performances have come against the Ravens, Vikings, Jets, and Dolphins, quarterbacked by Joe Fagnano, Carson Wentz, Geno Smith, and Kyle McCord, respectively.

The Chiefs are a different story this year than last, when they scuffled even before Patrick Mahomes' injury. He's back off a torn ACL though, and the Chiefs have found an elite running game with Kenneth Walker and Emmett Johnson as a 1-2 punch.

These two teams have put together quite the rivalry of late. The Bills have won each of the last five regular season meetings between the franchises, dating back to 2021, but the Chiefs have taken each of the four playoff matchups that have matched up the two elite quarterbacks.

The 8-2 Broncos are in the mix right now as well, but don't be shocked if the team that wins this game ends up as the AFC's #1 seed and earns a bye in the first round of the playoffs. Between the rivalry and the playoff impact this game could have, it should make for an excellent matchup to cap off a big day of football around the NFL.
