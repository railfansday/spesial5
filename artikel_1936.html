Second-half blitz sinks Littlehampton as Eastbourne Borough reach Senior Cup last eight

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

A five-goal second half blitz underlined the Sports' superior status - but only after the Golds had turned around at half-time at 1-1 and with a glint of an upset in the gleaming, rain-soaked Priory Lane stadium.

George Gaskin and Mitch Hand's squad had made the long trek along the Sussex coast against the background of a frankly awful season so far. They’re a step lower than the Sports, but actually 44 places lower in terms of current league tables.

And if it is ever possible to draw comfort from a 6-1 defeat, they can be satisfied this was a well contested game - which also banished memories of the two clubs' last encounter, an ill-tempered Senior Cup semi-final two seasons ago at Lancing.

Borough had thrown open the gates with free admission at the Connect Management Stadium - and those local football addicts who chose live footy ahead of a warm fireside were actually rather well rewarded.

Steve King's squad is strong on paper but stretched by injuries; but his line-up gave a starting shirt to FA Trophy hero Tyano Wilson as well as young reserve keeper Myles Watkins, and a place on the bench for patient striker Zack Tarrant - who would have the last word of the night.

Wilson created an early chance out of nothing, but his low cross flashed across goal with nobody able to convert. But on just seven minutes, the Sports were ahead: Arthur Grout played a perfectly weighted lofted pass to Liam Nash, who finished in style.

Cue a cascade of goals, through the silvery curtain of drizzle under gleaming lights? Nope - or at least not yet. Littlehampton kept their nerve, had a reasonable share of possession and kept their shape.

And, as the minutes drizzled away, half-time was suddenly approaching with the 1-0 scoreline unchanged. And just as suddenly, the Golds grabbed an equaliser through Marcel Powell - smartly taken if only just deserved on the balance of play.

With dressing room doors slammed tightly shut, King and his staff must have passed a few words of wisdom, and the second half saw a more determined and purposeful home performance.

In fact, it took just two minutes from the restart for Max Sheaf to restore the Sports' advantage with a beauty of a strike - and that set the second-half agenda.

Within five minutes, Myles-Watkins produced a strong save at his near post to deny Powell an equaliser. Then Gold Omotayo tucked one under the keeper to extend the home side's lead, or so they thought - but an offside flag chalked it off. No matter: Borough were now controlling most of the play.

Just past the hour, Laleye and Wilson combined beautifully at pace, to set up Gold's tap-in for 3-1. And three minutes later Owen Bellamy - often an unsung hero in the red Borough shirt - curled in the sweetest of strikes from 25 yards, to put the game effectively beyond Littlehampton's reach.

There was still time for Borough's strikers of the future to make their mark. Tarik Kadric and Tarrant enjoyed space against an understandably tiring visiting defence, and after Omotayo had converted a penalty for handball - furiously disputed by the visitors - it was Tarrant who claimed the final goal.

The tall youngster had too much pace for the Golds defence and his strike cannoned in off the underside of the bar.

For Littlehampton, a long and weary drive home, and that ever-ironic agenda to ‘concentrate on the league’. For the Sports, yet another victory - 20 in 22 games - and the ride rolls on.
