Newcastle jeers tell Matthias Jaissle his ‘vertical’ football is not working

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Newcastle United look like a poor team playing badly. The evidence is increasingly damning after Matthias Jaissle’s side were loudly booed off at the end of this limp defeat.

This has all the makings of a long, hard season. Anger is already rising to the surface, even though Jaissle claimed Newcastle deserved more from a game they “controlled” in the first half and “dominated” in the second. Those comments jarred with what we had just seen.

After four years of enjoying an upward trajectory under Eddie Howe, the team faded last season, sold their best players in the summer, lost the manager and there is no evidence yet they have replaced anything with better.

This was supposed to be a reaction to the embarrassing 4-1 thrashing at Coventry City, but it led to another loss and, in the first half at least, another abject display. Aston Villa were always the more fluent and more threatening. They should have won by more than just Johan Manzambi’s first-half header.

It is getting hard to comprehend what Jaissle wants his team to do. The much-hyped “vertical” style looks like an ugly incarnation of the long ball football of yesteryear. They did not like it at Newcastle in the 1980s and 1990s and some fashions should never return. It is in danger of resembling a shell suit that has emerged from the back of the wardrobe for a Saturday night out.

There is so much confidence in the German in the Newcastle boardroom that they believe improvement is inevitable, but that is far from assured. Newcastle are not getting better; they are making all the same mistakes and, if anything, as an attacking force, they are looking increasingly stale and predictable. They did not have a shot on target in the first half and, other than one fantastic save in the second, Villa goalkeeper Zion Suzuki had nothing to do.

“Our decision-making in the final third was lacking today,” Jaissle said. “I am frustrated, I understand the supporters’ reaction, that is their right [to boo after a defeat at home].”

It is time for a sharp dose of medicine on Tyneside. Expectations need to be adjusted, especially as none of the new signings have impressed. This was always the fear and the risk when you put all your faith in young players from abroad.

For Jaissle’s vertical football to work, Newcastle need to be able to win the ball high up the pitch, whether that is with a high press or competing for second balls from long passes. They do not do either well. More worryingly, they look so vulnerable, so easy to play through.

Villa did it to them all afternoon. Nicolas Jackson should have scored in the first half, Ibrahim Mbaye should have hit the target in the second.

On another day, Newcastle could also have had centre-back Sven Botman sent off for a lunge on Jackson. Then it really would have become ugly, but the Dutchman survived a VAR check.

Villa were always the better side. There was no fluency in Newcastle’s game, no structure, no patterns of play. Every time they turned over the ball, they looked stretched. Every time they got a set-piece, it was defended easily. Every time they went forward in the first half, the ball was sent aimlessly long and came straight back at them. In the second, they huffed and puffed but did not sustain any pressure on the Villa goal. The missed chances late on were not clear opportunities and they were snatched at by the hapless Yoane Wissa.

It was largely poor and so was the manner in which Villa took the lead. João Gomes was given the time and space to loft a cross to the far post for Manzambi, who had drifted away from Lewis Miley, to head home.

Newcastle were in disarray and it should have been 2-0 almost immediately when Emiliano Buendía received the ball in the middle of the area and crashed a shot against a post.

The home crowd tried to remain patient and supportive, but the sight of yet another long, aimless ball being punted forward straight to a Villa defender tipped some over the edge. There were boos and jeers in the stands while Jaissle screamed at Joe Willock and Wissa. There were more boos at half-time; things felt combustible.

Newcastle needed a big second half. They were better, but it was hardly anything to get excited about. A shot on the turn from Botman, superbly saved by Suzuki. Then a few half-chances were squandered, the occasional flash of a threat and that was it.
