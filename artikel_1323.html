Man Utd ‘Up For Sale’ as Financial Expert Reveals the Glazers’ Asking Price

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Manchester United fans remain disgruntled with the current ownership of the club, with another manager on the verge of the sack.

Michael Carrick is set to become the seventh permanent manager to be shown the exit door since Sir Alex Ferguson's reign ended in 2013.

However, many within the fan base are adamant that the real problem at Old Trafford is the hierarchy, and until that is in order, any manager will struggle to get the English giants back to the top of European football.

Protests took place inside the ground during United's 4-4 draw against Aston Villa on Sunday. Green and gold balloons were deployed along with a banner, which read: "We don't want a new stadium, we want the owners out."

The Glazer family have been major shareholders in the club since Malcolm Glazer assumed full control in May 2005. They have been far from popular figures in Manchester, with debt mounting ever since they arrived.

Speaking to talkSPORT, financial expert Stefan Borson revealed that United fans hoping for the Glazers to sell could get their wish in the near future.

Michael Carrick is set to be sacked as Manchester United's head coach with Mauricio Pochettino in line to replace him at Old Trafford

Borson stated: "My understanding from somebody, a senior person in the game, told me that there is a process and that they are for sale.

"You would question why they wouldn't make it public, although when they last made a process public, that of course ended with a slightly disappointing outcome (Ratcliffe's shares)."

He went on to claim that the Glazers hadn't made their intentions public, which could be linked to the fallout from Sir Jim Ratcliffe's arrival in 2024. "They may be trying something different by not making it formal, but they are now accepting that now is the time," he said.

"They (Glazers) are robust, and they will only go when their price is met.

"They believe this is a club that has such a robust brand that even if they are unsuccessful on the pitch, it has very little difference to the commercial viability and ability to generate revenue."

Borson expects the asking price to be in the region of £5 billion, citing FSG's deal to sell 38% of Liverpool to the 1892 Holdings Consortium as a yardstick.
