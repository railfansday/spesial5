Jordan Brunoli, Bassist With Australian Indie Rockers Bugs, Dies at 31

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Brunoli was diagnosed with stage 4 colorectal cancer in 2025.

Jordan Brunoli, bass player with Brisbane-based indie rock trio Bugs, died on Wednesday night (Nov. 18) following a public battle with cancer. He was 31.

Brunoli was diagnosed with stage 4 colorectal cancer in 2025, and, rather than choosing the quiet life, advocated for others suffering with illness. The rocker performed on the band’s four albums, including their latest collection, Head Noise, which dropped Aug. 28 and was shortlisted for a J Award for Australian album of the year. The band’s previous album, 2022’s Cooties (via Community Music), crashed the top 10 of the ARIA Top 20 Vinyl Albums Chart, peaking at No. 4.

In 2020, Brunoli and his bandmates had an unexpected breakthrough with their Like A Version cover of Mallrat’s “Charlie,” which broke into the Hottest 100 the following year at No. 91, and landed at No. 25 in 2023’s Hottest 100 Like A Versions of all time poll.

Bugs presented a fundraiser for Brunoli at local venue The Tivoli last October. A slew of Brisbane’s brightest participated in the show, bearing the name Saving A Bug’s Life, including Dear Seattle, Beddy Rays, Sly Withers, Hope D, The Buoys, TOWNS, RAAVE TAPES, and others.

Following his diagnosis, Jordan was a regular on triple j’s Hack program, on which he shared updates on his health, his outlook as a young person facing a terminal diagnosis, and to remind other young Australians to look after their health.

He described music as “therapy,” and nothing was better than playing with his bandmates Connor Brooker and Brock Weston.

“Especially when I’m really down, I’ll just play guitar as much as my fingers will allow,” he told Hack in 2025. “You’ll have to break my arms and peel my entire skin off before I’ll stop playing.”

ARIA and triple j paid tribute to the late artist. “Vale Jordan Brunoli, taken far, far too young,” reads a statement from the trade body. “He used his interviews with triple j’s Hack to tell young Australians to advocate for their health and heard back from some who’d been diagnosed early enough to be treated.”Brunoli is survived by his wife George, whom he married earlier this year. In his most recent interview with Hack, which aired in August, Brunoli thanked those nearest and dearest.“If I didn’t have Brock, Connor and my partner, G, keeping me grounded every day … I wouldn’t be anywhere near as comfortable as I am right now,” he remarked. “G and I were meant to be and always will be.”
