Paris Fury retracts Tyson schizophrenia claim

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Paris Fury says Tyson did not receive a diagnosis of schizophrenia; Netflix documentary has now been updated; The British Boxing Board of Control indicated it would look into comments about a schizophrenia diagnosis and could deny Fury a licence to face Anthony Joshua on December 11

Paris Fury has retracted her claim that husband Tyson Fury was diagnosed with schizophrenia.

A new Netflix documentary explores Tyson Fury's experiences of depression, anxiety and bipolar disorder, which became particularly acute after his unified heavyweight championship victory over Wladimir Klitschko in 2015.

Paris Fury made the comments about schizophrenia in an initial version of the documentary. Netflix said it has worked to "update" the footage in light of her clarification.

She has now said: "I want to clarify a statement I made in the original version of the Tyson Fury documentary. I recalled a conversation from many years ago when discussing Tyson's condition and I used the word 'schizophrenic'. Whilst Tyson did show some symptoms associated with schizophrenia, he doesn't have this condition and has never received a diagnosis.

"Mental health is complex, and it isn't always easy to find the right words, especially when you're living it with someone you love. I'm so proud of Tyson for really opening up in this documentary and want to say thank you for all the support we've received as a family."

Earlier on Thursday, the British Boxing Board of Control (BBBoC) indicated that it was investigating the comments and could deny Fury a licence to face Anthony Joshua in Cardiff on December 11 if he was deemed unfit to fight.

Sky Sports News has contacted the BBBoC to clarify if an investigation is ongoing after Paris Fury's retraction.

A spokesperson for Netflix said: "Following new information shared by the family relating to Tyson's official mental health diagnosis, we have worked alongside them to update the documentary accordingly ahead of launch."

After his victory over Klitschko, Fury revealed he had struggled with drug and alcohol addiction and had gained weight.

He was admitted to hospital in June 2016 with a suspected heart attack, and revealed that while in hospital he had heard voices in his head.

"Words can't explain the darkness I was in," Fury said during the Netflix documentary.

"It was like a living version of the pits of hell.

"I thought I would end up in a padded room with the door locked, and that scared me so much.

"The anxiety was crippling me. I just could not overcome it and that's when I went and saw the psychiatrist down in London. She asked me a gazillion questions, and I ticked all the boxes. Depression, anxiety, bipolar disorder."

The NHS says schizophrenia is a long-term mental health illness that is a form of psychosis.

Those suffering from the condition may, at times, be unable to distinguish their own thoughts from reality. Symptoms can include hallucinations and delusions.

The NHS describes bipolar disorder as a mental health issue where a person can experience extreme changes in their mood.
