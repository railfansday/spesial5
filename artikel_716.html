‘You Can See Everything’ Crosses $100M Globally, Becomes First Doc to Hit 1M Letterboxd Watches

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim's A24 documentary continues stirring conversation about Elizabeth Holmes through its sixth weekend in domestic release.

You Can See Everything continues visualizing box office success at a level that puts it in elite company as a documentary.

Focusing on disgraced Theranos founder Elizabeth Holmes‘ life in the lead-up to her prison sentence, A24‘s film surpassed $100 million globally over the weekend, a rare feat for any documentary. After opening Oct. 16 in North America, directors Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim‘s three-hour feature has grossed $93.4 million domestically over its six weekends in release and has more recently been rolling out in international markets. The movie has continued to experience sellout screenings after hitting theaters this weekend in such territories as Australia, the U.K. and Scandinavia, with Sony Pictures handling distribution in some international markets. Over the weekend, You Can See Everything also became the first documentary film to have been watched by one million Letterboxd members. It becomes the 19th movie released in 2026 to join the “One Million Watched Club,” and with its 4.61 average Letterboxd rating, it is the second-highest-rated feature to hit the milestone, behind only 12 Angry Men with 4.63. You Can See Everything is the highest-rated 2026 film and the second-highest-rated documentary ever, trailing only Stop Making Sense on the social platform.

Among the box office achievements for You Can See Everything include opening with $15.6 million in its debut frame to register the second-biggest domestic launch for a non-music documentary, behind 2004’s Fahrenheit 9/11. The Holmes documentary also scored the highest-grossing domestic opening weekend for a release in fewer than 1,000 theaters since 2006’s Borat. You Can See Everything went on become the first documentary to reach No. 1 domestically in its third weekend of release.

The movie remains the second-highest-grossing domestic non-music documentary, with only Fahrenheit 9/11 and its $119.1 million cume to beat.

You Can See Everything stands as a major Oscar contender as awards season heats up. The Hollywood Reporter critic Angie Han called it “smart, funny and full of surprises,” and it holds a 97 critics score on Rotten Tomatoes.

“It was sometimes a tense environment,” cinematographer David Bolen recalled about the shoot during a recent screening at Camerimage. Bolen noted that he would exchange pleasantries with Holmes and partner Billy Evans, but that was generally the extent of their conversations: “I really tried to stay out of the situation as much as possible. Sometimes it would get tense, and you see in the office scene, it gets a little bit tense, and [I would] just try to be quiet and polite and get the cameras out.”
