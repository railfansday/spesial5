Sony announces new PlayStation Portal with secret features coming in the future

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Sony has announced a new version of the PlayStation Portal featuring additional internal storage, teasing future functionality that has yet to be revealed.

The updated handheld, officially called the PlayStation Portal Player Midnight Black, will launch exclusively in Japan on December 10, 2026. While much of its hardware remains unchanged, Sony has confirmed one addition that could expand what the device is capable of.

In its November 9 announcement on the Japanese PlayStation Blog, Sony confirmed that the updated Portal includes additional internal storage intended to support functionality planned for the future.

“Furthermore, with an eye towards future functionality, the new model includes additional internal storage. We will announce details about this feature as soon as they are finalized,” the company said, according to a machine translation.

Sony did not reveal how much storage the updated handheld includes or explain what the additional capacity will eventually be used for.

The announcement has prompted speculation among PlayStation fans about whether the Portal could eventually support downloading games, running entertainment apps, or playing certain titles without an internet connection.

In a discussion on Push Square, one user questioned whether the additional storage could be used for screenshots or save data, while another suggested it might allow the device to run older PlayStation games locally.

Others have suggested that the storage could support streaming services such as Netflix and YouTube, potentially expanding the Portal’s functionality beyond gaming.

However, Sony has not confirmed any of these possibilities, and there is currently no indication that the handheld will support native PS5 game downloads or offline gameplay.

Alongside the storage addition, Sony is dropping the word “Remote” from the device’s official name, changing it from the PlayStation Portal Remote Player to the PlayStation Portal Player.

The company explained that the name change reflects the handheld’s expanded capabilities following the introduction of cloud streaming, rather than a newly announced feature.

Despite the updated name and additional storage, the new model retains its 8-inch LCD display with 1080p resolution and support for 60 frames per second. It also continues to offer haptic feedback, adaptive triggers, and 3D Audio.

The Japan-exclusive handheld will cost 39,980 yen, approximately $250 USD, and includes a one-month PlayStation Plus Premium subscription. Preorders begin November 13, ahead of its December 10 release.

The device is restricted to Japanese-language settings and requires a PlayStation account registered in Japan, meaning players in other regions cannot simply import it for use with their existing accounts.

Sony has not announced plans to release the updated PlayStation Portal internationally or confirmed whether its mysterious future functionality will also become available on existing models.
