GameStop CEO Says Digital-Only Games Have ‘Hurt Consumers’ as GTA 6 Skips Discs

GameStop CEO Ryan Cohen has criticized video game publishers for abandoning physical discs, as Grand Theft Auto 6, one of the most anticipated games ever made, launches on Thursday as a digital download only.

"When you buy a game, you should be able to sell it, trade it or lend it to a friend," Cohen told Bloomberg. "Unquestionably, the decision to move to all digital has hurt consumers, and it's also hurt GameStop."

Cohen declined to name specific companies, but Take-Two Interactive is releasing the $80 game only as a download, with retailers selling boxes containing download codes. Asked about presales, Cohen said: "It's a code in a box. I mean, we're selling a lot of it."

Take-Two declined to comment to Bloomberg.

The shift hits a core part of GameStop's business. Pre-owned and refurbished products made up 22% of the retailer's sales, about $171 million, in the quarter that ended August 1, down 32% from a year earlier, according to Bloomberg.

Cohen said he has spoken with publishers, who he argued benefit from limiting trade-ins because used copies compete with new sales. "They're taking away competition," he said.

Sony announced in July that it will stop producing physical discs for new PlayStation games in January 2028, a move experts have said will hit low-income gamers hardest. PlayStation founder Ken Kutaragi has dismissed the backlash as a "mentality issue," while an Infinity Ward veteran said publishers have pushed to cut discs since 2007.

Cohen said he has met with Rep. Ritchie Torres (D-NY), who is backing the Power to the Players Act, which would require gaming companies to give consumers ongoing access to the titles they buy. "A game that can be taken away the moment a company pulls the plug is not a purchase. It is a rental disguised as a sale," Torres said in October.

In Europe, the Stop Killing Games campaign gathered 1.3 million signatures for a European Citizens' Initiative calling for publishers to keep games playable after support ends.

The question of whether buyers own digital games has also reached the courts. Sony has argued in court that digital games can't be owned, prompting some in the crypto industry to pitch blockchain-based ownership as a fix.

Cohen's GameStop has also made bets beyond gaming. The company bought 4,710 BTC for more than $500 million in 2025, and in March moved all but one of them into a covered call strategy on Coinbase Prime to generate income. In May, after eBay rejected its takeover offer, GameStop sought shareholder approval to raise its authorized share count to 2.5 billion as it built a stake in the online marketplace.
