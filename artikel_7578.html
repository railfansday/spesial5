Netflix might want its new AI search to drum up more “quality” viewership

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Netflix‘s new strategy for combating YouTube‘s commanding share of living room TVs is to insist that it’s not the quantity of viewership, but the quality.

And it might be trying to pump up those quality views with a new recommendation tool.

Of course, the times being what they are, the tool is AI.

AI Discover is intended to curate Netflix’s offerings in a hyperpersonalized manner, so people only see what they like. If this sounds familiar, it’s because Netflix is doing what YouTube, X, and Meta have already done: shunt the responsibility for a quality recommendation algorithm onto viewers and their input.

“There’s more to enjoy and discover on Netflix than ever, but what sounds right for a quiet night in may look different for family movie night or a few spare minutes,” Netflix said in an announcement post. “We’ve spent years refining how you discover, with recommendations tailored to your tastes, from what you Thumbs Up to what you add to ‘My List.’ Now, AI Discover joins the mix as a new way to explore.”

Once a theme is in place, Netflix’s bot brings up a bunch of suggestions, and also additional narrower themes within the first themes. Netflix says there will be a process of “tuning” AI Discover’s recommendations until it “find[s] your next obsession.”

Users who think the bot is getting too far off what they want can refresh the whole deal. Netflix also lets people tell it titles they don’t like.

Unlike the aforementioned social media platforms, Netflix doesn’t seem to have included a ChatGPT-esque discussion aspect to this tool. Users just click through and select what they like, and what they don’t.

Makes sense, since YouTube, Meta, and X have their own in-house chatbots they can shove into everything; Netflix doesn’t have a Gemini or Grok or Muse equivalent, so a yes/no system is its best option. It did partner with OpenAI in 2025 to test an AI search feature that was rumored would replace its regular search function, but that never materialized in full across the service. Maybe this is the version of that idea it ultimately ended up with, sans GPT chatting.

Netflix does say AI Discover is “powered by a new intelligence layer we’ve been building that responds to what members tell us in real time and keeps learning.”

This brings us back to what we said at the top of this article: Netflix is looking for quality viewership. And, well, if it can gather an immense amount of information about viewers’ preferences with this new “intelligence layer,” it might be able to more accurately match them with what they want to watch.

That in turn could drive up the amount of time people spend watching a movie or a TV show–and Netflix might start shooting for average view duration as a way to supercede YouTube.

For now, Netflix’s new AI discovery is only available on TVs in the U.S. (interesting choice), and only in English. Netflix says it won’t appear on kids’ profiles. Per Variety, the streamer plans to expand to more international users in the coming weeks.
