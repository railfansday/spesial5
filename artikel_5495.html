Home Prices Hold Steady as Sales Drop and Inventory Expands

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Home prices remained relatively resilient in October despite a sharp slowdown in sales and continued growth in available inventory.

The national median sale price was $383,598, down 0.4% from October 2025, according to the latest housing market analysis from Homes.com. Home sales fell 11.7% year over year while inventory increased 4.8%.

The combination gave buyers more choices even as elevated mortgage rates limited demand while sellers faced greater competition for the smaller pool of buyers prepared to purchase.

“October’s results tell a more nuanced story than the nearly flat national price number alone would suggest,” says Brad Case, chief residential economist at Homes.com. “Sales were down substantially from a year ago even as buyers had more homes to choose from, yet prices barely moved. That suggests demand remains strong, but buyers appear increasingly selective about which homes they choose to purchase.”

Single-family homes posted the smallest annual price decline among the property types tracked by Homes.com. The median price declined 0.3% from October 2025, compared with 1.4% declines for both townhomes and condominiums.

Single-family sales fell 11.2% year over year, while inventory increased 5%. Townhome sales dropped 13.5% as inventory grew 5.3%, and condo sales fell 15.1% while inventory increased 3.6%.

The figures point to a market where additional inventory has yet to translate into significant national price declines, particularly for single-family homes, Homes.com points out.

The relatively flat national median masks significant differences among major housing markets. Among the 40 largest markets tracked, 10 posted annual price gains of at least 2%, while nine recorded declines of 2% or more.

San Francisco posted the largest highlighted gain, with its median sale price rising 17.7% year over year despite a 13% decline in sales. Jacksonville prices increased 6.1% even as sales fell 14.4%. On the opposite hand, Austin recorded a 9.7% annual price decline, followed by a 4.1% decrease in Nashville.

Inventory trends also varied widely. Seattle had 22.8% more homes for sale than a year earlier, while inventory in Jacksonville was 9.8% lower.

Leah Draffen is a senior editor at Builder. She earned a B.A. in journalism and minors in business administration and sociology from Louisiana State University.
