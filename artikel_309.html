U.S. Forces in Europe Face Uncertainty as Pentagon Reviews Troop Levels

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

A U.S. Army unit in Germany is not sure what’s next as the Pentagon considers a major drawdown of forces in Europe.

When a specialized U.S. Army unit in Germany was told it might move to South Korea this fall, soldiers started packing their cutting-edge equipment. Months later, their boxed-up gear was still visible during a tour of the base, and the unit remains in the city of Wiesbaden, waiting.

The uncertainty comes as the Pentagon undertakes a six-month review of U.S. forces in Europe, weighing a potentially significant drawdown even as Russia wages war on NATO’s border.

The fate of the unit in Germany, which includes 1,000 people and is known as the Multi-Domain Command Europe, is a snapshot of the uncertainty that roughly 78,000 American troops stationed in Europe are facing before the results of the review are announced, which should take place by the end of the year.

A drawdown could cut into ports in Spain, air bases in England and Italy, and pockets of soldiers across Eastern Europe. Germany is particularly vulnerable, given that it hosts 37,000 U.S. troops — more than any other country in Europe.

Complicating the picture is the fact that President Trump has been using the military as a geopolitical tool, ordering the Pentagon to curtail military exercises with South Korea in part because it did not offer sufficient support for the Iran war. It remains unclear whether the Germany-based unit will in fact move to Asia, according to five U.S. officials and a former diplomat, all of whom spoke on the condition of anonymity to describe internal Pentagon discussions. Three of the officials said the unit began packing last summer.

A Pentagon spokesman said the review was continuing and that officials were working through a “predeployment notification process” for the unit in Germany.
