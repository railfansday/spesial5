Mike Evans TD and Parker Washington non-TD raise more questions about NFL catch rules

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Few topics in the NFL cause more heated debates than what is or is not a catch. Two more big debates happened on Sunday, with two touchdown catch rulings that both appeared to be wrong on the field, and not corrected by replay.

On Sunday afternoon, Jaguars wide receiver Parker Washington caught a pass and had control with both feet down in the end zone, but it was ruled incomplete. On Sunday night, 49ers wide receiver Mike Evans didn’t have full control with both feet down as he went out of bounds in the end zone, but it was ruled a touchdown.

In the Jaguars game, former NFL referee Gene Steratore broke the play down and explained that while there was some minor movement of the football as Washington went to the ground, Washington had the ball with both feet down and never lost control.

“When Parker Washington, after the bobble, possesses that football, I still had the right foot down as he’s falling,” Steratore said on the CBS broadcast. “Then I had the left foot touch. I see the ball move a little bit, but I did not think he lost possession of the football. I have possession, right foot down, tap with the left foot, yes, the ball moves a little, but the right hand, in my opinion, continues to keep that secure enough, that I would say in my opinion he’s still controlling that football. I think it’s a touchdown.”

In the 49ers game, former NFL referee Terry McAulay said Evans was juggling the ball while running across the end zone and didn’t have control of it until his final step, with only one foot down in bounds.

“I don’t see him get final control until he’s got one foot down,” McAulay said. “The ball rolls around on his wrist. I don’t see it pinned to his body. When he gets control, there’s only one foot down. I think they should’ve reviewed it and reversed it to incomplete.”

Even if the NFL wants to insist that its officials got both calls right on the field, and that the replay review process was correct to uphold those calls, the league still needs to acknowledge that it has a problem on its hands if the catch rule is so confusing that even former referees who now work as on-air officiating analysts aren’t seeing what the on-field officials and the replay officials are seeing.
