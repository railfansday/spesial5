Fantasy Football Rankings: Hayden Winks' RB Blueprint for Week 12

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The Blueprint is my weekly column and basically everything that I use to build out the most accurate rankings I can. There will be this RB column, a WR column and a combination column on the onesie positions. You will find film notes and endless charts. Maybe enough charts to stress test the Yahoo website. I kid.

It's impossible to get every ranking thoroughly early in the week, so be sure to keep checking in on these columns through Sunday morning. As always, my full rankings at every position are available here.

1. Jahmyr Gibbs (vs. CHI) - 132.5 proj. total yards

Gibbs has recently finished as the RB5 and RB3 against the Vikings and Bucs, respectively, both defenses that sit at the top of every fantasy RB metric. There's no stopping this train.

The Bears defense is much better with DT Jeffery Simmons wreaking havoc, but Gibbs has shredded this defense for 100+ total yards in 4 straight games. Detroit enters as 2.5-point home underdogs with a 26.5-point implied total (sixth). Don't be surprised if his receiving volume ticks up here.

2. Emmett Johnson (at BUF) - 116.5 proj. total yards

With Kenneth Walker III (groin) ruled out again, this time for Thursday night's game against the Bills, Johnson remains a top-five play. The rookie popped off for RB3 and RB6 finishes the last two games, handling the usual Kenneth Walker III role. He reminds me of Bucky Irving in some ways, but there is still a physicality and long-speed gap to Walker.

Long term, Johnson will return to his part time role, perhaps with an extra handful of reps, but he's set for another heavy workload this week. The matchup is more mediocre. The Bills' defense is coming alive in Year 1 of a new system, thanks to a trade at the deadline for a safety and a few breakouts up front. They're middle of the pack in most metrics, and the Chiefs are only projected for 23.25 points (13th) as field-goal dogs.

3. Christian McCaffrey (vs. SEA) - 88.5 proj. total yards

McCaffrey is a patriot. He's posted RB22 and RB14 outings outside of the states but is the RB2 on RB6 usage inside the country. CMC will play the rest of his games in the USA for those tracking at home. There's nothing new with his role or production. He's the RB3 this season thanks to his usual receiving role (RB4 in receptions per game) and the 49ers' truly dominant scoring offense.

Seattle will be a real test, just like the Vikings were last week. Seattle sits 24th in half PPR points allowed to opposing backfields, and the Seahawks are fresh off a bye week. CMC was the RB11 with 83 total yards against them in Week 5 when the Niners won 32 to 29. He had 74, 57 and 142 scoreless yards against them in last year's matchups, too. It's reasonable to dock him for matchup reasons, but only so much. The 49ers dictate terms this year. San Francisco heads in as 3-point home favorites carrying a 25.25-point implied team total (eighth).

4. Bucky Irving (vs. CAR) - 102.5 proj. total yards

Irving has been fantastic since the end of September. He's been the RB5 on RB3 usage this past month, hulking 22.3 carries per game. Last week was another great outing, despite having an 80-yard TD called back for a rightful holding. Irving is the engine of the offense with the pass game completely broken for QB and WR reasons.

The Panthers are still poor stopping the run despite a DC change. They allowed Derrick Henry to go for 111 total yards and a score, and across the season Carolina is second-worst against the position. Irving hit them for 110-2 back in Week 7 (RB2) with RB18, RB41, RB5 and RB1 finishes in previous years. It's a great spot all around. The Bucs project for 23.25 points (13th) in a must-win game in the NFC South.

5. Javonte Williams (vs. PHI) - 88.5 proj. total yards

Williams is the RB8 on RB5 usage, riding the coattails of an amazing Dak Prescott season. He's easily leading the NFL in goal-line carries, and the Cowboys OL is one of the best. It's a great environment for fantasy points, that's for sure.

This week is no different. The Eagles traded away run-stopping DT Jordan Davis, placed All Pro LB Zack Baun on IR and won't have others on defense. It's a tanking defense that is now allowing 121 rushing YPG (12th most). Williams has already hit them for 155 total yards and a score this year back in Week 7, after finishing as the RB19 and RB3, respectively, against a better version of this defense in 2025. The Cowboys own a 28.0-point team total (fourth) on Thanksgiving as massive favorites.

6. James Cook III (vs. KC) - 98.5 proj. total yards

There is some serious weather expected here. Very cold rain with wind.

Cook sits as the RB9 in half PPR points per game on RB8 usage across his last four games. He leads the league with 23 carries per game in that span and has found the end zone on the ground in 4 straight contests. Nothing new here.

The matchup is an uphill one. Kansas City ranks 28th in half PPR points allowed to RBs, albeit with a fairly soft schedule so far. They did hold Bijan Robinson to RB13 a couple of weeks ago, for what it's worth. Offenses facing the Chiefs have attempted to run on them in neutral situations (league-low 46% neutral pass), and the Bills have the personnel to do that. Cook has had 125 and 134 total yards against the Chiefs in his most recent outings, respectively. Buffalo carries a 26.75-point implied total (fifth) as 3-point home favorites. I'm not adjusting too much for matchup.

7. Jonah Coleman (at PIT) - 100.5 proj. total yards

Coleman has completely dusted J.K. Dobbins, who had one snap in Week 11's blowout win. His vision is what stands out on tape. He knows when to cut back, find cheap yards and bounce it depending on the look. He's a pretty mediocre athlete, but Coleman will hit a ton of singles and doubles behind this quality OL and rushing scheme.

On top of that, Coleman is averaging 3.5 receptions per game this month with R.J. Harvey out of the way. He's been the RB2, RB9 and RB7 in usage most recently. Look at the names he's next to here:

The matchup is far more difficult this week, however, after beating up on the Panthers and Raiders. The Steelers are a full tier above those DLs, even if they are pretty banged up at the moment. They haven't allowed a 100-yard back in more than six weeks. Saquon Barkley (RB13) and Chase Brown (RB6) scored their way to nice games most recently, for what it's worth. Denver has a 20.25-point team total (21st).

8. Cam Skattebo (at IND) - 95.5 proj. total yards

Skattebo dominated touches again. He had 14 carries and seven targets in an upset win against the Jaguars, leading to 17.0 expected half PPR points. Despite fumbling in the second half, the Giants stuck with him and he ultimately closed the game out with a backflip. It's almost as simple as they don't want Devin Singletary on the field anymore, which means Skattebo should float to some nice RB1/2 weeks.

The bad news is his tape last week was pretty rough, as was J.J. McCarthy's. They won't face a loaded Jaguars defense every week, but Skattebo is at risk of being benched if he continues to put the ball on the dirt. His range of outcomes remains wide, but there is clear upside if he can keep it together.

This is a matchup to feel confident in with the Colts very clearly tanking. They've allowed 26.5 half PPR points to RB rooms over their past four games, on 152 rushing YPG. David Montgomery had 124 total yards, Ollie Gordon had 115, Javonte Williams had 124 and Bhayshul Tuten had 159. This is a blowup spot with the Giants projected for 24.0 points.

9. Bijan Robinson (at MIN) - 99.5 proj. total yards

I'm afraid a real reset is due. Bijan has been the RB11 on RB10 usage over the past month, sinking against the 49ers (RB19) and Bucs (RB28) with Michael Penix forgetting how to play QB. He's still handling a major workload and is too good not to have huge games down the stretch, but the Falcons did trade away their starting C and really struggling on defense, too.

This week, the Falcons are firmly in the "mid" quadrant. They're projected for just 17.5 points (28th) as touchdown dogs to the Vikings. Minnesota is obviously one of the best defenses, even if the Niners did whatever they wanted to last week. Penix's pressure panic could derail the entire offense if they aren't prepared. The plan will be to run the ball against them while they can, but hopefully it's not just a punt fest. The Vikings have recently faced a bunch of fantasy RB1s; Christian McCaffrey (RB15), James Cook (RB7), Jahmyr Gibbs (RB5) and Jonathan Taylor (RB8). Taking the average of that group would be an optimistic angle, as Atlanta is nowhere near as good as those offenses.

10. Chuba Hubbard (at TB) - 90.5 proj. total yards

Fantasy's MVP (Most Valuable Pick) without a doubt. Hubbard is the RB4 on RB4 usage this year, and the Panthers have rode him extra hard this past month (RB1 overall usage). He's the only stable part of their offense.

The Buccaneers represent the league's toughest positional test on paper, ranking 32nd in half PPR points allowed to RBs at 14.8 per game. Opposing backfields generate just 9.8 rushing points per game against Tampa Bay (29th), though Jahmyr Gibbs did post an RB3 finish against them in Week 11 in what I'd call a true bat fight. Hubbard was held to 60 scoreless yards in Week 7 and hasn't finished better than RB24 against them since Week 13 of 2023. Carolina heads on the road as 3-point road underdogs with an implied team total of 20.25 points (21st). Without question, this is a difficult spot.

We'll see if he's going to keep playing through this ankle/foot injury. Make sure Chris Rodriguez Jr. is picked up just in case. More later.

12. D'Andre Swift (at DET) - 66.5 proj. total yards

Swift had a rough game on tape and lost meaningful touches to others in the backfield. He bounced a zone run that should've been cut back, then proceeded to fumble. He didn't return after that rep in the fourth quarter. That said, the Kyle Monangai tape was lackluster, too. He's not 100% on his right toe injury and will have a short week with Thanksgiving here. The assumption is Swift starts, leads the backfield and doesn't have the RB1 role he had without Monangai. Since Week 5 of last year, Swift is the RB14 on RB16 usage. He was the RB16 on RB16 usage last week. View him as a boom-bust RB2.

The Lions defense is going to miss DE Aidan Hutchinson and LB Jack Campbell, though they still have some physical DEs and star DTs to worry about up front. Across the year, the Lions are fourth-best against fantasy RBs, but with the recent injuries and the fact that the Bears can stay in positive game scripts, this Lions defense seems more vulnerable than the spreadsheets would indicate. They have put a lid on Swift's fantasy value in recent matchups, for what it's worth, as Swift has mounted 48, 69, 53 and 74 yards, respectively, against them since 2024. A massive 29.0-point team total (third) is the most important mark, however. There is plenty of upside.

13. Kyren Williams (vs. GB) - 80.5 proj. total yards

Williams has had his two worst fantasy games of the year in back-to-back weeks. In Week 10 before the bye, Blake Corum outclassed him as a ball carrier, simply showcasing more short-area burst while offering the same high-level vision. The Rams also made sure to say Williams has a "sore toe", which, in a worst-case scenario, would be a turf toe. That's a terrible injury for a RB to try to play through. We're finally at the point that Corum has a chance to steal more carries from Williams, who is likely to remain the favorite in the pass game if the Rams are ever trailing. Even over this "bad" month stretch, Williams is the RB17 in expected half PPR points.

The Packers have been run on at times, allowing the sixth-most fantasy points to RBs. They're on a 6-game stretch of allowing a top-20 fantasy RB and have missed LB Edgerrin Cooper (Achilles). We'll see if Micah Parsons (ACL) is playing, as expected. While 3-point favorites, the Rams "only" project for 23.75 points (12th) this week. Williams is a suddenly volatile fantasy RB2.

14. Blake Corum (vs. GB) - 71.5 proj. total yards

To my eyes and the spreadsheet's charts, Corum is a better rusher than Kyren Williams, who is reportedly dealing with a "sore toe" coming out of the bye. It'd be a bit of a surprise if Corum was the early-down starter, but I'm open to it happening for the first time ever. Corum won't have the pass-game value that Williams has shown off, but he's still the RB12 on RB20 usage this past month with the offense losing Puka Nacua and Davante Adams. They really need the Corum explosive runs to keep the offense alive.

The Packers have been run on at times, allowing the sixth-most fantasy points to RBs. They're on a 6-game stretch of allowing a top-20 fantasy RB and have missed LB Edgerrin Cooper (Achilles). We'll see if Micah Parsons (ACL) is playing, as expected. While 3-point favorites, the Rams "only" project for 23.75 points (12th) this week. Corum can be viewed as a fantasy RB2 in the same tier as Williams.

15. Breece Hall (at MIA) - 78.5 proj. total yards

Hall played on 5-of-6 snaps inside the 10-yard line last week, so it's not as cut-and-dry as Braelon Allen is The Biggest, The Largest and will be subbed in for all red-zone work. Since returning from injury, Hall is the RB13 on RB15 usage. He had his best game last week in a win, while finishing as an RB2/FLEX in losses to the Chiefs and Bills. The Jets want to run the ball as much as they can, but the game script will need to cooperate to pull that off.

Enter the Dolphins. The Jets are favored, and Miami has allowed the 7th-most fantasy points to RBs this year. They've also recently placed three key DTs on injured reserve. James Cook just finished as the RB10 against them last week, and they've been on a 6-game streak of allowing at least one RB to finish as a top-24 back. It's a good spot of course.

16. Saquon Barkley (at DAL) - 76.5 proj. total yards

We'll see if Barkley is going to play after his second stinger of the season last week ...

Barkley hit the gas last week and still looks like one of the best backs on tape. That's the good news. The problem is he was still only the RB13 on RB21 usage, without a reception, in an offense that is absolutely stuck with bad QB play. He only played 64% of the snaps coming out of the bye, losing some pass-game work to Will Shipley. He'll need a big play and some infrequent goal-line scores to hold the line as a fantasy RB2. He's the RB19 on RB36 usage since returning. Consider that his low-end baseline.

The reason for hope here is the Cowboys defense. They were hilarious against the Titans last week and have allowed the most fantasy points to RBs (25.7 half PPR per game) throughout the year on the most rushing yards per game (152). It's somehow been worse over their past month (31.1 PG). Barkley had a couple of impressive reps when returning from injury against them in Week 7 (RB20 on RB32 usage) and is in a better spot now. Philadelphia enters the holiday matchup as 8.5-point road underdogs with an implied total of 19.5 points (23rd). He has to take over for the Eagles to pull off the upset.

17. Jonathan Taylor (vs. NYG) - 87.5 proj. total yards

There's a ton going on. The Colts releasing Daniel Jones for Anthony Richardson Sr. is a slight negative for Taylor, as ARich is more likely to score near the goal line and less likely to maintain long drives. Indianapolis also didn't have its starting OTs last week (and of course, it traded LG Quenton Nelson) and Richardson's play under pressure is concerning. That's something very much worth tracking. As a reminder, here are Taylor's historical splits based on how many points Indy scores:

The good news for Taylor's RB2 value is Seth McGowan (hamstring) is "going to miss some time." Over the past month, McGowan has stolen real work from Taylor before garbage time. That's included some red-zone scores, and I totally get why they were doing that, as McGowan was running well. They'll stop rotating Taylor out now. Over the past month, Taylor has been the RB52 (!!!) on RB16 usage. We're back to having hope for an RB15 on RB10 usage finish.

The matchup is daunting. The Giants suffocate games on both sides with a balanced offense and a hard-hitting defense. They upset the Jaguars last week, holding Bhayshul Tuten to just 53 yards. Ditto to the Commanders RBs in Week 10 and Saquon Barkley in Week 9. On the whole, New York is averaging just 16.6 half PPR points to RB rooms (27th).

18. Chase Brown (vs. NO) - 73.5 proj. total yards

That was a painful primetime game from Brown, who dropped multiple targets and took 6 carries for just 6 yards. He's the RB21 on RB15 usage this season, as one of the most inefficient players with the context of how great this Bengals offense is. I'm not sure if he's even better than Samaje Perine, who is one of the most underrated insurance options as we close out the fantasy season. This offense gives Brown multi-score upside as we saw in Week 10, but those were out of structure receiving touchdowns. Perine threatens at the goal line and now in the 2-minute drill. He's a volatile fantasy RB2, as he's been throughout the year. Brown is leading the position in receptions per game (5.2).

The Saints offer one of the friendliest matchups in football, ranking 2nd in half PPR points allowed to running backs at 25.6 points per game. New Orleans ranks 3rd in rushing points allowed (18.4 per game) and 8th in receiving points allowed to the position, having recently surrendered big games to Chuba Hubbard (RB4) and Jaylen Warren (RB17). Cincinnati features the league's highest neutral pass rate at 67% (1st) and enters as 10-point home favorites with an implied total of 30.75 points (2nd).

19. Tony Pollard (at JAX) - 79.5 proj. total yards

Pollard has been carrying the Titans offense, one that is playing at the league's fastest pace over the past month to better fit Cam Ward's chaotic skillset. More play volume is always welcomed for fantasy. Pollard has been the RB3 on RB7 usage over that stretch, handling 19.0 carries and 3.5 receptions per game. Tyjae Spears is on IR, so it's strictly Pollard until absolute garbage time. At any point, the Titans can completely disintegrate on offense without LG Peter Skoronski. He's best viewed as fantasy RB2 even if the results have been better than that of late.

The Jaguars have an awesome defense. Pollard broke a few tackles on a checkdown for a long score against them two games ago, finishing as the RB8 on RB12 usage. That's as good of an outcome as we can hope for. Since then, they've added two quality starters, including run-stopping DT Jordan Davis. Across the year, Jacksonville has allowed the 3rd-fewest fantasy points to RBs (15.1 half PPR per game). Pollard was the RB25 and RB47 against them last year, and they haven't allowed a 100-yard RB this season and only two have cleared 14 carries. Opposing backfields muster just 8.7 rushing fantasy points per game (30th) against the Jaguars, prompting offenses facing them to pass at a 59% rate in neutral scripts (second-highest). Tennessee travels as 14.5-point road underdogs with a slate-low implied team total of 16.5 points (32nd).

20. Ashton Jeanty (at CLE) - 75.5 proj. total yards

Jeanty had his best fantasy outing since Week 1, based on two explosive plays. He went untouched for 46 yards up the gut when the LB and S got washed up, and then he ran a wheel route against part-time OLB Quandarrius Robinson for a long score. It's awesome that this happened, but if we're being honest, he had a lot of daylight on each of them.

The good news is he still had 15.8 expected half PPR points (RB10) with five receptions while splitting carries fairly evenly with Mike Washington Jr. Jeanty has come out of the absolute bottom of his workload and looks healthy. We'll see if he can create more plays down the stretch as a boom-bust fantasy RB2. He's the RB17 on RB11 usage this year now.

21. Jaylen Warren (vs. DEN) - 78.5 proj. total yards

Last week was a nightmare game. There weren't many lanes on film review, and he couldn't bring in what could've been a 31-yard receiving TD on a ball that was a bit high on Aaron Rodgers. Beyond that, the Steelers were trailing, which meant Warren and Rico Dowdle combined for just five touches in the second half. Across the entire season, Warren is averaging 2.6 expected half PPR points fewer in games with Dowdle at this point, on 4.0 fewer carries and 1.2 fewer targets per game. Warren is back to being a firm fantasy RB2.

The matchup is one that comes with some advantages and some weaknesses. The Broncos have a strong DL, one that will put pressure on their backup LG. Denver also has very concerning LB play, while having standouts at all corner spots. The ball typically gets funneled to RBs against the Broncos, who are allowing the ninth-most fantasy points to the position on 126 rushing yards per game (10th). Ashton Jeanty popped up for 169 total yards last week, while Chuba Hubbard (96) and Emmett Johnson (143) hit them hard in the outings before that. The Steelers only project for 17.25 points (30th), so the lack of floor is alarming.

22. Quinshon Judkins (vs. LV) - 62.5 proj. total yards

Judkins is the RB23 on RB25 usage since pass-catching backup Dylan Sampson has returned to the lineup. Judkins' receiving role has been bad over the past month (1.8 receptions per game), so he's entirely touchdown dependent as a FLEX option. The good news is his workload is pretty stable, putting up between 10.7 and 12.6 expected half PPR points in five-straight games.

The Raiders rank 4th in half PPR points allowed to running backs, yielding 24.6 points per game. That vulnerability is almost entirely on the ground, where Las Vegas ranks 1st in rushing points allowed to backs (19.5 per game), compared to ranking 28th against receiving backs. That's okay for Judkins!!! Jonah Coleman (RB1) and Braelon Allen (RB7) each posted top-10 finishes against them recently. Cleveland sits as 4.5-point home favorites with an implied total of 23.0 points (15th).

23. Derrick Henry (at HOU) - 65.5 proj. total yards

The last two games with Jacoby Brissett have been totally fine. He's been the RB9 and RB12 on RB12 and RB11 usage. That's a good baseline for him moving forward, but we'll have to adjust for matchup as those games were close games against the Chargers and Panthers. He's catching more passes in an offense that will add 10+ dropbacks per game versus previous versions of this offense. That will offset the potential drop in goal line scores.

Houston ranks 19th in half PPR points allowed to running backs this season (18.5 per game), including a 20th-place ranking in rushing fantasy points surrendered to the position. The Texans recently held Jonathan Taylor to an RB33 finish in Week 11 after yielding an RB15 showing to Quinshon Judkins in Week 10. They won't have LB Azeez Al-Shair but have a strong DL still. Baltimore enters as 6.5-point road underdogs with an implied team total of 20 points (22nd).

24. Ollie Gordon (vs. NYJ) - 89.5 proj. total yards

Gordon is the RB16 on RB18 usage since being the Dolphins' starter, but the wind is coming out of the sail right now. He's down to the RB41 on RB27 usage this month with the offense losing Malik Willis. He's been the RB18 and RB24 over the past two weeks with Kyle McCord, and one of those was a Colts' game. It'll be hard for Gordon to see 16+ carries if the Dolphins are trailing early.

The Jets rank ninth in half PPR points allowed to running backs at 21.9 points per game. New York has surrendered heavy production on the ground, ranking 8th in rushing points allowed to backs, and gave up an RB1 finish to Kenneth Walker III and an RB7 finish to James Cook over their last four games with DT T'Vondre Sweat on IR. Gordon posted 16.8 half PPR points on 25 carries against them back in Week 7. Miami enters as 3.5-point home underdogs with an implied team total of 18.5 points (26th).

25. Jacory Croskey-Merritt (at ARI) - 61.5 proj. total yards

Croskey-Merritt returned from injury and was the Commanders' go-to option right away. They demoted Rachaad White to distant third duties, while Austin Ekeler spelled Bill. He ended up as the RB12 on RB17 usage in a close upset win. He handled the lone inside the five-yard line opportunity and offers the most juice on the ground. He can be phased out as a forgettable receiving option, but when the Commanders are rolling, he can get hot.

Well, Arizona up next! The Cardinals allow 20.8 half PPR points per game to running backs (12th-most), including 14.4 points per game specifically on the ground (13th). Arizona has surrendered big yardage and scoring on the ground recently, including Emmett Johnson (RB6) posting 105 rushing yards and a score in Week 11 and Blake Corum (RB9) gaining 100 yards and a touchdown in Week 10. Washington heads out as seven-point road favorites with a 23.75-point implied team total (13th).

26. Aaron Jones Sr. (vs. ATL) - 47.5 proj. total yards

Jones is only the RB45 on RB50 usage in 6 games with Jordan Mason, but Kirk Cousins is here to save the day. Over the past two weeks, Jones is the RB20 and RB19 on RB14 and RB35 usage. Cousins is willing to check the ball down, plus is going to sustain more drives than what Kyler Murray and Carson Wentz were able to do earlier on. Jones is still mixing in the occasion touchdown despite being the smaller back.

The Vikings are in a better spot this time around. They're touchdown favorites to the Falcons, who were smoked by Emmett Johnson last week (171 total yards). Bucky Irving (170) and Christian McCaffrey (100) were also top-10 backs against them this month. Across the entire season, Atlanta is allowing the 5th-most fantasy points to RBs. There is far more FLEX appeal this time around than in previous weeks. A 24.75-point team total (9th) backs that up.

27. David Montgomery (vs. BAL) - 54.5 proj. total yards

Montgomery is going back to a split backfield with Wood Marks expected to clear the concussion protocol, so we can largely through last week's predictable solid outing (RB13 on RB6 usage) against the tanking Colts. In Weeks 1-4 with Marks, Montgomery was the RB23 on RB30 usage. He lit up the Bills in Week 1, then was the RB47, RB36, and RB45 in the following games. I thought Marks was out-playing him on tape. After last week's clean outing, it'll be Monty as the starter and lead back most likely. Unfortunately, if it's somewhere around a 60/40 split, then it's harder to make the math work. The Texans' RB room is averaging 18.0 expected half PPR points, and their efficiency is almost always bad.

The good news is the Texans have found a grove on offense with C.J. Stroud throwing the ball well with a deep WR room. Houston owns a 26.5-point team total as big favorites to the struggling Ravens. Baltimore was gashed by Chuba Hubbard (RB2), Omarion Hampton (RB11), Chris Rodriguez (RB18) and James Cook (RB13) over the past month, leading to the 7th-most points over that span. Montgomery has fart into the end zone hope here. He's a solid FLEX.

28. Zach Charbonnet (at SF) - 71.5 proj. total yards

Charbonnet has been the RB39, RB22 and RB24 since returning from a torn ACL. He had the bye week to get his legs back under him, though that also plays into Post-Bye Rookie Bump Jadarian Price. They've split the workload in fairly predictable ways. Charbonnet is going to handle most of the goal-line and short-yardage carries, while Price is going to be designed a couple of targets and given some perimeter runs to take advantage of his better speed. The Seahawks are a balanced offense, so either one can have a random big game. For the most part, Charbonnet is not a big-play threat, so his role is far more touchdown-dependent.

The 49ers rank 25th in half PPR points allowed to opposing backfields at 16.8 per game, presenting a split matchup profile. San Francisco has been stout against pure ground work, sitting 31st against RB rushing (8.6 points per game allowed), but ranks sixth-most favorable against RB receiving at 8.0 half PPR points per game. Bijan Robinson (RB19) managed five receptions for 59 yards against them in Week 7, while Ashton Jeanty (RB43) was limited to 25 rushing yards on six carries in Week 9. Seattle enters as three-point road underdogs with an implied team total of 22.25 points (16th).

29. Rhamondre Stevenson (at LAC) - 65.5 proj. total yards

The Patriots are turning to Tommy DeVito this week, immediately being faced with a 17.0-point team total (30th). It's fair to say full adjustments are needed, even if TreVeyon Henderson (illness) is on IR and may not play again this year. Stevenson was phased out of the game in Germany before this bye, but hopefully he's fresher now. He'll split time with early-down option Corey Kiner. The matchup is just fine. The Chargers are middle of the back in all defensive metrics.

Unfortunately, we're in the Wild West for Stevenson's fantasy value. He's at the mercy of just how bad the QB play will be moving forward. We're probably at the point where the savior is Daniel Jones in a week or so. Never a good spot to be in.

30. Kaleb Johnson (at LAR) - 35.5 proj. total yards

Josh Jacobs (finger) will miss, leaving the Packers with Johnson and Marshawn Lloyd on the ground, with Chris Brooks in for pass protection. To me, Johnson is just way better than Lloyd as a rusher. The Packers seem to agree. He was the one to lead the backfield in carries in Week 10 without Jacobs, and there's no reason to believe he won't see 10-15 carries off the bye. Johnson is the RB26 on RB40 usage since Week 9 while facing the Patriots and Vikings.

The Packers will be challenged by a Rams' veteran-filled DL fresh off the bye. Los Angeles is the second-best defense against fantasy RBs this year, completely shutting out bad run games of late (ARI, WAS, LAC, LV, ARI). James Cook III was able to post 118 total yards against them in Week 5, at least. Johnson is a red-zone threat for a team projected for 20.75 points on Thanksgiving. He's a half-PPR FLEX.

31. Travis Etienne Jr. (at CIN) - 66.5 proj. total yards

He'll need a TD to pay off as a FLEX play. Last week, Kamara subbed in for a designed receiving touchdown near the goal line.
