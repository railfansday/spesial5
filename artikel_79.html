King Charles and Elton John pay tribute to Australian music legend Molly Meldrum

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

King Charles and Elton John have paid tribute to Australian music industry legend Molly Meldrum, who died aged 83 after a long illness.

Meldrum's family said he "passed away peacefully" at a hospital in Melbourne in the early hours of Monday morning.

The music promoter and broadcaster, born Ian Alexander Meldrum, helped define the nation's music tastes for decades, championing local artists like INXS and Kylie Minogue and global acts like Abba.

Known for his chaotic demeanour and trademark Stetson hat, he interviewed an array of stars, with the king on Monday calling him "a true giant of the music industry".

Born in Victoria in 1943, Meldrum displayed a love of music from an early age and went on to host the Australian Broadcasting Corporation's music television programme Countdown, and later a segment on variety show Hey Hey, It's Saturday.

Meldrum in 2014 became the first person who was not a recording artist to be inducted into the Australian Recording Industry Association (Aria) Hall of Fame.

Meldrum lived with an acquired brain injury after falling from a ladder in 2011.

"Affectionately dubbed 'the Guru', Molly was the heartbeat, the champion and the spirit of Australian music," a statement from Meldrum's family said.

Among his most famous interviews was a 1977 sit-down with King Charles, in which Meldrum could not calm his jitters.

He stumbled over his lines, declaring: "Your Royal Highness... Oh, oh, I'm sweating like a pig".

King Charles reflected on the infamous interview for Countdown's 40th anniversary special, saying: "There is an old showbusiness saying which warns never to work with animals or children. But nobody prepared me for Molly Meldrum".

On Monday, an aide to the King said he had been "deeply saddened" to hear of Meldrum's death.

"His Majesty's interview with Molly is a moment he and many others will never forget - he hopes that interview will once again raise a few smiles as Australians honour Molly's legacy as a true giant of the music industry."

Elton John - a friend and regular interviewee - paid tribute to him as "an Australian legend and an institution", while pop star Kylie Minogue said she would be turning up the music in his honour.

"As a kid, I watched Molly Meldrum on Countdown every single weekend, in awe of the show and the magic it brought to our little TV in the lounge room."

"He championed so many Aussie artists and welcomed international artists to Australia in his own wonderful and inimitable way. I'm so grateful for his unwavering love and support."

Australia's Arts Minister Tony Burke said the country had lost an icon: "One of his greatest legacies will be the generations of Australians who grew up with his unmistakable enthusiasm and love of music."
