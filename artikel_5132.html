EU Assembles Task Force To Counter Russia

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Home » Regulations » EU Assembles Task Force To Counter Russia

The EU announced two initiatives to bolster the bloc’s sanctions toolbox with enhanced global presence.

The European Union’s (EU) fresh crypto-focused task force is set to tackle top sanction evading challenges, mostly ascribed to Russia. Commissioner Maria Luís Albuquerque announced this to the public following the tenth meeting of the High-Level Expert Group on Union Restrictive Measures.

After the gathering in Brussels, this new EU regulatory arm aims to close gaps that allow Russia to sustain economic activity amid ongoing sanctions linked to its Ukraine invasion. The meeting brought together senior representatives from EU countries, the European Commission, and the External Action Service.

INTEL: EU sets up crypto task force to stop Russia dodging sanctions with digital assets

The examination of emerging financial threats revolves around crypto currency as a safe haven for tax or sanction evasion, keeping a strong commitment to Ukraine. Complementing the task force represents a broader dedicated sanctions office within the EU Commission. This improves coordination across member states.

The G7 is taking a more unified stance across policy, outreach & implementantion. It builds on 21 successive EU sanctions packages on Russia that have gradually targeted crypto-powered channels. Over the next 12 months, intelligence sharing and support from Ukraine and the UK will be used  to identify regular circumvention methods.

The latest sanction package, penned last Summer, freshly imposed transaction bans on 14 third country crypto platforms, enabling potential full bans on crypto services as well. By focusing on detection, enforcement consistency & international cooperation, the EU seeks to limit Russia’s ability to maintain economic resilience, still prevalent even after a flurry of sanction packages.

Meanwhile, Russia recently implemented a new digital-asset focused law, lifting transaction & investment bans for retail customers that pass a special knowledgeability test. For businesses, the landscape has shifted too: Russian currency exchanges are able to legally offer crypto services from November 22, 2026, abiding by the legal framework from the local bank authority.

With the geopolitical situation evolving, cross-border transactions powered by the blockchain is seen as a seamless way to lift sanctions & off-day barriers, but it also facilitates a gray area of liquidity for military-industrial circumvention, off-record energy exports & often shadow fleet operations. To mitigate the risks, the EU’s new task force launched new enforcement measures.
