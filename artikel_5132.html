Circle hands USDC reserves to EU regulators risking higher fees

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Circle’s EU-issued USDC moves under EBA supervision, potentially raising capital, liquidity and compliance requirements.

Nov. 12 marks the transfer of supervision of EU-issued USDC’s financial safeguards to the European Banking Authority, according to Circle’s quarterly filing.

Under the disclosed arrangements, the European regulator oversees reserves and redemption planning, while France’s financial regulator keeps governance responsibilities.

For Circle’s European issuer, the change could mean more capital or liquidity held in the EU and higher compliance costs. Circle says it is evaluating the impact, without putting a figure on those potential obligations in the disclosure.

The transfer follows the EBA’s Oct. 14 classification of EU-issued USDC as a significant e-money token under the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA). Circle describes USDC as the first e-money token to receive that designation.

The handover was planned since October, establishing the token’s classification. The November handover sets the effective date for the stated division of supervisory work.

The allocation applies to USDC issued in the EU by Circle Internet Financial Europe SAS, known as Circle France.

Circle says the EBA’s direct supervisory remit covers the issuer’s own funds (regulatory capital), reserve assets, and liquidity requirements. It also oversees recovery and redemption plans.

France’s Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) remains responsible for governance, qualifying holdings, fit-and-proper assessments, outsourcing, and conflicts of interest. Circle continues to face national oversight in those areas alongside the EBA’s financial-safeguard remit.

The filing also reports an EBA memorandum of understanding with the New York State Department of Financial Services to facilitate cooperation and information exchange on cross-border stablecoin activities.

The memorandum covers cooperation and information exchange, as well as the allocation of supervisory responsibilities for the EU issuer.

Circle says the significant-token classification may bring increased regulatory capital, reserve, liquidity and other prudential requirements under MiCA. These could require additional capital or liquidity in the EU, raise compliance costs or adversely affect its business and financial results.

The disclosure provides no estimate of possible additional capital, liquidity, or compliance spending, and identifies no quantified financial charge arising on Nov. 12. Whether additional commitments will be needed, and their potential size, remain unresolved in that assessment.

Supervision of redemption plans concerns the issuer’s safeguards, and the note announces no revised cash-out terms or new holder fees. The disclosure identifies the responsible supervisors but does not specify any change to holders’ redemption arrangements.
