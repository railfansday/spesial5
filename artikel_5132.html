Ripple now has a national trust bank charter, close to $900 million in yearly product revenue and big institutions as clients, yet XRP still trades near $0.90. Crypto commentator and analyst Kamilah Stevenson says the gap comes down to one thing: good news for Ripple the company does not always mean more people need to buy and hold XRP the token.

About 63 billion XRP are in circulation, not counting the coins Ripple keeps locked in escrow. At $10, those coins would be worth around $630 billion.

That would make XRP worth roughly half as much as all Bitcoin, which is valued near $1.2 trillion, and close to three times as much as Ether, at about $220 billion. Stevenson says a price like that would take huge numbers of people and institutions deciding they must own XRP and keep it. A run of good headlines would not be enough.

Most headlines cover the first two. The price depends on the third.

When XRP is used to move money across borders, it is often held for only a few seconds. One company buys it and sends it, and the receiver sells it almost right away. The same coins can carry billions of dollars a year without anyone holding much of them.

There is a catch that helps holders. As payment volumes grow, market makers must keep more XRP ready on both sides of each trade, so bigger flows lock up more coins.

Other sources of holding demand are also growing:

Around 31 billion XRP sit in Ripple’s escrow, and up to 1 billion can unlock each month. Ripple locks back up whatever it does not use, but traders know that supply is always there. As Ripple earns more from its products, it needs to sell less XRP to pay its bills.

Expectations also ran ahead of reality. Banks test new systems for years before moving real money through them. XRP’s past spikes came from hype and fresh money, not from banks needing to hold the token: it hit about $3.84 in 2017 and rose roughly sevenfold from November 2024.
