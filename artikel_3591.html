Bonfire of the Neocons: The MAGA Finger-Pointing Begins

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Good afternoon from Washington, where foie gras is now illegal and the local Stasi is going door-to-door to check that every citizen has downloaded Bluesky.

The town, one of the bluest in the country, is in a state of stunned ecstasy as it struggles to process the mind-bending midterm election results. Democrats won the House, the Senate, and a majority of governorships, penetrating deep into historically red strongholds like Texas, Kansas, Mississippi, and South Carolina. During a book party for Sophia Bush at The Jefferson hotel on Wednesday night, the actor nearly cried as she delivered a toast to the results—and to her new bestseller.

On the right, the finger-pointing has already begun. In this edition of Party Animals, you can read my dispatch from a night out with the DC Republicans in mourning and the Democrats celebrating in cocktail-fueled delirium. Plus, a MAGA civil war reignites over Iran, with Vice President JD Vance already wading into the factional fight that could determine the future of the party.

Also mentioned in this issue: Marc Thiessen, Curt Mills, Darline Graham, Ken Paxton, Greg Abbott, James Talarico, Annie Andrews, Mark Mitchell, Matt Walsh, and more.

Got tips? Hit the Party Animals hotline: aidan_mclaughlin@condenast.com. Or text me—my number is 917-817-8266. Complaints will be drowned in cocktail-fueled delirium. Praise will be run as a Democrat south of the Mason-Dixon Line.

The morning after the generational Republican midterm collapse that Party Animals is dubbing Blue Tuesday, JD Vance put on his political-analyst cap. “The reason we lost is the war,” he wrote in a lengthy X post. “The president himself said so last night.”

Never mind that Vance’s referenced war is one that his own administration started and is currently presiding over. Vance has often tried to cast himself as an outside observer of the Trump administration, its preeminent reply guy on social media and podcasts—a clever, if often ludicrous, pose for the vice president.

The Vance post was the highest-level opening salvo in a civil war that had been brewing for some time. It erupted into the open on Tuesday night as the results began to spell doom for Republicans and the blame started flying with velocity. The internecine battle is between the “America First” faction of the Trump movement and those who have supported the war in Iran, the latter labeled by detractors—with a sneer—as neocons.

The day before the blue tsunami, hopeful MAGA devotees attended a campaign rally in Ohio.

“The Iran War is the greatest political blunder in the last two decades at least,” wrote Matt Walsh in a post on X. “A catastrophically stupid decision. An unforgivable betrayal of America First principles.” As the dire results came down, even some on the right who had cheered on the war came to see, in the cold glow of their screens, the political devastation it had wrought. “Iran War is clearly catastrophically unpopular and has to end,” posted Will Chamberlain, who had spent the last few months loudly dismissing the war’s critics as subliterate simpletons.

On social media, MAGA luminaries shared a screenshot of an infamous op-ed written by Iran war booster Marc Thiessen, whose headline confidently claimed earlier this year: “There is no US downside to striking the Iranian regime.” Fox host Mark Levin lashed out at critics blasting his support for the war, insisting it had nothing to do with the results. Reality would seem to differ. One NBC News exit poll found that 74% of voters said Iran was a “crucial or major factor in how they voted.”

“It was the Iran war, stupid,” said Curt Mills, the executive director of The American Conservative, in an interview with Party Animals. Speaking about Thiessen and Levin, Mills was unsparing: “Professional liars. Professional liars. Not analysts, not honest people, and, honestly, not very invested in the future of the country.”

I asked Mills, who praised Vance’s statement, if it was not strange for the vice president to speak out against the administration while serving in it. “What option does he really have if he wants to keep his job, stop the war, and maybe run for president? I mean, it is strange. It is unprecedented in some ways,” he said. “Is it working? Maybe.”

In restaurants and barrooms across the capital, the ruling party’s apparatchiks watched and agonized as Democrats mounted a historic midterms rout.
