Lions’ Jared Goff downplays Bears’ role in false start woes, but Detroit O-Lineman isn’t as kind

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

As the Detroit Lions collapsed to the Chicago Bears on Thanksgiving Day on Thursday, losing 39-35, there is a conversation around the self-inflicted mistakes that the team went through that led to the defeat. While the Lions and Bears game will be remembered for Chicago scoring 29 points in the second half, there is also the topic of Detroit's false start penalties, which led to an interesting comment from quarterback Jared Goff.

The Lions committed four false start penalties, with Juice Scruggs charged with three of them and Christian Mahogany getting the other, as the latter said that players on Chicago were "emulating snap counts."

"No b****ing and complaining. We had self-inflicted wounds,' Mahogany said. 'The league can investigate."

Mahogany said that "the guards wear microphones, so that you can hear the QB on TV," further saying that "they'll be able to hear "clear as day" what was going on, according to Brad Galli.

"I don't think that should be overblown. I think it happened a couple times," Goff said, via Galli. "The officials talked to them, and if they were trying to simulate the snap count, or if they weren't, I don't know, they can probably say they aren't; it's impossible to officiate, and they'll never throw a flag on it. It happens. This isn't the first game it's happened. We got to be able to handle that a little bit better than we did."

The Lions' qualms received a response from the Bears, specifically star defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons, who said that "there's a lot of crying on that side of the ball when that happened," according to MLive.com.

"But as a defensive lineman, when your quarterback checks, I'm allowed to say he can check into a run. When Jared checked, and I said, 'It's a run; they're checking to a run,' it's not my fault your center jumped," Simmons said.

At any rate, Detroit let go of the rope after a 19-10 halftime lead and now is 8-3 on the season, putting them second in the NFC North.
