Space Heaters Cut Heating Bills With an Electric Furnace or Oil – Rarely With Gas

Check what heats your house before counting on a space heater to save money. With an electric furnace or oil, heating only the room you’re in while the rest of the house cools cuts the bill. With gas, the heater’s warmth costs far more than the furnace’s, so it rarely saves.

Every hour on high, a 1,500-watt heater costs 1.5 times your electric rate per kilowatt-hour, the unit your bill charges by. At the roughly 18 cents a kilowatt-hour that the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) expects electric-heated homes to pay this winter, that’s about 27 cents an hour.

Heating only the room you’re in, with the thermostat at 60, pays off when your heat costs close to the heater’s 27 cents. With an electric furnace or oil, it does. Buy one sized for the room, with its own thermostat, automatic shutoff when it tips over or overheats, and a testing lab’s safety mark, such as UL, ETL, or CSA. Keep it three feet from anything that burns, plug it straight into a wall outlet rather than an extension cord or power strip, and switch it off when you leave the room or go to bed.

With gas, it rarely pays off. Each degree you turn the thermostat down for eight hours saves about 1% of a heating bill, New York State’s energy agency estimates. On the $640 of gas EIA expects the average gas-heated home to buy this winter, all of it counted as heat, a 10-degree drop for those hours saves at most 42 cents a day, by our math. The heater, even held to half power by its thermostat, uses about $1.10 in those eight hours, more than twice that 42 cents.

In a gas-heated home, save with a 10-degree thermostat drop while you sleep instead.
