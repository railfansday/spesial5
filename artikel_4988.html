Inkling 2 Mini got 20 questions right but failed 4 out of 5 tests that required deeper thinking

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Inkling 2 Mini’s repeated queries on a hidden-pattern test spotlight the need for tighter limits as local AI agents reach Macs.

In a hosted test published by Plotly on Nov. 21, the AI model Inkling 2 Mini got all 20 routine data questions right.

On one question about a hidden pattern, it made 585 queries over about 48 minutes and repeated the same few queries dozens of times, but it still missed the pattern.

In Plotly’s benchmark, Inkling 2 Mini scored 36 of 43 overall and found one of five hidden patterns. Plotly found that on the hidden-pattern questions, the model often added up the biggest totals and stopped.

Plotly ran the model through its own analytics software, Plotly Studio Embedded, using a version hosted by Prime Intellect and accessed through OpenRouter. It reported one scored sample per model, combining results with reruns of 16 Inkling 2 Mini questions that encountered a provider outage.

The card lists a context window of up to 1 million tokens, which measures how much information the model can handle at once. How much of that window is usable on a particular Mac depends on the memory needed to process the request.

In the Nov. 22 release notes for oMLX 0.8.3, the Mac software’s maintainers reported about 20% faster prompt processing and 10% to 13% faster generation than in 0.8.2 for a reduced-precision build on an M5 Max.

The update also lets oMLX refuse prompts on a 32 GB Mac when it estimates they would exceed the memory budget.

In a Nov. 23 follow-up, an oMLX issue reporter said prompts of about 43,000 and 57,000 tokens were completed on one 32 GB M5 MacBook Pro using the updated software and a reduced-precision build. Reported memory use peaked at 23 GB and 24 GB, respectively.

Once a request fits, a separate limit can stop an agent from spending more turns on the same few queries.

Thinking Machines Lab says it saw the model repeatedly check its work without making progress in internal tests. The lab recommends caps on generated tokens and on the number of turns an agent can take.
