Nets searching for answers as early defensive collapses fuel losing streak

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

MINNEAPOLIS — The Nets will drag the NBA's longest losing skid into Milwaukee on Wednesday, a five-game slump, and pretty much all five losses have looked the same.

Embarrass themselves defensively in the first half. Give themselves no chance to win in the second. Scratch their heads looking for answers in the postgame.

Head coach Jordi Fernandez argues a call during the Nets' loss to the Magic in an NBA Cup game on Nov. 20, 2026 at Barclays Center. Getty Images

"That's happened already too many times, where we allow teams [to get on a roll early]. It's the best players in the world. If you allow one to go in and then everybody gets loose and makes shots, then it's a long night," Jordi Fernández said.

When asked what the biggest problem with their defense was, Julius Randle couldn't even pinpoint one.

"We've got a lot. We were better earlier in the year and now we've got slippage in multiple areas. So we've gotta figure out where it is. But defense just starts with competitive nature first and then go from there," Randle said. "It's disappointing. But all you can do is focus on where you're at and figure out how to get better. Figure out what's the problem, how we can consistently compete at a high level."

The problem is, in this skid the Nets have barely competed at any level.

They've allowed more than 70 points in the first half of four of the five losses, tied with Charlotte for the league's longest slump. They've shown a concerning glass jaw, getting hit early and not hitting back.

"Defensively, we just can't give up 140 points. We let [Minnesota] catch their rhythm early, and that's what happens when you give good teams rhythm early on in the game," Mikel Brown Jr. told The Post. "We've got to cut that water off early, take away those wide-open shots."

In this five-game malaise, the Nets have allowed an average of 72.2 points and a 147.3 defensive rating in their first halves. They've conceded 55.9 percent shooting and 52.3 percent from deep and a minus-15.0 net rating.

Every single one of those stats is not only dead last in the league, but the worst by a margin.

That kind of porous defense in the first half makes the second half all but moot.

"Yes, it's always been for us to get off to a good start," Brown told The Post. "I really just feel like just disrupting them early on. Anytime we disrupt the team early on in the game, we come out and we fight hard. And sometimes we pull out the win, and sometimes we don't.

"But it's really just the early [part] in the game. You just can't let good players get great shots early on in the games. You give those looks to those great players, they're gonna start flowing, and then next thing you know, they shoot 50 percent, 55 percent from the field as a team. It's a snowball effect that we just can't have."
