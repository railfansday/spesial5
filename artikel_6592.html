Where to Stay in Antwerp, Belgium’s Design-Filled City

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Antwerp isn’t a loud city. You don’t come here to be awed by ancient ruins or to tour record-breaking skyscrapers. There are no leafy stoops with picturesque brownstones enshrined on TV. Antwerp’s charm unfolds quietly alongside its cobblestoned streets and narrow brick townhouses; I’d argue it’s a place most stumble upon when traveling through Europe, rather than specifically seek out. Those who do make it a point to visit, however, will easily be won over by its unassuming beauty and artistic milieu—after all, there’s a reason why it’s long attracted a certain kind of creative. (Painter Peter Paul Rubens called it home in the 16th century, fashion designers Raf Simons and Pieter Mulier do so in the 21st.)

Winter is an especially lovely time to visit—even when it drizzles (trust me, it will), Antwerp feels delightfully festive: Christmas markets take over the Old Town square, with glowing lights strung up all over the city. The ongoing Antwerp Six exhibit at the MoMu makes a trip this holiday season particularly timely; the group’s first major retrospective closes on January 17, 2027.

The best places to stay in Antwerp offer a chance to adjust to the city’s quiet rhythm—whether that means finding calm in a former convent, or slowing down in one of the many well-appointed Airbnbs that dot the center. Like most major European towns, you can confidently skip the car rental; much, if not most, of Antwerp, can be comfortably discovered on foot and by bike.

An Augustinian convent transformed into a design lover’s dream, the work of famed Belgian architect Vincent Van Duysen makes for a serene refuge after a long journey. Modernist touches and an earthy color palette underscore the property’s monastic qualities, and the rooms are thoughtful but efficiently put together. (The artfully rumpled bed linens are one highlight; the exposed timber beams in the larger suites, another.)

Couples wishing to explore the grounds can unwind with a treatment in the spa—which includes an intimate swimming pond—then dine in the on-site restaurant. (If weather allows, try to book a table in the outdoor terrace.) While the hotel is located in one of Antwerp’s quieter neighborhoods, het Groen Kwartier, you’re still within walking distance from most of the action, including two local favorites: Bar Vert for a low-key lunch, and Funk (an essential personal bookmark) for breakfast pastries.

It doesn’t get more central than this. Antwerp’s Botanic Sanctuary, the sole five-star property in town, gets its name from its location: It’s a former monastery nestled besides the city’s Botanical Gardens. Rooms are awash in calming neutrals with multiple large suites available for families—some of which overlook the adjoining 15th-century chapel. Food-motivated travelers are spoilt for choice: Start with drinks at Henry’s Bar, then take your pick of one of three Michelin-starred restaurants for dinner. (Ed note: The property’s marquee wellness facilities are currently temporarily closed due to a recent fire.)

Hotels in European Old Towns aren’t exactly known for their modern charm—and yet, Hotel Julien is brimming with exactly that. Set across two former historic buildings, it precedes the opening of its sister property August, but remains a worthwhile destination for its location alone. Rooms are cozy but pared back, punctuated by touches of red, and you’ll appreciate all the sightseeing there is to do, starting with the rooftop terrace that overlooks the Cathedral of Our Lady—an exalted edifice of Gothic architecture. It’s the ideal cozy home base for a trip spent exploring: “Antwerp is a very historic city, so it’s worth spending a day wandering through its streets, admiring the old architecture, and stepping into the many independent boutiques and antique shops,” adds de Geyter.

Originally a mansion built in 1890, Boulevard Leopold has found new life as a bed and breakfast since 2006. It’s very much no-frills—the aforementioned breakfast, a daily spread that includes croissants and fresh orange juice and is served each morning in the orangerie, is pretty much the only standard “amenity”—but that doesn’t mean it’s sparse. On the contrary, this jewel box of a space is full of charming little touches everywhere you look, the effect of which is a collected feel akin to that of a slightly eccentric aunt’s home.

To wit: crystal chandeliers that play against antler mounts, taxidermy birds that mingle with ornate, gilded candlesticks, a large bamboo fan moonlighting as a headboard that sits above a spartan cot. There are just five bedrooms to book, one of which is a gold-painted suite that takes up the entirety of the first floor. If you’re looking for a quiet, design-forward spot, this B&B is it.

Art is the main motivator at Hotel Pilar, a stylish boutique stay in Antwerp’s hip Het Zuid area, where each of its 21 rooms is designed around unique works. Oil paintings and glossy photographs contrast exposed brick and raw concrete in some; colorful prints juxtapose stainless steel and marbled tiles in others. Lively cafés and bars surround the property, which also happens to overlook the Royal Museum of Fine Arts (KMSKA). For the intrepid gallery hopper, the Museum of Contemporary Art (M HKA) is similarly close by; so is the Ann Demeulemeester flagship if you’re down for a side quest. An art-filled salon adjoins the hotel’s ground-floor restaurant—you’ll want to bookend the day here on one of its velvet couches, no matter where you dined that night.

Set in a 19th-century building in the heart of Antwerp’s fashion district, this light-filled apartment is a more than comfortable base from which to explore the city. It sleeps up to four, with a large dining room and sweet little kitchen that encourages culinary creativity. Original features, like a marble fireplace and antique wood floors, mix seamlessly with modern amenities; you will feel instantly at home.

Stained glass windows, zellige tiling, gilded mirrors… there are more than enough special decorative touches in this home to keep the eye busy. This stay is ideal for a family: It sleeps 10, with five distinctly designed bedrooms (the flamingo pink one, complete with a crystal chandelier and brocade drapes, feels especially grand). It’s well-located, too, with a green park nearby and several main tramlines on your cobblestoned doorstep.
