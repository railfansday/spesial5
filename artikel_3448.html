Aaron Pierre on ‘Lanterns,’ the DC Shake-Up, and Landing a Role in Ryan Gosling’s ‘Star Wars’ Movie

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The topic du jour on this week’s episode of IndieWire’s “Screen Talk” podcast: “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth.” The movie screened for press ahead of opening on IMAX next week, with divisive reviews for David Fincher’s technician-like take on Quentin Tarantino’s colorfully discursive screenplay that brings back the character of stuntman Cliff Booth (Brad Pitt). Co-hosts Anne Thompson and Ryan Lattanzio debate the film and catch up on the latest developments in the Oscar race — including predictions for the upcoming coastal critics’ awards.

Afterward, Anne is joined by rising British star Aaron Pierre for a chat about HBO’s popular DC series “Lanterns.” When we spoke on Zoom, Pierre had just learned the news of the changes at DC, as his mentors James Gunn and Peter Safran were leaving, replaced by Marvel veteran Jeremy Latchan. He says the right things about how lovely it was to work with them, but he had to be nervous about the fate of his movie still in the editing room, the “Superman” sequel “Man of Tomorrow,” in which he reprises the role of John Stewart, the heir to the Green Lantern throne. Pierre relates how he landed the role of John Stewart in “Lanterns,” Chris Mundy, Damon Lindelof, and Tom King’s hit HBO series. First, he went through the exploratory “yes, and” audition process. Once he arrived on set, he had to figure out the creative challenge of distinguishing who John Stewart was in the first four episodes from who he was in the last three. The character changes from episode to episode. “The magic was on the page,” said Pierre. “It was our job to bring it to life, bring it into a different place. There were nuances discovered in the moment.”

Pierre also stars in Rachel Morrison’s second feature, “Love of Your Life.” He loved the script and, after he met the director/cinematographer for coffee, decided, “I need to do this role. She has a distinct way of directing. She is enchanted by micro-movements.”

He asked, “Where do I want to be creatively?”

His breakout role was as Michael Cassio on Shakespeare’s Globe stage, with Mark Rylance playing Iago and Andre Holland playing Othello. Before he could step onto the vast stage in front of 1,500 people, he had to conquer his fear. He asked if he could watch Mark Rylance’s rehearsal process and learned that even the greats have to figure it out as they go.

And Pierre explains how he ended up co-starring with Ryan Gosling in Shawn Levy’s “Starfighter,” the latest “Star Wars” movie.
