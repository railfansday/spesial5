The Rings Of Power Season 3's Best Storyline Redeems A Controversial Peter Jackson Idea

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Spoilers are never late (nor are they early; they arrive precisely when they mean to), so don't read ahead without first watching Season 3, Episodes 5 and 6 of "The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power."

Over halfway into its run, it's clear "The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power" Season 3 is spectacular whenever Sauron (Charlie Vickers) is on-screen. Indeed, this season is for the baddies, with the Dark Lord's menacing Nazgnagôl making their grand entrance and a thrilling civil war brewing in Númenor.

But it's not all doom and gloom, as Season 3 also introduces the brightest light of hope in Middle-earth: the elven golden retriver Celeborn (Jamie Campbell Bower). From the moment he shows up, it feels like he's been a part of the show from the start, fitting in perfectly as a strong, funny, flirty elf who has excellent chemistry with Galadriel (Morfydd Clark). Celeborn is the ultimate wife guy, and his relationship with Galadriel is the best romance we've seen in a Middle-earth adventure to date.

Nevertheless, there's trouble in Valinor for these two, as Celeborn has competition for Galadriel's heart. No, not Robert Aramayo's Elrond (despite them sharing a kiss in Season 2 to distract the orcs holding Galadriel captive), but Sauron, the Dark Lord on his dark throne in the Land of Mordor where the Shadows lie.

That's right: "The Rings of Power" Season 3 has finally turned "The Lord of the Rings" into a romantasy story, placing a love triangle at the heart of the saga of the One Ring. And while J.R.R. Tolkien purists may scoff at this, the show's love triangle is so good that it redeems the controversial romances from Peter Jackson's otherwise beloved "Lord of the Rings" films.

Peter Jackson's "Lord of the Rings" films are the most widely celebrated fantasy movies of all time and changed the genre forever. As such, it's easy nowadays to forget that Jackson's trilogy stirred up its fair share of controversy upon its initial release. After all, as faithful as they were to J.R.R. Tolkien's original books overall, those films also made some big alterations. That includes erasing the Scouring of The Shire from "The Return of the King" and removing characters like Tom Bombadil and Glorfindel, in addition to introducing a prominent love triangle.

In Jackson's movies, to jog your memory, a respectable amount of time is spent on Éowyn (Miranda Otto) falling in love with Aragorn (Viggo Mortensen), with even King Théoden (Bernard Hill) buying into their match and congratulating Éowyn on finding a good and honest man. Meanwhile, Aragorn keeps thinking about Arwen (Liv Tyler), who is about to leave Middle-earth for Valinor. In fact, the original plan was for Arwen to be at Helm's Deep alongside Éowyn and Aragorn in "The Two Towers," which presumably would've fueled their love triangle even further.

Things get even worse in Jackson's "The Hobbit" films. There, he and co-writers Philippa Boyens and Fran Walsh introduce Tauriel (Evangeline Lilly), an elf who develops romantic feelings for the dwarf Kili (Aidan Turner). In that case, it's Legolas (Orlando Bloom) who rounds out the love triangle, as he's still racist towards dwarves at the time.

Neither of these Middle-earth love triangles really work (especially not the latter), so it's hard not to be skeptical of "The Rings of Power" introducing yet another one. And yet, this ends up being the most compelling aspect of Season 3.

There are two things that make the love triangle in "The Rings of Power" work. The first is that the show is very clearly in on the joke, with Sauron often behaving like a teenager when it comes to his borderline hilarious fixation on Galadriel. That he even goes to the trouble of disguising himself as Galadriel and kissing Celeborn to get information out of him shows Sauron is not beyond being a little bi-curious.

Then there's the fact that the series has been building to this since Season 1, starting with Galadriel slowly falling for Sauron in disguise as Halbrand and being seduced by his promises of power. By the time Season 3 rolls along, she still has complicated feelings for him, as evidenced by her dreams and visions of Sauron waking up in bed next to her. Thus, when Celeborn re-enters her life, and Galadriel sees both men in front of her, there's a tiny bit of hesitation on her part.

And yet, Season 3 never suggests that Galadriel would actually leave her husband for the Dark Lord. Instead, it juxtaposes Sauron's jealousy with Celeborn's confidence. Specifically, in Episodes 5 and 6, Sauron gets visibly flustered when Celeborn talks about how much Galadriel loves him. In the end, it works just as well as the incredible romances in J.R.R. Tolkien's source material, proving that Middle-earth is not devoid of effective romantic storytelling.

Heck, the scene in Episode 6where Celeborn talks about making the elven smith that Sauron has been looking for tell him the secrets of the rings, just so he can hear about every minute the smith spent with Galadriel, is arguably the most romantic TV scene of 2026. Stream Season 3 on Prime Video, and see for yourself.
