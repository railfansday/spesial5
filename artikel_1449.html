Scott McTominay reveals hospital dash and heart shock treatment days before Scotland's World Cup opener

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

By CALUM CROWE, SCOTTISH DEPUTY CHIEF SPORTS WRITER

Published: 12:25 EST, 10 November 2026 | Updated: 03:20 EST, 11 November 2026

Scott McTominay has revealed he needed a procedure to reset the beat of his heart just two days before Scotland’s World Cup campaign kicked off back in June.

Heading into the opening game against Haiti in Boston, the explanation given from the Scottish Football Association was that McTominay had been carrying a sickness bug.

But, after the squad had flown from their training base in Charlotte, McTominay had actually been whisked away to hospital straight from the airport via a police escort.

The 29-year-old had shock treatment on his heart before then going on to play all three games as Steve Clarke’s side were knocked out at the group stage.

The Napoli midfielder subsequently underwent corrective surgery back at the start of the season and missed Scotland’s opening four fixtures in the Nations League.

Now back in the squad ahead of the upcoming double-header with North Macedonia and Switzerland, McTominay has opened up for the first time on his health issues.

Scott McTominay in action during Scotland's 1-0 World Cup win over Haiti

Asked about what happened, he replied: ‘The sickness bug? You’re not going to say I have something to do with my heart two days before the first game of the World Cup. It’s not an ideal scenario being in the hospital two days before — well, technically 36 hours before the first game of the World Cup.

‘But you have to take the cards you’re dealt on the chin. It’s part of the game. There’s nothing I could do about it and the shock went well, I was back in normal rhythm then 24 hours later you’re playing a World Cup game.

‘The World Cup, it was difficult for me, to be honest. I had to go and get the shock on my heart. That was two days before the Haiti game.

‘You prepare so well, I was in such good condition and something like that happens. You’re thinking: “How has this happened to me?” But then you have to get on with it. That’s the cards you are dealt in football.

‘Sometimes things don’t go your way and you have to continue to do your best. It’s nowhere near an excuse but it’s something you have to look back on and say: “Well, it’s part of the process”.

McTominay was given full reassurance from the doctors and medical staff that the procedure had gone well, insisting it was never in his thoughts to pull out of the squad.

Asked whether it was playing on his mind across the three games against Haiti, Morocco and Brazil, he said: ‘No. I put it to bed. I tried my best to put it to bed anyway. Whenever you go under anaesthetic and you have to get put to sleep, it decreases the athletic performance a little bit and sleep and stuff like that.

‘You have to get back round to doing it again. It was far from ideal but those are the cards that you’re dealt, there’s nothing you can do about it.’

Some fans had questioned McTominay’s form throughout the World Cup, oblivious to the fact that he was effectively playing through an ongoing heart issue.

Asked whether the criticism bothered him at the time, he continued: ‘It’s tough. It’s never easy, things like that, but you can’t use things like that as an excuse because that’s just not in my nature.

‘I take whole responsibility for a lot of the things that we didn’t create or didn’t score the goals in the World Cup.

‘I can put my face out there and say that that was nowhere near good enough. But that’s part of football. You have to get away and think about it and reflect and come back a better player.

‘I didn’t know that’s what people were saying. I probably had an idea.

‘I’ve been criticised a million times before in my career before but I don’t let it get under my skin or worry too much about it. You know yourself as a professional footballer if you’re not quite where you should be.

‘For me, I had half an idea but you just try to block it out because if you think about negative things you might speak it into existence.’
