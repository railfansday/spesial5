Germany news: Merz joins German-Ukrainian business forum

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Chancellor Merz is taking part in a forum to boost business ties with Ukraine. Meanwhile, youth protests are expected to take to the streets again. DW has the latest.

Here's a roundup of the biggest headlines in Germany on Friday, November 20:

Organizers of the Gen Z Takes Charge protest group spoke out about why they took to the streets again.

Co-organizer Akram Selo called on Germany's leadership to meet the young people's demands.

"Talks won't make us stop demonstrating," he told RBB-Inforadio in comments aired on Friday.

"Essentially, our demands should be addressed, and we should be given assurances. That is what needs to happen, not empty promises," Selo added.

"So far, we feel that we aren't being taken seriously at all," he said. "The way we're being treated suggests that the aim is to sideline our demands and discriminate against us by focusing on other trivial matters."

Another group, the alliance "School strike against conscription" held its own protest to demand an immediate halt to surveys about potential availability for the German Armed Forces, or Bundeswehr, that are currently non-binding and only obligatory for teenage boys.

Merz made the comment at a ‌business ​summit in Berlin that was organized by the German Chamber of Industry and Commerce.

"It is now essential to press ahead with these reforms swiftly and resolutely —particularly, and I say this openly, with regard to the rule of law and corruption," Merz told attendees.

"What we invest in Ukraine now, we will receive back many times over as dividends in the form of stability, export opportunities, joint projects, and growth," the German chancellor said.

In 2025, the two countries traded goods worth around €12 billion ($13 billion).

"Freedom and security are now unleashing forces of progress and resilience on a scale never seen before. This is precisely the stance we are now taking against the autocratic and imperialist actors of this world. We are the world of freedom," Merz added.

At home, Merz has faced increasing pressure to justify Germany's ongoing support for Ukraine. In recent regional elections, political parties opposing sending military aid have performed well.

Merz argued that Russia's war against Ukraine is an attack on democratic values everywhere.

Germany's electricity exports rose in the first three quarters of this year, according to official data released on Friday.

According to the energy association BDEW, Germany has been a net importer of electricity since 2023.

The neighbouring countries Austria, Denmark and the Netherlands, were the most important buyers of German electricity in the first nine months of 2026.

Together, they bought about half of all German electricity exports.

The volume of German electricity exported rose by 25.5% compared with the same period last year, to 48.8 terawatt-hours, while its value increased by 51.3% to €4.3 billion ($4.7 billion).    Electricity imports into Germany fell by 13.3% in volume, but at 49.2 terawatt-hours they remained slightly above exports.

While imported electricity cost an average of €116 per megawatt-hour, 39% more than a year earlier, exported electricity from Germany averaged only €87 per megawatt-hour, some 20.6% more than a year earlier.

With a high share of renewable energy sources, wind and solar, Germany tends to offer electricity when supply is high and prices are low, but in times of shortage it has to pay higher prices for imported electricity.

Finance Ministry officials said they are launching a dialogue process, dubbed "Germany 2035," to develop proposals for the future of the country's economy.

Officials want to study how Germany can secure growth and prosperity and what that could look like in terms of fiscal policy.

Finance Minister Lars ​Klingbeil will begin the dialogue process on December ​2, when he welcomes economists from around the world to Berlin.

Additionally, think tanks, research organizations, universities and civil society groups have been asked to host workshops, which would take place in the first half of 2027.

Among 12 economic topics that the ministry seeks to explore are tax and fiscal policy, investment ⁠and ​domestic demand, industrial policy, energy ​and climate transition, foreign trade and sovereignty, and housing ​and local infrastructure.

The dialogue will culminate in a white paper, to be published in 2028, that can provide recommendations for Germany's economic future up to 2035.

A group called "Gen Z Takes Charge" (Gen Z regelt) has called for thousands of young people to demonstrate in many German cities.

The gathering comes almost three weeks after the first major youth protests hit Germany, inspired by the protests that took place in France in the last weeks.

Those protests, which took place on October 31, saw tens of thousands take to the streets across Germany, with crowds remaining largely peaceful, although rioting broke out in several cities, including Frankfurt, later in the evening.

Among the demands of the "Gen Z Takes Charge" group are increased funding for schools, addiction support and psychotherapy, affordable living, opposition to conscription and consistent action on climate change.

Another group, the Federal Pupils' Conference, has also organized its own demonstration at the Brandenburg Gate in Berlin, with police saying that some 5,000 people have already signed up.

The pupils' conference advocates for improvements in education policy in Germany, including school renovation, school social work, relief for teachers and the inclusion of climate action in curricula.

The demonstrations are set to begin in the morning and run until late afternoon.

President Frank-Walter Steinmeier welcomed the Emir of Qatar Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani in Berlin with military honors.

Steinmeier will also host a state banquet for the emir and his delegation in the evening. Al Thani is also expected to meet with Chancellor Friedrich Merz during the day.

The visit comes in the midst of tensions in the Middle East, with the war in Iran likely to be a critical topic in the talks between the two governments. Qatar is currently one of the mediators in the deadlocked conflict between the US and Iran.

Economic and bilateral relations are also expected to take center stage.

On Saturday, the emir will visit Germany's most populous state of North Rhine-Westphalia, where he will be greeted by its state premier Hendrik Wüst at the military section of Cologne/Bonn Konrad Adenauer Airport.

During the visit, Al Thani and North Rhine-Wesphalia leaders will hold an economic conference in the small city of Königswinter, near Bonn, where the Emir will also visit Drachenburg Castle.

Following a political summit on Ukraine in Berlin yesterday, the German Chamber of Industry and Commerce (DIHK) said it would host a German-Ukrainian business forum.

Chancellor Friedrich Merz and German Economy Minister Katherina Reiche are expected to be in attendance, along with Ukrainian Prime Minister Sergii Koretskyi, among others.

Christian Bruch, deputy chair of the Eastern Committee of the German Economy and chief executive of Siemens Energy, said German companies are committed to invest in Ukraine, despite Russia's ongoing war and were open to expand their business partnerships.

Bruch said bilateral trade was expected to rise to around €13 billion ($14.1 billion) in 2026.

German flagship airline Lufthansa plans to cut 1,000 jobs at its airport hubs in Frankfurt and Munich, according to a company letter seen by German news agency DPA and news outlets.

The letter was reportedly signed by Lufthansa Airlines CEO Jens Ritter and four other managers.

The timeframe of the cuts is unclear. It's also unknown whether the cuts were apart of a previously announced downsizing.

In September 2025, Lufthansa said it would slash 4000 administrative positions due to the implementation of AI and digitalization.

Lufthansa's parent company Lufthansa Group had 67,100 employees in Germany by the end of last year. In the letter seen by news agencies, Lufthansa said financial struggles were the reason behind the planned cuts.

"Lufthansa is currently not competitive in terms of its cost structure, efficiency and productivity," the letter said, while pointing out that the company operated at a €180 million ($197 million) loss in the first nine months of this year.

In addition, Lufthansa Airlines is now in its third straight year of being unprofitable.

In regards to this year, Lufthansa has faced rising fuel costs spurred by the US-Israeli war with Iran, further complicating its financial outlook.

Leaked documents raise new questions about Deutsche Bank's dealings in Russia up to Moscow's invasion of Ukraine, adding to scrutiny over the bank's risk controls and past controversies.
