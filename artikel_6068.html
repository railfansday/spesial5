Norse’s efforts to diversify beyond low-cost, long-haul flying ultimately proved insufficient to keep its planes in the air. The focus now shifts to what, if anything, can be salvaged from the business.

Norse Atlantic Airways has canceled all flights and discontinued ticket sales after its Norwegian operating subsidiary said it is filing for bankruptcy.

The collapse comes a day after the low-cost, long-haul carrier suspended flights and two days after it described its financial situation as “critical.”

In a stock exchange filing on Friday, Norse confirmed that the board of its Norwegian operating subsidiary, Norse Atlantic Airways AS, had concluded there was no longer a basis for continued operations. The board resolved to file a bankruptcy petition with a local court, which will appoint a trustee to oversee the process.

The filing does not extend to the listed parent company, Norse Atlantic ASA, or its UK operating subsidiary. Norse said efforts are continuing to find a solution that would allow the remaining parts of the business to continue operating.

Norse Atlantic Airways has canceled all flights and stopped selling tickets after the board of its Norwegian operating subsidiary, Norse Atlantic Airways AS, resolved to file for bankruptcy. The filing does not extend to the listed parent, Norse Atlantic ASA, or the UK operating subsidiary, but flights operated by both the Norwegian and British airlines have been canceled and customer service has shut down across phone, email, chat, and social media. Norse said it had been unable to secure sufficient funding for its short-term liquidity needs despite a strategic review launched in July, takeover talks with investors, and a meeting with holders of its $52 million senior secured bonds. It also disclosed lease termination notices covering all 12 of its Boeing 787 Dreamliners, which it disputed. The fallout reaches beyond direct bookings: Norse had been shifting aircraft into ACMI work for other carriers under its "Airline on Demand" strategy, and Pakistan International Airlines canceled two UK flights before moving its own Boeing 777s onto the route, while P&O Cruises said it was exploring alternatives for its UK–Caribbean fly-cruise program. The collapse follows Primera Air, Wow Air, Norwegian's long-haul exit, and Play, extending the record of failures in low-cost, long-haul flying.
