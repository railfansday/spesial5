Scandic’s Dalata Deal Just Got Its First Report Card

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Scandic paid €500 million for a business that fills more rooms and earns more per room than its own operation. The first combined numbers back the deal. The test now is whether it can integrate slowly enough to protect the occupancy that made Dalata worth buying.

Dalata’s hotels fill 80% of their rooms. Scandic’s fill 65%. That gap is a big part of why Scandic paid €500 million (about $555 million) for it, and its third-quarter update on Tuesday, the first since the deal closed, showed what it adds up to: a 24% lift to pro forma earnings per share.

Scandic had run Dalata’s 57 hotels in Ireland and the UK under a management agreement for a year before completing the purchase eight days ago. On a pro forma basis, which treats the two companies as if they had been combined for the full 12 months to September 30, the group generated SEK 30.4 billion (about $3.05 billion) in revenue and SEK 3.35 billion (about $336 million) in adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization (EBITDA), an 11% margin.

Pro forma earnings per share, adjusted for new financing costs, came to SEK 6.65 (about $0.67), against SEK 5.35 (about $0.54) for Scandic on its own.

Scandic's first results since closing its €500 million ($555 million) purchase of Dalata show the Irish operator lifting pro forma earnings per share 24%, to SEK 6.65 (about $0.67) from SEK 5.35 (about $0.54) for Scandic alone. On a pro forma basis for the 12 months to September 30, the combined group generated SEK 30.4 billion (about $3.05 billion) in revenue and SEK 3.35 billion (about $336 million) in adjusted EBITDA, an 11% margin. The gap between the two businesses is wide: Dalata's trailing occupancy was 80.2% against Scandic's 64.7%, and its RevPAR was SEK 1,316 ($132) against SEK 844 ($85). Dalata also carries lower rental costs at 25.8% of revenue versus 29.8%, helped by revenue-based leases with Pandox, plus a younger portfolio that pulls combined maintenance capex to around 3% of net sales. Scandic has taken SEK 75 million (about $7.5 million) out of overlapping central administration and senior management, but CEO Jens Mathiesen said full synergies may take two to three years because moving too fast risks the guest experience behind Dalata's occupancy. Separately, Scandic's October partnership with Hilton — starting with a Hilton hotel at Copenhagen Airport in 2029 — gives it access to Hilton Honors' 260 million members at commission rates Mathiesen said are "far, far below" what Scandic pays online travel agencies.
