For something people talk about, think about, and Google pretty much constantly, sex has a surprisingly narrow reputation. Say the word, and chances are your brain immediately jumps to penetration. Which, sure, can be great. But treating intercourse as the default definition of sex leaves out a whole lot of ways people experience pleasure.

Because here's the thing: Sex isn't just one specific act. It can involve mouths, hands, toys, technology, or absolutely zero penetration. And depending on who you ask, what counts as sex might look completely different.

“Sex is a mental, physical, spiritual, and emotional erotic connection shared between those involved,” explains sexologist Shamyra Howard, author of Use Your Mouth. “It's not just about genitals and mouths.”

For one person, sex might include any kind of sensual touch, while someone else might define it as an activity involving penetration. Neither is inherently more valid. As psychotherapist Kristie Overstreet, PhD, puts it, “there isn't one that is ‘better’ or a ‘more real’ type of sex than the other.”

Ultimately, what matters is that everyone involved is on the same page about boundaries, expectations, and consent.
