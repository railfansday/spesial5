Call Of Duty Maker Activision Files Patent To Make AI NPCs Trash Talk

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Call of Duty lobbies are synonymous with the worst recesses of the internet, a toxic minefield of abuse and harassment that's better left on mute. So, naturally, Activision has filed a patent that would train AI-powered NPCs to talk just like players do.

As reported by Dexerto, the patent is called "Methods and Systems for Applying Machine Learning to Generate Gameplay Audio," and describes using the audio from voice chat to make in-game NPCs sound more realistic. It wouldn't copy your voice exactly, but what you say and how you react.

The patent doesn't explicitly mention Call of Duty, but as Activision's leading franchise, it's a safe bet that's where it would end up.

It would do so around 10 seconds before and after a flagged game event, i.e. kills, deaths, and losses, shaping how NPCs behave in the same scenarios. So, if players screamed every time they were trounced by a camper (as they often do), that data would be funneled into an AI model to make the most annoying Terminator.

The patent doesn't just mention in-game voice chat, however, but Discord and TeamSpeak too, meaning how you mouth off to your friends could be utilized in training AI models by matching timestamps across separate applications. I'm not sure that I want to hear Woods say he banged my mom, but if this patent were to go ahead, that's a disturbing possibility.

The Zen is actively being sold at retailers.

Abuse, slurs, bigotry, and the worst habits of anonymous gamers are a hallmark of COD lobbies, and that's why it's best to stay quiet and auto-mute everyone, if not disable voice chat outright. Training NPCs on that is a recipe for disaster, but the patent does note that it would include filters to stop offensive language.

Then again, AI filters have never been particularly effective. Just look at all the awful things people made Fortnite's AI Darth Vader say.

Of course, just because Activision patented this technology doesn't mean it will be implemented in-game.

Not only does the patent fail to consider the ethical quandaries of AI-generated dialogue and how it squeezes actors out of the creative process entirely — something that SAG-Aftra has repeatedly taken aim at over the years — but it makes no mention of asking for permission to use voice chat audio or the implications of using private conversations from third-party apps.
