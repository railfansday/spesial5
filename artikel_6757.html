The Fall Essentials You Need According to 4 of the Most Stylish Guys We Know

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Fall has the singular ability to turn even the most Spartan man into a bit of a style savant. It’s like a wolf awakens the second that temperatures start to drop. Suddenly you see an Instagram post from the junior analyst below you; he must fancy himself a budding A$AP Rocky, because he’s rocking a statement scarf. Your brother-in-law is wearing a turtleneck. Your buddy is in white jeans. Every day, you zone out in dumb stakeholder meetings and fantasize about a new jacket or that pair of buttery-soft leather loafers you saw on a dude at the grocery store.

It’s because fall invites more—more layers, more textures. It can be freeing. No more worrying if that T-shirt will become a body scan of where and how you sweat; no more worrying if those pants will suffocate you. But ... it can be too much. How should you layer? How can you get funky for the holiday party without looking like an asshole? What brands are even worth buying?

We answer all those questions elsewhere in this magazine, but today we want you to take a breath. Keep it simple. How? By following some good examples.

A lot of the best-dressed guys right now are trimming down their wardrobes in favor of focusing on the basics. “This fall I’ve tried to trim the fat and make do with a much smaller wardrobe,” says actor, content creator, and friend of the magazine Albert Muzquiz—better known online by his handle @edgyalbert. “I only have a few shirts, a few pants, and a couple jackets—and I feel like I’m dressing better than ever.”

So instead of lobbing a bunch of meaningless trends, or -cores, at you, we decided to send you to some of the best-dressed men of the moment. You’ve heard enough from us; here’s what they’re feeling this fall.

Jake Mueser, founder of the eponymous bespoke suiting brand J. Mueser, has a handful of fall essentials. That cohort includes a cream turtleneck, which “you can throw on with anything,” as well as a classic tweed or herringbone blazer—same idea. They’re easy but plenty damn sexy.

Mueser also extols the virtues of a classic flannel suit—perhaps like the one he makes. “It works with everything from a denim shirt to a tie,” he says. “I like to break it up, wearing the jacket with vintage light-wash Wrangler cowboy cuts.” (A move we wholeheartedly endorse.)

There are a few reasons Drew Thomas can’t stop recommending merino sweaters—the thin, almost jersey sort—to his more than 500,000 TikTok followers. “You can get so much wear out of one,” he says. “You can layer it in the fall, wear it on its own on a slightly warmer day, or tie it around your shoulders. It works in the spring too, and even on a summer night when it gets a little cooler.”

Another seasonally versatile piece Thomas will stand ten toes down for is cream pants. In fact, Thomas likes to wear his all through fall. He keeps a few pairs on constant rotation. “I feel like the cream breaks up those darker fall colors and lets them stand out more,” he says.

When it comes to his next fall investment, Thomas is taking cues from the British sartorial invasion (“It never really went away, but it definitely feels like it’s having a moment again”) and looking to Barbour. “Something timeless and practical that I can wear for years and that only gets better with age,” he says. “I love the idea of owning something that develops character over time and could eventually be passed down to someone else. Those are the kinds of pieces I feel are worth investing in.”

“A down vest is my favorite fall garment,” Esquire contributor Christopher Fenimore told us. “It’s an easy outerwear piece to layer stuff under. I run hot, and I like having parts of my body exposed to the elements.”

We second that, but more predictably he highlights the entire category of shirting. Jackets and layers get a lot of shine, but this is the layer that, no matter how much temperatures fluctuate, will be on the skin. It’s a great time to get a few good oxford-cloth button-downs, polos, or beefier tees.

This time of year is also a great time to get clinical. “I assess things I have that might need updates, in terms of fit and silhouette or different fabrications depending on how I’d wear them,” he says. That can be rebuying your preferred undershirt or socks, or it could be a good time to jump on that thinner, more versatile merino sweater wave.

“Fall is the best time to break in a new pair of denim,” Muzquiz says. Wear it hard enough now and “it’ll be soft enough and possibly breathable enough for wear during the spring and parts of the summer.” No better time to get something esoteric.

When it comes to fall shoes, Muzquiz is into a classic six-inch lace-up boot. “There are so many bullshit brands out there that are trying to fool you with budget options,” he says. “Just get a pair of Red Wing Heritage if you’re starting out or Viberg if you really wanna flex.”

Muzquiz also finds himself taking part in the “buttery, delicate loafer movement” that’s been picking up steam as of late. The Yardee Moccasin loafers from Vinny’s are his pick of the bunch.
