ESSENCE Festival of Culture Moving On From New Orleans After Three Decades: ‘Memories Were Made — And So Was History’

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The beloved event first took place in the Big Easy in 1995 with a lineup that included Aaliyah, Aretha Franklin, Mary J. Blige, Luther Vandross and more.

After three decades of bringing R&B, funk, hip-hop, soul, blues and zydeco to generations of fans, the ESSENCE Festival of Culture announced on Tuesday (Nov. 24) that it is moving on from New Orleans. In an open letter celebrating 31 years in the Big Easy, Essence thanked the city it has called home since launching in 1995. Explore Billboard See latest videos, charts and news

“For more than three decades, you’ve been more than the home of the ESSENCE Festival of Culture. Through every year and every chapter of our annual event, you’ve been part of our family: a destination and community instrumental to our journey, and a culture that has informed and inspired us with every gorgeous sound, unforgettable flavor, and vibrant color,” read the letter. “Your hospitality has welcomed us. Your traditions have taught us. Every summer, your spirit lifted us,” it continued. “From the sounds of Frankie Beverly & Maze echoing through the Caesars Superdome and the generations who came together in the Ernest N. Morial Convention Center, to the laughter filling the streets, the family-owned restaurants that fed us, and the warm drawl of a ‘bayyy-by,’ every July, you gave our celebration a rhythm that could only belong to New Orleans.”

The farewell noted that the annual gathering had grown into more than just a weekend of music, morphing over the years into a “reunion, a tradition, and a celebration that could only have been shaped by a city like yours. It was a homecoming … It was also a chance to connect with the community: the local artists who shared their gifts, the small business owners who opened their doors, the hospitality workers who made us feel at home, and the culture bearers, community leaders, and everyday New Orleanians who shared their city, their traditions and their hearts with us. We are forever grateful for your partnership. Thank you for helping us tell our stories, spark conversations, empower Black creators from all over the country at the We Love Us Market, and fill the Superdome with joyful spectacle year after year.”

ESSENCE Festival launched in 1995 as a celebration of the 25th anniversary of its namesake magazine with the event’s patented mix of old-school soul, blues, R&B and pop featuring Aaliyah, Aretha Franklin, B.B. King, Gladys Knight, Luther Vandross and Mary J. Blige, as well as appearances by speakers including Dr. Cornel West, Jesse Jackson and Johnny Cochran.

WDSU posted a statement from festival organizers the Sundial Media Group explaining that the decision to pull up stakes on the event that began as a concert and spread into the “largest celebration of Black culture in the world” featuring film, book, food and wine events and free community programming did not come easily. It noted that in 2025, the festival generated more than $300 million in economic impact for New Orleans and Louisiana, but that after several months seeking a multi-year commitment and a reported lack of engagement from city officials, talks appear to have broken down.

“The city’s RFQ (request for quote) this summer signaled it never intended to offer ESSENCE a multiyear agreement. To be clear, talks have not stalled over vendor payments. As shared, we are resolving all outstanding payments to New Orleans and Louisiana vendors for the 2026 Festival, and every vendor will be paid and reconciled,” read the statement from Sundial. “The largest Black festival of its kind has to be where it can grow, where it is valued, and where it serves the people who make it what it is. We need a partner that is committed to that growth and to the people it serves. ESSENCE has always evolved, and we are evolving in the best interest of our community. That is what Black women deserve.”

The station reported that New Orleans tourism officials are meeting with major festival producers as they look to launch a new festival after negotiations with Sundial Media over ESSENCE broke down over unresolved payment issues. In addition, the City of New Orleans issued a statement promising the upcoming announcement of a new festival.

“On December 9, the City of New Orleans and our tourism leaders will announce a new, world-class festival celebrating Black culture over the Fourth of July weekend,” read the city’s announcement. “We are partnering with some of the nation’s top Black entertainment producers who will bring major national talent to the Superdome while also showcasing our incredibly talented local artists and musicians.”

The letter from Essence noted that the 2027 event will take place elsewhere, but at press time organizers had not announced which city will host it next. “New Orleans, you helped make us who we are. And no matter where our journey takes us next, you’ll always be part of our story and in our hearts,” the farewell concluded.

Over the years, the festival has hosted everyone from LL Cool J to Usher, Lauryn Hill, D’Angelo, Destiny’s Child, Jill Scott, Al Green, Erykah Badu, Femi Kuti, New Edition, Jamie Foxx, Boyz II Men, Gary Clark Jr., Lizzo, Chance the Rapper, Bruno Mars and many more.

This year’s ESSENCE Festival featured sets from Cardi B, Kehlani, Latto, Patti LaBelle, Brandy and Monica, Leon Thomas, Babyface, Public Enemy, Doug E. Fresh, Big Boi and Big Freedia, among others.
