South Korea orders massive crypto wallet lockdown following the Binance hack

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

The FSC also requested stronger transfer monitoring, response staffing and reserves, while exchange implementation and any new custody threshold remain unspecified.

South Korea's Financial Services Commission has asked domestic crypto exchanges to keep a larger share of customer assets offline and strengthen their response to suspicious activity after the Binance hack.

The FSC urged operators to increase the proportion held in cold wallets, continuously monitor deposits and withdrawals, secure additional response staff, promptly report and share abnormal access attempts, and build additional reserves.

Together, the requests address how exchanges hold customer crypto, monitor transfers, respond to suspicious access, and maintain financial protection against incidents.

The regulator's Nov. 3 clarification said it immediately requested the measures at a meeting held at 10 a.m. on Nov. 2. Participants included the Financial Supervisory Service, Financial Security Institute, a crypto industry association, and major virtual-asset businesses. The FSC dates the Binance hack that prompted the review to Oct. 30.

The November meeting also examined controls over access to computer systems, approval and signing arrangements for wallet withdrawals, and administrator privileges. Those checks extend the review beyond the share of assets held offline to the systems and permissions used to move them.

South Korea's four requests for safer crypto custody

The FSC's Nov. 2 requests, reaffirmed Nov. 3, build on a custody framework adopted in 2024.

The four requests cover custody, transfers, incident response and financial buffers.

The releases do not report exchange-by-exchange implementation. The 80% ratio is the 2024 standard, not a newly announced threshold.

The FSC's July 2024 supervision-regulation notice set the custody share at 80% of customer crypto's defined economic value. The notice accompanied the legal framework taking effect on July 19, 2024.

The ratio uses a defined valuation method based on quantities and average won conversion values over the preceding year. The calculation multiplies each type's quantity by its preceding-year average won conversion value, then adds the amounts.

That gives the custody ratio a valuation basis across the different crypto assets held for customers, and also establishes standards for insurance, mutual-aid coverage, or reserves to meet liabilities arising from incidents such as hacking and computer failures.

On Oct. 28, regulators began on-site inspections after Bithumb reported signs of an employee-information leak. The Nov. 2 meeting concerns the subsequent response to Binance and the safeguards sought across domestic operators.
