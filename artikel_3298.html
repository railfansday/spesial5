The best smart scales on the market from Withings to Wyze

When you purchase through links on our site, we may earn an affiliate commission. Here’s how it works.

Your weight isn't the most important metric to track on the best smart scales if you're after a comprehensive look at your overall health and fitness. These bathroom scales do a lot more than that, tracking your body composition, heart rate and (depending on the model) even your heart health.

But the other reason you'd invest in a set of the best smart scales is that you want to make it quick and convenient to track your progress. These modern bathroom scales sync data via Wi-Fi or Bluetooth to your phone, where you can track trends over time and see how your activity influences your progress.

The Withings Body Smart is the one that'll suit most people. It's a great blend of features, ease of use and health metrics, all without breaking the bank. The French company specializes in health-focused devices, and the scale comes with athlete, pregnancy, eyes-closed and baby modes.

Withings has made smart scales with visceral fat index, body composition, and even hydration level metrics before, but never for around $100. That's why I recommend the Withings Body Smart as the best smart scale for most people, because it has all the brand's useful health metrics but at a more affordable price.

And unlike some budget scales, there are multiple modes on the Body Smart, including athlete (which adjusts the body fat and muscle composition algorithm), pregnancy (disables the electrical signals used to analyze body metrics), eyes-closed (doesn't display your weight) and baby (hold them in your arms to track your newborn's weight).

The data syncs via Wi-Fi to the Withings app. If you have any of the brand's other devices, you'll have a profile that combines all your data, but even if you just use the scale, you'll be able to see how your progress and metrics change over time. Each area expands into a quick-glance graph, and you can drill down to the numbers if you want.

Because Withings has always focused on the health side of things more than fitness, it's a great option if you want to share data with your doctor at your next check up. You can export specific areas or all your health data into easily digestible documents you can take with you or email ahead of time.

Over the past few years, Withings has introduced an annual subscription for some analysis. You don't need to pay for the data your scales capture, but if you want more in-depth insights, it's behind a $99 per year paywall. But this service isn't essential, so you can make the investment in the scales and get everything you need.

Wyze has a history of launching budget-friendly smart scales, but the Scale X is a huge improvement over the older scales, as it also has the option to track your muscle mass percentage. Of course, you could always figure this number out on your own by dividing muscle mass by body weight, but this does it for you.

It's compatible with Fitbit, whereas the previous Wyze Scale S wasn't. All of your in-depth body data is displayed clearly within the app, with a body weight trend graph and dedicated sections for each metric.

Tapping on a section will show your results, along with a slider bar and a definition of the specific measurement. It's an easy-to-use scale, with the option to store eight different profiles. There are also luggage, pet, and baby modes, which we appreciated during testing (especially when packing for a long-haul flight).

The downside with this budget smart scale is the app, which doesn't really give you an awful lot of information to support the wealth of data. While it's great that you can see your muscle mass, it doesn't give you any context, like whether it's in a good range. This is nitpicking, though — all in all, it's a fantastic, affordable scale.

The Withings BodyScan 2 is an upgraded version of the BodyScan. The original model was already one of the most detailed and comprehensive scales you could buy, but while the BodyScan 2 adds a few extra health features (like blood oxygen tracking), the main change here is improved accuracy.

For your money, you get body fat, muscle, basal metabolic rate, and water mass and info on 60 other biomarkers. This applies to all eight possible users, making it an ideal choice for households, small fitness teams or workout groups. Since you could buy six of the Wyze Scale X for the same price, it is a big investment, so I'd say it's only worth it if you really need these athlete-level insights.

The scale is also able to identify signs of potential hypertension (high blood pressure). It's not a medical device and can't replace a doctor's advice, but it's a useful prompt to talk to a medical professional. And the Withings app makes it easy to export some or all data to share with your doctor.

To gather some of this data, there's a retractable handle that you need to grab hold of when standing on the scales. We found it pretty easy to get used to, even if it takes a bit longer than a more typical weigh-in. Since the data syncs via Wi-Fi, you don't need your phone nearby either, and we found it was quick to appear on the Withings app.

If you're spending several hundred dollars, it follows you want the most accurate data possible, so the updated model may be worth it for you. But its launch price was several hundred dollars more than the original BodyScan. It's a close call between the two, but if you want the most reliable insights, this is the model you need.

The smart scales in the guide above aren't the only ones we've tested, as we're regularly trying out connected bathroom scales so that we can recommend the models that are actually worth your money. But if you're after more options, these are the smart scales that didn't quite make the cut.

Garmin has become one of the most popular fitness tracking brands in the past few years, taking over from Fitbit. Like that Google-owned platform, Garmin operates an ecosystem where data syncs to your Garmin account to create a holistic picture of your fitness and well-being. Own one of the best Garmin watches? This scale is for you.

This budget scale from the fitness tracking brand is designed to work well with Fitbit (now Google's) health ecosystem, so I'd only recommend this model if you already use a Fitbit or Pixel Watch. Otherwise, there are better models for this price that can do more.

The Withings BodyScan is the first generation of this comprehensive smart scale. It's almost as packed with features as the BodyScan 2, and a few hundred dollars less, but it's not as accurate as the newer model. If you're interested in this level of analysis, you probably also want the best insights, so I'd suggest the newer edition. But this older model is a great entry point to this kind of full-body health scan.

We reviewed the Renpho Smart Body Fat Scale back in 2021, and it felt like a good compromise between price and features. It's incredibly cheap — that's the main selling point — but you lose out on reliability and convenience, with inconsistent Bluetooth syncing and a lack of user profiles. If you need the cheapest, this is it, but if your budget can stretch, I'd still recommend the Wyze Scale X instead.

For our review of the best smart scales, we take a number of factors into consideration. First, we check that the scale offers an accurate reading using a traditional digital scale, the highly rated EatSmart digital bathroom scale. If the readings are within a fraction of a pound of each other, we can recommend them. The readings shouldn't take more than a few seconds, either.

Aside from price, we also explore the variety of metrics a scale offers, how many unique users the scale supports, the scale’s connectivity options and the user-friendliness of the scale’s companion app. We like to see whether the app provides background information on your body’s statistics, too. Plus, compatibility with third-party health programs plays a role in our testing.

The last thing anyone needs is more anxiety, so we look for the best smart scales to make stepping on the platform less intimidating. A smart scale’s metrics should show you that your body is more than your weight.

A smart scale isn’t a device you want to replace often, so you want it to be reliable, well-rounded, and to fill all your fitness tracking needs. In other words, don’t let price be your ultimate decision-maker.

It’s worth it to stretch your budget for one of the best smart scales that will last you at least a few years and support the exact data you’re interested in following. Review all the metrics a given smart scale offers before buying.

That said, if you’re in temporary living quarters or are working towards a short-term fitness goal, a budget smart scale, like the Wyze Scale, is an excellent choice. It won’t last a lifetime but will satisfy your immediate needs.

Another thing to pay attention to is whether a smart scale has some kind of ‘safe mode’ for people with pacemakers or people who are pregnant. Safe mode, or ‘weigh only’ mode, disables bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA), which uses a weak electric current to determine body fat percentage and could pose a safety threat to certain users.

Fitbit users could even connect one of the best Fitbits to the brand's smart scale, helping you keep on top of your daily health and fitness stats.

Smart scales can do so much more than just report back to you on your current weight. Moreover, weight can fluctuate throughout the day based on various factors like water retention. A smart scale can provide feedback and detailed metrics to health apps on your phone and send data wirelessly to your devices.

Obsessing over a number on the scale is not a true indication of health, but if your goal is to lose weight, a smart scale could help you understand your body. Many linked apps can also display the data in helpful and motivating ways, which could help you hit your goals.

Not that interested in the detailed numbers? Stick to a simple set of scales and save your dollars.

During testing, the budget-friendly smart scales were reasonably accurate. Of course, there's always some variation between models, but the relative accuracy (measurements on a single device) is the most important factor to consider, as this will help you keep track of changes.

Generally, the main reason that the scales are cheap is that they lack the features of the more premium models. So, some don't come with Wi-Fi and only sync via Bluetooth, or don't have multi-user support.

The difference between a smart scale and a regular bathroom scale is that these smart models sync your data to another device. They need to do this with a wireless connection, either Bluetooth or Wi-Fi.

Cheaper models generally only use Bluetooth, so you'll need your phone with you, connected to the scale, and an app to receive the data. These usually only work with a single account as well, so they are less ideal for multi-person households.

If you have the budget for it, a Wi-Fi scale is the more versatile and convenient option. You need to set up the network connection initially, but then you can leave your phone elsewhere, step onto the scale, and the data automatically syncs.

Because this process is automated, you need to set up individual users on the scale. So while this is useful for regular household measurements, it means that it's a bit less suited to casual weigh-ins from visitors, as the data syncs to the owner's account.

You may have noticed that in this guide we've included information on what 'modes' the scales listed have, but you may be wondering what specific modes are right for you.

This really depends on what you're planning on using the scale for, whether you plan on only weighing yourself, what training you do, and if you are or are planning on becoming pregnant.

For example, our number-one smart scale, Withings Body Smart, includes athlete, pregnancy, eyes-closed, and baby modes. This means that it has modes tailored for those who are in intensive training, those who are pregnant, those who would prefer not to see their weight or body composition data, and to log and track their baby's weight. The majority of these modes are more useful for those with specific goals in mind, and so will be useful to them.

In comparison, the Wyze Scale X has more practical, day-to-day modes, including luggage, pet, and baby. Each helps users measure and/or track the weight of things (e.g., their dog or cat) without impacting their recorded weights. This means that you can easily weigh things without your scale believing you have spontaneously gained several pounds, so you don't have to manually delete these recordings.

Overall, it comes down to what you want out of your scale and what you want to use it for.

While all of the smart scales on this list do provide body composition and body fat tracking, you should take this tracking with a grain of salt. While they can give an idea of your body composition, they may not be as accurate as things like DEXA scans.

Overall, it's a good idea not to just rely on your smart scale to track your body composition. Instead, look at things like fitness goals and achievements and how your body physically looks and feels.

If you do want a body fat reading that's as accurate as possible, you'll need to book a professional scan.

This depends on what smart scale you use. For example, while the Withings Body Smart and the Withings BodyScan 2 have paywalled features that you'll have to pay $99 per year to access, other scales like the Wyze Scale X and the Renpho Smart Body Fat Scale do not.

But most core features, like weight and body composition tracking, are free to use after you've purchased a scale. Most of the paywalled features analyze the data to produce actionable insights; useful but not essential.
