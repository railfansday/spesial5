David Fincher Officially Reveals Why Netflix Cancelled ‘Mindhunter’

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

For all the horrific things that were discussed over the course of two seasons of Mindhunter, some of the best scenes in the entire series involved two FBI agents sitting in a room talking to somebody. Having already made Seven and Zodiac, David Fincher gave us a different kind of serial killer story with his Netflix series: we already knew who the killers were and what they'd done, but we didn't necessarily understandwhy they'd done it. We're now seven years removed from the last time we saw Holden Ford and Bill Tench, and it turns out that one of the things that made Mindhunter so compelling was also something Netflix wanted to change.

Speaking to Josh Horowitz on the Happy Sad Confused podcast while promoting The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth, Fincher revealed that Netflix had asked him about making Mindhunter a little more like CSI, with the suggestion that we might actually see Ford and Tench chasing people with their guns drawn. To understand why Netflix wanted Fincher to go down that route, though, you need to appreciate just how much money the streaming service was spending on the show and how few people were actually watching it. As Fincher explained:

“That's when Netflix said, 'Look, we've got to be honest with you. There are two million people who love the show. And the show costs $100 million a season.'”

Netflix suggested making the series a little more like CSI, including having the agents chase suspects with their guns drawn. Of course, some of the most compelling scenes in Mindhunter involved little more than Holden Ford sitting across a table from Edmund Kemper, and the prospect of making an altogether different type of crime thriller wasn't particularly appealing to Fincher: “No, I can't. I won't. And I don't think you really want me to.”

Holt McCallany has previously discussed the possibility of revisiting Mindhunter in the form of three movies, although any such return would depend on Fincher being satisfied with the scripts. Fincher also explained that he was working 90-hour weeks on the series, and while there were almost two years between the first and second seasons, he anticipated a similarly lengthy turnaround for a third.

Created by Joe Penhall, Mindhunter features Jonathan Groff (Hamilton) as Holden Ford, McCallany (The Iron Claw) as Bill Tench, Anna Torv (Fringe) as Wendy Carr, Hannah Gross (Joker) as Debbie Mitford, Cotter Smith (X2: X-Men United) as Robert Shepard, and Cameron Britton (The Umbrella Academy) as Edmund Kemper.
