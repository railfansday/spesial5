Judge Declines To Dismiss Donald Trump’s Defamation Claim Against BBC

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

A federal judge on Tuesday declined to dismiss Donald Trump‘s defamation claim against the BBC over edits made to a documentary about January 6th that aired in advance of the 2024 election.

U.S. District Judge Jeffrey Kuntz rejected the BBC’s efforts to toss the case, ruling that Trump had sufficiently pleaded actual malice.

In his ruling, Kuntz wrote that the BBC confirmed “it had edited a video to make it appear as though President Trump made a statement he never made. And it acknowledged it chose to release the video in a documentary only a week before the 2024 Presidential Election, at the time it would have the most impact. He added, “Whether President Trump can prove these claims and establish he sustained damages at summary judgment or trial is left for another day.” Watch on Deadline

The judge kept alive Trump’s defamation claim but dismissed his claim of violation of Florida’s Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act. Trump sued the BBC for $10 billion.

In the documentary, Trump: A Second Chance?, a clip is shown from his January 6 speech, in which he says, “We’re going to walk down to the Capitol … and I’ll be there with you. And we fight. We fight like hell.” In fact, the remark was an edit of different portions of the speech.

Trump claims that the documentary — which was produced by October Films — was streamed on iPlayer by “hundreds of individuals” using a VPN in the U.S., despite it being geoblocked in America. His lawyers have accused the BBC of a “brazen” attempt to interfere in the 2024 U.S. election, which the corporation denies.

In the fallout from edited speech, BBC director general Tim Davie and Deborah Turness, who led the news division, stepped down. The BBC apologized, calling it an “error of judgment,” but they did not offer to pay any damages.

The judge wrote of the editing, “Trump properly alleges that BBC spliced and edited together a twelve-second segment that resulted in reputational harm based off ‘deceptive and defamatory distortion.'”

“On its face, a twelve-second clip seems insignificant, but in the context of the complaint, these twelve seconds are the result of splicing two segments that are separated by nearly fifty-five minutes in the speech from which the segment is sourced.”

Among other things, the BBC contended that Trump lacked personal jurisdiction, as the Panorama documentary did not air in Florida, where the case was filed.

The judge ruled that Trump had sufficiently claimed jurisdiction. The judge wrote, “Trump has alleged extensive contacts tying BBC to the State of Florida. BBC tries to frame this as an operation entirely disconnected from Florida. That cannot be the case. Trump is a Florida resident, and the Documentary is almost entirely about Trump. Portions were filmed in Florida and BBC identified Florida as an important part of the Documentary. Therefore, ‘it seems disingenuous’ for BBC to argue that they ‘could not have anticipated being haled into court in Florida.'”

Trump called the ruling a “huge victory” and called the BBC “a terrible abuser of truth.”

He wrote on Truth Social, “We will continue fighting against, and defeating, all of those who try to deceive the American People!”

A BBC spokesperson did not immediately return a request for comment.
