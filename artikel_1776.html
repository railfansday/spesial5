Martinez likens Ronaldo’s level to Messi, Maradona and Cruyff

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Syndicated reporting provided by Stats Perform and reviewed by the SoccerNews editorial team.

Former Portugal head coach Roberto Martinez said it was a "pleasure" to coach Cristiano Ronaldo, who he believes is on the same level as Diego Maradona, Johan Cruyff and Lionel Messi.

Ronaldo announced his international retirement just days before the most recent break, bringing down the curtain on a glittering career with Portugal.

He is the leading scorer and appearance-maker in the history of men's internationals, plundering 146 goals in 234 games since his Portugal debut in 2003.

His Portugal career ended on a bit of a dour note, as he left the September-October camp early after a falling-out with new boss Jorge Jesus, which resulted in him being handed a 30-day ban by the Portuguese Football Federation (FPF).

During Martinez's tenure, he led Portugal to the quarter-finals of Euro 2024 and won the 2024-25 Nations League but stepped down after their last-16 exit to eventual champions Spain at this year's World Cup.

The Spaniard highlighted just how big a mark Ronaldo made on Portugal's history and had high praise for his former captain.

"For me, it was a pleasure," Martinez said on Amazon Prime when asked about Ronaldo.

"For me and my technical staff, it was a joy to work with him for 37 games. He scored 28 goals [in 37 games under Martinez], and that's probably one of the iconic figures.

"I don't think we will see a player of that stature [again] in Portugal.

"In the last few days, you've had so many subjective opinions about what the player is now, [but] what is not subjective are the stats.

"The way he has impacted the game. We are talking about a player that maybe [is of the level of] Lionel Messi, [Diego] Maradona, Johan Cruyff; those are the stats that a player like Cristiano [has]."

Ronaldo retires as the only player to have scored at least 10 goals at both the World Cup (11) and the European Championships (14), with the latter figure being a competition record.

He became the first player to score in six different editions of the World Cup this year, when he helped himself to a brace in a group-stage win over Uzbekistan, and he is also the oldest player to feature in a World Cup knockout-stage match, at the age of 41.

"I don't think you can [surpass his numbers]," Martinez added.

"It's something that you learn with Cristiano; his mindset is not winning something or his motivation wasn't to win the Ballon d'Or.

"His motivation was to be better the next day. His commitment to the game is second to none, and I think the longevity is above any opinion.

"Portugal has a population of 10 million. When Cristiano Ronaldo arrives to the national team [in 2003], [Portugal in] 23-and-a-half seasons have never missed a major tournament.

"That's down to what he brought: the professionalism and his numbers – 234 games [for the national team]."
