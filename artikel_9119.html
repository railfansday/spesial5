Eleanor Antin Sues Dealer for Withholding Nearly 200 Works

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The artist filed the complaint six months after terminating her relationship with dealer Richard Saltoun.

Conceptual artist Eleanor Antin is seeking the return of at least 179 works or $975,000 in compensatory damages in a lawsuit against her dealer, Richard Saltoun. The complaint was filed in New York State Supreme Court on July 29, six months after the artist terminated her relationship with Saltoun’s namesake gallery, which operates locations in New York, London, and Rome.

Filed by the 91-year-old artist’s son and attorney-in-fact, Blaise Antin, and her studio, Paranoid Productions, the suit claims that Saltoun “initially purported to agree to return all of the works it held” upon their business separation at the gallery's London location on January 16. News of the lawsuit was first reported by Artnet.

Saltoun claimed in February that the gallery had returned all of Antin’s works to her family, according to filed documents. However, later that month, the artist’s archivist, citing an inventory produced by Saltoun himself, allegedly discovered that Antin was still missing at least 179 works, the majority of which were still held at the London gallery.
