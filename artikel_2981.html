Etsy vs. Five Below: Which Growth Stock Is the Better Buy?

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Etsy and Five Below take very different routes to growth, and the gap in their recent numbers may tip the scales for long-term investors.

Choosing between e-commerce innovation and traditional retail growth involves weighing different business models. This comparison examines whether Etsy (ETSY +0.80%) or Five Below (FIVE -0.19%) offers the more compelling opportunity for investors today.

Etsy connects millions of independent sellers with buyers seeking creative items, while Five Below operates high-energy physical stores selling trend-right products at extreme value. Both companies target discretionary spending but use vastly different methods to reach their core audiences.

Etsy operates a global online marketplace that specializes in unique and creative goods, connecting millions of active sellers with approximately millions of active buyers. The company focuses on a two-sided marketplace model where it provides the infrastructure for artisans and vintage collectors to reach a massive audience. To support its vast technical needs, the company relies on third-party service providers like Google Cloud and Amazon (AMZN -0.29%), and it is currently finalized the sale of its Depop business to eBay (EBAY +0.65%).

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $2.9 billion, which represents a modest growth rate compared to the prior year. Net income for this period was approximately $987.5 million. This financial performance highlights the company's ability to maintain its scale within the broader landscape of retail stocks even as consumer habits shift.

Five Below is a specialty value retailer that primarily targets kids, pre-teens, and teens, offering a wide variety of products mostly priced between $1 and $5. As of early 2026, the company operated 1,921 stores across 46 states, creating an immersive shopping experience across eight merchandise "worlds." The business model relies on a network of approximately 1,000 vendors to source trend-right products that appeal to its young demographic and their parents.

In the 12 months ended Aug. 1, 2026, revenue reached $5.3 billion, representing growth of 25.6% over the preceding twelve-month period. Net income for this same timeframe was approximately $619.2 million. This significant growth is largely driven by the company's aggressive store expansion strategy and its ability to maintain popularity among cost-conscious consumers.

Five Below must navigate risks associated with inflation and rising costs for commodities and labor, which can pressure its low-price business model. The company's reliance on a global supply chain makes it vulnerable to tariff impacts and logistics delays. Additionally, Five Below faces challenges in managing its rapid expansion, including the risks of inventory management, retail shrink, and federal class action lawsuits related to its growth projections.

Etsy appears more attractively priced based on future earnings estimates, though both companies show differences when comparing the price-to-sales (P/S) ratio.

Both of these stocks tap into consumer shopping habits, and Five Below has a unique value proposition with its pricing. But the key difference between the two is that Etsy operates on an asset-light model, while Five Below operates on an asset-heavy model. Between the two, I would lean more toward owning Etsy, given the uncertainty the markets are facing heading into 2027.

Five Below's model relies on opening more stores, acquiring and selling more inventory, hiring more employees, and spending more on logistics. If the economy faces a downturn, that hurts that model hard. In comparison, Etsy relies on sellers using its digital platform, which largely just requires connecting more sellers with buyers and doesn't require opening hundreds of stores a year for revenue growth.

As of now, Etsy is seeing revenue growth, as evidenced by its 2026 third-quarter results. Sales increased 11% from the prior-year period to $692 million, and net income totaled $114 million.

In terms of risks, one of the largest is the same for Etsy as it is for Five Below: a slowdown in consumer spending. Still, Etsy is better equipped to handle that with its asset-light model.
