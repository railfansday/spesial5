Second Amendment Roundup: The AK-47 Semiautomatic is Encompassed in the Viramontes Question Presented

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Mostly law professors | Sometimes contrarian | Often libertarian | Always independent

The Galil semiautomatic rifle is similar to the AR-15 platform.

In a last-ditch attempt to avoid Supreme Court scrutiny of its semiautomatic rifle ban, Cook County has suggested that because the last individual Petitioner in the case against it, Christopher Khaya, said in his deposition years ago that he wants to possess a banned Galil rifle, no Petitioner wants to possess a firearm encompassed within the question presented.  (Viramontes is now known as Khaya v. Cook County.)  That argument is unpersuasive. The question presented covers "AR-15 platform and similar semiautomatic rifles," not the AR-15 alone.   A Galil rifle is a semiautomatic version of the AK-47 which Cook County bans.  As shown in the excerpts below, several of the amicus briefs filed in support of petitioners establish that if any rifle is "similar" to the AR-15, the Galil and other AK-types are.

"The question presented asks whether the Constitution protects possession of 'AR-15 platform and similar semiautomatic rifles.'" That framing is important because 'AR-15' does not describe a single firearm." Palmetto State Armory Br. 6.

"The list of similarly styled rifles that are commonly classified as 'assault weapons' is extensive and would include, for example: AK-47 designs, FN FALs, M1 carbines, M1As, HK 91/93/94s and CETME/PTR clones, SKSs, Kel-Tec rifles, Thompsons, Ruger Mini-14/Mini-30s, FN SCARs, and Steyr AUGs and other bullpup designs. These rifles are likewise banned under most 'assault weapons' laws, including the laws at issue here. It is therefore essential to measure their popularity as well—not just that of the AR-15, which is but one instance of the banned firearm type." English Br. 13.

"Thus, in Smith & Wesson Brands, Inc. v. Estados Unidos Mexicanos, 605 U.S. 280 (2025), this Court unanimously explained that so-called 'military style' assault weapons, … includ[ing] AR-15 rifles, AK-47 rifles, and .50 caliber sniper rifles,' are 'both widely legal and bought by many ordinary consumers,' and '[t]he AR-15 is the most popular rifle in the country.' Id. at 297." GOA Br. 19.

"A Memphis man used a semiautomatic rifle to shoot two armed robbers who were 'ransack[ing]' his house. Action News 5, Homeowner shoots, kills 2 burglars with AK-47 (June 1, 2018), https://perma.cc/J47A-PFUV." Tennessee Br. 14.

"AR-15s and similar rifles have different characteristics that appeal to different users, but all are semiautomatic rifles at heart." Eleven State-Based Firearms Advocacy Organizations Br. 16.

"Semiautomatic operation fires one round for each trigger activation and uses the energy of firing to chamber the next round. That basic mechanism has been commercially available for generations." Rocky Mountain Gun Owners Br. 9.

My comment: Obviously, whether it is an AK-47 or AR-15, a semiautomatic action uses gas from firing a round to do the "autoloading function," so if semiautomatic rifles are to be protected under the Second Amendment, it should not matter whether the Court rules in a case involving an AR-15 semiautomatic rifle or an AK-47 style semiautomatic rifle.
