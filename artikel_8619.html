Park Campsites Cost a Fraction of a Hotel Room – Book the Day Reservations Open

A campsite in a state or national park costs a fraction of a hotel room, but popular campgrounds can fill the day reservations open. Book on Recreation.gov or your state’s park reservation site that morning, buy an annual pass if you’ll visit several parks, and borrow gear for your first trip.

A tent site at Bryce Canyon National Park costs $30 a night, while U.S. hotel rooms averaged $160 a night in 2025, according to CoStar. For a seven-night trip, that’s $210 for the campsite against $1,120 for the room.

The hard part is getting the site, since some popular campgrounds can book up within minutes. Recreation.gov takes reservations for campgrounds in national parks, national forests, and other federal lands. Most of its campsites go on sale six months before arrival, usually at 10 a.m. Eastern. State park campsites book through each state’s own reservation site, on its own schedule, such as 120 days ahead in Minnesota.

For a trip just weeks away, look for campgrounds that take reservations only a short time ahead, such as Bryce Canyon’s Sunset Campground, which books no more than 14 days before arrival.

The America the Beautiful pass costs U.S. residents $80 and covers one car’s entrance and day-use fees for a year at national parks, national forests, and other federal lands, though not campsite fees. Three parks charging Yellowstone’s $35 a car would cost $105 in entrance fees, more than the $80 pass, so buy it when you’ll visit three or more. Some states sell their own park pass, such as Minnesota’s $35 year-round vehicle permit, which costs the same as five $7 day permits.

For a first trip, borrow or rent the tent and sleeping bags, and buy your own once you know you’ll go again. Or skip the tent and rent a camper cabin, which Minnesota’s state parks offer for $80 to $90 a night, about half the $160 average hotel rate.

Look up the date and hour your campground’s sites go on sale, on its Recreation.gov page under Seasons and Fees or on your state’s reservation site, and log in well before then.
