When Hideo Kojima rather suddenly left Konami in 2016 and, soon after, opened his own studio, the move sent shockwaves throughout the industry. Today, Kojima Productions has established itself as a AAA development studio within Japan known most of all for 3D-scanning celebrities and developing the Death Stranding franchise, which will soon move beyond gaming as it’s adapted into anime and a live-action film. Looking forward, the studio also plans to expand its range with Physint and OD: Knock in partnership with Xbox.

Itâs one of the more fascinating stories from the last decade of the Japanese games industry, not just for how Kojima Productions came to be but for how it’s sustained itself as a studio. Kojima has leaned on his name recognition to turn the studio into a brand unto itself, and the director keeps himself and the studio in the spotlight through an array of film work, social media posts, radio shows, cameos in TV dramas, and more.

In the upmarket area of Azabudai in central Tokyo, with the historic Tokyo Tower landmark in view, an exhibition looking back on the past decade of the studio has opened for a limited time. Not just chronicling the public history of the studio, the event also featured new information and unseen concept art for Death Stranding, alongside details about the company’s in-development Xbox titles. After visiting the exhibition during its opening days earlier this week, here are ten things we learned about the studio and its array of upcoming titles.

When this exhibition was first announced, Kojima Productions promised not just a look back at the past 10 years of the studio, but a look forward at its in-development work on Physint and OD: Knock as well. On the scriptâs cover, it appears that there’s a word that comes after Physint and has been redacted, suggesting we don’t yet know the game’s full, final title. We also saw some models of things that appear in-game, including a few mechs that look suspiciously like Metal Gear. Considering Kojima referred to the title as being in the same vein as his earlier stealth series, that’s not entirely unsurprising.

Kojima Productions has become more than a studio: itâs a brand. The merchandise featuring the studioâs logo and spacesuit-wearing Lumens character is proof of this. Clearly not content with only scanning people for his games, Kojima has taken things further when it comes to what he will digitize. Detailing the 3D scanning process, the exhibition confirmed that the team partnered with 2024 TBS TV drama Sayonara, Maestro! in order to capture detailed 3D scans of many of the show’s sets for reuse in the game.

Similarly, the team has digitized a number of pieces of vintage furniture, such as old dressers, in order to ensure that such furniture in-game looked sufficiently aged.
