Nvidia earnings preview: How Jensen Huang updates could impact the stock, whether the results are strong or not

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

All eyes are always on Nvidia’s (Nasdaq: NVDA) earnings. The company is the backbone of the AI revolution, after all.

But today’s results—and its upcoming financial call—will be the first without cofounder and former CEO Jensen Huang at the helm, which could make this the company’s most consequential report in Nvidia’s history.

Investors will not only want to see favorable growth, but they will want an update on the company’s direction after Huang resigned amid multiple scandals last week.

And if that update doesn’t assuage fears—or if it leads to more uncertainty—the stock could continue its downward spiral. Here’s what you need to know.

Nvidia reports Q3 2027 results today

After markets close today, Nvidia will report results for its third quarter of fiscal 2027, which ended on October 25. The results are expected around 4:20 p.m. ET, with the earnings conference call scheduled to begin at 5 p.m. ET.

As far as numbers go, investors are expecting big results from Nvidia. As noted by CNBC, Wall Street analysts expect the company to report revenue of $109.1 billion and earnings per share (EPS) of $2.47. 

If Nvidia hits that revenue target, it will represent staggering growth of 91% versus the same quarter last year, when the company posted $59 billion in revenue.

However, even if Nvidia achieves 91% growth, it will be lower than the 106% YOY growth the company saw in its second quarter, when it reported $96.2 billion in revenue.

That suggests growth is slowing—something investors never like to hear. Indeed, in August, Nvidia’s then chief financial officer and current interim CEO, Colette Kress, said the company expects just 70% growth in fiscal 2028, which she attributed to a “supply constrained outlook.”

But numbers are one thing. What investors will really want to hear about is what the company plans to do in a post-Huang world.

Jensen Huang may be out, but he’s still at the top of investors’ minds

Last week, the AI world was rocked when Nvidia’s cofounder and CEO resigned amid a set of scandals.

The first scandal had to do with his affair with a woman who U.S. intelligence believed may have been spying for China. Beijing officials have strongly denied the allegations.

However, the allegations and their resulting national security concerns were enough to compromise Huang’s position at the company. Then, shortly after reports of a second alleged affair, this time with an Nvidia employee, emerged, Huang resigned as CEO.

Shortly after Huang resigned, Nvidia announced that its CFO, Colette Kress, would step into the interim chief executive role while it sought a new CEO. The company is reportedly considering both internal and external candidates.

In recent days, there have also been rumblings that Huang may be planning to make a comeback and return to the CEO role, much like OpenAI CEO Sam Altman did in 2023 after he was ousted.

Investors will want updates on both issues: how the search for Huang’s replacement is progressing and whether the board would even be open to Huang’s return.

They’ll also likely want an update on Nvidia’s internal investigation into whether the company’s secrets, or national security secrets, were compromised by Huang’s alleged affairs.

Which way will NVDA stock go?

Currently, NVDA stock is trading down about 1.2% ahead of the company’s results later today. Unfortunately for investors, that red on NVDA’s charts is par for the course as of late.

In the past five trading days alone, NVDA shares have lost more than 7.5% in value. Over the past month, shares have cratered more than 27%. 

The scandals and Huang’s departure have had an abysmal effect on Nvidia’s stock price. That fall was first exacerbated by fears that AI spending could be declining as companies start to rein in costs. The Huang scandals then acted as a pile-on.

Nvidia’s all-time closing high was $239.24 on October 6, just less than six weeks ago. At the time, the company had a valuation of more than $5.7 trillion.

But today, post-Huang scandal, the company’s share price is below $154, a level it hasn’t been at since mid-2025. The company currently has a valuation of $3.7 trillion—a fall of roughly $2 trillion.

If Nvidia’s Q3 results are stellar and the company’s new interim leadership reassures investors about its direction and future, investors could reward the stock after hours, sending it up.

But if Nvidia’s numbers don’t excite—and, more importantly, if Nvidia’s comments on Huang and the company’s future leadership don’t inspire confidence—NVDA could see more red ahead.
