Why Everton School Visits Are Making A Mark

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

“Captain Underpants”. The laughter that follows is infectious. It seems doubtful that it was an honest answer to the question posed to Zach Lee by the young boy located somewhere near the front of the classroom: “What is your favourite book?” But it got exactly the reaction he was after.

The room is abuzz. It has been for much of the past hour.

Goalkeeper Lee and five of his Everton Under-21s teammates have gleefully taken up the task of being reading buddies to the children of Ellesmere Port’s Meadow Primary School, one of six schools that make up the recently renamed Everton Multi-Academy Trust (EMAT). Seven miles away, six of their Under-21 peers are doing likewise at Oldfield Primary School in Chester.

Nearer to Liverpool, the hollers that greeted Everton Women’s Holly McNamara and Karen Holmgaard 24 hours earlier are still reverberating around the corridors of Bidston Avenue Primary School.

Christian Nørgaard and close friends Tyler Dibling and Tyrique George are also familiar with the noise the pupils at the Birkenhead school can make. Last month, the trio were serenaded with a ukulele rendition of Spirit of the Blues on a visit there. Blue ukuleles, of course.

If the colour of the musical instruments is a cute indicator of the strengthening ties between the football club and the EMAT schools, the time being spared by the players is of much greater significance.

It is intended to demonstrate a cross-Club connection with EMAT, which sits alongside Everton in the Community as one of Everton’s associated organisations. “Part of the Everton family,” as Everton’s Chief Tax and Sustainability Officer and Chair of Trustees for EMAT Alison Haynes puts it.

But there is a deeper intention, too. “We want to use the power of the Everton crest to inspire and engage youngsters in education,” says Steven Baker OBE, CEO of EMAT. Baker has seen this in evidence for more than a decade, having previously served as Principal of Everton Alternative Provision Free School, the award-winning L4 facility that offers youngsters who have struggled in mainstream schools a route back into education.

Lee and his Under-21 colleagues, McNamara and Holmgaard are all supporting one particular mission of EMAT - to inspire youngsters towards a life-long love of reading. More immediately, to ensure they have the requisite literacy level to adequately prepare them for what lies ahead in secondary school education.

In Ellesmere Port, Chester and Birkenhead, the players are tackling their role head on. Books - donated by Everton staff to help replenish the schools’ libraries - are strewn across the tables and spilling onto the floor. No-one’s troubled by the mess.

Julia Donaldson’s greatest hits are being played out across the room. Michael Morpurgo – a childhood favourite author of midfielder Jack Patterson – can be heard, too. ‘Bluey’ is an appropriately named hit on another table. And then there’s Lee and a group of little ones giggling at Captain Underpants.

Close by to the classroom at Meadow, Everton’s leading post-war goalscorer Graeme Sharp is here too, continuing the literacy theme by officially opening the school’s newly-refurbished library.

“These children will remember today,” says Baker. “They will remember players from Everton coming into school and taking the time to sit with them.

“They love hearing stories but they also love having someone to read to. Both aspects are so important for a child’s development.”

As is music. It’s why Dibling, George and Norgaard found themselves being taught the chords to one of Goodison’s – and now Hill Dickinson Stadium’s – favourite tunes last month.

Studies show a direct correlation between a child mastering a musical instrument and making their mark in maths and other creative fields.

The Toffees trio left behind gifts of recorders (blue ones), helping the school expand the opportunities it can offer to its pupils.

“We find it’s really important for their resilience and it's good for the children mentally and socially to have the opportunity to not only play an instrument, but to play to an audience,” says Bidston Avenue headteacher Stuart Brady, a Tranmere Rovers fan.

“It's great for us as a school to have the connection we do with Everton and the players. Days like this are great, but it's also about the children feeling they belong to something bigger.”

And the proof that EMAT is getting it right is stacking up. In 2024, Everton Free School and Everton Football College – the two original schools on which the Trust was founded – were jointly awarded an ‘Outstanding’ rating by Ofsted. Earlier this year, the respected national TES Awards named EMAT (then The People’s Learning Trust) as its Trust of the Year for trusts with nine schools or fewer.

With the support of a Premier League football club and its players behind them, it is hoped the collective will continue to go from strength to strength.

The intention is that Everton’s Under-21s will return to the schools to resume their reading buddy duties at junctures throughout the season, strengthening their bonds with the schools and pupils. It is seen as a vital part of the players’ professional development.

Representatives from the Men’s and Women’s team will continue to play their part also. Merlin Röhl has already been a guest of Oldfield Primary School’s after-school chess club, taking up an invitation to share his love of the ancient game with the next generation.

“We understand the responsibility and influence we have as a football club when it comes to our communities,” says Haynes. “And you cannot understate the impact the player visits have.

“The atmosphere and the smiles you see in the room, the energy they give to the children… it’s truly wonderful.”
