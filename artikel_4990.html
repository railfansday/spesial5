More than $2 billion rushed out of Sky’s stablecoin in a single day

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Sky Frontier Foundation says holders swapped more than $2 billion of USDS for USDC at par on Nov. 19. The shared USDC reserve fell by about 1.69 billion USDC before an Obex repayment helped refill it.

Holders swapped more than $2 billion of Sky’s USDS for USDC on Nov. 19, according to Sky Frontier Foundation. Sky’s Ethereum reserve for one-for-one swaps fell by about two-thirds in hours. At 17:55 UTC it held 867 million USDC, the amount immediately available to USDS and DAI holders using the shared pool. Seconds later, a debt repayment in Sky’s Obex allocation vault put about 100 million USDC back into the pool.

The foundation said its Peg Stability Module kept exchanging USDS for USDC at one-for-one throughout the day. The reported $2 billion-plus was gross volume in that direction.

The reserve fell from 2.56 billion USDC earlier on Nov. 19 to 867 million USDC before the Obex repayment, a net decline of 1.69 billion USDC. The foundation’s gross figure counts trades in one direction. The balance change reflects USDC moving both ways.

The foundation said the reserve stayed at or above 800 million USDC and later crossed 1.1 billion on Nov. 20. The 867 million reading comes from Ethereum block 26450759, 24 seconds before the Obex repayment. At 12:38 UTC on Nov. 23, Sky’s PSM dashboard showed 1.76 billion USDC, just over twice the pre-repayment balance.

Sky’s LitePSM guide explains how the Ethereum module serves both tokens. A DAI holder can trade directly. For a USDS holder, a separate contract converts USDS to DAI inside the same transaction and pays out USDC from the same reserve. The module keeps those USDC tokens at a separate address called the pocket.

At 15:48 UTC on Nov. 19, a holder exchanged 400 million USDS for 400 million USDC. The USDS was burned, the equivalent amount of DAI went into the module, and the pocket sent the holder USDC.

That shared inventory sets a ceiling for both tokens. A holder can take out only as much USDC as is available when the trade executes. Other swaps can change that amount within the same block. Governance can set fees or pause either direction. The one-for-one rate describes trades through the module itself. Sky has also integrated USDS and USDC routing with Uniswap, where Uniswap swap fees can depend on the route. Holders who want bank dollars after the exchange still need a separate USDC redemption route.

At 17:55:47 UTC, an Obex repayment sent about 100 million USDC into the pocket. In the same transaction, the module released an equal amount of DAI, which was converted to USDS and used to repay debt in the Obex vault.

At 19:00 UTC, another holder swapped 12 million USDC for 12 million USDS, sending USDC into the reserve.

Two November 19 transactions added USDC to the same pool. Each sent the USDS proceeds to a different destination.

The Obex trade paid down an allocation loan. The ordinary swap issued USDS to its sender.

At 17:41 UTC on Nov. 19, about 14 minutes before the Obex repayment, the pocket held 872 million USDC. At that same block, Ethereum had 6.92 billion issued USDS and DAI after subtracting the DAI already held in the module’s own buffer. The USDC available could cover 12.6% of that adjusted Ethereum supply at one-for-one.

Sky Frontier Foundation put protocol-wide collateral at about $9.3 billion on Nov. 20. That figure describes backing across Sky. For a holder looking to convert through LitePSM, the relevant balance is the USDC in the pocket.

In the Nov. 23 dashboard reading, the reserve stood at 28.3% of circulating USDS and DAI, below Sky’s 30% target. That ratio uses the dashboard’s definition of circulating supply. Sky’s stUSDS vault withdrawals depend on available USDS. Vault holders must redeem for USDS before using the swap module.
