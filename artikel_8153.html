6 Ways to Score Free Ski Lift Tickets and Mountain Perks This Winter

This year, beat high lift ticket prices by volunteering as a patroller, adaptive ski instructor, or buy qualifying flight tickets to a number of select locations (Photo: South_agency/Getty Images)

With lift ticket prices regularly topping $250 across North American mountains, budget skiing takes strategy. Outside dug into this season’s best hacks for scoring free lift tickets for skiers and snowboarders, from airline promotions at nine marquee resorts, to volunteer opportunities, and even to going downhill with a U.S. Forest Service ranger.

This winter, we’re tracking the snowiest mountains in America and the rare super El Niño on the way. Now, here’s what you need to know for the 2026-27 winter season when it comes to snagging your free passes.

Located in the San Juan Mountains of Southwest Colorado, Purgatory Resort is a 30-minute drive from Durango. With over 1,600 acres and an average snowfall of 260 inches annually, the mountain offers a blend of steep tree skiing and open groomers.

During the 2026-27 season, Purgatory is offering skiers and snowboarders a free season pass in exchange for 14 days of volunteering. Four of those shifts must fall during peak season, including winter holidays, Presidents’ Day weekend, and spring break.

Several other mountains across the country offer similar incentives for those volunteering on the slopes. Winter Park Resort in Colorado, North Carolina’s Beech Mountain Resort, and Whiteface in New York all provide free lift tickets in exchange for volunteering on ski patrol or the safety team.

Alaska Airlines’ Atmos Rewards Alpine Getaways for the winter ski season lets passengers show a qualifying boarding pass at nine participating western U.S. and Canadian ski resorts for free lift tickets, rental upgrades, and resort discounts.

Eligible passengers flying into the following airports get a free full, same-day, or half-day ticket.

Jay Peak Resort in Vermont is celebrating its annual Day for the Devoted on November 27, 2026, featuring 750 free lift tickets

“Day for the Devoted opens the season with a thank you to the people who make this place what it is, and an invitation to anyone ready to become a part of it,” the resort writes on its website. You can register for the event here.

Buying a qualifying Helly Hansen ski jacket or pair of pants gets you a free day or a buy-one-get-one ticket at participating resorts across North America and Europe. Clothing must be from the latest Helly Hansen ski collection.

Buy your gear, find the resort you’d like to ski, and submit your receipt to redeem a pass. More information is available on the company website here.

Adaptive skiing nonprofits rely on volunteers to help people with disabilities experience skiing. In many cases, volunteers receive a complimentary lift ticket for the day they’re helping.

At Eagle Mountain Therapeutic Recreation in Showdown, Montana, volunteers 16 and older can receive free passes and lunches on the days they work. Those who work four events can receive a day pass to be used at any time, while those who commit to eight or more days will receive a free pass for the season, according to the program website.

The Outdoors for All at The Summit at Snoqualmie and Stevens Pass in Washington offers a variety of volunteer programs, from ski and snowboard instructors to support in snowshoeing and Nordic skiing. Full-time volunteers are eligible for a discounted season pass, while part-time volunteers receive a same-day lift ticket. All volunteers also receive ExpertVoice pro deals and have access to instructor development, adaptive teaching experience, and clinics.

Every Friday beginning January 30 through April 3, 2027, intermediate skiers and snowboarders can spend an hour skiing with a U.S. Forest Service (USFS) staff member through the Ski With a Ranger Tour. Attendance is first-come, first-served and limited to 12 participants daily.

Participants must provide their own lift ticket, but the program is part of a free conservation education initiative that shares the USFS mission in ski area management. Participants will also learn about natural resources and environmental stewardship, the USFS writes on its website.

Kelso Carapia, a recreation specialist with the USFS Lake Tahoe Basin Management Unit, has been leading the program for 14 years. She told Outside that participants can expect to learn about a host of topics, including water ecology, forest health, animals, survival strategies, fire management, general land stewardship, and the cultural and geological history of the Tahoe Basin.

Ski experience is necessary. The route is on intermediate and blue terrain only, and skiers will access at least two lifts as part of their tour.

“This is not a ski lesson,” Carapia said. “It is an interpretive tour. As much as we prioritize safety and comfort of our participants, we are not there to instruct.”

Heavenly Mountain Resort straddles the California-Nevada border in South Lake Tahoe, California, and Stateline, Nevada. With 4,800 skiable acres across four base areas, it’s also a major winter destination.

Ski With A Ranger tours are led by the United States Department of Agriculture’s Forest Service Conservation Education staff and volunteers through a cooperative effort between the Forest Service and Heavenly Mountain Resort.
