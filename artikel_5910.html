A U.K.-EU Reset Is Bad News for the Special Relationship

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The renegotiation of trade deals would be like putting Brexit on the table all over again.

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and British Prime Minister Andy Burnham speak as they arrive for the EU-UK summit in Brussels, Belgium, November 20, 2026.

For those of us in Britain who care about democracy, November 20 marked a disturbing political development that Americans should take notice of. Prime Minister Andy Burnham traveled to Brussels for the U.K.-EU Summit — a renegotiation of Britain’s trading terms with the EU. Standing alongside the European Union’s chief bureaucrats, Burnham announced a series of deals — concentrating on food standards, youth mobility between Britain and the EU, and carbon and energy trading — that will erect fresh trade barriers between the U.K. and the United States.

In June 2016, over 17 million Britons voted to leave the European Union and take back control of our lawmaking abilities from Brussels. The principle of parliamentary sovereignty — the foundation of the British constitution, which holds that Parliament is the supreme lawmaking authority in the land — had been eroded during our membership in the EU as we increasingly outsourced decision-making to unelected foreign bureaucrats. The Brexit vote was a watershed in British politics that allowed us to become the sovereign state we are today.

Joseph Dinnage is senior press officer at the Prosperity Institute.
