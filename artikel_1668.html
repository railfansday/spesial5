IT'S A GAME CHANGER! Scotland to co-host Women's World Cup with England, Northern Ireland and Wales in 2035

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Published: 13:05 EST, 23 November 2026 | Updated: 17:59 EST, 23 November 2026

Scotland, England, Northern Ireland and Wales have been confirmed as the joint hosts of the 2035 Women’s World Cup.

The four-nation bid was given the official seal of approval in a vote by acclamation at an Extraordinary FIFA Congress on Monday, having been the sole valid bid in contention for the event.

It will be the first men’s or women’s World Cup finals to be staged in the UK since the 1966 men’s tournament was held in England.

Nineteen stadiums are proposed, with 14 in England, one in Northern Ireland, two in Scotland (Hampden and Easter Road) and two in Wales in a tournament which will feature 48 teams.

A further three stadiums could be added – Birmingham’s new Powerhouse Stadium, Wrexham’s redeveloped Racecourse Ground and a potential new Chelsea stadium – which would take the tournament to 16 cities and 22 stadiums across the four nations.

The bid team projected the tournament would attract 3.5 billion viewers worldwide, and that 4.5 million tickets would be sold.

Scotland captain Caroline Weir says the World Cup will take the nation's game to another level

FIFA’s evaluation of the bid, published earlier this month, rated it 4.3 out of five.

For England this represents another chance to host a major women’s tournament, having hosted the European Championship in 2022 which the Lionesses won.

The Congress also approved the joint bid by Mexico, the United States, Costa Rica and Jamaica to host the 2031 finals, which will be the first to feature 48 teams.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham said: 'The world is coming to the United Kingdom. In 2035 the biggest tournament in women’s sport will be played in cities across England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, bringing visitors, investment and jobs to every part of the country.

'Our players have inspired a generation of girls. In 2035 that generation will watch the best in the world play on their doorstep – and some of them will be on the pitch. But the impact of this World Cup will go much further. It will reach into communities across the country, bring more people into the game and leave a legacy that lasts long after the final whistle.'

Scotland captain Caroline Weir added: 'The women’s game in Scotland has so much potential and this tournament is a real opportunity to drive momentum and help take it to another level both nationally and internationally.

'We’re so proud to play our part in welcoming the world in 2035 and I know I speak on behalf of all of the players and staff when I say that we are ready to rise to the challenge and help deliver a truly memorable, world-class tournament.'

England forward Michelle Agyemang said it was 'incredibly special' and added: 'I’ve been lucky enough to know what it feels like to play for my country in a major tournament, and those are moments you dream about as a young girl playing football.

'The thought of another generation growing up knowing that the biggest stage in the game is coming to their home country is amazing. To have the chance to play in a home World Cup would be a dream, but even more exciting is thinking about all the girls watching who might see 2035 and believe they can be part of it one day.'

SFA chief Ian Maxwell says tournament will be 'most significant thing to happen to women's and girls' game in Scotland'

Scottish Football Association chief executive Ian Maxwell said hosting the tournament would be 'the most significant thing to happen to the women's and girls' game in Scotland'.

He said: 'The impact would go far beyond just the matches themselves. The level of investment into coaching, facilities and player pathways in the years beforehand will shape the future of the game for the generations to come.'

First Minister John Swinney said: 'This is fantastic news for Scotland. We have proved time and again that we are a world-class host, and I am delighted that the FIFA Women’s World Cup 2035 will be held in Scotland alongside England, Wales and Northern Ireland.

'The timing is ideal. Project 2035, our new long-term plan to grow the girls’ and women’s game, aims to establish Scotland as a leading nation for women’s football by 2035. Our newly established girls’ and women’s football taskforce will help to make this happen, capitalising on the World Cup and leaving a lasting legacy for communities across the country.

'Scotland is building a remarkable reputation for hosting major events, and the FIFA Women’s World Cup joins the Tour de France Grand Départ, The Open, the World Rally Championships and UEFA EURO 2028 in promising to showcase what our country has to offer. We will make sure each one of these amazing sporting events delivers for our economy, supporting jobs, businesses and tourism.'
