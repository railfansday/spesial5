Arsenal 1-0 Hull: Not efficient, but enough

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

In yesterday’s preview blog, I spoke about Arsenal’s attacking efficiency, and how that might be key to taking all three points. In yesterday’s game against Hull, Arsenal took 21 shots, 10 of which were on target, and scored one goal from that. Efficient?

Despite generally dominating possession, I thought there was a lack of precision in certain aspects of our first half play. Passes weren’t quite quick enough or accurate enough, but there were moments when we could have found the breakthrough. Gabriel almost got on the end of a Noni Madueke cross which was flicked on towards the back post, Rice had a tame shot from a free kick before a late header which he probably should have done better with.

And then there was Kai Havertz. It’s fair to say Hull didn’t leave a lot of room behind a defensive line that often had six players stretched across it, but late in the half a superb Martin Odegaard pass sent him clean through, one on one with the keeper. I haven’t seen replays but in real time I felt like he should have pulled the trigger – instead he over-complicated it. You have to be prepared to shoot from there, even if it’s on your wrong foot.

Then there was a penalty incident when he complained vociferously, play went on, the ref was then told to go to the screen and we all assumed it would be a penalty. However, the ref said it was a foul but Havertz was offside – and it felt like a massive waste of time. Afterwards, it emerged it should have been a penalty, but apparently the rule is ‘open to interpretation’. So clear as mud then.

In the second half, we seemed to play down our right a lot, with Madueke the outlet most of the time. I thought he did pretty well, much better than Christos Tzolis on the other side, but the breakthrough wouldn’t come. Mikel Merino replaced Rice (who seemed to be on the end of a wild challenge in midfield that the ref just ignored as he tumbled through the air), and Viktor Gyokeres came on for Havertz.

On 71 minutes though, we found the goal. Madueke’s shot was spilled by the keeper, Odegaard was quickest to react to poke the ball home for 1-0. And to be fair, you always felt confident that would be enough. We are the masters of the single goal margin win, and this was another one. Not a day that will live long in the memory or anything, but a 1-0 win that takes us into the Interlull in really good shape, and there’s not much arguing with that.

Or really, much more to say about this game. Mikel Arteta sounded happy enough, saying:

I’m delighted with the win. To go into the international break with a win is one of the best things about being a coach or player, I would say. Regarding the performance, I thought we were the better team, had enough chances to score and make it bigger for ourselves and we weren’t efficient enough to take them.

We then had to rely on generating volume, plenty of persisting until we finally broke them down. Martin then got the goal. Not the most beautiful one, but the most effective one, a simple ‘shoot and then follow the next action’, and that was ultimately enough to win the game.

So Arsenal sit top of the table, with 9 wins from 10 so far this season in the league, and it’s a very impressive run of form. Do I wish we had more attacking potency? Absolutely, and I do worry a bit that it might bite us in the arse somewhere down the line, but for now, we can see that this is a team that just knows how to win, and perhaps that’s the most important thing.
