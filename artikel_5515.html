Why QXO sees homebuilders as its mainstay of future growth

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

QXO wants to become a broader supplier to production and custom homebuilders, using cross-selling across building materials and installation. The company cited improved pricing, daily sales growth, and plans for procurement consolidation, private label expansion, and AI-enabled systems.  AI Summary

QXO, which aims to have $50 billion in annual revenue within the next several years, is counting on homebuilders as a launchpad for growth.

Chairman and CEO Brad Jacobs said Monday on a conference call that the company wants to supply builders with far more than lumber and trusses. QXO, which reports full Q3 2026 earnings after the market closes Thursday, has overhauled sales incentives, technology and pricing to become a primary supplier to production and custom builders.

The push follows its acquisitions of TopBuild and Kodiak Building Partners this year and Beacon Roofing Supply in 2025. Collectively, these acquisitions gave QXO exposure to a wide range of building products, including residential and commercial insulation, roofing materials, waterproofing, lumber and building materials, siding and exteriors, drywall and interior finishes, installation services, and tools and equipment such as fasteners, compressors and safety gear.

That broader footprint comes as the company’s core business is showing momentum. With $4.9 billion in combined revenue last quarter, the firm is well on its way to achieving Jacobs’ lofty annual revenue goal.

QXO, which generates roughly 50% of its revenue from residential construction, reported preliminary Q3 2026 results earlier this week showing organic revenue per day improved for the second consecutive quarter. Companywide pricing rose 2.2% year over year, with nine of 11 business areas posting positive pricing, and daily sales were up 5.4% year over year by the end of October.

Roofing and Waterproofing, QXO’s longest-held business after its April 2025 acquisition of Beacon Roofing Supply, grew daily sales 9.1%. Jacobs credited better on-time deliveries, invoice accuracy and inventory management for Beacon’s performance. Insulation was another strong performer, largely because of data center demand.

Beyond those gains, Jacobs said during the conference call that homebuilders represent a big growth opportunity. He outlined how QXO plans to capture more of their business, along with several ways QXO plans to grow in 2027.

During the call, Jacobs made it clear that QXO plans to cross-sell more to homebuilders, an important and growing customer segment.

“Our strong relationships with production homebuilders are typically rooted in our truss and wall panel capabilities, as well as insulation, where we solve some of their greatest needs. At the same time, it creates an opportunity for us to expand share of wallet by becoming their primary supplier for a wider range of building materials, with the added value of scale to support their growth,” Jacobs explained.

The company’s $17 billion acquisition of TopBuild, announced earlier this year, gave QXO an installation business with a presence at roughly 22,000 job sites every day. QXO, with an established distribution business, plans to leverage TopBuild’s installation services to supply more building materials, coordinate additional trades and manage more work and installations on individual job sites.

QXO’s $2.25 billion acquisition of Kodiak Building Partners, announced in February, additionally gave the firm exposure to building materials like lumber, engineered wood, doors, windows, trusses and gypsum products. These materials are essential to the company’s plans to sell more to Kodiak’s many existing homebuilder customers.

To incentivize cross-selling, Jacobs explained that QXO is changing its sales compensation structure to reward employees for selling customers a broader range of products across its businesses. Salespeople will have a financial incentive to introduce existing customers to other parts of QXO’s portfolio, including building materials and installation services.

“Homebuilders are a big opportunity,” Jacobs said. “One of the main reasons we bought Kodiak is that they brought phenomenal relationships with the big production homebuilders and the custom builders. And they were mainly selling them lumber or trusses or engineered products. So, we’re building on those great relationships, and we’re going to sell them much more. We’ve already started talking to many of these customers about this. There’s a lot of appetite for this.”

Cross-selling to homebuilders comprises just one focus area among QXO’s growth plans. Jacobs named five other areas where the company expects to improve margins and market share, including stronger supplier relationships, additional acquisitions, pricing improvements, smarter use of private-label products and technological improvements.

As QXO continues to integrate and improve its Beacon, Kodiak and TopBuild acquisitions, the combined company intends to leverage its larger scale in negotiations with suppliers.

The business has about $12 billion in combined spending, including about $11 billion in direct spending on products and materials. About $7.6 billion of that spending is with suppliers that serve at least two of QXO’s legacy businesses, including about $5 billion to suppliers serving all three.

QXO plans to consolidate its supplier base to capture that overlap, which could allow the company to push for better pricing and rebates and standardize purchasing agreements across the businesses.

“We want deep relationships with a smaller number of strategic supplier partners,” Jacobs said. “We want to be important partners with them and preferably their most important partner. And there’s quite a lot of overlap among the key suppliers across the three acquisitions we’ve recently done.”

If QXO strengthens its supplier relationships, its homebuilder clients could see better product availability and service, making the company a more appealing primary supplier for builders, particularly as it expands its offerings through additional acquisitions.

Jacobs sees additional acquisitions as a key way to expand its product portfolio, customer base and geographic reach. Following its blockbuster acquisition of TopBuild, QXO is now eyeing smaller, highly accretive acquisitions, but Jacobs did not identify specific acquisition targets or product categories.

Any future acquisitions could add complementary building-material categories, installation capabilities or regional relationships, all of which could support QXO’s push to become a primary homebuilding supplier and deepen QXO’s reach with builders.

QXO’s leadership changes also signal that it continues to eye acquisitions. On Thursday, the company named Dev Ahuja its next CFO, effective in January, succeeding Ihsan Essaid, who will become Vice Chairman. Essaid brings M&A experience from Novelis, where he helped complete and integrate the $2.8 billion Aleris acquisition.

During the call, Jacobs described pricing as one of QXO’s largest company-specific levers for expanding profitability. The company is moving to data-driven pricing based on demand, elasticity, inventory, fulfillment and customer profitability. Better service and availability, he said, can give QXO room to charge more, but the goal is value-based pricing, not blanket price increases.

“I’m not just talking about raising prices; it’s optimizing prices. I think we’re on a good trajectory to getting price where it needs to be, and I think we have an excellent pricing team,” Jacobs explained.

For homebuilders, a broader product and services offering could make QXO a more valuable supplier. However, the company also signaled that it is eyeing strategic price increases, and its expanding offerings, market share and value could give it the leverage to do just that.

QXO launched its private-label brand TRI-BUILT in 2024 and is now actively trying to grow the brand’s sales. Because it’s an original brand, it has stronger gross profit margins – about 2,000 basis points higher – than comparable branded products.

TRI-BUILT accounted for a record 10.6% of Roofing and Waterproofing sales in the third quarter, all from Beacon. QXO wants to expand the brand into more product categories and grow sales to more than $2 billion over the next three years, while also extending private-label offerings across Kodiak and TopBuild.

To support the push, QXO is redesigning sales incentives to encourage employees to sell more private-label products, and the company’s new compensation structure will also reward salespeople for selling private-label products.

QXO is integrating its acquired businesses into a connected technology platform that includes a new ERP, point-of-sale system, warehouse management system and AI-enabled CRM. The systems are meant to make it easier for customers to check inventory, receive quotes, place orders and track deliveries.

“When this is fully rolled out, our employees will be able to get their work done in one connected, AI-enabled environment. They’ll spend less time entering the same information into different systems or tracking down information that will be readily available. The new ecosystem will give our people more capacity to serve customers and run the business,” Jacobs explained.

The public can get a fuller look at whether these strategies are already translating into results when QXO reports full third-quarter earnings after the market closes Thursday.
