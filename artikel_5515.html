10 Zelda games ranked by Metacritic, from Ocarina of Time to Minish Cap

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

A 2017 Metacritic ordering of Zelda games lists 16 entries. Here are 10 of them, from Ocarina of Time on the N64 to Minish Cap.

Each new Zelda game that arrives to a wave of praise sets off the same argument among players over where it belongs in the series' history. Breath of the Wild, the Switch adventure, had sparked that debate by March 2017. Critics and fans were calling it one of the strongest games the franchise had produced. The acclaim raised a harder question. Could a newcomer unseat a long line of beloved adventures and claim the crown as the greatest Zelda? The series had turned 30, and the field of contenders was crowded. Settling the matter interests anyone deciding which older entries deserve their time, and it interests longtime fans who want to see a favorite vindicated.

Metacritic offers a tempting shortcut through the dispute. The site gathers verdicts from writers across the web and condenses them into a single score anyone can read at a glance. Some players trust the site, and others resent it. A lone number conceals how it came together. Two titles with similar standing might rest on pools of unequal size, and a handful of writers can carry as much weight for one release as dozens do for another. Audience opinion and professional judgment diverge as well, which shows that no composite figure can resolve a question of taste. Any ordering that draws on these scores captures a snapshot of expert opinion at one moment, and readers should weigh it that way.

GamesRadar+ turned to Metacritic for a verdict in a March 2, 2017, article by Sam Prell. The outlet's own review had held up Breath of the Wild as the benchmark other adventure games would have to clear, and Prell set out to learn what the aggregated marks revealed about the series. He declined to weigh the strengths and flaws of Metacritic's method. Prell sorted the games by their critic results, and his list prints no scores. He excluded re-releases with two exceptions. The Game Boy Advance versions of the first two games stayed in, because dropping them would have erased the original Legend of Zelda from the lineup. Twilight Princess appears on both GameCube and Wii, since its controls differ so much between the two machines. Prell cautioned that different outlets reviewed different games and that the tally of write-ups varies from title to title. Breath of the Wild had drawn 60 reviews at the time of writing, while Ocarina of Time had 22, a gap of 38. He added that user ratings put Majora's Mask ahead of Ocarina of Time, and he named Spirit Tracks as his personal favorite. His ordering covers 16 entries across 12 numbered positions, with tied games sharing a number. This article presents 10 of those entries in Metacritic's order.

Ocarina of Time holds first place in the Metacritic critic order, finishing ahead of all 15 other Zelda entries on the list, Breath of the Wild included. Breath of the Wild runs on physics and weather systems that shape every outing. A tree that Link fells on a hillside will topple and roll down the slope, clearing whatever sits in its path. Boulders obey the same logic, and a well-aimed shove sends one down an incline to flatten opponents. Sudden rain can snuff out fires. A temperature gauge pushes players to change into warmer or cooler clothes to suit their surroundings.

Combat and exploration demand the same improvisation. Axes, clubs, and long blades break with little use, and Link can grab replacements from the enemies he faces. Foes live in colonies and encampments instead of wandering at random. They sleep at night, which lets a patient hunter sneak up on them. Link has a dedicated jump button and can climb any vertical surface he reaches, from ruins and towers to cliff walls and tree trunks.
