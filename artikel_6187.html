Free Wi-Fi is anything but free for airlines. IAG now has to turn that bill into advertising revenue without annoying the people paying the fares.

International Airlines Group (IAG) is launching a standalone media and advertising business as it looks to turn its huge investment in Starlink Wi-Fi into a revenue opportunity.

The new company, IAG Media, will allow advertisers to reach the group's 122 million annual passengers through targeted campaigns, including personalized advertising on Starlink login portals and inflight entertainment systems.

Speaking exclusively to Skift, IAG Media CEO Nacho Tovar and Josh Peckham, chief commercial officer, outlined plans to transform how advertising is delivered onboard aircraft, using high-speed connectivity to move beyond the same commercials being shown to every passenger.

"Previously you got on board an aircraft... you were in this black box of no connectivity," explained Peckham. "Connectivity is the real driver of us being able to create that fully personalized experience."

International Airlines Group is launching IAG Media, a standalone U.K. company that will sell advertising against the group's 122 million annual passengers across British Airways, Iberia, Aer Lingus, Vueling, and LEVEL. The venture is tied to IAG's Starlink rollout, with half the fleet expected to be equipped by the end of 2026 as part of a program covering more than 500 aircraft. IAG Media CEO Nacho Tovar and Chief Commercial Officer Josh Peckham told Skift that connectivity allows advertising to be tailored by destination, cabin class, and customer profile, replacing a model in which every passenger sees the same adverts. The business spans two divisions — a products and platform arm building ad technology through connectivity, inflight entertainment, and AI, and an agency arm handling sales, campaigns, and creative — and plans personalized advertising across websites, apps, and PDF boarding passes, plus an audience extension product that reaches travelers on third-party platforms. Starlink is free for eligible IAG passengers, but industry estimates put installation costs at $300,000 to $500,000 per aircraft plus recurring fees, which frames the revenue question. IAG is not first: United launched Kinective Media in 2024, and Lufthansa Group has been developing its own media proposition. Tovar and Peckham declined to provide revenue forecasts or profitability targets, and both said a bad customer experience would damage the brands and the business.
