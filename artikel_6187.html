For small-business owners who love to fly with American Airlines, the (see rates and fees) should be in your crosshairs.

It's an affordable card with no annual fee for the first cardholder year (then a $99 annual fee; this fee increases to a $150 annual fee for renewals after Jan. 24, 2027). That gives you a chance to try out its free checked bags, preferred boarding and bonus miles on several common business expenses without committing to a fee for the first year.

This card's benefits favor travelers who prefer to fly with American Airlines, but it doesn't require a full commitment to the airline thanks to its manageable annual fee (after the first cardholder year).

The is a mid-tier airline card with standout perks like free checked bags, no-additional-fee employee cards and Loyalty Points on spending.

Given that checking bags with American costs at least $45 per bag one-way, you can practically recoup this card's annual fee after the first cardholder year by checking a bag on three one-way flights. If you travel with an employee or another companion, it becomes easy to extract even more value — assuming you would otherwise pay for checked bags.

You'll need to spend $30,000 in a cardholder year to earn a companion certificate ($99 plus taxes and fees). That's an incredibly high spending requirement for a mid-tier card that earns just 1 mile per dollar on nonbonus purchases.

The card includes basic business management resources, but its benefits still lean heavily toward American Airlines travel rather than premium business tools.

Remember that you'll earn 1 Loyalty Point per dollar spent on all purchases, helping you get closer to elite status with American.

When it's time to redeem the miles you earn, your best bet is to book award flights. In addition to flights operated by American Airlines, you can use your miles on partner airlines such as Alaska Airlines or Finnair.

TPG credit cards writer Becky Pokora recently redeemed 55,000 miles for a one-way trip from Johannesburg (JNB) to Berlin (BER) in business class on Oneworld partner Qatar Airways for travel in 2027. That’s more than 14 hours in a premium cabin for fewer miles than you can earn through the card’s introductory offer.

You can also use your AAdvantage miles for flight upgrades, hotels, car rentals, vacation packages, cruises and Admirals Club passes. Aside from cabin upgrades on flights, these options generally provide less value than award flights.

The Citi / AAdvantage Business will become more useful after its 2027 refresh, but its annual fee is rising from $99 to $150. That makes it harder to justify if you don’t use the new inflight or Global Entry or TSA PreCheck credits.

Most purchases will still earn just 1 mile per dollar, and only one of the three selectable business categories can earn 2 miles per dollar at a time.

Related: Which AAdvantage card is right for you? Here are 3 options you should consider

The AAdvantage Business and Platinum Select share most of their airline perks, so the decision comes down to whether you need a business account, which bonus categories fit your spending and whether you value the Business card’s companion certificate or the Platinum Select’s new lifestyle perks.

The Platinum Select, though, has stronger rewards on restaurant spending and will add bonus spending categories for select streaming services, electric vehicle charging and eligible rides and rails purchases beginning Jan. 24, 2027.

You'll still get free checked bags, preferred boarding, Loyalty Points on card spending and inflight savings with the Platinum Select.

Choose the Citi / AAdvantage Business if you want to separate your business expenses, add employee cards and earn bonus miles on more business-specific purchases, like telecommunications, U.S. advertising, U.S. shipping or office supplies.

The Platinum Select is the better fit if you don’t need a business account. Because their core American Airlines benefits overlap, most travelers won’t need both.

The Citi / AAdvantage Business is worth it for small-business owners who fly American often enough to use the airline benefits. The addition of statement credits toward eligible inflight purchases could provide more value to offset the annual fee, even though that cost is going up in 2027.

At $150 after the first year, it will be less compelling for cardholders who already receive similar American Airlines perks from another card or via elite status. It’s also not ideal as an everyday spending card, since most purchases earn just 1 mile per dollar spent.

For a limited time, new cardholders can earn 75,000 bonus miles after spending $5,000 on purchases in the first five months from account opening. This offer is the highest we've seen in the card’s recent public offers, making it an excellent time to apply.

According to TPG's November 2026 valuations, these bonus miles are worth $1,088.

The upcoming refresh will make the Citi / AAdvantage Business more versatile—but also more expensive. Its new business bonus category and statement credits can justify the upcharge for frequent American flyers who will use them naturally. If you already have an AAdvantage card, you might not find the AAdvantage Business as compelling. But if this is your first foray into American Airlines' cards, it's worth giving the AAdvantage Business a shot.
