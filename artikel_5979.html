American Airlines has dropped its lawsuit over Chicago's 2025 decision to reallocate O'Hare gate space to United, days after the Illinois Appellate Court's First District upheld a Cook County judge's refusal to temporarily block the move. The reallocation, effective October 1, 2025, cost American more than 500 feet of gate space — about four narrow-body gates — leaving it operating out of seven fewer, while United gained about four and a half. Delta lost three gates to Southwest, and three other airlines lost one each. American has since added three gates at O'Hare, two acquired from Spirit and one earned by growing at the airport, and said it will keep investing in Chicago. The fight matters because Chicago sits at the center of American's profitability push alongside New York and Los Angeles, markets with large shares of premium and corporate traffic. United CEO Scott Kirby has said he wants O'Hare to be a one-hub airport with at least 1,000 daily United flights and another 15 international destinations, while FAA flight caps at O'Hare limit how fast he can get there.
