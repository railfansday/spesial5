AC Milan 5-1 Frosinone: Counter-attacking clinic sees Rossoneri run riot

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

AC Milan put on a show for their fans at San Siro on Sunday evening as they beat Frosinone 5-1 to move back into the top four.

It was a game that promised entertainment from the early stages, and Milan took command thanks to an own-goal from Gabriele Calvani and a much-needed goal from Goncalo Ramos, before Patrizio Masini scored to halve the deficit.

The Rossoneri ran away with things in the second half, with Ardon Jashari and Alphadjo Cisse netting off the bench before Christian Pulisic got a goal of his own to add some gloss to the scoreline. There were chances to make it an even bigger margin, but five would have to do.

Given that Lazio, Roma, Como and Juventus had all won prior to kick-off, it was important that Milan matched their results to keep up the pressure at the top end of the table, and they did so in emphatic fashion.

Ruben Amorim made just one change to the starting line-up from the side that won against Genoa before the break, with Alphadjo Cisse dropping out and Davide Bartesaghi coming in. It was uncertain from the line-up named what the system would be, as has often been the case with the Portuguese coach so far.

Frosinone started the game better and called Mike Maignan into action inside the first minute with a close-range header that was saved, though the flag was up. Then, a deflected strike from Masini on the edge of the box ended up scraping the upright and going behind for a corner.

However, it was Milan who struck first at San Siro. Luka Modric deserves the plaudits for an outstanding through ball to find Adrien Rabiot’s run in behind, and the Frenchman’s square pass was heading towards Christian Pulisic’s run but was turned into his own net by Calvani, leaving Palmisani rooted.

The frantic pace of the early stages continued, as a lovely exchange between Diego Moreira and Pulisic saw a threatening cross turned behind. A few minutes later, Yunus Musah found Moreira who had a shot deflected for a corner, which Goncalo Ramos rose and met well but headed over the bar.

Frosinone kept providing reminders that they too pose a threat going forward, such as on the quarter-hour mark when Kvernadze surged down the left and got off a low shot from a narrow angle that Maignan had to parry behind. The corner wasn’t properly dealt with, and Cittadini flashed one across the six-yard box that Rabiot had to put into the stands.

Just like with the first goal, Milan punished Frosinone for not making the most of their spell of pressure. Goncalo Ramos was the man who started and finished a flowing counter-attack, robbing Calvani of possession before running towards goal. Musah’s through ball was run onto by the electric Moreira, who found Rabiot, and the Frenchman’s cut-back was tucked away by the Portuguese.

The two-goal lead was very quickly halved though, as Frosinone got the goal their pressure had threatened. Kvernadze’s run down the left and cut-back from the byline was scuffed onwards by Ghedjemis into the path of Masini, who toe-poked it in at the far post.

In the 34th minute, there was a moment that enraged the Milan players, staff and fans. The referee blew his whistle for a shirt pull on Rabiot just as Milan went through on goal with two men in behind and just the goalkeeper to beat. The official apologised, but it felt like a certain goal had been taken away.

It was a half played at a hundred miles an hour, and it ended that way too. Pulisic flashed an effort over the bar from a good position, while Davide Bartesaghi’s low cross towards Ramos was cut out in the nick of time as Milan continued to get joy down the flanks.

Amorim opted to make an intriguing change at half-time, bringing off Bartesaghi and replacing him with Ardon Jashari. This change in turn saw Musah shift to left-back, a role that he played against Genoa in the last match, and from minute one.

The change proved to be an absolute masterstroke, as Jashari scored inside three minutes. A ball was sent long to Ramos who knocked it down to Rabiot, whose first touch was a lovely through ball to the Swiss midfield. It looked like he had overrun it with the square option not used, but he lashed a shot in at the near post.

Not long after the goal, Ramos gestured towards the bench that he felt some discomfort. He wanted to play through it but Amorim took no risks, opting to bring Alphadjo Cisse on to lead the line.

There was a rather rare occurrence in the 54th minute, as Milan were awarded an indirect free-kick after Palmisani picked up a back pass from Calvani. Modric laid it off to Rabiot, but the goalkeeper made up for his error with a good save.

Continuing the trend of impact substitutions, Cisse got his ninth goal of the season right on the hour mark. A Frosinone corner was cleared and Pulisic found Moreira who had the Italian for company, and he finished the through ball by the Belgian past Palmisani with his trademark composure. The offside flag went up, but after a long check the goal was given.

Milan continued to look like scoring every time they got out of their own half, and Pulisic joined the fun in the 68th minute with a goal of his own. Frosinone again left one man back for a corner that was cleared, allowing the American to easily outpace Terzic with a run from inside his own half, dinking over Palmisani to make it 5-1.

With 20 minutes left on the clock, Amorim brought Strahinja Pavlovic and Pervis Estupinan on for Diawara and Musah. The final change saw Omari Hutchinson replace Pulisic.

Pavlovic looked far from 100% when he came on, appearing dizzy at certain moments. The head coach seemed to suggest coming off so as not to risk things, but he played on.

Milan had chances to make the scoreline even more impressive, with Moreira and Hutchinson squandering a couple of chances on the break. However, at full-time the team were rightly applauded by the San Siro crowd.
