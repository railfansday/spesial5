AD’s Editor Picks: Gifts for Friends and Family Who Love Design

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The holiday season is fast approaching, and we’ve rounded up our picks for the best gifts for friends and family with proclivities for great design—including the picky cousin who couldn’t decide on a sofa for months, your wellness-obsessed mother-in-law, best friend who loves to host, and anyone in between. No matter what winter has in store, we’re committed to making sure you come out smelling as fresh as pine this gifting season. So to relieve some stress and cut out the guesswork, we’ve rounded up 29 AD editor-approved options that’ll show them how much they mean to you (or that might make a nice treat for yourself).

“Ever since I visited Milan this year, I have craved a cool way to serve up aperitivos stateside,” says Baker. The Porta Natalia Criado Astrid Set—handcrafted in Italy—brings a creative edge to the senior editor’s cocktail hours, “all thanks to the geometric food bowls and sliding quartz stones that feel sophisticated yet playful.”

Harrison “hemmed and hawed over these knives for two years” before finally pulling the trigger. She says there’s no other knife like it on the market. “Its craftsmanship speaks for itself, but the signature stripe handle does so much for an otherwise spirit-less kitchen,” she says.

“Ninety percent of the meals I ate at home over the past year were eaten out of an Everyday Bowl,” admits commerce editor Audrey Lee. She likes that the design is a nice medium between a traditional bowl and a plate, and how the color options look stacked on her dinnerware shelf. “It’s also a gift that appeals to anyone who’s more picky about the plate than what goes on it.”

“Aesop is my answer to a foolproof gift for pretty much anyone,” says Lee, who’s given the brand’s sets to family members and friends both in and out of the holiday season. “And for anyone reading *wink, wink*, my personal favorite is any variant of the rind concentrate scent.”

This notepad is perfect for writing down any and all to-dos, to-buys, and to-remembers—exactly what Harrison intends for it. “This bedside notepad keeps me off my phone and away from blue light while I’m winding down,” she says. “It also makes my nightstand very literary-looking in the cold light of morning.”

Anyone wanting to buy a foolproof gift for commerce producer Lauren Arzbaecher should head to Anthropologie. “Call me basic, but I never tire of this glassware that regularly makes the rounds on TikTok,” she says. “The colored icons add a sense of personality to a daily drink—I’ve collected the croissant and lemon patterns so far.”

All your gift recipient will have to do is strike a match and light the wick to make them feel like they’re cruising down Highway 1. “Earthy and light, this Big Sur candle instantly transports me to the west coast with notes of fresh eucalyptus and magnolia,” says senior commerce editor Nashia Baker.

Baker calls these sculptural martini glasses “the cherry on top” in her bar cabinet. The cheery pink-and-blue color palette transforms toasts into something more tasteful, and the little cherry on the stem is actually helpful for keeping your hand steady when raising the glass—”a true win if you’re clumsy like me,” says Baker.

“Certainly a splurge gift, though I think worth it for a piece of Lenny Kravitz’s design genius,” says Arzbaecher. This lamp from his recent CB2 collection takes all the airiness of a Noguchi paper lantern and adds a jolt of chrome and marble for something seriously sexy.

This tea library unfolds like a book, filled with green, black and oolong tea varieties, a warming gift for cozy nights in. “Every night my roommates and I exchange glances that mean: time for our nightly cup,” says commerce writer Julia Harrison. “We’ve yet to try one that we aren’t enamored with.”

Explore 1,000 of the world’s most thoughtfully designed objects with this eye-catching coffee table book from Phaidon. A great gift for any design enthusiast, this visually compelling read is filled with beautiful photography and interesting anecdotes about some of our favorite pieces from the Eamese, Alvar and Aino Aalto, Isamu Noguchi, and more.

“This Japanese-designed sewing machine strays away from utilitarian-looking models, so I don’t feel like I have to hide it away,” says Lee. The shopping editor also notices that the petite machine is more compact than previous ones she’s sewn with, but it’s still well-equipped to handle hemming her too-long skirts and other low-maintenance projects.

These espresso cups will be a bright spot in their caffeine routine. Each set is handmade by artist Florence Girod at her workshop in the south of France so no two are the same.

We found the perfect dish to match her Van Cleef alhambra from Anastasio Home. It's made of solid marble and is the perfect way to display her everyday jewelry.

This robe from Lake makes Harrison “feel like a hard-working mother of three, living in a Pacific Northwest logging town, drinking black coffee all day long, and feeding my children baked beans”—a version of herself she’s aspiring to one day.

Pajamas discreet enough to be worn during sunlight hours will be the gift that keeps on giving. Harrison called this Olivia Von Halle set “impossibly high-quality.” While it’s most definitely a splurge, she insists they feel “like a million silk worms made it just for you,”

Mossy hues are an evergreen (pun intended) color scheme in Arzbaecher’s home. “This quilt adds a tranquility to my bedscape. Plus, it’s just weighty enough to serve throughout multiple seasons.” It’s an ideal present for the friend that’s perpetually cold—or maybe even a nice winter bedding upgrade for yourself.

“These sheets feel as close to my aspirational vibe as an object can get—vintage, bohemian, heirloom-quality,” says Harrison. “I want everything from this brand.” Made and embroidered in Jaipur using heritage techniques, it’ll feel like you’re being gifted an heirloom in the making.

Rest easy knowing that anyone who receives this breezy sheet set with cooling eucalyptus fibers will wake up on the right side of the bed. “The cool-to-the-touch nature of the material is ideal for hot sleepers, so I found the experience very nice,” tester Zoë Sessums told us.

“The Clementine Sleep mask’s pillowy soft material leaves me feeling refreshed by morning,” says Baker, so it’s a thoughtful gift for anyone who enjoys more luxurious sleep. She notes that it filters out light in her sunny bedroom “so sleeping in is the only thing on my mind.”

Ezcaray’s “Matisse” scarf feels like a fluffy, mini version of the wool throw blanket Baker curls up with on snow days. “I’ve survived too many NYC winters to underestimate the power of a good scarf,” she says. “This one is warm, beautifully designed, and the first thing I think about gifting friends who struggle when there’s a chill in the air.”

“I bumped into a friend who was modelling this sweater at the Eckhaus Latta x J.Crew party last fashion week and was instantly smitten,” says Lee. It’s made of 100% wool to stay cozy in the coldest winter temps, and the colorful palette makes it suit pretty much anything else in your wardrobe. “Be prepared to get stopped a million times from friends and strangers wanting to know where it’s from,” she warns.

These mules add print to a plain T-shirt and jeans outfit without overwhelming it. “Unlike other mules I’ve tried, these don’t seem to have the issue of flying off my foot with every step,” adds Arzbaecher. While AD’s producer is partial to the calf hair version, there are also lots of suede colorways for a sleek alternative.

Lee reaches for this necklace-scarf hybrid from Kara Yoo at least once a week since it can add flair to a basic sweater or looks sleek peeping out from a collared shirt. “The pendant can hang from different lengths, or you can loop the silk cord through just one side, so it feels like a different accessory every time,” she says.

The Petit Moyen Messenger from Clare V. has a permanent place in Lee’s packing list because of its versatile shape that can easily fit in overstuffed luggage. “Unlike some of my other bags, I don’t need to stuff my Moyen with my socks and T-shirts to keep its shape in my carry on,” she says. “It comes in many solid-colored options, but I’m partial to the two-tone checkerboard woven designs that have dimension.”

“Don’t sleep on Quince’s cashmere wearables,” says Lee. She told us this quarter zip looks like it could cost double its price tag—and who doesn’t like to get a little cashmere for Christmas? “The jewel tone-enthusiast in me is drawn to burgundy and storm blue, but truffle brown and ivory are great gift ideas for minimalists, and classic additions to any wardrobe,” she says.

Lee’s holiday wishlist also consists of silver or white gold charms that she can use to accessorize Mejuri’s chain necklace—a perfect gift in and of itself for jewelry lovers. “I was given this necklace earlier in the year and wear it multiple times a week. Since it’s easy to attach charms and pendants to the timeless silhouette, it feels like 100 necklaces in one.”

Lee can “hardly think of an occasion where a pair of these classic Pleats Please trousers by Issey Miyake aren’t appropriate.” She says they’re the perfect buy for the effortlessly cool, quiet luxury crowd since she’s worn them to formal events, to the office, and “on busy errand days with a T-shirt and sneakers—they’re that comfortable.”

With a unique open-ear silhouette, the nwm Ones challenge traditional headphone design using Sound Zone Technology that allows for complete auditory awareness—meaning your gift recipient can hear their own voice and surroundings while still listening to music. “These were hands-down my go-to headphones during the summer because they're so breathable,” says Lee. She added that “it's also really nice being able to hear your surroundings, especially if you live in a place like NYC.”
