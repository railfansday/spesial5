Legendary Photographer Ellen Von Unwerth Says ‘There’s No Money Left in Fashion Photography’

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Legendary Photographer Ellen Von Unwerth Says ‘There’s No Money Left in Fashion Photography’

Ellen von Unwerth, one of the most in-demand fashion photographers working today, says there is no money left in the industry today.

For more than four decades, Von Unwerth has remained one of the most influential figures in fashion photography, widely recognized for her bold and playful images of women.

However, in an interview with Fashion Network France, Von Unwerth has revealed how the resources allocated to fashion photography have dramatically changed and how the industry is no longer as lucrative as it once was.

“These days, there’s no money left in fashion photography,” Von Unwerth tells Fashion Network France. “It’s nothing like it used to be, when there were 10 pages in magazines and newspapers, and posters everywhere. You don’t see that anymore. You don’t see the big billboards anymore.”

Von Unwerth explains that fashion photography has become less financially rewarding as brands increasingly direct their budgets toward events and influencers rather than traditional print ad campaigns. She says photographers now face longer waits for payment, tighter deadlines, and less creative freedom than they had earlier in her career.

“Now, the money goes into events, influencers… So there’s almost no money left,” she adds. “You have to wait a very long time to get paid. And then they want the photos tomorrow — or even yesterday! Before, we were on our own on set. It was really just us, the photographers. We were the bosses; we did our own thing. It was our kingdom; it was secret.”

Von Unwerth says fashion photographers have increasingly lost control over their shoots, with multiple behind-the-scenes [BTS] photographers and teams now working on the same set. She says this makes it difficult to establish who actually took a particular image once it is published. She compares modern fashion shoots to film productions, where a director oversees several camera operators rather than capturing everything themselves.

“Nowadays, there’s the BTS photographer. Then there are the BTS of the BTS. There are three other photographers doing different styles,” she tells Fashion Network France. “And I fought against that for a long time, but I’ve given up. Now there are five people taking pictures behind me. And you don’t know, when it comes out: “Is it my photo or is it someone else’s?” In the end, I just say it’s my photo! It’s like in the film industry: there’s a director, who oversees everything, and there are other cameramen filming.”

Recalling a shoot for beauty brand L’Oréal, Von Unwerth says she became frustrated and ordered everyone to leave after discovering that around 50 people behind her were taking exactly the same photograph.

Von Unwerth’s comments come weeks after a report revealed that fashion photo studios are closing down as brands increasingly embrace AI thanks to the big savings offered by the technology.
