Asylum seekers on final ‘one in, one out’ UK-France flight feel they are being ‘punished’

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Many of those onboard are being deported for second time after fleeing threats from people smugglers in France

Many of the asylum seekers on Tuesday’s final “one in, one out” deportation flight are people who returned to the UK fleeing threats from smugglers after they were already forcibly returned to France.

Hundreds of asylum seekers who were sent back to France are thought to have returned to the UK, citing threats from smugglers who said their families still owed them money for the Channel crossing.

Asylum seekers who spoke to the Guardian described being punched, beaten or raped by the smugglers. Some said smugglers had caught them while they slept on the streets and forced them to work for them in conditions of modern slavery in a migrant camp in Calais, cleaning the smugglers’ tents and carrying water or firewood.

The charity Medical Justice, which has provided support to people detained for one in, one out, called the pilot scheme “a disaster”.

The one in, one out scheme involved forcibly returning hundreds of people who had travelled to the UK on small boats to France in exchange for bringing a similar number from northern France to the UK to claim asylum.

One asylum seeker with a ticket for Tuesday’s flight said he was captured by the smugglers after being returned to France by the Home Office earlier this year. He said he was a political dissident in Iran, and believed he would be executed if he was returned there.

“I came back to the UK a second time because the smugglers caught me in France,” he said. “I was sleeping on the streets in Paris because France did not accept my asylum case.

“Many of the people who have tickets for Tuesday’s flight are people like me, who came back to the UK a second time. I believe those of us who came back to the UK a second time are being punished by being sent back to France a second time. I believe that 100%.”

He said that although the Home Office stated that sufficient lawyers were available for asylum seekers detained for France, he and others had struggled to access good quality legal advice.

“Friends paid privately for a lawyer for me. The lawyer promised me that 24 hours before the flight, they would challenge my removal, but he has done nothing,” he said. “They do not even answer the phone although I have tried to call them 10 times.

“My mind is broken. I am scared that when I am returned to France on Tuesday, the smugglers will catch me and kill me.

“When the guards came to take me to the plane for France last time, three or four of them surrounded me and made me feel like a criminal. I have committed no crime apart from coming to UK to claim asylum.

“When I slept on the street last time the weather was warmer in France. Now it is too cold to sleep outside. I don’t know what I’m going to do.”

A second asylum seeker with a ticket for the flight, who also returned to the UK after being sent back to France, said he knew of dozens of people due to fly on Tuesday.

“All of us have bad solicitors who are not helping us,” he said. “One in, one out finished on 1 October. I do not understand why the Home Office wants to continue sending us to France.”

John Tuckett, the independent chief inspector of borders and immigration, said in a report published last week that it was not known how the Home Office would measure the success or failure of the one in, one out scheme, despite it being given the highest operational priority, with regular updates supplied to ministers and 10 Downing Street.

He cited a ministerial submission which stated that the aim of the scheme was to demonstrate that dangerous journeys on small boats were not a viable option for entry into the UK, with the aim of undermining the organised immigration crime model. Since the scheme ended on 1 October, 4,786 people have crossed the Channel in 69 small boats, according to government data.

A Medical Justice spokesperson said: “One in, one out has been a disaster, causing avoidable suffering to a vulnerable group and it has failed in the Home Office’s stated objective.

“The Home Office must start to learn lessons and put an end to dangerous detention conditions.”

Seema Syeda, of the Joint Council for the Welfare of Immigrants, said: “The UK government has forced people fleeing war, climate breakdown and persecution to take murderous and traumatising journeys across the Channel, only to treat them with brutality once they have arrived here, retraumatising them with detention and deportation.”

A Home Office spokesperson said: “We welcome the inspector’s recognition of the strong operational delivery of the UK-France pilot, which successfully demonstrated that a returns arrangement of this kind can work.

“The message was clear: if you returned to the UK after being transferred to France under the pilot, you would be sent back.

“The pilot has now concluded and we are taking forward lessons learned as part of wider efforts to strengthen returns. Any future operational decisions, including on evaluation and reporting, will be considered in the usual way.”
