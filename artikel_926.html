Ipswich also won three of their first 10 league matches of the season on their return to the top flight, but are winless in the last four. They went into the international break on the back of a 2-1 loss against Bournemouth, manager Gary O'Neil's old club.

The Tractor Boys have only won once away from Portman Road and it's noteworthy that the opponents on that occasion were Crystal Palace, now bottom of the Premier League with more than a quarter of the season played.

Last time these teams met was in February 2025, when Spurs won 4-1 in Suffolk to edge Ipswich closer to an increasingly inevitable relegation. Ipswich won the previous four matches against Spurs, all in the Premier League but mostly more than two decades ago.

Brennan Johnson, Djed Spence and Omari Hutchinson accounted for four of the five goals last time around. All of them have since moved on but Dejan Kulusevski, the last scorer, played for Sweden last week for the first time after nearly two years out with a knee injury.

Kulusevski's return coincided with Spurs' upturn in fortunes. His first three appearances were in Spurs' first three wins, though he played a total of 17 minutes against Coventry City, Chelsea and Palace.

He managed 25 minutes against Leeds and another half an hour in Sweden's Nations League victory over Bosnia and Herzegovina in Solna last Saturday before briefly featuring once again against Poland in midweek.

Getting key players fit is surely central to De Zerbi's hopes of keeping Spurs on track this season. There are a handful of players whose availability for this match is in doubt and it says everything about Spurs that this should be seen as a boost.

Savio, Lucas Bergvall, Mykhailo Mudryk and Martin Dubravka are among the players whose fitness has been tracked closely this week.

If De Zerbi's cracked the code again, Spurs are justifiably favourites today. There will be tough games ahead but a home win is the likeliest outcome based on recent form.
