Self-Employed Health Insurance – Estimate Your Income Carefully, Then Deduct the Premiums

If you’re self-employed and can’t join a spouse’s job-based plan, buy health insurance through HealthCare.gov or your state’s Marketplace, where the premium tax credit depends on your expected net income for the coverage year. Deduct the premiums you pay, and update your estimate as income changes, since you now repay any excess credit in full.

Price a spouse’s job-based plan first, since being able to join one usually rules out the credit and the deduction. Workers with job-based family coverage pay about 27% of the premium on average, KFF’s 2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey found.

Otherwise, the credit is based on the net income you expect for the coverage year, and your tax return checks it against what you earned. Starting with tax year 2026, the IRS makes you repay any excess credit in full. Household income above 400% of the federal poverty level gets no credit, so ending the year above that line means repaying every dollar of credit you received.

Build the estimate from the profit you expect after business expenses, and update it on HealthCare.gov or your state’s Marketplace as soon as your expected profit changes. A freelance designer projecting $45,000 of net income buys a plan with the credit. When a big client signs in June, she raises her estimate that week, which cuts her monthly credit now instead of leaving an excess to repay. Near the 400% line, take only part of the credit in advance and claim the rest you qualify for when you file.

A lean year can qualify you for Medicaid, which the same application checks. A professional association or freelancers group may offer a plan too, but the credit applies only to Marketplace plans. Outside the Marketplace, short-term plans may not cover a condition you already have. Medical discount plans and health care sharing ministries, which pool members’ money for one another’s bills, aren’t insurance at all, the National Association of Insurance Commissioners warns.

At tax time, deduct the part of the premium the credit didn’t cover, as long as the business made a profit. Since 2026, a bronze plan, the tier with the highest deductibles, also lets you save pretax money in a health savings account (HSA).

Each time you pay quarterly estimated taxes, compare your profit so far with the income on your Marketplace application, and update the estimate that day.
