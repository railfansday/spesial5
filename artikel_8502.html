Capital One Venture X Business Review – Pays for Itself on Bookings Through Capital One

Get the Capital One Venture X Business if your business will book at least $300 of travel a year through Capital One Business Travel. Its $300 credit and 10,000 anniversary miles return $400 toward travel each year, more than the $395 fee. Otherwise, take the $95 Venture Business, which earns the same 2 miles per dollar.

Premium travel cards charge a big fee up front and leave a business to earn it back through perks it may never use. The Capital One Venture X Business ties that payback to one habit, booking trips through Capital One Business Travel, since its $300 yearly credit works nowhere else. With the 10,000 miles it adds after each account anniversary, which Capital One values at $100 toward travel, the card returns $400 a year from its second year against its $395 fee.

Capital One, the McLean, Virginia, bank behind the Venture and Spark cards, opened the Venture X Business to all eligible business owners in September 2023. The Fortune 500 company says it serves more than 100 million customers, and it calls Capital One Business Travel the only free booking platform from a traditional card issuer designed for business owners.

The Venture X Business costs $300 a year more than the $95 Venture Business, which earns the same 2 miles per dollar on everyday purchases. The extra $300 buys higher travel earning, a yearly credit, anniversary miles, lounge access, and a bigger welcome offer.

Every purchase earns at least 2 miles per dollar, with no cap or categories to track, and employee cards earn for the business. Bookings through Capital One Business Travel earn more:

The higher rates cover only what you pay the booking site, so hotel incidentals and upgrades earn 2 miles per dollar. At a cent a mile toward travel, $2,000 of hotel stays booked there earn 20,000 miles, worth $200, while the same stays booked directly earn 4,000 miles, worth $40.

The booking site sells flights on more than 200 airlines, Southwest among them, and rooms at more than 1 million hotels. If a flight, hotel, or rental car you booked there turns up cheaper elsewhere within 24 hours of booking, Capital One refunds the difference on eligible bookings as a travel credit. Owners can also cap the prices and fare classes employees book and name admins to manage trips.

Each account year brings a $300 credit for Capital One Business Travel purchases, usable on one booking or several. It expires the day before the next account anniversary, earns no miles, and comes back if its booking is canceled before then. After each anniversary, starting with the first, the account also gets 10,000 bonus miles.

Together they return $400 a year against the $395 fee, but only the miles come without effort. Count the credit only if the business would book $300 of travel there anyway, the test any annual fee credit card has to pass. In the first year, before any anniversary miles, the credit offsets all but $95 of the fee, which the welcome offer can cover.

The perks, mostly for the primary cardholder:

The two free Priority Pass guests set it apart from the personal Venture X, whose cardholders pay $35 for each.

New cardholders can earn 150,000 miles after spending $40,000 on purchases in the first 4 months, plus another 150,000 miles and a $1,000 Capital One Business Travel credit at $120,000 in that window. The first tier alone takes about $10,000 of card spending a month. The $1,000 credit expires 16 months after the account opens, and current or former Capital One business cardholders may not qualify.

A mile is worth a cent when it pays for travel, the rate behind Capital One’s $100 figure for the anniversary miles. Get that rate by paying with miles on Capital One Business Travel, or by charging travel anywhere to the card and redeeming miles against it within 90 days, in full or in part.

Transfers can get more from a mile. Miles move to more than 15 airline and hotel programs, most airlines at one to one, so 1,000 miles become 1,000 Air Canada Aeroplan points. Emirates, EVA Air, and Japan Airlines give 1.5 miles for every 2, JetBlue 3 points for every 5, and Accor 1 point for every 2, while I Prefer Hotel Rewards gives 2 points per mile.

Covering a $400 fare at a cent a mile takes 40,000 miles, so transferring to an airline partner pays off when the airline prices the same seat at fewer miles, counting any taxes it charges in cash. Transfers can’t be undone, so confirm the seat first. Cash and gift cards pay rates Capital One sets and can change, and miles last as long as the account stays open.

The Venture X Business works like a charge card, with no preset spending limit and a balance due in full each month. What you can spend changes with your spending, payment history, and credit profile, and it isn’t unlimited. Pay Over Time lets you carry part of the balance, up to a limit Capital One sets, at a 25.99% variable APR (annual percentage rate).

There’s no foreign transaction fee. A late payment costs 2.99% of the unpaid part of the minimum payment, a cash advance costs $5 or 5%, whichever is greater, and balance transfers aren’t allowed.

Capital One asks for excellent credit, and the person who applies is liable for every charge along with the business, employee cards included. Late or missed payments may appear on that person’s personal credit report. Sole proprietors can apply with a Social Security number instead of an EIN, the federal tax ID number most businesses use.

For a business that will book its travel through Capital One, the Venture X Business has no net cost from its second year, since its $300 credit and 10,000 anniversary miles return more than the $395 fee. Every purchase also earns at least 2 miles per dollar, the owner gets Capital One’s lounges and Priority Pass with two free guests, and the booking site keeps employees within the owner’s limits.

It’s the wrong card for a business that books with a travel agent or directly with airlines and hotels, since its credit would go unused, or one that needs months to pay off big purchases. A business that won’t use Capital One’s booking site does better with the $95 Venture Business, which earns the same 2 miles per dollar on everyday purchases for $300 less a year. Before applying, add up last year’s flights, hotels, and rental cars. If $300 or more of them could have gone through Capital One Business Travel, the Venture X Business pays for itself.
