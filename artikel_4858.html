Will Bitcoin’s big rebound hold? More tampered Ledgers found: Hodler’s Digest

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Bitcoin rebounds sharply from its new cycle low, but is $55,111 really the bottom? Ledger investigates new reports of tampered devices in Berlin and the UAE.

Bitcoin saw a sharp recovery on the weekend to $61,034, marking an 11% rebound from its new cycle low around $55,111 on Coinbase. But will it hold?

It wasn’t a crypto only crash however, as concerns grow over the sustainability of the debt-financed AI build out. The tech-heavy Nasdaq fell another 5.3% on Friday and is on the verge of a bear market, having dropped almost 20% from its October peak. The S&P 500 fell 8.6% last week, for the 12th worst week since 1952, and gold lost another 4.3% in the past 24 hours, to be 21.6% down since September.

At the time of writing Bitcoin’s price was holding steady around $60,750 but there are plenty of predictions it has further to fall. Many point to historical parallels with previous four year cycles, which bottomed after the US mid-terms.

Despite the price being 71% below its USD market, Kraken said everything was fine, every order was processed correctly and its security teams found no evidence of a breach or compromised accounts. “Executed trades stand,” Kraken said.

Crypto influencer Cryptobull offered a tiny bit of hopium when he pointed out that Bitcoin’s Market Value to Realized Valu (MVRV) peak this cycle only reached 2.29 times above its cost basis, and if you extrapolate that to the cycle low, it would come in at around 1.03 times the cost basis of $53,700. Which would be almost exactly the $55,130 price that Bitcoin just hit.

Unfortunately, cycle theorist Benjamin Cowen says that in bear markets Bitcoin usually ends up much lower than the realized price — and drops down to the balanced price, which Coinglass puts at $38.7K.

Ledger has warned customers who purchased hardware wallets in Berlin since January 2026 to contact its support team as the company investigates reports of tampered devices in Germany and the United Arab Emirates.

Several suspicious devices have been reported in Berlin, while two customers in Dubai reported losing a combined $604,000 in cryptocurrency.

Ledger said the incidents were connected to the same distribution-channel tampering campaign first investigated in Southeast Asia in October. Ledger is also offering physical inspections, including X-ray analysis, at its factory in Vierzon, France, for customers concerned their devices may have been compromised.

Last week Ethlabs was pushing to make Ethereum post quantum and AI proof in 2027. This week’s All Core Devs Call suggested the chain may not even see Glamsterdam activated until May 2027.

However the devs sound as if they’re leaning towards testing it out without making any changes to the gas repricing Ethereum Improvement Proposals (EIPs), and then activating it in mid-January to give companies and projects time to upgrade affected contracts. They punted a decision until next week about testing the fork on Hoodi in December.If they do decide to make modifications to the gas pricing EIPs activation could be pushed until late February or early March, or even April or May.

The call also discussed the possibility of modifying the EIPs in the Hegota fork to prioritize post quantum features, although several devs argued that reducing the upgrade’s scope would offer little benefit unless it also shortened the rollout timeline.

Wallets linked to the Binance hack moved nearly $600 million in Ether over two days, with some of the stolen funds routed through cross-chain swap protocols, according to blockchain analysts.

The attackers moved about $248 million ETH to new wallets on Thursday and another $346 million ETH, on Friday. Attackers routed part of the funds through cross-chain protocols THORChain and Chainflip, according to blockchain data provider Onchain Lens.

THORChain’s 24-hour swap volume soared to $239 million on Friday, pushing the protocol’s earnings to $243,000, according to THORChain’s dashboard.

Retail investors traded $772 million in Anthropic shares on its Nasdaq debut Thursday, but their net purchases totaled just $60 million, about half the $118 million they put into SpaceX on its first day of trading in June.

Anthropic’s first-day retail trading volume topped SpaceX’s $669 million and was the highest for any market debut in Vanda Research’s data, which go back to 2012, Yahoo Finance reported.

Volume counts both purchases and sales, while net buying measures how much purchases exceeded sales.

ANT closed its first day of trading up 17% on the IPO price, but the stock fell to just 7% above the IPO price on day two amid a broad market sell off.

At the end of the week, Bitcoin (BTC) is down 6.5% to trade at $60,815, Ethereum (ETH) is down 5.9% to trade at $1,713 and XRP (XRP) is down 4.3% to $0.02. The total market cap is at $2.08 trillion according to CoinMarketCap.

Among the biggest 100 cryptocurrencies, the top three altcoin winners of the week are Pieverse (PIEVERSE) with a 91.2% gain, Venice Token (VVV) on 28.74%, and Falcon Finance (FF) on 4.1%.

The top three altcoin losers of the week are Sky (SKY) which was down 22.6%, Artificial Superintelligence Alliance (FET) down 18.4% and Aptos (APT) down 16.8%.

Bitcoin carry trades could regain their edge over US Treasury bills in 2027 as demand for leverage recovers, according to digital asset bank Sygnum.

In its 2027 outlook, it forecasts that the annualized premium on three-month Bitcoin futures at the Chicago Mercantile Exchange would average more than 2% above three-month US Treasury bill yields next year.

Fabian Dori, chief investment officer at Sygnum, told Cointelegraph that returns could recover as investors become more willing to take risks and fewer hedge funds compete for the same profits.

Senator Ruben Gallego says the crypto industry put too much money into Republican election campaigns.

The International Organization of Securities Commissions (IOSCO) warned that upgrading blockchains to withstand quantum attacks could slow transactions and raise fees.

In a report released Thursday, IOSCO said some quantum-resistant digital signatures take up far more space than those used to authorize transactions today. Larger signatures mean fewer transactions can fit in each block, which could increase congestion, push up fees and require more storage.

IOSCO cited an experiment in which a test version of Solana ran about 90% slower with quantum-resistant signatures.

The US Senate passed legislation Thursday that would prohibit federally regulated prediction markets from offering contracts tied to war, terrorism, assassination and events primarily determined by a person’s death, sending the bill to the House for consideration.

The DEATH BETS Act, introduced by Democratic Sen. Adam Schiff in March, passed by voice vote on Thursday after lawmakers adopted an amendment by unanimous consent.

Sky Protocol processed more than $2 billion in stablecoin redemptions on Thursday, the same day a CoinDesk investigation linked $518 million in transactions through the protocol to illicit activity.

The withdrawals came amid concerns raised by the investigation that Circle could freeze USDC held by Sky. Sky said on Friday in a statement that USDS maintained its $1 peg throughout the withdrawals and challenged the investigation, pointing to a review by Block Analitica.
