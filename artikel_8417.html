Te Araroa day 11: Failing to find a cafe

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

19.5km, the Stone Store (Kerikeri) to Waitangi Holiday Park

We got moving a bit earlier than the day before, as our kind host Amelia was going to drop us at the start of our walk on her way to work. The three of us squashed our packs into the boot of her little Mazda, and I tried valiently to shut the door. After a number of attempts, slamming it progressively harder each time, the ‘open door’ light went off and we were away.

We were back at the Stone Store where we had left off at around 8.30am. Zoe was maybe going to meet us at the start, so Ruby called her to find out where she was at. As it turned out, she could see us from the top of the hill we were about to walk up.

The track went a little bit further along the river and up the backs of people’s houses before popping out at the road. There was a pavement for the first bit, which suddenly ended in classic NZ fashion. But this did provide a photo opportunity, as there was a ‘Te Araroa trail users’ warning sign that I wanted a picture with. There were some earlier, but I had been alone at the time. In all honesty, this was not a very good spot for a photo op. But no hikers were harmed and we proceeded down the road for another kilometre or so.

Soon after, we turned into a pine forest. The trail went along some gravel roads, which were not the most exciting walking but were at least better than the main road. Despite warnings about logging trucks, we saw only two cars.

The view for the day: gravel road and pine forest

As we walked, Ruby mentioned that there was a nice cafe at the Waitangi treaty grounds, which we would be passing later in the day. There was also a little wooden sign for a cafe which was supposed to be open Friday to Monday. This day was Monday but it was difficult to believe that there would be a cafe along these roads! Eventually we stopped for lunch at a bench. Part of the route went through a mountain bike park, which did have a cafe at the entrance. But alas, it was closed. We were about to continue on when Lucy appeared from the forest. She joined the rest of us as we got out to the paved road.

Most people were planning to walk to the Paihia Top 10 holiday park. But Lucy wanted to go to the Waitangi treaty grounds, and I had an exciting side-quest for the next day! So we were both going to stay at the Waitangi holiday park instead. Eventually we got to the treaty grounds, and attempted to find the cafe. But it was closed! We weren’t having much luck with cafes today.

Approaching the Waitangi Treaty Grounds, via the golf club; ‘Where the tee meets the sea’

For anyone interested: The Treaty of Waitangi is considered the founding document of New Zealand, although it isn’t without its controversies. It was signed in 1840 in Waitangi, near Paihia, and the grounds today are a historical site you can visit. I think there is a museum and such, although I haven’t been inside myself.

Soon we reached the turning for the holiday park Lucy and I were going to stay at. It was only 1.45pm and I hoped we weren’t too early to check in! I was assigned a site at the far end of the campground, and found the neighbouring site occupied by a tent so small it could only belong to a thru-hiker. Indeed, the occupant was another British woman called Georgia, who is doing TA. Something I’ve been noticing is that there are a lot more solo women than I expected. I thought we would be a bit of a rare breed, but in fact the majority of other hikers I’ve met so far have been solo women! At Blackbridge campsite the other night, there were about 12 people and only two were men. There was one couple, and the other 9 were women hiking alone. Which is pretty cool!

I got set up and decided to go to the nearby supermarket to get dinner for that night and the next. I figured that I might as well splash out on some heavy stuff, since I wouldn’t need to carry it far. In the Woolworths I ended up running into Annabelle and Mauritz. Their group had struggled to find accomodation in Kerikeri so ended up taking a rest day in Paihia instead.

I met a friendly cat on the way back from the supermarket

Back at the campsite, I was feeling a bit flat. I had got so used to having people around me for the last few days that it felt odd to be alone. Combined with the short day, I felt a bit purposeless. But I knew I wanted to get some blogs done, so I got stuck in and the time went by reasonably quickly.

After dinner, I went back to my tent and found that the picnic table near my site had been colonised by some British boys and an American from either side of me. I had spoken to the American earlier, as his pack, tent, and the fact he arrived on foot had me thinking he was a hiker. But it seemed he was just travelling around, and sounded very impressed when I told him about TA. Sometimes I get so engrossed in the world of thru-hiking that I forget that it is actually a pretty cool and impressive thing to do. It’s nice to be reminded of that sometimes!

I hoped the three of them wouldn’t be too loud or keep talking too late, as they were literally two metres from my tent. I wanted to do some more writing but I couldn’t help listening in to their conversation. It seemed that the British boys were on their gap year after going to boarding school with some of the royal family. The American was travelling around the world after earning a lot of money doing a lucrative job – I didn’t quite catch what, but I think it was something in service of the super-rich. It was funny to hear their opinions and observations as recently-arrived travellers to New Zealand. They all sounded very young – and I know, I’m not exactly old either! But when I think back to how I was when I just finished school, I can appreciate how much I’ve matured.

Anyway, they didn’t talk too late, and I was able to write more blogs and go to bed at a reasonable time.
