File your own return with tax software when it holds W-2 pay, the standard deduction, common credits, or a simple side business. Pay a tax pro for a rental, an inheritance, a business sale, a home sale with a big profit, income from several states, stock pay, or an IRS letter you don’t understand.

Tax software asks you questions and does the math, and on a simple return you can answer them from your W-2, 1099s, and last year’s return.

H&R Block files a simple return free online, state included. A federal return that itemizes deductions, such as mortgage interest, costs $65 there, against $300 to $600 on average at accounting firms in a 2024 National Society of Accountants survey. Filers under an income limit can file a federal return free through IRS Free File.

A pro is worth paying where the right answer takes judgment, such as how much of a rental’s price was land or what an inherited house was worth when its owner died. Software accepts whatever you type.

A rental shows why a pro can be worth the fee. Say you’re in the 22% tax bracket, you start renting out a house in January, the building alone cost $220,000, and the rent nets $10,000 after other costs. Depreciation, the yearly deduction for a building’s wear, comes to about $7,700 that first year, which cuts your tax by about $1,700. Skip it, and you lose that $1,700 unless you fix it later, yet the IRS still adds the skipped depreciation to your taxable gain when you sell.

For a middle path, H&R Block’s paid tiers, from $65 plus $49 a state, include a tax pro on call, and TurboTax charges $59 to $209 plus state for its version. Or pay an online tax preparation service to do the whole return.

Start your return in tax software, and book a pro the moment it asks a question you’d have to guess at.
