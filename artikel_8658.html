Cut your wedding music bill by hiring a DJ instead of a band and booking the DJ for the reception hours only. Bring in a music student or two from a local college to play the ceremony, and run a playlist through the venue’s speakers for the cocktail hour.

A reception DJ cost $1,800 on average at 2025 weddings, against $4,500 for a band, according to The Knot’s Real Weddings Study, which surveyed 10,474 couples who married that year.

A band usually means four to six musicians playing for four to six hours, and the longer a band or DJ plays, the more it costs.

A ceremony needs about an hour of music, not four to six, and guests sit and listen through most of it. Put live musicians there and a DJ at the reception.

Many college music departments and conservatories run a referral service that passes event requests to their students. The University of Texas at Austin’s Butler School of Music, for one, typically recommends clients budget about $125 per musician per hour. At that rate, a violin-and-cello duo for an hour-long ceremony costs about $250. Pair that duo with a DJ at the $1,800 average, and the ceremony and reception music comes to about $2,050.

Ask each DJ to quote the reception alone, without the ceremony or cocktail hour, so you pay only for the hours you need a DJ. At a small wedding, a playlist on rented speakers can do the DJ’s job, with a confident friend on the microphone for the entrances and toasts.
