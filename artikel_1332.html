4 Things Mauricio Pochettino Got Badly Wrong in Liverpool vs Man Utd

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

An all-smiles Mauricio Pochettino took his seat in the dugout at Anfield, and just eight minutes after kick-off, he wore a face of despair and realised this Manchester United team have a mountain to climb.

Any hope of a new manager bounce coming into effect for United was quickly extinguished by Leny Yoro's eighth-minute sending off, followed almost immediately by Dominik Szoboszlai's 10th-minute free-kick. Down to ten men, his side conceded a second three minutes later when Florian Wirtz found the back of Senne Lammens' net.

Rio Ngumoha and Victor Munoz piled more misery on the Red Devils, whose new coach has been thrown back into Premier League management in the midst of a crisis. He made some bold decisions heading into today's game against Liverpool, and they played their part in a fifth league defeat of the campaign.

As soon as United released Pochettino's first lineup since his appointment, eyebrows were raised, and Sky Sports' Gary Neville admitted that he'd already conceded defeat upon seeing Matthijs de Ligt chosen in defence. That's not because of the Dutch defender's ability, but rather his lack of football over the past 10 months, having struggled on his return in a 4-1 defeat to Como at the end of October.

De Ligt seems to be shaking off the rust, and starting him at Anfield didn't pan out as Pochettino envisioned. He was largely at fault for Yoro's dismissal, caught way too high up the pitch, allowing Alexander Isak to pounce, burst forward, and buy the foul from De Ligt's defensive partner.

The 27-year-old has endured an injury-ridden spell since joining the club from Bayern Munich in August 2024. A sluggish outing against Liverpool raises question marks over Pochettino's understanding of his squad, especially when picking him over Harry Maguire.

Another head-scratching decision Pochettino made was to start Patrick Dorgu on the left wing. Bryan Mbeumo started over on the right and Matheus Cunha played through the middle, albeit for just 22 minutes before Maguire came on for Mbeumo.

Like De Ligt, Dorgu has similarly been working his way back to full fitness recently after a lengthy spell on the sidelines. The Dane is a versatile option, spending the majority of Ruben Amorim's three-at-the-back tenure as a left wing-back.

That's not to say Dorgu hasn't impressed occasionally over the course of his United career, and he was probably one of the few positives today. Still, prioritising him over Mbeumo, who scored in this fixture last season, and Cunha, whom Pochettino brought off for Joshua Zirkzee, was rather puzzling.

As was the omission of Shea Lacey, who has been catching the eye every time he's been afforded an opportunity. The English wonderkid can surely do no more, yet even with Amad struggling and benched, he seemingly finds himself third in the pecking order.

This brings us onto Pochettino's substitutions, which caused a stir online, especially the decision to hook Cunha for Zirkzee at half-time. The Brazilian has been a saviour in the past, most recently coming to the rescue in a 3-2 comeback win over AS Roma.

Cunha showed unbelievable passion that night, even refusing to leave the pitch after receiving a second yellow card for taking his shirt off while celebrating his winner. Of the attackers available to Pochettino right now, he's the one who can make something happen out of nothing.

Mbeumo fell victim to Yoro's sending off, with his new coach forced to bring Maguire on to address the one-man disadvantage. It's difficult to assess whether he'd have had much influence once Liverpool went two-nil ahead, but he's another forward who can come alive when the going gets tough.

Pochettino needs more time with his squad, and decisions such as starting De Ligt over Maguire are clear evidence of this. It's worth pointing out that the Argentine arrived at Carrington on Wednesday, four days prior to the trip to Anfield, and took his first training session on Thursday.

Before that, he took charge of two final games as the United States national team manager, overseeing wins over Haiti in the CONCACAF Nations League quarter-finals. This isn't so much Pochettino's fault, but for a club sitting 19th and in such a turbulent situation, having him introduced to his squad just four days before a clash with Liverpool is alarming.

United confirmed they came to an agreement with the USMNT for Pochettino to see out his role during the international break, during which only 13 of their 33-man squad were called up. Their official confirmation of his appointment came on November 13th, nine days ago, with the club already languishing in 19th place.

Had they turned to an available coach such as Eddie Howe or Antonio Conte, they surely would have been in the building as soon as the contract was signed. It's a damning example of everything wrong at the club that co-owners INEOS didn't feel it necessary to get Pochettino into Manchester as soon as possible to at least start planning before such a crucial game.
