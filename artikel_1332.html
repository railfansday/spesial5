Football video game EA Sports FC 27 to add AI-generated voice of iconic BBC commentator John Motson to new game - three years after legend's death

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Published: 10:33 EST, 25 November 2026 | Updated: 04:18 EST, 26 November 2026

The voice of the late BBC commentator John Motson will be featured on the EA Sports FC 27 video game through 'a mix of both his original audio and an AI-recreation'.

Motson, who died at the age of 77 in 2023, had served as the lead commentator on BBC's football coverage for decades.

The legendary commentator had also lent his voice to Electronic Arts's FIFA series of games in the 1990s and 2000s.

EA has announced that it has worked with the blessing and in partnership with Motson's family to have him feature as an optional commentary selection in an update of the latest edition of their EA Sports FC game.

The publisher stated its development studio 'has put Motson back into the commentary box with the aid of new AI-tools, bringing a mix of both his original audio and an AI-recreation trained by recordings of the man himself.'

EA said its team worked with 'extensive care and respect to create a digital tribute to one of the game's greatest voices'.

Motson's family gave permission for the late commentator, who died in 2023, to feature in the game

'My father adored being the voice of the game, and we are so grateful to the team at EA SPORTS FC for this touching tribute and preserving his memory in-game,' said Fred Motson, son of John Motson.

'His voice inspired millions of players across generations every time they picked up a controller, and now a whole new set of fans will get to experience his legacy and commentary.'

Motson's son also told the BBC his father would have 'absolutely' wanted to be involved in the game after his death.

The BBC reported that Motson's family were paid for the rights to use his likeness and to recreate his voice in the game.

EA added that AI had been the 'only way' for Motson's voice to feature, as there were no recordings of him talking about modern players.

While Motson's family gave EA's project their blessing, the use of AI to recreate voices has become an increasingly controversial topic.

In August, a group of 80 performers backed the Save Our Voices Now campaign, which is seeking to secure legal protection against technology which can clone a person's voice and make it say anything its operator chooses.

The campaign was founded by actor and voice artist Peter Caulfield and has been backed by stars including Hugh Bonneville, Nicola Coughlan and Matt Lucas.

Campaigners want Britain to follow the example set by Denmark, which has moved to give its citizens full legal control over AI-generated imitations of their face, body and voice.

The protections allow people to demand that any unauthorised content is removed and seek compensation for its misuse.
