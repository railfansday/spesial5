19-Year-Old Gets Probation (+ 6 Months Served) "After Firing over 20 Rounds, Shooting Boy Playing Basketball"

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Mostly law professors | Sometimes contrarian | Often libertarian | Always independent

Darius Drew pleaded guilty on Aug. 10 to aggravated assault and carrying a pistol without a license. Although prosecutors asked Superior Court Judge Jennifer Di Toro for a five-year prison term, she imposed a suspended 24-month sentence and placed him on probation under the Youth Rehabilitation Act, over the government's objection.

According to the government's evidence, Drew rode a Lime bike into an alley in the 700 block of Taylor Street NW on May 11.

Surveillance video showed him firing at a car parked in the alley. Seconds later, Drew fired toward a house near the alley in the 4100 block of Ninth Street NW, striking a boy who was playing basketball at a nearby recreation center. The boy suffered a leg injury.

Drew fired more than 20 rounds from two locations, prosecutors said….

From the opinion in U.S. v. Drew, released Monday by D.C. Superior Court Judge Jennifer A. Di Toro:

On August 10, 2026, Defendant Darius Drew entered a guilty plea to one count of Aggravated Assault and one count of Carrying a Pistol Without a License. Mr. Drew appeared before the Court for a sentencing hearing on November 13, 2026. At the hearing, the Court sentenced Mr. Drew to an aggregate term of incarceration of 24 months followed by 3 years of supervised release, with the execution of all but the 6 months he already served suspended in favor of 18 months of supervised probation.

In addition to the standard conditions of probation, the Court required that Mr. Drew participate in a comprehensive mental health assessment and treatment as deemed necessary by the Court Services and Offender Supervision Agency ("CSOSA"), undergo a substance abuse assessment and complete all treatment deemed necessary by CSOSA, and complete 90 hours of community service. He is required to register as a gun offender in the District of Columbia and remain registered for two years following the expiration of his period of probation. The Court entered an order requiring Mr. Drew to stay away from the location of the offense. During the period of probation, Mr. Drew is required to participate in either a GED program or vocational training and to maintain or seek employment. The Court also required Mr. Drew to appear before the Court for regularly-scheduled probation review hearings.

Because Mr. Drew was 19 years old at the time of this offense, he requested through counsel that he be sentenced under the Youth Rehabilitation Act ("YRA"), D.C. Code §§ 24-901 through 24-907. The Court now issues this "written statement on the record of the reasons for its determination" that Mr. Drew is eligible for and will be sentenced under the YRA, D.C. Code §24-903(c)(l).
