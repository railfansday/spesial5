A controversial bill approved in October in the lower house of Italy’s parliament includes measures that allow healthcare workers who were struck off their professional registers during the pandemic for serious violations connected to their anti-vaccination beliefs – for example, promoting treatments that lacked a scientific basis – to apply to be reinstated.

The bill is being championed by the prime minister Giorgia Meloni’s Brothers of Italy party and is strongly backed by its far-right coalition partner, the League.

But with allies in Forza Italia split, its approval in the lower house hinged on support from National Future (FN), a rival far-right force led by the former army general Roberto Vannacci, whose emergence is shaping the political landscape before general elections next year. Vannacci has voiced opposition to vaccine mandates, and his rhetoric frequently targets the vaccine-sceptic audience.

The measure still needs to clear the senate, with a vote scheduled in January. But its progress has alarmed medical experts, less because of the number of healthcare workers involved – it is believed to be very small – and more because of the risk of further eroding trust in vaccines.

Nearly one in two Italian adults are hesitant about vaccination, according to a report by the department of public health and paediatric sciences at the University of Turin published in the Lancet Regional Health Europe in February. The report, which sampled 50,000 adults across Italy, found the ambivalence was driven more by misinformation and lack of institutional trust than by fears over vaccine safety.

Meanwhile, a London-based naval drone maker founded by Ukrainians has reached a £4.5bn valuation after it raised more than £500m from investors including the UK government.

UForce, which makes military drones for land, air and sea, received £113m from the UK’s national wealth fund. The investment makes UForce one of the UK’s fastest-growing unicorns – or startups with a valuation of more than $1bn. Its drones have been used extensively in the war in Ukraine.

The UK defence secretary, Wes Streeting, met the UForce chief executive, Oleg Rogynskyy, on Thursday onboard the British aircraft carrier HMS Prince of Wales during a defence conference in Oslo, Norway.

The UK’s Ministry of Defence noted UForce’s maritime capabilities in particular, amid concerns over the increasing threat from Russia in the north Atlantic and North Sea. Russian naval vessels and its “shadow fleet” have been accused of sabotage and spying operations on cables and pipelines, while the UK is planning a fleet of sea-borne drones in the northern seas to track Russia’s nuclear submarine fleet.

Streeting on Thursday piloted a UForce MV7 uncrewed vessel located more than 1,500 miles away in the Atlantic Ocean near Portugal.

Vladimir Putin’s comments during his meeting with the two US envoys make it clear that getting a deal with Russia will not be easy.

During his talks with Witkoff and Kushner, the Russian president doubled down on his demand to retain the Russia-occupied Ukrainian territory and opposed any foreign peacekeeping troops in the country – a key part of Europe’s planned security guarantees for Kyiv.

Instead, he continues to push for broader talks on rolling back Europe’s post-1997 Nato security architecture, a move which Zelenskyy called “strange” and urged Europe to watch closely.

Putin’s proposal for “a humanitarian mission” in Kostyantynivka is likely to be seen by Europeans as merely a token gesture designed to distract from his otherwise hardline stance.
