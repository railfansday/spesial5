Turning 62 in 2027? Here's What You Need to Know Before Claiming Social Security

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Before you file for benefits, understand the impact of an early claim.

If you're turning 62 in 2027, there's a big milestone you may be looking forward to -- being eligible to sign up for Social Security. And you may be eager to claim benefits as soon as you reach age 62, whether to get the extra money for a specific purchase or to kick off your retirement.

But if you're thinking of claiming Social Security at 62, it's important to understand the consequences. And you should know that they could impact your finances for the rest of your retirement.

You're eligible for your Social Security benefits without a reduction at full retirement age. If you're turning 62 in 2027, your full retirement age is 67.

Filing for Social Security five years early will lead to a 30% reduction in your monthly checks. And that reduction is typically permanent. It could also cause you a world of stress during your senior years.

If you haven't saved much for retirement, being stuck with smaller Social Security checks could make it harder to keep up with your costs. And even if you have retirement savings, there's a chance that money could run out. Social Security is guaranteed to pay you a monthly benefit for life, so filing early slashes a crucial income stream.

Plus, each year, Social Security benefits are eligible for a cost-of-living adjustment, or COLA, to help those payments keep pace with inflation. If you file for Social Security early and reduce your benefits, those COLAs will be less valuable to you, since you'll be starting with a smaller payment.

Finally, if you're the higher earner in your household, claiming Social Security at 62 could mean leaving your spouse with a smaller monthly survivor benefit. That could cause them a world of stress as they navigate life without you.

Filing for Social Security at 62 isn't always a bad decision. It could make sense if you've lost your job and have struggled to find work, you have poor health and want to boost your lifetime Social Security benefit, or you have enough savings to sustain yourself and want to enjoy the money while you're in better shape.

The point, rather, is to understand the financial impact of claiming Social Security at 62 and make sure it won't cause you undue stress. You may realize that waiting to take benefits is the better choice.
