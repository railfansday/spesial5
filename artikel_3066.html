Retiring in 2027? Here's How Much Cash You Need to Set Aside

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

You need protection in case the market doesn't cooperate.

By Maurie Backman – Nov 24, 2026 at 5:22PM EST

You'll often hear that it's important to continue investing during retirement. And the reason is simple.

You want your money to keep pace with inflation. If you pull your entire IRA or 401(k) out of the stock market and put it into cash, you might lose out on buying power through the years.

At the same time, it's important to have some cash on hand in case the market doesn't perform well. And if you're retiring in 2027, now's the time to secure that cash reserve before you start living off your portfolio.

The reason you need a pile of cash in retirement is simple. You never know what the stock market is going to do on any given day.

If you're at a point in life when you're using your portfolio for income, you may not have the luxury to wait months or years for a recovery. Your bills need to get paid now.

If you set aside a cash reserve, you'll be able to leave your investments alone during a stock market downturn. That means instead of locking in permanent losses, you'll give your portfolio a chance to recover. If you don't keep some cash on hand and your bills can't wait, you could end up selling assets at a loss, increasing the risk of depleting your savings.

While it's important to have some cash available in retirement, you don't want to go overboard. Keeping too much cash on hand could mean losing out on stronger returns.

As a general rule of thumb, you may want to aim for one to three years' worth of living expenses in cash. That gives you time to ride out a prolonged market downturn. The exact amount you land on should hinge on how much peace of mind you want and what your spending and non-portfolio income sources look like.

If you're the type to lose sleep over a stock market crash, a three-year cushion may be best. On the other hand, if your budget is flexible, you have generous Social Security benefits coming your way, and you're open to working to supplement your income when needed, a one-year cash cushion may be enough.

Either way, if your retirement date is getting closer, start strategically moving assets into cash now. Decide on the amount you need, and shop around for a high-yield savings account so you can earn a decent return on your money.

You may also want to look at a CD ladder if you'll be setting aside a larger amount of cash, like two to three years' worth. If you set up those CDs to mature at regular intervals, you can retain access to your cash while potentially earning a higher return than a regular savings account.
