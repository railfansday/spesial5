Oregon and Virginia on Monday filed lawsuits against SpaceX, alleging that Elon Musk’s company violated consumer protection laws, months after people used Grok Imagine to generate millions of sexualized images of women and children.

Grok Imagine is an image- and video-generation tool developed by SpaceXSI, a division of SpaceX. Shortly after launching last summer, the tool added a “spicy mode,” which enabled people to generate images and videos showing partial nudity and adult-themed videos. Grok Imagine is integrated into Grok, which is Musk’s AI chatbot.

In the lawsuits, separate but jointly filed, Virginia and Oregon allege that the sexualized images — including child sexual abuse material — were generated without the knowledge and consent of the women and children shown in the images and videos.

Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones said in a press release that SpaceX misled people into thinking that their images and data would be safe.

“When xAI [now renamed SpaceXSI] allowed Grok’s creators to sideline consent to turn a profit, they prioritized growth and engagement over safety, and they deceived the public about it,” Jones said. “Today’s filings are the first step towards holding this company accountable.”

Donna Rice Hughes, CEO of child internet safety organization Enough Is Enough, praised Oregon and Virginia for taking action against SpaceX.

“It is abhorrent that women and children are preyed upon in this manner and even more repugnant that xAI’s Grok ‘spicy mode’ has not kept up with bad actors who abuse their terms of service,” Hughes told CNET, adding that Musk’s “team can find ways to prevent criminal misuse of Grok, and at the very least, disable Grok’s ‘spicy mode.'”

Grok Imagine launched on Aug. 5, 2025, and within months, it was used to generate millions of sexualized images, which were posted on the social media platform X, which is also owned by Musk’s SpaceX.

Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield alleged SpaceX “knowingly designed” Grok Imagine and promoted spicy mode’s lack of safeguards as a feature, not a bug.

“It’s no surprise that Grok and X became a haven for perpetrators using AI to create and share child sexual abuse material,” Rayfield said in a statement. “The lack of needed safety measures was considered a feature, not a bug, which they promoted as ‘spicy mode.’”

During a 9-day span starting in late December, Grok posted about 3 million sexualized images, according to an analysis from the Center for Countering Digital Hate.

In a statement posted Monday on X, SpaceX said that people using Grok to generate sexualized images and videos violated the platform’s terms of service and worked “aggressively to circumvent our safeguards.” The company said it tightened protections after the misuse and continues to test and adjust them as needed. SpaceX also said it notifies law enforcement authorities “when someone tries to break our safeguards and generate this content.”

Earlier this year, several minor Jane Does filed a proposed class action lawsuit against xAI after people used their photos to generate child sexual abuse material using Grok via spicy mode. That case is still ongoing.

In July, a victim’s family also filed suit after photographer Russell Bloodworth in Arkansas allegedly used spicy mode to generate explicit photos of children. xAI went on the offensive, suing Bloodworth and South Carolina man Terry Wayne Harwood, who is also accused of using Grok to generate child sexual abuse material. The company seeks damages for expenses incurred in fighting lawsuits and for reputational harm.

SpaceX and xAI have faced lawsuits for other things aside from illicit image generation. The companies were sued for allegedly sharing sensitive data and for allegedly causing users to be hospitalized for psychosis after harmful chat conversations on Grok.

Monday’s lawsuits against SpaceX are among nearly 400 cases brought against 129 AI companies in the US, according to the AI Lawsuit Tracker. Many Big Tech companies have been sued for allegedly harming the mental health of adults and children. In August, Meta agreed to pay $18 billion to settle a lawsuit brought by US states alleging the company intentionally designed Instagram and other platforms to be addictive to children.

Companies such as ByteDance — owner of TikTok — Snap, Google and others face more than 2,400 federal lawsuits alleging that their platforms harm the mental health of children.
