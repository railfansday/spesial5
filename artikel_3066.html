Where Will Palantir Stock Be in 1 Year?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

It's down right now, but there's reason to suspect that can change.

By Jennifer Saibil – Nov 18, 2026 at 4:39PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Palantir Technologies ›

The artificial intelligence (AI) play still looks intact even as Nvidia (NVDA -2.90%) stock plunges. The tech-heavy Nasdaq-100 is up 11% year to date, and the S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.23%), which is heavily weighted toward AI right now, is up 11%. Both of these gains are impressive, considering that Nvidia stock, which is a major component of the index, is down more than 12%.

However, Palantir Technologies (PLTR +0.07%) is also down. It's lost 5% this year despite fantastic operating results, and it's still wildly expensive. Where could it be in one year?

Palantir has been the poster child for AI stocks for years. Its groundbreaking AI platform helps companies analyze data to detect patterns and make decisions while keeping their information private.

What began as a program for the military and U.S. intelligence, including seed money from the CIA, has bloomed into a broad business serving all sectors. The company's AI platform (AIP), which is geared toward commercial organizations, is thriving, driving incredible growth for the whole company.

In the third quarter, total revenue increased 96% from the year-ago period, and U.S. commercial revenue was up 148%.

However, as AI continues to develop and handle more daily tasks, competition is creeping in, both from AI agents and competitors specifically in this space, like Databricks and Snowflake (SNOW +2.45%).

Palantir claims that its edge lies in its sovereignty, meaning clients keep their data private and proprietary. The results show that clients agree. They're seeing excellent results from the partnership, which is the main reason they're likely to stay, at least in the near term.

Palantir stock is dropping for two distinct reasons even though it's growing like wildfire. One is the threat of competition as AI flourishes, and the other is the stock's premium price tag. It trades at 118 times trailing 12-month earnings and 59 times trailing 12-month sales, and it's the most expensive stock in the S&P 500 on a price-to-sales basis.

With that kind of price, there's little room for error or negative market sentiment for any reason.

In the third quarter, for example, U.S. commercial revenue growth dropped down the tiniest of notches from 149% last quarter, and total contract value increased 6%, down from 49%. These things matter when the valuation is as high as Palantir’s.

If these trends continue, Palantir stock could keep declining over the next year until the valuation is more in line with expectations.

However, that's not a given. Management is guiding for total revenue to increase 91% for the full year, and it's likely to decelerate next year. If revenue is, for example, 75% higher next year than it is today, and the price-to-sales ratio falls to three-quarters of its current level, to 45, the market cap would be $575 billion. That’s 33% higher than today's value.
