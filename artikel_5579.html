Absolute Home Mortgage, First Option leaders on strengthening their partnership

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Absolute Home Mortgage and First Option Mortgage described a strategic equity partnership positioned as neither a merger nor an acquisition. Executives said the combined platform targets about $4 billion in annual originations and includes about 420 producing loan officers.  AI Summary

On Wednesday, Absolute Home Mortgage Corp. and First Option Mortgage announced a strategic transaction that combines the companies’ resources and aims for originations growth amid a consolidating market.

Matthew VanFossen, the CEO of New Jersey-based Absolute, and Alvin Shah, managing partner at Georgia-based First Option, sat down with HousingWire shortly after the announcement to offer further details.

The deal, which combines the two companies with Mortgage Equity Partners — a wholly owned subsidiary of Absolute — is positioned to originate about $4 billion annually across roughly 420 producing loan officers. The leadership team will also expand as Shah and Kurt Nikolai, president and managing partner at First Option, join Absolute’s ownership group and board of directors.

Editor’s note: This interview has been edited for length and clarity.

Neil Pierson: This transaction is not considered to be a merger or acquisition. How would you describe it?

Matthew VanFossen: It’s lane three. Alvin and I, when we do business together, we never go down the two traditional paths. You could do mergers, you could do you asset sales, or we could have a buy-in situation. Usually it’s one company taking over another, but this is First Option, the business partner, buying a piece of Absolute Home Mortgage in an equity deal.

Maybe the headline should have been First Option acquires part of Absolute Home Mortgage, because that’s what’s going on. Absolute is the surviving entity in the transaction, but First Option’s brand will be alive. Their entire team will be onboarded. Alvin and Kurt are joining the board of directors and have an ownership share in the entity — of course, pending regulatory approval.

Alvin Shah: Obviously there’s a much bigger picture in mind. For me personally, we were looking for an exit strategy for my partner Kurt over the next several years anyways, so that was top and center in terms of what was going through my head. But the opportunity to work with the Absolute team, it’s not something new. I mean, we’re already partners in a technology company, Mortgage Automation Technologies.

Pierson: What is some of the history behind your partnership?

Shah: Matt started the BIG Point of Sale several years ago, and because of the Lenders One network, I was exposed to it. And when we saw what he was building, it was great. It’s a point-of-sale system built by lenders for lenders, so we jumped on board right away. We made an investment into the company.

It kind of forced Matt’s hand, allowing me to be on the board of directors over there as well. So we’ve all been working together for three, four years now. When everything transpired with First Option and what we were looking for long term, and also knowing what Matt was building long term in terms of the previous acquisitions he was involved in, the fit made sense right right away. I wanted growth, he wanted growth, and we knew what kind of damage we can do when we work together.

The question was, will the cultures within the organizations match? Unbelievably, it was such a great fit where the leadership at First Option and the leadership at Absolute, we almost immediately became just like family. Now, it doesn’t hurt that we’ve been friends and partners for so long, so a lot of the things that we did from a process standpoint, and from a vendor and technology standpoint, were already in line for both companies. So change from that standpoint was minimal.

Pierson: Can you provide more perspective on what appears to be the start of more deals to come? What do you look at when thinking about growing through M&A or equity partnerships?

VanFossen: Typically, for the the past four transactions we’ve done, I led up the M&A efforts. I would do the due diligence on the relationship management, the financials, but it’s not my strongest suit. I might have been a little lucky.

My strong suit is looking at technology and vision for the future — what we should be bringing in-house, what we should be developing. We have strategic partnerships on the board of directors that we have to be looking at. We’re doing business with multiple mortgage companies. I quickly learned that Alvin was better a better deal-maker than I am. He was better at sorting out the strategic numbers, the fits, the technology alignment.

Part of these past three or four years, we got our companies 90% in line with the same vendors, just from saying, “Hey, these vendors work.” We wanted to get our workflows very similar through our relationships in the industry. When we look at that, Alvin is better suited than I am to be our chief strategy officer, to go out and be working through the vendor networks, figuring out where the alignment is. Because how do you change everything with not changing much for the end user? That’s how you have retention, in our eyes.

When we look at these strategies, yeah, we’re probably not going to buy a company that’s not on the same LOS as us, because that’s too much disruption, right? But when we go to companies and we say, “OK, these are our vendors that are in line together,” this means that your staff would have minimal disruption.

When you look at our new board of directors at Absolute, we’re all staying in our lanes. My job is everything that comes tomorrow — five to seven years from now — and steering the ship in the right direction. Alvin’s job is growth and making sure that the machine is continuing to add.

Shah: I’ve been an owner of a brokerage before leveling up to become part of First Option and then eventually owning First Option. I’ve seen different tiers, and I’ve seen the industry change because I’ve been in the business for 24 years. I know what kind of a situation people are in today because I was in it before.

Looking at what the next several years of our business is going to do — and knowing what shoes other people are in — it’s easy for me to be able to dissect and show somebody, “Look, here’s what’s happening because of your scale, because of the size of your company, and here’s how I was able to solve for that problem.”

The bottom line of the company needs to be healthy and everybody within the organization needs to have opportunity. On the flip side of it, at some point, I would think to myself, “How do I get to the next level in my career?” And that’s another value proposition that now we’re able to present, because we’ve created what I call the Absolute group of companies.

If somebody has $30 million, $40 million, $50 million worth of production under them, and they want to do their own thing but they’re pigeonholed wherever they are — now we’re we’re giving them the platform to say, “Run your own company, build your own brand, be the president of your company.” But use the resources we’ve already established for you and the scale power.

When you’re a smaller company and you’re negotiating, you’re not negotiating. The vendors are in the driver’s seat. But when you’re a bigger company and part of a bigger platform, you essentially get the benefit of the bigger company. And now you’re able to feed into that, really build something and focus your attention on the things you really like doing.
