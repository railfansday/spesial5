Provo Housing Market: Momentum Meets a Changing Market

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

As housing conditions shift, a market’s local economy offers an important starting point for understanding where the market is headed. In the Provo, Utah, metro, total nonfarm employment reached 324,000 payrolls in August, up 1.1% from a year earlier, while the unemployment rate declined to 3.4%.

That economic momentum is encouraging, but it is being tested by softer conditions in the housing market.

One important competitive dynamic is the relationship between new and existing home prices. As of July, the median new-home closing price was $495,800, compared with $541,000 for existing homes. That pricing relationship gives builders an avenue to compete with the resale market even as affordability remains challenging.

Sales conditions, however, are mixed. Annualized new-home sales totaled 4,594 units in August, down 6.1% year over year (YOY). Annualized detached sales increased 5.3% YOY, while attached sales declined 17.8%, underscoring different demand conditions by product type.

Supply is another increasingly important part of the story. Finished vacant inventory increased 18.9% YOY to 1,011 units as of Q2 2026. Zonda estimates the Provo market will be 3.89% oversupplied at year-end 2026.

The number of active detached new-home projects decreased roughly 1% YOY as of October while the number of active attached projects was flat. Total building permits are expected to decline by nearly 15% YOY in 2026.

Provo presents a market with two competing realities. Household and employment growth continue to support underlying housing demand, but elevated finished inventory, softer new-home sales, and affordability constraints argue for disciplined production. The projected decline in permitting could eventually help rebalance supply, but near-term execution will remain critical as builders compete for price sensitive buyers.
