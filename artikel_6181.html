The era of catch-up flight attendant raises is ending, but airlines shouldn’t expect labor costs to ease. The next fights are over regional pay and worker data.

Flight attendants and pilots saw massive pay jumps in recent negotiations. Association of Flight Attendants International President Sara Nelson doesn’t expect a repeat.

“I don’t think that we’re going to see the kind of increases that we saw this last time again, unless the industry turns around. Unless we have peace on Earth and everything is going swimmingly,” Nelson said Thursday at the Skift Aviation Forum.

Association of Flight Attendants International President Sara Nelson told the Skift Aviation Forum that the record pay increases won in the last bargaining round are unlikely to repeat. Nelson said that round was effectively the first real bargaining since 9/11, coming after years of bankruptcies, mergers, and pandemic delays that left contracts four to six years late. With mainline contracts at carriers like American and United "pretty much buttoned up," Nelson said the union now wants smaller, more regular raises through more frequent bargaining, and is shifting its attention to three fights: pay at regional carriers such as PSA Airlines and Endeavor Air, where she said flight attendants earn roughly half of mainline pay while based in high-cost cities like Chicago and New York; its organizing campaign at Delta, the only major U.S. airline without unionized flight attendants, which Nelson said is "really close" with a 52% increase in activists since January; and worker data protections, modeled on the union's objections to Google's bankruptcy auction win for Spirit's employee data. Nelson also said boarding pay, now standard at most major U.S. carriers, will be defended in future talks: "The public is on our side. So there's no way they're going to claw that back."
