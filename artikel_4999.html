There are a lot of laser hair removal devices on the market, which is why you should consider a few of these pointers before investing.

IPL and diode lasers both slow hair growth and reduce the thickness of individual strands by scanning for pigment and targeting the hair follicle, says dermatologist Melanie Kingsley, MD. But, they each have their benefits and limitations.

IPL is the most commonly used type of light in at-home laser hair removal devices. This technology uses broad-spectrum light to target melanin, the pigment that gives your hair its color. The light is also unsuitable for dark and deep skin tones because it can’t distinguish between melanin-rich skin and the pigment of dark hair, says dermatologist Rachel Nazarian, MD. However, if you have a fair to olive complexion with dark hair, then you’re an ideal candidate for IPL treatments.

Until recently, all at-home laser hair removal devices were IPL lasers. But Epilaser has a game-changing laser for home use. Unlike IPL lights, which emit a broad spectrum of light ranging from 400 to 1,400 nm, diode lasers are unique because they use a more concentrated wavelength of light (around 810 nm). This precise wavelength means it can better target the hair follicles rather than accidentally burning your skin, making it more accessible for some people with darker complexions.

Both are effective, but diode lasers are slightly more versatile. That said, no matter which one you go with, you’ll need multiple treatments to maintain your hair-free results.

One of the biggest considerations is the number of flashes a device is equipped to deliver. This number indicates how long the device will last, according to Dr. Nazarian. "Many devices contain about 200,000 flashes, which is typically good for more than 15 years, although it also depends on how many areas you’re treating," she says. Other recommendations on this list, like the Smoothskin, have unlimited flashes, which means they'll theoretically last forever, but are pricier.

Extra features like multiple intensity modes—lower options for sensitive skin; higher settings for those who want to see results fast (see the Nood)—or interchangeable heads for precise treatment all over the body (hi, Braun) can increase the price. The amount you spend comes down to the features you want in your device and how long you want it to last.

One of the best ways to reduce discomfort during laser hair removal is to use cooling technology. It lowers your skin’s temperature—before, during, and after each laser pulse—to help you better tolerate longer and higher treatment settings safely.

Devices with bigger heads are more efficient on large areas.
