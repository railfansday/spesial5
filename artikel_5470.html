Molson Coors cut its full-year forecast as beer demand stays soft

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The brewer lowered its net sales and earnings forecasts and expects softer industry volumes to continue in the fourth quarter

Molson Coors $TAP Beverage cut its full-year 2026 guidance on Thursday, pointing to how worldwide economic conditions are shaping both brewing-sector demand and shopper habits, together with continued heavy price promotions in the U.K.

The company said it now anticipates a 1% to 2% drop in net sales at constant currency, whereas its earlier forecast called for flat results, plus or minus 1%. It expects underlying earnings per share to fall 16% to 19% from 2025, versus a prior forecast of a decline of 11% to 15%. Underlying income before income taxes is projected to decline 21% to 24%, compared with a prior range of 15% to 18%. Underlying free cash flow is projected at $1.0 billion, plus or minus 10%, down from $1.1 billion, plus or minus 10%.

The outlook assumes that the weaker global beer industry volume trends seen in both the second and third quarters will carry into the final quarter of the year. It also assumes commodity and logistics costs stay elevated, with the impact of Midwest Premium pricing expected to exceed the prior year by approximately $130 million for the full year.

In its third-quarter results, Molson Coors said net sales fell 1.6% to $2.93 billion. Brand volume dropped 4.9%, and financial volume dropped 3.0%. Underlying income before income taxes decreased 17.4% in constant currency to $349.0 million. Underlying diluted earnings per share were $1.46, down 12.6%.

Net income attributable to the company totaled $271.5 million, equal to $1.45 per share, versus a $2.93 billion loss in the year-earlier period. The prior-year quarter was burdened by a $3.65 billion write-down that partly impaired goodwill.

Revenue missed analyst expectations of $2.94 billion, according to The Wall Street Journal. Adjusted earnings per share topped analyst estimates of $1.44.

Americas net sales fell 1.2%, with U.S. brand volume down 5.6% and industry softness in Canada. EMEA and APAC net sales fell 2.7% on lower U.K. volume, which the company attributed to soft market demand and a heightened competitive landscape. Cost of goods sold per hectoliter rose 8.6%, including approximately $30 million tied to Midwest Premium pricing.

"Those pressures weighed on our performance in the quarter and are likely to remain a factor in the near term," Chief Financial Officer Tracey Joubert said in a statement, according to The Wall Street Journal. She said the company has the balance sheet capacity to manage through periods of cost pressure.

President and CEO Rahul Goyal said in a statement that the company believes its strategy is on the right track, but that its outcomes "are not yet scaled enough to offset the headwinds that we experienced in the third quarter."

Molson Coors scheduled an earnings call with analysts and investors for 8:30 a.m. ET on Thursday.

Other brewers have posted weaker beer results. Carlsberg's beer sales contracted 3.5% on an organic basis in the third quarter, and Constellation Brands $STZ' depletions slipped 0.6% in its fiscal second quarter.
