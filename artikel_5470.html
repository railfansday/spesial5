Nvidia formed board committee to oversee national security and export controls

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The new standing panel, chaired by director Suzanne Nora Johnson, comes after CEO Jensen Huang's resignation last week

Nvidia $NVDA established a new board committee to oversee its national security policies, export control compliance and global trade risk management, the company said Tuesday.

The National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee is a standing panel chaired by director Suzanne Nora Johnson. Directors Stephen C. Neal and John O. Dabiri are members. The committee will convene at minimum once each quarter, meeting with management as well as in executive session.

Both the National Security Team and the Global Trade Compliance Team that Nvidia already has will answer to the committee and to interim CEO Colette Kress on day-to-day operations, the company said. The committee will supervise efforts to strengthen both teams and will engage independent outside specialists in legal matters, national security and trade compliance.

"We are committed to working constructively with government and industry partners to help establish and advance a robust framework that uses every tool at our disposal to safeguard national security while supporting continued technological innovation," Harvey Jones, chairman of Nvidia's board, said in a statement.

The committee follows the resignation last week of Nvidia founder and CEO Jensen Huang, according to Bloomberg. Huang stepped down after admitting to an affair with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy. Kress was appointed interim CEO last week.

Nvidia lacked a chief compliance officer, according to Reuters, which cited the company's 2026 proxy statement. Whether the board created the committee in response to the revelations about Huang was not immediately clear.

President Donald Trump described the committee as “a great idea” in response to a question about it Tuesday, according to Bloomberg. On Sunday, the Commerce Department issued an interim rule requiring licenses to export advanced U.S. AI chips to Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil. The department said the rule is meant to keep remote computing arrangements in third countries from being used to get around existing restrictions.

Nvidia stock edged up roughly 1% in premarket trading Tuesday, having finished Monday’s session at $156.14. The stock has lost 16% of its value since Huang disclosed the affair on Nov. 8. Nvidia will release its fiscal third-quarter results following Tuesday’s market close.
