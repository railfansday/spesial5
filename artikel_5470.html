U.K. households have hoarded up to £700 million in cash after 'Be Ready' push

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Cash in circulation rose 1.5% in five weeks, the biggest increase since 2014, after the government urged crisis stockpiling

U.K. households squirreled away up to £700 million ($917 million) more cash than normal during the last five weeks, after the government pressed the public, through its "Be Ready" campaign, to get ready for emergencies, according to Bloomberg, which cited Bank of England data.

Security Minister Dan Jarvis advised people on Oct. 22 to set aside enough supplies to last three days, such as wind-up radios, food, drinking water and cash in both coins and banknotes, in case they faced a hack on digital systems, storms or some other emergency, according to Bloomberg. Cash in circulation has risen by £1.5 billion ($1.97 billion) since that date, a 1.5% increase and the largest since 2014.

The Bank of England reported notes in circulation of £101.111 billion ($132.5 billion) as of Nov. 25, up from £100.739 billion a week earlier. The central bank published the figures in its weekly report on Thursday.

Cash withdrawals during the five weeks were roughly twice the pace recorded over the equivalent stretch in each of the three preceding years, according to Bloomberg. The one earlier occasion since 2000 when people pulled out a larger amount of cash was October to November 2009, amid the financial crisis.

The Bank of England would not say what is driving the demand, according to Bloomberg. Governor Andrew Bailey spoke about cash on Nov. 9 in a speech at the Swiss Institute of International Studies. "The amount of cash we have in issue, it's still going up," Bailey said, calling it "one of the great paradoxes of the central bank world."

Other data points to wider stockpiling. Official retail sales data released last week pointed to a jump in purchases of shelf-stable goods like canned food and UHT milk. Worldpanel by Numerator data indicated that Britons were purchasing more canned food, sanitizing gel and water in bottles, according to Bloomberg.

The Link network of 40,000 ATMs said this month that cash machine users pulled out an extra £16 million ($21 million) during the week ending Nov. 2 compared with the same week last year, even as the number of transactions fell, according to Bloomberg.

The Bank of England polls the public at regular intervals about their reasons for using cash. Officials found that people are keeping notes and coins in hand for extended periods rather than paying them into accounts, and may also be withdrawing bigger amounts from cash machines because inflation is higher, according to Bloomberg.

Victoria Cleland, then the bank's chief cashier, said last year that people kept physical money as a safeguard should the banking system break down or the electricity go out, according to Bloomberg.
