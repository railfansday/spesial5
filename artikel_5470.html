The White House isn't retreating on tariffs after an election wipeout

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Aide Jim Goyer said the administration will continue its pace, while Gary Cohn called tariffs a consumption tax on U.S. consumers

Jim Goyer, director of the White House Office of Public Liaison, spoke at the B20 USA Summit in Washington on Tuesday and said the administration will keep up its tariff pace through the end of President Donald Trump's term, according to CNBC.

Goyer argued that tariffs help bring industrial production back home, make supply lines more reliable and safeguard the country's security, according to CNBC. "We are just getting started," Goyer said.

Gary Cohn, IBM $IBM vice chairman and former director of the National Economic Council, offered a different view in an onstage interview with POLITICO Playbook co-author Dasha Burns at the summit. "Tariffs are a consumption tax on the U.S. consumer… The producers and the importers don't pay the tariff," Cohn said.

Cohn cited aluminum tariffs as an example. "You and I, if we're buying a new car, we are eating that cost," he said. On the elections, Cohn said, "The U.S. consumer voted their financial well-being."

Cohn believes Republicans and Democrats could reach a compromise on trade, according to CNBC. Cohn proposed that Trump and the Democrats who will soon control Congress could lock in permanent safeguards aimed at China, while tariffs on Canada are scaled back.

Dow Executive Chairman Jim Fitterling took the other side, according to CNBC. He conceded that tariffs can push prices higher but called them essential for guarding against unfair competition and defending domestic manufacturing.

Democrats emerged from the Nov. 3 midterm elections with their biggest Senate and House majorities since at least 2008, according to Reuters. They assume control once the incoming Congress is seated on Jan. 3.

A Reuters/Ipsos poll completed Monday showed that 62% of Americans favor rolling back Trump's tariffs, according to Reuters. The poll put Trump's approval rating at 32%.

Democrats intend to take on the tariffs once they are running Congress in January, according to CNBC. Trump's ability to veto legislation, along with his sweeping authority over trade, could make the duties hard to undo.

Price pressures are building as the debate unfolds. Consumer prices rose 0.4% in October after a 0.6% increase in September. Core prices, which exclude food and energy, rose 0.3%, matching September and August. Gasoline prices rose 37.4% over 12 months.
