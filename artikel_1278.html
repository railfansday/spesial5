Man Utd Make Jason Wilcox and Omar Berrada Sack Decision

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Manchester United are believed to have already reached their verdict not just on the future of Michael Carrick, but also regarding two other key higher-ups, Omar Berrada and Jason Wilcox, who have also received serious scrutiny over recent months.

With multiple outlets now reporting that the former Man Utd midfielder turned interim boss and now full-time head coach is set to be relieved of his duties in the very near future – and with United's preferred candidate to replace Michael Carrick already named – pundits, supporters, and football fans in general are debating where exactly the book stops at the giant of a sporting brand, as countless have questioned where the true accountability for their poor fortunes in the post-Fergie era lies.

Berrada and Wilcox, both of whom found success on the business side of things during their time at Man City, are now the latest to be targeted by chants around Old Trafford and protest banners hanging from stands in the Stretford End.

Manchester United chiefs are expected to sack Michael Carrick

Nevertheless, while they're still trying to back their manager and thank him for his efforts during one of the most difficult periods in the club's long history, GIVEMESPORT understands that the pair are safe. At least for now, anyway.

As per The Telegraph's James Ducker and other sources, the executive duo — Berrada serving as CEO and Wilcox as Director of Football, respectively — are expected to keep their jobs if/when Carrick is dismissed; it's said they maintain co-owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe's support as part of a core triumvirate.

Since INEOS' investment in Manchester United, they have already shuffled significantly not only when it comes to the backroom staff but within the boardroom too, but it looks as though he still considers them key advisers and pillars of the so-called 'Project 2028' rebuild.

Ratcliffe has reiterated that, despite stating that he doesn't mind being "unpopular" and making the tough decisions, the increasing levels of abuse he and his cohort have received over the course of their tenure at the club, stating that he would walk away from the whole franchise if it ever became severe and prolonged as the anti-ownership sentiment and demonstrations the Glazer family have seen.

On the one hand, Wilcox's involvement in footballing decisions has been perceived as an issue by many Reds (this being the ex-player's first role of this kind), with divisive defender Lisandro Martínez publicly criticising the sporting director's decision-making in his highly inflammatory post-match quotes following their Champions League defeat to Atlético Madrid.

Meanwhile, more senior 'leadership' figures such as the World Cup winner himself have equally come under fire for sub-par performances of late, making key match-deciding errors and, crucially, irrespective of whatever big statements made in high-profile interviews, not doing enough talking on the pitch.

For most United match-goers and fans who watch it with their eyes week in, week out, whether or not Carrick stays or goes won't be enough to fix the deep-seated problems running throughout Manchester United; they'd much prefer wholesale change from the very top of the hierarchy all the way down - though it's likely they'll be waiting a long time to see that on a big enough scale to quell their frustrations.

'Green gold until the club is sold', as the supporters' saying goes, but do any of you really foresee the Glazers selling up one of the biggest sporting franchises in the world anytime soon?
