A clothing swap lets you and your friends trade the clothes you’ve stopped wearing for ones you’ll wear, at no cost. Invite people whose sizes overlap, send the rules with the invitation (clean pieces only, a cap on what each guest takes, and a tiebreaker for a piece two people want), and donate what’s left that day.

The average U.S. household spent $2,010 on clothes, shoes, and services like dry cleaning in 2025, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics’ Consumer Expenditure Survey. Every piece you carry home from a swap is one you didn’t have to buy.

The guest list and the rules, both settled before anyone arrives, decide whether everyone goes home with something they’ll wear. Invite people whose sizes and styles overlap, so no one is the only person in their size. If few friends share your size, swapping sites let you trade clothes with strangers instead.

Set up the room the way a store would. Sort the clothes by category, such as tops, pants, dresses, outerwear, and shoes, then by size within each. Put a full-length mirror near a bedroom or bathroom where guests can try things on.

Start with a quick walk past every pile before anyone takes a piece, so the best finds don’t go to whoever arrived first. Then take turns choosing one piece at a time for the first round or two, and let everyone browse freely after that.

Donate what’s left the same day. IRS rules allow a deduction for donated clothing only in good used condition or better, which is the standard your clean-and-wearable rule already set. Bag the leftovers before guests leave, drop them at a charity that takes clothing, and ask for a receipt if you’ll deduct the donation.
