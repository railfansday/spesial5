'Game of Thrones' Meets Jack Reacher in Alexander Skarsgård's Near-Perfect Action Epic

Few movies of the past decade have been as ambitious and as meticulous in their approach to life and combat as Robert Eggers' The Northman. Released in 2022, the Viking revenge epic combines the violence and political intrigue of Game of Thrones with the "man on a mission" brutality of Reacher, fusing what makes those shows great while improving where they may fall short. Led by a career-defining performance by Alexander Skarsgård, The Northman is a bloody and haunting journey through betrayal and Norse mythology that deserves more recognition than it initially garnered upon release.

Although the film received widespread critical praise, especially for Skarsgård's performance, it never caught on in theaters and struggled to earn back its production budget. Filmed for between $70 and $90 million, it earned just $75 million at the box office, so while it's not considered a bomb, it had enough commercial appeal to leave some feeling disappointed. However, like many hidden gems and cult classics, The Northman's reputation has only grown in the years since release, particularly among viewers searching for the historical, adult-oriented action spectacle Hollywood has become reluctant to produce. With its stunning cinematogaphy, ferocious battle sequences, and emotional story of revenge, Eggers' Viking tale remains one of the best action movies of the 2020s.

Set in the late ninth century, The Northman follows Amleth (Skarsgård), a young prince whose life is forever changed when his villainous uncle Fjolnir (Claes Bang) murders his father, King Aurvandill (Ethan Hawke), and kidnaps his mother, Queen Gudrun (Nicole Kidman). As the young man manages to escape death, Amleth dedicates his life to avenging his father, rescuing his mother, and killing his uncle.

We shan't be going to the Viking times, thank you.

Years later, Amleth has become a hulking Viking berserker, the living embodiment of vengeance and someone who is willing to murder anyone who gets in the way of his quest for revenge. He soon discovers his uncle's whereabouts and travels back to Iceland to finally accomplish his mission. There, he meets and falls in love with a slave woman named Olga (Anya Taylor-Joy), who helps him in his quest.

Skarsgård is nearly unrecognizable in his performance, delivering a physically imposing portrayal that makes Amleth feel less like a man and more like a force of nature. His sheer brutality recalls the unstoppable presence of Jack Reacher, particularly in the Prime Video series Reacher, starring Alan Ritchson. Both characters possess the intimidating physicality and an almost supernatural ability to overwhelm their enemies, often in the most brutal ways possible.

While Reacher operates by a certain moral code, Amleth is consumed by obsession. Revenge dictates where he goes, what he eats and drinks, and who he interacts with, dominating every decision he makes regardless of the consequences. His unwavering, terrifying determination makes him a monster to his enemies while threatening to destroy everything he holds dear.

Beyond its ferocious action, The Northman's DNA is thoroughly mixed with HBO's classic Game of Thrones. Both focus on the brutality of a medieval setting while blending it with the loyalty and political ambition of the day, and both exist in a world where betrayal can be as devastating as any defeat on a bloody battlefield. Much like dragons and the ancient magic of Game of Thrones, Eggers imbues his world with the supernatural elements of Norse mythology, incorporating witches, draugr, and Valkyries to create a world where the line between history and fantasy becomes increasingly blurred.

However, The Northman does not focus on the maneuvering and scheming for a throne that Game of Thrones does, but instead on the violence that exists behind the scenes in a psychologically complex story about the consequences of revenge. One of the film's greatest strengths is its willingness to challenge what Amleth believes about his family. As with Thrones, family members can sometimes become your worst enemies, which is something Amleth did not expect or prepare for.

While the movie's ambition may have exceeded its success, its lackluster box-office performance was never a reflection of its quality. Viewers who love the ruthless medieval world of Game of Thrones and the bone-crunching brutality of Reacher will certainly find something to enjoy with The Northman. It's an unforgettable combination of genres that proves a movie doesn't need to fit into a box to succeed. Most importantly, it shows that action cinema can be visually spectacular, emotionally devastating, and intellectually ambitious without sacrificing the thrills of a fantasy epic.
