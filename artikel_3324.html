Michael Jackson’s ‘Second Family’ Sues Estate’s Lawyer Over ‘Hush Money’ Sex Abuse Deal

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The Cascio siblings, who claim Jackson molested them as children, are accusing celebrity attorney Bryan Freedman of a "despicable betrayal."

A group of siblings who allege they were sexually abused by Michael Jackson as children are suing celebrity lawyer Bryan Freedman over a “despicable betrayal.”

Freedman faces legal malpractice and fraud claims in a Wednesday (Nov. 18) lawsuit, submitted to Los Angeles court and reviewed by Billboard. It’s brought by the five Cascio siblings — Frank, Edward, Dominic, Aldo and Marie-Nicole — as well as Marie-Nicole’s husband, James Porte.

The Cascios grew up with Jackson and once referred to themselves as his “second family.” For years, they staunchly defended the King of Pop against pedophilia claims. But in 2019, they said the HBO documentary Leaving Neverland had deprogrammed them from Jackson’s “brainwashing,” and that they privately began alleging that the star had molested and raped Edward, Dominic, Aldo and Marie-Nicole as children.

Wednesday’s lawsuit alleges that upon these revelations, the Jackson estate enlisted Freedman — a major Hollywood litigator who has represented celebrities like Rebel Wilson, Vin Diesel and Justin Baldoni — “to deceive Plaintiffs into signing a hush-money agreement.” This settlement promised nearly $3.5 million to each of the five Cascio siblings, as long as they never went public with their claims and released all future legal claims over the alleged abuse.

According to the lawsuit, Freedman presented this deal to the family as a “life rights agreement” and did not explain its far-reaching ramifications. The siblings say Freedman dissuaded them from hiring an independent lawyer and said he was their advocate, when in reality he was working on behalf of the estate’s interests.

“He exploited the family’s vulnerability and trust to induce them into signing an unconscionable agreement, which stripped them of valuable legal rights, imposed sweeping confidentiality provisions and shielded Jackson’s estate and representatives from liability,” reads the lawsuit against Freedman.

This agreement has indeed proved inconvenient for the Cascios. They tried earlier this year to sue the Jackson estate for sex trafficking, but a judge ruled that the claims must be handled in confidential arbitration under the clear terms of the 2019 settlement.

Now, the siblings want financial damages from Freedman for putting them in this position. Wednesday’s lawsuit alleges that with a truly independent lawyer, they would have retained their legal rights, or at the very least “negotiated an agreement with a much higher settlement payment that would have been commensurate with Jackson’s molestation of the Cascio family.”

Freedman did not immediately return a request for comment on Wednesday. Reps for the Jackson estate, which is not named as a defendant in this lawsuit, also did not return an inquiry.

The estate has previously characterized the Cascios’ claims as a “desperate money grab,” pointing out that they “spent decades defending and affirming Michael’s innocence” before changing their story. The siblings stated during a 2010 appearance on The Oprah Winfrey Show, for example, that Jackson was “never” inappropriate with them, and Frank wrote in a 2011 memoir, “Michael’s love for children was innocent, and it was profoundly misunderstood.”

Jackson, who died in 2009, was never convicted or held legally liable for any accusation of child sex abuse during his lifetime; he settled a civil claim in 1994 without admitting any wrongdoing, and he was acquitted at a criminal trial in 2005. But such allegations have continued to dog his legacy, most notably when Leaving Neverland amplified claims from two men, Wade Robson and James Safechuck, in disturbing detail.

The Jackson estate vehemently denies all claims of sexual misconduct. It called Leaving Neverland a “one-sided hit job” and sued HBO, successfully getting the documentary removed from streaming. Robson and Safechuck are continuing to litigate civil abuse claims against the estate.
