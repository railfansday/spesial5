Salomon Aero Blaze 4 Grvl GTX Review: Built for Wet, Gritty Miles

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

The waterproof Gore-Tex upper and grippy lugs handle sloppy gravel—and slick floors during long shifts.

The Salomon Aero Blaze 4 Grvl GTX is technically a gravel running shoe, and it excels in that arena. It’s also becoming an absolute cult favorite among nurses, techs, and other healthcare workers who spend 12 hours on slick floors and want something wipeable, grippy, and weather/other substances-tough. Personally, I tested it on the one surface it was born for: wet, gritty, cursed early-morning gravel that always tries to murder me. Tough luck, gravel gods. I’m still alive.

The Gore-Tex of the Aero Blaze 4 Grvl GTX is the cold, wet, ugly-conditions sibling in Salomon’s trail running gravel lineup. It’s a boss of a gravel trainer that shares its midsole, outsole, drop, and stack with the standard Grvl running shoe; the difference is that Gore-Tex upper, which trades some airflow and a little weight for weather and moisture blocking power. If you train through damp autumns and slushy winters, like I do, the GTX is 100 percent what you want. If you run mostly in dry, warm conditions, save the $20 and some sweat and take the regular version.

When it comes to the Aero Blaze 4 Grvl GTX, the lugs make the shoe. At 2.5 mm, the lugs aren’t the aggressive, mud-clawing teeth you’ll get on a mountain shoe. They’re short, chevron-shaped, and made of Contagrip rubber that gives you enough hold without digging, and they’re short enough that road-to-trail is totally doable.

I ran them at Shirley Chisholm Park in Brooklyn, which is a landfill-turned-park full of gravel hills. The conditions were damp, slippery, and gloomy. I took them out in the early morning, with loose stone over wet packed base. It’s weather that makes you wonder why you’re there and not at the TJ Maxx across the highway. But, even as a Maxxinista, I settled in fast and happy because the lugs gripped like they were made of sticky tack. My uphills weren’t tripping me up and pulling my toes under my knees, and the downhills didn't turn into a gravel slide. They have just enough give and flex in that rock-protected sole to keep my wobbly body upright, in spite of what gravity would want me to do.

Short lugs also mean less trapped debris and more contact area, which is why the grip carries over to hard, wet surfaces. That’s a big part of the healthcare-worker love: it’s a grippy sole that doesn't feel like a clunky work clog or like you’re rounding in cleats.

The optiFoam² midsole sits in the moderate-cushion zone. It’s soft enough to take the edge off a long shift or a long run, and firm enough that it doesn't feel mushy on uneven stone. It’s not quite a Goldilocks for all situations, but where it works it sings. The 8mm drop is traditional and calf-friendly, so it suits people who are on their feet all day.

It’s not terribly responsive overall, but somehow, in spite of that, it feels fast on gravel, especially on the trickier uphills, where you lose a lot of oomph in most hybrid shoes. It has a nice “settle into your stride” ride feel that gives you a lot of pep for the price. It’s not a racer, but I could see myself racing in them.

The weather in the mornings was, for a period of time, frankly, unholy. My feet stayed dry and comfortable the entire time. My body: not so much. The Gore-Tex membrane handles puddle splash, wet grass, and gravel dust (read: concrete) without seepage, and the upper is easy to wipe down, which is the second reason it’s popular in hospitals and clinics: spills, splashes, and grime aren’t likely to be permanent.

The trade-off is the standard Gore-Tex trade-off: it’s just less dang breathable than a mesh upper, so it runs warmer. Fit-wise, it’s a standard width suitable for most runners—save for those with a really wide midfoot—with a secure, wraparound lockdown.

There’s a quirk to gravel nobody chats about, but I obsess over—the aforementioned concrete situation. The quickLace neo bungee system is great for quick, adjustable fit, but wet gravel dust can dry on the bungee and basically cement it in place. The laces get stuck.

The fix: wet them down a little. A bit of water de-cements the dust and the system moves freely again. Rinse your shoes after dusty runs and you’ll never notice.

Here’s where I must be honest about the limits. This is not the shoe for flat, slabby rock or highly technical trails. The lugs are too shallow to bite into loose, steep, or rooty terrain, and the sole isn’t quite flexible enough to conform around rock features. If your trails look like a scramble, look at a deeper-lugged trail shoe instead.

And it looks good doing it. The number of these I see on chic New Yorkers in my office is wild. But, the important bits, like the abrasion-resistant upper, hold up to gritty, scuffy use, and the reflective details are aces for dark early-morning starts.

Is the Salomon Aero Blaze 4 Grvl GTX waterproof? Yes. It uses a Gore-Tex membrane, so it’s waterproof and wind-resistant.

Is it a trail shoe or a road shoe? It’s a hybrid gravel shoe. It’s at home on gravel, fire roads, and mixed paths, but it isn’t designed for technical, rocky trails.

Are Salomon Aero Blaze 4 Grvl GTX shoes good for nurses? Many healthcare workers swear by the third version and are grabbing the fourth for the grip. It’s easy to clean and moisture-resistant.

GTX or standard Grvl? Pick the GTX for wet, cold conditions. Pick the standard version for dry, warm weather and better airflow.

The Aero Blaze 4 Grvl GTX is a stylish, abrasion-resistant, grippy gravel shoe that doubles as a great on-your-feet-all-day option. It’s not a technical trail mistress, or a trail racer, and it isn’t trying to be. For gritty gravel, fire roads, and messy mixed terrain in lousy weather, it’s one of the best new choices around.
