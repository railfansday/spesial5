Billy Sharp sends EFL Championship promotion warning to Birmingham City

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

EFL legend and all-time Championship top scorer Billy Sharp has warned Birmingham City that there's still "a long way to go" after Chris Davies' side reached the second-tier summit ahead of the ongoing international break.

The Blues enjoyed an exceptional run of games between the three-week break in September and October and this current one, winning six of their seven games, three by at least a three-goal margin.

They dropped points only at Cardiff City, a side that has yet to lose at home this season, and have maintained their own unbeaten home record, winning all four games to add some gloss to a St Andrew's record which was pretty draw-heavy in the infancy of the campaign.

The win against former table-toppers Swansea City saw them climb above Middlesbrough on goal difference, and they will hold that top spot for at least another week until action resumes ahead of the Christmas period.

The question in the meantime, though, is: Can they stay there?

Billy Sharp has been a regular pundit on EFL shows this season, after formally announcing his retirement from the professional game back in October. The 40-year-old was promoted out of the Championship three times in his career, so he knows what it takes to maintain top-two form.

The all-time Championship top scorer waxed lyrical over Birmingham through the opening 15 games of the campaign, and was actually surprised that their run to the top of the table hasn't been given as much attention as it arguably should have.

"I think you saw glimpses last season, but they obviously didn't have that consistency," he told the Essential EFL Podcast. "They've done it really quietly, actually; no one's really spoken about them.

"He’s got the two settled centre-halves, which I've liked since last season, and I think he's playing a 4-2-3-1, which really suits him at the minute.

"He seems to have a settled side and had a little sprinkle with Millar coming in, adding a bit of magic down the left, and Stansfield got the two goals, which should do him a world of good. We know he’s got the ability, and when he’s firing, he can win games for Birmingham."

It's so far, so good for the Blues this season, but we are just 15 games in. With over two-thirds of the year still to go, and just one point separating Chris Davies' side from West Brom in fifth, Sharp feels that they can't rest on their laurels at all.

"Am I surprised they're top? I am," he admitted. "But we were saying this about Swansea on the last international break.

"It’s still tight at the top, fair play to Birmingham; going into this international break they are top, but there’s still a long way to go."

As Sharp mentioned, Birmingham's current run of six wins in seven to reach the top of the Championship has gone slightly under the radar, with the main second-tier narratives surrounding West Ham United's drop-off, or Middlesbrough struggling without Will Lankshear, for example.

Now, though, this Blues side are at the forefront of the conversation, and will be until league action resumes after the international break. Chris Davies' side are there to be shot at, and all eyes will be on whether they can handle the weight of expectation.

It's arguable that they didn't last season, with many expecting them to follow in the footsteps of Ipswich Town and make it back-to-back promotions after their record-breaking 111-point campaign in League One the year prior.

But this year, the squad is much more settled. Partnerships which were forming last year are fixed now, and there hasn't been as much squad churn as there was last summer at St Andrew's.

Plus, Chris Davies' decision to move away from a patient, heavy possession-based style has worked wonders, with the Blues packing a real punch in an attacking sense due to their desire to get the ball forward quicker.

They look a lot more ready to maintain results and handle pressure as they become the side teams want to knock off the pedestal.

Sharp also spoke about how this exact conversation was being made around Swansea's chances at promotion, with Vitor Matos' side sitting atop the Championship at the last international break.

And while the Swans did return to action with back-to-back wins, they followed that up with four defeats in five leading up to the break, including that loss at St Andrew's last time out.

They're still in contention, sitting three points off Birmingham, but the difference in performances with the pressure on, compared to when it's not, was there to be seen, and Blues have to guard against the same happening to them.

Perhaps, by mid-December, this could be a side with some daylight between themselves and the chasing pack, and then people may be taking their current title charge a lot more seriously.
