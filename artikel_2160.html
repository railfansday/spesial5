Cardiff City 3 – 3 West Ham United | The morning after..

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

An absolutely mental evening still feels like a win the next morning, despite West Ham leaving behind another two Championship points.

On a night when league leaders Middlesborough lost to Wrexham (the same Wrexham who lost 6 – 0 to the Irons just a few weeks ago), West Ham United somehow managed to close the gap by a point after being 3 – 0 down, out and destroyed at half time.

Trying to pick out any performance of merit in that first half is likely to be fruitless, such was the extent of the Hammers’ capitulation.

Whether it was the stinging rebuke of 3000 away fans booing them off at half time, a dressing room dressing-down or inspired substitutions –  who knows – but the Hammers suddenly restarted with purpose, pace and directness.

Time, surely, in the international break which lies ahead for Parker to stamp his character on this West Ham side.  Compared with such a weak, floundering first half from Morato, Solomon and Castellanos, their replacements Piroe, Autsbø (better late than never) and Mayers looked relative world beaters.

Scott Parker, remember, brought Areola to Fulham in 2020 and with Hermansen conceding nine goals in the last three without even laying a glove on any of them, he simply has to go.

Plenty of cause for optimism: The transformation of Maximilian Kilman and Jarrod Bowen from anonymous to game-changers typified the metamorphosis of the whole side – which can only have been due to what went on in the dressing room at half time.

Engels’ excellent goal was his first for West Ham and typified the Irons’ second half performance

Engels and Mukasa both looking like genuine attacking midfielders in the second half, both scoring really well taken goals.

Everything that had been missing from Nuno’s half time responses seemed to unfurl before our very eyes, as a team seemingly on a mission came out in the second half.

After that showing I’m confident of three or four changes on Parker’s team sheet when it is announced on Saturday – whereas you’d feel sure his predecessor would have stuck to his same eleven, with unsurprisingly the same results forthcoming, stubborn to the last.

Instead I’m looking forward to the team sheet on Saturday finally reflecting what Scott Parker, his coaching team and every fan witnessed in that dreadful first ’45.
