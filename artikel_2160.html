Sheffield Wednesday decision Dave Challinor made in midweek has split opinion

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Dave Challinor fielded an incredibly young, inexperienced side with numerous debuts handed out as their EFL Trophy group phase ended in heavy defeat against League Two side Accrington Stanley, and the decision to do so has split fan opinion.

Wednesday headed into Wednesday's clash already through in the Northern Group A of the Jersey Mike's Trophy, owing to wins over Sunderland's Under-21s and Salford City over the last international break.

Therefore, new Owls boss Challinor took Wednesday's clash with Accrington as an opportunity to rest some senior players, as various members of the squad battle injury, and hand some academy members an evening they'll never forget, making their debuts at Hillsborough.

The oldest outfield player was 21-year-old Gabriel Otegbayo, and an Accy side that had a mix of teenagers and 39-year-old Shaun Whalley leading the line took advantage to win 4-0 and steal the top spot in the group.

The defeat means that Dave Challinor opens his managerial account at Hillsborough with two defeats in different competitions to League Two opposition, but unlike at Oldham Athletic last Sunday in the FA Cup, this loss against Accrington could have a small asterisk beside it.

Reflecting on the game after the final whistle, Challinor put the onus on a proud night for the academy, with Louis Poland making his senior debut from the off, and Charlie Liddell, Kleven De Oliveira, James Kay, Kailen Hatfield and Fortune Onoh adding to that off the bench.

"If there's anything to take from it, it's that [a debut at Hillsborough]," he said.

"So whether it's families here, parents, all those sorts of things, I'm sure it will be a night they'll all remember, perhaps not the result they'd want to remember, but the opportunity to play for this club and in this stadium will live with them for the rest of their lives."

It may not have been the ideal result, but the young Wednesday group battled well at 0-0 for 38 minutes, and two late goals from substitutes Charlie Warren and Charlie Caton added some extra gloss to the scoreline for the League Two side.

The majority of the Wednesday fanbase appreciate the decision made by their new manager to hand out debuts and give some senior players a much-needed rest ahead of a return to league action against Plymouth Argyle on Saturday.

"Nothing to lose from tonight. Both clubs are still through to the first round of the sandwich trophy. An ideal time to put youngsters out and see how they fare. As it was a nothing game. Nothing to prove tonight, at all," one fan stated on X.

Another fan commented, "We had already qualified why would he risk any of our first team. Give the kids a run out, move on 🤷🏽‍♂️."

However, there are still some of the more demanding sections of the fanbase who expect Sheffield Wednesday to be competitive in every game they play, and for those who attended the game, they didn't feel as if they got their money's worth.

"Ahhh well, so long as the parents had a good time that's alright," one fan commented in a sarcastic tone, while another added, "Great for the youngsters and their families, but what about the Mugs like me who drove an hour in each direction and paid good money to watch a competitive football match!"

"I know he’s brand new to the post and the game didn’t matter. Losing 4-0 at home to Accrington Stanley is bad in whatever competition #swfc," was also amongst the discourse after the game.

However valid you feel both sides of the coin are, it can be agreed that the exposure of some of Wednesday's next generation is, ultimately, a good thing for the club, and Challinor shouldn't, and won't be, judged by this defeat in a competition they'd already qualified out of the group in.

Ultimately, it was a wise decision from Challinor not to risk an already injury-plagued senior squad in a rather meaningless matchup with Accrington Stanley on Wednesday night, especially with the Owls in league action this weekend.

Despite having more than three players out on international duty, not all of them qualify under the EFL quota to warrant a postponement, so Sheffield Wednesday will take on Plymouth in a massive matchup at Hillsborough on Saturday.

While Wednesday fans may not have minded an FA Cup run or a more competitve matchup in Challinor's first home game in charge, this is ultimately going to be the game where reactionary early verdicts are given over whether this Owls side will be skyrocketing up the division under their new boss.

Argyle sit three points ahead of Wednesday in the play-offs, so a statement win over Tom Cleverley's side could see the Owls jump back into the top six in Challinor's first game in charge.

It'll be a more energised senior side, given their lack of involvement in midweek, infront of a packed Hillsborough expecting an end to their six-game winless run.
