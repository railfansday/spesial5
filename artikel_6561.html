I Tried Dolly Parton’s Appalachian Apple Stack Cake, and It’s One-of-a-Kind

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

This warmly spiced cake from Dolly Parton can be made days in advance and is the perfect addition to your holiday dessert table.

All the adjectives I imagine to describe Dolly Parton, I can also attribute to this one-of-a-kind cake from her cookbook. It’s unlike anything I have ever made before, but at the same time it is warm and comforting, all while standing tall in a class by itself. With scents of apple, vanilla, cinnamon and allspice, it’s certainly a recipe to try this time of year and could even be a great addition to a Dolly-inspired meal.

An apple stack cake is a popular Appalachian dessert comprised of layers of spiced cake and apple filling. Dolly’s version uses molasses in the “crust,” aka cake layers and fresh apples in the filling (whereas other recipes often call for dried apples).

The original recipe for Dolly’s apple stack “cake” was actually referred to as a “pie” in her 1986 cookbook Dollywood Presents Tennessee Mountain Home Cooking. I wasn’t exactly sure why there was a crisscrossing of names until I started to prepare this unique recipe. The flour and butter in the “crust” layers are cut together using a pastry blender, much like you might do when making a traditional pie crust. Then, eggs, molasses, sugar, milk and spices are also added. This transforms the recipe into a thicker batter, similar to a scone or even a quick bread. The filling is like a thick, spiced applesauce with a thin layer spread between each stack.

I found the directions in the original recipe to be a bit confusing. For example, there was no mention of using cold butter even though the dough starts off more like pastry crust. In the instructions below, I added a bit more detail in order to clarify the process for anyone who wants to try making this special Dolly dessert.

In a large bowl, sift together flour and baking soda. Cut the cold butter into cubes and then use a pastry blender to cut the cubes into the flour mixture until it looks like coarse sand. In a separate bowl, beat together eggs, molasses, sugar, vanilla, cinnamon and milk.

Add the wet ingredients to the flour mixture and mix until it looks like a thick dough. Cover the bottom of 6 round cake pans with parchment. Grease and flour the cake pans. Evenly divide the dough among the cake pans and, with floured hands, press it out toward the edges of each pan.

Bake at 300°F for 10 to 15 minutes, or until golden brown. Cool in the pans for 10 minutes, then remove to a wire rack and continue cooling.

Cook the apples slowly in water until soft. Strain any remaining liquid. This should yield about 1 cup of pulp. Add sugar, cinnamon and allspice. Cook over low heat until the sugar dissolves and the mixture thickens.

Place the filling between layers, and stack one layer on top of the other. This recipe will make two 3-layer cakes or one 6-layer cake. This cake is best after it sits for two or three days. Dust with powdered sugar before serving, if desired. Yield: 10 to 12 servings.

I was glad I did a little research before making this recipe to learn that it’s best to add parchment to the bottom of each cake pan to avoid sticking. Because I did this, the layers released easily from the pans, so I highly recommend this step. I also took a tip from the Taste of Home Apple Stack Cake recipe to add powdered sugar on top before serving. Since this cake needed a little something to finish off the look, this was just the right move. Plus, I doubt Dolly would disagree with a little extra accessorizing!

I agree that this recipe tastes best after it sits for a couple of days. The flavor of the molasses in the batter melds with the warm spices to create a dessert that is truly in a class by itself. Given that it can be made in advance, that is a great way to get a head start on a holiday meal and enjoy a stress-free treat that is already done and ready to enjoy. Serve with a scoop of ice cream or a dollop of whipped cream!
