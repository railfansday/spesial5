GTA 6 Workers Union Survived Execs, But Drowned at the Shore

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Rockstar Games has had an incredible week following the launch of GTA 6, but the same can't be said for the developers who were hoping to secure formal recognition for their union. The results of a ballot among Rockstar's UK employees are out, and roughly three-quarters of those who voted have rejected the proposed agreement with the Independent Workers' Union of Great Britain (IWGB).

Rockstar’s UK staff will vote on union recognition from November 12 to 26, with the voting period spanning GTA 6’s November 19 launch.

As VGC reports, the ballot had a high turnout among the roughly 1,600 eligible employees. Only 25% of votes were in favor of the deal. It's a disappointing outcome for the organizers, especially considering that they managed to get as far as negotiating an agreement with Rockstar after last year's controversial dismissals.

The ballot opened on November 12 and closed today, November 26. Had the vote gone the other way, Rockstar would have been home to the largest formally recognized union in the UK video game industry, according to the IWGB.

The negotiations weren't just about securing better salaries for GTA 6 developers. The proposed arrangement would have given staff a means of collectively negotiating everything from overtime compensation to pensions, as well as a say in how the company handles redundancies and the use of AI.

Rockstar had already said it would recognize the union if its staff wanted one. What makes today's outcome particularly striking is that the agreement didn't fall apart at the negotiating table. It was the vote among employees that ultimately put an end to it.

Speaking about the result, Rockstar's head of publishing, Jennifer Kolbe, said it demonstrated employees' preference for dealing directly with the company. The IWGB, meanwhile, acknowledged that it still needs to earn the confidence of more staff members.

That doesn't mean the Rockstar Game Workers Union has ceased to exist, nor does it mean that employees have lost their right to join it. The union can still represent its members, but Rockstar won't be bound by the formal bargaining arrangement that was put to a vote.

It's worth remembering how unlikely it might have seemed a year ago that Rockstar's union organizers would even get to hold this vote. In October 2025, the company dismissed dozens of employees in an incident that subsequently led to an employment tribunal in the UK.

The IWGB alleged that the staff were targeted because of their involvement in union organizing. Rockstar, on the other hand, maintained that the dismissals were related to the sharing of confidential information on Discord. Today's ballot result doesn't settle that dispute, and the tribunal's outcome is still pending.

The timing of the vote also makes for a rather odd contrast with what's been happening outside Rockstar's offices. GTA 6 accounted for more than half of active PS5 and Xbox players tracked by Circana on launch day, and the game has already brought in billions of dollars in retail sales.

For some developers, though, the question is what happens to their jobs now that the game they've spent years working on is finally out. Even the commercial success of a game as big as GTA 6 can't guarantee that there won't be redundancies further down the line. That was one of the issues the union wanted to address before the launch.

The IWGB says it intends to keep organizing at Rockstar despite the vote. Hundreds of employees backed recognition, so the organizers aren't starting from scratch. Convincing the rest of their colleagues, however, is going to be a considerably bigger task than getting Rockstar to agree to hold the ballot in the first place.

GTA 6 is on track to be one of the biggest games of all time, but developers at Rockstar Games still have worries about job security.
