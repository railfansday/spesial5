He Played For Man Utd and Liverpool - But Was Banned For Racist Remarks

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

There have been several racist incidents that have marred matches between Liverpool and Manchester United which is usually one of the most must-watch fixtures in club football.

None more so than back in 2011 when Luis Suarez racially abused Patrice Evra during a 1-1 draw at Anfield. While tempers often flare between both sets of players, resorting to racism is cowardly and an issue that football still needs to prevent from surfacing on the pitch.

Suarez received an eight-match ban and a £40,000 fine for his actions which tarnished his legacy in the Premier League. Whenever he played a game at Old Trafford thereafter, he was heavily booed by home fans for the incident.

If you asked most if not all, United fans, they wouldn't even sign Suarez in his prime for the current team that sits in the relegation zone in November 2026. But there have been several players who have played for both clubs during their careers and one landed himself in as much trouble as the Uruguayan striker.

The name Peter Beardsley is well known to English football fans because of his legendary playing career both at Newcastle United and Liverpool. He cemented himself as a hero at St James' Park and also earned fan favourite status during his four years at Anfield.

Some fans might be unaware that Beardsley actually played for Manchester United before joining the Magpies and the Reds. That's because he only made one competitive first-team appearance, coming on as a substitute in a League Cup match against Bournemouth in October 1982.

Beardsley struggled to break into the senior team and then-manager Ron Atkinson decided against making a conditional transfer permanent. A six-month spell with the Red Devils was spent mostly in the reserves before he became a household name in English football.

After becoming an England international, winning two First Division league titles and the FA Cup with Liverpool, he called time on his playing career in 1999 at the age of 38. Sir Alex Ferguson once admitted he considered trying to bring him back to Old Trafford in September 1992 while he struggled at Everton but a return didn't come to fruition.

Beardsley didn't bid farewell to football after retiring and instead continued in the sport as an assistant coach to the late Kevin Keegan. He eventually took on the same role at Newcastle before coaching the youth teams at St James' Park.

That was until March 2019 when he left his role as coach of the U23 team to supposedly 'seek a new challenge'. This came amid allegations of bullying and an investigation into his conduct, which he denied.

In September 2019, the FA banned Beardsley from football for 32 weeks after finding him guilty of three charges of racially abusing black players. An independent panel found that he had called a player of black African origin a monkey during a game of head tennis.

Beardsley was also said to have told black players 'you should be used to that' when they took part in a team-building trip to outdoor adventure park Go Ape. His comments were deemed 'obviously racist and wholly unnaceptable."

The panel weren't of the belief that Beardsley was a racist, saying in a statement: "We are satisfied that Mr Beardsley is not a racist in the sense of being ill-disposed to persons on grounds of their race or ethnicity. It is also relevant that he has not had the benefit of training and education about offensive racist remarks and the importance of not making them."

Beardsley said in a statement provided by his solicitors that he had been “very surprised and disappointed by the decision." They alleged that it was "almost impossible" for him to clear his name because of the "serious flaws and contamination of evidence" during the disciplinary process.

Sandro Tonali, Eric Cantona, Ivan Toney and Luis Suarez are among the players that have been slapped with lengthy bans.

Keegan, John Barnes, Les Ferdinand and Andrew Cole were among the names whom his legal team cited as fellow professionals who offered him their support. Despite the panel determining that he wasn't a racist, the findings have resulted in him not returning to coaching and instead making rare appearances at events.
