Everton In The Community Launches 'Christmas Spirit Of The Blues' Campaign

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

This Christmas, Everton in the Community is calling on supporters to help tackle loneliness and bring the true Christmas Spirit of the Blues to those who need it most. 

Loneliness can affect anyone, at any time of the year. It could be an older person facing another Christmas without their loved ones, a young person struggling to find where they belong, a parent finding it difficult to cope or someone surrounded by people but still feeling alone. 

That's why the official charitable arm of Everton Football Club is launching Christmas Spirit of the Blues, a campaign dedicated to raising awareness of loneliness in all its forms and ensuring everyone knows there is somewhere they can turn. 

Dedicated to breaking down barriers and supporting its local community since 1988, Everton in the Community delivers more than 50 inclusive, free programmes, each designed to tackle different societal challenges. 

From supporting young people back into education and giving new fathers access to vital support networks, to helping military veterans navigate life after the forces, each programme shares the same ambition - to improve confidence, provide a sense of belonging and remind people just how important they are. 

To launch the campaign, Everton in the Community brought together participants, staff, supporters and players past and present at Hill Dickinson Stadium for a special performance of the iconic Liverpool Express hit, Spirit of the Blues. 

Liverpool-based brass band Allerton Brass performed a slowed-down version of the track, creating a poignant moment of togetherness and highlighting the power of music to bring people together. 

Throughout the build-up to Christmas, the campaign will shine a light on the experiences of people who have faced loneliness and explore how finding support, friendship and a sense of belonging has made a difference to their lives. 

Among those sharing their stories is Gina, who has found cherished friendships through Everton in the Community's Stand Together programme following the loss of her husband, and a young man whose experience of loneliness brought him to the brink of suicide. 

The campaign will also hear from father-of-two Sean, who reached out to Everton in the Community in the middle of the night after his wife tragically died just 12 hours after giving birth to their second son. 

“My newborn baby and his two-year-old brother were asleep, and I found myself feeling completely alone, drowning in grief and crying uncontrollably. 

“It was a hail Mary. I just needed to reach out to someone and, as an Evertonian, I knew about Everton in the Community – but I didn't expect the level of help I did get. It was a lifesaver.” 

At a time when his world had been turned upside down, Sean says reaching out gave him someone to turn to when he felt he had nowhere else to go, and marked the first step towards navigating life without his wife. 

 

Dan Halliday, Head of Fundraising at Everton in the Community, said: “For thousands of people across the Liverpool City Region and beyond, loneliness, social isolation and grief can feel even heavier at Christmas. 

“The pressure to feel happy or the absence of familiar handwriting on a Christmas card can be painful reminders of what's missing. 

“This Christmas, a donation to Everton in the Community could help someone feel seen, supported and part of something bigger. Together, we can make sure nobody has to face loneliness alone.”
