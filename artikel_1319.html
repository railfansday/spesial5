How Many Games Jesus Owono Could Be Banned For After Colliding with Jesus de Miguel

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

How many games Jesus Owono could be set to miss after colliding with Jesus de Miguel has now emerged, following the striker's tragic passing early on Tuesday morning.

CD Tenerife had initially provided a worrying update on their player's neurological condition at 4am local time, confirming that De Miguel had suffered a cardiac arrest after taking a high knee from the opposition goalkeeper.

Four hours later, it was announced that he had passed away in hospital, having been rushed there after his side's Spanish second-tier clash against FC Andorra, which was poised at 2-2, had been abandoned following the distressing scenes.

An update has since emerged on Owono, with the goalkeeper reportedly seen 'crying and praying' in the dressing room after the shocking incident. And now, with Spanish police having launched an investigation, the potential punishment he could face has been outlined.

According to Guillem Balague, a renowned Spanish journalist, the RFEF has suspended today's Cup matchday, some of which had already been moved a week because of the Urda train accident.

Tenerife have asked to postpone Saturday's League game against Granada and Tuesday's Cup tie against Badajoz, and the League and RFEF are expected to accept that request. Ballague also wrote (See his full post below):

"[Jesus] Owono faces a ban of four to eight matches, or even more for the extreme consequence of his clash with de Miguel."

.Any current footballing investigations will likely look towards the intent of the 25-year-old Equatorial Guinea international, though any legal action is currently considered unlikely.

As per the same report, it is unlikely that Owono will face criminal charges that many on social media - mainly through online abuse directed at the goalkeeper - have called for.

Police have launched an investigation because there has been a death, and that always happens as standard protocol, but it is almost impossible that anything comes of it.

As per Ballague, the tackle was a circumstance of the game, and Spanish Supreme Court doctrine (STS 379/2025) only brings in criminal law where there was clear intent to hurt, and it isn't related to the game.
