Rocks, Ridges, and Roads

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

I continue my trek through Pennsylvania by facing off with rocky vistas, but also smooth gravel roads.

Progress in Pennsylvania has been swift, the trail follows a relatively straight path across Kittatinny Ridge. The rocks are always a threat to slow things down, but I haven’t found it hard to do 20-25 miles a day even with daylight dwindling as the year moves forward. It feels like my last proper week on the trail. After I finish hiking on Saturday I will have just two more days remaining before heading back to Maryland to attempt the 4 state challenge. First things first, I need to get through this week.

Today should be somewhat eventful. I am hoping to squeeze in 25 miles, I will be crossing through a section of especially difficult rocks known as the Knife’s Edge, passing the unique Eckville Shelter, and ending near a popular viewpoint called The Pinnacle. I did well getting to sleep at a reasonable hour last night, helping me to start southbound from the Bake Oven trailhead around 7:30AM after my morning chores. My first thoughts when I got on the trail: “I am going to love hiking this last bit tomorrow!”. The trail was more of a dirt ATV path, flat, troublesome rocks removed, I could hike flat out, which I now really appreciate after the rocky sections that look flat and easy on the map but are a nuisance.

The flat trail was good while it lasted, but after a mile or so it reverted back to the typical Pennsylvania ridge hiking – a narrow, twisting path with mostly small rocks that weren’t bothersome. But then you’d look ahead and see a sea of boulders and think “the trail can’t possibly be routed through that?” And yet, that is exactly where it goes. I must not be too bothered by the rocks because I only hesitated a little when I saw a sign reading “Bear Rock” pointing to an enormous pile of rocks before sighing deeply and making my way to the top. This was no easy climb, there are blue blazes but hikers can still find plenty of ways up, none of them particularly direct.

A few more miles down the trail I came to another rocky section, but this one wasn’t optional. The trail narrowed to effectively nothing, the trail was just to follow the steeply sloped rocks and find a way to the other side. There was a decent view of the valley to the south, though more of my focus was on staying upright. This section is known as the Knife’s Edge. I don’t imagine it compares to the more famous/infamous Knife’s Edge on Katahdin. Scooby, a thru-hiker I encountered earlier in the year seemed to agree based on her comment on FarOut which I am going to shamelessly steal for your entertainment. I apologize for any language readers may find offensive:

Knife’s Edge my biffin’ boppy! The only Knife’s Edge I know is In THE GREAT NORTHERN MAINE BAXTER WILDERNESS. How dare this thin squirrely rock formation disparage the great Northern edge.

View from the AT near the Allentown Hiking Club Shelter.

I swung through the Allentown Hiking Club Shelter to retrieve a package. In a case of déjà vu, Pebbles had become separated from her Z Seat. I am told that it is actually Greasetrap to blame this time around. With the package secured, I continued along the ridge, which made up the first 14 miles of today’s hike.

The ridge continues west, but soon is divided by the Little Schuylkill River, so the Appalachian Trail shifts south to cross the river in Port Clinton. I won’t get there today, instead the trail heads south through the town of Eckville. There is a unique shelter just off the trail. The property was acquired by the National Park Service and turned into a hiker hostel/shelter in cooperation with the Blue Mountain Eagle Climbing Club, which maintains the AT in the area. In addition, Mick Charowsky, spent decades serving as the shelter caretaker. The property has a flushing toilet and a solar shower. I figured it would be worth checking out and getting a bit of water for my journey. I took a few minutes to peruse the logbook, finding a handful of familiar names, and adding my own name to the historic register.

Come with me, seat pad, you are needed up the trail!

Looking across the valley at The Pinnacle.

Looking across the valley at The Pinnacle.

The trail out of Eckville was the best so far in Pennsylvania. It was mostly a graded gravel road. I didn’t mind the gradual climb which lasted almost the entire 5 miles to The Pinnacle. By this point I had decided I would try to camp nearby so I could catch the sunset/sunrise from the overlook. I’d be coming up a few miles short of the 25 I had in mind, but I had space in my next itinerary to scoop those up. I headed straight for the rocks with my pack on and took in the scene. The Pinnacle sits at the leading edge of a ridge that drops sharply down to the valley just ahead of the viewpoint. An abundance of rocks ensure clear views. The only hitch was that the sun would be setting at the exact opposite angle from the rocks, it was the one thing you could really see. I figured it would still be perfect for sunrise tomorrow and set about finding a flat spot. I found plenty, though there were far fewer that weren’t so overcome with rocks that they seemed like they might lead to ancient Rome. I thought I had a decent spot, no rocks were visible, but when it came time to drive my tent stakes it is became clear that I had merely found a spot with about an inch of soil on top of a lifetime supply of rocks. I wiggled the stakes down until they were just about firm enough to hold for the night.
