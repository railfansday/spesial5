‘A huge pain in the ass’: Microsoft 365 starts to recover after a day of global email turmoil

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

by Todd Bishop on Nov 13, 2026 at 7:24 amNovember 13, 2026 at 4:23 pm

Updated at 4 p.m. PT Nov. 13 with additional Microsoft statements.

If emails sent by Outlook users on Thursday are just now landing in your inbox, no, you haven’t gone back to the days of the Pony Express. Microsoft is still digging out from a nearly daylong outage that cut off email for businesses, schools and governments around the world.

As of early Friday, the company reported that email queues were “significantly reduced and approaching normal levels,” though some users could still have trouble getting in or see delayed messages.

In a post on X Friday morning, Microsoft said it was “deeply sorry” for the disruption to customers and partners. The company said the outage was a technical issue, not related to a cyberattack or AI agents.

The problem was introduced during an earlier network capacity upgrade and surfaced after a more recent network change, the company said, adding that it has addressed the underlying issue and is still restoring service to remaining customers and regions.

We are deeply sorry for the disruption this incident has caused our customers and partners. We know the trust they place in our services and take that responsibility extremely seriously. We are seeing broad recovery and stability across our services.This was a technical issue…

In an update on its status page at 5 a.m. Pacific, Microsoft said it was seeing “broader improvements now observed in North America and sustained improvements across much of Europe,” along with early signs of recovery in Asia Pacific.

The problems started Thursday morning with Exchange Online, the Microsoft service that runs Outlook email for businesses, and spread to other Microsoft 365 services that depend on it, including Teams calendars, Copilot and SharePoint.

Microsoft said servers were failing automated health checks and dropping out of service, which overloaded the ones that were left. The company paused all deployments while it worked on a fix, and some of its repairs Thursday evening were “not taking as expected.” Microsoft said it restored service gradually, checking stability at each stage, which took longer but helped ensure a stable restoration.

Earlier, The Verge’s Tom Warren reported on X that his sources linked the outage to configuration changes in Azure Virtual Network, Microsoft’s cloud networking service.

Australia’s Home Affairs Minister, Tony Burke, told reporters in Sydney that Microsoft believes it is dealing with something similar in some ways to the 2024 CrowdStrike outage, “where there is an update that has gone awry,” the Associated Press reported.

“We know our customers rely on our services to get important work done every day, and regret the impact these outages had on those who depend on us,” said Charles Lamanna, Microsoft’s executive vice president of Copilot, Agents and Platform, in a statement provided to GeekWire. He said Microsoft’s teams are reviewing what happened and how to prevent similar problems.

In a post on X Friday afternoon, Lamanna said the investigation points to “cascading failures + thundering herds at our network perimeter,” which made recovery harder than normal. A thundering herd happens when large numbers of clients try to reconnect at the same time, overwhelming systems as they come back online.

He said Microsoft will publish a post-mortem “shortly” with a step-by-step timeline and the repairs it plans to make to avoid similar outages.

The outage reached well beyond email. Government offices in Japan, Singapore and the Philippines reported disruptions, according to the AP. Workhorse Group delayed its quarterly earnings call, and OpenAI and Anthropic both reported problems connecting with Microsoft 365 tools.

Credit rating agency Morningstar DBRS estimated insured losses of $300 million to $500 million, well below estimates for CrowdStrike, which ranged from $400 million to $1.5 billion. Some cyber insurance policies cover only malicious attacks, the firm noted, though it said the toll could grow if the outage drags on for several days.

The disruption hit close to Microsoft’s home. Ansel Herz, a spokesperson for the Seattle mayor’s office, told the Seattle Times that the outage didn’t bring city government to a halt but knocked out the systems that move documents and messages around City Hall.

The newspaper quoted Herz calling the outage “a huge pain.” In a post on X, he chided the paper for not using his whole quote, which we presume was “a huge pain in the ass.”

We’ve followed up with him to confirm this important detail. Here’s hoping he gets the email.

Update, 10 a.m.: Herz did receive GeekWire’s message and responded, confirming the quote and saying that, to his knowledge, nothing public-facing was affected at the city. Email appears to be back and working properly as of Friday morning, he said.
