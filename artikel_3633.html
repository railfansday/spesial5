EA removes some live service fluff from Skate and ends seasonal structure "in favour of regular, agile content updates"

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

EA is ditching seasonal updates and shaving some of the live service fluff off Skate (for some reason stylised as skate.) going forward. Normally, such news would be cause for concern. Here, the publisher assured players the transition will lead to more regular updates since devs can now push out new features without waiting for the next season to roll around.

"EA and Full Circle are committed to keep driving skate. forward. Just as a reminder, we're still very much in Early Access," the studio wrote in a new blog post. "The game is still a work in progress, and we're using this valuable time to experiment, iterate, and figure out what works best for the long-term health of the game - especially when it comes to social features and progression. Ultimately, we want to build skate. in a way that lets us roll out fresh content much faster, even if it's not totally finished yet."

Skate's fifth and final season is coming to an end on 1st December, with Full Circle embracing a different model from there on out. "We are transitioning away from the traditional seasonal cadence in favor of regular, agile content updates," it explained.

Full Circle is also throwing out the skating sim's version of a seasonal battle pass (horribly named skate.Pass) by the end of the year, and will rethink how the game's progression system works: "We've said it many times: we have a long-term future vision for skate, and this is a key part of it. Update 0.33.1 will be our very first update in this post-season era."

The move away from rigid seasonal updates "gives the development team the freedom to launch features earlier in their lifecycle," the blog post continued. "This means we can deploy work-in-progress mechanics and gather your direct feedback to continuously shape and build upon San Vansterdam. We've started this with in-game testing of features such as Spot Battles, Block Parties, and Prox Chat before rolling it out officially, and we’re going to continue with this process."

Full Circle's live service rejig also means the in-game currency Tix is being retired, and some maps will no longer be locked behind paywalls. Skate still has real-money microtransactions and a cosmetics store, though it's nice to see some of the more aggressive live service bits being plucked out.
