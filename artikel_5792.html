Trump ‘directly and indirectly’ intervened to get Nvidia’s CEO reinstated, POTUS says

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Nvidia's board reinstates Jensen Huang as Nvidia's president and CEO. Cybersecurity expert Theresa Payton joins 'America's Newsroom' to weigh in on the move.

President Donald Trump said on Monday that he believes Jensen Huang's reinstatement as NVIDIA's chief executive officer would have been unlikely without his intervention.

"Yeah, the answer is no," Trump told CNBC in an interview when asked about the development. "I believe my intervention was very important. And I worked with other people, you know, in the administration, JD and Scott and, you know and [Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick]."

Trump applauded the news that Nvidia's board decided to bring back Huang after he resigned earlier this month following reports of an extramarital relationship with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive whom the New York Times reported  U.S. intelligence identified as a suspected Chinese intelligence asset operating under corporate cover. Jensen Huang previously acknowledged an affair in a Nov. 8 statement.

"The United States Government has no reason to believe that Jensen’s return to NVIDIA poses any risk to National Security," Trump wrote in a statement. "The Attorney General of the United States has confirmed that the U.S. Government does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct."

Trump’s account of his role in Huang’s return clashes with concerns from members of his own Cabinet who have questioned the company’s efforts under Huang’s leadership to implement the security needed to safeguard its sensitive AI information.

In addition to developing cutting-edge AI processing chips used by top U.S. companies, Nvidia also partners closely with the U.S. intelligence community. Earlier this year, Nvidia secured a Department of Defense contract to help develop and deploy classified network capabilities.

"This effort supports the Department's AI Acceleration Strategy by enabling new capabilities across its three core tenets of warfighting, intelligence and enterprise operations," the Pentagon stated in May.

Even as the partnership between Nvidia and the U.S. government has grown, figures like Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent have cast doubt on the company’s information safeguards. Bessent has raised concerns about China’s acquisition of some of NVIDIA’s chips against U.S. restrictions, characterizing the company to Fox as having a "culture of noncompliance."

Ahead of Trump’s intervention, Bessent had recommended the company bring on their next CEO from outside the organization.

"We have been in touch with the temporary management, with the lead directors, with their lawyers and we do need answers," Bessent said in a recent interview.

"It behooves NVIDIA to move quickly on this because we want to know what the culture was. Was there a promiscuous attitude towards this? If it happened, where were the faults? And then they can move on."

Bessent and the White House did not immediately respond to requests for comment on whether their concerns about NVIDIA's security scrutiny remain.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio, who also serves as national security advisor, also raised alarms over Huang's ties earlier this year. According to The New York Times, Rubio oversaw a highly classified memorandum in the spring warning senior administration officials that Huang was in a close relationship with a suspected Chinese intelligence asset. The Washington Post later reported that Rubio had recast a CIA report in more assertive terms to warn Trump about the relationship, and that a leak of the memo's findings helped set off the chain of events leading to Huang's resignation earlier this month.

Democrats cited similar concerns as Bessent, criticizing the president for, in their view, hastily putting his thumb on the scale of Nvidia’s internal deliberative process to benefit a political ally.

Nvidia pledged $25 million to support Trump’s White House ballroom project.

"Trump is putting his own financial interests and the needs of his billionaire buddies before our national security, emphasizing that he’s learned nothing from his resounding election defeat," Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, D-N.Y., wrote in a social media post on Monday.

Schumer promised to investigate the relationship between Huang and Trump when Democrats retake control of the Senate in January.

"Democrats will wield their full power to investigate any wrongdoing by the Trump Administration, Jensen Huang, other NVIDIA executives, and anyone selling out our national security for financial gain," Schumer added.

Fox News Digital reached out to Nvidia and Huang for additional comment.
