Stop Payment on a Check Before It Clears – and Put the Order in Writing

To stop payment on a check, give your bank the check’s number and exact amount before it clears, since a check already paid can’t be stopped. Place the order in your bank’s app or online, or confirm a phone order in writing, because one left unconfirmed lapses after 14 days while a written order lasts six months.

Your account activity in the app or online shows whether the check has cleared. If it hasn’t, place the order right away, since the payee, or a thief who took it from your mailbox, can deposit it any day.

The six-month and 14-day limits come from the Uniform Commercial Code, the state law that governs checks. The 14-day limit applies only to an order given out loud, so a request typed into your bank’s app or website needs no written follow-up.

Have the check number, the exact amount, the date, and the payee’s name in front of you. A bank isn’t responsible for paying a check it couldn’t identify from what you told it.

Expect a fee. Chase charges $25 for an order placed online or by automated phone and $30 through a banker, and Bank of America charges $30, as does Wells Fargo on Everyday Checking. Chase Private Client Checking and Wells Fargo’s Prime and Premier Checking carry no such fee. For a mistake on a check made out to someone you trust, skip the fee and ask them to hand it back or tear it up.

A cashier’s check, which a bank draws on its own funds, and a money order can’t be stopped the way your own check can, so report a lost or stolen one to whoever issued it. An automatic payment from your account falls under the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau’s Regulation E instead, which makes your bank honor a stop-payment order it gets three or more business days before the payment is due. Tell the company you’re revoking its permission too, using the bureau’s sample letters. Neither step cancels what you owe.

After six months a stop-payment order on a check expires, and the bank may still pay the check in good faith and charge your account. Set a reminder for five months out, and renew the order in writing then if the check is still missing.
