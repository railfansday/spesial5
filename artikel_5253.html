Bitcoin CPI Reaction: 10 of 13 Cool Inflation Reports Triggered an Hourly Gain – Can Inflation Data Reverse the Downtrend?

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Bitcoin CPI reaction: October CPI lands at 8:30 a.m. ET, core seen at 0.22%. Cool prints lifted BTC in the first hour 10 times out of 13.

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes October’s consumer price index (CPI) on Tuesday, November 10, at 8:30 a.m. in New York, or 13:30 UTC. The Bitcoin CPI reaction that follows could set the tone for the week. The CPI is the main U.S. measure of inflation, and it will shape the Federal Reserve’s next rate decision.

Economists expect core prices, which leave out food and energy, to have risen about 0.22% in the month. That would be a slowdown from September’s 0.33%.

Economists expect growth in the core CPI to have moderated in October to around 0.22%, though the full range of estimates is broad enough to round down to 0.1% or round up to 0.3%.

Bitcoin trades near $74,400, still close to Sunday’s two-month low of $73,510.

CPI days move crypto. We checked Bitcoin’s first hour after the last 24 releases. When core inflation came in at 0.2% or lower, BTC rose in that hour 10 times out of 13. When it came in at 0.3% or higher, it rose only 4 times out of 10.

So which way will today’s number push Bitcoin?

The forecasts point to a calmer month. Seventeen banks and research firms surveyed by The Wall Street Journal see a median rise of 0.25% for headline CPI and 0.22% for core. Journal reporter Nick Timiraos posted the table on X.

That spread matters. The government reports CPI rounded to one decimal place. With forecasts this spread out, the core reading could print anywhere from 0.1% to 0.3%. A 0.3% would match September’s pace and read as inflation that is not cooling.

The yearly comparison will also be messy. The government shutdown in October 2025 meant no CPI was published for that month, so there is no clean year-over-year figure.

This is the last CPI report the Federal Reserve will see before its December 8-9 meeting. Traders already expect a rate hike: on Tuesday morning, Polymarket gives an 86.5% chance of a 0.25-point increase and 11.5% for no change.

Higher rates tend to weigh on Bitcoin. They make cash and bonds pay more, which pulls money away from assets that pay no interest, like crypto.

A cool reading could shave those odds and give Bitcoin room to bounce. A hot one would lock in the hike.

We measured the Bitcoin CPI reaction on Binance. We took the 24 CPI reports published since October 2024 and measured Bitcoin’s move in the hour after each 8:30 a.m. release.

The pattern is a lean, not a rule. The biggest drop came on February 12, 2025, when core rose 0.4% and BTC lost 1.9% in an hour. Last month’s 0.3% reading cost it 1.4%.

So far it has. BTC trades at $74,398 on Binance, about 1.2% above Sunday’s low.

On the daily chart, Bitcoin sits below both of its exponential moving averages (EMAs), averages that weight recent prices more. The 200-day EMA is at $76,481 and the 50-day EMA at $79,128.

For anyone tracking the Bitcoin CPI reaction, the first hour after 8:30 a.m. is the one to watch. In 20 of the last 24 reports, Bitcoin ended the next 24 hours moving in the same direction as in that first hour.
