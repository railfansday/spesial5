BMW Alpina Will Debut with a Sedan and SUV, but There’s More to Come

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Along with gas-fed and electric versions of the B7 and XB7, an avant-garde two-door flagship and a V-12 are in the works.

Alpina returns next year under new ownership with the newly minted BMW Alpina 7-series-based B7 (G72) and the X7-based XB7, both in internal-combustion and electric form. While the sedan will be a four-seater unless you insist on a rear bench, the SUV comes with three rows and six seats standard, according to our inside sources.

Naturally, BMW Alpina buyers can choose from a wealth of fancy options, such as two-tone paint, gradient colors, a mix of matte and glossy shades, a choice of different wheel designs, variable-tint windows and glass roofs, bespoke lighting scenarios, and fully automatic remote-control doors. Alpina blue and green remain the traditional trademark hues. Both models will feature a fully adjustable, comfort-oriented, specially tuned adaptive suspension. Also, in the B7, a reprogrammed ECU will permit a higher top speed of over 186 mph.

BMW Alpina’s model line should read like this: B7 40, B7 60 and iB7 70. The same gas-fed engines—a turbocharged 3.0-liter straight-six with around 400 horsepower and a twin-turbo 4.4-liter V-8 rated at up to 600 horsepower—will be offered in the XB7, which boasts a bigger battery and more grunt in the fully electric iXB7 Alpina 100.

What happened to the rumoured Alpina V-12, you ask? It’s still in the cards, but insiders don’t expect it to come to market anytime soon. The wait revolves around a life-cycle extension, a possible second facelift, and the late arrival of the new convergence platform dubbed CLAR Flex. But let’s start at the beginning. The current 7-series (G70), launched in 2022, was updated earlier this year and was due to be phased out in 2029, but this plan has apparently changed. Because of financial constraints and reduced demand, it now looks like the 7 must soldier on through 2032, which would stretch its lifespan to 10 years.

The follow-up, internally known as CF0, will likely be based on the CLAR Flex architecture, which reportedly offers significant weight, packaging, and cost advantages. The first model to tap this advanced matrix is said to be the next 5-series (codenamed CE0), which is scheduled to debut in 2031. CLAR Flex not only fuses pure-gas, plug-in hybrid, extended-range EVs, and fully electric elements in a superior, scalable manner, but it could also accommodate that coveted V-12 as one of the group’s few remaining upmarket unique selling points.

The Alpina brand launches next year, so by 2028, all markets should be able to sell all versions of both models. In terms of volume, we’re still talking low five figures here. Come 2029, the marque could do with an image boost for what’s next, which will almost certainly be at least one more new product.

To keep up the interest, those in the know predict that the two-door Vision BMW Alpina concept shown at the Villa d’Este earlier this year will be marketed in small numbers at a lofty tariff. Like the BMW Skytop and Speedtop few-offs, the bespoke Alpina four-seater would be manufactured in the coachbuilding wing of the Dingolfing plant.

At this point, anything from 75 to a couple of hundred units appears to be a realistic grand total. The Munich grapevine suggests that the head-turner designed by Max Missioni and Alex Innes will be based on a modified 7-series chassis for utmost presence and relatively generous cabin space. Although the halo car is still a work in progress, it is seen as an important step towards plugging the gap between BMW and Rolls-Royce with a batch of stylish individualized cars conceived to not only match but eclipse alternatives from the likes of Mercedes-Maybach.

Looking even deeper into the crystal ball, we can see Alpina return to its roots with a dynamic and luxurious yet subtly reduced reincarnation of the discontinued B5 midliner. Although Bovensiepen just left its mark on this segment with the B05 GT curated by Frank Stephenson and his team, only BMW has deep enough pockets and a sufficiently wide parcel shelf to offer the high-performance B5 with internal-combustion and EV powertrains in sedan and touring guise.

If they put their mind to it, the Alpinists could even extend the portfolio with an XB5, which was off limits for the previous keepers of the brand. If it gets the nod, the new B5 would, of course, also be based on CLAR Flex, which automatically puts the launch date back to 2031, at the earliest. Since the B05 GT costs close to the equivalent of roughly $220,000 before options, a B5 could be an attainable stepping stone for those who cannot quite afford the B7. On the other hand, a B3 is too small, not sufficiently prestigious, and not profitable enough. If it happens at all, we can only imagine it will be very low volume.
