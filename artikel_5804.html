PJM urged to delay base capacity auction until data centers cut from forecast

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Let Utility Dive's free newsletter keep you informed, straight from your inbox.

State ratepayer advocates and the grid operator’s market monitor say holding the auction in December as planned threatens grid reliability and could hit consumers with billions in unnecessary costs.

The request from the ratepayer advocates comes amid nationwide concern about energy affordability and data centers, as reflected in the midterm elections. The letter says that given “the urgency of the approaching auction,” its signatories request a written response from PJM’s board no later than Nov. 23.

In PJM, affordability concerns have been driven by a recent spike in capacity prices caused by growing data center load forecasts. PJM’s current capacity market rules effectively prevent data center companies from paying for their own power supply and infrastructure costs.

PJM recognizes the financial impacts rising electricity bills place on households and businesses, Jeffrey Shields, a spokesman for the grid operator, told Utility Dive in an email.

“The December auction includes an established price cap, providing an important safeguard against how high prices can rise,” he said. “The system urgently needs capacity to meet rising demand. Postponing the auction this close to its execution could destabilize the market and increase the risk of power interruptions when customers need electricity most.”

Electricity demand is growing faster than new power supplies can be built, according to Shields.

“While decisions about whether data centers and other large electricity users can connect to the grid rest with policymakers and utilities, PJM must plan for the demand those decisions create.” Shields said. “That means moving forward with securing the power we need while continuing to advance solutions that address the challenges of growing demand and the pressure on electricity costs.”

So far, including existing and forecast data center demand in PJM’s capacity market has increased capacity costs by $29.4 billion for all customers, according to Monitoring Analytics’ Nov. 12 report that called for the auction to be postponed.

PJM has responded to pressure from states, federal regulators and consumers by proposing a two-part plan to meet data center demand by facilitating bilateral contracts outside its normal capacity auctions, and also holding a backstop procurement for large loads that do not have bilateral contracts. It is also pursuing reforms to its rules for interconnecting large loads and generating supplies. The status of these efforts is pending.

The market monitor’s concerns were amplified by Exelon’s Nov. 3 complaint at FERC that challenged PJM’s three-year forward auction, partly over the uncertainty in forecasting data center load and the resulting billions in potential excess capacity costs, the ratepayer advocates said.

The advocates noted that PJM plans to publish the final “planning parameters” for the upcoming auction, which may remove canceled or delayed data center projects from the auction’s demand curve, on Nov. 19.

However, the market monitor’s recommendation is not simply to update PJM’s load forecast, they added.

“It is to fundamentally change how forecasted data-center demand is treated in the capacity auction so that existing consumers are not required to subsidize speculative or uncommitted data center demand,” the advocates said. “The delay would allow PJM to implement the IMM recommendations, subject to FERC approval and appropriate reliability safeguards.”

A “statement of principles” endorsed in January by PJM and the governors of its 13 states emphasized that large-load forecasts must be “real and verified,” the ratepayer advocates said. “Proceeding with an auction that incorporates both real and speculative data-center demand, despite the [market monitor’s] findings, would undermine that commitment and expose consumers to billions in potentially avoidable costs.”

The letter was sent to PJM by the Office of the Ohio Consumers’ Counsel, the Delaware Public Advocate, the West Virginia Consumer Advocate, the Maryland Office of People’s Counsel, the Pennsylvania Office of Consumer Advocate, the New Jersey Division of Rate Counsel, the Illinois Attorney General’s Office and the Illinois Citizens Utility Board.
