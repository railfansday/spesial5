Hull City an inspiration for decades to come as Sheffield United and co take on Championship's parachute regiment

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Along with Ipswich Town and Coventry City, if Hull can stay in the Premier League next season, it will be a shot in the arm for the Championship, just as Leeds United and even more so Sunderland's 2025-26s were.

Because at times in recent years the gap between the top two divisions has felt almost unbridgeable.

Since the introduction of parachute payments in 2006-07, 29 of the 60 relegated teams were promoted back to the Premier League whilst being eligible for them, making them less parachutes, more bungee ropes.

Nevertheless, Coventry and Hull got up last season without them, Hull on the back of a 2024-25 where they only escaped relegation on goal difference, then rebuilt their squad under transfer restrictions which prevented them buying players or even paying loan fees in either window.

As Wilder's Blades try to scramble back this season after a summer of big cutbacks when they lost their parachute payment, it is something to take cheer from.

The veteran manager called the Tigers: "An unbelievable example for the next ten, 15, 20 years in the Championship is Hull City.

"They find themselves in the Premier League which in a way I love because it doesn't go on stats at all, it goes on character, togetherness, spirit.

"It goes on ability and a group that had young players as well as the senior players, and we know quite a lot of them because they played at this club (or Middlesbrough under Wilder) and it just shows you money doesn't buy everything.

"It's about constructing a team that can go nine months and find a way to win. They found a way to win all season, in the (play-off) semi-final and the final.

"They're in the Premier League and they won't give a damn about anyone's opinion about them. Good luck to them."

And despite signing 18 players in the summer, Hull have given themselves a good chance of staying up with a strong start to the campaign built around the core who defeated Boro at Wembley in the play-off final, rather than the shiny new additions presumably intended by the recruitment department to replace them.

Meanwhile, the three teams level on points at the top of the Championship in November's international break – Birmingham City, Boro and Stoke City – have mounted their challenges without parachute money. So too Swansea City and Queens Park Rangers in the play-off places and the Blades two points back.

"Knowing Middlesbrough, the owner (Steve Gibson)'s been incredibly supportive of that football club for a long time," reflected Wilder. "They buy, they sell, they've invested the money from Hayden Hackney and Morgan Rogers.

"There's all different ways but they've been ambitious in their approach.

"Coventry sold well – (Viktor) Gjokeres, Gus (Hamer) and other players.

"It (having a parachute) isn't a guarantee.

"Stoke City have got good owners, a great manager and I'm sure Mark (Robins) will crack it this season. They've not been in the top ten since they've come out of the Premier League (in 2018) and it was a club that under Tony Pulis were top eight for a number of years, accumulating funds.

"It's not always a gimme. That's how difficult the Championship is."
