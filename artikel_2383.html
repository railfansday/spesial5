Arsenal maintain perfect start but lack firepower of rivals

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Bukayo Saka maintained Arsenal’s perfect start to the season, but Mikel Arteta appeared to acknowledge that the Premier League champions are in danger of leaving themselves short in attack.

There is still time for Arteta to add another forward to his squad and whether he does so or not could be important, with the transfer window closing on Tuesday night.

Having sold Leandro Trossard and with Gabriel Martinelli and Gabriel Jesus on the verge of moves away, Arsenal appear light of forward options.

Lyon winger Malick Fofana has been offered to the club, but he is an underwhelming target given Morgan Rogers and Vinícius Júnior were pursued earlier in the summer.

“We are trying to improve the squad,” Arteta, the Arsenal head coach, said. “Until Tuesday night, we’re going to try to do that. I’ve been disconnected from the world for 24 hours because I think my energy and focus has to be on the match. I will give it [my energy] now.”

Asked if he currently has enough depth in forward positions, Arteta added: “It depends if everybody is fit or not. We know that the depth has to be really big when you are fighting for four competitions. So we’re going to try to do that.”

This was an Aston Villa team there for the taking after being gutted by their Premier League rivals and still waiting for new signings to be ready to play and gel.

But Arsenal made hard work of their victory and one senses there will be more games like this if Arteta has to rely on what he has already got until at least January.

Chelsea are showing they can score goals for fun having spent £117m on Rogers. Manchester City are strengthening their firepower with the signing of Iliman Ndiaye and Bradley Barcola has joined Liverpool. Arsenal have the defence and the midfield, but not necessarily the depth of quality in forward positions of their rivals.

Christos Tzolis is the lone attacking signing to date and there was evidence at Villa Park that it will be a risk to rely too heavily on the Greek winger.

Tzolis had made an exciting start to his Arsenal career in the Community Shield and the first Premier League victory over Coventry City, but he had to be taken off at half-time on Monday night.

The former Club Brugge winger not only lost out to Matty Cash, but was also at the centre of Villa appeals for him to be sent off.

Tzolis had already been booked for a foul on Cash, when, in the 36th minute, he appeared to foul John McGinn as Villa broke from the edge of their own penalty area.

McGinn appealed that Tzolis should have been shown a second yellow card, but referee Jarred Gillett let the Arsenal man off the hook.

Arteta was wise to take Tzolis off at the break and his replacement, Eberechi Eze, had a hand in what proved to be Arsenal’s 59th-minute winner.

Eze slipped in Riccardo Calafiori and Saka got in front of Ian Maatsen to poke the Arsenal full-back’s low cross into the net. Saka’s goal had been the first shot on target in the game.

Just before the goal, Arsenal thought they had been awarded a penalty. Ross Barkley lost possession with a sloppy pass, allowing Declan Rice to advance towards the Villa penalty area. He played the ball through for Saka and Maatsen barged into the back of the winger.

Gillett paused before pointing to the spot, but a VAR check showed that Maatsen’s foul had taken place outside the penalty area and Martin Odegaard fired the resulting free-kick wide.

The goal and the overturned penalty were pretty much it for Arsenal in terms of threatening the hosts, for whom goalkeeper Zion Suzuki made his debut. Suzuki did not have much to do with his hands, but impressed with his distribution.

It was Villa who went closest to scoring in the first half, through one of the club’s five surviving members of last season’s Europa League-winning team.

Emiliano Buendia scored Villa’s second goal in the final against Freiburg and the Argentine rattled the Arsenal crossbar with a 20-yard shot that had beaten visiting goalkeeper David Raya.

Before then, McGinn had sent a shot over Raya’s bar and into the building site where Villa are redeveloping the club’s old North Stand.

Buendia, McGinn, Cash, Pau Torres and Victor Lindelof are the only Villa players to have started the Europa League final who have not left this summer.

Defender Ezri Konsa is one of Villa’s Europa League winners to have departed and he made his Arsenal debut as a late substitute. The defender received a mixed reception, as he was first booed by some home fans but was then applauded.

Other than hitting the bar in the opening 45 minutes, Villa squandered a chance for a quick reply to Arsenal’s opener when McGinn hesitated instead of shooting after being played in by Nicolas Jackson.

Jackson lasted 71 minutes for Villa in what proved to be a promising debut. In the first half, he raced away down the right before sending the ball across the six-yard box. But there was no team-mate on hand to convert.

One of Villa’s other summer signings, Alejandro Garnacho, is yet to make an impact for his new club. The Argentine got an earful from manager Unai Emery for not being ready to go on. He eventually replaced Ross Barkley, but could not help conjure an equaliser.

This is the start Arteta wanted. But, in attacking positions, Arsenal will finish the transfer window weaker than they started it if the club are not active in the final hours of the transfer window. And that could prove costly.

Good evening and welcome to live coverage of the Premier League match between Aston Villa and Arsenal from Villa Park. The champions travel to the team that finished fourth last season in buoyant mood and form despite some misgivings within their fanbase about their recruitment this summer.

It amounts so far to making Pier Hincapié‘s loan into a proper transfer, revamping the left wing with Christos Tzolis replacing Leandro Trossard and Gabriel Martinelli, upgrading options in central midfield by buying Bruno Guimarães and selling Cristian Norgaard – and signing Ezri Konsa from today’s opponents to give them the greatest elite defensive depth that I can recall at club since Manchester City’s Treble winners had Kyle Walker, Ruben Dias, John Stones, Nathan Aké, João Cancelo, Aymeric Laporte, Manuel Akanji, Rico Lewis and Sergio Gómez on the books at the same time.

Villa, by contrast, have endured a turbulent summer of trading with the departures of Europa League winners Morgan Rogers, Youri Tielemans, Lucas Digne, the aforementioned Konsa, Ollie Watkins and Emi Martínez, raising, along with other departures, more than £315m. They have spent about £180m to remodel the team though Johan Manzambi, who is injured, and João Gomes, who is suspended after his red card in the 4-0 defeat by Brighton, are absent tonight.

We can expect to see Zion Suzuki, Matteo Ruggeri and hopefully Nicolas Jackson and Leon Goretzka who have been registered in time to play, plus the loan signings Aaron Wan-Bissaka and Alejandro Garnacho. Still to come are the pending purchases of Taylor Harwood-Bellis and Ibrahim Mbaye in a process that emphasises how profoundly Villa are invested in and dependent upon their manager, Unai Emery.

After so much upheaval, it would be foolish to set much store on Villa’s 2-1 victory last season, sealed by Emi Buendía’s winner in the fifth minute of stoppage time. Arsenal barely broke sweat in blowing Man City away in the Community Shield and cakewalked to victory over Coventry in the league’s opening fixture. His past work suggests Emery will be able to forge another formidable side from his talented new squad but this match feels two months too soon for him against his old club.
