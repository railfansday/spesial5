A Museum’s ‘Wanted’ Poster Campaign Brings Looted Painting Back to France

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

The Orléans Fine Arts Museum is on a quest to recover hundreds of works stolen during World War II — and the director wants your help.

Olivia Voisin discusses her “cold cases” like a police detective, but she is actually chief of the Orléans Fine Arts Museum in France, searching for paintings missing since the Nazis occupied the city in 1940.

The museum launched a campaign this summer to track down nearly 400 works looted during World War II. It created “Wanted” posters with images of lost art, plastering them in and around the city of Orléans and flooding the internet. Now, the first case has been solved.

An amateur sleuth in France discovered one of the looted paintings last month in Switzerland, as it was set for sale. Now instead of being put up for auction, “The Drinker,” a 17th-century work by the Dutch artist Willem Cornelisz Duyster, will soon be returned to Orléans.

It will eventually be displayed at the museum in a gallery dedicated to the missing works — with the hope that some day it will be joined by other recovered paintings, Ms. Voisin said.

The museum’s campaign to reclaim its missing collection comes as France has taken steps to make it easier to repatriate artwork looted in war or during colonization.

“The auction house was very cordial,” Ms. Voisin said of the Swiss business that had advertised the Dutch work. It had labeled the painting as a preparatory study, unaware that it was the known to be missing final version.
