12 Best Bar Carts to Elevate Your Entertaining Game (2026)

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Bar carts are not just for someone who likes to host a cocktail soirée. In fact, the predecessor of the bar cart was once a Victorian-era tea trolley. Only when the Prohibition hit did these side tables on wheels find their true calling. Many, many iterations later—think: Alvar Aalto’s iconic tea trolley, which has inspired many picks on this list, and USM’s blessedly minimalist spin on the genre—the beauty of this booze trolley remains that it can completely transform a room or give a useless nook some purpose. Though they’ve fallen out of favor in many modern homes, these rolling wonders make an elegant—and functional—home base for all your mixology essentials, from stemware and decanters to liquor bottles and mixers. They also don’t have to be used to haul booze at all, instead doubling as portable storage for crafts, books, beauty or bathroom supplies, and houseplants.

Employed outside, a stylish bar cart can give a dinner party some polish or emulate a poolside butler. Load it up with a pitcher of lemonade and a basket of sunscreen on top, and extra towels on the bottom and you’ll never have to leave your lounge.

Made of solid and veneered walnut with elegant brass details, the Cotu (from the makers of our favorite bar cabinets) is a delightful midcentury-style bar cart that has a distinctive asymmetrical incline on each shelf. Use the wider edge for housing smaller utensils and glasses, while leaving the shallower end for larger bottles that are less likely to topple over the edge.

Birch Lane’s Reinhart cart speaks to farmhouse design, especially in this punchy shade of blue. Turned legs give the overall look a polished appearance, but scalloped paneling on the top shelf and a fun hue ground the piece in something that’s suitable for more playful design aesthetes.

Think of the Wallace Serving Bar Cart as the anti-bar cart. The wooden design resembles a sleek-looking side table thanks to its grounded base, but underneath, a discreet set of wheels gives you the ability to roll the decor piece anywhere a gin and tonic or negroni needs mixing. Its clandestine silhouette also allows it to double as a shelf for non-drink making goods like magazines, coffee table books, or your favorite potted plants.

While this circular cart might lack ample space for large-scale barware, its Art Deco style gives it an Old Hollywood feel. Placed in an area that gets lots of sunshine, the amber tempered glass shelves diffuse a warm glow, layering in some unexpected coziness during cloudless days.

Business & Pleasure Co.’s Al Fresco bar cart is a throwback to a time of leisure under the Parisian sun. While the cart can be used indoors, we can’t imagine a better place for it than on a terrace surrounded by a garden. As if the charming scalloped trim wasn’t enough, the designated umbrella hole nails the summery vibe for poolside mojitos or curbside lemonade stands.

To find that heirloom quality of Victorian design lore, look no further than Greenrow’s Liona bar cart with Barley twist legs to charm you an dyour guests. The two shelves in a chestnut finish offer ample space for glassware and a floral arrangement, should you want to create a whole spread for summer spritzers.

Here’s a piece of decor with lots of Mad Men energy. With its sloped legs and clean-lined silhouette, this cart has enough midcentury-modern charm to appeal to retro design lovers. Two full-size shelves have ample room for old fashioned and martini ingredients, while a mid-tier half shelf can either support a few extra bottles or a stack of your favorite mixology books.

For studio apartment dwellers or homes where square footage is precious, this miniature cart is slim enough to slip into discreet spaces until happy hour rolls around. Made of sturdy beech wood, rounded edges and a light hue soften the beverage cart’s overall look. We think these elements also make it well-suited for adorning Scandi- or Japandi-centric spaces.

Leather accents, exposed wood grain shelves, and an antiqued gold finish bring a polished look and feel to this spacious bar accessory. Two levels with a generous amount of clearance makes it easy to style, whether you’re packing it full of bottles or prefer a more curated decor approach of sculptural glassware.

This sleek, minimalist bar cart will stay with you for many moves. The chrome steel frame defines the iconic silhouette, and you can choose from nine powder-coated steel panels that come in classic black and white, as well as ultra-bold yellow and emerald.

For Bauhaus style on a budget, the Cullin bar cart is more than ready to serve. The angular silhouette resembles less of a cart and more of a full on booze-fueled vehicle, and feels especially cheerful in green or brick red colorways (white and black are also available). With storage for four wine bottles, you can stock multiple kinds of sippers, from sparkling to organic, to please even the snobbiest of guests.

Sturdy and sustainably sourced, this midcentury beauty from West Elm is made in a Fair Trade facility from FSC-certified kiln-dried eucalyptus wood. The soft honey finish of acacia veneer gives this piece accessibility, while the antique brass details add a touch of flair.
