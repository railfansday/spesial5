Two players accept qualifying offers, other five decline

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Today was the deadline for accepting the qualifying offer around MLB. Eight players were given a qualifying offer. Jake Bauers was the first to decline after signing a four-year contract with the Red Sox. The remaining seven players made their decision today. Two have accepted their offers and the other five have declined.

Ryan Jeffers is one of two players who was set to test free agency this offseason. The catcher decided to accept the offer and return to the Twins for the 2027 season. Jeffers has been with the Twins for his entire career. In 2026, he posted a .256/.353/.461 batting line with 14 home runs and 56 RBIs. Over his career, he has posted an 11.2 bWAR and 10.9 fWAR.

David Bednar was the other player that accepted the qualifying offer. The Yankees' closer was also set to test free agency for the first time, but also accepted the offer and will return for one more season. Bednar has a 2.47 ERA and 2.61 FIP in 2026, recording 35 saves in 64 games over 69 1/3 innings. He was acquired by the Yankees from the Pirates at the 2025 trade deadline and has recorded 146 saves over the last six seasons.

The other five players who received the qualifying offer have declined it. This includes Randy Arozarena of the Mariners, Seiya Suzuki of the Cubs, Trevor Rogers of the Orioles, Brandon Lowe of the Pirates, and Adrián Morejón of the Padres.

All five of these players are predicted for major pay days, per MLB Trade Rumors. Rogers is the second best starting pitcher on the market and projected to receive a five-year contract over $100 million. Suzuki and Arozarena are the best outfielders and also looking at deals around $100 million. Morejón and Lowe are looking at smaller contracts, but could get deals with AAVs over the $23.15 million they would have received by taking the qualifying offer.

Meanwhile, Jeffers and Bednar were also on track for major contracts, but not necessarily above the $23.15 million value they will receive from the qualifying offer. Jeffers was projected for a four-year contract at $75 million, while Bednar was projected for four years at $68 million. Both will take the guaranteed money from the qualifying offer and look for a bigger contract in the 2027-28 offseason.

The five players who declined the offer will now enter the free agent market fully. Their original team will get draft-pick compensation based on the contract that they sign. This will also affect the pick that the Brewers will receive for Bauers signing with the Red Sox, dependent on the other contracts signed this offseason. Here is the breakdown of how those picks work.

For teams who lost a player that declined the qualifying offer:

If the team is a revenue-sharing recipient and the player signs for $50 million or more, they will receive a pick after the first round of the draft and before Competitive Balance Round A. This is where the pick will fall for the Brewers from losing Bauers.

If the team is a revenue-sharing recipient and the player signs for less than $50 million, they will receive a pick after Competitive Balance Round B.

If the team paid the Competitive Balance Tax, their pick is after the fourth round, regardless of contract.

Any other team would receive a pick after Competitive Balance Round B, regardless of contract.

For teams who sign a player that declined the qualifying offer:

If the team is a revenue-sharing recipient, they will lose their third-highest pick. If they sign multiple, they will also lose their fourth-highest pick. There is no penalty to the international bonus pool.

If the team paid the Competitive Balance Tax, they will lose their second and fifth-highest picks, along with $1 million from their international bonus pool. If they sign multiple, they also lose their third and sixth-highest picks.

Any other team will lose their second-highest pick and $500,000 from their international bonus pool. If they sign multiple, they will also lose their third-highest pick.
