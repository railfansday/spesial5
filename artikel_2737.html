Lights-out Luka and contagious chemistry: are these Lakers for real?

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

One of the components of Luka Dončić's shock trade from the Dallas Mavericks to the Los Angeles Lakers that caused great consternation was that he was just entering his prime as an NBA superstar. The worry was that the move could end that prime before it really even began – unceremoniously uprooting the Slovenian wunderkind from a team custom built around his strengths, fresh off a run to the NBA finals, to one who didn't really make sense at all. Sure, the bright lights of Hollywood would elevate Dončić's profile and usher him into a new echelon of stardom. But with Oklahoma City starting their run of dominance and rising stars around the league waiting in the wings, some believed his hopes of title contention (or even a long-elusive MVP award) had vanished.

But the start to this season raises the question: what if they were wrong? Dončić is neck-and-neck with Victor Wembanyama in the MVP race, and the Lakers have the best record in the NBA at 15-3, with the Spurs and 76ers just behind at 15-4. San Antonio and their gargantuan French superstar were title and MVP favorites by a wide margin entering this season. But Dončić and the Lakers are keeping pace by pretty much every margin. Friday night's NBA Cup showdown will be a duel with ripple effects and stakes rarely seen in the regular season, let alone in November. The two top records. The two leading MVP candidates. A basketball fan couldn't ask for much more.

While there's a general consensus, at least for now, that the MVP race will come down to Dončić and Wembanyama, pundits have been more reluctant to acknowledge the new-look Lakers' emerging contenderdom. They have hovered near or occupied first place in the Western Conference throughout the season's first month, and they are top-11 in both offensive and defensive rating. Those are usually the hallmarks of a bona fide contender, but the skepticism persists.

Maybe that's because there was a some dubiousness about the Lakers' brass pushing their chips in this summer on Walker Kessler, the 25-year-old with alluring talent but a limited résumé, whom the team have slotted as their center of the future. Or perhaps it's that, aside from Dončić and walking basketball Cinderella story Austin Reaves (who has been sidelined with a heel injury but is slated to return against San Antonio), the roster is lacking in household names. Many of their players, from shooting guard Quentin Grimes to forward Sandro Mamukelashvili, are NBA journeymen, having spent years bouncing around the league looking for the right home.

The Lakers turned over 11 of their 15 roster spots this summer, an unusually drastic move for a team coming off a trip to the conference semi-finals and a second straight season with at least 50 wins. But they were committed to building a team around Dončić, and that required a bold approach to overhaul. JJ Redick, the team's head coach and someone who reportedly played a substantial role in handpicking the roster, acknowledged that turning over essentially the entire team in one offseason is not without its challenges, especially in a market like Los Angeles, where excellence is the goal and immediacy is the decree. "I made this comment to the team after we lost to the Warriors [on 30 October, one of their three defeats to date]: 'We've played six games together. Draymond [Green] and Steph [Curry] have played 13 years together,'" he recalled to me. "Patience is really important. I think one of the things I said in Rob [Pelinka's] and in my press conference right before Media Day was the belief that this team can become a great team. And that requires patience, that requires a lot of coaching and teaching and buy-in. So that whole process takes time. And you also have to do all of that while playing every other day, or five games in seven nights, and getting results stamped on your forehead every 24 hours. As a human, that's just hard to do."

The Lakers, by any objective measure, are becoming a great team. They may not be one yet (the bout with the Spurs will be their first real test after a soft early schedule) but they appear well on their way. There are several factors to point to – chief among them Dončić's historic scoring pace – but buy-in and chemistry should not be overlooked. It started, Redick and several team members tell me, on a team bonding trip to Slovenia this summer where Dončić began to level up as a leader. He self-funded the trip, expediting the bonding process with such a new group while also letting his teammates see his origin story. It was widely appreciated, and proved effective.

With a group of eager role-players immediately willing to buy into Dončić and Redick's vision, friction has been at a minimum, and roles slotted into with relative ease. "When we started training camp, I saw a lot of potential in this team," Dončić said after an overtime win against the Charlotte Hornets. "At the end of the day we have great chemistry, which is, in my opinion, very important. But I think this group is very special." His teammate Collin Sexton, one of the aforementioned journeymen, agrees. "We're gelling a lot faster than anyone could've expected. That's something that's helping us win games," he said. "That camaraderie, and that togetherness is something that's going to continue to allow us to expedite the friendships on the court, but also off the court."

I asked Redick why he thinks the group has been able to gel so quickly. He immediately recalled a team dinner he hosted in Las Vegas at Carbone Riviera, the night before a preseason contest in October. It was a two-plus-hour dinner, and when the checks came he looked around and noticed something. "I get up to leave, and it struck me: nobody had left. Nobody had been on their phone," he said. "Part of this, beyond just the time spent together [in Slovenia and in the lead-up to the season], is the people in there – the people in that locker room. It's just, they want to be part of a special group."

Fresh off two "successful" regular seasons that ended in playoff disappointment, Redick makes a point to acknowledge that it's still early: "I don't know what this is going to look like after 82 games. I don't know what it's going to look like after 50. But I do know that there's something with this group. And every special group doesn't mean you win a championship. But it's a special group of guys."

No contender can summit the mountaintop on the power of friendship alone, so it really does, ultimately, come back to Dončić's brilliance. His teammates understand this, and are leaning into their jobs as supporting actors in his quest for his first proverbial Best Actor and Best Picture. Kessler, chuckling and shaking his head after another night of his teammate's dominance, stated definitively after Dončić's 53-point outburst against the Portland Trail Blazers this month: "He's my MVP, I think it's pretty clear right now."

Time will tell if these Lakers really have what it takes to win it all. But in a wide-open Western Conference, with everything seemingly falling into place faster than most imagined, they're operating with the ethos of a popular refrain: "Why not us?"
