Rams’ Kyren Williams, Blake Corum keep team afloat during rough waters

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The Rams have spent millions assembling a roster loaded with superstars, former MVPs and future Hall of Famers. Yet two of their most valuable offensive weapons continue to operate without nearly the recognition they deserve.

Kyren Williams and Blake Corum don't have the MVP trophy of Matthew Stafford, the celebrity status of Myles Garrett, the highlight-reel of Puka Nacua, the television commercials of Davante Adams or the Hall of Fame résumé of Aaron Donald.

But what they do have is production. And lately, they've been carrying the Rams' offense on their backs like a couple of firefighters hauling people out of a burning building.

Los Angeles Rams running back Blake Corum (24) leaps past Los Angeles Chargers cornerback Elijah Molden (2) on his way to scoring a touchdown during the second half of an NFL football game. AP Photo/Caroline Brehman

In doing so, they've rescued the Rams' season, and a new social media analysis suggests NFL fans are finally beginning to notice.

According to data compiled by BetOnline Sports & Casino through the first 10 weeks of the NFL season, Williams ranks third among the league's most underrated players based on fan conversations on X. Corum also appears among the players drawing attention for being overlooked.

It's hardly a scientific measure of talent. But the numbers on the football field make a much stronger argument.

Williams leads the NFL in rushing success rate for the second consecutive season, converting 61.4% of his carries into successful plays.

Corum, meanwhile, leads the league with 0.17 expected points added per rushing attempt, a measure of how much each carry improves his team's chances of scoring.

Among running backs who recorded at least 100 carries and averaged eight attempts per game in 2025, Corum ranked fifth in yards per carry and second in rushing success rate, trailing only Williams.

Corum also is top 10 in missed tackles, gains of 15 yards or more, and is one of the NFL's most efficient running backs. Oh, and he's only fumbled twice in two years.

Think about that. Two running backs sharing the same backfield finished first and second in a category measuring how consistently they keep an offense moving down the field.

And somehow, they're still being overlooked.

Corum's emergence has given coach Sean McVay the kind of reliable one-two punch the Rams haven't enjoyed in years. Williams brings toughness, receiving ability and dependable pass protection. Corum adds patience, vision and the explosiveness to turn a modest opening into a significant gain. And neither needs 25 carries to make an impact on the game.

Their value became impossible to ignore after injuries stripped the Rams of their top receiving weapons for Sunday's rematch in the desert with the Cardinals.

Blake Corum #22, Kyren Williams #23, and Ronnie Rivers #20 of the Los Angeles Rams celebrate. Getty Images

With Nacua and Adams sidelined, the Rams needed somebody to keep their offense functioning. Williams and Corum answered the bell.

Corum rushed for 100 yards and a touchdown on 17 carries, averaging 5.9 yards per attempt. He added three receptions for 19 yards as the Rams won their fourth consecutive game and improved to 6-4.

McVay even proved why many across the league see him as an innovative offensive genius when he opted for nearly two dozen plays of 20 personnel featuring Williams and Corum in the backfield together.

It was another reminder that Los Angeles doesn't need a healthy collection of superstar receivers to move the football when its running game is clicking.

Over a recent three-game winning stretch, the pair combined for 424 rushing yards, averaging 4.9 yards per carry. The Rams averaged 29 rushing attempts between them during those victories.

"We're just being able to get comfortable," Corum said. "It's one thing to watch film, but to actually be out there and feel the defense and figure out what they do, how they tackle, how you can set your blocks, how you can get them to trigger and then you bounce around."

The Cardinals game provided the clearest evidence yet that Corum deserves a bigger spotlight. But Williams' quieter afternoon — 23 rushing yards on 12 carries — shouldn't erase what he's delivered all season.

Los Angeles Rams running back Kyren Williams (23) runs the ball against Las Vegas Raiders cornerback Hezekiah Masses (35) during the second quarter of an NFL football game at Allegiant Stadium in Las Vegas, Nevada, Sunday, Oct. 25, 2026. Getty Images

Together, the two have given McVay something more valuable than a single workhorse: a backfield that can absorb injuries elsewhere, control the clock and keep Stafford from having to rescue the offense every Sunday.

With Green Bay looming on Thanksgiving Eve and the NFC playoff race tightening, that identity could determine how far Los Angeles goes.

Williams and Corum might not dominate the national conversation like the NFL's high-profile running backs.

But if being underrated means doing the dirty work while somebody else gets the headlines, the Rams have two of the best examples in football.
