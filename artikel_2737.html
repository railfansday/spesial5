Syracuse 74, Albany 53: Orange go 2-3 zone to defeat Great Danes

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

SYRACUSE, N.Y. — It was shades of Syracuse past on Wednesday night.

After being deadlocked for much of the night with the Albany Great Danes, Syracuse needed to go to a 2-3 zone to put away its in-state opponent by a final score of 74-53 from the JMA Wireless Dome. It was the second game in a row in which Gerry McNamara had to go to a different defense to change the tenor of the game.

Syracuse (5-2) was led by Abdramane Siby's 17 points on 8-8 shooting. Sadiq White once again posted a double-double with 10 points and 11 rebounds. Ryan Moesch flirted with a triple-double, going for 13 points, 9 rebounds, 8 assists and 4 steals.

Albany (4-5) was led by Ollie Engen's 23 points. Syracuse had a 44-26 points in the paint advantage and the Orange bench out-scored Albany's 33-16. Moesch made his second career start with Garwey Dual sidelined with an ankle injury. Aiden Tobiason remained sidelined with a hamstring injury but did go through warmups.

Siby checked in first off the bench and got things started in the scoring column for the Orange on a dunk. Syracuse showed press early, going to a 1-2-2 as the Orange jumped out to an 8-2 lead. Sadiq White picked up two early fouls, however, and had to sit for extended time on the Syracuse bench. Amarri Monroe checked in for White and Kiyan Anthony rounded out the rotation off the bench.

Anthony and Moesch scored back to back transitions layups as Syracuse went up 13-8. Albany responded with a 10-2 run to retake the lead 18-15. A frustrated McNamara called timeout at the 8:32 mark. Displeased with his starting and backup center, McNamara inserted Tasman Goodrick into the game to man the middle. Goodrick would close out the half with Luke Wilson and Siby on the bench.

White returned back into the game with his two fouls at the six minutes mark in the first half. He'd finish a transition lob for a layup and was one of the few Orange players bringing energy in the first half. Both teams struggled mightily on offense in the first and went into the break tied at 26.

Syracuse and Albany both shot 31% from the floor. Syracuse turned it over 12 times in the first half to Albany's eight.

The scoring picked up on both ends out of the half. Syracuse went inside to Wilson and Albany responded inside before Mark Morano connected on back-to-back threes.

White picked up two quick fouls and had to return to the bench at the 17:02 mark, his fourth a questionable call after some embellishing on Albany's Pedro Santos. After replay review, the call was reversed as Santos was called for the foul while White's fourth foul was wiped away. White stayed in the game with three personal fouls. Syracuse led 37-34 at the first media timeout of the second half.

Syracuse went 2-3 zone six minutes into the half with Moesch and Morano up top, White and Monroe on the wing and Siby in the middle. The zone was able to give Syracuse control of the game as the Orange opened up a double-digit lead and wouldn't look back the rest of the way.

Syracuse took an eight point lead after a pair of stops in the zone as White and Siby scored inside. Albany head coach Dwayne Killings called timeout. Anthony checked in for Morano out of the timeout as McNamara stayed in zone.

It was a mostly quiet night in the dome up until that point when Moesch and Monroe happened. Holding that eight point lead midway through the half Monroe worked for a baseline jumper to set the stage. Then, following a hustle play which resulted in a Moesch steal, Monroe hustled for an offensive rebound from an Anthony three and kicked it to Moesch, who promptly buried a three.

Then with Albany in zone, Moesch tossed a lob to Siby for a flush. On the next offensive play Monroe worked an extra pass to Moesch hit a three and he buried it. Killings called for his penultimate timeout with 7:51 to play. Syracuse led 59-43.

Moesch wasn't finished. He threw a deft bounce pass to a cutting Siby for a dunk. Syracuse stayed in zone on the other end and had waning success but preserved its margin. Monroe worked for another putback and with the game broken wide open he found Siby for a lob dunk that all but put the game to bed.

Syracuse was able to maintain a 20-point lead down the stretch and close this game out while staying unbeaten at home this season.

Syracuse hits the road to take on Oklahoma in the ACC/SEC Challenge from Lloyd Noble Center. That game tips on Dec. 1 at 9 p.m. and airs on ESPNU.
