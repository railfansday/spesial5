Keys to the GameDefense – Defense is always the key to winning. The Celtics are 2nd in the league with a defensive rating of 106.0. The Warriors are 15th in the league, with a defensive rating of 113.9. The Celtics are 6th with an offensive rating of 117.6 while the Warriors are 18th and have an offensive rating of 112.8. The Celtics have played good lock down defense for the most part this season, but they have also slacked off on defense in spurts. They need to get back to playing lock down defense consistently and for 48 minutes. The Celtics need to especially defend the perimeter as the Warriors shoot 40.9 threes a game at 36.4%.

Rebound – Rebounding is always a key to winning as you need the ball to score and a good way to get it is to grab rebounds. It takes effort to grab rebounds and the Celtics must make an extra effort to crash the boards and beat the Warriors to rebounds. If they put out extra effort on the boards, that usually carries through to the rest of their game. More rebounds gives them extra possessions and limits the possessions for the Warriors. The Warriors are 14th with 44.1 rebounds per game while the Celtics are 10th with 45.0 rebounds per game. The Warriors get 16.4 2nd chance points per game and the Celtics can limit those by aggressively crashing the boards. Focus and Effort – The Celtics need to stay focused for the entire game. Their loss to the Kings shows that they lacked focus. They shot just 60% from the free throw line and that definitely shows that they weren't focused. They shot 13 of 58 from 3 and they need to stay focused on making those shots. We know they are capable of hitting them but they lose focus and miss shots they normally would make. The Kings played with more effort than the Celtics in the 3rd quarter and that can't happen, especially against teams they should beat. Most of all, if the 3's aren't falling, stop shooting them and get to the basket. Stay FOCUSED!!

Move the Ball Carefully – The Celtics need to keep moving the ball to find the open man. Doing that makes it much harder to guard them. However, they have to be careful with their passes and they also need to be careful when they dribble the ball and not drive into a crowd and turn the ball over. The Celtics are usually very good at taking care of the ball but at times they lose focus and get sloppy like when they had 8 consecutive turnovers against the Nuggets. They can't afford to do that against the Warriors who average 19.1 points off turnovers per game.

X-FactorsOn the Road and Fatigue– The Celtics are on the road and have traveled across the country through 3 time zones to get there. The Celtics need to shake off the distractions of travel, being away from home, and playing on the road in front of a hostile crowd, not to mention jet lag and getting used to a different time zone. They need to focus on playing the right way and not on the distractions of playing on the road. The Celtics are playing on the second night of back to back games and so fatigue could come into play as well.

Underestimating – The Celtics underestimated the Kings. They rested 3 key players and they didn't give the effort they needed or have the focus they needed to step up in their absence to get the win. The Celtics can't underestimate the Warriors because even without Steph Curry, they have enough talent to beat a team that comes in expecting to win without the effort needed to beat them.

Officiating – Officiating always has the potential to be a factor in every game. Every officiating team calls the game differently. Some call it tight and others let them play. Some favor the home team while some call it evenly. However the game is called, the Celtics must adjust to it and not allow the officiating to take away their focus on playing the game. Complaining about calls rarely changes anything other than to take away from their focus on playing the game and irritate the officials.
