Zero Day on the Camino

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Day 36 was actually a zero day–the first one we have taken.

It felt weird to sleep in and not immediately pack up to hit the trail. While we still had to move to a new place later, we enjoyed a wonderfully leisurely morning. We stayed at our apartment until the last possible minute before checking out at 10:30 a.m. Luckily, the hosts let us leave our packs behind so we could head to the noon Pilgrims’ Mass.

Even though Mass didn’t start until noon, we knew the crowds would be massive. Plus, we were holding out hope that the famous Botafumeiro (the giant swinging censer) would fly today.

We arrived at 10:45 a.m. and scored incredible seats right near the front. Knockerz went off to explore the cathedral while I stayed behind on the hard wooden pew to hold our spots.

A woman sitting in front of us was saving the first two rows. As the cathedral filled to capacity, she constantly had to turn away people looking for seats. Many of the pilgrims couldn’t understand her language, and while I felt bad for the confusion, I was deeply grateful for what came next.

It turned out those rows were reserved for a group of American tourists who had paid the €500 donation required to make the Botafumeiro fly. We were absolutely elated!

Once Mass concluded, a group of ten priests wearing traditional velvet robes stepped up to the altar to begin the ceremony. One of them used a large coal shovel to load glowing hot coals into the Botafumeiro. The main priest then said a blessing and added incense over the heat.

Each of the ten priests held a 15-foot rope, all connected to one massive central rope attached to a pulley 80 feet above us in the vaulted ceiling.

Together, the priests pulled in unison while another started the giant censer swinging. Loud organ music and a soaring choir filled the air as they pulled the swinging Botafumeiro higher and higher.

“As it swung, massive clouds of incense smoke poured out. At its peak,

it looked like it would touch the cathedral ceiling.”

Sitting on the third row from the altar gave us a phenomenal, front-row view. It was one of those breathtaking sights you have to see in person to truly believe.

After the ceremony ended, we navigated dense crowds to join a long line snaking around the cathedral.

We descended beneath the main altar to view the silver reliquary housing the bones of St. James. Afterward, we climbed the stairs behind the altar to see the silver bust of the Saint.

Tradition dictates that pilgrims kiss the silver bust, but we opted for the “touch-only” approach. Looking out from the raised platform offered an incredible perspective: huge, sculpted golden angels stared directly down at us, almost looking straight into our eyes.

What do you do after such a spiritual experience? Naturally, you walk to Burger King for a Whopper.

I haven’t eaten at a Burger King in over a decade, but after weeks on the trail, there was something comforting about having a familiar taste of home.

With lunch finished, it was time to hunt down our next bed. Because we had only planned our trip up until yesterday, this extra night was a last-minute decision. Our accommodation choices were limited, but we managed to find another apartment in the Old City.

While it wasn’t quite as nice as the previous night’s stay, it had a massive 10-foot wooden front door and narrow patio doors opening onto a small balcony that overlooked a scenic alleyway below.

After taking a long, much-needed afternoon nap, we hit the streets once more to wander around. Knockerz was hunting for memorabilia, though she didn’t have much luck finding anything beyond t-shirts.
