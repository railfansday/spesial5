Ripple ETF Investors Hold On, But XRP Buyers Go Missing

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Home » Trendsetter News » Ripple ETF Investors Hold On, But XRP Buyers Go Missing

XRP ETF assets fell 31.6% despite modest redemptions, suggesting a lack of fresh demand, not a buyer exodus.

Ripple’s XRP-linked ETF assets fell 31.6% from September 30 through the Friday cited in the video, compared with a 20.2% decline for Bitcoin funds. Yet redemptions remained modest.

Dana Love, PhD, a popular market analyst examining ETF flows and corporate filings, argues that missing buyers, not an investor stampede, help explain the XRP’s deeper slide this month.

The XRP coin traded at $0.967 this Friday morning, following a last Friday low of $0.89.

Dana Love’s central claim: Bitcoin-related selling is weighing on XRP, while demand from XRP funds and a closely watched treasury company has failed to provide much support.

According to the figures presented, cumulative net inflows into XRP ETFs slipped from $1.809 billion on October 29 to $1.789 billion on Monday. That leaves roughly 98.9% of cumulative net inflows intact, despite the sharp decline in fund assets.

But November through that Monday brought no positive daily net flows. “Nobody is running for the exits,” the analyst says, “but nobody new was walking in.”

Bitcoin ETFs, meanwhile, recorded roughly $2.34 billion in November outflows through the same period. Monday’s $8 million inflow interrupted the selling, although the analyst cautions that one positive session does not establish a reversal.

The distinction matters: limited XRP ETF redemptions do not establish that XRP holders across the entire market are refusing to sell. Nor does a decline in fund assets measure withdrawals alone; falling token prices also reduce those balances.

Dana Love also questions Evernorth, the XRP treasury company whose October 14 release said it would “accumulate and deploy” XRP into institutional strategies beginning in the fourth quarter of 2026.

The video estimates that Evernorth had about $111 million in cash, including subsequent warrant proceeds, and says its shares still traded above estimated underlying asset value.

That could support further fundraising, although the company had not announced additional purchases in the period discussed.

Silence is not proof of inactivity. The analyst explicitly acknowledges that Evernorth may be buying without announcing every transaction.

For investors, the useful distinction is between broader market relief and renewed XRP-specific market demand.

Sustained Bitcoin ETF inflows would suggest selling pressure is easing; XRP inflows arriving independently, or a disclosed Evernorth purchase, would offer stronger evidence that XRP has found buyers of its own.
