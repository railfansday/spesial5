Warzone players blocked from multiplayer for using apps to find easy lobbies

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Warzone began shutting players out of multiplayer in Season 1 if they ran a paid app that scouted lobby skill levels.

The update, which went live on November 6, folded Modern Warfare 4 into the battle royale and swapped Verdansk and Rebirth Island for Hajin and Zodiac. It was a smorgasbord of new maps, guns, and perks, built to give everyone a clean slate. Some players, however, had already paid to skip the hard part.

Tucked into the November 5 patch notes, Activision labeled Warzone Agent, a paid third-party app, as unauthorized software that violated its Terms of Use.

The way the agent worked is players would copy the lobby ID shown in the bottom-left corner of the screen during pre-match warm-ups and entered it on Warzone Agent’s website, which returned the lobby’s average K/D ratio.

A low score signaled an easy crowd, while a high one gave them a reason to duck out and keep searching, a habit better known as lobby shopping. According to its site, the service cost $39.99 a year.

Under the new rules, RICOCHET Anti-Cheat flags anyone who launches Warzone with the app running and tells them to close it. Players who refuse lose access to Multiplayer modes, although Campaign stays open. Meanwhile, anyone who tries to slip around the block risks temporary or permanent account and hardware bans, and a hardware ban isn’t some small fry, since it can shut an entire device out of the game.

Warzone Agent never offered aimbots or wallhacks, and the devs acknowledged as much. Instead, it gave users intel the rest of the lobby simply didn’t have. Still, the patch notes stated that “any software that provides an unfair advantage undermines the experience for other players.”

The Call of Duty team doubled down in a thread on X the next day, saying it was acting against players willing to pay for “underhanded methods of gaining an edge.” It also pledged to keep going after tools and behaviors that compromise Warzone’s integrity.

The crackdown marked yet another chapter in Warzone’s long tug-of-war over matchmaking. Last year, Raven Software eased skill-based matchmaking with Open Matchmaking in Season 1, though plenty of players doubted skill had really taken a back seat.
