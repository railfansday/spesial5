Clinical landscape of cardiometabolic heart failure with preserved ejection fraction

Nature volume 659, pages 901–914 (2026) Cite this article

Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) has emerged as the predominant form of heart failure worldwide and is increasingly associated with obesity, type 2 diabetes and systemic cardiometabolic dysfunction. This cardiometabolic HFpEF entity can be conceptualized as a mechanistically defined endotype marked by endothelial dysfunction, metabolic inflexibility and maladaptive inter-organ signalling involving the liver, kidney, adipose tissue and skeletal muscle. The cardiometabolic endotype often corresponds to a clinically recognizable adiposity-related clinical phenotype. Despite the growing prevalence of cardiometabolic HFpEF, therapeutic progress has lagged. This is mostly because the classification structure used is based on a commonly over-interpreted biomarker—ejection fraction—although inadequate trial end points and a lack of mechanism-guided precision also have a role. Here we review the evolving epidemiology, mechanistic underpinnings and emerging therapeutic landscape of cardiometabolic HFpEF. We highlight limitations in current diagnostic criteria, the role of extracardiac drivers and the need for soft end points and systems-based modelling. Advances in '-omics', phenotyping and cross-disciplinary frameworks now offer a pathway towards mechanistically targeted interventions that could enable symptomatic control by modifying validated disease mechanisms that culminate in this syndrome.
