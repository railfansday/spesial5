‘Really making history’: five takeaways from the meeting between Trump, Xi and Putin

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Leaders focused on nuclear weapons and international court at their unprecedented gathering in Shenzhen

The presidents of the US, China and Russia met on Thursday for an unprecedented trilateral gathering on the sidelines of the Apec summit in Shenzhen. The session lasted more than an hour and Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin touched on nuclear weapons, the war in Ukraine and wider international security.

The trio lead the world’s three major nuclear powers and their decisions have far-reaching consequences for global security. However, their meeting in China ended without an announced breakthrough or concrete agreement on the main issues. Here are five key takeaways:

Trump opened the talks by telling Xi and Putin that “we are really making history”. He described the US, China and Russia as “uniquely positioned” to discuss reducing their nuclear stockpiles and lowering the risk of such weapons being used.

It was an unusually high-profile display of cooperation between three countries whose relations have been marked by significant tensions, especially over Ukraine, trade disputes and the Nvidia scandal.

Trump also argued that peace could not be achieved by turning down opportunities for dialogue. It was the first time the leaders of the three countries had held a trilateral summit of this kind.

The talks came against the backdrop of the New Start nuclear agreement expiring in February. The treaty limits the arsenals of the US and Russia, capping each side at 1,550 deployed strategic nuclear warheads.

In September last year, Putin proposed voluntarily maintaining these limits for another year, but Trump rejected that approach, calling instead for a new agreement that would include China.

In the meeting on Thursday, Xi called for every effort to be made to prevent nuclear war and for nuclear weapons to be “be completely prohibited and thoroughly destroyed”. However, no new arms control agreement was announced.

Putin told Trump and Xi that their countries had “many converging interests” when it came to resolving conflicts, explicitly mentioning the war in Ukraine. He also spoke of their shared responsibility for international peace and security.

Like Putin, Trump arrived at the meeting fighting a failing war of choice. The Iran conflict has become an increasingly uncertain and costly undertaking for the US with no clear end in sight, while Putin’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine, now in its fifth year, continues to exact a heavy toll.

The meeting offered little indication of progress toward ending the war in Ukraine, and Kyiv was not mentioned in the subsequent joint peace declaration.

Trump described the international criminal court as “out of control”, criticising what he regards as the court’s excessive overreach.

His remarks carry particular significance given that Putin is wanted by the ICC on charges of war crimes. The court issued an arrest warrant for the Russian president in March 2023, accusing him of unlawfully deporting and transferring Ukrainian children from occupied territories to Russia.

The US and China are not signatories to the Rome statute, the international treaty that established the court, and are not bound by a requirement for member states to carry out its arrest warrants.

A joint “peace declaration” issued after the meeting by Chinese and Russian state media called for UN reform “so that it may respond more effectively to global challenges”.

The statement said the shake-up would range from boosting fuel supplies to engaging in “dialogue to reduce nuclear risks”.

The US and China are already permanent members of the UN security council, each with veto powers. They, and Russia, have argued for a greater role for nations from Asia, Africa and South America.

Russia has openly opposed permanent security council bids from Germany and Japan, arguing that western and Nato countries are already overrepresented and vote as a single bloc against Russian interests.

The declaration also took aim at the ICC, saying the court and similar bodies “must not abuse their jurisdiction”. It emphasised state consent and respect for the immunity of officials, which could be interpreted as a challenge to the legal basis for the ICC’s arrest warrant for Putin.
