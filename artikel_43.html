Hi-tech Shenzhen takes centre stage as Xi sends ‘Chinamaxxing’ message at Apec summit

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Host city offers showcase of China’s rise into a confident and innovative power central to global economy

In a Shenzhen square on a balmy evening, locals and tourists watched the dozens of glinting towers that make up the city’s skyline ripple through the colour spectrum – cyan and violet, cyber green, China’s national scarlet and gold – repeatedly flashing the words “Apec 2026”.

The Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit is a dream showcase for one of China’s most advanced cities, and the country’s leader, Xi Jinping, is basking in its neon glow.

On the sidelines, a series of high-profile bilateral meetings and the announcement of a historic three-way summit with Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin have cemented Xi’s status as an agenda setter on the global stage.

The broader Apec summit, meanwhile, provides an opportunity for China to present itself as a steward of multilateralism against what it terms “unilateralism and protectionism” – that is to say, trade measures taken against Beijing by countries such as the US.

All this is set against the futuristic backdrop that is Shenzhen, a city that perhaps better than no other embodies China’s rise into a confident, technologically innovative power central to the global economy.

A photo display in the Apec media centre charts Shenzhen’s transformation from sparsely populated marshland, calling it “a miracle in the history of world development created by the Chinese people”.

“[Xi] wants people to go home Chinamaxxing,” said Prof Astrid Nordin, of King’s College London. “Shenzhen is itself part of the message. It’s a showcase of Chinese technological and economic achievements, and of China’s claim that it offers opportunities for prosperity in a world increasingly characterised by uncertainty and conflict.”

Like the light show, the summit is being carefully coordinated. The Financial Times cited people familiar with the matter as saying it would be one of the most tightly controlled in the organisation’s history, with leaders having to travel to a state guesthouse beside Shenzhen’s tiger safari park to meet Xi. The paper said diplomats had grumbled that the format “seems designed to reduce the free-flowing discussion characteristic of prior editions”.

Setting out its expectations for the summit, China’s foreign ministry has said it looks forward to “practical results in key areas such as trade, connectivity, digital economy, AI and so on”.

Already, Xi has announced trade wins with Canada’s Mark Carney, extending remission measures for certain Chinese steel and aluminium products by two years in exchange for tariff relief on Canadian pork, fish and seafood exports.

A meeting with Papua New ⁠Guinea’s leader, meanwhile, showed how Beijing’s geopolitical priorities have been absorbed and reflected by its allies, with James Marape recounting to Xi how he had closed Taiwan’s office in his country despite domestic challenges in doing so.

The most important meeting will be between Xi and Trump. The leaders of the world’s two superpowers are expected to tackle a broad range of issues including AI and trade. In a move that could strain the fragile tariff truce that the two leaders first agreed last year, the Trump administration said on Monday it would further restrict China’s access to advanced AI chips.

Christopher Adams, a senior adviser at the law firm Covington who was at the centre of trade negotiations with China for the Obama and first Trump administrations, said: “The trade truce may increasingly look like a ceasefire in a war where major combat operations remain on hold but skirmishes are escalating to the point where the line between peace and war is blurred.

“I don’t see pressure to announce a further extension of the trade truce in Shenzhen. If a significant extension is announced, that would be an upside surprise.”

China has not hesitated to leverage Apec’s multinational platform in service of its arguments. A commentary by the state news agency Xinhua on Tuesday said: “Amid deepening geopolitical rivalries and growing global divisions, [China’s hosting] sends a clear signal that great-power competition need not be a zero-sum game, and that one country’s development need not come at the expense of another.”

The chair of the Apec senior officials’ meeting, Chen Xu, said Beijing had made trade “openness” a key focus of its Apec year and looked forward to “achieving more practical outcomes on the trade front”.

The much anticipated trilateral summit with Trump and Putin, on the other hand, could prove largely symbolic. For Xi, “it provides the appearance of China shaping the world order as an equal to the United States,” said Dr Kevork Oskanian, an expert on Russia at the University of Exeter. “Agreement on substantial issues, which would, in any case, require extensive preparatory negotiations, is unlikely.”

For China’s ruling Communist party, the political pomp has another key purpose. Nordin said summits were “occasions for leaders to tell stories about the world, about their country’s place in it … and one of the most important audiences for those stories is at home.”

For the locals watching the skyscraper light show on Monday night, the symbolism was not lost. China’s export might had helped it become increasingly powerful on the world stage, said one local man, who did not give his name. “It’s so great that the summit will take place in Shenzhen,” he said. “I feel very proud.”
