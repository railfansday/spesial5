How Mauricio Pochettino's culture shock tactics will resurrect Man United: From 'Murderball' training that leaves players throwing up to telling Harry Kane to slim down and why even the smell of the kit must be spot on

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Published: 21:00 EST, 17 November 2026 | Updated: 21:00 EST, 17 November 2026

When the entertaining but ultimately rather pointless Ralf Rangnick was tasked with holding the fort as manager at Manchester United five years ago, he was asked whether his much-admired style of football – the high-intensity gegenpress aped by disciples such as Jurgen Klopp – could work with a team that had Cristiano Ronaldo, 36, and Edinson Cavani, 34, in it.

Rangnick replied that he was certain it could. The German was, of course, proved very wrong.

It is this kind of strange, blinkered hubris that we should not expect Mauricio Pochettino to bring with him to Old Trafford.

Heaven knows we have seen enough of that in recent times. Ruben Amorim and his back three. Erik ten Hag and… well maybe one day we will understand what all that was supposed to be about.

But no, this will not be Pochettino. This cannot be Pochettino.

This United squad is not one built for the new United manager’s favoured kind of front-foot, hurrying and harrying football. This is not Tottenham Hotspur 2016. It is not Kyle Walker, Toby Alderweireld, Son Heung-min and Harry Kane. It is not physical and youthful and energetic.

Mauricio Pochettino is waving goodbye to the US national team and hello to Man United this week. But he faces a daunting task at Old Trafford

This United squad is not one built for the new United manager’s favoured kind of front-foot, hurrying and harrying football

That is why Pochettino’s battle – at least for the remainder of this season – will be about hearts and minds as much as it is about anything revolutionary that he can bring to bear on the training fields of Carrington.

The good news for those hoping for quick improvement is that Pochettino – at least the one who took Spurs to a Champions League final in 2019 and to within a sniff of a Premier League title a few years earlier – is adept at the mental and emotional side of football. It is upon this that, initially at least, he may wish to rely.

Son told me about this when we sat down to look back on his career in England. He was prolific under Pochettino, a scorer of 75 goals in four-and-a-half seasons under the Argentine. But it was not always like that. Once – just like several current players at United – he was disaffected and unhappy.

‘Mauricio turned me round and it wasn’t about football,’ Son told me. ‘It was about me as a person and how he made he feel.

‘I needed help, basically. And he gave me it.

'He did so many good things that I can't even say the words. It was unbelievable.

‘Sometimes he had been my coach and sometimes he had been like my family.’

Pochettino inherits a jigsaw puzzle with the wrong-shaped pieces at Old Trafford. It’s a squad put together for and by six different managers.

Pochettino will want to improve the fitness levels at United and his intense methods might feel like an ice bath to their players

'He did so many good things that I can't even say the words,' says Son Heung-min of his manager at Tottenham. 'It was unbelievable... sometimes he was like my family’

On arrival this week, for example, he will renew acquaintances with Luke Shaw, one of his old Southampton players signed for Louis van Gaal at United back in 2014. Shaw – the club’s only real left back – is 31 now and recovering from injury.

The physical limitations of this United team were clear in the titanic but ultimately rather pitiful 4-4 draw with Aston Villa that proved to be Michael Carrick’s last game in charge just before the international break.

Invited back into a game they trailed 2-0, United’s front four briefly chased the life out of Villa when Unai Emery’s team were in possession. For 20 minutes, Villa could not get out of their own half.

But the likes of Bruno Fernandes, Matheus Cunha, Bryan Mbeumo and Amad Diallo simply could not maintain that level of exertion once the surge in adrenaline had spiked. By the time the game reached the hour mark, United were 4-2 down.

It is certain Pochettino will wish to improve some levels of fitness at United and his methods will perhaps feel like an ice bath to some of those United players who have approached the basic prerequisite of running like some kind of optional extra at times this season.

At Spurs, players were occasionally known to vomit during training. Pochettino told Harry Kane he could be the best striker in the world but not until he lost weight.

Six or seven years later at Chelsea, meanwhile, he punished striker Nicolas Jackson for picking up five largely needless yellow cards by ordering him to run laps of the training pitch. Jackson was only told why it was happening as he lay on the grass in a heap afterwards.

Pochettino took many of his principles from his compatriot and former manager Marcelo Bielsa whose ‘Murderball’ sessions eventually arrived in England during his spell at Leeds United eight years ago. The stated aim is simple.

The likes of Bruno Fernandes (centre) have struggled to maintain the physicality Pochettino will demand across 90 minutes in games this season

Pochettino took many of his principles from his compatriot and former manager Marcelo Bielsa

‘With Mauricio, there is no hiding,’ said former Southampton midfielder Adam Lallana on the Rest Is Football podcast.

‘Everything is high-intensity, pressing from the front, and repeating sprints again and again in training so that it becomes automatic on a Saturday.’

Trawl through Pochettino’s time as a frontline coach and the testimonies of unstinting, high-intensity work are strikingly similar. There has been no great change in the belief, even when he arrived in America to coach the US national team ahead of this summer’s World Cup.

'He brings that club intensity into the national team environment,’ says Juventus midfielder Weston McKennie. ‘You immediately feel the tempo he expects.’

For this to work well, a coach needs two things. A pre-season in which to embed that specific kind of fitness and a squad assembled to play that way.

At United, Pochettino has neither so we can expect it to be a slow burn. He is approaching the busiest period of the playing schedule – his team will play 12 games in the next six and a half weeks – and has immobile central defenders, full backs with limitations and a midfield unit that lacks legs when compared to the best in the Premier League and Europe.

Equally, Pochettino arrives back in a Premier League where many teams now trade off the same high-press principles that were once rather new. Ten years ago, it was Klopp’s Liverpool. Now it’s Fabian Hurzeler’s Brighton and many, many others.

Every player has a brain and a mind, though, and it is here that Pochettino will in all likelihood seek to make the greatest gains ahead of Sunday’s trip to Liverpool and beyond.

Pochettino arrives back in a Premier League where many teams now trade off the same high-press principles that were once rather new when he landed at Southampton in 2013

Pochettino can be blunt with players. He told Harry Kane he could be the best striker in the world but not until he lost weight

There is not a United manager since the end of the Sir Alex Ferguson era who has not run into trouble with individual players at some point. Ten Hag with Ronaldo, Jadon Sancho and Raphael Varane. Amorim with Marcus Rashford and Kobbie Mainoo. Carrick with his captain, Fernandes.

This, as much as anything, is what has held United back this season. United do not have a squad fit to win the league but that it sits in the bottom three says everything for the way Carrick and his coaching staff failed at their side of the deal.

Pochettino’s friendship with Ferguson is more than a decade old and endures. Ferguson will be thrilled at the appointment. Indeed, there are similarities to be found between the two. Pochettino, for example, is a small details man, a coach who understands that mood and motivation and cohesion can be just as important as tactics or indeed anything the opposition can bring on a Saturday afternoon.

Having reached out yesterday to a former Southampton player for input on his ex-manager’s methods, the former international gave an insight – albeit unwittingly – into the kind of loyalty Pochettino can inspire.

‘We need to let him integrate at United his own way,’ came the reply. ‘I don’t think he needs us talking about his secrets.’

Pochettino can be blunt with players. He was with Son. He was with Kane.

‘He said, “You need to do this and this and that’s why you’re not in the squad”,’ Kane once told Daily Mail Sport. ‘And it just hit me. This is what I’ve got to do.’

Equally, the 54-year-old trades in subtlety. At Tottenham, he would sit at the entrance to the training ground canteen to gauge his players’ state of mind as they walked in. He had staff video them at training and wondered why they always looked happier when they walked off at the end.

As strange as it sounds, Pochettino chose the detergent in the washing room as he believed the smell of the first team kit was important. He dropped a player simply because he had noticed him eating stodgy food before sessions and another because he believed his choice of car to be inappropriate to his relative lowly standing in the game.

These are the kinds of things that look clever when you win and like new-age bunkum when you don’t. At Spurs, Pochettino’s results spoke for him but what is critical now is what version has walked through the door at Old Trafford.

It’s true that, to a large extent, we all view him as Pochettino 2016. In the decade that has followed, there have been underwhelming spells at Paris Saint-Germain and Chelsea. His attempts at making the kind of emotional connections he thrives on in Paris, for example, soon ran into a brick wall with the words ‘Neymar’ and ‘Mbappe’ written on it.

To return to the Ferguson link again, Pochettino has not previously been shy of perceived similarities.

‘Ferguson used to tell his players that working hard is a talent,’ he wrote in his 2017 book Brave New World. ‘To me, if a player doesn’t work hard for the team, he won't play. Nothing happens by chance.’

What manager in football doesn’t value basic endeavour? Not one but, as mutual trust and shared belief starts to wane during a run of bad results, even the basics can get lost. With this in mind and with Pochettino’s energy and charisma in the building, it’s not unrealistic to expect a new manager bounce at Old Trafford.

His relationship with Fernandes will be crucial and the Portuguese should not expect the captaincy to be a given. At Tottenham, Pochettino gave it to his goalkeeper, Hugo Lloris.

Pochettino knows that coaches can’t speak to players the way they used to, or indeed may wish to. A greater degree of emotional intelligence is required.

Fernandes and Lisandro Martinez - both publicly critical of the set-up at United this season - can expect private words of advice from their new boss

Pochettino arrives at Old Trafford a wiser manager for working amid the background noise at Paris Saint-Germain and Chelsea

United’s players can therefore expect WhatsApp messages at 11pm at night – sometimes including attachments of training clips – quiet words of advice or observation when passing their manager in a corridor or even a short note, serious or otherwise, left in a dressing room locker. How the likes of Fernandes, Cunha, Rashford, Harry Maguire and Lisandro Martinez respond to all this will be key.

So, too, will Pochettino’s ability or otherwise to make the best of the clunky chain of executive command that sits above him. At Tottenham, Pochettino and chairman Daniel Levy hit a sweet spot that wasn’t to last but for a time saw them so in tune they even took a horse-riding trip together. It was a synchronicity that electrified the whole football club.

Here in Manchester in 2026-27, it will feel different. At United, senior players criticise executives to the media while the fans wave green-and-gold scarves on matchday and sing unpleasant songs about not one but two different ownership groups. None of that will go away just because yet another manager is through the door.

Pochettino may feel he has some experience of such background noise from his time at PSG and indeed Chelsea. Equally, much of what happens over the course of the rest of this season and, if he is lucky, beyond will depend on his own reserves of energy, desire and self-regeneration. Does his own well remain deep enough?

The man who swept into Southampton with little-to-no English but bags of ideas and energy in January 2013 was an irresistible force of nature and he ran hot through the Premier League for a while. If he brings some of that to Old Trafford then he has a chance of making an impression at least.

But Pochettino’s take on Murderball, punishment laps and the infamous ‘Gacon’ 5km running drill he has been known to use may have to wait until the summer. United’s immediate issues, he will find, are more about a failing culture, centring less on playing style and more on emotional substance.
