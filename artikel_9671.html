A balanced rota can still fail: a two-person scheduling example

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

A rota can give everyone the same number of assignments and still leave a shift uncovered. The problem often starts with a reasonable shortcut: assign the next shift to the person with the lowest current workload.

Here is a small, fictional example that exposes the limitation.

Both shifts need one person. At the start, Ada and Ben have equal workloads. If a greedy tie-break assigns Ada to the morning, the afternoon is left without an eligible person: Ben is unavailable, and Ada has reached her limit.

Yet a complete schedule exists: Ben in the morning, Ada in the afternoon.

Availability and maximum assignments are hard constraints. A schedule must satisfy them before its workload balance is useful.

The final two states are easy to confuse in a UI. A time limit is a statement about how much searching happened, not a proof about every possible schedule.

The same distinction is useful in packing, timetabling and small resource-allocation tools.

An exported schedule is easier to review when it travels with its assumptions. Keep the availability table, individual limits, time groups and solver status alongside the assignments. If an input changes, recompute the result before sharing it.

For an initial check, use a tiny example where you already know the answer. Then try an impossible case, such as two required assignments with a total capacity of one. Finally, test an invalid input and make sure an old successful result is not left looking current.
