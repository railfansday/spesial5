Accused double murderer gives evidence in high-profile case – as it happened

Beau Lamarre-Condon tells double murder trial he went through Jesse Baird’s phone and poured water into his fuel tank

The court has adjourned for the day. Here are some of the key moments from Lamarre-Condon’s evidence today.

The former police officer took the witness box as the first defence witness.

He became emotional as he spoke about his experience working for NSW police. Lamarre-Condon said he experienced bullying and torment at work and told the court of instances of homophobic comments from his superiors.

Lamarre-Condon told the court he poured water into the fuel tank of Baird’s vehicle in what he believed to be a “tit-for-tat”.

He alleged his experiences at work made him feel suicidal and his voice wavered as he spoke of the situation.

Lamarre-Condon said he read messages on Baird’s phone and accessed it while he was in the shower. He also told the court he looked through the device while Baird was sleeping and was chased out of Baird’s home.

That’s where we’ll leave our coverage for today. The trial will continue tomorrow morning.

Lamarre-Condon has told the court about an occasion when he attended the home of his friend – known to the court under the pseudonym Fiona Rosso.

At the time, he was commencing beginner acting classes and he asked Rosso if she could film with him.

According to Lamarre-Condon, they were rehearsing a “serious play” where the friend’s character wanted to go to the police, while the character Lamarre-Condon was playing did not.

Lamarre-Condon tells the court that he jokingly asked Rosso, “You would help me hide a body right,” and she responded, “Yes of course I would mate.”

double quotation markIt was joking banter in line with the play.

Lamarre-Condon tells the court the “banter” was something officers joke about:

double quotation markWhen you have a heavy suitcase, you might joke, ‘shit how many bodies are in here?’

It was not serious and now I would never, ever try to joke about that … if I could go back in time, I would never joke about those things ever again.

Lamarre-Condon afraid he would lose job over medication

Lamarre-Condon tells the court he wanted to start taking medication that belonged to his stepfather, who was prescribed medication for depression and anxiety:

double quotation markI was very fearful that I would lose my job as a police officer if I was prescribed any medication.

Lamarre-Condon says one of his superiors said that if he “finds out anyone is on medication, that we would not be able to keep our job”.

He tells the court he made a complaint about this.

Lamarre-Condon tried to avoid Paddington after breakup with Baird

Lamarre-Condon tells the court he tried not to go to Paddington after their breakup in November 2023:

double quotation markI did not want to see Jesse, did not want to run into Jesse.

Lamarre-Condon tells court he could have obtained a gun from youth gang member

Lamarre-Condon says his work in the youth command meant he could have gotten his own gun:

double quotation markI work with youth gangs. Some of their families are in the Comanchero … I could easily go to these kids that look up to me ‘here’s four grand … go get me a gun’, and have a gun six hours later.

Shortly after Baird asked Lamarre-Condon to refrain from contacting him in November 2023, Lamarre-Condon says he saw scratches on the rims of his Tesla.

double quotation markI parked so far away from the curb, I believed it was Jesse who did this.

He says his mother had seen Baird’s car earlier that day.

double quotation markI know tit-for-tat isn’t right, I did go to the street where Jesse parked his car and did pour water into the fuel tank.

Lamarre-Condon has told the court he posted a bouquet of flowers on to his story around mid-November 2023 while Baird was in Melbourne.

He says it was to give the impression it was from Baird and wanted to see if another man Baird was seeing would message him and say, “well I’m also dating Jesse”.

Asked if he had gone through Baird’s phone around this time, Lamarre-Condon tells the court he looked at Baird’s messages with that other man while the TV presenter was in the shower.

Baird later called Lamarre-Condon and asked if he went through his phone to block someone:

double quotation markI denied, denied, denied … within 10 hours of denying it I essentially came clean.

He and Baird went back and forth about the flower messages, he tells the court:

double quotation markI told Jesse the flowers were sent to my mum by her partner … that wasn’t true.

I then sent a voice not by text message, saying yes I did block [the man] on your phone, that was to try to insinuate they were from you when they weren’t.

I completely owned up to it, I was truthful and honest 10 hours later.

Lamarre-Condon tells the jury his experience at work made him feel suicidal:

double quotation markAll the constant pushback, the bullying, torment, being told I can go back to picking up dead bodies … [it] made me feel suicidal. I didn’t want to be at work any more.

His voice begins shaking as he describes how he felt:

double quotation markAs a youth engagement officer, I have a personal relationship with these young offenders.

Me not showing up to work is not like showing up to a general duties [shift] … This is me letting down young people that needed me as a role model, needed me to help them, needed me to be there for them.

It was too much for me and it made me feel like having thoughts to take my life.

Lamarre-Condon tells the court he tried to take his own life, picking up tissues and wiping his face as he gives his evidence.

Lamarre-Condon says he was having problems at work in 2023 after getting pushback on a proposal to run equestrian therapy for women.

When he escalated the issue beyond his bosses, Lamarre-Condon says direct supervisors were not happy:

double quotation markThey pretty much told me, if I wanted to involve them I can go back to picking up dead bodies on the street and not [go] back to youth command.

Lamarre-Condon tells court Baird was ‘screaming’ and thought he was a ‘random’

Lamarre-Condon says Baird did not realise it was him and began to gave chase.

double quotation markHe was screaming out to who he believed was a random saying, ‘Let’s go cunt you’re lucky I don’t have a fucking bat or a knife with me.’

When Lamarre-Condon realised he still had Baird’s wallet and phone, he threw it back at Baird.

The wallet fell on the footpath and the phone went down the drain, he tells the court.

Lamarre-Condon tells court about a night Baird left key out for him

Lamarre-Condon says he and Baird had a conversation about staying the night at the Paddington home on 17 August 2023 and he agreed to leaving the key in the letterbox.

He tells the court he arrived after 10pm, opened the back door and left a bobby pin in the front door.

The jury heard Baird was asleep when Lamarre-Condon walked into his room.

Baird’s phone was on his bedside table charging.

Lamarre-Condon says he picked up the phone and put it in airplane mode so it would not vibrate.

Lamarre-Condon then dropped the charger and “panicked”:

double quotation markI have a split-second decision: do I sit here and have a conversation about why I was on his phone … or do I run?

Lamarre-Condon says CCTV footage found on his devices of Baird and another man at the Royal hotel in Paddington was recorded during his police duties.

He coincidentally saw Baird that day and took the video of the screen, before sending him the footage.

Lamarre-Condon tells the court he said something like: “I came here to see you, but detective Beau is on the case.”

Lamarre-Condon says sexual relationship with Baird recommenced after birthday dinner

Lamarre-Condon has told the court he saw Baird on his birthday night in 2023 at the Unicorn hotel in Paddington:

double quotation markI did see Jesse, it was a very normal and amicable conversation … We had a normal conversation about normal things.

He says he received a birthday message from Baird earlier that day on social media.

Defence lawyer John Stratton SC asks: “Was there an occasion where you went to Jesse’s house and saw him through the bedroom window?”

Lamarre-Condon tells the court he asked Baird if he could collect his things from his home.

He says some time after 21 April, he went to Baird’s house and saw his bedroom light was on through his sheer curtain:

double quotation markI saw Jesse having sex with a man I did not know.

I paused for about 5 seconds … I immediately left, I didn’t stay like a little creep, I didn’t take any photos.

I left … and actually laughed about it with friends … we were no longer together …

Lamarre-Condon tells the court this happened on only one occasion.

Lamarre-Condon tells court Baird told him he ‘wasn’t ready for a relationship with anyone’

Lamarre-Condon tells the court he met with Baird on 17 April 2023 and told him he had read the messages between Baird and the other man.

double quotation markI told him this made me feel a bit upset because we were moving towards – I guess being a couple.

Jesse was honest with me [and] became emotional and started crying, said he wasn’t ready for a relationship with anyone not just me.

Lamarre-Condon said he was looking for something more serious and needed a few weeks away to “let my romantic emotions settle”.

He tells the court Baird then said: “Please let me know when you’re ready to hang out again. I would like to have you back in my life and see you.”

Lamarre-Condon says the situation was “very amicable”.

Lamarre-Condon tells the court it was “common” for Baird to leave his key out for him:

double quotation markI let myself in and I was waiting for him to come home from work.

A message sent from Baird to Lamarre-Condon, and read to the court, said: “Key is in the letter box for you.”

Lamarre-Condon tells court about using Baird’s phone

The former officer says in January 2023 he and Baird went to an escape room. Baird then gave Lamarre-Condon access to his phone and asked him to edit a video of them together at the escape room, Lamarre-Condon tells the court.

While editing the video, Instagram notifications began popping up from another man.

Lamarre-Condon says the messages were: “Jesse saying our relationship was somewhat casual.”

He tells the court he then used his own phone to take photos of the messages and sent it to other friends.

double quotation markI did not block … I did not delete any messages, I did not unfollow anybody at this stage.

Defence lawyer John Stratton SC is reading out messages between the accused and Jesse Baird from December 2022.

Lamarre-Condon messages: “Is it weird/too soon to say I kind of miss you already. Those cuddles and kisses on the nose though.”

Baird responds: “yeah I miss you too kind of wish you were coming down to Melbs to meet the fam.”

Lamarre-Condon says: “Hope you have an amazing day with the fam xx make sure you tell Helen I said merry Christmas.”

Baird replies: “Mum said to say hello, she already likes you.”

After a lunch break, Lamarre-Condon has returned to the witness box and is resuming his evidence.
