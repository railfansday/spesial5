MP Helen Haines’ first words since leaving hospital after alleged attack led to double amputation

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Independent MP says she is ‘overwhelmed’ by thousands of warm wishes and thanked Australians for charity donations

Get our breaking news email, free app or daily news podcast

The independent Australian MP Helen Haines has been discharged from hospital, four weeks after she was allegedly attacked by a man outside her electorate office in Victoria resulting in the double amputation of her legs.

In a statement, Haines said she had moved to the rehabilitation stage of her recovery, which would continue in Melbourne.

“This is a really positive moment, and I am full of gratitude for the expert and compassionate care I received at the Alfred [hospital] and am receiving in this next stage,” Haines said.

“I appreciate the thousands of warm wishes and supportive messages from people across Indi, throughout Australia and around the world. They are giving me strength as I focus on this next phase of healing.”

The prime minister, Anthony Albanese, said his thoughts were with Haines.

“We continue to provide support to Helen, her family, community and staff as she undertakes her rehabilitation,” Albanese said in a statement.

Haines said she was “overwhelmed” by the many Australians who had donated to the Save the Children charity fundraiser.

Her husband, Phil Haines, had made the request to the public on her behalf to donate to the charity in lieu of flowers in the aftermath of the alleged attack.

As of Monday afternoon, more than $1.46m has been raised for the charity through Haines’s fundraising appeal.

“I am overwhelmed by the generosity of Australians in the form of donations to Save the Children and I look forward to working with them in due course on applying these funds to improve the lives of children.”

Haines, the federal member for the Victorian seat of Indi, had been in a critical but stable condition after she was repeatedly struck by a vehicle in Wangaratta on 19 October. Her condition was then downgraded to “serious but stable” on 24 October.

Police will allege the 65-year-old was standing by the boot of her car outside her electoral office when a car did a U-turn and struck her repeatedly.

Tarrawingee man Brendan Nugent, 43, has been charged and the incident continues to be investigated. He is known to her family.

Police charged Nugent with dangerous driving causing serious injury, intentionally cause serious injury and the commonwealth offence of causing harm to a commonwealth public official.

Police have also charged him with two additional offences – cultivating and possessing cannabis – according to court documents.

Haines has been given leave of absence until 26 November, the last sitting day of the year. Parliamentarians during the last sitting week in October paid tribute to the independent MP, honouring her empathy, grace and intelligence.

MPs across parliament have expressed concern for their safety and that of their staff since the alleged attack, and prompted conversations about the temperature of political debate.

Albanese, during the last parliamentary sitting, warned against the rise in anger “that distorts and corrodes our public discourse”, and urged voters and politicians not to ignore it.

“Enabled by the anonymity and speed afforded by social media, we are seeing rage and aggression increasing, not just in politics, but towards workers in hospitality and retail, in schools and in hospitals, towards police, paramedics, and other officials, we are seeing a normalisation of the language of grievance and violence.”

Independent MP Allegra Spender said it was “wonderful” news that Haines was making good progress in her recovery.

“She is a remarkable person and her strength and positivity after everything that has happened inspires me.”
