Celtic legend tells protesters there is ‘better way forward’ as he reveals Michael Nicholson chat

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Paul McStay believes major shareholder Dermot Desmond is not planning to walk away “anytime soon” from Celtic as he underlined the importance of fans having a say in the running of the club.

The Celtic legend is a Celtic Season Ticket Alliance Trust ambassador and is back in Scotland from his base in Australia to launch his autobiography and raise awareness of Willie Haughey’s independent supporter initiative.

Haughey has pledged to buy £2 million worth of shares in the first instance if 10,000 supporters sign up. The principal ambition is to install a fans’ representative on the club’s board.

According to McStay, almost 5,000 of Celtic’s 54,000 season ticket holders have joined to date amid continued civil unrest at the club.

McStay, who plans to attend Saturday’s home game against St Mirren, is a link between the board and fans and witnessed the discontent first hand when he was invited to Parkhead last season to unfurl the title flag.

He cites the spirit of the team that overcame Hearts on the final day of the league season in 1986, when he was in his pomp in midfield, as the inspiration behind the Alliance’s efforts to reach a long-term goal.

“I go back to the ‘86 year, when we won the league,” he said. “Hearts were there dominant in pole position. If we wanted to have success and win that league as players and manager Davie Hay, we have to stay focused on what our objective was. It was win each game at a time. With the Alliance, our objective is 10,000 season ticket holders signed up and Willie Haughey says he will put money in and buy shares.

“I understand the frustrations last year, because of the European situation and Kairat,” he added. “The boycotts, the protests. I understand that. But I think there is a better way to move forward, a more professional, business-like way and try and get someone on the board to talk and communicate and provide sports opinion.”

He revealed he has discussed these aims with under-fire chief executive Michael Nicholson at a recent match.

“I go to games,” he said. “Last year I was back and invited as a guest to raise the flag so I was here when all the protests and boycotts were happening. The Celtic supporters want the best for their club but it is slightly different to what I think should be done. Again, that is about opinions. Even a couple of weeks ago I was at a game and I bumped into Michael – I am quite happy to give my opinions, whether that is in the dressing room with fellow players at the time or as captain talking to Fergus (McCann) or Billy McNeill or Liam Brady.”

An agm is scheduled for the start of next month. Last year’s meeting had to be cut short after Ross Desmond, Dermot’s son, was harangued while reading a pre-prepared statement from his father, who is not expected to attend this year's meeting either.

“You ask about Dermot and his position there,” said McStay. “He has put shares in the club. But to me I don’t think he is going to go anywhere soon. To me, this is a long-term objective. It might take time but we have to focus on what we are doing and build up support within our organisation to build up equity to give Celtic supporters a voice on the board. I think that will help things.”
