Julia Fox wants to pay women back for splitting bills on dates with men

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Women are fed up with being asked to split the bill on first dates. And now, Julia Fox, the actress and activist, is coming to the rescue.

If you’ve been dating in recent years, you’re aware that it’s a dumpster fire of an ordeal. Between conversations on dating apps that never go anywhere, unsolicited nudes, and a slew of unoriginal conversationalists, the only consistent thing about modern dating is low effort.

When it comes to women who are actively dating, it’s one of their biggest complaints—especially when it comes time to pay the tab. 

While it was once a standard practice in heteronormative romantic connections for men to pay the bill on a first date, women have been getting vocal about the fact that chivalry seems to be very, very dead. And they are over it.

The frustration comes as the cost of dating seems to be rising in lockstep with men’s perceived low effort. According to new research from the dating platform Seeking, 61% of Gen Z women have been asked to split the bill on a first date. That’s more than twice the rate of boomer women (27%) who have been asked to do the same. Meanwhile, 75% of women say they appreciate it when a man pays (like . . . duh?).

The gap could explain, in part, why so many women say they’ve given up on dating. According to a Pew Research Center study published in 2023, fewer than 35% of single women say they’re seeking romantic relationships, compared with 54% of single men.

Enter the Anti 50/50 Campaign, a movement led by Fox that aims to address one of women’s biggest gripes with modern dating. The campaign allows women who’ve been asked to go halfsies on a date to apply for reimbursement. 

All they have to do is create a profile on Seeking’s website, share the details of the experience, and upload a receipt of the date’s bill. Those who are selected will be reimbursed up to $50. The $50,000 reimbursement fund will be open until February 10, 2027, or until all the money has been claimed.

“I think we need to get over the idea that women should have to justify what they want out of dating,” Fox said in a press release. “I’m all for women being independent, but that doesn’t mean we have to entertain low-effort men who think splitting a check is the height of romance. If you’re going to court somebody, COURT THEM.” 

The campaign certainly aims to raise awareness about a common pink flag. But it also speaks to the fact that women don’t earn as much on the dollar as men. So many modern men seem to have forgotten that there’s still a glaring pay gap that hurts women’s financial stability. 

According to a recent report from the Center for American Progress, the pay gap has barely budged since the year 2000. In 2025, women workers made $13,760 less annually than men. Women who worked full-time earned around 84 cents for every dollar made by male counterparts.

“We’re giving women their money back while encouraging men to lead with generosity,” says Dana Rosewall, CEO of Seeking. “Somewhere along the way, the patriarchy convinced women that splitting the bill was empowerment, when really it just made being cheap socially acceptable.” 

In other words, if men are wondering whether or not women notice or appreciate it when they offer to pay for a first date, the short answer is: Yes, they do. It’s not just about money. 

It’s about effort, and not glossing over what may be easy for men to miss, but women surely don’t: that financial equality hasn’t quite arrived, no matter how hard women have worked to tip the scale.
