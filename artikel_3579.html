Model Alex Consani on Starring in American Horror Story and Pursuing a Career as an Actress

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Alex Consani, a mainstay of the runways and one of the most emblematic supermodels of her generation, was notably absent from the spring-summer 2027 shows in September and October, bar a few exceptions. That’s because the California native, 23, has been busy broadening her horizons: Consani is following in the footsteps of many supers before her—including Cindy Crawford, Gisele Bündchen, and Tyra Banks—and trading the runway for the silver screen to pursue a career as an actress. She’s currently starring in American Horror Story: 13, the latest installment of Ryan Murphy’s stylish horror anthology series. Up next, Consani will appear in Molly Gordon’s Peaked and Janicza Bravo’s Preppy.

On Saturday night, Consani joined Demna and his Gucci crew at the 15th annual LACMA Art + Film Gala, where she says she had to do a “Hannah Montana”–style wardrobe change so that her voluminous gown could make it to the step-and-repeat before changing again to “pump it in a side-slit” in the look she wore on the runway in September. Yesterday, the model and newly minted actress Zoomed in after touching back down in New York to break down her film ambitions.

Alex Consani at the 2026 LACMA Art + Film gala.

José Criales-Unzueta: I’m not going to lie, I miss you on the runway. But you’re busy and otherwise occupied!

Alex Consani: I miss being on the runway. I’ve been busy. I’ve been doing a lot of cool projects. You might not see me on the runway, but you’ll definitely see me on the silver screen.

I mean, American Horror Story, all the episodes just came out. I don’t know if you watched, but that was obviously super fun. I also have two projects that are coming out next year, Preppy and Peaked. I’m just really excited. I’m in this era now where I’m kind of shifting into a different space, and it’s exciting to be able to do it specifically with people that understand that. For fashion and film, there are many places where both come together in such a natural way, and I think the LACMA [gala] really is that.

I worked with Sarah Paulson for American Horror Story, and then I sat next to her at LACMA, and we’re both wearing witchy outfits. Sarah and I were looking at Demna and he was like, “Look at my witches,” [laughs]. I love that the fashion world and the film world come together in such a fun way. I think Demna specifically is really big into characters—it’s not just the clothes, but it’s the woman in the clothes.

Did you always intend to go into acting, or was it something that unfolded as you went into fashion?

It’s something that I’ve always been interested in. And I think, in a way, fashion for me has always been acting. I mean, what I enjoy most about fashion is being able to become the character, being able to ask the designer, who is the woman that’s wearing this? It gives me the most gratification when I’m able to build the world around the clothing. [Acting] came naturally, but obviously it was in the back of my head. When I was a kid, I did classes and stuff. With fashion, the time constraints or the time needed to be in fashion is a lot. So I think it was just difficult. I’m blessed to be at a point in my fashion career and also in the [acting] projects that are coming to me, that it isn’t impossible to do both.

I’m putting work into being a better actress. It’s a really exciting endeavor for me, especially considering all the projects that I’ve done that are coming out. There’s a lot of diversity within the projects. I did American Horror Story, which is obviously a horror TV show, and then I have a comedy movie coming out, and then I also have a period piece coming out. It’s fun to be able to bounce around those. And fashion-wise, fab, honestly, the fashion in the films that I’ve done is quite fun. It’s nice because then I get to give my insight as someone in fashion to all the teams, and it feels like a real collaboration, which is my favorite part of any creative job.

Alex Consani with Sarah Paulson (left) and Leslie Grossman (right) in the sixth episode of American Horror Story: 13.

You and I are from the generations that grew up on Ryan Murphy shows.

Coven, for me, is my favorite American Horror Story season by far. What was it like to join the project? Did you audition? Did you get a call?

I’m signed with a really incredible agency that manages that facet of my career, and they expressed that Ryan had some interest in seeing me. I sent in an audition and it fell into place in that way. I definitely was auditioning and going through that script a lot before I got to filming. It was really important for me to really understand the character. And so when I got there, it was obviously super exciting, but I was also a little bit nervous considering the fact that I’m with some of the most seasoned actors in the industry. It was so gratifying and comforting hearing how supportive these women were. My biggest takeaway from this experience is that the reason that those women and those people in Coven are so acclaimed, on top of being incredibly talented, they’re kind and they’re open arms to anyone that wants and that puts in the effort into the acting industry.

What I find so exciting about it is that it doesn’t feel as exclusive as fashion does, in a way. It opens up the conversation to anyone that’s willing to work for it. Fashion is being the right person in the right place at the right time. I think that acting is being the right person, yes, but putting the right energy and the right time into the job. It was just such an incredible experience. Jessica Lange, Emma Roberts, Sarah Paulson, Gabourey Sidibe… it was fab. It was fab! I have so many iconic memories made on that set that I’m going to have forever. I got so much knowledge from these women. I think that it was just really reassuring to see how kind and open-minded these people were, especially Billie Lourd. She’s an icon of mine, but then I got to work with her, and she was just so open and honest about how she’s felt in her first roles. It’s just really gratifying to hear this stuff.

There’s a long legacy of supermodels trying to crack into the silver screen. A lot of models have done it, some very successfully, some have done it once and then moved on. Do you feel any sort of pressure for this to work?

I think I was really ready to just lock in and have fun, but for me, what I was most excited about was being able to be ugly. I see this a lot in models and actresses, where it becomes difficult when you’re so aware of the camera, and you grow up being so aware of the camera to then unlearn that awareness. It was so exciting to be able to go into a space where I was able to get into the character without there being the perception of others there. Obviously there’s a camera, but it’s not a fashion show where you hear like, yeah, work! [laughs] It was a real experience that I worked on within myself.

I was reading The Artist’s Way, which, not to be all corny, I think I realized that the feeling of gratification and the feeling of completing something is only as good as that feeling. I think that perception, especially now with social media and [giving] a numeric value to art, it’s really important to be able to feel like you did the best you could and that’s enough. I think for me, that was really where it kind of progressed. I was able to lock down away from the facet of just being pretty. I didn’t get the touch-ups before set, I didn’t get the lighting right. I wasn’t asking to see the playback, which, as a model—I know you’ve been on many sets where you see the girls and with every single shot we’re looking at the screen, there’s a mirror behind the photographer— it’s very not that. I do the day, and I don’t know if I did a good job. I felt like I did a good job, and that’s enough for me. I’m excited to see it in seven months when it comes out. But that really took a lot of the pressure away. I was just enjoying the experience and doing my best. I think that that’s the beauty of it and that’s the beautiful difference between fashion and film.

I love a lot of animated characters. I was watching Rio yesterday. Did you know Anne Hathaway is the love interest? That’s my dream. Honestly, I would love to do some really fab, cool animation because I think that in itself is a challenge. It’s nice to be able to build a character with just your voice.

Fundamentally, I would really love to have a role that really personifies someone different for me. I also watched Catwoman yesterday with Halle Berry. I think powerful women roles are obviously exciting for any female actress. Having the opportunity to do that would be incredible. I’m very “divine timing” when it comes to this stuff, so I believe everything will happen and run its course in the way it should. But there’s also a lot of really interesting biopics that I would love to see. Maybe I write them, maybe I act. Who knows?

Consani arriving at the Los Angeles County Museum of Art.

A polaroid of the final glam for the night.

So you want to get into writing too, or have you been writing?

Yeah, I actually have. I have been in the background of my mind thinking about this and maybe moving into that space as well. I think it’s quite exciting. In my whole career, it’s always been if you’re not given the opportunity, you make it yourself. I think of other actresses, and the behind-the-camera work that goes into the shows that they do.

That’s a really good point. A lot of the actors that we grew up with—Nicole Kidman, Reese Witherspoon—have really gone into production, or are part of the writing process. Do you have a dream director to work with? Do you have a bucket list?

Totally. Wes Anderson is a really big one... I love Wes Anderson. I think he’s an incredible director. But on top of that, I just worked with Janicza Bravo, and I’m such a big fan. She’s so incredible with the way that she thinks. Lena Dunham [is another]. There’s so many incredible people, but I think I like the way that each different director works. My favorite part about working with Janicza was just the youth in her perspective. I’ve worked with multi-generations of directors at this point, and to see the difference between how Ryan works versus how Janicza works versus how Molly Gordon works, it’s just quite interesting. There’s a lot of directors that I’m a big fan of, people who are obviously focused on cinematography, but also understand how to have fun.
