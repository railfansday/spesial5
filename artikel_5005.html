Visa is testing an AI economy where agents work and humans collect the paycheck

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The payments giant is testing a system that allows verified AI agents to complete paid assignments and generate stablecoin income for their owners.

Visa is testing how AI agents can use traditional banking credentials to qualify for paid work that settles in stablecoins.

On Nov. 22, Visa Crypto Labs head Cuy Sheffield announced an update to the company's Visa CLI wallet that allows users to verify their AI agents and access paid assignments on Taskmarket, a marketplace for AI-generated work.

The initiative expands Visa's experiments in agentic commerce beyond enabling AI systems to spend money on behalf of their owners.

Under the new arrangement, agents can generate income by completing tasks, with payments made in Open USD, a dollar-denominated stablecoin, on the Tempo blockchain.

The service is currently available by invitation to eligible US users, who must register a personal Visa card and authenticate it using a one-time code from their issuing bank.

Once an owner authorizes an agent, Visa registers its public signing key in the company's Agent Directory, allowing participating platforms to verify its credentials.

Taskmarket uses that directory to authenticate agents when they claim assignments or submit completed work. New jobs posted through Visa require verified agents by default, although requesters can remove that restriction.

The arrangement gives Visa a role in determining which AI agents qualify for economic opportunities while blockchain infrastructure handles their earnings.

Taskmarket introduced settlements on Tempo on Oct. 22, expanding its marketplace beyond USDC-denominated assignments on Coinbase's Base network. Its Visa-linked worker verification feature is currently exclusive to Tempo.

Visa automatically screens assignments before agents can undertake them. Owners running agents on their own computers can authorize them to claim and submit screened tasks without individual approval, while remotely hosted agents require human authorization.

Payments are typically released when requesters accept completed assignments, with the proceeds transferred directly to the agent owner's Tempo wallet.

Owners also retain control over settlement decisions when requesters fail to review submitted work before the deadline.

By using cardholder authentication to qualify agents for work that pays in stablecoins, Visa is testing a model in which its verification infrastructure can support economic activity that settles outside its traditional card network.

The invitation-only rollout will test whether those credentials can gain traction among businesses and individuals commissioning AI-generated work.
