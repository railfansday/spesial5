South Korea Set to Let 3,500 Companies Buy Bitcoin, Report Says

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

South Korea’s Financial Services Commission plans to publish guidelines next week that would let about 3,500 listed companies and professional-investor firms buy bitcoin and other crypto for investment, a local news agency reported Wednesday. The guidelines will be released alongside the government’s draft of the Digital Asset Basic Act, the country’s planned framework for the crypto industry, according to the report. The FSC plans to convene its Virtual Asset Committee to coincide with the release.

The FSC itself has not publicly announced a date for either release. If it lands next week, the move would come one year and nine months after the regulator laid out its roadmap for corporate crypto access in February 2025.

That plan opened the market in stages, with nonprofits and crypto service providers coming first, and only to sell. Since June 2025, Korean nonprofits have been able to cash out crypto they receive as donations, and World Vision made the first such trade, selling donated ether on Upbit.

Exchanges have also been allowed to sell their own holdings to cover operating costs. Coinone and DigitalX, formerly Korbit, have done so several times after filing disclosures. The second stage, which lets listed companies and firms registered as professional investors trade crypto for investment, was slated for the second half of 2025. It never arrived.

The holdup is market plumbing. Corporate orders are large, and even Korea’s biggest exchanges have limited room to absorb them without moving prices. “If companies come into the market and trade 5 billion won each, even an exchange like Upbit would struggle to take that volume,” an unnamed industry official told News1. Large trades would normally run through over-the-counter desks, and Korea does not yet have rules governing how those desks operate.

The Digital Asset Basic Act is meant to close that gap. It would split crypto service providers into separate business types and set entry and conduct rules for OTC desks, brokers and other operators, the report said. The government’s draft is expected to go to the National Assembly next week.

Lawmakers want it passed quickly. The Assembly’s Political Affairs Committee aims to process the bill by January, timed to the implementation of the U.S. GENIUS Act, the stablecoin law. Rep. Min Byung-duk of the Democratic Party said at a recent forum that he intends to “wrap it up by Jan. 9.”

The report did not describe what the corporate guidelines will contain. In January, *Seoul Economic Daily* reported that a draft would cap a company’s annual crypto investment at 5% of equity capital and limit purchases to the top 20 coins by market value. The FSC responded the next day that key items, including investment limits and eligible assets, had not been finalized.

Analysts said at the time that corporate flows would likely concentrate in bitcoin, the largest crypto asset by market value, even with a wider menu of coins. Jeonbuk Bank said in October it would offer bitcoin-backed loans. Korea has also pushed back its 22% crypto tax to 2029, the fourth delay, while the Bank of Korea has lobbied to write stablecoins into foreign exchange law.

Retail investors in Korea have been able to trade crypto for years. Domestic demand has at times pushed local bitcoin prices above global levels, a gap known as the kimchi premium. Corporate money has been the missing piece. Under the roadmap, about 3,500 companies would become eligible once the guidelines take effect.

The guidelines’ release would not on its own open trading. The FSC still has to say when listed companies can place their first orders.
