JP Morgan boss says he will find ways to offset ‘unfair’ rise in UK tax on banks

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Jamie Dimon says he is ‘setting an example’ in seeking to claw back Labour’s increase in surcharge, from 3% to 6%

Jamie Dimon, the chief executive of JP Morgan, has said he will find ways to offset the chancellor’s tax rise for banks, calling the increase unfair.

Speaking at an event in London on Thursday, Dimon said he would seek to claw back the increase and was “setting an example so that [governments] understand the trade-offs of these unfair penalties”.

The bank surcharge is to rise from 3% to 6% in January as a temporary 15-month measure. It was unclear how JP Morgan, which employs 23,000 people in the UK, would make up for the extra cost.

In comments first reported by the Financial Times, citing people who attended the event, Dimon said he would set an example in making up for the added costs, and that the decision was “more in sorrow” than in anger.

Dimon has repeatedly lobbied Labour against raising taxes on banks, and suggested in May that he could scrap plans for a new £3bn tower in the Canary Wharf district of London that is expected to serve as JP Morgan’s UK headquarters.

Shortly after the chancellor, John Healey, announced the policy during his inaugural budget in October, a source familiar with the matter told the Guardian that plans for the 279,000 sq metre (3m sq ft) building were still going ahead. But the bank also said at the time that the policy “lacks principle” for singling the sector out, adding that it operated in a “highly competitive global marketplace”.

JP Morgan also has a sprawling campus in Bournemouth, where it employs more than 5,300 staff who process more than $1tn (£750bn) worth of global transactions each day. Earlier this month, the Guardian reported fears that the advance of artificial intelligence could put their jobs at risk.

Lenders in the UK pay a 28% corporation tax rate, higher than the standard 25%, and the separate surcharge on their UK balance sheets, which is set for the temporary rise. JP Morgan, the biggest bank in the US, expected to pay £120m more in taxes because of the increase, the FT reported.

Bank share prices rallied after Healey’s October announcement because the increase was less than many people in the industry had expected. The chancellor said at the time that he was “looking to our banks to help fund breathing space for British families”.
