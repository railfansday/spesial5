Texas puts to death man who beheaded three children, hours after Oklahoma execution

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Oklahoma man, 73, executed for killing ex’s estranged husband, as US nears 40 executions for second straight year

A Texas man convicted of fatally stabbing and beheading his three young children in a squalid, windowless apartment in 2003 was recently executed.

John Allen Rubio, 46, was pronounced dead at 6.47 pm on Thursday after a lethal injection at the state prison in Huntsville, Texas. His killings of his baby girl and two toddlers so rocked the city of Brownsville on the US border with Mexico that the apartment building was later demolished – and the land turned into a park and community garden.

After a spiritual adviser prayed over him briefly, Rubio thanked friends and family for their “love and understanding” and said he was “not the same person I once was”. He thanked prison staff for teaching him how to find God and also expressed love for Angela Camacho, his common-law wife who is serving life in prison for her role in the slayings.

“I want to say I’m sorry to my wife’s family,” he said. “I can’t change the past but I can tell you my life is going to be over. But that’s not going to make you happy.”

“I’m going to be with my kids in a little bit,” he added. “I’m ready to go to heaven to be with my lord and savior.”

As the lethal dose of the sedative pentobarbital began taking effect, Rubio took three breaths and began what sounded like snoring. All movement ceased after, and a physician pronounced him dead 21 minutes after the injection began.

Also on Thursday, 73-year-old James Pavatt received a lethal injection at an Oklahoma prison for the fatal shooting of his girlfriend’s estranged husband in 2001. The victim was killed as he arrived to pick up his two young children before Thanksgiving.

Thursday’s executions were the 36th and 37th this year, with the US on track to surpass 40 executions for a second consecutive year – a pace driven by Florida.

The death penalty has drawn renewed attention since the failed execution of Christa Pike in Tennessee in September. And on 5 November, California’s governor, Gavin Newsom, announced he would commute hundreds of death sentences before leaving office in January – what experts say would be the largest mass clemency in American history.

In Texas, Rubio and Camacho were accused of killing three-year-old Julissa Quesada, 14-month-old John E Rubio and two-month-old Mary Jane Rubio in the apartment they shared in Brownsville. Camacho received a life sentence after pleading guilty.

An appeals court overturned Rubio’s first capital murder conviction, ruling Camacho’s statements were improperly admitted as evidence in his trial. At his second trial, Rubio again pleaded not guilty by reason of insanity but again was convicted and sentenced to death.

According to court records, Rubio wasn’t the biological father of the two older children but raised them as his own.

At various points, Rubio claimed the children were possessed and that he was the “chosen one” intended to save the world.

Defense experts diagnosed him with paranoid schizophrenia, a finding prosecution experts rejected. The defense experts also testified his childhood – marked by violence at home, “toxic” parents, drug use and prostitution – damaged him developmentally.

Meanwhile, in Oklahoma, Pavatt was convicted of first-degree murder and conspiracy to commit murder in the killing of Rob Andrew, a 39-year-old advertising executive. Pavatt attended the same church as the Andrew family and had been having an affair with Rob Andrew’s wife, Brenda Andrew. She was convicted at a separate trial and is the only woman currently on Oklahoma’s death row.

Weeks before the shooting, Rob Andrew told police someone was trying to kill him after the brake lines on his car were cut. Brenda Andrew told police after her husband was killed that masked intruders shot her in the arm and fatally shot him.

Pavatt and Brenda Andrew fled to Mexico with her two children after the shooting and were arrested three months later while re-entering the US.

On Thursday, when he was asked if he had any last words before his execution, Pavatt shook his head no. As a chaplain read from the Bible, Pavatt appeared to mouth some words that couldn’t be heard.

Also on Thursday, former Oklahoma death row inmate Richard Glossip pleaded guilty to a lesser charge of being an accessory in a 1997 murder and awaits sentencing in December.

Glossip, who had been on the brink of execution three times, was released on bond in May after nearly 30 years in prison. He had been awaiting a retrial after the US supreme court overturned his conviction.
