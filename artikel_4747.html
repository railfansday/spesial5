The Most Important Sign You're in the Wrong Relationship

Your new paramour seems too good to be true. Attentive and thoughtful, he or she says all of the right things and anticipates your needs and desires before you do. Yet as time goes on, you feel unsettled and insecure when you are together, and even when you are apart—due to your partner's demand for constant communication. You begin to desire time to yourself, even though you are just getting to know your new love interest. Have you suddenly become more independent, or is there something else going on?

The most important indication you are in the wrong relationship is not necessarily what your friends think about your paramour, or his or her public persona; it is how your partner makes you feel. Fortunately, we are all gifted with the intuition to detect potential discomfort or even danger when spending time with other people, especially one-on-one.

Shahida Arabi (2023)[i] investigated the impact of partner narcissism and psychopathy on partner post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), noting that dark personalities may shower romantic prospects with excessive attention and flattery commonly referred to as “love bombing,” as a tactic of manipulation, particularly at the beginning of a relationship. Yet she also notes that dark personalities shut down conversations or "stonewall," and even “gaslight” partners by questioning their memory, reality, or perception. Arabi explains that one narcissism subtype, grandiose narcissism, is linked with high amounts of self-esteem and self-confidence, which, as a practical matter, could cause a partner on the receiving end of such negative behavior to second-guess their perception and self-worth.

Fortunately, regardless of partner personality type, there are ways to recognize your discomfort before remaining in the wrong relationship. Here are a few clues.

If you feel uncomfortable and unsettled, it is better to pull back sooner rather than later, because mismatched affection tends to get worse, not better. Just like the dashboard warning indicators in your car are yellow before they turn red, a relational partner who green-lights your feelings regarding the progress and pace of your relationship will allow you to safely journey into a quality relationship.

[i] Arabi, Shahida. “Narcissistic and Psychopathic Traits in Romantic Partners Predict Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder Symptomology: Evidence for Unique Impact in a Large Sample.” Personality and Individual Differences 201 (February 2023): 1–6.
