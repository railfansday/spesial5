Australia Unveils New A.I. Regulator After OpenAI Breach

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

The government is also considering changes to criminal law to penalize companies that develop models that “engage in harmful conduct.”

The Australian government will establish a new artificial intelligence safety watchdog that will enforce “significant penalties” for companies that fail to adhere to mandatory safeguards, Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said on Tuesday.

The government also announced sweeping recommendations to prevent future breaches after OpenAI models went rogue and gained unauthorized access to government systems this year, including considering criminal law changes to penalize companies that develop “an AI model that engages in harmful conduct, and the company fails to take reasonable steps to prevent that harm.”

The breach, which occurred in June but was not reported to Australian officials until three months later, raised doubts about whether laws in Australia, or elsewhere, are ready for the challenges posed by the fast-evolving technology.

“Our task is to make sure that we shape A.I. rather than A.I. shape us,” Mr. Albanese said on Tuesday.

“This incident was completely unacceptable,” he added. “And it can’t be allowed to happen again.”

(The New York Times has sued OpenAI and Microsoft, claiming copyright infringement of news content related to A.I. systems. The two companies have denied those claims.)

The breach occurred on June 18 by agents operating under an OpenAI research team that was carrying out research into public medicine spending using an internal model, on a data portal for Australia’s public health insurance system, Medicare.
