No Disc, No Buy and PS5 Blackout Protests Fail to Impact PlayStation

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Active users increases, digital sales percentage increases

Sony has published its financial report for the Q2 period of fiscal year 2026, the stretch of time in which some PS5 fans took a stand against PlayStation decisions of recent years to make their frustrations heard.

After years of proposed disappointment surrounding Sony's first-party game output, studio closures, and a shift into the live service, everything came to a head over PlayStation's decision to stop manufacturing physical versions of new titles from January 2028 onwards.

Fans and organisations set up protests to try and make their voices heard, such as the No Disc, No Buy slogan, and most famously, the PS5 blackout rally by Does It Play. The latter encouraged fans to turn their PS5 consoles off for a week to try and hit Sony where it hurts: its financial results, most notably where Sony reports its monthly active users per quarter.

Those numbers have now been made public, and they confirm the protests haven't affected Sony's results.

For the Q2 period in which the protests took place, Sony recorded 127 million monthly active users. This is eight million more users than it had last year during the same period, and an increase of two million over the first quarter of 2026.

There was a 7% increase in monthly active users for September compared to the same month in the last fiscal year, which resulted in a record high for September. Total playtime for the entire quarter increased by 4% year-on-year, as Sony sees its engagement on PlayStation "steadily improving".

On the other hand, the No Disc, No Buy campaign is designed to tell Sony that if a game isn't shipped on a physical disc, then that person won't buy it. During this period, 78% of all "full game software" purchases were for the digital version. That's purely "full game" purchases; the percentage doesn't include DLC or microtransactions.

That's a 6% increase compared to the same period of time last year.

Customer spending across physical and digital software is actually on the up for both types of media, but digital continues to dwarf physical. Sony made ¥43.1 billion in the Q2 quarter of this fiscal year from physical games, which is almost ¥8 billion more than the same period last year.

However, digital games brought in ¥330.8 billion. That's an increase of ¥60.5 billion compared to the Q2 period of fiscal year 2025.

It was always up for debate how much of an impact these kinds of campaigns could really have. It's likely Sony was aware they were taking place, but if they make no tangible impact, then it's something the company can very easily sweep under the rug. It also came out that nearly half of the social media accounts pushing the No Disc, No Buy campaign may have been fake bots.

PlayStation has never commented on the fan protests, and it hasn't shared any updates or changes to its plans to stop physical disc production from the start of 2028. With this evidence now to hand, it probably sees fit to continue with what it's doing.
