These Dune movie theories will make you feel like the Kwisatz-Haderach

Dune 3 is a little over a month away, and over on social media, fans of the lauded Denis Villeneuve movies have been sharing their "coolest theories". Some of them are so mind-blowing, in fact, that they'll make you feel like you've sipped the Water of Life and can see with absolute prescience.

Responding to a prompt post on X, one of the more popular theories from LULA al gaib is that what we're seeing in Dune is an "alternate timeline of Paul's visions from the books", so both the Frank Herbert novels and Villeneuve movies co-exist in the same universe, despite their differences. This explains the many adaptive changes Villeneuve has made with his movies; for example, why in the novel Paul imagines a timeline where it's him who kills his grandfather, Baron Harkonnen, and not his sister Alia. Paul does kill the Baron in Dune: Part Two, but it's Alia who plunges the knife into her grandfather in the novel.

It's even baked into the text of Dune, as Paul's miraculous abilities as Kwisatz-Haderach allow him to see a number of different timelines. In the movies, we've had a taste of this, as in Part Two, Paul sees visions of Jamis (Babs Olusanmokun) as an ally and friend, even though Jamis is killed by Paul at the climax of Dune: Part One. In Part One, we also see a vision of Chani standing by Paul's side as he unleashes the Fremen on the universe, but we know from the Dune 2 ending that Chani wants nothing to do with genocide and appears to have turned on Paul by Part Three, 19 years later, because of the atrocities being committed in his name.

Another theory from LULA al gaib is equally cool, but a bit of a spoiler for the books if you haven't read beyond Dune: Messiah, so be warned! Ever wondered who that mysterious voice that we first hear in Dune: Part One opening belongs to? What if it wasn't simply a Sardaukar throat-singer, but a descendant of Paul's from thousands of years in the future? Specifically, God Emperor Leto II, who eventually turns into a worm (it's a whole thing). "He would be a kind of 'narrator' and the last historical witness, since he experienced his father Paul Atreides's entire journey through genetic memories." Mind-expanding stuff.

These are all just theories, of course. What we know for certain is that Dune 3 is set to wrap up Villeneuve's trilogy in thrilling fashion, as star Timothée Chalamet has already said he's "absolutely satisfied" with the movie's ending. The final Dune: Part Three trailer released late last month and features plenty of Arrakis action, as well as Robert Pattinson Scytale and Zendaya's Chani trying to kill a maniacal Paul Atreides. And if you're wondering whether Dune 3 can live up to its predecessors, Pattinson has claimed there was a "350-foot-long model of a sandworm" on the set, which sounds pretty next-level to us.
