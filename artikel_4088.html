Zelda Ocarina's ReDead Were Set To Be "Very, Very Scary" In The Remake, But Nintendo Decided To "Dial It Back"

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

The team maybe went "a step too far", according to the producer

Ocarina of Time's Switch 2 remake isn't really a scary game, but there are still some enemies throughout this adventure that may give players a fright.

Perhaps the most notable are ReDead, the zombie-like creatures that are difficult to escape when they set their sights on Link. These return in the remake and apparently were even scarier at one point during development, until Nintendo decided to "dial it back".

Here's what producer Hidemaro Fujibayashi had to say about this during a recent interview with GameSpot:

"With this remake, the graphics are a lot improved, and so the ReDead–the sound, the graphics, and the movement–ended up being very, very scary. And the staff wanted to make this in service of making a good gameplay experience. But from our perspective, taking a step back, we felt like, let’s remember the experience that we don’t want to change, and taking that into consideration, maybe we’ve gone a step too far, so let’s dial it back."

So, the decision was made to ensure the experience was more in line with the original game. The final result is the ReDead are still somewhat scary, but not "very, very scary" to the point it changes the experience.

@Kingy I'd understand the argument with the fixed camera angles if it weren't for the fact that it's only used in 5% of the original game. It was only ever used within Hyrule Castle Town, and the interiors of buildings and shops. Something which didn't really contribute to the over-all feel of the game or the scope of it, but rather felt more like a limitation of what they could achieve on the console at the time.

I say this as someone who loves pre-rendered backgrounds in older PS1 and PS2 era games- along with being someone who loves the original God of War trilogy. I do believe that fixed cameras are a good way of conveying a certain emotion or sense of scale that otherwise wouldn't be nearly as impressive. The original God of War titles do this fairly effectively by how they choose to frame different locales. A lot of the battles in the older God of War games are against massive scaled enemies like Atlas, and the use of a fixed camera actually conveys the scale of that fight in a way that makes it feel compelling while also guiding the player to where they have to go and what they have to do.

I genuinely think if the God of War trilogy remakes get rid of the fixed camera of the original game, it'll genuinely kill the scale and scope of those games by proxy. However, I don't think the same is said for the Ocarina of Time remake. I think with the Ocarina of Time remake, the free-camera actually helped the game gain something by how they framed it. You get a true sense for how it feels to look at places through the eyes of a child, given how the camera is drawn in so close to Link. Areas like the Temple of Time or even Hyrule Castle Town and Hyrule Field feel so much more massive than they did in the original game because we're closely following behind a two to three foot tall child.

Once you get to the Adult era, the contrast is true. The areas feel far smaller than they did as young Link, as you're no longer seeing through the eyes of a two-to-three foot child, but rather a five foot tall grown adult man. Everything feels more surreal and I think it captures that loss of childhood innocence that the original Ocarina of Time was about.

I also still feel like Castle Town still captures that same atmosphere that the original version of the game had. Even with the green acid spewing out of a crack within the earth, or the ReDead having red eyes that light up the moment they scream. It's still the desolate ruins of a location that once used to be full of life, warped and twisted because of Ganondorf's magic and the curses he wove through the Kingdom of Hyrule.
