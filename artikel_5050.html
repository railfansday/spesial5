XRP ETF Shares Surface in Schwab’s Repo Collateral

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Home » Trendsetter News » XRP ETF Shares Surface in Schwab’s Repo Collateral

XRP ETF shares just appeared in collateral baskets pledged by JPMorgan & Bank of America to a Schwab money market fund.

XRP exchange-traded fund shares appeared in collateral baskets posted by JPMorgan and Bank of America to a Schwab money market fund, according to wealth coach Kamilah Stevenson.

In her latest YouTube episode, she argues that the reported holdings point to a less visible source of institutional demand: securities pledged against loans rather than bought for price exposure.

The distinction matters. Stevenson says Schwab did not buy XRP or the ETFs outright; its fund lent cash and received baskets of securities backing the transactions.

Citing monthly SEC disclosures, Stevenson identifies XRP ETF shares from Grayscale, Canary, Franklin and Bitwise among collateral supplied by the banks’ trading arms.

She puts their combined value at roughly $36,000 in April, $2 million in June and more than $8 million by August, an increase exceeding 200-fold.

Those figures remain claims presented in the video. The transcript does not specify the reporting year or filing identifiers, limiting verification of the sequence.

Stevenson acknowledges that the positions were small relative to the underlying loans. Their inclusion would demonstrate acceptance of particular ETF securities within collateral baskets, not necessarily an institutional commitment to holding XRP directly.

The YouTube video episode connects those reported positions to BlackRock’s effort to expand acceptance of tokenized fund shares as collateral. Dr. Stevenson says the appeal is straightforward: investors could pledge income-producing fund shares instead of redeeming them to provide cash.

She also points to Binance’s acceptance of BlackRock’s BUIDL fund as collateral and describes a wider shift toward blockchain-based collateral transfers. Her thesis is that assets used to secure financing can generate more persistent demand than assets held solely for trading.

“Trading creates excitement; collateral creates demand that stays,” Dr. Stevenson says.

That argument needs a boundary. Acceptance of XRP ETF shares does not establish that lenders accept XRP itself, and tokenized collateral does not automatically require XRP to move between markets. Stevenson presents direct XRP-backed lending and cross-currency settlement as potential next steps, not completed developments.
