The EV Takeover Is Taking Shape, Despite Heroic Efforts To Stop It

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Whelp, the AI boom has had one positive knock-on effect. It is creating new multi-millionaires by the bushel, any one of whom can afford to splurge on a pricey new electric vehicle. That aside, EV stakeholders in the US are springing back to life after a year-long slumber. Even General Motors is in play, stirring up long lost memories of its 1990’s-era EV1 debacle instead of hiding its head in shame. What’s up with that?

I’m just going to stick my neck out and say that the eponymous Aptera solar-powered autocycle from the US startup Aptera will attract enough AI-enriched drivers to make a go of it. The on-again, off-again, on-again automaker has been on this year, working towards volume production of a plug-in, three-wheeled two-seater EV that can travel up to 40 miles on its own solar-plus-storage system (see more Aptera news here).

Autocycles were once common here in the US and overseas markets, distinguished from motorcycles by their front-facing seats and steering wheels, rather than straddled seats and handlebars. They fell out of favor in the US by the middle of the 20th century, but 21st century hot-rodders have revived the form with a focus on eyebrow-raising, open-air, gas-guzzling showpieces. In particular, interest has spread in states that do not require autocycle drivers to invest in an additional motorcycle license.

The Aptera is showy in its own right, resembling a rocket ship with unique aerodynamic styling. Perhaps it may even show up in the next James Bond adventure with James Pattinson at the wheel. In the meantime, the company has just made good on its pledge to reward early investors in the world’s first solar-powered car with the first production models to role off the assembly line. Aptera #1 goes to the founder and CEO of GDP Venture, Martin Hartono, aka “Accelerator #1.”

Drivers can reserve an Aptera of their own for just $100, and they can expect to pay about $40,000 for the ride. Considering that the solar fuel is free, anyone in the mood for a head-turning ride will get quite a bargain for their $40,000 compared to other competitors in the zero emission two-seater field, such as the recently refreshed Tesla Roadster.

Speaking of refresh, General Motors has been weirdly lavishing attention on the EV1 sedan all this year, even though the automaker has been roundly criticized for the killing the short-lived car in its cradle. The automaker began producing EV1 electric sedans starting in 1996, following the unveiling of its Impact electric concept car in 1990. Unlike the Impact, EV1 was designed for use on the road. However, it was also designed mainly for the California market, and primarily for lease, not for sale. Production halted in 2003 at just about 1,000 vehicles, all to be recalled by GM and sent to the crusher as chronicled in Chris Paine’s 2006 documentary, “Who Killed The Electric Car?”

Or, maybe not so weird after all. GM’s electrification journey long predates the launch of Tesla Motors in 2003. The legacy automaker is now determined to stake out its claim on automotive history, with 2026 marking the 30th anniversary of the start of EV1 production.

GM has been celebrating the anniversary all this year with multiple long form articles about the Impact and EV1 on its website, eager to remind everybody that its industry-leading vehicle electrification efforts of the 1990’s continue to manifest decades later, in the form of heat pumps for climate control, by-wire technology, and regenerative braking among other elements.

GM’s latest love letter to the EV1 popped up on November 17, when the automaker officially celebrated the completed restoration of the EV1 “V212” alongside its a private owner, Billy Caruso. The rare survivor of GM’s the junkyard crusher was evidently hiding out in an impound lot in Georgia all these years, until Caruso acquired it at auction and enlisted Jared Pink of the popular show The Questionable Garage to fix it up. Considering TQG’s large following on YouTube, GM wisely lent its engineers to the effort, along with several donated parts from its own EV1.

US President Donald Trump and the Republican majority in Congress have made a heroic effort to dial down the vehicle electrification movement, but facts on the ground keep getting in their way. For starters, today’s advanced batteries apply across multiple energy resources, fossil and non-fossil alike. So, it’s no surprise to see the US Department of Energy continuing to throw taxpayer dollars at new energy storage projects (here’s one example), even though some of that activity will eventually ripple into the vehicle electrification field.

The US Department of Defense is also getting into the act. DoD’s electrification journey has been a tedious one, but in recent years it has begun creeping stealthily towards a vision of ground mobility enabled by electricity, providing the key tactical advantages of silent running and a low heat signature.

The latest news on that score involves a $1.4 billion loan commitment from DoD’s Office of Strategic Capital (OSC), enabling the silicon-based battery materials startup Sila to vault its signature product into commercial production. DoD’s primary interest in the company consists of applications in the electric drone and autonomous mobility fields, but Sila has long been eyeballing the EV market as well.

Sila secured the loan commitment in August, and it has not let the grass grow under its feet. On November 18 the company announced an off-take agreement with the well-known Tesla EV battery supplier Panasonic Energy Corporation of North America. “The multi-year agreement is part of a long-term commercial relationship with the potential for over $1 billion of offtake over time,” Sila explained in a press statement, further elaborating that Panasonic will deploy its proprietary Titan Silicon silicon-carbon anode material in battery cells.

“The partnership is also expected to support significant manufacturing and construction job creation in the United States. With Sila’s U.S.-based anode production underpinned by proprietary American technology and know-how, Panasonic and its OEM customers gain a clearer path toward a more secure, resilient, and sustainable domestic supply chain,” Sila emphasized.

That’s…interesting! Aside from Tesla as one of Sila’s OEM customers, keep an eye on another Panasonic offtaker, the up-and-comer US startup Harbinger Motors, which also has a foot in the DoD door.
