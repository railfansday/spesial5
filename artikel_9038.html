After Months of Delays, the Sony FX5 Is Finally Shipping to Customers

This is not a drill. The Sony FX5 really has launched.

Last month, after numerous delays, Sony said the highly anticipated FX5 cinema camera would finally launch on November 15. As Sony Alpha Rumors reports, Sony actually delivered, and preordered cameras have begun shipping to customers. Multiple Sony Alpha Rumors commenters confirmed they have received their orders from retailers including Adorama and B&H.

Video creator Brandon Washington took to Instagram to share that his preorder arrived, with numerous commenters chiming in that they have gotten their hands on the FX5 as well, including someone who walked into local stores without a preorder and picked up a unit.

That said, some who preordered have not yet received their cameras, so it’s unclear how many orders have been fulfilled versus how many customers are still waiting. It is, frankly, impossible to know because both Adorama and B&H are treating new FX5 orders as “preorders,” meaning the camera is not actively in stock yet. Meanwhile, the FX5 is not available to order directly through Sony in the U.S.

To say the FX5’s launch has been tumultuous would be an understatement. The camera was announced on July 22, and at the time, Sony said it would begin shipping just a few weeks later in “mid-August.”

When the planned release date arrived in August, Sony said the FX5 was delayed by at least a month. One month became two and then three. In early October, PetaPixel reported that the delay may have been tied to an issue with the rear LCD. Sony itself has never officially explained the delay.

In any event, the Sony FX5 has finally arrived. While it may take some time before all preorders are fulfilled, this is undeniably great news for videographers and creators. The Sony FX5 has certainly impressed us at PetaPixel so far, and we’re looking forward to doing a comprehensive review of the camera as soon as possible.

As PetaPixel wrote in July, there’s reason to believe the FX5 is Sony’s most important cinema camera to date. And now, finally, it’s here. It sure seems like Sony has a winner on its hands.
