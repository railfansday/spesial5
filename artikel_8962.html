The art style of Brad Bird's Ray Gunn has been quite divisive. Many fans, including one of our writers, expected a hand-drawn 2D film like 1999's The Iron Giant rather than 3D CG animation Some have branded the character models generic, and others aren't enthused about the movie's apparently relatively dated approach to shading and lighting.

On the other hand, the Art Deco architecture, clubs, retro-futuristic cityscape and costume design are wonderfully evocative, giving atmospheric 20th century noir vibes. We'll soon be able to see how it all comes together when the movie finally arrives in select cinemas next week ahead of release on Netflix.

In the meantime, you can dive deeper into the long journey of the movie's art style with the free tease of a rather beautiful art book. The art of Ray Gunn reveals concept art that provides insights into everything from character design to costume, environments and graphic design for billboards, flyers and other details.

As director Brad Bird recalls in the book's forward, it's been 30 years since he pitched Ray Gunn. In the intervening period, he made The Iron Giant and went on to Academy Award glory at Pixar with The Incredibles and Ratatouille.

He notes that from the beginning Ray Gunn was supposed to be different. His original vision was “Buck Rogers meets The Maltese Falcon. Film noir, but funnier, and with a lot more action” – with femme fatales, aliens and flying cars.

But the movie, set in Metropia in a fantasy vision of the future as it may have been imagined in 1939, was perhaps too different at that time. “Multiple studios, several corporate mergers, a few technical revolutions and a pandemic” all pushed back the project's fruition.

It eventually found a home at Skydance, Netflix came on board and the movie finally advanced with animation from Cinesite. Brad describes the book as a describe the book as a "tribute to the believers, the many wonderful artists behind Ray Gunn".
