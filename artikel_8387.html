College credit you earn in high school pays off when it replaces courses your degree requires, and enough of it can let you graduate a semester early. Before you sign up for AP, IB, or CLEP exams, or for dual-enrollment classes, check which scores and courses the colleges on your list count toward a degree.

At 2026-27 sticker prices, a year at the average in-state public university costs $26,720 for tuition, fees, housing, and food, by College Board figures. A student who starts college with enough credits to graduate a semester early skips half of that, $13,360.

Passing one of Modern States’ free online courses earns a voucher that covers the fee for the matching CLEP exam.

Each college decides which exam scores earn credit, how many credits they earn, and whether those credits and dual-enrollment classes count toward its degrees. Dual-enrollment grades also start a permanent college transcript, so a D or an F follows the student to every college application.

So choose exams and classes that match courses the degree requires, such as freshman writing and introductory math. A 120-credit degree takes eight semesters at 15 credits each. A student who arrives with 24 credits that count toward it needs seven semesters instead of eight and skips the last one’s $13,360.

Before the student signs up, look up each college on the list in College Board’s AP Credit Policy Search, and check the college’s own credit-by-exam and transfer pages for IB, CLEP, and dual enrollment.
