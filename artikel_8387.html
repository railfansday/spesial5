Look What the Cat Dragged In

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

This is me. This is what I look like except 30 plus years older now.

A quick rundown about me. I was born in Japan and moved to the States with my family when I was… maybe three or four? We lived in a suburb of Chicago for a while and moved near Boulder, Colorado at the end of my freshman year of high school. During this larva stage of my life, I had the privilege to go on some outdoor adventures via general curiosity in the woods, friends and family, and summer camp. From exploring mountains in the southwest to a 2 month canoe trip through Algonquin National Park, my love for the outdoors was baked into me from an early start.

The concept of thru-hiking was brought to my attention when I was in middle school. I was at a summer camp somewhere and we had just conducted a highly accurate stress test of a Nalgene bottle (back then, they were nigh indestructible). You see, what had happened was, we ran it over with an old truck on a dirt road.

The bottle flew by us at the speed of mach Jesus as it made contact with the tire and I had my first near-death experience via beheading.

We all laughed as we went searching for the bottle but came up empty handed. It was then that my camp counselor was reminded of a little event in his life that he referred to as his “thru-hike of the PCT”.

He regaled us with epic tales of adventure and exploration. The random side quests and people he met embedded stars in my 11 year old eyes that exist to this day (albeit, maybe a little less twinkle and shine these days).

Oddly enough, he wouldn’t be the first thru-hiker I’d meet. Every time someone told me about their thru-hike, it just reinforced the idea in my head that it’s something I want to do… Some day.

Anyway, after high school I enlisted in the Marines where I served for four years as a Reconnaissance Scout with combat deployments to Iraq and Afghanistan. The unit I was in was a little different from the average infantry unit in that we usually got sent outside the wire for 3-4 months at a time while living in some form of wilderness (no running water – so we can count the amount of showers we took on one hand over the course of each 9 month deployment).

I suppose it was a natural progression of sorts in my outdoor journey. Not only did it prepare me for not showering for extended periods of time, but my official transcript from the military listed “Adventure” as one of the bullet points.

After I got out, I moved to Austin, Texas where I lived for a little over 15 years where I would drive myself to different parts of the state (and beyond) to go hiking, camping, and exploring (I even spent a year living in a van while going to school – that’s a whole ‘nother story). I also started volunteering with a disaster response non-profit where I got to help people in austere environments where I met some more thru-hikers. My most recent big boy job was working at Austin Community College teaching photojournalism in the Photography + Visual Media department.

I just recently moved to Las Vegas (not the one in New Mexico) after some events and revelations that I won’t get into detail here, however it’s important to note that there’s a little known fact about the area that helped me finalize my move out here.

What do you think of when you think of Vegas? Once you think of that, do you generally think, “hell yeah, let’s goooo!” or do you think “ew, no”? My assumption is that most people fall under one of these two categories.

I consider the next little fact as Vegas’ best kept secret… Are you ready?…

Oh? Did you not know that? Well, here’s some more info.

Surrounding this patch of desert is a range of mountains. Because of this unique mix of desert and mountain terrain, there’s a hidden/not so hidden outdoor community here. Do you see where I’m going with this?

I think it’s safe to say that most people hear Las Vegas and they think about casinos, strip clubs, and general shenanigans. However, that’s just the Strip. There’s a whole ass city here and surrounding this whole ass city is an expanse of wilderness waiting to be explored.

Well, some recent unfortunate events made me realize that after my current lease is over, I have the freedom to go do whatever I want. In addition, with the development of some health issues, my doctor basically told me that the most that can be done is to manage it. Fate has a funny way to kick you in the shins.

This was the signal for me to go do all of the physically demanding things I’ve wanted to do, while I still can – cause it ain’t getting any better.

There’s more to my story but that’s for another time.

Long story, long – I’m currently spending my time prepping for an AT thru hike attempt in 2028 by backpacking throughout the areas near me. The next year and some change will be spent going on shake downs, working out, learning, and seeing what works and doesn’t work for me. If any of this seemed interesting to you, stick around! I hope what I write might be entertaining or helpful to you.

And with that, I leave you to your regular scheduled programming.
