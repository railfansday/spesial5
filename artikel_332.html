Your teen wants to play GTA 6. Heres what to know.

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Grand Theft Auto VI fans waited more than a decade for a new installment of the best-selling video game of all time. That includes teens who were toddlers when the game's last chapter debuted.

Now they've grown up and they're itching to not just play GTA 6; they're also eager to participate in the Grand Theft Auto discourse.

From the debate over killing or sparing a character named Arnold to cataloguing all the wildlife cameos to the game's hot soundtrack, GTA 6 is driving countless conversations amongst players, including teens.

But the game is rated 17+ and features adult content, including sex, strip clubs, drugs, graphic violence, and murder. So, many parents whose teens want to play GTA 6 will likely find themselves in the familiar yet tricky position of balancing their child's interests and social life with exposure to content they feel violates their family's values.

Dr. Brett Biller, a child psychologist at Hackensack University Medical Center, wants parents to know it's OK — even advisable, in his opinion — to say no to GTA 6.

Biller has no problem with video games in general. In fact, he acknowledges how online interactive games can have benefits, like socializing with friends over play.

Ultimately, however, he believes exposing tweens and teens to GTA 6's hyperrealistic community violence is simply not worth it.

"The negative consequences can outweigh the positive impact," Biller says.

While research doesn't prove that video game violence causes aggressive behavior, Biller notes there's a correlation between between violence in the home and aggression. That makes Biller cautious about exposing kids and teens to violent video games.

Killing zombies, he notes, is much different than murdering human characters, and younger children don't consistently grasp the difference between fantasy and reality. Yet Biller says older teens can also struggle in this way, especially given the realism of GTA 6. Plus, the game may normalize community violence by putting a teen player in the shoes of a thief or killer.

Meanwhile, teens with a history of witnessing or experiencing violence who play GTA 6 may find it difficult to manage the game's stimuli and their feelings about the content, Biller adds.

Beyond concerns about violence, parents may say no because they understand how playing GTA 6 will affect their child's mood, sleep, and personality.

"We, as caregivers, are responsible for making decisions that are in the best interest of our children," Biller says.

He recommends parents who won't permit their teen to play GTA 6 validate their child's frustration or fears of being left out.

Parents can also model how peer pressure is real and hard, because they feel it when other adults let their teen play GTA 6.

Biller says this conversation presents a good opportunity to help a teen understand that standing up for their principles isn't always easy. He suggests parents explore with their teen other activities they can do with friends instead of playing GTA 6.

While some parents might feel better about playing alongside their teen, instead of the teen playing with friends, Biller says this approach is a "double-edged sword" that requires care and vigilance.

On one hand, parents who co-play have ample chances to point out and discuss concerning content or themes with their teen. On the other, co-playing can model to a teen that violence is fun or not a big deal.

In more cases than not, Biller finds that parents use this approach when they don't feel confident saying no or to justify their own choices.

Common Sense Media, a nonprofit organization that reviews and rates entertainment content, described GTA 6 as an "extremely mature crime game" for adults 18 and older.

Still, its review notes that only parents will know if their older teen is ready to play. Either way, Jasmine Hood Miller, director of family resources and content strategy for Common Sense Media, says parents should talk to their teen about the game.

Topics for discussion can include what looks fun about GTA 6, how women are depicted in sex scenes and strip clubs, and whether they think they'd hurt people in the game, or have done so.

"It can be uncomfortable to talk about some of those topics," Miller says. "But honestly, if your kid is not talking to you, they're talking to someone else."

Miller adds that these conversations should feel low pressure and not like a lecture. Instead, Miller encourages parents to be curious and nonjudgemental.

If a teen plays or watches the game at a friend's house, even after their parent has said no, Biller recommends better understanding the context and validating their teen's feelings.

"Reward them for being honest," he says. "We want them to continue to be open with us."
