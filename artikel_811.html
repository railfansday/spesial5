Why do teams mark man-to-man? Watch Affengruber's masterclass against Haaland

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Manchester United's defensive mishap in their 4-0 defeat to Liverpool sparked a debate about man-to-man marking. But David Affengruber's performance against Erling Haaland in Fulham's draw against Manchester City showed why teams do it, writes Adam Bate...

Football Features Writer @ghostgoal

The sight of Matthijs de Ligt, arms outstretched in disbelief at the lack of cover as Alexander Isak raced away from him, immediately felt symbolic. The Dutchman is only 27 years old but this looked like a zonal defender now living in a man-to-man marking world.

There was a time when Leny Yoro might have been there to sweep up behind him. But Florian Wirtz was his priority, not covering for his colleague. Jamie Carragher's excellent analysis on Monday Night Football discussed this key tactical shift of recent years.

"This is a theme of the game now and also young defenders coming into it, they are obsessed with men and they do not understand the nuts and bolts of defending and the positions that they should be in," explained Carragher, speaking on Sky Sports.

The former Liverpool defender highlighted numerous examples from over the weekend. "It is probably different from when we played. The modern way of thinking is, 'he is my man and that is your man'. There is none of this zonal where you help each other."

But there was one perfect example - and, therefore, explanation - of why teams adopt this system. It came one day before Manchester United's unravelling at Anfield back in their own home city as Fulham's ambitious approach paid off away to Manchester City.

David Affengruber was left isolated up against Erling Haaland for much of the game. His central-defensive partner Calvin Bassey tracked Phil Foden deep into opposition territory, which, on the face of it, left the Austria international in a vulnerable position.

Haaland has a four-inch height advantage on Affengruber and boasts a huge frame with which to protect the ball. The defender might have been overwhelmed but through canny body positioning was able to deliver a masterclass in one-against-one defending.

The City fans became increasingly frustrated by Haaland's inability to make the ball stick. The striker finished the game having completed fewer than half of his passes for the first time this season. It was largely due to the constant attentions of his marker.

But Affengruber's performance did more than just prove that a man-to-man system could stop even the best out-and-out striker on the planet. It gave his Fulham side the platform from which they were able to earn a deserved 1-1 draw at the Etihad Stadium.

Speaking to Alvaro Arbeloa afterwards, it was an opportunity to ask why it was that the Fulham coach had taken this brave move to trust his defender to deal with Haaland. This was not some sado-masochistic assignment. It was done for a specific purpose.

"For me, as a manager, if you want to be aggressive, if you want to press high, in the last line you cannot defend with one more player," explained the former Real Madrid head coach. "You can defend with one more player in other lines but not in the last one."

Arbeloa added: "Obviously, there are many players who know how to defend really aggressive, and press really aggressive, with two centre-backs against Haaland. For me, the best way to defend is aggressive, it is man-to-man in the last line."

The reason is that by going man-to-man Fulham ensured that the City side did not have a numerical advantage elsewhere. "I felt that we could press them really well if we go man-to-man. And David and Calvin, especially David, did really well against Erling."

This commitment to pressing high gave Fulham a foothold. They had 45 per cent of the possession in the first half with a passing accuracy of almost 87 per cent. That is the highest of any visiting team against Manchester City in the Premier League so far this season.

The man-to-man system enabled Fulham's forwards to lock on to an opponent and impose enough of their game on City to cause Enzo Maresca's team problems from the start. It made for an enjoyable game - and an enjoyable away day for the travelling fans.

Whether Affengruber enjoyed his tightrope walk of an afternoon quite so much is unclear. There is no doubt that the 25-year-old summer signing from Elche would have been in Arbeloa's thoughts when praising the courage of his players. He facilitated it all.

And, in stark contrast to De Ligt at Anfield, Affengruber showed what was possible when a defender wins their individual duel with the striker. Man-to-man marking is a risky business. But when it can work even up against Haaland, the rewards are there too.
