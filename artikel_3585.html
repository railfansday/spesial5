Oprah Winfrey Answers the Proust Questionnaire

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

A rainy day, a fireplace, a great book, and 300 pages to go.

Which historical figure do you most identify with?

What is the trait you most deplore in others?

Seven parties in seven days for Maya Angelou’s 70th birthday.

What do you regard as the lowest depth of misery?

If you could change one thing about yourself, what would it be?

To be less judgmental and more confrontational.

Not taking a year off after ending The Oprah Show and jumping right into runninga network.

All-time? Filming The Color Purple in 1985, hosting the Legends Ball for African American She-roes, and Christmas Kindness 2002, where we surprised thousands of kids throughout South Africa with gifts.

What Glinda the Good Witch said: “You’ve always had the power.”

What do you dislike most about your appearance?

What or who is the greatest love of your life?

The Oprah Show, and all 21 dogs I’ve had throughout my life, who’ve taught me so much about living and dying.

Chewing gum—and people who chew with their mouths open, showing the gum. Ugh!

What do you consider your greatest achievement?

Creating spaces where people could see themselves more clearly and recognize the best of who they are.

If you were to die and come back as a person or thing, what do you think it would be?

My first-edition Pulitzer Prize books, dating back to 1918; slave rosters with the names and ages of each enslaved person in the household; and six charcoal drawings Nelson Mandela drew from prison. He called me into his office and gave them to me by hand.

Ordinary folks who do extraordinary things: what author Dacher Keltner calls acts of moral beauty.

As the traditional African American spiritual goes: “I wanna die easy when I die / Shout salvation as I fly.”
