The Slopeside Bubble

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

It may be November but it doesn’t feel like it. The day is warm and I treat myself to a late lunch at a resort. I run into a group of thru-hikers.

How would I fare on 4 hours of sleep? I was going to find out. In my determination to get the blog posts done yesterday I worked myself into a focused state and couldn’t fall asleep when I laid my head down. It didn’t help that I drove to the wrong trailhead last night, not my brightest moment. I remember it being close to 2:30AM when I was finally able to keep my eyes closed, and it was hardly past 6:00AM when I woke up.

The wet weather from the previous day had brought warmer temperatures, so it was easier to wake up and get ready for my final 2-day hiking stint of the week. Shortly after 7:00AM, I was heading north from the trailhead on Bake Oven Road.

Less than half a mile from the trailhead is Bake Oven Knob, a rocky section of the Blue Mountain ridge with amazing views of the valley below. Today it was overcast and a light rain fell as I approached the rocks. Patches of fog blanketed much of the land to the Northwest. To the South, it was more clear and there were a few openings in the clouds letting beams of light through. Below the rocks was a sea of reddish brown trees. It would be an amazing place to catch a sunrise. I pulled on my poncho and prepared to deal with miles of wet rocks, fun!

The Blue Mountain Ridge stretched on, and was fairly light on rocks. I made good time but felt a little let down by the views. I could see the valley through the trees, but there weren’t any open areas to get a clear view. I settled for partial views and remained thankful for the smooth trail conditions.

Interestingly, the trail crossed over a major highway, I-476, without the use of a bridge. The highway burrows straight through the mountain via a tunnel some 600+ feet below the trail. I stopped for a moment to see if I could detect any noise or vibrations permeating through the ground, but could only hear the sound of traffic before it entered the tunnel.

Low and high clouds along the trail today.

Further down the trail was the George W. Outerbridge Shelter, an older shelter but an important one as it has a piped spring directly on the trail. For a northbound thru-hiker it would be the last one for the next 20 miles. I’ve seen many comments on the road crossings about trail angles leaving water caches for hikers. I think they’ve probably stopped for the season by now, so I knew I’d either be making treks down the trail for water or coming up with an alternative.

Less than a mile down the trail from the shelter the trail crosses the Lehigh River on a road bridge. The river was almost perfectly still today, replicating the overcast sky perfectly. I looked at the north side of Lehigh Gap and saw the next stage of the trail was a steep rock scramble. The rain had stopped a while ago but it was intense enough to wet my wool socks. Those were then rubbing my ankles uncomfortably. At the far end of the bridge I decided to swap the wet socks out for my spares, something I hadn’t done since the day I climbed Katahdin. On that day, the difficult climb and high miles were enough to irritate my feet. I did learn that my other socks were much more comfortable, so I was looking forward to the incoming relief. I sat down, took the bag that has my extra socks and boxers out of my backpack, took my shoe off, and pulled off the wet sock. I turned to grab my fresh socks from the bag and saw they were no longer there. I was somewhat surprised, but I convinced myself that I must have removed them weeks ago to shed weight. An odd choice, but what other explanation could there be. I figured I’d just have to hike in the wool socks and probably end up with some gnarly blisters. I put the socks on and laced up my shoes. When I turned to put the bag back in my pack I saw that the fresh socks laying on the ground under my pack. They had popped out during my shuffling. With great relief, I went through the whole shoe removal process again. The ordeal reminded me of “losing” my tent straps for months when they were in the bag in my center console. I again managed to convince myself that I had lost something when in reality it was never far away.

Cozy feet were going to be essential for the rock scramble. While it was certainly steep, it was surprisingly short. I had to use my hands a few times but it looked far worse from below than it was. I do think it would be less fun to go down, so I planned to use the handy “winter trail” on my return journey to avoid the long step downs that have rattled my ankles a few times.

Looking up at the rock scramble north of Lehigh Gap.

Nothing like some fresh socks for ailing feet.

Looking north toward Weissport from Lehigh Gap.

Once I had cleared the rocks, the trail leveled off. A few rocky outcrops provided more valley views. I was hoping to avoid the 0.6 mile one way trek off the trail to collect water and noticed several commenters on FarOut pointing out that the Slopeside Pub and Grill, part of the Blue Mountain Resort, had a water spigot and was friendly to hikers. It was close to the trail, so after confirming it was open I figured I’d give it a shot. I strolled up after a few quick miles, during which time the day had gotten considerably nicer. It may have been early November, but it felt like late Spring. It was sunny and around 67 degrees. I decided I might as well have a bite to eat, it had been months since I last stopped for food on the trail. I walked into the resort and around to the pub. I was fully aware that  probably smelled a bit worse than the average resort guest. The first words out of my mouth to the hostesses were “Do you have outdoor seating?” I know my place. They showed me the way and I was even lucky enough to arrive during the lunch special featuring a bacon cheeseburger and fries for $10. Being a late spring day, it was only natural to pair the greasy fare with an adult beverage. The burger was served promptly and I sat back watching the other guests exploring the grounds. They seemed to be either early for a relatively formal event or maybe just surveying the locations. I might have felt out of place but I could practically see the AT from my table, this was as much my turf as theirs.

With my stomach full and my mind set on getting in another batch of miles, I left the resort via some maintenance roads and a short bushwhack back to the AT. I had topped off my water so I could at least make my breakfast in the morning. A short distance down the trail I turned a corner and saw a hiker building a fire. I had no trouble recognizing Rebar, the hiker I’d met last week at the Secret Shelter. I walked up and was pleased to hear that Pebbles and 2 other hikers, Greasetrap and Soaked, were on their way. Long distance hikers were starting to feel elusive, every time I ran into one they seemed to know several others that for one reason or another I hadn’t run into. We all chatted about the hiking in the area and being the most dense people on the trail – meaning we are the closest thing to a “bubble” on the AT. They are all planning a slack pack tomorrow with Mary Poppins, proprietor of the Breezy Acres hostel. I was tempted to join the group since socialization opportunities are few and far between these days but I had my mind set on getting in more miles.

Still battling the rocks, they never go away for long.

A well deserved and enjoyed afternoon meal.

Soaked, Greasetrap, Rebar, Pebbles, and See You Tomorrow chilling by the trail.

I stayed behind to make sure the fire we had going was dead out and then continued down the trail. I was hoping to accumulate more miles and make a dent in tomorrow’s mileage back to the van but by the time I reached the point I turned around yesterday it was going to be dark soon. Despite the lack of sleep, I only had one moment on trail where I felt sleepy. That changed suddenly when the thought of crawling into my tent first entered my mind. I also hoped to find a spot with an unobstructed view of either the sunset or tomorrow’s sunrise, but such views were few and far between on this section of the ridge. I found a decent spot nearby and made camp. It had been warm all day, but the temperature was dropping quickly. I passed on dinner since I still felt sated after clearing my plate back at the Slopeside Pub and Grill. I was asleep before 7:00PM, some 7 hours sooner than I’d managed the night before.
