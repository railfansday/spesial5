Personal Investments • Re: Inherited munis - prioritizing which to sell

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Look at each bond’s prospectus using Emma MSRB (type that into Google, I don’t have the exact name). Many muni bonds will have sinking funds that call a portion of the bond each year starting at a particular year (sometimes as far as 5 years before maturity). You may want to keep bonds that are nearing the beginning of their sinking fund payments. That website will also show “issuer events.” Look at those. They will be things like partial call notices, credit rating upgrade/downgrade, annual financial updates, etc. The issuer events could provide some insights on whether a bond is being partially called each year (which would be good), or a credit rating downgrade (which would be a bad sign).
If your state has a state income tax then you will want to prioritize keeping munis from your state over munis from a different state.
Munis with a higher coupon payment will sell better on the secondary market because the low coupon bonds will have their appreciation to par taxed as regular interest when they mature (so their taxable equivalent yield is lower than a higher coupon with the same yield to maturity).
Lastly, there are muni bonds that are more frequently traded (usually from a large issuer like the state, airport authority, or major school districts). They will sell easier than bonds from muni issuers that are small (individual project, redevelopment agencies, hospitals, govt building leases) or from issuers where the payment for the bonds comes from revenue rather than taxes.
Good luck! You are correct that around 4% below the “3rd party price estimate” is where the actual sales price will trend toward. I don’t see an easy way out and the current bond market isn’t kind to sellers.
