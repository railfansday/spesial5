Kili Day 8 – Mweka Camp to Mweka Gate

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Mon Oct 26 5.76m Climb 4 ft (that’s what my Garmin recorded) From Mweka Camp: ~10,170 (3100m) To Mweka Gate: 5400 (1640m) stay @ Summit Lodge in Arusha

It seems that it is common practice among all trekking companies to tip the porters and guides. I don’t know how other companies handle it, but Climb Kili gives us a sheet of paper with everyone’s name and position listed. We decide how much to tip and how to split it up among all the porters and guides. Some in the group had looked up the range for the total tip. We all felt like tipping at the high end of the range, and a couple hikers contributed a bit more than that. Then we all decided how to distribute our portion of the tip to each porter (some porters also had additional jobs) and guide and added everything up.

What porters carry. I didn’t know what group these porters are from, but the combination of backpack and large duffel on their heads was very common

At our final morning song/dance, we announced his much to everyone would receive. We were told this was required by KPAP (Kili Porters Assistance Program) for transparency. We didn’t carry the cash on the mountain with us – paying when we got back to the lodge – but this let everyone know what to expect before they went their separate ways.

Not being sure if anyone had recorded the enthusiasm of the morning song/dance, I put my camera on my mini tripod and video recorded it. Additionally, we all seemed to have impromptu words of thanks for the amazing job every porter and guide did in helping all of us get to the top of Kili.

Today’s trail was steep and quite muddy as we got lower in elevation. That resulted in a couple cases where our feet slippef out from under us in the mud. But it was nothing dangerous.

To limit wisdom, sections of trail were covered in rock and cement. This was actually much more common while approaching Mweka Camp.

Lots of steps on this section of trail to allow it to descend quickly

One nice thing was that the air felt like it had so much oxygen in it

I spent much of the morning talking to Herment. He says he knows many of the guides and porters on the mountain, that they are from local villages and he knows many from there. I asked about aiding hikers. He says they help around 5-10% of hikers. Most of the ones that require a lot of help were doing shorter trips, so they hadn’t acclimated as well.

At Mweka Gate, when sitting down I can feel that my thighs are a little tired. They aren’t bad, but sitting feels good.

We got certificates and medals for making it to the top and posed with them for one last group photo.

The end is near. We can see some vehicles parked ahead.

Medal for finishing (and Peppermint – my camping turtle)

Our group with our certificates saying we climbed Kili

We had a nice lunch in the village near the gate. They also had a pretty big souvenir shop that accepted cards and electronic payments. It seemed like smart business. Lots of tour groups enjoyed lunch there. I was surprised they didn’t have any Kili patches or stickers (only magnets)

James’s Saudi Arabia plans got messed up by world events, so he is now going to join Dave and Joel on their 5 day safari. I’m sure they’ll have fun continuing a few more days together.

As should be obvious to anyone reading these blog entries, I was very satisfied with Climb Kili. I can’t compare them with other trekking companies, but I felt like they took care of all our needs. The food was nutritious and plentiful – usually more than we could eat. The guides were always looking out for our safety and were willing to help in just about any way conceivable. Their knowledge of the mountain went well beyond what was necessary to just get is to the top. Maybe the trekking companies that charge substantially more provide a lot luxury. But I certainly didn’t feel like anything was missing with Climb Kill. I would gladly recommend them to anyone else considering a Kilimanjaro trek.
