The 6 Best Laptops You Can Buy

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

From wallet-friendly buys to big-money AI and content-creation powerhouses, we ranked today’s best portable computers.

Whether you're in the mood for a movie night, want to keep your social media game strong, plan an online grocery run, or settle in for a marathon gaming session, a good laptop is a trusty sidekick. Choosing one, however, is not a quick or simple decision. You’ll need to find the right machine to supercharge your productivity or fuel your passion for gaming. Just beware: Picking the wrong machine could get in the way of your passions instead of enhancing them.

Luckily, the laptop market offers options for every need and budget, and we've handpicked the best for you. Still, before you whip out that credit card, you first need to choose sides in the age-old Mac vs. Windows debate. Apple's MacBooks shine with top-tier build quality and a rock-solid platform. They're a dream come true for those who don't rely on Windows-specific software.

However, some folks think that Apple's ecosystem feels a bit like the Hotel California—you can check out anytime you like, but you can never leave. Separately, despite Apple's best efforts, the Mac ecosystem still has some software limitations, especially for avid PC gamers.

Now, let's talk style. Do you want a traditional laptop or one that can transform into a superhero? If you desire a laptop that moonlights as a tablet, a 2-in-1 device is your new BFF. These nifty gadgets come with a keyboard that vanishes when you don't need it, making them perfect for streaming, unleashing your inner artist on the touchscreen, and more. But here's the catch: touchscreens can sometimes make you miss the crystal-clear clarity of a traditional screen, especially when you're knee-deep in an epic writing session.

We pored over the specs and reviews of dozens of the latest laptops to assemble this list of the best notebooks you can buy today. We looked for laptops we would be happy to use ourselves, while also finding models that fit a range of budgets and preferences. Our tech staff personally own and use several of these machines at home, including the MacBook Pro, the Microsoft Surface lineup, and MacBook Neo, and can vouch for long-term quality and durability.

Apple’s current MacBook Pro is a bit of a lame duck, with a new and improved version coming out very soon with upgraded M6 silicon, but the current M5 model is still my favorite laptop, pound for pound. The M5 MacBook Pro offers an unbeatable mix of timelessly elegant design, top-tier craftsmanship, outstanding performance, and long battery life in a compact package.

On the performance front, the entry-level MacBook Pro handled everything we threw at it—from toggling between multiple apps to running blockbuster games like Cyberpunk 2077. Best of all, unlike PC rivals with similar hardware capabilities, the notebook can deliver a full day of mixed use on a single charge.

The M5 MacBook Pro is only available with a 14.2-inch display. Much more powerful variants with M5 Pro and M5 Max chips are available in two screen sizes—14.2- and 16.2-inch. With more CPU and GPU cores and significantly higher memory bandwidth, the M5 Pro and Max can handle heavy workflows—from AI-related tasks to high-resolution video editing projects—anytime, anywhere.

True to its “Pro” moniker, even the base MacBook Pro is powerful, made for users who need more than basic compute power for creative projects like image or video editing, audio production, or local AI workflows. If you’re using your Mac for email, Google Docs, and web browsing, you may be better off with a MacBook Air or even my budget pick, the MacBook Neo.

MApple genuinely shook up the entire computing world when it launched the Macbook Neo, a budget-tier Mac built around an iPhone processor that starts at less than $700. Up to this point, the cheapest new Mac was traditionally the Macbook Air, which cost just over a $1,000, which forced Mac users to spend a pretty penny to gain access to their preferred operating system, even if they didn’t need the power it offered.

You’d think that a “budget” Mac would somehow feel less than to justify the higher spend for an Air, but the truth is that the Neo is realistically more than enough machine for folks who just need the basic. Compared to similarly priced Windows machines, the Neo offers stellar all-around performance and great battery life, making it kind of a no-brainer for a lot of folks. It’s a bit thicker than an Air, but makes up for it on the style front with a small array of iMac-esque colorways.

Make no mistake, this is not a powerhouse, though: the Neo runs on the A18 Pro chip, which is not the newest mobile processor, and only features 8GB of RAM in the base configuration, which is enough for browsing and email, but will likely slow down and struggle with anything complex. While it drives the price up a bit, I would urge most folks to go with the slightly pricier 512GB version, which also adds a TouchID fingerprint sensor. Even with the base model, though, the Neo is cooler, and a better buy than just about any other machine under $1,000 you’ll find on this side of a Black Friday sale.

The Microsoft Surface Laptop is a formidable PC alternative to our top Apple pick, pairing beautiful design and craftsmanship with top-tier Qualcomm silicon that delivers stellar performance at a similar price. Weighing either just under or slightly over three pounds, depending on the screen size, this metal-clad notebook is impressively light and slim, given its hardware specs and performance.

Hardware-wise, the Microsoft Surface Laptop impresses with a sharp PixelSense touchscreen, the latest Qualcomm Snapdragon X2 Plus or X2 Elite chip, plenty of memory, and a comprehensive set of futureproof wired and wireless connectivity features. In my experience, both the entry-level and top-spec versions delivered excellent all-around performance and long battery life.

The Microsoft Surface Laptop comes in three screen sizes—13-, 13.8-, and 15-inch — unlike the MacBook Air or Pro and any PC laptop rival. The 13-inch version with a six-core Qualcomm Snapdragon X2 chip is the most affordable. Upping your budget for a 13.8- or 15-inch model, alongside a more powerful chip, will get you a sharper and brighter display panel with a higher refresh rate and Dolby Vision HDR support.

The recently launched Microsoft Surface Laptop Ultra with the brand-new Nvidia RTX Spark chip is the PC laptop to get if you are a PC power user with a big upgrade budget. The notebook’s first-of-its-kind silicon makes it great for demanding workflows—from AI-related tasks to AAA gameplay.

The Asus ROG Strix G16 is an upgrade-worthy gaming laptop for players looking to balance serious gaming specs with portability. With gaming laptops, there’s often a fine line between making an underpowered machine that lacks the specs to keep up with even a budget-tier desktop, and a huge “desktop replacement” that’s too heavy to carry on a daily basis. The Strix G16 walks that line well, delivering strong performance while keeping things relatively light.

Gaming laptops generally run heavier than performance machines, so while six pounds may sound like a lot, it's not that bad when you factor in the power at your disposal. The configuration we tested, with an Nvidia GeForce RTX 5070 Ti GPU, an Intel Core Ultra 9 processor, and 32 GB of RAM, will handle new games on high or ultra settings with ease. They'll also look great on a 2.5K G-Sync display that maxes out at 240Hz, ensuring smooth, lifelike gameplay.

Depending on your budget, you can scale this laptop's hardware up or down to meet your needs. If you can live with slightly less performance, there's a more budget-friendly sub-$1,500 version with an Nvidia GeForce RTX 5060 GPU. On the other end, this monstrously powerful config costs nearly $4,000, but your games will look absolutely incredible.

Chromebooks are historically known for being a cheap, basic notebook option, which have been widely adopted for young students who need a school-only machine. But with tech prices going the way they’ve been going and Google releasing a new, more powerful class of AI-focused Googlebooks starting at $899, that price advantage has largely evaporated. Given that, we’re only inclined to recommend high-performance Chromebooks, rather than the cheapest models.

For $750, Lenovo’s Chromebook Plus 14 is a high-end Chromebook, but still feels fairly budget-friendly compared to the wider world of laptops. It features a MediaTek chip and 16 GB of RAM, which gives you pretty snappy responsiveness for basic email and web browsing, and across ChromeOS-friendly Android apps.

The best part, I think, is its display: A sharp, 14-inch OLED touchscreen that makes streams look great. For work and school, it also has a surprisingly good high-resolution webcam and Dolby Atmos-capable speakers. And it can run up to 17 hours on a single charge, which should easily get it through a workday of mixed use.

If you want a lightweight machine that looks and feels ready for hard work using Google’s vast suite of productivity apps and Gemini, this is a very reasonable option that can save you a few hundred bucks.

The Microsoft Surface Pro has been a stellar laptop-caliber tablet experience for a long time, and this year is no different. This Windows-powered slate is sleek, capable, and supremely versatile. Yes, you’ll need to buy a keyboard case to complete its transformation to full-blown laptop, but I think it’s absolutely worth it to do so.

Getting the laptop option mentioned above requires spending more on a Surface Pro keyboard and Slim Pen 2 bundle, but the investment is well worth it. The essential accessories, along with the Pro’s adjustable viewing stand, are instrumental in giving it such commendable versatility.

Equipped with Qualcomm Snapdragon X2 Series silicon, the Microsoft Surface Pro balances abundant power, stellar multitasking, and long battery life. We prefer the higher-end version, which features a stunning OLED screen. Lower-priced iterations use an IPS panel, down to a 12-inch display panel in the starter model.

You'll want a laptop that cuts through every task you throw at it while also lasting as long as you do. The selections below are packed with the latest processing and graphics technology to rise to the occasion.

If you travel frequently or commute with your computer, look for thin, light designs like the MacBook Air—the 13-inch model weighs in at just 2.7 pounds and the 15-inch at 3.3 pounds.

Gamers or content creators will want higher-end processors and graphics cards as well as bright, detailed screens. Students, and those who will spend most of their time logging into classes and watching the occasional movie or surfing the web, might want to consider a convertible laptop that also functions as a tablet.

With the latest laptops, battery life keeps improving. The reason newer laptops last longer on the same size battery is because modern processors are getting more energy efficient. Today’s laptops will run between 8 and 20 hours on a single charge. Look for a battery that lasts at least 10 hours.

There are more than a dozen types of ports, including HDMI for video out and USB-C, which can be for almost anything with the right adaptor, such as charging, but most users will only need a USB-C port. That’s because it’s possible to connect almost any type of peripheral to USB-C by itself or using an adaptor.

If you don’t want to pack an adapter, consider a laptop with HDMI ports that can output video and audio from your laptop to a TV, monitor, or projector. They can support high-definition resolutions up to 4K and surround sound formats. Another handy option is ethernet ports that can connect your laptop to a wired network for faster and more reliable internet access.

The fastest processors on the market now offer blistering speeds and are great for gaming or video editing. But quicker speeds can sometimes make for a pricier laptop that has less battery life.

In laptop chips, Intel’s most popular lineup is its Core series, while Apple’s is its proprietary M-series processors (starting with the base M1). The middle ground in the Core and Ryzen lineups offers the best mix of performance and value. The most expensive laptops offer Intel Core i9 H-Series processors while mid-range models tend to have Core i5 processors that offer a good balance between price and performance.

Screen quality is also crucial to a satisfying experience. Buy the best screen you can afford; your eyes will thank you later. Higher-resolution screens allow for greater image detail, though fonts on high-resolution displays can be small and difficult to read.

The most suitable screen size for most people ranges from 13- to 15-inches—the sweet spot between portability and enough real estate to get things done. The most common resolutions on laptops and 2-in-1 PCs currently hover just above 1920 x 1080 (also known as 1080p or Full HD). This keeps panel prices relatively low and fonts still large enough to view comfortably.

Pick a keyboard with a broad enough design and widely spaced keys if you’ll be doing a lot of typing. Also consider key travel (how far a key needs to be depressed until the keystroke is recognized), which can vary widely between models. Larger laptops tend to have better keyboards since the dimensions don't have be downsized for smaller frames.

The more money you spend on your laptop, the longer you can expect it to last. In general, Macs have a reputation for lasting longer than Windows laptops. I have an eight-year-old MacBook Air that’s still going strong.

One factor to consider is that operating system updates that are released regularly tend to take up hard drive space, so if you buy a model with a larger drive, your laptop may last longer. To make the most of your laptop’s lifespan, keep the laptop clean, cool, updated, and backed up.

The weak spots of inexpensive laptops tend to be the keyboard, trackpad, and display. Manufacturers may skimp on these items hoping that potential buyers will focus more on processor speed. For starters, even if your funds are limited, look for a screen with at least Full HD (1920-by-1080) resolution.

It pays to be fussy when it comes to a laptop keyboard as that’s the component you’ll be interacting with the most. Buying a laptop with keyboard backlighting can help you see the keys better and avoid typing mistakes.

Some keyboards have adjustable brightness and color options for backlighting. Another important feature is a laptop keyboard with a full-size layout instead of a compact or tenkeyless layout without a numeric keypad.
