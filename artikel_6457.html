Shape-Shifting Robotic "Slugs" Could One Day Crawl Across Alien Worlds

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Robots have been a mainstay of our early space exploration efforts. But they normally have wheels. Or in some cases, rotors. While those solutions are great on flat ground, they struggle with jagged boulders or tight crevices. This isnât the first time the idea has been brought to space exploration, though. But this is one of the first times the idea has been applied to robotics. The CSU researchers have developed what they call the Tensegrity-based Continuum Robot (TeCoBot). Measuring in at around 587 mm (~2 ft) long and weighing around 600 grams, the cylindrical bot combined a squishy, flexible spine with adaptable dual mechanical claws, which provide the real interesting use cases of the technology.

Each module is built around six lightweight rods suspended inside a 3D printed stretchy lattice. Three cables made up of 40-lb braided fishing wire run through the center of the robot, and by pulling on each of them with small motors attached to its base, the robot can bend in any direction - like an âelephantâs trunkâ as a press release puts it. It can also scrunch down to around half its original size if all three lines are pulled at the same time.

But whatâs most interesting about the technology is its modularity, which relies on the claws on either end. These claws are swappable, so if a robot needs to handle delicate cargo engineers can put a soft-padded claw on it. But, if needed, those soft pads can be swapped out for a heavier duty gripper claw that uses a worm gear to clamp and hold onto another gripper without draining any battery.

This unlocks all sorts of additional opportunities. As a stand-alone single robot, TeCoBot is already relatively capable - it can inch along like a caterpillar, roll sideways, and even climb vertically. The paper discusses three robots wrapping themselves around a large pipe, and forming a H-shaped walker to crawl through tight spaces. But perhaps most interestingly for anyone who hates cleaning as much as this author, they are also capable of teaming up to clean off a table.

Those feats might not seem immediately applicable to space exploration, but the underlying modularity and adaptability would be extremely useful in the rough craters of the Moon or underground caverns on Mars.
