Hyatt Hotel Promises Jonathan Adler Toiletries—But There’s Drugstore Shampoo On Its Housekeeping Cart

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Hotel Avante in Mountain View, California advertises Jonathan Adler bath amenities. A reader photographed its housekeeping cart with a retail bottle of HASK shampoo resting among unbranded squeeze bottles. This is another JdV by Hyatt property, just a few miles from the Wild Palms hotel where I reported on DRIFT bulk toiletries on housekeeping carts while the guest bathroom bottles displayed Jonathan Adler branding. Apparently readers need to keep checking what’s on the carts. The brown bottle is HASK Tea Tree Oil & Rosemary Invigorating Shampoo, in the 12-ounce size. HASK is an affordable retail hair care brand, with products sold at CVS and other stores. Around it are condiment-style squeeze bottles. At least one has “Body wash” handwritten on tape. That identifies the type of product, without telling guests whose product it is. It seems like Hyatt a good reason to check what this hotel is using to refill its toiletries. But they had this reason at its sister property, too. There needs to be much stronger monitoring. Hyatt’s Hotel Avante page lists “Jonathan Adler bath amenities” across all six room categories. The hotel’s separate website promises that as well. I have no particular opinion on HASK shampoo. My issue is that a hotel should deliver the product they specifically advertise to guests. And if the bulk toiletries in the room say they’re a specific brand, they should be that brand. The reader describes Avante as Wild Palms’ sister property and they’re both part of the same ownership group. In November 2025, I published a reader’s photos from Wild Palms. The bathroom had Jonathan Adler bottles, while the housekeeping cart carried large jugs of DRIFT Hydrated Body Care. Then in January, Ben at One Mile at a Time published follow-up photos showing the bulk jugs with their labels removed. Wild Palms still advertises Jonathan Adler toiletries. I just wrote about the ketchup, mustard and mayonnaise bottle approach to hotel toiletries. Once the hotel keeps reusing the branded packaging, it can change the contents without changing anything the guest sees. An owner who substitutes a cheaper product keeps the savings, while Hyatt and the advertised toiletry company risk their reputations. Remind me again why bulk dispensers were a better idea than single use biodegradable packaging? This is another JdV by Hyatt property, just a few miles from the Wild Palms hotel where I reported on DRIFT bulk toiletries on housekeeping carts while the guest bathroom bottles displayed Jonathan Adler branding. Apparently readers need to keep checking what’s on the carts.

The brown bottle is HASK Tea Tree Oil & Rosemary Invigorating Shampoo, in the 12-ounce size. HASK is an affordable retail hair care brand, with products sold at CVS and other stores. Around it are condiment-style squeeze bottles. At least one has “Body wash” handwritten on tape. That identifies the type of product, without telling guests whose product it is.

It seems like Hyatt a good reason to check what this hotel is using to refill its toiletries. But they had this reason at its sister property, too. There needs to be much stronger monitoring.

Hyatt’s Hotel Avante page lists “Jonathan Adler bath amenities” across all six room categories. The hotel’s separate website promises that as well.

I have no particular opinion on HASK shampoo. My issue is that a hotel should deliver the product they specifically advertise to guests. And if the bulk toiletries in the room say they’re a specific brand, they should be that brand.

The reader describes Avante as Wild Palms’ sister property and they’re both part of the same ownership group. In November 2025, I published a reader’s photos from Wild Palms. The bathroom had Jonathan Adler bottles, while the housekeeping cart carried large jugs of DRIFT Hydrated Body Care.

Then in January, Ben at One Mile at a Time published follow-up photos showing the bulk jugs with their labels removed. Wild Palms still advertises Jonathan Adler toiletries.

I just wrote about the ketchup, mustard and mayonnaise bottle approach to hotel toiletries. Once the hotel keeps reusing the branded packaging, it can change the contents without changing anything the guest sees. An owner who substitutes a cheaper product keeps the savings, while Hyatt and the advertised toiletry company risk their reputations. Remind me again why bulk dispensers were a better idea than single use biodegradable packaging?

There are real working solutions already employed by other industries for this problem that keep operators honest while still allowing multi use containers. Welcome to the stage Heinz ketchup.

That company that figured it all out and have pretty successfully stopped every trick in the book by restaurants trying to get away with refilling Heinz bottles with cheaper crappier products.

They do things like: – Sell large multi use bottles to restaurants but do not allowing refilling in their contracts, they must be replaced. – they have created dispenser systems that can only be refilled by replacing internal sealed bags that cant be refilled (those big Heinz branded condiment boxes you use the pump handle to get ketchup out of). – They give restaurants Heinz bottles that are always transparent and the label has a red outline around the ages that is perfectly color matched with Heinz ketchup so is largely invisible when filled with Heinz ketchup but sticks out clearly when the bottle is full of a different brand’s ketchup. – And most of all they aggressively audit restaurants and sue if they find violations. – Probably way more tricks of the trade then that too.

The toiletry brands should employ some of the same techniques with hotels, they are not that hard. As long as the hotel is not lying about it and keeping them sanitized I actually like the user experience of larger multi use products as I like liquid soap better then bars and you don’t run out if your sharing a room with another person.
