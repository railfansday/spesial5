Europe's biggest gas supplier battling cyberattack – with no timeline for recovery

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Norwegian energy giant Equinor says oil and gas production remains unaffected

Norwegian energy giant Equinor is battling a cyberattack on its IT systems, though the company insists that hackers haven't disrupted its oil and gas production.

Equinor, Europe's largest supplier of natural gas, first disclosed the attack on Tuesday evening, saying it had called in the relevant resources to deal with the intrusion and had found no indication that any data had been stolen.

In an update on Wednesday, the company said it had a better handle on how the attack was carried out and which of its systems had been compromised, though it stopped short of sharing either of those details.

"As this is not a virus attack, there is no risk of the attack spreading," Equinor said. The company added that it had found nothing to suggest the incident would affect employees, partners, or suppliers.

In a further update on Thursday, Equinor said the attack remained ongoing but did not appear to be specifically targeting the company. Production continued normally, with no indications that data had been stolen or altered. The incident has been reported to police.

What kind of attack Equinor is dealing with remains a mystery. The company hasn't identified those responsible, disclosed whether it's received any demands from the hackers, or said when it expects the affected systems to be fully restored.

The Register has asked Equinor to elaborate, including whether any of its UK operations have been affected and whether the hackers managed to venture beyond its corporate IT network.

Equinor spokesperson Ellen Maria Skjelsbæk told Norwegian news agency NTB that the company hadn't lost access to its IT systems, though staff could have trouble accessing some corporate applications and services. Email appears to be among the casualties, with Reuters reporting that the company's internal email system was down.

Equinor, which traded as Statoil until 2018, is still largely in the hands of the Norwegian state, which owns 67 percent of the company. The oil and gas biz employs around 25,000 people and operates in more than 20 countries.

The bulk of Equinor's oil and gas production takes place off the coast of Norway, but the company also pumps oil and gas in the UK, US, Brazil, and Canada. It's one of the companies behind Dogger Bank, the enormous offshore wind project off England's northeast coast, and is also in the business of capturing and storing carbon dioxide.

Equinor's assurances about production will be particularly welcome in Europe, which has become increasingly reliant on Norwegian gas since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

The attack comes just weeks after Norway's National Security Authority (NSM) raised its cyberattack risk assessment to four on a five-point scale. The agency said the number of successful cyber operations against Norwegian businesses had tripled over the preceding six months and warned of potential disruption to essential services, including water and electricity supplies.

Europe has already had a few nasty reminders this year of what happens when hackers get their hands on industrial equipment. Last week, the French government pinned a series of cyberattacks targeting refrigeration systems at more than 700 retail sites in October on a hacking unit affiliated with Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. The crooks turned up the temperature in supermarket fridges and freezers, ruining tens of millions of euros' worth of food and starting fires at some premises.

Nothing suggests Equinor's hackers have similar ambitions, or that they've got anywhere near the kit used to extract and transport oil and gas. Given how much of Europe depends on that kit continuing to work, a little more detail from the Norwegian energy giant wouldn't go amiss. ®
