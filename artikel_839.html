Erasmus set for France watch-along as SA coaches deny contact during Ireland loss

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

South Africa coaches have denied contact with suspended head coach Rassie Erasmus during their 20-17 Nations Championship defeat to Ireland in Dublin; Erasmus streamed a live watch-along in which he could have breached team rules by communicating with staff or criticising officials

South Africa’s coaching staff denied suggestions they were receiving instructions from suspended head coach Rassie Erasmus during his online watch-along of Saturday’s 20-17 loss against Ireland in Dublin.

Erasmus was barred from having any involvement in matchday activity as part of a six-game ban from World Rugby for questioning whether "match-fixing" and "manipulation" was occurring in rugby after his side's thrilling defeat to France in Paris.

The 54-year-old nevertheless hosted the watch-along from the team's hotel, where he was muted for large spells of the event but could be seen speaking to an unknown party while wearing earbuds and covering his mouth.

The head coach has now announced that he will host another watch-along for South Africa's Nations Championship final clash against France at Twickenham on Sunday, two weeks after he received his ban for his comments on the officiating against the same opposition.

Asked if Erasmus was passing on messages during the Ireland game, Springboks assistant coach Felix Jones said: "I've no idea, we were busy with a Test match."

Fellow assistant coach Mzwandile Stick added: "Maybe he was communicating with his followers on that party that he was having, but with us we were just in the game."

Build-up to the heavyweight contest at the Aviva Stadium was dominated by Erasmus' sanction, with the watch-along representing a potential breach of World Rugby's rules should Erasmus have been recorded criticising any decisions or communicating with any members of the playing or coaching staff.

Jones continued by saying the situation was not a distraction as the Springboks slipped to a second successive loss, before questions on Erasmus were quickly shut down by the team's media officer.

"Thank you for everything this week. The messages, the photos and the support mean a great deal to me," Erasmus said in a post on X on Monday.

"Losing is never nice, but we are still number one in the world and we are in the final of the Nations Cup. So let's go again.

"I'd love to watch it with you. Put the final on your own TV on Sunday and join me."

Ireland head coach Andy Farrell savoured a "big night for everyone who's Irish" and a major stepping stone in his side's journey towards next year's World Cup.

Robert Baloucoune claimed two first-half tries and Ronan Kelleher added another, with a long-range penalty from Sam Prendergast ultimately proving decisive amid a colossal defensive effort.

"It's something that we've been looking forward to and tried to challenge ourselves with about delivering when it matters against top-line sides," said Farrell.

"We knew that this fixture was coming, playing against the world's best - world's best by a stretch at this moment in time, I would have thought so.

"It was a game that we wanted to challenge ourselves and see how we went and obviously fronting up and showing the character that we did and coming out the right side of the win is a big night for everyone who's Irish."

South Africa ensured a pulsating encounter remained tantalisingly poised until the final whistle thanks to scores from Morne van den Berg and Malcolm Marx, plus seven points from the boot of Sacha Feinberg-Mngomezulu.

"I think we've taken a step forward and that was the aim," said Farrell.

"This game mattered to us in how we prepped for it. We didn't shy away from that and we took it on for what it was, which was a top of the tree game.

"We were able to perform. It wasn't perfect but we managed to come out with a win. The World Cup, it's all about stepping stones and this is a big one that's for sure."

Despite their defeat, South Africa progressed to the Nations Championship final at Twickenham after picking up a losing bonus point, with Ireland contesting the third-place final against Australia.

Asked about Erasmus, Farrell said: "Rassie is his own man and he's a smart guy. He does things for a reason.

"There's always going to be a plan or whatever. I don't know the ins and outs of it.

"The South African boys are in our dressing room having a beer and it's an unspoken rule that you just leave it alone, I would have thought. It is what it is."
