A Thief Grabbed His Backpack From A Delta Carousel At JFK, But A Friend Tracked Him Down In Washington, DC

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

A stolen backpack at New York JFK could have been the start of a miserable few weeks for travel writer JT Genter. Instead, it became an interstate detective story involving a luggage tracker, a bus number, and fellow travel writer Jamie Larounis riding in a Washington, D.C. police car. This one has a happy ending…and Jamie deserves a huge round of applause.

JT, who writes about points and miles and nomadically travels full-time with his wife Katie, reported that his checked bag had been stolen from Delta’s baggage claim belt at JFK Terminal 4. Before leaving the terminal, he filed a missing-baggage claim with Delta and a police report.

Ugh. Checked baggage stolen off of @Delta baggage claim belt in JFK Terminal 4.

Delta missing baggage claim and police report filed before leaving the terminal.

Never had this happen before. Happy for any tips on what to do next.

— JT Genter (@JTGenter) November 11, 2026

He subsequently explained that the backpack contained his clothing, medication, toiletries, and irreplaceable mementos. He was also decidedly unimpressed by the response from Delta employees.

For someone who lives on the road, that is considerably more than losing a suitcase of vacation clothes. Understandably, his bag contains much of his daily life. Replacing the contents becomes another job, and some things cannot simply be purchased again.

But there was something JT had deliberately left out of his initial account.

People understandably suggested that he should have put a tracker inside. In a subsequent update, JT revealed that he already had one. He had kept that information private while police were tracking the man.

And to be precise, this was a Tile tracker, rather than an Apple AirTag.

I appreciate the restraint. When something like this happens, the temptation is to post every development, especially when people are offering advice (and condemnation) about the precaution you already took.

But the more useful audience for those location updates was the people who could actually help recover the bag.

According to Jamie’s account, the man took the stolen backpack from New York to Washington, DC on Amtrak.

Using the Tile location, Jamie found a man with the bag at a bus station. Then the man boarded a bus.

That could have been the end of the opportunity, but Jamie caught the bus number and found two police officers willing to help. The officers located the suspect a few blocks away and apprehended him.

They then asked Jamie to ride in the police car to identify the man. Afterward, they went to the station to retrieve the items.

Jamie summed up his unexpected afternoon rather nicely:

“Gotta say, I didn’t have being in a Metropolitan Police cop car on my bingo card today”

https://twitter.com/TheForwardCabin/status/2120615885626835334?a=hFcZDL9PoGdHYVjUzks&s=20

Usually when someone ends up in a police car, the story has taken a less encouraging turn. This was a very welcome exception!

I also love that the bus number became such a useful detail. The tracker got Jamie close enough to spot the bag, but paying attention when the man boarded gave the officers something concrete to work with.

I know Jamie, and I am delighted to see him get credit for this.

There is a considerable difference between feeling sorry for someone who has lost a bag and going out to help find it. Jamie gave his time and followed through when the situation became more complicated than simply spotting a backpack.

The police officers deserve credit too. A location on a tracking app is useful, but recovering stolen property still requires someone to act on that information and I don’t consider it very smart to confront a thief directly. In this case, Jamie found officers who helped turn a promising lead into an actual recovery.

JT’s response to Jamie was understandably enthusiastic:

He also expressed amazement at how much had been recovered. A great story involving two people I consider friends.

A backpack stolen from a JFK baggage carousel ended up in Washington, where a tracker and a determined friend helped police catch the suspect and recover belongings.

What a satisfying ending. So many missing-bag stories conclude with a claim number and a disappointing reimbursement discussion. This one concluded with Jamie doing some excellent detective work and JT getting back possessions he thought he might never see again.

Well done, Jamie. That is friendship in action!
