Exceptionally high tides, El Niño-fueled conditions and abnormally warm water are converging on California's coast, with authorities urging residents to limit unnecessary travel this Thanksgiving.

Major flooding of up to 2.7 feet (0.8 meters) is expected in parts of California today (Nov. 25) as record-breaking tides, El Niño-fueled conditions and warmer-than-average ocean water converge along the state's coast.

The triple flooding threat is expected to hit the San Francisco Bay Area especially hard, with San Francisco International Airport and other low-lying areas of the city bracing for deluges, while officials warned of dangerous breaking waves measuring 20 to 30 feet (6 to 9 m) tall along San Francisco's coast, the San Francisco Chronicle reported.

Meanwhile, in Southern California, coastal communities should expect potentially record-breaking "king tides" and breaking waves up to 15 feet (4.6 m) high, the National Weather Service warned on its website. King tides are exceptionally high tides that occur every year due to seasonal factors and the alignment of Earth, the sun and the moon — but this year's "super" El Niño has amplified the risk by pushing a massive "Kelvin wave" up California's coast.

Citing the forecast, several cities on California's central coast have closed beaches to shield people from hazards, including sudden waves that could wash beachgoers out to sea. Local authorities have also urged residents to limit unnecessary travel during the Thanksgiving holiday.

"As Californians head into the holiday, take a few minutes to check travel conditions, sign up for local emergency alerts, and make a plan to keep your family safe," California governor Gavin Newsom said in a statement Tuesday (Nov. 24).

The flooding comes as a perfect storm of adverse conditions has gathered on California's coastline. Scientists expected the king tides and the coastal flooding associated with them to emerge this time of the year. However, these powerful tides are now arriving on top of blistering sea temperatures due to accelerating climate change and the current El Niño event — the warm phase of a multiyear natural climate pattern that's in full swing and set to intensify through the end of the year.

Ocean water expands as it warms, swelling tides and raising the risk of strong surf and big waves. As a result, this year's tides are predicted to be at their highest levels since January 1983, when tides also coincided with El Niño-fueled storms, AFP reported.

Record-breaking “king tides,” combined with elevated surf and a large Pacific swell, are expected to bring significant coastal flooding to California during Thanksgiving week.

In January 1983, "all hell broke loose," Reinhard Flick, a researcher at the Scripps Institution of Oceanography at the University of California, San Diego, told AFP. "We had 13-foot (4-meter) waves offshore, the highest tides of the year, and hundreds of millions of dollars in damage. It hit Northern California, Southern California, everybody."

Forecasts suggest this week's conditions won't be quite that bad. But there are risks to be aware of going into winter, especially as El Niño has yet to peak and more king tides are expected in December and January.

"It could potentially be quite bad if we also have rainfall and high waves at the same time during any one of these big events, especially December and January, when rainfall generally increases on our coast," Flick said.
