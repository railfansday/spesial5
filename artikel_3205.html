Andy Taylor Dies: Duran Duran Guitarist Was 65

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Andy Taylor, the founding guitarist of hitmaking English band Duran Duran, has died of prostate cancer. He was 65.

His passing comes “following a long and courageous battle with prostate cancer,” according to a statement from his old band members. “Andy passed away peacefully at home, surrounded by his family, on Monday 23rd November.” Read more of the band’s statement below.

Taylor was with the New Romantic band from its early years and through its massive success in the 1980s, before leaving in 1986. He rejoined in 2001 for a reunion of the original members and remained with Duran Duran until 2006. Taylor later played on most of the group’s 2023 album Danse Macabre. He and the band were inducted into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame in 2022.

Riding the mania of the always camera-ready group’s mega-stardom in the early 1980s, Duran Duran broke out with its 1981 single — and risqué video — “Girls on Film,” which became the first of its 14 Top 10 hits in the UK. The track from the group’s self-titled debut LP, which also went Top 10 in Britain. The single failed to chart Stateside, but the group’s next album would push Duran Duran into the Top 10 in America. The group’s U.S. breakout came with the slick first single from its 1982 sophomore album, Rio. “Hungry Like the Wolf” hit No. 3 on the Billboard Hot 100, fueled by the photogenic group’s video that was in heavy rotation on then-nascent MTV. The song and LP were hits in many countries around the world and cemented the band as pop-idol heroes. Watch the “Hungry Like a Wolf” video here:

After the title track from Rio stalled at No. 14 in the States, the band would have five consecutive Top 10 singles in the country, with “The Reflex” topping the Billboard Hot 100 in 1984. The string also included “Union of the Snake,” “Is There Something I Should Know,” New Moon on Monday” and “The Wild Boys.”

“The Reflex,” from the band’s third album Seven and the Ragged Tiger, also topped the UK singles chart. It’s other No. 1 single there was “Is There Something I Should Know.”

Duran Duran’s other No. 1 U.S. single was “A View to a Kill,” the title track from the 1985 James Bond film starring Roger Moore. It was atop the Billboard Hot 100 when the band played at Live Aid that summer.

But the band’s commercial fortunes were about to turn. Andy Taylor and non-related drummer Roger Taylor would leave Duran Duran before the recording of the group’s fourth studio album Notorious. After the title single reached the U.S. Top 10 in 1986, the band would have one more Top 10 single with 1988’s “I Don’t Want Your Love” before hitting a dry spell in popularity that would last until 1993’s eponymous comeback album.

Andy and Roger Taylor would rejoin Duran Duran in 2001 for a reunion of the classic lineup that also included frontman Simon LeBon, keyboardist Nick Rhodes and bassist John Taylor — also not related to the two other Taylors in the band. The group staged a massive tour spanning three continents and received the MTV Lifetime Achievement Award in 2003. The band recorded another album, 2004’s Astronaut, that reached the UK Top 5 and U.S. Top 20. Andy and John Taylor would exit again in 2006.

During Andy Taylor’s first two stints with Duran Duran, the group has eight platinum albums, including one live disc and a hits package, and two other gold discs.

During a hiatus amid his first stint with Duran Duran, Taylor and non-related DD bassist John Taylor formed The Power Station with singer Robert Palmer and drummer Tony Thompson. That band was active in 1984-85, scoring hits with “Some Like It Hot” and “Get It On” — a U.S. Top 10 cover of T.Rex’s “Bang a Gong (Get It On)” — then gone for a decade before reforming from 1995-97 and releasing the album Living in Fear.

Born in the working class area of Cullercoats in the UK on February 16, 1981, Andy Taylor left school in 1976 amid the punk explosion and played in local punk bands before joining Duran Duran. He was diagnosed with prostate cancer in 2018 but didn’t make his condition public until 2022, when he announced he would not be able to join the rest of the band at their Rock & Roll Hall of Fame induction. In 2023, Taylor started taking the medication Lutetium-177, which was assumed to be able to help extend his life by several years.

In his blunt 2008 autobiography Wild Boy, Taylor admitted to wholeheartedly embracing the rock star lifestyle — until he crashed and burned. In terms of his musical career, besides Duran Duran and Power Station, Taylor put out a solo album, toured with ex-Sex Pistol Steve Jones and went on the other side of the studio to produce. Taylor put out his last solo album, Man’s a Wolf to Man, in 2023 — the same year he played on Duran Duran’s Danse Macabre.

In a statement posted online, Le Bon, Rhodes, John Taylor and Roger Taylor — who recently stepped back as the band’s drummer himself due to tinnitus — paid tribute to their six-stringer.

“Andy was there at the very beginning of our story,” the quartet wrote of their early-1980s years in England’s north. “From the moment he arrived in Birmingham from the North East with a guitar and an unshakeable belief in what we could become, he brought an indelible fire and swagger to our music.” The remaining members of Duran Duran added of the rocker: “His playing drove the songs that took us around the world, and with a dry wit that was matched only by his wonderfully unsentimental, no-nonsense outlook on life he kept us grounded and gave the band its edge through the extraordinary years that followed.”

In a statement of her own, Tracey Taylor said of her husband: “To the world, Andy was a brilliant guitarist, but to us, he was the most wonderful husband, a loving dad and the proudest grandfather. He was the rock of our family.

Along with his wife, Taylor is survived Taylor is survived by his adult children, Georgina, Isabella, Bethany and Andrew, and grandson Albie. Donations is his name may to be made to Prostate Cancer UK.
