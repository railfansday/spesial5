Stubborn input inflation pressures builders despite energy pullback

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

NAHB’s analysis of the October Producer Price Index shows total inputs to new residential construction up 0.3% on the month and 10.4% year over year, outpacing overall PPI inflation. Goods costs were flat in October, but building materials rose 0.5% and 5.8% annually, with diesel, plywood and board products elevated. Service inputs climbed 0.7% in October and 11.3% year over year, led by double-digit gains in trade services and transportation. The data underscores ongoing pressure on bids, budgets and pro formas, even as headline energy prices ease.  AI Summary

Residential construction input costs continued to outpace overall inflation in October, as gains in building material and service prices offset a monthly decline in energy, according to a National Association of Home Builders (NAHB) analysis of this morning’s Producer Price Index (PPI) release.

The price index for inputs to new residential construction — which combines goods and services — rose 0.3% in October and is 10.4% higher than a year ago, NAHB economist Jesse Wade said. By comparison, the PPI for final demand overall increased 0.3% in October and 6.5% year over year.

For builders, that gap underscores a persistent cost squeeze even as some headline inflation measures appear to cool.

Goods make up roughly 60% of the residential construction inputs index. Overall, the price of input goods used in new residential construction (including energy) was unchanged in October, following a 1.7% jump in September, and is up 9.8% over the past 12 months.
