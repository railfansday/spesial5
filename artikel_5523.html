The last chimpanzees owned by the U.S. government and still living in a laboratory will be moved to a sanctuary next year, a move that comes more than a decade after the National Institutes of Health agreed to end its funding for biomedical research on chimpanzees.

Beginning early next year, nearly three dozen chimpanzees housed at the Keeling Center for Comparative Medicine and Research in Bastrop, Texas, will be transferred to a 200-acre federal sanctuary operated by Chimp Haven, in Keithville, La., which is the only such sanctuary in the U.S.

“Completing their transfers will fulfill NIH’s commitment, announced in November 2015, to retire all NIH-owned chimpanzees to sanctuary,” said NIH Director Jay Bhattacharya in a statement.
