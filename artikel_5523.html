Going Up: Sharp Uptick in Rates After Four Weeks of Declines

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Estate tax,Model house with Percentage symbol icon on green background,Business investment and Property tax concept

Mortgage rates moved higher this week, but experts say even against the backdrop of potential Fed rate increases and record-low home sales, there are some advantages in the housing market for buyers who remain active.

The latest Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey® released Thursday shows the average 30-year fixed-rate mortgage rate at 7.28%, up from 7.11% last week. The 30-year FRM is still up year-over-year from 6.26% last year. The 15-year FRM averaged 6.66%, up from last week’s 6.45%. A year ago at this time, the 15-year FRM averaged 5.54%.

At press time, the average rate was up to 7.46% Thursday afternoon, according to Mortgage News Daily.

Bright MLS Chief Economist Lisa Sturtevant broke down the numbers for buyers, explaining at current interest rates, and assuming a 10% down payment and average property tax and home insurance costs, the monthly payment on the median-priced home in the U.S. is $3,100. A homebuyer needs an income of over $130,000 to qualify to purchase the median priced home. The median household income in the U.S. is currently about $90,000.  Renters have lower incomes, with a median of about $60,000, she said.

“Last year at this time, when mortgage rates were 100 basis points lower, the median mortgage payment was $250 lower. The difference is enough to price some prospective homebuyers out of the market,” Sturtevant added. “There are other would-be homebuyers who could take on this higher rate but instead are looking ahead to 2027 hoping for more favorable conditions.”

She also said the desire for homeownership remains strong, particularly among young adults, according to recent surveys. “We should expect buyers to return to the market in the coming years; however, higher rates are forcing many young adults to delay, which means they are also delaying the wealth accumulation that comes through homeownership.”

Realtor.com Senior Economist Anthony Smith said hot inflation data and the persistence of the conflict in Iran is placing upward pressure on bond yields and borrowing costs.

“The October CPI report came in above expectations on both the headline and core measures, cementing market expectations for a December rate hike and giving the 10-year little room to fall.

“The debate has shifted from whether the Fed hikes in December to how aggressively it moves,” he added, noting that backdrop is tough for mortgage rates heading into what is already a seasonally slow stretch for the housing market.

“Pending home sales fell to a record low in October, and existing home sales hit their lowest level since 2010, as the surge in rates through mid-October took its toll on buyers and sellers alike. Builder confidence has also fallen to a 14-year low, and permit activity dropped to its lowest level since the early days of the pandemic, signaling that the supply pipeline will face continued pressure in the months ahead,” Smith said.

But for buyers who remain active, Smith noted conditions offer some real advantages.

“With fewer shoppers in the market, competition has thinned, sellers have shown more flexibility on price, and active inventory continues to rise even as new listings have pulled back. The challenge is that until inflation cools enough to put mortgage rates on a durable downward path, affordability will remain the dominant constraint on the market.”
