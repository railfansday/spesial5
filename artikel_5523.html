Pending Home Sales Tumble to New Record Low as High Mortgage Rates Freeze Buyers

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Pending home sales descended to a new all-time low in October as mortgage rates surged to a three-year peak, keeping would-be buyers firmly on the sidelines.

Contract signings fell 4.7% compared to September, and plummeted 16.7% year over year, according to the National Association of Realtors® Pending Home Sales report released on Wednesday.

"October could mark the low point in contract signings for this housing cycle, as mortgage rates appear to have topped out," says NAR Chief Economist Lawrence Yun.

The October readout comes on the heels of the previous record low set in September, when pending sales fell 7.3% month over month and 11.3% year over year.

"Two consecutive months of record-low pending contracts point to very sluggish fourth-quarter home sales closings, and annual existing-home sales could be the lowest since the mid-1990s," says Yun.

Same as in September, pending sales were down both month over month and year over year in all four U.S. regions. The West saw the largest monthly pullback with a 6.9% drop, followed by the South (-6.3%), Midwest (-2.7%), and Northeast (-0.8%).

Annually, the West was again at the forefront with a 20.3% drop, followed by the South (-18.7%), Midwest (-14%), and Northeast (-11.1%).

"Even though all four regions saw dramatic cooling, the relative strength of the Midwest and Northeast are clear, due largely to still-scarce inventory conditions," says Realtor.com® senior economist Hannah Jones.

At the metro level, pending sales fell year over year even in the hitherto most resilient markets. Across the 50 largest metros, the smallest decreases were recorded in Cincinnati, OH (-4.7% year over year), Austin, TX (-9.7%), and Hartford, CT (-10%).

"It is somewhat puzzling that home sales would fall to levels last seen three decades ago, even as the U.S. population has grown by roughly 75 million," notes Yun. "Either sizable housing demand is building in the background, or the dream of homeownership is increasingly out of reach for many Americans."

On Oct. 8, the average rate on 30-year fixed home loans reached 7.40%, the highest since Nov. 2023, eroding buyers' purchasing power. Rates have since retreated, but October’s hot inflation data released last week is expected to push them higher again.

"Until inflation cools enough to put mortgage rates on a downward path, affordability problems are likely to persist, keeping contract signings and home sales subdued," says Jones.

Jones suggests that while uncertainty preceding the November midterm elections may have played a role in stalling homebuying activity, she strongly believes that surging mortgage rates were the more likely culprit.

Mortgage rates climbed nearly 75 basis points from late August to mid-October, adding roughly $200 a month to the typical mortgage payment on a median-priced home, assuming a 10% down payment.

"That is more than many already-strained buyers can easily absorb," argues Jones.

According to the economist, affordability remains the limiting factor in today’s housing market. The typical mortgage-holder would see a $1,100 increase in their monthly payment if they bought a median-priced home today.

While sellers have shown some flexibility, with lower asking prices and more price cuts, buyers have not responded with much enthusiasm at these rates.

"Sellers are only willing to come down so far and buyers are only willing to stretch so far, and a gap still separates the two," adds Jones.

As a result, transaction volume in October hit its lowest level in 16 years. However, there are upsides for buyers who can afford to act: less competition, more homes to choose from, and sellers who are willing to negotiate.
