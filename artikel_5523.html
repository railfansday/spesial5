Kalshi is dropping Donald Trump Jr. as an advisor over Polymarket conflict

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Trump Jr.'s firm 1789 Capital holds a stake in Polymarket, where he remains an advisor, as Democrats take power in Congress

Kalshi is cutting ties with Donald Trump Jr. as an advisor because 1789 Capital, his venture firm, owns a piece of competing prediction market Polymarket, according to CNBC.

A Kalshi spokesperson said the company and Trump Jr. both concluded that it was a conflict of interest for the president's son to advise Kalshi while also being a major backer of Polymarket, according to CNBC. Discussions about his departure got underway in August, following 1789 Capital's announcement of its Polymarket stake, the spokesperson said.

"We have mutually agreed to end Don's advisory role after his firm's significant investment in a competitor this past summer. We are grateful for his contributions in helping us market Kalshi to more customers," the spokesperson said, according to Bloomberg.

Trump Jr. remains an advisor to Polymarket, according to CNBC. He was paid in Kalshi equity for the advice he provided, according to Bloomberg. A spokesperson for Trump Jr. had not responded to a request for comment.

The departure follows Democratic gains in the House and the Senate, according to CNBC. The party is anticipated to take a harder line on prediction markets. Bloomberg reports that Democrats are entering their new congressional majority pledging to examine the Trump family's dealings with prediction markets and crypto firms.

Kalshi named Trump Jr. a strategic advisor in January 2025, according to CNBC. Bloomberg reports that Kalshi CEO Tarek Mansour said at the time that Trump Jr. would advise on strategy, including expansion. Kalshi has said Trump Jr. gives advice on marketing strategy, not regulatory matters, according to Bloomberg.

Early in October, Mansour told MS NOW that the company's tie to Trump Jr. amounts to an "optics issue." He added that Kalshi has never used the connection to win over government officials, according to CNBC.

Trump's appointees at the Commodity Futures Trading Commission have adopted a friendlier stance toward prediction markets since the president returned to office, according to Bloomberg. Democrats have led most of the pushback, though some Republicans have voiced concern. Several states have filed lawsuits against Kalshi and Polymarket, contending that the platforms break state gambling laws.
