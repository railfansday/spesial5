Wall Street's fear gauge posts its biggest jump since Liberation Day as AI stocks sell off

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The Philadelphia semiconductor index was down 9.2% as investors weighed what a Democratic Congress means for data center spending

The Cboe Volatility Index, often called Wall Street's fear gauge, jumped to 28.09 on Thursday as chip and data center stocks sold off. Measured at its intraday peak, the index had climbed more than it had in any single session since the aftermath of President Donald Trump's April 2025 "Liberation Day" announcement of broad import tariffs, and it hit an eight-month high, according to Reuters.

The Philadelphia Stock Exchange Semiconductor Index had dropped 9.2%, heading toward its steepest one-day loss since June, Bloomberg said. The index has shed roughly 12% so far in the week.

Nvidia $NVDA stock sank by as much as 7.5% during the session, its steepest decline within a trading day since April 2025. Advanced Micro Devices stock slumped 15%, Micron $MU Technology stock lost 13%, Intel $INTC stock dropped 9.2% and Broadcom $AVGO stock fell 7.2%. Qualcomm $QCOM stock tumbled 10% and Arm Holdings stock shed 13%.

The selloff extended to memory storage and cloud computing companies. Shares of Western Digital $WDC and Seagate $STX Technology slid more than 14%, and CoreWeave stock and Nebius Group stock also fell.

Democrats won majorities in both chambers of Congress in Tuesday's midterms, while many voters indicated opposition to data centers, according to Bloomberg. Investors worry that new leadership in Washington could put the brakes on the buildout of AI infrastructure, according to Reuters.

Steve Sosnick, chief strategist at Interactive Brokers, told Reuters that the selling partly reflects investors reversing Wednesday's rally and pulling back from the heavily favored AI trade that has powered markets for months. "Yesterday there was the perception that no one needed protection and now it's 'Oh my god, we need it,'" said Sosnick.

Jim Carroll, senior wealth adviser and portfolio manager at Ballast Rock Private Wealth, said, "It really did look as though things were going to relax a little bit and then, you know, the steamroller showed up today."

Leveraged and inverse ETFs and certain rules-based strategies usually reset their holdings shortly before the closing bell, a process that can magnify moves late in the day, according to Reuters. Analysts said some investors sold to get ahead of that activity.

By the middle of the day, close to 800,000 call options had changed hands on the VIX, roughly 3.5 times its 30-day average, according to CNBC, citing Cboe data.

The drop follows a rally on Wednesday, when the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 2% after the opening bell as Democrats won majorities in the House and the Senate. On Thursday the Dow gave up 1,639 points, or 3%, wiping out most of that gain, and the Nasdaq $NDAQ Composite fell more than 5% and was on pace to enter correction territory.

Democrat Gina Hinojosa, who won the Texas governor's race, has called for ending data center development in the state. Republicans hold the majority in the Texas Legislature, which limits how much Hinojosa can change policy.
