An extremophile transgene improves drought resistance and yield in cereals

Drought is a major environmental constraint limiting global crop production1,2. On average, crop productivity typically declines by 30–40% in moderate-drought years, whereas extreme droughts trigger far greater reductions3,4. As droughts become longer and more intense due to climate change, adverse effects on crop productivity are expected to escalate5. Breaking the trade-offs between stress resistance and yield is key to advancing crop genetic improvement as climate change sets in and the world population grows, but this is challenging for drought stress6. Here we show that heterologous expression of EpTFIIAγ, a gene encoding a transcription factor induced by osmotic stress in an extremophile lichen mycobiont, partially decouples trade-offs between stress resistance and grain production, enhancing plot yields of five wheat cultivars under water deficit by 19.4–32.8%, and per-plant yields of rice and maize by 19.7–42.4% in the greenhouse and multiple field environments. EpTFIIAγ physically interacts with endogenous transcriptional activators to synergistically enhance both stress-responsive and developmental transcriptional programs, simultaneously boosting photosynthetic capacity and water-use efficiency, while delaying senescence via multiple physiological and developmental enhancements, ultimately increasing grain yields. EpTFIIAγ uncouples antagonistic stress-response and biomass production pathways in multiple crops and delivers a straightforwards and broad-spectrum strategy for sustainably improving staple-crop yields regardless of water availability.

The bulk of crop production worldwide strictly depends on rainfall to support irrigation needs, and limitations on water availability pose a global challenge for agricultural productivity, with water scarcity and drought stress incurring unpredictable and potentially catastrophic effects on yield1,2. The adverse effects of climate change on crop productivity have historically been underestimated5. With the average global temperature expected to increase by 1.5–2.0 °C by 2050, the frequency and severity of drought events will be further increased, which could be accompanied by 30–40% reductions in food production3,4. Despite facing the greatest food-security challenges in the modern era7,8, annual crop yield improvement rates have not kept pace with projected food demands, that is, a 50–70% increase to accommodate approximately 9.8 billion people worldwide by 2050 (ref. 9), further compounding the effects of disaster events and decreasing availability of arable land.

Conventional plant-breeding efforts have boosted crop yields in water-limited environments, for example, through improved stress resistance, greater biomass accumulation, delayed leaf senescence or ‘stay-green’ strategies7,10, but broad potential remains for improving yield and yield stability across growing regions regardless of water availability11,12. Increasingly precise genetic modification strategies have advanced crop engineering for drought resistance13,14,15,16,17. For example, heterologous expression of a rice trehalose-6-phosphate phosphatase in developing maize ears improves yield under both well-watered and drought-stress conditions in the field16. In cassava, expressing a modified Arabidopsis potassium (K+) channel in vascular tissues improves K+ transport, photosynthesis and root biomass, allowing enhanced productivity in nutrient-poor and drought-prone environments17. However, genetic modification usually cannot provide a single, universal solution to multifactorial drought stress, and this approach frequently fails to improve yields in the field13,18 because drought resistance is a complex quantitative trait involving a multitude of small-effect loci or genes mediating diverse stress responses, such as stomatal closure (for reduced transpiration) or modified root architecture (for enhanced water uptake)10,19. Drought resistance typically accompanies trade-offs by way of reduced yield in the absence of stress6,20, undermining the effectiveness of certain genetic modification strategies. Therefore, of the genes identified in controlled conditions that often show limited effects in real-world environments, relatively few undergo rigorous field trials to properly demonstrate consistent agronomic value21.

Among known genetic resources for drought resistance, lichens have been generally overlooked despite their ubiquitous distribution across extreme ecological niches. In arid-adapted lichens, mycobionts appear to contribute more to survival under water deficit than photobionts22,23. We have previously isolated the lichen-forming fungus Endocarpon pusillum, which has extreme drought resistance. Exposure to dehydration stress identified 1,781 differentially expressed E. pusillum genes24. Through mining those data, here we identified the transcription factor gene EpTFIIAγ that broadly enhances yield and biomass production under field conditions when heterologously expressed in the world’s top-three-produced staple cereal crops of bread wheat (Triticum aestivum L.), maize (Zea mays L.) and rice (Oryza sativa L.). EpTFIIAγ delays plant senescence, enhances photosynthetic capacity, water-use efficiency (WUE) and drought resistance by functioning as a transcriptional co-factor to coordinately target multiple genes and pathways. EpTFIIAγ binds to TATA-box-containing motifs and interacts with several endogenous wheat transcriptional activators to synergistically enhance networks involved in stress responses, plant development, photosynthesis and grain yield. Our results provide an unprecedented broad-spectrum resource for crop improvement and highlight the untapped potential of lichen mycobionts as sources of versatile transgenes for biotechnology.

Among 25 differentially expressed genes predicted to encode transcription factors identified under dehydration stress in E. pusillum (Fig. 1a), EPUS02736 exhibits the strongest upregulation and encodes a transcription initiation factor IIA γ-chain (designated EpTFIIAγ). EpTFIIAγ shares homology with TFIIAs from plants, such as Arabidopsis, wheat, rice and maize, although the genetic relatedness between EpTFIIAγ and these homologues is relatively distant (Fig. 1b). Strong expression under dehydration stress implicates EpTFIIAγ in drought adaptation of the lichen mycobiont. Towards understanding its functionality, EpTFIIAγ was expressed in the yeast ∆hog1 mutant (deficient in HOG1, which encodes the high osmolarity glycerol 1 protein kinase), resulting in remarkably enhanced growth on solid synthetic dropout (SD) −Ura medium with 0.75 M sorbitol (Fig. 1c), and statistically significantly higher growth in corresponding liquid medium than the control (Fig. 1d). These results suggested that EpTFIIAγ contributes to regulating osmotic stress.

To determine whether EpTFIIAγ could also enhance drought resistance in plants, we first expressed endogenous TFIIAγ genes from Arabidopsis and wheat in the yeast hog1 mutant; however, no obvious effect on osmotic stress tolerance was observed (Fig. 1c), suggesting that TFIIAγ genes probably mediate divergent biological functions between plants and E. pusillum. We then generated three genetically independent transgenic Arabidopsis lines (TL1–TL3) constitutively and heterologously expressing EpTFIIAγ and examined drought resistance. To our surprise, seedlings expressing EpTFIIAγ had survival rates approximately seven times higher than wild-type Col-0 controls under drought-stress conditions (Fig. 1e). This improved drought resistance was not limited to the eudicot Arabidopsis. Transgenic wheat cv. Fielder seedlings heterologously expressing EpTFIIAγ from the strong and constitutive Ubiquitin promoter showed an approximately 3.5-fold increase in survival rate compared with wild-type Fielder under drought stress (Fig. 1f). However, overexpression of endogenous AtTFIIAγ or TaTFIIAγ in their respective native hosts had no such positive effect on drought resistance (Extended Data Fig. 1a–f), supporting their functional divergence from EpTFIIAγ. Analysis of key drought-resistance traits, including root architecture and biomass production (Extended Data Fig. 1g–o), stomatal density and aperture, and leaf water loss and transpiration (that is, leaf temperature; Extended Data Fig. 1p–u and Supplementary Fig. 1), suggested that the enhanced drought resistance among EpTFIIAγ transgenic Arabidopsis and wheat lines was mainly attributable to enhanced root growth and decreased stomatal aperture and transpiration compared with wild-type controls. Transgenic EpTFIIAγ wheat seedlings over-accumulated abscisic acid, auxin and salicylic acid hormones than wild-type Fielder plants under drought stress, whereas their accumulation remained comparable with that of controls under well-watered conditions (Supplementary Fig. 2).

Drought resistance varies widely across wheat germplasm25. To understand whether EpTFIIAγ can endow major commercially relevant wheat cultivars in China with enhanced drought resistance, we generated four genetically independent transgenic EpTFIIAγ lines in each of cvs. Xinong979 (XN979), Xinong511 (XN511), Jimai22 (JM22) and Xingzi9104 (XZ9104), which show different degrees of stress resistance and yield25,26. Greenhouse drought-resistance assays revealed that survival rates were 2–3 times higher for transgenic lines than corresponding wild-type controls (Fig. 1g–j), whereas stomatal aperture, leaf water loss rates and transpiration were also decreased in transgenic lines under drought stress (Supplementary Figs. 3 and 4). Therefore, heterologous EpTFIIAγ expression consistently endowed drought resistance and key physiological changes across four additional wheat cultivars. EpTFIIAγ plants also displayed broad-spectrum resilience to heat stress, lodging and multiple races of the major wheat stripe-rust fungal pathogen Puccinia striiformis f. sp. tritici (Extended Data Fig. 2), further supporting potential EpTFIIAγ deployment for engineering stacked resistance to environmental stressors in wheat.

Certain transgenes are known to improve resistance to drought stress but at the undesirable expense of reduced plant growth or productivity, known as yield drag13,18,20. We examined performance of all five sets of transgenic EpTFIIAγ wheat lines to identify potential yield drag in 3-year field trials under full-irrigation and water-limited conditions with a rain shelter in Yangling, China, which is characterized by a warm temperate semi-humid and semi-arid climate with 200–400 mm of rainfall during the growing season (Supplementary Fig. 5). All tested EpTFIIAγ lines had consistently increased grain yield across different genetic backgrounds and years compared with respective wild-type controls under full-irrigation control conditions. This increase was more pronounced under water-limited conditions (Fig. 1k–n; Supplementary Tables 1–3 and Supplementary Figs. 6–9). In 2025, grain yields surpassed that of the two previous years under water-limited conditions for EpTFIIAγ lines because of the higher temperature, lower rainfall and severe drought stress (Supplementary Fig. 5). Across all five backgrounds, EpTFIIAγ lines displayed 7.6–13.9% increases in grain yield per plant and 7.9–13.5% increases in yield per plot relative to controls under full-irrigation conditions, and increases of 22.3–38.3% in yield per plant and 19.4–32.8% higher yield per plot under water-limited conditions (Fig. 1m,n, Supplementary Tables 1–3 and Supplementary Fig. 7c,i). EpTFIIAγ lines were taller, had increased plant biomass, grain length, grain width and grain weight relative to respective wild-type controls, potentially contributing to increased plot yields. Despite higher grain yields, EpTFIIAγ plants had similar straw weight compared with wild-type plants, resulting in 6.1–14.8% higher harvest indices (that is, ratio of grain yield to aboveground biomass) under full irrigation and 7.6–15.5% increases in harvest indices under water-limited conditions (Supplementary Tables 1–3 and Supplementary Figs. 6 and 7). Growth duration was similar between genotypes under full irrigation, whereas time to maturity was 2–5 days longer for EpTFIIAγ transgenic lines than maturation time in the wild-type under water-limited conditions (Supplementary Fig. 7f,l), reflecting adaptation to drought stress.

To evaluate trait stability, additional field trials were conducted in 2025 in three arid growing regions in China (Heyang, Shaanxi; Linfen, Shanxi; and Cangzhou, Hebei) with rainfall of 120–250 mm during the growing season. Grain yield per plant for EpTFIIAγ lines increased by 10.9–25.6% compared with wild-type controls, with the highest yield increases (15.6–25.6%) observed in Heyang (Supplementary Tables 4–6). These results underscored the potential of EpTFIIAγ as a valuable genetic resource for developing drought-resistant, high-yield wheat varieties in elite backgrounds regardless of water availability. In detail, EpTFIIAγ plants had expanded flag leaves, accelerated grain filling, larger pericarp cells, higher starch granule density and increased starch biosynthesis relative to wild-type controls under both full-irrigation and water-limited conditions (Extended Data Fig. 3 and Supplementary Fig. 10). This accelerated filling led to heavier grains beginning at 5 days after flowering, regardless of water conditions, resulting in 3.3–8.8% greater final grain weight in well-watered EpTFIIAγ plants and 15.2–21.4% higher grain weight in drought-stressed transgenic plants than in their respective wild-type controls (Fig. 1m,n, Supplementary Tables 1–3 and Supplementary Figs. 6 and 7). These improvements were consistent with the higher sucrose, glucose and fructose levels, as well as increased sucrose synthase, cell-wall invertase and ADP-glucose pyrophosphorylase activities observed in EpTFIIAγ grains during the early filling stage (Extended Data Fig. 4). Conversely, flag-leaf sheaths of EpTFIIAγ plants had lower levels of these sugars than wild-type controls. These results suggested that the extended duration and enhanced rate of grain filling may be key determinants of increased final grain weight and yield in EpTFIIAγ plants.

Given the drought resistance and yield boosts endowed by EpTFIIAγ in transgenic wheat lines, we examined which physiological processes are altered. EpTFIIAγ plants had delayed senescence compared with controls under water-limited conditions (Supplementary Figs. 8 and 9), which we hypothesized might be due to increased WUE. We also hypothesised that carbon assimilation might be altered given that increases in yield and biomass require enhanced carbon fixation. To examine possible differences in water status and carbon assimilation between wild-type Fielder and EpTFIIAγ plants, we first used plant multispectral imaging to estimate normalized difference vegetation index and normalized water index values at seedling stage in a growth chamber. Transgenic plants indeed had higher normalized difference vegetation index and lower normalized water index values than Fielder plants under both well-watered and drought-stress conditions (Supplementary Fig. 11). EpTFIIAγ seedling leaves had lower 13C fractionation than Fielder during the 18 days of drought treatment following water withdrawal (Fig. 2a), indicating enhanced WUE in these transgenic plants. These results are consistent with the lower water loss rate and transpiration for EpTFIIAγ plants under drought-stress conditions (Extended Data Fig. 1p–u).

To further assess the effects of EpTFIIAγ expression on carbon assimilation, we measured quantum yield of photosystem II photochemistry (ΦPSII). Transgenic EpTFIIAγ seedlings had approximately 14.6% higher ΦPSII than Fielder control plants under well-watered conditions, and approximately 29.9% greater ΦPSII under drought stress (Extended Data Fig. 5a,b). These results suggested that EpTFIIAγ expression led to enhanced maintenance of PSII activity. EpTFIIAγ plants had improved light–response and CO2–response curves of net photosynthesis (Fig. 2b,c). In addition, the abundance of PSI, PSII, cytochrome b6/f and LHC proteins, as well as RuBisCO subunits were all increased in transgenic plants under well-watered or drought-stress conditions (Extended Data Fig. 5d). RuBisCO activity was increased in EpTFIIAγ plants (Extended Data Fig. 5c), which agreed with A–Ci curves showing higher maximum rates of RuBisCO carboxylation and electron transport (Extended Data Fig. 5e–h). Consistent with these increases, EpTFIIAγ leaves over-accumulated sucrose, glucose, starch and soluble sugars (Extended Data Fig. 5i), further demonstrating enhanced carbon assimilation in these plants. Of note, drought stress triggered a marked accumulation of root soluble sugars (for example, sucrose, glucose and fructose) in EpTFIIAγ plants compared with Fielder, whereas no such difference was observed under optimal conditions (Extended Data Fig. 5j), aligning with enhanced root growth under drought stress.

We next evaluated how EpTFIIAγ contributes to lifting photosynthetic capacity in field-grown plants. Chlorophyll content (soil and plant analyser development, SPAD), maximum quantum yield of PSII photochemistry (Fv/Fm) and photosynthetic rate were all enhanced in EpTFIIAγ transgenic plants in all five wheat cultivars compared with their corresponding wild-type controls at all of the tillering, stem elongation, flowering and milk development stages under both full-irrigation and water-limited conditions (Fig. 2d–g and Supplementary Figs. 12–17). However, stomatal conductance and transpiration rate were lower in transgenic plants under water-limited conditions, although some EpTFIIAγ lines had slightly higher stomatal conductance under full-irrigation conditions. In agreement with these results, WUE (that is, the ratio of net photosynthetic rate to transpiration rate) was improved in EpTFIIAγ plants under water-limited conditions (Fig. 2d–g and Supplementary Figs. 14–17). Under full-irrigation field conditions, no obvious phenotypic changes in root development were seen between wild-type Fielder and transgenic plants. By contrast, all EpTFIIAγ lines had increased root length and biomass compared with Fielder under water-limited conditions (Fig. 2h–j), aligning with the stay-green phenotype observed in shoots (Supplementary Figs. 8 and 9). Together, field-grown EpTFIIAγ wheat lines have enhanced WUE, resulting in slower water consumption, delayed senescence under water-limited conditions, and consequently more extensive root development, thereby facilitating greater water uptake. These data also suggested that enhanced photosynthetic capacity and CO2 assimilation contributed to the increased biomass and grain yield observed in EpTFIIAγ wheat plants.

Eukaryotic transcription initiation by RNA polymerase II (Pol II) requires numerous general transcription factors and cofactors to nucleate at core promoter sites, which typically contain several DNA sequence elements (for example, TATA boxes), to form the pre-initiation complex27. Among them, the transcription initiation factor TFIIA is a general cofactor that acts as an anti-repressor or co-activator27,28. In multicellular eukaryotes, the TFIIA complex typically comprises α-subunit, β-subunit and γ-subunit, encoded by TFIIAαβ and TFIIAγ genes27,29. To characterize the function of EpTFIIAγ, we first examined TFIIAγ sequences across species. EpTFIIAγ retains a conserved protein architecture while also harbouring a unique, lineage-specific N-terminal extension (Supplementary Table 7 and Supplementary Fig. 18). To determine whether EpTFIIAγ participates in transcription initiation, we first examined its subcellular localization in wheat protoplasts. EpTFIIAγ–GFP mainly localized to the nucleus, whereas intact TaTFIIAγ–GFP and AtTFIIAγ–GFP fusions showed both nuclear and cytoplasmic localization (Extended Data Fig. 6a,b). To investigate whether EpTFIIAγ could interact with endogenous TFIIAαβ to form the TFIIA holocomplex in plants, we conducted yeast two-hybrid, luciferase-complementation imaging and pulldown assays. These experiments consistently demonstrated that EpTFIIAγ fails to detectably interact with either wheat or Arabidopsis TFIIAαβ, whereas both TaTFIIAγ and AtTFIIAγ could interact with their corresponding TFIIAαβ proteins to form complete TFIIA complexes (Extended Data Fig. 6c–g). These results indicated that EpTFIIAγ does not appear to function as a transcription initiation factor in plants, or at least does so differently from these expression hosts.

The TFIIA recognition element (IIARE) located upstream of the TATA box enhances recruitment of TFIIA and other components to activate transcription at TATA-box promoter motifs27,29. We asked whether EpTFIIAγ could similarly bind to such recognition sites. Electrophoretic mobility shift assays (EMSAs) indicated that MBP–EpTFIIAγ could indeed directly bind to an IIARE-containing TATA box, whereas neither wheat TaTFIIAγ nor Arabidopsis AtTFIIAγ displayed detectable binding (Extended Data Fig. 6h,i). Although fungal and plant TFIIAγ proteins share conserved α-helical and β-sheet architectures, EpTFIIAγ uniquely harbours an N-terminal intrinsically disordered region (IDR; Fig. 3a). Hypothesizing that this region may confer specialized biological function, we generated an IDR deletion variant (EpTFIIAγ(ΔIDR)). This truncation did not bind to IIARE-containing TATA-box fragments, and likewise, its nuclear localization was impaired (Fig. 3b,c and Extended Data Fig. 6j). Conversely, fusing the EpTFIIAγ-derived IDR to TaTFIIAγ or AtTFIIAγ conferred binding to probes carrying an IIARE-containing TATA box (Fig. 3b and Extended Data Fig. 6j).

To test whether this IDR could drive liquid–liquid phase separation (LLPS), we expressed EpTFIIAγ–GFP in Nicotiana benthamiana leaf epidermal cells. Upon hyperosmotic stress, EpTFIIAγ maintained a homogeneous nuclear distribution and did not coalesce into condensates under either stress or non-stress conditions (Supplementary Fig. 19a). Moreover, His–EpTFIIAγ did not detectably form dynamic droplets in vitro, indicating that its relatively short IDR was insufficient to trigger LLPS (Supplementary Fig. 19b). Further evaluation of chromatin-binding dynamics through fluorescence recovery after photobleaching and fluorescence correlation spectroscopy showed that EpTFIIAγ(ΔIDR) exhibited markedly shallower bleach depth and accelerated recovery kinetics (Fig. 3c and Supplementary Fig. 19c–h), indicating attenuated chromatin association and higher mobility than full-length control. Stable-transgenic EpTFIIAγ(ΔIDR) expression in wheat cv. Fielder conferred no obvious improvement in drought resistance relative to wild-type (Extended Data Fig. 6k–m), indicating that the IDR is indeed indispensable for EpTFIIAγ function in the drought-stress response.

To identify EpTFIIAγ-binding sites across the wheat genome, we performed chromatin immunoprecipitation followed by sequencing (ChIP–seq) with transgenic plants stably expressing EpTFIIAγ–HA in the cv. Fielder (Fig. 3d). Across two replicates, 17,131 putative EpTFIIAγ-binding sites were identified, 85% of which are in intergenic regions and 15% in genic regions, including gene bodies as well as the 3-kb upstream and 300-bp downstream flanking sequences presumed to harbour the promoter and transcriptional terminator, respectively (Fig. 3e). Analysis of 2,570 genic binding sites identified 1,347 annotated genes bound by EpTFIIAγ–HA (Fig. 3f and Supplementary Table 8), and the core motif within these binding regions was enriched with a TATA-containing element (Fig. 3g). Gene Ontology analysis found that these EpTFIIAγ–HA target genes were enriched in terms related to stress responses, such as ‘transcriptional regulation’, ‘response to water deprivation’, ‘response to osmotic stress’, ‘response to cold’, ‘regulation of stomatal movement’ and ‘transpiration’; hormone responses, such as ‘response to abscisic acid (ABA)’, ‘response to auxin’ and ‘response to brassinosteroid’, as well as ‘photosynthesis’ and ‘chlorophyll biosynthesis’ (Fig. 3h). Further EMSAs confirmed that EpTFIIAγ could bind to promoter regions of 387 wheat genes, underscoring its capacity to regulate diverse biological pathways in planta, such as those related to stress response, development, photosynthesis and yield formation (Extended Data Fig. 7a, Supplementary Table 9 and Supplementary Fig. 20).

To define the regulatory network involved in the effects of EpTFIIAγ on photosynthetic efficiency, WUE and the drought-stress response, we compared transcriptomes of 20-day-old wild-type Fielder and EpTFIIAγ transgenic seedlings. This identified 1,953 differentially expressed genes (DEGs; fold change of more than 2 or less than 0.5, P < 0.05) in transgenic plants relative to Fielder, including 1,405 upregulated and 548 downregulated DEGs (Extended Data Fig. 7b–g and Supplementary Table 10). By examining overlap between ChIP–seq and RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) datasets, we identified 1,056 candidate target genes that are potentially bound and transcriptionally regulated by EpTFIIAγ, including 824 upregulated and 232 downregulated genes in EpTFIIAγ plants (Extended Data Fig. 8a,b). Among them, stress-response-related genes involved in ABA and drought responses showed strong enrichment (Extended Data Fig. 8c), such as wheat homologues of Arabidopsis MYBR1 (ref. 30), FBH4 (ref. 31) and DREB1C32 (Supplementary Tables 9 and 10). Other genes known to function in plant development, source–sink partitioning and grain yield were also present among upregulated DEGs, including the CONSTANS-like gene TaCol-B5 (ref. 33), wheat homologues of rice OsCIN2 (ref. 34) and OsSWEET4 (ref. 35), and homologues of Arabidopsis VND7 (ref. 36) and SWEET12 (ref. 37). Among photosynthesis-related DEGs, we found TaYCF9 and TaCP47, which are homologues of Arabidopsis PsbZ38 and CP47 (ref. 39) encoding subunits of the PSII reaction centre. In addition, we identified TaCOG1, a homologue of the Arabidopsis Dof transcription factor COG1, that promotes biomass accumulation by enhancing photosynthetic capacity40. TaGAPC1, a redox-dependent regulator of energy metabolism that mediates stress responses in Arabidopsis41, was also upregulated (Supplementary Tables 9 and 10).

MBP–EpTFIIAγ could bind in vitro to approximately 30-bp promoter fragments containing TATA motifs from TaDREB1C, TaFBH4, TaMYBR1, TaVND7, TaCol-B5, TaCIN2, TaSWEET4, TaSWEET12, TaYCF9, TaCP47, TaCOG1 and TaGAPC1, and this interaction could be compromised by competition from unlabelled probes (Fig. 3i, Extended Data Fig. 8d and Supplementary Fig. 21a). ChIP–quantitative PCR (qPCR) indicated that these promoter fragments showed greater enrichment in EpTFIIAγ–HA genomic DNA precipitated by anti-haemagglutinin antibody than by the immunoglobulin G (IgG) control (Fig. 3j and Extended Data Fig. 8e), demonstrating that EpTFIIAγ could indeed bind to TATA-containing core promoter motifs. In dual-luciferase reporter assays using approximately 1.5-kb promoter fragments, activities of target promoters were upregulated in the presence of EpTFIIAγ compared with empty vector controls (Fig. 3k, Extended Data Fig. 8f and Supplementary Fig. 21b), supported by real-time (RT)–qPCR analyses revealing that TaDREB1C, TaFBH4, TaMYBR1, TaVND7, TaCol-B5, TaCIN2, TaSWEET4, TaSWEET12, TaYCF9, TaCP47, TaCOG1 and TaGAPC1 mRNAs were all expressed at higher levels in all three EpTFIIAγ transgenic lines than in wild-type Fielder (Extended Data Fig. 8g). These results collectively indicated that EpTFIIAγ functions as a transcriptional cofactor to regulate target gene expression.

To determine how downstream regulatory targets contribute to enhancing drought resistance, photosynthetic efficiency and grain yield in EpTFIIAγ plants, we generated transgenic wheat lines overexpressing TaDREB1C, TaCOG1 or TaCIN2 in the cv. Fielder background. TaDREB1C lines had enhanced drought resistance and WUE through accelerated stomatal closure and reduced transpiration compared with Fielder under drought stress (Extended Data Fig. 9a–g). This overexpression did not detectably alter growth period, which remained comparable with controls under full irrigation (Extended Data Fig. 9h–j). Plants overexpressing TaCOG1 had enhanced photosynthetic and carbon-fixation capacities, resulting in increased biomass and grain yield relative to controls (Extended Data Fig. 9k–p). Performance of EpTFIIAγ rice and maize lines was tested in the field over multiple seasons. Two-year evaluation (2023 and 2024) of rice in Hanzhong (Shaanxi Province) found that plant height, grain yield per plant, grain size (ratio of grain length to width) and 1,000-grain weight were all increased in the transgenic lines compared with wild-type controls under full-irrigation conditions, and this difference was more pronounced under water deficit (Fig. 5g and Extended Data Fig. 12c,e). EpTFIIAγ rice had 10.2–11.9% increases in grain yield per plant under full irrigation and 19.7–24.1% increases under water-deficit conditions relative to wild-type Nipponbare. EpTFIIAγ maize was examined over 3-year (2023–2025) field trials in Zhangye (Gansu Province), Changji and Aksu (Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region), which are arid regions and important maize production areas (Supplementary Fig. 5). All EpTFIIAγ maize lines were taller than wild-type B73 under both full-irrigation and water-limited conditions, although grain number per ear, yield per plant and 100-grain weight were only increased under water-limited conditions (Fig. 5h and Extended Data Fig. 12d,f,g). Grain yield per plant increased by a striking 27.9–37.2% in Zhangye (Extended Data Fig. 12f), 31.9–42.4% in Changji (Fig. 5h) and 31.1–35.4% in Aksu (Extended Data Fig. 12g) under water-limited conditions. These cumulative results indicated that transgenic expression of EpTFIIAγ consistently and substantially improved drought resistance and yield of major cereal crops.

Using multifunctional, pleiotropic genes to simultaneously modulate multiple pathways could potentially streamline genetic improvement efforts aimed at enhancing crop stress resistance and yield16,46. Here we demonstrate that transgenic expression of EpTFIIAγ synergistically enhances both drought resistance and yield in cereal crops. In field trials, transgenic wheat and rice expressing EpTFIIAγ had greater yield and biomass production than respective wild-type controls across full water availability and water-deficit conditions (Fig. 5g, Extended Data Fig. 12e and Supplementary Fig. 7). In maize, these benefits were exclusively observed under water-deficit conditions (Fig. 5h and Extended Data Fig. 12f,g), whereas the lack of a yield response under well-watered conditions could potentially reflect functional redundancy in regulating grain production in C4 species with high metabolic efficiency and strict constraints on reproductive synchrony47,48. Plants expressing EpTFIIAγ consistently showed higher drought resistance and grain yield than wild-type counterparts across different genetic backgrounds and cultivars (Fig. 1, Supplementary Tables 1–6 and Supplementary Figs. 6 and 7). Moreover, EpTFIIAγ expression also confers resistance to other major stressors, including excessive heat, lodging and stripe rust (Extended Data Fig. 2), indicating that the inverse relationship between stress resistance and yield can be decoupled or even reversed.

Although used as a transgene expressed in cereal crops, EpTFIIAγ was isolated from E. pusillum, an extremophile fungus adapted to drought-prone conditions24. The N-terminal IDR of EpTFIIAγ endows it with unique functions in plants, enabling binding to TATA-box-containing motifs and physical interactions with endogenous transcriptional activators (Figs. 3a,b and 4a–e and Supplementary Fig. 22). By contrast, plant TFIIAγ homologues are restricted to basal transcriptional initiation and do not appear to mediate drought responses (Extended Data Figs. 1 and 6). IDRs are known to mediate LLPS49, yet EpTFIIAγ did not detectably undergo LLPS (Fig. 3c and Supplementary Fig. 19). Instead, its IDR is thought to undergo transient, weak DNA interactions that emerge as precise localization patterns on chromatin50,51. This mechanism allows EpTFIIAγ to integrate into the plant regulatory network for coordinately targeting pathways involved in transcriptional regulation, stress and hormone responses, transpiration, photosynthesis, chlorophyll biosynthesis, sugar transport and grain filling, ultimately enhancing drought resistance and grain yield under greenhouse and field conditions (Extended Data Fig. 7a and Supplementary Fig. 24).

Crucially, enhanced photosynthesis in flag leaves and grain starch accumulation under well-watered conditions, together with a slightly longer growth cycle under drought, suggest that EpTFIIAγ improves yield by increasing source-to-sink transport of photoassimilates, thereby promoting starch biosynthesis during the 5–15-day period after pollination (Fig. 2, Extended Data Figs. 3–5 and Supplementary Figs. 7f,l and 12–17). Improved carbon assimilation in EpTFIIAγ plants did not promote root development under well-watered conditions, but did lead to enhanced root growth under drought stress (Fig. 2h–j). In this context, we propose that excess photoassimilates are preferentially allocated to aboveground biomass under non-limiting water conditions, whereas drought stress triggers ABA signalling to redirect carbon flux towards the roots to enhance water absorption and retention37. Several lines of evidence support the hypothesis that EpTFIIAγ confers drought resistance and yield enhancement in wheat through the following pathways: (1) ameliorating drought resistance and WUE through TaDREB1C; (2) boosting photosynthesis, biomass and grain yield through TaCOG1 and TaCol-B5; (3) enhancing photosynthetic assimilation and grain filling through TaCIN2; and (4) interacting with several transcriptional activators to enhance the expression of target genes (Fig. 4, Extended Data Figs. 9–11 and Supplementary Fig. 23). These synergistic effects reproducibly conferred enhanced plant fitness and yield for all three cereal crops. Next steps include introducing EpTFIIAγ into contemporary, farmer-preferred rice cultivars and elite maize hybrid backgrounds, as well as exploring functionality in other staple crops not adapted to drought.

Heterologous expression of other extremophile transgenes can also improve resistance to abiotic stressors in Arabidopsis or tobacco, such as EpANN from E. pusillum52, versatile peroxidase from Bjerkandera adusta53 and hsp70/Thkel1 from Trichoderma harzianum54. However, whether these genes can improve stress resistance in cereals has not been reported, and mechanisms for regulating resistance remain poorly understood. Given the restrictions on transgenic plants in some growing regions, it may be possible to enhance crop drought resilience without genetic modification by targeting pathways controlled by EpTFIIAγ through chemical or biological strategies. For example, certain ABA receptor agonists can mimic ABA signalling to trigger stomatal closure and stress responses55,56,57. A photoactivatable T6P precursor overcomes source and sink limitations under diverse conditions, boosting wheat yields by 5–17%8. Certain symbiotic fungi (for example, Trichoderma) secrete metabolites that can induce systemic drought resistance58,59,60. Although these strategies circumvent certain biosafety concerns associated with transgenics, their transient and environment-dependent efficacy potentially increases long-term costs. Transgenic approaches provide durable and heritable resistance, thereby lowering costs. In agricultural practice, combining genetic engineering with certain chemical and/or biological treatments may lead to synergistic improvements in yield and stress mitigation.

Despite these advances, solutions remain urgently needed to overcome obstacles of diminishing arable land and intensifying climate change while doubling global food production by 2050. This study demonstrates the potential to substantially improve yields among the major staple crops, despite centuries of previous breeding efforts, at least in countries where genetically modified plants can be grown. Beyond demonstrating that EpTFIIAγ can dramatically boost productivity by enhancing drought resistance and grain yield, our study unveils a conserved mechanism for orchestrating stress responses, development, photosynthesis and yield to ultimately decouple antagonistic regulatory pathways. These findings open new avenues for engineering plant growth and stress resilience by key factors such as EpTFIIAγ.

Stomatal density measurements were performed following a published protocol66. To determine stomatal density in wheat, the middle region of the abaxial surface of the fully expanded second leaf of seedlings was selected for analysis. Three-leaf-stage seedling leaves of transgenic wheat expressing EpTFIIAγ–HA in the cv. Fielder background were collected and used for ChIP as previously described70. In brief, 2 g of plant tissue was washed twice with cold phosphate-buffered saline buffer and crosslinked with 1% v/v formaldehyde for 10 min at room temperature. The crosslinking was quenched by adding glycine to a final concentration of 125 mM. Samples were then lysed in a buffer containing 50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8.0), 10 mM EDTA, 1% w/v SDS and protease inhibitor cocktail. Chromatin was extracted on ice and sonicated to obtain soluble, sheared fragments with an average DNA length of 200–500 bp. A 20-μl aliquot was reserved as the input control and stored at –20 °C, whereas 100 μl of the sheared chromatin was used for immunoprecipitation with 10 μg of anti-HA antibody (ChIP grade, ab9110, Abcam) overnight at 4 °C. Subsequently, 30 μl of protein A/G magnetic beads was added, followed by incubation for 3 h. Beads were sequentially washed with low-salt buffer (20 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.1, 50 mM NaCl, 2 mM EDTA, 1% v/v Triton X-100 and 0.1% w/v SDS), twice with high-salt buffer (10 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.1, 250 mM LiCl, 1 mM EDTA, 1% v/v NP-40 and 1% w/v deoxycholic acid), and twice with TE buffer (10 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.5 and 1 mM EDTA). Chromatin was eluted in 300 μl of elution buffer (100 mM NaHCO3 and 1% w/v SDS), treated with RNase A (8 μg ml−1) at 65 °C for 6 h, followed by proteinase K (345 μg ml−1) digestion overnight at 45 °C. Immunoprecipitated DNA was then used to prepare sequencing libraries using the NEXTFLEX ChIP–seq Library Prep Kit for Illumina (NOVA-5143-02, Bioo Scientific) and sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform using a paired-end 150-bp strategy.

Low-quality sequencing reads were filtered using Trimmomatic (v0.36)71. Clean reads were aligned to the wheat reference genome (IWGSC RefSeq v1.1) using bowtie2 (v2.5.4)72. SAMtools (v1.3.1)73 was used to remove PCR duplicates from the aligned data. MACS3 (v3.0.3)74 was used for peak calling using default parameters (bandwidth = 300 bp; model fold = 5, 50; q value = 0.05). Each peak was assigned to the gene for the TSS of which was closest to the peak midpoint. Motif analysis within the peaks was conducted using HOMER (v3)75 with default settings and a maximum motif length of 12 bp. Gene Ontology functional enrichment analysis was performed using the clusterProfiler package in R76. Statistical significance was determined using a hypergeometric test with a P value threshold of 0.05.

Reads with mapping quality < 20 were removed, and duplicate reads were removed after processing with samtools fixmate and samtools markdup73. To assess transcriptional regulation mediated by EpTFIIAγ, 1.5-kb promoter regions of selected target genes were cloned into the pGreenII-0800-LUC vector to produce reporter constructs, whereas the EpTFIIAγ coding sequence was cloned into the pGreenII-62-SK vector to produce an effector construct25. Empty pGreenII-0800-LUC and pGreenII-62-SK vectors were used as negative controls. LUC and REN activities were subsequently measured using the Dual Luciferase Reporter Gene Assay Kit (RG027, Beyotime), and relative luciferase activity was calculated as the ratio of LUC-to-REN activity.

Fungal and plant TFIIAγ candidates were retrieved from the JGI MycoCosm portal85 and the OneKP project86, respectively. Protein-level similarity searches were conducted using EpTFIIAγ and representative TFIIAγ proteins as queries. Partial fragments and sequences with abnormal lengths (outside 70–150 amino acids) were removed. Redundant identical sequences from the same species were collapsed, but distinct isoforms passing the filtering criteria were retained. Ultimately, 3,183 fungal and 1,245 Viridiplantae sequences were included, resulting in a final dataset of 4,428 TFIIAγ proteins (Supplementary Table 7).

Filtered protein sequences were aligned with MAFFT (v7.525)87 using the automatic strategy selection mode. Maximum-likelihood phylogenetic inference was performed with IQ-TREE (v3.0.1)88. The best-fitting amino acid substitution model was selected with ModelFinder, and branch support was estimated using 1,000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates and 1,000 Shimodaira–Hasegawa-like approximate likelihood ratio test replicates. The resulting tree was visualized as a circular phylogeny using custom Python scripts with Biopython (v1.84) and Matplotlib (v3.10.8).
