XRP, Cardano, Polygon Face Brutal Snub as Crypto Executive Exposes Why He’s Avoiding These Altcoins in 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Are Ripple’s XRP, Cardano (ADA), and Polygon (POL) losing their appeal among crypto investors in 2026? Ben Simpson, founder of crypto investment platform Collective Shift, has named all three among six altcoins he says he wouldn’t touch with a 10-foot pole, citing concerns ranging from weak network activity and questionable valuations to intensifying competition. His blunt assessment raises fresh questions about which altcoins have the strongest fundamentals to survive the increasingly competitive crypto market.

In his post on X, Simpson acknowledged XRP’s role in enabling near-instant, inexpensive payments on the XRP Ledger but argued that the token lacks a compelling mechanism for capturing the value generated by Ripple’s business growth.

XRP enables fast, low-cost payments on the XRP Ledger.My issue with XRP is it's "value capture".When a business grows, I want to see how that success benefits the token I'm holding. In this case, there is none. Ripple can grow its revenue, but holders don't receive those…

His main concern is that Ripple’s commercial success does not necessarily translate into direct financial benefits for XRP holders. Even as the San Francisco-headquartered company expands its revenue and boosts its payment operations, investors do not receive a share of those profits.

He also pointed out that Ripple does not use its business earnings to buy back XRP, a mechanism that could potentially create additional demand for the token. For Simpson, the absence of both profit-sharing and a buyback program makes it difficult to establish a direct link between Ripple’s growing business and XRP’s price in the long term.

He considers these shortcomings significant enough to keep XRP off his investment list, despite the network’s payment capabilities.

Simpson also expressed reservations about Cardano, arguing that its adoption level does not justify its market valuation. Although ADA powers a blockchain designed to support smart contracts and decentralized applications, he believes the network is generating relatively little economic activity compared with its overall market size.

To support his argument, Simpson pointed to Cardano’s fee generation, claiming that the network collected just $140,000 in transaction fees over a 30-day period despite ADA carrying a market capitalization of approximately $7.5 billion.

For him, the disparity raises questions about whether Cardano is attracting enough users and developers to sustain its valuation. He believes a blockchain with such a substantial market capitalization should demonstrate considerably stronger usage and demand for its services.

Ultimately, Simpson’s concern is that Cardano needs to translate its technological ambitions and established ecosystem into greater real-world activity. Without a meaningful increase in network usage, he appears unconvinced that ADA offers an attractive investment proposition.

Polygon also made the list, with concerns centered on the growing competition in Ethereum’s scaling ecosystem. Although POL supports a network designed to make transactions faster and cheaper, the increasing number of competing blockchain solutions has made it harder for Polygon to maintain a distinctive advantage.

Simpson’s concern is that newer networks are offering similar benefits, including lower transaction costs and faster processing speeds, leaving Polygon with fewer obvious ways to differentiate itself.

With several projects competing for developers, users, and blockchain activity, POL faces the challenge of proving why it deserves a place ahead of its rivals.

Beyond XRP, Cardano, and Polygon, three other cryptocurrencies also fell short of Ben Simpson’s investment criteria: Sui (SUI), Ronin (RON), and Dogwifhat (WIF). Unlike his concerns about the first three crypto projects, his reservations about these tokens stem largely from changing investment convictions, disappointing adoption, and excessive reliance on market hype.

Sui was among the projects Simpson previously held before changing his investment outlook. The high-speed blockchain is designed to support decentralized applications and transactions, but he eventually dumped his SUI holdings at a loss of between 20% and 30% after his investment thesis changed. He did not elaborate on the specific developments that prompted his decision.

Ronin was another project he abandoned after its growth failed to match his expectations. Initially attracted by the potential of blockchain gaming, the pundit bought into the network, which uses RON for transaction fees and staking. However, the anticipated expansion in gaming adoption did not materialize as he had hoped, prompting him to exit the position relatively quickly. He argued that strong sector potential means little without meaningful retail participation.

WIF, meanwhile, represented a different kind of investment mistake. Simpson admitted that his decision to buy the Solana-based meme coin was driven largely by hype rather than underlying fundamentals. After his investment thesis changed, he sold his holdings to limit further losses.

His experience with WIF appears to have reinforced his preference for cryptocurrencies backed by stronger fundamentals rather than speculative excitement.
