Strategy (MSTR) Shares Tumble for Sixth Straight Day to $86 as Bitcoin Slides

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Strategy (MSTR) shares fell 4.6% on Thursday to close at $86.13, their sixth straight daily decline, as bitcoin dipped below $62,000 and a broad selloff hit U.S. stocks. In after hours trading, shares were down to $85.50.

The stock opened near $93 and slid through the afternoon, finishing near its low of about $86. The Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped more than 1,000 points, and the Nasdaq Composite lost about 2.1%. Bitcoin touched a low of $61,658 during the session, its weakest price since July. It was back near $62,000 late in the afternoon.

The drop adds to a brutal month for the largest corporate holder of bitcoin. Strategy shares are down about 37% from their $136.02 close on Oct. 19, and down about 29% from the $121.07 close on Nov. 10, which is when the latest leg lower began. Thursday was the stock’s sixth consecutive losing session.

The slide has wiped out most of the year’s value. Strategy closed 2025 at $151.95, which puts the stock down about 43% in 2026. The shares fell below $100 on Tuesday and now trade about 11% below Tuesday’s $96.50 close. That day they also dipped under the company’s STRC preferred for the first time since mid-August.

Thursday’s move tracked bitcoin closely. Institutions have kept buying even so, and a New York state pension fund boosted its stake by more than 10% in a recent filing. The bitcoin price slumped under $62,000 as traders weighed the risk of Federal Reserve rate hikes and a sharp pullback in technology stocks.

Strategy added 485 bitcoin in the week ending Nov. 15, according to its Nov. 16 Form 8-K. It paid $33.4 million, or an average of $68,887 per coin, and its total reached 850,849 BTC.

Those coins cost $64.20 billion, or an average of $75,452 each. With bitcoin near $62,000, the stack is worth roughly $52.8 billion, which leaves Strategy with an unrealized loss of about $11 billion. Bitcoin is trading about 18% below the company’s average purchase price.

The 850,849 coins are more than 4% of bitcoin’s 21 million supply cap, and the company also held about $4.9 billion in U.S. dollar reserves as of Nov. 15, which gives it a cushion for preferred dividends and buybacks. Strategy has not announced a new purchase since that filing. It has reported its buys on Mondays in recent weeks, so the next update would normally come Monday.

Most of Strategy’s recent cash has gone to its preferred stock instead of bitcoin. In the same filing, the company said it repurchased 2,282,510 shares of its STRC preferred for $228.1 million, nearly seven times what it spent on bitcoin.

It funded the purchases with $244.7 million raised from selling common stock. The 8-K shows $332.2 million remaining under the preferred repurchase program and $1.0 billion under its common stock buyback authorization.

Executive Chairman Michael Saylor said on X that Strategy plans to keep going. He wrote that STRC traded more than $600 million on Wednesday, calling it the only one of 456 U.S.-listed income preferreds to do so this year.

He said the buybacks add liquidity, lower dividend obligations and strengthen the credit. “We intend to keep buying below $100,” Saylor wrote.
