4 managers Lincoln City must target after Chris Cohen, Tom Shaw sacking decision

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Lincoln City slipped to within three points of the Championship relegation zone on Tuesday night as they were thumped 4-0 by Charlton Athletic. It made it five consecutive losses for the Imps, and they’ve taken action as a result.

Head coaches Chris Cohen and Tom Shaw have been relieved of their duties, as confirmed on Wednesday morning. The duo served as part of Michael Skubala‘s backroom staff in the League One title-winning season, and stepped into the lead role together after the former boss exited for Bristol City.

Now, after 19 games in charge, the Imps have opted for change. Here, we put forward four contenders the club should consider for their vacant position…

Given that Lincoln City are likely to be in a Championship relegation battle, they could do with someone who has experience in this league. Among the available bosses, few have the level of second-tier pedigree that Paul Heckingbottom has.

The Yorkshireman was let go by Preston North End after a poor start to the season but he has been there, done that and got the T-shirt in the Championship. Not only has he led successful survival fights, but he took Sheffield United to promotion as well.

Heckingbottom’s pragmatic management can make the most of the squad on offer at Sincil Bank. He can lead them to safety before looking to push up the division in the long term.

A similar but perhaps slightly more ambitious option than Heckingbottom is Steve Cooper. The Welshman is on the market after his spell in charge of Danish side Brondby came to an end in May.

Like Heckingbottom, Cooper has led Championship struggles before and led them up the table. His stint at Nottingham Forest was perhaps the best of his career to date, taking them from the lower reaches of the second tier all the way to the Premier League.

The 46-year-old prioritises a stern defensive structure and organisation out of possession, looking to break on counter-attacks with wide threats. His recent Premier League jobs could make this a tough sell for Lincoln City, but they could tempt him into a key role in their exciting project.

In Juan Carlos Osorio, Lincoln City would be landing a manager whose vast experience and meticulous match preparation could help them stave off relegation from the Championship and build for the next chapter. The 65-year-old has been out of work since March 2026 after a short stint in Brazil with Clube do Remo.

Osorio’s most notable club achievements include a dominant spell in charge of Atlético Nacional from 2012 to 2015, during which he won three league titles and a further three domestic trophies. The Colombian also managed Mexico for three years, winning 33 of 52 games, including a memorable FIFA World Cup group-stage victory over Germany.

He is no stranger to English football either. Osorio coached at Manchester City early in his career, and he completed his UEFA Pro Licence with the FA, alongside Frank Lampard, Anthony Barry and Michael Carrick. He knows the demands of the English game and the culture of its dressing rooms, so he would need little time to settle.

Naturally, the boss has had highs and lows over the course of a 20-year management career. However, Osorio’s pedigree and attention to detail could make him a hit with the Imps, and his strong connections in South and North American football could open up new avenues for the club’s expanding scouting network.

Last but not least is another left-field option, but a move to appoint 63-year-old Antoine Kombouare would be an eye-catching one, and he’s a good fit too.

Kombouare has been managing since 1999 and over the years, he’s led the likes of PSG, RC Lens and FC Nantes. He was most recently in charge of Paris FC earlier this year, and he helped them take 21 points from 11 Ligue 1 games to stave off relegation before resigning.

He might not have a move to the Championship or Lincoln City in mind, but he is an active and successful manager despite his age. He is a no-nonsense boss known for turning around struggling teams with defensive discipline and mental resilience – two key attributes in a relegation fight.

Kombouare may be an unfeasible option, but perhaps he could be tempted to take on a new challenge in England having spent almost his entire managerial career in France.
