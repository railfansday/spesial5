The Best Weighted Ab Exercises for More Complete Core Training

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Why would you train your core differently than any other muscle? Fix that mistake with these moves.

WHEN YOU FINISH out the bulk of your workout and turn your focus to your abs, there’s one key detail you need to include if you want to make real muscle and strength progress: Your routine needs to evolve beyond bodyweight. Whether your goal is to train for core aesthetics or performance (hopefully, you'll be concerned about both), you'll only get so far by running through rep after rep of unloaded movements and minutes of static holds when you focus on your core muscles. For real muscle, you'll need to use some real resistance.

"I think a lot of people out there approach abs the incorrect way," MH Advisory Board member Don Saladino said when he and MH fitness director Ebenezer Samuel, C.S.C.S. outlined their picks for must-do abs exercises. "They're going to sit there and pump out a thousand crunches, they're just trying to focus on that burn. I've always tried to approach ab training in the way I'd train every other muscle group."

There's a good reason you'll be better served approaching your core training the same way you train your chest, arms, or legs: The same principle of progressive overload applies. As Saladino notes, the way lots of people train their abs only depends on ramping up the volume of reps to increase the muscle-building stimulus.

You might not be able to move big weights like you would with other muscle groups, but adding load is another important way that you can progressively overload your core muscles. That helps to kick-start hypertrophy, the process of building muscle, and after enough focused work, you'll likely begin to see (and importantly, feel) results.

Adding weighted ab exercises to your core training has more benefits than just cutting down your your rep count.

Your core muscles aren't any different than the other muscles in the body, which we know will grow best through resistance training. To build real core muscle and strength, you need to work against a load.

When you create a stronger core, you'll see a trickle-down effect to your other muscles and exercises. Your core is important for bracing to protect your spine, which helps to reinforce good posture and is key for big time lifts.

"Don't be afraid to load your abs, period," Samuel says. "Your abs need to be strong so you can squat better, deadlift better—you can hit all your exercises better."

YOUR ABS ARE more than just one muscle. The entire group is composed of the obliques, transverse abdominis, rectus abdominis, and spinal erectors, and to a degree your glutes and other muscles.

Along with targeted exercises that hit your obliques (side planks), transverse abdominis (crunches and spinal flexion movements), and rectus abdominis (ab wheel rollouts), heavy loaded compound movements like deadlifts and squats will also challenge you to recruit your core muscles as a unit to stabilize the spine. When you want to have this emphasis, perform these lifts without a weight belt.
