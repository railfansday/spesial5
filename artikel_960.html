Leicestershire's relegation stands as points deduction for 'poor' pitch upheld

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Leicestershire join Hampshire in being relegated from Division One; Foxes argued that state of pitch for victory over Glamorgan had been due to shooting of Bollywood film days before fixture; Leicestershire finish 17 points behind third-bottom Essex in table as 19-point sanction stands

Leicestershire's relegation to County Championship Division Two has been confirmed after the club's points deduction for breaching pitch regulations was upheld.

The Foxes were hit with a 27-point penalty, later cut to 19, after the surface at Grace Road was rated "poor" by the match referee following the win over Glamorgan in August.

Leicestershire, who argued the state of the pitch was due to the unscheduled shooting of a Bollywood film days before the Glamorgan fixture, had eight points from the original punishment of 27 reinstated but then appealed against the 19-point sanction.

However, the appeals panel supported the initial finding that the Midlanders had failed to act reasonably in preparing the wicket and, as a result, the club have slipped into the second tier of the Championship after finishing 17 points behind third-bottom Essex.

The panel rejected Leicestershire's attempt to introduce fresh evidence, presented by the Pakistan Cricket Board's chief curator Tony Hemmings, saying the "the club is the author of its own misfortune" and there appeared to be "a woeful lack of understanding" of regulations by those in charge.

Had the appeal been successful and Leicestershire awarded all of their points back, Essex would have joined bottom team Hampshire in going down to Division Two.

A section of the County Championship's playing conditions stipulates that teams charged with producing a pitch rated as poor or below average take zero points from that particular game.

Leicestershire earned 19 points from the 42-run win over Glamorgan (16 for the victory itself and a further three bonus points) - a match in which batters were hit on the body and there were several instances of variable bounce.

The club insisted the filming of the Bollywood movie prevented the pitch from being covered for eight hours in heatwave temperatures.

Leicestershire chief executive Emma White said in a statement that the club "acknowledges and respects the conclusion of this process" but she also voiced "deep frustrations" that new evidence was not allowed to be presented.

White wrote: "As part of our appeal, we sought to introduce fresh expert evidence from one of the world's leading curators. We were not permitted to do so.

"We firmly believe this evidence would have made a significant difference to the outcome, and it is deeply frustrating that it could not be heard.

"From the outset, the club felt rushed and under considerable pressure throughout this process."

White continued: "Pitch regulations are vitally important. They exist to ensure fairness across our competition, and we fully support them.

"However, the club believes there is an opportunity to bring greater clarity and consistency to pitch inspections, ratings and the processes that follow.

"We recognise the challenges involved in assessing pitches and would welcome further dialogue with the Cricket Regulator and the ECB to help counties better understand how decisions are reached."

The Foxes - who finished runners-up to Middlesex in this season's One Day Cup - were playing in Division One of the County Championship for the first time since 2003 having won promotion by winning the second tier in 2025.

White added: "We are hugely disappointed by this outcome, but we will not allow it to define us.

"Our grounds team continues to have my full support, and I remain immensely proud of the work they do.

"Come pre-season, we will be ready to battle it out in Division Two, and to fight our way back."

Durham and Kent will take Leicestershire and Hampshire's spots in the top flight next season after finishing first and second respectively in Division Two in 2026.
