Arsenal won this fixture last season. It was an eventful afternoon at St James' Park, packed with VAR reviews, penalty shouts and late drama, culminating in Gabriel's winner in the sixth minute of stoppage time.

Both of Arsenal's goals were headers from corners after Nick Woltemade had given the Magpies the lead by the very same route. They had to wait until as late as the 84th minute for Mikel Merino's equaliser.

Arteta's side also won the last two meetings between these teams at the Emirates Stadium and haven't lost a league fixture against Newcastle for just over two years. There's been a significant amount of turnover on Tyneside since then.

The champions are firing on all cylinders this season. They won nine of their first 10 Premier League matches and started this weekend with a five-point lead over second-placed Brighton & Hove Albion.

There are five teams in the league with more goals scored than Arsenal, but nobody else can lay claim to a goals-against average of one every two matches. It's all very familiar and, for Arsenal's rivals, rather ominous.

Newcastle supporters are enduring a rougher ride, and the difficulties experienced by new manager Matthias Jaissle are doing little to lift the mood. The Magpies boss is under pressure, with his team in mid-table.

They've beaten only bottom-placed Crystal Palace in their last five matches, a run that began with a 4-1 loss at the hands of promoted Coventry City. Brighton and a potentially reinvigorated Manchester United are next.

But the story of the day is the return to St James' Park of former Newcastle captain Bruno Guimarães. The Brazilian midfielder is sure to receive an interesting reception after his summer move to North London.

FourFourTwo foresees one-nil to the Arsenal, who seem to be in exactly that kind of vein of form at the moment.
