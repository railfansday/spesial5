Bitcoin missed AI stocks’ 85% rally but could still face the fallout if the boom unravels

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Bitcoin fell on 77% of major Nasdaq down days since January 2024, a pattern drawing attention as the Fed examines risks from AI-related debt and financing.

DWF Labs reported on Nov. 19 that Bitcoin fell on 77% of US trading days when QQQ, an ETF that tracks the Nasdaq-100, lost at least 1%.

The analysis covers the period from the start of US spot Bitcoin ETF trading on Jan. 11, 2024, through Nov. 13, 2026. DWF put Bitcoin’s average decline on large Nasdaq down days at about 2%.

In the Federal Reserve’s October meeting minutes, released Nov. 18, almost all participants discussed what could happen if returns on artificial intelligence investments disappointed. Most pointed to rapid growth in AI-related debt and the complex, opaque structures used to finance much of it.

From September 2025 through the end of the study’s data on Nov. 13, the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) gained 85% while Bitcoin fell 39%, according to DWF Labs.

If an AI selloff spreads from chip stocks to the wider Nasdaq-100, Bitcoin’s record on those down days becomes more relevant than its trading during the chip rally.

At the Oct. 27-28 meeting, many participants discussed the potential consequences of AI-related assets losing value if investors became less confident about the returns.

A few noted that exposure was becoming concentrated among a small number of firms, raising questions about how the buildout was financed and what the projects themselves might earn.

Fed staff flagged two structures that could amplify losses if returns disappointed.

One was off-balance-sheet leases, which can leave firms committed to future payments that do not appear on their balance sheets. The other was circular financing among AI-related firms, which ties their funding to one another.

Investors who borrowed to buy assets might have to sell holdings to reduce their debt, while fund managers might also need to sell to meet withdrawals. In the most severe scenarios, sales by vehicles such as hedge funds, bond mutual funds and target-date funds could make it harder for markets to absorb trades.

Elevated asset valuations and hedge fund leverage were the biggest vulnerabilities in the staff assessment. Strong bank and household balance sheets offered a buffer against more severe outcomes, and several participants noted the staff’s finding that banks were well capitalized.

Some participants saw gaps in the picture, and several questioned whether supervisors had enough data on banks’ indirect exposure through nonbank institutions, such as credit lines to private credit funds.

The minutes describe a stress scenario in which an asset-price decline compounds through financing commitments, leverage and the need to raise cash.

The connection for Bitcoin investors would depend on whether those pressures move beyond AI-related firms and pull down the wider market.

DWF Labs measured Bitcoin’s performance on days when a fund tracking the Nasdaq-100 dropped at least 1%.

The report found a similar pattern before US spot Bitcoin ETFs began trading. From 2020 until the January 2024 launch, Bitcoin fell on 74% of the Nasdaq-100 proxy’s large down days, and it fell on 77% from the launch through Nov. 13, 2026.

Yahoo Finance’s adjusted closes show 91 Bitcoin declines across 118 days when QQQ fell at least 1% in the latter period. Bitcoin’s average log return across all 118 days was -1.93%, including days when Bitcoin rose or held steady as well as days when it fell.

QQQ’s return runs from the prior US trading session’s close. Bitcoin’s return runs across consecutive calendar-day closes, with its daily candle ending at midnight UTC, three to four hours after the US stock market closes.

Closing prices alone don't capture the order of moves, and Bitcoin can keep moving for several hours after the US trading session ends, so the comparison includes part of that post-close window.

CME Group research found that Bitcoin and US stocks often moved more closely together in periods of market stress. One possible explanation is the need for cash, since an investor reducing borrowing or covering losses can sell Bitcoin alongside stocks to raise it.

DWF’s results subtracted QQQ’s daily return from Bitcoin’s and SOXX’s returns. On that measure, the two had a correlation of negative 0.30 from March 2026 through the end of the sample. When chip stocks beat the Nasdaq-100 proxy, Bitcoin tended to lag it.

From September 2025 through Nov. 13, the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) gained 85% and Bitcoin fell 39%.

A separate analysis by Block Scholes researcher Thahbib Rahman found that broader market moves explained part of a recent Bitcoin decline. His firm found little evidence that investors sold Bitcoin specifically to buy AI assets.

The Fed held rates in October, and almost all participants judged further increases likely under their baseline outlooks.

US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October from September, and the advance estimate showed nominal retail and food services sales up 0.2%, within its margin of error, while industrial production fell 0.4%.

In the Philadelphia Fed’s November manufacturing survey, regional activity fell to 6.3 from 20.9 as the prices-received index rose to 47.0, the highest since June 2022.

Investors should watch whether declines spread across the Nasdaq-100 and how Treasury yields and the Fed rate outlook respond.

A chip-sector drop with little effect on the index would signal Bitcoin differently than a selloff that reaches the rest of the market. Bitcoin’s response would show how closely it is moving with equities in that episode.
