Meanwhile, more than 22,000 customers may be affected, including 16,000 JPMorgan Reserve cardholders.

The attack did not begin inside Visa, Mastercard, or JPMorgan. Instead, ransomware group n0n went after third-party providers connected to the three financial companies.

When the deadline expired on November 19, n0n published the stolen marketing spreadsheets online.

The leaked information differed across the three companies, but the data was largely connected to customer marketing and benefits programs rather than payment processing.

Around 16,000 JPMorgan Reserve cardholders had marketing information exposed, which may include names and email addresses. More than 6,000 premium Visa customers were affected through a third-party provider operating in the Asia-Pacific region.

Mastercard said a limited amount of information belonging to customers enrolled in a specific benefits program was also exposed.

However, one important detail separates this incident from a direct payment-system breach, no card numbers or payment data were exposed.
