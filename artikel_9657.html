In four months, a direct-to-consumer skincare brand went from barely showing up in AI answers to being named in 108 of the 152 buyer questions we tracked. Monthly visits from AI search went from 882 to 4,268, and monthly revenue from those visits went from $199 to $1,155.

The brand got there by fixing its product pages, writing pages for the questions buyers ask AI, and checking the results every week.

We’re Auden AI, a done-for-you GEO service. Our dashboard tracks how AI talks about a brand, and our team does the work to change it. This is what we did for this brand and what the data showed.

The brand’s customers had started asking AI assistants for skincare advice before buying. Questions like “are exfoliating gloves good for your skin” or “how do I exfoliate my back in the shower” were going to ChatGPT and Google’s AI Overviews instead of a search results page.

When AI answered those questions, it usually recommended other brands.

In March 2026, AI search sent the brand 882 visits a month. Those visits brought in $199 in revenue. The brand had good products and a working website, but AI rarely picked it.

We worked through four steps, then repeated them every week.

We mapped 152 questions people ask AI assistants before buying in this category. For each one, the Auden AI dashboard tracked whether AI named the brand, which competitors it named instead, and which web pages it pulled the answer from.

That list became the scoreboard for everything else.

Product pages and skin condition pages were restructured with clear claims near the top, FAQ sections, and structured data (the code that tells Google and AI what a page is about). The goal was simple. When AI looks for an answer, the brand’s page should contain one it can lift word for word.

AI assistants lean heavily on reviews, comparison articles and expert sources when they recommend products. We placed the brand in those sources so AI would see it named by someone other than the brand itself.

Every week the dashboard re-ran all 152 questions. When a question flipped to the brand, we looked at why and repeated that on other pages. When it didn’t, we changed the page and checked again the following week.

By July 2026, AI named the brand in 108 of the 152 buyer questions we tracked, which is 71%. Visits and revenue from AI search grew with it.

The visibility score shows how visible a brand is in AI answers to its tracked questions. Each AI engine gets its own score, and the overall score is the average of the engines. The brand’s score peaked at 86 during the four months before settling at 71, and it stayed ahead of every tracked competitor.

Visits and revenue come from the brand’s own analytics, counting sessions referred by AI assistants and the sales tied to them.

We kept tracking after July. The dashboard records every page AI links to for every question, so we could see why the brand was winning and where it still wasn’t. By late October, 73% of all brand mentions in the tracked AI answers went to this brand. The closest competitor had 14%. The tracking also showed a few patterns that apply well beyond skincare.

Mostly product pages. About 43% of the links AI gave to the brand pointed to product pages, and another 38% to the homepage. When someone asks AI which product to buy, AI quotes the product page that answers best. If your product page doesn’t answer the question, a competitor’s will.

Yes, when they answer one specific question. Around 15% of the brand’s AI links went to blog posts. Two of the most cited were a post on why to exfoliate after sun exposure and a guide to exfoliating your back in the shower.

Not always, and the gap can be big. In October the brand’s visibility score was 86% in Google’s AI Overviews and 37% in ChatGPT. ChatGPT also linked to the brand’s site far less often, even when it mentioned the brand by name. Each AI engine needs its own plan.

On comparison and how-to questions. Competitors were still named for questions like “exfoliating glove vs exfoliating brush” and “how to exfoliate hard to reach areas like the back.” In most cases the brand had no page that answered those questions directly. Those are the next pages to write.

There’s room left on product pages too. Star ratings and offer details like price, shipping and returns are still missing from the structured data on 30 product pages. Those are the details AI shopping answers compare, so adding them is the next quick win.
