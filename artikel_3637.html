Even Pierce Brosnan Is “Eagerly” Awaiting News of the Next James Bond

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Like the rest of us, Pierce Brosnan has been following the seemingly endless and exhaustive search for the next James Bond. Having played 007 across four films, Brosnan says he admires all the actors in the running, but Callum Turner, who played his angsty Manhattanite son in The Only Living Boy in New York, gets a particularly warm mention. “Callum is someone I’ve worked with, and he’s so strong,” Brosnan tells Vanity Fair. As for Dune director Denis Villeneuve’s vision for the franchise, Brosnan is already sold. “I think we are going to be blown away.”

Away from Hollywood, the Irish actor stars in Burberry’s new holiday campaign, celebrating 170 years of the luxury fashion house, alongside actor Fiona Shaw, his son Dylan Thomas Brosnan, and a cast of characters including Dwyane Wade, Cara Delevingne, Gabrielle Union, Nico Parker, and more. Set at the cinematic Longleat House in Wiltshire, England, it’s a festive British Christmas, complete with Burberry’s signature checks and partygoers draped in classic garments. On set, Brosnan found a kindred spirit in Shaw. The two bonded over their shared love of art—though he wishes they had much more time to chat, “It was all too brief with Fiona,” he muses.

Pierce Brosnan in the Burberry Festive 2026 Campaign video.

Pierce Brosnan and Fiona Shaw in the Burberry Festive 2026 Campaign video.

Brosnan is no stranger to good style. Having spent a career playing some of the most impeccably dressed men on screen, from James Bond to Thomas Crown, Brosnan has come to re-embrace the joys of good tailoring after an era of rebelling against the gentlemanly image he created for himself. Now, he’s looking forward to seeing a new generation of leading men step into some of those familiar roles, including Michael B. Jordan, whose upcoming take on Thomas Crown he’s cheering on.

In conversation with Vanity Fair, the former 007 talks favorite Christmas traditions, the Burberry Mac he’s kept for years, the art of playing it cool, how filming MobLand shaped his personal style, and the paintings waiting for him in London.

This interview has been edited and condensed for clarity.

Vanity Fair: The new Burberry campaign celebrates 170 years of the fashion house with a festive Christmas party in the English countryside. How was your time on the campaign set?

Pierce Brosnan: I was with my son, Dylan Thomas Brosnan, which was just absolutely wonderful, and we had the most magnificent time. I’d never seen so many people on a set in all my days for such an event. Everybody was very charming.

Do you typically go all-in on Christmas? What’s your favorite holiday tradition?

I think it starts with the Christmas tree, and my wife really decks the halls, as it were. She loves decorating the tree, and that becomes a great ritual. The boys are much older now, but we do have grandchildren joining us for Christmas. My son Sean Brosnan and his family will be coming down. My wife is a wonderful cook; she’ll make a feast for us all.

Midnight Mass is a tradition and a great joy if we’re in London. Friends, family, and reflection on the year past. Having been brought up within the Catholic religion, the season and all of those traditions are very much ingrained in me.

Harmony Korine (Gummo, Kids, Spring Breakers) directing a holiday campaign feels like such an inspired choice, given how experimental his films can be. What was it like working with him?

I thought he was charming! Very charming. It moved very fast. He was busy and deep in the workload of the day. The highlight was meeting and being with Fiona Shaw. That was just absolutely magnificent. I’m such a huge fan of the lady’s work.

The Burberry trench is such a cornerstone of British style. Is there a clothing item in your wardrobe that holds that kind of status for you?

Oh, dear. There are so many beautiful items in my closet. One of my favorite suits has become a workhorse because it’s so durable. The cloth is wool, and it’s just a two-piece, beautifully tailored suit by Anglo-Italian. I’m very partial to my shoes and belts by Bottega Veneta. And of course, a Burberry Mac, which I’ve had for many, many years. It is the classic, dream trench coat of anyone who has grown up on the movies.

It was wonderful to celebrate such a garment of classic time, and to be in the company of Fiona Shaw. [She’s] someone who I would adore to work with again on any level, really. We had a wonderful time talking about art. We’re both painters, and art is very much part of the tapestry of our lives now.

Pierce Brosnan in The Thomas Crown Affair, 1999.

Speaking of art, I wanted to ask you about playing Thomas Crown in The Thomas Crown Affair. Michael B. Jordan is stepping into that role now, in a new version of the film, which he’s also directing.

I love Michael B. Jordan. I think he’s an outstanding actor and artist, and I wish him every great success with his vision of Thomas Crown. There’s something just beautifully cinematic about the character. He was brought onto the stage by one of the great actors, Steve McQueen, who was so gifted in the art of film acting, stillness, and inner life. For me, the romance with the film came from his body of work as an actor and has always been with me since I was a young lad in Ireland. These characters are very hard to really understand, but they create a sensation.

He was so cool, Steve McQueen. How do you play cool? You play it with sincerity. You play it with stillness, with just the knowingness—and hopefully some humanity and humility, and a kind of inner knowing of being the actor you are.

What do you think makes Thomas Crown such an enduring character?

When I made my rendition and produced it with my late dear friend Beau St. Clair, I wanted to steal art. I didn’t want to steal diamonds or money. I wanted to steal paintings. It was against all the political correctness of his time and space. Thomas Crown was a man who had everything in life: wealth, fortune, intellect. But he didn’t have love. So it was a love story for me.

When we were looking for the film’s art, I suggested René Magritte: the bowler hats, the red tie, the great coat. Magritte is someone I find spellbinding, captivating, and always has a narrative which is almost cinematic. So that’s where the bowler hats came from. The visible and the invisible: Visible with an apple in front of a face, but you don’t see the face. And I thought that translated well to the enigma of Thomas Crown.

Pierce Brosnan and Callum Turner, The Only Living Boy In New York, 2017.

Having played James Bond yourself, I’m curious how you’re following the conversation around this upcoming chapter.

[Laughs.] Oh, I’m just an audience member now. I’m just the man in the street who is eager to see who is going to play this epic role of cinematic history. All the men who are on the list now are actors I admire. Callum, Callum Turner is someone I’ve worked with, and he’s so strong. They all have a voice and a physique, and a presence on film. They all have longevity as actors, all of these young men.

Pierce Brosnan as 007 in The World Is Not Enough, 1999.

What would you say to the next actor stepping into 007?

Whoever plays the role, I wish him the very best of life and strength and courage. You know, it’s hard to tell who it would be, but I eagerly await to hear the news and the celebration of the next James Bond.

Martin Campbell gave your era such a memorable introduction with GoldenEye. Now, with Denis Villeneuve taking on the franchise, what are you most curious to see him bring to Bond?

I think we are going to be blown away. I really do. [Villeneuve] is such an iconoclastic director. His work is visionary, and his stealth and imagination of camera work and storytelling is so visceral and sensuous. It’s going to be great. It’s going to be truly magnificent. Of course, we all get sentimental and nostalgic, and you have to give it time and be patient, and it’s always such a delightful game. But Villeneuve is someone who I have great admiration for, and I look forward to seeing the work.

Pierce Brosnan as Remington Steele, 1986.

Pierce Brosnan as Remington Steele, 1986.

You’ve played some of cinema’s most impeccably dressed men! Have those characters influenced the way you dress in your own life?

I have always been, I suppose, a clothes horse. When I played Remington Steele, I delved into the work of Cary Grant, another emblem of cinema that I’ve admired. Bob Butler, who directed it, said, “We’re making an old movie,” so I styled myself and had the joy of wearing beautiful suits every week. That continues through my life.

As I’ve gotten older, I used to try to rebel against the look, character, and style of actor I had created for myself. Now I embrace it more because it excites me. I love the elegance of tailored clothes and dressing up, and fitting into the season, the gale of emotion, the world around you.

You’ve got some great hunting coats in MobLand too.

Rebecca Hale, the costume designer, has brought so many great ingredients to the world of Conrad. He’s an Irishman from a hard part of Irish life, the Troubles, a mangled life of abuse and violence. He wants to fit into the world of the Cotswolds and be the gentleman. There’s this magnificent coat in MobLand, where Conrad has a shootout, and they made a magnificent overcoat, which I managed to keep from last season. And, of course, I step back and just revel at what Dame Helen Mirren does. She is such an empress of fashion herself.

When Vanity Fair last caught up with you, around your first solo exhibition, you’d already been painting and curating work for years. How has your relationship with art evolved since then?

I had, yes. I had the exhibition three years ago, and it was a great success in the sense that I had the courage to step out and show the work. I have a body of work now that has progressed, and hopefully I will have another showing here, maybe in London.

Do you find that painting gives you something different from acting, or do the two creative practices feed into each other?

Both are essential to my life. Acting is how I make my living and provide for my family. And to be able to have a lifestyle that is not only creative but productive, and then is hopefully entertaining, because you’re working towards an audience.

The practice of my art is a constant exploration of inner life, inner thoughts, inner emotions expressed through color and form. Having lived in Kauai for 25 years, there’s a wonderful artist there who’s become my teacher, Saim Caglayan. Both are necessary to my life. They soothe me and frustrate me and challenge me and exhilarate me—sometimes all in the same space of day and time.

Yes! I was sketching when I was doing Burberry. There was a lovely landscape down there, and I have my sketchbook with me at all times. I wanted so much to be an artist, to be a writer, a poet, to live a creative life. I have no real head for figures or the abstraction of figures, but I certainly have a great love for art and the power of art on the wall.

With the holidays coming up, and hopefully a little time to slow down, is there anything you’re hoping to make more time for, creatively or personally?

I have a little time left before I go back to London with my wife, Keely. London is home, and my mother of 94 is there, so I’m looking forward to seeing her. In my studio, I have a number of paintings that I’m quite pleased with, and I’d like to finish a couple more. And I’m really excited to jump into season three of MobLand. I have no idea where the story is going. Conrad is slowly, slowly going bonkers. But the thing is, he’s aware of it, and the love story of Maeve and Conrad lives on. It’s very exciting.
