New Scientific Analysis Suggests Jeffrey Epstein Was Murdered. His Brother, For One, Feels Validated.

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

One day in August 2019, Mark Epstein was having breakfast at a Virginia hotel when he looked at the TV and saw CNN reporting that his older brother, Jeffrey Epstein, had died by suicide in a Manhattan federal prison. As jarring as the news was, he had no reason to doubt it.

“I knew he wouldn’t want to live the rest of his life in jail,” Mark told Vanity Fair this week. “He had no children to worry about. Our parents were gone. He knew he didn’t have to worry about me.”

Mark says he finished his omelet and headed back to New York. Then, like so many other Americans, he started to become skeptical of the official narrative.

Over the last seven years, the endless conspiracy theories surrounding Jeffrey Epstein’s death have largely been relegated to the backwaters of YouTube and Reddit, while local and federal authorities have stuck to the suicide story. But this week there was a breakthrough. On Monday, Michael Freeman, a leading forensic scientist who testified for the prosecution in the trial of Derek Chauvin for the murder of George Floyd, issued the most legitimate challenge to date to the prevailing consensus.

Freeman presented his findings at a historic London legal society, saying, “The pattern and distribution and multitude of injuries are not explained by a suicidal hanging.” In a New York Times story published the same day as the presentation, Freeman told the paper that he had no view on who might have killed Jeffrey: “All I’m interested in is, how did this get so sideways?” Along with several global news organizations, including CBS and the BBC, the Times covered the development with a straight face even as it noted that Freeman’s efforts to publish his findings in a peer-reviewed journal had thus far been rejected.

The relationship between Donald Trump and Jeffrey Epstein has loomed large over the president’s second term.

In his thick Brooklyn accent, Mark Epstein, who has long been trying to prove that his brother was murdered while awaiting trial on sex trafficking charges, excitedly recounted this milestone over the phone the day after Freeman’s presentation, which he attended.

“This crosses all party lines,” Mark said. “Most people believe he didn’t commit suicide. So it’s a mystery. People want an answer to that. I think now they have the answer.”

Mark emphasized that the story of the recent findings was not about him but rather about his brother’s death and Freeman’s work. It was Freeman who reached out to him, Mark said, based on what the scientist independently thought was shoddy work on Jeffrey’s autopsy, and Mark agreed to turn over all the records, reports, and photographs he had as next of kin.

“I want to make it really clear,” he said. “I didn’t pay for this. He’s never asked me for anything other than the information, which I gave him willingly. And he’s been studying this. As a matter of fact, the night before last was the first time I ever met him face-to-face.”

Still, Mark has made his suspicions both his calling card and his personal mission in recent years. After working in real estate investing and serving as the chair of Cooper Union’s board of trustees, he had his own business dealings come under some scrutiny as Jeffrey’s life and crimes dominated political and cultural discourse beginning in the late 2010s—Jeffrey allegedly housed models and employees in an Upper East Side building that was predominantly owned by his brother’s real estate holding company. In the years since his brother’s death, Mark has said that Jeffrey “definitely had dirt” on Donald Trump, and he became a minor fixation amid the Epstein files rollout on account of emails containing banter between him and his brother. (Trump has denied any prior knowledge of Jeffrey’s crimes and said that the release of the Epstein files “totally exonerated” him.) Mark has dismissed a suicide note purportedly written by Jeffrey as a fabrication.

Jeffrey’s death certificate initially said “pending further study” in regard to the cause of death, rather than ruling it a homicide or suicide, but the chief medical examiner ruled it a suicide days later. “All the pathologists I’ve spoken to,” says Mark, “they said, [if] an initial death certificate says ‘pending further study,’ you don’t get an answer usually for weeks or more because something’s being studied.” Mark hired the celebrity forensic pathologist Michael Baden to observe and evaluate the autopsy, and Baden previously stated that “the evidence points to homicide.” Federal officials’ handling of the matter in the years to come only amplified Mark’s concern.

“You know a suicide when you see one,” FBI director Kash Patel said last year while he was under fire for not producing any proof of the Epstein conspiracies he had been touting during his time as a mere conservative podcast personality, “and that’s what that was.”

“I actually laughed out loud when I heard that,” Mark said, “because I thought, How many suicides has this fucking jackass actually seen?”

So who does Mark think killed his brother?

“It’s like in a Mob hit,” he said. “You try to find who the hit man was, and then you try to find who ordered the hit.” The hit man, he figured, could only be someone among a limited number of people: prison employees and other prisoners who had been on Jeffrey’s security tier. “Then the question becomes,” said Mark, “who would have the ability to orchestrate this in a federal prison, and who would have the ability to have the DOJ cover it up?” He couldn’t think of anyone besides Trump. (Trump has not been accused of any wrongdoing in connection to Jeffrey’s death; in a 2023 interview with Tucker Carlson, when asked whether Jeffrey could have been killed, he said, “Oh sure, it’s possible. I mean, I don’t really believe—I think he probably committed suicide.”)

I thought Mark and I had developed some rapport over the conversation, particularly after I mentioned that I’d attended the same Coney Island middle school where he and his brother had been students; I emphasized that I was trying to listen to him carefully and take him seriously. But there was a bit more rancor in his tone after I said it was still difficult to believe that the Department of Justice could have successfully covered up the murder of the most high-profile prisoner in the world.

“If you don’t think that after what I just told you,” Mark said, “then you, sir, are a fucking idiot also.”
