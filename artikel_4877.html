Hackers inject credit card, crypto-stealing malware into 178 major websites

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Ledger and Coinpass were among the businesses whose websites served malware designed to steal visitors’ crypto and credit card details, according to security researchers.

Update (Nov. 17 at 4:43 am UTC): This article has been updated to include a statement from Salesforce and analysis of the stolen funds by Galaxy Digital’s Alex Thorn.

Update (Nov. 18 at 4:50 pm UTC): This article has been updated to include a statement from OANDA on behalf of Coinpass and TMS Brokers.

Major global brands including Ledger, Salesforce and BBC served cryptocurrency and credit card-stealing malware to website visitors on Monday, according to security researchers, the latest in a wave of wide-reaching cyberattacks.

Dubbed “Optimjack” by researcher Amirul Iman, the campaign delivered malicious code through website scripts, including those hosted by Optimizely, a website optimization platform. The malware can replace legitimate cryptocurrency wallet addresses with attacker-controlled alternatives when visitors copy addresses. It also captured credit card details entered into website forms, according to a separate analysis by cybersecurity firm StepSecurity.

The campaign affected website scripts belonging to 178 organizations, including hardware wallet Ledger, OANDA / Coinpass and trading platforms IG, Interactive Investor and TMS Brokers, according to Report URI’s investigation.

Other affected brands included IKEA, Salesforce, Zoom, the BBC and CNN — websites that collectively attract millions of visitors a day.

A spokesperson for Optimizely confirmed to Cointelegraph on Monday that an unauthorized party exploited a vulnerability in its Experimentation account signup flow to add malicious JavaScript to some customer projects.

“We have blocked access to the vulnerable path and deployed a fix,” they said. “We have removed the injected code from affected projects. We have also ended active sessions, revoked tokens, and reset access for affected user accounts. Finally, we have blocked any code from being delivered in Cloudflare,” they said, adding that they’re continuing to investigate and have notified all affected customers.

BBC was among the first websites seen serving the Optimizely injection at 4:21 am UTC on Monday, according to Report URI CEO Scott Helme. By 9:52 pm UTC, only one of the 188 infected snippets still contained malicious code, with Ledger’s snippet cleaned at about 8:41 pm UTC.

Cointelegraph reached out to Ledger, Coinpass, and a number of other major businesses reported as impacted by the malicious injection.

An OANDA spokesperson, responding on behalf of Coinpass and TMS Brokers, told Cointelegraph on Tuesday that its security team had begun reviewing its environment following the Optimizely incident.

“Our internal systems and infrastructure were not breached, and we have seen no indication of any impact on client funds or account data. We are continuing to monitor the situation closely,” the spokesperson said.

A spokesperson for Salesforce told Cointelegraph it was investigating unauthorized modifications to Optimizely scripts used on certain Salesforce websites.

“As a precaution, Salesforce is confirming Optimizely scripts are disabled across preview and production instances.”

Optimizely lets businesses run experiments and custom code on their websites through JavaScript snippets. Attackers inserted malicious code into those customer projects, causing otherwise legitimate scripts to load the malware when visitors opened affected pages.

Once loaded, the malware could replace legitimate cryptocurrency wallet addresses with attacker-controlled alternatives, including addresses copied to a visitor’s clipboard. A user could then unknowingly paste the attacker’s address when making a transfer.

The script also watched for credit card numbers, expiry dates and security codes entered into website forms and sent captured details to an attacker-controlled server. However, it could not read payment fields isolated inside third-party payment providers’ cross-origin frames, StepSecurity said.

Visitors using iPhones and iPads also faced an additional threat: the malicious script loaded code linked to the Coruna and DarkSword iOS exploit kits, according to Helme.

Researchers have yet to establish how attackers obtained access to modify the Optimizely projects.

Despite the widespread attack, crypto addresses identified as linked to the malware received only around $7,100 in cryptocurrency on Monday, according to blockchain records checked by Cointelegraph.

StepSecurity’s separate analysis put funds received or held across the wallets at roughly $6,600, noting that some addresses had activity predating the attack.

Alex Thorn, head of firmwide research at Galaxy Digital, estimated that victims have only sent around $2,300 in total to the addresses. In an analysis posted on X, Thorn said most of the Bitcoin receipts appeared to be the attacker’s own funds routed through an exchange back to their wallet, rather than proceeds from victims.

The campaign follows a string of recent threats to website security. In October, government agencies and other organizations took websites offline as a precaution over critical PHP vulnerabilities that could allow attackers to take control of servers. Polymarket also temporarily suspended trading on Oct. 29 after a third-party provider was compromised, amid reports of users’ accounts being drained.
