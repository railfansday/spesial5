Eagles News: Makai Lemon or Max Iheanachor?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Welcome to The Linc, the daily Philadelphia Eagles links roundup at Bleeding Green Nation!

Do you feel like the Eagles should've picked Max Iheanachor in their trade up to No. 20? Or do you stand by the team's decision to go with Makai Lemon? Lemon was clearly the higher-rated pick in terms of the consensus draft board, which the Eagles have leaned into in recent years. Lemon has been OK for the Eagles thus far, though it's not easy to get a great evaluation of him in this current lackluster situation. That being said, Iheanachor plays a very valuable position and his upside is intriguing. He was my pick for the Eagles in my final mock draft. Which player would you rather have on the Birds right now?

Let's get to the Philadelphia Eagles news and links …

An alternate reality where Max Iheanachor is on the Eagles, and everything else to know about Eagles-Steelers – Inquirer

With the Eagles at 2-7 and preparing to face the Pittsburgh Steelers on Sunday, it's a perfect time to hop into the Cold Tub Time Machine — I just made that up, I think — and head back to April 23. What if Makai Lemon didn't slide down the draft board? What if the Eagles didn't move ahead of the Steelers — who literally were calling Lemon — to select him? What would the Eagles have done with their pick? It's an interesting thought experiment, given what the Steelers did after realizing they couldn't draft Lemon. They took offensive tackle Max Iheanachor, who visited the Eagles and plays a position they needed, one that came more into focus four weeks into the season when Lane Johnson retired. But here are some numbers to chew on as Markel Bell, the team's third-round pick, prepares to make another start at right tackle. Iheanachor's pressure rate allowed is 7.83%, while Bell's is 7.14%, according to Pro Football Focus. Even Monroe Freeling, who was mocked to the Eagles by some and was drafted 19th by Carolina, is having a rough start to his NFL career with a pressure rate of 8.47%. Only Blake Miller (6.23%), picked 17th, is having a better season in pass protection than Bell among the tackles picked in the area of where the Eagles did. Caleb Lomu, who is a backup in New England, went seven picks after Iheanachor at No. 28, and then no tackle was selected until the Eagles drafted Bell 40 picks later. Bell's first season hasn't been perfect, and the circumstances haven't been ideal, but he's playing just as well as his peers drafted two rounds earlier. How would we all be viewing Iheanachor if he was the first-round pick? How would the Eagles? Would they still be entering the offseason wondering if they had the right tackle of the future in the building?

One interesting subplot in this game will be the RTs. Max Iheanachor and Markel Bell are both rookies and now starting. Had the Eagles not traded up, there is a good chance they would have taken Iheanachor. Instead the Eagles moved up for Makai Lemon and took Bell in the third round. Bell and Iheanachor are having similar rookie seasons. Who will play better on Sunday? Bell has to go up against TJ Watt so that could make for a long day. Pittsburgh wants to move closer to a playoff berth. The Eagles just want to win a game. Eagles players and coaches say they haven't given up on the season. They need to prove that with a good showing on Sunday.

Eagles-Steelers questions: Reflecting on the Makai Lemon pick, previewing 2027 NFL Draft – The Athletic

Berman: The context, of course, is that the Steelers were on the phone with Lemon when the Eagles traded up to acquire the wide receiver one spot ahead of them. Pittsburgh settled for Iheanachor. The Eagles would be better with a promising option at offensive tackle than at slot receiver, although it depends on their evaluation of Iheanachor and whether he would have been a markedly better long-term option than Markel Bell in the third round. Before the draft, I thought the Eagles should address the offensive line in the first round if the board allowed. They hadn't addressed the position in the first two rounds since 2022. I also thought they could get in position for Kadyn Proctor and/or Monroe Freeling, but it did not happen. Iheanachor was in a different tier than those tackles. I'd still lean toward building through the line of scrimmage, and a combination of Iheanachor and perhaps Williams or Chris Bell in Round 3 would be better than Lemon and Markel Bell. Given how Lemon has been used, I wonder if that's a role better addressed in a middle round, but it's too soon to determine that. So I'd say yes on the positions, TBD on the players.

What will make the 2026 Eagles season a successful one following the bye week? – BGN

The 2026 Philadelphia Eagles season won't be resulting in a Super Bowl championship. The playoff streak is also likely ending at five seasons. This season is a lost cause in terms of expectations from the summer. That doesn't mean the 2026 season can't be deemed a success. Bear with me here. Since the Eagles are 2-7 and aren't expected to make the playoffs this year, there are some factors remaining in this season that can carry over into 2027. The wins and losses aren't as important (well maybe the losses), but some aspects from 2026 can play into how the 2027 Eagles shape out. So what can make the remainder of this Eagles season a success? There are a few things to hope for in this lost season.

Bowen: Nick Sirianni stumbles upon a truth about the Eagles' offense under Sean Mannion – PHLY Eagles

"I think one thing I always value about him is, anybody can come and give a play and say, 'Hey, I want to do this, or I want to do that.' I think he just has a really good monotonous approach to figuring out everything that could potentially happen in that situation, what the downfalls are, what the upside is and whether we move forward with some of those things. I think that just speaks to the (attention to) detail that he has." I kept reading this quote over and over. "Monotonous approach"? I mean, it kinda fits. Sheil Kapadia noted after the Eagles lost to the Giants that the Birds have gone three and out on 43 percent of their offensive possessions, the highest rate for any team in the last three seasons, also highest percentage for any Eagles team in the 21st century. Pretty monotonous. But given that Sirianni did not fire Mannion or take away play-calling during the bye week, as had been widely anticipated, I don't think Nick was insulting Mannion. After thinking it over, I'm pretty sure Sirianni meant meticulous approach, which is a little different. But the malapropism sums up so much. For me, anyway.

Steelers EDGE Nick Herbig OUT vs. Eagles – Behind The Steel Curtain

The Pittsburgh Steelers have downgraded edge rusher Nick Herbig to out for Sunday's Week 11 game against the Philadelphia Eagles, per Adam Schefter. Herbig has been dealing with a rib injury and didn't practice at all throughout the week. This will likely give more opportunities to Jack Sawyer. Herbig signed a four-year, $100 million extension this offseason, keeping him in Pittsburgh for the long-term future. He has three sacks so far in the 2026 season, as well as seven tackles for loss and a forced fumble.

Edge Jonathan Greenard loves to makes plays, despite Eagles' standing – ESPN

It would be a nice story arc if Greenard were at full health and ready to play without limitation coming off the team's bye, but that's simply not the reality of his season. The left ankle injury he suffered while pressuring Matthew Stafford in Week 4 against the Los Angeles Rams lingers. It swelled up on him during a flight to Georgia last week as he returned home to take care of some things at his property, he said, and remains a limiting factor on the field. The physical demands of the NFL simply do not allow the healing time required to get to 100%. But there has been improvement, something that Greenard sensed against New York whether it was setting the edge against the run or effectively executing an inside pass rush. "Whatever move feels good we're going to find a way to get to that move and be physical, be myself as much as I can. I think slowly in that game I started getting that confidence back of, 'OK, I can do certain things with this move that I used to be able to do,'" he said. "I can finally run to the ball. With this foot I was only able to strike and make a tackle. I wasn't able to run for real in past times I've been in the game. But this past game it was like I can actually run more comfortable. It still bothers [me] but I can kind of do a lot more."

Roob's Observations: Why on Earth is Johnny Mundt playing more than Eli Stowers? – NBCSP

Someone has to explain to me why Johnny Mundt is playing more than Eli Stowers. Mundt is a 10-year veteran who has four catches for 16 yards this year and has not been close to the blocker we all expected when the Eagles signed him. For literally zero value, Mundt has played a ridiculous 220 snaps, or 24 ½ per game. Why? He brings nothing to the table. Then there's Stowers, a promising rookie who is finally healthy after dealing with a lingering hamstring injury. The Eagles invested a 2nd-round pick on Stowers, yet he's buried on the bench. In the three games since he came back healthy, Mundt has played 37 snaps and Stowers has played 32. There is absolutely no reason on Earth a 31-year-old journeyman with no receiving ability and minimal blocking prowess should be playing more than a 23-year-old rookie 2nd-round pick. None. Even if Mundt was good, the Eagles are 2-7 and this is a time to see what the young guys can do and get them as many reps as possible. Stowers got a few targets late in the Washington game and caught three passes for 53 yards, including a 34-yarder. He had more yards on that one 34-yard catch than Mundt has all year. And do you know when Mundt's last 34-yard catch was? It was in 2020. Six years and four teams ago. You're 2-7. What are we doing here? Play the young guys. Especially ones who have shown some promise. I don't know if Stowers will ever be a legit NFL tight end. But I do know we're not going to find out if he's not playing.
