The young players to watch for the Falcons after the bye

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Coming off the bye week, the Falcons need to show urgency following three humbling defeats. The performances have been unacceptable after exceeding expectations during the early portion of the season. While nobody expects this team to compete for the divisional title, they must be more competitive with younger players emerging in these challenging matchups. Facing the Vikings and Lions over the next two weeks will be massive litmus tests to see what this team is made of, knowing those teams are much more talented than them.

There is still plenty to watch for even if the losses will likely continue to pile up in Atlanta. The trade deadline showed the organization is looking toward the future. What the front office did should resemble how the coaching staff approaches the second half of the season.

Younger players should receive more opportunities to prove they are ready to elevate the team long-term. There are also growing, well-acclaimed players who need to show more than they currently have this season. Here are four players who could make their mark to help the Falcons build toward the future.

It's not that the playmaking safety has been a liability. It's more about failing to build on last year's tremendous rookie season. The high-impact plays haven't been there. Poor angles in the open field and missed coverage assignments are happening more often than expected. It all feels underwhelming when assessing Watts this season. You don't see him making his presence felt anywhere near as much by closing in on passes and bursting in to make standout open-field tackles.

There have been moments when he's been around the ball, with two forced fumbles against Carolina and San Francisco. He also produced an interception and a pass breakup against Green Bay. It's hard to pinpoint many other signature plays. Meanwhile, his tackling was abysmal against Tampa Bay, and his positioning in coverage against Cincinnati was all over the place. After setting the bar so high in his rookie season, he is falling well short of being a potential defensive cornerstone.

With Jessie Bates traded to Jacksonville, the time is now for Watts to set the tone as one of the players the defense can build around. A player with his ball skills and intelligence should be making a greater impact. It's not as if he's consistently lining up as a deep-lying safety 15 to 20 yards away from plays. Jeff Ulbrich is moving him around often between the box and the slot, while handling those deep-lying responsibilities. He needs to start impacting games like he did last season.

The rookie linebacker hasn't been underwhelming or underutilized. His inclusion on the list comes down to taking on greater responsibility and making progress as a three-down linebacker. That has happened following Divine Deablo's recent injury troubles. Even with Deablo's imminent return, it would be encouraging to see Daniels have more opportunities as a three-down linebacker and be challenged in coverage as a loop defender in zone or in man coverage against tight ends.

The fourth-round pick does look far more comfortable accelerating downhill and closing down run gaps. That doesn't mean he shouldn't play in more obvious passing downs. More challenges are needed to determine whether he can be a three-down linebacker the coaching staff can trust next season.

As well as Harold Perkins Jr. has flashed in glimpses and should continue to earn more playing time, Daniels needs to be prioritized as a player who needs all the reps to grow into a well-rounded linebacker. There is plenty to be excited about watching him on tape as a tone-setter against the run. His long arms and strong instincts can make a difference in coverage, as Brock Purdy learned in Atlanta's upset win. Whether Daniels grows in an expanded role as one of the more valuable defensive pieces will be pivotal for the coaching staff for the rest of the season.

Before suffering a hamstring injury against Tampa Bay, the dynamic dual playmaking prospect was starting to earn more playing time. He received 19 snaps against Chicago and 23 against San Francisco, up from averaging 12 to 14 snaps at the start of the season. While his production was minimal in those matchups, it shows the coaching staff is trusting him more as a wide receiver.

Although Branch will need to regain trust as a returner after fumbling twice against Chicago, he should be able to bounce back and bring the necessary explosiveness to bolster the team. More opportunities could help spark a fragmented offense while putting them in better field position as a returner. More reps as a receiver should help the Falcons determine his place in the offense's long-term outlook.

The upside is certainly there, particularly after the catch, as he proved on a huge third-down conversion against the Ravens. His change-of-direction ability and feel for space can make a real difference for a team searching for production through the air. Michael Penix Jr. could use as many high-percentage looks as possible with how he's played in recent games. Branch's skill set can provide that while he's finding his niche as a pass catcher.

This is a purely speculative selection based on potential opportunity. Avieon Terrell and DeMarcco Hellams were both considered, but there is skepticism about each player. Terrell may not get many opportunities given how crowded the defensive back room is. Hellams doesn't seem like a long-term answer at safety with his limited range in coverage.

With Jawaan Taylor's maddening penalties increasing and poor run blocking becoming a greater issue, it wouldn't be surprising to see the coaching staff bench him once the team is completely out of the NFC South race. Onianwa earned encouraging reviews from the coaching staff during training camp. His measurables stood out coming out of Ohio State. There is plenty of reason to see what he is made of in the final stretch of the season against more favorable opposition.

There is no way you would throw a seventh-round rookie into the fire against Brian Flores' defense. Seeing what he is made of against primarily NFC South teams would be a plausible move. Offensive tackle will be a critical need for the Falcons in 2027. Why not see what a young, intriguing talent is made of to see if he can help a positional flaw? Even if he ends up only being a swing tackle option, the opportunity to give a player with his attributes reps needs to be maximized when the time is right, especially under Bill Callahan's guidance.
