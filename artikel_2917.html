Ankeny Christian captures fourth state volleyball title since 2022

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

CORALVILLE — Ankeny Christian has qualified for the state volleyball tournament four times — and each time the Eagles have won a state title, all under head coach Michele Quick.

No. 1 Ankeny Christian (32-7) took down No. 3 Janesville (32-13) to win the Class 1A state title on Nov. 5. Scores were 15-25, 25-18, 25-15, and 25-15.

The Eagles secured their fourth 1A championship since 2022. They dropped only one set during the tournament.

Quick has never lost a match in the state tournament. She has led Ankeny Christian to state titles in 2022, 2023, 2024 and now 2026.

"My daughter was here, and she said she could just feel the excitement when she walked into the arena," Quick said after winning her fourth state title. "She was on three state tournament teams, and then having another daughter part of it, it's really surreal. But all these girls are just so connected. I don't really know how it feels right now other than really good."

This season was unique for the Eagles. The only senior was libero Katelyn Weathers. Among the other top contributors were juniors Liv Nehring and Breeze Bentz, sophomores Avlyn Davis and Havanna Dolbeare and freshmen Gabbie Quick and Dani Craighead.

Dolbeare finished with 20 kills on Thursday. Bentz (8) and Maleah Porter (8) combined for 16. Quick ended with 26 assists.

Janesville's Quinn Sweeney won the top performer award with 23 kills, while setter Tayah Pollock had 40 assists.

Ankeny Christian won in straight sets in the quarterfinals and semifinals. Then Janesville handed the Eagles a shocking first-set loss in the final, 25-15.

Behind a number of ace serves and Sweeney's eight kills, the Wildcats had momentum.

In set two, Dolbeare and Bentz turned up the heat and were putting down kills. Quick finished the set with four kills, seven digs, and 11 assists. Dolbeare had seven kills.

Set three was more of the same as Ankeny Christian got out to a 10-6 lead, and it only increased to 19-11 as Janesville took a timeout.

Bentz picked up a big kill to make it 23-13. On the next point, the Eagles capitalized on a free ball as Dolbeare put down a kill to make it 24-15. And on match point, Quick ended the match 25-15 off the Janesville block.

Each point the Eagles won sounded like the final point of the match, and that energy was infectious on the court from the Eagles players.

"I feel like our team really needs it. We really thrive off that energy together," Dolbeare said. "After any play happens, honestly we use that energy, feed off each other, and it's what really gets us going."

The Wildcats battled in the fourth set. They took an early 3-1 lead, Ankeny Christian then took the lead 6-5. Sweeney had a big block to tie the set 6-6, and a hitting error from Dolbeare allowed Janesville to take the lead.

Porter used some luck as her kill rolled off the top of the net and in play, making the score 13-10 in favor of Ankeny Christian. Trailing 14-10, Janesville used a timeout, but by then the Eagles appeared to be in control.

Dolbeare struck for three straight kills, making the score 24-15. Quick aced the Janesville serve receive as all 13 Eagles rushed the court to celebrate the win.

"I knew this team could battle and knew that we could fight back," Michele Quick said. "I did think it could be a five-set match, but I told our team to just play our game. After that, they were starting to own it. And we knew every point was going to be a big point. And when we scored, we had to make it bigger. We always talk about giving each other hugs and big encouragement because we all trust each other."
