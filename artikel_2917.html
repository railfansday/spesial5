Walker Kessler’s defensive impact goes far beyond the box score

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

When the Lakers initially traded for Walker Kessler, the on-court fit was easy to envision. One of the best shot blockers and rebounders in basketball, paired with the league's highest-scoring duo.

He's been as advertised. Kessler leads the team in blocks, rebounds, and double-doubles, but his impact goes beyond the traditional box score.

While the team is just 18th in defense, lineups with him next to Luka Dončić and Austin Reaves are a +26.6 net, with a 104.2 defensive rating so far, which would rank second overall league-wide. He's played and started every game for the 11-2 Lakers, who've won seven straight.

His main value comes from protecting the basket from all types of attacks. He's the anchor of the team's defense and gives the perimeter players the freedom to play more aggressively with him behind them.

His reputation as a rim protector showed itself instantly. Even when he doesn't get a block, Kessler's presence in the paint alters shots in the restricted area or prevents them from occurring at all.

Watch below as Milwaukee's athletic big man Kel'el Ware tries to attack him head-on from the top of the key. He drives right into the massive 7'2" frame, uses a pump fake and sees daylight, but leaves the shot short out of fear of his shot-blocking ability.

Verticality and contesting without fouling are two must-haves for any defensive center in the modern NBA. Kessler pairs both with excellent timing and instincts around the rim. The Lakers are switching on the perimeter in the clip below, which can be susceptible to straight-line drives when not communicated correctly.

Watch as Matisse Thybulle is late ball-watching and recovering to his switch, allowing his man an open driving lane.

Kessler jumps with two hands and puts the player in an impossible situation. There's no window for a shot or pass, and he's forced to throw it to the corner where it's picked off.

"They do show up in the stats. It's not box score stats," head coach JJ Redick said at practice Saturday. "We shared this with the group the other day, just to give a sense of how valuable he is to our defense. … Going into yesterday's game, Walker was second in the league, and only a point behind Wemby [in rim points saved]. Talking about one of the best rim protectors in the NBA. That doesn't always show up in blocked shots and steals. He's doing a fantastic job for us."

Rim protection is normally the final result of the defended action, but Kessler is involved in multiple plays throughout a possession..

Notice in the clip below how the Bucks attempt to run two ball screen actions. One is a regular pick and roll, while the other is a double drag with two screeners. Kessler is in his traditional drop coverage on both.

The ball never falls below the 3-point line, and the Bucks are forced into a late-clock heave that doesn't hit iron. It turns into another stop going the other way.

The mark of a good defensive big man is how many efforts they can make on a given possession. In the clip below, the Bucks get multiple drives to the rim, with Kessler there to not only protect the rim but also recover and get into the right help positions after a kick-out pass. After three different attacks, it ends in a shot clock violation.

He gets almost nothing in the box score from the possessions shown above, but the Lakers get a stop on all of them. The Lakers signed Kessler to a four-year, $130M deal in the offseason. With his contract locked up, his role couldn't be clearer.

Even if the traditional stats don't immediately scream out his value, the Lakers' defensive numbers do. And most importantly, so do the wins.
