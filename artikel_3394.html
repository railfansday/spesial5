7 Forgotten Netflix Miniseries That Are Perfect From Start to Finish

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Content Warning: This list contains a brief mention of sexual assault.Netflix is highly known for its original productions, which include many compelling miniseries. Its hits include The Queen's Gambit, Baby Reindeer, and Adolescence, all of which are award-winning masterpieces. Some of the streaming platform's miniseries were also responsible for skyrocketing actors to stardom through debut or breakout roles, including Owen Cooper for his portrayal of Jamie Miller in Adolescence and Victoria Pedretti for playing Danielle "Dani" Clayton in The Haunting of Bly Manor.

Netflix has shown no sign of slowing down its miniseries productions, and is already planning to drop more next year. However, like many of the streaming platform's critically acclaimed miniseries, the shows could go from record-breaking hits, to fan-favorite series in the "Bingeworthy TV Shows" section in less than six months. With that in mind, here are forgotten Netflix miniseries that are perfect from start to finish.

Maniac is the perfect miniseries for when someone is looking to watch a combination of a science fiction and psychological drama series. The show centers around Annie Landsberg (Emma Stone) and Owen Milgrim (Jonah Hill), and the hallucinations they begin to have while experiencing the effects of the intense pharmaceutical trial that is being overseen by Dr. James K. Mantleray (Josh Theroux), and Dr. Azumi Fujita (Sonoya Mizuno) of Neberdine Pharmaceutical Biotech (NPB). Annie and Owen were attracted to the trial after learning the point of it was to cure all psychological disorders, but neither of them had no idea what they'd be doing until the trial began.

Stone and Hill's acting matured quite a bit in the years since they starred in Superbad together, and it was easy to see during their portrayals of a woman with borderline personality disorder, and a man with schizophrenia. The dedication they had while portraying their characters was felt throughout every episode, and the smiles their characters had in the final scene was the perfect finishing touch for the miniseries, and their portrayals. Although the miniseries was not an award winner, it did debut in the 10 most-watched Netflix shows list, and stayed there for almost a week.

Alias Grace is an adaption of the 1996 novel written by Margaret Atwood, who first gained prominence in the entertainment industry for writing The Handmaid's Tale. The miniseries centers around Grace Marks (Sarah Gadon), a housemaid convicted of murder and discusses her story with a psychiatrist named Dr. Simon Jordan (Edward Holcroft). The more she explained her friend and family background to her psychiatrist, the harder it got for viewers to believe she deserved to be in prison for the rest of her life.

The show was highly praised by critics, who gave the miniseries a 99% on Rotten Tomatoes for its plot lines and characters. Gadon did a tremendous job in bringing her character's complexity to the screen, which allowed audiences to get to know Grace, a grieving victim of abuse, not Grace, the woman accused of murdering Thomas Kinnear (Paul Gross), and Nancy Montgomery (Anna Paquin), with co-conspirator, James McDermott (Kerr Logan). Although her character was pardoned, audiences will always wonder what motivated Grace to kill Thomas and Nancy, if she was indeed the murderer.

Based on the 2008-2011 Washington and Colorado serial rape cases, Unbelievable is both a painful and must-watch miniseries, especially for women. The show starts with Marie Adler (Kaitlyn Dever), a victim of a brutal rape who is ultimately arrested for what police claim was "false reporting." In the meantime, detectives Grace Rasmussen (Toni Collette) and Karen Duvall (Merritt Wever) work to catch a serial rapist, and through their work, they find the truth, and fight to bring justice to all his victims, including Marie.

Collette and Wever's performances were an inspiration, and the fierceness they brought to their characters proved they were the right actresses for the roles of detectives seeking the truth. Dever's was just as phenomenal, and she made her character grow stronger every episode, showing viewers they too could find the strength to keep going forward, and not look back. It was stunning to see that the miniseries did not take home Emmys or Golden Globes, but Collette took home a Critics Choice Award, and the show won a Peabody Award.

Based on a true story featured in the 2012 book Unorthodox: The Scandalous Rejection of My Hasidic Roots, Unorthodox is a touching miniseries about ultra-Orthodox community member Esther "Esty" Shapiro (Shira Haas) working to rebuild her life in Berlin after leaving her husband, Yaakov "Yanky" Shapiro (Amit Rahav), with whom she's had an unhappy marriage. Esty's husband, along with his cousin, Moses "Moishe" Lefkovitch (Jeff Wilbusch), travel to Berlin to find Esty, and bring her home to their community in New York. She eventually decides to try and earn a scholarship to a music conservatory, and stops at nothing to do so.

Haas' portrayal of Esty was her international breakthrough, and it was the determination she brought out in her character that made fans realize they could have the life they deserve. Her vocals were also incredible, and to hear her character say she was not allowed to sing out loud in her community made her performance even more powerful and tear-jerking. Haas became the first Israeli actress to be nominated for a Primetime Emmy for her role, and the show has since been considered to be one of the greatest Netflix miniseries of all time.

Four Netflix characters, four completely different ways of showing up for somebody. This is the one who fits how you actually do friendship, and what having them in your life would be like.

You do not do friendship at half volume, and neither does he. What you want is somebody who says yes to the plan before you have finished describing the plan.

You are the friend who makes things happen, and so is she. Together you would be mildly exhausting to everyone around you and completely delighted with yourselves.

You have no patience for pleasant lies, and she has no ability to tell them. What looks like coldness from the outside is the most reliable friendship on this list.

You look after whoever is having the worst day. So does he. It is the least strategic way to move through the world and the best possible quality in a friend.

Ripley centers around Tom Ripley (Andrew Scott), a petty con-artist turned murderer who takes advantage of someone's mistake to attempt to bring home the spoiled, Dickie Greenleaf (Johnny Flynn) from Italy. He meets Dickie and his girlfriend, Marge Sherwood, (Dakota Fanning), and is introduced to the lavish lifestyle the pair have for themselves in Atrani. Things take a dark turn early on, and the con man's cockiness builds as he continues to get away with the lies he's told.

The decision to film this 1960s-based miniseries in black and white makes this memorable, as show runner, Steven Zaillian, and cinematographer, Robert Elswitt, wanted to pay tribute to the original novel. Even if it wasn't filmed in black and white, Ripley would still be memorable for its stars' performances, especially by Scott and Fanning. The personalities each actor brought out in their characters were unbeatable, and the miniseries has since become one of the only psychological thriller miniseries that are perfect from beginning to end.

Bill Skarsgård did not mess around while portraying a condescending criminal in the 2022 miniseries Clark. The series is based on the life of Clark Olofsson, a Swedish gangster with the desire to "be his best at being the worst." The series mainly chronicles the crimes he committed over the years, including the events of the Norrmalmstorg robbery, which created the well-known term "Stockholm Syndrome."

It was unsurprising to see Skarsgård in this role due to his phenomenal performances in horror productions, but his tremendous portrayal of the twisted criminal made viewers have a hard time not believing the actor himself was a conceited sociopath. Critics and audiences loved Clark, which is why it's shocking that the show did not debut in the top 10 most-watched Netflix shows list at the time of its release. Although its popularity diminished years ago, it still led to Skarsgård winning a Kristallen Award for his performance.

Not only is Pluto a spin-off of the hit manga Astro-Boy, it's also one of the highest-rated anime shows on Netflix. The show centers around a string of murdered robots and humans in a futuristic world, and how the victims' connections to one another turn the investigation's intensity up to a whole new level. As the miniseries progresses, the investigation turns into a fight to end the cycle of hatred, and save more lives in the process.

It's difficult to find an anime show on Netflix that can only work if themes like grief and PTSD are addressed. These themes were introduced beautifully by the characters, and the actors' stayed true to every character until the end. Although Pluto is not an award-winning miniseries, it's still an emotional one that shows fans the importance of choosing love over hate, especially toward their peers.
