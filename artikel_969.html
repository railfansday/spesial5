This time last month, it looked possible that Fulham manager Alvaro Arbeloa might not still be in charge come the November international break. However, some big results in the past three weeks have put the Spaniard on steadier ground.

Back-to-back wins over Hull City and Coventry City were followed by an impressive draw at Aston Villa last Saturday, in which the Cottagers came from behind to take a point against one of the Premier League's form sides.

The visit of out-of-form Newcastle United is a great opportunity to continue their good run and Arbeloa has nearly a full squad to pick from, with Tom Cairney the only injury absentee.

Ryan Sessegnon returns after serving a three-match suspension for his red card against Hull, while forward Josh King will hope to celebrate his first call-up to England's senior squad with a match-winning performance.

It has already been a week to forget for Newcastle, with Monday's 1-0 limp home defeat by Everton followed by the bizarre news that defender Amar Dedic had been stopped by police after being spotted carrying a bow and arrows at a beauty spot in the North East.

There was no ill intention from the full-back, but it was hardly the sort of story that boss Matthias Jaissle would have wanted ahead of a big game at the weekend.

Despite a run of four defeats in the past six Premier League matches, Newcastle could head into the international break into the top half of the table if they beat Fulham. However, a defeat would see them leapfrogged by their opponents and increase the pressure on Jaissle.

Newcastle are missing Joelinton through injury, while Matias Fernandez-Pardo is doubtful and Jacob Ramsey is suspended.

The Cottagers have improved of late and could edge a tight game.
