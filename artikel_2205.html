QPR ‘man of the moment’ in line for quick starting XI return after telling impact upon injury comeback: Opinion

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

QPR entered the November international break off the back of a win as they defeated Lincoln City 2-1 at home on Saturday. After Richard Kone‘s early goal was cancelled out by Dom Jefferies, the Hoops won it through an 80th-minute strike from Glen Kamara.

The Finn earned much of the plaudits post-game, and rightly so. However, another man in the spotlight was Ilias Chair, who set up Kamara’s winner to help secure a result for the R’s at Loftus Road.

It wasn’t the first time he has done so off the bench since his return from a spell out through injury. Chair is in form as he makes his comeback, and he will likely be rewarded with a starting spot in Julien Stephan’s side once the international break is out of the way.

Following a seven-game absence, Chair returned as a late substitute in QPR’s 4-1 defeat to Southampton. Stephan has expressed a need to ease the Moroccan back in, and he has been used off the bench in the last two matches as well as a result.

Against Burnley, he cut inside and teed up Jonathan Varane for the equaliser in a 1-1 draw. His assist for Kamara on Saturday was similar, coming in from the left onto his right, this time sliding a slick pass through to the Hoops midfielder to curl into the far corner.

Chair’s last three substitute appearances have amounted to 64 minutes on the pitch. In that time, he has provided two assists and created four chances in total, offering a crucial spark off the bench to help secure four points (stats via FotMob).

His impact has drawn wide praise, including from local outlet West London Sport. Their post-match player ratings wrote: “Kamara got the winner, but Chair was the man of the moment. He again made a major impact as a substitute, this time setting Kamara up with a sublime pass. Rangers improved massively after he came on.”

Given his recent absence and injury issues over past seasons, QPR and Stephan are wise to have taken their time with Chair’s return.

Now, he has the ideal opportunity to prepare for a return to the starting lineup. The 29-year-old has come through his substitute appearances scot-free, so he can take the break to get up to full sharpness to be considered for a starting role upon the return of club football.

QPR face Millwall after the break, and Chair’s form will likely see him start. He could replace Harvey Vale, who has started for 12 of his 13 Championship appearances this term.
