Delta Air Lines Abruptly Cancels 1-Hour Airbus A350 Route: See Map Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

US airlines regularly deploy widebody aircraft on domestic routes, especially between major hubs and airports with significant connecting traffic. According to scheduling data from aviation analytics provider Cirium, thousands of domestic widebody flights are scheduled between December and October next year. Delta Air Lines alone has scheduled more than 17,000 domestic widebody flights, including around 660 operated by the Airbus A350-900.

One of the shortest routes on which the airline had planned to deploy the aircraft was the roughly one-hour flight between Harry Reid International Airport (LAS) in Las Vegas and Los Angeles International Airport (LAX). However, in its latest weekend schedule update, the SkyTeam member removed the A350 from that sector.

Delta had scheduled the A350-900 on the Las Vegas-to-Los Angeles route on just three occasions in January 2027. It was originally assigned to DL39 on January 4 and 5, departing Las Vegas at 7:05 PM and arriving in Los Angeles at 8:22 PM. A third service, operating as DL8783, was scheduled for January 3 with the same departure and arrival times.

According to AeroLOPA, the Atlanta-based carrier has two configurations of its A350-900 aircraft. The first accommodates 306 passengers, with 32 business class seats, 48 premium economy seats and 226 economy seats. The second has 275 seats, comprising 40 in business class, 40 in premium economy and 195 in economy. Delta had planned to deploy the latter configuration.

The planned A350-900 deployment may have been linked to the Consumer Electronics Show (CES), an annual technology trade show taking place in Las Vegas from January 6 to 9. The event attracts visitors from around the world, and Delta is increasing service to the city to meet expected demand.

The airline has confirmed it will operate more than 120 flights per day during the peak of CES 2027, connecting Las Vegas with major business markets in the US and overseas. Its plans include additional domestic frequencies and new nonstop international services from Hong Kong International Airport (HKG) and Taipei International Airport (TPE), two major Asian technology markets. Both routes will be operated by A350 widebody aircraft.

Cirium scheduling data also shows several other A350-900 flights planned to and from Las Vegas around the event, including services to Seoul Incheon International Airport (ICN), Paris Charles de Gaulle Airport (CDG) and Shanghai Pudong International Airport (PVG). The airline has also scheduled individual flights to Minneapolis–Saint Paul International Airport (MSP) and Salt Lake City International Airport (SLC), which may be related to aircraft positioning for its CES operations.

Delta is expanding its Airbus A350 network with four new deployments, including Manila, Riyadh, and additional Atlanta services.

Delta is one of the largest operators of the A350-900. According to ch-aviation, the airline currently operates a fleet of 43 aircraft of the type and is awaiting deliveries for 16 more (along with 20 A350-1000s). For January 2027, it has scheduled nearly 800 departures from US airports, with Los Angeles and Hartsfield–Jackson Atlanta International Airport (ATL) accounting for much of this activity.

Los Angeles is scheduled to see around eight to nine daily A350-900 departures most days, and Atlanta will see between seven and nine departures. By route, Atlanta to Seoul Incheon is the busiest, with two daily flights using the aircraft. This is followed by the domestic service between Atlanta and Los Angeles, where Delta has scheduled up to two daily A350-900 flights.
