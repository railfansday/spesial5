Virgin Atlantic Just Axed This 11-Hour Nonstop Heathrow Route: See Map Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Virgin Atlantic is ending its route from London Heathrow to the ever-popular Mexican tourist destination of Cancun. Flights from the UK's busiest airport only began in late 2025. While the seasonal service was due to pause in mid-April 2027, flights will not return in winter 2027/2028. Two weeks ago, Virgin confirmed that it'll stop Manchester-Las Vegas service.

It will be the second time that Virgin has ceased flying to Cancun. The route originally operated from London Gatwick between June 2012 and June 2019, a year or so before the carrier pulled out of the UK's second-busiest airport as a result of the COVID-19 pandemic.

Covering 4,300 nautical miles (7,964 km) on a great circle basis each way, Virgin first flew between Heathrow and Cancun in October 2025. It was, at the time, the first time that the UK's busiest airport had ever had flights to anywhere in Mexico except the capital, which is served by British Airways and Aeromexico.

Operating on Wednesdays, Fridays, and Sundays, Cirium Diio data shows that Virgin originally deployed the 258-seat Boeing 787-9. The variant was used for the first season, with the final departure being on April 19, 2026. When the route returned on October 18, 2026, for the winter, the 397-seat Airbus A350-1000 was used instead. The final departure will now be on April 11, 2027.

After being approached by Simple Flying, Virgin commented that:

“Following a review of our flying program, we’ve made the difficult decision to permanently suspend [end] our Cancun service with the final flight operating on April 11, 2027. We know this will be disappointing news for customers who were planning to book flights with us to this destination, but we’re pleased to offer numerous other options for some winter sun, including Phuket, the Maldives and South Africa."

Switching from the Dreamliner to Virgin's highest-capacity equipment meant round-trip seats for sale jumped from 1,548 a week in the first season to 2,382 in the second season. That was a considerable increase of 54% in very little time. It would, of course, be very easy to conclude that the route was doing well. After all, there would be little need to grow capacity if it wasn't, let alone substantially.

But it's not quite that simple. It is also about how the number of seats changed in each cabin. Swapping to the much larger A350 resulted in the volume of business class seats decreasing by 48% per week, from 186 to just 96. The number in premium economy rose by 60%, from 210 to 336.

Economy seats inevitably jumped dramatically. They rose from 1,152 a week to 1,950, up by 69%. Of course, the A350 configuration's much larger total volume played a big role in this. This had another major consequence: the proportion of business class capacity fell from 12% with the 787 to just 4% with the A350. It is fair to assume that traffic to Cancun was strong, but that Virgin was not happy with the yields. The higher fuel price does not help.

Both of these places stopped seeing the carrier in 2026, but things could be changing...

The UK Civil Aviation Authority (CAA) provides a breakdown of many interesting things, including the traffic on all UK routes. Such data currently only exists until September 2026, which rules out looking at Virgin's second season. However, information exists for the first season.

Between October 2025 and April 2026, the CAA shows that Virgin transported 35,790 passengers to/from Cancun, when the 787-9 was used. As the carrier's schedule submission to Cirium shows 40,764 round-trip seats for sale, the load factor was 87.8%. The usual caveats apply.

That was a very good result for the route's first-ever season, although fare and yield information is unavailable. It also does not indicate the extent to which the business cabin was full. However, given that the number of seats was subsequently halved, the answer seems obvious. Despite the changes to the route, Virgin is keen to use the A350 and the Heathrow slots for hopefully more prosperous endeavors.
