Pokemon Go Battle League – Best LAIC 2026 Cup team

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

The Pokemon Go LAIC 2026 Cup brings a new competitive challenge to the Go Battle League during the final stage of the Twilight Trails season, with trainers battling under standard Great League rules.

With a 1,500 CP limit and no type restrictions, the Latin America International Championships Cup allows some of the strongest PvP Pokemon to compete, making team composition and move coverage essential.

Here are the best Pokemon and teams for the LAIC 2026 Cup, including recommended movesets to help you climb the ranks.

One of the best teams for the LAIC 2026 Cup includes Cetitan, Swalot, and Crustle, three of the strongest Pokemon that cover most common opponents.

Another team that players could try out is Froslass, Toxapex, and Shadow Persian.

As with any League or Cup, it’s impossible to say what a true “best” team looks like in the LAIC 2026 Cup, as it really depends on the Pokemon your opponent sends out – and you won’t know that until the battle begins.

Still, there are a few Pokemon that can perform well in most situations, which we’ve listed below alongside their optimal movesets and weaknesses.

Even though Grumpig doesn’t have impressive bulk, this Pokemon is a great choice for this Cup as it offers wide coverage and decent, solid stats.

While Charge Beam is a good fast-charge Fast Move, Psywave is way better, as it also benefits from STAB. Pair it with the Charged Move Dynamic Punch. It is cheap and can expand – even more – this Pokemon’s coverage.

As a secondary Charged Move, go for Shadow Ball. It is actually not expensive at all, and it is a Ghost-type nuke that will leave your Grumpig protected against several opponents.

Ever since it got access to a good moveset during the January 2022 Community Day, Walrein has risen to the top tier of various formats in the Go Battle League, and this year’s LAIC 2026 Cup is arguably where it shines the brightest.

To use this strong beast, you’ll need Powder Snow as a Fast Move, as this gives Walrein quick enough energy to access two powerful Charged Moves: Icicle Spear, which can be spammed to take down shields, and Earthquake for huge damage and extra coverage.

Piloswine relies on a fast-paced offensive strategy, using Powder Snow to generate energy quickly and keep opponents under pressure. Its Ice/Ground typing gives it valuable protection against Electric and Poison attacks, but five weaknesses leave it vulnerable to Fighting, Fire, Grass, Steel, and Water opponents.

Icicle Spear is the go-to Charged Move, offering inexpensive Ice-type STAB to repeatedly threaten Dragon, Flying, Grass, and Ground enemies. High Horsepower packs a much heavier punch, delivering Ground-type STAB against Steel, Fire, Electric, Poison, and Rock Pokemon, making it particularly useful when Piloswine needs to land a decisive hit.

Froslass is a dangerous offensive pick that can threaten opponents from two different angles. Powder Snow generates energy quickly, allowing it to reach its powerful Charged Moves while its Ice/Ghost typing provides useful resistances to Fighting, Normal, Bug, Ice, and Poison attacks. However, five weaknesses make it vulnerable when caught in unfavorable matchups.

Avalanche delivers heavy Ice-type STAB against Dragon, Flying, Grass, and Ground Pokemon, while Shadow Ball brings equally threatening Ghost damage against Psychic and opposing Ghost-types. With both attacks capable of dealing serious damage, Froslass can force difficult shield decisions and become especially dangerous when opponents have no protection left.

Dragonair doesn’t need powerful Charged Moves to make its presence felt, as Dragon Breath delivers relentless STAB damage that can quickly wear down opponents. Its pure Dragon typing also resists Electric, Fire, Grass, and Water attacks, although Fairy, Ice, and opposing Dragon creatures remain dangerous matchups.

Wrap gives Dragonair an inexpensive Normal-type attack for maintaining pressure and forcing shields, while Aqua Tail adds Water coverage against Fire, Ground, and Rock opponents. Both moves are relatively quick to access, allowing Dragonair to keep attacking while Dragon Breath does most of the heavy lifting.

Toxapex can make opponents work hard for every bit of damage, combining exceptional bulk with eight resistances that help it survive prolonged battles. Bite adds steady Dark-type damage against Ghost and Psychic opponents, including Psychic-types that threaten it, although its limited energy generation makes Charged Moves harder to reach.

Brine offers Water-type STAB to chip away at Fire, Ground, and Rock opponents, while Sludge Wave delivers a much heavier Poison-type hit against Fairy and Grass creatures. Both attacks require patience, so Toxapex is best suited to matchups where its durability can compensate for its slower offensive pace.

Shelgon is a surprisingly bulky Dragon-type that relies on its solid defenses and consistent damage rather than overwhelming offensive stats. Its useful resistances let it comfortably take on common Fire, Water, Grass, and Electric-types while slowly wearing opponents down.

Dragon Breath is the ideal Fast Move thanks to its high STAB damage and constant pressure. Twister is the cheaper Charged Move, allowing Shelgon to bait shields and chip away at opponents, while Flamethrower provides valuable coverage against Steel-types that would otherwise resist its Dragon-type attacks.

Lunatone has an unusual Rock/Psychic typing that offers useful protection against Fire, Flying, Normal, Poison, and Psychic attacks, but its seven weaknesses make choosing the right matchup essential. Psywave supplies Psychic-type STAB damage while generating energy, allowing Lunatone to keep its Charged Moves within reach.

Weather Ball (Ice) gives Lunatone a quick answer to Dragon, Flying, Grass, and Ground Pokemon, including two types that exploit its weaknesses. Rock Slide adds stronger STAB damage against Fire, Flying, Bug, and Ice opponents, making it a valuable option for pressuring shields or securing knockouts.

This Pokemon works best as a lead or team charger. Plus, it has the necessary Defense to stand its ground while dealing good damage.

Astonish is a very spammy move that will allow it to access its Charged Moves fast while also getting rid of the opponent’s shields. The key to Dusknoir making it into the League is mainly because of Shadow Punch, an excellent Ghost-type move, and Dynamic Punch, a Fighting-type attack that expands its coverage.

Abomasnow is at its best when it can keep opponents on the defensive, using Powder Snow to generate energy quickly and fire off repeated Charged Moves. Its Grass/Ice typing gives it useful resistances to Water, Electric, Grass, and Ground, but seven weaknesses, including a double vulnerability to Fire, make unfavorable matchups especially punishing.

Weather Ball (Ice) is the main source of pressure, delivering quick STAB damage against Dragon, Flying, Grass, and Ground enemies. Energy Ball provides heavier Grass-type coverage for Water, Rock, and Ground opponents, with a chance to lower their Defense and make subsequent attacks even more threatening.

Besides its great bulk, Lapras has an impressive move pool, with four Fast Moves and seven Charged Moves to pick from.

Even though focusing only on Water and Ice moves to benefit from STAB would be the logical thing to do, for the Fast Move, it is best to go with Psywave. It offers the most energy generation out of all the options, and it expands Lapras’s coverage.

Skull Bash, Blizzard, and Hydro Pump are quite expensive moves, and with a few other options available, they are just not worth it. Instead, use Sparkling Aria for a solid Charged Move with STAB. It costs the same as Surf but does five more damage.

As for the secondary Charged Move, Ice Beam will offer more coverage against Grass, Dragon, and Flying opponents while also benefiting from STAB.

Aurorus made it to the list mainly because of its outstanding Stamina (214) and Ice typing, which is offensively valuable as it hits a specific niche.

This dual Rock/Ice-type Pokemon works ideally as a lead, as it can push the enemy shields with its spammy Fast Move Powder Snow and then devastate them with Weather Ball or the nuke Meteor Beam, depending on the coverage.

Crustle has never been a PvP star in Pokemon Go, but the new meta and the addition of Rock Wrecker to its move pool allowed it to rise in the Battle League.

Fury Cutter is the optimal Fast Move for energy generation. Then, with a combination of X-Scissor (used to bait enemy shields) and Rock Wrecker (strong and very cheap) as Charged Moves, Crustle will be able to take down plenty of the cup’s biggest players like Aurorus and Swalot with no problem at all.

Swalot can be a nuisance for opponents that rely on Fairy, Fighting, or Grass attacks, using its pure Poison typing and solid bulk to withstand damage. Infestation prioritizes energy generation over raw power, giving Swalot time to build toward its Charged Moves while keeping its Ground and Psychic weaknesses in mind.

Sludge provides Poison-type STAB to punish Fairy and Grass Pokemon, while Ice Beam offers a much more unexpected threat. Its Ice coverage can catch Dragon, Flying, Grass, and Ground opponents for super-effective damage, including Ground-types that would otherwise have a favorable matchup against Swalot.

Cetitan makes up for its limited defensive typing with impressive HP and a fast-paced moveset. Powder Snow generates energy quickly, letting it threaten opponents with repeated Ice-type attacks, although its weaknesses to Fighting, Fire, Rock, and Steel leave it with several dangerous matchups.

Icicle Spear is the go-to Charged Move, offering cheap Ice-type STAB that can wear down Dragon, Flying, Grass, and Ground opponents. Superpower adds much-needed Fighting coverage against Steel, Rock, and Ice-types, but its Attack and Defense debuffs make timing crucial, especially when Cetitan needs to stay in battle.

The LAIC 2026 Cup has a 1,500 CP limit, with Fire, Dark, Steel, and Fairy-type Pokemon banned. Legendaries, Mythicals, and Ultra Beasts are also ineligible.

Unlike the in-person Latin America International Championships, the Go Battle League version does not allow Mega Evolutions.
