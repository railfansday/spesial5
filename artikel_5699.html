CDC Still Lacks Key Center Leadership

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

— New CDC director has named only one permanent center director since August

Since Erica Schwartz, MD, MPH, JD, became CDC director in August, the agency has named only one permanent center director, but has wasted no time making what some perceive as "political" appointments.

Last month, Ermias Belay, MD, was named director of the Public Health Infrastructure Center (PHIC), the first permanent center director named during Schwartz's tenure, according to an internal email shared with MedPage Today. (This center is also known by its longer name, the National Center for State, Tribal, Local, and Territorial Public Health Infrastructure and Workforce.)

No other permanent directors for the agency's 12 institutes and centers have been named since Schwartz took the helm.

During an all-hands meeting at one center last week, employees were told new center leadership positions won't be announced until January 2027.

That's a delay from the timeline Schwartz initially gave during an agency-wide all-hands meeting last month. At that time, she said the agency had selected four permanent center directors, and another five candidates were still in the interview process. The four new leaders were expected to begin their roles in December, she said at the time.

Notifications about various management and operations positions, however, have been flowing.

On Nov. 3, the agency told employees that Fred Thomas III, MPH, was appointed deputy chief operating officer, reporting to Sean Slovenski, the agency's principal deputy director and chief operating officer.

And last week, Schwartz told emailed employees about two new communications hires: Brandon Charochak was hired as the agency's deputy director for strategic communications, and Brian Wright as deputy director for public affairs. Caroline DeBerry, who was serving as acting deputy director for public affairs and acting director of communications, will serve in a "new capacity," an agency email said.

Just a few days after announcing the communications team, employees received the first edition of "Director's Brief," a new internal newsletter from Schwartz aimed at providing "an opportunity for us to stay connected, highlight CDC's work, and share updates on my visits, meetings, and key activities."

In October, Schwartz announced that Mina Zadeh, PhD, would be her acting chief of staff. Some employees expressed concerns about Zadeh, noting that last year she served as the designated federal official for the CDC's Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices, which had just recently been reconfigured by HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy, Jr.

In addition to the personnel changes, Schwartz oversaw changes to the CDC's reporting structure. Slovenski outlined in a September memo that he would oversee the chief operating office "and related enterprise functions," while the Global and Response portfolio would be led by Matt Ferreira.

Slovenski also named Jennifer Shuford, MD, MPH, as leader of the infectious and non-infectious disease programs, but Shuford, who was serving as chief medical officer and deputy director, has since left the agency. Capt. Satish Pillai, MD, MPH, was named as her acting replacement.

In the email, Slovenski reassured employees that these new reporting structures won't cause major changes for them.

"These interim reporting relationships are intended to provide clearer executive oversight and better coordination across related areas of work," he wrote. "They do not constitute a reorganization, restructuring, or permanent realignment of CDC programs or organizational components."

The CDC already has seen an influx of political appointees that experts have said is a major departure from how the agency previously operated.

Meanwhile, its centers and institutes still suffer from a severe dearth of leadership, as MedPage Today previously reported. Only 4 of the 12 centers and institutes have a permanent director, according to the CDC's "Leadership" webpage. Those 4 centers are PHIC, the National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health, the Center for Forecasting and Outbreak Analytics, and the National Center for Environmental Health/Agency for Toxic Substances and Disease Registry.

Some of the information on that page appears to be out of date. Rebecca Greco Kone, MPH, stepped down as director of the National Center for Immunization and Respiratory Diseases in September, and last week the agency announced that Kristin Holland, PhD, MPH, would become the new acting director of the National Center for Injury Prevention and Control.

Speaking on Thursday at the Milken Institute Future of Health Summit, Schwartz said she "is [trying to] add stability to the CDC," according to the WP Intelligence Health Brief. "We can only control what we can control, and we've been trying to hire leaders within the [centers, institutes and offices within the CDC] so that they have permanent leadership at the top."
