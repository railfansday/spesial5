Personal Investments • Re: supporting my 85 year old father

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Being a 73-year old father, I am trying to deal with this issue from the other side, when to give access and control to our children. Although I hate to admit it, my short-term memory is not what it was. That revelation was crystal clear lately when our 5-yr granddaughter beat me 4 games to none in a candy card matching game.

We gave our daughter, who is the personal representative of our wills and successor trustee of our revocable living trust, DPOA using the institution's form for accounts covering most of our cash flow (banking accounts for incoming $ and credit card for outgoing funds). I gave her login credentials for view-only access to our credit card spending so she could see if my wife and I are spending money on questionable purchases (more serious indication of cognitive decline). She is busy with her own family and she did not initiate that access yet, which probably has expired.

The harder question is when to give our children access (and knowledge) of our financial investments. They have a rough idea (probably within a factor of 2 or 3) and our concern for having funds for self-funding end-of-life expenses. We will be making that decision of granting access and/or control based on our continuous assessment of each other's cognitive abilities, along with input from our children. I am pretty confident that they will have at least view access of our portfolio by the time we reach 85 to prevent fraud and other action inconsistent with our comprehensive financial plan.
We are about the same age. Our two adult children have a very good overview of our assets/income and know that all important information can be found in Quicken on my PC.

But neither has any access to our accounts as yet, so they aren’t in a position to step in if we become incapacitated. That actually concerns me more than what will happen to our estate (we do have a slightly outdated will in place).

We are meeting with an estates attorney in the near future to update our plan, and to get the access part resolved. It’s my understanding that a RLT can be very helpful in the event of incapacity.
