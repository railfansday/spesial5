Schumer boasts early advantage in Senate power struggle as Dem rival warns against 'coronation'

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Political strategists Tiffany Smiley and Al Mottur join 'The Faulkner Focus' to analyze the growing progressive push for a sweeping legislative agenda on Capitol Hill.

With a historic race to be the top Senate Democrat locked in, both candidates eyeing the job of leading Democrats into their majority believe they have a path to victory.

Sen. Chris Van Hollen, D-Md., has raised a challenge to Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, D-N.Y., to steer Democrats through the final two years of President Donald Trump’s time in office.

It’s the first time in three decades that the race has been contested, and both believe they have or can get the 28 votes needed to claim the mantle of Democratic leader and become majority leader. But neither has publicly locked down enough support to eclipse the magic number.

Both Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, D-N.Y., and Sen. Chris Van Hollen, D-Md., are confident they have the votes to lead Democrats, but Schumer, so far, has the lead in public endorsements. (Finn Gomez/Getty Images; Alex Brandon-Pool/Getty Images)

Schumer, who has led Senate Democrats for nearly a decade as both minority and majority leader, so far has 15 senators in his camp, making him the clear front-runner.

The longtime Democratic leader has remained confident, but quiet, about his chances of retaining his job, posting shortly after Van Hollen’s announcement last week that he welcomed "discussion about the future of our caucus and the important work ahead," but asserted, "I have the votes to continue serving as Democratic leader."

Schumer’s allies, like Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee Chair Kirsten Gillibrand, D-N.Y., argue that he has led Senate Democrats to a 55-seat majority, which could expand by another seat if Alaska flips.

Van Hollen, who acknowledged that he is the "underdog," has five public backers who can vote in the forthcoming leadership elections — Sen. Tina Smith, D-Minn., who is retiring, is backing Van Hollen but doesn’t have a vote.

His base of support includes Sen. Bernie Sanders, I-Vt., who caucuses with Senate Democrats, Sen. Jeff Merkley, D-Ore., and Sens.-elect Abdul El-Sayed and Peggy Flanagan, both progressives whom Schumer did not initially back in their respective races.

Van Hollen, however, argued that he has as many as 15 in his corner. He also alleged in an interview with Politico that Democrats were trying to move up the Dec. 1 secret-ballot leadership election to kneecap his chances.

"Let’s not rush into a coronation," Van Hollen said.

Fox News Digital reached out for comment from Van Hollen and Schumer’s offices about Van Hollen's claim.

Sen. Chris Van Hollen, D-Md., speaks to reporters at the What’s Next conference in Denver on Nov. 13, 2026, as he campaigns to lead Senate Democrats. (Paul Steinhauser/Fox News Digital)

While Schumer is playing the quiet game, Van Hollen has launched a public push for the job, making several appearances across the gamut of cable news and launching his own website declaring that voters’ backing of Democrats across the country was a rejection of the "status quo," and that "to deliver, we need new leadership."

There’s also a request for donations on the site, raising questions about Van Hollen’s future ambitions. He initially was "kicking the tires" on a 2028 bid for the White House, but since then, he has dropped the idea.

Just a week earlier, Van Hollen was mulling higher office, but now he’s giving up a potential bid for the White House in 2028 to do one thing: beat Schumer and grapple with President Donald Trump.

"I’m no longer pursuing that," Van Hollen told reporters at the What’s Next conference in Denver.

But it raises the question whether a leadership bid is a launch pad for higher office.

"At a minimum, it’s good for his base fundraising," a Democratic source told Fox News Digital.
