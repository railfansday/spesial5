Russian National Pleads Guilty to Evading Economic Sanctions on Russia

Alexander Suvorov, 52, a Russian national formerly residing in Sweden, pleaded guilty today to conspiracy to violate the Export Control Reform Act (ECRA).

As part of his guilty plea, Suvorov admitted that between about March 2022 and April 2023, he led an illicit procurement network responsible for unlawfully exporting U.S. electronic components out of the United States to Russia. One of the procurement network’s main goals was to fraudulently obtain dual-use electronic components — that is, electronic components with both civilian and military applications — from U.S. companies for shipment to Russia. Suvorov’s primary role in the procurement network was to maintain front companies based in Hong Kong, including GS Group Limited, which the procurement network used to order electronic components from U.S. companies in an effort to conceal that the end users of the components were in Russia, and to then transship, via Hong Kong, the components from the U.S. companies to the end users in Russia. Suvorov and his companies also sent multiple wires, totaling more than $70,000 to various U.S. companies to pay for the electronic components. Some of the components unlawfully purchased and exported to Russia by Suvorov and his procurement network can be used in military communication and radar systems, as well as precision location systems.

Sentencing is scheduled for March 29, 2027.

Conspiracy to violate the Export Control Reform Act (ECRA) carries a maximum penalty of 20 years in prison, a fine of up to $1 million, and three years of supervised release. A federal district court judge will determine any sentence after considering the U.S. Sentencing Guidelines and other statutory factors.

The FBI and Department of Commerce are investigating the case. Assistant U.S. Attorney Rick Belliss for the Northern District of New York and Trial Attorney Garrett Coyle of the National Security Division’s Counterintelligence and Export Control Section are prosecuting the case.
