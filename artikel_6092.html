Delta Air Lines New-Hire Pilot Reportedly Fired After Answering His Cellphone While Taxiing

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

A new-hire Delta Air Lines pilot reportedly answered his cellphone while taxiing for departure, prompting the captain to return to the gate and have him removed. According to an account shared on the Black Box Aviation Podcast, the first officer subsequently lost his job. If accurate, this was a remarkably poor way to begin an airline career at Delta!

The story comes from the Black Box Aviation Podcast, which recently discussed distractions on the flight deck and the consequences of using personal phones while working.

According to the account, a Delta first officer was undergoing Initial Operating Experience, or IOE, when he answered his cellphone while the aircraft was taxiing for departure. Rather than continue to the runway, the captain returned to the gate. The first officer was removed and reportedly fired.

IOE involves flying actual airline trips under the supervision of a check pilot. A new hire may have substantial flying experience elsewhere, but is still learning how to apply the new airline’s procedures in everyday operations.

https://twitter.com/the_black_box01/status/2122695070885290294

There are limitations to this account. I have not obtained confirmation from Delta (understandably, no response). Furthermore, no flight number or date was referenced, so the recent discussion does not necessarily imply that this just happened. The reported dismissal should be understood as the podcast’s account…and incidents like this have been part of airline pilot lore in online forums for many years.

I suspect some passengers might hear this story and think firing someone over a phone call sounds excessive. After all, the airplane was still on the ground.

But taxiing requires the attention of both pilots. A captain may be steering, but the first officer still has work to do, including monitoring the aircraft’s progress and air traffic control instructions. A wrong turn or missed runway instruction can have very serious consequences…being a pilot is one of the professions that leaves no room for error.

The federal sterile cockpit rule specifically includes taxi among the critical phases of flight. It prohibits activities that could distract flight crew members from their duties.

A separate provision restricts the use of personal wireless communications devices at a flight crew duty station during flight, with exceptions for specified purposes under approved airline procedures. The FAA’s guidance makes clear that prohibited personal use includes telephone conversations, texting, and browsing the internet.

Pilots can use approved electronic flight bags (EFBs are the iPads that replace paper manuals and charts) and make necessary operational communications. Taking an ordinary personal call while taxiing is another matter.

I spend far too much time on my phone, as my weekly screen time reports reminds me every Sunday morning. I understand how reflexively we reach for it when it rings or lights up. But there are times when it simply needs to remain put away, and working in the cockpit of a moving passenger jet is certainly one of them.

What strikes me about this account is the setting. This was reportedly a new hire being supervised during training. That should be a time to demonstrate that you understand the procedures and can be trusted to follow them!

If the account is complete and the call was personal, I understand why the captain would have concerns about continuing the flight with that first officer. Returning to the gate would inconvenience passengers, but I would rather be delayed than have a captain continue after losing confidence in the person beside him.

The employment decision is harder to assess without the full record. We do not know whether there were previous concerns or what explanation the first officer offered. I tend to be more forgiving and don’t think that every mistake during training should end a career. Even so, this would be a serious lapse in judgment. A pilot arriving at Delta should already understand why answering a personal call during taxi is unacceptable.

The Black Box Aviation Podcast recounts a Delta new-hire first officer answering his cellphone during taxi, being removed after a return to the gate, and subsequently losing his job. The incident remains unconfirmed beyond that account.

If it happened as described, I understand the captain’s decision. Passengers should be able to expect both pilots to give their attention to operating the aircraft safely. Answering personal phone call is unacceptable.
