The fight over Heathrow's third runway has been about whether it fits net zero. Lawmakers have now added a more basic question: who pays, and how much ends up in fares? Until ministers can answer that, and price in the cost of cutting emissions, the plan is stalled.

British lawmakers have called for plans for a third runway at Heathrow to be paused, saying ministers haven't shown what it will cost airlines and passengers or how it fits the UK’s climate targets.

The chair of the cross-party Transport Committee Ruth Cadbury said plans for an extra runway at the country's busiest airport, and a key global hub, “should be grounded for now,” and that the government should go back to the drawing board.

A cross-party committee of British lawmakers has called for Heathrow's third runway plans to be paused, saying the government hasn't shown what expansion will cost airlines and passengers, or how it squares with the UK's legally binding net zero target for 2050. Transport Committee chair Ruth Cadbury said the plans "should be grounded for now," citing an incomplete economic analysis from the Department for Transport and what she called a damning verdict from the Climate Change Committee on the runway's climate impact. The cost gap is wide: Heathrow puts expansion at about £49 billion ($65.2 billion), while IAG, owner of British Airways and the airport's biggest customer, says it should be no more than £30 billion ($39.9 billion), with IAG CEO Luis Gallego calling the current plan "unaffordable and doomed to failure" in a letter reported by The Times. The committee also said the plan leans on sustainable aviation fuel and carbon removal that isn't yet at the scale needed — the Department for Transport has cut its 2050 SAF uptake forecast to 30% from 50%. Heathrow's own projections have landing charges rising from £25.28 ($33.13) per passenger to as much as £60 ($80) by 2034.
