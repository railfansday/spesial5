Fuel hedges turned a likely loss into a profit for Emirates. The war’s full cost will show only once that cover runs out.

Emirates airline’s first-half net profit fell 66% to AED 3.3 billion ($899 million), with the result resting on AED 7.1 billion ($1.93 billion) in fuel hedging-derived gains. At open-market fuel prices, the airline would have lost money after the U.S.-Iran war closed regional airspace and caused jet fuel costs to soar.

The Dubai-based carrier’s revenue fell 13% to AED 56.9 billion ($15.5 billion) in the half, despite what it described as strong cargo performance and enduring travel demand across its markets.

“It has been a demanding six months,” Sheikh Ahmed bin Saeed Al Maktoum, chairman and CEO, said in a statement on Thursday, citing “unprecedented regional challenges and high fuel prices.”

Emirates posted a 66% drop in first-half net profit to AED 3.3 billion ($899 million), and the result rested on AED 7.1 billion ($1.93 billion) in fuel hedging gains. The airline said that without those gains, at open-market fuel prices, it would have lost money after the U.S.-Iran war closed regional airspace and sent jet fuel costs soaring. Revenue fell 13% to AED 56.9 billion ($15.5 billion). Passenger traffic dropped 22% to 21.8 million, capacity fell 17%, and seat factor slipped to 72% from 79.5%, as connecting traffic moved to Istanbul, Singapore, and other hubs. Pre-hedge fuel costs rose 60% year over year; hedging cut the net increase to 13%, with fuel still 34% of operating cost. Cargo was the bright spot: tonnage fell 1% but revenue rose 52% as conflict squeezed airfreight capacity and shipping disruption pushed volume to air. Emirates kept spending through the downturn, taking nine A350s, refitting 12 aircraft under a $7 billion-plus retrofit program, and adding Starlink to 36 more aircraft. Linus Benjamin Bauer of BAA & Partners said the core flying business was likely loss-making on an unhedged basis and that the second half will show whether the operating model has recovered. Emirates has not disclosed its hedging position for the fiscal second half.
