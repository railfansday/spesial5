New Perinatal Guidance for Women with ADHD

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

ADHD medication should not be reflexively stopped during pregnancy or breastfeeding, according to new research.

During pregnancy and the post-partum period, hormonal fluctuations and mounting demands on executive function intensify ADHD symptoms. Poor memory, dysregulated emotions, and disrupted sleep become elevated (and exhausting) during an already-stressful time.

Paradoxically, the vast majority of pregnant women with ADHD stop treating the condition with medication on the advice of their healthcare providers. Patients have been historically told to cease using stimulant and non-stimulant medication during pregnancy due to a lack of safety data and fear of potential risks to the fetus. But new research has produced generally reassuring reproductive safety data — and pushed more providers to weigh risks on both sides of the treatment equation.

How might untreated ADHD impact a woman’s mental health and her ability to manage the extraordinary demands of caring for an infant? Could ceasing treatment pose a bigger risk to the fetus than continuing it? What are the dangers associated with abruptly stopping treatment without monitoring, support, or a plan? These questions deserve to be asked.

Not every woman should continue medication, but neither should every woman stop. This decision should account for symptom severity, individual history, co-occurring conditions, gestational risks, and the consequences of untreated ADHD. These factors should be discussed with a medical team over multiple visits.

Use these recommendations — adapted in part from guidelines published in the American Journal of Obstetrics & Gynecology — to begin conversations with your medical team about your health and your baby’s health.

Most studies have shown no adverse effects among infants exposed to maternal ADHD medications. Amphetamines may increase the risk of preeclampsia and other adverse gestational outcomes, but the absolute risk appears low. Meanwhile, studies show that discontinuing medication during pregnancy may worsen ADHD symptoms, mental health, and daily functioning.

Medication decisions should consider the risks associated with a fetus’ medication exposure and those associated with untreated ADHD. Some women choose to limit or stop medication during pregnancy and optimize treatment after delivery. If stimulant use is a concern, ask about lowering the dose, using medication intermittently, or considering another treatment.

Regular care allows your clinician to monitor fetal growth as well as your blood pressure, weight, and other health measures that may be affected by stimulant use.

Inattention, emotional dysregulation, and executive dysfunction intensify in the postpartum period. Women with ADHD face a substantially greater risk of postpartum mood and anxiety symptoms.

Allison S. Baker, M.D., is a clinician, researcher, and educator with the Perinatal and Reproductive Psychiatry Clinical Research Program at Massachusetts General Hospital.
