Island Mayor Pleads With Japan Airlines Mileage Runners After Locals Can’t Book Flights

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The concept of mileage running is making headlines in Japan, and not in a good way. A lucrative promotion from Japan Airlines reportedly caused passengers to take up to 12 flights per day in remote markets, in some cases making it difficult for locals to book flights. What a story this is!

News On Japan has the story of how mileage running has caused issues for locals on some of Okinawa’s remote islands, as they’ve struggled to purchase Japan Airlines tickets due to a flood of mileage runners.

While the story has been published in recent days, it seems to date back to February 2026 (best I can tell), when Japan Airlines’ Mileage Bank program was offering double Life Status Points for flights. This promotion incentivized all domestic flights equally, so some savvy frequent flyers decided to seek out the shortest flights, so that they could cram in as many flights in a day as possible.

This ended up bringing them to Okinawa, where you’ll find some of the shortest flights in Japan Airlines’ system. These include flights between Ishigaki (ISG), Miyako (MMY), Naha (OKA), and Tarama (TRA). According to reports, some mileage runners booked up to a staggering 12 flights per day, leaving few empty seats on some essential flights.

The issue is that this mileage running reportedly created serious difficulties for locals, who depend on the island flights for medical appointments, shopping, and other essential travel. Tarama Mayor Mitsuo Iranami even called on mileage runners to consider the impact their activities were having on the island community, commenting “I would like those doing mileage runs to understand the situation on remote islands and exercise some consideration, taking into account the number of people participating.”

Japan Airlines reportedly even introduced measures intended to counteract this, including offering full refunds without cancellation fees, along with 500 bonus miles as an incentive. Below is a news story in Japanese about this, so you can watch that with subtitles, if you’d like.

I think this story is a good reminder of just how well frequent flyer programs can work with the purpose of filling seats on planes… for better or worse!

While it’s fairly common to see loyalty programs offer promotions, nowadays it’s rare to see deals that are actually that lucrative. When it does happen, we sometimes see that the people behind the promotion clearly didn’t think through all possible scenarios, and this is one of those situations.

While Japan Airlines’ double status points promotion in February is pretty standard, the airline has added a new restriction for the offer this coming year, by capping the number of bonus points that can be earned during the promotion period, presumably to prevent something like this from happening.

Obviously I feel bad for the locals in this situation, but it’s also hard to blame frequent flyers here for simply taking advantage of a promotion as advertised.

Japan Airlines’ Mileage Bank frequent flyer program ran a promotion that might have just done too good of a job getting people to book flights, as a double Life Status Points offer caused some people to book up to a dozen flights per day, with some flights reportedly having up to 80% of passengers be mileage runners.

Ordinarily that would be considered a success in terms of filling planes, but the issue here is that mileage runners descended on island routes that are also a lifeline for residents, given the efficiency of flying there. It’s not often you hear a mayor make a plea with mileage runners, but that’s exactly what happened here.
