The cruise world welcomed another giant on Wednesday as MSC Cruises took delivery of one of its largest ships ever, the 216,638-ton MSC World Asia.

Built at the massive Chantiers de l'Atlantique shipyard in Saint-Nazaire, France, the 20-deck-high, Asia-themed vessel is the third in the line's new World Class series of ships — by far the biggest ships that MSC Cruises has ever built.

Perhaps more notably, it is the tenth largest cruise ship ever built by any line, smaller only than Royal Caribbean's three Icon Class ships and six Oasis Class ships.

"Our ambition is simple: To give every guest the best holiday ever," MSC Group executive chairman Pierfrancesco Vago said Wednesday during a dockside ceremony at the Chantiers de l'Atlantique shipyard to mark the vessel's official handover to MSC Cruises.

MSC Group is the parent company of MSC Cruises and sister brand Explora Journeys, a luxury line.

"We invested in rethinking spaces, developing new concepts and reshaping the experience," Vago added during remarks from a stage set up just in front of the MSC World Asia's bow.

Vago noted that 60% of the spaces on MSC World Asia are either new or significantly redesigned as compared to the two World Class ships already sailing, MSC World Europa and MSC World America.

Like those two vessels, MSC World Asia has nearly 2,600 rooms and is capable of holding a whopping 6,758 passengers with every berth filled — more than almost any other passenger vessel afloat.

It also has similarly massive dimensions with a length of 1,093 feet — the equivalent of nearly four football fields stacked end-to-end — and a width of 154 feet.

But many of its interior and exterior venues, including a sprawling decktop water park with multiple waterslides and a record-setting dry slide that drops 11 decks, are different from what you'll find on the earlier World Class ships in ways big and small.

Among the most significant changes is the addition of new dining and food concepts, including MSC Cruises' first pan-Asian restaurant and a Venchi chocolate and gelato venue.

Like the earlier World Class vessels, the ship also offers a sushi bar, teppanyaki restaurant, American-style steakhouse and a branch of the Italian chain Eataly (currently found only on MSC World America), among 20 dining options in all.

In addition to the water park and dry slide, MSC World Asia's top decks offer multiple pool areas; a suspended-over-the-ocean swing ride called The Cliffhangar; and a ropes course.

Notable interior features include a three-deck-high dining and entertainment zone called the World Galleria, which is covered by one of the largest LED-lit ceilings at sea. An outdoor extension of this area, called the World Promenade, features more dining and entertainment, plus a new suspended, illuminated Dragon sculpture — part of the ship's Asia theming.

The arrival of MSC World Asia marks the latest milestone in MSC Cruises' rapid expansion.

With the addition of MSC World Asia, the brand now operates 24 ships, up from just 13 vessels as recently as 2017. It has nine more on order.

The ships on the way include five more World Class vessels scheduled for delivery by 2031. Also coming is a new class of four somewhat smaller, 180,000-ton vessels that will be known as the Frontier Class.

Wednesday's delivery ceremony included a ceremonial lowering of the Chantiers de l'Atlantique and French flags in front of attendees and the raising of the MSC Cruises flag as well as the flag of Malta, where the vessel is registered.

MSC Group chief financial officer Alexa Aponte Vago, who is married to MSC Group executive chairman Pierfrancesco Vago, served as the traditional "godmother" for the event, which included the smashing of a bottle of Champagne against the ship's hull.

Joining her husband on stage, Alexa Aponte Vago cut a ceremonial blue ribbon to release the bottle of Champagne against the side of the vessel right on the letter C in its name.

While it included a bottle smash, Wednesday's event was not the vessel's official naming. That will take place Saturday in Le Havre, France, at a much larger event.

MSC World Asia will then head to the Mediterranean, where it will begin its first cruises with paying passengers on Dec. 4 with a voyage out of Barcelona.

The ship initially will operate seven-night Mediterranean voyages that include stops in France, Italy, Malta and Spain.

In November 2027, it will reposition to the Southern Caribbean for seven- to 14-night sailings. The itineraries will include stops in Saint Lucia, Grenada, St. Maarten, Antigua and Barbuda, Saint Kitts and Nevis, Dominica, and Saint Vincent and the Grenadines.

Founded in 1861, the Chantiers d'Atlantique shipyard in St. Nazaire is one of the world's most famous shipyards, known for building many of the world's largest cruise ships as well as naval vessels and wind-powered commercial ships. Notable vessels built at the shipyard include the SS Normandie, SS France, Cunard's Queen Mary 2 and several of Royal Caribbean's Oasis Class ships.

The shipyard has built 20 vessels for MSC Cruises over the past 25 years.

It is located at the mouth of the Loire River on the West coast of France.
