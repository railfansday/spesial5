"Where's the ding?!!" - I asked the PowerWash Simulator 2 team what they thought of Jason's nozzle work in GTA 6

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Among the many, many side activities you can do in GTA 6, there's powerwashing. Relatively early on, as part of Chapter 1's Down to Earth mission, Jason whips out his nozzle and blasts Brian's boat to a gleaming sheen. Disappointingly for powerwashing fans, though, it's a bit of a one-and-done gag, never to return. But powerwashing is powerwashing, and we immediately wondered what the experts made of it all. Had Rockstar, with its legendary attention to detail, accurately captured the intricacies of powerwashing in those leisurely moments of carefree squirting?

A question had been asked and an answer was required. So who better to cast judgement on the authenticity of Jason's sway-and-spraying than FuturLab, the studio behind the delightfully zen PowerWash Simulator series? Thus, in a moment of whimsy, I asked if it might like to 'review' GTA 6's hosiest activity. At best, I was hoping for a cheeky little statement; I certainly wasn't expecting a big chunk of the PowerWash Simulator 2 team to join in the fun. "Fair to say," the intermediary PR noted, "the Futurlab team was excited about this one!"

So what's the verdict? Let us turn to the pros. "They've got the sounds and satisfaction of clearly a nice shiny flat surface just right," PowerWash Simulator 2 lead game designer Nick McCarthy opined, "but where's the ding!? 0/10, needs 200 percent more layers of muck and 20 percent more parkour." And quickly a trend emerged. "Great visuals, satisfying feel, but lacks the crucial ding and went by too quickly to listen to a podcast," senior programmer Will Dyball wrote. Then still more sadness for the lack of PowerWash Simulator's most motivating sound: "Didn't hear a ding," associate publishing producer Jess Crosby wrote. "Must have missed a bit."

A few team members also spotted some powerwashing faux pas. "No soap?" wrote 3D artist Eilidh Norris. "Rookie mistake, Jason." Programmer Jordan Lewis also reckoned Jason was making things unnecessarily hard for himself: "[He'd] have had an easier time had he taken the time to upgrade his washer and purchased some more nozzles," they wrote. "PS. Soap would have gotten that off in a flash." Others had safety concerns: "I find Jason's lack of protective overalls disturbing," said 3D artist Rory Peace, and data principal Russell Young agreed. "Clean speedboat, ruined clothes. Don't want to bring them 'Down to Earth', but we need to talk about PPE." People advisor Sarah Robshaw, meanwhile, spotted a more heinous omission: "Where's the cats????"

Elsewhere, game designer Sam Jenkins was delighted by the powerwashing representation. "I'm so glad that the Art of Powerwashing is being taken more seriously in mainstream games!" they wrote. "However, I think the dirt on the boat is too scarce for that satisfying cleaning contrast to come through."

Jenkins even had some expert suggestions to make Jason's little squirt-out more rewarding: "Maybe the boat should have been left in a flooded warehouse for 30 years to coat it in grime?" they continued. "Or even have a crushed fruit festival come through town to get some nice big colourful blobs all over the boat. Ooh! And we could even have the boat be the home of various squids to get some difficult-to-clean Ink streaked down it too!" Truly the words of someone who takes their powerwashing seriously!

Junior build engineer Owen Layton, programmer Jordan Lewis, and lead designer Nick McCarthy also provided 'reviews' in pictoral form, but technical complications mean they'll just have to exist in your imagination. Senior narrative designer Mark Ginbey, though, expressed admiration for all the in-world encouragement players are given. "Some excellent motivational text updates from Cal, like 'Mmhmm, half way there...' to indicate 50 percent completion," they wrote. "But Jason needs to concentrate on the work in hand. A quality powerwasher never talks on the job."

Senior game designer António Salreta had praise for GTA 6, with one exception: "It's an amazing game with so many fun systems - but. Needs. More. Nozzles." And how's this for a ringing endorsement from senior QA tester Paul Comse? "I love coming home after a hard day of work powerwashing in PowerWash Sim and unwinding by powerwashing some more in GTA," they wrote. But if we're scoring Jason's efforts? "7.8/10, too much water."

So there you go! The experts have spoken, and I'd call it a success for Rockstar's inaugural attempt at powerwashing, despite a handful of 'must try harders'. An unexpectedly generous response indeed! And if you're curious how FuturLab does it, look no further than last year's PowerWash Simulator 2.
