The 25 Best Movies on Netflix to Watch in November 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

It can be hard to find the best movies on Netflix. We all understand the struggle of scrolling time—hours lost to wading through all of the Netflix movie options that could instead have been spent, you know, watching something. Or maybe something has been sitting patiently in your queue, waiting for someone to give you a nudge to finally press play. So, like a beacon in the night, here’s a guide to 25 of the best films within Netflix’s huge selection—including everything from landmark films to cult classics to Netflix-original hidden gems—updated monthly as films appear on and leave the platform. Take that, decision fatigue. (And if you want a list of the best shows on Netflix, we’ve got one of those too.)

Director: Colin HigginsGenre: ComedyNotable cast: Jane Fonda, Lily Tomlin, Dolly Parton, Dabney Coleman, and Elizabeth WilsonMPA rating: PGRotten Tomatoes: 71%Metacritic: 58

After the passing of Dolly Parton this year, rewatching her in her first film role here can feel bittersweet, but it also proves how she was able to conquer all art forms she tried with seeming ease.

The multihyphenate icon once joked to Vanity Fair about taking the role of Doralee Rhodes, saying, “I thought, Well, if it’s a big success, I can just join in there with Lily [Tomlin] and Jane [Fonda]. And if it’s a big flop, I’ll just blame it on them ’cause nobody thinks about me being a movie star.” Parton would also get her first Oscar nomination and two Grammys for the title track, but the comedy gold of this trio taking on their sexist, egotistical, lying, hypocritical bigot boss remains a classic.

Director: James GrayGenre: Space DramaNotable cast: Brad Pitt, Tommy Lee Jones, Ruth Negga, Liv Tyler, Donald Sutherland, John Ortiz, and Natasha LyonneMPA rating: PG-13Rotten Tomatoes: 83%Metacritic: 80

As director James Gray prepares to storm the awards season with Paper Tiger—a return to the crime genre that put him on the map—it’s maybe time to reassess his misunderstood and mishandled space elegy Ad Astra. It’s not your typical space exploration adventure, but something much more personal. Starring Brad Pitt as an astronaut who ventures to the depths of our solar system to recover his father (Tommy Lee Jones) after a failed mission, Ad Astra is a more soulful and introspective space film than most audiences were expecting. It didn’t help that post Disney merger, Fox whimpered the film into theatres. “But what has long distinguished Gray, and what rings true in even this [film], his grandest and in some ways most complex movie, is that his movies peer inward, not outward—toward longing, frustration, uncertainty, and rattled expectations, rather than toward resolution,” praised Vanity Fair about what makes Gray’s film so special.

Director: Ridley ScottGenre: HorrorNotable cast: Sigourney Weaver, Tom Skerritt, John Hurt, Ian Holm, Veronica Cartwright, Yaphet Kotto, and Harry Dean StantonMPA rating: RRotten Tomatoes: 93%Metacritic: 89

As the franchise spins its ever divisive sequels and now television shows, it all goes back to the sheer paralyzing terror of Ridley Scott’s original Alien. It’s maybe Jaws’ only rival for the title of greatest monster movie of all time, following the spaceship Nostromo as its crew is besieged by a vicious space creature that picks each member off one by one–eventually meeting its match in Sigourney Weaver’s beloved Ellen Ripley. The film’s influential, Giger-inspired aesthetic arguably remains Scott’s biggest visual calling card and one of the high-water marks for his career, which the Academy will finally recognize this year when he receives his honorary Oscar.

Director: Mati DiopGenre: DramaNotable cast: Mame Bineta Sane, Amadou Mbow, and Ibrahima TraoréMPA rating: PG-13Rotten Tomatoes: 96%Metacritic: 85

Achingly romantic and fiercely original, Atlantics is a shape-shifting ghost story of sorts that defies simple categorization. Mati Diop’s first feature follows a young woman set to marry a man she does not love, while her lover flees their native Senegal by sea in search of work. Overnight, spirits begin to possess the townsfolk, seeking revenge against an exploitative corporation. Never less than captivating, the film features unforgettable visuals and an arresting blending of themes that make for one of the most daring Netflix originals—and signal Diop as one of the major breakthrough directors of the past decade.

Director: Paul FeigGenre: ComedyNotable cast: Kristen Wiig, Maya Rudolph, Melissa McCarthy, Rose Byrne, Chris O’Dowd, Ellie Kemper, Wendi McLendon-Covey, Jon Hamm, and Jill ClayburghMPA rating: RRotten Tomatoes: 89%Metacritic: 75

Though Vanity Fair once posited that Bridesmaids was the best comedy of 2011 (amidst stiff competition like Crazy, Stupid, Love), by now we can safely call it the best and funniest comedy of that decade. Kristen Wiig plays a depressed woman whose best friend (Maya Rudolph) gets engaged, kicking off a slow-motion meltdown. Melissa McCarthy got an Oscar nomination as an abrasive but tough-loving puppy-stealer, and an underrated Rose Byrne should have been nominated alongside her as Wiig’s rival, who masks her own insecurities with judgment. Its unforgettable sequences come relentlessly–from Jon Hamm’s sexual lunacy to the notorious food poisoning sequence to a colonial woman churning butter on the wing of an airplane–and it’s only gotten funnier in recent years when we have been served too few comedies.

Director: Kirsten JohnsonGenre: DocumentaryMPA rating: PG-13Rotten Tomatoes: 99%Metacritic: 89

Kirsten Johnson is always breaking the boundaries of what we think a documentary can be—never more so than in her deeply moving yet not-depressing tribute to her father on the eve of his death. Between candid interviews with her father, Dick, Johnson stages his death in various, surreally funny scenarios, such as a fall down the stairs or being struck by a rogue air conditioner. The director told Vanity Fair that making the film was an act of coping with her beloved parent’s dementia: “How can my father and I together confront the fact that he, who is irreplaceable, will disappear?” The effect is whimsical, profound, and restorative, making for the wildest study of love and loss in Netflix’s vast stable.

Director: Takashi YamazakiGenre: ActionNotable cast: Ryunosuke Kamiki, Minami Hamabe, Yuki Yamada, Munetaka Aoki, Hidetaka Yoshioka, and Sakura AndoMPA rating: PG-13Rotten Tomatoes: 99%Metacritic: 81

We called its newly arriving sequel, Godzilla Minus Zero, one of the most anticipated films of the fall season, and for good reason. Godzilla Minus One was a surprise stateside box office success in 2023, resulting in a wholly deserved visual-effects Oscar (the first for a non-English-language film). The franchise has had many different takes over the years, but Takashi Yamazaki’s telling of the monster tale is the perfect pairing of large-scale bravura visuals and grounded human emotion. This revamp centers around the end of WW II, using the aftereffects of war to tell a human story of survivor’s guilt amidst all the destruction caused by the world’s most iconic movie monster.

Director: Alice RohrwacherGenre: DramaNotable cast: Adriano Tardiolo, Sergi López, and Alba RohrwacherMPA rating: PG-13Rotten Tomatoes: 91%Metacritic: 87

Director Alice Rohrwacher earned a legion of new fans from 2023’s Josh O’Conner–led La Chimera—but prior to that masterpiece, she made another with this idiosyncratic fable. Another foray into symbolic magical realism, the story of frozen-in-time sweetheart farmhand Lazzaro is used by Rohwacher to weave a magical examination of class and exploitation. Around its midpoint, the film takes a daring leap that recontextualizes everything we have seen as more than meets the eye, setting the tender Lazzaro on a course for tragedy to come. It’s one of Rohrwacher’s bespoke visual wonders that seem to commune with some kind of cinematic deity, casting a spell that lingers long after viewing.

Director: Michael MannGenre: CrimeNotable cast: [Deep breath] Al Pacino, Robert De Niro, Val Kilmer, Tom Sizemore, Amy Brenneman, Natalie Portman, Ashley Judd, Dennis Haysbert, Mykelti Williamson, Jon Voight, Wes Studi, and at least a dozen moreMPA rating: RRotten Tomatoes: 84%Metacritic: 76

Audiences were initially slightly underwhelmed by Heat in 1995—while they were promised a fireworks showdown between Al Pacino and Robert De Niro for the first time onscreen, director Michael Mann gave them something more introspective and psychological. Heat is instead something of an existential heist epic that took a few years for folks to really appreciate. Now considered Mann’s defining masterpiece, the film has earned a devoted, die-hard cult following. Thirty years on, this crime thriller is finally appreciated for being more than an action film—its sensory precision is as captivating as its brooding, masculine psychology.

Director: Azazel JacobsGenre: DramaNotable cast: Carrie Coon, Natasha Lyonne, Elizabeth Olsen, Jovan Adepo, and Jay O. SandersMPA rating: RRotten Tomatoes: 98%Metacritic: 83

Carrie Coon, Natasha Lyonne, and Elizabeth Olsen give three revelatory performances as diametrically opposed estranged siblings—the dictator, the hippie, and the burnout, respectively. They cram together in a tiny New York City apartment as they await the death of their father. They battle over groceries, monologue about the Grateful Dead, and try (and fail) to keep their many past resentments at bay. Sure, His Three Daughters is at times a painful watch, brimming with claustrophobic tension and biting wit. But writer-director Azazel Jacobs gives this chamber piece on death and family a light touch, lifting it off into something unexpected in its emotional depth and making for one of 2024’s best films.

Director: Brett HaleyGenre: RomanceNotable cast: Blythe Danner, Sam Elliott, Martin Starr, June Squibb, Mary Kay Place, Rhea Perlman, and Malin AkermanMPA rating: PG-13Rotten Tomatoes: 93%Metacritic: 75

Featuring one of the standout performances of Blythe Danner’s career, I’ll See You in My Dreams was a bittersweet, small-scale hit in 2015. Danner stars as a widowed woman who gets a new lease on life when she tiptoes back into dating, resulting in a love affair with a dashing Sam Elliott. It’s a delicate and memorable tale of aging—not only due to Danner’s incredible performance, but also because of its surprising musical edge (Danner sings again onscreen) and delightful trio of actors as Danner’s hilarious friends: June Squibb, Mary Kay Place, and Rhea Perlman. Elliott is also a remarkable romantic star here, and this film (paired with another strong performance in Grandma the same year) helped kick off a career resurgence for the star that culminated in his first Oscar nomination in 2019 for A Star Is Born.

Director: Martin ScorseseGenre: CrimeNotable cast: Robert De Niro, Al Pacino, Joe Pesci, Harvey Keitel, Bobby Cannavale, Ray Romano, Stephen Graham, Jesse Plemons, Jack Huston, Marin Ireland, and Anna PaquinMPA rating: RRotten Tomatoes: 95%Metacritic: 94

Martin Scorsese’s epic fable on legacy, loyalty, and unions follows the life of Mob hit man Frank Sheeran, played by Robert De Niro. As Vanity Fair put it, “There’s something crucial about sitting with the same actors for so long. It communicates the weight and ravages of time more keenly than if the actors had been swapped out halfway through.” Much was made of the de-aging visual effects that allowed De Niro to play the character at all stages of Sheeran’s life, as was about Netflix’s refusal to play ball with the theater chains for the film’s limited theatrical release. Now that all that dust has settled, what remains is one of the defining masterpieces of this late stage of Scorsese’s career, one with a lot to say about where to place your loyalties and priorities in life.

Director: David LeanGenre: Epic dramaNotable cast: Peter O’Toole, Omar Sharif, Alec Guinness, Anthony Quinn, Jack Hawkins, Claude Rains, and José FerrerMPA rating: PGRotten Tomatoes: 93%Metacritic: 100

Netflix is not prone to offering many films from before the 1990s, so when a landmark work is on the platform, attention must be paid. David Lean’s masterpiece is widely beloved for good reason: In telling of the WW I exploits of T.E. Lawrence, Lean crafted a vivid cinematic language that would be emulated by the best directors for generations to come. Indelibly tied to the legacy of its star, Peter O’Toole, the film remains the gold standard for war epics and biopics about complicated historical figures alike. Though the film has often been trimmed and abbreviated in various releases over its lifetime, Netflix is streaming the “restored version,” which offers the film in full three-hour-and-47-minute splendor—the trick is not minding that it’s long!

Director: Rian JohnsonGenre: Sci-fiNotable cast: Joseph Gordon-Levitt, Bruce Willis, Emily Blunt, Jeff Daniels, Paul Dano, Garret Dillahunt, Noah Segan, and Piper PeraboMPA rating: RRotten Tomatoes: 93%Metacritic: 84

Rian Johnson may have spent the last decade in the realm of multiple franchises, between Benoit Blanc and Star Wars, but his first hit came with 2012’s heady sci-fi film Looper. The film casts Joseph Gordon-Levitt as a futuristic assassin tasked with killing his older self (played to career-topping brilliance by Bruce Willis) via time travel. As you might expect, nothing goes to plan. But this is just the beginning of Johnson’s unexpected twists, including an incredible performance from Emily Blunt that showed off for the first time her ability to balance action-star bravado with emotionally affecting pathos. Even after Johnson’s head-spinning, big-budget success, this is maybe the biggest benchmark for his ability to deliver something that’s several steps ahead of the audience.

Director: Todd HaynesGenre: DramaNotable cast: Natalie Portman, Julianne Moore, Charles MeltonMPA rating: RRotten Tomatoes: 91%Metacritic: 86

Todd Haynes’s darkly comedic, sleazy satire stars Natalie Portman as an actor who becomes obsessed with the real-life figure she is about to play: a former teacher (Julianne Moore) who became a tabloid fixture for her sexual relationship with a teenage student (Charles Melton). As much as the film skewers tabloid culture (it’s loosely inspired by Mary Kay Letourneau), it’s also a darkly comedic look at performance and the lies we tell ourselves. In calling it one of the best films of 2023, Vanity Fair wrote, “May December could probably be endlessly unpacked, so varied are its tones and textures and piercing insights.”

Director: Christopher NolanGenre: Noir thrillerNotable cast: Guy Pearce, Carrie-Anne Moss, Joe Pantoliano, Stephen Tobolowsky, and Harriet Sansom HarrisMPA rating: RRotten Tomatoes: 94%Metacritic: 83

Who? Christopher Nolan? Oh yeah, heard of him! As the world-adored director takes audiences back to early civilization this summer with The Odyssey, Netflix takes you back to his early career with the neo-noir dazzler Memento. His sophomore feature, the film made major waves by telling its grimy vigilante tale from the beginning and the end, working both backward and forward until it all meets in the middle for a shocking twist. The creative editing trick put Nolan’s name on the map, propelling him from indie darling to must-have director for the studios, and the rest is history. If you haven’t caught up to the film, it’s proof that Nolan has always been a master of mixing up timelines in order to blow our minds.

Director: Barry JenkinsGenre: DramaNotable cast: Mahershala Ali, Trevante Rhodes, Naomie Harris, André Holland, Ashton Sanders, Jharrel Jerome, and Janelle MonáeMPA rating: RRotten Tomatoes: 98%Metacritic: 99

Celebrating its 10th anniversary this year, Barry Jenkins’s landmark Black queer drama Moonlight follows the coming-of-age and queer acceptance of Chiron over three chapters of his life. Through childhood, teen years, and early adulthood, we see Chiron taken under the wing of a drug dealer (an immaculate and Oscar-winning Mahershala Ali) and face the addiction battles of his mother (Naomie Harris) and the inner demons of masculinity and homosexuality. “Jenkins has made a breathtaking film, one with political urgency and a deep, compassionate humanity,” said Vanity Fair in its review upon the film’s festival debut, “Moonlight is timely and timeless, a study in limits that casts its gaze up toward something transcendent.” While we remember the fraught circumstances of its best picture win at the Academy Awards, the film remains one of the key masterpieces of this generation of filmmakers.

Director: Paul Thomas AndersonGenre: RomanceNotable cast: Daniel Day-Lewis, Vicky Krieps, Lesley Manville, and Harriet Sansom HarrisMPA rating: RRotten Tomatoes: 91%Metacritic: 90Now that Paul Thomas Anderson has won his Oscar(s) with last year’s One Battle After Another, we can rest easy knowing that his eccentric filmography has finally been recognized. But his most atypical film is perhaps 2017’s Phantom Thread, a romance that’s swoony and caustic in equal measure. Demanding diva couturier Reynolds Woodcock (Daniel Day-Lewis, in what was then supposed to be his final performance) falls for straight-shooting waitress Alma (Vicky Krieps, in her breakout role), resulting in a marriage that by turns fuels Reynolds’s creative inspiration and has them hilariously at each other’s throats. In true PTA fashion, any tenderness is never without its sharp corners, but it’s nevertheless his most sumptuous movie.

Director: Jane CampionGenre: DramaNotable cast: Benedict Cumberbatch, Kirsten Dunst, Jesse Plemons, Kodi Smit-McPhee, Thomasin McKenzie, Keith Carradine, and Frances ConroyMPA rating: RRotten Tomatoes: 94%Metacritic: 89

Jane Campion’s big-screen return earned high praise for its psychologically intense study of masculinity in the American West. It’s never quite the movie you expect: In calling it one of 2021’s best films, Vanity Fair critic Richard Lawson said this “stately and mysterious film isn’t quite a character-study drama, not quite a thriller, and not really a Western. It is an elusive and mesmerizing thing unto itself.” An all-consuming visual experience, The Power of the Dog features top-to-bottom outstanding performances from its cast, including Benedict Cumberbatch as one of the most terrifying movie villains in recent memory. It’s not just one of the best movies on Netflix right now; it’s one of the best movies ever.

Director: Tamara JenkinsGenre: ComedyNotable cast: Paul Giamatti, Kathryn Hahn, Molly Shannon, John Carroll Lynch, Kayli Carter, and Denis O’HareMPA rating: RRotten Tomatoes: 94%Metacritic: 83

In one of Netflix’s best movies and most overlooked masterworks, Kathryn Hahn and Paul Giamatti star as an infertile New York City couple who, after several unsuccessful attempts at fertility treatment, consider surrogacy with their wayward niece (Kayli Carter). It’s only the third entry in writer-director Tamara Jenkins’s (flawless) filmography, but it’s maybe the best showcase of her bittersweet comic pathos yet: There’s nude ranting, people shoving their feet in their mouths, and bits about overly specific lox portions. If the film’s final shot doesn’t heal whatever ails you, please consult your doctor. All that, plus a powder keg, career-best performance from Hahn–before she would become a household name with her MCU debut as Agatha in WandaVision–makes this essential viewing.

Director: Alfred HitchcockGenre: ThrillerNotable cast: Anthony Perkins, Janet Leigh, Vera MilesMPA rating: RRotten Tomatoes: 97%Metacritic: 97

Cue the sharp violin strings because it’s Hitchcock-tober! This notorious game-changing thriller that made an entire generation afraid to go into the shower remains Hitchcock’s most iconic movie for a reason. It practically invented the plot twist, and it has two of them that movies have been borrowing from since. However, Psycho still reigns supreme for its narrative nerve and pervy ability to shock, led by Anthony Perkins’s tetchy, slowly unnerving performance as the killer, Norman Bates. Like the aforementioned Lawrence of Arabia, Psycho is currently one of the rare films from before the 1990s available on Netflix, so for folks looking more for film history (whether for a rewatch or a first watch), now is the time.

Director: Rob ReinerGenre: Coming-of-age dramaNotable cast: Wil Wheaton, River Phoenix, Corey Feldman, Jerry O’Connell, Kiefer Sutherland, and Richard DreyfussMPA rating: RRotten Tomatoes: 89%Metacritic: 76

After Rob Reiner’s tragic death, the love for his greatest cultural touchstones was reignited. Though he gave us such beloved films as When Harry Met Sally…, Misery, and The Princess Bride, many would still anoint Stand by Me as his career peak—not for nothing, Vanity Fair has called it the best Stephen King movie. Based on King’s novella The Body, it follows a group of young friends who go in search of a dead body in the wilderness, gently touching on themes of death and masculinity that bruise us in much the same way that the experience does its young heroes. Forty years on, it’s still an enduring classic about the trials of growing up—and for good reason.

Director: Yance FordGenre: DocumentaryMPA rating: UnratedRotten Tomatoes: 100%Metacritic: 86

Ford tackles his own family history and the injustice of the legal system with this nonfiction stunner, a recounting of his brother’s 1992 murder and how the killer went free. Where other documentaries navel-gaze at the details of a murder, Strong Island is more interested in the aftermath of the crime—how his brother’s killer going free shaped both his identity and his family’s. Ford approaches such personal subject matter with a sober and unflinching formal rigor, including some audacious moments where he directly addresses the camera. The result is a film that’s something of an antidote to the rise of the exploitative true crime documentary, a deeply intimate and searingly introspective portrait of a still-grieving family denied justice.

Director: Sarah PolleyGenre: DramaNotable cast: Michelle Williams, Seth Rogen, Luke Kirby, and Sarah SilvermanMPA rating: RRotten Tomatoes: 79%Metacritic: 68

Sarah Polley is one of the greatest living multihyphenates (she’s acted and written books with equal brilliance!), but her directorial output has proven to be the most singular part of her career. Between giving Julie Christie a career resurgence with Away From Her and winning an Oscar for Women Talking, Polley made a more divisive film in the infidelity character study Take This Waltz. The sexy, frankly observed film stars Michelle Williams as a young woman who, despite her idyllic life, finds herself surprisingly adrift as she approaches 30, leading her to drift away from her all-too-perfect husband, played by Seth Rogen (before he would bring Polley out of acting retirement for The Studio). The movie makes some uncomfortable, perhaps because it’s unflinchingly honest (and nonjudgmental) about the displacement we can feel at a certain stage of our life.

Director: Todd FieldGenre: DramaNotable cast: Cate Blanchett, Nina Hoss, Noémie Merlant, Adam Gopnik, and Mark StrongMPA rating: RRotten Tomatoes: 91%Metacritic: 93

After a 16-year absence, director Todd Field returned with Tár, a story of ego and disgrace for a fallen conductor, played with towering conviction by (a maybe career best) Cate Blanchett. “Field’s film is enormous, contemplating the end of idolism in all its loss and revolution,” raved Vanity Fair. “Tár bracingly responds to the dawning of a new era and reintroduces the world to a filmmaker who seems only better for having taken some time, a very long time indeed, to listen and to learn.” What’s immediately apparent is the precision with which Field takes to this dissection of a post-#MeToo downfall of a powerful person who has done bad things (with the film told entirely from their myopic perspective), but a rewatch reveals it’s also ruefully funny as well.
