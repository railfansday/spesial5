Nets topple Bucks in NBA Cup clash to snap five-game skid

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The teams with the two worst records in the Eastern Conference faced each other Wednesday night in Milwaukee, but no one needed to accuse either the Nets or the Bucks of tanking in this one.

Neither team can win the draft lottery after the season because both franchises essentially traded their respective 2027 first-round picks years ago, but the Nets at least halted their five-game losing streak with a defensively improved 104-97 victory in an NBA Cup game at Fiserv Forum.

"Just do it again. Every time we have a good performance where we fight and we compete, we follow it up with some bulls–t, with some bad ones," Noah Clowney told the YES Network after the game. "So we're trying to stack it this time and keep two or three good ones in a row."

Julius Randle netted a team-high 28 points as the Nets (4-13) posted their first NBA Cup victory after losses earlier in the season against the Pistons and the Magic.

Julius Randle, who scored a team-high 28 points, shoots a floater during the Nets' 104-97 win over the Bucks on Nov. 25, 2026, in Milwaukee. Jeff Hanisch-Imagn Images

Michael Porter Jr. was held to 12 points on 4-for-14 shooting with 11 rebounds to follow another quiet scoring night (11) Monday in Minnesota.

Egor Dëmin had an active game with 15 points and a plus-15 rating in 25 minutes off the bench.

Clowney nailed three 3-pointers among his 17 points, and Day'Ron Sharpe contributed 11 points, 14 boards and five assists for the Nets.

Tyler Herro, who had a career-best 50-point game Saturday against the 76ers, scored 21 of his 28 points in the second half for the Bucks (2-17).

"We've lost a few in a row, so we've gotta try to get a win no matter who we're playing against," Porter told YES before the game. "They're an NBA team. They are at the bottom of the standings right now, but so are we, so it's gonna be a dogfight, and we gotta try to get out of here with a win."

The NBA instituted rule changes in the offseason to discourage tanking for draft position, essentially penalizing the teams with the worst three records in the league with lower odds to secure the No. 1 overall pick in the lottery.

The Nets' 2027 first-round pick is controlled by the Rockets from an unprotected pick swap as part of Brooklyn's deal to acquire James Harden in January 2021.

The Bucks, who dealt away superstar forward Giannis Antetokounmpo to the Heat over the summer, also had committed to a pick swap involving their 2027 first-rounder when they acquired guard Jrue Holiday from New Orleans in 2020.

Noah Clowney, who scored 17 points off the bench, puts up a shot during the second quarter of the Nets' road win over the Bucks. Jeff Hanisch-Imagn Images

The Nets had allowed at least 70 points in the first half four times during their five-game skid, but this slow start was more due to offensive inefficiency.

Brooklyn missed five of its first six shots and spotted the Bucks a 9-2 lead in the first three minutes.

But Porter knocked down two straight 3s as part of a 15-1 run en route to a 28-24 edge through 12 minutes.

A 3-pointer by Clowney and a jumper by Randle extended the cushion to 10 early in the second. The Nets led by as many as 13 before the Bucks closed within 51-47 before intermission.

"We have to close quarters better," Randle, who had 16 in the half, told YES.

Rookie point guard Mikel Brown Jr. scored all six of his points in the second half after posting 17 in his first NBA start Monday in Minnesota.

Brown finally recorded his first two buckets after seven misses to push Brooklyn back ahead before two late dunks by Sharpe and a traditional 3-point play by Dëmin helped the Nets carry a 77-71 edge into the final quarter.

"It all came from the defensive side. I was more locked in, and everything else came to me naturally," Dëmin said.

Day'Ron Sharpe, who scored 11 points and grabbed 14 rebounds, slams home a dunk during the Nets' win over the Bucks in Milwaukee. Jeff Hanisch-Imagn Images

Keon Ellis (plus-14) and Grant Nelson provided some defensive energy off the bench to open the fourth, and Porter converted an and-1 to stretch the lead back to 11 with 10:38 remaining.

"Overall, proud of the effort. Our guys played hard," Nets coach Jordi Fernández said. "Guys were asking for subs, and that tells me that everybody did everything possible.

"That's how it's supposed to be … and that's who we want to be. Now let's see if we can play this hard two games in a row."

Moritz Wagner didn't play in the second half due to what the Nets said was left knee soreness.
