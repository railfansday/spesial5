Ranking 8 potential trade targets for the Phoenix Suns

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

If you're a Suns fan, you've probably noticed all the trade rumors surrounding the team since their disastrous 1-5 start to the season. You also probably noticed that they're getting absolutely annihilated by the playoff-caliber teams that they play, most recently the Lakers and the Spurs.

We knew going into the season that the team lacked a viable point guard other than Collin Gillespie, and that the only true power forward on the team was Rasheer Fleming. Analysts point out that rebounding and three-point shooting were also likely to be an issue. The hope was that someone would step up and fill those roles.

Unfortunately, the guys that needed to step up and address those weaknesses have not, and probably cannot. Jalen Green has been a great teammate and obviously been trying to buy into Coach Ott's system, but it's obvious to all observers he's not able to fill the role of a point guard and is a terrible fit next to Booker. Collin Gillespie had a miserable start to the season, shooting only 34.1% from the field through the team's first 11 games. Rasheer Fleming has been exiled to the deep bench while looking slow and lost when he does play. Miles Bridges is a good rebounder…for a small forward…but struggles against the taller players he's expected to compete with on the boards.

There have been unconfirmed rumors of disgruntlement in the locker room over the lack of a point guard. When Dejounte Murray made a trade demand, rumors immediately swirled that the Suns were interested. Even John "The Trade Reaper" Gambodoro has admitted the Suns are likely going to be looking for defensive rebounders starting in late January.

Complicating matters for most rumors, though, is that the Suns do not have many assets to move. Most of their future draft picks have already been traded away until the early 20230's (ouch). Only a few players on the roster are trade-eligible now: chief among them Jalen Green, Ryan Dunn, Khaman Maluach, Koa Peat, and Miles Bridges. Mark Williams won't be trade-eligible until January 15, 2027. Dunn is playing exceptionally well, and Bridges is doing better than Brooks, Booker, and Green as a starter (also ouch), and trading him now would be a loss since they gave up an unprotected first for him.

So, all the Suns really have to offer, or are ready to offer, is Jalen Green, and he's an extremely distressed asset. Meaning whomever the Suns are likely to get back is also likely to be playing poorly on a big salary. Despite this, the list of rumored trade targets is long. I'll be taking a look at each: why the Suns would want them, the red flags, what it would take to get them at a minimum, and the overall plausibility that they land in Phoenix.

Or, put another way, plausibility is what the odds are that the Suns could put together an offer that another team would accept, and that other teams likely wouldn't outbid. It also takes into account whether the Suns would WANT to do it. Holden Sherman covered three players recently (Dejounte Murray, Tyler Herro, and Jrue Holiday). To spare you all redundancy, I'm only covering Murray (because he's the hot rumor du jour); the other two are too remote a possibility in my mind to be worth it.

Why The Suns Would Do This: Turner is a PF/C who anchored the Pacers' defense through a thrilling Finals run. He can stretch the floor, moves very well in space, and is switchable on defense. The spacing he provides would allow him to play alongside Ighodaro.

Red Flags: 30 years old already. Turner has been God-awful the past couple of years with the Milwaukee Bucks. Terrible rebounder for a 6'11", 250-pound player, and he looks out of shape, so he really doesn't help with that problem. Lots of questions about whether he's already cooked.

What it Would Take: Jalen Green for Turner and Ouasmane Dieng as salary filler and maybe a pick or two coming back to Phoenix.

Plausibility: Highly plausible if either Oso or Man-Man get hurt and will miss significant time before Mark Williams comes back. Otherwise, possible but not likely.

Why the Suns Would Do This: The Suns obviously need a ball handler to play alongside Booker. Years ago, Murray once put up 8.8 assists per game, so he used to be capable of running the point, and there aren't a lot of guys on the market that can claim that much.

Red Flags: Hoo-boy, are there a lot of these. Declining performance, Achilles tear in 2025, age, low shooting percentage, reputation for being a locker room cancer, off-putting social media behavior, and rumors that he is straight up cray-cray based on what may be a burner profile on social media.

What it Would Take: Green for Murray works straight up.

Plausibility: Almost nil unless something changes. Gambo says no interest. Suns will likely try moving Gillespie into the starting unit before resorting to this. The equation changes if New Orleans is desperate enough to dangle a first-round pick or two in front of the Suns to make their headache go away.

Why the Suns Would Do This: Reid is an earth-moving, wide-bodied PF/C who rebounds and shoots threes while averaging 17 ppg and 8.5 rpg. He's only 27 years old and would seemingly mesh well with the current roster construction, including the ability to play alongside Ighodaro.

Red Flags: Nothing wrong with Reid, but are the Suns really going to try to trade with the Hornets again?

What it Would Take: Reid for Green works straight up despite the salary differential due to Charlotte being below the cap threshold.

Plausibility: Low. Why would Charlotte do this unless they have something else coming back in the form of picks? Also, is the plan for Charlotte and the Suns to just keep swapping until they have each other's rosters?

Why the Suns Would Do It: Sabonis looks like a tremendous fit as a PF/C who is an elite rebounder and league-average three-point shooter, meaning you have to at least pay token respect to the danger of leaving him completely alone outside the arc.

Red Flags: Sabonis is a sub-par (but not awful) defender and an average-at-best scorer, but he's still better than either Maluach or Ighodaro. The biggest problems are his salary and what the Kings want back for him.

What it Would Take: Mark Williams and Jalen Green for Sabonis meet the salary requirements, but the Kings would likely want young guys (Maluach, Dunn, Peat) and/or draft picks.

Plausibility: Very low but not completely impossible. Gambo says the Suns aren't interested because of Sabonis's high salary, and the Kings are almost certainly looking for draft capital (that the Suns don't have) in return for a guy that has an elite skill coveted by contending teams. In other words, other teams can easily put together a more enticing package.

Why the Suns Would Do It: Randle is a legit 6'9", 250-pound power forward who can shoot the three a little bit, rebound, and pass the ball (5.0 apg). He's a bulldozer on the inside with a nasty streak. He'd be a great fit in a lot of ways given positional need and the fact that he does a lot of things the Suns lack.

Red Flags: Randle is almost 32 years old. His production has been declining for several years, particularly his rebounding, which is down to 6.5 per game. That said, it would probably come back up a lot on a team where no one else seems capable of grabbing the ball. He's also considered somewhat "selfish" as a player.

What it Would Take: Green for Randle would work straight up.

Plausibility: Low. Minnesota gave him away for basically nothing last year, which should tell you something. The Suns chose Miles-freaking-Bridges at a higher price than Randle, which should tell you something about the Suns' level of interest. The Nets would probably also like something back in addition to Jalen Green, but both players' value is dropping by the day.

Why the Suns Would Do It: Giddey is a top-tier passer and rebounder for a guard at 6'7" and 214 pounds. He's 24 with career averages of 14.5 ppg, 7.5 rpg, and 6.6 apg. Very good court vision with the size to exploit it as a PG. He's averaging 8.5 apg this year. He checks the blocks to help the Suns as a distributor who can initiate offense and improve the team's defensive rebounding. Potential to be a triple-double guy.

Red Flags: Giddey is a "meh" three-point shooter and defender. He can't really create his own shot, which would hamper his ability to pull teams away from Booker as a second option.

What it Would Take: Jalen Green for Giddey and Zach Collins as filler works. Given Chicago would likely want some sort of draft or young player compensation (Peat, Fleming, Dunn) in exchange, Giddey is clearly the more valuable player right now.

Plausibility: Doable, if the Suns were to put a pick or a young prospect (Fleming?) on the table. Mostly depends on whether Chicago is ready to move on from him, which has been rumored for a while.

Why the Suns Would Do It: Uhh, who wouldn't want Darius Garland? 26 years old, 2-time All-Star. He'll give you 20 ppg and 7 apg night in, night out. Good three-point shooter and a "classic" point guard who would slot in next to Booker seamlessly.

Red Flags: He's 6-1 and an average defender. But other than that, his game is solid, and there's nothing to suggest he's a problem in the clubhouse.

What it Would Take: Jalen Green and Williams plus draft capital, or Jalen Green and Khaman Maluach.

Plausibility: Roughly the same as me marrying Kim Kardashian. Which is to say, zero. If the Clippers decide to move him, the price tag is far more than the Suns can pay, or would be willing to. Again. Other teams can easily put together more attractive trade packages than the Suns for a high-demand asset.

Why the Suns Would Do It: Even at 33, the 6'10", 253-pound PF/C is still a potent defender and well above average player on offense. He's putting down 25 ppg and 10 rpg right now for the woeful Wizards. The Suns have lacked anyone remotely like this since the heyday of Amare Stoudemire. Deandre Ayton (of all people) had his best seasons with Booker and CP3: imagine how Davis would look with Book (and vice versa).

Red Flags: Anthony "Street Clothes" Davis is 33 years, 8 months old and played a grand total of 71 games over the preceding two seasons. He's got a huge contract, is on the decline, and will be looking for another maximum payday.

What it Would Take:  Jalen Green and Miles Bridges or Jalen Green, Khaman Maluach, and Mark Williams

Plausibility: Very close to zero. If Ishbia thought that the younger, healthier Sabonis was too expensive, I can't imagine much interest in Davis. Plus, going after an aging superstar smells too much like the KD and Beal trades.
