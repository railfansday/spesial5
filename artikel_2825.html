Is Cricket Australia re-opening investigation into Sandpapergate? CA announces new 'forum' as Usman Khawaja expresses concern

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Cricket Australia has set up a new forum linked to the 2018 Cape Town incident.

Usman Khawaja's book triggered the move, and several former colleagues have rejected his claims.

The board insists this is not a fresh inquiry, yet Khawaja still has doubts.

Cricket Australia has stepped back into one of the most uncomfortable chapters in its recent history, and the way it has done so has left many people asking what the board is really trying to achieve. The governing body announced on Wednesday that it will create a forum for those connected with the 2018 sandpaper incident in South Africa.

Interim chair David Boon and chief executive Todd Greenberg made the announcement at Bellerive Oval, a little more than an hour before the third ODI between Australia and England was due to begin.

The board had met the night before and agreed on the plan, which allows players and staff who were involved in the events of that tour to share their experiences.

The decision comes only days after Usman Khawaja made a series of allegations about the Cape Town incident in his autobiography, Sabr, which was published last week. His claims drew responses from captain Pat Cummins and others, and they have kept a subject that many in Australian cricket hoped was behind them in the headlines.

According to the board, the forum will be confidential, and it will be open to anyone who was present at the time, although nobody will be forced to take part. An independent person will be appointed in the coming days, along with the terms of reference, and CA will decide later how much of the process is made public.

Boon stressed that the aim is not to go back over the incident but to let those involved share their recollections as part of a push to move forward.

"Last night, the Cricket Australia Board met, and we reached a decision that we believe is in the best interests of the game," Boon said. "After careful discussion, we agreed to establish a forum that will allow players and staff involved in the events of South Africa in 2018 to share their experiences and their perspectives."

The interim chair then described how the long-running debate has affected the sport, which he said has become a distraction over time.

He added: "For almost a decade, the fallout from these events, or from those events, has remained part of the conversation around Australian cricket. It has been a difficult chapter for many people, and increasingly, the ongoing debate has become a distraction for our game. We believe it is time for a circuit breaker."

Boon repeated that the forum is not intended to revisit the past in a legal sense, and he closed with a call for unity.

"This forum is not about re-litigating the past at all. It's about creating an opportunity for those who wish to be heard to tell their stories in a respectful, safe environment, and for our sport to listen."

"Australian cricket is at its strongest when we're united, and we owe it to everyone who loves the game to make every reasonable effort to move forward together with understanding, respect, and perspective."

The short answer from the board is no. Greenberg confirmed that the original 2018 investigation will neither be reopened nor made public, with confidentiality given as the reason.

Greenberg said the game is hurting right now, and then spoke about the need to recover.

"It's not good for anyone. We need to find a path through, and we need some time to heal."

He also made clear what the new process is and is not meant to do, and he repeated that taking part is a personal choice.

He said: "As part of this process, and so that all parties who wish to can tell their version of events, we have set up now an independent forum. It's a chance to give everyone a voice in an independent and a safe setting. It's not a re-investigation. It's not designed to re-prosecute or to sanction."

"No one is compelled to participate. It is entirely up to each individual. It's important to remember we're talking about events that happened eight and a half years ago, and I hope everyone will respect this process."

Khawaja welcomed the idea of a safe space but told Kayo Sports that he still has questions about the forum. He wants the public to hear the full story, and he believes that is impossible unless every person involved speaks.

He said: "I have always wanted a safe space to chat… I also have some questions about Cricket Australia because the public wants to know what happened there. It's hard to get the whole truth unless everyone is speaking."

He then said he does not want to see anyone punished, and he raised a concern about what the board will do with the material.

He further asked: "I don't think there should be any sanctions… the board makes the decisions but what comes out at the end? I have a really good relationship with Todd Greenberg, and I respect what he's trying to do there.

"But at the end of the day if you're going to cherry-pick what is said in this report, the board can choose whatever they want to come in, or come out. Are we just going in a circle here? That's the thing I'm not sure about."

Warner, who was speaking alongside Khawaja, also ruled out any desire for further penalties. He pointed to what the players accepted at the time and to what he sees as the real purpose of the forum.

He said: "We put our hands up, we accepted what Cricket Australia handed out to us. It's not about penalties. It's about transparency and accountability - which Todd's acknowledged, on CA's behalf, about the culture at the time with the players and CA."

The board's intention is easy to understand, because nearly a decade of argument has simply worn people down. A confidential, voluntary forum offers a way for everyone to speak without the threat of sanctions, and that is a reasonable starting point.

However, the same features that make the forum safe may also limit its value. Khawaja has already pointed out the central weakness: CA will decide what is released at the end. If the public sees only a short summary, those who want answers are likely to treat it as a polished version of events, and the debate will carry on.

The voluntary nature of the process is a second problem. Cummins and Starc have strongly rejected Khawaja's version, and if they choose not to take part, the forum will hear one side of the dispute far more than the other. A discussion with missing voices is unlikely to produce the shared understanding Boon hopes for.

CA can improve its chances by publishing the terms of reference early, by naming a respected independent figure, and by being clear about what will be shared. The board has said it wants a circuit breaker, but a breaker only works if people trust it. That trust will depend on how openly the process is run.
