The Day After the Day After: Houston Texans stomp the Indianapolis Colts on Thursday Night

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The Textbook Texans' Sequence: If the Texans could script their ideal game sequence, look no further than what happened between the 11:55 and 4:19 marks of the 3rd Quarter. It started with Kai Kroeger, whose punting has been sporadic at best, uncorking a 56-yard punt that landed out of bounds at the IND 5. This put the Colts in bad field position, allowing the Texans' defense to go on the attack. Two plays yielded only 2 yards for the Colts. On 3rd and long, the Colts wisely thought to double Will Anderson Jr. However, Anderson soundly beat that double team, all but driving the tackle right into Jones, as Anderson turned that sack into a safety. On the subsequent possession, Houston used a Smith 32-yard kick return to start at their 43. From there, Houston used a 10-play (6 run/4 pass), 57-yard drive that took over 6 minutes off the clock and ended in a TD that put the Texans up 23. Strong special teams, dominant defense and a steady, scoring offense. When a team is 4-6, that usually means the ideal hasn't happened a lot.

The End of the Jones Era: The Texans' defense beating of the Colts was so severe, it reverberated beyond the gridiron. As the second half wore on, sideline cameras showed visible frustration between QB Daniel Jones and HC Shane Steichen, as the Colts proved unable to move the ball. After an 87-yard, 3-point 1st quarter, the Colts only managed 80 yards and no points over the next 3. On the Colts' final offensive possession, Jones gave way to Anthony Richardson. Might the Colts face a QB controversy for their next game?

Guess not. Not 18 hours after the final whistle in Houston, the Colts announced they cut Daniel Jones. The same QB who led them to an 8-2 start last season and whom was signed to a 2-year/$88M contract this past offseason. The same QB who the Colts did not want to ship to Minnesota or Baltimore before last week's trade deadline, who would have offered some draft compensation. Granted, the Colts' season is the road to nowhere, but this sort of move only amplifies the missteps the planning of the franchise. They do save some cap space, but this only amplifies matters.

3:54, 1nd Quarter, 2nd and 10, IND Ball @ the HOU 38; Daniel Jones pass intended for TE Tyler Warren incomplete (pass defended by LB Jake Hansen).

At this point in the game, the Colts led the Texans 3-0 and had just ended up with a rather silly but effective interception. Mainly on the strength of a 26-yard pass play from Jones to Quez Watkins, the Colts were again in scoring position in HOU territory. After a short incompletion on 1st and 10, Jones was again in a shotgun set, taking the snap. TE Warren, the Colts most dangerous receiver at this point, lined up on the offensive right side and was running an in-breaking route that would have netted at least a Colts' first down closer to the Houston red zone. However, the LB in coverage, Hansen, read the play and got his hand out to deflect the pass. This allowed Houston to force the Colts into an obvious pass play. After another incompletion, the Colts settled for a long FG, which they missed. Houston then scored on three of the next four possessions to take complete control of the game.

Had the Colts made that completion, maybe they only go up 6-0, but they would have taken greater control. Houston, already under immense pressure from the implosion in Cleveland, might have pressed too much, leading to even more mistakes. Likely this Colts' team wasn't winning on Thursday but had Jones thrown that pass to where Warren could get it for a good completion, momentum might have favored the Colts even more. For Houston, a team that seems to produce more bad luck for itself than good, they got some good fortune for once.

3: Stroud's consecutive game streak for a number of stats: CJ Stroud's done all of the following over the past three games:

Thrown for at least 3 TDs (uh, actually, really, really good)

Now, if Stroud and the Texans could spin that into at least a 3-game or greater win streak…that would be even more helpful.

4813: Days since the Houston Texans last won a game at "Reliant Stadium". This stat has the caveat that after 2013, the Texans renamed Reliant to NRG. However, this past offseason, the Texans switched it back to Reliant. The last win at Reliant when it was Reliant: Sept 15, 2013, when the Texans came back to beat the Titans 30-24. This also snapped an 11-game losing streak at "Reliant", as Houston lost the remaining 7 games in 2013 as well as the 1st 4 of the new "Reliant" era in 2026.

RB David Montgomery: Since his 60 yard/2 TD rushing performance in Week 1, he's only rushed for 78 yards and no TDs. This game was his first since Week 5. 26 carries for 107 yards (season highs for any Texans back in a game) and 3 receptions for 17 yards did quite nicely. How invaluable would this have been against the Browns in the second half? With a potentially treacherous road slate coming up, if Montgomery can run like this, Houston may yet have reason for hope.

DE Will Anderson, Jr.: 5 Tackles/2 Solo/1 sack/2 TFLs/4 QB Hits/1 safety. Even without Danielle Hunter, Anderson almost single-handedly destroyed the Colts' offense.

RG Ed Ingram/RT Braden Smith: Maybe it was only a Colts' squad playing out the string but consider: 83 of the Texans' season-high 167 yards rushing came going to the offensive right, with the average rush going for over 5 yards/play. While a couple of the plays had Blake Fisher in as the extra blocker, the right side of the line was mainly Ingram and Smith. With winter coming, keeping up that high standard of play is a must.

DE Chris Braswell: Gets elevated from the practice squad to the active roster with a chance to make something happen for a team that could use the help, and he gets injured on the opening kickoff. It won't make up for the injury, but we'll ship a game ball his way.

Houston's Kickoff Coverage: Not everything was perfect in this game for the Texans. For the Texans, they did manage to give up a lot of yards on kickoff returns. Even on the opening kickoff, which could have been downed for a touchback to the IND 35, Colts RB/KR Seth McGowan ran it back for 40 yards, giving the Colts the ball in prime field position, allowing them to get that opening FG. McGowan gashed Houston for 117 yards on 3 kickoff returns. That put a lot of pressure on a mercurial defense. However, the defense did respond. Once McGowan left due to injury, the numbers worked out a little better, but this season has not been the best on the coverage front.

Colts PK Spencer Shrader: A TDATDA Game Ball winner the last time these two met, it was the complete opposite this time. 1-of-3 on FGs, including a momentum-killing hook of a makable 56-yarder in the 1st quarter.

With this win, Houston moves to 4-6, alone in second place in the AFC South and two games (effectively three) games back of the 7-seed Browns in the AFC. Houston gets an extended break before a defacto playoff game next Sunday against the struggling Ravens at Reliant. Kickoff slated for noon CST and can be seen on CBS/Paramount+
