Miami Dolphins vs. Buffalo Bills: Three reasons why Miami will win, lose

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

This week has not been kind to the making of content for this post. The flow of hope for getting win from here on out is down to a trickle. Let's see if we can salvage any for this contest.

The Bills pass defense has been the weaker part of their defense this season. They had games against Joey Fagnano and Carson Wentz where they held them to under 200 combined yards. But they have allowed 250+ passing yards to just about everyone else. Last week, Kyle McCord made his debut against a weak Colts defense and played alright. He threw for 209 yards and got the RBs involved in the passing game. I don't expect McCord to light it up, but he has the opportunity for some success against a middle of the pack pass defense.

Despite being one of two 8-1 teams in the league, they have a turnover differential of zero. They have 14 takeaways and 14 giveaways. QB Josh Allen is playing well, particularly as a rusher, leading the team with 11 rushing TDs. He is in the middle of the pack for passing TDs. But he has 7 INTS and 4 fumbles on the season. James Cook has 4 fumbles this season. Turnovers are the great equalizer, and Miami is getting better at generating them. The Dolphins have generated at least one turnover in each of the last 5 games. All it takes is for one errant throw or fumbled ball to swing momentum and turn the tide.

I generally don't think divisional games can be trap games, but the odds for that are never zero. The Bills face the 7-2 Chiefs the following week. Then go on the road to face New England, Green Bay, before facing Chicago and Denver. Part of me thinks Buffalo will look at this game as a must win – the final "gimme game" before a slate of playoff bound opponents. Another part of me thinks they can be looking ahead to the Chiefs and might slip up. The 1-8 Dolphins led by Kyle McCord will be easy to overlook. But if the defense steps up just enough, they can make this a tougher out than expected.

Miami lost 3 of their top interior defensive linemen in the span of a week. Sieler, Phillips, and Biggers are out for the season. The Dolphins are stuck relying on Kenneth Grant and some guys. The current IDL on the roster are Grant, Keith Cooper Jr., and Jordan Jefferson. They have some bigger edge players like Eric Watts (6'5" 277) and Rodney McGraw (6'4", 273) that can perhaps move inside on some passing downs. They can also big the bigger edge players in run defense. Miami also signed Matthew Butler to the practice squad. Butler was with the Dolphins in 2025 and in camp this offseason before being cut. He adds some depth and scheme familiarity. But the Bills have one of the better offensive lines, ranked 3rd in pass block win rate and 1st in run block win rate. The Bills are the 3rd in rushing attempts, 2nd in rushing yards, 6th in yards per attempt, and 1st in rushing TDs. The Dolphins run defense had a solid game against Jahmyr Gibbs and the Lions, but had their worst outing against the Colts, allowing 172 yards, the most in any game this season. Some good news is that Grant and Cooper both had solid games against the Colts. But even if they play out of their minds this week, it is still a horrible matchup for the Dolphins.

The Bills are at the top in terms of winning pass and run blocks. On the defensive side, they are less so. They are currently 29th in pass rush win rate and 23rd in run stop win rate. That said, the Dolphins' run offense is stagnating. They haven't rushed for more than 78 yards in any of the last 3 games. Having an injured Malik Willis contributed to that. However, after an early breakout for Ollie Gordon II, he has been limited to under 4 YPC in the last 3 games. Part of that for last week could be having a backup QB as the starter – defenses will worry more about the run. But part of that is the offensive line. The Lions and Pats were better run defenses. The Colts were not – one of the worst in the league and the Dolphins still couldn't get going on the ground The offensive line has to take their share of that blame. Starting center Aaron Brewer missed last week in concussion protocol and did not practice Wednesday. He returned Thursday, but hasn't cleared protocol just yet. His backup, Andrew Meyer, was bad against a good-not-great Colts front. Meyer was also a problem with the batted balls for McCord that cost Miami some big plays, including a potential TD. RG Jonah Savaiinaea looked like an improved player early on but has been moving in the opposite direction.

In the passing game, the Bills aren't high at the win rate, but they are 4th in the league in pressure percentage at 28%. They are tied with 4 other teams at 17th for sacks with 21. They only have 2 players with more than 1.5 sacks on the season. BUT… they have one of the league's top rushers in Gregory Rousseau, who is tied for 4th with 8 sacks on the season. He leads the league in pressures with 37. He is NOT in the top 22 in pass rush win rate, which would lead me to believe he is getting pressures and sacks due to volume – only 4 other teams have as many or more pass attempts against them than the Bills. This leads back to Miami's OL and Austin Jackson. He has been a liability against every top edge rusher he has faced; Aidan Hutchinson took his lunch money 2 weeks ago. Now he has to face Rousseau, who may not win as early and as often as Hutchinson but still gets to the QB. If the Dolphins can't keep the Bills defense honest by running the ball and/or get too far behind, the Bills pass rush is very likely to heat up McCord.

Do you remember the last time Miami won in Buffalo? I certainly do. It was the 2016 season where the Dolphins snuck out an overtime win thanks to Jay Ajayi rushing for 206 yards on 32 carries and some heroic kicks made by Andrew Franks. Outside of that game, the Dolphins haven't had much success on the road against Buffalo. Since 2000, the Dolphins have played in Buffalo 27 times including the playoffs and have gotten 7 wins. They haven't beaten Josh Allen in Buffalo, but this isn't just about the Allen-led Bills. From 2000 until 2019, when Allen took over as the full-time starter, the Bills had 3 winning seasons and they were 9-7 seasons. They had three 8-8 seasons. Their record was 127-177 and the average season was 6.7 wins and 9.3 losses. Allen has started 9 games against the Dolphins in Buffalo, so that's still 7 wins in the other 18 attempts. When they DID win, the average record for Buffalo was 6-10. The average margin of victory in those games was 10.7, and it's been 7 or less in the last 3 times.

So even when Buffalo was mid, the Dolphins still couldn't win there. They absolutely haven't won there any time Buffalo has finished with a winning record. The last time the Dolphins won in Buffalo in the same season Buffalo finished with a winning record was 1996. This week, the Dolphins take a talent deficient team that is now weaker due to injuries and starting a backup QB in his second ever NFL game on the road to a place they don't win at, even when the opponent is bad. Now… the last 2 times Miami has marched into Buffalo with a backup QB starting the game, they have a record of 1-1. They got their last win with Matt Moore starting for Ryan Tannehill. Skylar Thompson went into Buffalo in the playoffs and fell just short, losing 34-31. If there is ANY hope in this regard, it's that recent history shows Miami backups making the game a tougher out than Buffalo expected.

The injuries, the roster, the season outlook… this game is more likely to look like the week 2 bloodbath against the 49ers than the recent close games. Buffalo is churning along with the top seed in the AFC in mind. The Dolphins are in the middle of a rebuild. Perhaps Miami can catch lightning in a bottle and pull out a big upset. But I have a feeling most fans will be tuned out before halftime.
