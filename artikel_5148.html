Bitcoin Price Could Crash to $39K Despite Slowing ETF Outflows: Is Worst Yet to Come?

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

ETF outflows are slowing, but historical cycles and the 0.786 Fibonacci level point to a potential BTC bottom between $39,000 and $44,000.

In Bitcoin news this week, the selling from US spot Bitcoin ETFs is easing, but the price has not followed. On Tuesday, November 24, the funds lost a net $232 million. That was less than half of Monday’s $570 million.

It was still the sixth day of outflows in a row. The funds have shed about $2.6 billion since November 17 and nearly $5 billion this month, led by BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT).

Bitcoin spot ETFs have recorded SIX consecutive trading sessions of net outflows, with approximately 41,540 BTC withdrawn over that period.

The latest session saw another 3,780 BTC leave the funds, bringing further pressure on institutional demand.

Bitcoin keeps sliding anyway. It trades near $58,900 on Wednesday, down 53% from its October 2025 record of $126,272. On Saturday, it touched $55,081, its lowest price in more than two years.

So if the funds are selling less, where could Bitcoin’s bear market end? We checked where Bitcoin bottomed in every past cycle, using Fibonacci retracement levels, ratios traders use to measure how much of a rally gets erased.

SoSoValue’s ETF flow figures, as reported this week, show the pressure easing:

Even after the selling, the funds still hold about $52 billion of net inflows since launch. That is roughly 83% of their October 2025 peak, so most ETF buyers have not left.

For Bitcoin news readers, slower outflows don’t mean buyers are back, though. They mean less forced selling from one big source. For the price to turn, the flows need to flip to inflows.

Fibonacci retracements measure how much of a rally a market gives back. Traders watch a handful of ratios, mainly 0.618 and 0.786, as places where a drop may stop.

We measured each Bitcoin cycle on Bitstamp’s daily price history since 2011. Each runs from one bear-market bottom to the next record high. We then checked how much of that move was erased by the next bottom.

That is the less-reported Bitcoin news: each bear market stopped a little earlier than the last, by three to four points each time. If that trend holds, the next bottom would land near the 0.786 Fibonacci level, a 78.6% retracement.

This cycle runs from the 2022 low of $15,479 to the $126,272 record. On that range, 0.786 sits at about $39,200. So far, Bitcoin has given back 64.3%. Saturday’s low already cut through the 0.618 level at $57,800.

A log scale treats a doubling the same at any price. On that scale, past bottoms came at shallower ratios, rising from 33% to 48%. The 0.5 level on that scale for this cycle is about $44,200. Both methods point to a $39,000 to $44,000 zone.
