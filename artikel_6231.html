I have a personal preference for Lyft, as regular readers know. One of the things that I really like is that they have the best rewards in rideshare. But I didn’t realize things get even more lucrative for business travelers using a Lyft business profile. They have benefits, like:

keeping all of your regular rewards to use on personal or business rides. earning with Lyft’s airline or hotel partners goes up significantly for eligible business travelers. and pocketing 6% – 8% back in Lyft Credits on business profile rides, to use on your own rides later. That’s why it makes sense to sign up for a business account. Bonus, it lets you separate personal and business rides, which makes expense management easier (and you won’t mistakenly apply personal credits to reimbursable trips as I sometimes do by accident). Earn Lyft Credits On Top of Travel Rewards The original idea of a frequent flyer program was that you’d earn rewards on business travel. Your employer pays for a ticket, you benefit personally with your miles. That kept you loyal. This is really the same idea that the airlines pioneered about 45 years ago. When you ride for work, you can get Lyft Credits to use on your own trips later – 6% for standard rides taken with your business profile and 8% for premium, airport, and scheduled business profile rides. And you can do this even while earning with your preferred travel partner, and you earn those points or miles faster than you do with a personal account (where I’ve already been happy with the points-earning). How To Join I already use Lyft for business travel, so enrolling is a no-brainer since it’s fast and free and you accelerate your earnings. This also lets you bill a business card or directly to your employer and it integrates with popular expense tools. Lyft estimates that over half of business travelers forget to take advantage of automatic company expensing, leading to missed rewards and lack of visibility for organizations. Can We Just Appreciate Rideshare For A Moment? For most of my life there was no such thing as rideshare. For a brief time in Austin where I live, the city government chased it away, and I actually had to re-learn how to get to the airport. Now I pull up my Lyft app and I’m frustrated if I see that my driver is six minutes away. Six minutes. That’s my complaint. A stranger is coming to my exact location, in their personal car, because I touched glass. That’s sorcery with seatbelts. It used to be that you had to stand on the curb with your arm out like the Statue of Liberty, just waving at traffic. “Please! Someone! I have money and a destination!” And the taxis would just drive by. Sometimes they’d slow down just enough to let you experience hope before crushing it. You’d make eye contact with the driver. He’d look at you, look through you, and drive right past. And if it rained? You were done. Some cab would blast through a puddle and soak you like you’re Carrie Bradshaw in Sex and the City. Now you press a button and a tiny map shows you a little car cartoon coming toward you. You get in, you get out. Start to finish, it’s frictionless. And to top it off, you even get rewarded for it. Just by using a business profile, you can earn Lyft Credits and extra travel points or miles on rides your company is already paying for. George Clooney said it best in Up In The Air, “I don’t spend a nickel if I can help it unless it somehow benefits my mileage account.” Your company is spending the nickel. You might as well get the miles. And to top it off, you even get rewarded for it. Just by using a business profile, you can earn Lyft Credits and extra travel points or miles on rides your company is already paying for.
