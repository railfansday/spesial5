Aonuma Calls the Water Temple His 'Overdue Homework' for Ocarina of Time Remake

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Daniel is a lifelong gamer with a love for various genres and gameplay styles who enjoys exploring games and playing from various angles.

With the release of the full remake of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time for the Nintendo Switch 2 later this week, old-school fans have been once again reminiscing on their many fond and not-so-fond memories from the N64 original. Whenever the topic of Ocarina of Time comes up, it’s often a safe bet that, sooner or later, things will turn to the infamous Water Temple, one of the elemental-themed dungeons in the game’s back half.

The Water Temple has long held a reputation for being one of the most legendarily frustrating dungeons in the Zelda franchise. Admittedly, a lot of the discourse is hyperbolic, based on memories of collective childhood, but the dungeon also definitely had some irritating quirks, from its confusing layout to the way it forced you to use your items and menus. If you didn’t have the little jingle that played every time you opened the pause menu permanently seared into your head before that dungeon, you sure did afterward.

The first gameplay trailer for The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake has confirmed that it has added a beloved feature from the 3DS version.

The designers of the Ocarina of Time remake are very aware of the long-standing vibe toward the Water Temple, as they discussed in an Ask the Developer feature on Nintendo’s official blog. Eiji Aonuma, the director and dungeon designer of the original game, now a senior officer and producer on the remake, spoke on how he created the original dungeon and how his perspective has changed since, with the remake’s director, Hokwai Ng, discussing how they’re making improvements without changing the fundamental vibe of the dungeon.

As part of the interview, Aonuma, Ng, and other members of the development team were asked how they approached the development of dungeons in the Ocarina of Time remake, particularly in relation to the original.

With a laugh, Aonuma said, “I had some overdue homework: I wanted to do something about the Water Temple I created 28 years ago.” He said that, back in the days of the N64, there were very few games that allowed you to do much of anything in an underwater setting. Therefore, if he was going to make an underwater dungeon, he wanted it to be distinct, something that brings across what could make being in such an environment fun. Aonuma is actually a hobbyist freediver, so he’s very acquainted with the distinct sensations of being completely underwater.

However, he concedes that the biggest problem with the Water Temple was one of navigation. “With the dungeon’s complex structure and a lot of item toggling involved, you often had to open the menu screen repeatedly, making it hard to keep track of where you were and which way you were facing.”

A look at the Water Temple from The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time and how it should be remade with the same design.

Aonuma explained that, given the infamy that the Water Temple has accrued due to its difficulty, he had the dev team make some adjustments, not necessarily to make it flat-out easier, but to make it more playable and user-friendly.

Ng chimed in here to explain the team’s perspective, noting that most of the dungeon’s basic mechanisms have been preserved, but the layout has been slightly shifted in places to improve the overall signposting and flow of the dungeon. Additionally, some of the walls of the temple have been changed to a distinct lattice pattern, allowing you to see bits of the temple beyond the current room and get a better idea of where you are in relation to everything else, which will hopefully keep players from getting lost. Finally, item toggling has been streamlined, so you don’t need to keep opening the menu to equip and unequip your Iron Boots.

At the end of the interview, Ng added that he hopes the slight differences between the remake and the original will spark some inter-generational conversation. He imagined a father playing the game with his child and saying, “You know, your Dad got stuck in the Water Temple plenty of times back in the day.”

Aonuma laughed and responded, “Then kids playing this version might say, “Come on, it’s not that hard.” Poor old Dad might end up feeling deflated.”
