Live service games have it rough in the current gaming industry, forced to fight tooth and nail against half a dozen well-established titles that have all but consumed the worthwhile player base. Anyone new on the block needs to really justify their airtime, and for many, it’s simply not worth the hassle of getting torn apart by consumers.

Some, like Capcom’s Exoprimal, try to throw everything at the wall to see what sticks with players, and I can’t lie, mech suits vs dinosaurs was a compelling first pitch. After three solid years of support, however, Exoprimal is facing End of Service, just announced by Capcom.

Marked for death after September 2027, players have less than a year to enjoy their Jurassic jungle gym.

Exoprimal was an odd title, announced back in 2022 and released in July of 2023. One of Capcom's few big-budget standalone titles, surrounded by its long-running franchises, Exoprimal was Capcom’s attempt to leap into the third-person shooter live-service genre dominated by Warframe.

Exoprimal itself found a niche audience that enjoyed what was on offer, with a humble but positive review on Steam to this day. Despite the receptive crowd, players were disappointed by the lack of content on launch, leaving them with plenty of hunger for more dino-killing, mech-fueled action.

Capcom eventually relented in a series of effective but spaced-out patches, leaving players feeling like the second fiddle as the company continued to focus on its heavy hitters. The End of Service announcement is a genuine death sentence for a title like Exoprimal, not just revealing an end to active development, but a date the game will be simply unplayable after.

The announcement is unfortunate, but sadly not at all unexpected. When compared to other middling live service ventures, Exoprimal managing a total of 4 years of support is on the higher end. This, sadly, just means players who put in the time are left in the dust, at least until they find a third-party way to keep playing.

Anyone new on the block needs to really justify their airtime, and for many, it’s simply not worth the hassle of getting torn apart by consumers

Modders have been the absolute team players when it comes to keeping the lights on for live service communities, and Exoprimal is no different. If they can’t, Exoprimal is facing downright elimination, a sad sight for one of Capcom’s most video-gamey new titles that could absolutely have made it in the long haul.

End-of-Service announcements have killed tons of games, both strong and weak. It’s been a major hit to the industry, with half of the recent releases that drop threatened by an eventual death knell. Any investment in a title that isn’t guaranteed to succeed, like massive franchise sequels, feels like a gamble, and it can be absolutely exhausting.

Capcom is one company that can absolutely afford to put out smaller, standalone titles to make some cash and potentially introduce whole new franchises. Pragmata is one such gem, a fantastic little puzzle shooter that came out of nowhere. A perfect little bite-sized adventure is perfectly Capcom, and their care for anything other than Monster Hunter shows they just truly aren’t great with long-term support.

Compared to Monster Hunter, with its constant new hunts, events, and short-term content to keep players engaged over time, all the while hyping a massive expansion just over the horizon. Exoprimal struggled to offer anything beyond cosmetics and microtransactions for short-term content between missions, making it clear the small team working on the title didn’t get much focus.

Sadly, Exoprimal’s cancellation isn't surprising, but the fanbase isn’t going anywhere. They may be small, but they’ve got the passion and effort to try and keep the party going, at least for a while longer.
