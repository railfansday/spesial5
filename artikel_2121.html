Spurs included one Ash Phillips clause that Middlesbrough won’t stop thinking about

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Middlesbrough are looking to end a 10-year exile from the Premier League at the end of this season, and are certainly well-placed to do so based on their early-campaign form.

Kim Hellberg was appointed at the Riverside Stadium 12 months ago, but has completely transformed the outlook of the North East side.

The Swede is now one of the most highly-rated head coaches outside the Premier League, whilst many of his squad on Teesside are in similar boats due to their current ability and exceptionally high potential.

Having fallen short in last season's automatic promotion race before losing May's play-off final to Hull City in crushing circumstances, Hellberg was significantly backed by Steve Gibson in the summer to make the desired changes in order to ensure such feats were a one-off.

As many as 12 new signings were recruited by Boro throughout the summer window, with depth and quality being added in equal measure right throughout their squad.

However, three of said dealings were particularly eye-catching in the form of Radek Vitek, Ash Phillips and Will Lankshear, with the latter duo both arriving in big-money moves from Tottenham Hotspur.

Spurs sold Will Lankshear to Boro in the summer, but, they have clauses written into the contract to protect their position in the future.

Phillips is no stranger to the Championship despite being just 21 years of age, having risen through the academy ranks at Blackburn Rovers before featuring 14 times for the Ewood Park outfit.

Spurs then activated a £2m release clause three summers ago, but the centre-back was swiftly loaned to Plymouth Argyle for the second period of 2023/24, with the Devon side narrowly avoiding relegation to League One.

Phillips then spent the past two seasons on loan at Stoke City, racking up 83 appearances in North Staffordshire, with his form and situation in North London subsequently attracting Middlesbrough's attention, whilst the Potters also sought a permanent move.

However, after a long-standing summer saga, during which talks were 'advanced' before hitting the buffers, the Salford native penned a five-year contract at the Riverside Stadium on August 15th.

Despite being so highly-rated, Phillips never made a senior appearance for the North London club, albeit the managerial shake-up which followed his arrival is likely to have contributed to such factors.

Roberto De Zerbi opted to cash in on the England youth international, who would otherwise be competing against the likes of Jan Paul van Hecke, Marcos Senesi and Micky van de Ven for a starting berth.

That said, Middlesbrough will certainly be wary of the fact Spurs still stand a chance of making their own significant funds if Phillips reaches his expected potential.

The aforementioned £7m initial deal is followed up by £13m in add-ons, meaning Spurs could make as much as £20m regardless, whilst also inserting a 20 percent sell-on clause within the agreement.

Spurs also have matching rights inserted into the deal, allowing them the potential to reunite with Phillips, irrespective of the fee another club puts on Middlesbrough's desk.

It remains to be seen what the future holds for Phillips, who has already become a mainstay in Hellberg's new-look defence as Middlesbrough set their sights on promotion.

The defender, no doubt, will eventually hope to make his mark in the Premier League too, and the Boro bosses' recent verdict only backs up why Spurs looked to protect themselves during negotiations.
