HBO's 28-Episode Dystopian Series Is So Good, Even Its Weakest Episode Beats Most Sci-Fi Shows

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The weakest episode of one of HBO’s best Mystery Box shows arrived early in the three-season run of the series, but even this outing still had plenty to praise, which highlights the overall quality of the show as a whole. Mystery Box shows are somewhat infamous for their inconsistency. While early seasons of Lost include some of the most enthralling and engaging network TV moments of the 2000s, the show’s filler episodes can feel truly pointless.

Even the earliest Mystery Box shows weren’t immune to this. The best episodes of Twin Peaks were funny, scary, heartbreaking, disturbing, and beautiful, often at the same time, but anyone who has sat through the latter half of season 2 can attest to the show’s plentiful padding. Similarly, while there were mytharc episodes of the ‘90s classic The X-Files that were truly unmissable, there were plenty of other outings that made the central conspiracy more confusing, but not more interesting.

The fact that almost every Mystery Box show has at least a few duds among its great episodes makes HBO’s The Leftovers all the more impressive. Based on the novel of the same name by author Tom Perrotta, HBO’s dark mystery drama takes place after an unexplained disaster sees 2% of the global human population seemingly vanish without a trace. The show follows the stories of the devastated people left behind, from Justin Theroux’s troubled police officer Kevin to Christopher Eccleston’s unhinged religious zealot Reverend Matt Jamison.

Although the characters of The Leftovers gradually crossed paths throughout the HBO show’s story, the series wasn’t in a rush to reveal the links between its sprawling ensemble. With a starry cast that included Liv Tyler, Regina Hall, and Carrie Coon, The Leftovers wasn’t short on talent, but the show’s most divisive outing did leave viewers wondering whether its diffuse storytelling style would ever pay off.

The Leftovers season 1, episode 4, “BJ and the AC,” is often singled out as a rare miss for the show, and one of the only skippable episodes of the series as a whole. At the time of its release, some reviewers noted that episode 4 was left out of screeners for the HBO series, which can sometimes indicate that an episode isn’t likely to be particularly notable or memorable in the long run.

Compared to the preceding episode’s fascinating, focused character study, the plot of “BJ and the AC” felt shapeless and saggy. The subplot involving Jill and her friends didn’t capture believable teen dialogue particularly well, while The Leftovers was a little too on-the-nose in its religious symbolism when it came to the Nativity scene’s missing Baby Jesus doll. Even the very concept of the police assigning Kevin to search for a toy doll stretched credulity for some viewers.

However, especially when compared to controversial creator Damon Lindelof’s later Watchmen re-imagining from 2019, “BJ and the AC” is far from terrible. In fact, despite how heavy-handed its unsubtle symbolism is at times, the episode still finds time to make The Guilty Remnant a more authentically unsettling presence, while Kevin’s character flaws get more airtime in an outing that complicates his initial introduction.

As The Leftovers continued, characters like Jill were fleshed out further and the show outdid itself so many times that “BJ and the AC” started to look like a comparably messy, weak early effort. However, in comparison to many of the show’s competitors, this episode of The Leftovers is still an immersive, intriguing entry into the show’s larger story.

It is arguably unfortunate for the legacy of The Leftovers that HBO’s far shorter, dystopian, character-driven literary adaptation, Station Eleven, never released a single bad episode. However, The Leftovers lasted a lot longer and had many more episodes, so it is easy to forgive a few missteps from the typically stellar Mystery Box series.
