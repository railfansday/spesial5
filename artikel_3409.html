The Body’s 9 Main Characters, Ranked

The Body is quickly becoming the hottest show on Netflix, largely thanks to its ensemble cast. The psychological drama is a one-of-a-kind exploration of religious guilt, mass hysteria, and the dangers of herd mentality, all wrapped up in the deceptively glitzy packaging of a 2004 period piece. Between the mysterious circumstances surrounding Grace's (Sofia Wylie) minimally conscious state and the central dance team's rocky road to regionals, the series never outright confirms whether the viewer is watching something supernatural or buying into cult fever themselves.

Without question, the coming-of-age series is Netflix's best thriller of the year— although its gripping story couldn't succeed without its stellar cast. Side characters like Tyler (Marcel Ruiz), Katie (Bella Neuspiel), and Coach Miller (Gabby Windey) stole the show with comedic one-liners, while figures like Mr. Anderson haunt the narrative, despite dying off-screen prior to the first episode. Yet, the main cast is what irrefutably takes The Body from good to great. In a refreshing departure from the norm, every single major character is deliciously complex, never fully victim or villain.

The shades of grey make it difficult to pin down one true hero, saturating each scene with a sense of esoteric abstraction that forces the audience to pay close attention, lest they give in to internal biases and forget the drama's most poignant message: everyone is either an antihero or an immensely sympathetic antagonist. Should Netflix capitalize on the show's global appeal and renew The Body season 2, viewers can only hope showrunners Quinn Shephard and Gina Gammell will keep that same energy and prioritize depth over easily digestible archetypes, even if certain characters are decidedly more amiable than others.

As much as The Body enjoys challenging dichotomies like "good" and "evil", Catherine Anderson (Louisa Krause) is undoubtedly the closest thing the series has to a full-blown villain. Within the show itself, she's likened to Carrie's Margaret White, as both characters are religious fanatics who abuse their children in the name of spiritual cleansing. From the moment the viewer meets Catherine, it's clear she has a dangerously loose tether to reality, which only worsens as her control over Lisbeth (Geena Meszaros) and Leo (Jackson Kelly) weakens.

Be it exposing herself to a school full of teenagers or unplugging Grace's breathing tube, Catherine seems fairly irredeemable throughout the series. That said, considering her secret drug addiction, lingering grief over her husband's death, and underlying mental health condition, it's clear that Catherine's actions don't exist in a vacuum. Of course, that doesn't justify the way she treats her children, and it's difficult to empathize with her wholeheartedly.

Likewise, Lisbeth does awful things nearly every episode, but she isn't necessarily a bad person herself. Being raised by religious fundamentalists and hailed as a child "anointed" by God, Lisbeth's complicated relationship with faith has taught her that manipulation is a tool for survival. As such, while much of The Body's psychological horror is spurred on by the young girl, she isn't a cut-and-dry demonic harbinger of doom.

At heart, Lisbeth is a sheltered child who desperately wants to fit in, but sympathizing for her character doesn't mean excusing the harm she causes. Throughout the series, she has no qualms about blackmailing Anya (Kristina Bogic), weaponizing Maddie's (Shirley Chen) eating disorder, and leveraging her teammates' Catholic upbringing to incite mass panic— all in the hope of clinching a spot on the Lions as a less-than-coordinated dancer. Nevertheless, Lisbeth isn't pure evil, and ignoring her understandable motivations would be a criminal dilution of her character.

While Anya Butler is far from innocent in The Body's overarching conflict, she has some of the most fascinating subplots in the entire series. In many ways, Anya subverts the classic trope of the Queen Bee, typically using her role as captain of the Lions for good. As much as she uplifts her closest friends, however, Anya is also a key player in Grace's comatose fate. Similarly, as a closeted lesbian, she's plagued by internalized homophobia that causes her to push away the girl she loves while stringing along the boy she doesn't.

Whether you tuned in for The Body's queer representation or to see the best-dressed TV characters of the season, Anya likely isn't who you expected her to be, for better and for worse. She expresses all-consuming guilt and fear of eternal damnation in one breath and, in the next, doubles down on total secrecy to protect her future. Be it selfishness or cowardice, there is evidence of Anya's culpability beyond Lisbeth's manipulation. Regardless, Anya also isn't a ruthless wolf in sheep's clothing, and it's simple to see why she chooses to do what she does.​​​​​​

The other half of The Body's brother-sister duo is Leo, an awkward boy who is constantly at war with himself. In addition to the strict fundamentalist belief system that was ingrained in him, he exhibits symptoms of obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD), which compounds in a vicious cycle of Leo punishing himself for anything he deems unholy. Granted, the nude drawings he made of Grace were disturbing, but his constant self-flagellation and inability to understand the world aside from the Bible paints a more intricate picture.

Like Lisbeth, practically everything Leo says, does, and even thinks is a tragic reflection of the religious abuse he experienced growing up. Catherine and Mr. Anderson hammered into Leo's head an overly literal interpretation of the Bible, to the point where the teen genuinely believes he caused Grace's accident merely by thinking "impure" thoughts. That blind devotion makes Leo especially susceptible to manipulation, culminating in him claiming Grace as his new god.

Steven Maxwell (Orlando Norman) could easily have been an unlikable character, as many boyfriends in early 2000s media were. In a refreshing change of pace, The Body makes Steven one of the nicest characters in the group. Despite Anya having trouble reaching orgasm during sex, Steven makes a vested effort to understand her, make her comfortable, and reassure his girlfriend that there's nothing wrong with her.

Naturally, nobody is a saint in The Body. Early on, Steven all but waterboards Leo to gauge whether he was involved in Grace's accident. Furthermore, when the jock himself begins to think he murdered Grace while being blackout drunk, he initially plans on skipping town. Yet, he returns for Anya's debutante ball, fully prepared to face the consequences of a crime he didn't commit. As numerous characters note, Steven is a gentleman— arguably to a fault.

From Mike Flanagan's Midnight Mass to Prime Video's Fleabag to Wake Up Dead Man: A Knives Out Mystery, dynamic priests have emerged as a fan-favorite trope in modern TV and film. Father Sam Franklin (Nnamdi Asomugha) is no different, navigating the events of The Body with a unique perspective as both Grace's uncle and the school's headmaster.

Father Franklin is instantly endearing, though his commitment to the cloth ends up being his character's biggest weakness. As public opinion oscillates between Grace being possessed by the devil and being a divine miracle worker, Father Franklin undergoes his own crisis of faith. Consequently, when the truth comes out in confession that Grace took drugs the night she fell unconscious, the priest fails to act immediately, weighing the sanctity of his career against a potentially life-changing development for his niece. As a result, he loses a few brownie points.​​​​​​

If anyone on the Lions could have slept soundly after the Halloween party shenanigans, it should have been Maddie Delaney. Following an unexpected ankle injury, Maddie helps recruit Grace to the dance team, and she even warns her ahead of time about the hazing ritual that kicks the entire story into motion. Despite her attempts to protect Grace, Maddie is the first to express remorse and pleads with the team to come clean to Father Franklin.

At the same time, Maddie deals with bulimia, and her own health problems run parallel to a worsening feeling of responsibility over Grace's coma. Plus, she is one of the most devout dancers on the Lion, which Lisbeth exploits to spread her own gospel and use Maddie as a pawn. There may be no angels in The Body, but Maddie is definitely one of the more righteous students in the main cast.​​​​​​

At first glance, Elise Zakaria (Sara Boustany) is the most outwardly antagonist student. Anyone who dares look past the surface, however, will see that her abrasive nature does not equate to villainy. She's judgmental, disrespectful, and even cruel at times, but Elise also deals with far more than most of her peers. Though it isn't explicitly spelled out for the audience, the young girl's Arab identity makes her the target of racist microaggressions at school, while Anya's rejection of her queerness is an ongoing battle that claims Elise as a casualty.

Given Elise is also the only Lion to be fully immune from Lisbeth's cult-like preaching, she soon becomes prey to The Body's psychological drama, even being held down by her supposed friends for a traumatic "exorcism" in the middle of the night. Elise has her own list of sins, like pressuring Grace to take ecstasy in episode 1, but she's a prime example of how absurd the "perfect victim" myth is, as her character's struggles mustn't be overlooked simply because of her irreverent personality.​​​​​​

Grace has an astounding impact on the series, despite being absent for most of it. Within the first episode, we get the foundation of a well-rounded character. Although she is a devoted Catholic, Grace pushes back against oppressive interpretations of the Bible, is extremely friendly to outcast Leo, and doesn't look down on the Lions for their risqué routines or underage partying. Even in her minimally conscious state, The Body continues showing glimpses of Grace's true self, from home videos to a video blog. ​​​​​​​

Prior to The Body, Sofia Wylie starred alongside Orlando Norman (Steven) in 2025's Map That Leads to You, with their characters ironically becoming love interests.
