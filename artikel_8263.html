The 15 Best Gifts for Birders and Bird Lovers in 2026

Shopping for the best gifts for birders? Well, you’ve come to the right perch. Whether the bird lover on your list is happiest in the woods with a pair of binoculars, keeping tabs on the birds at their kitchen window, or planning their next big birding vacation, there’s plenty of bird watching gear and gear-adjacent goodies to fuel their obsession. We rounded up gifts for every kind of bird enthusiast, from useful field essentials and clever gadgets to books, games, and home goods that let them feather their nest (literally). So whether you’re shopping for Christmas, a birthday, or just because, there’s something here for every bird watcher in your life.

Don’t let a little precipitation derail a day in the field, and give them this waterproof hat. Its breathable construction helps prevent overheating during active hikes, while the brim keeps rain off the face and out of the eyes—particularly useful when trying to keep binoculars focused on a moving target. Even better, it’s packable, so they can bring it with them on vacations and not worry about it getting crushed in transit.

Birding doesn’t always happen in sunny, shorts-friendly weather. Kuhl’s durable lined pants add a little extra warmth for chilly mornings spent scanning the treetops without making them feel like they’re wearing a sleeping bag. Best of all, they don’t scream “birding pants” and are still cute and comfy enough to become an everyday wardrobe staple.

A smartphone’s built-in zoom can only do so much when a hawk or an eagle is perched high in the canopy. This 10x telephoto lens adds serious reach to a phone camera, helping birders capture more detailed photos of distant subjects without carrying a dedicated telephoto lens. When paired with the stick-on lens mount, they can simply twist on their lens and snap away.

For birders who want to document their sightings without investing in a bulky DSLR setup, this cool point-and-shoot camera offers an easy way to snap away in the field. It can be used on its own or connected to Nocs’ binos to bring distant subjects closer, allowing them to capture magnified photos and videos of their feathered friends. Plus, its tough, weather-sealed design makes it a practical companion for other outdoor excursions, like hikes, ski days, paddles, and more.

It wouldn’t be a birding gift guide without a high-quality pair of birding binoculars. Bushnell’s 10x50s bring distant birds into closer view, while their large 50mm lenses gather plenty of light for brighter, clearer images, even in low-light conditions. They’re also waterproof and fog-resistant, so rain or a sudden temperature swing won’t cut a birding trip short.

Give them a front-row seat to the backyard action—even when they’re nowhere near the window. This smart bird feeder uses a built-in camera and AI-powered species recognition to capture visiting birds and send photos and videos straight to their phone. It’s basically a wildlife documentary starring their own backyard regulars.

A birdhouse that doesn’t look like it came from the clearance aisle at Home Depot. Handcrafted in Portugal, the Koti’s sculptural design offers a chic nesting spot for backyard birds while doubling as an architectural accent for the garden. And despite its minimalist look, it’s filled with thoughtful details—a textured interior allows new chicks to climb out safely, while the 1.75-inch hole is the perfect size to let birds in and keep predators out.

Want to splurge on your favorite birder? If the $15M Kelly Clarkson bird is out of your price range, this clever piece of art-tech is the next best thing. It uses a microphone and bird-call identification software to detect nearby species, then gorgeously illustrates versions of its feathered visitors on a digital screen. Think of it as a constantly updating art print for their own backyard that changes every 24 hours—cool, right? And a whole lot more unique than another Audubon print or identification chart.

A little bird identification practice disguised as a rainy-day activity. This illustrated, 1,000-piece puzzle features a lineup of Eastern North American species, giving birders a chance to brush up on their knowledge while piecing together their favorite feathered faces. It’s also a fun way to get the whole family invested in birding—no early-morning alarm required.

Whether they’re just a fledgling birder or a lifelong bird nerd, a gift card to Cornell’s online Bird Lab will let them brush up on their avian knowledge. The self-paced online courses cover everything from birding basics and backyard identification to birdsong, migration, and species-specific studies. There are also classes on wildlife photography, nature journaling, and attracting birds to the garden, so they can learn a completely new skill that’ll take their passion to new flights heights.

You don’t have to be a birder to get hooked on this game (though it certainly helps). In Wingspan, players build wildlife preserves by attracting birds, collecting food, and laying eggs, with each species bringing its own abilities to the mix. It’s gorgeous, surprisingly strategic, and the kind of game that can turn a casual game night into a heated debate about everyone’s favorite bird.

Helen Macdonald’s celebrated memoir about training a goshawk to cope with the loss of her father is part nature writing, part personal reckoning, and entirely engrossing. This beautifully illustrated Folio Society edition gives the award-winning book a bespoke upgrade, making it a particularly good pick for the birder whose bookshelf is already full of field guides.

Keeping field notes when the forecast has other plans is… frustrating. This weather-resistant notebook is designed to hold up to rain, mud, humidity, and whatever else the weather throws their way, so birders can jot down sightings without their notes melting away. That way, they can record species, locations, behaviors, and other details worth remembering without a worry.

This classic diner-style ceramic mug from Bird Collective says it all. Even better, Bird Collective donates a portion of its profits to bird conservation groups, so this cup does some good beyond just keeping their coffee warm. And if coffee isn’t their thing, the brand has plenty of other birdy-themed gifts worth wrapping up for the holidays, including species-specific apparel, home gifts, and gear.

This crafty embroidery kit gives them a cute, tangible way to bring their feathered friends everywhere they go. It comes with a cotton pouch, 16 colors of thread, and a pigeon-shaped thread holder to organize their spools while they stitch owls, songbirds, and more. The finished product is handy for corralling field notes, pencils, or all the tiny bits of gear that otherwise disappear into a backpack.
