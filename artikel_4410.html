Tested: The 2028 Volvo XC60 T8 Is the Rangiest Plug-In Hybrid

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Volvo’s aging middleweight SUV is a surprisingly strong performance contender with its revised powertrain.

On the journey to the future of automotive propulsion, Volvo was an early adopter of the all-electric ethos. The Swedish automaker initially vowed to stop selling all gasoline-powered cars—including hybrids—by 2030. A few years and three new all-electric models later, Volvo has radically altered that plan and will continue to sell hybrid models past that point. That includes extending the life of the existing lineup, with the already-near-decade-old second-gen XC60 getting a new T8 powertrain, which gives it the longest pure electric range of any plug-in hybrid on sale in the U.S.

We have always appreciated the charms of the current-generation XC60, with a substantial package of changes sharpening its case for a few more years. Visually, the most obvious changes are a new front end incorporating LED matrix headlights and smaller revisions at the rear. The technical highlight is an improved 78-mile EPA electric range from a mammoth 37.9-kWh battery pack, this delivered to a motor on the rear axle; a smaller starter-generator can add modest assistance at the front as well. Volvo quotes a peak electric output of 208 hp, with the 2.0-liter turbo four that drives the front wheels able to deliver 247 horses. Inside, the XC60 receives small material and trim adjustments.

Engine: 1969-cc turbocharged inline-four, hybridized

Transmission: eight-speed automatic, all-wheel drive

As I pulled into Copenhagen’s morning traffic, Volvo’s Pure drive mode, which idles the gasoline-powered engine in favor of an all-electric experience, felt prudent among the multitude of commuting bicyclists. Silent running was entirely suited to the Scandinavian environment, but the XC60 T8 wasn’t stumbling over itself either when running under electrical power, Volvo having significantly juiced up the electric motor alongside the bigger battery pack.

While 208 hp in Pure EV mode has to work against 4855 pounds of weight, the T8 felt well suited to both metropolitan driving and the open road, the effective surge of electrical power proving itself on highways and the 9.9-mile-long Øresund Link that connects Denmark to Malmö, Sweden. Power delivery definitely wasn’t as aggressive as that of a spicy EV, but it wasn’t gentle either, and the electric motor had enough punch to drive the XC60 without me yearning for the gas engine to intervene. Something that won’t happen unless the accelerator is pushed to a kickdown position or by switching drive modes. The 78-mile EPA range is genuinely class-leading, and selecting the T8’s Charge mode rapidly replenished e-miles when selected.

Switching from the Pure to Hybrid mode was where the real fun began. And fun isn’t too strong a word for the unlikely performance of the split hybrid system as the 2.0-liter turbo adds its contribution to the front axle, giving a combined peak of 455 hp and what Volvo claims is 4.5-second 0–60-mph capability. In practice, the XC60 T8 felt shockingly quick, rapidly spinning up the speedometer at wide-open throttle. The accelerator has a two-part action, with softer response at the top of its travel metering electrical power before the combustion horses arrive with a suddenness. This was fun, but the transition also felt a bit too obvious considering how subtle some hybrids now are. Switching it up to Power mode refocused the powertrain by keeping the engine running and solved the on-and-off drama. The eight-speed automatic transmission shifts without drama, and there’s enough electrical torque that I didn’t even want for the missing steering-wheel paddle shifters.

We liked the way the pre-facelift XC60 T8 drove, and the new version impressed as much. Volvos are traditionally heavy and somewhat leisurely to respond, but the XC60 successfully belied its scale with impressively neutral handling on narrow Swedish country roads. My XC60 test vehicle was fitted with the optional air suspension and active shock absorbers, fighting body roll and delivering a reactive front end.

Similarly, Volvo’s power-steering system was smooth and direct, though both weight settings skewed overly light for my tastes. It’s not as overtly sporty as some performance SUVs, but its combination of qualities was definitely appealing. My only complaints were a somewhat agricultural soundtrack under full throttle and what was often a harsh ride over rough surfaces, bumps, and imperfections being transferred into the cabin.

I didn’t get a chance to plug in the XC60, but Volvo said the 2028 model can charge at speeds up to 7.2 kWh. All told, the XC60 PHEV will take around 5.7 hours to fully charge up at maximum speed.

Volvo has long succeeded at creating minimalist cabins without them looking sparse or cheap, and the XC60 continues to carry that torch with a few exceptions. And one of these exceptions was the 11.2-inch center touchscreen, which has been blown out of the casing found on previous model years to take over the entire console.

Annoyingly, climate-control functions are integrated into this display with multiple menus required for adjustment, the Android Auto software beneath it starting to show its age. Similarly, Apple CarPlay is only available through a wired connection—that’s so 2017. That said, the infotainment system was easy to operate despite its limitations. Plus, Volvo has perfected the art of displaying turn-by-turn directions on the instrument cluster; a dial speedometer and tachometer sit opposite each other with an appropriately scaled navigation map centered in between.

Beyond the technology, the XC60’s interior was a charming place to spend time. New Scandinavian-inspired materials on the dashboard included brushed wood and knit fabric, which looked upmarket and were pleasing to the touch. The light leather seats were firm yet comfortable and included massage, heating, and cooling functions, which can be operated all at once. Other notable interior design wins include a wooden sliding cup-holder cover, an alluring crystal shift knob, and satisfying brushed steel door handles. The Bowers & Wilkins stereo system on my test car was fantastic, and Volvo deserves points for retaining a traditional volume knob, as well as tactile buttons to skip songs.

If you want a properly long-range plug-in hybrid SUV, the Volvo XC60 may be your best option, at least for now. The next longest-range mid-size luxury SUV option is the Mercedes-Benz GLC Hybrid, which offers 54 all-electric miles. Toyota also offers a RAV4 Plug-In Hybrid with 52 miles of range. Volvo has not announced finalized U.S. pricing for the 2028 XC60 T8 yet.

Volvo interiors are well known for minimalist luxury, but even the Swedes can’t help themselves from including an easter egg here and there. Placed on the right edge of the passenger-side dashboard, Volvo has engraved a miniature Swedish flag on the brushed aluminum trim.
