Qualys's 2027 Outlook: Achieving Federal Authorization Solidifies Public Sector Moat

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

This under-the-radar cybersecurity stock boasts a Superscore of 85 from our Hidden Gems Primary database, part of The Motley Fool's Moneyball Database system. Here's why.

By Robert Izquierdo – Updated Nov 25, 2026 at 3:53PM EST

When a large enterprise needs to lock down its entire digital footprint, it turns to Qualys (QLYS +2.20%). Picture a security officer managing thousands of servers, cloud containers, and remote endpoints, all of which represent a potential entry point for a breach. Qualys provides the unified platform that identifies those risks and automates the fix, turning a chaotic network into a defensible one. As of November 19, 2026, the stock trades at $192.51, having risen 39% over the past year.

Our proprietary Hidden Gems scoring system assigns Qualys an overall Superscore of 85 out of 100, placing it in the Strong category. The Superscore is an AI-powered score that evaluates a company's overall strength by combining financial performance, product market position, technological capabilities, leadership quality, and relative valuation. It represents the unification of all our scores into a single score for public companies, with five rating bands: Exceptional (90-100), Strong (75-89), Above Average (60-74), Average (40-59), and Cautious (0-39).

An 85 Superscore places the company in the Top ~4% of every company we score, which is ahead of roughly 96 out of every 100 companies we evaluate. This Superscore is one data-driven signal worth investigating, and this article pairs the reasons the score is high with the reasons it is not higher so you can weigh both sides before doing more work.

Qualys is a highly capital-efficient business, earning outsized profits on a small base of hard tangible assets. This efficiency allows the company to turn each point of revenue growth into strong returns, a trait the market often rewards with a premium valuation. While this efficiency is a clear advantage, it does not erase the risks posed by elevated valuation multiples and aggressive competition.

Qualys is well positioned in the cybersecurity space. Organizations and government agencies are shifting to platforms that not only identify security vulnerabilities, but also fixes them. That is one of the company's key strengths, which has grown in importance now that hackers can employ artificial intelligence to quickly attack a system before an organization can respond. As a result, Qualys saw its federal government-related sales outperform expectations in Q3 as agencies consolidated spending onto its platform.

Moreover, while many software companies operate at a loss, Qualys is profitable with an impressive Q3 gross margin of 83%. It sports a fortress balance sheet, exiting the third quarter with total assets of $1.2 billion compared to total liabilities of $536.3 million, and of those liabilities, more than $425 million is deferred revenue, which will transition to sales after services are delivered.

Given the company's many strengths, Qualys is well positioned for years of growth ahead. Yet these factors have contributed to the stock's elevated valuation, with a trailing P/E ratio that's around a high point for the past year. This suggests now is not the best time to buy. Wait for the price to drop first before deciding to pick up shares.
