BigBear.ai vs. Rigetti Computing: Which Technology Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

BigBear.ai has a growing backlog of AI contracts, but it still loses money. Rigetti is hitting key quantum milestones and has plenty of cash, but its sales are tiny and its valuation is sky-high.

By Sara Appino – Updated Nov 27, 2026 at 6:08PM EST

BigBear.ai (BBAI +6.92%) and Rigetti Computing (RGTI +8.66%) represent the frontier of data processing. Investors must decide which specialized firm offers a more sustainable path to growth by 2027.

BigBear.ai focuses on decision intelligence, helping government and commercial clients make sense of complex data sets. Rigetti develops superconducting quantum processors, aiming to revolutionize computing power. Both companies navigate a volatile landscape, catering to high-stakes defense and research sectors while seeking long-term commercial scale.

BigBear.ai provides mission-ready AI solutions focused on global supply chains, autonomous systems, and cybersecurity for both commercial and government clients among tech stocks. Most of the company's 579 employees hold active security clearances, which helps maintain deep relationships within national security agencies. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business, as revenue from customers contributing greater than 10% of total sales accounted for 51% of revenue in 2025.

Reliable recent quarterly figures for at least one of these companies aren't available, so this comparison uses each company's latest full fiscal year. In its latest annual report, filed for FY 2025, revenue reached about $127.7 million, a decrease of approximately 19.3% from the prior year. The company reported a GAAP net loss of around $293.9 million during this period, driven primarily by non-cash goodwill impairments and derivative fair-value adjustments.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the company carried a debt-to-equity ratio of roughly 0.2x, which measures total debt against shareholder equity, before a January 2026 note conversion reduced it near 0.0x. The current ratio, showing if a company can cover short-term debts with current assets, is about 1.8x. Free cash flow for the period was approximately negative $46.3 million, representing the actual cash used after capital investments.

Rigetti Computing builds full-stack quantum computers that utilize superconducting quantum processors to solve problems beyond the reach of classical systems. Its applications span diverse fields such as climate simulation, fusion energy, and drug discovery through collaborations with commercial and academic partners like Amazon (AMZN +3.96%) and Microsoft (MSFT +0.60%). In 2025, sales to government entities like the Department of Energy accounted for about 90.2% of total revenue.

In its latest annual report, filed for FY 2025, revenue reached approximately $7.1 million, which was a decrease of around 34.3% year over year. The company reported a GAAP net loss of about $216.2 million for the same time frame. Roughly three-quarters of this loss stemmed from non-cash fair-value adjustments on warrant and earn-out liabilities alongside ongoing R&D expenses.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, Rigetti maintains a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.0x, meaning it carries no total debt compared to its equity base. Its current ratio is about 37.4x, indicating it has significantly more current assets than current liabilities to fund operations in the near term. Free cash flow for the year was approximately negative $77.2 million, which represents the cash burned to support ongoing engineering and software development.

BigBear.ai faces significant risks due to its heavy reliance on a small group of government customers. The company recently recorded around $76.7 million in goodwill impairment charges in late 2026 and is managing legal challenges related to accounting disclosures. Furthermore, it faces intense competition from established enterprise software providers in the AI space.

Rigetti Computing operates with extreme financial risk, having an accumulated deficit of about $771 million as of 2025. The company must hit difficult engineering milestones to achieve quantum advantage, which is far from certain. It competes against massive, well-funded technology giants like IBM and Alphabet in a field sensitive to supply chain disruptions.

BigBear.ai appears significantly cheaper based on its P/S ratio, which measures market value against sales over the past twelve months, versus Rigetti.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

I'd go with BigBear.ai. Governments and companies are racing to put AI to work on high-stakes decisions, from border security to critical infrastructure, and BigBear.ai already has the contracts to show for it, with a backlog of about $330 million. What really caught my eye is how fast it's growing: Sales are up 31% year over year, and management just raised its full-year outlook for the first time this year.

Valuation tells the story, too. Measured against yearly sales, BigBear.ai's stock is valued at roughly 5 to 10 times, while Rigetti's is valued at several hundred times, so Rigetti's price already assumes a lot of success that hasn't happened yet. BigBear.ai has its own flaws, though. It still loses money, and government contracts can be slow and uneven.

To its credit, Rigetti is hitting its technical goals. Its newest quantum computer just reached its accuracy target, and it has plenty of cash and no debt. But its sales are tiny and slipped last quarter, so it is still years from earning its keep.

BigBear.ai, meanwhile, already has customers, a growing backlog, and sales to build on. For long-term investors, an AI company with this much momentum and a far lower valuation relative to its sales makes BigBear.ai the better buy for 2027.
