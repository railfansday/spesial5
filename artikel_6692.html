13 Fab Old Photos of 2026 Rock & Roll Hall of Fame Inductee Sade Adu

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Think Sade, and a few things likely pop to mind. There’s the signature beauty look: the dark eyeliner, shapely brows, red lip, and long, slicked-back plait. There are the clothes: good earrings—often big gold hoops—and stage outfits that were sexy, yes, but also slyly demure; high-waisted trousers, semi-sheer turtlenecks, cropped jackets, qipao-style dresses. You may nerdily make the distinction between Sade the woman—Helen Folasade Adu, OBE, born on January 16, 1959, in Ibadan, Nigeria—and Sade the Grammy-winning band, formed in 1982 and made up of Adu, Paul Denman, Andrew Hale, and Stuart Matthewman.

Now there’s one more thing to add to that list: the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame. This weekend, the band will celebrate their induction—along with acts including Phil Collins, Billy Idol, Oasis, and the late Luther Vandross—in a ceremony at Los Angeles’s Peacock Theater. Now, will any of Sade’s members actually be present for that ceremony? No, according to a statement released on November 10. Even so, they called the recognition “both a gift and a timely reminder to give thanks, not only to those who voted for us but also every single person who lets our music into their lives.”

And what music that is: from “Smooth Operator” to “Kiss of Life,” “Is It a Crime” to “No Ordinary Love,” “The Sweetest Taboo” to “I Couldn’t Love You More,” Sade’s soulful, sui generis sound remains a benchmark for timeless cool.

Here, in celebration of her group’s latest honor, we’ve rounded up 13 striking old photographs of the divine Sade Adu, a generational talent and forever style icon.

Sporting spotty shirting, high-waisted trousers, and a wide belt during a 1984 set in Rotterdam.

This is no ordinary love–and those are no ordinary neutrals. A graduate of London’s Saint Martin’s School of Art, Sade shows off her style bonafides at the Hammersmith Odeon in 1984.

Cooler than cool in sunglasses and a simple white button-down.

Performing at the Live Aid charity concert at Wembley Stadium in 1985.

Gold jewelry, jaunty bolero jacket, can’t lose.

In textured whites—and that classic red lip—at the Ambassador East Hotel in Chicago, 1985.

Making her Saturday Night Live debut on December 14, 1985, with Tom Hanks as host. The band performed “Is It a Crime” and “The Sweetest Taboo,” both from their second studio album, Promise.

In 2010, recalling the release of Sade’s debut album, Diamond Life, in 1984, The New Yorker’s Sasha Frere-Jones poetically described Adu’s voice on as “a grainy contralto full of air that betrays a slight ache but no agony, and values even imperfect dignity over a show of pain.”
