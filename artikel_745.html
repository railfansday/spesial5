Ariana Grande Addresses "Insane" Backlash After "Petal" Music Video

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Ariana Grande Addresses Ariana grande reflected on the backlash for her physical appearance in the music video for “Petal” received, noting the irony since “that was largely what the video was about.”

Ariana Grande was just tryna feel something real.

And yet, when she released the music video for “Petal”—a gory reimagining of feminine rage felt after years in the spotlight—she was met with a maelstrom of criticism, predominantly for her physical appearance in the almost seven-minute video. Which was, in fact, kind of the whole underlying message of the video.

“It's just it's kind of like oh when will it be about the art? When will it be about the work?” Grande mused during an appearance on Las Culturistas’ Nov. 26 epsiode, as seen in a preview clip shared Nov. 25. “That was largely what the video was about, is like, ‘Oh here comes this girl, talented, little,’ and there's always some distortion around the performance or around the art, whatever it is. And for a while she tries to accommodate and be valuable.”

But as the video shows, despite the protagonist’s efforts to conform and reshape herself to what she thought the entertainment executives wanted, it proved never to be enough.

“My favorite part is that she comes back to herself at the end,” Grande continued. “She's wearing the same color. She's wearing the same hair. She's wearing the same thing. And then that's when they say to her, ‘Oh, it's just so weird. Go back to your old you.’ And she's like, ‘I am. I found it. I connected with my inner—this is me. Wait, but I did. I did. This is it. This is me trying again. As me.’”

And despite the video’s larger message about physical expectations and conforming, Grande’s physical appearance soon became the subject of conversation in what the 33-year-old described as “its own meta, insane perception” she found “really interesting.”

Even though the point of the video seemed to pass some viewers by, she stands by the art she created.

“I feel so proud and grateful,” the Wicked star added. “I would put it out again today, exactly as is. I wouldn't change a thing. I think my experience of putting art out and having people receive it is so different than how it used to be, even just a few years ago.”

“Well, I'll say like people's reactions have never dictated how I've created moving forward,” she explained. “I've never changed my musical style or whatever based on what I think people want from me. I've always moved authentically in that way, that's something I'm proud of.”

But still, there was a period of time when the outside noise took a larger toll.

“It has always, in the past, I think, hurt me a lot,” she reflected. “And now I really do feel like as long as I'm creating from an authentic place and being honest and sharing what I know needs to come out and making things that I love—let the chips fall where they may. My people will be my people, and it's okay if they're not.”
