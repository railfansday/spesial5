Why AI Quality Assurance Belongs In The Boardroom

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Manish Gupta, Founder & CEO of TestingXperts, a global QE leader with 1,500+ professionals. Champion of AI-led Quality Transformation.

For decades, technology companies have competed on speed: faster development, shorter release cycles and quicker innovation. Artificial intelligence (AI) is changing that equation, making software creation faster and more accessible.

But this revolution presents a paradox: As software becomes easier to create, how do we ensure it can be trusted?

Software is becoming abundant. Trust is becoming scarce.

AI is raising the stakes for quality assurance. When software primarily executed predefined instructions, enterprises needed confidence that applications worked correctly. As intelligent systems interpret information, make decisions and act, the imperative has been elevated from assuring functionality to assuring intelligent behavior and its business consequences.

Trust is often viewed through the lens of ethics or reputation. For enterprise leaders, it also has an economic dimension.

Consider an AI-powered customer service platform handling thousands of interactions. Its efficiency benefits may appear compelling until an unusual request produces an inaccurate commitment, an unauthorized refund or exposure of sensitive information.

Without evidence that such exceptions can be managed, the organization may add manual approvals, restrict autonomy or delay expansion. Necessary safeguards can then consume some of the productivity gains AI was meant to deliver.

The true return on AI investment is not determined by how much work AI can automate, but by how much autonomy a business can safely trust it with.

This is why quality assurance matters commercially. It helps leaders distinguish where intelligent systems can operate independently, where safeguards are essential and where human judgment must remain in control. ​

Quality assurance has evolved beyond defect detection before release. Agile, DevOps and continuous testing brought it earlier into software engineering. AI is now elevating its importance in a different way.

AI may reduce the effort required to develop and test software, while increasing the volume of change and the complexity of what must be validated. The more decisions systems make on behalf of businesses, the more valuable credible, independent assurance becomes.

Consider an autonomous insurance claims agent. It may retrieve the correct policy, interpret documents and complete a transaction. Yet functional success does not establish whether the decision is defensible. Can it recognize ambiguous coverage, escalate conflicting evidence and stop when an action exceeds its authority?

A technically successful workflow can still produce an unacceptable business outcome.

Stanford University’s 2026 AI Index documents 362 reported AI incidents in 2025, compared with 233 in 2024. It also notes that responsible AI benchmarking is not keeping pace with advances in capabilities. These reports do not capture every failure, but they underscore the need for rigorous evaluation.

The mandate is no longer only to find defects. It is to establish whether intelligent systems can be relied upon when business consequences are real.

Traditional metrics such as automation coverage, defect leakage and release readiness remain useful. They cannot, by themselves, establish whether an autonomous system deserves greater authority.

I see three priorities for the next generation of assurance.

First, evaluate outcomes in business context. An AI assistant summarizing documents requires different thresholds from an agent approving financial transactions. Evaluation must reflect the consequences of a wrong decision.

Second, challenge behavior beyond expected scenarios. Test ambiguity, conflicting instructions, misuse, authorization boundaries and escalation. How safely a system fails matters alongside how reliably it succeeds.

Third, maintain evidence after deployment. Models, data and operating conditions evolve. Production monitoring, exception analysis and periodic reassessment must therefore complement prerelease evaluation.

Deloitte’s State of AI in the Enterprise 2026 finds that only 1 in 5 surveyed companies has a mature governance model for autonomous AI agents.

Governance defines the boundaries in which AI should operate; quality assurance supplies the evidence that systems respect those boundaries. Where the stakes are high, independent assessment provides additional challenge to internal assumptions.

When AI influences customers, financial decisions and critical operations, failures become business problems, not isolated technology incidents. Yet authority and accountability can remain fragmented across technology, business, security and risk teams.

IBM’s 2026 technology leadership study found that “two-thirds of surveyed CIOs and CTOs report being held accountable for AI systems they do not fully control.”​

Before expanding autonomy, executives should ask: What can the system decide? What evidence supports that authority? How will harmful behavior be detected and contained? Who owns the consequences when it fails?

The objective is not zero risk. It is a defensible decision about which risks the enterprise will accept, supported by proportionate assurance and clear accountability.

AI will increasingly automate test creation, execution and analysis. That may reduce traditional testing effort without reducing the strategic importance of quality engineering. Indeed, its value must move beyond testing volume toward domain expertise, independent evaluation and measurable business outcomes.

AI may democratize intelligence, but it will not automatically democratize trust. That must be earned through evidence, independent assurance and accountability.

As intelligent capabilities become more accessible, the competitive advantage will belong to enterprises that can demonstrate why their systems deserve responsibility and stand behind the decisions they make.

Because when software becomes abundant, trust becomes priceless.
