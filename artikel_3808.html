Ubisoft Says It Has A ‘Very Big Pipeline Of Productions’ As It Lays Off Over 150 More Developers And Starts An AI Studio

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Assassin's Creed Black Flag: ResyncedUbisoft

Ahead of its quarterly earnings release and without any major releases on the horizon, Ubisoft is laying off 166 of its employees in Montreal and Quebec.

This was reported today by Vertical Slice, which received confirmation from Ubisoft via the following statement:

Ubisoft is reducing its workforce by 166 positions across its MontrÃ©al and QuÃ©bec City operations. As our portfolio evolves, our production needs are changing, requiring us to adjust the size of some teams to ensure our resources are aligned with current production priorities and business needs. We deeply value the contributions of these teams and recognize the impact this may have on our colleagues.

Ubisoft has been steadily laying off swathes of employees in the last year alone, letting one in six German employees go across Ubisoft DÃ¼sseldorf, Ubisoft Berlin and Ubisoft Mainz in October, shutting down Finland’s Ubisoft Redlynx, cutting 380 staff in June alongside the closure of Ubisoft Winnipeg and Ubisoft Belgrade, and laying off 40 at Ubisoft Toronto in February, and that’s just in 2026. There were hundreds more layoffs the year prior and the year before that, as well as restructurings and studio closures, with Ubisoft opting to slowly chip away at its many, many global studios rather than just lay off thousands in one sweep as other companies have. The company is, and has been for a while, in trouble, and investors are not happy.

Even so, it’s still apparently bidding to buy Arkane off Xbox.

Right now, it’s unclear what the company’s strategy is going to be to pull things back together. Though Assassin’s Creed: Black Flag Resynced was well-received earlier this year, it’s…kind of the last massive thing Ubisoft had going for it. The company has a few games in the pipeline to come, including Rayman Legends Retold in December, Rainbow Six Tactics and Heroes of Might and Magic III Remake next year, and some mythical Assassin’s Creed games and Beyond Good and Evil 2, none of which have release dates and the latter of which has been in development hell for nearly two decades.

The company just dropped its quarterly earnings report, which showed its sales up 16.8 percent year-over-year and its net bookings down 20.8 percent. The report details no specific figures for how well any of its games did, just saying that Assassin’s Creed Black Flag Resynced “outperformed expectations.” The company claims its new operating model is “fully in place” and that its cost reduction plan is “on track,” while touting its new “Motherbrain” AI initiative, which we have now learned is not just an initiative but a brand new AI studio. Ubisoft says that its headcount has gone down by nearly 1,100 over the last two quarters.
