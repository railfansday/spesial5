All that money spent and Spurs do not have a proper striker or goalkeeper

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

For all the money spent, Tottenham Hotspur have started this season without a reliable out-and-out striker and short of a top goalkeeper. And it is already costing them dear.

We keep hearing that Dominic Solanke will be the man to give Spurs a goalscoring threat, but how long has it been since we have seen him fully fit and firing? Maybe not since his Bournemouth days.

Antonin Kinsky may have finished last season well, but there was further evidence in this defeat that he is well short of the best goalkeepers in the division.

It is a puzzle how Tottenham signed 10 new players, but did not upgrade two of the most important positions on the pitch.

Only when Aston Villa were out of sight and the game was effectively over did Spurs break their Premier League goal drought. And even then it was a midfielder, Conor Gallagher, and a defender, Jan Paul van Hecke, who were on target.

Solanke had gone off just before Villa scored their second goal, which highlighted the big difference between the two sides on Saturday and where Spurs are badly lacking.

Zion Suzuki, the Villa goalkeeper, launched a long ball upfield, Nicolas Jackson managed to lay it off for Johan Mazambi, who returned the favour. Jackson touched the ball past Micky van de Ven’s challenge before beating Kinsky with a well-struck shot.

It was an excellent goal, but Kinsky got a good hand to it and a top goalkeeper would have pushed it around the post. On the evidence of the first half, when he made four saves, Suzuki would have also stopped the ball creeping into the net.

Suzuki cost Villa in the region of £30m and he looks a more than able replacement for Emiliano Martínez. Spurs, on the other hand, have not had a top-class goalkeeper since Hugo Lloris and it is a puzzle that so much faith has been placed in Kinsky.

Spurs spent most of the summer firmly denying any links to Jackson before Villa signed him from Chelsea for an initial £47.5m that could rise to £65m. Given the London club paid an initial £75m for Sávio and committed to a £60m obligation to make Omar Marmoush’s season-long loan permanent, Jackson might have been a relative bargain for them.

Asked if Tottenham had made a big mistake by failing to sign a proper number nine, head coach Robert De Zerbi said: “The transfer market was long. The situation changed many times. There was also the financial rules, at the end of the transfer market. And we had to respect the rules.

“We didn’t sell the players who we thought, in the beginning, there was the possibility, the chance to sell. There are a lot of reasons. I think the club and the coach, we are not sleeping, we know the characteristics of the players, we know everything. And if we didn’t make any different decisions, it was because we couldn’t.”

That sounded a bit like excuses, but De Zerbi did not put up much of a defence for Solanke as he said: “We need more from him, for sure, but he’s one of my players. I like Dom as a player and I stay with him until the end. He needs to stay better, to feel better. The physical condition is not good enough, but it’s normal for him. Maybe he is not confident enough in himself at this moment, because he’s much better than this. I think we can’t say everything is bad, but it’s not enough.”

It was Sávio who forced Suzuki into making a brilliant double save from him in the first half, when Tottenham were well on top. The Japan international also stopped an Archie Gray shot from squirming through his legs and into the net, and dived to turn another Sávio effort around his right-hand post.

Jackson was not the only Villa new boy who upstaged Tottenham’s expensive new attackers. Manzambi not only assisted for Jackson, but also marked his first start for the Midlands club with a goal that put the visitors ahead in first-half stoppage time.

Good afternoon and welcome to our coverage of today’s bottom-of-the-table epic: Tottenham Hotspur vs Aston Villa. This tie is incidentally the match-up between the last two winners of the Europa League, though so far neither of them have looked like European champions.

It is definitely fair to say that this season has not started the way either of these teams would have wanted, as the host side currently sit in what is apparently their favourite position – 17th – and the visitors are all the way in 19th, only above Coventry.

Despite getting off to a fairly convincing start in the Champions League with a 3-2 win over Club Brugge, Villa only scored their first league goal last week in their match against Nottingham Forest, though it was a futile one as they still lost 2-1 at Villa Park.

Tottenham meanwhile are still waiting for their first league goal, despite putting one past Liverpool in their 3-1 defeat in the Carabao Cup third round. With no European football and already out of one cup competition, Roberto De Zerbi has only the FA Cup in January and the rest of the league season to drag Spurs out of the perpetual rut they appear to find themselves in. They sit only one point above Villa as they have been able to see out two 0-0 draws – against Everton and Nottingham Forest – instead of one.

There has been plenty of talk about the money spent by both teams, without the results yet to show for it. The total sits around half a billion between them, adding to the mounting pressure on both De Zerbi and Unai Emery. Summer signings for both teams have struggled to land, something which will not be helped by the upcoming international break.

But before then, the teams have 90 minutes to try and get their seasons up and running, and we will be bringing you the team news, lineups and all the action from the match! Stay tuned!
