The complete, corrected release follows, with additional details at the end:

Merchants who use AI report a chargeback dispute median win rate of 38%, up from 27.75%

NEW YORK, Nov. 17, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Chargeflow, the AI chargeback platform used by more than 20,000 merchants, today published "The State of Chargeback Management: How AI Is Reshaping Performance," a survey of 121 enterprise merchants in the U.S. and U.K. conducted by Juniper Research. Merchants that use AI win a median 38% of the chargebacks they dispute, against 27.75% for those that don't. Yet the median merchant in both groups disputes and wins the same share of its eligible chargebacks: 6.65%.

AI users also contest the same median share of their eligible chargebacks as non-users. Juniper Research points to a gap between adopting AI and changing how the work gets done: 80% of respondents use AI in chargeback management, but only 11% have mostly or fully automated it.

The report's findings come as pressure on merchants grows. Chargebacks rose over the past 18 months for 65% of respondents, and nine in 10 of those saw growth of 10% or more. Almost half put the annual cost of chargebacks at 0.5% to 1% of revenue. Friendly fraud is a large part of the problem: 43% believe it accounts for more than a quarter of their chargebacks.

"Most merchants have added AI to a manual process," said Dan Moshkovich, VP of Go-to-Market at Chargeflow. "It helps them win more of the disputes they file, but people still decide which ones to file, so they fight the same small share. That's the next step for AI in chargebacks: knowing which disputes are worth fighting and building a case for each one."

Merchants want evidence of higher win rates to justify AI investment

Asked what would justify spending on AI for chargebacks, 39% of merchants chose a higher win rate and 34% chose more revenue recovered. Fewer than one in five chose time saved or lower costs. Most want a big gain before they buy: 69% said a tool would need to lift their win rate by at least 11 percentage points, taking a merchant that wins 30% of its disputes to 41% or more. AI hasn't cleared that bar yet: AI users report a median win rate 10.25 percentage points higher than non-users, just short of the 11-point mark. Juniper Research concludes that "while AI has delivered incremental improvements, it is not yet enough to meet enterprise expectations." When choosing a provider, 72% rated the quality of its data as very important and 70% said the same of security and compliance, against 31% for total cost.

Of respondents, 77% said chargebacks rise over the December holidays. Most are still optimistic: 58% expect lower chargeback losses in the 2026 season than in 2025. Chargeflow's own data shows what changes over that peak. The Chargeflow Data Lab, launched Oct. 22, studied about 3 million disputes across the last three peak seasons. Over the 2025 Black Friday to Cyber Monday weekend, 34.7% of chargebacks said the order never arrived, up from 19.9% in a normal week. Fraud claims fell to 27.2% from 37.8%, and the same shift appeared in 2023, 2024 and 2025.

As AI matures, chargeback tools will be judged on whether they turn intelligence into better decisions and measurable financial gains," said Nick Maynard, VP of Research, Juniper Research. "The opportunity is not simply to automate chargeback management. It is to make chargeback management materially more effective."

Juniper Research surveyed 121 C-level executives and senior managers at U.S. and U.K. businesses with at least $100 million in annual gross merchandise value in September 2026: 80 in the U.S. and 41 in the U.K. The full report is available at chargeflow.io/data-lab/the-state-of-chargeback-management.
