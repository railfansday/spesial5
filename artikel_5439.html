After 7 Years and 99% Speed Leap, Solana (SOL) Finally Drops Its 'Beta' Label

Solana has officially removed the "beta" tag from its mainnet's name and introduced the Solana 1.0 standard. The switch was announced on Nov. 16 at the Breakpoint conference in London.

"Solana 1.0 marks the network's graduation from 'mainnet beta' to 'mainnet,' retiring the beta designation it has carried since launch," the Solana Foundation said.

Solana launched on March 16, 2020, and the network had been working under the "mainnet-beta" label for six years and eight months. According to the foundation, the label "never evolved to signal its production status."

The Solana Foundation coordinated the rollout with the Anza and Firedancer teams. Co-founder Anatoly Yakovenko replied to the announcement on X with three exploding-head emojis.

"For users, this is the same network, nothing changes. Your SOL, accounts, assets, applications, and transaction history remain the same," the foundation wrote. On Monday, SOL is trading at $71.35.

According to the foundation, two upgrades made the change possible. In October, Solana activated Alpenglow with the Agave 4.3 release, and the time to final confirmation went down by almost 99%, from 12.8 seconds to around 150 milliseconds.

Firedancer, a validator client developed by Jump Crypto independently from Agave, is also now running on the Solana mainnet. Independent implementations "limit the impact of bugs affecting one client, and strengthen network resilience," the foundation said.

Solana 1.0 also gives the network a shared protocol version, and it does not depend on the version numbers of individual clients such as Agave and Firedancer.

"A shared protocol version lets everyone refer to Solana's evolution without using one client's release number as shorthand for the entire network," the Solana Foundation explained. "Announcing a protocol version does not activate every associated improvement at once."

Earlier this year, Solana raised the compute limit per block from 60 million to 100 million compute units in May, and the slot time went down from 400 to 200 milliseconds in August. The maximum transaction size also went up from 1,232 to 4,096 bytes.

"Documentation, websites, explorers, and other ecosystem surfaces will progressively reflect the updated designation," the foundation added.
