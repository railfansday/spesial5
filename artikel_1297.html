England manager Thomas Tuchel has admitted that the Premier League’s financial strength can make it difficult for young English players to break through, but insists there are still several pathways to the highest level.

Speaking ahead of England’s UEFA Nations League clash with Croatia at Wembley on Thursday, Tuchel was asked by GIVEMESPORT reporter Sam Cohen, whether the Premier League’s ability to recruit established international stars was making it increasingly difficult for homegrown players to secure regular first-team opportunities.

The question came following Jobe Bellingham’s first senior England call-up, with the midfielder having continued his development in Germany with Borussia Dortmund.

Tuchel, who previously managed Borussia Dortmund and Bayern Munich, acknowledged the potential difficulties but stressed that every player’s journey is different.

“Yes and no, it can be, but I think everyone has to find his own way,” Tuchel explained.

“I think there are enough examples where you go through the pathway and you find your way, and you’re at the right moment, in the right club, with the right coach, and things just fall your way.”

The England boss also highlighted the importance of timing and opportunity, suggesting that factors outside a young player’s control can have a significant influence on their development.

Don’t forget, you also need a lot of luck, sometimes an injury from an established player, sometimes a coach that just trusts you or knows you from the pathway.

“There are so many different ways to make it to the top, and so few players really make it.”

Bellingham has followed a similar route to his older brother Jude, who also left English football to develop at Dortmund before establishing himself as one of Europe’s leading midfielders.

Tuchel believes the environment at the Bundesliga club provides an excellent platform for young players to develop under pressure.

“Jobe went a bit of a similar way, like Jude, and went abroad and got his next step of development in the Bundesliga, in a very emotional, proper football club like Dortmund, where you’re under pressure to perform.

“To perform for the club and for the city means a lot, and they play with a lot of pride. So it’s a good school, a good school of development and his way.”

The England manager was also full of praise for Bellingham’s progress through the international youth setup, revealing that the midfielder has already impressed during his first senior training sessions.

Jobe Bellingham record for Borussia Dortmund

He said: “Jobe deserves to be here because he had an excellent spell over a long period of time with the U21s as a captain, and with Dortmund he played a strong season.

“You could see it, he had two good training sessions already. So it’s good to have him, good to see him.”

Tuchel’s comments underline his belief that there is no single route to international football, with Bellingham’s move abroad demonstrating how young English players can establish themselves outside the Premier League.
