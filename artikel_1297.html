Rio Ngumoha dazzles on coming-of-age night in Europe

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

There will be many more Champions League nights for Rio Ngumoha, although the 18-year-old will always remember this career landmark when his teenage skill plunged the opposition into fearful indecision and decided the game.

This young man has not yet played 40 senior games but there is no doubting the quality. Alexis Mac Allister scored twice, Liverpool came from two goals behind, and it all ended with a panic that meant they came close to chucking it away again. But what everyone could agree on from this strange match of fluctuating fortunes was that Ngumoha looks like one of the best young players in Europe. He is also a bona fide two-sided winger, winning this game for Liverpool from the right wing rather than his conventional left-wing role.

Ngumoha created the second Liverpool goal for Ronald Araújo and then the third for Mac Allister with the game’s best moment. That was an electrifying run into a group of converging yellow shirts in which he beat two men and still retained the composure to pick out Mac Allister in the area. Even as the Argentine ran to the Kop to celebrate, that stand was chanting the name of the teenager from east London. Ngumoha was one of three academy boys in Andoni Iraola’s XI, along with Lewis Koumas and Trey Nyoni, and all had an impact.

Iraola noted later that Ngumoha had taken care of the defensive work too against Sergi Cardona, an attacking left-back who scored his team’s second goal. From an attacking point of view, Iraola agreed, Ngumoha had periods of the game when he looked unstoppable. “If we can put Rio in situations one-v-one, he makes plays, [and] he produces things,” Iraola said. “The assist for Alexis is a lovely play. He could go down for a penalty but he decides to continue which is a great decision. Also endurance-wise he is able to play more minutes.”

He becomes the youngest Liverpool player in Champions League history to register an assist in the competition. For Iraola it will be hard to leave Ngumoha out of the game against in-form Brighton and Hove Albion on Sunday. “I believe in myself to do it,” Ngumoha said to Amazon Prime TV. “The more chances we get, the more it makes the opposition worry. I just keep going and be ruthless.”

Iraola would later bring on the three £100m attackers whom he had left on the bench, with Sunday’s game in mind. Florian Wirtz, a half-time arrival, scored the fourth and Bradley Barcola and Alexander Isak would later come on. Hard to call how it might go at home to Brighton. Liverpool’s defending was erratic at times, but their comeback was impressive.

Iraola made major changes at half-time, substituting Araújo and playing without a conventional right-back for the second half. It was brave and changed the momentum of the game, pushing his team higher up the pitch in what looked like a man-to-man marking arrangement. It enabled Iraola’s players to deny Villarreal the space and possession they had enjoyed in the first half when they had taken a two-goal lead – chiefly because of the mistakes of the home team. However much that was destabilising for Liverpool, by half-time they were level.

“They are so good on the ball,” Iraola said of Villarreal afterwards. Indeed, Villarreal’s manager Iñigo Pérez, a former Athletic team-mate of the Liverpool manager, was a coach whom Iraola sought to appoint at Bournemouth. “People probably don’t rate them [Villarreal],” Iraola said, “but after Barça they are probably the best in La Liga. They have 61 per cent of possession. Probably they have not had the results they deserve. If you are not really aggressive, you are not going to get the ball.

“We didn’t take as much risk. We were struggling because it is not a team that you can just press a bit. Either you go all the way or you will struggle without the ball.” Once Liverpool had done so, he said, his team had “won a lot of duels”. He was right in that respect. With the space squeezed, it was the power of Liverpool’s midfield that was telling.

Virgil van Dijk gave the first goal to Villarreal’s small No 9 Georges Mikautadze who had looked up to that point like he was hopelessly mismatched against the Liverpool captain and Jérémy Jacquet. Van Dijk’s misjudged header back to Alisson let in the striker, who did well to gather the ball and then hit it early. The second Villarreal goal at the midpoint of the first half was a mistake from Kostas Tsimikas, who failed to step up and played both Mikautadze and Cardona onside from Nathan Saliba’s ball. Either might have tucked it in but it was the left-back Cardona who took it on.
