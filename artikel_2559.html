Raoni Barcelos reveals his UFC collapse remains a medical mystery nearly 2 months later

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Raoni Barcelos faced one of the biggest scares in UFC history in his last fight.

This past September, the bantamweight veteran headlined a UFC event for the first time in his 16-fight run with the promotion, taking on rising contender Raul Rosas Jr. in a Fight of the Year-caliber tilt. Barcelos was pushing the pace against Rosas and winning on the judges' scorecards going into the fifth and final round. Once Rosas shot in for an early takedown, however, Barcelos collapsed without resistance and went inexplicably unconscious, losing the fight via what was declared a slam knockout.

Barcelos, 39, was stretchered out of the arena and unconscious all the way until he came to in the ambulance, he said Monday on "The Ariel Helwani Show." Nearly two months after the incident, he says he's feeling great and getting better every day, but still has no idea what exactly happened.

"I still don't understand it, honestly," Barcelos told Uncrowned. "I was doing really well in the fight. I was strong, I was super conscious. It was a tough, high-paced fight, but all of a sudden, I had a sudden illness, a sudden medical episode, and I crashed and passed out.

"We did every exam possible and ruled out a stroke or anything more serious, but to be honest, I still don't understand what happened, because I think I was doing great in the fight."

In the days that followed, Barcelos' condition was unclear but he remained in the hospital for testing. He claims whatever happened to him wasn't as dangerous as it seemed.

"I didn't have any serious episode or anything like that that would be life-threatening, or anything like that," Barcelos said.

"I think it was just a bodily function. I can't explain it. My body just shut off, but honestly, I'm a man of faith. I have a lot of faith in God, and it just kind of feels like this is how it was supposed to be."

After enduring a tough 1-4 stretch in the UFC from 2021 to 2023, Barcelos turned things around and won five straight fights, including collisions with former champion Cody Garbrandt and fast-rising contender Payton Talbott. He had the most momentum of his career heading into the Rosas bout.

Barcelos carried that momentum into the fight and it showed in his performance. Presumably, the physical toll of the back-and-forth, chaotic fight led to Barcelos shutting down late. Prior to to that final moment, he says he still remembers everything.

The only blank spot for Barcelos was from when he passed out until coming to in the ambulance with his father.

"I didn't feel anything before that," Barcelos said. "I felt great and strong throughout the fight, and then when Raul pulled me up against the cage in the fifth round, I just kind of felt my body like, freeze, and just kind of went out.

"I started the round well. I felt like I was strong. He came towards me, I took him down. I mean, everything was going super well; I didn't feel off at all. In fact, I felt really strong and well in the fight."

In the aftermath of the incident, Barcelos admitted that he feels different physically. But he believes that could be attributed to his current medical suspension, which prevents him from training at his usual rate.

"The feeling, honestly, is different from all the other fights, given everything that went down. But in my mind, I want to get back," Barcelos said. "I'm looking forward to getting back. I'm doing everything, all the protocols that the UFC doctor and PI stipulated for me. I have a six-month suspension where I'm not working out or doing anything. I don't want to get my heart rate up above 150. I'm doing weekly check-ins with the PI and giving them my heart rate and all my vitals and all of that.

"It's obviously a different feeling just because of everything that went down, but I'm really focused on going through this time, recovering, and looking forward to coming back."

Barcelos' episode was one of the more horrific scenes in MMA history, and thankfully it ended without the worst possible outcome.

Unsurprisingly, Barcelos is hopeful to return to action and someday end his career on a better note than how he last went out. Right now, his father and those around him have supported his decision to keep fighting, but they still want him to take things day by day.

Regarding concerns about getting licensed to compete after such a scene, Barcelos doesn't see why it should be a problem if he meets all the medical requirements.

"I think that if I follow everything correctly and then we do all these exams and everything looks good, I don't see why not, why I wouldn't be licensed," he said.

"This is my job. This is what I've always done. Obviously, I want to continue working. I have a home, I have a family to support, and I have fans that want to watch me fight. I think that if everything goes well and if everything goes as expected, I don't see why I wouldn't be licensed."
