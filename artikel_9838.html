Inception - possibly a ShinyHunters reboot - claims seven victims in a week

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Whatever they call themselves these days: be on the lookout for voice phishing attempts

Don't mind the recent arrests. The ShinyHunters extortion gang may still be operating, just under the new name of Inception, according to Okta's threat intelligence group.

ShinyHunters is the brash cybercriminal group that, most recently, breached rival gang Clop before hacking the FBI, resulting in at least three arrests of key suspects in September and October. The crims - and their old data leak site - have since gone dark.

“While this threat cluster has changed their brand affiliation, the operators remain active in targeting organizations with voice phishing attacks and account takeovers resulting in data theft and extortion,” Okta threat hunters Moussa Diallo and Katie Nickels said on Monday.

Okta tracks this threat cluster as O-UNC-053 - the “O-UNC” prefix stands for an Okta Uncategorized cluster, and it uses this label before formally attributing the activity to a named threat group.

While Okta hasn't formally confirmed O-UNC-053 to be the same crime crew as ShinyHunters, the identity and access management provider’s researchers note that it does use nearly identical social engineering tactics: voice phishing, during which the scammers trick targets into entering credentials into phishing pages and approving multi-factor authentication (MFA).

Plus, they note: O-UNC-053 operators change the themes of their phishing domains about every four to six weeks, and they also post claimed victims on ShinyHunters’ data leak site. In November, however, organizations targeted by O-UNC-053 began appearing on a new data leak site branded as Inception and not ShinyHunters.

The first five - Advanced Micro Devices, Cigna Healthcare, Paychex, PepsiCo, and Henry Schein - appeared November 17 on the site, and all were marked “leaked.” Soon after, security maven Dominic Alvieri questioned if Inception was linked to ShinyHunters. “ShinyHunters are not all in jail,” he wrote on social media.

On November 19, AutoZone appeared on the site, with Inception again claiming to have leaked the auto-parts retailer's files. On Saturday, EV technology firm Lucid Group joined the claimed victims.

None of the seven companies have confirmed breaches, and none responded to The Register’s inquiries. We will update this story when we receive more information.

Okta Threat Intelligence “is unable to validate if published data is authentic,” the researchers said in response to our questions.

In the Monday report, the Okta team does note that Inception’s new data leak site listings “included multiple targets for which Okta Threat Intelligence previously observed O-UNC-053 infrastructure registration.”

The infrastructure was registered between six and 19 days before Inception listed the companies on its leak site, and this gap “is consistent with prior shifts from Com-affiliated actors,” Diallo and Nickels added.

The Com (short for community) is a loosely knit network of online groups whose young members participate in a range of illicit activities including hacking, swatting, digital extortion, real-life shootings, and other physical violence. Many security researchers consider ShinyHunters to be connected to The Com, along with groups such as Scattered Spider and LAPSUS$, with overlapping memberships and shared infrastructure.

“Before and after the change in DLS publication, O-UNC-053 maintained consistent infrastructure and TTPs, leading us to assess that the cluster has very likely changed their brand affiliation,” the Okta researchers wrote. “We cannot currently make a high confidence assessment as to whether O-UNC-053 is operating the DLS themselves or is partnering with another extortion actor.”

Regardless, members of the group formerly known as ShinyHunters and/or O-UNC-053 are still roaming the internet, looking for new victims, and conducting voice phishing attacks to steal data and then extort victims' companies for exorbitant sums of money.
