France’s Senate Rejects Rule Forcing Bitcoin Holders to Declare Self-Custody Wallets

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

France’s Senate has rejected a proposal that would have forced bitcoin holders to report self-custody wallets worth €100,000 or more to the tax authorities, along with two other crypto tax measures in the 2027 budget.

Senators voted down two identical amendments on Nov. 24 during debate on the first part of the 2027 finance bill. Both would have required taxpayers to declare crypto wallets they hold directly, outside a crypto service provider, when the total value inside reached at least €100,000 on Dec. 31 of the tax year.

The first, amendment I-2447, came from Sens. Pascal Savoldelli and Pierre Barros and the Communist-led CRCE-K group. The second, amendment I-2992, came from Sen. Anne-Sophie Romagny and seven other senators. The government opposed both, and both were rejected.

Backers argued that France already makes taxpayers declare accounts on foreign crypto platforms, just like foreign bank accounts. Wallets that holders manage themselves, without an intermediary, escape any such obligation.

“They have become the digital equivalent of the undeclared safe,” the CRCE-K group wrote in its amendment, adding that tax authorities can’t see them, check gains on them or reach them in an estate or when a holder moves abroad.

Romagny’s version pointed to a gap in the numbers. Crypto holders in France realized €3.5 billion in gains in 2021, but only €400 million in digital assets were declared to the tax administration that same year, the amendment said. It proposed fines of up to €10,000 for failing to declare.

The National Assembly had approved the same wallet rule on Nov. 3. It fell away the next day, when deputies rejected the entire revenue section of the budget. Senators then took up the same measure as an amendment to the budget.

Senators also rejected two other proposals from the CRCE-K group. Amendment I-2421 would have ended France’s tax deferral on swaps into electronic money tokens under the EU’s MiCA rules. That would have made converting bitcoin into a regulated stablecoin a taxable event.

Amendment I-2428 would have pulled crypto into France’s exit tax on unrealized gains for people who move their tax home abroad. It counted all crypto toward the €800,000 threshold, including coins held in self-custody or outside France.

David Amiel, France’s minister for public action and accounts, argued against the wallet rule, saying the EU’s DAC8 directive already puts reporting duties on crypto firms. He said he was “more circumspect” about a “purely declarative” obligation that would not help authorities track crypto used in money laundering or tax fraud.

The Senate’s finance committee also opposed the measures, and rapporteur general Christine Lavarde recommended rejecting all three. Lavarde noted that the wallet amendments drew on a recommendation from a Senate information report filed in June, and she left the question of the threshold to the government.

Holders have also raised safety concerns. In August, bitcoiners were warned of wrench attacks after France’s tax authority confirmed a data breach affecting hundreds of thousands of people.

Reporting rules for platforms are still expanding. The National Assembly is set to debate approval of the OECD’s Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework, or CARF, which shares crypto tax data between countries, on Dec. 7.

The wallet fight is not fully over. The Senate is still working through the 2027 budget and is set to vote on the full bill on Dec. 9, before a joint committee of both chambers takes it up.
