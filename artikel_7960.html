Most of us have a side to our personality that we don't show the world every day, though, and these plants are the perfect way to shine a light on our dark side.

These remarkable plants are far from ordinary. From the Venus flytrap's sudden snapping jaws to pitcher plants luring in unsuspecting insects, carnivorous plants have developed some ingenious ways to survive. Some are bold and imposing, others are subtle and deceptive, and each and every single one has its own strategy for getting what it needs.

So how does this tie into you? Well, from birth month birds to birth month animals, nature offers us countless ways to reflect on our character. The lens of carnivorous plants, though? Why, that adds a much more intriguing twist! Behind ambition, for example, there may be a need for control. Sensitivity can sometimes turn into possessiveness, while confidence can tip into arrogance. Even our most admirable qualities can have a less flattering side... and there's no point denying it.

Now, we’re already familiar with the idea of a birth month owl, birth month tree, and birth month spider (oh yes, I'm deadly serious!), but carnivorous plants perfectly pair with the traits, instincts and impulses that sit beneath the surface of your everyday personality.

You might be determined to the point of stubborn, much like the Venus flytrap; you might, like the bold pitcher plant, have a more domineering side hidden away. Secrecy, perfectionism or an almost obsessive need to prove a point? Well, sometimes it's worth reflecting on the less flattering habits you might not always recognise in yourself.

Ready to meet your botanical shadow self? Let's dive on in...

January babies like to believe they're in control, but their biggest weakness may be their need to have things go their way. No wonder, then, that the Venus flytrap is their birth month carnivorous plant.

Like this iconic houseplant, you know when to strike, and once you've made up your mind, you can be remarkably difficult to sway. You may have little patience for people who waste your time or challenge your authority.

Your dark side emerges when determination becomes stubbornness and protecting your boundaries turns into shutting people out. You don't always give second chances, especially when you feel someone has crossed you. Why not buy yourself a Venus flytrap from Amazon, then, and spend some time watching it and reflecting on what it says about you?

February belongs to the fanged pitcher plant, which feels wholly appropriate. If this is you, then your imagination is one of your greatest strengths, but it can also help you become someone others struggle to read.

Like your birth month carnivorous plant, you know how to draw people in with something fascinating, unusual or irresistible. You may enjoy being unpredictable, keeping parts of yourself hidden and making others work to understand you. And, at your darkest, you can use charm to get your way, leaving people wondering whether they ever really knew you at all.

March babies notice what other people feel, sometimes before they even say it. That sensitivity can make you compassionate, but it can also give you an advantage you don't always use wisely.

Like the sundew plant, you can appear delicate and harmless while holding on tighter than anyone realises. As such, you may struggle to let go of hurt, replaying conversations and remembering every slight. And, when you're wounded, you might use silence, guilt or emotional distance to make others feel the weight of what they've done.

You have a powerful presence and rarely enjoy being told what to do. Indeed, much like the purple pitcher plant, you can draw attention without trying, but your confidence sometimes hides a tendency to rush headfirst into situations you haven't fully considered.

You may mistake resistance for a challenge and disagreement for disrespect. As such, your darker side appears when your need to win becomes more important than understanding others, because you'd rather take a spectacular risk than admit you might be wrong.

Why not treat yourself to a purple pitcher plant from the Ragnaroc Store, then? They're colorful and a cute way to keep pests at bay, not to mention a great way to sit and reflect on the qualities you keep hidden from the world.

If this is your birth month carnivorous plant, you prefer stability... but that doesn't mean you're always as easygoing as you appear.

Like the butterwort, your strategy is subtle: you don't need to chase what you want when you can let it come to you. No wonder, then, that you hide frustration behind politeness, hold grudges long after an argument has ended or quietly keep score when you feel underappreciated.

Your shadow side emerges when patience becomes a way to avoid confrontation and kindness becomes a silent expectation of loyalty in return. Hey, that's not a bad thing; there's a reason this pretty houseplant is so good at killing fungus gnats. Time to bag yourself some butterwort seeds from Amazon, maybe?

Are you a June baby? Say hello to your (very unusual) carnivorous plant!

Always curious about people, you thrive on connection... but you may become restless when the excitement fades. Like the spoon-leaved sundew, then, you attract attention through a delicate and inviting appearance while being far more complicated underneath.

You might tell different people different versions of yourself – not out of malice, but because you want to be understood and liked by everyone. This explains why your darker side appears when the need for validation makes your loyalties unpredictable and your promises easier to break.

Born in the height of summer, July babies care deeply about the people they let into your world, but their loyalty can have a dangerous edge.

If your birth month carnivorous plant is the cobra lily, you present a striking warning to anything that gets too close, and sometimes may convince yourself that you know what's best for the people you love, even when they haven't asked for your help.

No surprises, then, that your shadow side emerges when protection becomes control, jealousy disguises itself as concern and you struggle to accept that the people you love have the right to make mistakes without your permission. Time to pick yourself up some cobra lily seeds from Seedville, then, and pour some love into it.

August belongs to the white-tipped pitcher plant, which means that you (much like your carnivorous friend here) know how to make an impression, and you rarely enjoy being overlooked. Indeed, much like the white-topped pitcher plant, you have a dramatic presence that naturally draws the eye.

Beneath that confidence, though? Well, you may be more dependent on admiration than you care to admit. You might turn everything into a competition, struggle when someone else takes the spotlight, or dismiss criticism as jealousy. Perhaps, then, your darkest tendency is believing (strike that: knowing!) you're exceptional means you should always get your way.

September's birth month carnivorous plant is the rabbit-eared bladderwort, which is incredibly fitting. After all, you notice everything: the inconsistency in someone's story, the mistake everyone else missed, the detail that doesn't quite add up.

Like the water-dwelling bladderwort, your most interesting mechanisms operate largely out of sight. You may appear helpful while mentally cataloguing other people's flaws but, when things don't meet your standards, frustration can turn into criticism, and careful planning can become an obsession with controlling every possible outcome.

Your shadow side, then, is the belief that if you can find a flaw in everything, nothing can ever catch you off guard. An oddly comforting notion, right?

Halloween month (this author's birth month, no less!) belongs to the bowl pitcher plant, a particularly fitting match for anyone who happens to be a little, or a lottle, emotionally evasive.

Oh sure, you know how to keep the peace, but sometimes harmony matters to you more than honesty. Like the bowl pitcher plant, you can thrive in ways that others don't immediately understand; think concealing your real feelings, avoiding difficult conversations or telling yourself that keeping your options open is better than making a decision.

Your darker side emerges when diplomacy becomes avoidance and your desire not to upset anyone leaves others guessing where they truly stand with you.

November babies get a twist on a classic carnivorous plant, in the form of an all-red variety of Venus flytrap. Why? Well, because you rarely feel things halfway. When someone earns your trust, you can be fiercely loyal; when they betray it, you may find forgiveness far more difficult.

Like the Red Dragon Venus flytrap, your dark coloring conceals a mechanism that reacts decisively when triggered. You might dwell on betrayal, replay old arguments or become fixated on proving that someone underestimated you, which means your shadow side appears when you stop seeking closure and start wanting the other person to feel the same hurt you experienced. Eek.

Last but not least, December belongs to the corkscrew plant, which means you are something of an escapist as you crave discovery, freedom and the feeling that something exciting is just around the corner.

Like your birth month carnivorous plant, whose unusual traps lead prey through a one-way route, your own curiosity can take you further than you intended. You may chase new experiences whenever life becomes predictable, abandon plans when they start to feel restrictive or use humor to dodge uncomfortable truths.

Occasionally, your darker side emerges when freedom becomes an excuse to run from responsibility, as every new beginning becomes a way to avoid finishing what you started.

Whichever birth month carnivorous plant you are, and whatever shadowy trait it holds bare to the light, rest assured that you are still a wonderful person (just check out all of your amazingly positive traits in your classic birth month flower).

Still, there's no denying that it can be fun to embrace our dark sides every once in a while... especially if it helps us embrace a whole new world of plants in the meantime. All hail, then, the bitey side of the botanical world! Sometimes, it's good to be bad...
