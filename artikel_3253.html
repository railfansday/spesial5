Warren Buffett's Successor, Greg Abel, Has 71% of Berkshire Hathaway's $362 Billion Portfolio Invested in 7 Standout Stocks

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Berkshire’s new boss is overseeing a concentrated portfolio, with $255 billion invested across seven proven winners.

It's been a year of historic change for Berkshire Hathaway (BRKA -1.45%)(BRKB -1.17%) and its shareholders. Longtime CEO Warren Buffett retired at the end of last year, handing the reins to the company's day-to-day operations and its closely followed $362 billion investment portfolio to Greg Abel.

But while some things have changed -- Abel dumped 16 of Berkshire's non-core positions in the first quarter -- one core principle has remained the same: portfolio concentration. As of the end of October, seven standout stocks accounted for nearly 71% ($255.2 billion) of Berkshire's portfolio:

Several of Warren Buffett's legacy holdings remain pillars of Berkshire Hathaway's portfolio. Image source: Getty Images.

Despite Warren Buffett retiring as CEO, several of his core or "indefinite" holdings have remained pillars in Berkshire Hathaway's portfolio, including American Express, Coca-Cola, and Bank of America.

Coca-Cola and Amex are the two longest-held stocks in Berkshire's portfolio (since 1988 and 1991, respectively), and two of the companies Buffett referred to as "indefinite" holdings in his 2023 annual letter to shareholders. Aside from both companies possessing well-defined competitive advantages, they're generating jaw-dropping yields of 65% for Coca-Cola and 45% for Amex, relative to Berkshire's cost basis for each company.

Though Bank of America isn't guaranteed to stick around under Abel's leadership, its share price premium to book value has notably declined over the last two months, and it's ideally positioned to take advantage of a rising interest-rate environment.

Even though the Oracle of Omaha sold 75% of iPhone maker Apple's shares in the nine quarters leading up to his retirement, it remains the undisputed No. 1 holding by market value. Aside from Apple's market-leading $879 billion in cumulative share repurchases since the start of 2013, Abel appreciates its innovative capacity and believes it can be a multidecade compounder.

But since taking the reins in January, Abel has found a new "Apple" of his eye. While Buffett initiated Berkshire's stake in Google's parent Alphabet, it's Abel who's been relentlessly building this position through the first half of 2026.

Although Alphabet possesses the sustainable moat that both Buffett and Abel love -- Google accounts for about 90% of global internet traffic -- it's the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) solutions within Google Cloud that has Berkshire's new boss excited. The substantially higher-margin Google Cloud more than doubled sales in the latest quarter.

Lastly, energy remains a core theme, with Chevron and Occidental Petroleum collectively accounting for more than 10% of Berkshire Hathaway's invested assets.

While both positions were entered well before Abel became CEO, don't forget that Abel spent several years as CEO of MidAmerican Energy (later renamed Berkshire Hathaway Energy). He has a keen understanding of the global energy market and the growing demand for energy solutions in the wake of the AI data center build-out.

With Warren Buffett labeling Occidental as an "indefinite" holding, it's unlikely to be sold.
