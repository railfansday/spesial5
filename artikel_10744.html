Principal makes chess mandatory for every sixth grader in hopes it’ll improve their failing grades

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

A New Jersey principal has made chess lessons mandatory for every sixth grader at his school in hopes of improving students’ test scores and critical thinking skills.

Richard L. Wilson Jr., principal of Benjamin Banneker Preparatory Charter School, has introduced compulsory chess lessons twice a week across the school’s campuses in Westampton and Willingboro.

According to The Philadelphia Inquirer, students must learn how to play chess by the end of the school year, with Wilson believing the game can help improve academic performance.

“I just want them to be excited about playing chess,” Wilson said. “Anything to get them enthused.”

The decision comes as the school looks to improve its academic performance following disappointing standardized test results.

During the 2024-25 school year, just 16.4% of students demonstrated proficiency in math, while 37.5% met the required standard in language arts. Both figures were below New Jersey’s statewide averages.

Wilson believes chess can help students develop problem-solving abilities, patience, discipline, and strategic thinking that could translate into better classroom performance.

The program initially began as an optional activity during recess, but proved so popular that students were voluntarily skipping their breaks to play.

It has since expanded into a mandatory part of the sixth-grade curriculum, with the school’s official website highlighting chess as a way to develop decision-making and perseverance.

Students receive lessons covering everything from chess etiquette and the movement of pieces to more advanced strategies.

There are no grades for the lessons, although teachers monitor students’ progress. Wilson also regularly challenges students to games, with anyone who beats the principal having their name announced over the school’s PA system.

The school isn’t alone in experimenting with games as teaching tools.

Earlier in 2026, a teacher went viral for bringing a PS5 to class to teach history using Assassin’s Creed, while Valorant has also been incorporated into school lessons focused on teamwork and strategy.

However, research into whether chess improves academic results remains mixed. A 2018 study involving more than 4,000 children in England found no evidence that chess instruction improved math, reading, or science test scores.

For now, Wilson is hoping the program will encourage students to enjoy learning while improving their skills.

Students can also compete in an optional chess tournament at the end of the school year, where the winner will receive a trophy and a $300 cash prize.
