Projected 2026-27 cap hits for 20 NHL teams, from $101.66 million to $112.97 million, against a $104 million salary cap.

A general manager can scout, draft, and trade with skill, yet one number decides how many of those moves a club can afford to keep. The NHL salary cap sets the outer edge of every roster, and each contract a team signs claims part of the space beneath it. Hockey payroll behaves like a container of fixed size. Every new deal crowds the ones that came before it, and a club with little room left has fewer ways to add a player, retain one, or recover from a misjudged signing. Fans who follow trade rumors and contract talks are watching teams bargain with that ceiling.

The 2026-27 season limits spending at $104 million and sets the floor at $76.9 million, a band of $27.1 million between the least a franchise must lay out and the most it may. Franchises near the bottom of that band have money to deploy on acquisitions and free agents. Those pressed against the top carry obligations, and every dollar such a franchise commits to its current group becomes a dollar unavailable for the next addition. Those commitments reach past a single year. Agreements a front office reaches today shape budgets in seasons that have yet to begin. A crowded ledger in 2026-27 can narrow an organization's options for several summers.

PuckPedia gathers all 32 NHL teams into one table for 2026-27, setting each projected cap hit beside cap space, dead cap, contract count, active roster size, average age, and the cost of forwards, defensemen, and goalies. The site notes that its summary updates in real time, and the page gives no date for its figures. Each total here reflects a moment that could shift with the next transaction. The table offers no ranking of its own. This list sorts 20 of those teams by projected cap hit, from highest to lowest, and each one projects above $101 million. Every position reflects that ordering alone.
