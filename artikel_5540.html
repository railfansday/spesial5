The Food and Drug Administration on Monday approved a new breast cancer drug from Roche, teeing up the launch of a medicine that the Swiss company has said could become its top-selling product ever.

The approval of Wilserdly, also known as giredestrant, was one of the most important regulatory decisions Roche had been awaiting. It also gives the company another foothold in breast cancer treatments, a field in which it has a storied history with the drug Herceptin through its subsidiary Genentech, though in recent years it’s fallen behind rival pharma companies in oncology.

The approval is specifically for patients with HR-positive, HER2-negative advanced breast cancer that has picked up a mutation in the ESR1 gene, a sign that the tumor is developing resistance to standard treatments. The evERA trial showed that the drug, an oral treatment known as a selective estrogen receptor degrader, or SERD, extended the time period before patients’ tumors progressed.
