Vincent Kompany says he is back in “very good health” as he prepares to make his return to the Bayern Munich dugout for the first time since collapsing at training.

Kompany was hospitalised after collapsing at a reserve team training session on 23 October and was diagnosed with meningeal irritation, an inflammation of the tissue around the brain or spinal cord.

He missed five Bayern games as the club prioritised his health, with assistant coach Aaron Danks taking the reins and going unbeaten during that period.

But after returning to the club’s training ground during the international break, Kompany is raring to make his managerial comeback and take charge of his first game since drawing 2-2 with Arsenal a month ago.

“I was grateful for the support,” Kompany said. “It was very intense. At the time, it was a new experience. Looking back, everything went perfectly.

“I received immediate support from the club and from the doctors. I’m very grateful.

“I’m in very good health. We’ve done lots of tests, all of which have come back clear.”

The Belgian was sporting a sling on his left arm after injuring his shoulder during the collapse.

Kompany said the incident prompted him to think differently about his health and that he “perhaps didn't listen closely” enough to his body when he returned for the Arsenal game despite having battled a viral infection that week.

Nevertheless, he revealed he had fun watching his team from a “fascinating” new perspective as Danks took charge, and claimed that Bayern's 2-0 win over Atletico Madrid earlier this month “was one of the finest moments of my coaching career, even though I wasn’t there”.

“I’m coming off the longest holidays I ever had, it’s been the longest international break I ever had," Kompany said. “It happened, it can happen to everyone, but you can learn from it."

His first game back sees Bayern take on Koln on Friday evening, with the club top of the Bundesliga - ahead of Borussia Dortmund on goal difference.
