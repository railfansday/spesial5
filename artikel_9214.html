Apple’s Multi-Camera Smart Glasses Could Arrive by the End of 2027

Apple’s smart glasses will reportedly begin shipping before the end of 2027 and may have multiple cameras.

According to a new report by Bloomberg’s Mark Gurman, Apple plans to unveil its upcoming smart glasses at WWDC in 2027. The company hopes the device will be its flagship new product of the year.

“After the planned WWDC 2027 unveiling, glasses shipments would begin before the end of the year,” Gurman writes in his latest Power On newsletter for Bloomberg.

In terms of design, Apple’s internal prototypes are slightly thinner than Meta’s second-generation Ray-Ban glasses. Gurman also refers to multiple cameras on the company’s smart glasses, while current eyewear models on the market feature only one.

The first version of the glasses will not include augmented reality (AR), meaning they will not display digital information or images over the user’s view of the real world. Instead, Apple is relying on Siri AI and its extensive App Store ecosystem to help distinguish the product from competing smart glasses. The glasses will integrate with apps that people already use on their iPhones, removing the need for developers to create separate applications for the eyewear.

“Apple is counting on integration with the apps people already use on their iPhones to be a key differentiator for the glasses,” Gurman writes.

Apple is considering several ways Siri AI could work with third-party apps on the glasses. These include making restaurant reservations through an app like OpenTable, booking a ride through Uber or Lyft, ordering coffee through a Starbucks integration, shopping on Amazon, and purchasing movie tickets through an app like Fandango. Third-party applications will not run directly on Apple’s smart glasses, at least in the first version. Instead, they will remain on a paired iPhone, with the two devices working together to handle requests. The approach is similar to Apple’s original smartwatch strategy.

Under current plans, the glasses will require a recent iPhone and will not support Android devices. This arrangement is intended to compensate for the limited computing power and memory available in the eyewear. Apple is also placing an emphasis on industry leading audio quality, with the glasses expected to support phone calls, music playback, and FaceTime audio communication.

The glasses will also feature Wi-Fi, allowing some functions to continue working when the paired iPhone is not nearby. For example, users could continue listening to audio over Wi-Fi after walking away from their phones. However, the glasses will not operate as a fully independent device.
