Welcome to CricketNews.com's live coverage of the fourth ODI between New Zealand and India at Bay Oval, Mount Maunganui.

New Zealand posted 214/3 at the 30-over mark (and reached 218/4 by Drinks at 32.0 overs), as they maintained a blistering run rate above seven an over.

Dropped twice on 5 early in the innings, Will Young made India pay dearly, sitting on 97* off 84 balls at the end of Over 30 before reaching his hundred off 88 deliveries (11 fours, 3 sixes) right at the Drinks break.

Finn Allen raced to 41* off just 29 balls (5 fours, 2 sixes) at a strike rate of 141.37 to stitch an unbroken 89-run stand with Young at the 30-over mark.

Kuldeep Yadav endured a tough spell (0/39 in 3 overs) alongside Gurnoor Brar (1/61 in 7 overs), while Axar Patel (1/30 in 7 overs) remained India's most economical option.

After conceding 72 without loss in the first powerplay, India's bowlers hit back with three key dismissals between overs 12 and 20 to check New Zealand's momentum at 125/3.

Gurnoor Brar drew first blood on the final ball of the 12th over (11.6 ov), having a dangerous Tim Seifert (58 off 48, 6 fours, 3 sixes) caught behind by Ishan Kishan at 85/1.

Axar Patel deceived Nick Kelly (4 off 8) for Kishan to complete a stumping in the 15th over (14.2 ov), before Auqib Nabi returned to remove Dean Foxcroft (8 off 13), caught by Brar in the 20th over (19.3 ov).

Making India pay for dropping him twice on 5, Will Young anchored the innings through the middle phase to reach a brisk 49-ball half-century (6 fours, 2 sixes) right at the 20-over mark.

New Zealand capitalized on a sloppy Indian fielding display to race to 72/0 at the conclusion of Powerplay 1.

Auqib Nabi induced consecutive edges from Will Young while on 5, only for Rohit Sharma to shell a straightforward chance at first slip and keeper Ishan Kishan to drop a diving effort to his right on the very next delivery.

Tim Seifert punished the Indian attack, reaching his half-century off just 39 deliveries (5 fours, 3 sixes).

While Arshdeep Singh kept things relatively tidy (19 runs off 4.5 overs), Auqib Nabi (0/34 in 4 overs) and Gurnoor Brar (14 runs in his opening over) bore the brunt of Seifert's early aggression.

Toss result: India decided to bowl first.

New Zealand take on India in the fourth ODI at Mount Maunganui on Friday.

It has been seven years since they last lost an ODI series on their own soil, and India were the side that did it then.

That unbeaten run of 11 home series is already over, in the sense that the Black Caps have been unable to beat the same opponents in any of the three games so far. India won two of them, and the third was washed out, so the winning habit has been broken even though the series itself is still alive.

The maths is blunt for the hosts, as another defeat would give India the series with a game to spare. If New Zealand win both remaining matches, the series would finish level at 2-2 with one no result, which would keep their home record unbeaten.

India are going through a change at the top of the order of command. Shubman Gill has played his last game of the tour and will turn his attention to red-ball nets, so Shreyas Iyer takes over as captain.

The forecast is much better than the one that spoiled Hamilton, and both games on Friday and Sunday should be played in full.
