Apple faces potential $80 million fine and App Store overhaul in Chile

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Chile’s national competition authority, Fiscalía Nacional Económica (FNE), has filed an antitrust lawsuit against Apple, accusing the company of abusing its dominant position in iOS app distribution and in-app purchases. Here are the details.

As reported by Reuters, Chile’s competition watchdog is seeking an $80 million fine against Apple, along with a court order requiring a series of changes to the company’s App Store policies.

In its complaint, the FNE says (translation ours):

The defendant has violated Article 3, paragraphs one and two, subsection (b), of Decree Law 211 by abusing its dominant position through practices that prevent, restrict, or hinder free competition, or have the potential to do so, in the markets for app distribution and the distribution of paid digital goods within apps, both on the iOS operating system nationwide, from at least 2019 to the present day.

The FNE goes on to say that Apple forbids both sideloading and alternative app marketplaces, requires app developers to exclusively use the company’s In-App Purchase system to sell digital goods and services within their apps, and restricts their ability to inform users about or direct them to alternative payment methods.

It then goes on to claim that “these anticompetitive practices have allowed the defendant to ensure that the App Store is the only channel for downloading apps,” adding that this “has led to a degradation of the consumer experience and allowed Apple to secure profits that it would not obtain without such practices.”

That includes Apple’s agreement with Brazil’s competition authority, the European Union’s Digital Markets Act, the Epic Games v. Apple ruling in Australia, regulatory and judicial developments in Japan, and the lifting of anti-steering restrictions in the US.

At one point, the FNE even suggests that Apple knowingly maintained anticompetitive practices in the country, despite regulatory and judicial developments elsewhere:

Furthermore, there is evidence suggesting that the defendant was aware of the unlawfulness of its conduct, given the existence of precedents in other jurisdictions involving facts similar to those alleged in this complaint, which demonstrate the anticompetitive nature of the alleged practices. Nevertheless, Apple decided to maintain its restrictions in Chile, as well as in other jurisdictions where it has not been compelled to remove them or has not agreed with the relevant authority to eliminate or modify them.

After presenting its case, the FNE asks Chile’s antitrust court to:

Additionally, the FNE asks the court to require Apple to implement these measures “in good faith, such that it may not impose any other technical or contractual restrictions, or commercial conditions, that undermine their purpose,” to cease the alleged anticompetitive practices, and to cover the legal costs of the proceedings.

It also leaves room for the court to impose any additional measures it deems appropriate, particularly those aimed at facilitating access to alternative channels for app distribution and the sale of digital goods.
