Cannes 2026 Movies Sold: Kino Lorber Buys Clio Barnard’s BIFA Contender ‘I See Buildings Fall Like Lightning’

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Last year’s Cannes Film Festival saw Neon acquire its eventual Palme D’Or winner “It Was Just an Accident” and make splashes with “The Secret Agent” and “Sirât,” while Netflix bought “Nouvelle Vague” and “Left-Handed Girl.” But we also saw some titles like MUBI’s “Die, My Love” and “Sound of Falling” that didn’t make the same splash when they arrived stateside, despite big critical buzz out of the festival.

Neon came into Cannes with a glut of titles, and there weren’t many American films, but that didn’t slow the market. We’ll be tracking every sale as it happens.

Buyer: Kino LorberSection: Directors’ FortnightDirector: Clio BarnardBuzz: Kino Lorber has quietly been building up its slate with curated, compelling Cannes picks. Their latest: Clio Barnard’s Audience Award winner, based on Keiran Goddard’s novel of the same name. The film is packed with rising names, includig Anthony Boyle, Joe Cole, Jay Lycurgo, Daryl McCormack, and Lola Petticrew, and follows five friends as they contend with the unexpected realities of modern living, set against the UK housing crisis. The film was adapted by Irish playwright and screenwriter Enda Walsh. It has continued to screen on the international festival circuit, most recently at BFI London Film Festival, AFI Fest, and Rome Film Festival. It was also recently nominated for eight British Independent Film Awards, including best British independent film, best director, best screenplay, and best ensemble performance. Kino Lorber is planning a 2027 theatrical release followed by a digital, educational, and home video release.

Buyer: Sumerian PicturesSection: Directors’ FortnightDirector: Eivind LandsvikBuzz: Sumerian Pictures before it was a film distributor is a long time record label, and the still upstart film side has finally picked up a feature that has some music ties. “Low Expectations” is a Norwegian film that stars Marie Ulven, better known as the Norwegian indie bedroom singer girl in red. She stars in the film alongside “The Worst Person in the World” actor Anders Danielsen Lie as arguably a version of herself, a rising pop star whose growing success becomes harder to reconcile with her inner life, leading her to take a part-time job at a high school from her hometown. “Low Expectations” is Landsvik’s feature directorial debut after directing two shorts, one that was accepted into Cannes in 2023 called “Tits,” and another that won a Norwegian Film Critics prize, “Sofia, Last Summer” from 2021. The film actually opens this week, September 11 in Norway, but Sumerian will release it in North America in 2027.

Buyer: Independent Film Company and Sapan StudioSection: Out of CompetitionDirector: Leah NelsonBuzz: This hand-drawn animated black and white film has a stacked voice cast featuring Abbi Jacobson, Julia Louis-Dreyfus, Bryan Cranston, Beanie Feldstein, Seth Rogen, and Samira Wiley, as well as Pamela Adlon, Sarah Silverman, Bowen Yang, Wanda Sykes, Phil Rosenthal, Adam Shapiro, Pat Finn, and Sugar Lyn Beard. It’s adapted from a memoir/graphic novel and is a story about a mother and daughter struggling with an Alzheimer’s diagnosis. The film will first play in the Centrepiece section at TIFF, then the distributors will plan an awards season rollout late this year before opening wider in early 2027.

Buyer: KimStimSection: Un Certain RegardDirector: Katharina RivillisBuzz: Wim Wenders produced this debut film about a German exchange student living in New Mexico shortly after the 9/11 attacks on the U.S. and how the teenager begins to feel homesickness while abroad in the desert heat. KimStim is an upstart indie distributor, and it is planning a North American theatrical release in 2027.

Buyer: Zeitgeist Films in association with Kino LorberSection: Un Certain RegardDirector: Judith GodrècheBuzz: Based on a memoir by a Nobel Prize-winning author Annie Ernaux, “A Girl’s Story” follows Ernaux in 2020 on a book tour and then all the way back to her teen years in the summer of 1958 and is a reflection on the “cruelty of other girls and the bitterness of sexual initiation,” per the film’s synopsis. Our positive review called it a very faithful and loyal adaptation of the Nobel Laureate’s work and a strong entry among other recent Ernaux adaptations like “Happening” and “Simple Passion.” Godrèche, who was a figurehead in the French #MeToo movement, also directs her own daughter Tess Barthélemy in the lead role as the young Ernaux. Zeitgeist is planning a release at New York’s Film Forum in April 2027 followed by a national rollout.

Buyer: Kino LorberSection: CompetitionDirector: Rodrigo SorogoyenBuzz: Per IndieWire’s review by Ryan Lattanzio — Javier Bardem plays a hotheaded filmmaker, one of those “brilliant assholes” mostly incapable of making great art without wringing it out of tyrannical behavior, in Rodrigo Sorogoyen’s Spanish industry drama “The Beloved.” The director of a memorable 2019 movie called “Madre,” a warped tale of motherhood in which a woman strikes up an affair with a dead ringer of her long-lost son, now turns his camera (or cameras, as Sorogoyen experiments with many visual formats to bewildering effect here) on fatherhood and its sins. Bardem stars as Esteban Martinez, who is casting the leading female role in his new film “Desierto,” a role he has written for his estranged daughter Emilia (Victoria Luengo).

Kino Lorber plans a qualifying run in 2026 with an awards push for Bardem, then a broader North American release in 2027. Deadline first reported the news.

Buyer: Watermelon PicturesSection: Special ScreeningsDirector: Diego LunaBuzz: The “Andor” star directed this immigrant drama film about a young woman who relocates from Mexico to Spain in order to reconnect with her mother. Star Anna Díaz earned praise for her performance, and she holds her own alongside her mother played by Adriana Paz. The film next plays TIFF, and no release plans have been set. Read more here.

Buyer: Greenwich EntertainmentSection: CompetitionDirector: Kōji FukadaBuzz: This Japanese drama follows an artist and an architect each grappling with the remnants of past relationships who connect in the rural Nagi region of Japan. Greenwich is planning a theatrical release in the U.S. and Canada in early 2027.

Buyer: Sony Pictures ClassicsSection: Out of CompetitionDirector: Andy GarciaBuzz: A true labor of love, star Andy Garcia also wrote, directed, and produced this throwback detective noir (he also designed all the music and co-wrote the original score with his longtime collaborator Arturo Sandoval), which he’s been working on for nearly 20 years.

In the film, Garcia stars as Joe Diamond, billed by SPC as “a man out of time, an urban legend with an emotional traumatic past. He has the uncanny ability to solve crimes that even the LAPD cannot — armed only with the savvy and dry humor reminiscent of the gumshoe detectives of that genre. It is a love letter to Los Angeles and an homage to the great noir films of the past.”

The film also stars Vicky Krieps, Brendan Fraser, Bill Murray, Dustin Hoffman, Rosemarie DeWitt, and more. Sony Pictures Classics acquired worldwide rights (ex. France, Germany, Italy, CIS) to the film and will announce release plans at a later date.

Buyer: Kino LorberSection: Director’s FortnightDirector: Reed Van DykBuzz: The directorial debut of Reed Van Dyk was a stunner out of Director’s Fortnight, with our critic writing that it is an “intimate and psychologically astute” war film based on a 2012 New Yorker article about the aftermath of the Iraq War and the impact of U.S. influence in the region. The film follows a U.S. Marine who returns to Iraq to visit with an Iraqi woman and her family whom were caught up in a firefight when he was deployed. The film stars Boyd Holbrook, Kenneth Branagh, and Hiam Abbass. Kino Lorber is planning a fall release with some awards ambitions in mind, and it will play some other fall festivals before then.

Buyer: 1-2 SpecialSection: CompetitionDirector: Emmanuel MarreBuzz: 1-2 Special’s third acquisition out of Cannes is writer/director Emmanuel Marre’s (“Zero Fucks Given”) historical drama “A Man of His Time,” starring Swann Arlaud, best known for his role in 2023’s Palme d’Or winner “Anatomy of a Fall.” Set in 1940, it’s inspired by the life of the filmmaker’s own great-grandfather, with Arlaud’s Henri arriving in Vichy, France, as Nazis began to install themselves. There, he ingratiates himself with the regime against the backdrop of French resistance. Per the distributor, “As he sets his plan into motion, he does whatever is required to remain useful, serving the machinery of the new order with increasing skill, but as the war continues he may be pursuing something more urgent: escape from his own ruin.” Winner of the Best Screenplay prize out of the Cannes competition, “A Man of His Time” will open in theaters next year.

Buyer: Kino LorberSection: Un Certain RegardDirector: Manuela MartelliBuzz: Kino Lorber’s first acquisition from Cannes is this Chilean film from the director of the Pinochet-era political thriller “Chile ’76.” This one though is set in 1992 after the end of the Pinochet regime and follows a young girl at a ski resort who befriends a German competitive skier, only for the German to disappear without a trace. Our critic compared the eerie mystery to “Picnic at Hanging Rock” and “The Spirit of the Beehive.” Kino Lorber picked up U.S. rights and is planning a theatrical release for early 2027.

Buyer: 1-2 SpecialSection: Un Certain RegardDirector: Sandra WollnerBuzz: Rising outfit 1-2 Special’s mandate at this year’s Cannes? It seems like “get proven winners.” After previously picking up Critics’ Week winner “La Gradiva,” the label has next bought Un Certain Regard winner “Everytime” the third film from filmmaker Sandra Wollner. The features tracks a family as they take a trip to the Canary Islands after a horrible tragedy.

Buyer: Janus FilmsSection: CompetitionDirector: Valeska GrisebachBuzz: “Western” filmmaker Grisebach’s Cannes contender, starring Yana Radeva and Syuleyman Letifov, premiered in the Competition section at the festival, where it was ultimately awarded the Jury Prize. The dreamy drama also sparkles with other genre beats, and our critic hailed its inventive spin on the Western, also noting that “Grisebach has a flair for finding faces with striking, weatherbeaten features that might have grown out of the landscape itself. We are in an era where the cinema normalizes faces interrupted by Botox and fillers, amounting to propaganda against aging and even mortality itself; by contrast, the faces that fill Grisebach’s frames are a cool drink of mountain water.” Janus Films will announce theatrical release plans in the coming weeks.

Buyer: NetflixSection: CompetitionDirector: Marie KreutzerBuzz: “Corsage” director Marie Kreutzer’s latest feature “Gentle Monster,” one of two competition films led by French star Léa Seydoux, marked Netflix’s third Cannes acquisition after the animated “In Waves” and Spanish epic “The Black Ball.” (Deadline first reported the news; IndieWire independently confirmed it.) The streamer obviously sees awards prospects in all three films, with Seydoux as a possible Best Actress contender for her performance as an avant-garde pianist whose life is blown apart by the news that her husband may be a child sex offender. Of the two Léa Seydoux films, “Gentle Monster” stood out over Neon’s “The Unknown” in terms of review response.

Buyer: MubiSection: CompetitionDirector: Lukas DhontBuzz: Mubi, which came to Cannes with “Minotaur” and “Fatherland” already in competition, just made its first acquisition for North America since the start of the festival with Lukas Dhont’s “Coward.” The Belgian filmmaker was nominated for the Best International Feature Oscar in 2023 for “Close”; here, he directs the furtive romance between two soldiers on the Western front in World War I. The film stars Emmanuel Macchia in his debut film performance as Pierre and Valentin Campagne (“Colours of Time,” “Case 137”) as Francis. Mubi already had rights in UK, Ireland, Germany, Austria, Italy, Spain, Turkey, Latin America, Australia, and New Zealand. The Match Factory, which has been part of Mubi since 2022, handles international sales.

Buyer: NetflixSection: CompetitionDirector: Javier Calvo and Javier AmbrossiBuzz: Rising filmmakers (and exes!) Javier Calvo and Javier Ambrossi premiered “The Black Ball” late in the competition to strong reviews and enthusiastic audience response. As Variety reported, a multi-studio bidding war ensued, with Mubi, A24, and Netflix, who eventually won out, in the mix. Set in 1932, 1937, and 2017 and following the lives of three men, the film is a tribute to poet Federico García Lorca and his unfinished works.

Buyer: Sony Pictures ClassicsSection: Special ScreeningsDirector: Pegah AhangaraniBuzz: Winner of the L’Oeil d’Or, this debut feature documentary from Iranian filmmaker Pegah Ahangarani is a deeply personal and autobiographical portrait of her own family that stretches 40 years, dating back to 1979 and up through the war going on in Iran as we speak. SPC, after grabbing “Iron Boy” earlier this week, landed all rights for North and Latin America, Asia (except Japan), New Zealand, Turkey, Portugal, and worldwide airlines.

Buyer: 1-2 SpecialSection: Critics’ WeekDirector: Marine AtlanBuzz: Our critic was enamored with the directorial debut of Marine Atlan, writing that it takes the simple premise of high school teens on a class trip and resists all tropes to find a film that nears “the sublime” and manages to “explore the vast worlds of their individual interiority with insight, empathy, and grace.” Just today the film won the Grand Prize from Critics’ Week, and it’s already one of the top 5 rated films on Letterboxd among this year’s crop of Cannes titles. 1-2 Special is planning a theatrical release.

Buyer: Sony Pictures ClassicsSection: Un Certain RegardDirector: Louis ClichyBuzz: Yet another animated sale among the Official Selection, “Iron Boy” is a hand-painted animated feature with director Clichy, who cut his teeth as a 3D animator on “WALL-E” and “Up,” returning to ink line drawings inspired by Manga comics and his own early short films. “Iron Boy” is his first feature as a solo director. The film follows 11-year-old in rural France as he tries to live up to his rigid and distant father on the family farm. But when the young boy starts to lean over and collapse without warning, he must wear an iron corset to keep himself upright. Clichy co-wrote the film with Franck Salomé.

Buyer: NetflixSection: Critics WeekDirector: Phuong Mai NguyenBuzz: The opening night film for the Critics Week sidebar, Netflix acquired the worldwide rights (excluding France) for this animated film that features the voice talent of Will Sharpe and Stephanie Hsu. The film is based on a graphic novel by AJ Dungo and follows a shy teenager in Los Angeles who falls madly in love with a surfer girl, only for their love and future to be tested when she faces a sudden illness, leaning on their new passion for surfing and the ocean to get them through adversity. Fanny Burdino and Samuel Doux. Read our review here.

Buyer: A24Section: Un Certain RegardDirector: Jordan FirstmanBuzz: Arguably the smash hit of the Cannes Film Festival, comedian/actor Jordan Firstman writes, directs, and stars in this New York comedy as a washed-up party promoter who wakes up one day to find the child he never knew he had on his doorstep. The cast includes Cara Delevigne, Diego Calva, and newcomer Reggie Absolom, with producers including Oscar-winning “Anora” producer Alex Coco. and Galen Core, alongside Topic Studios’ Ryan Heller and Michael Bloom. A24 acquired the film’s rights in a competitive situation, with most major distributors bidding at Cannes. Read our review here.

Buyer: Clockwork (Warner Bros.)Section: Cannes ClassicsDirector: Ken RussellBuzz: This uncut restoration of Ken Russell’s super controversial — and largely unavailable — gem “The Devils” is being positioned by Warner Bros.’ new indie label Clockwork as the experience Russell always envisioned but never got to show properly. It’s the first repertory release from the new WB label, and frankly the first release period after announcing that Sean Baker’s next film “Ti Amo” was its inaugural acquisition. After it premieres in Cannes Classics, Clockwork is planning a one-week special engagement of screenings in theaters beginning October 16. Read more about the re-release here.

Buyer: NeonSection: CompetitionDirector: James GrayBuzz: “Paper Tiger” is the sixth film James Gray has brought to Cannes, but it’s the first time Neon has partnered with him, and given that it’s one of just two American films in the competition slate this year and one of the starriest, it seems like a match made in heaven. Gray’s crime thriller stars Adam Driver, Scarlett Johansson, and Miles Teller in a story about two brothers striving to achieve the American Dream only to get caught up with the Russian Mafia.

Buyer: Janus FilmsSection: Cannes PremieresDirector: Kiyoshi KurosawaBuzz: The latest film from the director of “Cloud” is a samurai film set in 16th century Japan that follows a Lord who rebelled against a tyrannical ruler and found himself beseiged within his own castle by a traitor and a prisoner being held in his own dungeon. Though based on real individuals, the film is an original story by Honobu Yonezawa.

Section: CompetitionDistributor: MUBIDirector: Andrey ZvyagintsevBuzz: The first film from the Russian director behind “Leviathan” and “Loveless” in almost a decade is a political fable and crime thriller about a businessman caught up in personal, political, and professional scandals.

Title: “All of a Sudden” Section: CompetitionDistributor: NeonDirector: Ryusuke HamaguchiBuzz: After winning at Venice with “Drive My Car” and at Berlin with “Evil Does Not Exist,” the Japanese auteur is looking for the European festival trifecta with Neon backing his Palme D’Or bid.

Title: “Bitter Christmas” Section: CompetitionDistributor: Sony Pictures ClassicsDirector: Pedro AlmodóvarBuzz: Almodóvar’s latest film curiously already opened to strong reviews in his native Spain back in March. A lush, colorful, and messy melodrama about pain and mourning sounds like an Amodóvar film to us.

Title: “Clarissa” Section: Director’s FortnightDistributor: NeonDirector: Arie Esiri and Chuko EsiriBuzz: The Nigerian directors behind “This Is My Desire” from 2020 team with Ayo Edebiri, David Oyelowo and Sophie Okonedo for a reimagining of Virginia Woolf’s “Ms. Dalloway” as set in Lagos.

Title: “Fatherland” Section: CompetitionDistributor: MUBIDirector: Pawel PawlikowskiBuzz: Polish director Pawlikowski’s first film in eight years since the searing “Cold War” is a docudrama about German author Thomas Mann in post-War Germany after having stood against the Nazis. Sandra Huller also stars.

Title: “Fjord” Section: CompetitionDistributor: NeonDirector: Cristian MungiuBuzz: Sebastian Stan and Renate Reinsve star in the latest film from Romanian auteur Mungiu, his first since 2022’s “R.M.N.,” which is about a Romanian immigrant family under scrutiny for investigation by the Norwegian government.

Title: “Her Private Hell” Section: Out of CompetitionDistributor: NeonDirector: Nicolas Winding RefnBuzz: “Her Private Hell” is “Drive” provocateur Nicolas Winding Refn’s first feature since 2016, and the film is a futuristic, fantastical story about a woman searching for her father and a soldier trying to recover his daughter from hell. It stars Charles Melton and Sophie Thatcher and should be loaded with stylized sex, violence, and glamor. The film opens July 24 in theaters.

Title: “Hope” Section: CompetitionDistributor: NeonDirector: Na Hong-JinBuzz: Like an arthouse “A Quiet Place,” this action-horror monster movie from a South Korean director also stars some major Korean talent like “Squid Game” star Hoyeon as well as Michael Fassbender and Alicia Vikander.

Title: “Once Upon a Time in Harlem” Section: Director’s FortnightDistributor: NeonDirector: David GreavesBuzz: The final film of director William Greaves completed by his son and released more than a decade after his death, “Once Upon a Time in Harlem” focuses on the “Harlem Renaissance” of 1972 New York.

Title: “Sheep in a Box” Section: CompetitionDistributor: NeonDirector: Hirokazu Kore-edaBuzz: The Japanese auteur turns his family drama attention to AI and looks at the emotional impact of introducing a humanoid robot as a couple’s son.

Title: “Teenage Sex and Death at Camp Miasma” Section: Un Certain RegardDistributor: MUBIDirector: Jane SchoenbrunBuzz: Riding high off “I Saw the TV Glow,” Jane Schoenbrun’s follow-up is a queer horror comedy obsessed with ’90s slashers, final girls, sexuality, and so much more. The film stars “Hacks” star Hannah Einbinder and Gillian Anderson.

Title: “The Unknown” Section: CompetitionDistributor: NeonDirector: Arthur HarariBuzz: A body swap drama from the writer of “Anatomy of a Fall,” Léa Seydoux stars in this film about a man who follows a mysterious woman at a party and wakes up in her body the next morning.

Title: “Victorian Psycho” Section: Un Certain RegardDistributor: Bleecker StreetDirector: Zachary WigonBuzz: Acquired at last year’s Cannes market, this haunting Gothic horror from the director of “Sanctuary” stars Maika Monroe in what is said to be an unexpected and eccentric turn.
