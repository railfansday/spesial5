GTA 6 Learned All the Right Lessons From Zelda: Breath of the Wild

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Like BotW, GTA 6 has the perfect tutorial area

Throwing players into an open-world game can be challenging. When your game has an enormous map, you risk giving players almost too much freedom up front. There’s a very careful art in helping players get acclimated to how exploration works in a game without totally restricting any sense of exploration. The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild famously nailed the art of an open-world introduction in 2017 with its meticulously crafted starting area, the Great Plateau. Now, nearly a decade later, Grand Theft Auto 6 has pulled off the same trick.

In GTA 6, you don’t start your life of crime in the heart of Vice City. In fact, it can take up to 20 hours to get there if you’re focused on the story. Instead, the entire first chapter takes place in the southernmost part of its map, the Leonida Keys. It’s a series of small, isolated islands where Jason has holed up in a dilapidated shack.

For those unfamiliar with Florida, the Leonida Keys are a near-perfect representation of the real-life Florida Keys. The islands, in reality, are narrow stretches of land chained together by bridges. Since you’re close to the water no matter where you are, most of the houses in the area are built on tall stilts to account for flooding. The Keys are densely packed with buildings, but on very limited patches of land, an approach to urban planning that can make them feel a little removed from reality. I was down in the Keys for a wedding a few years ago, and I couldn’t stop wondering what someone would do if they had an emergency and needed to get to the one nearby hospital during a single-lane traffic jam. It’s a truly unique place in America, which makes it a perfect locale for a series that’s obsessed with eccentric corners of the country.

But more than that, the layout of the Leonida Keys perfectly lends itself to a great open-world introduction. Key Lento, where you’ll spend most of your time in the early hours of GTA 6, is a thin town that feels about as big as a Like a Dragon map. Even though it’s not a huge space that’s lined with clubs, it still includes everything you need to get a feel for Leonida’s wider design. There’s a central highway that runs through the middle, giving you a place to zip through town while learning traffic laws. There are enough roadside shops to teach you how to buy guns, food, clothing, and more. On the edges of all that, you have your suburban side roads. And then you have shores on all sides, giving you easy access to the water for some early game fishing and diving.

Basically, you’ll learn all the basics you need to know about moving through Leonida in that compact space. You’ll be able to do that without getting pulled into too many distractions that take you far from home, as there’s only one way in and out of town. On the flipside, there’s still enough to discover in Key Lento that it doesn’t feel like you’re on a leash. Even when I went back to Key Lento after 60 hours, I found a hidden car livery in a garage I’d passed many times. You could easily spend the length of a normal GTA campaign just seeing it all in Chapter 1.

In a weird way, the Leonida Keys’ perfect design brings me back to the Great Plateau. Nintendo had a difficult task on its hand when it decided to turn Zelda into an open-world game with Breath of the Wild. How do you adapt the basic principles of the series, especially its precise tool-based puzzle-solving, into a version of Hyrule that you can explore freely? The solution was to ease players in with a controlled tutorial zone. When you zoom out and consider it in context of the full map, the Great Plateau is miniscule. It only has a few points of interest, and a handful of items to collect and wandering monsters to fight.

The magic of it, though, is that it never actually feels small. For players coming to an open-world Zelda game for the first time, it felt revelatory. It didn’t even feel like you were being subjected to a very well-designed set of tutorials. There was so much to see and do! It was only once you glided off the Plateau that you realized that you had only seen a tiny fraction of Hyrule, a revelation that made the true open-world reveal that much more impressive. And once you got there, you had all the tools and knowledge you needed to start climbing cliffs, seeking out shrines, and hunting for Koroks. The rest of the game comes from the mounting complexity of how those tools are used to solve trickier puzzles, a design philosophy that has cemented the Great Plateau in history as one of gaming’s most beloved introductions.

Everything that works about the Great Plateau in Breath of the Wild hit me when I explored the Leonida Keys in GTA 6. It provided a natural playground where I could learn the ropes before reaching the mainland and having my attention pulled in all directions. By the time I finally got to Vice City, I actually came to miss how serene and compact it all was compared to the bustle of city life. I still find myself driving back down there once and a while just to check in on my old neighbors or go on a little fishing expedition. (Okay, and also to rob a local bank, sue me.) After touring an entire state, Key Lento still feels like home for Jason.
