SJ-O football notes: McKinney almost didn't become the Spartans' quarterback

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

ST. JOSEPH — Lane McKinney, like he has done for the first 13 St. Joseph-Ogden football games this season, will get the start as the Spartans' quarterback for the team's 14th game this season.

And easily the program's biggest game this decade.

The consistent production from McKinney, 6-foot, 155-pound junior, is a main reason why SJ-O (13-0) gets to face IC Catholic (12-1) at 4 p.m. on Friday in the Class 3A state championship game at Hancock Stadium in Normal.

McKinney has completed 144 of 184 passes for a staggering 78.3 percent completion rate, throwing for 2,569 yards, 33 touchdowns and just four interceptions.

On top of his passing acumen, he has rushed for 1,610 yards on 230 carries and 30 touchdowns while also recording 51 tackles and a team-high six interceptions at cornerback.

"It's insane the amount of plays he'll play in one game," said Cameron Wagner, an SJ-O senior and Oregon commit who starts at left tackle and along the defensive line for the Spartans. "He's just making plays all over the place. He throws his body all over the place. He's not a big dude, but he will rock everybody. He just does not care."

But all the plays McKinney has made this season after receiving the snap almost didn't happen. That's because SJ-O coach Shawn Skinner and his staff were contemplating starting sophomore Brock Franzen at quarterback before the season and keeping McKinney at wide receiver. McKinney caught 58 passes for 521 yards and three touchdowns in 2025, helping SJ-O reach the 3A state semifinals.

"Brock just dominated freshman football and was tremendous at the JV level, too," Skinner said. "Brock is more of a traditional pocket passer. He's a big kid who can throw the ball a country mile. We were content with keeping a really good wide receiver at wide receiver and our best corner out there. Lane took it upon himself to really work on his quarterback fundamentals."

Now, McKinney operates the state's highest-scoring offense all season. SJ-O averages 52.6 points and has scored 683 points, with McKinney responsible for 63 total touchdowns. He is quick to credit, though, the Spartans' offensive line of Wagner, senior left guard Willis Canamore, junior center Liam Center, junior right guard Landon May and junior right tackle Mason Osterbur. Along with the efforts of junior running backs Logan Umbarger and Ryder Van Meenen and senior wide receiver Kaden Wedig.

"With us three guys back there, I feel Ryder and Umbarger can run the ball whenever we want," McKinney said. "Our offensive line making holes, that really opens up the passing game. We're very multi-dimensional."

Just because Franzen isn't the SJ-O starting quarterback, though, doesn't mean he hasn't contributed to the Spartans' success this season. The 6-2, 180-pound Franzen is just doing so at wide receiver. He has 13 catches for 228 yards and four touchdowns, ranking fourth on the Spartans behind Wedig (53 catches for 1,100 yards, 14 TDs), sophomore Logan Wedig (43 catches for 705 yards, eight TDs) and Van Meenen (23 catches for 356 yards, four TDs).

After watching Franzen play quarterback last season, Skinner and SJ-O offensive coordinator Dalton Walsh decided they needed to play him at some spot even if it wasn't at quarterback.

"He's so good. He's so athletic," Skinner said of Franzen. "He just wants to be a part of it. When we said, 'Hey, we want you to get some receiver reps,' his smile was so wide. At first, I think he thought I was crazy. I said, 'No, man. You're going to be a part of this.' It's been very rewarding for him, and at the end of the day, we're trying to get our best athletes on the field."

One of the Spartans' best athletes is their biggest player. The 6-7, 305-pound Wagner is fresh off a 1-yard touchdown run during last Saturday's 48-9 win against Williamsville in the state semifinals, but he has made a name for himself with his play along the offensive line.

A starter since late in his freshman season, Wagner received two dozen Division I offers before committing to Oregon this past May in front of a packed auditorium at SJ-O and live on 'The Pat McAfee Show,' on ESPN.

"You could never want a kid in his position to handle this any better," Skinner said. "He is a great teammate, and he is a great player to have on your team."

Wagner has contributed more this season than in any other during his SJ-O career along the defensive line. He has 25 tackles, including four for loss, and a fumble recovery this season in the Spartans' 3-4 base defense.

"He's played every one of our front five spots, whether it's a boundary outside linebacker, field outside linebacker, our field defensive end, strong defensive end, our weak side defensive end and our nose guard," Skinner said. "That's a testament to his football IQ and to his strength and conditioning."

Wagner will play in his final game with SJ-O on Friday night and only has a few weeks of high school left after that. He will graduate from SJ-O early, take part in the U.S. Army All-American Bowl in mid-December in Frisco, Texas, and then depart for Oregon shortly after Christmas.

When Oregon opens spring practices next year, Wagner will go through those workouts ahead of the 2027 season.

"It is surreal that in less than a month, I'll be done with high school," he said. "I'll be living with the three other offensive line commits out there, and when my parents leave after they help move me in, I've got to figure it out."

Three members of the SJ-O football team could win two state titles in the same year if the Spartans win on Friday night. Senior safety Tyler Wright, junior center Liam Carter and sophomore wide receiver Kiptyn Rosenthal were part of the SJ-O baseball team that won a 2A state title on June 13 at Illinois Field in Champaign.

"Obviously, baseball was a lot of fun," Carter said, "but now I get to play my favorite sport, football, and it feels even better."

All three had reserve roles on the 40-win SJ-O baseball team, but Carter and Wright both start on the football field. Wright has 59 tackles, including four for loss, and two interceptions. Carter has helped SJ-O compile 6,275 yards of offense and Rosenthal has seven catches for 162 yards and three touchdowns.

"It feels like just a couple months ago we were just at the state tournament in baseball and experiencing that," Rosenthal said. "Now, we're back here again with another chance to do the same thing. It's really cool."

The moment means even more to Wright. He played football during his freshman year at SJ-O, but then opted to not play the next two years in an effort to focus on baseball. The fact he is back and has such a prominent role isn't lost on him.

"It's been so much fun," Wright said. "In baseball, I wasn't an everyday player and a leader on the team. With football, I help lead the defense. It's a big difference and a lot more responsibility, but I love it."

If SJ-O pulls off the feat of a state title in football, it would become the fifth school to win both baseball and football state championships in the same year. Nazareth in 2022 and 2023, Providence in 2014, Chicago Mt. Carmel in 2013 and Glenbrook North in 1974 are the only ones to earn the rare accomplishment.

"Winning the baseball state title was one of the favorite nights of my life," Wright said, "and now, coming to play in a football state championship on Friday and being a starter, winning it would mean the world."
