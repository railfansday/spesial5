Eagles vs. Giants: Statistical breakdown and key matchups for Week 9

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

The Philadelphia Eagles are preparing for a critical NFC East matchup against the New York Giants on Sunday, and the numbers reveal just how far Nick Sirianni's team has fallen during its six-game losing streak. Philadelphia enters Week 9 at 2-6, ranking last in the NFL in total offense at 265.3 yards per game and 30th in scoring at 17.1 points. The Giants, meanwhile, lead the division at 4-2-1 and arrive at Lincoln Financial Field with one of the league's better defenses.

New York ranks ninth in total defense, allowing 313 yards per game, and third against the pass at 184.9 yards. The Giants also average 23.3 points and 346 total yards offensively, giving them significant statistical advantages entering Sunday's matchup.

However, the Eagles have one surprising advantage that could influence the outcome.

Philadelphia's offensive struggles become more confusing when examining its ability to finish drives. The Eagles rank fourth in the NFL with a 71.4% red-zone touchdown rate, outperforming a Giants offense ranked tied for ninth at 66.7%.

The problem is consistently reaching scoring territory.

Philadelphia averages just 177.1 passing yards and 88.1 rushing yards per game, ranking 27th in both categories. Their inability to produce consistently has left the Eagles with too few chances to capitalize on their red-zone success.

With Tanner McKee remaining the starting quarterback, Philadelphia must generate more explosive plays and sustain possessions rather than relying on occasional scoring opportunities.

The Eagles' passing game faces another difficult assignment against a Giants secondary allowing just 184.9 passing yards per contest. New York's defensive production has helped the team remain atop the NFC East despite losing starting quarterback Jaxson Dart earlier this season.

McKee has struggled to produce consistently since replacing Jalen Hurts, and Philadelphia's offensive problems were evident again in Sunday's 27-10 loss to Washington. The Giants have allowed 54.3 fewer passing yards per game than Philadelphia, highlighting the difference between the two defenses. McKee will need improved protection and more consistent contributions from his receivers to prevent New York from controlling the game.

New York averages 80.7 more total yards and 6.2 more points per game than Philadelphia. The Giants also rank 15th in rushing offense at 116.7 yards per game, compared with Philadelphia's 88.1. That difference could matter for an Eagles defense that allows 367.3 total yards per game, ranking 27th in the NFL.

New York quarterback J.J. McCarthy has provided another dimension to the offense since arriving from Minnesota, while the Giants have demonstrated an ability to sustain drives and control possession. Philadelphia cannot afford another game where its defense stays on the field for extended stretches because the offense struggles to move the chains.

The Giants hold advantages in six of the seven major statistical categories entering Sunday's matchup, including scoring offense, total offense, rushing offense, passing offense, total defense and passing defense. Philadelphia's only advantage comes inside the red zone, where its 71.4% touchdown conversion rate ranks among the NFL's best.

That efficiency could help the Eagles remain competitive, but only if they generate enough opportunities to use it.

With a Week 10 bye approaching, Philadelphia needs a victory to stop its losing streak and avoid falling further behind in the NFC East. The Eagles and Giants will kick off Sunday at 1 p.m. ET at Lincoln Financial Field, with the game airing on FOX.
