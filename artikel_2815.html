Why this year is different for 49ers superstar Christian McCaffrey

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Christian McCaffrey has made it clear: If it were up to him, he'd take all the handoffs.

At 30 years old, in his 10th season, he has also been around the block enough to know that his personal desires aren't necessarily what's best for the team's ultimate goal.

Christian McCaffrey of the San Francisco 49ers leaves the field after a game against the Dallas Cowboys on Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026, in Arlington, Texas. Getty Images

And that is something that all 53 players in the 49ers' locker room agree on:

What happens in Weeks 1 through 18 isn't how they'll judge this season. What's going to make or break a successful season is what happens from then on.

It's the same goal that this veteran-laden team has had basically going back to Kyle Shanahan's hiring 10 seasons ago.

And, like every other year, the 49ers will lean heavily on McCaffrey to get there.

What's different this time? McCaffrey may just be hitting his stride at just the right time.

The 49ers earned a lot of attention for their total offensive output in Sunday's shootout win over the Cowboys — 584 yards — as well as the portion for which McCaffrey was responsible.

The game was the Niners' most productive under Shanahan, arguably the NFL's best offensive coach, and their do-it-all running back accounted for almost 40% of it.

San Francisco 49ers running back Christian McCaffrey (23) runs for a touchdown against the Dallas Cowboys in Arlington, Texas, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect

McCaffrey went off for 223 yards — 62 on the ground, 161 through the air — an impressive showing if there ever was one. It was McCaffrey's third-highest total of his career and the most yards from scrimmage a 49ers player has accumulated since Frank Gore's 244-yard game in 2009. Nobody had racked up 200-plus yards and three touchdowns besides Jerry Rice.

But here's the number that the 49ers Faithful should really care about:

That's the number of touches McCaffrey required to eclipse 200 yards. Catch-and-runs of 74 and 66 yards for scores, as well as another 16-yard touchdown scamper, will help with that.

That's the percentage of the 49ers' offensive snaps this season that McCaffrey has been on the field. In other words, almost one-third of the plays Shanahan has drawn up haven't involved McCaffrey at all. That's 31% of plays that McCaffrey isn't taking bruising hits, exerting himself or putting further strain on the ligaments and muscles that will carry the 49ers' load come January.

San Francisco 49ers running back Christian McCaffrey answers a question after an NFL football game against the Dallas Cowboys in Arlington, Texas, Nov. 15, 2026. AP Photo/Julio Cortez

That's a far cry from McCaffrey's NFL-high 413 touches last season. He was on the field for 83% of the 49ers' snaps last year, or 932 total plays.

This season, he's on pace to log more than 200 fewer plays by the end of the regular season.

The responsibility to carry the ball no longer exclusively falls on his shoulders.

"As talents and as people," Shanahan said, "I think they pair together very well."

That's not something that would have come out of the coach's mouth last season, when McCaffrey was their only trusted ball carrier (and still struggled, averaging fewer than 4 yards per carry for his first time with the 49ers). This year, he's averaging 4.5 yards per carry, his most efficient running since he was Offensive Player of the Year in 2023.

As soon as McCaffrey met the running back they drafted 90th overall out of Indiana, he knew this season had a chance to be different.

McCaffrey saw a kindred spirit in Kaelon Black. He's lived up to the billing.

San Francisco 49ers running back Kaelon Black (26) rushes for a touchdown against the Dallas Cowboys on Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026, in Arlington, Texas. IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect

"I think Christian really respects Kaelon," Shanahan said. "I remember Christian telling me right when he got here how much of a pro that guy seemed like and seemed like he was made of the right stuff. It's because K.B., similar to Christian, he works at everything. He's very hard on himself. He's very tough and approaches each day to get better."

Shanahan called McCaffrey the "perfect mentor." In Black, he found a subject worthy of his wisdom. At 25 years old, he has a maturity not typically found in first-year players.

And as a bruising back who does his best work outside the tackles, he's the perfect backfield partner for McCaffrey. He's racked up 186 yards on 34 rushing attempts the past three weeks.

San Francisco 49ers' Kaelon Black (26) avoids a tackle by Dallas Cowboys' Caleb Downs (13) during a game in Arlington, Texas, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. Getty Images

McCaffrey, meanwhile, has taken 38 handoffs for 169 yards over that same span. Shanahan's trust in Black has freed up McCaffrey to contribute more in the passing game, hauling in 12 passes for 241 yards to help offset Mike Evans' absence.

McCaffrey is still the 49ers' most important weapon in an offense full of them.

It's in everyone's best interests that his fuel tank is as full as possible come the playoffs.

Then again, the way Black is running, the 49ers seem dangerous no matter who's taking the handoffs.

"We love our room," McCaffrey said last week. "I think with KB, when he comes in it's such a spark. When I can come in and come out and get my wind, he goes in, balls out, comes out, gets his wind. If we can just continue to do that, man, I think we can be really special."
