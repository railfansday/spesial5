You’ve Been Serb’d: Illinois 99, Duke 78

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

First of all, there's no doubt that Illinos deserved the win at Cameron on Tuesday night. The Illini just thumped the Blue Devils in almost every way. They outrebounded Duke, 54-34, and Duke had 14 turnovers. Illinois also outscored Duke 31-21 from the line, and ruthlessly controlled the paint. Duke took 40 threes in this game, hitting just 9. Illinois shot just 6-32, but their control of the paint made that irrelevant. And so did the performance of Andrej Stojakovic.

When Duke played Cal in 2024, Stojakovic finished with 10 points on 4-14. Tuesday night, he lit Duke up for 46 points on 14-21/2-3 from the floor, along with 16-20 from the free throw line.

It was a record-breaking performance: the most points ever scored by an opponent in Cameron was 43 by South Carolina's Grady Wallace in 1957, when the ACC was 3 years, 9 months, and 15 days old.

It came perilously close to breaking Dick Groats' record of 48 points, set in 1952.

He was superb and nearly unguardable, but he's not the only reason that Illinois won. Quentin Coleman racked up 27, but it was more than that.

Here's something you may not have caught: Zavier Zens, cousin of Kon and Kager Knueppel, who is 6-7, got 8 rebounds. He out-rebounded every Duke player who got on the floor. So did Coleman, who is 6-4, and so did reserve Stevan Vaaks, who, like Zens, is 6-7.

JoJo Boumtje Boumtje got 5 rebounds. He's 7-2. Patrick Ngongba got 4 rebounds. He's 6-11. Cameron Williams, 7-0, got 3. Drew Scharnowski, 6-10, got 2.

Three players, all 6-7 or under, and two of them freshmen, outrebounded Duke's stable of big men. The leading rebounder for Duke?

Basically, the same thing happened with Illinois that happened at Michigan State: both teams were significantly tougher and more focused than was Duke, and the Blue Devils paid the price.

At the half, Scheyer pulled two starters: Boumtje Boumtje and Cayden Boozer, opting for Foster and Sebastian Wilkins instead.

Wilkins has played very well all season, and deserved his shot, and Foster is highly reliable, but it didn't make any difference, because Stojakovic stayed red hot, and to a lesser extent, so did Coleman.

Those two finished with 73 of the 99 Illinois hung up in Cameron.

It was a dismal night, but nonetheless, there were some bright moments. You could argue that Duke's second five outplayed the starters. Cameron Williams came in and almost immediately blocked two shots. That's at least the second time he's done that, and possibly the third. He has great instincts for blocks.

Scharnowski hit a three, which, we believe, was the first he's ever hit in college. He played hard and certainly didn't back down. Deron Rippey didn't score, but he had 7 assists to two turnovers. And when Bryson Howard was in, he may have defended Stojakovic more effectively than anyone else on the team. He really gave him trouble in a way no one else did.

We've already talked about Wilkins and Foster, but we should say this about Wilkins: he's looking better and better. He moves incredibly well on the court, he defends, and he's the furthest thing from selfish.

We're not sure what lessons Jon Scheyer will draw from this game, but from our vantage point, we saw that Duke was soft inside. And until that gets fixed, there are going to be games like this.

We've seen enough of Ngongba to know that a bad game doesn't mean the next one will be bad, too, but we can't say that about Boumtje Boumtje.

Clearly, he's got a perimeter game, but 7-2 is a gift from God that one shouldn't squander. In the second half, he was far more willing to take it to Illinois, and got to the line as a result. At whatever point he gets mad enough to rip someone's head off on a dunk, you'll know that part of the problem with this team has been solved.

Side note: remember when Dahntay Jones did pushups after that insane dunk against Virginia?

The reason he did it was because he was angry at his coach, Mike Krzyzewski, who had challenged him in some pretty elemental ways, as we understand it, and Jones wanted to make a point.

We're not sure how Scheyer will do that, but it might need to be done.

And in fairness to Boumtje Boumtje, and we're not picking on him, but the kid is 17. He's adapting to a very new situation. Let's give them both time and space to figure that out.

The reality though is that in sports, there's a goal, and that goal is to win. As we saw in the second half, sometimes changes need to be made. It's going to be hard not to give minutes to Williams, Scharnowski, Howard, and most of all, Wilkins, if the starters let themselves get pushed around.

Notes – Scheyer got a technical in the first half, which we think is his third as head coach…so did Dame Sarr…people are upset about the loss, but remember that Coach K took some beatings during his long and storied career…we wouldn't judge this team in November…not only was that Scharnowski's first made three, but it looks like it's the only one he's taken in three years…we'd love to see Blackwell go off soon…
