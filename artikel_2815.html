Jaguars saw something in Liam Coen that other teams didn't

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

In a season and a half with the Jaguars, Liam Coen has established himself as one of the best coaches in the NFL. Last year, Coen took over a team that had gone 4-13 the year before and improved their record to 13-4 — the best record ever for a first-year coach taking over a team that had won four or fewer games the year before. This year Coen is showing that last year was no fluke, with the Jaguars at 7-2 with an NFL-best point differential of +125.

Coen’s great success should have other teams that hired new head coaches in 2025 asking themselves: What did we miss? Because the Jaguars were so high on Coen that he was their No. 1 target and they were willing to do whatever it took to get him, while no other team even bothered to give Coen an interview.

During the 2025 NFL coaching search, we tracked dozens of reports of head coaching interviews and requests among the seven teams that had vacancies. The Jaguars were the only team that so much as asked Coen to interview.

This week there’s been some media scrutiny on the Jets, who hired Aaron Glenn without considering Coen as a candidate. Glenn is 5-22; Coen is 20-6. But it’s unfair to single out the Jets for failing to interview Coen, because no team other than the Jaguars did, either.

The 41-year-old Coen had a résumé that should have made him a good candidate. NFL teams are always on the lookout for coaches who can nurture a franchise quarterback and run an innovative offense. Coen played quarterback at UMass and coached quarterbacks in college before getting hired by Sean McVay to work on the Rams’ staff, eventually became the Rams’ offensive coordinator under McVay, and had been a successful playcaller as offensive coordinator for the Buccaneers, as well as at Kentucky. With that background, it’s surprising that only the Jaguars wanted him.

And the Jaguars really wanted him, to the point that they were willing to do whatever it took to hire him. Coen initially turned the Jaguars down and agreed to a contract extension to stay with the Buccaneers, but Jaguars owner Shad Khan wouldn’t take no for an answer. When Coen said he was concerned about the direction of the Jaguars’ front office, Khan fired General Manager Trent Baalke and agreed to reshape the front office in a way that Coen would approve of if Coen would take the job. Coen was persuaded to take the Jaguars job, and the Jaguars later hired James Gladstone, who had worked with Coen on the Rams, as GM.

The result has been the Jaguars going from one of the worst teams in the NFL to one of the best. Coen’s success in Jacksonville has justified the faith Khan had in him. And raises questions about the total lack of interest from the rest of the league.
