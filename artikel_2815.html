Knicks Bulletin: ‘I got out of bed and played the game’

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

I missed yesterday's game, so I had to watch the replay early today.

Good to know the world healed and the Knicks righted all wrongs, beating Indy again to make it three wins in their last four meetings and 5-2 in the past calendar year.

Here are a few quotes straight outta Sunday.

On preferring disciplined defenders over players who chase steals and blocks:

"I've never been a steal guy. I've never been a block guy. I would prefer a guy that relied more on just going vertical at the rim than going for blocks and I would prefer a position guy defensively more than a steal guy.

"Because to me, this is just my personal opinion, when you talk about steals and you talk about blocks, you might get two or three steals per game, but I promise that you're going to miss four to six a game. Meaning if that pass is going [and] I try to jump the lane, and now I miss it, now they have an advantage of five-on-four behind me because I'm out of position.

"Now everybody is scrambling to make up for my miss. If I go to block his shot and I swing down, three out of the four times or three out of the five times, he's going to the free throw line and/or if I'm going to chase a block because I'm a big, now somebody on the backside, or my [opponent] who is seven feet, is getting an offensive rebound because I'm going to block a shot.

"I'm either going to foul him or, every once in a while, I'll block it, everyone will go 'oooh'. Every once in a while I'll get a steal and go dunk it and everyone will go 'oooh', but it's not worth it for me. I've never been that way.

"So if you do the other things and you're solid as a group out on the floor, you have a better chance of moving up in the rating than if you're running all over the place, gambling for steals and/or blocks."

On the Knicks' defense being ahead of their offense despite gradual shooting improvements:

"I didn't know it was down overall. I don't know. I don't know if the defenses are getting better. I do know we, just our team, we haven't shot the ball as well as we'd like. But every game we're getting a little bit better, everybody's percentages are going up just a little bit every game, and that's exciting. I think eventually for us, right now our defense is way ahead of our offense, but our offense is coming, and hopefully we'll get there sooner than later."

On setting Mohamed Diawara and Pacome Dadiet to compete for minutes throughout the season:

"It's gonna be a competition all year, and it's gonna be back and forth."

On giving Dadiet an opportunity after his strong performances in practice:

"I said the first opportunity I get, I'm gonna try to throw [Dadiet] in the game 'cause he's been playing well in practice and stuff like that. And it's just, in my opinion, his turn … but it's not permanent."

On apologizing to the Knicks for failing to make a defensive substitution before Damian Lillard's game-tying three-pointer:

"I apologized to my team. I wanted to make a defensive substitution to get Tyler [Kolek] off the floor at the end of the game and I did not do it quick enough. That [game-tying] three that Dame [Lillard] made was on me. The first thing I did was apologize to our group for not being on point with that and getting that done quick enough."

On holding himself accountable for coaching mistakes even after victories:

"I would have done it if we lost, too, because I made a mistake. I just know if I can go in a locker room and say 'you didn't play hard' or 'you didn't do your job,' I've got to be held accountable, too. . . . It's just the right thing to do, to take ownership of what happened."

On scoring 30 points against Indiana after missing the previous game with an illness:

"I just wanted to be aggressive and try to put this team in a position to win."

On Mike Brown increasingly trusting his input and decision-making:

"Shoutout to the coach for trusting me more and more every day. And hearing me out. I'm appreciative of the trust this team has in me and the coaching staff has in me."
