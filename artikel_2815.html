5 takeaways from the Steelers’ 38-18 loss to the Cincinnati Bengals

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The Steelers fell woefully flat in a battle for the AFC North lead on Sunday Night Football, losing to the Cincinnati Bengals 38-13 in primetime.

As always, there are plenty of takeaways to be had:

The 20-point margin of victory probably speaks for itself here, no?

There were a solid five weeks of building hype for the Steelers, with a four-game winning streak and a Week 9 bye ahead of Sunday's Bengals matchup. However, despite the Steelers' last game being a dominant, 27-6 victory over the Cleveland Browns, I couldn't shake the feeling that Pittsburgh wasn't for real just yet. Frankly, we've seen enough good, not great Steelers seasons in recent years that the "Pittsburgh is a Super Bowl contender" narrative felt incredibly premature yet again, as predicted.

While the Steelers haven't been entirely unchallenged this season up to this point, the unavoidable truth was that the team hadn't exactly faced a murderer's row of quarterbacks outside of Joe Burrow in Week 3 (who saw success against the Pittsburgh secondary for 282 yards and three touchdowns) and Drake Maye in a down year for him and the Patriots in Week 2 (the Steelers still lost by 17).

And facing a white-hot Bengals offense in Cincinnati Sunday night, the Steelers didn't even look like they belonged in the same sport. It's still a quarterback-driven league, and the Steelers' effort on Sunday night showed what many have known all season: Pittsburgh doesn't have what it takes yet to consistently compete with elite quarterbacks, and the team doesn't have one itself.

It's worth clarifying that while a loss to the Bengals wasn't exactly unsurprising, the way in which the Steelers lost was unbecoming of a professional football team. The internet is already exploding with takes along the lines of "I've been a Steelers fan since the Paleolithic Age and I've never seen the team lose like this" — I feel like I see that exact post every season — but they're not entirely wrong.

But as the Steelers veterans clarified postgame, these losses are par for the course in the NFL. Teams will move on and rebound — something the Steelers have done time after time. However, even if the Steelers had protected Aaron Rodgers for a tick longer on each play, won more in the pass rush, or cleaned up miscommunications in coverage, it was clear the team just doesn't have the firepower to compete with a roster operating on the level Cincinnati was on Sunday.

Pittsburgh beaten the Bengals earlier this season. But there's a reason why coaches such as Mike McCarthy preach the differences of "November football." The games are tougher. The stakes are higher. And the Steelers, once again, didn't look like a team built for that stretch of the season.

Despite two weeks to prepare for what was without a doubt the biggest game of their season so far, the Steelers couldn't even manage to look competitive against the Bengals.

Is it time to be worried about the Steelers? Not necessarily, unless you legitimately believed they were part of the AFC's elite. But it was a brutal way to prove where Pittsburgh needs to grow before it can get back to contending status. To improve from the late Tomlin era, the Steelers need to win in the playoffs, and Sunday's matchup was the closest thing to postseason football Pittsburgh has faced all year.

TL;DR: Joe Burrow had 302 passing yards and three touchdowns… in the first half. Not great!

I don't think any NFL defense matches up well with the Bengals, but for the Steelers, it's an especially bad pairing. Pittsburgh relies on strong run defense and a chaotic pass rush that masks a vulnerable secondary. Cincinnati simply doesn't need to run that much because of its overpowered passing game. And Burrow is ruthlessly efficient, even versus pressure — and it helps that he's throwing to the best wide receiver duo in the league.

It's a perfect storm for the Steelers, and the results on Sunday showed that.

Film review of even Pittsburgh's big win over Cleveland in Week 8 revealed that the defense wasn't as lockdown against the pass as the numbers might suggest, and the Bengals' offense is tailor-made for exploiting any weaknesses in a secondary. The Steelers were beat through the air in every way imaginable on Sunday: over the top, missed tackles underneath, miscommunications, and simply losing matchups to better athletes.

Meanwhile, the Steelers couldn't counter with their star-studded pass rush. Burrow was pressured the second-least of any quarterback in Week 10 (pending Monday Night Football) at just 17.4%, with right tackle Amarius Mims once again stonewalling T.J. Watt and the Bengals having an answer for every blitz thrown at them by Steelers defensive coordinator Patrick Graham.

The coverage struggles were expected. But the Steelers' pass rush is too highly-paid and celebrated to drop off like it did in such a big game, even against an offensive line as underrated as Cincinnati's.

The Steelers' offense, like its defense, is still far from "bad" despite some games firmly in that territory. Over the course of the season, the Steelers' rushing game has proven to be one of the better ones in the league, but Pittsburgh is still somewhat limited in the games where it can succeed.

The patented "bully ball" approach really only works when you're punching down — and in a negative game script against the Bengals, the Steelers looked like they were floundering. A standout play in that sense was a third-and-long fake screen that resulted in a deep incompletion to tight end Darnell Washington. For one, it wasn't much of a surprise that the Bengals defense didn't bite on the fake short pass in that scenario. And for another, the Steelers ran that exact play last week!

Despite an extra week of preparation in a battle for the AFC North lead, the Steelers tried to run back the hits on offense instead of innovating.

Honestly, I'd put the Bengals loss more on the Pittsburgh offense than the defense. The game was always going to be a high-scoring one, but by the time the Steelers gained their second first-down of the night, they were already down two scores with 3:28 remaining in the second quarter. Pittsburgh had just 8:19 of time of possession at the half. No wonder the defense looks exhausted and uninspired!

One of the benefits of writing this column a bit later than usual is that there's a bit more time to digest the game and see some tape breakdowns, which backed up the eye test from watching the game: The Steelers' offensive struggles weren't just the fault of scheme and play-calling, but also poor execution on just about everyone's part.

The offensive line struggled to move Dexter Lawrence in the run game and protect Rodgers from pressure. DK Metcalf, despite scoring the rare NFL "octopus", had another game where he failed to make a contested catch and recorded a procedural penalty. And Rodgers, when he was kept clean, failed to capitalize on opportunities downfield.

Remember Joe Burrow's halftime numbers? Rodgers, on the other hand, was 4/12 for 25 yards at the break.

Big surprise: the loss to the Bengals was a group failure. When your team has clear flaws and quarterback play that can't cover it, you'll end up with results like Sunday's.

A common reaction to the Steelers' struggles on Sunday was that Pittsburgh was undermanned and missed an opportunity to fix that at the NFL trade deadline, which occurred Nov. 10.

Sure, an improvement at cornerback or wide receiver might've lowered the Bengals' margin of victory a bit. But to circle back to my first point in this article, the Steelers are not Super Bowl contenders in 2026 — and to build on that, they weren't a Josh Downs or Charvarius Ward away from reaching that status, either. If you replace Asante Samuel Jr. or Brandon Smith on the Steelers' Sunday roster with a better player, that still wouldn't have covered for the other 10 starters not doing their job.

Not to belabor the point any further, but it's important to recognize this Steelers team for what it is: A collection of young players building the foundation for an eventual franchise quarterback, while also punching above their weight class at times thanks to a core of veterans and an experienced coaching staff. At the deadline, Pittsburgh general manager Omar Khan rightfully saw that the team is not a win-now window, not falling for the hype of a 6-2 start.

That doesn't mean that the Steelers can't be fun in 2026. The big win over Cleveland in Week 9 proved that there's still a lot of potential on the Pittsburgh roster.

However, Week 10's loss was a stark reminder of how much growth is still required.

Not too many positives in this one, but I can rattle off a few: For one, it was good to see wide receiver/special teams ace Ben Skowronek back in the lineup this week. The Bengals muffed (but recovered) a first-quarter punt, and it was No. 15 who was in the middle of it.

The Steelers' interior rushers had some clear wins on Sunday. The pass rush wasn't always completely ineffective; Joe Burrow is just fantastic and navigating a messy pocket. I'm still rather high on the Pittsburgh interior D-line.

Keep in mind this same Bengals defense gave up 36 points to the Titans last week.

To my eyes, Kaden Wetjen looked a bit more shifty against the Bengals than in past games this season. He still averaged a middling 26.6 yards per return, but seems to be gaining his sea legs.

Payton Wilson logged a whopping 19 tackles on Sunday, but considering how often Steelers defenders were simply chasing down Bengals after a chunk gain, I don't think that's a stat worth spinning positively.

Pat Freiermuth's 27-yard touchdown was easily the Steelers' best offensive play of the game. That play was also one of the tight end's two catches on Sunday. I'm surprised Pittsburgh didn't try and utilize him more.

Mason McCormick's ankle injury looks like a long-term one, and his teammates' postgame comments seemed to agree. If and when McCormick misses time, that's a brutal turn of events for Pittsburgh, as the left side of the offensive line has been the team's greatest strength on offense this season.

Lew Nichols III finished with a team-best 43 rushing yards, averaging 7.2 yards per carry. Sure, those stats came in garbage time, but it was good to see the running back, who has impressed the last two preseasons, get some regular season production.

Daylen Everette continues to get some dimebacker snaps for the Steelers. An interesting use for the rookie, who was largely seen as a boundary corner coming out of the draft.

A friendly reminder that the Steelers are still the No. 6 seed in the AFC playoffs, 6-3, and second in the AFC North. They've split both of their played division series so far. The sky is definitely not falling. There's a lot of season left, and it could still be a fun one with the right expectations.

Next week, Pittsburgh will face the 2-7 Eagles on Sunday in Philadelphia, where the Steelers haven't won since the 1960s.
