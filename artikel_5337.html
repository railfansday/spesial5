Perplexity AI predicts what the US CPI data may read and, if so, what could potentially happen to Bitcoin and the broader market. CPI data remains one of the most market-moving economic releases for both stocks and crypto. The next one is in two days, on November 10, and both crypto and the stock market seem to be waiting to see which direction it goes from here.

Traditionally, a cooler-than-expected Consumer Price Index print is bullish for risk assets. Lower inflation reduces pressure on the Federal Reserve to keep interest rates elevated or hike them further, which supports lower Treasury yields, a softer dollar, and higher valuations for growth-oriented assets.

The inflation rate increased in September on the back of higher gasoline and energy prices amid the Iran war, further squeezing households’ budgets and perhaps pressuring the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates, economists said.

The consumer price index rose by 3.7% in September from 12 months earlier, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported Wednesday. That’s up from 3.4% annually in August.

September’s report came in hotter than expected, triggering an immediate sell-off across cryptocurrency and broader risk-asset markets.

Bitcoin slid from roughly $83,900–$84,000 down toward $81,000–$81,500, hitting lows around $80,210 in subsequent early trading sessions.

The total cryptocurrency market capitalization compressed to $2.7 trillion, accompanied by over $160M in leveraged long liquidations within a 24-hour window following the October 14 CPI data drop.

Equities, particularly the Nasdaq and rate-sensitive sectors, tend to rally as the discount rate on future earnings declines. Crypto often amplifies this reaction: Bitcoin and major altcoins historically perform well in environments of easing monetary expectations because they benefit from improved liquidity conditions and renewed risk appetite.

Conversely, a hotter-than-expected CPI reading typically pressures stocks and crypto lower, reinforcing a “higher-for-longer” rates narrative, strengthening the dollar, and lifting yields.

For the October 2026 CPI data due Tuesday, November 10 at 8:30 a.m. ET, current market consensus points to a clear cooling from September’s strong print.

Economists surveyed by Bloomberg and other sources expect headline CPI to rise 0.3% month-over-month (down from +0.6% previously), with core CPI (excluding food and energy) advancing 0.2% MoM (down from +0.3%).

Some forecasts cluster slightly lower around 0.2–0.25% for headline. Year-over-year figures are complicated by prior data gaps, but the monthly numbers are the primary focus.

Sentiment leans toward a soft-to-in-line outcome that would confirm inflation is decelerating after two firmer months, though markets remain sensitive to any upside surprise in core services or shelter components.

Perplexity AI predicts a modestly softer print than the median consensus: headline CPI at +0.2% MoM and core CPI at +0.2% MoM. This would represent a more pronounced cooling than many expect, driven by slower energy contribution, softer used-vehicle prices, and some moderation in owners’ equivalent rent after recent spikes.

While not a dramatic undershoot, it would land on the dovish side of the range. If this prediction materializes, risk assets should react positively. Treasury yields would likely dip, the dollar could soften, and both the S&P 500 and Nasdaq would find support for a relief rally.

Crypto would stand to benefit disproportionately in the short term, with Bitcoin potentially extending recent recovery gains and higher-beta alts seeing stronger percentage moves as liquidity expectations improve.

A confirmatory soft print would also increase the odds that the Federal Reserve maintains a more patient stance into the December meeting, reducing near-term hawkish risks.

However, the market impact would still depend on the details, particularly the composition of core inflation, and any accompanying commentary on sticky services categories. Overall, a result in line with or slightly below the Perplexity forecast would reinforce the recent post-midterm risk-on tone rather than derail it.
