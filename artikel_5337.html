Tokenized stocks split in two this week. Chipmakers led a rebound while crypto-linked names kept falling, data from CoinGecko showed on Friday, November 13.

The tokenized stock market is now worth $3.05 billion across 750 tokens from issuers such as Binance’s bStocks, xStocks and Ondo. These tokens track shares of U.S. companies and trade around the clock. About $1.4 billion of them changed hands in the past 24 hours.

We ranked the 32 companies with at least $10 million in tokenized stock outstanding by their seven-day move. Tokenized AMD led with a 33.4% gain, and chip stocks rose a median 12.5%. Crypto-linked names fell a median 8%, with Strategy down 13.6%.

The gap follows the news. Chip stocks bounced after a sharp sell-off last week, Bitcoin slid to $67,568 on Friday, and Nvidia lost its CEO.

A tokenized stock is a crypto token backed by a real share held by a custodian. It gives holders the price of the stock, usually without voting rights, and it can trade on weekends and overnight when Wall Street is shut.

Eighteen of the 32 companies rose over the week, and the median move was a 4.4% gain. The top of the list belongs to chips.

The losers tell the other half of the story. Five of the 10 worst performers are tied to crypto, and Bitcoin is down 9% over the same week.

Strategy’s preferred stock was the steady one. Its tokenized STRC, worth $184 million, moved just 0.1% because its dividend is adjusted to keep the price near $100.

On the daily chart, where each candle covers one day, Hyperliquid’s 24/7 AMD contract, xyz: AMD, trades at $572.30. That is 43% above its November 5 low of $399.88. It is still 13% short of the October 6 record of $658.57.

It sits above its 50-day exponential moving average (EMA), an average that gives recent prices more weight, at $532.87. The 200-day EMA is lower at $474.48.

For holders of tokenized stocks, this week was a sector bet more than a market bet. Chips bounced, while anything tied to crypto followed Bitcoin lower. Until Bitcoin steadies, the crypto names are likely to stay at the bottom of the table.
