RJ Luis looks as advertised in LSU basketball win - after signing NBA deal

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

RJ Luis is officially back in college basketball. He certainly looked a level above the rest at times, as expected.

The LSU guard, who received a preliminary injunction on Friday, Nov. 13, checked into the game for the first time this season against Louisiana-Monroe on Nov. 18, adding a new wrinkle to his ongoing eligibility saga. He subbed in with 2:29 left in the first half, playing the remainder of the period before the Warhawks took a 39-34 lead into halftime.

In just 15 minutes off the bench a day after he was officially added to LSU's roster, the former St. John's star and NBA contract signee scored 11 points with nine rebounds, an assist and a block in the 79-67 win over Louisiana-Monroe. His 15 minutes were the second fewest for the Tigers in the game, but his 11 points were the fourth-most among nine players.

Luis signed both a two-way contract and an Exhibit 10 deal with the Utah Jazz and Boston Celtics, although he never played in a regular season NBA game after going undrafted in the 2025 NBA Draft. His strong play for LSU off the bench will undoubtably spark even more controversy, especially given his journey back to college basketball.

LSU coach Will Wade didn't elaborate on his decision to play Luis after the game.

"I'm going to refer to our university's statement from yesterday," he said. "I'll be happy to answer any questions about how he played, but going to refer in terms of anything about his eligibility and rostering him to the university's statement yesterday.

"Everybody who played tonight is eligible."

The 2025 Big East Player of the Year at St. John's is a huge pickup for the Tigers, particularly if he's ruled eligible for the remainder of the season. He was officially added to LSU's roster on Nov. 17 in a move that drew concerns from the SEC and outside forces, such as the attorney general of Georgia, who wrote a letter about Luis' eligibility to SEC commissioner Greg Sankey on Nov. 18.

Luis' preliminary injunction included terms that barred Luis and LSU from being punished for him playing in games. The SEC won't seek punishment for LSU adding Luis to its roster, despite not supporting the move.

"This is a deeply concerning and disappointing development," the conference wrote in a statement on Nov. 17. "The conference will continue to uphold and defend the standards and policies established by its member universities."

The SEC adopted rules this offseason that ban players that have signed an NBA contract or did not properly withdraw from the NBA draft. Luis' attorneys have maintained the stance that since he never appeared in a regular season game, he was never a professional, despite appearing to be included in the SEC's new rules.

It's unclear if this is the end of Luis' eligibility saga, although it doesn't appear the SEC will seek any further action against the standout guard.

Luis averaged 18.2 points with 7.2 rebounds in his last season of college basketball in 2024-25. He started his career at UMass in 2022-23, leaving college early for the NBA draft as a junior.
