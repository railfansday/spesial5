The Kevin O'Connell-Jets connection resurfaces, not surprisingly

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

As the Jets move toward their inevitable next coaching search, folks who cover the Jets have begun to compile the list of possibilities for the successor to Aaron Glenn.

Rich Cimini, who covers the Jets for ESPN, planted the flag on Vikings coach Kevin O’Connell being “the name I would watch for.”

It’s not the first time the connection has been made. Two years ago, when O’Connell was entering the final season of his first contract in Minnesota and, on the day the Vikings were preparing to play the Lions for the No. 1 seed in the NFC playoff field, Jay Glazer of Fox Sports reported that O’Connell could be traded to a new team. As Darren Mougey emerged as one of the top candidates to become the Jets’ new G.M., the obvious dots were being connected to O’Connell, given that Mougey and O’Connell were roommates at San Diego State.

Cimini’s recent take is premised on the Vikings falling apart down the stretch and/or the team deciding to elevate defensive coordinator Brian Flores to head coach before he gets hired by another team.

Obviously, if the Vikings were independently considering moving on from O’Connell, it would make sense to get compensation by trading him to a new team. But there’s no reason to think the Vikings are considering moving on from O’Connell.

While the “quarterback whisperer” vibe has changed significantly since the start of the 2025 season, the blame for the revolving door of players who ultimately weren’t good enough falls on plenty of people. After the 2024 season, when the Vikings had to pick a starter from Sam Darnold, Daniel Jones, and J.J. McCarthy, it appears that someone other than O’Connell made the call to let Darnold and Jones leave and to entrust the job to McCarthy, who had played only one preseason game and nothing more in his rookie season.

The fact that the Vikings fired G.M. Kwesi Adofo-Mensah after the 2025 season suggests that the organization did a deep dive into the factors that scrapped an all-in financial commitment that failed to result in the franchise’s first back-to-back playoff appearances since 2008 and 2009, and that they put the blame for the unfulfilled expectations on him.

O’Connell’s preferences would be a factor, too. Much of that depends on his relationship with new G.M. Nolan Teasley.

As to Flores, the chances of another team hiring him to be a head coach seem to be small, given his pending lawsuit against the league and four teams (the Dolphins, Giants, Broncos, and Texans). While it would be wrong (legally and morally) for the league at large to hold the litigation against Flores, he has already been snubbed multiple times since the case was filed. And that Korn Ferry document that recently surfaced specifically calls the ongoing legal fight a potential “distraction.”

The other question is whether Flores would jump to another team as a defensive coordinator, if the aversion to hiring him as a head coach would not keep another team from giving him that role.

Then there’s the Mougey angle. Cimini said that Mougey has spoken highly of O’Connell in internal meetings with the Jets. (Awkward, if Glenn was in the room when those comments were made.) As it becomes more clear that Glenn isn’t long for Gang Green, Mougey has a clear incentive to devise a plan based on him sticking around and not being supplanted and/or significantly undermined (see the Giants) by a new coach who would want to hire his own G.M. O’Connell could be Mougey’s Hail Mary.

Beyond that, teams don’t often trade draft capital for head coaches (even though they should). Whether it’s rooted in honor among oligarchs or it’s a manifestation of the effort to remove power from the coaches (which has primarily occurred through efforts to hold down their pay), it doesn’t happen nearly as frequently as it could or should.

For now, it’s all speculation. And, as history shows us, the Jets are going to Jet. They’ve done nothing in recent years to create the impression that they’re capable of identifying and hiring the best candidate to coach the team.

Until they do, there’s no reason to assume they’ll suddenly get it right.
