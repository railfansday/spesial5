Dodgers protect four prospects from Rule 5 draft, including top pitcher snatched from Yankees

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

There might not be a Rule 5 draft in Major League Baseball this winter, with an upcoming lockout threatening to cancel the annual event.

But the Dodgers didn't take any chances on Tuesday, still electing to protect four minor-league prospects who would've been Rule 5 eligible.

In a series of roster moves Tuesday, the Dodgers added pitchers Christian Zazueta, Jackson Ferris, Adam Serwinoski and Luke Fox to their 40-man roster, while designating for assignment pitcher Jake Cousins and recent waiver claim infielder Christian Cairo.

The Dodgers' reigning Minor League Pitcher of the Year Christian Zazueta is continuing his rise through the franchise's minor-league system. MLB Photos via Getty Images

The moves ensure none of the four young pitchers will be subject to a possible Rule 5 draft — in which non-40-man players who have accrued a certain amount of time in their organizations become available for other teams to "draft" and add to their own roster — while also subjecting Cousins (a veteran reliever recovering from Tommy John surgery) and Cairo (who was claimed from the Mets last week) to waivers if they are to remain in the organization.

The last time MLB experienced an offseason lockout, during the 2021-22 offseason, the Rule 5 draft (which is usually held during the winter meetings in December) was canceled and not rescheduled.

It's possible that could be the case again this year, with the league's current CBA set to expire on Dec. 1. But last week, general manager Brandon Gomes said the club would operate as if things were "business as usual," leading the Dodgers to protect their four prospects ahead of a Tuesday afternoon deadline.

Brandon Gomes, general manager of the Los Angeles Dodgers, speaks with media during the MLB general manager meetings in Las Vegas, Tuesday, Nov. 10, 2026. AP Photo/John Locher

The most obvious choice was Zazueta, a 22-year-old right-handed starter whom the Dodgers acquired for reliever Caleb Ferguson from the Yankees three years ago in what has become a steal of a transaction.

A little-known talent at that time, Zazueta has since blossomed into one of the most promising young arms in baseball. He is ranked as the top pitcher (and fifth overall player) in the Dodgers' farm system. He is the No. 33 overall prospect in the sport, according to MLB Pipeline. And he is coming off a strong 2026 campaign in which he posted a 3.44 ERA in his first stint in Double-A and won the club's Minor League Pitcher of the Year award for a second straight season.

Serwinoski and Ferris, two other trade acquisitions in recent years, were also added to the 40-man roster as expected.

Ferris has been on a bit of a roller coaster since joining the organization in the Michael Busch trade from the Cubs, along with since-traded outfielder Zyhir Hope (who was a key part of the deadline blockbuster for Tarik Skubal this past summer). The 22-year-old had impressive stints in Double-A in 2024 and 2025, but then saw his ERA balloon over 7.00 during his first four months in Triple-A this year.

Former Yankees pitcher Jake Cousins, posing for a photo at Steinbrenner Field during spring training in 2025, was DFA'd by the Dodgers as part of a roster shuffle on Tuesday. Charles Wenzelberg / New York Post

From Aug. 16 on, however, the left-hander posted a 2.08 ERA over his final seven appearances (39 innings), showing enough promise to reestablish himself as a future piece for the Dodgers — potentially as early as next year.

Serwinoski, another left-hander, has had his own ups and downs (including a 5.31 ERA in Double-A this year) but is still seen as a projectable big-league southpaw further down the line.

Fox was the lone homegrown player of the group, drafted by the Dodgers out of Duke as a 17th-rounder in 2023. He was recovering from Tommy John surgery at the time but has pitched to a 2.91 ERA since his return in 2024, finishing this past season in Double-A.

Cousins, a 32-year-old reliever who hasn't appeared in the big leagues since 2024 with the Yankees because of injuries, was the most notable roster cut, after spending all of last year with the team rehabbing from a 2025 Tommy John procedure.

Cairo, a 25-year-old utilityman who has yet to make his MLB debut, was only acquired in a waiver claim last week from the Mets, who had DFA'd him in their own roster crunch.
