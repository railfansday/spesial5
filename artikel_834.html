Palace docked points for using four sub windows in WSL win

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Scottish striker Kirsty Howat signed for Crystal Palace in September 2025

Crystal Palace have been deducted three points after using too many substitution windows during their win on the opening day of the 2026-27 Women's Super League season.

Palace, who were playing their first match following promotion back to the WSL, beat Everton 1-0 on 6 September at Gander Green Lane.

However, manager Jo Potter brought on Kirsty Howat for Shannon O'Brien in the seventh minute of second-half stoppage time to try and shore up the victory.

It was the fourth separate substitution made by Potter, who had also made changes in the 62nd, 83rd and 90th minutes.

While WSL managers are allowed to make five changes in a game, they must be made across only three stoppages in play to avoid excessive time wasting.

Crystal Palace deducted points following WSL win

Palace have been stripped of the three points they earned in the match - one of only two WSL games the Eagles have won this season.

This punishment drops Palace down one place in the WSL table to 13th - the relegation play-off place.

Palace are now below fellow promoted side Birmingham City in the table on goal difference, with both sides now having six points - six behind Everton in 11th.

In a statement on their club website, external Palace said they were "disappointed" by the decision.

The decision was made by an independent tribunal after Palace admitted the breach of competition rule 5.19.1.

The rule states: "A substitution can only be made when play is stopped for any reason and the referee has given permission. A club at its discretion may use five substitute players during a match. For the avoidance of doubt, each club has no more than three substitution opportunities in each match."

The tribunal said that while the rule breach was inadvertent and "not intended to secure an unfair advantage", it said a points deduction was necessary in order to provide a deterrent to clubs against making a similar mistake in future.

"The integrity of the competition would be damaged if a club that had breached the rules on substitution opportunities, and had won the match, were allowed to keep the points it obtained, save in exceptional circumstances," the tribunal stated in its full written decision., external

They said the lateness of the substitution in the match - so late that Howat did not touch the ball after coming on - did not excuse Palace from punishment.

It also ruled "that responsibility for complying with the rule rests with clubs, not match officials".

Palace - who placed the word "fourth" in inverted commas in their response to the ruling - are aiming to improve on their only previous WSL campaign in 2024-25, where they only won twice in 22 matches and were relegated by a 10-point margin.

Their statement read: "Everyone at the club is obviously frustrated that such a minor infraction, given that the player was on the pitch for only 46 seconds while the ball was in play, has led to such a significant penalty â a penalty which we do not agree is reasonable or proportionate.

"We are disappointed for the players and staff who worked so hard to secure a deserved victory on our return to the WSL."

Forward Lexi Lloyd-Smith had scored the only goal of the game in the 34th minute on her Palace debut.
