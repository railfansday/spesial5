Germany news: Friedrich Merz set to confirm presidential nominee

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

The executive committee of the German chancellor's CDU party is expected to finalize its nomination for the country's president. DW has more.

This is a roundup of the top stories from and about Germany on the weekend of Saturday, November 7, and Sunday, November 8, 2026:

Germany's largest union, IG Metall, has called on workers at a Mercedes-Benz plant outside Berlin to walk out for two hours on Monday amid stalled wage negotiations.

Employees at local car supplier Gestamp in Ludwigsfelde, a town south of Berlin, have also been called upon to take part in the industrial action.

IG Metall is demanding 5% more pay for the around 96,000 employees in the metal and electrical industry in the states of Berlin and Brandenburg as part of a 12-month collective wage agreement, as well as job and location security.

The strike is being called after the union failed to reach agreement in a third round of a wage dispute.

During a second round of talks, the employers presented a coordinated nationwide offer of a one-off payment next year and a 1.3% pay rise in July 2028, which was rejected by the union as inadequate.

Young people are increasingly denying the facts of the Holocaust or downplaying its horrors, according to the head of the Anne Frank Zentrum in Berlin.

In remarks made to the Catholic news agency KNA ahead of the 88th anniversary of the November pogroms in Germany, Veronika Nahm said that people attending traveling exhibitions organized by the center sometimes asked, "Is that even true?"

She said that people downplaying or denying the Holocaust was particularly apparent in social media posts connected with the center.

She said there were comments such as, "Why do you commemorate Anne Frank, why not a child in Gaza?"

Nahm attributes the rise in such opinions to ignorance rather than bad intentions on the part of young people, saying they were not as well-informed on the topic in schools as they were 15 years ago.

"There has been a generational change of teachers. For many older teachers, it was close to their hearts, and it was mentioned in many places and subjects during lessons. That is different today," she said.

Nahm said it was important that visits by young people to sites commemorating the Holocaust be used to strengthen their feeling of empathy, something that worked particularly well with the story of Anne Frank.

The Anne Frank Zentrum in Berlin is a partner organization to the Anne Frank House in Amsterdam.

Anne Frank was a Jewish girl who hid in that house during the 1940-1945 German occupation of the Netherlands and wrote a famous diary, later published, about her experiences and feelings.

She was discovered and deported by the Nazis and died in the Bergen-Belsen concentration camp in early 1945.

The November pogroms, during which Jews and Jewish property and institutions were attacked, took place on November 9-10, 1938, across the German Reich at the instigation of Germany's Nazi regime.

Most people in Germany favor the government imposing a cap on prices for gasoline and diesel, a survey has shown.

The government is currently debating such a cap, which would replace a current fuel tax rebate that runs out at the end of the year.

According to the survey by the NIM Institute, 71% of the people asked said they would like the government to control fuel prices.

Altogether 66% of respondents were in favor of levying a windfall tax on oil corporations, which have been turning in massive profits amid shortages caused largely by the war in Iran.

Other measures to relieve the pressure on motorists amid surging fuel prices were less popular, according to the survey.

Just 56% of respondents wanted to see a rise in the allowed tax deduction for commuters, and just 52% favored direct payments from the state to drivers on low incomes.

German Environment Minister Carsten Schneider has said he hopes agreement will be reached on measures to limit global warming at the UN Climate Change Conference over the next two weeks.

"The faster global emissions fall, the faster we can bring the temperature curve back down to 1.5 degrees Celsius (2.7 degrees Fahrenheit). At the same time, we must do more to protect people from the effects of heat waves, droughts and floods," Schneider said.

Schneider was referring to the target of limiting global warming to 1.5 C compared with pre-industrial levels agreed on under the Paris climate accords.

The UN said last month, however, that this target will be missed within the century, with most nations failing to reduce their greenhouse gas emissions by a significant amount.

Schneider also said moving toward electricity as the main source of energy was the best way to combat climate change, which is being driven largely by the human burning of fossil fuels such as oil and gas.

Schneider will be the only German Cabinet member to attend the  COP31 summit, which kicks off in Turkey on Monday.

Chancellor Friedrich Merz has decided to skip the event, citing "other commitments."

His decision has drawn considerable criticism, with leading German climate activist Luisa Neubauer saying it sent "the worst possible signal to the international community."

"It is unworthy of Germany and an insult, particularly to those nations around the world that are already facing an existential threat from rising sea levels," she said.

Media reports say Merz is planning a visit to an Italian restaurant with his wife and daughter for his 71st birthday on Wednesday while the summit is underway, leading Neubauer to describe his non-appearance as a "Bella Italia absence."

Nearly 200 states will convene for the summit in Antalya.

Police in Berlin are still looking for a 13-year-old girl who disappeared on the way to school on Tuesday.

In a statement, Berlin police said they could not rule out that the girl, who has been identified as Lisa T., "has been the victim of a crime."

"Based on the current state of the investigation, it is assumed that the child did not leave her home environment voluntarily," police said.

Around 220 police officers were deployed in the Marzahn district of the German capital on Saturday, with a police spokesman saying that the search was time-consuming because of the large number of houses and parks in the area.

The search began on Friday, supported by sniffer dogs and a police helicopter.

Women on the executive boards of Germany's leading listed companies were earning almost as much on average as their male colleagues in 2025, a study by consultancy EY says.

According to the study, female board members at companies listed on the DAX, MDAX and SDAX indices, not including chief executives, earned an average of €2.20 million ($2.45 million) last year.

Their male counterparts, on the other hand, saw a nearly 2% reduction in their pay down to €2.23 million, which reduced the pay gap from €121,000 to €26,000.

The study also found that there were more women serving on executive boards in 2025 than a year previously, with 101 female board members compared with 88 in 2024 and just 33 in 2013.

"Women on executive boards are no longer the exception. That is also reflected in salaries," said EY partner Jens Massmann.

The executive committee of Chancellor Friedrich Merz's Christian Democrats (CDU) is scheduled to meet in the evening to finalize the party's nomination for the post of German president.

So far, Merz has declined to confirm widespread reports that his party will join with Bavarian allies, the Christian Social Union (CSU), in putting forward Bavarian parliamentary speaker Ilse Aignerfor the job, but he is expected to officially do so at the meeting.

The actual announcement of the choice of the conservative CDU/CSU bloc for a successor to President Frank-Walter Steinmeier will be made on Monday.

The new German head of state is to be chosen on January 30 by a special assembly, the Federal Convention, which comprises all 630 members of the Bundestag and an equal number of electors who represent the state parliaments.

Aigner, 61, who enjoys support from within all the major parties, is seen as have good chances of taking the largely ceremonial job as German head of state.

If she were to become president, she would become the first woman and the first CSU member to hold the position.

Iranian opposition leader Reza Pahlavi on Saturday received a "Freedom Prize" in Frankfurt in the central German state of Hesse.

Some 18,000 people rallied to show support for Reza Pahlavi, according to a police statement.

The event was held at St Paul's Church. The building hosted the Germany's first-ever elected parliament in 1848-1849, known as the Frankfurt Assembly.

Pahlavi is the son of the last shah of Iran, Mohammed Reza Pahlavi, an autocrat who fled into exile after being deposed in a 1979 revolution.

The 1979 revolution was followed by the transformation of Iran into an Islamic republic and the consolidation of power around the Shiite Muslim clergy.

Pahlavi was handed the prize by the Friedrich Naumann Foundation, which is tied to the pro-business Free Democratic Party (FDP).

A man allegedly doused an 18-year-old with a flammable liquid and set him on fire in southwest Berlin.

According to a statement issued on Saturday by police in the German capital, the victim was hospitalized with burns.

The 18-year-old had been out with a 17-year-old female companion on Friday evening. An argument broke out with a 24-year-old man who allegedly sexually harassed the woman.

The suspect who was arrested by police reportedly poured flammable liquid over the younger man and set him on fire.

Police were initially unable to say how the fire was extinguished. "He was wearing a jacket," said a police spokesman.

Emergency services took the 18-year-old to hospital to be treated for burns on his hand and torso.

Chancellor Merz has declined to confirm media reports that his Christian Democrats (CDU) has agreed with its conservative ally, the Bavarian Christian Social Union (CSU), on the CSU's Ilse Aigner as nomination for the next German president.

Merz said on Friday only that his party had agreed with the CSU to present its nomination on Monday.

According to media reports, Aigner, the current speaker of the Bavarian state parliament, would be put forward as a joint candidate of the three coalition parties — CDU, CSU and center-left Social Democrats (SPD) — as a successor to President Frank-Walter Steinmeier.

Merz said earlier this week he wanted to see a woman ​elected as president.

Aigner has also received strong verbal backing from some SPD and Green lawmakers.

In his interview with papers of the Funke media group published on Saturday, Defense Minister Boris Pistorius said he soon expected a replacement for the French-German FCAS fighter jet project that fell flat this year.

Pistorius said he believed that an alternative would be decided on by the end of March 2027, adding that he was very interested in Germany's aviation industry playing a role in an such project.

He said there were "two options on the table."

One was "participation in the Global Combat Air Program (GCAP) of the British, Italians and Japanese," with discussions underway as to whether Germany's involvement could "lead to a long delay, which would barely be acceptable to all participants."

The second option, he said, was that the European aerospace company Airbus developed its own project with other European partners for the start of the 2040s.

In October, British Prime Minister Andy Burnham offered Chancellor Friedrich Merz the chance to become involved in the joint development of a new fighter jet within the GCAP program.

Justice Minister Stefanie Hubig has defended the right of young people to call for their rights in demonstrations, despite the violence that marred last weekend's Gen Z protests.

"The overwhelming majority of demonstrators were peaceful," she told the daily Neue Osnabrücker Zeitung.

"And it is young people's good right to demonstrate on the issues that concern them," she said, adding that she did not feel that the situation was equivalent to that in neighboring France, where recent protests have been accompanied by considerable rioting.

At the same time, she stressed that looting and violence toward security forces were "serious offenses," saying that she was already working on a law that would allow harsher penalties for attacks on police and other emergency personnel.

But Hubig spoke out against imposing a general ban on protests that have been planned in two weeks' time, saying that would send "a completely wrong signal."

"We are always expecting young people to become actively involved in politics," she said.

"It is the right of young people to take to the streets and call for better education and social justice."

Hubig is a member of the center-left Social Democrats (SPD), the junior partner in Germany's conservative-led coalition government.

Defense Minister Boris Pistorius has said he believes that the German armed forces, or Bundeswehr, will not need conscripts to achieve the necessary level of recruitment.

Pistorius told newspapers from the Funke media group that the Bundeswehr currently had some 190,000 members, the highest level in 13 years, meaning that voluntary recruitment measures were working.

"If this trajectory continues, we will have no problems with growth," he said, and there would not have be be "a discussion about conscription of whatever kind."

He said he understood the concerns of teenagers and young adults who recently protested against the idea of military conscription.

"But when people say at demonstrations, 'we don't want to die at the front or in war,' I can only say: We don't want that either," he said.

He said that was precisely the reason why strong armed forces were necessary "that can act as a deterrent so we don't get into a situation in the first place where we have to fight somewhere."

Pistorius also said the Bundeswehr, currently male-dominated, was seeking to employ more women, saying the armed forces would be "particularly strong if it represents the breadth of our society."
