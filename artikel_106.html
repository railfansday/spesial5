As Gambia gears up for election, can Barrow secure a third term?

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

President Adama Barrow is seeking a third term as Gambians vote on December 5. The election will test the opposition's appeal and the country's progress on democratic reform.

Gambia votes on December 5 in a presidential election that takes place nearly 10 years after voters ended the 22-year rule of military ruler Yahya Jammeh.

President Adama Barrow, who succeeded Jammeh in 2017, is running for a third term despite an earlier pledge not to do so.

Barrow is running against the tiny West African country's main opposition leader Ousainou Darboe of the United Democratic Party, youth candidate Talib Ahmed Bensouda, who until last week was mayor of Kanifing Municipality, and four other candidates.

The field also includes independent candidates Almameh Gibba, a lawmaker who is backed by Jammeh, and former Vice President Isatou Touray, the only woman in the presidential race.

Gambia is still governed under its 1997 constitution, which was adopted during Jammeh's rule.

A proposed replacement, which included presidential term limits, was rejected by the National Assembly in July 2025 after failing to secure the required three-quarter majority.

Barrow has defended his decision to seek a third term, saying voters should decide who leads the country. While he says he supports presidential term limits, he has argued that their introduction depends on securing enough parliamentary backing for a new constitution.

"I believe in term limits. And the Gambian people are the ones who elect a president," Barrow told reporters after submitting his nomination papers to the Independent Electoral Commission (IEC) earlier in November. "So they decide the fate of a president and it is up to the Gambian people to decide whether I should be president for a third time or not."

Opposition parties have said their focus is not only on defeating Barrow but also on ensuring the election is conducted fairly.

Lamin Manneh, the United Democratic Party's administrative secretary, said the party's immediate priority is to ensure that the IEC conducts credible elections.

"The IEC has the responsibility of putting Gambians into confidence, ensuring that they guarantee us a credible election, a free election and a fair election," said Manneh.

Political analyst Malang Faye said the campaign was still taking shape after the official period began on November 16.

"The incumbent is already ahead," he told DW. "Some of the parties are yet to start their campaign." But he cautioned against judging electoral support by rally attendance alone.

"A large crowd at a political rally does not necessarily mean the same level of support at the ballot box," Faye added, noting that the election will test whether the democratic transition that began in 2016 can be consolidated.

Many voters say their immediate concerns are jobs, affordable living costs and better public services.

The World Bank has projected economic growth of 5.3% in 2026, but said extreme poverty is expected to remain at about 21%.

The 2025 Labour Force Survey puts youth unemployment at 11.5%, while 41.3% of young people are not in employment, education or training.

The figures reflect concerns voiced by many young Gambians about the lack of economic opportunities.

Alhagie Sama, a voter in Banjul, said young people need opportunities at home, rather than having to risk dangerous journeys abroad.

"We need a country where young people do not have to risk their lives crossing the Mediterranean Sea in search of opportunities," he said, adding that he wants the next government to deliver decent jobs, make basic necessities more affordable and improve access to health care and education.

Another voter and activist, Binta Bojang, said unemployment remains one of the country's biggest challenges.

"I would want the next president to prioritize employment, like particularly youth employment," she told DW, noting that joblessness goes beyond conventional definitions.

"We need to understand unemployment beyond the conventional definition, I mean the dictionary definition. Because unemployment definitely manifests differently in the lives of ordinary Gambians."

The Independent Electoral Commission has said 1,146,474 people are registered to vote. They will cast their ballots across 53 constituencies, 730 polling centers and 1,773 polling stations.

The commission has been training election officials in voting procedures, counting, verification and the transmission of results. It said the preparations are aimed at ensuring a peaceful and credible election.

International observers are also monitoring the process.

Following an invitation from Gambian authorities, the European Union has deployed an election observation mission, with teams of analysts and long-term observers across the country.

The United Nations has also urged candidates to honor the peace pact signed on November 16.

"History will judge not what you sign today, but what you do tomorrow and in the days to come," Barrie Freeman, the UN's deputy special representative for West Africa and the Sahel, told candidates.

Under the pact, presidential candidates pledged "to respect the independence and constitutional mandate of the IEC, to cooperate with it throughout the electoral process, and to seek redress through lawful and appropriate channels should disputes arise."

The December 5 election will test not only Barrow's bid for another term, but also whether the democratic gains made since 2016 can be translated into lasting reforms and improved living conditions.

DW correspondent Aminata Sanyang in Gambia contributed reporting.
