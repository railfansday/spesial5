How Mannington Commercial Approaches Design as a System

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Family-owned flooring manufacturer Mannington Commercial aims to solve problems for interior designers and their clients. That’s why the company focuses on giving designers more freedom and flexibility with product, as discussed in The Art of Versatility last year. Building on that concept, Mannington Commercial designs products in families so individual flooring collections work together across an entire project.

“An entryway has different demands than a lobby, patient room, corridor, or collaborative area, but those spaces still need to feel connected. If every collection lives on its own island, then the designer has to create all of those relationships,” says Stacey Gerbman, Mannington Commercial Director of Design. “The shift is from thinking only about individual launches to thinking about the life of the entire portfolio.”

Rather than creating matching sets, Mannington Commercial establishes a design language that allows products to complement one another and carry a design concept throughout a project. Each new product should be distinct yet work with existing and future collections, simplifying specification for interior designers.

Stepping Stones. Photo courtesy of Mannington Commercial.

Mannington Commercial’s design team plans ahead to ensure new collections pair naturally with existing lines. “Before a collection is fully developed, we look at the portfolio as a whole: where there are gaps, which colors and undertones are important, what scales are missing, and which materials the new collection is likely to sit beside,” says Gerbman. The team considers how a collection might work with carpet, LVT, sheet, rubber, or an entryway product, as well as how scale and color should transition between materials.

For Mannington Commercial, the key to connecting carpet with LVT and other materials is its yarn system, which Gerbman describes as “color architecture.” A core palette of solids and twists provides a foundation that carries across collections while allowing for different effects. “We do not look at carpet in isolation,” Gerbman notes. “We place the poms and tufted trials next to LVT, sheet, rubber, and other materials and study undertone, value, temperature, and contrast.” Rather than seeking exact color matches, the goal is a “believable relationship.” Gerbman adds, “The hard surface may provide clarity and structure, while the carpet adds softness, depth, and variation. When the yarn bank is built thoughtfully, those materials can play different roles and still feel as if they belong in the same environment.”

Curated Underfoot. Photo courtesy of Mannington Commercial.

Curated Underfoot puts this approach into practice with Mannington Commercial’s first system of carpet and LVT for commercial interiors. Five neutral tones carry across the LVT and five carpet patterns, creating a shared color foundation. “But each surface and pattern brings a different level of texture and movement,” Gerbman explains. “A designer can stay tonal or create contrast through pattern and scale, and the result still feels connected without looking overly matched. It supports the system while leaving room for the designer’s own authorship.”

“Curated Underfoot reflects the way designers think about a space as a complete environment rather than a series of individual flooring decisions,” says Whitney LeGate, Senior Vice President, Commercial Product. “By bringing carpet and LVT together through one synchronized collection, we’re making it easier to create interiors that are visually cohesive and built to perform.”

Atto, a terrazzo-inspired LVT, anchors the system with bold pattern, layered movement and contemporary color that translates to carpet for a seamless transition between hard- and soft-surface flooring.

Stepping Stones. Photo courtesy of Mannington Commercial.

Another product family, Stepping Stones, combines performance and aesthetics in an LVT collection for healthcare settings. Six curated palettes pair complementary colors and textures across wood, stone, and other material expressions, with tonal terrazzo anchoring each palette. Patterns inspired by nature take on softer, warmer qualities to promote a serene, restorative environment. The palettes simplify specification and help designers maintain visual continuity throughout a healthcare facility, where arrival, waiting, treatment and recovery areas require different flooring but should still feel connected.

BioSpec. Photo courtesy of Mannington Commercial.

Mannington Commercial also makes BioSpec, a line of homogeneous sheet vinyl designed for high-use commercial and healthcare spaces. Mannington Commercial is the only manufacturer producing homogeneous resilient sheet vinyl in the United States. And it’s the only one making heterogeneous sheet vinyl in 6-foot, 9-foot, and 12-foot widths for easier installation, fewer seams, and less waste. Similar to the relationships in other product families, this line includes three variations tailored to specific needs: BioSpec Armor for demanding, high-traffic environments; BioSpec SR for enhanced slip resistance; and BioSpec MD for superior abrasion resistance and an extensive color selection. The products work together and pair with other Mannington Commercial resilient flooring and carpet.

LayeredLogic. Photo courtesy of Mannington Commercial.

Continuing this systems-based approach in 2027, Mannington Commercial is launching LayeredLogic, a heterogeneous sheet collection for healthcare interiors. Its three patterns share a visual language, allowing them to be layered, as the prints progress from subtle texture to more expressive designs. Created for acute care facilities, LayeredLogic gives designers a way to define spaces and create rhythm through an intentional palette designed to last and work with current and future Mannington Commercial LVT and carpet collections. By designing individual products to work together as part of a larger system, Mannington Commercial takes an approach that feels familiar and fitting for a family-owned company.
