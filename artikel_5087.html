Tesla FSD ‘subscriptions’ hit 1.74M, counted the way Musk’s pay package wants

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Tesla says it now has 1.74 million active “Full Self-Driving” subscriptions, up 67% from a year ago. A footnote in its Q3 2026 shareholder letter explains how it gets there: the metric follows “our 2025 CEO Performance Award” and “includes both upfront payment and monthly subscriptions.”

Here’s what Tesla has reported under that definition:

Tesla stopped selling FSD as a one-time purchase on February 14, 2026. New customers can only get it as a subscription, at $99 a month in the US. But everyone who paid upfront before that, at prices that reached $15,000, stays in the count as long as the car is active. The only exclusion is free trials.

We flagged this in April when Tesla reported 1.28 million. Six months later, Tesla still doesn’t say how many of the 1.74 million actually pay every month.

The same approach shows up elsewhere in the letter. Tesla’s “cumulative deliveries” figure, now 10.2 million, also counts “any unsupervised Robotaxis placed into commercial operation,” again to match the pay package, which has a 20 million vehicle milestone.

Tesla also says more than 75% of new deliveries in North America included FSD subscriptions in Q3, “a nearly twenty percentage point sequential increase.” It says it believes “FSD is driving demand in markets where it is available.”

That’s a big number, and Tesla doesn’t define it. The letter doesn’t say whether it counts subscriptions started at delivery with an introductory offer, or how many of those buyers are still paying after a few months. The active-subscription metric excludes free trials, but Tesla doesn’t say the same about the attach rate.

Musk’s 2025 award has 12 tranches. Each requires a market cap milestone and one operational milestone. The operational list includes 20 million vehicles delivered, 10 million active FSD subscriptions, 1 million Optimus robots, 1 million robotaxis in commercial operation, and adjusted EBITDA targets. As of June 30, Tesla considered only one of them probable: 20 million deliveries.

Tesla added 260,000 net FSD subscriptions in Q3, its best quarter yet. At that pace, it would take about eight years to reach 10 million. At the pace of the last 12 months, about 700,000 a year, it would take closer to 12. The award runs until 2035.

Counting people who bought FSD outright isn’t wrong on its own. Those cars do have the software. The problem is calling them “subscriptions” and presenting the total as proof of momentum, when the definition was written for Musk’s pay package and not for investors trying to understand the business.

The metrics that are far off in Musk's 2025 comp plan don't matter because if there is a change of control (i.e., SpaceX buys Tesla), all the comp plan metrics vest and count as achieved, and good old Elon gets paid. And you wonder why he wants this merger?

This is now Tesla’s headline software metric. The board wrote the definition, Musk’s compensation depends on it, and the company won’t say how many people pay $99 a month. Tesla obviously knows that number.

The attach rate is the more interesting claim, if it holds up. A 75% take rate on a $99-a-month product would be a real business. So show us the retention: how many North American buyers who started a subscription in Q2 are still paying today?
