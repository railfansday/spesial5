Investing - Theory, News &amp; General • Re: Mathematical Error in 15% Savings Rule

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The famous "15%" and "70%" are catchy rules-of-thumb and like all thumbs are meant to be broken.
Just passed the one-year retirement anniversary, and our yearly spending is about 25% of that in the year prior, with the biggest reductions being:

Much lower taxes paid (this alone was nearly 60% of our pre-retirement spending)
No retirement plan contributions (about 15% of our pre-retirement spending)

We also injected much less into our brokerage accounts, so if you call that "spending" then our reduction was even greater. We also have no more college or other child-related costs, but those ended a few years before retirement. Our mortgage is down by about 20% due to a refi in 2021.

So this 70% rule of thumb may be pretty meaningless to a lot of people, depending on what expenses one categorizes as "spending" and a lot of individualized considerations. Our spending net of taxes and retirement plan contributions stayed pretty level in year one, although I think our standard of living generally went up with more vacations and some upgrades around the house. Planning/estimating one's expenses in retirement is extremely important and requires a great deal of thought and consideration - assuming 70% or some other number is likely a big mistake imho.
