‘No margin for error’: Ohio State faces costly decision on Ryan Day

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The Ohio State Buckeyes' disappointing season hit a new low on Saturday, as Nebraska snapped a 31-game losing streak against ranked opponents with a 31-28 win in Lincoln. The loss dropped Ohio State to 7-4 and all but ended the Buckeyes' hopes of making the College Football Playoff.

With the frustration surrounding head coach Ryan Day reaching new heights, plenty of fans are wondering whether Ohio State will finally make a change. But if the Buckeyes do decide to move on, it's going to cost them a massive buyout of $57.5 million.

Ohio State's season looked promising early on, as the Buckeyes started 6-1 with their only loss coming by one point at Texas. But quarterback Julian Sayin got hurt late in a 19-15 win over Indiana, and things quickly fell apart from there.

The Buckeyes lost 30-18 to USC, then blew a 14-0 lead against Oregon as the Ducks scored 38 straight points in a 41-28 win. After that loss, Ohio State fired special teams coordinator Robby Discher.

The loss to Nebraska was Ohio State's fourth of the season, and the reaction was brutal. After the game, Day declined to give defensive coordinator Matt Patricia a vote of confidence, saying that Ohio State will "evaluate everything" after its game against Michigan.

It's just the latest disappointment in what has been a rocky tenure for Day. Ohio State won the national title after the 2024 season, but last season the Buckeyes lost to Indiana in the Big Ten Championship Game, then fell 24-14 to Miami in their first College Football Playoff game.

Day has also had to deal with critics like Barstool Sports founder Dave Portnoy, who demanded an apology from Ohio State fans after the Oregon loss for being right about Day all along.

According to Robby Kalland of CBS Sports, a four-loss season has historically spelled trouble for Ohio State coaches. The last time the Buckeyes lost four games was 2011, when Luke Fickell went 6-7 in his only season before being replaced by Urban Meyer, and John Cooper was fired after an 8-4 season in 2000.

But firing Day wouldn't come cheap. Day has five years left on his contract after this season, and his buyout calls for him to be paid $11.5 million for each remaining year.

That means Ohio State would owe Day $57.5 million if it moved on after this season. According to Kalland, that would be the second-largest buyout ever, behind only the $76 million that Texas A&M owed Jimbo Fisher.

"The real question is whether the Buckeyes identify an obviously better candidate to take over for Day. You don't make a $57.5 million coaching firing without having a clear target in mind, and the Buckeyes would have to be confident in their ability to get an upgrade to make the move," Kalland wrote.

Kalland believes that Day's 89-16 record at Ohio State, the lack of a clear standout candidate on the coaching market, and the cost of his buyout could buy him another year.

"But even if he's afforded a chance in 2027, there will be no margin for error granted to him after exhausting his goodwill this season," Kalland wrote.

Before any decision is made, Day still has one more chance to win over Ohio State fans, with Michigan up next on Saturday.
