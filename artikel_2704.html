Tuesday Miami Dolphins Thoughts With The Earl

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

This game really depends upon your outlook for the season. If you are a "get the first draft pick at all costs" person, then you like this outcome. If you want to see a win and positive development, this was a horrible outcome. It was another of the "close, but no cigar" games the Dolphins have played this season. Here's what I saw.

In previous games, there was a tradeoff between the offense and defense over which would show up. In this game, the offense was expected to be worse than the defense, with a backup QB getting his first-ever NFL action. However, the Dolphins still couldn't go a game without some form of tradeoff. In this matchup, it was a tradeoff between the pass defense and the run defense. The Dolphins allowed a completion percentage over 70% and had a run defense that allowed less than 4 yards per carry. The pass defense had its best performance of the season. They held Danny Dimes to 50% completion and a 4.9 YPA average. They picked him off twice, one of which was returned for a TD. They allowed a passing TD and a couple of big plays but were solid in coverage. In a shock to everyone, the Dolphins allowed more rushing yards to TE Tyler Warren (3) than receiving yards (1). Warren was held without a catch until the 4th quarter and was a complete non-factor.

Unfortunately, while the Dolphins pass defense stepped up covering the one receiving threat the Colts had, the run defense couldn't stop the other threat the Colts posed. The Colts rushed for 172 yards on 34 carries, 106 of those yards coming in the 1st half on 19 carries. That's a 5.6 YPA allowed. They improved a little in the second half, allowing 66 yards on 15 carries and a 4.4 YPA. But on the final drive, they allowed 12 yards on 2 carries that finished off the game. Just once, the team needs a complete performance on both sides of the ball. Now they need a complete performance on both sides of the ball. I will give them some due credit. They limited the Colts to 17 offensive points and were hamstrung by a limited offense, but the run defense was the backbreaker in this game. With the injuries mounting on the DL, I don't know how they can fix it.

We'll get to the obvious problem in a moment. Bradley Pinion had 3 punts for 134 yards, with a 55-yarder. That's an average of 44.7 overall and 39.5 on the other two punts. Indy's punter had 6 for 290, averaging 48.3. The league average is 47.5. Miami still allowed too many yards in the kick return game. They had the 15-yard penalty on Louis, though I won't complain about penalties since the total was lopsided in Miami's favor and that penalty was ultimately inconsequential. It is still unacceptable, though.

I said on Friday that the Dolphins simply aren't good enough to win without winning in the margins. This is a young team working to find its identity. They come to work every day and put it all on the line every game. This isn't the 2024 or 2025 Dolphins with players obviously checking out and Tua laughing on the sideline or Hill taking himself out of a game. This is a bad team playing like they are trying to get the top seed in the AFC. When the kicker, the second oldest player on the team, shanks not one, but two FGs and misses a PAT as the icing on the cake, that is a level of terrible that cannot be measured. After I watched the game and went to bed thinking of all I planned to say about how he cannot remain on the team if the coaches wanna keep the locker room, the Dolphins made my job easier and waived him on Monday. So that is a non-issue now.

And I've seen the tweets about how the misses keep the tank alive and all that. But I cannot stress enough how not only is tanking a bad idea in general, but you really only tank for a QB, and the 2027 QB class is buns. They are bad. It's an elevated class due to absences and wouldn't be considered top prospects in a class like 2018 or 2020. I would love for the Dolphins to get Jeremiah Smith as much as any other fan. But this team is NOT trying to lose. They aren't playing like they are trying to lose. They are talent deficient, but they aren't tanking. So when you play as hard as they do and the veteran kicker can't do the basic requirement of putting the ball through the uprights, then a message needs to be sent, and it was.

Kyle McCord's play will be dissected by many others, so I don't want to get deep into it. I thought he played ok. I thought he played like a player getting his first NFL snaps. His performance isn't worth getting worked up over one way or another. I said in Friday's post that a healthy Willis wins this game by 2 scores, and I still believe it. But McCord played well enough for the Dolphins to get a win against another equally terrible team and was let down. The next step will be to see what, if any, level of improvements can be made.

The run game has disappeared, and it probably won't come back for a while. I tried to watch to see how much Indy loaded the box, but I didn't truly keep track. Indy is a bad team, but Miami will face some good teams coming up; teams with solid to great defenses. My guess is they will load the box and force McCord to beat them. Having Meyer in there instead of Brewer hurt as well, but the OL can only do so much. The good news for the RBs is they were involved in the passing game. All three RBs were targeted 9 times with 8 catches for 62 yards, including the 4-yard loss for Kaytron Allen. If the RBs can contribute in the pass game, then the offense can function about as normally as we can expect. Even with the drop-off, Gordon is still on pace for a 1000 yard season.

Caleb Douglas was invisible for most of the game but stepped up late. He will continue to be a force. Washington had the play of the game IMO with the one-handed catch down the sideline. He continues to improve in getting separation and being a factor in the offense. The TD throw was nice also.

The TEs were invisible, and that's a concern. I like Dulcich, and he has potential, but he is playing his way off the roster for 2027.

The passing defense stepped up as I mentioned, and its leaders are two rookies. Michael Taaffe logged his 3rd INT this season, and Chris Johnson got his 2nd in consecutive weeks. It should have been his 3rd in 4 weeks, but that Jets WR fell down and they said Johnson interfered. He was a ballhawk in college, and it is showing up now. There are 17 players in the league with 3 or more INTs and only 7 with more than 3. Taaffe has the instincts to be around the ball a lot. His development going into the rest of this season will be interesting to watch.

Regarding the kicker position, I would not be opposed to the Dolphins adding a young player – an undrafted rookie or second-year player – to fill the position. Vets are what they are, and while they offer some reliability, I'm all for giving a young player a chance to make his mark. They probably won't because, well, they are trying to win games. But if they swing and miss with young players, at least they tried. If they get a hit, that's something they can build on moving forward.

Circling back to the opening paragraph, I am not a fan of losing games for a draft pick. I want the Dolphins to win. This game was frustrating because they had it and gave it away. They lost by the margins and lost the game. Unfortunately, this was the last win on the schedule for a while. Unless McCord magically turns into Tom Brady in the next few games, the Dolphins are looking at 1-16. They host the Jets in 2 weeks, but I don't like that matchup with McCord. They host the hapless Chargers in week 16, and if Willis is back healthy, they'll have a good chance of winning. They have the 8-1 Bills and 7-2 Broncos on the road in 2 of the next 3 games. They get the 7-2 Bears and 6-4 Packers, all battling for playoff seeding and that division title. Perhaps the Bills will lock up home-field advantage by week 17, and the Dolphins will get backups. Otherwise, the outlook is grim. To paraphrase Captain Jack Sparrow: if you were waiting for the opportune moment to get a win… that was it.

We all knew this season wasn't about the wins, but 1-16 is a tough mark for anyone. Even if the Dolphins go 12-5 over the next 2 seasons (they won't), that is still 25-26 over 3 seasons. It's going to be much worse than that. It is going to be a long road back to relevance for this franchise. I'll cover that later on (either this week or next), so stay tuned.
