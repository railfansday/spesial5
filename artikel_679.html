‘Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping’ Cast Guide: All the Tributes, Mentors and Younger Versions of Characters in Prequel

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Before Katniss Everdeen ever volunteered as tribute for the Hunger Games, another District 12 victor made his way out of the arena, but with much fewer odds in his favor.

“The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping” is set 24 years before the first “Hunger Games” film (and 40 years after “The Ballad of Songbird and Snakes), following the 50th Hunger Games that crowned Haymitch Abernathy (Joseph Zada) as victor. To “celebrate” the second Quarter Quell, a higher-stakes edition of the games held every 25 years, the Capitol required twice the amount of children to participate, sending 48 tributes to the arena instead of the traditional 24.

With original stars Jennifer Lawrence, Josh Hutcherson and Woody Harrelson returning, the “Sunrise on the Reaping” brings together a star-studded lineup to play younger versions of some of the franchise’s well-known characters, including President Snow (Ralph Fiennes), Effie Trinket (Elle Fanning), Plutarch Heavensbee (Jesse Plemons), Caesar Flickerman (Kieran Culkin), Wiress (Maya Hawke) and Beetee (Kelvin Harrison Jr.). The film also introduces new characters such as fellow tribute Maysilee Donner (Mckenna Grace); District 12’s escort, Drusilla Sickle (Glenn Close) and stylist, Magno Stift (Billy Porter); and Haymitch’s girlfriend, Lenore Dove (Whitney Peak).

Part of Suzanne Collins’ best-selling book series, “Sunrise on the Reaping” is the sixth film of the franchise and arrives in theaters Nov. 20. Francis Lawrence and Nina Jacobson, who have collaborated on every “Hunger Games” film, return as director and producer, respectively. The most recent film, the President Snow prequel “The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes,” was released in 2024 and starred Tom Blyth and Rachel Zegler.

See the full cast of “The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping” below.

Joseph Zada stars as Haymitch Abernathy, a tribute from District 12 who is the future mentor to the main series’ protagonist, Katniss Everdeen. Despite being born on the same day as the Reaping, Haymitch’s birthday luck runs short when he is selected for the second Quarter Quell and soon finds himself on President Snow’s radar after publicly defying the capitol.

Throughout the film, Haymitch is thrust into several intense moments, whether they are grounded in emotion or consist of demanding action sequences. One of Zada’s favorite scenes is a particular battle near the end of the film, which he tells Variety makes him “look like a fucking action star.”

“In terms of acting, I just want to throw myself completely in the deep end with every project. And it helps so much having that attitude in the arena, because that’s just my character the whole time,” Zada says. “He’s really going for it, and I was like, ‘There’s no way for me to do this believably unless I’m throwing myself down that mountain or pushing myself to the edge of death and almost injuring myself every single day. There was no other way for me to do that and make it believable without just completely diving in the deep end.”

Haymitch Abernathy was played by Woody Harrelson in the original films.

Joining Haymitch in the second Quarter Quell is District 12 tribute Maysilee Donner, who’s played by Mckenna Grace and is the original owner of Katniss’ mockingjay pin. Initially at odds with her fellow District 12 tributes, Maysilee eventually joins their alliance with the Newcomers and grows particularly close to Haymitch, encouraging him to defy the Capitol.

As a long-time fan of the “Hunger Games” franchise, Grace tells Variety she wanted her portrayal of Maysilee to be accurate to Collins’ source material. One of the things she prioritized was keeping the character’s anger consistent throughout the film instead of having her arc of “learning to be kinder diminish her anger.

“While she finds happiness in being kind to the people around her and protecting them, she’s still a very angry character. And I think that it’s really important to show young women and anyone in general that the two can coexist,” she says. “You can be very angry at the world, or you can be very loud about the things that you believe in and in standing up for yourself and the people around you. But you can also be kind throughout that.

Grace also portrays Merilee Donner, Maysilee’s twin sister. Her previous credits include “I, Tonya,” “Regretting You,” “The Handmaid’s Tale” and “Ghostbusters: Afterlife.” She is next set to star in “Scooby Doo: Origins” as a young Daphne Blake.

Lenore Dove Baird is Haymitch’s girlfriend and a descendant of the Covey, which Lucy Gray Baird (Rachel Zelger) was a part of in “Songbirds and Snakes.” After her mother died during childbirth, Lenore Dove was raised by her uncles Tam Amber and Clerk Carmine Clade, whom audiences met in the previous prequel. Whitney Peak plays the character after starring in films like “Molly’s Game” and series such as “Chilling Adventures of Sabrina.”

Ralph Fiennes stars as Coriolanus Snow, the cruel and tyrannical president of Panem who previously mentored Lucy Gray Baird, a District 12 tribute who won the 10th Hunger Games. After Haymitch publicly defies the Capitol, Snow marks him as a target and stops at nothing to suppress his resistance. No stranger to playing the villain, Fiennes has also starred as the antagonist in “The Menu” and as Voldemort in the “Harry Potter” franchise. Snow was played by Donald Sutherland in the original films and by Tom Blyth in “The Hunger Games: The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes.”

Elle Fanning plays Effie Trinket, a Capitol stylist who steps in to help dress the District 12 tributes for their interviews. Effie ultimately becomes the district’s escort in the original films, where she was played by Elizabeth Banks. Earlier this year, Fanning received an Emmy nomination for “Margo’s Got Money Troubles” after previously starring in “A Complete Unknown,” “Maleficent” and “Super 8.”

Plutarch Heavensbee is a cameraman assigned to cover District 12 during the Hunger Games. Although he serves as a trusted advisor to President Snow, Plutarch is secretly involved in the growing rebellion against the Capitol and helps Haymitch execute his plan to break the arena. He’s played by Jesse Plemons, who also starred in “Bugonia,” “The Power of the Dog,” “Kinds of Kindness” and “Game Night.” Plutarch ultimately becomes a gamemaker for the third Quarter Quell in “Catching Fire,” where he is played by Philip Seymour Hoffman for the remainder of the original films.

As the host of the Hunger Games, Caesar Flickerman is responsible for keeping Capitol citizens entertained and invested throughout the annual event. Typically known for his bold outfits and quick-witted humor, the role was originated by Stanley Tucci and is now played by Kieran Culkin, whose previous credits include “Succession” and “A Real Pain.” Caesar’s grandfather Lucky (Jason Schwartzman) was the announcer in “Songbirds and Snakes.”

Drusilla Sickle is a Capitol chaperone for the District 12 tributes. Openly disdainful of the poor district, particularly Maysilee, Drusilla hopes to earn a promotion to a higher-ranking group of tributes and takes extreme measures to ensure her presentations are nothing short of perfection. Glenn Close stars as the vain escort after playing a similarly eccentric character in “101 Dalmatians.” She is also known for her roles in “Fatal Attraction” and “Dangerous Liaisons.”

Billy Porter plays Magno Stift, a reptile-loving Capitol stylist unwillingly assigned to the District 12 tributes. Magno puts little effort into dressing the tributes on the rare occasion he decides to show up. Porter is largely known for his stage career, having starred in Broadway shows including “Kinky Boots,” “Cabaret” and “Shuffle Along.”

After winning the 34th Hunger Games, Beetee secretly conspired against the Capitol, which President Snow eventually discovered. Now, Snow is punishing the District 3 victor by reaping his son, Ampert, for the second Quarter Quell. Kelvin Harrison Jr. plays the character after starring in films including “Mufasa: The Lion King” and “Elvis.” Jeffrey Wright originated the role in the previous films.

Also a fan of the franchise, Harrison Jr. tells Variety that, while he would refer to the books and Wright’s performance, he primarily focused on the world and character that the screenwriters of “Sunrise on the Reaping” created for that specific moment in time.

“What they [the screenwriters] gave me the real estate for was really fleshing out a sense of knowingness that there is no space for grief for him right now. He is in a position where there isn’t self-preservation,” Harrison Jr. says. “There is overcoming in order to have a larger cause to take care of a larger party, and what’s at stake is his own flesh and blood. And I wanted to figure out how to navigate the feeling of grief as something you can’t really control, but you’re trying to outrun it. But you really can’t.”

Harrison Jr. and Wright previously played father and son in 2018’s “Monster” and will do so again in “Samo Lives,” the upcoming Jean-Michel Basquiat biopic.

Wiress is the exceptionally intelligent victor of the 49th Hunger Games who, alongside Mags, mentors Haymitch, Maysilee, Wyatt and Lou Lou for the second Quarter Quell. She is played by Maya Hawke, who is known for her roles in “Stranger Things,” “Do Revenge” and “Inside Out 2,” and was originated by Amanda Plummer in “The Hunger Games: Catching Fire.”

Little is known about Wiress beyond the fact that her arena was filled with mirrors and that she won without killing a single tribute. Hawke tells Variety she “relished in” every piece of information she could find about the character, hoping to portray a version of Wiress who was “beautiful, curious, kind of shy and uniquely capable.”

“I imagine that she probably lost some allies and some friends in her games, and that emotionally attaching to these young people now would be a living nightmare,” Hawke says about Wiress’ relationships with the District 12 tributes. “It’s like I’m trying to see them as pigs, and could one of these pigs work for my protest? I’m trying not to see them as human, and what I tried to build in was that she slowly starts to see them as human, and that’s how she gets so messed up.”

Mags, the winner of the 11th Hunger Games from District 4, mentors the District 12 tributes for the 50th games. She is played by Lili Taylor, who also starred in “The Conjuring,” “Mystic Pizza” and “American Crime,” and was originated by Lynn Cohen in “The Hunger Games: Catching Fire.”

Taylor tells Variety she avoided relying too heavily on Cohen’s performance, instead focusing on who Mags was at this particular point in her life rather than the character’s eventual fate. Working with director Francis Lawrence, the two created a version of Mags that served as a motherly figure to her tributes.

“In some ways, Mags just really cares about those young mentees and will do whatever she can to help them, even under the circumstances,” she says. “It’s so strange when you really think about it deeply, but it’s sort of like they [the tributes] have a job to do, and I really like them, and I want them to do well.”

Jennifer Lawrence reprises her role as Katniss Everdeen, the protagonist of the original “Hunger Games” films. Reaped for the 74th games, the District 12 tribute became the face of the rebellion with her mockingjay pin, which was passed down from Maysilee Donner. Since joining the franchise in 2012, Lawrence has starred in films including “Silver Linings Playbook,” “Die My Love” and “No Hard Feelings.”

Peeta Mellark is Katniss’ fellow District 12 victor of the 74th Hunger Games and her love interest in the original films, making him a key role in the rebellion. Josh Hutcherson returns to the role 11 years after the release of “The Hunger Games: Mockingjay – Part 2″ and has since starred in the “Five Nights at Freddy’s” films and “I Love LA.”

The role of Haymitch Abernathy was originated by Woody Harrelson in the original films, where he served as the heavy-drinking, hard-shelled mentor to Katniss and Peeta. Secretly conspiring with Capitol insiders, Haymitch helped launch the start of the rebellion and oversaw the dethroning of President Snow. Harrelson’s additional credits include “Cheers,” “Brothers” and “Now You See Me.”

Proserpina Trinket is Effie’s younger sister and a Capitol prep team member assigned to Haymitch for the Quarter Quell. Played by Iris Apatow, the university student is constantly worrying her grade will be affected due to Magno’s failure to dress the District 12 tributes. Apatow’s previous credits include “Funny People,” “Knocked Up” and “This is 40.”

Working alongside Proserpina, Vitus is also a part of Haymitch’s Capitol prep team who is similarly apprehensive about how his grade will be affected. He is played by Edvin Ryding, who previously starred in “28 Years Later” and “Mother Goose.”

Ben Wang plays Wyatt Callow, the first male tribute from District 12 to be reaped. He and his family are widely known throughout the district as the “booker boys,” a name that reflects their illegal gambling business that takes bets on the annual Hunger Games. Wang plays the character after appearing in films like “The Long Walk,” “Karate Kid: Legends” and “Mean Girls.”

Molly McCann stars as Louella, Haymitch’s young neighbor in District 12 who is also selected to fight in the Quarter Quell. Often styled in two braids and having earned the nickname “sweetheart” by Haymitch, Louella offers many similarities to Katniss.

Iona Bell plays Lou Lou, a lookalike of Louella McCoy assigned by President Snow to serve as a District 12 tribute. Though her origins in the film are unknown, she was said to be from District 11 in Collins’ novel. This is Bell’s first major film role.

Ampert is a District 3 tribute who is also the son of former victor Beetee Latier. He created the Newcomers alliance that would outnumber the Careers and helped Haymitch with his plans inside the arena. Ampert is played by Percy Daggs IV, who has also acted in “Never Let Go” and “Paradise.”

Silka is one of the female Careers — a tribute who’s trained to compete in the games from a young age — from District 1. Played by Laura Marcus, Silka is set on targeting Maysilee from their early interactions during training. Marcus’ credits also include “Death by Lightning” and “The Jetty.”

Panache is one of two male tributes from District 1 and a member of the Careers who personally targeted Haymitch since their first interaction, physically attacking him during training and vowing to kill him in the arena. He’s played by Jhaleil Swaby, who also appeared in “Neagley” earlier this year.

Rada Rae plays Wellie, one of the two female tributes from District 6. Though her role in the film is relatively limited, Wellie forms a trusting friendship with Haymitch in the novel, with the protagonist vowing to protect her as much as he could in the arena. Rae previously starred in Netflix’s “Exterritorial.”

Ringina is a District 7 tribute who is a part of the Newcomers alliance. She is played by Tatyana Muzondo, who is making her film debut.

Salimou Thiam plays District 9 tribute Clayton, who joins the Newcomers alongside Haymitch and Ampert.

Smylie Bradwell stars as Haymitch’s younger brother, Sid, who is two years short of having his name entered in the annual reaping. Largely introduced to audiences via “Hamnet,” Bradwell has also starred in “Faith” and “The Crow Girl.”

Burdock is the future father of Katniss Everdeen and Haymitch’s best friend in District 12. Played by Scot Greenan, Burdock first began dating Asterid around the second Quarter Quell and is also Lenore Dove’s distant relative. Phillip Troy Linger played the character in the original films.

Asterid is the future mother of Katniss who first began dating Burdock during the events of the second Quarter Quell. She is also a close friend of the Donner twins, standing with them at the reaping and sharing an emotional goodbye after Maysilee’s name was called.
