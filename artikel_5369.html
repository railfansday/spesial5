Trump turns to blaming Biden after a nationwide ground stop snarled air travel

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The president urged Congress to fund air traffic control upgrades, while the FAA traced the ground stop to a data flood on its network

Chen Mengtong/China News Service/VCG via Getty Images

President Donald Trump on Sunday blamed the Biden administration for worsening an "old and broken" Federal Aviation Administration system and urged Congress to provide money "FAST" after Friday's nationwide ground stop.

Trump wrote on Truth Social that "big money" was "spent unwisely" under the previous administration. He said his administration has "the knowledge and plan for the Flight Control Systems" and that the FAA "is doing a great job, and is ready to go." The post did not mention the ground stop by name. The comments were Trump's first on the ground stop, according to CNBC.

The FAA said a technical problem at about 3:30 p.m. ET on Nov. 13 led it to issue the ground stop, and air traffic resumed at 8:15 p.m. that day. Operators canceled about 12,462 flights, including 9,082 by major airlines, though not all cancellations were directly caused by the ground stop, the agency said. About 3,500 aircraft were in the air and all landed.

The FAA said its legacy equipment destabilized once its network was swamped with traffic, at a rate of 1,000 connection attempts every 4 to 5 seconds. The initial investigation points to a radar site in Amarillo, Texas, and a radio communications site in El Dorado, Arkansas. L3Harris, the FAA's network service provider, had been working at the El Dorado site, including deploying a new router. The FAA said it has no indication of a cyberattack, and it and L3Harris are still investigating the root cause.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy and FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford provided an update on the restoration of airspace. Duffy said officials have not determined whether contractor error or a faulty router caused the flood.

Duffy said Congress gave the department a partial payment of $12.5 billion for air traffic control modernization. He said he hopes lawmakers "invest in what will be the state-of-the-art" system. The FAA said none of the upgraded towers that received modifications funded by that payment went down.

Bedford said the router replacement was routine maintenance of the existing telecommunications network and not part of the $12.5 billion modernization effort. He said the FAA spends $1 billion a year maintaining that network.

Nationwide ground stops rarely happen. The previous two came after the Sept. 11 attacks and during a brief FAA computer failure in January 2023.
