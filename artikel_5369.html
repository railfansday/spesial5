Brazil’s Largest Crypto Exchange Hit by Cyberattack as Losses Top $45M

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Brazil’s largest digital asset exchange, Mercado, suffered a cyberattack that drained more than $45 million, adding to a string of crypto attacks in 30 days. Blockchain reporters have warned that repeated hacks hurt sentiment, regardless of which platforms cover the losses.

Blockchain security firms confirmed the attack, sparking fresh debates on the state of digital assets amid advanced bad actors’ tools. Initial research placed losses at $40 million, before Arkham Intelligence analyst Emmett Gallic reported an additional $5 million.

According to him, losses are growing, and bad actors are targeting old deposit addresses to sell off memecoins and altcoins. This adds to the growing list of digital asset exchanges hacked within a short period.

The exchange confirmed the incident to quell fears, adding that the attack method remains unknown. In a statement released on X, they stated that no harm was done to customers and there was no data breach.

However, they temporarily suspended deposits and withdrawals while the investigation and tracking continued. They later released a list of addresses where funds were stolen to widen community efforts in tracking.

“We continue working to safely restore operations and will keep our customers informed through our official channels. To contribute to the monitoring and tracking of assets on blockchain networks, we are sharing the destination addresses identified in the transactions related to the incident.”

The platform placed the value of assets involved at $38 million, and $11 million was blocked with the help of crypto firms including Circle, Tether, Paxos, Chainalysis, BRL1, etc.

“Update on the incident: Mercado Bitcoin assures you that you will not suffer any loss of assets due to the security incident we identified today on our platform.”

Last week, Binance faced the largest crypto breach, involving $6 billion in assets, although a large chunk was traced. This trumps Bybit’s infamous saga where bad actors drained nearly $1.5 billion.

The FBI and other intelligence agencies flagged North Korean hackers for the incident, calling for wider cooperation to tackle the recurring attacks.

Crypto policy watchers have warned against the hacks, especially on top platforms, as they crash broader market confidence. Users also pointed to growing AI tools and reiterated fears of Q-Day.
