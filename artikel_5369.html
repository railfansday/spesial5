Amazon is warning NYC customers a delivery bill could add up to $5 per order

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Amazon said Intro 518 could force it to end work with small delivery firms and consider moving operations out of the city

Amazon $AMZN warned New York City customers on Thursday that a City Council bill might tack as much as $5 onto every order.

The company said the legislation, Intro 518, might push up prices and alter the way Amazon and other businesses get packages and groceries to customers. Amazon said it relies on a group of small delivery companies and would have to stop partnering with them if the bill passes. It said it would have to weigh relocating its operations beyond city limits, which could slow deliveries, raise their cost for millions of customers, and endanger the small businesses and their employees.

Amazon said it is cooperating with the council and has extended an invitation to all members to tour its delivery stations and meet the small business owners and their workers ahead of a vote. It is also urging New York City customers to reach out to their representative on the council.

The bill is sponsored by Councilmember Tiffany Cabán and would mandate that city licenses be held by those running last-mile delivery facilities, and that delivery workers be hired as employees instead of being contracted out, according to Reuters.

The measure, known as the Delivery Protection Act, has 35 co-sponsors, which is more than a majority of the council, according to Gothamist. Mayor Zohran Mamdani has pledged to sign the measure should it pass.

Amazon spokesperson Kelly Nantel said the bill would endanger upward of 5,000 jobs held by workers at small delivery firms and force Amazon to look at moving its delivery operations beyond the city limits, according to Gothamist. Amazon depends on over 40 outside firms in the city that handle deliveries on its behalf.

Backers of the measure, among them the International Brotherhood of Teamsters, contend that relying on contractors lets Amazon avoid responsibility when things go wrong and results in weak safety practices, according to Gothamist.

Council Speaker Julie Menin said her office is working to get the bill passed and that her staff is drafting amendments meant to answer legal objections, according to Gothamist. "We will be the first city in the country to regulate Amazon in a very significant way," Menin said, according to Gothamist.
