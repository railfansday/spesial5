How arteries keep their shape in the face of overgrowth-inducing mutations

Holger Gerhardt is in the Integrative Vascular Biology Laboratory, Max Delbrück Center for Molecular Medicine in the Helmholtz Association, 13125 Berlin, Germany, and in the German Center for Cardiovascular Research, Berlin, Germany.

Mutations in the cancer-associated gene PIK3CA can cause vascular malformations1. These abnormalities can affect people from birth or can be acquired over a person’s lifetime, and they involve veins, capillaries and lymphatic vessels — but rarely arteries2,3. Why are arteries spared? This mystery is investigated by Sabata et al.4 in their paper in Nature.
