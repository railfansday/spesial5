7 Near-Perfect Showtime Series With 10 Episodes Or Fewer

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Since the age of streaming, Showtime has found itself outpaced by HBO, and its streaming service efforts were short-lived and have, at this point, been completely subsumed by its parent Paramount+. There are still original shows under the Showtime banner, however, and these seven are worth subscribing for.

The Showtime miniseries Picture Windows premiered in 1995 and ran for six episodes. An anthology, each episode of Picture Windows is a short film based on a different iconic painting. There's one based on Primavera by Sandro Botticelli, another based on Ballet Rehearsal by Edgar Degas, and so on.

Each episode also features a notable person behind the camera. Peter Bogdanovich, Joe Dante, and John Boorman are credited in the series. It's a beautiful, thoughtful miniseries, and though 30 minutes may sound like not enough time these days, it allows for sharp, quick-moving stories.

It's a series filled with some impressive stars as well, with Alan Arkin, Sally Kirkland, Robert Loggia, Steve Zahn, and John Hurt all making appearances. It only takes about three hours to watch, but it's also the kind of show that makes you pause after each episode and consider everything you've seen.

Out of Order is a six-episode miniseries from Donna and Wayne Powers about a man, Mark Colm (Eric Stoltz), who is in an unsatisfying marriage with Lorna (Felicity Huffman). Kim Dickens, William H. Macy, and Justine Bateman also star. The series was edited into a feature film for the DVD.

Mark decides that his midlife crisis will involve a woman named Danni (Dickens) and imagining his life as a sort of reality show. Lorna ends up with Steven (Macy) and begins drinking a lot. There's more to the series than that, though, and it ends up being a moving story of love lost and gained.

There's a meta quality to the series that may divide viewers, but the story is told with such brutal honesty that even if Out of Order's quirks are not your cup of tea, you'll be swept up in the tale. It takes a nuanced approach to relationships and has empathy for everyone involved.

Escape at Dannemora is a seven-episode miniseries based on the real-life 2015 Clinton Correctional Facility escape. Patricia Arquette stars as Joyce "Tilly" Mitchell, a prison worker at Clinton who is in a loveless marriage and is having an affair with David Sweat (Paul Dano), a convicted murderer.

Her relationship with Sweat eventually results in her willingness to help Sweat and another convicted murderer, Richard Matt (Benicio del Toro), escape Clinton, with disastrous consequences for all involved. The series begins with Tilly's interrogation after the manhunt is over, and backtracks.

The series takes its time to set up all the pieces that lead to the main event, and for a show with a promised prison break, there is a lot more character work than you may initially expect. It's all critical, though, and when the escape does happen, the groundwork that goes into the beginning of the show pays off.

Patrick Melrose is a five-episode series starring Benedict Cumberbatch as the wealthy, titular Englishman, Patrick Melrose. The series tracks five decades of Patrick's life, starting in the 1960s and ending in the 2000s. Over this time, Patrick attempts to overcome the demons imparted to him by a childhood of parental abuse.

The series uses flashbacks to help us understand Patrick's difficult upbringing and also tear down the tapestry of upper-class British society. Based on Edward St Aubyn's semi-autobiographical novels, Patrick Melrose is unflinching in its look at childhood trauma and its reverberations felt decades later.

It's a powerful series, with a moving ending that may not exactly satisfy, but is the right choice for such a hard show. It's a scathing look at aristocracy, and is unafraid to point the finger at societal problems as much as it is at personal problems. Cumberbatch is doing some of his best work as the complicated, haunted Melrose.

The Good Lord Bird is a seven-episode Western series based on the 2013 novel of the same name by James McBride. The series is based on the real-life exploits of John Brown, an abolitionist during Bleeding Kansas, whose anti-slavery efforts eventually resulted in the famous raid on Harper's Ferry.

Joshua Caleb Johnson stars as Henry "Onion" Shackleford, a fictional enslaved boy who escapes his captors and joins Brown's (Ethan Hawke) retinue. Onion comes to trust and believe in the eccentric John Brown, even as he appears to become more erratic over the years, and his fight against slavery more desperate.

It's a masterclass performance from Hawke, who imbues Brown with a supernatural fury and religious fervor that might make him seem crazy to a modern audience, but is a symbol of his disgust and revulsion at the practice of slavery. It's a thrilling, even fun series, with sobering moments that hit you hard in the chest.

Fellow Travelers is a seven-episode series based on Thomas Mallon's 2007 novel of the same name. Matt Bomer and Jonathan Bailey star as Hawk Fuller and Tim Laughlin, respectively, two men who begin a romance at the height of McCarthyism in America, tracing their relationship over the decades.

From Adolescence to Mare of Easttown to the classic Roots, these incredible TV miniseries didn't need a lot of episodes to make their point.

Hawk is a World War II veteran and State Department official who is terrified of being outed, while Tim is a devout Catholic and hard-line supporter of McCarthy whose worldview is suddenly flipped upside down after his encounter with Hawk. Their relationship winds through the tumultuous '60s and beyond.

It's an epic and full depiction of gay romance, including everything from Fire Island to the AIDs epidemic, and each moment in history is brought to life, often sadly, with the incredible performances of Bomer and Bailey, who share a moving chemistry. It's an expansive series that uses Hawk and Tim's beautiful relationship to personalize a story we don't see often enough.

A Gentleman in Moscow is a Paramount+ co-production that runs eight episodes and is based on Amor Towles' 2016 novel. Ewan McGregor stars as Count Alexander Ilyich Rostov, an aristocrat who returns to Russia from Paris after being exiled following the events of the October Revolution.

As an aristocrat, Alexander is under constant surveillance from the Russian state, but over the decades, he manages to make a life for himself despite the rules and restrictions placed on him. He meets and falls in love with actress Anna Urbanova (Mary Elizabeth Winstead), and the two attempt to survive the upheaval in the USSR.

McGregor and Winstead provide a winning combo, one that we saw once before in Fargo, but is much sweeter this time around. It's a beautiful show, where the history is secondary to the relationship between the pair, and it makes for one of Showtime's most moving, short shows.
