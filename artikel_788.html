How landmark Everton compensation prevented Burnley record loss

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Burnley were bought by ALK Capital in December 2020 using loans secured against the club's assets and other external debt

Six years ago, Burnley were considered one of the best-run clubs in the country.

The Clarets were debt free, with Â£41.65m cash in the bank, and enjoying their fifth successive season of Premier League football.

Now they sit in the Championship relegation zone, with concerns over debt and the way the Clarets were bought by ALK Capital using loans and the club's own money.

Fans have been very concerned about what the future holds - although on Wednesday there was some rare positive news in the club's accounts.

BBC Sport takes a closer look at what is going on at Turf Moor.

You may be familiar with the term "leverage buyout" after the Glazer family's purchase of Manchester United in 2005.

It effectively means that a purchase is carried out using debt secured on the club's assets, its own bank balance and other loans, rather than the equity of the buyer.

On top of that, Velocity Capital, the holding company, owed Burnley Â£80.1m - money which was also used to buy the club.

The original Â£65m loan was partially paid off, but then refinanced and extended to reach Â£105m in the 2024-25 accounts. Interest rates were as high as 12%.

On top of that were factored debts, when a club mortgages off future transfer instalments for a one-off payment from the bank. Burnley owed Â£37m at an interest rate of 12.75%.

In total, the football club's borrowings stood at Â£142.3m, the 10th-highest external debt in English football.

Then came the 2025-26 accounts, published today, which painted a different picture.

Now, back to that leveraged buyout which has been attracting so much criticism from fans.

Last season's accounts contained a big surprise.

It cleared a large chunk of the debt used to buy the football club and helped to make the latest accounts look healthier.

This was needed because, over the past six years, debts related to the leveraged buyout and associated interest payments had increased.

Removing the Everton payment might present a more realistic picture of their finances.

Kieran Maguire, professor of football finance at the University of Liverpool, told BBC Sport that the timing of the accounts is noteworthy, too, coming six months earlier than expected.

"It was a surprise that Burnley filed their accounts now, but it makes sense considering the Everton payment and pending appeal," Maguire said.

On the field, Burnley need a revival - and fast.

It is still early in the Championship season - 14 games have been played - but Burnley sit in the relegation zone.

Over the past six years, they have been a yo-yo club between the Premier League and the Championship, trading the riches of the top flight with strong revenues from a promotion campaign each season.

A big turnaround is needed for that to still be the case under the new management of Swiss Fabio Celestini, who is yet to win in his two games in charge.

Burnley have recorded just one victory in the opening 14 Championship games, leaving them four points adrift in the bottom three.

Recently they set a new English league record for the longest run without a home win, which will reach 400 days when arch-rivals Blackburn visit Turf Moor on 22 November.

Relegation looks more likely than promotion at the moment, and Burnley cannot afford to become the third team in successive seasons to do the double drop from the Premier League to League One.

But time will tell whether the improved results in the accounts start to be replicated on the pitch.
