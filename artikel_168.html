Pontins owner fined £1m over death of woman at Somerset holiday park

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Wendy Jones, 68, was fatally injured when part of a ceiling collapsed during a bingo game at Pontins Brean Sands

The owner of Pontins holiday parks has been fined £1m over the death of a woman who was fatally injured when part of a ceiling collapsed during a bingo game at a fun park in Somerset.

Eighteen people were injured at the Brean Sands site, near Weston-super-Mare, in February 2019 after ventilation ducting and a 40-metre section of ceiling fell inside an entertainment area known as the Fun Factory.

Wendy Jones, 68, a grandmother from Hereford, was sitting in her mobility scooter for bingo with about 100 others when the ceiling collapsed.

She was among six guests to be treated in hospital after the incident and died six months later. A postmortem examination revealed a clear link between the injuries she suffered and her subsequent death.

Britannia Jinky Jersey, the parent company of Pontins, pleaded guilty at Bristol crown court to failing to discharge its duty to keep the public safe under the Health and Safety at Work Act 1974.

On Friday the company was fined £1m for the offence. Stephen Bennison, 60, an unqualified contractor who had been employed by Britannia Jinky Jersey to install soundproofing in the ceiling, was sentenced to 10 months’ imprisonment, suspended for 18 months.

An investigation by Avon and Somerset police, Somerset council and the Health and Safety Executive found that the ventilation ducting had originally been poorly installed decades earlier, before the site became a Pontins holiday park. But the immediate cause of the collapse was linked to soundproofing work carried out in 2015 when it was under Pontins’ control.

During that work, Bennison stacked heavy sound-insulation slabs on top of the ventilation ducting, the investigation found. It concluded that the insulation in the ceiling appeared to have been leftover material which could not be returned to the supplier – and stacking it on the ducting served no practical purpose.

The court heard that neither Britannia Jinky Jersey Ltd nor Bennison properly assessed whether the ducting could safely bear the significant additional weight.

Rachael Taylor, a specialist prosecutor with the Crown Prosecution Service, said: “A family holiday ended in tragedy because basic safety responsibilities were not met.

“Wendy Jones had every right to expect she was entering a safe environment. Instead, failures by Britannia Jinky Jersey Limited and Stephen Bennison resulted in the collapse of ventilation ducting and the death of a grandmother who was there to play bingo.”

Taylor said Jones’s death was avoidable. She added: “Those responsible for the safety of the public must ensure work is carried out competently and safely. Our thoughts remain with the family and friends of Mrs Jones, and anyone else affected by this tragic incident.”
