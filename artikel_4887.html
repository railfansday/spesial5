The Lame Duck: A Leader's Exit Can Be Bad or Good

It happens all the time. A leader’s time in the role is coming to an end. A leader who is completing the end of tenure in a top-level leadership position can either do an organization great harm or much good. What happens when a leader is a lame duck, with a known date for terminating their tenure? It all depends on the leader’s intent, their motivation, and how the leader’s team deals with it.

Neglect. What happens when a leader mentally disengages from their position and takes a “who cares” attitude? In one instance, a department director who was finishing his last few months simply stopped leading—communicating much less, canceling meetings that focused on moving the company forward, and not providing much information or feedback to direct reports. The organization came to a virtual standstill that lasted for a while, and it took new, energetic leadership to recover.

Trashing the Place. Even worse, a leader can decide to cause real damage. This can occur when the leader has been fired or feels unfairly treated. The leader might end promising and/or profitable projects. Resources might be rerouted to the leader’s benefit. More subtly, the leader might pit team members against one another and create a toxic work culture.

Motivational Malaise. A leader’s lack of interest and activity as they come to the end of their tenure can be contagious, affecting the motivation of other employees (“If the leader doesn’t seem to care, why should I?” “The leader is taking a lot of personal time off, so I guess it’s OK.”). The leader’s negativity or simple disinterest can be infectious and affect the motivation of others.

Allow Others to Step Up to Leadership. A good leader develops leadership capacity in others. When a leader is exiting, it is important to provide opportunities for direct reports to take on leadership roles and activities. A much-celebrated CEO who was retiring told me that he knew that the organization would be in good hands after his departure because he had worked to develop his successors’ leadership. Delegating responsibility and sharing the leadership is the key to developing followers into leaders.

Strengthen Existing Processes. When a leader’s departure is inevitable, it is a good time for the leader to do a comprehensive evaluation of the organization’s operations. Rather than winding things down, it’s a good time to focus on improvement. What could be working better? What new initiatives should be fast-tracked? Rather than taking it easy, the departing leader should ramp things up.

Leave a Legacy. An effective leader who is in the waning stages of tenure needs to realize that it is important to continue efforts to improve the organization and its operations. This is the legacy that a good leader leaves behind. The departure should be positive, formative, and free from regrets.
