Rejected for 10 Years, Then She Made History

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Peggy Whitson, a biochemist, applied for 10 years to become an astronaut. Every time she applied, she was turned down. Who applies for the same job for a decade, I thought? Dr. Whitson made it very clear to me that she would become an astronaut. She realized that there was a divide between what the selection committee was looking for and what she was showing them.

Talent is important, as is grit, resilience and a strong work ethic. That’s a baseline, not a differentiator. The difference between those who succeed despite the setbacks, and sustain that success, might just be how they approach challenges.Whitson focused her energy and talents on closing that delta to prove to the astronaut selection committee that she was worthy of their confidence and would become a great astronaut.

When faced with a challenge, high-performers focus on what they can control. Some circumstances are simply beyond their influence. No one can control the weather or the construction noise outside a building where they’re taking an important exam. What distinguishes high-performers is their ability to double down on the variables within their control while managing their response to those beyond it. How you react to what is beyond your control might separate those who can from those who won’t.

High-performers use setbacks as data points. Each one teaches them something new. They review it with painstaking detail. NBA player Zaza Pachulia played in 1,098 basketball games over his 15-year NBA career. After each game, he told me, he would review the video of the game, not to see what he got right, but rather, what he could improve upon (watch my conversation with Zaza Pachulia). High-performers like Pachulia review the feedback and evidence and look to see how they can improve even a fraction of a percent. Compounded they know that it will have an outsized difference between their output and everyone else’s. Then they take action. In their mind, it’s not a question if they will succeed, rather they focus on how. That’s how high-performers focus on controlling what they can control.

It’s a good thing Whitson didn’t quit on her quest to become an astronaut. Eventually, she was accepted and became the first female commander of the International Space Station (ISS), a role she’s held twice. For years she held the record for spending more days in space than any American astronaut of any gender, and eventually, she was promoted to NASA’s Chief Astronaut.

The next time you are faced with a challenge, examine what needs to happen for you to meet your goal, then look at where you are now. Then figure out how to close that gap. A setback is only a failure if you let it simmer. Take control and plan your next steps.
