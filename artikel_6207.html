New Hyatt Credit Card Offer: Earn 4 Free Nights—Why I Spend On The Card Towards Elite Status

The World Of Hyatt Credit Card (See rates and fees) has a new initial bonus: earn 4 free nights at any Category 1-4 Hyatt hotel or resort after spending $5,000 on purchases in the first 3 months from account opening. That’s an improved offer from a card whose bonuses have usually been more modest than competing travel cards.

I have this card, and I put actual spending on it. Hyatt points are still valuable, but what gets me to use the card is the credit toward elite status and the rewards earned along the way. Hyatt status does more to improve my hotel stays than status with other major chains.

These are four separate certificates, so you can use them for one trip or spread them across different stays. You’ll need standard room award availability, and the certificates expire 12 months after they’re issued. The terms require checkout before the expiration date. Under Hyatt’s current award chart, a Category 4 standard room can cost as much as 25,000 points per night. Four certificates could therefore cover nights that would otherwise require 100,000 points. Their category restrictions and expiration date make them less flexible than having those points in your account. The card has a $95 annual fee. Chase excludes current holders of this card and previous cardmembers who received its initial bonus within the past 24 months. The usual Chase restriction on applicants who’ve opened five or more personal cards in the past 24 months also generally applies. Why I Keep The Card And Spend On It The annual Category 1-4 free night after each cardmember anniversary makes the $95 fee easy to justify. There’s also a second Category 1-4 night after $15,000 in purchases during a calendar year which makes that spending more rewarding. And these nights are transferable to another World of Hyatt member. If I won’t use one, I can give it to someone who will. For spending, the card earns 4 Hyatt points per dollar on qualifying Hyatt purchases; 2 on restaurants, airline tickets bought directly from airlines, local transit and commuting, and fitness club and gym memberships; and 1 on other purchases. More significantly, it comes with 5 qualifying nights each year, plus 2 more qualifying nights for every $5,000 spent. Those nights count toward both status and Milestone Rewards. Put $15,000 of otherwise unbonused spending on the card during a calendar year and you earn: 15,000 Hyatt points. An additional Category 1-4 free night certificate. 6 qualifying nights from spending, giving you 11 including the card’s annual 5. That means 49 qualifying nights actually spent in hotels, plus the card’s 5 annual nights and 6 from spending, gets you to the 60 needed for Globalist. And the $5,000 spent earning the initial bonus counts toward the $15,000 free-night threshold in the calendar year when you spend it. Milestone Rewards start at 20 qualifying nights and continue every 10 nights through 150. Some of the rewards on the way to Globalist are: Qualifying Nights Notable Rewards 30 A Category 1-4 free night, plus a choice of additional reward. 40 A Guest of Honor Award, plus a choice that includes a Suite Upgrade Award. 50 A choice that includes 2 Suite Upgrade Awards. 60 Globalist status, a Category 1-7 free night, 2 Suite Upgrade Awards, 2 Guest of Honor Awards, and My Hyatt Concierge. The value of another $5,000 in spending depends on where those two nights get me. Crossing a threshold for suite upgrades or Globalist can be worth giving up a higher earning rate on another card. Hyatt Status Is Stronger Than At Other Major Chains The card itself comes with Discoverist status. That’s better than a hole in the head as my grandfather used to say. But reaching Globalist has real value – as well as the milestone choices along the way. The biggest benefit is confirming a suite at the time of booking with a Suite Upgrade Award for up to seven nights when an eligible standard suite is available for the stay. That’s a huge value on family trips. I also value having a My Hyatt Concierge I can email requests to, actual delivery of 4 p.m. late checkout at eligible properties, and a more generous breakfast benefit than I get from other chains. At participating hotels without an available club lounge, Globalist breakfast generally covers a full breakfast for up to two adults and two children registered in the room, including tax and gratuity. Late checkout has exceptions, including availability limits at resorts, but I’m usually proactively offered late checkout everywhere else (and personally find it honored more often than not at resorts). The Free Night Certificates Should Be More Flexible The current version of this card launched in 2018. Its annual certificate is still capped at Category 4, while category creep has taken away many of the hotels where I’d most want to use it. Just this year, 14 hotels moved from Category 4 to Category 5, including Hyatt Regency Seattle, Hyatt Regency Grand Cypress and Hyatt Regency Jersey City. Five moved the other way, bringing options like Hyatt Centric Congress Avenue Austin and Dream Nashville within reach, but the certificate lost more hotels than it gained. I’d like Hyatt to at least let members add points to a certificate to book a higher category. The certificate works at a Category 4 hotel charging 25,000 points, but won’t work at a Category 5 hotel when it’s charging 15,000 points. And I wish Hyatt would let us combine these free nights with Suite Upgrade Awards or Guest of Honor Awards. I value them most traveling with my family when I’m paying for rooms out of pocket. That’s always when I want to use my suite upgrades! I don’t need a suite on a business stay (paid by someone else, when it’s just me) or to use the free night award.
