Delta CEO Ed Bastian Slams ‘Short-Sighted’ Heathrow Shake-Up

Virgin Atlantic's anger over Heathrow's terminal shake-up now has heavyweight backing from Atlanta. With Ed Bastian joining the fight, the airport faces mounting pressure to rethink its plans.

Delta Air Lines CEO Ed Bastian has sharply criticized plans to separate the carrier from its joint venture partner Virgin Atlantic at London Heathrow Airport. In a LinkedIn post, Bastian described the proposals as "short-sighted, deeply disappointing and bad for airline consumers."

The intervention marks a major escalation in the dispute over Heathrow's future terminal arrangements. It follows a series of strongly worded statements from Virgin Atlantic executives and the airline's founder, Sir Richard Branson.

Under the proposals, Delta would relocate from Terminal 3 to Terminal 4 in 2028. The move would end its arrangement alongside Virgin Atlantic, which is set to remain at Terminal 3.

"For more than a decade, our airlines have served millions of customers through our co-located operation in Terminal 3, offering travelers convenient connections, premium amenities and access to an expanded global network," Bastian wrote.

Delta CEO Ed Bastian has publicly attacked Heathrow's plan to move Delta from Terminal 3 to Terminal 4 in 2028, calling it "short-sighted, deeply disappointing and bad for airline consumers" in a LinkedIn post and saying Delta will "pursue all available remedies to correct this decision." The move, part of Heathrow's once-in-a-decade Terminal Occupancy Review, would end Delta's co-located operation with joint venture partner Virgin Atlantic, which stays in Terminal 3. Four other carriers — Finnair, Japan Airlines, Latam, and China Southern — would also switch terminals, while British Airways keeps its existing arrangements at Terminal 5. Heathrow says the changes free up capacity, reduce congestion, and improve resilience, and airport insiders cited by Sky News said airlines representing around 81% of Heathrow passengers backed the final proposal — a figure weighted by British Airways, which carries roughly half of Heathrow's traffic. Virgin Atlantic CEO Corneel Koster told The Sunday Times minimum connecting times between the two airlines could rise from 60 to 90 minutes, and said there is still time to reverse the decision before 2028. North America is Heathrow's largest market after the EU, with 1.86 million passengers in September 2026, or 25.9% of traffic.
