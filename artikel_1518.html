Liverpool track Swedish left-back with £38m price tag

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Liverpool transfer news: Liverpool are tracking Borussia Dortmund left-back Daniel Svensson, according to fussball-news.de, which names them and Arsenal as the most interested clubs.

Manchester United, Leeds, Milan, Juventus, Inter and Real Madrid are also watching the 24-year-old. Liverpool and Milan are reportedly preparing concrete steps for the winter window, although the report attributes no bid to either.

Dortmund will not make it easy. They want to keep a player contracted until June 2029 and plan to raise his salary, while Svensson feels settled and has no wish for a rushed move. A sale would cost at least €45m (about £38m), and Premier League clubs are ready to test Dortmund with a €30m (roughly £25m) offer, yet FotMob values him at just €23m.

The numbers explain the interest. Svensson has started all 10 Bundesliga games this season. His expected assists total of 1.13 comes without a single assist, and he has made 22 tackles, 14 interceptions and 45 recoveries while passing at 86.1%. He can play in midfield.

The flaws are clear. He has won only eight of 29 aerial duels and landed six of 32 crosses. He also plays as a wing-back in front of a back three, so Andoni Iraola’s back four would ask him to defend more of the flank alone.

Iraola’s 4-2-3-1 sends full-backs forward, and Milos Kerkez, bought for a reported £40m last year, has no goals or assists in the Premier League this season. Kostas Tsimikas, 30, is the backup on a contract that expires next year.

Not in January, and not at €45m. A €30m offer would fail, and £38m for a player FotMob values at €23m is poor business. Summer 2027 is the realistic window, once Tsimikas’s deal has run down.

The bigger problem sits on the other flank. Ronald Araujo, a centre-back by trade, is filling in at right-back while injuries have hit that position all season, so Iraola needs a natural right-back more urgently than a second left-back. Kerkez is 22 and signed until 2030, so Svensson would challenge him rather than replace him, which is healthy given Kerkez’s lack of end product.

Kerkez arrived with two Premier League seasons behind him, while Svensson would have to adapt to a new league and a new system. Liverpool should buy a right-back first and hold a ceiling of around £35m for Svensson, because above that they would repeat the Kerkez gamble with less evidence.
