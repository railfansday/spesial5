Bitdefender Antivirus Plus Review: Undercutting the Competition

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Antivirus programs are a dime a dozen these days, and while the vast array of security threats that assail modern devices can at times feel so overwhelming that protection seems impossible, a good antivirus solution remains essential.

Unfortunately, purchasing an antivirus can prove rather confusing. Companies offer a huge range of bundles with long lists of features, many of which are obscured in vague language. If you dig deep enough into a company’s website, however, you’ll find that most still offer the option to purchase the antivirus with few add-ons at a lower price. And if that’s what you want, Bitdefender’s Antivirus Plus is a good option.

Buying Bitdefender Antivirus Plus on its own is arguably a bit confusing, as the company strongly pushes shoppers toward more expensive and all-inclusive Bitdefender bundles, which include other features like a password manager, dark web monitoring, and lots more. In total, there are four different bundles to choose from: Total Security, Premium Security, Ultimate Security, and Ultimate Security Plus Extended, ranging from $60 up to $140 for the first year. There are also Family versions of these plans, which are more expensive but add protection for up to 25 devices and parental controls. All these bundles include the same antivirus protection, but if you search for Antivirus Plus on its own, though, you will find it. And it’s arguably worth seeking out, as Bitdefender's entry-level options are priced most aggressively.

Bitdefender Antivirus Plus currently offers an introductory price of $25 for the first year (and $50 after) for a single device or $30 for the first year ($70 after) for three devices. This is quite a bit less than Bitdefender Total Security, which is $60 for the first year (and $110 after) and only offers two benefits in exchange: a password manager and protection for up to five devices instead of just one. The lower tiers tend to be better deals, especially if you're after something specific.

That’s because Bitdefender’s pricing is most competitive at its lowest tiers, which may by itself be enough reason for many to choose it. Rivals like Norton Antivirus Standard, McAfee Essential, Avira Internet Security, and Malwarebytes Standard typically ask more for a similar level of service.

There is some variation here; Norton throws in a password manager at a lower price point, for instance, and some competitors have superior VPN service included (Bitdefender Antivirus Plus limits you to 200 MB of VPN data per day, which isn’t much). Still, Bitdefender Antivirus Plus is among the more affordable options, particularly if you’re more interested in the antivirus than the add-ons.

Savvy users might balk at paying at all, as many free antivirus options exist. Bitdefender itself offers a free antivirus download for both Windows and Android (but not macOS, iOS, or iPadOS). This is a fair choice, though it often means you’ll have to tolerate advertisements urging you to sign up.

Buying, downloading, and installing Bitdefender Antivirus Plus is a smooth experience. The payment portal is straightforward. The installer is really a downloader, so it’s a small file; the full download required by the installer is roughly 800 megabytes. Once the installation is finished, Antivirus Plus automatically runs an initial virus check. This took less than five minutes on a Microsoft Surface Laptop 7 running Windows 11.

Once installed, the Bitdefender Antivirus Plus interface provides a default dashboard that includes several common actions, including a quick scan, total system scan, and vulnerability scan (the latter looks for problems like a weak account password or an overly permissive network configuration). You’ll also find an option to turn on Bitdefender VPN, which is installed alongside the antivirus—though, as mentioned, you’re limited to 200 MB of data per day unless you upgrade to Bitdefender Premium VPN.

These features are largely standard for an antivirus program, though one extra feature requires more explanation: Safepay. This creates a sandboxed browsing environment meant to thwart threats like keyloggers and man-in-the-middle attacks. To be honest, I’m doubtful of the feature’s utility. I don’t believe these are the most common avenues cybercriminals use to obtain payment information; they’re far more likely to buy huge repositories of payment data stolen from compromised companies. It’s also rather clunky to launch Safepay for an online purchase, so I don’t think most people will want to use it.

I spent most of my time using Bitdefender Antivirus Plus on Windows, which I think is the best version of the software. The Android and iOS versions look largely similar to it, though of course compressed for a mobile device. However, macOS is the odd one out with an interface that looks different from its peers and, to my eyes, seems about five years out of date. It’s not terrible and has similar functionality, but I find this a bit odd. It seems like the macOS software is missing a software refresh that other platforms received.

A nice software interface is a perk but, at its core, antivirus software is supposed to stop viruses and other malware. So, how does Bitdefender Antivirus Plus rank?

Bitdefender scored well in AV-Test’s latest report, where it detected 100 percent of the tested attacks and received an AV-Test Top Product certification. It also received high marks from AV-Comparatives, which found it detected 99.5 percent of threats, enough to earn it the top three-star score. Bitdefender’s consumer products weren’t ranked in the latest SE Labs consumer antivirus report, but the small-business product detected 99 percent of threats and received an AAA ranking in the April 2026 report.

The performance hit from Bitdefender Antivirus Plus wasn’t noticeable in typical use of either my Windows 11 laptop or my Apple MacBook Air M4. Bitdefender services consumed about 700 MB of memory in total on both platforms. That’s not a lot, and it's in line with what I often see from antivirus programs, though it’s also not trivial. I can imagine this could at times result in a performance hit on a device with 8 GB of RAM. My devices have 32 GB and 24 GB, respectively, however, so I didn’t encounter any issues.

Bitdefender Antivirus Plus checks off most boxes for an antivirus program. It’s affordable, easy to use, earns high marks from malware testing labs, and avoids significant performance issues when installed. But I do want to return to a problem I’ve mentioned before: the VPN.

Antivirus Plus advertises an included VPN. This is true, but it’s actually the free VPN service that anyone can obtain. The service has a strict 200-MB (per day, per device) data cap, which isn’t going to be enough if you want to use it as your main VPN. To be fair, Bitdefender does detail this limitation on its website—but it does seem like an added feature when, in reality, it’s available for free whether you buy Antivirus Plus or not.

That could steer you away from Bitdefender if you want an antivirus and VPN. Competitors like Norton and McAfee bundle an unlimited VPN service into lower tiers of service, which, though more expensive than Bitdefender Antivirus Plus, are less expensive than Bitdefender Antivirus Plus and Bitdefender Premium VPN combined.

Unfortunate VPN bundling aside, Bitdefender Antivirus Plus is a good choice if you want antivirus for Windows, macOS, Android, and iOS.

It’s particularly competitive if you just want the antivirus protection on its own. All of the major cybersecurity companies would really prefer it if you bought a bundle (including Bitdefender), and they’re often good value if you actually use all the features. But there are plenty of reasons to just go for the antivirus itself (for example, credit and dark web monitoring features might already be available through your bank or another institution). And if antivirus protection is all you want, Bitdefender Antivirus Plus is a superb choice.

Bitdefender’s advantage is quite simple. It provides antivirus protection that detects most threats at a price that often undercuts the competition. It’s hard to think of a reason to pay more for an alternative when Bitdefender does the job well for as little as $25 for the first year.
