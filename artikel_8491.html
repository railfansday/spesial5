Start with a no-fee business card that pays 2% on every purchase with no yearly cap, the best fit for most freelancers. Add a card with a higher rate on phone, internet, or gas only if those bills take a big share of your spending, since such cards pay 1% on everything else.

A business card puts your freelance costs on a statement of their own, so they’re easy to add up when you deduct them at tax time. Pay it from a business bank account to keep the business money apart from yours too. You can apply as a sole proprietor without forming an LLC first, and no card here charges an annual fee.

Choose by where your money goes. If it’s spread across many kinds of purchases, a card that pays the same rate on everything earns more, with no categories to track. Some freelancers put a big share of their spending into a few costs, like phone and internet, gas, or Amazon orders. For them, a card with a higher rate on those costs can earn more, up to any yearly cap on that rate.
