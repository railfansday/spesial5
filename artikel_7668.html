Texas-Based Company Pleads Guilty to Trafficking Deadly Synthetic Opioids

A business-to-business online marketplace headquartered in Texas — eWorldTrade LLC — pleaded guilty to one count of conspiracy to distribute synthetic opioids for unlawful importation into the United States and agreed to pay a criminal fine of $1,350,000 based on its ability to pay.

“The Criminal Division will hold businesses accountable that facilitate the distribution of deadly drugs that put the lives of Americans at risk – just like we would any other drug dealer,” said Assistant Attorney General A. Tysen Duva of the Justice Department’s Criminal Division. “These incredibly dangerous, illegal synthetic opioids were manufactured abroad but brought into the U.S. because of eWorldTrade. Companies who knowingly facilitate the illicit sale of controlled substances will pay the price for the Americans they put in harm’s way.”

“Businesses that knowingly facilitate the online sale of synthetic opioids are no less dangerous than cartels and foreign terrorist organizations,” said Acting Special Agent in Charge Michael Perez of Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) Nashville. “Through close coordination with our law enforcement partners domestically and abroad, HSI will continue to identify and disrupt those networks.”

According to court documents, from March 2024 through March 2025, eWorldTrade offered registered users access to dozens of products through its website, including synthetic opioids such as fentanyl, carfentanil, and nitazenes. EWorldTrade knew that these products would reach the United States through its sales. Through its paid membership tiers, eWorldTrade offered customers easier access to premium suppliers of illegal drugs if they paid more to eWorldTrade, including to China-based sellers that openly offered and sold synthetic opioids through its website. The defendant admitted that its agents knowingly and intentionally facilitated prolific sales of these illicit controlled substances and others, including heroin and methamphetamine, from international sellers to potential buyers in the United States.

The Justice Department reached its resolution with eWorldTrade based on several factors, including the nature and seriousness of the offense. As part of the plea, eWorldTrade has agreed to enhance its compliance and internal controls and report to the Justice Department on remediation and implementation of the compliance measures throughout the term. EWorldTrade did not make a timely and voluntary disclosure of wrongdoing, but it received credit for its acceptance of responsibility and cooperation with the investigation, including collecting, analyzing, and producing voluminous information, providing timely updates on facts learned during its internal investigation, and engaging in timely remedial measures. The court will sentence eWorldTrade on Dec. 14.

The HSI Knoxville Office and the U.S. Postal Inspection Service’s Chattanooga Office investigated the case.

Trial Attorneys Jayce Born and Max J. Goldman of the Criminal Division’s Money Laundering, Narcotics and Forfeiture Section (MNF) are prosecuting the case.

MNF’s mission is to take the profit out of crime, eliminate drug cartels, and protect the U.S. financial system. MNF pursues criminal prosecutions and criminal and civil asset recovery actions involving: financial facilitators who launder profits for criminals; financial institutions and their officers and employees whose actions threaten the U.S. financial system and financial institutions; international money launderers who support transnational organized crime; and the top command and control of international drug trafficking organizations.

MNF’s Narcotic and Dangerous Drug Unit investigates and prosecutes the top command and control elements of international drug cartels, drug trafficking organizations, and related transnational criminal organizations.
