Rackspace pulls plug on Spot cloud service after security breach

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Customers face frantic scramble to migrate workloads and rotate compromised credentials

Rackspace is shutting down its low-cost Spot cloud service after a security breach, giving customers two weeks' notice to move their workloads and retrieve their data.

In a notice posted on Tuesday, the cloud computing biz said its Rackspace Spot service will cease operations at 1700 Central Time (2300 UTC) on December 8. Any workloads still running at that point will be terminated, and customers will no longer be able to access their data.

"Following the recent security incident affecting Rackspace Spot, Rackspace has made the decision to wind down the service," the company said. "Spot customers are being notified with the wind-down timeline, instructions for retrieving their data and steps to secure their environments."

Customers will have to contend with the fallout from the breach while simultaneously trying to get their applications and data off the platform.

According to a customer notification seen by The Register, the attackers used leaked credentials to access parts of Rackspace Spot over a period of almost 22 hours on November 17. Rackspace only learned of the intrusion the following day, after a customer raised the alarm.

The intruders impersonated legitimate users, created pods in customer Kubernetes clusters, gained access to stored credentials known as Kubernetes Secrets, and listed nodes and storage volumes.

"With that access, they could have read any Secret in an affected user's clusters, and any Spot API token or refresh token that user held," Rackspace told customers.

Rackspace has since revoked the affected tokens and sessions, removed the pods planted by the intruders, and changed the affected platform and storage keys. Customers must also reissue API tokens, download new Kubernetes configuration files, and rotate credentials stored in their clusters, including database passwords and third-party API keys.

Sirish Raghuram, co-founder and chief growth officer of Platform9, which co-developed Spot with Rackspace, shed some light on the root cause in a November 20 GitHub post.

"An observability system was breached using a compromised credential," Raghuram wrote. "This was obviously a critical error on our part and we are very sorry we let this happen."

Raghuram said the incident necessitated significant changes to the underlying infrastructure clusters, prolonging the outage.

Spot's selling point was cheap compute. The service operated an auction-based pricing model, allowing customers to bid for cloud infrastructure at a fraction of the cost of conventional on-demand instances. Rackspace advertised savings of up to 90 percent for customers willing to tolerate interruptions to their workloads. A permanent interruption probably wasn't what they had in mind.

Rackspace hasn't explained why it chose closure over recovery, revealed how many customers were affected, or said whether it will offer migration extensions. It did not respond to The Register's questions. ®
