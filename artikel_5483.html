Three insights you may have missed from theCUBE’s coverage of NetApp INSIGHT

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Intelligent data infrastructure is becoming a priority as enterprises move artificial intelligence beyond isolated pilots and into production environments.

The shift places new demands on storage systems built to support traditional applications, especially as organizations introduce AI workloads across hybrid and multicloud environments. NetApp Inc. is responding with a strategy that emphasizes infrastructure modernization without requiring enterprises to abandon existing investments, according to George Kurian (pictured), chief executive officer of NetApp.

“I think that the AI era requires organizations to be really good stewards of their data, because data is the foundation for knowledge and knowledge is the intellectual property,” he told theCUBE. “To have knowledge, you need really good intelligent data infrastructure that not only allows you to extract memory and context from your data, but also to keep it secure, well governed and a resource that you can trust.”

Kurian spoke with theCUBE Research’s Christophe Bertrand and co-host Rebecca Knight during NetApp INSIGHT, in an exclusive broadcast on theCUBE, SiliconANGLE Media’s livestreaming studio. The event’s coverage featured NetApp executives and technology partners discussing the evolving demands on enterprise data infrastructure and the challenges of bringing AI into production. Here are three insights you may have missed from theCUBE’s coverage of NetApp INSIGHT:

Enterprise AI workloads are exposing limitations in storage architectures designed for more predictable application demands. NetApp’s Novus architecture separates data and metadata management so each can scale independently. The company’s AI Data Engine is designed to discover, classify and prepare enterprise data for AI applications, according to Syam Nair, chief product officer of NetApp.

“Performance and throughput of accessing the data, that’s a necessity for AI, but that won’t be sufficient for agents to actually act on the data,” he said during the event. “If the data is not ready and it’s not protected, you get fast results. You get compromised results, but they may not be the ones you want.”

The demands of AI factories are also changing how storage resources must be managed. NetApp’s approach aims to support high-throughput AI pipelines without sacrificing the responsiveness other applications require, according to Arindam Banerjee, chief platform and technology officer of NetApp, who spoke alongside Jason Hardy, vice president of storage technology at Nvidia Corp.

“What happens is [that] different types of workloads like transactional workloads for metadata and heavy sequential workloads for, say, checkpoints, happen at the same time,” Banerjee told theCUBE. “Previous architectures did one or the other very well. They never were able to combine it and do both at the same time.”

Modernizing enterprise infrastructure often requires organizations to accommodate existing applications while introducing new AI capabilities. NetApp and Oracle Corp. are addressing that challenge through a co-engineered, fully managed storage service that runs ONTAP natively within Oracle Cloud Infrastructure. The offering is designed to ease migration of existing ONTAP workloads without requiring application redesign, according to Konstantinos Papamiltiadis, senior vice president of partnerships at Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, a division of Oracle.

“When I’m going into the OCI console, I have the option to use the ONTAP as a native service,” he told theCUBE. “For me, the most important thing that we managed to build is, from an operational perspective, we bring everything under one single interface that provides simplicity and consistency.”

The integration challenge extends beyond cloud storage to the networking and compute resources that support AI workloads. NetApp and Cisco Systems Inc. are expanding their FlexPod infrastructure with validated designs intended to reduce deployment complexity. The partnership has served 10,000 customers over 16 years, according to James Cole, vice president of data center networking, business development, partnerships and go-to-market at Cisco, and Alvaro Celis, chief partner and ecosystem officer at NetApp.

“Twenty years ago, 10 years ago, you would’ve been designing your data structures and your applications around the infrastructure. Now we’ve turned that on its head,” Cole said during the event. “You’re really designing the infrastructure around the movement of data. Who better to do that together than the person you trust with your data and the person you trust with your network?”

Another partnership focuses on private AI deployment and measurable business outcomes. The NetApp AIPod Mini combines NetApp infrastructure with the Generate platform from Iterate Studio Inc., doing business as Iterate.ai, according to Brian Sathianathan, co-founder and chief technology officer of Iterate.ai. In one insurance deployment cited during the discussion, an agent reduced accident-report analysis from approximately eight hours to eight minutes.

“AIPod Mini has attached connections to the NetApp storage, and it talks to NetApp using the ONTAP protocol that’s time-tested,” he said during the event. “Then, on top of the AIPod Mini, we are running our Generate software. That software has a [large language model] embedded in it, so you can run all your AI queries locally and fully privately within your four walls.”

As enterprises introduce autonomous agents into infrastructure management, intelligent data infrastructure must accommodate increasingly complex operational demands. NetApp’s unified control plane, delivered through NetApp Console, is designed to reduce routine administrative work through policy-driven automation. In one keynote demonstration, agents responded to a nighttime performance anomaly, according to Sandeep Singh, senior vice president and general manager of enterprise storage at NetApp.

“Agents can, in real time, implement the necessary quality of service rules to ensure there’s compliance to the boundary conditions there,” he told theCUBE. “And that way, no human is woken up. The right outcome is achieved, and there’s no noisy neighbor effect. And there’s an audit trail presented to the humans for going and following up.”

Automation, however, can’t resolve organizational questions about data ownership and responsibility. As enterprises extend intelligent data infrastructure across applications and environments, leadership must establish accountability for the information those systems rely on, according to Helen Yu, founder and chief executive officer of Tigon Advisory Corp., who appeared alongside Singh.

“It’s not a storage problem. It’s a leadership and accountability issue,” she said during the event. “When teams don’t have access to … trusted data consistently, they actually stop making decisions. They start making workarounds.”
