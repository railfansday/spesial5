How 4 finance executives rose, and how many S&P 500 CFOs became CEOs

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The CFO to CEO path, traced through careers at Oracle, TikTok, Hertz and Deutsche Bank, plus data on how many S&P 500 CFOs who switched jobs became CEOs

Russell Reynolds Associates found that 33% of S&P 500 chief financial officers who changed jobs in 2022 moved into chief executive roles. In 2021, that share was 8.8%. Those two figures frame this slideshow: the move from CFO to CEO, and the executives whose careers run alongside it.

The finance job itself has widened. The role has moved well beyond number crunching. Today's CFO analyzes data to spot trends, weighs risks and plans for growth. Finance chiefs also often drive digital transformation, the effort to rebuild how a company runs around newer technology.

The careers in this selection trace that move in different ways. Safra Catz has been an Oracle $ORCL executive since April 1999. She served as co-president and CFO starting in 2011 and later became the company's sole CEO. Shou Zi Chew was CFO at Xiaomi and then at ByteDance before taking over as TikTok's chief executive. Stephen M. Scheer started at Goldman Sachs $GS in 1993, held a CFO role from 2018 and became CEO of Hertz in February 2022.

Not every path here ends in the top job. James von Moltke became Deutsche Bank's CFO in 2017. He has also served as the bank's president since March 2022 while keeping the finance role. At TikTok, the stretch between Kevin Mayer's exit after a few months as CEO and Chew's appointment was covered by Vanessa Pappas. She was the company's General Manager, not its CFO, and served as interim global head.

The slides that follow trace these careers one at a time. They then step back to the wider pattern. That includes the share of S&P 500 finance chiefs moving into chief executive roles and the analytical and technology work that now sits with the CFO. It also covers the banking backgrounds several of these executives share. Goldman Sachs appears in the early careers of both Chew and Scheer. Von Moltke started at Credit Suisse First Boston in London in 1992 and spent eight years at Citigroup $C. Catz's route ran instead through a long tenure inside Oracle.

Safra Catz reached the top of Oracle $ORCL by moving up through the company's own ranks. She joined as an executive in April 1999 and later served as co-president and chief financial officer. She then shared the chief executive role with Mark Hurd before becoming Oracle's sole CEO.

Her climb took place inside a single company. Catz was not an outside hire brought in to run Oracle.

A board seat came early. Oracle added Catz to its board of directors in 2001, about two years after she started as an executive there. She had a place in the company's governance before she held the president title.

In 2004, she was named president. Her board appointment had come three years earlier.

The finance role followed in 2011, when Catz became co-president and CFO. In that position, she reported to Larry Ellison, Oracle's cofounder. The post put her in charge of the company's finances while she kept a share of the president's title.

The order of those appointments sets her career apart from a straight finance track. Seven years separated her appointment as president from her move into the finance job. She did not rise through finance first and then move into general management. The CFO post came after she had already reached a senior leadership title.

The route to chief executive opened when Ellison stepped down as CEO. Catz did not take the post alone at first. She became co-CEO alongside Mark Hurd, and the two split the top job.

She later became Oracle's sole CEO. That ended the period in which the top job was divided between two executives.

Her profile extends beyond Oracle. Fortune has listed her among the most powerful women in business. She has also sat on Disney $DIS's board since 2018. That seat gives her a governance role at a second large U.S. company.

Shou Zi Chew became TikTok's CEO after serving as chief financial officer at two technology companies, Xiaomi and ByteDance. He took over the role after Kevin Mayer quit as TikTok's chief executive.

Chew began his career in banking. He spent two years as a banker at Goldman Sachs $GS in London.

He then moved to the investing side. Chew worked at venture firms, including DST Global.

During that stretch he led investments in JD.com and Xiaomi. Those deals put him on the investor side of both companies before he held a job inside either one.

One of those investments became his next employer. Chew was named Xiaomi's CFO in 2015. The appointment moved him from backing the smartphone maker to running its finances.

His second finance chief role came at ByteDance, TikTok's parent company. Chew became ByteDance's CFO in March 2021.

That post placed him inside the company that owns TikTok. When he later became TikTok's chief executive, he moved from the parent company's finance office to the head of its subsidiary.

The opening came when Mayer left the CEO post after a few months in the role. Vanessa Pappas, TikTok's General Manager, served as interim global head until Chew took over.

Each stage of Chew's career connects to the one before it. A banking start led to venture investing. An investment in Xiaomi led to a finance job there. A finance job at ByteDance led to the top job at TikTok.

He held the CFO title at two different companies before he ran one.

Vanessa Pappas, TikTok's General Manager, served as the company's interim global head after Kevin Mayer quit as CEO. She held that position until Shou Zi Chew took over the top job. She is the only interim leader among the executives profiled here.

Mayer's stint as chief executive was brief. He left after only a few months. Pappas then led the company during the gap before a permanent CEO was named.

An interim chief's job is to keep the company running through a leadership transition. At TikTok, that role went to an executive who already worked at the company.

Pappas did not come to the job through finance. Her title at TikTok was General Manager, not chief financial officer. Every other executive profiled in this selection held a CFO title at some point. That makes her the exception in a list built around finance leaders. Her case shows that a temporary top post does not have to go to the finance chief.

Pappas is Australian. She graduated from the University of Queensland in Brisbane. Before joining TikTok, she worked at Next New Networks and at YouTube. Both companies are online video businesses. That background sits in the same field as TikTok, an app built around short videos.

She also commented on the app's commercial direction. In an interview with CNBC, Pappas said she saw ways Walmart $WMT could work together with the e-commerce features newly added to the TikTok app. Walmart is a U.S. retail chain.

The permanent job went to Chew, whose career had run through finance roles. His appointment ended the interim period that Pappas had overseen.

The TikTok handover ran from Mayer to Pappas to Chew. The temporary leader in that sequence came from outside the finance function. The finance executive arrived afterward and took the CEO title.

Stephen M. Scheer became CEO of Hertz in February 2022 after a career spent in finance. He also holds a seat on the Hertz board. That places him on the company's governing body as well as at the head of its management.

His career started in 1993 in the Financial Institutions Group at Goldman Sachs $GS. At an investment bank, a financial institutions group advises banks, insurers, asset managers and other financial companies on mergers, acquisitions and capital raising. That work centers on the balance sheets and regulation of lenders and other financial firms.

Scheer moved into a chief financial officer role in 2018. He held that post in 2019, the year Goldman Sachs and Apple $AAPL launched Apple Card. The card took a Wall Street bank into consumer credit cards in partnership with a consumer technology company.

The Hertz appointment put a longtime finance executive in charge of a car rental company. A rental business of that kind buys large numbers of vehicles, finances them, rents them out and later sells them. Fleet funding and used-car values sit close to the core of its economics.

Outside Hertz, Scheer sits on the boards of the Jewish Museum and the New York Stem Cell Foundation. The Jewish Museum is an art and culture museum on Fifth Avenue in Manhattan. The New York Stem Cell Foundation is a nonprofit that funds and conducts stem cell research.

He is also a member of the Business Roundtable, the Council on Foreign Relations and the U.S. Travel and Tourism Advisory Board. The Business Roundtable is an association of chief executives of major U.S. companies. The Council on Foreign Relations is a New York-based membership organization and think tank focused on foreign policy. The travel advisory board counsels the U.S. secretary of commerce on policy affecting travel and tourism. That industry is the market Hertz serves.

James von Moltke has been Deutsche Bank's chief financial officer since 2017, and since March 2022 he has also served as the bank's president. He kept the finance job when he took the second title. He now holds both posts at once.

That sets his case apart from the others in this selection. Von Moltke is not Deutsche Bank's CEO. His career shows a finance chief whose remit widened inside one company. It does not show a move from the CFO seat into the chief executive's chair.

His banking career spans more than 30 years. It began in 1992 at Credit Suisse First Boston in London. He later spent eight years at Citigroup $C. He became Deutsche Bank's finance chief in 2017. That put 25 years of banking between his first job and his arrival in the bank's CFO role. Deutsche Bank is the third bank named in that record.

The president title came about five years into his tenure as CFO. A Deutsche Bank release describing changes to the responsibilities of its management board stated that von Moltke was to remain chief financial officer.

His responsibilities grew again in 2023. Since that year he has overseen Asset Management. That adds a business division to a portfolio that already combined the CFO and president roles.

Read in sequence, the changes follow one line. Von Moltke became CFO in 2017, added the president's title in March 2022 and took on Asset Management in 2023. Each change happened at the same bank. None of them involved the chief executive's job.

His titles are president and CFO. That places him outside the CFO to CEO path followed by Safra Catz at Oracle $ORCL, Shou Zi Chew at TikTok and Stephen M. Scheer at Hertz. Catz, Chew and Scheer each reached the chief executive's office. Von Moltke widened his remit without leaving the finance post.

In 2022, 33% of S&P 500 chief financial officers who changed jobs became chief executives, according to Russell Reynolds Associates. In 2021, the share was 8.8%.

That is a rise of 24.2 percentage points in one year. The 2022 share was about 3.75 times the 2021 level.

Put in plainer terms, roughly one in three job-changing finance chiefs landed the top post in 2022. A year earlier, fewer than one in 10 did.

The figure covers a defined group. It counts S&P 500 CFOs who moved to a new position during the year, not every finance chief in the index. A CFO who stayed in the same role through 2022 is outside the calculation.

Both numbers are shares, not head counts. The data does not state how many CFOs changed jobs in either year. A percentage can rise because more finance chiefs became CEOs, because fewer changed jobs overall, or both. The figures alone cannot separate those effects.

The measure records one step in a career. It shows whether a departing finance chief's next job was the top executive post. The two percentages do not say whether those CEO roles were permanent or interim. They also say nothing about how the new chief executives performed after taking over.

This limits what the figures can support about stand-in leaders. The data cannot show how often a company names its own CFO as an interim CEO. It also does not record whether a new CEO post came at the same company or at a different one. Safra Catz followed the same-company route at Oracle $ORCL, where she was CFO before becoming CEO.

The data spans two years, 2021 and 2022. It describes a one-year jump among job-changing CFOs at the large U.S. public companies in the S&P 500, not a longer trend. The figures carry no breakdown by sector and no stated cause for the jump.

The modern CFO's job reaches beyond bookkeeping. Rydoo describes finance chiefs who study company data to find trends, assess risks and map out growth, not only produce the numbers.

Number crunching remains part of the work. In Rydoo's account, it is no longer the whole of it.

Each of the three tasks Rydoo names covers a different part of the job. Spotting trends means reading data for patterns that matter to the business. Weighing risk means judging what could go wrong and how much exposure a company can carry. Planning growth means deciding where the business should put its money and effort next.

Rydoo sets out this wider scope in its CFO Corner piece on finance leaders who moved into chief executive roles.

Finance chiefs often drive digital transformation inside their companies, according to Rydoo's CFO Corner. The publication lists that work among the responsibilities that now sit with the CFO.

Rydoo presents digital transformation as one sign of how far the finance job has broadened.

The publication makes the point in its coverage of finance leaders who went on to become chief executives.

Shou Zi Chew, Stephen M. Scheer and James von Moltke all began their careers at large investment banks before reaching finance leadership. Chew and Scheer started at Goldman Sachs $GS. Von Moltke started at Credit Suisse First Boston.

Scheer's first job, in 1993, was in the Financial Institutions Group at Goldman Sachs. Von Moltke began a year earlier, in 1992, at Credit Suisse First Boston in London. He went on to spend eight years at Citigroup $C. In total, he has more than 30 years in banking.

The two reached the finance chief's office on nearly matching timelines. Von Moltke became Deutsche Bank's CFO in 2017. Scheer held a CFO post from 2018. In both cases, 25 years separated the first banking job from the CFO title.

Chew's banking stint was much shorter. He spent two years as a Goldman Sachs banker in London. He then moved into venture investing at firms including DST Global. There he led investments in JD.com and Xiaomi. In 2015 he became CFO of Xiaomi, a company he had backed as an investor. His route to finance leadership ran through deal-making rather than a long run inside a bank.

The banking start did not lead to a single outcome. Chew moved on to the CFO post at ByteDance in March 2021. He later took over as TikTok's CEO after Kevin Mayer quit. Scheer became CEO of Hertz in February 2022. Von Moltke has stayed in finance at Deutsche Bank. He added the president's title in March 2022 and oversight of Asset Management in 2023 while keeping the CFO role. He does not hold the CEO post.

Catz's route is measured in years at one company. She has been an Oracle $ORCL executive since April 1999. She became co-president and CFO in 2011, 12 years later. After Larry Ellison stepped down as CEO, she shared the top job with Mark Hurd. She later held it alone.

Across these four careers, two routes to the CFO office appear. One starts at a bank and then moves through other firms. The other runs through long tenure inside a single company, with the finance role serving as one step toward the chief executive's office.
