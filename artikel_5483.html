Late Film Critic Rex Reed Bought His Dakota Apartment for $30,000 in 1969—Now It’s Listed for Nearly $6 Million

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The Upper West Side home of late film critic Rex Reed in the landmark building, The Dakota—where he lived for nearly 60 years—has hit the market, with an asking price just under $6 million.

The two-bedroom, two-bathroom abode spans about 2,000 square feet—and decades of cultural history.

Proceeds from the sale of the $5.99 million residence—as well as an upcoming estate auction—will fund the Rex Reed Foundation, an organization that seeks to uplift storytelling initiatives.

“Residence 86 at the Dakota is an oversized, high-floor corner two-bedroom made for serious entertaining,” listing agent Philip McCarthy of Sotheby’s International Realty tells Realtor.com®.

The dwelling has certainly been used for exactly that. According to the New York Times, Reed hosted guests including Hollywood legends Lauren Bacall, Boris Karloff, and Truman Capote.

“He entertained a lot in his heyday, when he was hitting his stride in the late ‘60s and ‘70s,” Reed’s friend, Eric Baker, told the publication, noting that the parties had slowed in the last 15 years.

Reed—who passed in his home at 87, after a brief illness—snapped up the apartment in 1969 for just $30,000. Over the years, he would fill the home with curated antiques, artwork, and fascinating furnishings, despite having very few belongings in the beginning.

In a 2018 New York Times interview, the journalist recalled moving in with “an A.&.P shopping cart, some books, and whatever I could drag over from my little walk-up on 73rd street” Reportedly, he had long dreamt of living in the iconic building, as it was where “Rosemary’s Baby” was filmed.

According to the listing, the building is the “oldest surviving luxury apartment building in New York City.”

“Apartments here rarely come to market, and a sale from a single owner after nearly six decades is rarer still,” the listing adds.

Reed clearly understood the gem he was sitting on, apparently turning down “The Phantom of the Opera” composer Andrew Lloyd Webber’s $8 million offer for the residence.

“I spent a lot of time getting this apartment just the way I wanted it, and I wouldn’t want to start over now,” Reed told The Observer at the time.

Although the home has been repainted and staged to sell, McCarthy told the New York Times that some of the writer’s “personal belongings were mixed in.”

Still donning its original prewar layout, the unit makes an elegant first impression with a large formal gallery leading way to a generously sized living room and formal dining room, equipped with a dry bar and pantry.

The primary bedroom has an en suite bathroom, ample closet space, and a windowed nook well-suited for a home office or an idyllic reading spot.

The secondary bedroom, which Reed used as an office, also boasts an en suite, and has ample wardrobe storage, too. A full wall of built-in shelving once hosted his impressive media collection.

With high ceilings and north and west-facing windows that sit “well above the low-rise buildings” neighboring the Dakota, the stately residence is brimming with character.

Original architectural details—such as Regency doors and crown moldings—can still be found throughout the home.

Built in 1884, the Dakota sits on Central Park West and was designed by Henry J. Hardenbergh—who also completed the Plaza Hotel.

The building includes a gated porte-cochère entrance, which leads to a central courtyard with original, restored fountains. The facade underwent a full restoration in 2017.

The building also has a state-of-the art fitness center, additional storage, and auxiliary laundry. The listing states that pets and pieds-à-terre are allowed, and buyers are permitted to finance up to 50%.
