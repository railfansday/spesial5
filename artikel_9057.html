hyan adapts 1970s timber frame into live-work residence on the hills of hayama

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

the existing columns and beams are retained and structurally strengthened, while the interior is reorganized around a double-height living room and a recessed doma created from a former tatami room.

HYAN edited by christina vergopoulou November 13, 2026 3 min

Located in a hillside residential district of Hayama, Kanagawa, Japan, Green Sandwich House is the adaptive reuse of a late-1970s timber residence converted into a combined home and studio for architect Anna Naganuma and designer Shohei Hasegawa of Japanese architecture practice HYAN. Rather than demolishing the pre-1981 seismic-code structure, the studio retained the undamaged timber columns and beams, integrated seismic reinforcements, and reconfigured the interior layout to support overlapping domestic and studio functions.

Studio HYAN anchors the ground floor around a central double-height living room that connects the kitchen, pantry, study, garden, and a newly formed doma. Carved from a former tatami room by removing the traditional matting and altering the floor elevation, the doma incorporates a converted closet nook to serve as a multi-purpose threshold for working, hosting, and lounging. Instead of relying on solid partitions, the floor plan delineates programmatic zones through changes in floor level, sightlines, material transitions, and movable textiles. Custom curtains designed by textile artist Haruka Shoji frame the large openings and the doma, filtering daylight and garden views throughout the day. New functional surfaces, including the kitchen countertops and washbasin, are finished in hand-ground, in-situ terrazzo, establishing a tactile contrast against the exposed, aged timber frame.
