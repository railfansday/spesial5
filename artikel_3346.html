David Fincher Officially Reveals Real Budget for His New Quentin Tarantino Movie

The budget for the upcoming Netflix film The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth has been a subject of controversy, especially after rumors began to swirl about the movie having cost $200 million. It was suggested that director David Fincher, star Brad Pitt, and writer Quentin Tarantino earned $20 million each for the movie, a sequel to Tarantino's 2019 blockbuster Once Upon a Time in Hollywood. Now, with mere hours to go before the film is released — it'll play exclusively in IMAX theaters for two weeks before arriving on Netflix — Fincher has set the record straight, providing a concrete answer to the question of what the film really cost.

In an interview with Josh Horowitz, the filmmaker spoke about escalating costs affecting his Netflix series Mindhunter. Fincher has long maintained that the show will not return for a third season because Netflix felt that the costs were simply too high for a show with the cult following Mindhunter had attracted. Mindhunter's second season was released in 2019, after which Netflix and Fincher had a conversation about making it more palatable to the mainstream. But Fincher wasn't ready to turn his psychological thriller into an elevated version of CSI, and Netflix wasn't prepared to spend another $100 million on a season that would be watched by 2 million people.

Fincher said that the budget for a third season, were one made, would likely have been even higher because of ballooning costs of doing business. He said that The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth is a good example, revealing that not only was the rumored $200 million budget inaccurate, but that he told Netflix that he could make the movie for roughly the same price as Tarantino's original film, which grossed more than $400 million worldwide and won Pitt a Best Supporting Actor Oscar.

“Cliff Booth is rumored at $200 million. I wish we had $200 million. I’d still be shooting. I told Ted Sarandos I’d make the movie for in and around what Quentin made the first one for, and he said, okay. Now, there are IA increases, it’s more expensive to shoot in Los Angeles six years later. So the movie came in at around $120 million or $123 million. Something like that. The first one was $97 million. It’s ostensibly the same amount of days. It’s ostensibly the same size of crew. We employed the same amount of people. But there are real fiscal consequences to being able to say, ‘Back off, man, we’re doctors’.”

The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth is the third film in Netflix's recent embrace of theatrical distribution, after the Oscar contender La Bola Negra and the World War II drama The Mosquito Bowl. The streamer will release the film exclusively in IMAX screens beginning November 25, before its Netflix debut on December 23. Also starring Elizabeth Debicki, Scott Caan, Yahya Abdul-Mateen II, Carla Gugino, and Peter Weller, the movie has received positive early reviews and is currently sitting at a "Certified Fresh" 83% score on Rotten Tomatoes.
