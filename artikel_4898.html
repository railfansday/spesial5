'The Biggest Opportunity in Crypto Is Already Underway': Solana Bets Big on AI Agents

AI agents are becoming the internet's main users, and the Solana Foundation wants its blockchain to be how they pay for things.

"The biggest opportunity in crypto is already underway," Rishin Sharma, who leads AI at the Solana Foundation, said at the Breakpoint conference in London.

Sharma used his presentation to showcase Solana.ai, a new homepage for the network's AI ecosystem, which the foundation launched last week. More than 120 teams are now building AI products on Solana across payments, trading and infrastructure, he said.

Bots and other automated software accounted for more than 53% of global web traffic in 2025, up from 51% the year before, according to cybersecurity firm Imperva's latest Bad Bot Report, which Sharma cited. Agents have also come to dominate AI usage: API keys classified as agentic accounted for 76% of the tokens processed through model marketplace OpenRouter in October 2026, up from 8% a year earlier, according to the company's monthly data recap.

At the same time, AI is getting much cheaper. Sharma's slides showed the price of a million tokens falling from $37.50 for GPT-4 to $0.14 for GPT-5 nano, a 270-fold drop, while monthly token use has risen around 330-fold.

"Which model is smarter matters a little bit less, and what matters more is what the model can actually do," Sharma said. "What are the tools it's using? What are the data it's able to access? And what money can it functionally use?"

Payments are where that shift is showing up first, Sharma said. The x402 standard, which lets AI agents pay for online services, has processed more than 400 million payments and $100 million in volume, up from zero a year ago, he said. Solana-based BlockRun, which he described as the largest x402 server, has handled more than 60 million transactions in the past two months.

The foundation has also launched payment channels that it says can handle 1 million payments per second, a private AI service offering attested private inference on Solana, and Pay.sh, a server integrating more than 100 APIs that agents can pay for, Sharma said. He added that the foundation is working with companies including Google, Alibaba and ByteDance to let them charge for their AI models through agent payments.

Next, the foundation plans to publish benchmarks for how well AI models perform financial tasks, and to launch an on-ramp that lets users convert cash to stablecoins and hand them to their AI assistants, he said.

Macro investor and Real Vision founder Raoul Pal, speaking earlier at the conference, argued that agents are not yet real economic actors. "They're like kids," he said. "These are young kids who spend nothing on a few sweets and stuff." But as stocks and other assets move onchain, he said, agents will start using blockchains to trade and settle, and "the value accrues to the underlying layer one."

Solana has been building toward this for months. The foundation teamed up with Google Cloud on a stablecoin payments service for AI agents in May, while the x402 Foundation, backed by Coinbase and the Linux Foundation, is developing the payments standard. Franklin Templeton has described agentic AI as a killer use case for crypto.
