Judge Sides With ABC In First Amendment Lawsuit Against FCC, Puts Halt On Early License Renewal Proceeding

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

A federal judge has largely sided with ABC in its First Amendment lawsuit claiming that an FCC order to review its broadcast licenses was a retaliatory act by the Trump administration, which from the start has attacked its news content, The View and Jimmy Kimmel.

U.S. District Judge Loren AliKhan issued a preliminary injunction on Wednesday that prohibits the FCC from taking any further action in its license review, concluding that the network was “likely to succeed in their retaliation claim.”

AliKhan wrote that the network had “sufficiently cast the early license renewal proceedings as a retaliatory act,” and she cited moves that the network has already taken out of fear of losing their licenses. They include the choice to place James Talarico’s guest appearance on Jimmy Kimmel Live on YouTube, outside of the FCC’s authority, as well as ABC’s decision to livestream Trump’s July primetime speech out of fear concern of administration retaliation.

“These alterations to Plaintiffs’ editorial decision making showcase how the Commission’s early license renewal proceedings have already curtailed Plaintiffs’ protected activity,” the judge wrote. “That an objectively reasonable broadcaster faced with the same existential threat would follow suit is beyond debate.”

The network is the first major media conglomerate to challenge the FCC’s wave of investigations since the start of Donald Trump‘s second term.

ABC filed suit against the FCC in August, claiming that the agency was violating the First Amendment as it ordered the network’s broadcast stations to submit their licenses to an early review.

The network noted that the agency’s action was unprecedented, creating a chill to its speech and that of other broadcasters given the risk to losing licenses for their eight owned stations. The first of the licenses was not set to expire until 2028.

In claiming regulatory retaliation, the network cited Trump’s attacks on Kimmel and its news coverage, as well as warnings from the president’s FCC Chairman Brendan Carr.

The judge referenced Trump’s attacks and warnings in her opinion and noted that three of the co-hosts of The View, as well as Kimmel and ABC News reporters have entrires of the White House’s “media offenders” webpage.

She also noted the timing of the FCC’s order for ABC stations to submit to the license review, coming a day after Trump posted on Truth Social that Kimmel should be fired for a skit the previous week.

The FCC has argued that the early license renewal was tied to its inability to obtain documents from the network as part of an investigation of its diversity, equity and inclusion practices. But toward the end of a two-hour hearing last month, the judge asked the agency whether there were other, less drastic alternatives it could have chosen rather than the license review. And in her decision, AliKahn wrote that she did not find the agency’s explanation to be credible.

She wrote, “What is more, Chairman Carr has taken President Trump’s rhetoric as his marching orders. The same week he was tapped to be President Trump’s FCC chair, following months of President Trump’s calling for ‘ABC [to] lose its license’ for how it covered the presidential campaign …. then-Commissioner Carr lauded ‘license revocation’ as a ‘remedy’ to ‘deter this kind of conduct.'”

During the hearing, Beth Wilkinson, representing the network, compared ABC’s situation to the French Revolution, with people forced on a guillotine, wondering if anything will come down. Losing broadcast licenses, she said, is akin to a “death penalty” for stations.

The FCC argued that the network had to wait until the review process played out before challenging it, and that the D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals, not the district court, is the venue to hear such a case.

But AliKhan concluded that ABC’s First Amendment retaliation claim fell outside the kind of statutory review set up in the Communications Act, directing challenges to the federal appellate court and likely adding months of delay.

The judge wrote, “To be sure, Plaintiffs would face even greater injury if the FCC ultimately denied their renewal applications. But that does not mean that they are not currently being injured by allegedly being singled out for differential treatment—that is, from facing early license renewal proceedings years in advance of the standard expiration dates as a cudgel to stamp out unfavorable content about President Trump.”

Carr has initiated investigations and reviews of all of the major broadcast networks. Among other things, he launched a probe of The View earlier this year and whether it had to comply with the agency’s Equal Time Rule.

Spokespersons for the FCC and ABC did not immediately comment.

Anna Gomez, the sole Democrat on the FCC, said in a statement, “For nearly two years, the agency has weaponized its licensing authority to retaliate against broadcasters for exercising their right to free expression, and now a federal court has stepped in to put a check on that conduct. That brings badly needed accountability to a Commission and an administration that have politicized the machinery of government in a sustained campaign of censorship and control.”
