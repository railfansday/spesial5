Circle Internet Group vs. Strategy: Crypto's Brutal Sell-Off Is Exposing a Critical Difference Between These 2 Stocks

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The cryptocurrency downturn is putting two fundamentally different business models to the test, raising questions about which offers investors a more sustainable path to long-term returns.

By RJ Fulton – Updated Nov 18, 2026 at 5:25AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Circle Internet Group ›

The ongoing cryptocurrency bear market is exposing fundamental differences between businesses operating in the digital asset economy. Circle Internet Group (CRCL -1.54%) and Strategy (MSTR -4.46%) offer investors two distinct ways to gain exposure to the industry, but their financial performance depends on very different factors.

Circle is building the financial infrastructure behind digital payments through its USDC (USDC -0.01%) stablecoin, generating revenue primarily from interest earned on its reserves. Strategy, meanwhile, has transformed itself from an enterprise software company into a Bitcoin (BTC -2.27%) treasury business, leveraging capital markets to accumulate increasingly large holdings of the cryptocurrency.

While both companies stand to benefit from the long-term adoption of digital assets, their contrasting business models, capital structures, and exposure to cryptocurrency volatility could have significant implications for shareholder returns heading into 2027.

With services spanning 185 countries, Circle is positioned to benefit from the growing integration of blockchain technology into traditional finance. Unlike many cryptocurrency businesses, Circle does not depend directly on rising digital asset prices to drive revenue. Instead, it earns the majority of its revenue from interest generated on USDC reserves.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $3 billion, representing year-over-year growth of approximately 25.7%. Net income totaled $462.9 million, translating to a profit margin of roughly 15.4%. The expanding use of stablecoins for payments and financial settlement provides Circle with a structural growth opportunity that extends beyond speculative cryptocurrency trading.

As of December 2025, Circle maintained a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.0 and a current ratio of 1.0. The company generated approximately $529.7 million in free cash flow during fiscal 2025. However, stock-based compensation represented 104.4% of operating cash flow, warranting closer scrutiny of the quality of its cash generation.

Although Strategy continues to operate its enterprise analytics software business, its investment thesis increasingly centers on accumulating Bitcoin. The company utilizes a combination of common stock, preferred securities, and debt financing to expand its Bitcoin holdings, providing shareholders with exposure to the cryptocurrency through a traditional equity structure.

Over the latest 12-month reporting period, revenue reached approximately $495.1 million, representing year-over-year growth of 4.2%. However, Strategy reported a net loss of approximately $13.7 billion, primarily reflecting declines in the fair value of its Bitcoin holdings. The disparity between revenue and net income illustrates how much Bitcoin's price movements now overshadow the financial performance of its software business.

As of December 2025, Strategy maintained a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.2 and a current ratio of 5.6. Despite these seemingly favorable balance sheet metrics, the company recorded negative free cash flow of $112.5 million in fiscal 2025. With limited internal cash generation, Strategy remains heavily dependent on external financing to support its Bitcoin accumulation strategy.

One of Circle's primary risks is intensifying competition. Efforts to formalize stablecoin regulation, most notably through the GENIUS Act, lend legitimacy to the industry but also open the door for banks, payment processors, and fintech companies to issue competing stablecoins, potentially eroding Circle's market share and profitability. Circle is also sensitive to interest rates, as falling short-term Treasury yields could reduce reserve income unless offset by growth in USDC circulation.

Strategy faces a different set of risks. The company frequently issues new shares to fund Bitcoin purchases, which can dilute the ownership of existing shareholders. While selling new shares has historically worked in Strategy's favor during bull markets, a prolonged bear market makes this approach less effective. As Bitcoin's price declines, Strategy risks having to issue additional shares to fund further purchases, potentially diluting existing shareholders without delivering a corresponding increase in per-share value.

While Circle appears more reasonably valued based on its P/S ratio, Strategy currently offers a lower multiple relative to its future earnings estimates.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

Investors should note that the Forward P/E compares the current stock price to future earnings estimates. The P/S ratio measures the company market cap against sales over the past twelve months.

Despite Circle's relatively expensive forward price-to-earnings ratio of 53.2 compared to Strategy's 6.5, I believe Circle offers the more compelling investment opportunity heading into 2027. Strategy's seemingly attractive earnings multiple warrants caution, as its profitability is heavily influenced by fluctuations in Bitcoin's price rather than the underlying performance of its software business. Circle also trades at just 5.4 times sales, compared to Strategy's 93.1, although traditional valuation metrics are less useful when evaluating a Bitcoin treasury company.

Ultimately, the deciding factor for me is the sustainability of each company's business model. Stablecoins represent one of the most practical applications of blockchain technology, with opportunities extending across cross-border payments, commercial transactions, and institutional financial settlement. Circle can capitalize on this growing adoption regardless of whether Bitcoin enters another bull market.

Strategy could deliver substantial returns if Bitcoin rebounds in 2027. However, its dependence on capital markets and potential shareholder dilution introduce additional risks that investors would not encounter by simply owning Bitcoin. While Circle faces competitive pressures and a demanding earnings valuation of its own, I prefer its ability to generate revenue from the broader adoption of digital assets rather than relying on their price appreciation.
