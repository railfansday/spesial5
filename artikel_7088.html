Increases in pre-K discipline rates alarm advocates

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Let K-12 Dive's free newsletter keep you informed, straight from your inbox.

Preschool enrollment grew just 12% between 2021-22 and 2023-24, but suspensions increased 67%, an analysis by two equity groups found.

The number of U.S. preschoolers receiving out-of-school suspensions increased 67% and the expulsion rate nearly doubled between the 2021-22 and 2023-24 school years, according to an analysis of federal data by EdTrust and AdvancED Equity.

The rise in discipline rates for the youngest students may, in part, be due to a growing preschool enrollment, said Maithreyi Gopalan, co-author of the report and co-lead of AdvancEd Equity. Enrollment in school district-led preschool increased from 1.4 million in 2021-22 to 1.5 million in 2023-24.

Enrollment could be inching up due to a rebound from sharp drops during the COVID-19 pandemic, as well as expansion of public preschool programs in recent years, said Gopalan, who is also an associate professor at the University of Oregon's College of Education.

But the increase in students disciplined outpaced the overall enrollment growth of preschoolers, Gopalan said. "It's kind of shocking, isn't it, when you're thinking about 3- to 5-year-olds. What are they getting suspended for or expelled from school?"

The analysis by EdTrust and AdvancED Equity of preschool discipline rates uses data from the U.S. Department of Education's Civil Rights Data Collection and does not include information about what types of incidents led to the discipline.

A recent report from The Children’s Equity Project at Arizona State University found that harsh punishments used in preschool programs can lead to negative, long-term impacts like lower academic achievement and reduced high school graduation rates.

The CRDC only includes statistics from school district-supported preschool programs, Gopalan said. That means preschool enrollment would be included for district-operated early childhood Head Start programs, but not for Head Start programs run by community grantees.

And while the CRDC data is critical for tracking national trends, it does not capture the whole picture of discipline activities throughout the early childhood education system, said Gopalan, adding she and other researchers are continuing to look at different data collections to "get a better sense of what's going on."

What the CRDC data does reveal is that, like K-12 exclusionary discipline data, disparities in preschool punishments exist. The 2023-24 CRDC data shows Black preschoolers made up 18% of the pre-K population but were 37% of students who received suspensions. Boys were 54% of preschool enrollment but accounted for 81% of those suspended. White preschoolers were also overrepresented with 44% of the pre-K population but 51% of those suspended or expelled, according to the EdTrust and AdvancED Equity report and Gopalan.

Gopalan also noted in a follow-up email that the CRDC is limited, as it only collects formal out-of-school suspensions and expulsions. She said there is no federal collection that tracks more informal preschool discipline activities like shortened days, repeated pick-up calls or when parents are counseled to withdraw their child from a preschool.

Social-emotional learning interventions can help prevent escalating and challenging student behaviors, Gopalan said. And teacher training and resources also can reduce educators' stress and increase their capacity to address difficult behaviors.

"I think we as a society need to ask, 'Do we want to suspend them, or do we want to provide additional supports for teachers to help manage this problem?'" Gopalan said.
