Feature: 20 Wii Exclusives You Should Track Down While You Still Can

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Somehow, someway, it's been two whole decades since the Revolution began and an elderly relative who had never played a video game in their life somehow, someway, thrashed you at Wii Sports bowling.

The little system that could went on to sell over 100 million units across its lifetime, which means that 20 years on it's still possible to pick up a good clean one for a reasonable price - something which can't be said for a certain autostereoscopic handheld with a divergent family tree.

It also means that secondhand software is still plentiful for the most part, with much of the system's catalogue still available for not-silly money (plus some for silly money). So today we're rounding up 20 gems to consider picking up for your trusty Wii.

If you're just looking for the Best Wii Games™, we've got you covered elsewhere. Here, we're specifically looking for games slightly off the beaten track, games that were either full-on exclusives or, in a couple of cases where I've stretched the definition, are difficult to find anywhere else.

Kicking things off with the Batman, Wayforward's take on the Cartoon Network series captured perfectly the audio-visual elements of the show with a ton of amusing dialogue and a fun, animated take on a character that — outside of his Lego iteration — had gotten pretty dark and self-serious in the movies and other games of the era.

Batman: The Brave and the Bold - The Videogame (or Das Videospiel as my German copy has it) offers simple side-scrolling brawling, but the BAM-BOOM-POW energy is infectious, and this is a great one to play with the kids. There was a DS version, although that's single-player only, so stick with Wii.

Steven Spielberg's making a game, everybody! Legendary Hollywood director Steven Spielberg! The moment we heard that, we imagined Indiana Jones-style derring-do, or something with aliens or dinosaurs. We didn't imagine that the title prominently flagged as "A Steven Spielberg / EA Game" would be a physics-based block puzzler/party game in the Jenga mould.

However, 2008's Boom Blox turned out to be an absolute blast, especially with friends, and a game that made great use of the system's unique controller. it absolutely deserves to be revisited.

A sequel, Boom Blox Bash Party, followed in 2009, and that's a good time, too.

Dead Space: Extraction was a prequel to the 360-era sci-fi/survival horror game PS3/360 game, and perhaps benefited from low expectations, but it's a cracking, moody little rail shooter.

This one came to PS3 two years later with Move controller support, but this rail shooter was built for the Wii and made use of one of the system's best features: the IR camera-powered pointer in the Wiimotes. For accuracy and responsiveness, this fairly low-tech solution to pointer controls still trumps the gyro alternative (which constantly needs resetting/recentring) in our book, and this game is worth setting up the sensor bar.

One of the few games Europeans got which never saw the light of day in North America, Disaster: Day of Crisis is a Monolith Soft joint that showcases the Wiimote's versatility with different control schemes across its four main gameplay styles, which naturally includes those video game staples, shooting and driving.

The latter is handled with the Remote held horizontally, while outside the Nunchuk-controlled walking sections, the game becomes a pointer-based rail shooter.

With a blockbusting story that crams in volcanoes alongside shady outfits and plenty of (melo)drama, it doesn't quite cohere as it should, and there are certainly better-looking titles on the system. But as a curio from a studio with an enviable pedigree — and a game that apparently didn't pass Reggie Fils-Aimé's quality bar for release — it's a fascinating glimpse at Monolith's Wii-era thinking before Xenoblade Chronicles dropped two years later.

Excite Truck was an underrated gem in the Wii's early life, with satisfying motion controls that paired beautifully with the wallow-y physics of the arcade monster trucks you raced.

It also let you load up MP3s on your SD card and make your own soundtrack, which felt like a bit of magic to some Wii gamers (read: this Wii gamer) back in 2006. Nothing like boosting over a canyon with Gwen Stefani booming through the speakers. Good times.

Having recently fired up Excite Truck, I can confirm that this holds up beautifully, with subtle controls and a suitable sense of heft and face-blasting speed. Helluva game.

Excitebots, Monster Games' 2009 follow-up, kept the arcade sensibilities and challenging terrain but switched out the trucks for on-wheel robots. It's a fantastic game that injects a little more personality into the basic structure of Excite Truck while keeping the speed and excitement.

Sadly, it's also a game which never launched in Europe, so we're currently tit-for-tat after Disaster: Day of Crisis. North America got the better deal, there.

Look, it's an inconsequential Zelda spin-off designed to showcase the Wii Zapper, but it is a system-exclusive inconsequential Zelda spin-off. Can you afford not to have it in your collection?

Hmm, probably. There's a reason this target-practice game is bottom of our Zelda list. Still, it's not unenjoyable, still dirt cheap, and it's got Twilight Princess-era Link on the box. I've somehow ended up with two copies. One of those 'oh, I don't have this' moments when you get home and realise 'yes, I already have this.'

Another 'built for Wii' game that came to other platforms later (Vita in 2012, PC in 2016), Cing's Little King's Story added dashes of Doshin The Giant and Pikmin to a real-time strategy tale of the titular diminutive king working to expand the reach of his kingdom one citizen at a time and conquer neighbouring territories,

An engaging genre mash-up that holds its own alongside the previously mentioned Pikmin series while putting a fresh spin on things, this was one of the last games Cing (Hotel Dusk, Another Code) released. Two years later, the company filed for bankruptcy.

When PlatinumGames' name is on the box, you expect style - and MadWorld certainly delivered that with its inky, Sin City-esque aesthetic that splattered blood red across a black-and-white canvas.

At a time when all the serious games elsewhere were shiny and brown and grey, this Sega-published chainsaw-'em-up also helped demonstrate that Wii wasn't only a console for fluffy technicolour worlds and cuddly mascots.

As a hack-and-slash brawler, it's not up there with the developer's finest work - there's definitely an element of style over substance here. But when the style is this good, it's still worth the space on the shelf.

Honestly, at this point we all thought we'd have access to this on Switch one way or another — via NSO if not a proper remaster. However, at the time of writing (and discounting the now-unavailable Wii U eShop release of the Trilogy), this Metroid is still a Wii exclusive.

We can only assume it will arrive at some point — Nintendo now has the four Pikmin games on Switch, so it's about time to plug the Prime gap, too.
