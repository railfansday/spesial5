Tour Confidential: Tiger's back, Bryson's future and the LPGA finale

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

GOLF's weekly Tour Confidential roundtable breaks down the game's biggest headlines. This week, our writers and editors discuss Tiger Woods' next start, Bryson DeChambeau's future on the DP World Tour, Jeeno Thitikul's season and more.

Golf's biggest unanswered question has, well, an answer, as it was announced Bryson DeChambeau will join the DP World Tour in 2027, ending speculation about his immediate playing future. This seemed to be inevitable, right? And what are your thoughts on DeChambeau on the DP World Tour in 2027?

Josh Sens, senior writer (@Josh_Sens): I'm shocked. I thought he was going to become a full-time YouTube golfer. Kidding. Yes. Once the other pathways back closed, this seemed like his most likely destiny. Love him or not, DeChambeau makes for interesting theater so the news counts as a score for the DP World Tour. My question is how motivated DeChambeau will be to give his all on this circuit, especially in the short run, since he's exempt into all the majors through 2029 and has a spot in the U.S. Open through 2034. He doesn't need the money. And he doesn't need to play well to get into the events that matter most. At least not for now.

Josh Schrock, associate news editor (@schrock_and_awe): He had nowhere else to play other than the Your Golf Tour. It's a big win for the DP World Tour, depending on how much Bryson plays. Given that he has Category 1 status, I doubt we'll see him in Kenya or Turkey, but that would be cool to see him tee it up at lesser events and not just the Rolex Series and a few Middle East stops. I'm also interested to see his motivation and overall play now that he has to find a way to rejoin the professional golf ecosystem post-LIV's demise. Will be interesting to watch.

Josh Berhow, managing editor (@Josh_Berhow): This was probably the outcome all along. Bryson was obviously kicking around his options, but I think he liked the extra attention, as he remained something of the ultimate wild card. The guy has star power and he'll be a big boost to that league, and for that I'm happy for the DP World Tour, which was due for a good break. Also, and this sounds like a joke, but I think I'm serious, don't count out Bryson going to the lesser-known stops: think of the YouTube content he could create off the beaten path!

The DP World Tour also announced a handful of other former LIV players — Dustin Johnson, Cameron Smith, Bubba Watson and Joaquin Niemann among them — will receive special tournament invitations next year, essentially allowing them to play on the tour but not get into the big-money Rolex Series events. Better for the DP World Tour? Or worse for DP World Tour players who stayed loyal to the league?

Sens: I'm not sure there are legions of fans eagerly awaiting updates on where the likes of Dustin Johnson and Bubba Watson will be playing next. But it certainly doesn't hurt the DP World Tour to have a few more recognizable names in the mix in select events. It sure doesn't seem fair that some who stayed loyal may lose their spots to more famous players, but if life were fair, school teachers would get paid something closer to what these guys make for playing golf.

Schrock: It's a big win for the DP World Tour that these guys have to play in non-Rolex events. That will be huge for their fields, especially at a time when Rory, Tommy and others are plying their trade on the PGA Tour. It's not fair to guys who stayed loyal but in the end, that's how life works, as Sens noted. I'm more interested to watch a bunch of these guys battle for what Rory said would only be a few spots into the Championship Series in 2028. How many guys are going to go for Round 2 on the DPWT if they don't make it back to the top circuit in 2027?

Berhow: It's great for the DP World Tour, which will have its most star-studded roster in decades, but there's obviously a domino effect, and some rank-and-file players will now have to battle Bryson and Hatton for PGA Tour cards next season. I'm sure they aren't thrilled but life isn't fair. Just play better golf than the other guys and it takes care of itself. But for now, it's going to be a lot of fun tracking some of these DP World Tour events in the early morning hours in the States and will be fantastic theater as we see which players can earn PGA Tour cards a year from now.

Tiger Woods, who hasn't played competitive golf since the 2024 Open Championship, announced he'd join the 20-player field for his Hero World Challenge in the Bahamas that begins Dec. 3. Are you surprised he's playing? And what hints does this give you about his 2027 playing schedule?

Sens: I wasn't counting on this after all he's been through recently. But there's no more sensible way for him to dip his toes back into competition than this. Given the major venues on the docket this year — not just Augusta, but St. Andrews and Pebble — you can only imagine how much Woods is chomping at the bit. I can only assume we'll see him in Georgia this spring. If he can walk four rounds there, he can walk them anywhere.

Schrock: I'm not surprised he's going to make his competitive re-entrance at his event, with a limited, no-cut field. I don't think it tells us much about his schedule other than he wants to play and try to contend at this group of major venues. I've long said this year, PGA Frisco notwithstanding, would be a good time for Woods to take his final competitive lap and wave goodbye and head to the PGA Tour Champions. Of course, he's not wired like that.

Berhow: It makes sense, and we could see the writing on the wall. But Albany isn't a tough walk — Augusta is. He's got this and the PNC Championship (if he decides to play) for some spring training reps and then it would be great to see him at Augusta. But Tiger, we have learned, can be unpredictable. Selfishly, whenever he is done, I'm hoping he gets a proper sendoff, for himself and the fans. But life doesn't always have those storybook endings. And, honestly, maybe we already got ours with Tiger — the 2019 Masters.

Jeeno Thitikul won the LPGA's season-ending CME Group Tour Championship for the third straight year to claim her fifth win of the season, giving her one more than Nelly Korda, who just missed out on securing a spot in the LPGA Hall of Fame on Sunday. How much did Korda's hot start to the season and Hall-of-Fame quest take away from Thitikul's memorable year?

Sens: For the casual fan, there's no doubt Korda stole some of the limelight. But this is also in keeping with a long history of terrific female players – and especially terrific non-American female players – not getting the attention they deserve. Good news is, Thitikul is such a great personality, with the talent to back it up, that that's gotta change. Right?

Schrock: Another brilliant season for Jeeno, who is still just 23 and the second-best player in the world. But Nelly is the LPGA's needle and the tour needs her to do what she did in order to try and draw more eyeballs and new fans who can get to know Jeeno and others and connect with them. Jeeno has world-class talent and an electric personality. She'll start getting more attention but Nelly stirs the drink and the LPGA needs that to keep happening.

Berhow: Jeeno snuck up on even the most educated LPGA fans since she won three of her last six starts, and that finish to the season is kind of what everyone expected Nelly to do en route to securing enough points to get into the Hall. The LPGA needs stars, and Nelly and Jeeno, the top-two players in the world, are good starting points. But rivalries are sometimes even better than stars. And those are harder to cultivate.

The PGA Tour's Future Competitions Committee announced its final sweep of radical changes to go into place in 2028 (you can read all about them here). Now that we have our most clear picture of what the future of the Tour looks like, did the Tour get it right? What do you like? What's left you scratching your head?

Sens: It's a good start in addressing some of the bigger issues. A simplified schedule, clarity of fields, and a relegation/elevation system that adds some extra juice. But it left me wondering: what's up with the Tour's charitable efforts, a major point of pride (with a lot of positive impacts) for so many years? Also, though I understand the thinking behind it, I don't love the fact that a big name from the top circuit (sorry, Championship Series) can't drop in to play a beloved local event. That was always fun — to see a needle-mover of a player add some star power to a smaller event with strong local support.

Schrock: Rolapp has the right idea. He comes from the NFL where there are clear stakes that drive narratives and build interest. The PGA Tour's postseason has been impossible to understand and the new system simplifies everything. It's good that they are doing away with sponsor invites and there is clear promotion/relegation that fans can understand instead of what we have now where someone loses their card but things are basically the same (i.e. Billy Horschel). There will need to be tweaks but pro golf needed a change and this is a great start.

Berhow: In theory, this should be so much better. For as much as we like to point out that the PGA Tour schedule and points system and playoffs are impossible to follow, it's a different sport than the NFL and NBA, etc., and it has its own challenges. I think the promotion and relegation has the ingredients for some fun fall golf, and match play to end the season could be a nice boost. But I think the real test comes when some fan favorites drop down to the Challenge Series. Does the Tour create some sort of pathway or exemption to keep them and sponsors happy? I would hope not.
