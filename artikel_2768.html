Eagles-Steelers Film Review: A very good defensive performance

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

I enjoyed watching the defense this week! The Eagles played with the speed and conviction that have been missing at times this season. They were without Jordan Davis (obviously) and Zack Baun, and they faced a Pittsburgh offense that had been excellent on the ground this year, yet the front held up and pretty much shut down the Steelers' running game. There was some really good defensive football here.

I think you have to be fair and say that this Steelers' offense made it easy for them. Their passing game barely threatened the Eagles downfield, and Aaron Rodgers looked incapable of escaping pressure once things broke down. Some of the seven sacks were excellent defensive plays. Others came with a lot of help from an offense that made life almost too easy. The Cowboys will test this defense in a very different way, so I am looking at this as a performance to enjoy rather than expecting a repeat this Thursday.

What interested me was how Vic Fangio handled the missing pieces. The Eagles used five-man fronts against heavier personnel, often with Jalen Carter, Moro Ojomo, and Byron Young together, and the linebackers extremely aggressively downfield. I also felt we saw more Cover 4 and Cover 8 than usual. I have been calling for more split-safety coverage, and I prefer this defense when we see more of this coverage than Cover 3.

I'll start with Quinyon Mitchell because this small play tells you plenty about the player. On third and short, the Steelers throw underneath, and Mitchell gets there to make the tackle before the receiver can pick up the first down. His coverage ability is obvious, but this is the part of cornerback play that's easy to overlook.

Mitchell is not the most imposing tackler in the league, and he doesn't need to be. He does need to take the assignment seriously, and he consistently does. Watching that rep after the discussion around Tariq Woolen's tackling made the difference particularly striking. Every good defense needs cornerbacks who are willing to tackle.

The two starting linebackers' play was my favorite part of the game. Jihaad Campbell looked decisive on this run, recognizing the gap and getting downhill immediately rather than waiting for the lineman to reach him. That is the version of Campbell I have wanted to see more often. Both Campbell and Jeremiah Trotter Jr. did that repeatedly.

This clip also shows the Eagles' response to playing without Davis. Carter, Ojomo, and Young give them a five-man look against heavier personnel and help define the run fits for the players behind them. Watch Carter's first step. It is absurdly quick. I liked the structure, but it's pretty clear Young doesn't have the size or anchor to survive as an everydown run defender. Dallas will get after him, and I hope he can hold up!

This is why you draft a linebacker in the 1st round. This is such a good play. It's Cover 6, and Campbell has a lot of grass to defend as the linebacker toward the top of the screen. Marcus Epps ends up helping on a vertical route from the other side, leaving Campbell with even more to manage underneath. Campbell stays aware of the route developing across his area and makes a very difficult coverage responsibility look comfortable. That combination of range and awareness is why I have remained patient with him. This is the skill set you hope to get when you use a 1st round pick on a modern linebacker.

Trotter is a different player, and he will never offer quite the same range in coverage. That doesn't mean he is helpless in space. He closes on the underneath throw here and makes a really good open-field tackle. I like the balance of these two together. The Eagles had Trotter in the middle, seeing the run develop, with Campbell able to line up closer to the line of scrimmage or handle more ground in coverage. It feels like a natural fit for what each does best.

The Eagles' run defense has had enough messy moments this year that I didn't expect to praise it this much without Davis and Baun. Carter and Ojomo deserve a lot of credit. They were disruptive at the point of attack and made it far easier for the linebackers to arrive with momentum. The Steelers' running backs managed just 13 yards on nine carries in the first half. The Steelers eventually found some success later on, but that early control shaped the game.

I also noticed a tweak to one of the fronts I complained about a few weeks ago. Previously, Campbell moving out to the edge sometimes left Jalyx Hunt with his hand in the dirt in an interior position that did not suit him. This time, the Eagles still used Campbell outside while keeping Hunt wider and upright. They retained the advantages of an extra player near the line without asking Hunt to live in that unfavorable alignment. That is a sensible adjustment, and it's something I always like about Vic Fangio. He is more than willing to adjust.

I have liked Trotter since before the draft, so seeing him get an opportunity and play like this has been a lot of fun. He sees the pulling guard, identifies where the ball is going, and fires into the C-gap to wreck the play. Trotter recognizes the picture earlier than most players and commits to it. You can have all the athletic ability in the world, but if you are late getting to the right place, it doesn't matter.

I understand the questions about his coverage ceiling. They are legitimate, and opponents will force the Eagles to think carefully about how they use him. But his run defense is too good to leave on the sideline on early downs moving forward. Trotter gives this front someone who sees what is going on, attacks the gap, and finishes the play. He is one of those guys that just makes so many tackles when he plays. I'm so happy for him.

The Steelers' backup left guard had a dreadful game, and we should say so when talking about the Eagles' pass rush. Some of the protection and offensive design made things far too easy. Still, defensive linemen have to take the opportunities in front of them. Young did that, and it was good to see him rewarded with sacks after a game in which the staff again trusted him to play meaningful snaps.

I have been critical of Young's work against the run, including earlier in this very article, and one good pass-rushing day doesn't mean I suddenly think he is great. He helped the Eagles get after Rodgers and played his part in a front that coped better than I expected without Davis. I just want to see how that front holds up when an offense is more committed to attacking its weak points.

The contrast at cornerback was hard to miss. Woolen has never been known as a strong tackler, but this rep goes beyond a player simply lacking that skill. The approach and the finish aren't good enough. When the opponent is content to keep throwing short, every missed opportunity to close down a receiver matters. The Eagles cannot defend the perimeter with one player opting out of that part of the job.

Fangio's decision to take Woolen off the field was a strong one, particularly given the investment in him this offseason. I respect it. A defense has to have standards that apply even to players with obvious physical talent. Mitchell's earlier rep showed what it looks like when a corner accepts the less glamorous work. It will be interesting to see how Woolen responds, because he is playing for a new contract with someone else next year.

Marcus Epps surprised me. I thought we would see this, but he has not been good this year. He read the game quickly, came downhill repeatedly, and played with real aggression. This clip captures the feel of the whole defensive performance. The Eagles trusted what they saw, arrived early, and finished hard. Sometimes we can get so caught up in coverage structures that we forget how much of good defense is simply recognizing the play and getting to the football. The fundamentals are so important.

I also like Epps in a simpler role as a boundary safety most of the time. It lets him play fast and gives him less coverage responsibility. That arrangement will need to withstand a more ambitious passing attack (tomorrow night!) before I call it a lasting solution, but Epps deserves credit for how well he played the assignment in this game. He gave the defense some physicality it needed.

I haven't included every sack because some felt almost too easy. For example, Nolan Smith just ran straight into Rodgers without being blocked. Rodgers was a statue in the pocket, and the Steelers' passing concepts offered nothing. This one is probably the easiest sack Gabe Hall will ever get. He comes free through the A-gap and finds the quarterback waiting for him. But it is his first career sack, and there was no way I was leaving it out!

That is also where I want to keep the performance in perspective. The Eagles' rush and coverage worked well enough to make this Steelers' offense look miserable, but they rarely had to defend a serious downfield threat. The Cowboys will give them a much bigger test.

This was a very good defensive performance and, honestly, a fun one to watch. Campbell and Trotter gave the Eagles an aggressive linebacker pairing, which I am excited about moving forward. Carter and Ojomo helped the five-man fronts survive without Davis, and Epps brought urgency to the back end. I don't think I even mentioned his coverage in this game, but Quinyon Mitchell was awesome once again.

Fangio also deserves credit for finding an adjustment to the Hunt alignment that bothered me before. I am pleased with what the Eagles showed, especially the speed and aggression with which they played, and very interested to see how much of it survives a far tougher opponent.
