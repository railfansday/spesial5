The Best Holiday Cocktails, According to Eater Editors

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Don’t let cocktails fall by the wayside in the flurry of holiday dinner prep — these drinks make the party

In the endless iterating that goes into devising a menu for a holiday dinner, there’s one finishing touch that really makes or breaks the vibe: the drinks. The wine and beer will, of course, flow — at these festive events, a bottle of Champagne or sparkling rose is likely to make an appearance — and it’s easy enough to throw together a gin and tonic or screwdriver. But nothing makes a holiday gathering feel chic, totally put together, gloriously intentional, like a batch cocktail, made-to-order martini, or gussied-up punch.

Now, we all have our preferences when it comes to strength, spirit, and format. The host’s bandwidth is also a consideration; do you want to make a lot of one thing, or tailor your options to your guests’ preferences? Those questions are for you to answer, but our staff is here to help by providing this rundown of six tried, vetted, and loved drinks to make at your own celebration.

Below, find Eater editor-approved recipes for cocktails that will add the most festive of feelings to every holiday gathering.

I host a holiday potluck with a core group of friends in December, and one of my favorite potential additions is whatever punch that drinks columnist M. Carrie Allan is cooking up over at the Washington Post. A favorite from 2022 was a meringue-topped citrus punch, featuring blood orange, Aperol, and a dramatic meringue topping. Said topping adds undeniable flair and drama to the presentation, but if you’re daunted by the idea, the punch itself is still a festive, not-too-sweet drink to serve, celebrating winter citrus. —Missy Frederick, editorial director, dining

Meaghan Dorman’s modern classic is a favorite year-round, but there’s something about the holidays that calls for Scotch. No shade to the nog freaks, but the subtly spiced sour is a pleasant counterpoint to the onslaught of holiday food and beverage maximalism. No need to break out the single malt either, your dusty bottle of Dewars or Monkey Shoulder will do just fine. —Allison Hamlin, director of network development

The French 75 — the effervescent classic cocktail made with gin, Champagne, and lemon — is already one of my easy-drinking favorites, and I like it even better in punch form, accented with an aesthetically pleasing ice block of cheery lemon slices. What makes it 77 instead of 75? The substitution of St-Germain (or any elderflower liqueur) for simple syrup, adding just a tiny touch of herbal complexity. It’s perfect for New Year’s Eve, but just as refreshing at Thanksgiving or any other holiday table where you want something light and zesty. —Hilary Pollack, deputy editor

As the de facto cook for Thanksgiving, I like to keep the drinks options simple so I can focus on food prep. This cocktail recipe requires only two ingredients, doesn’t require pre-batching, and is easy enough for people to pour on their own. I also like how it uses Basque cider, which is drier than the typical ciders, so it’s not too sweet while still tapping into that cozy fall feeling —Rayna Rossitto, editor, branded content

I grew up guzzling Broguiere’s eggnog, but I love the more tropical combination of rich, creamy coconut milk, rum, and cinnamon in coquito; the Puerto Rican holiday cocktail was first described to me as “eggnog, if it went on vacation,” and I haven’t looked back since. This version is by bartender Shannon Ponche, and gets extra dressed up in spice for the holidays with the addition of a homemade cinnamon simple syrup, a woody, nutty oloroso sherry, and a splash of spiced Braulio. —Francky Knapp, staff writer

There are two schools of thought on a post-dinner drink: You could go for a bracing digestif to “aid digestion,” or you could indulge in a liquid dessert that swaddles you in sugar, fat, and everything nice. I’m notoriously bad at decision making, so I tend to split the difference with an eggy flip tinged with something bitter. Together, a whole egg and syrup (in this case honey syrup) build a comforting base, while an entire 1.5 ounces of cocktail bitters (as opposed to the usual dash or two) ensure you don’t entirely fall asleep in your glass. Angostura is a great option, delivering some classic holiday spice, but any style of cocktail bitters would probably work, as would an amaro. Dry shaking (shaking the liquid ingredients without ice) is key to achieving a frothy texture, and don’t forget the half-ounce of whiskey, which punches up the rest of the flavors. —Nick Mancall-Bitel, editor

Several members of my husband’s family are Manhattan drinkers, so I expect that my decision to serve the Grandfather cocktail during Thanksgiving appetizers will be a hit. It’s a riff on the classic drink with a hint of fall, courtesy of the addition of Applejack, an apple brandy. It’s smooth and well-balanced. It’s also quite boozy, so I’ll probably look for a slightly lower-proof option to tide us over after that initial round. I’ve had success with a bourbon and ginger ale bar (with various sodas and mixers) in the past, as well as Smitten Kitchen’s fall sangria; maybe this year we’ll go with her (okay, still pretty boozy) apple cider Old Fashioneds. —Missy Frederick, cities director
