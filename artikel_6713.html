Pumpkin Chiffon Pie

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Classic pumpkin pie may be the most well-known pumpkin pie come the holidays, but let us make our case for its underappreciated, fancy cousin: Pumpkin Chiffon Pie. Impossibly light and fluffy, the filling is made by folding meringue into a pumpkin curd-esque mixture for a slice that truly is light as a feather. With this at your Thanksgiving table, we doubt anyone would miss the classic.

Before making the filling, we need to make and bake our pie crust. Start by preheating the oven to 375° and lightly greasing a 9” pie dish with cooking spray.

On a clean, lightly floured surface, roll out your pie dough into a 12” circle of even thickness. Carefully drape it over the prepared pie dish and gently press it into the bottom and edges, taking care not to stretch the dough and create weak points. Prick the bottom of the dough with a fork to prevent bubbling while baking, then trim any excess dough from the edges so you have about 1” of overhang. Tuck the overhang under itself and crimp the edges. Then, place the dough into the fridge to chill for 30 minutes (or, if you’re in a rush, into the freezer for 10 minutes).

Remove the chilled crust from the refrigerator and line it with parchment paper. Fill the pie dish with dried beans or pie weights and place it into the oven to blind bake for about 18 to 20 minutes. Once the edges are lightly golden, remove the parchment and weights and bake for 5 to 10 minutes more, until fully golden and cooked through. Remove the crust from the oven and place it in the fridge to cool for at least 2 hours.

Once the crust has chilled completely, place a medium saucepan on the stove and whisk together ½ cup sugar with the gelatin, pumpkin pie spice, and salt.

Then, in a medium bowl, whisk together the pumpkin puree, egg yolks, and milk until smooth, then pour the mixture into the saucepan with the sugar and gelatin mixture and whisk to combine.

Turn the burner under the saucepan on to medium heat and stir constantly until the mixture is hot and small bubbles are beginning to form around the edges. Remove the saucepan from the heat and transfer the mixture to a large bowl to cool to room temperature.

In a separate bowl, use a hand mixer to beat together the egg whites and cream of tartar until soft peaks form. Then, add the remaining ¼ cup sugar, one tablespoon at a time, continuing to beat until stiff peaks form.

Transfer ¼ of the whipped egg whites into the cooled pumpkin mixture and gently fold to combine. Then add the remaining egg whites and fold to combine again. Pour the mixture into the cooled pie crust and smooth the top before placing it into the refrigerator for at least 4 hours and up to overnight. Once it has cooled and set completely, top the pie with whipped cream and a sprinkle of pumpkin spice before slicing and serving.

A chiffon is typically made with a filling (often containing gelatin, like in this recipe) that's usually cooked over a double boiler before whipped cream gets folded in for a luxurious texture. The whole thing is poured into a pre-baked (like in this recipe) or no-bake crust and refrigerated.

If you want to save time (and counter space) by making your Thanksgiving dishes ahead of time, this is the pie for you. The entire pie requires chilling for at least four hours, up to overnight before serving, so you can easily make this the day before, then simply top with whipped cream when you're ready to serve. Leftovers can be stored covered in the refrigerator for up to 3 days.
