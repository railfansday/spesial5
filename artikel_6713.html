The 20 Most Iconic Colognes of All Time, According to GQ in 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The first notes of The Beach Boys’ God Only Knows are enough to understand you're listening to a masterpiece. Well, as a longtime fragrance editor, I get a similar feeling when I smell Guerlain’s iconic Vétiver scent (or, more recently, Penhaligon’s Halfeti). These fragrances smelled exceptional when they debuted, still do today, probably still will a hundred years from now. And while fragrance, like any art form, has its trends, the truly great scents (and songs) have a way of deflecting them and singing on.

All of this is why we wanted to take a moment to celebrate not only the colognes our editors are wearing today, but also the fragrances that have been enjoyed by millions of GQ readers over the years. To do so, I dug through the history of men’s fragrance to put together a list of some of the most iconic colognes of all time. Scents that defined an era, changed the course of perfumery, or smelled so good that folks never stopped wearing them.

Our criteria came down to three things: legacy, or how a fragrance helped shape the scent landscape; longevity, meaning it has remained relevant or recognizable; and wearability, because the best classics should (mostly) still smell great today, not just serve as nostalgic reminders of another time. You might love some more than others, but all have earned their place in the fragrance pantheon. Here are 20 of the most popular, influential, and enduring colognes ever made, and exactly why they’ve lasted.

Is there a more classic cologne than Acqua di Parma’s Colonia? Launched in 1916, this timeless citrus is the original Italian splash, and it would be criminal not to include it on a list of this caliber. That crisp, bright citrus opening is unmistakable, but there’s so much more going on beneath the surface as it dries down, with the supporting notes weaving in and out without ever stealing the show. Herbaceous hints of lavender and rosemary add herbal hues, while earthy vetiver and patchouli mingle with the smooth warmth of sandalwood as it settles on the skin. This is one of the great daily drivers, if not the very archetype of what an everyday cologne could or should be. It’s a 10/10 blend of citrus, aromatics, and woods, and more than a century on, still smells as sophisticated as ever.

I’ve written about Guerlain Vétiver so many times, you’d think I’d have run out of ways to sing its praises. But I could go forever. Few fragrances have done more to establish vetiver as a leading note, and since its 1959 debut, this masterpiece has remained every bit as relevant and wearable. It beautifully captures vetiver’s fresher, greener side, rather than leaning into its smoky, earthy character. There’s a crispness, with bright citrus, a touch of spice, and grassy vetiver balanced by subtle tobacco and woods, like the first cool, earthy dew on a spring morning. Elegant and confident, it oozes the kind of sophistication I imagine old-school Hollywood leading men appreciating, yet it wears just as well today, whether you're suited up or simply going about your day.

Ask me to name the most influential luxury fragrance of the 21st century, and there’s a good chance I’d say Aventus (notice, I said “luxury”). I’ve seen firsthand just how many guys made their first foray into high-end fragrance with this icon, and while I now often gravitate toward some of the deeper cuts in Creed’s storied catalog, every time I wear Aventus, the compliments roll in. Since its 2010 debut, it has inspired countless imitators and the inevitable fragrance fatigue that comes with being a phenomenon, yet I don’t think it’s lost an ounce of its appeal. To me, it's the ultimate wedding fragrance, a perfect blend of bright citrus and pineapple, aromatic freshness, and rich, smoky woods that’s refined without being fussy. The defining fragrance of the 2010s? I’d make that argument any day. Aventus will always have a place on my shelf.

No. 89 is one of the great British gentlemen’s fragrances, and few colognes capture Bond-like elegance quite like it. And it tracks: Introduced in 1951, No. 89 counted James Bond creator Ian Fleming among its famous admirers, while Cary Grant and Sir Alec Guinness were reportedly devotees as well. The scent itself is traditional in the best way, a classically composed fougère with crisp, almost barbershop-like citrus and lavender freshness, subtle florals, warming spice, and a smooth, woody base of vetiver and oakmoss. Aromatic, gently spicy, and impeccably polished, it’s the olfactory equivalent of a beautifully tailored Savile Row suit. More than seven decades on, No. 89 is proof that class and character never go out of style.

Back in the ’90s, CK One was everywhere. From Wall Street execs to flannel-wearing creatives; its intoxicating freshness just captured the zeitgeist. It was also one of the first mainstream fragrances marketed as unisex. (Almost an afterthought for fragrance fans today, it was a fairly novel concept then.) And while countless fragrances have followed in its footsteps, this citrusy, green, woodsy, lightly sweet profile still more than holds up. A surprising amount is going on here, too: a bright medley of citrus and tropical fruits, a floral heart with hints of spice, and a clean, musky base of cedar, sandalwood, and amber. It’s an icon that helped define a generation, and it works just as well day or night, on pretty much anyone. And perhaps most impressively, after all these years, CK One still smells unmistakably like CK One.

Much like his clothing, Ralph Lauren’s original Polo fragrance has left an indelible mark on American style. Launched in 1978, the unmistakable green-bottled cologne helped define what a classic masculine fragrance could smell like: sophisticated, confident, and unapologetically aromatic. And while nearly five decades have passed, there’s still nothing quite like it. That signature pine-heavy character is front and center, surrounded by a rich blend of herb and spices with bergamot adding brightness and leather lending rugged, earthy depth. It’s bold, woody, green, and distinctly old-school, but that’s precisely its charm. It’s undergone reformulations over the years, and its assertive character won’t appeal to everyone, but there’s no denying its place in the fragrance hall of fame. Some colognes come and go, but Polo isn’t going anywhere.

When I spotted that frosted bottle in my dad’s medicine cabinet and wore it for the first time, I had no idea that this scent would spark a lifelong fragrance obsession. Armani’s 1996 icon, Acqua di Giò, has been worn by millions, referenced endlessly, and spawned countless flankers, but the original remains one of those rare mass-appeal designer colognes that have stood the test of time. Why? Because it’s just so wearable. Long before the niche fragrance boom, when designer colognes dominated department store counters, Acqua di Giò caught on like wildfire, appealing to just about every kind of guy, whether for the office or date night. At its core is a beautifully nuanced blend of citrus, fresh marine accords, and soft florals, grounded by a musky amber base. On skin, it’s breezy, clean, and subtly woody, with an oceanic quality. If you’ve never rocked it or haven’t reached for it in years, do yourself a favor and revisit this classic.

Imitation is the highest form of flattery, and so many colognes still try to smell like Cool Water. Launched in 1988, it became a full-blown phenomenon in the ’90s, and for a generation of guys, it was practically a rite of passage. Along with CK One, it was non-negotiable during my formative years, and even then, before I’d developed my own distinct taste, I knew I didn’t want to smell like everyone else. But there was something about this intoxicating green aquatic I just couldn’t resist. Its blend of lavender, mint, rosemary, and neroli nodded to classic gentleman’s fougères, but Cool Water took that aromatic DNA somewhere new—a singular scent that’s herbal and richly marine, softened by a woody, musky warmth. While it might smell dated to some, there’s no denying its place among the most iconic fragrances of all time, and I’d argue it still smells amazing on the right dude, especially on a spring or summer day.

Terre d’Hermès is (probably) my favorite designer fragrance of all time. When Jean-Claude Ellena created it back in 2006, he delivered something entirely different from the crowd-pleasing designer colognes of the day. There’s an earthiness to it that I find irresistible, a marriage of citrus, dry woods, spice, and that unmistakable flinty, mineral character that gives it so much personality. It’s sophisticated without being stuffy, with a gorgeous, almost boozy warmth that makes it feel sultry and downright sexy. I particularly love wearing it in colder weather, when those warm, woody facets really come alive, but I can reach for it almost anytime. Plenty of designer colognes are simply pleasant, but Terre d’Hermès is distinctive and endlessly wearable.

Few colognes in modern history have generated as much conversation, inspired as many memes, or become quite as zeitgeist-y as Dior Sauvage. Love it or roll your eyes at its popularity, its influence on modern men’s perfumery is undeniable. Since its debut in 2015, Sauvage has become one of the world’s bestselling fragrances, at times even claiming the number-one spot. And it’s easy to see why. That unmistakable blend of citrus, spice, and all things ambery nice is crisp and fresh, with a warm, woody drydown that’s oh so smooth. It’s a crowd-pleaser in the truest sense, versatile enough to wear just about anywhere, though I think it really shines after dark. Sauvage has since spawned an entire family of flankers, but the original EDT started it all.

You've smelled Bleu de Chanel on plenty of guys. A beautifully composed citrus-spice-wood classic, it works just as well with a navy suit as it does with a white tee and jeans. Note: When Chanel's legendary perfumer Jacques Polge introduced it in 2010, he didn't just create another handsome, crowd-pleasing cologne; he helped define the modern blue fragrance, a fresh, clean, citrusy, and woody genre that's become a mainstay. Sixteen years on, the original remains one of the most approachable and endlessly versatile colognes ever. Its bright citrus opening gives way to an aromatic blend of cool mint, warming spices, and subtle florals, all grounded by a rich, woody base with touches of earthy vetiver, patchouli, and resinous warmth. There really is only one Bleu.

This was a tough call. Ask any scent head about Tom Ford's fragrances, and they’ll surely bring up Neroli Portofino and Tobacco Vanille, both arguably just as iconic (and good) as this one. But if you polled fans on the most influential Tom Ford fragrances, I think Oud Wood would edge out the competition. Launched in 2007, it helped introduce Oud to a broader Western audience, making the traditionally intense ingredient feel approachable. It’s elegant and beautifully blended, with a smoky, almost creamy heart of oud, sandalwood, and rosewood, accented by subtle spice and the warmth of vanilla and amber. It’s heady yet easy to wear, with an addictive richness that never wears thin.

I’ve worn pretty much every Le Labo under the sun, and while my personal favorites lean toward the eucalyptus and vetiver side of the house, there’s simply no telling the story of modern perfumery without Santal 33. Love it or find it utterly overplayed, you’ve smelled it. Since its 2011 debut, it’s wafted through elevators, the backseats of cabs, countless weddings and date nights, and just about everywhere in between. More importantly, it helped bring sandalwood into the mainstream, and became a reference point for an entire genre of warm, woody fragrances. But before everyone started rocking it, Santal 33 was, and still is, a brilliant composition: creamy sandalwood, dry cedar, cardamom spice, and leathery warmth. It’s a niche legend that has suffered from its own success, yes, but give it another ten years, and don’t be surprised if the very fragrance many got tired of smelling becomes cool all over again.

If you’re remotely a scent head, chances are you dig at least one, if not several, of Parfums de Marly’s fragrances (my personal favorite is the oud-praline magic that is Haltane). But if I had to sum up the house in one word, it would be regal. It does refined, layered elegance exceptionally well, and while some folks might champion Althaïr or Castley, to my nose, the fougère-forward Layton best epitomizes the house. Launched in 2016, it’s one of the finest fougères I’ve ever smelled, rich yet airy, with classic lavender reimagined alongside crisp apple, bright citrus, cardamom, woods, and a healthy dose of vanilla. It’s practically the template for what a modern fougère aspires to be, and its enduring popularity suggests plenty of fragrance fans would agree. It doesn’t hurt that the matte blue bottle with its signature silver dome is so flauntworthy.

Few fragrances in recent times have had the same cultural impact as Baccarat Rouge 540, becoming a signature scent for everyone from socialites to fragrance obsessives. Yes, it's polarizing, and some might argue its ubiquity has stripped it of some of its magic. But it's become a modern classic for a reason, starting with the masterful nose behind it: Francis Kurkdjian, founder of his namesake maison. Launched in 2015, it's a luminous floral amber, with saffron lending spice, jasmine bringing a soft warmth, and a generous dose of amberwood creating that glowing aura. Sultry, heady, and diffusive, it's a straight-up unisex phenomenon that smells a little different on everyone who wears it, yet still announces itself before you even enter a room. Whether you're a devotee or a detractor, there's no denying it's left its mark.

It came down to Bal d’Afrique and Gypsy Water, though plenty of Byredo fragrances could have earned a spot here. But I’m the editor, and I’m going with my all-time favorite from one of the houses that helped bring niche perfumery into the mainstream. Launched in 2008, Gypsy Water was one of the first niche fragrances I ever fell for, and it holds such a special place in my heart that I still wear it regularly. There’s something almost holy about its airy blend of incense, soft woods, bright lemon, and a whisper of vanilla and amber, all coming together with that subtle yet magnetic, almost skin-scent quality Byredo does so well. Artsy, chic, and easy to love, this woody aromatic never tries too hard to make an impression yet often leaves a lasting one.

Since its 2015 debut, Xerjoff’s Naxos has achieved near-mythic status, winning over everyone from fragrance-obsessed TikTokers to yacht-owning gentlemen with decidedly expensive taste. And I get it. This is an almost impossibly well-blended composition that somehow manages to be both decadent and incredibly wearable, making it a great place to start if you’re looking to venture into niche perfumery. On paper, the combination of tobacco, aromatic lavender, fruity freshness, and a prominent honey accord sounds like it shouldn’t quite work, but man, does it ever. That rich sweetness is a defining feature, especially in the opening, before the herbal, earthy tones and a hefty dose of vanilla come together in a downright sumptuous yet crisp way. The hype may have quieted down since its social media heyday, but Naxos deserves its flowers as a modern niche standout.

Halfeti has become a signature of mine, though I’m admittedly a little late to the game, having received my first bottle as a birthday gift last year, well after it had achieved iconic status in scent circles. But better late than never, especially when a fragrance is this good. Named after a Turkish town known for its roses, it leans into a rich, heady floral style, layered with citrus, herbs, and spice. Bergamot and grapefruit lift the opening before saffron and cardamom give way to a deep base of leather, oud, cedar, sandalwood, and amber. It’s a lot, but it all hangs together gorgeously, creating a woody, spicy, aromatic scent that’s elegant and sexy, perfect for special occasions or date nights. But I’d happily wear it just about anywhere, including when I’m not going anywhere at all.

Musc Ravageur embodies the risk-taking spirit of niche perfumery. Launched in 2000, it helped redefine what a modern musk fragrance could be at a time when fresh, clean fragrances dominated the men’s market. More than a quarter century later, it remains a cult favorite, thanks in no small part to its warm, almost funky (in a good way) richness. It opens with herbal lavender, spicy coriander, and a hint of bright citrus before melting into clove and rose, all resting on a base of musk, vanilla, tonka, amber, and patchouli. A benchmark for animalic musks, it’s an alluring, sultry, and unapologetically sensual aroma, equally suited to being a signature scent or an olfactory companion for wherever the evening leads.

Amouage has dropped so many olfactory bangers over the years that choosing just one for this list wasn't easy. The Omani house has built its reputation on rich, complex compositions that keep you coming back to sniff your pulse points, and while I could make a case for several of its fragrances, Reflection Man feels quintessentially Amouage. Launched in 2007, it’s become a luxury niche benchmark, proving that a floral-forward fragrance can be every bit as rich and sophisticated as its woody or spicy counterparts. It opens with an herbaceous blend of rosemary, berries, and orange before orris, jasmine, and ylang-ylang bring a lush, earthy depth, all grounded by a smooth base of vetiver, patchouli, and sandalwood. The result is a layered floral-woody scent that’s distinctive without being difficult to wear, and versatile enough for everything from a boardroom to a black-tie gala. Pricey, yes, but worth every spritz.
