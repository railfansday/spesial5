Blackburn Rovers defender has had an excellent breakout year – but patience is required: Opinion

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Blackburn Rovers have had a largely frustrating 2026, but there has been reason to believe. The return of Tony Mowbray has seen the club build on Michael O’Neill’s work and pushed the club up into mid-table.

Another huge positive over the last year has been the emergence of Tom Atcheson. The 20-year-old becoming one of Rovers’ most encouraging academy success stories, with his development over the past 12 months earning him recognition on the international stage.

His form has also attracted attention from several top clubs. Atcheson was the subject of enquiries from Manchester City, Liverpool, Chelsea, Newcastle United, Sunderland and Crystal Palace in the summer window.

The 20-year-old has progressed from a promising youngster to a regular Championship starter and an increasingly important member of Northern Ireland’s defence. His senior breakthrough has not simply been a reward for potential, either.

Consistent performances at club level have given O’Neill plenty of reasons to trust him, with Atcheson starting all four of Northern Ireland’s Nations League fixtures in September and October, helping them record a run of clean sheets.

That is a significant achievement for a player who only made his senior international debut in March. Having joined Blackburn at under-12 level, Atcheson enjoyed a loan spell with Marine before making his Championship debut against Millwall in December 2025.

14 league appearances followed last season, and he has since established himself as a regular under Tony Mowbray.

Atcheson’s underlying numbers this campaign suggest there is plenty to like, although they also highlight why expectations should remain measured. His average FotMob rating of 6.54 also indicates that, despite his impressive international progress, he has not consistently been among the Championship’s standout defenders.

There have been occasional reminders of his inexperience, too. His first international start against Guinea in June ended with a goal and a red card, while a difficult 45 minutes against Swansea last month preceded a minor groin problem that forced him to miss the subsequent Stoke fixture.

Nevertheless, Blackburn have every reason to be encouraged. Atcheson has already demonstrated that he can translate Championship reliability into international performances, and his physical profile makes him an appealing long-term prospect.

The Premier League comparisons may be premature, but his trajectory is undeniably positive. Another six to 12 months of consistent performances could take his reputation, and his market value, to another level.
