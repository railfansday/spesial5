Mendocino, a small coastal town in northern California, has welcomed its first points property. The nearly 50-year-old Hill House hotel was renovated and reopened in June 2026 as Hill House at Trailborn Mendocino, Outdoor Collection by Marriott Bonvoy — making it the first and only member of a major hotel loyalty program in Mendocino proper. (The closest options are members of IHG One Rewards and Wyndham Rewards in neighboring Fort Bragg.)

The Mendocino Coast region is known for its charming small-town vibes, natural beauty and outdoor pursuits, so the Trailborn brand’s laidback nature lodge concept fits in here like a seal in nearby Mendocino Bay. I stayed three nights in late October to get a first look at the property (and the town, which is on TPG’s 2027 Where to Go list).

Nestled just a few minutes’ walk outside downtown Mendocino, Hill House at Trailborn Mendocino’s unassuming two-story buildings fit right in with the surrounding hotel scene. The design is inspired by nature — specifically, the redwood forests and tide pools of the surrounding Mendocino Coast — while still feeling modern and polished.

The grounds are bursting with native plants, many of them still in bloom when I visited in late October. Botanical art prints and zoological illustrations on the walls give a nod to the great outdoors, and curious guests can lose themselves in the lobby’s curiosity cabinet, a revamped relic of the original building. Visitors are encouraged to sign the guestbook with a stamp of a dried leaf or flower.

The hotel offers just 44 accommodations — relatively large for a Mendocino property — including 14 double queen rooms, 25 king rooms and 5 junior suites. Within each room type, travelers can choose to upgrade to features such as a balcony or deck, an ocean view or both.

My room, a spacious King Junior Suite Ocean View, felt like a relaxing retreat with its muted color palette of greens, browns and yellows and its cozy linen spa robes. After a day of exploring Mendocino, I enjoyed relaxing on the sofa, perusing the coffee table book Botanicum, and sampling locally made goodies from the snack basket and minibar (for a fee).

Trailborn Hill House has one on-site restaurant: Pizza Standard Time, a casual bar and diner offering mostly pizza, plus salads, charcuterie boards and small Italian bites like arancini. The fare was limited but tasty; my friend and I tried a pizza, salad, and local Husch chardonnay and were quite happy. It’s nice having a quick, casual meal available on-site in case you arrive late and tired from the drive, but it’s also just a few minutes’ walk to Mendocino if you want more options.

Like all Trailborn properties, this one offers a lobby convenience store called Hill House Wine & Grocer. Guests can purchase local drinks and snacks (mostly the kind you’d find on a charcuterie board), plus apparel, toys, candles with the property’s signature scent and other souvenirs.

The hotel also offered a small but delicious complimentary breakfast spread. As with everything else during my stay, the breakfast was high-quality yet unpretentious, with guests serving and bussing their own tables. Instead of having a designated breakfast area, guests can find a cozy spot to eat inside Pizza Standard Time or the attached Cabot Cove Detective Library, outside by one of the fire pits or back to their room.

However, this will change when Trailborn’s second Mendocino property, The Mendocino Hotel, opens in 2027. Instead of complimentary breakfast, Hill House plans to provide guests with a voucher to Moonwave, The Mendocino Hotel’s dining concept, which will serve “coastal comfort” food three meals a day plus drinks at the lobby bar.

If you didn’t realize you were in a small town, you will as soon as you interact with any of the hotel staff — everyone I spoke to, from front desk staff to custodians, was beyond friendly.

While Mendocino’s remoteness makes it a haven for crowd-weary travelers, it’s becoming easier than ever to reach thanks to recent roadwork funding and new direct flights to Charles M. Schulz–Sonoma County Airport (STS), including on Alaska Airlines, Delta and Southwest. That’s one reason Mendo made TPG’s 2027 Where to Go list.

But travelers can also fly into San Francisco International Airport (SFO), Oakland San Francisco Bay Airport (OAK) or Sacramento International Airport (SAC), which are a bit farther away but offer more flight options.

Depending on the airport you choose, you’ll need to rent a car and drive two to four hours to Mendocino.

Basic rooms start as low as $140 per night in the off-season, while my King Junior Suite Ocean View starts around $350 per night (though these prices can climb much higher on summer weekends). Award rates start at 48,000 Marriott Bonvoy points per night, or you could redeem a 50,000-point free night certificate.

Hill House has three wheelchair-accessible rooms, including two double queens and one king; these feature accessible tubs and, in some cases, roll-in showers. The property and its parking lot are also wheelchair accessible. For hearing-impaired guests, a portable kit can be installed in any room.

As a points traveler who loves Mendocino, I’m thrilled to see the town’s first points property not only opening but thriving. As a traveler who appreciates unique stays and has experienced some of the town’s charming independent properties, I’m also glad that Hill House at Trailborn Mendocino has brought enough character to fit seamlessly into the local scene.

It seems the Trailborn brand — and Marriott Bonvoy — is betting big on Mendocino. Hill House is one of two Mendocino properties purchased by The Trailborn brand. The second, the 150-year-old Mendocino Hotel, is undergoing renovation and slated to open in 2027.
