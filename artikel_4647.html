Are Your Dating Standards Too High?

Here's something that you never see on social media: men accusing women of having dating standards that are too high. Yep, that's never happened. Neither has women accusing men of the same. In fact, no one of any gender has ever accused anyone else of such things, right?

Umm, not exactly. Anytime someone posts something about having certain dating requirements—unless it's something irrefutable like "I would like someone human"—it's not too surprising to see responses like "you are being unrealistic," "you are too high-maintenance," "no wonder you are single," or "look at yourself." Then the person may fire back with something like "Well, sorry for having standards," and then back and forth it goes.

The question then is where you fall on the whole "got to have some kind of standards" to "being unrealistic" spectrum. That ranges from people who clearly should lower their standards—because a supermodel may not want to date a guy who spends all his days in pajamas posting about red pills on Reddit—to those who actually should raise their standards. Well, here are some signs that your standards may be too high:

If your standards focus on superficial things like looks, then it could be impeding your looking for a partner. For example, a 2020 survey by Pew Research Center found that 15% of respondents “Definitely/probably would not” date someone of a different race/ethnicity—which could end up ruling out lots of people who could be great fits from personality and values standpoints. The same might happen when you base your standards around hair (beyond asking that it be washed occasionally), clothes (with the possible exception of ruling out those who wear a hazmat suit all the time), and other stuff that in the long run won't matter that much for a relationship.

Many superficial things like looks, hobbies, and fame can change with time—as could your perception and feelings about them. So, be careful about anchoring your preferences on something that may be fleeting versus enduring—especially since the enduring stuff is what often becomes most endearing.

Beware of sticking rigidly to any rules such as the so-called "6-6-6 rule" that I detailed before for "A Funny Bone to Pick." If you are completely unwilling to consider anyone who's a bit under any of the sixes—height, salary, or abs—ask yourself why. Otherwise, you could end up like the guy I knew who specified that his wife needed to look a certain way, speak at least five languages, and have certain types of degrees, but kind of neglected putting "kind" and "loyal" on that list. Guess what ended up happening?

The "6-6-6 rule" is just one example of a standard that you may have heard from elsewhere and then adopted it without really thinking why. Using standards set by others may make others—like fake friends, advertisers, politicians, and Hollywood execs—happy. But what about you?

Of course, don't summarily reject all suggestions from others—especially when those who are close to and care about you tell you repeatedly that your standards are too high. That includes your therapist, whom you are presumably paying to care about you.

Another potential sign is when dates for you have been about as rare as Halley's Comet sightings. Sure, there may be legit reasons for your dry spell—such as a lack of singles or diversity around you. But what exactly is keeping you from dating the single Halley, who's already expressed interest in you?

A flip-side of this sign is treating dating like casting calls for the next James Bond or Wonder Woman—where you go through a ton of first looks but very, very few second looks. You may believe that dating is a numbers game in some ways. But too exacting standards may be wasting a number of people's time.

Speaking of numbers, how about those social media posts that use census data to calculate the actual chances of meeting someone based on your dating standards? Or calculators like the one from Keeper? These may be rough calculations, but they can show how rough things may be for you—especially when your probabilities of success dip below 1%.

It's a bad sign if you've never, ever seen anywhere on Earth anything that comes close to what your standards indicate. And someone from Middle Earth, a persona portrayed by some celebrity, or any other fantasy character doesn't count. Reality is a lot messier. So, use the successful pairings around you, instead, to help benchmark what's possible.

Remember, too, that your standards should be based in part on what you currently offer in return. If you yourself aren't loyal and haven't yet gone through an emotional healing journey, then, maybe, just maybe, you can't expect the same out of others. And remember each demand of yours demands more trade-offs from you. If, for example, you want someone driven to achieve boatloads of money and high social status, expect to play second fiddle to that person—or maybe even third or fourth fiddle.

Finally, check how well your standards match what truly would be a match for you. Will your criteria help or hurt your chances of finding someone with a personality and core values that fit well with yours? If, for example, a primary interest of yours is finding someone who will be very loyal, then maybe being super rich and having a hot, hot, hot body shouldn't be such absolute criteria. You could remove at least one of the "hots" from there.

Ultimately, it is important to have standards. But they do have to stand—stand the test of time and really stand for who you are. If not, you might end up with someone you simply cannot stand.
