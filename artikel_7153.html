The Art of Using AI as ‘Power Steering,’ Not Autopilot ft. Kathy Collins

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Moving from CMO to Chief Revenue Officer changes the questions you ask (and which numbers keep you up at night).

In this episode of Marketing Vanguard, Jenny Rooney sits down with Kathy Collins, Senior Vice President and Chief Revenue Officer at Frontdoor, to unpack what happens when a career built in marketing expands into sales, finance and full revenue accountability.

Kathy shares how cross-category experience sharpened her ability to find the right audience and make unfamiliar products relevant, why she became “obsessed with numbers” and what leading sales taught her about communication.

Plus, her candid thoughts on why curiosity matters more than comfort.

Kathy Collins is Senior Vice President and Chief Revenue Officer at Frontdoor, where she started as Chief Marketing Officer. Today, her role brings marketing, sales and revenue thinking much closer together.

Her career has spanned several major consumer brands and categories, including Lee Jeans, H&R Block and Massage Envy, before she joined Frontdoor.

That current broader scope of her work has also changed how she approaches marketing, from a stronger focus on unit economics and financial accountability to translating strategy across sales, finance and analytics teams.

08:33 From CMO to CRO: What Changes When You Start Owning the Numbers? — Kathy’s move into revenue leadership changed how she looks at the business. She now works more closely with the CFO and analytics teams, pays closer attention to what can be dialed up or down and thinks much more deliberately about the top line, bottom line and everything in between. Her unexpected takeaway? She has become “a little obsessed with numbers.”

18:42 If Sales Doesn’t Understand the Question, Ask It Differently — Leading a sales organization forced Kathy to rethink how she communicates. What sounds perfectly clear in marketing language may not land with sales at all. Her solution is simple: reframe the question through the listener’s lens. Small changes in language can create much better alignment across teams.

20:15 How “Edutainment” Breaks a Sea of Sameness — Home warranties require explanation, but explanation alone is easy to ignore, which is why Frontdoor’s answer is “edutainment,” combining category education with humor through its work with Rachel Dratch. Kathy says the approach has helped American Home Shield differentiate itself while improving awareness, demand and conversion.

23:00 Your Audience Assumptions Can Expire Faster Than You Think — Frontdoor has historically wanted to reach younger homeowners, but rising home prices and changing buying patterns complicate that assumption. Kathy’s point for marketers is broader: consumer segments move. The job is to keep checking the data, challenge inherited audience assumptions and pivot when real behavior changes.
