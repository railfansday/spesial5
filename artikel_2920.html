UCF becomes bowl-eligible with Josh Derry hat trick, fat guy TD vs. Iowa State

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

ORLANDO — Thanks to a hat trick of touchdown receptions by senior standout Josh Derry, UCF is going bowling in 2026.

Derry torched Iowa State's secondary for 125 yards and three scores on four catches as the host Knights cruised to a 48-17 victory at Acrisure Bounce House, closing the home campaign on a high note. The Knights (6-5, 4-4) set a single-season high mark for conference wins since joining the Big 12 ahead of next weekend's trip to Colorado.

Alonza Barnett III completed 17 of 27 attempts for 255 yards, and added 43 yards and another touchdown on the ground. The Knights forced four Iowa State turnovers, though Aiden Flora — the nation's leading rusher — gained 171 yards with a couple of touchdowns.

"We played up to our standard. We played up to the amount of talent that we knew we had," Barnett said. "That's part of the ebbs and flows of the season. It's cliché, but we're better than a 6-5 team. We have way too much talent in all three phases — guys that care, guys that love each other, guys that fight.

"When we're at our best, we really believe we're one of the top teams in the country. I believe we showcased how explosive we can be in all three phases when we're sharp and clicking."

UCF last reached a bowl game in 2023, going 9-15 the previous two years — with just four Big 12 wins combined.

"I told the guys I don't want to celebrate this too much," head coach Scott Frost said. "I think they had high expectations for the year. We've been close a lot of times; won some close ones, lost some close ones. We've got another game left, and we can do a diagnosis after that."

Arguably UCF's most successful pull from the transfer portal this past offseason, Derry is developing into one of the Big 12's most dangerous weapons.

The 5-foot-9, 175-pound receiver broke an early 3-3 deadlock by winning one-on-one on third-and-13. Derry peeled away from Beni Ngoyi on a corner route and hauled in Barnett's 21-yard lob. He doubled UCF's lead less than five minutes later on another third-down conversion, outrunning David Coffey across the field and down the left sideline for a 65-yard strike.

Derry ran free up the seam to close an impressive nine-play, 75-yard drive out of halftime with a 24-yard touchdown. In so doing, he became the first Knights receiver with a three-TD day since Kobe Hudson during a 45-3 rout of Oklahoma State in 2023.

"I've been waiting for this opportunity my whole life," said Derry, who dominated the Football Championship Subdivision the past two years at Monmouth University in New Jersey. "I've been underlooked forever. To have the opportunity to be on a great team with great staff and great players, to be comfortable and to play my best ball, it's everything to me."

According to ESPN Insights, Derry is the shortest Big 12 player to record 125 receiving yards and three touchdowns in a game since TCU's Kavontae Turpin had 138 with four scores against Texas in 2015.

Since the start of November, Derry has 19 receptions for 405 yards and four touchdowns.

Motivated by the head-to-head matchup with Flora, Duke Watson returned with a vengeance after missing the Arizona State game with a foot injury.

Watson had 16 carries for 113 yards, topping the century mark for the first time since Sept. 19.

"We definitely challenged ourselves. He can't leave with more yards than me. Or, if he (does), they're going to be hard yards," Watson said.

Frost highlighted one of Watson's carries as the biggest non-turnover play of the evening. On first down from UCF's 45-yard line, Watson changed direction on a seemingly doomed toss play and turned a three-yard loss into a nine-yard gain.

Five plays later, Barnett found Derry wide open for their third touchdown, restoring a 21-point lead.

"That was a huge play, and that's just individual effort," Frost said. "The line blocked well for him, the tight ends blocked well for him, the receivers sold out. And we've got some talented guys in the backfield that are home run threats. It's good to have (Watson) back, and like I said, I think this is the healthiest we've been in a long time, so that made a difference."

UCF has routinely struggled to establish the run against Power Four defensive fronts, but the Knights pushed around Iowa State and capitalized on its significant speed advantage on the perimeter.

Entering the night, UCF averaged a paltry 98 rushing yards per game in Big 12 play — second-worst only to TCU (77.6). Factoring in Barnett's scrambling, Taevion Swint's change-of-pace contributions and Duane Thomas Jr.'s 2-yard touchdown scamper early in the fourth, the Knights churned out 205 yards on the night.

UCF capped a dominant second half with a visually satisfying final score.

Redshirt freshman center Jacob Maiava, deployed as an H-back several times in short-yardage packages, uncovered in the flat for a 5-yard touchdown — his first career reception, wearing an unfamiliar No. 48 jersey.

Derry awarded Maiava a "perfect" 10 for his receiving form, adding, "Being able to see that on the sideline, it's insane."

Frost considered it a special moment not only for Hawaiian-born Maiava but for the entire offensive line, which took its lumps in previous home losses to Baylor and Arizona State.

"It's a special day when a fat guy gets a touchdown," Frost said.
