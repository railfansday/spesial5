Albanese signs on to Trump’s AI supply chain pact before leaders’ meeting at Quad summit

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Quad leaders’ summit in Philippines is Trump’s first, as Australia joins US and Japan to protect AI technologies in face of China’s efforts to dominate

Anthony Albanese will attempt to walk a tightrope in his relationship with Donald Trump when the two leaders meet on Tuesday, as Australia signed on to a US-led pact to secure the technologies ​and critical infrastructure for artificial intelligence supply chains.

The Quad leaders’ summit in the Philippines’ capital of Manila will be Trump’s first, in either of his presidencies. The US president, fresh from a crushing midterms result, will take centre stage in gatherings of Asian leaders and prepare for historic three-way talks with Xi Jinping of China and Russia’s Vladimir Putin this week.

Underlying the diplomatic swing are efforts by the federal government to avoid angering China as the Quad leaders gather for the first time since 2024 – and after Australia joined countries, including the US and Japan, in an agreement for new protections around key AI technologies.

Designed to firm up supply chains for chips and critical minerals components, the San Francisco Declaration was crafted to promote cooperation in the face of China’s efforts to control areas of AI development.

Part of the Pax Silica international accord, officials said the agreement would help ensure Trump worked with partners to set standards in AI, rather than relying on others for leadership.

The declaration vows to protect sensitive technologies – “including goods, software, materials, knowledge, components and inputs used to produce them, and their means of production essential to [AI] supply chains”.

It also cites critical infrastructure – “the ports, terminals, transport, communications, and logistics networks, the ICT and energy infrastructure that their operations depend on, as well as the digital platforms that facilitate their coordination and management”.

Signatories include the UK, India, Israel, South Korea and Singapore.

Albanese told the Asean-Australia summit on Monday that the region needed friendship, mutual trust and optimism to prosper.

“At a time of global instability, the cooperation we demonstrate here will help us to confront the challenges we share at home and in our region,” he said.

Federal government sources told Guardian Australia the intense international efforts, including Albanese’s attendance at the Apec leaders’ meeting in Shenzhen, China, at the end of the week, was about maintaining engagement with Trump in the final two years of his presidency and stopping the US from withdrawing from global leadership.

Asia Society senior fellow Premesha Saha said the Quad talks would focus on critical minerals, maritime security and energy security.

“For Australia, keeping the United States engaged in the Indo-Pacific is a core strategic interest, and the Quad is one of the few settings where the president is tied into a regional agenda alongside three close partners,” Saha said.

“After a year in which Washington has been engaged elsewhere and has opened its own channel with Beijing, getting Trump to a Quad leaders’ meeting in the region matters to Australia.”

Saha said Albanese’s objectives included keeping Washington engaged in the region and building a Quad capable of delivering results, even during shifts in US policy and interest.

“Beijing will criticise it, as it always does, and will cast it as bloc politics. But I will be cautious about overstating how much this will anger China,” she said.

“The Quad isn’t a military alliance and Washington has gone out of its way to say it isn’t an anti-China coalition.”

Jennifer Parker, a non-resident fellow at the Lowy Institute, said criticism of Albanese’s involvement in the annual international summit season was shortsighted.

“Our relationships in the region are essential to our security in an increasingly chaotic world, and our security is the backbone of our national prosperity,” she said.

“It is important for Australia to be seen on the international stage and advocate for our national interests.”

Parker disagreed that Australia was working to keep the US engaged in the Asia Pacific region.

“The US has enduring interests here, both economically and strategically. But sometimes it pays for Australia to remind them of that,” she said.

Albanese announced a new Asean-Australia digital economy program on Monday to support AI safety and intelligence standards and digital trade standards.

He said humans must remain in control of AI, shaping the technology “rather than let it shape us”.

Albanese also held one-on-one talks with the Philippines president, Ferdinand Marcos Jr.

He told his Laos counterpart, Saleumxay Kommasith, that Australia remained very concerned about the deaths of backpackers Bianca Jones and Holly Morton-Bowles, who died after drinking methanol-laced alcohol at a hostel in Vientiane in 2024.

Albanese said Australia remained concerned about the seriousness of charges over the deaths. Investigators said they did not have enough evidence to do more because autopsies were not carried out.

Albanese called the case a “tragedy” and said it remained a serious issue in Australia.

Kommasith said the Laos government had attached importance to the case and officials were “trying our best to put an end to this by bringing those perpetrators into justice”.
