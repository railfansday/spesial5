‘The Rings of Power’ Just Dropped a Direct Callback to Peter Jackson’s Trilogy in 3 Minutes

Editor's note: The below contains spoilers for The Rings of Power Season 3.

It's been a long wait, but The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power is finally back, bigger and better, with Season 3. There are many great storylines in the four-episode premiere, although none as interesting as the journey Galadriel (Morfydd Clark), Arondir (Ismael Cruz Córdova), and Marnûkh (Adam Young) embark on toward the fortress of Gundabad in the North. The unlikely trio faces many dangers along the way, chief among them a close encounter in Episode 3, "The Secret Fire," in a scene that is a direct callback to an iconic (and terrifying) moment in Peter Jackson's The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring.

The Rings of Power's introduction of the Nazgnagôl is one of Season 3's most compelling elements; they are technically an early version of the Nazgûl since the One Ring hasn't been forged yet, and they still haven't been consumed by its will. They are already fearsome and powerful, though, and have sharp tracking skills, as they nearly catch Galadriel, Arondir, and Marnûkh in a clearing in the woods just as they almost catch Frodo (Elijah Wood), Sam (Sean Astin), Merry (Dominic Monaghan), and Pippin (Billy Boyd) in the Shire millennia later in The Fellowship of the Ring.

Both scenes align almost perfectly, with clear visual and narrative callbacks, from the Black Riders' sudden arrival to how the heroes manage to sneak out. Galadriel is warned of their presence by her own ring of power, just as Frodo feels it through the One Ring. In both, the heroes hide among the vegetation, with Arondir and Frodo coming dangerously close to revealing their locations and being stopped by Galadriel and Sam, respectively. They eventually distract the Riders and are able to sneak out — Galadriel invokes a swarm of butterflies, and Merry throws a decoy bag to lead the Nazgûl away.

Córdova shares how personal loss and close on-set bonds shaped the culmination of Arondir's Season 3 storyline.

The obvious difference is that, in The Rings of Power, the moment features two Black Riders, while, in the movie, only one comes close to discovering the hiding Hobbits. This actually says much about who the Nazgnagôl are right now; they're still able to recognize and be swayed by beauty. The Nazgûl, meanwhile, feel nothing but the pull of the One Ring. But it shouldn't take much longer for the Nazgnagôl to get to that stage.

The Rings of Power Season 3's four-episode premiere has many such nods to J.R.R. Tolkien lore, but, curiously, the scene when Galadriel, Arondir, and Marnûkh hide from the Nazgnagôl in the clearing in the woods isn't one of them. That's because the equivalent moment in the movies doesn't originate from the source material. There is a similar moment in the books, but the concept of the Hobbits huddling under a tree root comes from Ralph Bakshi, with Jackson giving it his own spin in his movie trilogy.

Before The Lord of the Rings, Jackson was largely known for his work in horror, with movies like Braindead and The Frighteners becoming cult classics, and those origins clearly inform Fellowship of the Ring's Nazgûl scene. It's the proper introduction of the Black Riders, and one of the most chilling moments in the movie. Jackson keeps the monster from us for as long as possible, and raises the tension by focusing on Frodo and the Ring instead of the Nazgûl — with the Ring being a monster in itself, too, in a way.

The Rings of Power approaches its own Nazgnagôl moment the exact same way. We've just been introduced to the Black Riders, and they already show many mannerisms they will carry well into their existence as Nazgûl, too. Instead of tension, however, the focus is on showing how even simple things can always prevail over darkness, hence the shift to the butterfly and the following swarm.

One of the biggest changes in The Rings of Power Season 3 is the Harfoots' plot being cut, but there are still plenty of nods and references to the Hobbits besides the Nazgnagôl encounter in the clearing. In fact, Galadriel and Arondir's journey with Marnûkh directly echoes Frodo and Sam's journey with Gollum (Andy Serkis) in The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers and The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King in almost every way, including how the Orc tries to pit the two Elves against each other.

The culmination of the story in The Rings of Power, however, is much more tragic, including the deaths of two of the three members of this strange company. Sad as that outcome is, the scene in the clearing is this brief fellowship's finest hour, with all of them working together in some capacity to evade the Black Riders.
