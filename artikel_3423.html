GTA 6 Really Is Better On PS5 Than Xbox

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

If you’re planning to get Grand Theft Auto 6 at launch, you’ll be able to buy it on two consoles: PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X. But while both are capable pieces of hardware, it sure seems like developer Rockstar Games has a preference between the two. Advertisements for the game have declared that it “plays best on PS5.” Sony has even gotten custom GTA 6 DualSense controllers in celebration of the launch. To some, it could look like GTA 6 is a PS5 exclusive, even though it very much is not.

Now that I’ve finally played GTA 6 for myself, it’s very clear why Sony’s console is the center of attention. It’s not so much about raw power, though the PS5 Pro is likely the best way for the techiest gamers to play, until we get a PC port; it has more to do with the DualSense. GTA 6 feels like it was built with the controller and its unique features in mind, making it a stronger sales pitch for the PS5 than most of Sony’s actual exclusives. It’s just a shame it’s coming so late in the console’s life cycle.

In terms of how GTA 6 runs on PS5, there are no real surprises there. I largely played on PS5 Pro, which was able to give me a relatively smooth 30 frames per second performance. I did experience some framerate dips during high-level police chases, but it otherwise impresses considering the sheer scale of the world and its high level of detail throughout. I’ve yet to test it on Xbox Series X, but there are no red flags here that suggest it might struggle on Microsoft’s console. (The Xbox Series S is a wildcard.)

What was instantly more noticeable during my playthrough was how GTA 6 goes all-in on the DualSense, to the point where it feels like Rockstar built the game specifically around the controller. You can see that in the little details. For instance, Jason and Lucia have cell phones that they can bring up at any time to send texts, make calls, and check apps. The sounds from that phone come through the DualSense’s built-in speaker. If someone calls you, you’ll hear the ring from the controller instead of the TV. That makes it feel like you, the player, are literally holding that phone in your hands — a small but effective bit of immersion.

In that same vein, Rockstar also goes all in on haptic feedback to great effect. When you’re swimming, for example, you can feel each individual stroke Jason or Lucia takes. Again, much of the game feels like it was designed with that level of detail in mind. When cloning a car key, you’ll need to move the right joystick around in a circle and find the strongest vibration point. As you can imagine, haptic feedback only makes that minigame that much more tactile.

Even the DualSense’s adaptive triggers get put to good use. Step on the gas in a car, and you’ll get a hit of resistance that simulates the feeling of stepping on a gas pedal. Crack open a shipping container with a saw, and you’ll need to very carefully increase pressure on the right trigger to make the cut. Stop to meditate, and you’ll feel the triggers rattle under your fingers as you press them to breathe in and out. Every little minigame interaction plays with the triggers or feedback in some way.

The more I played, the more GTA 6 reminded me of early-generation PS5 tech showpieces; it has the same toy-like quality that Returnal and Astro’s Playroom had in the early PS5 years. While there have been plenty of third-party games that have integrated the DualSense in their own ways, GTA 6 has the most fully realized implementation of its features outside of Sony’s first-party games. That’s why it almost feels like an exclusive; six years into the PS5’s life, we finally have the game that really sells how the tech.

None of that is necessary to enjoy GTA 6, mind you. The extra feedback is a great perk if you own a PS5 already, but you’re not going to miss anything because your in-game phone rings from your TV speakers instead of your controller. The Xbox Series X will likely get the job done, and you might even prefer it if you don’t love the occasional strain that comes with adaptive triggers. But when Sony says that GTA 6 plays best on PS5, it’s not exactly a bluff. It does, but "play” is the key word there. It’s not that it looks or runs the best on PS5. It’s just the best platform for those who love toying around with their games.
