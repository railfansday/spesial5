Michael Connelly Finally Gives Us The Bosch Backstory We’ve Been Waiting For In New Release

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

This article contains mention of child abuse.

For over 30 years, Bosch has been a standout thriller series, and the author, Michael Connelly, hasn't stopped surprising readers. In his new release, Connelly delves further into the background and origin of the protagonist, Harry Bosch, than ever, shedding new light on his development.

When audiences first met Bosch in the 1992 novel, The Black Echo, Connelly introduced us to the world of the LAPD homicide detective and the cases he solves. Of course, things quickly get personal for Bosch as his background as a soldier in the Vietnam War and troubled past influence how he solves some grisly murders in Los Angeles. The Bosch TV show has done an amazing job bringing Connelly's novels to life, but it's always exciting when the writer adds texture to his backstory in the novels.

The most recent Bosch book, The Hollow, was released this past year in October 2026, and is the 26th volume in the Bosch franchise. Though Harry Bosch has come a long way since his early days solving crimes for the LAPD, Connelly keeps raising the bar, finding new ways to expand his story and crafting unbelievable thrillers that readers can't put down. Currently rated at 4.48 stars on Goodreads, The Hollow debuted to a warm reception because it takes us back to the beginning of Bosch's history.

Of course, readers know that Harry Bosch hasn't always had the easiest life, and Connelly has explored his past before, but The Hollow goes further than ever before, digging into Bosch's childhood. Just because Bosch retired from the LAPD, his time stopping criminals is far from over, and in the 2026 book, it's more emotional than ever. The Hollow touches upon Bosch's experience as a ward of the state as a child in the wake of his mother's murder.

His half-brother, Mickey Haller, tells Bosch that MacLaren Children’s Center, where Bosch suffered abuse as a child, has finally been busted in a class-action lawsuit, and Bosch is entitled to a huge settlement. However, money isn't what interests Bosch. Though he's tried to hide from this dark period of his past for a long time, Bosch is determined to solve the long-cold case of a child, Chops, he knew at MacLaren, who was found dead.

With his investigative skills and the fact that he can no longer pretend that Chops' death doesn't haunt him, Bosch has all the fuel he needs to track down the predator whom he believes killed Chops. The central villain in The Hollow is a shocking antagonist, making Bosch's investigation all the more compelling as he closes in on him. However, the most arresting part of The Hollow isn't the case, but Bosch's arc coming to grips with the abuse he faced as a child.

Harry Bosch will face something truly tragic in Michael Connelly's next Bosch book, and readers should prepare to see him in an entirely new light.

The Hollow is an emotional, intense book and one of Connelly's most cathartic, as the novel pulls no punches when it comes to bringing Bosch face to face with some of the most traumatic moments of his childhood. The Bosch franchise has always touched upon heavy themes, as Connelly's thriller novels explore dark criminal cases and murders. However, the personal element of The Hollow, and the way Bosch grows as a character throughout the story, make it a worthy addition to Connelly's work.

It makes sense that reviews of The Hollow have been positive, as it satisfies longtime fans who were hoping to get deeper insight into Bosch and his background. Though it's not the best place to start when beginning the Bosch novels, The Hollow was a vital addition to the canon and will be remembered as a pivotal volume that changed the way we see Harry Bosch forever.
