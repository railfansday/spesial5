Bitcoin options and futures became one of the most appreciated assets in the crypto market due to their diversification possibilities. Launching BTC futures in 2017 and options in 2020 by the Chicago Mercantile Exchange (CME) was a significant event for investors and traders, as they could leverage Bitcoin’s price dynamic without owning it.

Each category has something unique. BTC options allow users the right to buy or sell the underlying asset, while futures work through an obligation to settle the contract. While this may seem like a disadvantage, professionals can make the most of these contracts by using control strategies like the stop-loss order to prevent the consequences of rising or falling prices, whereas choosing how to buy Bitcoin can help manage your portfolio better.

Thus, the debate between options and futures continues, as each category benefits a certain audience. But are options better than futures?

Bitcoin options stand in the category of financial derivatives through which users have the right to buy or sell Bitcoin at a strike price (a specific amount), within a certain time frame before the contract expires. The first step in owning an option is buying a premium, which is the cost of opening a position.

Working with options instead of straight-up Bitcoin allows users to settle a speculative position on whether the market will go up and down, so they don’t need an intermediary for the transaction. Users can trade BTC options for speculating on the coin’s price or to hedge against any risk on the market. That’s why speculative traders must adopt a different approach to capitalize on volatility. This could be buying and selling the same number of Bitcoins with the same strike price and expiration date to ensure a net profit.

The method for hedging is known as a covered call, in which traders sell, or write a call option equivalent to the number of coins they already own. It offers the chance to offset some losses in the case of a market price decline, whereas a growth of the market requires selling the holdings.

Bitcoin futures offer the same chance to gain exposure to the underlying asset without holding it, just like options do. However, the difference lies in the obligation to buy or sell BTC. While they seem overwhelming to navigate, getting a hold of Bitcoin futures offers several advantages, such as the opportunity to open long or short positions. This makes profit achievable even in price declines through short contracts. Moreover, traders have easy access to leverage, so they can borrow funds to adjust their positions.

Those interested in diversifying their portfolio with futures and options should take small steps in approaching them. Generally, new investors and traders might choose BTC options since they carry lower risks due to the maximum loss being capped at the premium they pay upfront. When buying options, users have the right but not the obligation to fulfil their contract, and they also benefit from the flexibility to exercise these rights before expiration.

Therefore, Bitcoin futures might be more suitable for experienced traders. The contracts include the obligation to buy or sell the underlying asset, with higher risks coming from an unlimited potential for profit and losses. Futures are also margin-based, involving the deposit of a small fraction of the total contract value. This exposes the trader to the risk of the market moving against their position, requiring additional funds to keep the trade open.
