Severe Flooding Impacts CDT Trail Towns in Colorado and New Mexico

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Severe rainfall across southwestern Colorado has triggered major flooding, widespread infrastructure damage, and mandatory evacuations in the CDT trail towns of Pagosa Springs, Colorado (NOBO mile 866.4) and Chama, New Mexico (NOBO mile 703.5).

In Pagosa Springs, the San Juan River reached major flood thresholds this weekend for the second time in about two weeks. Local authorities issued urgent evacuation orders on Saturday for low-lying zones along local rivers, fearing a potentially “catastrophic” flood event that authorities warned could be even worse than the historic flood of 1911, which wiped out most of the town’s infrastructure at the time.

The river ultimately crested at 16.81 feet on Sunday — significantly lower than feared, but still one of the most significant floods in Pagosa Springs history. Water levels rose enough to completely inundate municipal areas and damage or destroy roads and bridges. Portions of the highway through downtown are closed due to washouts.

The river runs directly through the center of town in Pagosa Springs. This photo was taken in June 2011 and shows the river at its normal volume. Photo via David

The floodwaters began to recede on Monday but left widespread infrastructure damage in their wake. Evacuation orders remain in place. Emergency response agencies and state infrastructure crews are under extreme strain as communities grapple with the aftereffects of these two disastrous, back-to-back flood events.

Flooding has been widespread throughout the southwest this fall as the region has received heavy rainfall driven by a strong El Niño year, with large swaths of southern Utah and cities including Sedona, Arizona and Durango, Colorado also badly affected.

A post shared by Since JulEYE (@sincejuleye)

While this area of the CDT sees fewer hikers this late in the year, the intense flooding has still impacted trail access. In Chama, New Mexico, officials have ordered a mandatory evacuation for residents and businesses from Elkhorn Restaurant south to Unser’s at Highway 64/84.

In a statement, the Continental Divide Trail Coalition stated that “The extent of damage to the CDT, access roads, and CDT communities is still unknown … Trail towns and communities are at the heart of the CDT experience. Our thoughts are with our friends and the community members on the ground in the areas affected by these extreme weather events, and we hope everyone stays safe.”
