Hello Bond, goodbye Batman: Due to The Batman 2 delays, Robert Pattinson is no longer obligated to make another Batman movie

The Batman 2 has taken so long to make that Robert Pattinson is reportedly no longer legally contracted to appear in a third movie, leaving the future of DC Elseworlds' Caped Crusader unclear.

According to a new report from The Wrap, "the protracted production of The Batman: Part II has allowed Pattinson to slip out from under his contract." The deal originally held the star to make a third and final outing as the Gotham vigilante. But now that the contract will have seemingly timed out by the time of a third movie, it is not clear what will happen to Pattinson's Bat.

The Batman sequel has seen its fair share of setbacks. Originally set to release in October 2025, it was pushed back multiple times all the way to early 2028. Most recently, the project was momentarily put on hold when director Matt Reeves stepped away from production for personal reasons. However, filmmaker Steven Soderbergh has now taken the reins. Still, The Batman: Part II is certainly a long time coming, as the first movie hit screens in 2022 – six years before the sequel is set to release.

Pattinson's recent casting as a legendary agent 007 further complicates things. On November 16, it was announced that Pattinson had been cast as the next James Bond, in Amazon MGM Studios' first take on the British spy led by Dune director Denis Villeneuve. Despite Pattinson previously stating he would only sign on to three Bond movies and being a few years older than the other candidates, he still landed the role.

James Bond movies are a huge undertaking, which will certainly take up a lot of Pattinson's time moving forward, leaving little room for The Batman. Alas, Pattinson has proven he can take on big projects one after the other. Just this year, Pattinson has starred in The Drama, The Odyssey, Primetime, and will be introduced to the world of Dune this December in Villeneuve's third and final movie.

There is also the issue of DC Studios. Just last week, it was announced that James Gunn and Peter Safran would be stepping down as DC Studios co-CEOs, and are set to be replaced by former Marvel Studios executive Jeremy Latcham on December 1, 2026. The shakeup arrived not long after Skydance-owned Paramount acquired Warner Bros. Reports suggest that Skydance plans to "dismantle" Gunn's DCU Chapter One.

Though The Batman isn't part of the DCU, the fate of all current DC projects remains ambiguous. DC Studios, Pattinson, and Reeves have yet to comment on the matter.
