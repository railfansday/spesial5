Before you buy a thrift store find to resell, look up what the same item has actually sold for online, not what sellers are asking. Buy it only if that sold price, minus the selling site’s fee and the shipping, beats the store’s price by enough to pay you for your time.

That profit is taxable from the first dollar, even though selling your own used things for less than you paid isn’t. A marketplace has to report your sales to the IRS on Form 1099-K only once it pays you more than $20,000 in more than 200 transactions in a year, so report the profit yourself whether or not a form arrives.

Flip regularly as a business, and you also owe self-employment tax once your net earnings reach $400. Most flips earn a few dollars to a few dozen, so a fee or label you didn’t count can turn one into a loss.

Say a vintage Pyrex bowl costs you $6 and sells on eBay for $40, and the buyer pays $10 for shipping and $4 in sales tax while the label costs you $10. eBay takes 13.6% of the whole $54 plus 40 cents, or $7.74, so the $50 you collect drops to $32.26 after the fee and the label, a $26.26 profit on your $6.

Log each find’s cost, sale price, fees, and shipping in a spreadsheet as you go. The log shows which kinds of finds pay you, and it’s the record that proves your profit at tax time.
