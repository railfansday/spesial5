Where to launch a side project in 2026: what each free launch site asks for

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

In November 2026 we listed two small web products on about 40 launch sites and directories, using only the free options. Writing the listing took an hour. Getting past what each site asks for before it accepts a listing took days. Below is what we met, grouped by the kind of hurdle, so you can plan your own launch week. Prices and queue lengths are what the sites showed us at the time and will change.

The free tier is real here. It just sits at the back of a line.

Join these on day one. Their wait starts when you sign up, not when you are ready.

Many newer sites trade a free listing for a link from your site.

Decide your badge policy before you start. We ended up with four badges in one footer. Keep a note of which badge belongs to which listing so you can remove it if the listing goes away.

These were the most common reason we stopped.

Use one shared address for all launch accounts and a password manager. Several sites send a sign-in code by email, and one verification mail never arrived for us until we tried a second address on our own domain.

Then keep a table with one row per site: URL, cost on the last page you saw, state, date, and the link to your listing or thanks page. After 40 sites you will not remember which ones are waiting on you.

Do the quick ones on the first day and join every free queue at the same time. Leave the badge deals for last, when you know which listings you really want.

Most of these sites send a small number of visitors. The value is that each listing is one more page on the web that names your product, and some of them link to you. Treat it as a few evenings of work, not a launch plan.

That gap is part of why we built Hatchlit (https://hatchlit.com). It lists new products every day, and makers can launch there for free.
