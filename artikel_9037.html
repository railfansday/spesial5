BIG stacks timber volumes with alpine views for new neighborhood in switzerland

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

bjarke ingels group proposes four timber buildings in thun, switzerland with stepped hotel terraces overlooking the alps.

Bjarke Ingels Group / BIG unveils plans for four timber buildings in Thun, Switzerland, where the architecture firm will help shape the entrance to the city’s emerging Siegenthalergut district. The 13,000-square-meter development brings together two hotels and residential buildings, with stacked wooden volumes that project outward above the street. Set against the Bernese Alps, the architecture is defined by deep window grids and large terraces carved into the buildings’ stepped facades.

The proposal, developed with landscape architects August + Margrith Künzel, was selected through a competition organized by Frutiger AG and the city of Thun. Titled Nachwachsende Stadt, or Growing City, the scheme occupies one of the first two plots planned for the new neighborhood. The site sits opposite a sports center and the future headquarters of the Swiss Football Association to give the BIG-designed buildings a prominent position along the city’s western entrance.

At the southwest corner of the site, the team at BIG brings a three-star and a four-star hotel together under one roof. Shared amenity spaces cut diagonally through the structure, creating a series of setbacks that interrupt its otherwise regular timber grid. These shifts make room for outdoor terraces overlooking the neighboring sports facilities, with the Bernese Alps rising in the distance. A restaurant at the top of the building will give guests an even broader view of the surrounding landscape.

At street level, a cantilevered corner marks the main hotel entrance, opening onto a drop-off plaza beneath the projecting upper floors. Commercial spaces line the adjacent boulevard, bringing activity to the building’s ground floor. Across the facade, large square windows sit within deeply framed timber modules, while recessed portions reveal warmer, sheltered spaces behind the exterior grid. The alternating volumes give the hotel a sculptural profile that changes depending on where it is viewed from.

To the east, the remaining timber buildings accommodate residences with flexible layouts and projecting bays. Here, the same geometric language takes on a more domestic scale. Balconies extend outward from the facades, while recessed loggias create sheltered outdoor rooms. The shifting arrangement of these openings gives each elevation a different rhythm, with the projecting volumes bringing daylight deeper into the apartments.

The residential buildings face an internal courtyard, where winding paths move through gardens and planted gathering spaces. Renderings picture grassy areas beneath the timber facades, with balconies overlooking the shared landscape. Small plazas and seating areas are distributed throughout the development, connecting the homes to the surrounding neighborhood.

Of the project’s total 13,000 square meters, approximately 6,500 are dedicated to residential uses, alongside 6,000 square meters of hotel space and another 400 square meters for commercial activities.

The use of timber extends across all four buildings, establishing a consistent material language while helping reduce the development’s embodied carbon footprint. BIG has designed the timber elements for future disassembly and reuse, allowing components to be recovered as the buildings change over time. The deep balconies and roof overhangs also serve a practical purpose, providing natural shade in summer and shelter during the colder months.

Green roofs and rainwater retention systems will help manage water across the site, while approximately 1,800 square meters of photovoltaic panels are planned to generate renewable energy. The development is targeting Switzerland’s SNBS Gold sustainability certification.

The winning proposal will now progress through further design and approval stages, with construction of the district’s first buildings possible from 2028. For BIG, which opened its Zurich office in 2023, the project adds another timber-focused development to its growing Swiss portfolio, alongside the planned Dock A terminal at Zurich Airport.
