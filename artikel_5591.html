Contractor at Site of Brooklyn Building Collapse Was Previously Sued for Negligence

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The general contractor overseeing the construction site adjacent to a deadly building collapse in Brooklyn, NY, was previously accused in a lawsuit of compromising the structural integrity of nearby properties during an excavation project, court records show.

Signage at the construction site at 2625 Bedford Ave., as well as planning documents filed with the Department of Buildings, show that Townhouse Builders, Inc, doing business as Promont, was the general contractor overseeing the construction project.

Building records show that the project involved excavation work directly adjacent to the three-story mixed-use building at 1321 Flatbush Ave. that fully collapsed on Wednesday, killing eight people, including two children, and leaving 18 others injured.

The construction project had a documented history of safety concerns flagged by DOB inspectors, and Mayor Zohran Mamdani says there will be a full investigation into whether any issues at the Bedford Ave. construction site contributed to the collapse.

"If there is anyone or any entity operating in this city that bears responsibility for what happened, we will hold them accountable," the mayor said.

Now, court records reviewed by Realtor.com® show that Townhouse Builders was accused in a 2020 lawsuit of causing "a breached wall, substantial shifting, settlement and cracking" to an adjacent property during a separate construction job.

New York state records list Mordachai Waisbrod as the CEO of Townhouse Builders. The company and an attorney listed as representing the Townhouse in the 2020 lawsuit did not respond to requests for comment from Realtor.com.

Townhouse Builders has worked on numerous projects across New York, including a retail location on Atlantic Ave., a residential tower on Delancey St. in the Lower East Side, and the redevelopment of a residential tower on Park Row.

In 2016, Townhouse Builders was hired by developer Moris Yeroshalm to serve as general contractors on the construction of a six-story hotel at 1200 Broadway in Brooklyn's Bed-Stuy neighborhood.

In a lawsuit filed in Kings County Supreme Court in 2020, Yeroshalmi alleged that "the construction activity performed by Townhouse at the Project caused damage and compromised the structural integrity of Plaintiff's property and adjacent properties."

The suit alleges that "the construction caused, among other things, a breached wall, substantial shifting, settlement and cracking of Plaintiff's property and the adjacent properties, in addition to broken footing of the adjacent properties and structural destabilization of Plaintiff's property and the adjacent properties."

The suit lists the allegedly damaged properties as 1196 Broadway, and 15 and 17 Patchen Avenue, all located in Brooklyn.

The lawsuit further alleges that 1196 Broadway "was severely damaged by the excavation activity" and that 15 and 17 Patchen Ave. "were severely damaged by the pile driving activities."

In the suit, Yeroshalmi alleges Townhouse "breached their duty and were negligent … by allowing construction activities to compromise and destroy the structural integrity of Plaintiff's property and adjacent properties."

Further, the lawsuit accuses Townhouse of "failing to perform a physical inspection and prepare a proper pre-construction survey of Plaintiff's property prior to performing excavation and underpinning, failing to support the premises walls and by allowing the excavation and underpinning to compromise the structural integrity of Plaintiff's property and adjacent properties."

Yeroshalmi sued for damages totaling more than $1.1 million. The suit remains open with the next hearing in the case to be held in January 2027.

A review of Department of Buildings complaints and violations related to 1200 Broadway show that Townhouse Builders received a Class 2 or "major" violation in 2016 for "failure to monitor adjoining structures for movement in all directions during excavation-related activity that may adversely affect adjoining structures."

That same year, it received a Class 1 violation for "failure to safeguard all persons and property affected by construction operations," noting that the excavator's bucket damaged the brick foundation wall of an adjoining building during excavation. Class 1 violations are "Immediately Hazardous violations" that "must be corrected immediately."

In 2017, it received another Class 2 violation for failure to monitor cracks on adjacent buildings during excavation.

In total, the DOB fined Townhouse Builders $10,400 for those three violations.

Court records show Townhouse Builders, under their own name and Promont, have been sued 67 times since 2012. Thirteen of those cases, including the one mentioned above, remain open. It appears the bulk of those cases have to do with injuries on job sites.

On Wednesday, the Brooklyn district attorney's office announced it was opening a criminal investigation into the cause of the collapse.

“We are working closely with the Department of Buildings and our law enforcement partners to determine whether any criminal conduct contributed to this tragedy,” District Attorney Eric Gonzalez said in the statement.

On Thursday afternoon, Mamdani announced the city would be sweeping 100 other sites associated with five "entities" involved in a construction project adjacent to the collapsed building. The city has since expanded that search to 160 properties. Mamdani and Department of Buildings Commissioner Ahmed Tigani declined to name the five companies they would be focusing their investigation on.
