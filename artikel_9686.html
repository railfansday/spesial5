Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang admitted on November 8, 2026 that he "was unfaithful to my wife," in an all-staff email Nvidia posted to its newsroom. The woman, per The New York Times as summarized by the SF Standard, is Alice Huang (Huang Le, no relation), a former executive at Nvidia customer Megaspeed whom U.S. intelligence officials suspect of working for China's Ministry of State Security. No public record gives the affair's start date. The earliest documented meeting is a June 2024 Taipei bar night during Computex; TechCrunch reports they "reportedly met at an Nvidia event in early 2024."

The espionage claims come from unnamed U.S. officials. Alice Huang has not been charged or commented, China calls the claims a "smear," and Nvidia's board says it has "no reason to believe" secrets were compromised.

Alice Huang is a Chinese citizen based in Singapore who ran Megaspeed, the company that became Nvidia's biggest Southeast Asian customer. Most of her résumé cannot be independently verified.

Megaspeed was registered in Singapore in 2015 as 7Road International and renamed in March 2023, the same month she joined, according to IBTimes. Its Malaysian subsidiary, Speedmatrix, bought nearly $2 billion in Nvidia chips, mostly through the U.S. arm of entity-listed Inspur, the NYT reported. A Megaspeed-linked Malaysian company bought at least $4.8 billion in Nvidia equipment between June 2024 and February 2026, per OfficeChai's summary, and the Financial Times reported its balance sheet grew from $33 million to $3 billion in 2024. Megaspeed denies smuggling and has not been charged.

The affair's start date is not public. A U.S. memo reported by the Financial Times says officials do not know whether her alleged recruitment by Chinese intelligence came before or after the relationship began (OfficeChai's summary).

Nvidia published Jensen's Nov. 8, 2026 email on its own newsroom under the title "A Message to Our Company", alongside a separate "Statement From NVIDIA Board of Directors." The email never names Alice Huang and never mentions espionage directly; it refers only to "national security concerns raised in news reports."
