Broncos vs. Raiders: 3 Things to Watch in Week 11

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

After a well-deserved break, the Denver Broncos return to the gridiron this weekend. On Sunday, they will host the Las Vegas Raiders. It's an important AFC West rivalry matchup and the Broncos need a win to keep pace in a tough AFC playoff race. Here are three storylines I'll be watching closely in Sunday's matchup.

The first big storyline I'll be watching on Sunday is how Denver's offense performs after the bye. It's been a rollercoaster of a ride for that side of the ball all season long. Even though they haven't been at their best, they've had a penchant for coming alive late in games.

The Raiders made a lot of additions to their defense in free agency, but they really haven't paid off. The unit itself is one of the worst in the NFL at stopping the run and are a bottom 10 unit against the pass. The silver-lining for them is that they've been very good at forcing turnovers with 16 on the season—one of the best marks in the league.

With an extra week of preparation, I'm confident Bo Nix and his teammates can execute Head Coach Sean Payton's gameplan. With the Raiders being dreadful stopping the run, I'd love to see the Broncos deploy a run-first attack that will take pressure of Nix's shoulders. If they can block better up front and run more effectively, that should help their third-year quarterback's ability to dial it up on play-action calls and pockets on the move.

Prior to the NFL trade deadline, the Raiders traded Kirk Cousins to the Minnesota Vikings for a third-round draft selection. That means the 2026 NFL Draft's top selection, quarterback Fernando Mendoza, will be under center against the Broncos on Sunday.

In his first NFL start going up against the Seattle Seahawks in Week 10, Mendoza struggled immensely. He completed just 17 of 19 pass attempts for 142 yards. Even worse, he was sacked six times after the Raiders' offensive line only allowed 11 through the season prior to Week 10.

Denver's defense has been banged up the past few weeks. Particularly in their secondary, where a handful of starters have missed time and games. Hopefully several of those players, especially at safety, will be able to return to help their pursuit of an eighth victory on the season.

While the Broncos haven't put up the sack numbers like their did the past two seasons, they're still a Top 10 unit in the league with 26 on the season. I think the defensive front seven shows up in a big way and finds a way to rattle and bring down Mendoza early and often.

Another thing to watch for is the availability of Raiders' tight end Brock Bowers. He suffered an ankle injury against the Seahawks and is considered day-to-day. If he isn't able to go, that will make things a lot harder for Mendoza to have success against Denver on Sunday.

Over the bye week, Coach Payton made several comments about what needs to happen for Denver's offense to perform better. Speaking to Colin Cohwerd, he specifically pointed to the Broncos' offensive line having to do a better job blocking up front for them to fire on all cylinders in the second half of the season.

Left tackle Garett Bolles and right guard Quinn Meinerz have been fantastic. However, there is no doubt left guard Ben Powers, center Luke Wattenberg, and right tackle Mike McGlinchey have struggled a bit. Whether or not a lineup change happens at a particular position remains to be seen, but I'll be keeping my eye on this.

If I had to guess on any potential switch, I would wager Alex Palczewski would get the nod over Powers. When Powers went down last season with an injury, Palczewski played pretty well for Denver in his first extended action as a starter. Will we see that move happen on Sunday? I'm anxious to find out.
