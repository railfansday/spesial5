RAMalytics: 3 stats that defined Rams’ win over Packers

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

A 26-13 win isn't exactly a blowout. Then again, the Los Angeles Rams completely suffocated the Green Bay Packers by dominating time of possession—especially in the second half.

Blake Corum was the boa constrictor. He amassed over 200 total yards on the game with at least 180 and a score on the ground alone. The Rams' rushing attack was productive despite Davis Allen, Tyler Higbee, Warren McClendon, and Bill Murray all leaving the game due to injury.

The Rams can hang their hat on this impressive victory, although the slate does not let up any time soon.

Sure, it helps that across LA's five-game winning streak the first four victories came against middling-at-best competition. Still, in their last six outings, the Rams allowed just four touchdowns. Myles Garrett wasn't available for that full stretch. Trent McDuffie either. They are still finding a way to get the job done.

The biggest tell that LA dominated Jordan Love and company? The Packers offense finished with 244 total yards while Corum accumulated 205 himself. Kyren Williams added another 65 yards and a score.

It was jarring to see Corum open the game in the backfield. I've grown accustomed to seeing Kyren Williams earn the start, even if running back workload has been fairly split in recent years. The Rams have come to understand that Corum gives them a more dynamic option on the ground. When they needed an offensive spark this season, Corum provided that while very little else is working. LA is taking the path of least resistance and leaning on their best playmaker.

The most impressive part of Corum's performance? He finished with 99 yards after contact against the Packers. If those were all of his yards on Wednesday night, that is a strong individual performance. He broke tackles and created yardage that wasn't readily there to a final output of 184 rushing yards. According to SumerSports, Corum had an EPA per rush of +0.19. It's rare to see an EPA per rush that isn't negative across the NFL.

For the full game, the Rams held the ball for 40:40 versus the Packers' 19:20. On the surface that lends itself to winning. When you split the time of possession by half (23:45 to 6.55), you realize Green Bay really had no opportunity to mount a comeback. Corum and the Rams were playing keep away. The defense was also able to force turnovers even when Love and the Packers offense was on the field. Green Bay's offense largely watched their demise from the sidelines.

The Rams similarly dominated the second half against the Arizona Cardinals last week. This is their second-straight game—and only the second game across the entire NFL season so far—where a team accumulated more than 24 minutes of possession in the second half.

You can't win if you can't score. By playing complimentary football, the Rams aren't even giving opposing offenses a chance to do so.
