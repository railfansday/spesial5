How to Grow a Beard

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

So you want to learn how to grow a beard. First off, you should know that it helps to have the right genetics. Second, you have to stay committed to the endeavor. Too often, beard-growing attempts end before they’ve even had a chance to establish itself.

“I’d say give it at least three to four weeks of solid growth before you even think about touching it with clippers,” Victor Mendoza, co-founder of beard care brand Viking Revolution, says. “Your beard needs time to actually show you what it’s going to look like, and that growth period lets you see the natural direction everything’s growing in. Plus, some hairs take longer to come in than others, so patience is key.”

There’s a sweet spot between letting your facial hair grow out and knowing when it’s time to take the trimmers for the best results possible, even if you can’t go full lumberjack. These are the best tips to maximize your hair follicles’ potential whether this is your first beard or your fiftieth.

There are many different beard styles to choose from; you don’t need to stick to neatly manicured, uniform look. Let your growth be influenced by the style you want to achieve.

“Your genes determine hair thickness, growth rate, density, and color,” Mendoza says. “If your dad or granddad had a thin beard, you’re probably dealing with the same hand, and that’s just life.”

If your beard is patchy, or if it has a unique growth pattern (sparse on the cheeks, full on the goatee, for example), then you should set your expectations properly. This goes for beards big, small, and medium alike. And while nutrition is important for hair growth, you can’t cheat genetics with your diet.

Patience is a virtue. Not only can a little time help fill in some patchiness, but it’s also requisite for almost any style. A beard transforms into something new every week. So, if it’s seeming like you’ll never reach your goal—in particular if it’s a long beard—then you honestly might wake up one morning and realize that you’ve arrived at the destination.

Mendoza says to wait at least two weeks before you start to do a cleanup, but only around the ends, i.e. the hairs that aren’t within the perimeter in which you want your beard to grow. That’ll help you figure out your beard’s natural shape.

One of the tenets of beard maintenance is to keep it defined. In short: shave the neckline and clean up the cheek lines. These keep it more beard-like and less neckbeard-like—unless, for some reason, a wooly stoner beard is your aim. In that case, let it grow wild and free.

The second way to keep things looking intentional is to sculpt and trim your beard as it grows. It might feel counterproductive—and counter to #2—but it’s the same way you need to get haircuts while growing out your hair. As Mendoza says, this helps direct the hair exactly where it needs to be: Fuller in some spots, less so in others. The styling products in the next tip can of course help influence things, but you foremost want to keep hairs in check as needed: This can mean spot-checking strays, snipping away split ends, or simply taking out some bulk. You’ll want a beard trimmer or a sharp pair of scissors.

There are a handful of beard products you should consider picking up if you’re serious about proper care and maintenance. A few drops of beard oil will absorb quickly and give your hair the right nutrients (plus a pinch of shine), while also making it less wild and frizzy. You can also swap in a beard balm or cream, something slightly heftier than an oil, to help style the beard and keep it in place all day. What’s important is that you clean your beard every day with an actual beard wash and not just a regular shampoo, which Mendoza says will dry out your beard and your face.

Weekly maintenance will consist of a deep condition, which you can do in the shower. “If you’re doing those things consistently, your beard is going to look way healthier and fuller than most guys out there,” he says.

Beard brushes help exfoliate the skin beneath the beard, for starters, which lifts dead skin cells and prevents future flaking. Moreover, brushing your beard at the end of each day will ensure its health and tame-ability in the long haul. This distributes the natural oils produced in the skin, which otherwise collect at the base of the shaft and only nourish the bottom of each hair. This oil distribution prevents breakage, frizzing, and split ends. (This is a great time to apply a few drops of beard oil as a booster, especially after a shower.)

Once your base has grown out—this can take months—it’s time to really make it yours. One of our favorites is to snip the bridge between the mustache and the chin, thus marooning the mustache. Or, pare back the beard itself, leaving a fuller mustache. You have lots of options, and it’s your privilege as a beard grower to flaunt it.
