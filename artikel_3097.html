Marlowe raises a $277M seed to run AI on chips that aren’t Nvidia’s

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The six-month-old startup is valued at $1.15B and opens its first site, built on Etched chips, in December.

Marlowe, a startup founded in May by former Convoy and Microsoft executives, has raised $277M in seed funding led by Accel at a $1.15B valuation. It builds AI data centres for inference using a mix of chips from companies such as Etched and AMD, rather than Nvidia alone.

Marlowe, a startup that builds AI data centres around chips from companies other than Nvidia, has raised $277M in seed funding, it announced on Monday. Accel led the round, which values the six-month-old company at $1.15B, GeekWire reported.

Bessemer, Altimeter, Atreides and Eclipse also invested, along with angels including Box CEO Aaron Levie and Uber CEO Dara Khosrowshahi.

Marlowe focuses on inference, the work of running AI models for users. It argues that demand is outgrowing supply, and that relying on one chipmaker creates a bottleneck for the whole industry. It plans to match each type of AI task, such as coding agents or voice assistants, with the chips and data centre design that suit it best.

Its first site, built around chips from Etched, goes live in December and will mainly serve a large AI coding company. A second site using AMD’s Helios racks is due in the second quarter of 2027. AMD has also backed TensorWave’s AI cloud, which runs only on its chips.

“Marlowe is a true partner that optimizes across our full stack,” said Vamsi Boppana, AMD’s senior vice president of AI.

CEO Dan Lewis co-founded freight startup Convoy, and CTO Kutta Srinivasan led AI infrastructure at Microsoft. Marlowe says tuning every layer of its stack can deliver a two- to three-fold edge in performance and cost. Investors are betting big on cheaper inference: chipmaker SambaNova reached an $11B valuation in July, and Nebius paid $643M for 20-person Eigen AI.
