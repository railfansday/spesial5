Planet Labs PBC vs. Red Cat: Which Industrials Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Satellite scale meets drone agility, but one company's revenue jumped 508% while the other's forward multiple tells a very different valuation story.

Space and defense innovation are driving investor interest as hardware costs fall and demand for data rises. Choosing between Planet Labs PBC (PL -0.65%) and Red Cat (RCAT -3.43%) requires weighing satellite scale against drone agility.

Planet Labs focuses on geospatial data, capturing daily imagery used by government and commercial clients to monitor global changes. Red Cat provides robotic solutions, specifically unmanned aircraft for defense missions. While both companies target the defense and intelligence sectors, their underlying technologies and stages of financial development differ significantly.

Planet Labs provides geospatial data and Earth-imaging services, positioning it as a unique player among defense stocks and environmental monitoring firms. It serves over 700 customers, including various intelligence and defense agencies, by imaging Earth's landmass every day. Recent market analysis highlights its growing focus on sovereign satellite demand and on integrating imagery with Alphabet. A limited number of customers account for a substantial share of revenue, adding risk to the business.

In the 12 months ended July 31, 2026, revenue reached nearly $378.3 million, representing growth of approximately 44.1% compared to the prior period. Despite this top-line expansion, the company reported a net loss of nearly $359.9 million. This result reflects the high costs associated with maintaining its extensive satellite constellation and investing in its proprietary data analytics platforms.

As of its January 2026 balance sheet, the current ratio is approximately 1.7x, indicating how well the company can cover short-term liabilities with its current assets. The debt-to-equity ratio is nearly 2.5x, meaning total debt, including short- and long-term obligations, is two and a half times shareholder equity. In the fiscal year ended Jan. 31, 2026, free cash flow was nearly $57.6 million. Note that stock-based compensation accounted for roughly 40.9% of operating cash flow, inflating reported cash generation because it is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Red Cat focuses on providing advanced robotic solutions for the military and national security markets. In its latest annual report, the company disclosed that approximately 73% of its total revenue was generated from contracts with the U.S. Government. This high level of customer concentration risk is mitigated by strategic partnerships with AeroVironment (AVAV -2.75%) and Palantir Technologies (PLTR -2.09%) to enhance mission capabilities.

In the 12 months ended June 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $46.9 million, representing significant growth of approximately 508.2% over the prior year. Despite this rapid scaling, the company reported a net loss of nearly $101.0 million. This loss is primarily driven by heavy research and development spending as the company attempts to capture a larger share of the tactical drone market.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the current ratio is approximately 15.3x, indicating a strong ability to meet short-term obligations. The debt-to-equity ratio is nearly 0.1x, indicating the company carries very little debt relative to its shareholders' equity. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was nearly negative $95.8 million, reflecting the investment needed to scale its manufacturing and software capabilities.

Planet Labs PBC faces significant customer concentration, as a limited number of clients account for a substantial portion of its revenue. Operational risks include potential launch failures or hardware degradation within its satellite constellation. The company also faces competition from BlackSky Technology (BKSY +2.41%), Satellogic (SATL -6.25%), and Airbus (EADSF -0.98%), as well as from governments that provide free imagery.

Red Cat relies heavily on U.S. Government contracts, making it vulnerable to budget uncertainty and potential spending cuts. The company's active acquisition strategy carries integration risks, while competition from larger defense contractors remains intense. Additionally, it faces supply chain vulnerabilities, particularly regarding its reliance on third-party manufacturers for critical circuit chip sets.

Red Cat looks more attractive based on future earnings estimates, while Planet Labs trades at a slightly lower valuation relative to its sales over the past 12 months.

When comparing Planet Labs and Red Cat, investors should consider a few key factors. Let's have a look at them and see what that tells us about each stock.

Firstly, we should consider revenue growth. On the surface, Red Cat wins the category, with average revenue growth of 995% compared to Planet Labs' 47%. However, with both companies, the revenue base is relatively small: $378 million for Planet Labs and $72 million for Red Cat. So while Red Cat is growing faster, this category is difficult to judge.

Another factor to consider is each company's balance sheet. Since neither company is profitable, it makes sense to examine their respective balance sheets. Planet Labs has no net debt, with over $368 million in cash on hand. Red Cat has no net debt, with over $310 million in cash on hand. That's important, as each company is still burning cash. Planet Labs has free cash flow of -$18 million over the last 12 months, while Red Cat has burned through about $157 million. Since it has a longer cash runway, Planet Labs gains an edge in this category.

Finally, there's valuation. We'll examine price-to-sales (P/S) multiples, since neither company is profitable. Red Cat is more affordable, with a P/S ratio of 6.3x, while Planet Labs' 12.2x multiple is higher.

To sum up, investors may want to take a closer look at each of these growth stocks. However, in the direct head-to-head matchup, I view Planet Labs as the winner, given its longer cash runway, which is vitally important to stocks at this early stage.
