The Resignation of CEO Jensen Huang Isn't Nvidia's Only Problem. Where the Stock Could Fall to by the End of 2027.

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The resignation of CEO Jensen Huang could be the least of Nvidia's problems.

In a shocking turn of events, Nvidia (NVDA -2.01%) co-founder and CEO Jensen Huang officially resigned from his post following allegations of misconduct. Huang earlier admitted to an affair with an alleged Chinese spy, which was then followed up with reports that he also apparently had an affair with a work subordinate.

Huang was a visionary, and his impact on Nvidia is immeasurable. The company's greatness came from Huang's ability to see where the market was headed well before anyone else. This could be seen with his move to create the company's CUDA software platform to easily program its graphics processing units (GPUs) for different tasks and then to seed CUDA into universities and research labs that were doing early work on artificial intelligence (AI), cementing the company's early dominance in the space. He also spearheaded important acquisitions, including the acquisition of the next-generation networking company Mellanox in 2020, whose networking portfolio today serves as the nervous system for its end-to-end AI servers.

Jensen Huang. Image source: Getty Images.

Huang stepping down as CEO is a big blow to the company. However, it may not be the biggest issue Nvidia is facing. The company appears to have a serious China problem; separately, its CUDA software moat may have just shrunk considerably.

Jensen Huang's relationship with suspected Chinese spy and former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang (no relation) perhaps should not be a total surprise, as short-seller Culper Research highlighted the pair's "uncomfortably close relationship" and partying in a 40-page report entitled: "Nvidia: The China Problem" back in May. With Culper being spot on about this inappropriate relationship, other parts of its allegations need to be taken more seriously.

At the top of this list for investors is Culper’s estimate that over 20% of Nvidia's 2026 computing revenue was driven by illegal sales to China. In the report, the company quoted one unnamed former Nvidia director-level employee who believed that 20% to 40% of Nvidia's data center revenue was going to China after the April 2025 restriction went into effect. This was done both through chip smuggling and intermediaries in Europe and Asia that would rent their AI servers to China.

Interestingly, the short report ties Super Micro Computer's (SMCI -0.46%) smuggling issues to a Southeast Asian company called OBON, which Culper believes is associated with Megaspeed. I always felt it was odd that Nvidia brushed off Supermicro’s continued shenanigans while keeping close ties to the company. Meanwhile, the report notes that Nvidia has always vouched for Megaspeed despite the company potentially being financed by Alibaba (BABA +0.02%) and Jensen Huang knowing this. It also states that Nvidia has repeatedly said it has no concerns about chips being rerouted (chip diversion) to China

At its worst, if Culper's report proves true, it's not out of the realm of possibility that Huang could be charged with treason. However, for investors, the possibility of up to 40% of the company's data center revenue going away is a huge risk.

In a completely separate issue, which is coming at a terrible time, Nvidia suddenly sees its CUDA software moat shrinking. Anthropic recently was able to use its Claude Mythos model to port and optimize Claude to run on Advanced Micro Devices (AMD -8.75%) GPUs in just three days with no human intervention. In the past, this would have taken a dozen engineers six to 12 months to accomplish. With AMD GPUs and custom AI chips as cheaper alternatives to Nvidia's solution, this presents a serious potential risk to Nvidia and its stock.

If Nvidia's gross margins were to fall to 60% due to competitive pressures and its sales growth gets slashed to 20% growth next year and 15% growth in fiscal 2029 due to the end of illegal Chinese sales and reduced sale prices, it would suddenly be doing around $9.27 in fiscal 2028 (ending January 2028) earnings per share (EPS) and $10.57 for fiscal 2029. Place a 12 times forward price-to-earnings (P/E) multiple on those fiscal 2029 estimates, and the stock would fall to around $125 in 2027. That's roughly 25% downside in this scenario.

Calculations by author. FY = Fiscal year.

The risk of owning Nvidia just went up a whole lot, and I would not be a buyer of the semiconductor stock on this dip just yet.
