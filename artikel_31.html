German spies handed new powers to tackle 'hybrid threats' from abroad

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

In future, German spies working for the BND foreign intelligence service will be able to conduct covert operations abroad

Germany's foreign and domestic intelligence services are to be given greater powers to combat espionage, terrorism and sabotage after the country's parliament, the Bundestag, approved a major overhaul of its legislation.

Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt, who championed the bill, has called it a "reform of the century", necessary because of the increasing threats from foreign powers.

But the Left Party's security spokeswoman, Clara BÃ¼nger, said Dobrindt was creating a new German secret police.

The legislation gives numerous new powers to Germany's BND federal intelligence service, and its BfV domestic service, the Federal Office for the Protection of the Constitution.

In the Bundestag, 309 MPs voted in favour of the new law on intelligence services, while 190 voted against and 71 abstained.

For historical reasons, German intelligence has mainly focused on gathering information rather than acting on it.

In future, German spies working for the BND, which is responsible for foreign intelligence, will be able to conduct covert operations abroad, including sabotage.

This could include paralysing the servers of state-sponsored hacker groups, disrupting disinformation networks, or even disrupting supply chains for weapons.

Agents for the domestic intelligence service, the BfV, will no longer to be limited to gathering information. For the first time they will be able to take protective action against property and IT systems, if specific threats are identified.

That means they will be allowed to dismantle or break explosives or weapons, divert or block data flows during cyber-attacks, delete or falsify data stored for malicious purposes, and plant false information on those involved.

Under certain conditions, BfV agents will also be permitted to enter homes covertly. However, the measure must always be authorised in advance.

They will also be given wider powers of digital surveillance, including monitoring encrypted telecommunications and carrying out online searches. They will also be allowed to use AI-supported data analysis and biometric image matching on the internet.

The government says the reform is needed because of an increase in foreign espionage, particularly from Russia, China and Iran.

"We are the target of hybrid threats," said Dobrindt. "Germany is a theatre of operations for terrorism, extremism and foreign powers."

In August, a drone carrying an explosive device was discovered at Leipzig Airport, close to Ukrainian cargo planes. However, it failed to explode as the bomb's detonator was apparently faulty.

German authorities say Russia was behind the attack.
