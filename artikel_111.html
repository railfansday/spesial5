EU ‘to close door’ to talks on long-term UK ties until Burnham has decided on Brexit

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Diplomats say piecemeal approach of resets is coming to an end and Europe wants clarity on whether Britain wants to reverse its exit

The EU will shut the door to discussions with Andy Burnham about the UK’s longer-term relationship with the bloc until the government has decided whether it wants to proceed with reversing Brexit, diplomats have said.

European capitals are also warning they have reached the end of the line for piecemeal “reset” summits with the UK and want the prime minister to focus on his next steps domestically before they proceed.

But they suggested a joint statement after a summit this Friday would signal that further discussions would follow on the UK’s entry to the EU’s energy single markets, irregular migration and a closer defence and security relationship.

“Clarity is what we need more than anything from the UK. Don’t come back until you know what you want. Work out what your options really are and then come to negotiate,” one EU diplomat said.

“The prime minister needs to bring the public with him. We can’t have another negotiation based on just half the country. We’ll only put the effort in if we know it’s not going to change down the line.”

Burnham is expected to set out his own next steps after the summit on Monday, including his hopes for a “national conversation” on what a closer relationship might look like.

With less than 48 hours before the Brussels summit, negotiators were still racing to finalise Friday’s agreement. A leaked draft seen by the Guardian shows continued disagreement over demands by the EU that the UK commit to aligning with the bloc’s trade defence measures, such as tariffs on Chinese electric vehicles, in exchange for inclusion in Made in Europe legislation.

The text shows the UK is pushing for a weaker commitment, only promising an “aim to protect supply chains consistently across our markets and guard against potential risks of circumvention”.

A Cabinet Office source said Made in Europe language was “the highest jeopardy” in the negotiations, while an EU official suggested it was the part of the discussions most likely to go “down to the wire” on Friday.

Though there is a formal commitment to annual reset summits, senior EU diplomats say the current approach, designed by Keir Starmer, will quickly “reach its limits” and more “mini deals” do not make sense.

One said the “piecemeal approach is coming to an end” and that Burnham’s intentions for the UK’s long-term relationship created a natural juncture for the EU too.

Another said: “It is like a buffet: you can choose to nibble on one or two dishes to start, but at a certain point you have to go for the whole of the buffet.”

One EU diplomat said there would be a “pause for reflection” after the Friday summit.

Burnham has said there is no rush to decide on the longer-term relationship, as he comes under pressure from within his own party to slow the pace of pursuing of closer ties.

He is not now expected to set out the full range of options for the future relationship – the status quo, customs union, single market or full rejoin – until he announces his 10-year plan in December.

Sources said the government was considering holding a year-long “national conversation” in 2027, including town halls and citizens assemblies, before deciding which route to pursue in advance of the next election.

However, the potential length of that debate has alarmed Brussels officials, who were caught off-guard by the launch of the new pro-Brexit campaign group Stay Out, set up by architects of the Vote Leave campaign.

One EU diplomat said: “The government would spend the year making evidence-based arguments about each of the four options, while Boris Johnson and Michael Gove and Nigel Farage will spend it shouting about traitors.”

After the reset deal is agreed on Friday, it will be enacted by the European partnership bill, covering the deals on food and agriculture, emissions trading and youth mobility. It is expected to be introduced before Christmas, with legislation laid possibly as soon as next week.

EU diplomats said the two sides were expected to settle on a quota of about 100,000 people a year being allowed to work, travel or study in each other’s territories. Originally, the UK had pitched for 50,000 and then 80,000, while Brussels has consistently sought a higher number.

Both sides have agreed that tuition fees for European students coming to the UK should be discussed at a later date, although there have been attempts by some member states to link the debate to the ratification of the whole reset deal.

Senior Labour figures are already beginning their push to have a full rejoining on the table. The former business secretary Peter Kyle said Burnham should fight the next election on a promise to rejoin the EU, with voters given a referendum on the outcome of any deal.

Kyle, who was a key member of Starmer’s cabinet, said the “only way through” the challenges of the domestic political debate was for the government to offer voters the choice to accept or reject any agreement.

He is the latest senior Labour MP to publicly wade into the Brexit debate, after the former foreign secretary David Lammy said the party should go into the next election clear that its intention was to rejoin.

Rachel Reeves, the former chancellor, has also said Britain’s future should be back in the bloc, arguing that leaving had made the UK “poorer, weaker and less secure”.
