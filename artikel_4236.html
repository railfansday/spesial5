8 Metroidvanias Where the World Feels Bigger the More You Learn About It

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Eric Warner is a Journalist and Multimedia Producer based in New England with over 7 years of experience producing stories for multiple publications

One of the best aspects of video games overall is how they allow people to learn about and explore enthralling worlds in fantasy, sci-fi, or historical settings.

Many gamers would choose to play third-person open-world or narrative-focused adventure titles to explore such worlds, but there are many other game genres that allow people to delve deeper into a world’s environments and lore, such as the backtracking-centered metroidvania.

While most metroidvania games are limited to a 2D side-scrolling-like perspective, many metroidvania games can feel just as large and expansive as those found in RPGs. Especially as players continue to travel through the titles' maps and learn more about their history and inhabitants.

DrinkBox Studios' Guacamelee! is a stylish Mexican folk art and lucha libre-inspired metroidvania that saw players travel throughout the lands of the living and the dead as agave farmer turned luchador Juan Aguacate.

However, DrinkBox strove to go bigger and better with Guacamelee’s sequel, Guacamelee! 2, as that game had Aguacate travel beyond his homeland into the Mexiverse, a multiverse full of wondrous and intriguing alternate timelines.

This game almost immediately made the universe of Guacamelee far larger and more complex than most players would have imagined by just playing the first game, with Guacamelee! 2 allowing Aguacate to meet alternate versions of friends and villains as well as entirely different realms such as El Otromundo.

Konami’s Castlevania franchise is full of titles that allow players to explore and learn more about the supernatural settings and subsequent entries, as they fight vampires and other monsters. One of the more accessible entries for new players would be Castlevania: Lords of Shadow.

A third-person hack and slash metroidvania reboot of the series, Castlevania: Lords of Shadow has players embody the Brotherhood of Light knight Gabriel Belmont in 11th century Europe, as he sets out to defeat the titular Lords of Shadow and Satan himself.

However, Belmont quickly learns that there are far more dark powers hiding throughout the world as, in the Reverie and Resurrection DLCs, he travels to Carmilla's castle to defeat the ancient Forgotten One, only to become Dracula and menace humanity for hundreds of years.

From the best bosses to essential lore explanations, these are eight Metroidvanias that hide their best content behind the most unexpected paths.

Xbox consoles have become homes for many metroidvania titles over the years, with one of the earliest to debut exclusively on a Xbox console in an action-packed way being Chair Entertainment’s Shadow Complex.

Set in the same universe as Orson Scott Card's Empire book series, Shadow Complex is a military sci-fi metroidvania following Jason Fleming as he and his girlfriend, Claire, set out to explore some caves hidden in a mountain.

Things quickly go awry, though, as it’s revealed that the caves were an entrance to a labyrinth-like shadow complex run by the militant Progressive Restoration. Fleming is forced to explore the complex and steal the PR’s gear in order to rescue Clair and stop the PR from inciting a second civil war in the U.S.

One of the better metroidvanias to launch in 2026 was Drop Rate Studio’s Lovecraftian horror game DIVE or DIE - Children of Rain which takes place in the near future after most of the world was flooded by a supernatural, monster-making black rain.

Here, players must manage one of the last human settlements and routinely send people to dive into the leviathan and ruin-filled Abyssal Pool, in search of supplies and ways to appease the Cthulhu-like Rainmaker in 40 days before it destroys the world.

When players initially dive into the Abyssal Pool, they’ll first find the remains of skyscrapers and other buildings. As they continue to dive deeper and deeper below the surface, they’ll find that the Pool has almost no end in sight and is filled with all kinds of unspeakable horrors.

If you're looking for games that subvert your expectations, try these eight Metroidvanias that totally reinvent themselves halfway through.

As mentioned before, Xbox and Microsoft have supported and published many metroidvanias over the years, with some of the best they published undoubtedly being Moon Studios’ Ori and the Blind Forest and its sequel Ori and the Will of the Wisps.

The Blind Forest primarily takes places in the amazing Nibel Forest and its surrounding areas, but the first game teased other lands that were set to be explored in the Will of the Wisps.

Here, the cat-like spirit Ori and the owl Ku find themselves in the Niwen region, which has since suffered from the loss of its own Spirit Tree. This has resulted in Niwen’s inhabitants becoming corrupted and terrorizing each other, showcasing that the world of Ori is full of all kinds of magical forces and creatures.

Of all the Metroid games released over the years, the Metroid Prime subseries is arguably the most popular, with the series notably transitioning its sci-fi gameplay and maneuverability into first-person.

However, if players wanted to explore areas that feel bigger the more people learn about them, then there’s no better entry in the MP series to try out than Metroid Prime 2: Echoes.

Set roughly one year after the first MP, Metroid Prime 2: Echoes sees the bounty hunter Samus Aran travel to the planet Aether to rescue Galactic Federation Marines, only to discover that the planet is home to an advanced moth-like sentient race called the Luminoth and is at the center of a dimensional rift to an alternate hostile world called Dark Aether.

Some of the more popular metroidvania series may feature vast, enthralling worlds, but sometimes metroidvanias created by small independent studios are those with the largest worlds, with one such game being Valdis Story: Abyssal City.

The second title created by Endlessfluff Games, Valdis Story: Abyssal City follows the fantasy sword-wielding warrior Wyatt and the mage Reina as they set out in search of Wyatt's father only to wind up in the sunken city of Sitheil.

From here, the two young heroes are forced to not only face off against endless waves of angels and demons but also explore and learn more about the seemingly ever-expanding Atlantis-like city of Sitheil, with the game’s ending even suggesting that other magical cities may be hidden throughout the world.

Without a doubt, the best metroidvania to play where the world feels bigger the more you learn about it is Team Cherry’s 2D masterpiece Hollow Knight.

Set primarily in the subterranean insectoid kingdom of Hallownest, Hollow Knight follows an unnamed horned Knight as they set out to explore a world that has since fallen into ruin following an outbreak of a mysterious, violence-inducing infection.

During the Knight’s travels, players will not only be able to interact with numerous insectoid inhabitants and learn more about Hallownest’s past, but also explore several subregions of the kingdom and learn about other far away lands and realms such as the City of Tears, Crystal Peak, and Dream Realm.
