DOJ accuses former cyber executive of defrauding ransomware victims by secretly paying hackers

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The U.S. Department of Justice has indicted a former executive of a ransomware recovery firm after he allegedly defrauded victims of cyberattacks by claiming the company could get their encrypted files back.

According to federal prosecutors, Juan Ricardo Palacio Escobar, 52, was the chief technology officer at RansomwareHelp, which purported to offer data recovery services to victims of ransomware. The company’s website, which currently reads as “under maintenance,” claimed it could decrypt hacked data by using its proprietary technology, according to an archived copy of the site from earlier this year. RansomwareHelp touted a success rate of “over 99%.”

But the company allegedly had no technical ability to unscramble data and instead “negotiated with and paid a ransom to the ransomware actors to decrypt the victim data,” while charging the ransomware victim more and keeping the difference,” per the Justice Department’s statement.

Palacio Escobar then allegedly used the funds to buy several properties in Florida.

The DOJ said the FBI carried out an undercover operation in June, in part posing as a company that claimed it had been hit by ransomware. The company claimed to the undercover agents that it never negotiated with hackers or paid a ransom. But then Palacio Escobar paid the apparent hackers some $6,000 in cryptocurrency, without the knowledge that FBI agents were also posing as the ransomware hackers.

404 Media first reported the indictment last week against Palacio Escobar. He faces three charges of wire fraud, and could receive 20 years in prison on each count.

Palacio Escobar is the latest, albeit rare, example of cybersecurity professionals going rogue. Earlier this year, two ransomware negotiators working for cybersecurity companies were jailed on hacking and extortion charges, after being caught moonlighting with the ransomware gangs that they were ostensibly investigating. A third ransomware negotiator was also convicted of helping hackers deploy ransomware against several companies.

Last month, independent cybersecurity journalist Brian Krebs reported that FBI agents arrested a Canadian cybersecurity executive in Philadelphia on allegations that he was involved with the theft of FBI personnel records by the hacking and extortion gang ShinyHunters. The hacking group told TechCrunch that they took credit for the theft of FBI data in September, which they said they would not publicly release, a departure from its earlier extortion-based cyberattacks.

The federal charges against the Canadian cybersecurity executive relate to conspiracy to commit extortion, though much of the court docket — and his alleged links to the hacking group — remains under seal.
