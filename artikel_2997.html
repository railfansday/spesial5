Can Nvidia Become a $5 Trillion Company Once Again? The Answer May Surprise You

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Nvidia has slipped considerably lately, but its financial performance remains solid.

By Harsh Chauhan – Nov 27, 2026 at 5:46PM EST

Nvidia (NVDA +0.80%) became the first company in the world to achieve a $5 trillion market capitalization in October 2025, but that feat now seems to be in the distant past.

Nvidia has witnessed a spectacular fall from grace lately. From concerns about the company's circular financing deals to questions about the viability of the huge amount of money being spent on building artificial intelligence (AI) data centers to hotter-than-expected inflation, several factors have kept Nvidia stock under pressure in 2026. However, recent developments at the company have led to a massive erosion in investor confidence.

Nvidia stock has plummeted 29% over the past month. The losses would have been higher, but the reinstatement of co-founder Jensen Huang as CEO led to a sharp spike in Nvidia's shares earlier this week. However, investigations into the company's Chinese business and accounting practices continue to weigh on Nvidia. Its market cap has dropped to $3.6 trillion as of this writing, and it is no longer the largest company in the world.

But can this AI pioneer win back investor confidence and become a $5 trillion company once again? Let's find out.

A look at Nvidia's financial performance and outlook clearly indicates that the stock is trading at a deep discount right now. Its fiscal 2027 third-quarter revenue (for the three months ended Oct. 25) increased by an impressive 103% year over year to $115.8 billion. Non-GAAP earnings per share jumped by 111% year over year to $2.68.

Nvidia's revenue and earnings easily topped consensus expectations, but it was the guidance that turned out to be a major surprise. Despite the ongoing turmoil at the company, Nvidia management noted on the earnings call that the demand for its AI chips exceeds supply. The strong demand and Nvidia's strengthening supply chain explain why it expects $130 billion in revenue in the current quarter at the midpoint of its guidance range, a potential increase of 91% over the year-ago period.

Even better, Nvidia anticipates a 75% increase in revenue in fiscal 2028, up from the prior expectation of 70% growth. All this indicates that Nvidia continues to capitalize on the healthy AI infrastructure spending. Moreover, the guidance for next year clearly indicates that companies will continue to invest heavily in data centers.

That's not surprising, as Nvidia noted that cloud computing companies are sitting on a contractual backlog worth a whopping $2.5 trillion. This backlog has increased by $500 billion in just three months, implying robust demand for AI services. So, cloud computing companies, hyperscalers, and AI companies will have to keep investing in data center infrastructure to satisfy this massive backlog.

This will ensure strong demand for Nvidia's chips. Investors should also note that Nvidia is making tremendous progress in the fast-growing optical networking market. Its networking revenue surged to $21 billion last quarter from $8.2 billion in the year-ago period, fueled by the growing adoption of its co-packaged optics (CPO) switches.

Nvidia can sustain the solid momentum in this market. Bank of America is forecasting a 2.7x jump in Nvidia's CPO switch shipments next year, followed by a 2.65x increase in 2028. So, the company still has impressive growth drivers that should send its top line higher. This is precisely what analysts predict.

NVDA Revenue Estimates for Current Fiscal Year data by YCharts

Its revenue growth estimates have inched higher following the latest quarterly report. In fact, analysts seem to be underestimating Nvidia's fiscal 2028 revenue growth potential, as the chart above points to a 71% increase in revenue next year. Also, its top line is expected to jump by 28% in fiscal 2029 to almost $927 billion.

Nvidia trades at 10 times sales right now, a premium to the U.S. technology sector's price-to-sales ratio of 5.9. The company's impressive growth justifies that premium, though it should ideally trade at a much higher sales multiple under normal circumstances. But even if Nvidia trades in line with the tech sector's average sales multiple at the end of fiscal 2029 and its revenue reaches $927 billion, the company's market cap can jump to $5.6 trillion.

That's 55% higher than Nvidia's current market cap. So, Nvidia could reclaim the $5 trillion market cap milestone once again. Does that make this AI stock a no-brainer buy?

Nvidia stock is definitely worth buying based on pure financial results, fundamentals, outlook, and valuation. The stock is oversold considering the points discussed above. However, there are other factors at play here that are impacting the stock.

Reuters recently reported that Nvidia could be at the center of an investigation by the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) into its accounting practices. So, anyone buying Nvidia stock right now will most likely need to endure volatility until the investigations are over. However, if Nvidia continues to deliver financially and gets its house in order, it can indeed win back investor confidence and soar impressively once again.

In the end, investors' decision to buy Nvidia at these levels depends on their risk profile. Those with a higher risk appetite can consider accumulating the stock while it is beaten down. However, conservative investors may want to look at safer options to capitalize on the AI boom and wait for the dust to settle around Nvidia before taking a call.
