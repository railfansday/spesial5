Midterms 2026: Here's Exactly What I'm Doing With My Investment Portfolio Right Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Investors don’t need to do a lot of post-election portfolio tinkering, but some ETFs may be worth examining.

By Todd Shriber – Nov 21, 2026 at 10:30PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Vanguard Energy ETF ›

Elections have a way of eliciting emotional, borderline-visceral reactions in the investment community. Still, the knee-jerk responses are often confined to the immediate aftermath and, over time, cooler heads ultimately prevail.

For those in the camp of doing nothing with their portfolios following the blue wave, there's nothing wrong with that line of thinking. That's a calm, rational approach, and one that is validated by history.

Since 1950, the S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%) has been positive in the year following every midterm election, with an average gain of 15% over those 12 months. So if history holds up, now isn't the time to sell stocks.

Investors don’t need to do a lot post-election portfolio tinkering, but these ETFs may be worth examining. Image source: Getty Images.

On the other hand, changes in congressional majorities may usher in opportunities for tactical investors to capitalize on the new legislative landscape. Likewise (and perhaps surprisingly), some sectors and industries previously considered vulnerable to the Democratic sweep may prove to be less so. With that in mind, here are some exchange-traded funds (ETFs) I'm spying following the election.

It's not surprising that the healthcare sector is in the spotlight following Election Day, but this is an example of investors needing to be tactical. Said another way, a standard pharmaceutical-heavy ETF may not be the best way to capitalize on the shift of power in Congress. That's why I'm evaluating the iShares U.S. Healthcare Providers ETF (IHF +0.74%).

This $1.6 billion ETF, which turned 20 years old in May, holds shares of health insurance providers and service operators. Those could be some of the best healthcare stocks to own at a time when Democrats could push to restore, even in part, the Affordable Care Act's enhanced premium subsidies that fell by the wayside at the end of 2025. There's also chatter about momentum to add long-term care coverage to Medicare, which could benefit some of this ETF's 61 holdings.

The new congressional majority could also scrap or significantly limit planned cuts to Medicaid. If Democrats are successful in leveraging government funding to increase insurance coverage, some of this ETF's holdings will benefit from higher enrollment and reduced exposure to uncompensated care.

Be advised that this ETF is top-heavy, as UnitedHealth and CVS Health combine for nearly a third of its portfolio.

The Vanguard Energy ETF (VDE -0.73%) and the Vanguard Information Technology ETF (VGT -7.00%) are both examples of sector ETFs that were decked following Election Day. Since Nov. 4, the first trading day after Election Day, the energy fund dipped 5.3% while its tech counterpart slumped 15.3%.

The reasons differ. As has been widely noted, data center stocks are retreating not only because Democrats will control Congress but also because the party holds 30 of 50 governors' mansions, including some in states where data centers were front and center on Election Day.

Clearly, rhetoric opposed to data centers is effective on the campaign trail. Still, the Vanguard tech ETF may not be worth dumping today because it's hard to get data center moratoriums across the legislative finish line. Before Election Day, only New York state was successful in climbing that mountain.

As for the Vanguard Energy ETF, its post-election weakness isn't surprising. Renewable energy stocks often rally when polls and election results favor Democrats while the fossil fuel names that dominate this Vanguard ETF languish. That scenario played out this year.

Don't rush to abandon or ignore this Vanguard fund. The reasoning is simple: The energy sector has noteworthy short-term earnings momentum, and the group is proving to be an effective portfolio hedge while high interest rates diminish the diversification benefits of bonds.

On that note, it should be acknowledged that energy stocks have been negatively correlated with the broader market for much of this year, implying that the Vanguard Energy ETF can enhance a portfolio’s diversification.
