Zepp Health (ZEPP) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Premium Balance and Active activations rose sharply despite supply constraints.

By Motley Fool Transcribing – Nov 23, 2026 at 9:43AM EST

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

Zepp Health (ZEPP -4.33%) reported a sequential revenue increase as it shifted toward a premium product mix despite persistent supply chain and macroeconomic challenges. Management stated that the company is resolving production constraints for key models like the Balance Ultra and Helio Strap while expanding its physical retail presence through partners like Best Buy and REI. The company reported a narrowing adjusted operating loss and continued its strategy of converting short-term debt to long-term obligations to manage its balance sheet.

Leon Cheng Deng: Thank you, Wang. Greetings, everyone. Let me talk you through our financial performance for the third quarter of 2026 and provide some additional context on the operating environment, our cost structure, balance sheet and outlook. Starting with our top line. Revenue for the third quarter come in at USD 68.3 million, within the guidance range we previously provided. Revenue increased 7.5% sequentially while declining 9.9% from the same quarter last year. During the quarter, our performance continued to reflect a combination of the broader macroeconomic environment, product availability and the timing of our channel expansion. Across the consumer electronics industry, we continue to see a challenging and competitive operating environment.

Inflationary pressures and broader economic uncertainties have made consumers more selective in their discretionary spending. At the same time, we believe that the longer-term trends supporting health, fitness and performance-oriented wearables remain attractive. In Q3, certain products, particularly Balance 3 and Helio Strap continued to experience supply constraints, which limited our ability to fully capture demand and realize the benefits of our improved product portfolio. In addition, as we expand our distribution channels, particularly through physical retail, there's naturally a timing difference between the initial investments, product placement inventory deployment and the eventual revenue contribution. On a positive note, we continue to see encouraging sales momentum within our active series, demonstrating the appeal of our expanding product portfolio.

Turning to gross margin. As mentioned before, our gross margin continued to reflect a combination of product mix dynamics and external cost pressures. Gross margin for the third quarter was 35% compared with 37.4% in the second quarter of 2026 and 38.2% in the same period last year. There are 3 primary factors affecting our gross margin performance. First, we continue to face unfavorable foreign exchange movements, particularly the appreciation of the RMB against the U.S. dollars, which increased our U.S. dollars denominated operating and product costs. Second, we experienced continued inflation in memory chip costs. This is an industrial-wide challenge affecting consumer electronics manufacturers.

Higher demand for the memory components, together with supply constraints and changes in industry production priorities have created a significant upward pressure on component pricing. These cost pressures have become increasingly visible in our cost of goods sold and will remain an important factor in our margin management. Third, supply constraints affecting certain products limited the revenue contribution from some of our higher-margin models. This prevented us from fully recognizing the favorable product mix that we had anticipated. Importantly, when we compare our gross margin with the prior quarter, we also need to consider the tariff-related benefit affecting our Q2 results. On a normalized basis, excluding that benefit, our underlying gross margin improved sequentially.

On a year-over-year basis, gross margin declined, primarily reflecting the combined impact of foreign exchange and higher memory costs. However, what is particularly encouraging is that the continued improvement in our product mix helped offset a meaningful portion of these external cost pressures. This demonstrates the progress we have made in strengthening our premium product portfolio and improving the underlying economics of our business. Looking ahead, we remain focused on mitigating this industry-wide cost pressures through supply chain optimization, engineering efficiencies, disciplined pricing and continued improvement in product mix. While the external cost environment remains challenging, we believe these actions will help reinforce our longer-term margin structure. Now turning to operating expenses.

During the third quarter, our total adjusted operating expenses were approximately USD 34.2 million compared with $34.8 million in the second quarter and $28.6 million in the same period last year. Our operating expenses decreased sequentially by USD 0.6 million, while increasing year-over-year by approximately $5.6 million. The year-over-year increase was primarily driven by selling and marketing expenses, while we continue to demonstrate discipline in managing our G&A costs. Let me walk you through the 3 expenses categories. Starting with selling and marketing. Selling and marketing expenses were approximately USD 18 million compared with $12 million in the third quarter of last year.

The $6 million year-over-year increase was primarily attributed to $1.9 million investment across online and offline channels for content refreshes, in-store displays and holiday season readiness and $1.5 million of launch campaigns for new products. The remainder of the increase reflects $1.4 million for marathon, UTMD and media sponsorships and $1.2 million related to the HYROX and New Athlete partnerships. I would like to provide some additional context to these investments. For our online business, we continue to prioritize efficient marketing, product visibility and customer conversion while maintaining discipline around promotional spending and channel economics. At the same time, our offline retail expansion is an important part of our long-term growth strategy.

Physical retail allows consumers to experience our products directly, strengthen brand credibility and creates additional opportunities to reach new customers. However, expanding into those channels require upfront investments in retail presence, merchandising, brand activation and distribution support. The financial benefit of these investments do not necessarily materialize in the same quarter in which the expenses were occurred. We, therefore, view these activities as targeted investments in our future growth rather than an increase in our underlying fixed cost base. Importantly, we will continue to evaluate these investments based on their effectiveness while balancing near-term profitability with longer-term market development. Moving on R&D expenses, which were USD 10.7 million, broadly stable compared with the same period last year and previous quarter.

We continue to invest in product innovation, proprietary technologies, software capabilities, including AI and our broader wearable ecosystems. At the same time, we remain disciplined in our resource allocation and continue to focus on improving development efficiency and return on our R&D investments. Turning to G&A expenses, which were USD 5.6 million, decreasing both sequentially and year-over-year. This is an encouraging development and demonstrates our continued efforts to streamline our overhead structure and improve operational efficiency. While certain external factors, particularly foreign exchange movements remain outside our control, we continue to actively manage the areas of our cost structure that we can influence.

Overall, our approach remains consistent, maintaining structured cost discipline while selectively investing in product innovation, brand development and distribution capabilities that support sustainable future growth. Looking ahead, we expect our overall operating expenses level to moderate as we continue to optimize our cost structure. Turning to profitability. Our operating loss for the third quarter was USD 10.3 million, narrowing by approximately $1 million quarter-over-quarter, but widening by $10.7 million year-over-year. Our adjusted net loss showed a similar trend with a year-over-year decrease of approximately $10.9 million.

The decrease was -- the increase was primarily attributed to USD 6 million in higher selling and marketing expenses to support new product launches and brand building initiatives, and USD 3.2 million unfavorable foreign exchange impact and then a USD 2.7 million gross profit headwind from higher memory and other component costs, partially mitigated by a more favorable product mix. These pressures were partially offset by USD 1.1 million in G&A expenses savings. While our year-over-year profitability was affected by external headwinds and strategic investments, we remain focused on disciplined cost structure management and improving our financial performance. Moving on to working capital. We continue to manage our inventory carefully throughout the quarter.

Our inventory balance stood at $68.6 million at the end of September compared with $62.4 million at the end of the second quarter and $87.7 million a year ago. We believe this demonstrates continued progress in inventory management and our efforts to align procurement, manufacturing and product availability more closely with market demand. At the same time, we remain focused on ensuring sufficient availability of key products, particularly as we approach the holiday selling season. Turning to our balance sheet and the liquidity position. We ended the quarter with $99 million in cash compared with $106 million at the end of second quarter and $102 million a year ago.

The sequential decline primarily reflected losses incurred during the quarter, while we continue to actively manage working capital and preserve liquidity. As discussed previously, we remain focused on optimizing our debt structure by extending maturities and reducing near-term refinancing pressure while keeping overall borrowing levels broadly stable. During the third quarter, we converted an additional $26.6 million of short-term borrowing into long-term obligations, bringing the year-to-date total to USD 56.5 million. In addition, cumulative debt retirement since the beginning of 2023 reached approximately $38.1 million. Looking ahead, we expect to continue extending our debt maturity profile, supported by sufficient financing headroom. We remain committed to maintain a prudent capital structure and adequate liquidity to support our operations and long-term growth.

We also stay committed to our share repurchase program as a means of returning value to shareholders. As of September, we had used approximately $17.8 million under the $20 million authorization. Furthermore, our Board of Directors have recently approved an additional 1-year extension of the existing share repurchase program. As we look ahead, our priorities remain clear. We'll continue to strengthen our premium product mix, improve the economics of our entry-level products, expand our distribution presence in a disciplined manner and build deeper engagement with athletes and fitness communities. At the same time, we will maintain tight control over structural operating costs, working capital and capital allocation.
