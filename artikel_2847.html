Game Preview: San Antonio Spurs vs Detroit Pistons

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

I know that (as my colleague Charlie Thaddeus pointed out) there's a tendency to embellish in the basketball blogosphere and on social media, but I feel like the significance of this win streak has really snuck up on us all.

And I get it. It's a weak portion of the schedule, no doubt about it. The Spurs have only played four teams currently at .500 or better during this run, with the benefit of some injury absences.

And yes, it's very early in the season, as opposed to when everyone else in rhythm and fighting for seeding and playoff/play-in berths. The competitive desperation just isn't on the same level.

Still, that's five out of twelve games, and you can really only play the teams in front of you. That's just how it is.

Consider for a moment that with a win against the Pistons, this win streak would tie for 4th longest in franchise history.

Do you know how many times the Spurs won 13+ games in the Duncan era? Just five times.

How about the David Robinson era? Just four.

[Side note: How crazy is it that the combination of Duncan and Robinson only resulted in one 13+ game win streak?!]

Consider that if the Spurs win this game, their next three games will be against three of the bottom four teams in the Western Conference (The Clippers, The Pelicans, and The Kings).

They will have a very good shot at extending this streak to 16 games, which would be the 3rd longest streak in Spurs team history.

Tim and David only managed to win 16+ consecutive games once each.*

(*In the regular season. Duncan did it twice if you count the combined 20-game win streak in 2012 from April 12th to the end of Game 2 of the Western Conference Finals. Personally, I try to forget that one.)

Consider that the Spurs had two(!) separate 11-game win streaks last season, and went undefeated over the month of February, and still couldn't get to 12, much less 13.

Consider that they've done this without the debut of an important off-season addition in Tobias Harris, and with short absences from both De'Aaron Fox and Devin Vassell.

It might not feel like it, but all things considered, this streak is a pretty big deal.

The Pistons could give them real trouble, though.

While they don't have as explosive an offense as the Spurs (who are 4th in points, 3rd in Offensive Rating vs. 12th and 13th), the Pistons rebound well enough (13th), pass well enough (10th in assists, 9th in assist percentage), and both avoid turnovers (7th in turnover percentage and assist-to-turnover ration) and force them (5th in steals, 2nd in blocks) to such an extent that they really have no glaring weaknesses to exploit.

The Spurs will have to play patient, tough, mistake free basketball against them more than most.

Even the Pistons' three-point shooting can take advantage of San Antonio sloppiness, as they sit in the top 10 in three-point percentage (9th), and their Defensive Rating is only a few ticks behind San Antonio's (6th vs. 3rd).

Yes, the Spurs will have to play exceptional ball to put Detroit away. The good news is that they have been.

Over the last 12 games the Spurs are 2nd in points scored, 1st in +/-, 1st in limiting fouls, 1st in limiting turnovers (shocking, I know), 1st in rebounds, 1st in blocks, 1st in offensive rating, 1st in net rating, 1st in player impact estimate, 2nd in 2nd chance points, 2nd in fast-break points, 1st in points in the paint, 1st in limiting points off of turnovers, and 5th in field-goal percentage. *whew*

And all of that in spite of being 23rd in three-pointers made, 15th in three-point percentage, 13th in effective field goal percentage, and 13th in free-throw attempt rate.

Basically, you have to pray that this team has a cold shooting night.

And (as the Mavericks recently learned) that still might not be enough. God forbid they starting hitting their open looks (they're near the bottom of the league in actual shooting vs quality of looks).

I know they made the Finals last season, but I just cannot emphasize how special this Spurs team really is.

If they get even a little hot from outside, my money's on them.

But my money would be on them anyway. They're just that good.

Spurs Injuries: Tobias Harris: Out (Calf), Jayden Quaintance: Out (Knee)

Pistons Injuries: Jalen Duren: Out (Hamstring), Duncan Robinson: Out (Quad), Isaiah Joe: Doubtful (Hip)
