Kirk Cousins is still a Costco hot dog, but a wiener the Vikings needed

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

There's been a lot of confusion since I first compared Kirk Cousins to a Costco hot dog over three years ago. It was received as a knock on low-price, emulsified, forced-meat — when in reality it was all about the fact that the Costco hot dog is always going to be serviceable, but it's never going to wow you.

Fast forward to November of 2026, and I'm still resolute that Cousins is a Costco hot dog, but I've absolutely changed my belief that the Vikings can't win with a big-box cafeteria tube of mystery meat at quarterback. This Minnesota team might be the best in the NFC right now, and everyone should take notice.

A chasm separates the Vikings of 2023 from now, and it's entirely due to Brian Flores' defense. In the past we've seen these Cousins-led teams live and die by the offense, routinely turning games into whether or not they can win in a shootout. That naturally meant that everything was on Kirk, and far too often he just couldn't do enough when it mattered — hence the hot dog.

This year it's different, and Minnesota's 30-10 win over the Packers typified how much this team has morphed over the years. While it was far from an incredible Cousins game, finishing with 207 passing yards, 2 TDs, and a pick, it was good enough to secure a blowout win, because the defense is just that dominant. We knew that getting an upgrade at quarterback would help revamp the Vikes, but an underrated quality beyond scoring, was how a better offense would have a domino effect on transforming opponents into one-dimensional offenses, allowing the defense to feast even more.

The Packers are known for their explosive feast-or-famine passing game, but this is also a team that is 5-2 this season when they rush for 60+ yards, and a perfect 3-0 when they run for more than 70. By scoring touchdowns early, the Vikings forced Green Bay away from its run game, pressuring them to fully lean on the passing game. This technique worked, as Flores was able to generate eight sacks on Jordan Love, and take away the deep routes that have been the offensive engine of the Packers. The longest reception the Packers pulled off all day was 32 yards, and this was a team that completed multiple 40+ yard passes against the Patriots in their Week 9 win.

What happened in this NFC North matchup should truly scare the rest of the NFC. The Packers boast one of the best deep passing offenses in the NFL, and the Vikings completely neutered them, despite having a reputation for possessing a less-than-stellar secondary. The best (perhaps only) way to beat the Vikings' defense is to overwhelm them with explosive plays, and one of the league's most explosive offenses just failed miserably against them — twice this season.

The difference in the second meeting was having Cousins under center, who is able to do enough to keep things ticking along. In that first meeting, we saw Carson Wentz play serviceable football, which let the defense shine. The difference is that Wentz only managed 133 passing yards in that first game, while Cousins topped 200. It's these thin margins that make a mammoth difference when you're a defensive football team, and it's why Minnesota knew they couldn't keep chipping away with a mediocre QB room.

If we look ahead to the remainder of the regular season, the Vikings play: 49ers, Falcons, Panthers, Patriots, Lions, Commanders, Jets, Bears. That looks a lot like five easy wins, and then three toss-ups. There is a very real chance that Minnesota finishes the season at 14-3, which wouldn't just win the NFC North, but give them a first-round bye.

We saw Kirk Cousins struggle to break through with the Vikings for years. In 2026 he might just do it. Minnesota learned that they had to go old in order to become new again, and it's beautiful to see. Even if Cousins is still a Costco hot dog.

It's beginning to feel like this is Brock Purdy's MVP award to lose. I'm not sure he's the best player in football this year, but we know that MVP is a quarterback award.

Each week we see Purdy play near-perfect football and there's nothing slowing him down. He leads the NFL in QBR, he's completing almost 70% of his passes this season, and when it comes to his TD/INT ratio, he's on pace to finish at 36/10 this year. There will be a challenge from Jared Goff and Joe Burrow (who is putting up stupid numbers), but in my eyes Purdy is playing better football right now.

On Sunday he completely dismantled the Cowboys, finishing 22-for-28, 389 yards, 3 TD and 0 INT. Just an incredible day that is solidifying an incredible season.

That's probably it for the Houston Texans, and it's a damn shame. C.J. Stroud was individually brilliant on Sunday, the entire Texans offense played well — but the defense dropped the ball once more. Houston now sits at 3-6, and it's very difficult to envision them catching the Jaguars in the AFC South.

Whatever happened to the Texans' defense this season needs to be studied. The team went from being a unit so scary there was hype they might make the Super Bowl just on the back of their aggressiveness, but the offense let them down. Now we have the reverse, where the offense closed the gap and the defense faltered.

Is there a chance Houston could still pull this out? Absolutely. They have a soft schedule down the stretch, but they're in a massive hole. Three teams are currently at six wins in the AFC North, meaning there's already math to be done to find a magic number for the Texans. It's an uphill battle.

I was really, really impressed with Fernando Mendoza's first start for the Raiders, even if it was a pretty pedestrian game. The box score shows fairly mediocre stats, but I saw the No. 1 overall pick do a lot of the little things well, and didn't appear lose in the weeds at any point.

There are obviously things to work on. You want him to look a little deeper instead of settling for shallow passes, and perhaps hang in the pocket a touch longer before flushing out — but this was against the Seahawks' defense, which is one of the most creative and confusing units in the NFL. It wouldn't have blamed him for coming out extremely flat, but instead he managed the game really well under the circumstances.

It's very early in the process, but I saw flashes that he's the real deal.

It's abundantly clear that the playoffs run through the AFC North when it comes to the wild card, much as it does with the NFC North. The Bengals and Browns both winning does less to hurt Pittsburgh's chances overall, and more to the state of the playoff race down the line.

Teams are going to need to be well over .500 to hope to get a spot. Really only Kansas City or Denver (depending on how the AFC West shakes out) has a chance of nabbing a spot. That means if you're the Texans or Patriots right now, well, it was a rough Sunday of football.

If you were unfortunate enough to spend your afternoon watching Panthers vs. Saints then you experienced a very weird five minutes in the second quarter when the announce team became hopelessly obsessed with the history of the Turducken.

I'm not going to gripe with announcers needing to fill dead air with banter, but this was an entire Saints drive in a close game. Fox kept cutting to the booth showing a Turducken, then showed archival footage of the Turducken, before throwing down to the sideline TWICE to have their sideline reporter eat some Turducken.

It's not even Thanksgiving for another week and a half. What are we even doing here? Naturally fans were confused too.

I don't like football when it's sponsored by Big Turducken.
