Rangers should lay the groundwork for Pierce Charles deal after update

Rangers must lay the groundwork to make a fresh attempt to sign Manchester City goalkeeper Pierce Charles next summer.

The Gers were heavily linked with the Northern Ireland international in this summer's window, but ultimately lost out when he left Sheffield Wednesday.

City ultimately won the race for the 21-year-old's signature, and tied him down to a lengthy five-year deal in the process.

Charles has since been excelling in the Championship on loan with QPR, where he has already notched three clean sheets so far this season.

The youngster has a growing reputation behind him already, and he has only enhanced that with his early-season form at Loftus Road.

With Man City's financial situation still firmly up in the air, Rangers must sense an opportunity to pounce next summer.

Charles has excelled in the Championship so far this term, and has quickly established himself as one of the division's brightest young talents.

That is highlighted by Football Ranked on their X account, which has ranked Charles as the league's second best-performing U23 player in terms of Elo.

Elo is a numerical rating designed to assess the performance of players compared to other players of a similar level.

With the final verdict from Man City's 114 charges still looming, the futures of each and every player on their books are firmly up in the air.

That should be enough to see Rangers ready a potential swoop for Charles next summer, in case a fire sale is triggered at the Etihad Stadium.

Their interest in Charles was first established when Danny Rohl was at the helm, but the starlet has surely now shown enough to justify a move even without the German's connection with him.

Former Aberdeen chief executive Keith Wyness told Football Insider on Monday that he wouldn't be surprised to see Vanja Dragojevic's agent push for a move away from Rangers in January.

The Serbian only moved to Ibrox in the summer, but has hardly featured in the Gers' starting XIs in recent months.

Wyness expects the player's agent to start "putting word in" over a potential move, if Rangers cannot find a pathway for him to get more game time.

Dragojevic is a highly-rated young player, but has not had the chance to prove himself at Ibrox just yet.
