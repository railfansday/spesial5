The Best Gifts Under $25 for Everyone on Your List (2026)

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Twenty-five bucks doesn’t go as far as it used to. As recently as a decade ago, $25 could fill up your gas tank or buy a pretty good gift, with plenty of affordable options for just about every interest and occasion. Nowadays, it’s a struggle to even find a $25 takeout meal for one person, and average national gas prices are inching toward $5 a gallon. And we all know wages have not kept up.

Good news on the gifting front, though: There are still plenty of thoughtful, interesting items out there to be had for $25, which are sure to appeal to everyone from coworkers and neighbors to relatives you don’t know well and the friend who seems to have everything. At least one person on the WIRED Reviews team has tested and vetted all things on this list—often more than one—to ensure it is indeed a good gift you’d be proud to give. Check out more of our gift guides to find something for everyone on your list this year.

Updated November 2026: I've swapped out some gifts for a keychain multitool, a self-watering plant, a candle in a unique jar, city-themed ornaments, a condiment, olive oil, and a fun umbrella. I've also ensured up-to-date links and prices.

WIRED senior Gear editor Julian Chokkattu recommends this colorful acrylic desk accessory that keeps pencils, scissors, and other large items on top and smaller items like paper clips (or, in Julian’s case as a phone reviewer, SIM ejection pins) on the bottom. Shown is the pink-and-amber color combination, but there’s also a blue-and-green option.

I’ve used Field Notes for years—they’re lightweight, sturdy, and perfectly pocketable. The National Parks Series elevates what would otherwise be a utilitarian notebook to its giftable status, with artist-created covers inspired by our nation’s natural wonders—from Acadia to the Hawai'i Volcanoes.

My city recently got its own brick-and-mortar Muji store, and if you’ve never visited the famed Japanese retailer in person, it’s basically a mecca for stationery and desk accessories. These pens, in particular, are famous for their smooth ink and precision tips; they make a perfect companion for the Field Notes notebooks, at left.

These drink cozies are WIRED reviewer Nena Farrell's go-to white elephant gift (“It’s like playing Barbie with your drinks,” she says). I like them because they remind me of Will Ferrell and his friend Harper Steele in the movie Will & Harper, where they go on a road trip, dress up their drinks in these exact cozies, and come up with ridiculous names for them, like Cornelius Danderhoff. It reminds me of road trips I took with friends in college, which reminds me that I’m old, which … makes me want a drink.

Set the right vibe in any room with this WIRED-recommended, Matter-compatible smart light bulb that features millions of colors, scenes, and effects you can control with Alexa, Google Assistant, or the Govee app. You can also sync it to music with the latter, using the microphone on your phone.

Let your giftee be heard wherever they go with their very own Bluetooth microphone. Former WIRED A/V editor Parker Hall especially liked this one for its series of playback and mixing controls, in addition to adjustable volume, echo effects, and the ability to play, skip, or pause your backing track.

A sleep mask is a must for proper sleep hygiene, and WIRED reviewer and sleep-mask aficionado Louryn Strampe loves the roomy fit of this 22-momme silk model from Quince. It’s half the price of similar 100-percent silk sleep masks, and there’s free shipping to keep it extra affordable. Choose from among 12 color options, including Eucalyptus, Espresso, Pink, Terra Cotta, and Light Blue.

Crowned our best earplugs for sleep, these silicone Loop Quiet 2s are capable of blocking out 24 dB and fit flush to your head, ensuring that comfort when you lie on your side. The little handle makes them easy to pull out, and they come in four fun colors with a variety of ear tips to so your giftee can get the perfect fit.

Help your giftee’s personal space stay cool and fresh with this 4-in-1 fan, the best handheld fan I’ve personally tried. Set it on a table or desk, hold it in your hand, clip it onto something with the bottom carabiner, or wear it around your neck. It’s quiet enough to not interrupt your conversation, and it comes in four fun colors.

My entire family is obsessed with this finishing salt, which is harvested in Netarts Bay, Oregon, just a couple of hours from our house. The large, irregular crystals look (and taste) fantastic on meat, roasted vegetables, and desserts, offering the perfect amount of crunch. Either the salt itself or the stylish arched salt cellar—handmade by Pigeon Toe Ceramics in Portland, Oregon—would make a terrific gift on its own.

A sharp, trusty chef’s knife is a staple in any kitchen, and Kiwi knives are beloved by multiple members of the WIRED Reviews team for their ability to hold an edge just as well as knives that cost 10 times as much. I’ve had a Kiwi model in my own knife drawer for more than 20 years, and it’s still going strong.

Who doesn’t love a colorful Le Creuset Dutch oven? The real-deal, enameled cast iron version may not be in your gifting budget, but the 8-ounce stoneware cocotte is perfect as a salt cellar, garlic roaster, or even a bowl to eat from. Most of the classic colors are available, so you can match your giftee’s kitchen or other Le Creuset pots they may already own.

This single-origin extra-virgin olive oil from Morocco features a QR code for harvest-date and batch traceability. It comes in a giftable, opaque yellow glass bottle. It’s not too bold or spicy, so it’s a great pick for someone whose palate you’re unsure of. (The unfiltered version has a richer flavor, but the bottle is plainer.) Since it’s only $14, there’s room in the budget to pair it with a bottle of za’atar seasoning or some decorative dipping dishes.

Granted, I make a lot of pizza at home, but this chili-crisp–adjacent condiment is one of my favorite things I’ve tried all year. It’s the perfect mixture of Calabrian chili flakes, fennel seeds, oregano, Parmesan cheese, garlic, and sun-dried tomatoes, ready to transform pasta, bagels, sandwiches, grilled meat, or even—as I have done many times—a plain cheese pizza. I especially recommend the truffle crunch, but either version would make a great gift, packaged with an inexpensive pizza wheel.

I’ve tested all manner of plant-delivery services for my guide to the Best Gifts for Plant Lovers. Most charge $50 or even more for a delivered houseplant. You can provide the same gift service for half the price by ordering one of these trendy tropical plants from Lowe’s or Walmart, which comes in Costa Farms’ new Wick & Grow self-watering pot. (Order for pickup at Lowe’s and save even more) I tried this with a Chameleon ZZ Plant, and it arrived intact, healthy, and beautiful, with nary a crumb of spilled dirt.

I received this dehydrated fruiting block in a box for my birthday a couple of years ago, and I was super impressed with how quickly and abundantly the oyster mushrooms grew—right on my windowsill. Growing mushrooms isn’t always easy, but this is a fun and educational method for kids and adults alike.

Tropical Monstera deliciosa houseplants are all the rage right now, but many people may not know that they actually bear fruit. It’s rarely produced outside the monstera’s native climate, so you may not have the opportunity to taste it, but you can get the gist with this one-of-a-kind monstera fruit taffy. (It’s tart!)

Being able to keep tabs on keys, luggage, and other gear is critical, and these Bluetooth trackers work just as well as AirTags for a quarter of the price. A pack of four with little lanyards and fun stickers retails for $24 but is often on sale for even less. They work with Apple’s Find My and take just seconds to sync to your phone. I have four of these in use in my household, and they are truly indistinguishable from AirTags in both tracking and quality. There’s also a rechargeable set that works with both iOS and Android, but we haven’t tried that one.

WIRED contributing reviewer Alexis Berger organized multiple zones of her house with these QR Code stickers. They come with a free app that will scan and catalog all of a drawer or box’s contents using AI—that way you can just look on your phone for the location of an item instead of spending all day tearing apart your home and garage.

WIRED editor and travel expert Les Shu swears by this TSA-friendly carabiner keychain, which has been part of his everyday carry for the past six years. It’s easier than carrying a real multi-tool, and he’s been able to use it for breaking down boxes, as a safety device when walking at night, and more.

I cannot even begin to list all the times I’ve been glad to be carrying a reusable bag, but the ones I usually have in my purse are small and flimsy. WIRED editor Harry Rabinowitz swears by these bags, which can carry 50 pounds yet fold into a flat 5-by-5-inch pouch. They’re also machine-washable and come in dozens of fun colors and prints.

WIRED contributing reviewer and former barista Pete Cottell loves this espresso accessory for its durability and design—and it can be found in seemingly every coffee shop where I live in the Pacific Northwest. However, even if you don’t have an espresso machine, the uses for a little spouted measuring cup are endless. I have a plastic one and use it for measuring small quantities of liquid ingredients and fertilizing plants alike.

WIRED reviewer Boutayna Chokrane recommends this Hydro Flask in her guide to the best travel mugs for its stainless steel rim that makes for smooth sipping. I have the tumbler version, but I wish it had a handle like this mug. Mine is not easy to hold when it’s cold outside and I’m wearing gloves. (Mug is hand-wash-only.)

Though this simple warmer only turns on when you have a mug on it, it was effective and reliable when we tested it, and it makes a great gift for anyone who works from home and ends up having their coffee or tea run cold during meetings. I bought one for my husband for Christmas last year, and it’s still going strong.

WIRED reviewer Louryn Strampe has this mini gaming controller, which is compatible with Nintendo Switch, PC, macOS, iOS, and Android devices. It’s tiny, cute, and actually works—she uses it to play Stardew Valley and Bastion on her iPhone. If your teen loves gaming, it would make a great stocking stuffer, too. (It gets about 10 hours of battery life per USB-C charge.)

Dr. Squatch soap is a pretty safe gift for teen boys (source: I have a teen boy), and a three-pack makes it more likely they’ll at least get one scent that’s a favorite. There are always lots of fun collabs each season, too—this year’s include Tajín seasoning and FIFA.

Yes, they are $1 over the limit, but these bag charms are worth the extra buck. They include little roller-ball bottles of the viral Sand + Fog fragrance oils and come in a ready-made gift box. The charms look great on bags and backpacks, and would be a perfect stocking stuffer or angel-tree gift for teen girls.

WIRED reviewer Simon Hill’s family loves this hilarious drawing game that’s based on Telephone. Sketch a visual representation of a word or phrase and then pass it around the table for people to guess what it is. It’s great for parties and can accommodate four to eight people. The recommended minimum age is 12, but Simon’s 9-year-old daughter loves it.

These puzzles look like normal landscapes but contain a secret section not pictured on the box, with something jarringly incongruent, like a dog pooping or a cat barfing. Remove the red sleeve and your giftee will have no idea anything is amiss until they put it together. I gave one of these to my husband, and the ruse worked flawlessly and was just as hilarious as I’d hoped.

I have given these boxes countless times with either a gift certificate or homemade coupon inside, and they’re always a hit, especially in group settings. Many of the dozen or so designs are toilet-themed, but each one is just serious-looking enough that it usually takes people a few long (and hilarious) minutes to realize it’s not a real item.

Nothing says city living like pigeons and raccoons in the trash. Spend a few dollars over the $25 budget to commemorate your—or someone else’s—urban ecosystem with these ornaments that are hand-sewn from wool felt. Each one comes with an attached loop for hanging and a little linen storage bag to keep it safe between holidays. Or you could just leave it out year-round, as I’m tempted to do.

Consumable gifts are key for people with limited space, but this brand serves double duty. They’re great candles in their own right, made with vegan soy wax. They also come in a variety of beautiful lidded jars that are reusable for all sorts of purposes, from Q-tips in the bathroom to planters to pens on a desk. Scents are hit or miss, but Starlit Golden Fir was my favorite of the ones I tried, with great throw and a true natural pine aroma. You can choose from a variety of jar styles that fit your giftee’s style, including floral motif, hobnail, art deco, ribbed, and more.

City dwellers do a lot of walking, which means they’re often caught unexpectedly in the rain. ShedRain is a frequent flier in our guide to the Best Umbrellas, and for good reason—they're small and portable but can stand up to wind and rough handling. This model comes in a variety of fun designs and colors; I tested the colorful Metro style during a wet, windy weekend on the Oregon Coast (yes, we do in fact use umbrellas in the Pacific Northwest), and I liked it so much that it earned a permanent spot in my bag.

WIRED reviewer and esteemed outdoorsman Scott Gilbertson swears by this tiny telescoping bellows that will help any fire get going again, whether that’s a campfire or your wood stove at home. It stretches out to be surprisingly long and is a must-have for wet firewood, bug-out bags, or even backyard cookouts.

I carried a Nalgene all through college in the late ’90s before the brand’s polycarbonate was found to contain BPA. Now Nalgenes are back with BPA-free Tritan Renew plastic, which makes them lightweight and perfect for both outdoor activities and everyday use.

I’m a Leatherman kind of person myself, but sometimes you just need a bigger knife. In those cases, you won’t do better than this folding, portable number featured in our Buy It for Life guide. French Alps–based Opinel has been making knives since 1890, and the No. 8, in particular, is so well-designed, it’s been displayed in museums.
