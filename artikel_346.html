The Man Who Helped the C.I.A. Secretly Operate in Mexico

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

President Claudia Sheinbaum drew a firm line barring U.S. forces from operating on Mexican soil. But in a northern border state, one local partner quietly helped the agency build a foothold.

Mexican soldiers on patrol in Ciudad Juárez in Chihuahua State, Mexico, in August.Credit...Herika Martinez/Agence France-Presse — Getty Images

Reporting from Chihuahua City and Ciudad Juárez, Mexico

When the Central Intelligence Agency was looking to quietly expand its operations in Mexico last year, it found an especially useful partner.

Mexico had tight restrictions on how freely U.S. forces could operate in the country. The nation’s president, Claudia Sheinbaum, had repeatedly said she didn’t want them taking part in operations on the ground in Mexico, asserting that it was a violation of her country’s sovereignty.

To the C.I.A., Guillermo Zuany Portillo, a prosecutor and former police commander in the border state of Chihuahua, offered a different kind of partnership.

He could pair C.I.A. officers with Mexican police units on counter-cartel operations — and he was in a bind that made him uniquely cooperative, Mexican and U.S. officials said.

Just months earlier, the United States had revoked Mr. Zuany’s visa because of his suspected ties to the very cartels the C.I.A. wanted to root out, according to a letter Mexican officials sent to the F.B.I. that was viewed by The New York Times.

And the C.I.A. wanted help going beyond the logistical support and intelligence sharing that Ms. Sheinbaum repeatedly said was the extent of the two countries’ security cooperation.
