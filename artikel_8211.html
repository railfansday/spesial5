This Massive Swordfish Smashed the State Record, But It Was Disqualified Over a Technicality

Capt. Jake Kinzie had a crew of three buddies aboard his 28-foot boat while fishing off Ocean City, Maryland the morning of November 11. The anglers ran 60 miles out to fish the deep “Poorman’s Canyon” for prized swordfish — and they would end up catching the largest sword in state history.

Swordfish action has been good this year along the Atlantic Coast. And the “Way Out” crew had caught a couple big swords earlier off Ocean City.

In 1,000 feet of water, they put out a swordfish squid bait on a 10/0 Mustad hook, taken deep with a strobe light attached to the leader to get the attention of a heavy fish.

“Jake is an old friend I’ve known for over two decades,” Capt. Keith Jacobs tells Outdoor Life. “We were with our other buddies Joe Spayd and Blaine Beyer, and about 2:30 p.m. we hooked a deep, very large fish.”

Spayd was on the rod, fighting the tough swordfish for about three hours. It never jumped, and the anglers never saw it until late in the fight.

“We knew it was huge because it was so powerful,” says Jacobs, a professional boat captain and owner of “OC-anic,” a fishing gear and apparel company. “But we figured it was only 300 pounds or so until we saw it near the boat as the sun was setting.”

Jacobs said it was all they could do to keep the swordfish from getting close to their boat and perhaps breaking the 80-pound test braided line on the Daiwa reel that Spayd used. He fought the fish from a gunnel rod holder on a swivel.

“Jake had to spin the boat a lot to keep the line from rubbing against the boat hull,” Jacobs says. “Three hours into the fight we got the fish close to our boat, but it was still full of fight.”

While Spayd stayed on the rod battling the sword, Beyer hit the fish with a harpoon dart. The metal dart head, with a heavy line fitted to it, detached from the pole.

“Then I hit the fish in the head with a big gaff – which wasn’t such a good idea,” says Jacobs. “It went crazy and ripped the gaff handle out of my hands and we lost it overboard.”

Jacobs hit the fish with another gaff to help control and kill it.

“We struggled to get a tail rope around the fish,” Jacobs says. “All that time lines were crossing, we’re wet, tired, the fish is thrashing beside the boat – and it’s getting dark in three-foot seas.”

Finally, the anglers secured the massive swordfish with a tail rope. After the fish died, they had to haul it into their 28-foot boat.

“We didn’t have a block-and-tackle or come-along, so we had to grab it and haul it over the stern and around the boat’s two outboards,” Jacobs says. “We had to do it twice, because a fin got wedged in one of the outboards.

“We were exhausted when we finally got it in the boat. It took up about half of the deck space. That’s when we headed back in to Ocean City.”

The anglers arrived at Ocean City’s Snug Harbor waterfront neighborhood about 9 p.m. There the 12-foot long fish was hauled out of their boat with a crane and weighed on a digital scale at 558 pounds.

It topped the largest swordfish caught by a sport angler by 165 pounds. That fish was boated in 2022 by angler Jeff Jacobs and weighed 393 pounds.

The anglers covered their 558-pound swordfish with fish bags and ice, and the next day contacted authorities to enter it as a state record catch.

“But we learned that because we used an electric Daiwa reel it doesn’t qualify as a Maryland record,” Jacobs says. “Other states have record fish that are certified if electric reels are used by sporting tackle anglers.

“The tackle we used – even with electric reels – is far different than what commercial fishermen use.

“I wish Maryland had another records division that allows electric reels for heavyweight marine gamefish like swordfish, tuna, and giant marlin.”
