Jensen Huang’s affair with an alleged spy is powering a new class of realistic memes that Nvidia made possible

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

The scandal surrounding Jensen Huang’s extramarital affair with an alleged spy has escaped the sandbox of Silicon Valley and spread across social media.

But in one particularly bizarre corner of the internet, a cinematic universe inspired by the affair is spawning AI-generated music videos and short films starring the Nvidia CEO.

The high-stakes drama is like something straight out of a movie. Huang has since admitted to a relationship with Chinese businesswoman Alice Huang, who is a suspected spy for the Chinese government—potentially compromising sensitive information inside a company whose technology is powering the AI boom. (Despite their surnames, the two are unrelated.)

Nvidia’s stock has taken a hit since the reveal, losing nearly $700 billion in market value amid ongoing scrutiny. The semiconductor giant has opened an investigation into Jensen Huang and the allegations he was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort.

A cinematic universe powered by AI

Meanwhile, creators on social media are taking a different approach to the situation, making Huang, Alice Huang, and his wife, Lori Huang, the stars of hyper-realistic AI-generated videos drawn from pop culture—all without their consent.

In one video posted to X, Huang sings Shaggy’s “It Wasn’t Me.” He’s not alone: Other tech leaders like Jeff Bezos, Elon Musk, and Sergey Brin join Huang in the AI music video, belting those famous lines of denial.

In a second clip, Huang is joined by Alice Huang, reenacting the viral Rosé and Bruno Mars “Apt.” music video, set against a pink background with exaggerated singing.

The two can also be spotted on an AI recreation of Drake’s “Hotline Bling” music video and George Michael’s “Careless Whisper.”

Some creators have inserted Huang into popular movie scenes, like one reimagining the viral “Why would I deceive you?” exchange between Nathan Fielder and Elizabeth Holmes in the recent documentary You Can See Everything.

Another video finds Huang and the businesswoman in the elevator scene made famous by Joseph Gordon-Levitt and Zooey Deschanel in the rom-com (500) Days of Summer, while yet another drops them in the viral club scene from Three Steps Above Heaven.

Brought to you by . . . Nvidia’s own tech

The irony of the trend is hard to escape. Generating such detailed videos of real people is only possible thanks to the technology that Huang has helped create and scale—Nvidia’s powerful graphics processing units (GPUs).

“Whether someone uses a cloud tool or runs a model on their own machine, the heavy lifting is usually completed by Nvidia GPUs, simply because that’s the hardware that most AI software is built to run on,” Ricardo Amper, CEO of AI identity verification company Incode Technologies, tells Fast Company.

And often, these videos are made using the Nvidia technology, even inadvertently.

“Most people making AI videos never touch a GPU. They open a browser, type a prompt, or upload a photo,” says Michael Bismuth, cofounder of AI-production studio Cuz.

For some digital media producers, the sudden demand for Huang Spy Scandal content has even become a platform to advertise their own AI products, like one who made a microdrama inspired by the affair.

Sophia Xing, founder and CEO of the AI microdrama production platform PlotParty, posted a vertical series made with the software, with the quintessential clichéd script, a revenge arc, and an overly long and descriptive title: I Divorced My Trillionaire Husband and the God of Compute Let Me Respawn at 19.

None of it would be possible with out the company at the heart of the scandal. “Nvidia’s chips are the engine behind almost every fake video on the internet,” Bismuth says. “The company that made AI video possible is now one of its targets.”
