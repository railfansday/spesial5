If you have a vagina (or happen to be particularly fond of them), there's a decent chance you've heard of Kegels. You know, those pelvic floor exercises that involve squeezing and releasing muscles you might not think about all that often, but the second someone mentions them, you immediately start clenching (like right now).

But what if you could put those same muscles to work during sex? That's where pompoir comes in, using different rhythms and contractions to create new sensations during penetration, sometimes without moving your hips at all.

Sometimes referred to as vaginal gymnastics, pompoir takes things a step further than your standard Kegel routine. “Think of Kegels as going to the gym and pompoir as playing the sport,” says Karen Toubi, DO, a board-certified OB/GYN and menopause and vulvovaginal disorders specialist at Lavida Women's Health, Beverly Hills.

But before you start treating your vagina like it's training for the Olympics, there's something worth knowing: Pompoir isn't about permanently tightening your vagina, performing for your partner, or guaranteeing better orgasms. It's another way to explore pleasure, provided you're doing it safely and actually enjoying yourself.

Here's how pompoir works, how to try it, and what to know before giving it a go.

Unlike regular Kegels, pompoir is less about individual squeezes and more about controlling the timing, rhythm, and intensity of those contractions during sex. The idea is to create different sensations around a partner's penis or sex toy without relying entirely on thrusting.

The muscles doing the work are the same ones you engage during Kegels, including a group called the levator ani, which helps support your pelvic organs. And while you might be able to squeeze those muscles on command, getting them to cooperate in different rhythms takes a little more finesse. “Control comes with practice, like any motor skill,” says Liz Miracle, a pelvic floor physical therapist and head of clinical quality and education at Origin.

As for what it feels like? The person practicing pompoir might notice an internal pulsing sensation, while their partner could feel anything from a gentle squeeze to a fluttering movement. Of course, everyone's experience is different, and some partners might barely notice the contractions at all.

Before trying pompoir during sex, it helps to get familiar with your pelvic floor muscles on your own. Here's where to start.

Lie on your back with your knees bent and imagine you're trying to stop yourself from peeing or passing gas. Feel that gentle squeeze and lift? That's your pelvic floor doing its thing. Just don't actually practice by stopping your pee midstream, since that can interfere with normal bladder emptying.

“Your glutes, thighs, and belly should stay quiet,” says Miracle. Focus on squeezing and fully releasing the muscles without holding your breath. If you're struggling to figure out whether you're doing it correctly, a pelvic floor physical therapist can help.

Once you've identified the right muscles, try changing up the rhythm. Dr. Toubi suggests holding a contraction for up to 10 seconds before fully relaxing, or trying quick squeezes followed by an immediate release. You can also play around with different patterns, like two slow contractions followed by a faster one.

But don't focus solely on how hard you can squeeze. Amanda Neri, PT, DPT, pelvic health expert at Smile Makers Collection, emphasizes that learning to fully relax your pelvic floor between contractions is just as important as strengthening it. Otherwise, you risk creating unnecessary tension.

According to Bryony Lees, sex and relationships educator at Little Leaf Agency, missionary, cowgirl, and spooning can all be good positions for practicing pompoir, especially if you can keep your hips relatively still. Choose whichever feels most comfortable, and have your partner stay relatively still while you experiment with different squeezes and releases.

And yes, tell your partner what you're doing. Ask what feels good, pay attention to your own sensations, and don't feel like you need to master some elaborate routine to make it worthwhile. If anything hurts, stop.
