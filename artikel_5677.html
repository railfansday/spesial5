How National Grid, Eversource are using VPPs to solve local-level constraints

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The Northeast’s virtual power plant foundations are far stronger today than a decade ago, but utilities still need more visibility into distribution-system loads and energy resources, executives said.

National Grid and Eversource have both built robust, multi-device DER portfolios spanning multiple customer classes, Caldwell and Eversource vice president Tilak Subrahmanian said.

Eversource’s portfolio includes about 100,000 thermostats and 25,000 commercial and industrial customers across a territory that includes Greater Boston, ISO-New England’s biggest load zone, Subrahmanian said.

National Grid has about 100 MW of DER load in New England and more than 350 MW in upstate New York, respectively representing about 2% and 5% of regional system peak, Caldwell said. National Grid’s residential portfolio is mostly thermostats, but stationary storage and managed electric vehicle charging are increasingly meaningful, he added.

Eversource feels fortunate to operate in a region where state regulators provide time, funding and encouragement for utilities to “test hypotheses” about alternatives to traditional poles-and-wires investments, Subrahmanian said. That allows Eversource to explore use cases where distributed resources offer economic and system benefits over more capital-intensive infrastructure projects — which isn’t always the case, he added.

“It’s a learning process … [with] behind-the-meter devices, we have to be very specific about what problem we’re trying to solve,” he said. “We are cautiously optimistic, but a lot of it needs to play out still.”

National Grid and Eversource both participate in ConnectedSolutions+, a souped-up version of the long-running New England VPP program that offers higher incentives for distributed resource hosts on constrained distribution feeders.

Eversource announced two CS+ pilots earlier this year: one focused on areas served by three substations in Greater Boston and another focused on a single substation in southeastern Massachusetts. The Boston-area program aims to shift load away from demand peaks, while the southeastern Massachusetts program is intended in part to soak up excess distributed solar generation during the middle of the day.

At least one provider of low-cost home batteries, Haven Energy, is targeting customers of both utilities with deeply discounted energy storage subscriptions in areas where batteries deliver clear grid or resilience benefits. Earlier this month, National Grid said it would partner with Haven to provide up to 125 income-qualified households with no-cost battery systems as part of a pilot program it calls BackupBattery+.

“The value of a battery to the grid can be fundamentally different even one mile apart,” Haven Energy CEO Vinnie Campo told Utility Dive in August.

With increased DER adoption and regulation allowing for flexible cost recovery, the Northeast’s VPP foundations are far stronger today than a decade ago, when Caldwell was new to National Grid, he said.

Back then, “nothing ever really penciled, [it] was really hard to do … now, nearly a decade later, everything seems to be clicking. [There is] a sea change in how utilities see these things as practical tools for solving challenges,” Caldwell said.

More capable DER orchestration software is also unlocking potential, Diamond said. Traditional demand response events typically begin “firm” and then lose effectiveness as customers drop out, she explained. But this summer, across 70 dynamic load-shaping events for 12 different utilities, EnergyHub was “able to deliver consistent load shape” with up to 90% consistency for the entire duration, she said.

“[That] is huge for utilities, especially if you are trying to hit a peak hour — it’s really hard to do that if you don't have that consistency,” Diamond said.

Eversource is eager to gain more visibility into the position, sizing and behavior of loads and distributed energy resources on its system so that it can dial in location-specific solutions, Subrahmanian said. It only takes a few unmanaged EVs to overload the small, poletop transformers that remain common in parts of Eversource’s territory — infrastructure whose inevitable replacement can perhaps be deferred by a targeted managed charging program, he said.

Right now, Eversource can’t even confidently say which of its customers have air conditioning, but its ongoing advanced metering infrastructure rollout should improve the situation soon, he added.

“We will be in a much better position in a year,” Subrahmanian said.
