The best European strikers this century - ranked by you

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The top 10 European strikers of the 21st century

Classic number nines, penalty-box poachers, troublesome targetmen and tireless all-rounders.

There is no one-size fits all when it comes to determining great strikers, but they do all share a common trait - a ruthlessness in front of goal.

This week, however, we put the power in your hands to decide the best European frontmen of the 21st century.

Tens of thousands of you voted on the 20-man shortlist drawn up by our panel.

We then asked European football experts Guillem Balague, Julien Laurens and Raphael Honigstein to discuss your choices on the Euro Leagues podcast.

So from 10-1, here are the best European strikers this century - as voted for by you.

Top 10 European Strikers of the Century - Part One

Karim Benzema comes in at number 10, the former Lyon and Real Madrid frontman's closest challengers coming from across the continent in the shape of Raul, Robin van Persie, Michael Owen and Miroslav Klose.

Laurens: "From a young age you could see he was something special. I know it was difficult at the beginning at Real Madrid, but in the post-Cristiano era he carried that team, wore the armband and won big titles and trophies by being decisive in big moments. Like I used to say 'Karim the dream'."

Honigstein: "The selflessness, the ability to work for the team. A sense of elegance and humility on the pitch, which is unusual in a goalscorer. I think he is unusual in that list. I think not many will define themselves through their teamwork and assists as much as their goals, but he was that player."

Ruud van Nistelrooy edges Benzema, with whom he briefly overlapped at Real Madrid - the Dutchman teed up the Frenchman and scored himself in their only La Liga appearance together, before he left for Hamburg in January 2010 following injury problems.

The former PSV striker's most consistent spell, of course, came at Manchester United, where he scored 150 goals.

Honigstein: "He is the nicest guy but he never came across that way on the pitch. Typical trait of a striker, he would kill to score the goal. I think anyone who has grown up with him scoring goals, for United especially, must have a soft spot for him because he was a phenomenal poacher."

Laurens: "He was a strong player physically, and despite the lack of pace the awareness in the box was just very special even with all the injuries."

Zlatan Ibrahimovic slots in at eight, comfortably clear of Van Nistelrooy and Benzema. The Swede netted more than 500 times in a career that spanned Malmo, Ajax, Juventus, both Milans, Barcelona, Paris St-Germain, Manchester United and LA Galaxy.

Balague: "He was a big-game player and that explains the league titles with PSG. Scoring four goals against England, the overhead kick. The personality thing has put him one or two places in this list, saying 'I am great, I am the best'. If you keep saying you're great it makes people stop and think."

Laurens: "The mentality of really believing he was the best and nobody was better than him, apart from maybe Brazilian Ronaldo, who was his idol growing up, and he would watch videos of him and try to replicate in his council estate. If you watch a highlight reel of his best goals he was incredible."

Honigstein: "What makes him unique among the strikers is his technique, You could see elements of martial arts in his goals. No-one scores goals like that."

The first Englishman on the list, although the third to have represented Manchester United - it's the Old Trafford outfit's record goalscorer, Wayne Rooney.

Honigstein: "I don't think there is anyone who would've watched English football at that time [early in his career] and not been enthralled by his game. The only downside is the combination of injuries and not the most professional lifestyle meant he did not fulfil his potential. He could've been Messi."

Balague: "What could have been, yes. He was the national team's record scorer too before Harry Kane took it. I find him to deserve this place as he had to adapt to a lot of strikers and he did it well. He had the ability to link up and score. He was very intelligent."

Laurens: "He is the most street footballer England has ever seen. There's no rules, that kind of anger you saw on the pitch. I don't think people in England realise how good he was."

What does becoming the all-time World Cup top goalscorer get you? Sixth place on the best European strikers this century list. The Real Madrid forward, 27, just misses out on the top five.

Laurens: "Maybe if we do this ranking again in a few years he will be higher. I would have had him top five, for sure. He has scored more than 400 goals. I don't think Messi and Ronaldo had scored that many at that age, so he's ahead. I don't know if he will play for as long as them but he hasn't done much wrong in his career."

Balague: "This list has to do what you feel about the player, not science - 22 goals in 22 appearances in World Cups and [Euro] Championships. He should be at least top three because of that. Great stats, but we're not in love with him."

Honigstein: "I don't think there is a forward that is as good as footballer as him in 2026. On that basis alone he should be higher, but people when they vote have other things in their head. The only way to win everyone over is to score more goals and win more World Cups for France, which I think is possible."

There was little between Mbappe and Erling Haaland, but it is Manchester City's prolific Norwegian who slips in at fifth. The 26-year-old has already scored more than 300 club goals and has a staggering 68 in 61 games for his country.

Balague: "Mbappe should be higher than him. Haaland scored 52 goals in 53 games [in 2022-23], that is astonishing, but he's gone 11 finals without scoring. We're talking about the best. We're talking about what we see at the moment and what we see is someone who is calculated but in a more charming way. An ability to score we haven't seen in a while; power, ability to finish. He is not world class to the level of others."

Honigstein: "I don't think it's fair to judge him on just that. They [Manchester City] won the Champions League when he was there. When Haaland arrived with Pep Guardiola, they found a way to do it. What makes him unique is the physical attributes and the pace. To have both is unique. He can sprint like a 100m sprinter. Is he the fifth best striker of the 21st Century? I don't think he's that far behind."

Interesting, this, because Robert Lewandowski is well clear of Haaland in fourth, but the leap from the Polish forward to the top three is massive - more than 10,000 votes.

The 38-year-old is still chipping away for Chicago Fire in Major League Soccer, having been a reliably ruthless assassin for Barcelona, Bayern and Dortmund.

Honigstein: "He used to play basketball, judo, handball - his father wanted him to have a wholesome education. You could see his completeness that he had as a centre-forward. Nothing in his game was extraordinary but in terms of ticking all the boxes, none come close to him."

Balague: "He said he learned to be an elite striker when he forgot what he had just done, like a fish. Mistakes he had in his head affected his performance. One day he just continued like it didn't matter."

Laurens: "He won the Ballon d'Or in Covid, it won't be on his mantlepiece but we all know it was his."

Fresh from his Ballon d'Or triumph, England's record goalscorer and appearance maker rides into the top three on the back of 61 goals in 51 outings for Bayern Munich last season.

Kane left the Premier League three years ago with the second-most goals ever in the competition, and is also Tottenham's all-time record scorer.

Honigstein: "Third place, at least. He is doing incredible things. At 33, still not quite at his peak. I don't think he relies on his pace, physicality, it's all about intelligence, technique. I don't think there's anyone in the game that strikes a ball as well as him right now. I don't think any player comes close that we've talked about so far in overall work-rate, defending, helping the team, making tackles in your box in the 90th minute when you're 3-0 up because he's that kind of guy. He is humble and a superb human being."

Laurens: "What I love about Kane, from a young age, a lot of this list you just knew the trajectory would be all the way to the top. Kane was not like that. It could have been very different for him. Nobody at 17 would've said he was going to win the Ballon d'Or. Nobody believed in him like the others. Kane had a very different journey."

Runner-up Thierry Henry is the only striker in the top six who is retired. A World Cup and Euros winner with France, Henry won titles with Arsenal - where he scored 228 goals - and a Champions League with Barcelona.

Honigstein: "During his time, I don't think there was a player like him in the Premier League. The way he played football was stylish, elegant, but there was almost a kind of a romantic connection between him and the ball. I don't think there was anyone near that kind of level of technique and, for want of a better word, va-va-voom he exuded. That just made for amazing viewing."

Laurens: "For me as a fellow Parisien, he's a very special player, a very special man. A career that has been incredible from a young age. It's that season in the Premier League - 24 goals, 20 assists in 38 games - not even Kane or Benzema or Cristiano, OK, Messi is different, but they haven't done that and yet he did it.

"There are still some people who undervalue him and underappreciate him and the monster that he was on the football pitch. He had it all. The pace, the style, the elegance, the technique, the mentality, the goals, the aura, the charisma. Just everything you wanted if you were to build a striker. One of the greatest football brains ever."

Of course, at number one, it's the man chasing the 1,000-goal dream. Cristiano Ronaldo may have retired from Portugal duty with 146 notches on his strike chart but the quest goes on at Al-Nassr.

The 41-year-old's evolution from tricky winger to prolific frontman has taken him from Sporting to Manchester United, twice, Real Madrid, Juventus and now Saudi Arabia.

Balague: "He's the best striker in history. His numbers are incredible. He won't stop until he gets to 1,000. He is somebody who is special. We know his background, how he wanted to be Ballon d'Or winner at 12 and didn't stop until he got there. He ran up the mountains of Funchal with weights in his ankles and snuck into the gym when it was closed to get the physique he wanted."

Laurens: "Always in the right place at the right time. The instinct. He was born with a gift, like all in this list. But you still have to work hard. Nobody will ever reach those levels again. 1,000 goals for club and country will never be done again, I think."

Honigstein: "Peak Ronaldo for me was Gareth Bale scoring and him looking very unhappy. I don't think there will be any dispute about him being the greatest. Somebody who has done it all, won it all, and he's still going for the love of it."
