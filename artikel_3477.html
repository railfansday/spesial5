Missing Coach Travis Turner's Wife Addresses His Disappearance 1-Year Later

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

One year after Travis Turner allegedly vanished into the woods behind his Virginia home, his wife, Leslie Caudill Turner, is telling her side of the story.

“I just don’t have any closure, I cry every day,” Leslie told the Daily Mail in a story published on Thursday, November 19.

Travis, 46, disappeared on November 20, 2025, allegedly last seen by his family fleeing while carrying a rifle. Shortly after he went missing, Travis – the former head football coach at Union High School in Big Stone Gap, Virginia – was charged with five counts of child pornography and five counts of using a computer to solicit a minor.

“It’s like day one all over again,” Leslie said of the one-year anniversary of her husband’s disappearance. “Every day that I’ve not been told that there’s a body found, I still have a little bit of hope. But I don’t think he’d be able to go an entire year without letting somebody know he’s okay.”

She continued, “We’re already grieving like he’s dead.”

Leslie and Travis share three children: sons Bailey, 24, and Grayden, 21, and daughter Brynlee, 12. Since her husband disappeared, Leslie has kept “his stuff where he left it.”

“I sleep on the couch or in my son’s room,” she said. “I can’t sleep upstairs in our bed anymore.”

Leslie is now taking antidepressants, which help keep her from “crying for hours.”

“If I’m off them, anything makes me cry,” she admitted. “It’s every day that he just consumes me.”

Despite her husband’s abrupt disappearance, Leslie still defends her husband from the allegations levied against him.

“I didn’t see anything. And I checked his phone before he left,” Leslie argued. “It’s just it doesn’t do me any good because he’s not here. And I don’t get to fight him or cuss him. Nobody will ever get me to say anything bad about him.”

Leslie explained that all of her years as a football coach’s wife have given her “thick skin.”

“I don’t care what people say about me,” she said. “I don’t pay attention to it. I know what’s true. And I haven’t done anything.”

Last month, Virginia State Police exclusively told Us that the search for Travis “remains active.”

“There are no new developments to share at this time,” the VSP said on October 27. “Because Turner is considered a fugitive, the U.S. Marshals Service continues to lead the investigation. We understand the public’s concerns and frustrations as this case reaches the one-year mark.”

The statement continued, “The Virginia State Police, along with our federal and local partners, remain committed to finding answers. This is a complex investigation, and we ask for the community’s continued understanding and patience as we work through every layer of the case and pursue every credible lead. We encourage the public to continue reporting any actionable information — no matter how small — as it may help move the investigation forward. Law enforcement remains actively engaged, evaluating new information and dedicating all available resources to bringing this case to a resolution. We are grateful for the public’s support and urge anyone with information on Turner’s whereabouts to contact the Virginia State Police or the U.S. Marshals Service.”
