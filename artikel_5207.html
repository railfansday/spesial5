A trade can move in your favour and still leave you with a loss. The difference often comes to the spread between the buy and sell prices and unpredictable funding payments on perpetual contracts.

Grvt’s Stable Funding Perps (SFP) are designed to address both costs, starting with real-world commodities. Its gold contract XAU/USDC lets you take a long or short position on gold without an expiry date. Trades and funding settle in USDC on Grvt’s self-custodial platform.

Instead of relying on a crypto-native order book, SFP uses quotes from market makers who hedge their exposure in traditional markets. Grvt charges no trading fee or additional spread, although the market makers’ quotes still include a spread.

As for the funding, it can change during the day but it is applied only once at the weekday market close, based on the rate in effect at that time.

In this guide, we’ll walk through a gold trade to show how SFP works, from opening a position and understanding the spread to calculating funding and checking the payment in Fee History.

The first step is to enable Multi-asset margin on your Grvt account which allows you to use USDC and USDT as collateral, although SFP trades and funding settle in USDC.

On the Grvt trading page, open your trading account’s mode settings and select Multi-asset margin if it isn’t already enabled. You can then check your available collateral and make sure you have sufficient margin to open a position.

Open the instrument selector and choose Stable Funding Perps then XAU/USDC.

Unlike crypto perps, which generally trade around the clock, SFP follows the trading hours of the underlying traditional markets.

Most of the launch markets are structured to be trading for around 23 hours a day and five days a week, although there are exceptions such as Brent which has shorter hours.

The trade screen interface also features a countdown making it easy to track markets open and close. What’s worth keeping in mind though is that your position remains open and liquidation is still possible if your account falls below its maintenance margin requirement.

Grvt uses a request-for-quote (RFQ) model whereby market makers provide live Buy and Sell prices, with the difference being the spread.

To provide some more context, let’s say gold is quoted at 3,001 USDC to buy and 2,999 USDC to sell. Buying and immediately selling one unit at those prices would cost 2 USDC even if gold’s market price hadn’t moved.

Grvt charges a 0% trading fee and doesn’t add its own markup to the market maker’s spread. You still pay the spread built into the quotes, however, so check the prices for the size you want to trade. Larger orders may receive different quotes.

The spread tells you what entering and exiting costs. Funding determines what you pay or receive for holding the position through the market’s weekday close.

Unlike conventional crypto perps, where funding generally helps align contract and reference prices, SFP links funding to market makers’ financing costs in traditional markets, with an adjustment applied by Grvt.

The displayed rate can change throughout the day. If you’re still holding at the close, Grvt uses the rate then in effect and the full position value to calculate funding.

Metals have 3x funding on Wednesday, while oil and gas have 3x funding on Friday to account for the weekend. Funding also settles on weekday holidays.

Always check whether Grvt displays a daily or annualised rate. The Funding tab can show a 24-hour rate, while annualised rates must be divided by 365 before applying the appropriate multiplier.

The final step is to enter a position, either go long (buy) or go short (sell). Key in the size you want and leverage; and make sure it is above the minimum order size. Once done, you can review the quote again before confirming.

Most launch markets allow up to 20x leverage, while silver is capped at 10x. Larger positions may have lower limits.

For market orders, set your slippage tolerance but also understand that an unfavourable price change can result in a partial fill or no fill and market makers can also reject an order at the final quote check.

Once filled, check your entry price under Positions. To exit, review the opposite-side quote and close the position while the market is open. You can then check Fee History for funding debits or credits.

Before you open an SFP position, estimate how far the price needs to move for you to break even. Use the spread for your order size and the funding you expect to pay or receive over your holding period. Revisit that estimate if the quotes change or you decide to hold through another close.
