Thailand Proposes Daily Stablecoin Transfer Cap for Regulated Platforms

Thailand's Securities and Exchange Commission is seeking feedback on restrictions that could significantly change how customers move stablecoins through regulated digital-asset operators. The proposed rules would generally limit eligible transfers into and out of a customer's account to 5 million baht per day in each direction, while requiring wallets at both ends to belong to that customer.

This is a consultation, not a rule that has already taken effect. The details, exemptions and eventual text matter more than the headline dollar equivalent, especially for businesses using stablecoins for legitimate cross-border payments.

In its November 12 announcement, the Thai SEC said the draft aims to reduce the risk that regulated crypto businesses facilitate money laundering, cybercrime or circumvention of cross-border transfer rules. The proposal would generally require stablecoins entering or leaving a customer's account at a digital-asset operator to come from, or go to, an account or wallet verified as belonging to that same person.

The draft also calls for controls aligned with the Travel Rule, screening for risky wallets and transaction monitoring. Those measures aim to improve traceability where a licensed intermediary is involved. They do not mean all movements of stablecoins across every blockchain would suddenly be visible to, or controlled by, one Thai exchange.

Under the proposal, inbound and outbound transfers would each be limited separately to 5 million baht per customer, per day, with each operator. The SEC's announcement also describes exemptions, including qualifying transfers between the customer's own accounts at regulated operators, certain regulated businesses and approved liquidity-provision activities. The exact scope depends on the final regulation and whether participants satisfy the relevant requirements.

That nuance is easy to lose in a short headline. It would be inaccurate to say Thailand is proposing a universal daily blockchain transfer limit, or that every business must stop moving more than that amount under all circumstances. The consultation is specifically focused on activity conducted through supervised digital-asset operators.

Stablecoins have become useful tools for businesses moving money across borders, particularly where traditional transfers are expensive or slow. NewsBTC's report on Bitso Business's stablecoin payments shows the commercial use case behind this growth. For such companies, wallet verification and transfer limits create compliance work that must fit alongside existing customer checks.

The risk is that rules designed to catch illicit activity could also make ordinary, lawful operations cumbersome if identity matching is difficult or exceptions are unclear. The regulator, meanwhile, has a legitimate interest in ensuring supervised firms know who is using their systems. That tension is precisely what a public consultation is supposed to examine.

The SEC has invited feedback through November 26, 2026. Businesses should review the official draft rather than rely on simplified social-media summaries, particularly when deciding whether their specific transaction flows would be covered. The same distinction between technology and regulated access appears in NewsBTC's coverage of USDC on Solana through Samsung Wallet.

Thailand's proposal reflects a growing regulatory emphasis on the point where blockchain assets interact with licensed businesses. Whether the final rules strike the intended balance will depend on clear exemptions, workable verification and evidence that the controls reduce risk without unnecessarily obstructing legitimate payments.
