IDFA Awards Ceremony: ‘The Year Of The Ghost’ And ‘Memories Of Lightning’ Earn Top Prizes

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Director Louis HotHotHot is living up to his name. His feature documentary The Year of the Ghost won Best Film in International Competition Thursday as the 39th International Documentary Festival Amsterdam presented its awards.

The Chinese-born filmmaker, also known as Louis Liu, also won Best Editing in International Competition, sharing that prize with his co-editor Chris van Oers.

The IDFA program says in The Year of the Ghost, HotHotHot “fearlessly turns his camera on his own family… Using archival footage, animation, and unflinchingly candid conversations, Louis HotHotHot explores how modern Chinese history and misogyny affect the lives of women. This intimate family portrait unfolds into a compelling story about the status of women and changing family culture in China.” Related Stories Documentary IDFA Announces Winning Projects In Forum Section, With Prizes Worth More Than €30,000 Documentary 'NAZA' Team Sticking To Plan To Release Shattering Documentary On The Guardian Website Within Weeks -- IDFA

“The jury was astonished by this work of singular artistic vision exploring deep family dynamics and connections, set against the wider societal forces and gender constraints inherited across generations,” wrote jury members Barbara Orlicz-Szczypuła, Hania Mroué, Katy Léna N’diaye, Oscar winner Laura Poitras, and Luuk Bouwman. “Told with bold honesty, a rare attention to detail, cinematic curiosity, and love.”

Separately, The Year of the Ghost won Best Dutch Film, earning a €5,000 prize.

The Special Mention for International Competition went to Hazara Town by Khadim Dai. Scroll for the full list of awards.

The IDFA Award for Best Directing (worth €5,000) in the International Competition went to Joël Richmond Mathieu Akafou for Lonan Tché.

The jury wrote, “Through its deeply attaching characters and the subtitle proximity the filmmaker established with them, the film depicts two overgrown kids in their quest for a dream they might never reach, elevating them to almost mythical figures, enduring their human condition.”

Best Cinematography in the International Competition went to Bardi by Tala Hadid, with cinematography by Tala Hadid.

In the Envision Competition, which IDFA considers to be of equal importance to International Competition, Best Film was awarded to Riccardo Giacconi for Memories of Lightning. That award also comes with a €15,000 cash prize.

In the feature documentary, “Thirty-year old freelance radio journalist Sibilla travels to a village in Le Marche, central Italy. She plans to make an audio documentary about a shocking event that took place there in 1982, but the village is now little more than a ghost town – earthquakes and an artificial lake have destroyed much of it,” reads the IDFA program description. “Still, she tries to capture memories to create a soundscape of the past with her microphone.”

Jury members for the Envision Competition included Firouzeh Khosrovani, Hélder Beja, Hicham Khalidi, Isabel Pagliai, and Jonathan Perel. They wrote, “A studio, a village, a hill. A device that renders irrelevant the distinction between what is real and unreal, that exercises precise control over what is said and what is left unsaid, that stretches time and hunts down stories. A film that captures the silence of things falling, like distant flashes of lightning that are sure to herald the storm.”

Sibilla Scinti Roger, the protagonist of Memories of Lightning, won the award for Outstanding Artistic Contribution. Best Directing (worth €5,000) in the Envision Competition was awarded to Yuula Benivolski for The Black Box Is Orange.

The Special Mention for Envision Competition went to Memories by Anna Falguères.

all that appears solid is built on blood, directed by Izdihar Afyouni and Gelare Khoshgozaran, won the IDFA Award for Best Short Documentary, which comes with a €5,000 cash prize.

“In a world saturated with images of violence, this short film interrogates what images can hold when representation becomes impossible,” said the jury, comprised of Ana Bravo-Pérez, Diana Toucedo, and Yaser Kassab. “Through abstraction, fragmentation, grain, texture, and the material transformation of the film image, documented atrocities are displaced from their conventional modes of representation. The image becomes matter: a site of rupture, disturbance and resistance, where memory exceeds the limits of visibility and what can be shown.”

A Special Mention in the IDFA Competition for Short Documentary went to Six Weeks, 3 Days, and All the Years Before by Ankita Gusain.

The IDFA DocLab Award for Immersive Non-Fiction went to Michał Stankiewicz for Being in You. The award is accompanied by a €5,000 cash prize.

“The winner proposes a new way of perceiving the relationship between the protagonist and the viewer: a new immersive point of view in which the digital protagonist and the physical viewer melt together into one,” observed jurors Blake Kammerdiener, Natalie Paneng, and Roel Wouters. “Through a tender exploration of life and death, this project invited us to see how VR can be a tool to memorialize while also bringing a sense of embodied presence.”

The Special Mention for the IDFA DocLab Competition for Immersive Non-Fiction went to Shifting Connections by Tungyen Chou.

Director Ant Hampton won the IDFA DocLab Award for Digital Storytelling with All the Downtime, earning a €5,000 cash prize.

“Out of visual noise; slowly, you are instructed how to use your fingers to read a performance through a book, synchronizing multiple temporalities,” said jurors Abel Enklaar, Claudix Vanesix, and Pegah Tabassinejad. “This project gently crosses the borders. It exists in between mediums, crafting a slowly escalating waterfall of cinematic moments drifting up from the pages. The work invites us to see the increasing entanglements between technocapitalism, precarious labor conditions, automation of human work, disregards of natural ecosystems, and the new warlords of corporate fascism.”

The Special Mention for the IDFA DocLab Competition for Digital Storytelling went to Plum Road Tea Dream by Samuel Baidoo.

Along with the Special Mention for International Competition, Hazara Town won two awards: Best First Feature, which comes with a €5,000 prize, and the FIPRESCI Award chosen by international film critics (FIPRESCI jurors included Hrvoje Pukšec, Loeke de Waal, and Paola Casella).

The Beeld & Geluid IDFA ReFrame Award went to The Clearing by Sabine Groenewegen. The award is accompanied by a €5,000 prize.

The 39th edition of IDFA, the world’s biggest documentary film festival, continues through Sunday. The festival reports admissions of 270,000 to its screenings. The number of professionals attending from around the world was roughly 2,500.
