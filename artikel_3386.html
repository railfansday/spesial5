9 Greatest Crime Movies of the 2000s, Ranked

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Ben Brewster is a filmmaker, critic, screenwriter, and actor whose work sits at the intersection of film culture, craft analysis, and storytelling.

He is a movie list contributing writer at Collider and a contributing writer for High on Films, where he covers contemporary cinema, independent film, and festival releases with an eye toward narrative structure, performance, and visual language.

Ben is an active film festival participant, having worked with Newport Film, the Nantucket Film Festival and as a screener for the Austin, Boston & Woodstock film festivals.  He brings to that work a programming instinct shaped by years of close engagement with independent and documentary cinema. This comes with a deep investment in the kinds of films that rarely find their way into the mainstream conversation without someone willing to advocate for them.

His background spans documentary and narrative filmmaking, film marketing, and production, including work with award-winning directors. This as well as developing his own indie production company, Lilac Creative Studio, through which he develops and produces original work across fiction and nonfiction forms.  His debut documentary, Diamond in the Rust, is an examination of economic redevelopment and displacement in the Queens area of New York City. The film was recently accepted to the Unisphere Film Festival, the Defense of Democracy Film Festival, and the Urbanite Arts & Film Festival. This would mark his first festival recognition as a director.  In addition to his writing and programming work, Ben hosts Beneath the Bloom, a film podcast exploring the work behind the scenes in cinema, and the struggles one can face to make a movie become a reality. The show reflects the same sensibility that runs through all of his work. A conviction that film is not merely entertainment but one of the most powerful tools human beings have for understanding each other.

Across mediums, his work is driven by a deep belief in film as a communal, transformative art form.

Ask 10 true cinephiles for the best crime movie of the 2000s, and you will likely get 10 different answers. It's what makes this particular topic so fascinating to argue about. There is a smorgasbord of intensely dramatic genre-related crime films that most other decades cannot match. Directors who had spent the 1990s learning the genre suddenly had the budgets and the freedom to push those conventions into even further realms. This is a highly thought of list, critically speaking, with three of the entries appearing on The New York Times list of the 100 best movies of the 21st century.

Several of these films have returned to the pop culture conversation. The Departed turned 20 on October 6th, and returns to U.S. theaters on December 4th for a limited anniversary run. Memento turned 25 in March, with a new 4K Blu-ray with a Dolby Vision master that comes Christopher Nolan approved. Catch Me If You Can has bounced between Paramount+, Hulu and Pluto TV this year. If you want a weekend marathon for the holiday, most of these are easy to find. Streaming rights switch constantly, so check before you go to queue up.

A few notes on how this list works. I leaned on rewatchability, critical response, and cultural influence. It means that a few louder box office hits land lower than some might expect. Two of the movies are directorial debuts, which say something about the health of the genre during the decade. Spoilers stay light, though the entries mention a few pivotal moments.

Gone Baby Gone was Ben Affleck's debut as a director and may be one of the lesser known movies in this list. The narrative unfolds with a missing child, and rapidly dissolves into a story about how far people will go to fix a mistake. Casey Affleck plays Patrick Kenzie, a young private investigator in Dorchester, with a boyish look that hides a stubborn moral streak. Michelle Monaghan is Angie Gennaro, his partner and girlfriend. She gets some of the sharpest scenes, and is key in helping with the search for four-year-old Amanda McCready. The ending turns on a decision that splits viewers and becomes quite the moral puzzle in its final stretch.

Ben Affleck co-wrote the script with Aaron Stockhard from a Dennis Lehane novel. Amy Madigan and Titus Welliver play the girl's aunt and uncle. Morgan Freeman is the police captain in charge of the case, and Ed Harris appears as a hard-edged detective. Amy Ryan earned an Oscar nomination for Best Supporting Actress, and Affleck later went on to direct The Town and then Argo, which won Best Picture. This was certainly a very promising first step, a project that landed a 94% RT critics score and an 86% audience score. The movie runs just under two hours, and it has been streaming on Paramount+ all year.

Critics, viewers, and Emmy voters rarely agree, but they do on these five.

Christopher Nolan's second feature stars Guy Pearce and opens with a Polaroid fading back into a camera. Nolan keeps the reversals coming from there, as the audience learns about Leonard Shelby (Pearce), a man with anterograde amnesia. He cannot form new memories after an attack that killed his wife. Leonard tracks her killer using Polaroids, notes and tattoos on his body. Carrie-Anne Moss and Joe Pantoliano play the people who may or may not be helping him.

Memento premiered at the Venice Film Festival in 2000. Early on several distributors reportedly passed because they worried audiences could not follow the structure. The narrative structure is culturally known for moving backwards in color and forward in black and white. Nolan adapted the movie from a short story by his brother Jonathan called Memento Mori. The DVD included a menu puzzle that viewers had to solve to watch the story in chronological order. It added to its cult status, along with the multiple Oscar nominations.

Daniel Craig is credited only as XXXX in Layer Cake, Matthew Vaughn's first film as a director. He had previously produced two Guy Ritchie movies, and directs here from J.J. Connolly's adaptation of his own novel. XXXX is a cocaine dealer who treats crime as a day job. He plans to retire, but his boss hands him two tasks to complete. Of course, neither went as he had planned. One is finding a missing young woman, and the other is brokering a massive London drug deal. Michael Gambon, Colm Meaney, Siena Miller, and a young Tom Hardy round out the ensemble cast.

Craig's calm and tailored performance reportedly caught the eye of Bond's producers, and he was cast as 007 in 2005. Metacritic scores Layer Cake at 73. Vaughn's debut project operates like a British gangster story that treats the job like an office with bosses, middlemen, and deliberately bad communication. Vaughn would later go on to direct Kick-Ass, X Men: First Class and Kingsman.

The Departed is a Boston remake of the 2002 Hong Kong thriller, Infernal Affairs. Martin Scorsese directs, and Leonardo DiCaprio is Billy Costigan, a young state trooper sent undercover into the crew of Jack Nicholson's Frank Costello. The premise is built on the clever idea that a cop and criminal's worlds are much closer than one might expect. Matt Damon plays Colin Sullivan, a criminal who has risen inside the State Police as Costello's mole. Nicholson plays the gangster with his usual gusto, and Martin Sheen and Mark Wahlberg provide the police side of things.

Vera Farmiga plays a psychiatrist caught between both men. Scorsese hides X marks in the frame before characters die, a nod to the 1932 Scarface. The Departed won four Oscars, including Best Picture, Best Director, Best Adapted Screenplay and Best Film Editing. Wahlberg netted a Best Supporting Actor nomination, and the movie grossed about $291 million worldwide. It turned 20 this year, and returns to the big screen for a limited theatrical run starting December 4th.

Antoine Fuqua directed Training Day from a script by David Ayer. The story unfolds over a single day in the LAPD's narcotics unit. Ethan Hawke plays rookie Jake Hoyt, an eager officer on his first day with Denzel Washington's Alonzo Harris, a veteran detective who breaks every rule imaginable. The supporting cast includes Scott Glenn, Tom Berenger, Eva Mendes, Dr. Dre, and Snoop Dogg. Washington, known for playing heroes, plays Alonzo with a grin that keeps the audience off balance.

The suspense is drawn from watching Jake try to figure out where the lessons end and the crimes begin. It makes it so the audience and Jake can never truly relax. Washington won the Oscar for Best Actor at the 2002 ceremony, and Hawke saw a Best Supporting Actor nomination. Fuqua's career took off after Training Day, and gave Ayer a platform for the cop stories he would write later. Ayer went on to make Harsh Times and End of Watch, and Training Day turned 25 in October. The final stretch pulls Jake into a confrontation with Alonzo that Washington sells with sheer intensity. Alonzo remains one of the most quoted villains of the 2000s.

Catch Me If You Can opens with stylish animated credits that set the 1960s tone before Leonardo DiCaprio even shows up. The narrative focuses on DiCaprio's Frank Abagnale Jr., a teenage con man who poses as an airline pilot, a doctor, and a lawyer all while forging millions of dollars in checks. DiCaprio plays Frank with his early career boyish charm, and Tom Hanks is Carl Hanratty, the FBI agent who chases him across the country with growing admiration.

John Williams wrote a fantastic jazzy score, and Christopher Walken is Frank's father who yearns to be proud of his son. Both earned Oscar nominations for their contributions. Martin Sheen, Amy Adams, and Nathalie Baye appear in smaller roles. The movie grossed more than $352 million worldwide on a $52 million budget and sits at 96% on RT. Spielberg released Minority Report earlier the same year, which makes 2002 one of his busiest and brightest years. Hanratty's Christmas Eve phone call with Frank is one of the movie's most memorable scenes.

Zodiac is a crime movie about people who cannot let go. The story follows the hunt for the Zodiac Killer, who terrorized the San Francisco Bay Area in the late 1960s and early 1970s. Jake Gyllenhaal plays obsessive cartoonist Robert Graysmith, Mark Ruffalo is inspector Dave Toschi, and Robert Downey Jr. plays reporter Paul Avery, whose life unravels as the case drags on. Anthony Edwards, Brian Cox and Chloë Sevigny fill out the supporting cast. David Fincher directs from James Vanderbilt's script, based on Graysmith's books.

Fincher stages the killer's attacks with a cold realism, including the Lake Berryessa scene and the cab murder in San Francisco. A late sequence in a basement may be the most frightening scene in the movie, even though almost nothing happens. Zodiac did modestly at the box office, but it has since grown massively in critical and commercial reputation. The case never reaches a clear answer, much like the reality of it all, yet the attention to period piece detail and the frustration surrounding the mystery fuels the narrative engine.

No Country for Old Men feels like a western that wandered into a nightmare. Joel and Ethan Coen adapted Cormac McCarthy's novel into a bleak, darkly funny crime story set in West Texas in 1980. Josh Brolin is Llewelyn Moss, a welder who finds drug money in the desert, and Javier Bardem plays Anton Chigurh, a hitman with a pageboy haircut and a captive bolt pistol. The epic won four Oscars, including Best Picture, Best Director for the Coens, Best Adapted Screenplay, and Best Supporting Actor for Bardem.

Roger Deakins shoots the project with a stark clarity. Tommy Lee Jones plays Sheriff Ed Tom Bell, who opens the movie with a weary narration about the passing of time and the increase in violence in the modern world. Woody Harrelson and Kelly Macdonald add key supporting roles and Carter Burwell's score is so sparse that the silence does much of the work. The gas station coin toss scene is a masterclass in tension built simply out of dialogue and RT lists the western at 93%. The ending still divides viewers, which keeps people arguing about it to this day.

Christopher Nolan's The Dark Knight turns Gotham into a crime city and Batman into a detective in a bodysuit. The sequel to Batman Begins opens with a bank robbery that sets the tone for everything that follows. Christian Bale returns as Bruce Wayne, Aaron Eckhart is district attorney Harvey Dent, and Gary Oldman is Commissioner Jim Gordon. Heath Ledger's Joker plans the heist, and he disposes of his crew in a manner that foreshadows to the audience, the societal collapse he hopes to execute. Maggie Gyllenhaal, Michael Caine, and Morgan Freeman complete the main cast.

Nolan shot key action sequences with IMAX cameras and filmed in Chicago, which gives the city its scale. Ledger passed away in January 2008, six months before release, and he won a posthumous Oscar for Best Supporting Actor. The Dark Knight grossed more than $1 billion worldwide and appears on the New York Times list of the best movies of the 21st century. Nolan has cited Michael Mann's Heat as an influence, which explains why Gotham feels like a crime city with a manic sociopathic clown in the middle. The interrogation scene between Batman and the Joker is the centerpiece and still is quoted to this day.
