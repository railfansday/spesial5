Anchorage Digital Sets Up Asset Manager to Offer Bitcoin Yield to Clients

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Anchorage Digital, the first federally chartered crypto bank in the U.S., is moving into asset management with a new subsidiary that will offer custody clients a way to earn yield on their bitcoin.

The unit, called 61st Parallel Asset Management, is expected to start operating by early December, CEO Nathan McCauley said in an interview to CoinDesk. Its first product will be a bitcoin-denominated fund, meaning returns are measured in BTC rather than dollars. The name nods to the city of Anchorage, Alaska, which sits at 61 degrees north latitude.

“We have increasingly heard clients say they want to earn on top of the assets they hold at Anchorage — something that’s very common among the large financial institutions of the world,” McCauley said.

Anchorage has filed a Form ADV, the disclosure document investment advisers use to register with the Securities and Exchange Commission or state regulators, or to report as exempt advisers. The form covers a firm’s ownership, business, assets under management, fees and conflicts of interest. As of Tuesday afternoon, the SEC’s public adviser database showed no record for 61st Parallel, so the filing’s details were not yet public.

The entity itself is not new. New York state records show that 61st Parallel Asset Management LLC, a Delaware company, registered to do business in New York on July 8, listing an office on Park Avenue South in Manhattan.

Anchorage already lets clients earn on some holdings. Its website lists staking, which pays rewards for helping secure proof-of-stake networks, and a separate rewards product for eligible assets. Bitcoin runs on proof-of-work and cannot be staked natively.

Anchorage has not said publicly how the fund will generate yield, what it will charge or which clients will qualify. Those details matter to bitcoin holders, because bitcoin pays no interest on its own. Common ways to earn on it include lending coins out, posting them as collateral or selling options against them, and each carries its own risk.

Public companies have already tested some of those approaches. Trump Media disclosed this month that it had pledged about 73% of its bitcoin to an options program in which the counterparty can rehypothecate, or reuse, the coins.

Founded in 2017, Anchorage received a national trust bank charter from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency in January 2021, the first crypto firm to win one. The OCC lifted a 2022 consent order over the bank’s anti-money laundering controls in August 2025.

The bank no longer stands alone. Last December, the OCC granted conditional approvals to five more digital asset firms, including Circle, Ripple, BitGo, Paxos and Fidelity Digital Assets, to become national trust banks.

The asset manager is the latest in a run of expansions. On Nov. 5, Anchorage Digital Swap Dealer LLC applied to the National Futures Association to register as a swap dealer with the Commodity Futures Trading Commission. The company said much of its options growth has come from public companies holding bitcoin and ether on their balance sheets.

In February, Tether made a $100 million strategic investment in Anchorage at a $4.2 billion valuation, after picking Anchorage Digital Bank as the issuer of its U.S. stablecoin. Anchorage also ran its first employee tender offer at that valuation instead of raising new primary capital.

In June, the company rolled out off-exchange settlement with Binance, letting institutions trade on the exchange while their assets stay in segregated custody at the bank. Anchorage also offers trading, staking and settlement to institutional clients.

The swap dealer application remains under review by the National Futures Association, and McCauley said at the time that the company was not in a position to speculate on timing.
