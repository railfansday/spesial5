Bitcoin’s supply squeeze narrative faces scrutiny after 11,000 BTC moves to Kraken

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Coinbase and Binance sent large amounts of Bitcoin to Kraken during the downturn, even as combined exchange reserves declined.

More than 11,000 BTC moved from Coinbase and Binance to Kraken during Saturday's selloff, complicating Bitcoin's supply-squeeze narrative.

The five transactions, identified by Ask The Tape (ATT), came as major exchanges recorded hundreds of millions of dollars in net Bitcoin withdrawals, fueling expectations that fewer coins were available for trading.

However, the transfers revealed substantial movements between trading venues, with Bitcoin leaving some exchanges only to replenish reserves elsewhere.

Coinbase accounted for approximately 4,028 BTC across two transactions. The first delivered 2,177.79 BTC to Kraken-tagged addresses at 03:47 UTC, followed by another 1,850 BTC at 10:04 UTC.

Binance then transferred 7,000 BTC through three transactions: two transfers of 2,000 BTC each and a third involving 3,000 BTC.

Binance recorded $680 million in net Bitcoin withdrawals on Nov. 21, while Coinbase and Bybit registered $373 million and $103 million, respectively. Together, the three exchanges lost approximately $1.16 billion.

Kraken moved in the opposite direction, receiving $478 million in net inflows and offsetting 41% of those withdrawals.

That left the four platforms with a combined net outflow of $678 million, even as substantial amounts of Bitcoin circulated between their wallets.

Kraken also recorded significant outgoing transactions that day, including one involving approximately 4,028 BTC, illustrating the rapid movement of liquidity across exchanges during the downturn.

The exchange movements have become particularly significant as Bitcoin struggles to recover from its recent decline below $60,000.

Although declining exchange reserves can reduce the amount of Bitcoin immediately available for sale, sustained buying demand remains essential to turning that scarcity into upward price pressure.

That demand has weakened considerably among US institutional investors.

According to Farside Investors, US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded $1.7 billion in net withdrawals last week, extending a period of heavy redemptions that has stripped billions of dollars from the funds this month.

While Bitcoin subsequently recovered above $61,000 on Nov. 23, the ETF withdrawals have yet to show a corresponding recovery in institutional appetite.

The next test arrives Wednesday, when the US Bureau of Economic Analysis publishes October's personal consumption expenditures inflation report alongside its second estimate of third-quarter economic growth.

Both releases are scheduled for 8:30 a.m. ET and could influence expectations for the Federal Reserve's December interest rate decision.

Stronger inflation could push Treasury yields and the dollar higher, adding pressure to Bitcoin at a time when ETF investors are withdrawing capital.

That leaves Bitcoin's recovery dependent on two developments: exchange reserves continuing to decline across trading venues and fresh buying demand returning to absorb available supply.

If ETF withdrawals persist while Bitcoin continues circulating between exchanges, the apparent supply squeeze may offer little support against another wave of selling.
