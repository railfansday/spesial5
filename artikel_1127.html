Sir Jim Ratcliffe's Ineos settles lawsuit over £180m boat with Sir Ben Ainslie - after Man United owner's acrimonious America's Cup split

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Published: 16:43 EST, 16 November 2026 | Updated: 16:54 EST, 16 November 2026

Sir Jim Ratcliffe’s Ineos have reached a settlement in their lawsuit against Sir Ben Ainslie’s Athena Racing over the £180million boat used in the America’s Cup.

Ineos had taken High Court action in a bid to reclaim the boat, known as Britannia, which had been raced in the last edition of the America’s Cup in 2024.

Britannia had remained in the possession of Athena Racing after Ineos, who had been their sponsors and financial backers, split with Ainslie’s team at the start of last year.

Ineos had argued the boat and intellectual property belonged to them.

Ratcliffe's company had reportedly claimed around £10million over the apparent failure to return the boat, but confirmed the end of their legal action on Monday.

‘INEOS has reached a settlement with Athena Racing and considers the matter now closed,’ an INEOS statement read.

Sir Jim Ratcliffe's (right) Ineos has settled a lawsuit with Sir Ben Ainslie's Athena Racing over a £180m boat used in the America's Cup

Ineos had taken legal action over the ownership of the Britannia, which was used at the 2024 America's Cup, before Ineos split with Ainslie's team

Ainslie’s Athena Racing, which rebranded as GB1 at the start of this year, confirmed they still own the boat.

‘The terms of the settlement are confidential, and we will not be commenting further on the settlement or the litigation,’ a statement read.

‘Athena Racing/GB1 have always been, and remain, the owners of their assets.’

Ratcliffe had invested around £350million into two America’s Cup campaigns alongside four-time Olympic champion Ainslie after the pair met over a gin and tonic in 2018.

After the first one flopped in 2021, the 2024 attempt on sport’s oldest trophy saw Ainslie’s crew win the challenger series before losing in the final against the holders Emirates Team New Zealand.

Following the split, Ineos had initially planned to continue to pursue the goal of delivering a first British winner of the competition, which began in 1851.

Ineos ultimately announced last April that they were withdrawing, blaming 'protracted negotiation with Athena Racing'.

Ratcliffe's company had claimed a six-month delay had 'undermined its ability to prepare for the next Cup'.

Ineos had invested around £350m across two America's Cup campaigns in 2021 and 2024

Speaking to The Times last year, Ainslie had stressed he still had fond memories of his time working with Ratcliffe, who is also the minority owner of Manchester United.

'I mean we achieved a lot as a team and Jim and Ineos were a big part of that.

'Obviously, it's got a lot of interest, both in sport and business, but we always got on very well personally. So let's see.

'This [how his split with Ineos came about] is the world we live in. There's a lot at stake and it's cut-throat to a certain extent.

'None of it's necessarily surprising and I'm still really proud of what we achieved in Barcelona.

'We had a fantastic partnership, and we achieved a lot together, but we've definitely moved on. We've both moved on.
