Vincent Kompany to RETURN to Bayern dugout less than a month after collapsing at training ground: Club chief reveals 'meningeal irritation' has fully 'healed'

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Published: 14:59 EST, 18 November 2026 | Updated: 15:01 EST, 18 November 2026

Bayern Munich's chief executive has confirmed Vincent Kompany is expected to return to the dugout on Friday, less than a month after collapsing at the club's training ground.

Kompany collapsed on October 23 while watching Bayern's Under-23 side and reportedly suffered from seizures.

The 40-year-old, who suffered a dislocated shoulder in the fall, underwent tests in hospital which determined that a 'meningeal irritation' had been the cause of his collapse.

If left untreated meningeal irritation can become meningitis. The meninges are the membranes around the brain and spinal cord.

Kompany was discharged from hospital the following week to continue his recovery at home.

The Belgian coach is now set to return to the dugout when Bayern face Cologne on Friday, with Kompany due to also address the media tomorrow morning.

Vincent Kompany is set to return to the Bayern Munich dugout for their Bundesliga match on Friday

Kompany's return was officially confirmed by Bayern chief executive Jan-Christian Dreesen.

'Yes, Vincent is in pretty good spirits. I ran into him today,' Dreesen told German outlet Abendzeitung.

'The meningeal irritation has healed. He has already undergone shoulder surgery.'

Kompany had led training for the first time since his collapse earlier this week, with his arm reportedly in a sling following his shoulder injury.

He had missed five matches during his recovery with Bayern winning two and drawing against Borussia Dortmund in the Bundesliga.

The German champions also beat Atletico Madrid in the Champions League in his absence, as well as progressing in the DFB Cup.

Kompany's assistant Aaron Danks had overseen the first team during his recovery.

Danks previously worked as Kompany's assistant at Anderlecht before deputising for Dean Smith and Steven Gerrard at Aston Villa and Michael Carrick at Middlesbrough.

Kompany has missed five matches following his collapse at Bayern's training ground on October 23

Bayern had stressed they wanted to give Kompany as much time as required to recover before returning to work.

The German giants play eight matches in the next month, including two matches in the Champions League, before the Bundesliga takes its winter break.

Reports had emerged after Kompany's collapse that club officials had been concerned by his irregular eating habits, as well as barely exercising, drinking excessive amounts of coffee, and taking on too much work.

Bayern sporting director Max Eberl dismissed the claims last month, reiterating that Kompany's illness had been the cause of his collapse rather than his workload.
