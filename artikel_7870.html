Washington May Permit Nudity-Focused Businesses "to Set Entry Requirements Based on Genitalia"

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Mostly law professors | Sometimes contrarian | Often libertarian | Always independent

The Washington State Human Rights Commission (WSHRC) voted last month to permit businesses to set entry requirements based on genitalia when nudity is a core part of the operation—thus allowing, say, an all-female nude establishment to reject customers with male genitals without having to fear anti-discrimination enforcement from the state.

This is not a sentence you necessarily see every day. But it is the latest plot twist related to the legal odyssey that is Olympus Spa v. Armstrong, a case concerning a nude spa that has been embroiled in a yearslong, ever-crescendoing fight with the state after it declined to admit a preoperative transgender woman to its location in Lynnwood, Washington….

"In the limited circumstance where a business requires nudity as a central purpose of the business," the addition reads, "such business may maintain a rule that entry to spaces where nudity is required be based on genital characteristics." After the vote, the executive director was to work with the Office of the Attorney General to officially file the rule….

The [underlying Olympus Spa] story, while niche, has elicited some strong reactions. "This is a case about swinging dicks," wrote Judge Lawrence VanDyke of the U.S. Court of Appeals for the 9th Circuit in March. "You may think that swinging dicks shouldn't appear in a judicial opinion. You're not wrong. But as much as you might understandably be shocked and displeased to merely encounter that phrase in this opinion, I hope we all can agree that it is far more jarring for the unsuspecting and exposed women at Olympus Spa—some as young as thirteen—to be visually assaulted by the real thing."
