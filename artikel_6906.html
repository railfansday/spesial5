A Kitchen in Bloom: How Tiffany Howell and Miele Seductively Reimagine the Heart of the Home

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Eschewing conventional design for an immersive, romantic kitchen, this wildly glamorous space proves that high-tech convenience can foster intimacy, slowness, and the art of living well.

When Tiffany Howell of Night Palm Interiors designs a home, her extraordinary vision transcends conventional norms, veering into an ethereal realm—an approach she calls “the poetry of place.” In Howell’s mind film, fashion, and music are as essential as decorative art, and, when designing a home, she uses all these inspirations to create theatrical spaces that ignite all the senses— layering texture, sound, scent, light, and form to evoke deep emotion. When executed with this level of intention, a room becomes an environment you don't merely look at but fundamentally feel.

When Miele asked Howell to create a dream kitchen for an installation at Art Basel, she seized the opportunity to reimagine what a kitchen can be. Set against the vibrant creative energy of Miami's premier art fair, the commission gave her a blank canvas to challenge utilitarian conventions and treat the culinary space as an ambitious work of living art. “I wanted an elevated sexiness, a beautiful, sensory experience,” she says. “I created the space with characters in mind that I’d invite to a dinner party in this kitchen.” After all, the kitchen is a home’s heartbeat, an interactive space with multiple purposes.

“I do think the kitchen is really one of the most tender rooms in the house,” Howell says. She leaned into that romance by installing black cabinetry with blossoming branches and birds in iridescent greens and pinks. The cabinets are an homage to her love of 1920s chinoiserie furniture and fashion, seen through a very glamourous, Los Angeles lens. That California focus also meant bringing the outdoors in. The grand conservatory space harnesses diffused, natural light with the promise of the scent of intoxicating jasmine wafting into the room. Howell’s ideal playlist sets the tone with jazz greats, and ambient synth-pop.

“I wanted to create a kitchen that’s a completely sensory experience,” Howell explains. “The sound of a crackling warm fireplace, the aroma of a meal cooked for loved ones, or of making coffee in the morning.” For Howell, these aren't incidental details; they are the sensory architecture that turns raw space into lived emotion. In Howell’s view, this kitchen is “also a place where you can take a nap, read a book, or write in your journal. Every moment in this room can become a ritualistic experience of living well.”

When it came to the practical needs of a kitchen, Howell used Miele as a muse, an invitation to eschew the pale minimalism so common in luxury kitchens. “The appliances are so elegant, they kind of act as the jewelry of the room,” she says. “And their functionality and ease of operation give you back time to live better, relax with a drink, hang out with family and friends, and create the stories that make up a life.”

Consider the Miele Built-In Coffee System and wine cooler in matching dark finishes which are nearly camouflaged so the technology reads more as furniture than appliance. That tension between heirloom design and high-tech styling becomes a unified part of a lived-in, well collected home. Accent pieces including a vintage Fortuny pendant lamp over the kitchen island, and upholstered chairs in the conservatory with detailing reminiscent of antique lingerie, create a moody environment well suited to Miele's black-finished lines that resemble rich obsidian.

The 36” Full Surface Induction Cooktop features a frameless, flush design that disappears into a dark-veined countertop. Transforming the entire glass surface into one expansive cooking area, it fits up to six pots or pans at once and with Intelligent Pan Recognition and SmartSelect White controls seamlessly transfer power settings wherever pans are moved. On the same wall is the 30” Handleless Combi-Steam Oven XXL, with DualSteam technology that emanates ASMR sounds of steam cooking, and a black glass façade that resembles a framed panel or window. From the cool, flush glide of the induction cooktop to the whisper-quiet hum of precision steam, the technology engages the senses rather than interrupting them, turning modern convenience into an intuitive, tactile ritual. Rather than breaking up the wall with a stack of conspicuous appliances, these seamless integrations keep the architecture artful. And for added convenience, Miele’s elegant touch controls are enhanced with WIFI connectivity for an easier user experience.

Howell’s concealment of technology is exhibited in her use of the 42” French Door Refrigerator with its elongated custom handles and a dark lacquered paneled front that echoes the floral chinoiserie motifs—an unconventional solution that turns a mundane appliance into a piece of decorative art. And once the refrigerator doors are open, the Brilliant Light Pro illumination makes it easy to survey the food inside the large capacity interior.

“How do you nurture yourself and others in the kitchen?” Howell asks. Her answer: a design that creates a restorative space evoking intimacy and slowness, where technology can encourage a lightness and give us back more time. By composing every sight, surface, scent, and sound into a single harmonious narrative, Howell proves that a kitchen shouldn't just be an aesthetic statement—it should be something you feel in your bones. “What excites me is building a new world with more possibilities,” Howell says. “I don’t think I could design any other way.”
