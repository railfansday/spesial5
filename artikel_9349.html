The Fibre-Rich Foods Linked To Extra Weight Loss

Beans, lentils and chickpeas may help people lose weight and trim their waistlines, according to a review of 36 randomised trials.

Adults who ate diets containing these foods lost about a kilogram more than those on comparison diets.

Their waists also shrank by about 1.6 centimetres more on average.

One reason may be that beans and lentils deliver both fibre and protein, which can help a meal feel more filling.

If people feel satisfied after eating, they may find it easier to eat less later in the day.

The researchers brought together trials in which adults were randomly assigned to diets containing legumes other than soya or to a comparison diet.

The trials covered a range of pulses, including dried beans, peas, chickpeas and lentils.

That makes the findings relevant to several familiar foods, although the pooled result tells us little about how a serving of lentils stacks up against a serving of chickpeas.

The researchers looked at body weight, waist circumference, body fat and body mass index, which relates a person’s weight to their height.

The results pointed to modest reductions in weight and waist size, with a reduction in body fat in the trials that measured it.

A bowl of lentil soup and a bowl of soup made with refined grains can feel quite different to eat, partly because lentils bring fibre and protein to the meal.

The researchers suggest that these nutrients could help people feel satisfied and reduce how much they go on to eat.

What the beans or lentils replace may matter as well.

Replacing some of the rice in a meal with chickpeas gives you a different mix of foods from adding chickpeas to your usual portion of rice.

That distinction helps explain why the comparison diets matter when interpreting the results.

The study was published in the journal Food Science & Nutrition (Rahmanian et al., 2026).
