I’ve Tested Vacuums for Years. These Are the 6 Best Cordless Stick Vacs Under $300, Ranked.

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Clean hardwood, carpet, pet hair, and everything in between for less.

Gear-obsessed editors choose every product we review. We may earn commission if you buy from a link. Why Trust Us?

The most powerful cordless stick vacuums will easily run you $500 or more, but in my experience, there are plenty of capable cleaners under $300. They may not rival the suction power, battery life, or features of their higher-priced counterparts, but many are perfectly capable of keeping up with typical household messes on hardwood, carpet, and every surface in between.

I’ve spent years testing vacuums for Popular Mechanics, and that experience informs a lot of my recommendations below. Some of these models have been through my own series of hands-on tests, while others made the cut based on my familiarity with their brands and product lines, along with research into their features and real-world performance. From budget-friendly everyday cleaners to options for pet owners, these are the best cordless vacuums that deliver the most for under $300.

The Shark PowerDetect is a feature-packed option for anyone who wants a cordless stick vacuum that does a lot of the thinking for them. The Detect mode automatically adjusts suction based on floor type and amount of debris, and a dual-roller floor head tackles tough messes on hardwood and carpet.

The hinged wand is great for cleaning under furniture and makes it a lot easier to reach hidden areas without having to bend down at all kinds of weird angles. It’s on the heavier side, and the long recharge time is a drawback, but strong pickup and smart features make it a good pick for whole-home cleaning.

Don’t let the price fool you: The Bissell PowerClean FurFinder is a strong and versatile cordless vacuum. It isn’t too heavy to carry between rooms, and the self-standing design means you don’t have to remount it or lay it flat any time you need to pause during your cleaning session.

It has a tangle-free brush roll that does a great job picking up pet hair (as well as your own stray strands around the house). I also like the LED headlights that show hidden dirt, dust, and debris on floors and furniture. The attachments are great for above-floor cleaning, too. Battery life drops considerably when you crank up the suction power, so it’s best for maintenance cleaning.

The LVAC-300 packs cleaning power and everyday convenience into a lightweight machine. It’s easy to maneuver, and it transitions between hard floors, rugs, and low- to medium-pile carpet without scattering debris all over the floor. The tangle-resistant brush roll is a major pro for homes with pets or long hair, and five-stage filtration and washable filters are valuable additions.

It converts to a handheld vacuum cleaner with attachments for upholstery, stairs, and car interiors, and it uses a pleated mechanical HEPA filter with a dense arrangement of fibers to capture even the tiniest dust particles. The smaller dustbin and shorter runtime on max power might negatively impact your overall experience using this vacuum, but still, it’s versatile enough to take care of a variety of common household messes.

The Tineco Pure One Station 5 is the sibling to the Station 5 Pro that I’ve tested and still use to this day. And luckily, this more affordable model doesn’t cut key features that I love, such as the self-emptying and self-cleaning bagless docking station and green laser light for dust detection.

This model also has HEPA filtration and smart features. Internal sensors detect dust and adjust suction accordingly, and the anti-tangle brush roll helps prevent hair buildup. You get a shorter runtime with this model than you do with the Pro (estimated 70 minutes compared to the Pro’s 100), but the automated cleaning capability is hard to beat.

The Dyson V8 is right at the edge of our $300 budget, but it’s a powerful option for homes with lots of carpeting or area rugs. I haven’t tried this specific model yet, but the motorbar floor cleaner is the same design that comes with other Dyson vacuums I’ve tried, such as the Dyson V15 Detect. It works well for hard floors, but I’ve found it particularly useful for dislodging embedded hair and debris from carpet fibers.

The V8 comes with three attachments and converts into a handheld vacuum if you need more portability. Runtime is on the lower end for Dyson, but if you’re looking for a premium design without the premium price tag, the V8 is it.

The Eureka ReactiSense 440 offers a number of features that make vacuuming less of a chore. It has dust detection sensors, automatic suction adjustment, floor head lights, and an anti-tangle brush roll with fins and a comb strip to prevent hair from getting wrapped around it.

I like that this model uses HEPA filtration to capture up to 99.7 percent of airborne particles and odors (which is great if you’re a pet owner). This model is lightweight and portable, converts to a handheld, and provides up to 60 minutes of runtime. Battery life will likely vary if you’re regularly cleaning at high power levels, but I’d reach for this vacuum for daily maintenance cleaning and quick pickups.
