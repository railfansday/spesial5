Goldman Sachs More Than Doubles Its Bitcoin ETF Holdings to $1.28 Billion

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Goldman Sachs more than doubled its spot bitcoin ETF holdings in the third quarter to about $1.28 billion, driven by a 52-fold jump in Fidelity’s fund, while cutting its stake in Strategy (MSTR) by more than half, a Monday filing shows.

The bank reported the positions in a Form 13F covering the quarter ended Sept. 30. Its long holdings across five spot bitcoin funds were valued at $1.283 billion, up from about $580 million in its filing for June 30, an increase of roughly 121%.

BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust remained Goldman’s largest bitcoin fund holding, at 16,440,599 shares worth $778.3 million. The share count actually slipped about 5% from 17,232,201 at midyear. The value rose because IBIT climbed from $33.29 to $47.34 a share during the quarter, a gain of about 42%.

The bigger change was in the Fidelity Wise Origin Bitcoin Fund. Goldman held 5,477,583 FBTC shares worth $397.9 million at Sept. 30, up from just 105,840 shares, worth $5.4 million, three months earlier.

Goldman also reported 2,000,020 shares of the Morgan Stanley Bitcoin Trust, worth $47.9 million. The fund, ticker MSBT, launched in April as the first bitcoin product from a U.S. bank-affiliated asset manager, with a 0.14% sponsor fee. Goldman’s June filing did not list it. The trust reported 36.42 million shares outstanding and $873.4 million in net assets at Sept. 30, so Goldman held about 5.5% of the fund.

The rest of the bitcoin fund book was smaller. Grayscale’s Bitcoin Mini Trust rose to 1,383,498 shares, worth $51.1 million, from 22,905. Goldman also listed 120,600 shares of the Grayscale Bitcoin Trust, worth $7.8 million, alongside puts on the same number of shares, which suggests that position is hedged.

The bank’s options book in IBIT is large, too. It reported puts on 7,513,000 IBIT shares, valued at $355.7 million, and calls on 4,895,600 shares, valued at $231.8 million. Those figures reflect the value of the underlying shares, not premiums paid, so they do not measure money at risk. Goldman runs one of Wall Street’s biggest market-making and derivatives businesses, and its 13F does not say which positions are hedges for clients and which are its own views.

The bank went the other way on Strategy. Goldman’s long position in the bitcoin treasury company fell to 2,760,766 shares, worth $422.6 million at Sept. 30, from 5,810,162 shares at midyear, a drop of about 52%.

Its Strategy put options barely moved, covering 4,738,700 shares worth $725.4 million, compared with 4,735,800 in June. Calls rose to 707,200 shares from 575,900. Strategy closed at $103.03 on Monday, down from $153.09 at the end of September, as the company keeps buying bitcoin and buying back preferred stock.

The pattern mirrors the one at Wells Fargo, whose own 13F, also filed Monday, showed its IBIT stake more than doubling while its Strategy position fell by about a third. JPMorgan reported $540 million in bitcoin ETFs in its third-quarter filing last week.

The Goldman positions are a snapshot as of Sept. 30, before bitcoin’s fall from above $83,000 to about $62,000 over the weekend. At Monday’s closing price of $36.79, the bank’s 16.4 million IBIT shares would be worth about $605 million, roughly $173 million less than the value in the filing.

U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs have been bleeding money since the quarter ended. IBIT lost about $2 billion over nine straight sessions through mid-November before taking in roughly $76.5 million on Monday.

The filing does not show whether Goldman added to or trimmed its bitcoin fund positions in October or November. That answer arrives with the fourth-quarter 13F, due in mid-February.
