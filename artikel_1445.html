Russell must do something wacky now after disappointing Bahrain GP qualifying

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Never mind the lack of an ice vest, George Russell is somehow going to need to be the coolest man in Malaysia if he is to turn seventh on the grid into a positive result in Sunday’s Bahrain Grand Prix.The first race in Sepang in nearly a decade – a late addition to the calendar because of the conflict in the Middle East – promises to be an absolute scorcher. It might even be worth setting your alarm for the 8am UK start time. Red Bull’s Max Verstappen will start in pole position for the first time this year. He will have Ferrari’s Lewis Hamilton alongside him.

It will be the first time since Hungary 2023 that those two gladiators have started on a front row together. Throw in sweltering temperatures, low grip, high tyre degradation, and the ever-present threat of a Malaysian monsoon sweeping through Sepang, and you have the potential for fireworks.

Russell might need something wacky if he is to do anything from that far back. This was a desperately disappointing result for the British driver. After his victory in Baku last weekend, Russell was hopeful he had finally found the winning formula and that he could continue to take the fight to his team-mate Kimi Antonelli, whom he trails in the drivers’ championship by 66pts.

Eighth, four places behind, and 0.24sec slower than the Italian, was not what Russell would have had in mind, although both Mercedes drivers will rise a spot because of the five-place grid penalty meted out to Red Bull’s Isack Hadjar for exceeding his engine allowance for the season.Russell will have felt particularly disappointed as Antonelli only got one run in Q3 after using up a set of soft tyres when he made a mistake and went off track in Q2. He now trails his team mate nine-seven in qualifying head-to-head this year.“No to be honest,” Russell admitted when asked whether he knew why he was so slow. “This circuit is an outlier in terms of the abrasiveness of the tarmac.“The lap times seven years ago were six or seven seconds faster and at certain points on the track it feels like you are driving on gravel, which is the same for everyone, but it is clearly affecting us more than others.”Mercedes’ lack of performance was doubly unexpected because not only did they arrive here with a massive upgrade package, but Russell revealed this week that he and Antonelli were foregoing the cooling vests you see some drivers sporting on the grid, in favour of car performance.

Toto Wolff, the Mercedes team principal, admitted it was a head-scratcher. “Very difficult to understand,” the Austrian said. “We have been a little bit caught out on Friday with some car behaviour that we did not see all year, but then you can say it is very hot and the tarmac is different to all the [other] tracks. It looked really good this morning. We seemed to have, at least with Kimi, made a step in understanding. And then, qualifying was a little bit back to what we saw before, just no performance.”

Still, both titles are in the bag so Wolff cannot be too unhappy. Russell is the one who will be really miffed. He really needs to be beating his team-mate pretty much every race weekend if he is to get his title hopes back on track.

At the front, the mood was somewhat different. Verstappen became the first non-Mercedes-powered pilot to take a pole position this year. It was a first pole position for Red Bull Powertrains, too. And with his long run pace looking more than decent, Verstappen should really hold on for his first win of 2026. Hamilton will do his best to hang on to the Dutchman’s coattails. The seven-time champion was delighted when told he had taken P2. “Let’s go guys! Finally! Great job guys, really great job. Car felt great.”

This was indeed an excellent performance from the Ferrari man – and just the tonic for the Scuderia after weeks of gossip and speculation. Hamilton was admittedly three tenths slower than his rival, which he put purely down to a power deficit on the straights, but he said he was hopeful his long run pace was more competitive. The last time a F1 held a race was held here back in 2017, the British driver claimed pole only to be overhauled by a youthful Verstappen. He will be desperate to turn the tables this time.

“It was really hard to find a good balance, and we made one change before qualifying and we hit the sweet spot,” he reflected afterwards. “I am so, so grateful. I will try my best to hold on to Max tomorrow, and if we are in the fight that would be awesome. I’ve been talking too much, but I guess you talk more when you are happy. It was my best lap of the year. It feels like I won, and I will give it everything tomorrow.”

It is Russell, though, who needs a result even more badly if he is to remain a factor in the championship. “As a team, we have yet to have a bad race this whole season and this was clearly a very bad qualifying for us,” he admitted, adding that “all the numbers” from the upgrade package suggested it was an improvement on the old car. “But I think there is going to be a lot happening tomorrow. The tyre deg is huge and race pace will be king. So even though today was a real disappointment it is not the be all and end all.”

The traffic this weekend has been horrendous. After 10 years without a race in Malaysia, during which time F1 has grown massively, I think they have forgotten how to do traffic management. Shuttles are inconsistent, taxis can’t get in/out, it’s taking forever just to reach the airport a few kilometres away - some fans have been braving the heat and walking. It took over two hours to get back into Kuala Lumpur last night (48km). My understanding is the Bahrainis are organising the race this year, while local forces are handling the traffic outside the circuit.

“I am much happier with where the car is, but tomorrow is a completely different ballgame. Making these tyres last around this track, they are overheating from the get-go. Every little bit of wheelspin you have, every little snap you have makes a difference of how far you are going to go. I like to think we have got a good balance. Max was really quick on his long runs, so I will just try my best to hold onto him tomorrow.”

Lots of chat about cooling vests this week, which are optional. George Russell is among the drivers who isn’t using one, which is interesting a) because Russell has suffered badly from heat in the past (Qatar, Singapore) and b) because he says Mercedes chose to put the development money into car upgrades instead.

It’s an interesting admission. Not everyone is sold on the idea of cooling vests. Ferrari and McLaren will definitely be using them this weekend, for instance. Max Verstappen won’t as he doesn’t like them. Russell thinks the FIA should just develop the tech themselves and it shouldn’t be part of the budget cap.

“Our team have put a lot of emphasis this year on the cockpit temperature,” he said. “We won’t be running the cooling vest because we haven’t put the resource into making it robust enough. I feel we need the FIA just to step in and say ‘Here’s some money extra in the cost cap or there’s an external company who’s going to develop it for everybody’. Because we should all be wearing it. When it works, it works great. So, I would like to wear it this week, and I would like to wear it next week. But the team asked me...look, we can develop it, and we can put the money towards it...[but at a cost].

He added: “The top itself is not expensive ..you’ve got to effectively make a system where you can fit a block of dry ice or actual ice, and then has the airflow to circulate the cool water throughout. If that system isn’t robust enough, then suddenly you have no circulation, and then you get hot. You get ambient temperature circulating. The vest is one-design, but the system that circulates the water is different [at different teams].”

Formula One returns to Malaysia for round 16 of the season for the Bahrain Grand Prix. That probably sounds strange but after the Bahrain Grand Prix was originally postponed in March, F1 reinstated it to the calendar with Malaysia hosting it because of the ongoing war in the Middle East. Despite being held in Malaysia, Bahrain have retained the naming rights for the event, which is officially known as the Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia. The last time Formula One was in Malaysia was nine years ago back in 2017, when a young whippersnapper named Max Verstappen, still four years away from winning his first world championship, claimed just his second victory.

Last time out around the streets of Baku, Mercedes’ George Russell claimed victory at the Azerbaijan Grand Prix, pipping Verstappen by just a tenth of a second. That was Russell’s first grand prix win since June and cuts his team-mate Kimi Antonelli’s lead in the drivers’ standings to 66 points. Antonelli had a challenging weekend in Baku, starting 16th after a crash in qualifying and coming home in fifth to see his championship lead to Russell cut by 15 points.

Verstappen was quickest in first practice yesterday, with the Ferrari of Charles Leclerc fastest in second practice. Earlier today, championship leader Antonelli was nearly three tenths clear of the field in third practice. Mercedes, 160 points clear at the top of the constructors’ standings, have brought significant upgrades this weekend, which includes a new floor, rear suspension and rear corner. It is the team’s first significant car update since the Canadian Grand at the end of May.

This race in Malaysia represents the second leg of the season’s first flyaway triple header, with the Singapore Grand Prix to come next weekend, so the demands on the teams and the drivers are incredibly high. Qualifying from Sepang gets under way at 9am (GMT).
