An Exercise Trampoline Can Make Cardio More Fun. These Are 6 We Like.

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Experts share what makes a rebounder safe, comfortable, and worth adding to your routine.

As a lifelong distance runner, I’ve had trouble finding low-impact cardio workouts that feel challenging enough when I’m not pounding the pavement. But that all changed when I discovered an exercise trampoline, also referred to as a rebounder. They use a similar style of trampoline to what you used to bounce around on as a kid in the backyard, just with a fitness twist. The result is that the sized-down versions are just as fun, but you also work up a sweat.

Using an exercise trampoline is known as rebounding, and this type of workout is considered a high-intensity movement that’s low impact. That sounds a little confusing, but it basically means using an exercise trampoline will get your heart rate up there while giving your joints a break, something that can be especially appealing for runners. If you’re like me and crave a challenging lower-impact workout that doesn’t include an exercise bike or rower, rebounding might be your new favorite cardio workout.

While rebounding workouts will get your heart rate up quickly, there are plenty of other health benefits to consider, too. A 2024 scientific review found that rebounding can be great for enhancing blood circulation, bone health, and balance. And a small, 2018 study of 18 women found that participants were able to lower their blood pressure and improve their VO2 max—a marker of cardiovascular fitness—on a 12-week rebounding program.

Outside of research studies, Nikki Gnozzio, CPT lists off rebounding benefits of better balance, overall strength, and, of course, more fun. That last part is worth considering, especially if you’ve been struggling with workout motivation or boredom.

Of course, you need a trampoline before you can try this out. According to the personal trainers we spoke with, a rebounder’s tension, adjustability, maximum weight, sturdiness, and size should all factor into your buying decision. Below are six options that stood out.

There are a lot of brands to choose from, but JumpSport stands out as one that’s stood the test of time. “JumpSport has been around since 1996 and is a well-respected brand,” says Melissa Cantillo, an ACE-certified personal trainer and fitness manager at Canyon Ranch. The brand’s mini trampoline line, which includes the JumpSport 530 Pro, popularized the bungee cord system that’s used in many exercise trampolines, she points out.

The JumpSport 530 Pro has an extension skirt to protect your feet in case you accidentally land on the cords. The legs are slightly arched at the top to lower the risk your trampoline will tip over. If space is a premium at your place, simply remove the legs and stash your trampoline in a closet or up against a wall to free up floor space.

The trampoline is relatively small to begin with—it has a 39-inch diameter—but that works in your favor when it comes to storage.

There’s a lot more to love about this trampoline, though. It has a built-in, adjustable handlebar for balance, which is especially handy given that smaller bouncing surface. The frame and skirt are already installed once it arrives, so you can simply snap in the legs and handlebar to get bouncing within minutes.

Exercise trampolines generally fall into a category known as rebounders or “mini trampolines,” which basically means they’re smaller than those massive ones kids use in backyards. But rebounders have a relatively small bouncing surface, so you have to be a little mindful about where you jump. That’s worth considering, whether you prefer a larger bouncing area or just want to build in some added insurance that you won’t accidentally rebound off.

This trampoline has a built-in handlebar that’s adjustable, in case you want extra help with balancing while you jump. The reinforced steel frame has six arched legs for stability. Keep in mind that the legs aren’t removable, just in case you want to store your trampoline in a closet or other smaller space.

The elastic cords allow for a quieter bouncing experience. (The trampoline comes with extra bungees in case one breaks.) This trampoline comes with a 10-year warranty on the frame and two-year coverage on the mat to help protect your investment.

The Sunny Health Springless trampoline delivers the basics at a relatively moderate price tag, making it ideal for those new to rebounding and still figuring out if it’s for them.

This trampoline has a less common square shape, which tends to provide a more consistent, uniform bounce across the entire mat. Since this trampoline is on the smaller side, that’s especially handy for small spaces. The smaller size and removable legs mean it won’t hog a lot of room at your place.

Another perk for beginners? The Sunny Health Springless comes with the free SunnyFit app, which includes rebounding workouts to get you started.

Tension is the tightness and level of resistance you’ll have on the trampoline as you jump. “Smaller exercise trampolines will be more resistant (have higher tension) because they sit closer to the ground, and you don’t want to be tapping the ground with each jump,” says Sydney Sherman, NASM-CPT. “They’re meant to absorb the shock as you land your jump, which is why they’re a great low-impact option for those of us with sensitive joints or coming back from an injury."

Some trampolines allow you to tweak the settings to better suit your needs. An adjustable handle is key (if your trampoline has a handle), Gnozzio says. But some will also allow you to adjust the tension to create a bouncier or less bouncy feeling.

The maximum weight limit for an exercise trampoline will be different based on how it is constructed or the brand. Generally, the weight limit often varies from 250 to 450 pounds. The sturdiness of an exercise trampoline will also vary, but you'll want to make sure it has rubber grip covers for the legs to stay in place (and not scrape your floors) and a handlebar that doesn't easily move for stability.

Sure, you’re already aware that your trampoline needs to fit in your room. But you need plenty of room around it, like a foot on either side, just to be safe. "A lot of trampoline workouts involve arm movements, so you’ll want to have space around you, as well as above you for jumping," says Gnozzio.

While you’re at it, don’t forget to factor in your ceiling height. A minimum 8-foot ceiling height is recommended, according to Cantillo. You don’t need to bounce in a room with an extra-high ceiling since you push into the trampoline more than you jump up, she explains. “Your feet may come off the trampoline anywhere from 1 inch to 8 inches depending on the workout,” Cantillo says.
