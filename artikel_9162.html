How iPhone Duo’s Unlock With Front Camera Works (and How It’s Different Than Face ID)

How iPhone Duo’s Unlock With Front Camera Works (and How It’s Different Than Face ID)

The newest iOS 27 developer beta partially addresses one of the iPhone Duo’s biggest shortcomings: its lack of Face ID.

When PetaPixel reviewed the iPhone Duo, scoring it a 7.5, Jaron Schneider knocked Apple’s debut foldable for its reliance on relatively slow, inconsistent Touch ID. While Apple’s recent smartphones have all used Face ID to unlock quickly, the iPhone Duo sticks with Touch ID. It’s finicky, and users must hold the phone in just the right way to unlock it. Even when it works as expected, it’s not quite as swift as Face ID.

“I have found that Touch ID is slower than Face ID for unlocking the phone. It got to the point where I was so grateful that I have an Apple Watch because I could set it to automatically unlock it when it sensed I was wearing the watch. Unlock with Watch is also, somehow, faster than using Touch ID. It removed a lot of the friction I was butting up against with Touch ID, but it requires a Watch,” Schneider wrote of his Apple Watch-powered workaround.

However, with today’s launch of the iOS 27.3 developer beta, Apple has begun testing a new feature: Unlock with Front Camera.

To be clear, this is not Face ID. The iPhone Duo still lacks the necessary hardware to offer the iPhone’s typical unlocking method. However, Unlock with Front Camera is set up in a similar way, requiring users to look at the front camera and turn their head from side to side. Like Face ID, it is also fast and works at off-angles.

I have been using an iPhone Duo review unit for the past few weeks as part of a long-term follow-up review and have been forced to rely on Touch ID on numerous occasions, often finding it frustrating. I installed the new developer beta on the Duo, set up Unlock with Front Camera, and am very much on board with it.

Again, it’s not a perfect Face ID replacement. It requires the user to use Touch ID every so often, for starters, about every 45 minutes. Just as other iPhone users occasionally need to enter their passcode for certain tasks or after a restart, iPhone Duo users will still need Touch ID even with this new feature.

Unlock with Front Camera does seem to work in poor lighting conditions, although I have not been able to test it yet in total darkness. Given the front camera’s lack of an infrared sensor, I expect some challenges.

Face ID relies on the iPhone’s TrueDepth sensor to quickly and securely confirm the user’s identity against existing robust data. Without Face ID and TrueDepth, the iPhone Duo doesn’t promise the same level of security or performance. Even so, Apple is clearly confident that Unlock with Front Camera is sufficiently secure, at the very least.

Unlock with Front Camera promises at least some of Face ID’s convenience, which is ultimately more important to most users than Apple’s absolute top-notch security. The iPhone Duo still stores facial data privately and securely to confirm (or deny) a match between the user attempting to unlock the phone and the initial setup imagery.

The Duo can definitely tell the difference between me and a picture of me. When I attempted to fool the Duo, it not only didn’t fall for my cleverly laid trap, but it also locked the device and required me to use Touch ID again. This is also its response when someone else tries to unlock my phone. If something doesn’t jibe, users must either use Touch ID again or enter their passcode.

It also works only with the front-facing camera, not the under-display one on the inner screen. That’s not a huge issue, but it’s worth noting.

So no, the iPhone Duo doesn’t suddenly have Face ID thanks to a software update, but it does have something that is at least better than what we had and addresses a very common complaint lodged against Apple’s new phone.

I will continue my long-term testing of the iPhone Duo, using it as my only smartphone. It has not been without its shortcomings, but Apple said today that there are now more than 200,000 apps that natively support the Duo. Between that and Unlock with Front Camera, the Duo genuinely keeps getting better.
