LeBron James Sues Sports-Betting App for Using AI-Generated Images of Him

NBA superstar LeBron James has sued the makers of an AI sports-betting app for allegedly using AI-generated images of him in its marketing.

On Tuesday, James filed a lawsuit in the Southern District of New York against the founders of Bron, an AI-powered sports-betting app. The lawsuit focuses on a video ad released by the Bron app’s founders earlier this month. It shows an AI-generated version of James using the Bron app to bet on basketball games from a locker room.

According to the complaint, the ad depicts James wearing a basketball jersey bearing his nickname and the iconic number 23. It also reproduces his tattoos and a simulated version of his voice. The video shows the AI-generated figure scrolling through NBA betting options on the app.

“The ad catapulted the Bron app from obscurity to controversy, with the ad gaining a million views within a day — exactly as defendants intended,” James argues in the civil complaint, according to a report by Courthouse News Service.

The complaint also says the AI-generated image of the basketball star “falsely communicates that Mr. James wagers, or considers wagering, on NBA games and NBA player ​performance—conduct that NBA rules prohibit.”

James argues that the depiction of him sitting in a locker room and betting on basketball could also harm his reputation.

“This is at odds with Mr. James’s well-known and highly televised routine of being on time for games and being one of the first people out on the court,” he argues.

James also claims that the app’s name was chosen to attract attention by exploiting his well-known “Bron” nickname. He accuses PlayVision, the company that operates the app, and its co-founders, Anish Gupta and Marc Zoghby, of deliberately using the name to mislead consumers into thinking he endorsed or was affiliated with the product.

James’s lawyers sent the company a cease-and-desist letter on November 5. Following the letter, the startup removed the ad from X (the platform formerly known as Twitter) and Instagram and said it was working to change the app’s name, according to the complaint. However, the lawsuit alleges that the video remained available through third-party reposts and that the company had not changed the app’s name by the time the lawsuit was filed.

LeBron’s lawsuit comes after Norwegian soccer player Erling Haaland sued an airline last month over a social media post that featured an edited image referencing his distinctive hairstyle during the 2026 World Cup.
