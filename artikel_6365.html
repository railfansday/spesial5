New York Liberty Star Jonquel Jones Unpacks a Trip Home to the Bahamas

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

When Jonquel Jones picks up the phone to chat with us at Condé Nast Traveler, she’s in the back of her team bus, having just arrived in Minneapolis with the New York Liberty. In two days, she and her teammates will make WNBA history, defeating the Minnesota Lynx in Game 1 of the 2026 Playoffs. Their win will be the first time a No. 8 seed—the lowest-ranked team to qualify for the playoffs—beats a No. 1 seed, the league’s top-ranked team. But for Jones, such a victory will hardly qualify as new territory.

The 32-year-old New York Liberty center has spent the better part of the last decade breaking records: She has taken home all four major MVP honors, became the first international player named All-Star MVP, and helped lead the Liberty to their first-ever championship in 2024.

An athletic career like hers means rarely staying in one place for long—a reality reflected in the setting of our conversation. Between WNBA seasons and stints playing overseas, Jones has built a life where traveling has become a part of the game (off the court, of course). But no matter how far basketball takes her, there’s one destination that brings her back to herself: her home country, the Bahamas.

When her schedule finally allows for a pause, returning to the islands offers Jones a different kind of home-court advantage—and a chance to reconnect with the places and people that matter most to her. We caught up with the WNBA All-Star to talk through a typical trip back to the Bahamas, from the comforts she packs before leaving New York City and her in-flight essentials to the Bahamian delicacy she never returns without—plus how to make it yourself!

What trip are you unpacking for us?A trip to Grand Bahama, where I grew up.

Tell us about the itinerary: Where did you stay? What were the highlights?I always stay at my condo, which is close to where my family lives. I have homes both in the Bahamas and in New York. My grandmother, parents, cousins, nieces, and nephews are all still on the island. Aside from two of my siblings, pretty much my entire family still lives in the Bahamas, so getting to spend time with them is really special.

How often do you visit?I try to go back whenever I can, but it all depends on my schedule. This year’s off-season gives me a few months off, so I’ll be spending at least two months back home. During the FIBA Women's Basketball World Cup, though, I was only able to go for seven days. It really varies from year to year.

Who were you traveling with?I always bring my dog Rylo with me when I go back home. He’s a seven-year-old poodle mix, and he loves the Bahamas. I got him in 2019, right before COVID. I grew up around a lot of dogs—mostly yard dogs—and I’ve always had a special connection with animals.

Growing up, I had a dog named Princess, and she was my dog, for real—no pun intended. She went everywhere with me. Whether I was riding over to my grandmother’s house, walking to the beach, or playing basketball, she was always right there. She’d sit on the sidelines and watch me play, making sure I was okay. Princess was my first experience with unconditional love, and she’s the reason I fell in love with dogs.

On this trip, did you overpack, underpack, or get it just right?I’m definitely an overpacker. I like bringing little comforts from home in New York, like my favorite matcha and coffee beans. I’m a big matcha fan, so I’ll even bring my entire matcha set. There’s a Japanese market called Midoriya Japanese Grocery on Fifth Avenue in Brooklyn that has amazing matcha, and I always stock up before a trip. I’m also a big fan of Stumptown Coffee; Hair Bender is my go-to blend. It’s nice to have a few pieces of my everyday routine with me. They help me settle in.

What drugstore essentials do you always have in your backpack?Claritin! I have to have it with me. I have terrible allergies, and something about the pine trees in the Bahamas make them go crazy. I actually recently brought a friend back home with me, and the two of us spent the entire trip passing the bottle back and forth. Without my Claritin, it definitely takes me longer to acclimate.

What is a little luxury you always bring with you?A nighttime face mask. I love having something I can use to pamper myself during a little girls’ night in. A good overnight mask is my go-to when I want to firm up my skin or wake up with a little extra glow.

Do you have a favorite item of travel clothing to fly in?Comfort is definitely my priority when I travel. I'm not just looking after myself, but Rylo, too, so I want to make sure we're both comfortable. Dogs can pick up on nervous energy, and I don't want him to sense that from me. I try to stay in a relaxed, zen state so we can both enjoy the trip.I'm a Nike athlete, and I love their top and bottom sets. They have great two-piece options that are simple but fashionable, which makes them perfect for traveling.

What's one piece of home you always pack?I’m probably going to sound crazy, but I’m a gamer, and bringing my PlayStation 5 console is a big part of how I get comfortable and settle in when I get back to the Bahamas. I have a whole gaming setup waiting for me at my condo, so all I have to do is plug in my PS5 and I’m ready to go. It’s one item I never travel home without.

What's your go-to snack to keep on hand for long travel days?It’s probably not the healthiest choice, but I grew up eating Cadbury Crunchie Bars. I loved them as a kid, then went through a phase where I stopped eating them. For some reason, though, I’ve recently fallen back in love with them, and they’ve become my go-to. Now they also make Cadbury Crunchie Rocks, which are bite-size, cluster versions of the bar that come in a resealable bag, making them a little easier to travel with. I also love Cadbury Dairy Milk Fruit & Nut. Oh my god, that bar? So good. I feel a little like a granny saying this, but it’s one of my favorites. It has almonds, raisins, and dried fruit—it's delicious. My mom used to buy it when I was growing up, and as a kid, I couldn't understand why she liked it so much. But, lo and behold, I get it now.

What's your in-flight routine like?I’d say either my Steam Deck, a portable gaming system, or a good book. I’m a big reader and use it to escape. I especially love fiction and fantasy. I recently read The Sword of Kaigen: A Theonite War Story, which has a similar feel to Avatar: The Last Airbender. It’s such a great book, but fair warning: the fight scenes are brutal, so prepare yourself. It’s worth it, though.I also can’t travel without my Bose headphones. Those are an absolute must.

What's a fragrance that never leave home without?I love, love, love the LV Lovers fragrance by Pharrell Williams in collaboration with Louis Vuitton.

Last question: What souvenirs do you bring home?Whenever I leave the Bahamas, I always bring back conch. It’s a Bahamian delicacy that you’ll find in every dish, and can be prepared in so many different ways. My favorite is conch fritters, made by tenderizing the conch and mixing it into a batter with onions, thyme, and celery.I always bring back bags of frozen conch fritter batter so I can enjoy a taste of home once I'm back in the United States. Whenever I'm craving them, I just thaw the batter, add a little baking powder, and deep-fry it. And just like that, I have fresh conch fritters. The closest comparison I can make is a hush puppy. If you can't find conch, you could probably try making them with calamari, too.
