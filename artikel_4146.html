AMD FSR Upscaling 4.1 Has Officially Arrived For Xbox Series X|S

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

"We can't wait to see what developers create with it."

A couple of weeks ago, the folks at AMD made headlines when they announced that AMD FSR 4.1 would be coming to Xbox Series X and Xbox Series S in the near future, and the rollout has officially begun today!

As a result of this, developers will have access to a new method of improving visual fidelity (and in some cases boosting performance) in their games across both consoles, which should prove particularly useful when it comes to the lower-powered Xbox Series S.

"Working closely with AMD, the XBOX team has optimized AMD FSR™ Upscaling 4 for XBOX Series X|S, making ML-based upscaling practical on the consoles' RDNA 2-based GPUs. These optimizations enable developers to benefit from improved image quality while balancing GPU performance, helping them deliver better visual experiences on existing XBOX hardware."

"The new Microsoft GDK sample demonstrates how to integrate FSR4 across XBOX Series X|S and Windows using AMD’s FSR SDK. It includes both Full and Lite INT8 models, allowing developers to explore different rendering resolutions and evaluate the tradeoffs between image quality and performance."

Of course, it's still early days right now and we don't have any real-world examples of AMD FSR 4.1 working on XBOX Series X|S just yet, but we assume developers of all kinds will start integrating it into their games going forward.

Not only should this lead to improved experiences in demanding XBOX Series X|S titles, but it should also mean the two consoles remain capable for many years to come, even after Project Helix arrives in the next generation.

As for your comparison with QSSR, it is currently only supported by two games on PS5: Wolverine and Ghost of Yōtei. At this stage, it is mostly a marketing feature with very little real impact for players. In reality, AMD's QSSR is derived from FSR, but in a much more limited form because, as you mentioned, the base PS5 lacks some native hardware features that are available on the Xbox Series consoles.

Furthermore, it is more complex for third-party developers, as it requires genuine porting work. As a result, this technology will likely remain limited to a small number of PlayStation-exclusive titles.

FSR, on the other hand, is a technology that has been deployed much more broadly by game developers, as it is already widely used on PC.

The case of PlayStation's PSSR, which is only available on the PS5 Pro, is different. The PS5 Pro already includes more native hardware capabilities than the base PS5 and is significantly more powerful. Even then, PSSR is also derived from FSR 4, although it still does not match FSR 4 at equivalent hardware performance levels. PSSR has seen greater adoption among developers, but the numbers remain relatively modest in practice. We're talking about roughly 200 games that support PSSR, which is still a small figure compared to the total number of games released each year.

What is certain is that PlayStation's marketing around GTA VI is misleading, or at the very least requires significant nuance. If we're talking about the base PS5, then the claim is false: GTA VI would run better on Xbox Series consoles with FSR 4.1.2. On PS5 Pro, however, GTA VI would likely perform better, which is hardly surprising given that it is a more powerful mid-generation refresh released later and sold at a significantly higher price. However, its price-to-performance ratio remains relatively poor.

Given the relatively small install base of the PS5 Pro compared to the standard PS5, the Xbox Series remains the most obvious value-for-money choice for the majority of players interested in GTA VI.

The claim that GTA VI or other games runs better on PS5 is simply false, on the contrary.
