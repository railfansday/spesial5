If you're hoping to make cool miniatures, props, and all manner of nerdy goodies, Black Friday 3D printer deals provide so many great offers for beginners. Actually, it's hands down the best time to dive in; both machines and materials typically hit record lows, so you're unlikely to get a better (or cheaper) opportunity.

Because you aren't short on options, I've rounded up a couple of my favorite newcomer-friendly machines and the gear you'll need to kick things off. These have all been slashed in price for 2026's Black Friday 3D printer deals, so beginners can get into the hobby for as little as possible. These have all been chosen based on my years of experience testing 3D printers at GamesRadar+ as well as reporting on the sale, so rest assured you're getting maximum value for money here.

If you're not sure what to go for, don't worry; here's a quick breakdown. Want to make terrain, props, tokens, or generally useful stuff like storage boxes or dividers for the best board games? Filament printers are the way to go. Looking for incredibly high-detail miniatures or more detailed cosplay gear? Resin printers are what you want. I've included my entry-level picks for both that come from extensive hands-on experience, and they're what I use at home right now.

No matter whether you're new to 3D printing or have experience with the hobby, I think this may be my favorite filament machine. Besides being incredibly easy to use (something I praised in my Elegoo Centauri Carbon 2 Combo review), it comes with multi-color print functionality so that you can create more advanced projects without having to manually swap out material. Plus, it being so enclosed keeps things tidy and much quieter too. To cap things off, I'm pretty sure that's a record low price.UK: £398 £329 at Elegoo (with code)

Even though they're smaller spools (most normal ones are 1KG, whereas these are 250g each), this multi-color pack is a great way to kick off your hobby and provide the widest range of shades as cheaply as possible. It's never been cheaper than it is now for this year's Black Friday 3D printer deals.UK: £34.99 £28.25 at Amazon

If you want a fuss-free, foolproof way of printing resin miniatures that isn't going to make you want to tear your hair out, this is the perfect choice. As noted in my Anycubic Photon P1 review, it's able to overcome any problem you throw at it due to a user-friendly interface, a heated vat to avoid failures if you're printing in a colder environment, incredibly high print resolutions, and a new 'Wave Release Technology' for a better overall print. When you throw in good positioning accuracy and stability to avoid failures, it's a great choice.UK: £649 £399 at Anycubic

If you're buying a resin 3D printer, you'll need one of these - a wash and cure machine. That's because models aren't ready once they're made in the printer; they've got to be rinsed off with either IPA or water (depending on the resin you've used) and then cured using UV lamps. It's not a particularly drawn-out process, but it is important. If you're getting Anycubic's machine, I'd go for its accompanying Wash and Cure system for ease.UK: £113 £79 at Anycubic

Want the easiest entry-point to resin 3D printing with the least fuss? I'd recommend water-washable resin, because you don't then need to worry about grabbing smelly IPA as well. With this material, you can just use normal water in your wash and cure machine which is much, much easier to then dispose of.UK: £21.80 £13.59 at Amazon
