4 Speed Records and Free Solos That Went Down in the Last 2 Weeks

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Becca Droz (left) and Kate Kelleghan (right) set a new female speed record on the classic 'Naked Edge' in Boulder's Eldorado Canyon on October 26. (Photo: Jon and Jess Glassberg / Courtesy COROS)

Maybe it’s the erratic weather pressures of El Niño, or perhaps there’s just a particularly competitive spirit in the air right now. In the past two weeks, several climbers have broken records, made improbable repeats, and soloed terrain that would make many of us squirm.

Last week, we looked into the culture of free solo speed climbing in Boulder, Colorado, speaking with several athletes about the risks and influences they carry in their high-stakes sport. On the heels of this deep dive, two of the athletes who spoke to us went on to break additional records of their own within the past few days. This includes a new FKT on Half Dome and a fresh women’s speed record on Eldorado’s most famous route.

More broadly, we’ve seen a few historic achievements in the past couple weeks by free soloists and speed athletes alike. Here are four record-breakers we’ve been watching lately, in chronological order.

Just a few weeks ago, Alex Honnold told Adrian Ballinger that he thought someone would likely repeat his free solo of El Capitan before anyone dared to repeat his insanely hard linkup of peaks and scrambles in Red Rock. Back in 2022, he dubbed that linkup the Honnold Ultimate Red Rock Traverse (aka the HURT).

Honnold will have to eat his words, because on October 19, Nevada climbing guide Nik Mirhashemi put down the world’s second HURT, with slight modifications. Friends have been calling his revised route the NURT: Nik’s Ultimate Red Rock Traverse.

For the NURT, the 36-year-old skipped four non-technical summits, including Goodman, Buffalo Wall, Sandstone North, and Limestone North. Instead he traversed from White Rock Peak to ascend the seven-pitch Frigid Air Buttress (5.9+; 800ft). He then continued to the top of Icebox Peak. “Basically, I had to choose between adding Frigid Air or sticking with the OG route, where those nontechnical peaks are,” Mirhashemi said. “I didn’t want to skip Frigid Air as it is quite classic.” He also called the section he swapped in one of the traverse’s “routefinding cruxes.”

Despite the variations, Mirhashemi doesn’t think there’s a significant difference in effort level. He said that though his traverse is slightly shorter, he added more technical terrain, making the difference essentially a wash. “Nice to add a little personal flair to these things I suppose,” Mirhashemi said.

“I wanted to try the HURT for similar reasons to Alex,” Mirhashemi told Climbing. “Super long days of soloing moderate terrain is one of my favorite things to do. I’ve been coming out to Red Rock for many years and just moved here a year ago. I’m always looking for new ways to challenge myself and it just made sense as a goal right in my backyard.”

Last spring, Mirhashemi made his first HURT attempt, then completed several “scoping missions”  to prepare for his fall go. “I was never sure if I could pull it off,” he said. “Stoked it’s not haunting me anymore!”

Kate Kelleghan has been busy breaking her own record again, shaving minutes and seconds off the female fastest known time on The Naked Edge (5.11b), a classic multi-pitch in Colorado’s Eldorado Canyon.

Kelleghan, along with her French partner Laura Pineau, already held the record for the route’s women’s speed record of 37 minutes and eight seconds, which was 32 seconds faster than Kelleghan’s previous go in 2021 with Becca Droz, a Boulder-based climbing guide.

In late October, Kelleghan began working the route again with Droz. And on October 26, the athletes set a new record of 36 minutes, 26 seconds, shaving 42 seconds off the previous record.

“Becca and I were the team that started the women’s record, so taking it back together feels full circle for our friendship and our community,” Kelleghan said. Droz added that being able to track splits, as well as time out the approach, actual climb, and descent with their COROS watches “was a game changer”; both women are COROS athletes.

The men’s record is held by John Ebers and Ben Wilbur at 24 minutes and 14 seconds.

Just a couple months after smashing the speed record on the First Flatiron in Boulder, 32-year-old American climber Michael Reese established a fastest known time on Yosemite’s Half Dome on October 28. In so doing, he broke an 11-year FKT set by the late Dean Potter. Reese climbed the 2,000-foot batholith from the Mist Trailhead to its summit in 1 hour, 12 minutes, and 58 seconds. He followed Potter’s original line up Grizzly Gully, then free soloed Snake Dike (5.7 R; 800ft). He broke Potter’s previous record set on May 3, 2015 by about six minutes.

A few days after his Half Dome lap, our reporter Holly Chen, who also reported on his Flatiron speed record, caught up with Reese.

“In 2021, one of Dean Potter’s good friends told me about this as a potential record to try. He became one of my main climbing partners,” Reese told Chen. Since then, Reese has made 16 attempts and several scouting missions before his record-breaking attempt in late October.

“Records are made to be broken. I’m not gonna be surprised if it’s broken one day,” Reese said.

In the second-hardest free solo by a woman, Aleksandra “Ola” Taistra climbed a seven-pitch, 720-foot route in Sardinia, Italy on October 29. The line in question is Margarites (7a+/5.12a) up the limestone cliffs of Monte Oddeu. Established in 1998 by Andrea Calvo and Lorenzo Nadali, the route has become one of Sardinia’s “most sought-after lines,” according to Taistra. It includes a bouldery 7a+/5.12a crux pitch high off the deck; two 7a/5.11d pitches; and four additional pitches graded 6c+/5.11c, 6b+/5.11a, 6a+/5.10c, and 5c/5.10a, in order of descending difficulty.

“Climbing Margarites for the first time I thought that climbing it without a rope was impossible for me,” Taistra shared with Climbing, “and that’s exactly how the plan was born—first get to know the route, and then properly assess my skills.” Taistra says that when she finished free soloing Margarites, she thought: “Oh, that’s it?”

Taistra already put down one of the hardest female free solos in history in May, when she climbed Alfredo Alfredo (5.11d/7a) ropeless. Now, the only known harder female free solo belongs to French legend Catherine Destivelle, who climbed an unnamed route in Mali without a rope that she said maxed out at 7b or 5.12a/b.

In May, 44-year-old Taistra told Climbing that free soloing gives her “so much peace” and feels less stressful than climbing with a rope. The 44-year-old climber rehearses her routes on toprope until she feels confident in the moves.
