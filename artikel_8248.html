SHT Days 21, 22: Wrapping Up 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Today was one of the most delightful days I’ve ever had on any trail! My husband decided to skip hiking with me today and opted to catch up on some emails. There was supposed to be weather rolling in later anyway, so I could just put my head down and fly. But that’s not the reason today was delightful. Let me tell you why.

We got an early start today to beat the rain that is due this afternoon. Bill dropped me back off at that spot on Old Ski Hill Road. There’s not technically a trailhead here, and the area seems country-residential, but there is a wide spot on the gravel road near where the trail crosses that one could park briefly. I continued SOBO down easy snowmobile track for the first few miles until the trail crossed Cook County Road 6. I found Bill in his truck here, parked on the side of the road, waiting for me with a hot latte and a chocolate croissant! He had run into Grand Marais and back out to this trail crossing. How sweet!

I enjoyed my jolt of caffeine and sugar and continued on. The trail travels through the boggy and wet Sundling Creek area on a long section of raised boardwalk here. What a chore that must have been for the trail builders when they did this! I admired their work and took a few pictures before and after I crossed, and was thankful the rain hadn’t started yet. I imagined the horror of slipping on the wet boards and falling into the flooded pond entirely.

Crossing the flooded Sundling Creek on a long boardwalk.

I passed the Bally Creek Pond Campsites just in time, needing to complete at least one of my L&L’s (courtesy of aforementioned latte) before crossing Bally Creek Road and trailhead. Who do I find here? None of than Bill again, of course! This time it was a doozy. He had brought along our small air fryer from the camper and cooked up a few frozen burritos for lunch! What a swell guy! At this point a light rain had begun to fall, so it was nice to sit inside a dry vehicle and enjoy my grub in the warmth.

A heavy mist blanketed the landscape this afternoon.

I could have called it a day here, but I really wanted to make tomorrow, my last planned hiking day, short and easy. With the amount of rain forecast for tonight, I was worried about the state of the trail as well. So, I said goodbye to Bill once again and marched on.

The next five and a half miles can only be described as a very pleasant forest walk. The terrain was pretty nice, and the inclines and declines were gentle. There was another random section of big, beautiful pines. I just love walking through these giants. It smells good, pine needles soften the trail, and right at the moment they were sheltering me from a light rain.

Approaching Cty Rd 45 and a glimpse of the river we will be following tomorrow.

I eventually reached the North Cascade River Campsite and signed the trail register here. The precipitation was already beginning to become more than a light sprinkle as I followed the Cascade River down the last mile out of 12 total to Cook County Road 45 where I met Bill for the final time. He had a thermos of hot vegetable beef soup ready to warm me back up. I could get used to this! I had my partner and best friend meet me at every trail crossing today with a smile and a treat. That, my friends, is how today was made exceptionally delightful!

Last night it rained cats and dogs. I mean, it just kept downpouring for hours and hours. We have just a small teardrop camper, without a bathroom, and the galley is outside. Even though we had a nice tarp system set up, the rain was finding a way to seep through and soak everything.

When we came back from the trail yesterday, we sheltered inside the camper for a few hours but we started to get hungry for supper. There was definitely no way we were going to venture outside to scrounge up something to eat, so we decided to splurge on town food. We quickly hopped in the truck and drove just down the road to the Cascade Lodge Restaurant for dinner. The place was packed to the brim. Everyone else had the same idea again to escape the weather.

This morning we slept in. We knew it would be a wet world once we clambered out of our small sleeping space, so we were avoiding it, at least until it warmed up a little. Eventually, we opened the hatch to be greeted with sunshine, albeit cold and wet. We had a quick breakfast and made our way back to Cook County Road 45.

Bill was going to finish this out with me today. We had about 4 miles left to do, and we were hoping for nice scenery alongside the Cascade River. There was a short uphill road walk upon leaving the trailhead before it entered the woods on single track. It was a cold and wet start to the day, but the sunshine was promising.

The natural curve of this tree made for a perfect break spot!

The trail was mostly gentle for the first couple of miles, except where it crossed a few streams where campsites Cut Log and Boreal had been placed. The poor campers who chose either of these spots would have very steep jaunts from camp to filter water.

The first of many waterfalls and cascades we will see today.

Shortly after passing the Boreal campsite the trail hugs the Cascade River. The remaining trail back to our campsite was stunning. The Cascade River is absolutely beautiful, constantly showing off gorgeous waterfalls, and well, cascades. We couldn’t take enough pictures. What a way to finish the trail this year! It was a beautiful sunny day, hiking along what has to be one of the most amazing sections of the Superior Hiking Trail.

As we neared the end of the river where it spills into Lake Superior, we hopped onto a short spur trail that leads back into Cascade River State Park. The trail re-entered the park practically right at our campsite. It was an incredibly convenient end to not only today’s hike, but my 2026 SHT adventure. The SHT handed me everything I could expect from a backpacking journey-wildly varying weather, challenging terrain, and rewarding views. I feel proud of what I accomplished this year, and had a lot of fun doing it. As Bill and I packed up our campsite to head home, I feel myself already excited to return for the remaining 80 miles.
