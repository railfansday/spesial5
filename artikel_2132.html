How Dave Challinor honed his craft with Celtic, Brentford trips before taking Sheffield Wednesday job

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Out-of-work managers often take the time out of the spotlight to evolve their coaching game, and this was no different for new Sheffield Wednesday boss Dave Challinor. Between leaving Stockport County and taking the SWFC job, the 51-year-old has revealed Celtic’s Martin O’Neill and Brentford boss Keith Andrews’ influences.

In an interview with The Star, Challinor revealed he had dinner with O’Neill, alongside watching his Celtic side train and play. On O’Neill, the Owls boss said: “He is a really inspirational character. He is a figurehead for that football club. The success he’s had at such a massive place is enormous.”

Challinor then toured Brentford’s training ground and took inspiration from how the Bees prepare for set pieces.

“Brentford, I suppose for any club in England, or across the world, really, just the way they go about it and the way they’ve done it from the very, very top, is unbelievable,” he said. “They are an inspiration not just to me as a manager, but I’d suggest football clubs the length and breadth of the country.”

Challinor has plenty of experience in the dugout, but for someone who has followed his trajectory, there is always more to learn. He has journeyed through the football pyramid from Colwyn Bay to Sheffield Wednesday in 16 years, so he can take valuable lessons from those who have pedigree at a higher level.

In terms of his managerial abilities as an individual, Challinor will have taken plenty from O’Neill at Celtic. The 74-year-old progressed through lower leagues like the Sheffield Wednesday boss before managing big clubs in top leagues, such as Leicester City, Celtic and Aston Villa.

The sheer amount of knowledge and experience O’Neill has gained over the years means he will have had some valuable nuggets of advice to pass down to Challinor.

As for Brentford, touring their training ground would have been more of a lesson in full club management, and what can help traditionally ‘smaller’ clubs compete with the elite. The Bees are not the wealthiest club by any means, but they are sustainably run from the top to the bottom, and meticulous training sessions and match preparation means they are now in their sixth season as a Premier League side.

Challinor will have taken valuable lessons from these trips, and it will only benefit SWFC.

David Storch has expressed that Sheffield Wednesday are just at the start of a long-term rebuild. Challinor was not appointed with immediate success in mind, he was brought in to lead a new era at Hillsborough.

Some of the Hillsborough faithful will expect a strong push for promotion from League One this season, and justifiably so. Despite Storch stating the need for patience, the squad is strong enough to emerge as a strong contender at the top end of an evenly matched third tier.

Challinor’s willingness to learn and take inspiration from other managers and model clubs is an excellent attribute. Despite the success he has found in the dugout already, his desire to grow as a boss is only a good thing for SWFC as they rebuild under his leadership.
