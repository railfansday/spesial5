The Birmingham City, Tom Wagner & Jay Stansfield trend that is now unmissable

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

In the 2023-24 campaign, Birmingham City found themselves in the top six and the play-off places when they decided to part company with John Eustace heading into the October international break.

Star of the show for The Blues in those opening couple of months had been young attacker Jay Stansfield on loan from Fulham with Stansfield building on an impressive campaign in League One at Exeter City the year before.

Stansfield’s story is well known and an emotional one, having represented Exeter and given the then retired ‘number nine’ shirt over a decade after his father, Adam, had worn that shirt for The Grecians before passing away.

In the second city at Birmingham, and still only 20 when he arrived, Stansfield had notched five goals in his first 13 starts in the second-tier but after Eustace’s surprise departure, Birmingham fell away from being early promotion challengers, eventually being relegated at the end of the campaign.

Despite that, Stansfield had continued to catch the eye with some memorable strikes in his 12-goal tally, with two assists, in the Championship – as well as a match-winning performance with a goal and an assist against Hull City in an FA Cup third round replay that season.

Such was the form shown by Stansfield, he had begun to become sought-after by many but, with Birmingham beginning a new era, it would have taken something special to bring him to League One.

Given that Chris Davies was newly-appointed and with a record-breaking summer spending spree, Stansfield was the cherry on top as he arrived from Fulham for a club-record fee of £15 million – driven by owner and chairman Tom Wagner.

Stansfield remained essential for Birmingham as Blues broke the all-time EFL points tally in the 2024-25 League One campaign with Stansfield their top scorer, hitting 19 in the league and 23 across all competitions.

Even last season, back in the Championship and only 22 having helped England’s U21 side defend the UEFA U21 European Championship over the summer, Stansfield hit double figures in the 2025-26 campaign and there would be reason to believe, with those numbers, Birmingham have landed themselves a future legend with over 50 goals for the club already and still as such a young age.

There would have to be some concerns, though, that the unreasonable expectation placed upon him due to his fee has contributed to periods of poor form or potentially low confidence.

For example, for a player that smashed the all-time transfer record for League One and was the spearhead of the most dominant team ever at that level; Stansfield’s initial goals tally of 19 seems reasonable. Once delved into further and seeing that nine of those 19 were from the penalty spot.

Ten open play goals for a team as dominant as that Birmingham and for the fee shelled out; it wouldn’t be unreasonable to suggest the pressure was on Stansfield, perhaps more from outsiders than City supporters, to kick on and prove himself more emphatically in the Championship last season.

He eventually did scrape himself into double figures with a goal in Birmingham’s penultimate home game of the campaign but that, again, is a fairly underwhelming return after hitting eight in their first 16 games of the season.

What is a trend that has occurred would be that Stansfield appears to be at his best when chairman and owner, and the architect of his big money move, Tom Wagner is in attendance at St Andrew’s.

Wagner, who continues to fully recover from a stroke that he suffered in February but is said to now be in better shape, was in attendance at the weekend as Birmingham pulled off an impressive and potentially pivotal 2-1 defeat of Swansea City with Stansfield notching a brace.

That made it three goals in two games for Stansfield after had scored their third in a 3-0 defeat of Millwall a few days’ earlier – having previously managed just one strike in his first 13 appearances so far this season.

A theme of all of those matches in which Stansfield had scored in was that Wagner was in attendance, with the first goal back in September coming in a 2-2 draw against Wolverhampton Wanderers before those goals in successive matches in the past week.

Wagner, as he continues to recover, is unable to attend every match but a theme is now repeating that when he does, it tends to lead to Stansfield getting himself on the scoresheet.

Correlation does not mean causation, obviously, but there is reason to believe or at least acknowledge that Stansfield, should he indeed be someone to struggle with some confidence issues as any footballer does from time to time, does feel that confidence when Wagner is in attendance.

In fact, Stansfield himself has previously discussed the impact of having Wagner in the stands, doing so after the American returned for his first game back at St Andrew’s since suffering that stroke in February by going to Blues’ 2-2 draw with Wolves:

Stansfield said: “Tom is a special person so for him to be here and knowing he’s recovering well is really positive.

“He gave the stadium a massive lift. That’s what Tom’s character is, he puts smiles on people’s faces. As a team, for us to walk out and see him there and for the crowd to be signing his name special for him as well.

“He’s been in contact a lot more in the last few weeks which is good, giving me confidence, trying to push the team, saying everything is going to be good and things.

“He kept saying ‘I’ll see you soon’ so I was waiting for the time to be able to go and see him. I’m really looking forward to going to see him and making sure he’s alright and having a good conversation.”

Stansfield has even gone as far as to describe Wagner as a ‘father figure’ more recently than that and so him once again finding his best with Wagner in attendance is no real surprise:

Marko Stamenic's red card for the visitors at St. Andrew's has caused plenty of online debate

The former Fulham man outlined: “When I first joined here he had faith in me, he backed me all the way, and even when times got tough during League One he always messaged me saying 'stay confident, do this, do that.'

“I got a message from him before the Wolves game saying 'Just go out and be yourself, I'm sure you'll score' and that happened so it was a great feeling and a great day.

“I can't thank him enough for the opportunity that he gave me to come back to this football club. The first season we were in the Championship we built a good relationship, he liked the way I pressed, got around the pitch and also showed my quality.

“For him to have faith in me and bring me back at a crucial time in my career... I was looking at it and I needed first team football week in, week out and a lot of people asked questions why I moved back to Birmingham and it's probably a decision I'll never look back on. It's changed my life and also my family's life as well.

“He's a father figure and even the little messages that he sends, you read them and you think let's go out and do it.”

So, while correlation does not mean causation, there is probably just cause to believe the trend and pattern of Stansfield’s performance and confidence improving in the presence of Wagner is real.
