AI has become a creeping force across the gaming industry, driven by studios and AI users even as the bubble pops worldwide. In response, many online stores and mod workshops now require users to disclose AI usage, letting those opposed to the practice keep their distance while still allowing users to use the tools.

Leaking in a similar vein to Persona 3 Reload, footage showcasing a Persona 1 Remake has appeared, looking almost too good to be true.

After a user poll asking players if they should implement such a system, VR Chat’s community overwhelmingly said yes, with a 95% swing towards disclosing AI assets, models, and maps. Despite this clear answer, the team at VR Chat Inc. has ignored the request, leading to a massive wave of negative reviews on the game’s Steam page, lowering the overall rating to Mixed.

VR Chat is less of a game and more of a virtual hangout spot, incredibly modular to house different experiences, players, and artwork. The fanbase is incredibly eccentric, boasting some truly fantastic creations and real creative use of the space for group gatherings.

Whether it's used to meet new people, get together for a big showcase, or catch up with friends across the globe, VR Chat is a title that relies on its active fanbase heavily, and needs them to be actively creative and excited to share these creations in order to thrive.

Like many creative spheres of gaming, VR Chat has a rising tide of AI creations flooding its boards, oftentimes created using other popular creations from the community as a base. The VR Chat player base has vocally complained about these AI outliers for a while, leading to an official response by VR Chat Inc.

In order to gather as much community sentiment as possible, they ran a poll asking players if AI creations should be marked as such. This seemed like a decent answer to the issue, allowing those against AI to identify it from a distance while still allowing those who didn’t care to use AI offerings.

The community made their opinion very clear, smashing the poll with a 95% lean towards disclosing AI creations. Despite this clear answer, VR Chat Inc. refused to implement the feature, which lead to the current review bombing from thousands of players.

Every online platform needs to make sure its community is satisfied to a degree, but a platform for community and creation like VR Chat is all about that community. With them upset, VR Chat might be forced to make some serious concessions to bring the fanbase back.

Fandom outcry has been a very powerful tool over the last few weeks, and it seems like more and more studios are learning that fact a little too late. I don’t personally play VR Chat, but every time it comes up, it's because of some cool new system or hangout the community has managed to set up.

Digital theaters, VR Tabletop RPGs and card games, even the truly wild functions of certain models and maps. VR Chat is a great showcase of fun, community-driven creation, something that's become harder and harder to maintain in the modern gaming space.
