Browns offense effective at the goal line and perimeter against Texans

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Down 14-0, down 17-0, it doesn't seem to phase this Cleveland Browns team in games against the Titans and Texans. Maybe it's just something with bad AFC South teams, who knows. But Cleveland only had one cold quarter of offense this week, and then they put it together for the remaining three quarters, showing a great deal of consistency by stretching the field with the long ball, working the perimeter, and having success at the goal line.

Below, we analyze the snap counts and stats on offense for the Cleveland Browns' Week 10 game against the Houston Texans.

25-of-40 (62.5%) for 312 yards, 1 INT. 8 carries, 49 yards (6.1 YPC), 1 TD.

There were a couple miscues by Deshaun Watson, with the biggest being a missed touchdown pass to Jerry Jeudy at the end of the first half that saw the Texans playing a bizarre defense. It's unclear if that was a mix-up between the two or an inaccurate throw. Watson also had a little trouble hitting some receivers in the right spot then they were crossing the field, as evidenced by passes behind KC Concepcion and ahead of Blake Whiteheart, leading to an interception.

Besides that, though, it was a solid game for Watson. The biggest key was his ability to push the ball downfield. With the Browns down big, he got the long ball to Jeudy. On the drive before that, he had a perfect one dropped into Concepcion too, but he couldn't hang on. Watson also got away from defenders against the Texans to gain 49 yards on the ground, including a touchdown run. He graded out to a 77.7 by PFF on offense, fourth highest on the team.

4 carries, 6 yards (1.5 YPC). 4 catches, 31 yards (6 targets).

10 carries, 38 yards (3.8 YPC), 1 TD. 3 catches, 33 yards (5 targets).

There still wasn't a ton of production in the ground game, although Quinshon Judkins did get the all-important go-ahead touchdown in the final minute. Both running backs were assets as receiving targets, though, taking advantage of the Texans' defense along the perimeter.

3 catches, 21 yards (8 targets). 1 carry, 6 yards (6.0 YPC), 1 TD.

Finally, the Browns actually got a completion to Malachi Corley. And then they did it a couple more times. It's about time they used him, because if he's playing snaps as a receiver, he can't just block 100% of the time. Some may say that you do that to catch a team by surprise like they did this week, but they should've done that sooner. Now, that play will be an option moving forward, and if teams collapse on it, you might have someone leaking out behind Corley. He was the team's highest-graded player on offense by PFF (89.8).

This was a little bit of a rougher day for KC Concepcion. I wouldn't say he had point blank dropped passes, but there were about three contested balls that I think he'd tell you that he wishes he could've held on to. Nonetheless, he still had a touchdown run as part of the Browns' goal line success.

Jerry Jeudy is getting open against these down-the-depth-chart defenders. As mentioned earlier, he also got open before the half, but Watson missed him.

Only one catch for Denzel Boston, who had a toe injury that bothered him again late. Thankfully, the bye is coming up. Funny enough, they used him heavily on punts this week.

Harold Fannin is just a beast, he refuses to go down. He now leads the team in catches (44) and yards (544) heading into the bye.

Blake Whiteheart was the team's lowest-graded offensive player. I'm not saying he should be replaced this year, but during the bye, seeing if the team can find a better contributing second tight end might be on their radar.

The offensive line was quite good in pass protection this week, as the sacks were minimal. Penalties weren't a thing either!

Tytus Howard was the team's second-highest graded player on offense (82.1), per PFF. Zion Johnson, Spencer Fano, and Austin Barber also did a solid job as far as grading goes. Elgton Jenkins is the only player who had a rough grade.
