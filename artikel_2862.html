Knicks refind clutch gene to hold on for tight overtime win over Raptors

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

TORONTO — At least for one night in Canada, the clutch Knicks were back.

Jalen Brunson saved his best shot for last, Karl-Anthony Towns stuffed the boxscore on his 31st birthday, Landry Shamet shined off the bench and OG Anunoby sealed a win at the foul line as the Knicks reaffirmed their long-standing dominance over the Raptors with a 112-108 overtime victory Sunday.

Brunson's 3-pointer from the corner gave the Knicks the lead for good with 33 seconds left in overtime, a shot that allowed the point guard redemption after he botched the final possession of regulation with a slip into a turnover.

In OT, Brunson received an inbounds pass from Mikal Bridges — who was stellar defensively down the stretch — and knocked down the pullup jumper over Scottie Barnes. It was Brunson's biggest shot of the young season.

"I work on that shot," Brunson said, "and I'm happy it went in."

Anunoby hit a pair of clutch free throws, then thwarted a potential tying trey from Barnes, completing a comeback from 13 points down in the fourth quarter. Before Sunday, the Knicks were among the NBA's worst clutch teams, going 1-6 in games that were within one possession at any point in the final five minutes.

"That feels good. That was our time of the season where it's like, 'Let's buckle down. Let's go and work our way back for this one,' " said Shamet, who finished with 23 points in 30 minutes. "We had a few early in the year last year and obviously in the playoffs, but to get one under the belt and win one that way — that's an encouraging win for the group where maybe things didn't go the way you wanted to at times."

The Knicks (7-6) have won a remarkable 14 straight against the Raptors, a streak dating to 2023. Brunson has never lost to the division foe since joining the Knicks, but none of his victories last season were as dicey as this one.

"We know what we are capable of in those situations," Brunson said. "I'm just happy the way we fought. We could've easily given up, chalked it up to the next game. But we kept fighting."

The Knicks play the Raptors again Tuesday, giving the Knicks four days here. They left Sunday feeling good about themselves — even if they weren't satisfied with their play for much of the game.

Landry Shamet (44) of the New York Knicks shoots as Allen Graves (22) of the Toronto Raptors defends in Toronto, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. NBAE via Getty Images

"It's always good to win close ballgames like this. Especially when you feel like you played particularly well. You give Toronto a lot of credit because they were physical and tried to push the pace and got their hands on the ball a couple times. So for us to be able to find a way to get a win in a very ugly game was huge. … It helps with that belief. And that's a huge component of what our standard and what our culture is about."

The Raptors (5-8) were playing without oft-injured star Kawhi Leonard, who is out indefinitely with knee soreness.

Towns, who turned 31 on Sunday, extended his double-double streak with 28 points and 17 boards. Brunson dropped 28 points on 9-for-22 shooting in a season-high 41 minutes.

Anunoby (44 minutes) and Towns (43) also played longer than anticipated. But sometimes it's worth it to win a close game.
