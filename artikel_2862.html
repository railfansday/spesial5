Knicks 102, Timberwolves 99: Knicks Cut Down Wolves

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

After couple great resume wins that included the first time in Minnesota Timberwolves franchise history where they defeated the same team, twice in a row, on back-to-back nights, the home stand continued with a familiar face back in town. Karl-Anthony Towns and the defending champion New York Knicks arrived at Target Center with hopes of toppling his old squad. KAT returning to Minnesota is always a big game and an emotional matchup for the fanbase and, as per usual, he was welcomed back with open arms.

The Timberwolves have been on a hot streak as of late, winners of seven of eight games with a single lapse in play against the Chicago Bulls. They looked to continue a successful home stand and take advantage of the early season uneasiness the Knicks have been experiencing.

While the Timberwolves still are dealing with a handful of injuries so far this season, they did get Jonathan Kuminga back to give a much needed boost to a team that had to grind out a win against the Blazers on Tuesday night.

The first quarter started at a breakneck pace as both teams kept finding the bottom of the net. Towns started the game well at his former home, while LaMelo Ball's aggression showed up early on as he was launching shots.

It was a game of runs early on, as each team had their stretch of dominance – Knicks had a 10-0 run early, and the Timberwolves matched pace with a 13-2 run of their own. The Knicks continued to be red hot as they shot over 70% from both the floor and from three in the first quarter. KAT led the way with 10 points while LaMelo held serve from the other side with nine of his own. New York took the advantage into the second by a score of 33-26.

Chris Finch started the quarter with a technical foul as he'd been in the referees ear for much of the start of the game. KAT and the Knicks continued their bucket-getting at an absurd clip as Josh Hart woke up from his season long slumber, draining a few threes.

During the middling minutes, the Timberwolves fell into too much isolation offense and struggled to generate good looks on the offensive end of the floor. New York went on a 9-0 run down the stretch and shot 10/16 from three in the first half which gave them a healthy 15-point lead heading into halftime. Jaden McDaniels was perfect from the floor in the first half and led the way with 11 points for Minnesota.

Jonathan Kuminga came out of halftime swinging, taking it strong to the basket and mixing in outside shots as he scored 14 points in the frame. Minnesota kept trying to claw back into the game, but the turnovers they committed made it hard for them to mount a run. They cut the lead a few times, but Jalen Brunson did a great job staving off runs with his shotmaking.

The Wolves continued to struggle to spur a fourth quarter run to start the final period. A Jaden three brought some wind into the sails as they went on to tighten up the defense as the Knicks had zero field goals over a seven minute span in the fourth.

Still, it wasn't enough. The Wolves had trouble getting over the hump, but a reviewed KAT flopping technical gave them life again.

Minnesota had their chances, but missed their final five shots of the game including a LaMelo Ball three that could've tied the game with seconds left. New York played keep away and took the close win, 102-99.

New York got greedy tonight even though Thanksgiving isn't until next week.

Second chance points are always backbreakers, and the Knicks took advantage in winning that battle 12 to four.

New York had hit their shots throughout much of the first half, which is already tough to deal with, and when that is compounded with multiple opportunities on the same possession it makes it feel that much worse.

Throughout the game, the score always seemed much closer than it felt. With Jalen Brunson denying any run in the third quarter, it started to feel like one of those games where the Timberwolves wouldn't be able to get the boulder rolling down the hill. It felt that way for much of the game, but then the Timberwolves started to gain some traction in the fourth.

This run was defined with their top ranked defense putting the Knicks in jail. Minnesota held New York to 12 points in the fourth quarter, including that aforementioned seven minute stretch where they did not score a field goal. The Timberwolves made it extremely hard on Jalen Brunson and navigated screens adeptly on defense to avoid the matchup hunting the Knicks were searching for.

Unfortunately, the Wolves wasted the great defense with tough turnovers that plagued them during the earlier quarters as well. They played extremely hard to get back into the game and rode the momentum that they created, but came up short in the closing minutes.

While they didn't deserve to win today, their best chance to tie the game was unfairly taken by an inadvertent whistle that Chris Finch was livid about in his postgame press conference.

Finch said, "The one that everybody in the arena saw hit the rim? … I mean it was clear that it hit the rim, it hit the rim and it went straight down to the floor… I mean were continually told that these are the best in the world and you know you can't miss that one".
