Shearling Jackets, Luxury Carry-Ons, and All the New Launches Esquire’s Editors Loved This Week

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Every product was carefully curated by an Esquire editor. We may earn a commission from these links. Here’s how we test products and why you should trust us.

Plus, James Bond’s luggage of choice gets an update.

The theme this week is shearling jackets. Three-of-thirteen gives a pretty clear look at what the best of the week really was leading into fall. First, we have Fortela, which released an absolutely insane reproduction of a grizzly jacket. With shearling on the outside panel, I’ve always wondered why the grizzly shape never stuck around much after the World Wars, but I have been seeing it pop up a lot more as vintage and reproduction recently. Otherwise, I’m into this Husbands bomber as well as this ladies’ shearling bomber from Buck Mason.

Elsewhere, it’s more confirmation that we are hitting the twilight weeks of 2026. It’s all sweater season and travel. In that section, Globe Trotter is the week’s big winner. The brand’s new polycarbonate suitcase gives it a bit more modern traveler appeal, but the look is still unmistakably Globe Trotter. It’s a rare pairing you don’t see too much. If you’re looking for a suitcase to handle all your holiday travel, I can’t think of anything better to recommend; this is the pinnacle. Scroll, shop, and enjoy.

My problem with Fortela is that the reproductions are so good I don’t even want to keep hunting for the real thing. An interwar grizzly jacket is a grail for me. This one, made in partnership with Italian motorcycle clothing brand Shangri-La Heritage, is so good that I’d feel like an idiot paying $2,000 for a vintage one. —Luke Guillory, commerce editor

Noah and Converse just released their second Chuck 70 collaboration, reimagining Converse’s cult-favorite silhouette in Noah’s signature tartan, this time in navy and green. —Emma Saxon, contributing fashion assistant

An unfathomably good Christmas cardigan from Thom Browne’s capsule collection that’s exclusive to Mr Porter. Fair Isle motif on the four-bar pairs with the Fair Isle Hector (Thom Browne’s dachshund mascot) on the back. —L.G.

I’m a huge Globe-Trotter fan, so when I heard about its lightest suitcase ever was made out of polycarbonate (like every brand does these days) I was scared. Upon review, I was wrong; its absolutely smashed it out of the park. Modern amenities with a look all it’s own—reset of the market should take notes. —L.G.

On the other side of the luxury suitcase aisle, Margiela paint on a Rimowa really, really gets me. I’m a sucker for the patina gimmick. —L.G.

Irish design company Orior is making some of my favorite out of anyone in the world. Design-forward without being too cerebral—just laser focused on material and craft. —L.G.

The best tee on the market, now made from the best cotton in the world. —L.G.

Every year when that first fall chill hits, I try to find a scarf that beats what Begg x Co. makes. This year marks five years of being unsuccessful in that search; this is the scarf you want. —L.G.

I talk about Casatlantic in this column every season. The fall-winter collection goes to classic charcoal and navy—incredible. —L.G.

Carl Friedrik makes some of my favorite luggage ever. This week, we got a new collection of compact leather crossbody bags, perfect for everything from running errands to going to a nice dinner to going on a fancy vacation. —Bellamy Richardson, associate commerce editor

Sorry to overdose on shearling jackets, but this one jumped out at me from Buck Mason’s holiday collection. In fact, all of Buck Mason’s women’s jumped out at me. The men’s stuff is good, but women’s is god-tier. Boys, take my lead and buy your lady a gift this season. —L.G.

It’s always good to have options. This sampler set lets you try eight of the brand’s fragrances and see which ones are just a fling and which are here to stay. —Andrea Ríos, fashion assistant
