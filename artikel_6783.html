The 6 Best Milk Frothers for Coffee Aficionados

These handheld and automatic machines will give you the foamy upgrade you need.

With the increasing prices of, well, everything, it’s more important than ever to save in all the little places you can. For the caffeine addicted, making your daily beverage at home is an easy place to start. And you can pretty easily trick yourself into thinking your home coffee is as good as your neighborhood café when you invest in a milk frother. Frothed milk is certainly not a necessity for making good coffee, but it is a requirement for any half-decent latte or espresso drink. If you've already got the basics, like fresh beans, a good coffee machine, a grinder you love, and something to drink the coffee out of, a milk frother is the final piece of the puzzle. At last, you can call yourself a home coffee connoisseur.

All milk frothers essentially work the same way, by aerating the milk to make varying degrees of bubbles. Milks with higher fat content make a silkier froth; lower-fat milks (and nondairy milks) make for airier froth. If you have an espresso machine, you can make a cappuccino or latte, or you can top your regular coffee (or matcha) with foam for a café au lait. It’s mostly science, but you can inject as much artistry as you want to the process depending on which route you go.

There are more options than you think on the market. You can go with something handheld, automatic, or manual. Some let you control the amount of foam made and dial in the texture you want; others have presets that do the deciding for you. Some have settings dedicated to plant milk. No matter your coffee preferences, you’ll find something that will fit you with these six top-notch milk frothers.

Instant Pot might not be the first name you think of when you think coffee, but this affordable gadget will change that. The MagicFroth is easy to use, with two buttons that let you set the temperature and froth level between low, medium, and high settings. This makes it easy to get the hang of, although it does lack the depth and nuance that some more expensive machines offer.

One of the big advantages of any frother of this style is it can be used to heat up milk for other beverages like hot chocolates or a matcha latte. With that in mind, this is a pretty tremendous value. Plus, the stainless-steel pitcher, plastic lid, and whisk all are dishwasher-safe, making cleanup as easy as humanly possible.

If you’re really dedicated to the idea of a milk frother, you probably want something that looks good on your countertop. The Smeg mini frother is a one-button smooth operator that has that striking retro ’50s look we’ve come to expect (and sometimes lose our minds over) from the brand. You can choose between hot froth, cold foam, or a warm milk mode, and those are the only choices. That can be a pro or a con depending on what you want from your frother. If you value aesthetics and simplicity, you can’t go wrong with this single-serve milk frother.

We praised this handheld, travel-friendly hand frother in this year’s Esquire gadget awards. It’s small and dead quiet, but has enough power to it to make a decent little milk foam. Plus, it can be quickly disassembled into the storage case to either be safely put away for later or packed into your luggage.

It foams—it can even be used as a whisk or mixer with the other two of its three attachments. Use it to whisk your eggs or blend your protein shake in the morning. It can handle it all, and once it runs out of juice just plug it in with a USB-C cable to recharge it in a matter of minutes.

People love their Nespressos, and the Aeroccino matches the performance and aesthetic of most machines. If you have an original Nespresso or Vertuo machine and want to elevate your pod drinks with some latte art, well, this is the next step. Four different touch buttons get you to any of the four settings: cold frothed milk, airy frothed milk, silkier frothed milk, and just straight up warmed milk. It’s just the one compartment, so it’s not dishwasher safe. You’ll be cleaning by hand and rinsing by hand between batches too. It’s a little more hassle than some of these other machines in the long run.

If you have a family of latte lovers, you might need something with a bit more capacity for foaming multiple drinks’ worth of milk at once. That something is the stainless steel Breville Café Milk Frother. It’s a high-capacity frother that can hold about double what the average one does.

For control freaks, Breville's milk frother has the most amount of options for you to play with. You can dial in the exact temperature of your foam, which most other models don't let you do. Plus, the top half comes out, and you can just put it in the dishwasher. Despite its size, this makes it easier to clean than pretty much any other model out there.

If, like me, you just can’t do whole milk in your lattes anymore (or ever), you want a machine that will do just as good a job with any alternative. Be it oat, almond, or any plant milk of your choice, you want to be able to put anything in your frother and have it whip up a delicious foam. This De’Longhi frother has a button dedicated to it (as well as one dedicated to cold foam). It also happens to be the second-best-looking option on this list.
