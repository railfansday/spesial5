Vox users can watch videos and listen to podcasts in the app, but reading stories requires a paid subscription.

Vox has launched its first mobile application, the publication best known for its explanatory journalism announced Wednesday morning.

Growing membership is a “key priority,” managing editor for audience and membership Bill Carey said. Vox believes launching the app is one way to do that. A Vox membership costs $5 per month, or $60 per year, and offers ad-free versions of podcasts and unlimited access to all of the website’s contents.

The app launch comes at a time when news publishers are increasingly trying to keep users on their own platforms amid growing preferences for AI summaries, news creators, and industry-wide traffic declines.

“The move toward developing our membership program in the last couple of years has really been about understanding how important it is for us to build more community and to foster that loyalty [of our] audience,” Carey said. “When we were looking at that, it really felt like an app was a good way to continue pointing in that direction.”

Vox isn’t the only news outlet focusing on mobile apps. The Verge — Vox’s former sister publication under Vox Media — named traffic declines as one of the reasons why it plans to launch its own app next year. The Wall Street Journal’s head of digital, Taneth Evans, recently said that the Journal makes its app more useful to users by offering all of its mediums in one place. Evans also said that news publishers “need to give people reasons to open an app beyond simply offering news.”

“Websites will become a service for agents,” Evans said. “Apps are gonna be the thing that humans will connect with.”

Vox declined to provide website traffic numbers, but data from Comscore shows that the website’s unique visitors decreased by 36% year-over-year between September 2025 (7.4 million) and September 2026 (4.8 million). Archived data from SimilarWeb shows the site had 20.8 million visits in September 2021.

“Vox isn’t immune to the larger trends that have led to web traffic declines in recent years, but those only represent a portion of our audience,” Carey said in an email. “Vox is focused on building audience engagement across platforms, with social video views up 86% year-over-year and newsletter audience up 14%.”

“Even if there wasn’t a decline in search traffic, as everyone has been seeing — and we still get members from search — we want to create that relationship with our audience,” publisher and editor-in-chief Swati Sharma said. “People know Vox and they have a relationship with Vox. We have a younger audience that’s on all of these different platforms. I think it is definitely a part of building that relationship ourselves.”

That the app makes all Vox content accessible in one place for current and future members also signals the “un-siloing” of the newsroom overall, Sharma said. Vox currently has 78 editorial employees.

But while teams discussed story ideas with each other and during editorial meetings, “they all [needed] to go into their creative spaces and find what works for their medium and not be in service of just one larger section.”

That’s changed over the past five years, Sharma said. Each team — text, video, and audio — does a little bit of everything now. Text reporters appear on podcasts and in videos, while podcasters and hosts also write their own newsletters.

“In a lot of newsrooms, there’s one dominant form, and at Vox, there wasn’t. They’re all really important,” Sharma said. “[Senior correspondent] Dylan Scott is on our Unexplainable podcast talking about Alzheimer’s. [Host and editorial director] Astead Herndon does a newsletter and a Substack Live every week, and he does a podcast. That’s the future for us, and that’s where our whole newsroom is moving, where those silos are done and you can basically do everything.”
