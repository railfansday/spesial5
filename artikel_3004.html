AI Is Creating A More Dynamic Future For Airline Revenue Management

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The airline industry has always used technology and data to help teams make decisions that improve the customer experience or increase revenue. Today, airlines have an opportunity to automate and improve many of those decisions.

AI can help them respond more dynamically to demand, manage inventory more effectively and determine the best action to take for a particular flight or customer.

This shift is especially important after a customer has already purchased a ticket. The post-booking period has traditionally received less attention than initial pricing and sales, but it may represent one of the industry’s largest opportunities to improve both profitability and customer satisfaction.

When airlines sell all their inventory on a flight, there is very little they can do to accept additional last-minute, high-yield demand.

When a flight is full, airlines may be unable to accommodate business travelers and other passengers who really need to get on that specific flight. Those customers may then purchase tickets from a competitor, even though some passengers already booked on the original flight might have been willing to travel at another time.

A sold-out flight does not always represent the best revenue or customer outcome. The airline may still be turning away high-value demand while carrying passengers with more flexible schedules.

AI can help airlines move toward managing their inventory much more dynamically. Rather than treating a booking as final, airlines can continue evaluating demand, capacity and customer needs as departure approaches.

Post-booking optimization focuses on what happens after the customer has purchased a ticket.

Historically, customers could keep their itinerary, cancel without a refund or receive a credit. Airlines had limited ability to create better alternatives as demand changed.

AI-informed systems provide an efficient way to recapture that opportunity. Once the airline understands where that opportunity may exist, it can identify customers who could be interested in moving to another flight in exchange for an incentive, such as a travel voucher.

That voluntary change frees inventory for passengers who need the peak flight, allowing the airline to capture incremental demand while offering flexible customers more options.

The goal is not simply to move people from one flight to another. It is to optimize revenues and load factors at the same time while giving customers more flexibility.

Making this model work requires airlines to understand two things.

The first is which flights are likely to sell out and overflow high-yield demand. Predictive models can anticipate where that opportunity will appear.

The second is the willingness to change and the flexibility of individual customers. Using different features associated with each booking, AI can help estimate which passengers may be open to moving and what the right offer for each customer might be.

This allows the airline to maximize both conversion and revenue per transaction. Rather than sending the same incentive to everyone, the airline can make a more relevant offer based on the context of the booking and the customer’s likely preferences.

AI-driven optimization should give passengers a clear, voluntary choice and something valuable in exchange for changing their itinerary.

When done correctly, post-booking optimization can create what I see as a win-win-win situation.

The first winner is the customer who needs to purchase a ticket on a particular flight. By creating available inventory, the airline can allow that passenger to get onto the flight they really need instead of losing them to a competitor.

The second winner is the flexible customer, who voluntarily switches flights in exchange for an incentive they consider worthwhile.

The third winner is the airline. It can improve customer satisfaction while also generating a revenue uplift and making better use of its available capacity.

The incremental profit can be significant, and implementation may require relatively few resources. Airlines can introduce targeted capabilities and begin seeing results within months.

Post-booking optimization is part of a broader opportunity for AI across the customer journey.

Conversational AI, including large language models, can help airlines improve customer satisfaction and become more efficient. Unlike a traditional call center, an AI-supported system is not limited by the number of agents available to have conversations with customers.

However, the greatest opportunity is not simply creating a conversational user experience. Airlines should connect that experience to an intelligence or context layer that can determine the best offer or decision for each customer.

A call center employee may be working with a limited amount of information. An intelligent system can consider millions of data points and a much broader context before recommending an action.

The result is a more efficient and intelligent interaction that identifies the best decision and most relevant offer at that moment in the journey.

For decades, airlines have often lagged behind online travel agencies and other retailers in their retailing capabilities. AI creates an opportunity to accelerate the process of closing that gap.

By building or accessing the right knowledge, intelligence and context layers, airlines can orchestrate decisions in real time across the customer journey. They can become much better retailers while improving both the customer experience and profitability.

Every relevant technology change creates winners and losers. The speed at which companies embrace new technologies often helps define which category they enter.

We saw this with the internet and online sales, which transformed airline retailing and contributed to the rise of low-cost carriers. AI may create a similar dividing line over the next several years.

The airlines that succeed will not necessarily be the ones that deploy AI everywhere at once. They will be the ones that identify the decisions where intelligence can create real value and move quickly enough to capture the opportunity. Post-booking optimization is one of those decisions, and it illustrates how the industry can become more dynamic, flexible and profitable.​​
