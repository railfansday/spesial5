$1,000 Split Across These 3 Cybersecurity Stocks Could Be Worth This Much in 5 Years

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

AI is making hackers faster and more dangerous, but Fortinet, Cloudflare, and CrowdStrike have shown investors how they plan to win as the technology advances.

Here's a simple way to think about cybersecurity stocks right now: Companies are handing more of their work to artificial intelligence, and hackers are using AI more, too. That turns robust cybersecurity from a nice-to-have into the essential lock on the front door, and three companies in that industry just shared news that hints at where they'll be in five years.

Split an investment of $1,000 evenly across Fortinet (FTNT +3.67%), Cloudflare (NET +3.01%), and CrowdStrike (CRWD +1.46%), and you'd put about $333 into each. In my base cases for how those stocks will perform, that $1,000 would grow to roughly $1,500 by late 2031. These are my own scenarios, built on how fast I expect their sales to grow each year and what valuations investors will be willing to pay for them.

Fortinet sells firewalls -- the boxes that decide what gets into a company's network. On Oct. 28, it reported third-quarter revenue up 20% to $2.08 billion, with product sales, mostly hardware, up 37%.  But it trimmed its forecast for service revenue -- the subscriptions that customers continue to pay after they buy the boxes -- to a range of $5.15 billion to $5.17 billion, down from $5.18 billion to $5.22 billion three months earlier. The stock sold off hard and now trades around $122, down from a 52-week high near $197.

I think the market is reading that backward. CFO Christiane Ohlgart said big new customers switch on their subscriptions in stages, and pointed out that service billings, which offer an early look at future subscription revenue, grew by 26%. Management also expects service revenue to grow faster in 2027 than in 2026.

My five-year scenario: Fortinet sales will grow at an annualized rate of 12% a year, well below this year's pace, and investors will pay 20% less per dollar of sales than today. That would turn a $333 stake into one worth roughly $470.

Cloudflare runs a giant network in front of millions of websites, blocking attacks and speeding up traffic. On Nov. 5, it reported third-quarter revenue up 39% to $782.5 million, its fastest growth rate in nearly four years. It added more than 158,000 paying customers in the quarter, and developers building on its platform jumped from 7 million to 10 million in three months.

What I like most is how it wins them. CEO Matthew Prince said that when AI labs find new software flaws, they quietly tell Cloudflare, which protects customers "whether they pay us or not." He described small banks facing a "tsunami of threats" being pointed by peers and others to Cloudflare. The company also expects to deliver a GAAP profit this quarter, well ahead of its 2028 target.

The catch for investors is the stock's valuation. At about $294 a share, Cloudflare trades at around 32 times this year's expected sales. However, if sales grow 28% a year (slower than they have recently) and that P/s multiple gets cut in half, $333 will still become about $573.

CrowdStrike's Falcon software monitors laptops, servers, and logins for break-ins. Now it's also watching AI agents, programs that complete assigned tasks on their own. On Nov. 3, it agreed to buy Fiddler AI, which tracks what AI agents did and why, and CEO George Kurtz called Fiddler "the flight recorder for every agent."

Here's why that matters. When a plane goes down, investigators pull the black box. When an AI agent sends money to the wrong place, boards and regulators will want answers, and I'd bet the vendor that can produce them will win cybersecurity contracts. Annual recurring revenue, the yearly value of its subscriptions, grew 25% to $5.84 billion last quarter, and CrowdStrike raised its outlook for new subscription growth.

CrowdStrike is also the priciest of the three, trading at about 37 times this year's expected sales. If sales grow 22% a year and that multiple is cut in half, $333 will turn into about $450. That's my lowest scenario, not because the business is weakest, but because investors today have to pay the largest premium up front. If that worries you, building your stake in CrowdStrike by buying shares gradually over time is a reasonable way in.

In my scenarios, the $333 in Fortinet grows to about $470, Cloudflare's to about $573, and CrowdStrike's to about $450, so the original $1,000 would be worth roughly $1,500 in five years. That's not a guarantee, but if AI keeps making security a must-have, splitting your investment across these companies gives you portfolio exposure to a cheaper comeback story, a fast grower, and a leader in AI security.
