Scotland best and worst case Euro 2028 qualifying draw scenarios as seeding pots confirmed

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Scotland's potential qualification opponents for Euro 2028 - a tournament they will co-host - have been finalised following the conclusion of the Nations League group stage.

Seeding pots for the Euro 2028 qualifying draw, which takes place in Belfast on Sunday, December 6, were based solely on the performance of each team in the Nations League across the 2026 campaign.

Sebastien Pocognoli's men have been placed in Pot 3 due to finishing third in Group B1 after taking seven points from a possible 18 in an underwhelming start to life under the Belgian head coach.

Scotland finished nine points behind group winners Switzerland, who put seven goals past them without reply over two matches, and four points behind runners-up Slovenia, who also won at Hampden after a goalless draw in Ljubjlana. North Macedonia, who Scotland defeated home and away, finished bottom of the group on zero points.

Attention now turns to the Euro 2028 qualifiers, which will feature six groups of four and six groups of five. The five-team groups will begin their qualification campaign in March 2027 while the groups of four will not start until September 2027.

If Scotland are drawn into a group of four they will face a 10-month wait for their next competitive fixture with friendlies required to fill the empty calendar for March and June next year.

However, Scotland are more likely to be drawn into a group of five due to Uefa tilting the odds in favour of teams who are not involved in any Nations League play-offs or quarter-finals in March.

This would see Scotland play eight qualifiers instead of six, kicking off in March with further games in June, September/October and November.

Scotland cannot be drawn in a group with their co-hosts England, Wales or Republic of Ireland. While Northern Ireland are no longer staging tournament matches after Casement Park was dropped from the bid, they retain a special status in the qualifying draw and will also be kept apart from the co-hosts.

Scotland will draw one team each from Pots 1, 2, and 4, plus one team from Pot 5 if placed into one of the six five-team groups.

Here are the confirmed seeding pots and possible opponents for Scotland:

Pot 1: Belgium, Croatia, Denmark, France, Germany, Greece, Italy, Netherlands, Norway, Portugal, Spain. (England excluded).

Pot 2: Austria, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Czech Republic, Hungary, Serbia, Slovenia, Sweden, Switzerland, Turkey. (Northern Ireland, Republic of Ireland, Wales excluded).

Pot 3: Albania, Georgia, Iceland, Israel, Kosovo, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Poland, Romania, Scotland, Slovakia, Ukraine.

Pot 4: Armenia, Belarus, Bulgaria, Cyprus, Estonia, Faroe Islands, Finland, Kazakhstan, Latvia, Luxembourg, Moldova, San Marino.

Pot 5: Andorra, Azerbaijan, Gibraltar, Liechtenstein, Lithuania, Malta.

A dream draw for Scotland would involve facing the lowest ranked nations from each pot. Such a group would involve Greece, Bosnia & Herzegovina, San Marino and Gibraltar.

Another more favourable draw would pair Scotland with Denmark, Czechia, Moldova and Liechtenstein.

The nightmare scenario for Scotland would be drawing the highest ranked nation from each pot, which would involve matches against Spain, Switzerland, Finland and Azerbaijan.

Another more difficult draw would involve Scotland facing France, Austria, Bulgaria and Lithuania.

To qualify automatically for Euro 2028 Scotland must finish as one of the 12 group winners or eight best runners-up. The other four group runners-up enter the play-offs.

There is, however, a potential safety net for Scotland with Uefa reserving two spots for co-hosts who do not achieve qualification on the pitch. Assuming England as pot one seeds qualify, Scotland will be competing with Wales and Republic of Ireland to get in through the back door.

If two co-hosts qualify on the pitch, then Scotland and the other co-host who fail to qualify would automatically take advantage of the safety net.

The only scenario where Scotland would fail to make it to Euro 2028 via the safety net is if three co-hosts fail to qualify and Scotland's qualification record is the worst of the trio.

Scotland are only guaranteed to play Euro 2028 matches at Hampden if they qualify the proper way. Using the safety net opens the door to potentially playing their matches elsewhere.
