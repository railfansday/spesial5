San Francisco 49ers training at altitude ahead of Mexico game

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

San Francisco 49ers are looking to extend their record to 8-1 with a victory against Minnesota Vikings in Mexico City on Monday; the team travelled to Colorado last Sunday and have been training at altitude this week: watch the game live on Sky Sports NFL

San Francisco 49ers are preparing for their upcoming clash against Minnesota Vikings at Mexico City's Estadio Azteca by training 7,258 feet above sea level in Colorado.

The 87,523-capacity stadium is situated at an altitude of 7,349 feet, and Kyle Shanahan's side are hoping to gain every advantage to improve on their 8-1 record this season against the Vikings on Monday morning, live on Sky Sports NFL.

Their training facility in Santa Clara, California, is at sea level, and the 49ers travelled to the Air Force Academy in Colorado on Sunday, following their 46-40 win over the Dallas Cowboys.

Kevin O'Connell's Vikings chose to practise in Minnesota rather than try to adjust to the altitude ahead of time.

The 49ers have historically altered their schedule to account for where games are played. They previously trained at the Air Force Academy four years ago in preparation for a game against Arizona Cardinals in Mexico City.

"One thing I know about our organisation that I really respect is everybody, from the top down, they'll do anything to try to help us get acclimated to whatever adversities or things that we have to face throughout the week," said running back Christian McCaffrey, who went to high school in Highlands Ranch, Colorado, 41 miles north of the Air Force Academy.

"So if we're going to play at altitude, coming here and getting acclimated is part of that. So I appreciate it."

Prior to week one, the 49ers spent a week in Melbourne, Australia, before their season curtain-raiser against Los Angeles Rams, who flew in shortly before the game.

San Francisco beat the Rams 27-7, looking sharper after adjusting to the 19-hour time difference.

"I think it's been nice to be here at practice and get some workouts in, and even just doing meetings and just living here for a week," quarterback Brock Purdy said.

"It's been a real thing that we've had to adjust to, and I'm glad that we're here. I think it's the right move for sure."

A half-speed training session was held on Wednesday, with Raheem Morris walking to the lectern on Thursday as if he was labouring to breathe the Colorado air.

"I'm out of breath right now," San Francisco's defensive coordinator said.

"You won't see the effect obviously into the game, but definitely good to get out here and get used to it."

Wide receiver Mike Evans (Achilles tendinitis) and linebacker Dre Greenlaw (neck) returned to practice on Thursday after sitting out Wednesday.

It was Evans' first practice since he left an October 19 win over Washington. He has missed the last three games.

"I thought he looked great," Purdy said. "He looked just like good old Mike from what I've been throwing him all year.

"Obviously, for me, I'm extremely encouraged by it. I'm going to go back, watch the film, see how it all looked. But just my initial thoughts were he looked great out there."

Evans was doing work on the side at the start of Thursday's session and then took part in drills with the receiving corps. Cornerbacks Renardo Green (back) and Upton Stout (shoulder), safeties Ji'Ayir Brown (ankle) and Siran Neal (foot) and defensive end Khalid Kareem (ankle) took part in the open portion of practice.

The only player not in uniform was back-up quarterback Mac Jones, who sat out with an illness.
