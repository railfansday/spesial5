Fiona the Hippo Dies Days After Eating Visitor’s Hat

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Fiona the Hippo has died after accidentally eating a visitor's hat that was thrown into her enclosure, the Cincinnati Zoo confirmed: "She fought until she couldn’t anymore.”

The Cincinnati Zoo has suffered an enormously devastating loss.

Fiona the Hippo—who first gained fame in 2017 after being born six weeks premature—has died after accidentally eating a visitor’s hat earlier this month, the Cincinnati Zoo confirmed.

“We are in shock at the moment,” the zoo’s director Dave Jenike said in a Nov. 19 press release. “We did not expect this outcome.  She fought until she couldn’t anymore.”

According to the zoo, the veterinary staff tried everything they could to save the 9-year-old hippo as they attempted to move the knit hat out of her system.

“Our animal care team was heroic in their efforts to save her. They did everything possible, but her body couldn’t fight anymore,” Jenike added. “She was dearly loved by her Zoo family and countless others around the world. Her loss will be felt deeply by so many.”

The Ohio zoo asked the public to “keep the people who worked with Fiona and tried to save her in your thoughts and prayers.”

The Nile hippopotamus’ health woes began on Nov. 8 when she accidentally swallowed a hat that a zoo visitor had thrown in the air, which a ceiling fan then moved into her enclosure, zoo spokesperson Michelle Curley had confirmed to the Associated Press.

Prior to her passing, the Cincinnati Zoo had been hopeful about her odds, saying in a Nov. 17 Facebook post that they were “monitoring her around the clock” and were “cautiously optimistic that she will pass it and return to her usual self.”

Fiona—who would have turned 10 in January—also received a colonoscopy, courtesy of the Columbus Zoo, as well as an ultrasound, which showed that her intestines were bunching, proving that she was working on passing something through her body.

Much like her death, Fiona’s birth was difficult, as she was born six weeks premature in 2017, weighing in at just 25 pounds, the lowest recorded birth weight for her species ever. She quickly became a celebrity across the internet as zoo staffers shared her progress with the world and less than two months later reached 90 pounds.

“Fiona was such a gift,” Cincinnati Zoo Executive Chair of the Board Thane Maynard shared Nov. 19. “I have never known an animal so beloved in all my life. From the day she was born, she was like lightning in a bottle. And, she will be remembered and beloved many years from now.”
