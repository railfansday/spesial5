LaFinteca connects global merchants directly to local payment rails across Latin America, cutting out the intermediaries that sit between a checkout and the actual transaction.

The starting point is a straightforward observation: each country in the region runs on its own rules and infrastructure. Pix in Brazil, SPEI in Mexico, PSE in Colombia, they don’t work the same way, and they don’t talk to each other. LaFinteca’s answer is a single API that covers more than 20 local payment methods across 30 markets, so a merchant adding a new country changes a configuration, not a codebase. A sandbox environment lets engineering teams test each market against real payment logic before going live.

The company holds a license from Brazil’s Banco Central do Brasil, the most heavily regulated payments market in the region, and operates under local regulatory frameworks in the other markets it covers. In Brazil, it is also a member of ABFintechs, the national fintech association, which gives it a formal channel with the BCB.

LaFinteca reports over 41 million transactions processed to date. The platform runs on direct connections, which means decline reasons and routing decisions reach the merchant rather than stopping at an intermediary.

The company is based in Barcelona and São Paulo and focuses exclusively on B2B cross-border payments.
