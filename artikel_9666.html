Add This Fresh Herb To Your Bowl Of Chili For 10x The Flavor (Thanks, Ina Garten)

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

A bowl of chili welcomes a wide array of renditions. You can make the dish red or green, use proteins like turkey, pork, or chicken instead of the classic beef, and include many aromatics in the mix. Each detail makes a chili rendition unique. Even a fresh herb gives a bowl a whole new character — just look toward Ina Garten's inclusion of basil. Ina Garten often uses basil as a swap for cilantro, so her inclusion of the herb in her chili makes sense.

Using the Mediterranean herb in the Tex-Mex recipe may sound unusual, but the flavors align surprisingly well. Garten uses basil's mildly citrusy sweet and lightly peppery flavor specifically in a chicken-based chili, integrating the herb during both the initial simmering stage and as a final garnish. It's a one-two combo that establishes an herbal character that infuses into every bite, lending more than just a basil-inflected finish.

Garten's rendition works the basil into a tomato sauce base, full of sweet bell peppers and onions, lightly recalling a pasta sauce in composition. Still, seasonings like cumin, chili powder, and a dash of spicy cayenne appear in the mix, cementing the dish as a bowl of chili, only brighter and more herbal in nature.

Ina Garten's tips for cooking chicken perfectly dependably deliver, but don't rule out employing the basil in chili recipes with other proteins, too. The herb also works well in an extra-hearty bean and brisket rendition that's spiced with chiles and even coffee. Cooked down right alongside the kidney beans, basil lends the legumes its distinct herbal character, all without becoming a cornerstone flavor. You can try out such a technique in a classic ground beef chili, too; using the herb early in cooking makes it one of many layered flavors.

If you're after a more basil-forward spin, consider working the herb into a ground turkey chili recipe. You can still go heavy on the spice, using chili powder and jalapeños alongside garlic powder and cumin. Add dried basil early on and raw basil atop the finished chili, and you'll get a delicate bowl, herbal-tasting and fresh, yet still pretty classic.

And for a more inventive approach, take a page from Thai cuisine, specifically Phat ka-phrao (stir-fried basil beef). Made with sturdier Thai basil, this version of the herb stands up better to heat. Saute the meat alongside extra-spicy chiles, lots of garlic, and this type of basil, and you can lay down the foundation for an extra-bold chili, still delicious over rice or with tortilla chips.
