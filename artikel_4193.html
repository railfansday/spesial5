How To Get Money Quickly In Probably Stolen

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Earn enough credits to buy the shop quickly with a few key tips and tricks.

Ah, the life of a business owner: wake up, stock the shelves, shoot a rogue AI, sell toilet paper and narcotics, scavenge in the dump, go to bed. A simple, quiet life, really. Who doesn't want to earn an honest living from drugs, weapons, and bribery? I mean, you can totally do normal, boring business things like selling food and water, but where's the fun in that?

In Probably Stolen, there are tons of ways to make money — both legal and illegal — but the key to making it quickly is knowing how to stack your resources and balance your expenses. Here's what you need to know about all the different ways you can bank tons of credits quickly.

Making credits in the early game is all about balancing inventory and purchases. You'll want to make sure you have enough supplies on hand to meet demand without buying too much and spending all your credits.

If you're starting the game with the Apprentice scenario, your best bet is to frequent the Dumping Grounds and look for supplies there. It's fast and free, so you can turn a 100% profit on anything you find. Unfortunately, it's also the most likely to turn up with practically worthless things like junk and empty beer bottles, so be prepared to work around those. Focus on perks that can give you a boon when you're in the Dumping grounds, like partnering with Georgio or keeping bandages in your backpack so you can keep scavenging even if you're minorly injured.

You can turn junk into profits using a furnace. If you're able to snag a furnace and a Junk Processing module from Dr. Jackson, you can turn six junk into four scrap metal at a time. This is a hefty investment, but if you're relying on the Dumping Grounds, it can be worth it. It can also make materials more widely available in the later game.

Starting with a trade scenario is another great way to make money quickly. Water trading and hydroponic farming are the most accessible early game moneymakers, but if you're a more experienced player, then wine making, aquaculture, and rat ranching can ramp up your income incredibly quickly. You can also make a lot of money from contraband and weaponry, but that's best left to experienced players who can juggle production with criminal record management and dodging Security.

Invest in the appraisal permit as soon as it's offered. Appraisals are relatively easy to do and great moneymakers, especially in the early game when getting rent is a real challenge every week.

Making money is nice, but worthless if you lose it all immediately. Keep in mind that there will be things you have to pay for to keep your store running; you need to factor these expenses into your budget if your goal is having as fat a bank account as possible.

Chief among your expenses is rent. The Landlord charges 200 credits in the first week, and the rent goes up about two and a half percent every week until you buy the store outright for 12,000 credits (up to day 50; after that, the cost of buying the store goes up by 50 credits per day). If you can't pay the rent, you get kicked out and your run ends, so prioritize having enough credits to pay rent every week, even if that means skipping on upgrades or selling off extra inventory at a discount.

If you miss a rent payment, you can use Joe Wild's network for a week of grace. This has a 15 day cooldown, so you can't use it back to back.

You'll also need to balance buying stock with your income. My rule of thumb is to buy the bare minimum I need to fulfill the demands of the current week; sometimes that means skipping trades or sellers completely. Take wholesale discounts whenever you can reasonably do so.

Finally, there are fees to consider. Fees to things like the appraisal board can be good investments, as they open a new income flow that's consistent and quick to scale. Fees for issues with Security, however, can pile up quickly and lead to an arrest, which ends your run, so try to avoid them whenever possible. You'll also eventually need to buy a business permit from the trade board; it's 1500 credits, but worth it to avoid later weeks of fines and bribes that can add up quickly.
