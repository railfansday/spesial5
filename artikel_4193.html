Pikmin 4's Switch 2 Edition Has A User Score Of Just 1.8 On Metacritic

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Nintendo added a new game to its growing library of Switch 2 Editions on November 12 via Pikmin 4, adding new features and attempting to add value to the paid upgrade by introducing a new mode based on the base game’s popular Dandori Academy. However, the user reviews are in, and fans of the series don’t believe the upgraded game offers good value for money.

Pikmin 4’s Switch 2 Edition scored an 83 on Metacritic with reviewers. Not had at all, and just a few points shy of the 87 its base game scored. The user reviews tell a different story. The upgraded version of the game currently has a user score of just 1.8, making it the worst-rated entry in the series by some distance.

Metacritic scores out of ten too, not five, so even worse than you might have initially thought.

Pikmin is a series that has served Nintendo well, scoring highly with both critics and players. In fact, until now, every numbered entry has a user score of at least 8.1. Pikmin 4 actually has one of the highest, with an 8.9. The limited additions to its Dandori Academy edition upgrade, however, have clearly left its fans feeling burned, and unlike red Pikmin, they aren’t immune to fire.

The user reviews for the Switch 2 Edition are a mix of the paid upgrade not being worth the asking price overall, and a focus on the Dandori Academy, the new version’s flagship feature, using too much content from the base game rather than adding anything truly new.

“Why spend 10 bucks for a slower game and rehashed levels?” one 0/10 user review reads. It also compares Pikmin 4’s new edition to the deluxe version of Pikmin 3 that launched on Nintendo Switch, which added a lot more new content to the game.

“The Dandori Academy has reused content, half of the maps so far are taken directly from the base game, and the other half share the same exact layout,” a 2/10 review reads. Complaints that the upgraded game now runs significantly more slowly seem to be a common complaint too. “This ‘upgrade’ of Pikmin 4 somehow manages to make the game run significantly worse, with standard load times going from six seconds to 30+ seconds,” another review claims.

This isn’t the first time Nintendo has come under fire for its paid Switch 2 Editions of games originally released on the Switch. Mario Wonder, Mario Party Jamboree, and Kirby and the Forgotten Land all now have Switch 2 Editions that mean the games cost $80, or you have to pay extra for the additional content if you own the game on Switch.

The complaints that those other Switch 2 Editions haven’t offered enough new content have been there, but have been much more muted than the backlash being thrown Pikmin 4’s way. It’s unclear if the content in this situation is so minimal that it warrants additional blowback, or if this is a case of Switch 2 owners being fed up with the paid upgrades offering limited new content and finally deciding to try and send Nintendo a message.

Pikmin 4's Switch 2 Edition brings some welcome upgrades to the original game, but whether they're worth the cost depends on your love for Pikmin.
