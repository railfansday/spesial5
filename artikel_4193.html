Toy Story 3 Remaster Shows How Movie Games Should Be Done

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Toy Story 3 is often regarded as one of the better games based on a movie. A legitimately great 3D platformer that also immerses fans of the films in its world in ways that not many other tie-in games have ever managed to pull off. That’s why those of us who played it when it originally released were thrilled to find out it was part of Digital Eclipse’s surprising effort to bring pretty much all past Toy Story games to modern platforms.

Not only bring those games back, but bundle them together. The Retro Roundup, which combines all of the older games from the ‘90s and early ‘00s into one collection, and a remaster of Toy Story 3 for modern platforms. Best of all, the Roundup and the remaster can be bought as part of the same bundle, not just digitally, but physically, too.

For decades, movie tie-in games were commonplace. Almost expected, particularly for the bigger Disney blockbusters. Aladdin, Hercules, Tarzan - they all had games that were released alongside their movies, giving fans another way to experience what they had just seen on the big screen.

That tradition has all but died out for several reasons. Trying to release a game and a movie at the same time is no longer possible, largely due to how long it takes to make games that would be considered worthy of big-budget blockbusters. Even if a developer were to hit the ground running the second the mere idea for a movie was uttered, that movie would still end up getting made long before the game was even close to being finished, likely by a margin of years.

What's the point in playing these games when we know they will get remastered sooner or later?

It’s why Toy Story 3 was probably one of the last true movie tie-in games we will ever get, which is somewhat ironic as it’s also one of the best. The trouble is, especially now with the release of its remaster, people are looking back on it and longing for another era where movies can have tie-in games released alongside them. It’s not the feeling of long-lost nostalgia we should be focusing on when playing this remaster, though. Developers and movie studios should be looking at what Disney and Avalanche did with that original game, what Digital Eclipse has done with the remaster, and using all of that as the blueprint for the future of movie games.

Even though the Toy Story 3 game and movie were released at the same time, the former isn’t just a game version of the latter. It’s effectively a love letter to the entire series, or at least the original trilogy, that pulls from those first three beloved movies. Its developers were given more freedom, which is how we wound up with the more open Woody’s Roundup area, one of the - if not the - main reasons the game was so popular.

The game didn’t need to release alongside the movie to be a success, something its Complete Edition has now proved 16 years later. Those who played the original have been reminded of just how good it was, and how it told a separate Toy Story story while remaining faithful to the films, and Disney now has a shiny new polished version to point new, younger fans towards not just when a new Toy Story movie is released, but whenever they need to.

That’s the lesson that other studios which have all-but given up on movie games really need to try and learn from. That you don’t need to pull off the almost-impossible and try to get the launch of a movie and its game to perfectly align. What’s more important is making sure that game is good and can stand the test of time. If so, you can not only release it whenever, but whenever you need it again, you have something to point to. Or, if it’s been long enough, you can give it the remaster treatment like Toy Story 3.

I wouldn’t even be averse to seeing Toy Story 3 dusted off and remastered a second time a decade from now, perhaps to give Toy Story fans who haven’t even been born yet something to play two console generations from now when the sixth, seventh, and eighth movies are released. Add an extra area to Woody’s Roundup. Throw in a few more story missions. It’s certainly a lot easier than making a whole new game from scratch, which is the problem that has effectively ended the movie video game era.

I appreciate that “Hey, just make a game that’s as good as Toy Story 3 and you’ll be set for life” is easier said than done. The thing is, I don’t think anyone is even trying. Just look at the excitement surrounding the reveal of the Coco world in Kingdom Hearts 4. There’s a Coco sequel coming, and a game like Toy Story 3 set in that world could be huge, even if it doesn’t line up with the release of its sequel. Sadly, I think Coco’s inclusion in KH is the closest we’ll ever get to a video game set in Disney’s Land of the Dead.
