Lawyers’ AI use doesn’t justify slashing $115K fee award, appellate court says

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

A federal appeals court has rejected an argument that a law firm’s use of artificial intelligence should shrink its fee award. (Image from Shutterstock)

A federal appeals court has rejected an argument that a law firm’s use of artificial intelligence should shrink its fee award.

Reuters reported Monday that the 10th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals at Denver upheld a $115,000 fee awarded to defense lawyers at Brownstein Hyatt Farber Schreck by the lower court in a trade secrets dispute. The trial court had ruled that the fee was reasonable.

“Plaintiffs have not shown that defense counsel’s use of AI rendered the hours that ​they actually expended unreasonable,” wrote 10th Circuit Judge Carolyn McHugh for a unanimous three-judge panel.

The appeals court also upheld the ​dismissal of the lawsuit.

In her opinion, McHugh acknowledged that AI and similar research tools can make attorneys more efficient. But she stressed that lawyers must still verify legal authorities and arguments themselves.

According to Reuters, the case began in 2024, when industrial motor-efficiency equipment maker Flux Drive filed a suit against a Chinese manufacturer, alleging that one of its former engineers had stolen proprietary information and given it to them. After the case was dismissed, Flux Drive was ordered to pay the defendants’ legal fees. On appeal, the company argued that the defense billed for “ministerial tasks,” and that AI use should have lowered the bill.

The defendants countered that their lawyers checked every piece of AI-assisted research, and that Flux Drive pointed to no specific inflated entry, redundant task or unreasonable charge.

Brownstein Hyatt partner Christopher Ottele, who represented the defendants, called AI a powerful tool that sophisticated firms should embrace.

“This ​decision confirms that the use of AI is not a substitute for the judgment and analysis that attorneys still ​must exercise in every filing with the court,” Ottele told Reuters.

Counsel for the plaintiffs did not immediately respond to Reuters’ requests for comment.
