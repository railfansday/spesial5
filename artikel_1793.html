Sheffield United homework paying off as they raise the set-piece standards

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

After a difficult summer transfer window, the Blades are one point outside the Championship play-off places are 17 matches.

Whilst the players will always deserve the lion's share of the credit for that, with manager Wilder next in the queue, he pointed to the large number of people working hard behind the scenes in largely unheralded roles.

"Now with Wyescout and all the other sources that we can get individually on players, unit meetings on individual players to give the players an idea of what they're coming up against, gameplan, so much work goes in from the staff, the coaches, myself, analysing it, seeing what little areas that we can find an advantage, looking at strengths and weaknesses as always," he said.

"With tree-game weeks as well, it's an incredible amount of work on every front, putting together training sessions, loading on the players what they need to do, what they don't need to do, when we need to come off and when we need to stick it on them.

"For players that are not starting, what they need to do as well, you know.

"The amount of work that the analysts do in flagging stuff up and then we're all trying to put it together and put a gameplan together to give us the best opportunity.

"It's not me at 10 o'clock asking, 'Boys, what happened last night?' and let's go and put the nets up and get on with it attitude.

"A lot of work goes into it, but when you do get it right, whether it's over the season or whether it's just that chasing that winning feeling that we talk about on a Saturday night, it all feels worthwhile."

The Blades have scored the most (nine) and conceded the fewest (one) goals from set pieces in this season's Championship.

"It's an attitude from the players," said Wilder. "It's a review, a best practise. A lot of work goes into it away from the lights – unit meetings, set-piece meetings, all stuff that's at every club and has been a big part of the game for a long time.

"We want to be the best in every department – creating chances, keeping clean sheets, set plays, running stats, physical stats.

"We've got good delivery, we've got players that want to go and attack it."

Meanwhile, Wilder believes Jordan Ayew has “found a home” at Bramall Lane after finding himself unemployed when the summer transfer window shut.

The 35-year-old was released when Leicester City were relegated last season but is starting to flourish after being picked up by the Blades, as shown by his brilliant assist for Sydie Peck's equaliser against Southampton on Tuesday.

“He’s a signing that we made and one that was well talked about,” said Wilder.

“He’s been outstanding for us. He’s linked the play up, dropped in and got hold of the ball, played in the pockets, created chances and then set Sydie up for his equaliser.

"There was a lot of noise when he signed, as there has been for quite a lot of things that have happened at the start.

“All we have to do is get our heads down, and all Jordan has to do is go and play in the way that he has done. I think he's found a home; I'm sure he has. He really enjoys playing for us and I'm enjoying managing and coaching him.”
