Doncaster Rovers legend Billy Sharp on Grant McCann - and blunt message to some players ahead of Huddersfield Town derby

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Rovers find themselves in the final relegation spot ahead of Thursday night's televised home derby with second-placed Huddersfield Town.

McCann's side have won just once in their past six league outings, losing four - although the sole victory arrived against Yorkshire opposition, a 2-0 home success over Sheffield Wednesday on October 24.

A gutsy late derby point at Bradford City, last time out, was also welcome, even if McCann has not been spared criticism from supporters in recent times, particularly following atrocious losses at MK Dons and Kidderminster Harriers.

Sharp, 40, who left Rovers at the end of last season - hitting a hat-trick in his final game in May—said: "I think Grant's showed his pedigree with two promotions from League Two and League One.

"He'll be chomping at the bit trying to get up the table because he's an ambitious manager who wants promotion, which is no different to what I would be.

"I think it's tough because he believes a lot in the players and I think it needs to rub off on the players.

"He always used to say it to me and the group: 'don't let me believe in you as much as you believe in each other'. And I think that may be the difference at the minute.

"I think some of the players need to step up a little bit more. I'm not going to name names, but they'll know who they are. They've got to all start pulling in the right direction and getting that club moving up the table."

Despite a difficult season so far for Rovers, boardroom support for McCann appears solid and unwavering - with the Northern Irishman having a strong working relationship with owner Terry Bramall and chief executive officer Gavin Baldwin.

Sharp, at Doncaster Sports College earlier this week as part of the EFL's Power of 72 campaign that celebrates the work that clubs and their charities do in the local community, continued: "I think (most) managers are three losses away from being under pressure. I think that's how it is in this day and age; you don't get the time.

"Managers are always asking for time. But I think if you don't win games, you're always under pressure as the media are on your back and fans.

"Ultimately, if that's happening, some of the players might turn on you. So the most important thing is the players and keeping them happy, especially the ones who aren't in the team, because you always need them throughout the season.

"Having as much good noise as you can have in your dressing room and your club fends off the media and all the negative noise surrounding it.

"If you can keep it out when it isn't going great - it’s probably not going as great as Grant and Doncaster would like at the minute - (that's good).

"But I know that he's got full backing of the board and the players. It's about building (on pitch) momentum. And I think that's something that Doncaster have really struggled with. They win a game, then lose two. That's why they are where they are at the minute."
