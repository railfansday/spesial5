From young players to women's game to the national team, how can SFA duo Maxwell and Mulholland possibly be trusted to move things forward?

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Published: 13:33 EST, 27 November 2026 | Updated: 15:30 EST, 27 November 2026

With the Scottish FA’s widely trumpeted plan to get young players more game time now lying in a ditch off the M74, it was hard not to raise a wry smile at the midweek communique from Hampden over the intention to come up with some other kind of just-as-good idea somewhere in the new year.

‘Given the implications for our game, and our young talent, it is imperative that we take the necessary time to get those proposals right,’ it closed.

Bit late for that chat, is it not? Shouldn’t that work behind the scenes have been done before the grand summit at St Andrews back at the start of September when SFA chief executive Ian Maxwell and his chief football officer Craig Mulholland announced themselves lords and saviours of the national game with their scheme to shoehorn Under-21 players – no matter how good, bad or indifferent they may be – into club sides all over the country?

It all sounded good at the time, of course, if you put your head in the clouds and daydreamed over how pretty the world might be if everyone just got along.

Handwringers nationwide fell over themselves to simper the usual platitudes over how self-interest must be sacrificed for the greater good. There was probably some kind of online petition to link hands between all 42 senior grounds for a chorus of Kumbaya.

Meanwhile, anyone who has the faintest idea of the realpolitik of Scottish football looked at what was on the table and knew it was destined for the knacker’s yard.

SFA top brass Craig Mulholland and Ian Maxwell flank Scotland boss Sebastien Pocognoli

The SFA should have been consulting their more prominent clubs before going public over what might work and what might not, moulding something that the teams which matter were willing to support.

Instead, almost half the outfits in the Premiership blew their proposals out of the water in the end.

The sound of silence from Hampden, of course, had already become deafening as they realised they’d made a pig’s ear of it. Right now, it looks like everyone is back to the drawing board.

It’s embarrassing for the SFA. It has just made Maxwell and Mulholland look like they are incapable of taking the game with them on important matters.

Naturally, though, there wasn’t a single word from Haudit or Daudit in that press release to more or less confirm a major reverse ferret is going to be required. When things go belly-up, the high heid yins at Hampden are often nowhere to be seen.

Maxwell was too busy turning his mind to other matters during the week anyway, namely the fact that Scotland will be co-hosting the Women’s World Cup with the rest of the UK in 2035.

Given that was the only actual bid, the award of the event was hardly earth-shattering, but that didn’t stop Maxwell treating it as his Next Big Thing.

Maxwell has been in position at the SFA for more than eight years but has lacked real impact

‘I think it is going to be an absolutely transformational event to host in the UK and Ireland,’ he gushed. ‘The hard work starts now in terms of legacy and impact and driving everything we can from it.’

Hmmmm. Nice as it is that he’s managed to put being custard-pied at home by a large chunk of the Premiership over youth opportunities behind him, it might be wise to take Maxwell’s words over the women’s game with a pinch of salt.

He also called Scotland going to their first World Cup in 2019 ‘transformational’. Months later, Glasgow City won their 13th straight title with a ten-goal win over Motherwell on a council-run pitch in Wishaw in front of 100 people with no shelter, no seating and no obvious way for an ambulance to gain entry should a medical emergency occur. One Motherwell player had to nick across to the attached sports centre to get a chair for an injured team-mate to sit on.

A year had passed since the national team’s qualification, but there was still no kind of plan for the £400,000 prize money that they earned.

City’s Hayley Lauder, who’d been at the World Cup in France, tweeted: ‘Just won a league title in a public park at a sports centre. Not how I envisaged the game in 2019.’

It was a message that encapsulated the catastrophic failure to build on the incredible platform France 2019 provided under Maxwell’s reign.

Scotland celebrate an Erin Cuthbert goal against Argentina in 2019 before it all turned sour

That should have been the real launchpad for the women’s game here. Instead, it turned into a toxic shambles that killed any kind of momentum.

Progress since has been nothing like what it should have been. The national team, out to reach the next World Cup in next week’s play-off against Sweden, has been mired in failure. The women’s game lives back in the shadows amid pitiful attendances and an inability – or, it sometimes seems, refusal – to sell itself.

Yet, it could all have been so different. Just remember the scene back in ‘19. Scotland have to beat Argentina to make the last 16. A TV audience of six million watched the meeting with England earlier in the campaign.

The drama as Shelley Kerr’s side let slip a three-goal lead with 16 minutes to play – eventually drawing 3-3 thanks to a stoppage-time penalty retaken after an initial save by keeper Lee Gibson – was just incredible. It was the talk of the nation the next day.

And, then, it all just died a death. The head coach Shelley Kerr eventually coughed to the fact ‘a few drinks’ had been taken before a debrief the next day that reportedly left some players in tears.

No one wanted to talk about it. No one ever has, really. Yours truly asked Glasgow City for an interview with Gibson, then Alexander, in the early stages of the following season.

Top-flight matches between teams in the SWPL regularly take place on glorified public pitches

She’d been at the centre of a major global event. There was a story to be told. City insisted all requests had to be run past the SFA and their media office pretty much made it clear it wouldn’t be happening before she had even been asked.

No one told their stories of the biggest event in the history of women’s football here. Not properly. It felt like some kind of Omerta had been put in place over it.

Kerr, inexplicably, was allowed to keep her job by Maxwell and then failed to make it to Euro 2022. Instead, that year was marked by the women’s team, with Kerr gone, threatening to take legal action against the SFA over a lack of resources – with then captain Rachel Corsie referring to ‘years of iniquity, disrespect, and, in some cases, abuse.’

Yeah, the aftermath of France 2019 was ‘transformational’ all right. It transformed the hundreds of thousands of folk compelled to watch women’s football on national TV that summer back into people who forget it even exists.

Colleagues tell me there was no scope midweek to even interview anyone in a position of power within the SFA over what should have been a Good News Story regarding 2035. Hardly ‘driving everything we can from it’, is it?

I mean, how can you trust the likes of Maxwell to harness the opportunities afforded by that event – or Euro 2028 on home soil – when reflecting upon the raging binfire France 2019 became?

Mulholland is a regular sight being entertained by football matches up and down the country

How can you trust him and Mulholland to get it right on forging pathways for young players when they made such a Horlicks of trying to implement that poorly-planned proposal for Scotland-eligible Under-21s and failed again when changing it to involve Under-23s?

There’s a far wider issue with the production of players that involves going deep into the SFA’s own workings, addressing that lack of knowledge St Mirren boss Craig McLeish talked about at the lower levels of grassroots.

Indeed, there are problems all over the SFA. With refereeing standards. With VAR. With Steve Clarke admitting he forced the top brass into ‘panicking’ before the World Cup and giving him a new four-year deal.

If Maxwell and Mulholland’s blue-eyed boy Sebastien Pocognoli doesn’t start looking like he’s got a handle on his role as Clarke’s successor as national boss after a grim start to his reign, everything points to the two of them having to go the same way as their original Youth Plan. By being binned.
