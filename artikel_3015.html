Stock Market Today, Nov. 10: Wall Street's Rally Hits a Roadblock as Hot Inflation Raises the Stakes for the Fed

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Yesterday's rebound lost momentum as rising Treasury yields and renewed inflation concerns weighed on stocks

Wall Street's rebound stalled on Tuesday, November 10, as hotter-than-expected inflation data reignited concerns about Federal Reserve interest rate policy and sent Treasury yields higher. The Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC -0.74%) dropped 0.74% to 25,066, retreating from Monday's gains, while the S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.38%) slipped 0.38% to 7,521 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average (^DJI -0.30%) fell 0.30% to 52,232.

Selling pressure extended beyond equities as gold fell 1.84% to $4,038.70, while the benchmark 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 4.88%, approaching the psychologically important 5% threshold. Market performance was mixed across sectors, with consumer cyclical stocks leading gains and industrials advancing, while healthcare lagged. The divergence highlighted an increasingly selective market as investors reassessed the outlook for interest rates and economic growth.

Despite the broader market decline, several prominent stocks posted strong gains on Tuesday. Meta Platforms (META +0.00%) surged more than 5% after JPMorgan (JPM +0.00%) added the social media giant to its Focus List, while Sea Limited (SE -1.49%) jumped 8.1% following stronger-than-expected quarterly revenue.

On the downside, Nvidia (NVDA -5.47%) plunged 5.52% amid an ongoing investigation involving CEO Jensen Huang, adding to uncertainty ahead of the chipmaker's upcoming earnings report.

Tuesday's sell-off underscores a growing challenge for equity investors heading into year-end. With inflation proving more persistent than expected, markets are increasingly pricing in another Federal Reserve rate hike in December. Higher borrowing costs could weigh on corporate profitability and put additional pressure on stock valuations, particularly among growth companies whose valuations depend heavily on future earnings.

However, the divergence in individual stock performance suggests investors are becoming more selective rather than abandoning equities altogether. Strong corporate earnings and favorable company-specific developments continue to attract capital, even as broader market conditions deteriorate. This could favor businesses with healthy balance sheets, strong cash flow, and the ability to sustain growth without relying heavily on external financing.

Looking ahead, investors should watch upcoming inflation data, Federal Reserve commentary, and Treasury yields for signs of whether monetary conditions will tighten further. With the 10-year yield approaching 5%, the sustainability of Wall Street's recent rebound may depend increasingly on corporate earnings delivering enough growth to justify elevated valuations.
