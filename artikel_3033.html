3 Stocks I'm Ready to Buy if the Market Crashes

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

These three stocks are growing fast, but they're expensive.

By Jennifer Saibil – Nov 21, 2026 at 8:33PM EST

Every time it looks like the S&P 500 is starting to drop, investors send it back up. As of this writing, it's no longer a double-digit increase year to date, but it's still up an admirable 7%. That's cautious optimism considering high inflation and rising interest rates.

However, there's bound to be a crash at some point, whether before the year's out or into the future. Whenever it happens, I would buy Shopify (SHOP -6.25%), Costco Wholesale (COST +0.76%), and e.l.f. Beauty (ELF -5.01%) stocks, all of which are great businesses but have rich price tags.

Shopify is a top e-commerce company that provides services to e-commerce retailers. Its products range from total, all-inclusive websites to single products like payment systems or cross-border e-commerce, and it has moved from helping small businesses thrive to working with enterprise retailers like Estee Lauder's (EL -2.27%) Clinique and Lululemon Athletica (LULU -2.76%). That's because it takes away the pain points of companies having to develop these kinds of systems themselves, and it has developed a reputation for its services that make it their partner of choice.

Even though it started as an e-commerce company, offering an easy path for small retailers to get online, it now calls itself simply a commerce company. It works with omnichannel retailers, unifying all of a company's brick-and-mortar and digital channels into one management system. The company describes its evolution as "However commerce evolves, Shopify will be the engine behind it. The merchants who are the backbone of the economy demand it of us."

Despite inflation and lots of competition, Shopify's revenue is accelerating, up 32% year over year in the third quarter, and its gross merchandise volume (GMV), or the volume of sales on its platform from its clients, increased 29%. At $119 billion, it was higher than Amazon's (AMZN -4.10%) online and third-party sales of $116 billion. Amazon and Shopify have a partnership, too, whereby shoppers can use Amazon's Buy With Prime as a checkout through participating Shopify retailers.

What's been stopping me from buying so far is the expensive price tag. At the current price, Shopify stock trades at 75 times trailing 12-month earnings, making it the most expensive stock on this list. That speaks to investor confidence, but it also builds in a lot of growth.

Costco has a unique retail model that has proven to work through any kind of economic backdrop. It offers rock-bottom prices, but shoppers have to pay an annual fee to shop there. Considering its high renewal rates, consistently increasing membership metrics, and the number of members who upgrade to the executive membership, which costs double the standard rate, shoppers find the deal worthwhile.

In fact, as inflation rises and customers become more cautious about spending, Costco’s sales growth has accelerated. In the 2026 fiscal fourth quarter (ended Aug. 30), sales increased 11.2% year over year, driven by a 9.4% increase in comparable sales. Some of that came from the company's gas stations as oil prices increased, and that's become an important growth driver. It's also highly profitable, and earnings per share (EPS) increased from $5.87 to $6.75.

But it gets better. Costco also reports monthly metrics, and sales increased 13% in both September and October. On top of that, Costco just announced a $30 special dividend. Costco's regular dividend only yields 0.53%, but it pays a special dividend on average every two and a half years. Adding $30 to this year's payout would bring the effective yield to 3.2%.

However, Costco stock trades at a price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of 53, which is a major premium to other retailers. The market obviously thinks it's worth it, but the stock is priced for perfection.

E.l.f. has carved out a niche in mass cosmetics as an eco-friendly brand with luxury dupes. It has become one of the largest companies of its kind, breaking into an industry dominated by legacy brands from which it’s pulling market share. It's also a newcomer in the luxury cosmetics industry, with its recent acquisition of Hailey Bieber's Rhode brand.

In the 2027 fiscal Q2 (ended Sept. 30), sales increased 51% year over year, and gross margin increased 1.7 percentage points to 71%. E.l.f. stock has plunged after tariffs were introduced last year, but tariff refunds have improved its gross margin, and the stock has nearly doubled since it bottomed out last June.

It's the most popular cosmetics company in the Piper Sandler (PIPR -4.03%) Taking Stock With Teens Survey, and Rhode is the third. It's now also the top or second-largest brand in 22 cosmetics categories, up from 11 in 2021.

E.l.f. stock trades at 72 times trailing 12-month earnings, and if a broader market sell-off knocks that multiple closer to 40 or 45, I'd be ready to buy the stock—alongside Shopify and Costco.
