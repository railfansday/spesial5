Stifel Says SpaceX Could Buy Stalled Data Centers for One Reason: Their Power. Here’s Why Electricity Is the Real Asset.

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

There is increasing pushback against AI because of its impact on the power grid, but that's also an opportunity.

By Reuben Gregg Brewer – Nov 22, 2026 at 8:01PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Bloom Energy ›

Wall Street analysts can only make educated guesses, just like every other investor. But they do have an advantage since they are often industry experts. This is why Stifel analyst Jonathan Siegmann's suggestion that Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -7.69%) could buy up stalled data centers is so interesting.

The key theme, however, is that SpaceX will likely only be interested in data centers that already have dedicated power. Here's why this is so important and what it could mean for your stock selection process as you look at companies like Bloom Energy (BE -15.30%), NextEra Energy (NEE -0.41%), and Brookfield Renewable (BEP -2.97%).

SpaceX has a large AI data center in Memphis that it has dubbed Colossus. It is a huge asset, and it uses massive amounts of power. To ensure there is enough electricity to run the data center, SpaceX built its own dedicated natural-gas power plant. Local residents aren't happy about this, but it highlights that SpaceX is clearly aware of the power problem posed by AI data centers.

As the backlash against AI data centers grows, access to electricity is becoming an increasingly important and contentious issue. Which is why Stifel analyst Jonathan Siegmann thinks SpaceX could end up buying data centers that have dedicated power agreements in place. However, you don't need to play this specific angle to benefit. Increasingly, AI companies are being forced to supply their own power. And there are a handful of companies that look set to benefit, including Bloom Energy, NextEra Energy, and Brookfield Renewable.

Bloom Energy makes hydrogen fuel cells. Notably, each fuel cell it sells comes along with a long-term service contract, creating an annuity-like income stream. The company's products are factory-built and can be easily deployed wherever they are needed, providing off-grid power. The company has been losing money for years but is likely to be profitable in 2026. That could be an important inflection point for the business.

But investors are well aware of the opportunity, pushing the stock higher by more than 1,200% over the past three years. With a lofty price-to-earnings ratio of roughly 100x, it is very clearly a well-loved stock. And yet, the opportunity to power AI data centers remains substantial, and perhaps only getting larger. If you are an aggressive growth investor, you should at least consider Bloom Energy, even if you only end up adding it to your wish list.

NextEra Energy is one of the world's largest utilities. In fact, it is looking to get even bigger, with its proposed acquisition of Dominion Energy (D -0.56%). Notably, Dominion's regulated electricity operations include one of the world's most important data center markets. The pairing will also vault NextEra into the number two spot with regard to U.S. nuclear power generation. Basically, NextEra looks like it is doubling down on electricity demand and AI at the same time.

But there's an interesting twist here. NextEra is well positioned to benefit from data centers that get attached to the grid. However, it is also one of the world's largest producers of solar and wind power, a business that falls outside of the regulated framework. So it can also sell power directly to AI customers under long-term contracts. For more conservative investors, NextEra could be an attractive way to play AI's power demand, both from within and outside the regulated framework. You'll also get to collect NextEra's well-above-market 2.9% dividend yield.

For investors looking to maximize the income they generate, Brookfield Renewable and its 5.2% may be even more attractive. The partnership is focused on clean energy, but has a surprisingly diversified portfolio. For starters, it operates hydroelectric, solar, and wind power facilities. It has increasingly been investing in battery storage. And it owns half of Westinghouse, which is one of the world's largest service providers to the nuclear power industry. On top of that, it is also geographically diversified, with assets located in North America, South America, Europe, and Asia. It is a one-stop shop for investors seeking a focused clean-energy investment and for customers seeking renewable energy.

The key here, however, is that Brookfield Renewable largely sells power under long-term contracts outside of the regulated framework. It already has large deals with AI giants Microsoft (MSFT -2.77%) and Google. Add in the goal of at least 5% annual distribution growth, and income lovers would be remiss if they didn't at least examine Brookfield Renewable and the AI opportunity ahead of it.

If you think Stifel analyst Jonathan Siegmann is on to something, you could simply buy SpaceX. That's fine, but it isn't the only takeaway from his insight. The real story is that access to electricity is increasingly important. And you can also play that investment theme with companies like Bloom Energy, NextEra Energy, and Brookfield Renewable.
