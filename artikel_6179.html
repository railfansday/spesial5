Where To Spot The Airbus A380 In 2026: The 10 Airports With The Most Superjumbo Flights

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

The Airbus A380 remains one of the most recognizable aircraft in commercial aviation, and despite production having ended, the world's largest passenger airliner continues to operate an impressive international network. For enthusiasts hoping to catch the Superjumbo in action during 2026, some airports offer considerably better opportunities than others.

The Simple Flying Flight Tracker reveals where the aircraft makes the most appearances, which airlines operate the greatest number of services, and where spotters can find the widest variety of individual A380s. This article covers every Airbus A380 movement recorded between October 5 and November 3, 2026, providing a snapshot of the aircraft's continued global presence. Movements count each flight at both its departure and arrival airports, meaning a total of 14,140 movements represent 7,070 individual flights.

Manchester Airport (MAN) recorded 167 flights operated by the Airbus A380 between October 5 and November 3, with Emirates the sole operator of the type. Although its overall activity is considerably lower than that of the major international hubs, Manchester is notable because the Dubai-based carrier provides a regular Superjumbo presence, making it a convenient destination for those in northern England. Its position also gives British spotters an alternative to traveling to London to see the aircraft.

Meanwhile, New York John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK) saw 284 Airbus A380 flights over the same period. These services were operated by the likes of Korean Air, Emirates, and Asiana Airlines, reflecting continuing demand for high-capacity international services connecting the US with major overseas destinations. This airport's presence in the list highlights how the A380 remains an important part of the transatlantic and transpacific aviation landscape.

Other airports also benefit from Emirates' continued commitment to the Airbus A380. Birmingham (BHX), Prague (PRG), Denpasar (DPS), and Christchurch (CHC) are among the destinations where enthusiasts can encounter the aircraft on daily services, while Glasgow (GLA) receives less frequent visits. These locations demonstrate that the Superjumbo's presence extends well beyond the world's largest connecting hubs, offering spotting opportunities at destinations that might otherwise appear unlikely to feature such a large aircraft.

According to the Simple Flying Flight Tracker, Paris Charles De Gaulle Airport (CDG) recorded 333 flights operated by the Airbus A380 between October 5 and November 3, 2026. Operators of the A380 to the French capital include Emirates, Qatar Airways, and Etihad Airways, demonstrating the continuing importance of Gulf-based airlines in maintaining the aircraft's European presence. Air France formerly operated a fleet of 10 A380s before retiring the aircraft type during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Meanwhile, Los Angeles International Airport (LAX) saw 342 Airbus A380 flights in the same period, making it one of the leading destinations in the United States. Its airline list includes Asiana Airlines, British Airways, Qantas, Korean Air, Emirates, and Lufthansa, giving it six different operators.

The airport's extensive long-haul connections explain its position in the ranking. Airlines deploy the A380 on selected routes linking Los Angeles with major international hubs across Europe, Asia, and the Middle East. Its role as a major gateway to the US ensures that several international carriers continue to bring the aircraft to California from destinations including London Heathrow Airport (LHR), Seoul Incheon International Airport (ICN), and Dubai International Airport (DXB).

Bangkok Suvarnabhumi Airport (BKK) recorded 356 Airbus A380 flights, operated by Asiana Airlines, Etihad Airways, Emirates, Qatar Airways, and Lufthansa, providing a healthy selection of operators to and from the Thai capital. The airport's position as a major international gateway to one of the world's most popular tourist destinations helps sustain its appeal among airlines operating busy long-haul routes.

Emirates, Qatar Airways and Etihad Airways provide important connections through their respective Gulf hubs, while Lufthansa and Asiana Airlines further diversify the airport's offering. Although Bangkok cannot match Dubai's flight frequency, its combination of aircraft numbers and airline variety makes it an attractive alternative. The presence of five operators also gives aviation enthusiasts a broader selection of aircraft to look out for on the Simple Flying Flight Tracker.

The US is a key long-haul market for superjumbo operators.

Sydney Kingsford Smith Airport (SYD) recorded 628 flights operated by the Airbus A380, placing it among the busiest airports in the world for the aircraft type. Airlines that operate the A380 to and from Sydney include Asiana Airlines, Singapore Airlines, Qantas, and Emirates, reflecting Australia's strong international travel market and demand for high-capacity long-haul services. The airport's extensive international connections make it an important destination for several of the world's remaining Superjumbo operators. Qantas is naturally an important part of the Sydney spotting experience, given its Australian headquarters and long-standing investment in the Airbus A380. Singapore Airlines and Emirates also contribute to the airport's activity, providing opportunities to see several different aircraft and liveries. This combination makes Sydney particularly interesting for those looking to compare the different configurations and appearances of international A380 operators.

Singapore Changi Airport (SIN) follows with a total of 895 flights operated by the Airbus A380 recorded between October 5 and November 3, 2026. British Airways, Qantas, Emirates, and Singapore Airlines all deploy their Superjumbos to the airport, reflecting its importance as a major destination for tourism, business, and transit flights. Singapore Airlines is particularly significant as the launch operator of the Airbus A380 back in 2007, and the aircraft remains part of its long-haul operations today, almost 20 years later.

London Heathrow Airport (LHR) saw 1,162 Airbus A380 flights, placing it second overall. However, its most impressive statistic concerns the number of different aircraft and airlines represented. Six different airlines operate the aircraft type to and from the UK's largest airport, which is also one of the busiest in the world for international traffic.

Airbus A380s from British Airways, Qantas, Emirates, Qatar Airways, Singapore Airlines, and Etihad Airways all appeared at the airport between October 5 and November 3, 2026. This makes London Heathrow particularly appealing for those looking to track a wide variety of different liveries.

With a total of 12 Airbus A380s in its fleet, British Airways is the joint-second largest operator of the aircraft, together with Singapore Airlines. The UK flag carrier deploys its A380s on selected long-haul services, including to Miami International Airport (MIA), San Francisco International Airport (SFO), and Singapore Changi Airport (SIN).

Emirates also maintains a significant presence with multiple daily frequencies, while Qatar Airways and Singapore Airlines connect London Heathrow with their respective international networks. Qantas and Etihad Airways provide further variety, ensuring that the airport regularly welcomes aircraft from several major global operators.

Dubai International Airport (DXB) is the undisputed center of the world's Airbus A380 operation, recording a staggering 4,285 flights during the same period. That figure is substantially higher than any other airport, reflecting the facility's importance as the primary hub for Emirates and making it the busiest Superjumbo spotting destination by a wide margin. Unlike airports where several airlines contribute to the Airbus A380 schedule, Dubai's operation is overwhelmingly dominated by Emirates. The airline has built much of its international network around the aircraft, deploying it on routes connecting the UAE with destinations across Europe, Asia, Africa, North America, and Australia. Its extensive fleet allows it to maintain an extraordinary number of daily services.

Emirates uses the Airbus A380 to bring passengers together from across its network, with the aircraft providing substantial capacity on particularly busy routes. This creates repeated opportunities to observe different aircraft arriving and departing, with the airport's extensive international schedule keeping the Superjumbo firmly at the center of its operations.
