Allegiant Air To Expand Fleet, Exercises Options For 6 New Boeing 737 MAX 8s Worth $720 Million

US ultra-low-cost carrier (ULCC) Allegiant Air has exercised options to obtain an six additional Boeing 737 MAX 8-200 aircraft. This will support the airline's extended fleet renewal and marks a move from the carrier, which has historically relied on Airbus narrowbodies before its pivot to Boeing for larger new-generation aircraft.

According to Air Data News, the new aircraft for Allegiant are expected to be delivered in 2028 and will become the first aircraft delivered through the purchase agreement between Allegiant and Boeing. Allegiant initially increased its firm commitment to 56 jets, of which 22 have already been delivered.

Allegiant holds options for up to 74 MAX aircraft, allowing the Las Vegas-based airline to gradually replace its aging Airbus fleet over the next decade. The six jets set to arrive at Allegiant in 2028 have a combined list price of up to $720 million. The actual purchase price is likely to be considerably lower and has not been disclosed to the public.

As a long-time Airbus operator, Allegiant's transition to the 737 MAX is unusual among US budget airlines. Allegiant historically has built its network and operations around the Airbus A319 and A320; however, when considering its latest investment in new aircraft, the airline switched to the US-manufactured 737 MAX narrowbody.

This decision came after the airline approached both manufacturers for proposals in 2021, and the deal was ultimately signed with Boeing in January 2022. Simple Flying reached out to Allegiant Air for comment on its latest exercised options for six additional MAX 8 aircraft and received the following:

We are pleased to exercise options for six additional Boeing 737 MAX 8 aircraft, scheduled for delivery in 2028. This order supports Allegiant's long-term fleet strategy and reflects our confidence in the company's future growth. The 737 MAX 8 offers enhanced efficiency, reliability, and operational flexibility, helping us meet future capacity needs while continuing to provide affordable, nonstop service.

Since its first delivery in September 2024, Allegiant has operated 22 MAX 8-200 aircraft,a high-density, high-capacity version of the MAX 8. This design is specifically for low-cost carriers and has a higher-than-usual seating capacity with space for up to 200 passengers; Allegiant has it configured for up to 190 passengers.

To legally fit extra seats on the MAX 8 airframe, additional mid-cabin emergency exits are installed behind the wing, and this has made the sub-variant popular with budget airlines. Ryanair is the largest operator of the type and has named the MAX 8-200 as game-changing. Other low-cost operators include Akasa Air, Vietjet Air, and Arajet.

The MAX 8200 is at the heart of a seismic shift towards Boeing.

For Allegiant, the transition to this aircraft is unique: it is the only US low-cost carrier to move its fleet away from the Airbus family of planes to the MAX. The advantages of operating this aircraft extend beyond just additional seating capacity. The airline has boasted that the jets offer lower fuel consumption and improved operational reliability, while having a longer depreciable life than new aircraft.

Allegiant Air merged with Sun Country Airlines earlier this year, bringing in Sun Country's substantial 737-800 fleet. The two airlines continue to operate as separate airlines; they are expected to move to a single operating certificate in 2028.
