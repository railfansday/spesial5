Day 11 – Pulling the Ripcord

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

After a long night of contemplation and hard choices, I decided to get off the trail.  I considered continuing to Tan Hill and its famous pub.  However, I believe that Tan Hill is even more remote than Keld.  If I arrived and decided that I needed medical assistance, it would be nearly impossible to arrange.  Additionally, the pub at Tan Hill was completely booked, so I would have to rely on camping on the grounds around the pub.

I would need to find my way to the nearest town and make a plan from there.  Somehow, I still need to get to Kirk Yetholm by my previously-planned arrival date.  I mailed my extra gear to the Border Hotel and I have a two-night reservation.  Graeme and Rachel told me that they will be finishing the Way on that same day.  If I keep to my schedule, I could meet up with them at the end of the Pennine and celebrate their accomplishment.

Two days after that, I need to be in Edinburgh for my scheduled flight home.

After packing up my camp, I walked into the Keld proper.  It was even smaller and more remote than I realized.  Sitting at the Keld Green Café, sipping a cappuccino, I discovered that the nearest town was Kirkby Stephan, about 10 miles away.  There are no buses in Keld.  I’d be looking at a 4 hour walk at best.

I tried using the Uber app, but after an hour of searching, the app literally apologized and gave up.  I tried a taxi app as well and received the same result.  No one was willing to make the drive from Kirkby Stephen.  I talked to the café owner; he suggested I try hitchhiking.

As I sat contemplating my options, I met Richard and Jackie, two people who were walking England’s Coast-to-Coast trail.  That trail intersects the Pennine Way at Keld.  Jackie was not taking my situation lightly.  She got on her phone and started calling taxi companies in Kirkby Stephen.  Before long, she had arranged to have a taxi make the long haul to Keld and back.  It was pricey, but at this point, I was glad to pay it.

It was almost an hour before the cab showed up.  While I waited, Jackie insisted on buying me another cappuccino.  When the driver pulled up, I thanked her profusely, declared to everyone at the café that she was a living angel, and ran for the cab.

On the drive down to Kirkby Stephan, I could see why drivers were reluctant to pick up fares from Keld.  The country road is long, narrow, and winding.  I’m not prone to motion sickness, but this road really pushed my tolerance.  After 40 minutes, we pulled up in front of the Pennine Inn.  I tipped the driver generously and went inside.  The room was lovely, and the staff, even lovelier.  Realizing that I was injured, they went out of their way to help me.  They were absolutely incredible.

I made all my reservations at hotels and inns within walking distance of the nearest train station.  Many of them were across the street from the station.

The next issue was timing the day of arrival in Kirk Yetholm.  My impression was that this tiny village would not offer a lot of entertainment or distraction.  Getting there too early was not going to be much fun.  Therefore, I decided that I would take a day or two at the end of each travel leg on my schedule.  Be a tourist!  See some of the sites along my route and do my best to enjoy my time in the UK.

And that’s what I did.  I visited towns and cities, toured castles, ate good food, touched the North Sea, and generally just wandered around admiring whatever crossed my path.  Unfortunately, I was still limping, even with the ankle brace, and had to limit my walking distance.  There were occasional bouts of melancholy over my failed attempt at the Pennine.  I felt good about walking nearly 120 miles of the Way, certainly the longest distance I had ever experienced by a very wide margin.  Mostly, I just tried to enjoy my travels.
