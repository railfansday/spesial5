Hundreds of subpoenas fly in DOJ’s ‘Decoy Dan’ probe as Democrat Peltola declared winner in Alaska

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Reacting to a New York Times article, Senator Dan Sullivan details serious allegations of a long-term secret scheme run by Peltola campaign aides. Sullivan expresses strong outrage over reports of focus groups laughing at Alaskans and circulating rumors of hush money, calling on voters to reject what he describes as the most outrageous election cheating scandal in Alaska history.

EXCLUSIVE: The Justice Department has issued more than 200 subpoenas in its criminal investigation into Dan J. Sullivan — "Decoy Dan" — who ran against Republican Sen. Dan S. Sullivan in Alaska's U.S. Senate race, Fox News Digital has learned.

News of the scope of the sprawling criminal probe comes after Democrat Mary Peltola was officially declared the winner of the four-way general election on Tuesday night — two weeks after Election Day. She won 144,302 votes to the incumbent Sullivan's 131,844. A GOP candidate named Gerald Heikes won 6,105 votes, while so-called "Decoy Dan" won 4,760 votes as a non-party affiliated candidate.

A law enforcement source says the criminal case is continuing, and that Peltola being named the winner doesn't change anything from an investigative standpoint. The DOJ's investigation spans multiple states, and there are multiple criminal targets in the case, the source said.

Senator Dan Sullivan, a Republican from Alaska, stands in the Senate Subway of the U.S. Capitol in Washington, D.C., on June 17, 2026. (Daniel Heuer/Bloomberg)

A second source familiar with the investigation confirmed that more than 200 subpoenas have been issued.

Allegations have swirled that Democrats searched for a person with the name Dan Sullivan to run against the incumbent senator of the same name in order to confuse voters and potentially spoil the Republicans' chances of reelection.

High-profile Democrat attorney Marc Elias, whose law firm represents the Peltola campaign, is among those who received a criminal target letter from the DOJ earlier this month.

The controversy exploded just days before the midterm election day on Nov. 3, 2026, when The New York Times reported that Peltola campaign aides, the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee (DSCC) and political operatives outside of Alaska participated in what they called the "ballot composition project," with the goal of recruiting a second Dan Sullivan to enter the race.

Peltola's campaign and the DSCC have denied any wrongdoing.

Dan J. Sullivan, who filed to run for U.S. Senate in Alaska, poses for a photo in Petersburg, Alaska, on June 26, 2026. (Katie Holmlund/Associated Press)

"Decoy Dan" is a retired teacher from the tiny southeastern Alaskan island town of Petersburg, who had virtually no presence on the campaign trail — even his neighbors reported never seeing campaign materials from him. His campaign raised $35,556.

Peltola, a former state lawmaker, made history in 2022 when she became the first Alaska Native elected to Congress. She was unseated in 2024 by Rep. Nick Begich, R-Alaska.

Alaska election officials initially moved to remove the second Sullivan from the ballot amid allegations that his candidacy was intended to confuse voters or siphon support from the senator. The candidate denied wrongdoing, and the Alaska Supreme Court ruled that he could remain on the ballot.

The DOJ sued in October to mitigate any "misleading" impact of the newcomer Sullivan.

Then-Rep. Mary Peltola, D-Alaska, smiles in a photo. (Spencer Platt/Getty Images)

President Donald Trump called for Peltola to drop out of the race, and blasted the Democratic Party over the alleged scheme.

"You had the Democrat Party headed up by Elias, their lawyer, who was a bad guy, very bad guy — should go to jail, in my opinion," Trump said on "Fox & Friends" on Election Day. "But you had the Democrat Party look all over Alaska for somebody named Sullivan."

Neither Peltola nor Sullivan's campaigns returned comment requests — and neither did the DSCC. The White House deferred Fox News Digital to the president's earlier comments on the case.

Fox News Digital's Charles Creitz contributed to this report.
