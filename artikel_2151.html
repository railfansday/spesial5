'I hope we are being incredibly careful' - Bradford City sent takeover warning

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Bradford City are potentially up for sale, with prospective Thai buyers linked to the League One club at present.

There is excitement building as takeover talk heats up at Bradford City before the midpoint of the 2026/27 League One season, with the January transfer window just over a month away.

Bradford are in an excellent position in the League One table to kick on, with Michael Duff taking over from Graham Alexander. However, they have fallen out of the automatic promotion places and fresh investment could kick them on, be that this season or next year.

FLW's Bradford City fan pundit Boris Thomson was asked about the fact Bradford are a surprise target for a Thai-based group who are thinking of a takeover bid.

The Bantams are flying high in League One and come backed by a big support and that has caught the eye of a group of Far East speculators who have had previous interest in buying EFL clubs.

Bradford could be for sale at the right price and the Thais are keen, so we asked what he makes of this news and if he would welcome a takeover. He said: "Seeing this news about a proposed Thai takeover and it's an interesting one.

"I'm not too clued-up at all on what the sort of financial regulations are for League One clubs. I assume that the brackets for financial fair play are of lower figures than in the Premier League.

"We've all seen Man City. The first thing I will say is that I hope we are being incredibly careful with what we are doing, but it depends how incredibly large the financial backing is from the Thais.

"But I wouldn't not welcome it if that makes sense? But I'm not sure we need a revolution, at the moment. A bit more financial backing for Duff and particularly for David Sharpe would be a wonderful thing.

"They both know the club well enough now, maybe not Duff as much but Sharpe definitely, and he knows the club well enough to make the right investments for us in order to improve us on the pitch.

"I think a little more dosh available to help us with some real infrastructural problems would help. Like a training ground; that's the first one we need — to own a training ground.

"I'm not going to jump on the bandwagon just yet. I probably need to hear more about it first, but then we'll see. I think a proposed takeover from a Thai consortium leaves a lot to be desired if the details aren't fully out there yet."

One of the issues, as Boris states, is the training ground. However, it goes beyond that, with the stadium another aspect to consider in a £10 million issue for Bradford or investors going forwards.

Per The Yorkshire Post, Bradford have been giving some consideration to potentially leaving Valley Parade due to the cost of the rent for at least a decade-and-a-half now.

Earlier this year, the Bantams Supporters Trust entered into a period of consultation over what to do about the stadium with Bradford fans, with a valuation of it put at between £8 million and £10 million.

But when The Athletic's Matt Slater reported on the possibility of the Bantams being sold earlier this year back in March 2026, a potential sale price could be just £10 million as well, which could help if investment is needed into a training ground or new stadium entirely.

The recent valuation back in March seems very low for an upwardly mobile League One club, but there's a very good reason for this modest valuation, and it comes down to what the club doesn't own via the stadium.

That said, Bradford fans should be excited, even if Boris is somewhat skeptical, as they have plenty of reasons to feel optimistic following recent reports linking the Thai-based consortium with a potential bid for the Bantams.

Fresh investment could provide new manager Michael Duff with the financial firepower needed to reignite their League One promotion push and finally return the West Yorkshire outfit to the Championship after a very long time away.

Crucially, current owner Stefan Rupp has previously insisted to The Telegraph and Argus that he would only pass the keys to ambitious buyers capable of taking the club forward, meaning any successful deal should guarantee serious backing.

Coupled with Bradford's massive 'one-club city' fanbase and potential Valley Parade stadium developments, a takeover could truly unlock this sleeping giant's long-awaited potential. They could be promoted without it, of course, but consolidating at a higher level would be easier with proper investment.
