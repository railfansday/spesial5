Trump to embark on Asia trip in apparent foreign policy push before end of term

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Experts say US president may lean toward global affairs after domestic influence curtailed in midterm elections

Donald Trump is embarking on his first overseas trip since his Republican party’s thumping midterm repudiation by US voters, in what may herald a renewed focus on foreign policy for the remainder of his presidency.

The US president will meet his Chinese and Russian counterparts, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin, for their first trilateral summit, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (Apec) forum in the Chinese city of Shenzhen. Before that, he will land in Manila in the Philippines on Tuesday for the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (Asean) summit.

At the Asean summit, Trump is expected to sign a trade agreement with Thailand, push deals with other countries and lobby fellow leaders to favour the US over China on artificial intelligence.

He will also take part in the first leaders’ meeting of the Quad partnership – consisting of the US, Japan, India and Australia – since he returned to the White House in January 2025.

White House officials said the president would be accompanied to Apec by Elon Musk, the Tesla and SpaceX entrepreneur who spent almost $500m (£380m) unsuccessfully supporting Republican candidates in this month’s midterms. The reason for Musk’s presence has not been explained.

The China leg of the trip will take place amid the continuing fallout from the Nvidia spying scandal, which prompted the company’s chief executive, Jensen Huang, to resign amid revelations he had had an affair with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy.

Trump is under pressure to raise the matter with Xi when they hold a bilateral meeting on the sidelines of Apec.

The US president has said he does not plan to bring up the subject; administration officials insist he will not do so and that the meeting will be dedicated to implementing a trade agreement between the two superpowers.

Huang persuaded the Trump administration to lift restrictions on the sale of Nvidia’s AI chips to China that had been imposed during Joe Biden’s presidency.

Mao Ning, a Chinese foreign ministry spokesperson, told reporters on Monday that at the trilateral summit, Xi, Trump and Putin “will exchange views in depth on safeguarding world peace and promoting strategic stability in major-power relations, which is of groundbreaking significance”.

Some analysts have characterised the meeting as a “big three” gathering echoing the 1945 Yalta meeting of Winston Churchill, Franklin D Roosevelt and Joseph Stalin that in effect carved up postwar Europe, between a communist-ruled east and a democratic, capitalist west.

Such a prospect has alarmed Ukraine, which fears that Putin – who initially suggested the trilateral meeting – will use it to persuade Trump and Xi to agree to a settlement of the war with Russia on Moscow’s terms.

However, Trump has said Ukraine will not be up for debate at the meeting and that its main purpose will be to discuss ending the US’s war with Iran and “denuclearisation” – shorthand for a deal among the three powers to limit nuclear arms.

That is believed to be unpalatable to China, which has been increasing its nuclear arsenal, and has signalled an unwillingness to enter into reduction agreements until it is closer to parity with the US and Russia.

Analysts say the main beneficiary of the three-way meeting is likely to be Putin, whose standing will be boosted by being treated on an equal footing by two other leaders whose countries are, in reality, much more powerful.

Expectations of a substantial agreement are low.

Lyle Goldstein, an international affairs fellow and China specialist at Brown University in the US, said the Trump-Xi-Putin meeting would not have a substantive result. “There will be no ‘Yalta’, as many have feared,” he said.

Jonathan Czin, the Michael Armacost chair in foreign policy studies at the Brookings Institution, said Trump’s lame duck status would deter Xi and Putin from trying to reach long-term arrangements with the US.

“Both men know Trump’s time is expiring even more quickly than his leverage – and that he will be irrelevant in a mere 26 months [when his term expires],” Czin told an online Brookings forum. “Why would either man make a deal with a leader whose time and leverage are waning? They will only do it if they think they can pilfer more from Trump diplomatically than they will be able to gain from the next American president.”

Nevertheless, Goldstein – who took soundings on Chinese expectations for the summit in a visit to Shenzhen last week – predicted Trump would follow the pattern of previous presidents by turning increasingly to global affairs while he faces the prospect of his influence on the domestic front being curtailed after the Democrats’ victories in the House of Representatives and the Senate.

“The president has been badly damaged by the election and will have limited prospects for the next two years,” he said. “That may cause him to be more active in foreign policy since he can act in that domain with fewer constraints. It would not surprise me if Trump is ready to go all-out to make some further splash in foreign policy.”

With the risks of reigniting the war with Iran high and the Russia-Ukraine war still resistant to a diplomatic resolution despite White House efforts, Goldstein suggested Trump could attempt “a dramatic foreign policy flourish in Asia”, such as an agreement with Kim Jong-un, the North Korean dictator with whom he has frequently boasted of having a close relationship. Kim is not expected to attend the Apec gathering, although there have been rumours he may appear.
