Anthropic’s blockbuster IPO raises the stakes for its enterprise AI business

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Good morning. Founded just five years ago, Anthropic is a powerhouse in AI. Now, it’s making its public debut on Nasdaq.

The company is expected to start trading today under the ticker ANT, with the deal expected to close on Friday. Anthropic announced yesterday that it had priced its IPO at $83 per share, with 980 million shares on offer—about 956.4 million from the company and 23.6 million from existing shareholders.

The offering price is above the initially indicated range of $70 to $78 per share and values the AI company at $1.5 trillion, Fortune’s tech editor Alexei Oreskovic writes. The company said it will receive no proceeds from existing shareholders’ sales. Underwriters have 30 days to buy up to 147 million additional shares.

Founded in 2021 and led by CEO Dario Amodei, Anthropic develops the Claude family of AI models and products, including Claude Code and Cowork. Krishna Rao joined Anthropic as the company’s first CFO in May 2024.

The offering also gives some of Anthropic’s seven cofounders an opportunity to sell a portion of their holdings. Dario Amodei, President Daniela Amodei, and Chief Science Officer Jared Kaplan are among those selling small portions of their equity, Oreskovic reports. Read more here.

Accelerating growth in its enterprise business is a priority for the company. “Since Anthropic was founded, we have focused on building AI for the enterprise,” Rao wrote in a LinkedIn post in September. He added: “We will keep building for the use cases that matter most to businesses.”

According to Anthropic’s IPO disclosures, as of Sept. 30, more than 11,900 customers had generated at least $100,000 in revenue for the company over the preceding 12 months. That included more than 1,900 customers generating at least $1 million each.

For Rao, financing that growth means securing the computing capacity needed to sustain it without allowing infrastructure commitments to overwhelm the business.

Some investors are making a long-term case for investing in Anthropic. “It may not make money until 2030, and the valuation is enormous,” investor Kevin O’Leary posted Tuesday on X. “But you’re investing in the role AI could play across all 11 sectors of the economy. We’re already seeing AI improve productivity and margins, and Claude has become a major enterprise platform.”

Meanwhile, Pierre Baqué, CEO and co-founder of engineering AI software company Neural Concept, told me in an email that everyone is focused on Anthropic’s IPO valuation, but the most overlooked aspect is its governance. The founders’ voting power and the Long-Term Benefit Trust’s board-selection rights, he argued, give management more room to prioritize AI safety and long-term goals over short-term financial results.

Investors will certainly be watching both Anthropic’s financial returns and how its governance commitments hold up under public-market pressure.

Gallup’s latest report suggests that AI is changing specific tasks faster than it is transforming the workday as a whole. Frequent AI use reached 32% of U.S. employees in the third quarter of 2026.

However, an additional finding is 78% report no change in time spent collaborating with colleagues, and 65% say their jobs require the same mental effort. Just 29% of employees in AI-adopting organizations say the technology has raised expectations for daily output.

Taken together, the findings point to a gap between making individual tasks more efficient and redesigning work to translate those gains into broader organizational change.
