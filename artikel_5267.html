Investors weighing Nvidia stock ahead of Tuesday’s third-quarter results now face a new question: will the company keep funding its customers and suppliers after Jensen Huang’s exit? Nvidia has used its balance sheet heavily to support AI buildouts, disclosing more than $540 billion in financing arrangements this year, Bloomberg reported. It also proposed a $500 billion chip securitisation programme in August, offering to cover up to a quarter of the value. Michael Lebowitz of RIA Advisors warned that interim CEO Colette Kress may take a more careful approach. That could hit companies relying on Nvidia’s support, such as CoreWeave, which holds a $6.3 billion Nvidia backstop and carries $24.9 billion in debt. Nvidia has also invested about $2 billion each in Marvell, Synopsys, Coherent and Lumentum, and up to $3.2 billion in Corning. Those stocks fell between 1.6% and 6.1% on Monday, while Nvidia slipped 2%. Nvidia has announced no changes. Wall Street expects quarterly revenue near $109 billion and earnings of $2.49 a share.
