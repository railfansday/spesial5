Base makes quantum security a 2027 priority as regulators warn of risks

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Base creator Jesse Pollak says the upcoming Everest upgrade will help prepare wallets for future quantum threats, although further changes will be needed.

Base, an Ethereum layer-2 blockchain developed by Coinbase, is making quantum security a priority for 2027 as it prepares to protect its network against future threats from quantum computers.

Base creator Jesse Pollak told Cointelegraph in an exclusive interview that the upcoming Everest upgrade will introduce a new type of transaction designed to lay the groundwork for quantum-secure wallets.

The network is the second-largest blockchain by decentralized finance (DeFi) total value locked (TVL), with approximately $5.1 billion in assets deposited across its protocols, according to DefiLlama.

The upgrade will not make wallets quantum-resistant right away, but Pollak said it is an early step toward introducing further protections in the future. “That doesn’t mean we’ll be able to solve all of it in 2027, but [it’s] something the team’s going to be working on,” Pollak said.

The plans come as global regulators warn that quantum computers could eventually break the security systems used across financial markets, including blockchain networks.

Quantum computers process information differently from regular computers and could one day allow hackers to access crypto wallets and steal funds. However, many experts have concluded that today’s quantum computers aren’t powerful enough to do this.

One of Base’s first steps will come through Everest, its upcoming fifth network upgrade. Pollak said the upgrade introduces a new transaction type called Base Transactions, which he expects to support future improvements to wallet security.

“That network upgrade doesn’t specifically solve quantum security for these wallets, but it does put in place the framework,” Pollak said.

The current technical specifications for Base Transactions, outlined in EIP-8430, still rely on existing cryptography rather than quantum-resistant signatures. However, the proposed transaction format separates how a transaction is signed from how its signature is verified, allowing the network to introduce new security methods in the future without requiring users to change their wallet addresses.

Base has not set a timeline for introducing quantum-resistant protections, beyond making the work a priority for 2027.

On Thursday, the International Organization of Securities Commissions (IOSCO) warned in a report that sufficiently powerful quantum computers could threaten the security of financial systems.

The international regulator said blockchain networks could be especially difficult to protect because making security changes would require cooperation from many participants. It also warned that these upgrades could create new security problems, raise costs and disrupt transactions.

Meanwhile, advances in artificial intelligence (AI) could make cyberattacks harder to detect and analyze, the report notes.

Governments are also preparing for the threat, with the US National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) introducing its first three security standards designed to protect against quantum attacks in 2024, and releasing further guidance in October 2026. The agency has set 2035 as the primary target for completing the transition to quantum-resistant cryptography across US federal systems.

Beyond quantum security, Pollak outlined four priorities for Base in 2027: expanding trading, payments and lending, while preparing for a future in which AI agents carry out more financial transactions.

Pollak pointed to growing demand for tokenized stocks, saying weekly trading volume on Base had reached $1 billion. He said much of the interest came from investors outside the US seeking easier access to American stocks.

AI agents are also expected to play a bigger role in the network. “Agentic payments was a big trend in 2026. I think that agentic finance is going to be one of the biggest trends of 2027,” he said.

Base’s latest network upgrade, Denim, was rolled out on Nov. 5 and reduced block times from two seconds to 200 milliseconds, allowing the network to produce blocks 10 times faster.

The upgrade was followed by transaction delays on Base. Pollak said Base reviewed the incidents, made fixes and introduced additional testing to help prevent similar problems.

Looking ahead, Pollak said he wants future upgrades to happen without users noticing. “Our goal in 2027 is to make it so that upgrades are non-event. So no one’s even talking about them,” he said.
