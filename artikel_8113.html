Field Tested From the High Sierra to the French Alps: Sportiva’s Newest Approach Shoe

Hand cracks are not the ideal terrain for the TX1, but they are just too fun to pass up. (Photo: Anthony Walsh)

There are three things that make an approach shoe “high value:” its price, versatility, and durability. At $159, La Sportiva’s all-new TX1 (replacing the cult-classic TX2) is decently affordable, and was versatile enough to take testers across a range of objectives, including fifth-class ridges in the Sierras and daily crag commutes, and be worn as a slip-on between limit boulder problems. I logged dozens of scrambling pitches in my TX1s and noted no signs of premature wear along the TPU-coated polyester upper or its Vibram MegaGrip outsole.

The breathable upper and shallow dot lugs make the TX1 an excellent shoe for the fair-weather rock climber who dodges rainy forecasts and muddy approaches. Those little lugs provide just enough grip for your average walkoff, but really shine on technical, slabby approaches where surface contact is king. On the mile-long Eagle Lake Buttress Traverse (5.0), the TX1 smeared well across its opening slabs—but I wished for a taller rand while jamming hand-sized cracks.

Fit wise, all testers found the TX1 to be comfortable out of the box, thanks to a stock 4mm Ortholite Hybrid insole and a plush Air Mesh lining. (The TX1 fits a touch wider than the TX2, for those familiar.) One tester, who has a relatively narrow, low-volume foot, loved the fit of the TX1, saying its toebox felt roomy and allowed her toes “to spread and relax naturally.” My wider foot, meanwhile, had me eager to take the TX1 off after an hour-long approach. I wish this shoe came in a slightly wider last.
