Tom Hanks’ Heart-Breaking Drama Is Still His Greatest Movie 33 Years Later

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Tom Hanks is viewed by many audiences as a comforting figure in culture – Mr. Rogers, Santa, Woody, and Walt Disney have all been brought to life by his singular warmth and charm. However, the movie that first propelled him to dramatic actor status is one that remains his best and riskiest bet. The 1993 Oscar-winning drama Philadelphia took an urgent subject for the time and gave it a human perspective, proving that then-comedy star Hanks could also deliver a heart-breaking performance while sharing the screen with a heavyweight like co-star Denzel Washington. Over three decades later, it remains his finest acting moment.

Set in the titular city, Hanks plays Andrew Beckett, a successful lawyer who finds himself fired from his firm after his employers find out that he is both gay and has contracted the AIDS virus. Determined to sue them for discrimination, he approaches personal injury lawyer Joe Miller (Washington) to represent him. Joe initially refuses due to homophobic preconceptions, but has a change of heart once he sees what Andrew is up against. The pair fight against bigotry and Andrew’s worsening health in an attempt to find justice.

Loosely based on the stories of real-life lawyers, the film earned Tom Hanks his first Oscar for Best Actor, while Bruce Springsteen won Best Original Song for his haunting theme “Streets of Philadelphia.” For Washington, it was another classic performance, one that addressed the ignorance toward HIV/AIDS and the gay community at the time. For Hanks, it kicked off a decade of prestige performances that would make him a Hollywood legend.

There’s no doubt that Hanks was a star by the time he took on the role of Andrew Beckett. For much of the 1980s, he had been the lead in a number of box office hits, primarily in the comedy genre. Big, Turner & Hooch, The ‘Burbs, Splash, and A League of Their Own had cemented him in the minds of audiences as someone who was adept at making you laugh. But in Philadelphia, he proved he could also make you sob.

As Beckett, he is strong, principled, but also vulnerable. He knows there is nothing wrong with who he is, despite the prejudice he faces, but at the same time, knows he is facing a disease that, in that moment, meant he did not have much time to make his case. Hanks committed to the part fully, losing 35 pounds for the role and acknowledging that his likable presence made people face their own preconceptions. In a 2022 interview with The New York Times, he said: “One of the reasons people weren't afraid of that movie is that I was playing a gay man.”

Given the attitudes at the time, it was a risk to his crowd-pleasing reputation. However, the movie was a deeply emotional, human journey spearheaded by his earnest portrayal. Any gamble he took paid off, as the Oscar win confirmed he could make a dramatic hit as well as a comedy. In the following years, he doubled down on that reputation, starring in classics like Apollo 13, Saving Private Ryan, and The Green Mile before the 1990s were over.

Some of the exposition in director Jonathan Demme’s film may seem simplistic while watching over three decades later, as so much more progress has been made with societal attitudes as well as the treatment of HIV/AIDS. However, context is key in understanding how impactful the movie had been in spreading mainstream awareness of these issues. 1993 was the peak of the HIV/AIDS epidemic, with millions dying of the disease by the end of the decade. Parallel to this was a cultural rift, where false information about the virus had given the public an inaccurate picture.

While it did not address the political issues that activists felt needed highlighting, what it did was take people outside the crisis and make them see a human instead of a headline. This was a major Hollywood picture talking about subjects for the first time that were uncomfortable, but important, and only great art can do that. By focusing on one man’s struggle, it was easier to put yourself in his shoes and see it as a global struggle.33 years later, Philadelphia is a landmark moment in Hollywood history, creating discourse off-screen and launching one of the industry's finest stars on it. It's arguable that the first step toward Tom Hanks producing numerous classics of the '90s and beyond begins by taking a risk that shows how good he can be.
