Tilda Swinton, Mark Ruffalo, Jacques Audiard, and Others Join in Protest of Jafar Panahi’s Prison Sentence

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The film community is marshaling to support Oscar-nominated, dissident Iranian filmmaker Jafar Panahi, who was given a prison sentence of five years and one month by the Tehran Revolutionary Court back in October.

A letter initiated by the International Coalition for Filmmakers at Risk (and shared with Variety) so far features more than 200 signatories “deeply concerned by reports of the new prison sentence of five years and one month issued by the Tehran Revolutionary Court against Iranian filmmaker Jafar Panahi, in absentia.” Panahi returned to Iran in the spring following the end of Neon’s awards run for his Cannes Film Festival Palme d’Or winner “It Was Just an Accident,” nominated for Best International Feature and Best Original Screenplay at the 2026 Academy Awards. Actors and filmmakers who’ve signed the letter include Mark Ruffalo, Tilda Swinton, Jacques Audiard, Kleber Mendonça Filho, Catherine Breillat, Bertrand Bonello, Alice Braga, Arthur Harari, Asif Kapadia, Lucrecia Martel, Rob Epstein, Rodrigo Teixeira, Apichatpong Weerasethakul, Geeta Gandbhir, and many more. Festival leaders who signed the letter include Berlinale director Tricia Tuttle, New York Film Festival artistic director Dennis Lim, Venice’s Alberto Barbera, Telluride head Julie Huntsinger, and others — many of whom have hosted Panahi’s films across the last four decades.

Previously, Panahi was sentenced to one year in prison and a two-year ban on leaving Iran in December, in absentia. The new October 2026 sentence carries additional penalties, as the letter notes, including confiscation of his assets, freezing of his bank accounts, a two-year travel ban, and a two-year social media ban.

Panahi has a long history of legal entanglements with the Iranian government, which his films have criticized, including numerous imprisonments, a temporary house arrest, and being barred from participating in filmmaking activities. These punitive actions have frequently forced Panahi to make his films in secret or structure them around his filmmaking process or legal status — such as in the 2022 Venice prize winner “No Bears.”

Panahi’s bank accounts were frozen around the October sentencing, according to Panahi’s attorney Mostafa Nili. Panahi could not be reached in October for the sentencing, according to reports, despite being present in Iran as recently as September.

“In March 2026, the 66-year-old Mr. Panahi courageously decided to return to Iran, aware of the risks but having always spoken of his deep love for his country, and for his desire to stay and work there. In October 2026 he learned of the new court verdict against him after his bank accounts were frozen, his lawyer, Mostafa Nili, said in a statement to the Iranian press. The new charges against him remain unclear, but appear consistent with repeated efforts to intimidate him into ceasing his filmmaking. The case will now go to regional court, and the sentence can be appealed. This requires resources, but all of Mr. Panahi’s accounts have been blocked,” the letter states.

“We stand in solidarity with Mr. Panahi and all Iranians facing persecution for their work at the hands of their own government. We call on the Tehran Revolutionary Court to rescind all actions to penalize and imprison Mr. Panahi and to abide by the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights to which the Iranian government is a signatory.”
