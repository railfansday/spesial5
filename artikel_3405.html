The Best Visual Effects in ‘Wildwood’ Are the Ones You Can’t See — Watch

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

There are all sorts of magic worlds waiting to be discovered in the stop-motion epic “Wildwood,” but it’s a credit to the film that one of them is Portland, Oregon. Laika Studio’s Head of CG Animation Benoit Dubuc was involved early and often in order to determine what would be shot practically by the stop-motion animation team and what CG effects would be used to achieve. Even in the most mundane and ordinary moments of the film question of either/or. It was one of how the collaboration between different kinds of animation would work.

“We mostly shoot two or three puppets at a time, so any time that there’s background characters, they’re gonna be digital,” Dubuc said during IndieWire’s Craft Roundtable on visual effects. He pointed out that when Baby Mac gets kidnapped by a murder of crows under the command of the dread sorceress Alexandra (Carey Mulligan), the amount of work to achieve that moment fully through stop-motion would mean they’d probably still be animating it. “We’ll often get [animators] to produce some practical work to lead the visual effects work. We built a crow puppet to spec. We didn’t animate it in the film, but it informed the visual effects work. We try to stay away from simulations as much as possible, knowing that what we’re chasing is something that feels handmade and something that is slightly imperfect,” Dubuc said.

That goes for individual characters and for the vibrant backgrounds of “Wildwood” alike. “The audience would think that we built sets the size of an airport hangar with a hundred thousand practical puppets running downhill. It’s not possible,” Dubuc said. The fact that it looks possible, in both the most intricate battle sequences and the ordinary chaos of biking past Powell’s Books, means that the VFX team has done its job. “ The portrayal of Portland that we were striving for is a beautiful, vibrant city,” Dubuc said.
