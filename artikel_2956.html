If You'd Invested $1,000 in Berkshire Hathaway (BRKB) Stock 20 Years Ago, Here's How Much You'd Have Today

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Spoiler: Your stake would have grown impressively -- and at a slower rate than the stock has grown in the past.

By Selena Maranjian – Nov 18, 2026 at 9:35AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Berkshire Hathaway ›

Just about all of us know that Warren Buffett is one of the greatest investors ever, and many of us are familiar with the company he built into a trillion-dollar enterprise -- Berkshire Hathaway (BRKA -0.88%) (BRKB -1.12%). He grew the company's value by nearly 20% annually over some 60 years!

Let's say you parked a modest $1,000 in Berkshire Hathaway 20 years ago. What would it be worth today? Well, the answer is $8,310. The shares would have been trading for around $71.29 per share in November 2006, and more recently, they've been around $590 apiece. That's a total gain of 732%, and an annualized one of 11.16% (which still tops the S&P 500's average of 9.3% over the same period).

Whenever I report these kinds of numbers, I like to include reinvested dividends. Berkshire is not a dividend-paying stock at this point, as its leaders have preferred to plow excess earnings into furthering its growth -- and they've occasionally repurchased some shares, too. The S&P 500 does feature dividends, though, and with dividends reinvested over 20 years, its average annual gain would come to 10.6% -- still below Berkshire's gain, but not by a lot.

Multiplying your investment's value eightfold is pretty great. Can you expect that kind of growth over the next 20 years with Berkshire? Well, maybe. I certainly wouldn't count on average annual gains of 20% or more, as the company has delivered in the past -- because it can be especially hard for really large companies to grow.

But Berkshire is built to last, and features a lot of diversification. It owns many businesses in the insurance, energy, transportation, and manufacturing sectors, among others, and has a large portfolio of stocks, too. It's reasonable to hope or expect it to surpass the S&P 500's performance over the long run.
