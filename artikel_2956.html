Future Eagles Fits: Five interior defensive linemen to watch in 2027 NFL draft

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Future Eagles Fits: Five interior defensive linemen to watch in 2027 NFL draft originally appeared on NBC Sports Philadelphia

Welcome back to Future Eagles Fits, our weekly deep dive into the prospects who could eventually find their way to Philadelphia.

Every week, we're going inside the tape and looking at prospects through an Eagles-centric lens — breaking down the athletic profile, positional versatility and scheme fit that could ultimately determine whether a player makes sense in Philly. Along the way, we'll examine how prospects are being evaluated across the league as draft boards begin to take shape and conversations are had with league representatives across the scouting landscape.

For the Eagles, the interior of their defensive line has quickly become a position to monitor as the organization begins to look ahead. A first and second round pick in return for Jordan Davis is outstanding additional draft capital to work with, but it also leaves a significant piece of the defensive front to replace moving forward. Jalen Carter remains the centerpiece of the group, while Moro Ojomo, Ty Robinson, and Byron Young will have opportunities to take on larger roles within the rotation. Still, adding another young, explosive presence to the interior could go a long way toward maintaining the physicality and disruptive ability that has become such an important part of the Eagles' defensive identity.

The good news? It's an intriguing class up front with several players who offer unique skill sets that could make sense for what the Eagles are looking to add. Whether it's finding another high-end interior pass rusher, adding a versatile defensive lineman who can align up and down the front, or bringing in a bigger-bodied presence who can control the line of scrimmage, there are legitimate options to consider.

While there is still a ton of football left to be played and the entirety of the pre-draft process still to play out — which will alter boards — here are five interior defensive linemen to know in the upcoming class — two potential Day 1 targets, two Day 2 prospects and one Day 3 option who could provide some additional juice up front.

Day 1: Xavier Gilliam, Tennessee | 6'2″, 307 lbs (verified measurements)

If you're looking for an interior presence who can immediately change the way an opposing offense has to approach its protection schemes, it starts with Gilliam in this class.

A former transfer from Penn State, Gilliam has developed into one of the most explosive defensive players in the entire country this season and he's currently a top-10 player on my personal board. Below is a quick peek at the type of pop he provides up front.

At nearly 310 pounds, Gilliam has an outstanding combination of lower-body explosiveness, strength and initial quickness off the snap. He doesn't need much space to generate momentum, and his ability to explode out of his stance allows him to create immediate problems for guards attempting to establish angles.

There's a level of violence to his game that really stands out, as well. He plays with outstanding energy, consistently attacks the line of scrimmage and has the type of first-step quickness that can make even well-positioned offensive linemen uncomfortable. He's not simply a defensive tackle who occupies space and looks to disrupt the run game, he has legitimate pass-rush juice and the athletic profile to create negative plays without needing a blitz or additional help from the second level.

For Philly, he and Carter would be a true nightmare to counter due to their explosiveness up front. Add in Ojomo – we'll see if they do in fact extend him – and all three would give the Eagles an outstanding foundation along the line of scrimmage. If you're looking to invest first-round capital in an interior defensive lineman, finding someone who can make an impact in both phases of the game is a pretty good place to start. He's one of the most exciting defensive prospects in the entire class.

Day 1: Devan Thompkins, Alabama | 6'5″, 290 lbs (verified measurements)

Gilliam is the explosive interior disruptor, but Thompkins might be the most versatile defensive lineman in the entire draft.

A transfer from USC to Alabama, Thompkins has the opportunity to be the first interior/edge hybrid selected next April. At 290 pounds, he brings a unique physical profile to the position, and I'm not sure there's another player in this class who offers quite the same flexibility when it comes to moving around a defensive front.

The biggest selling point with Thompkins is that he doesn't necessarily need to be confined to one particular alignment. He can line up directly across from a guard and attack the interior, work outside an offensive tackle, or even operate as a wide-nine defender outside the tight end. It's an incredibly valuable skill set for any defensive coordinator who wants to manufacture different looks, create favorable matchups and keep opposing offensive lines guessing.

There's real power behind his game, too, which matters for young players making the transition to the pro game. It can't all be finesse, and Thompkins has herculean strength to generate movement at the point of attack, and his ability to attack from different alignments makes him particularly difficult to counter from down to down. He has the athleticism to challenge offensive tackles on the outside, but also possesses the strength and power to create problems when working inside against guards.

What makes his evaluation particularly interesting is the number of different ways an NFL coaching staff could utilize him. Want to kick him inside and create a mismatch against a guard? Sure. Line him up outside the tackle and let him use his length and athleticism to get after the quarterback? That's another option. He's the type of player who gives a defensive coordinator the freedom to be creative with personnel and alignment without sacrificing physicality, and for the Eagles, that versatility could be especially appealing. Philadelphia has traditionally valued defensive linemen who can move around the front and create problems from multiple alignments. With Carter and Ojomo already providing interior options, Thompkins could give the Eagles another player who allows them to change their defensive presentation depending on the opponent.

Day 2: A.J. Holmes Jr, Texas Tech | 6'2″, 300 lbs, 32 ⅜ arm (verified measurements)

Holmes is a name whose draft stock has continued to trend upward throughout the process, and there's a lot to like about what he brings to the interior defensive line.

At 300 pounds, he has a compact, powerful frame that allows him to hold his ground against offensive linemen while also providing pass-rush upside.

He's capable of winning against individual blocks with his power using his upper-body strength to create separation and disrupt the timing of opposing run concepts, but he's not a player who needs to rely exclusively on athleticism to make an impact. There's a physical element to his game that allows him to compete at the point of attack and create problems for offensive linemen who aren't prepared for his initial punch.

He's also shown the ability to provide something as a pass rusher, which is particularly important when evaluating interior defensive linemen in this range. Holmes has enough quickness to attack gaps and create pressure from the inside, giving him a pathway to contribute on passing downs rather than simply being a rotational run defender. His ability to generate disruption without requiring a favorable matchup is an important part of his evaluation, and he's excelled so far this year totaling 11 sacks through 10 games for the Red Raiders.

There are still areas to develop, particularly when it comes to consistently anchoring against combination blocks and maintaining his positioning when offenses devote additional attention to him, but that's part of the appeal of finding a player here on Day 2.

For Philly, Holmes would provide another young body who can compete for snaps in the middle of the defensive line. Established pieces are present, but continuing to add depth and competition to the rotation is important, particularly after moving Davis. Holmes has the ability to contribute against the run while offering pass-rush upside, making him an intriguing developmental option who could eventually grow into a larger role.

Day 2: Jordan Hall, Georgia | 6'4″, 320 lbs, 35 ¼ arm (verified measurements)

The Bulldogs have consistently produced NFL-caliber defensive linemen, and Hall is another player whose combination of size, length, and physicality makes him an intriguing prospect in the class.

The bottom line for the Georgia product – he's an immovable force in the ground game.

There's a level of natural strength to his game that makes it extremely difficult for offensive linemen to create movement against him. He has the length to control a rep early on, size to occupy space, the lower-body strength to anchor against double teams and the physicality to make life difficult for opposing offensive lines attempting to establish their running game. He's not necessarily going to win every snap by making a spectacular play in the backfield, but his ability to screw his cleats into the grass and prevent offenses from creating movement at the point of attack is what makes him so appealing.

And sometimes, that's exactly what a defense needs.

There's also a natural fit when considering the Eagles' recent investment in Georgia defenders. Roseman has consistently shown an appreciation for the program's defensive talent, and Hall would give the Eagles another opportunity to bring in a player from a pipeline they know well.

After moving Davis, adding another big-bodied presence who can control the middle of the defensive line would make plenty of sense. Carter and Ojomo offer different skill sets, and Hall could complement those players by providing a more traditional interior run defender who can eat up space and make it difficult for opposing offenses to establish their identity on the ground.

Day 3: Jide Abasiri, USC | 6'4″, 281 lbs (verified measurements)

I hope Abasiri ends up at an All-Star game in the pre-draft process for another evaluation exposure as he could end up being one of the more intriguing value selections in the middle to late stages of the draft.

A young prospect who just turned 21 years old, Abasiri has all the physical tools NFL teams drool over when going through the evaluation process. At 6-foot-4 and 285 pounds, he brings excellent length to the interior defensive line and has shown enough flashes throughout the season to suggest there's a lot more potential to unlock as he continues developing.

The first thing that stands out is his physical profile, as Abasiri has the type of length that can create immediate problems for opposing offensive linemen, particularly when he's able to extend and establish his hands to keep blockers from getting into his frame. For a younger player, that combination of size and length is an appealing starting point, especially when considering how much development can take place within ideal coaching surroundings in the NFL.

At this stage of the process, consistency is going to be an important part of his evaluation. There's a difference between flashing the ability to win at the college level and consistently executing those techniques against NFL-caliber offensive linemen. Abasiri still has room to develop his overall game, particularly when it comes to translating those physical tools into more consistent production, and it's a main reason why he's in this range to begin with.

Right now, he's someone who could land anywhere from the late third round into the early-mid portion of Day 3, depending on how the rest of the evaluation process unfolds. His age, physical makeup and flashes of disruption give him an appealing developmental profile, and a strong finish to the season followed by a good showing throughout the pre-draft process will absolutely help his stock continue to climb.

For the Eagles, they don't necessarily need every defensive lineman it drafts to come in and immediately command a significant snap share. Finding younger players with legitimate physical upside who can develop behind established veterans is an important part of maintaining a productive defensive front over time, and Abasiri has the length, athleticism and developmental runway to make him an intriguing option later in the draft.
