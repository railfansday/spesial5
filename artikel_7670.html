Judge receives admonishment for offensive comment about Native Americans

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

A Washington superior court judge received an admonishment Friday from the Washington Commission on Judicial Conduct for commenting on “violence from the Native American community,” causing courtroom onlookers to react in shock and offense. (Image from Shutterstock)

A Washington superior court judge received an admonishment Friday from the Washington Commission on Judicial Conduct for commenting on “violence from the Native American community,” causing courtroom onlookers to react in shock and offense.

Mason County Superior Court Judge Monty D. Cobb made the remark while sentencing a defendant who pleaded guilty to killing a fellow member of the Skokomish Tribe.

Cobb said it was “extremely unfortunate that I see way too many acts of violence from the Native community,” according to coverage by Law360.

Many tribal members were inside the courtroom when Cobb made the statement, and two complaints reported to the Washington Commission on Judicial Conduct prompted a confidential investigation.

The investigation “determined that the quoted comments caused shock and offense among the onlookers and a sense that respondent was blaming the community for its experience of high rates of violence,” the judicial conduct commission’s report said.

Cobb admitted to violating two rules: Canon 1, Rules 1.1 and 1.2 of the Washington Code of Judicial Conduct, which requires judges to conduct themselves in a manner that promotes public confidence in the judiciary’s integrity and impartiality and to avoid the appearance of impropriety.

The judge’s sanction, in part, includes reading the state’s Code of Judicial Conduct “in its entirety” and confirming in writing. The investigation’s document noted that courtroom observers think that Cobb “does not harbor a bias against Indigenous people or other marginalized groups, and that everyone is treated fairly in his courtroom.”
