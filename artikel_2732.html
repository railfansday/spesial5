Game Recap: Suns defeat Pelicans 123-119 in OT for fourth straight road win

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The Phoenix Suns played their first overtime game of the season, as their battle in the Big Easy was anything but. They walked away with a 123-119 victory over the Pelicans, surviving a Jordan Poole variance magnet and extending their winning streak to four games, all of which have come on this road trip. It's also the sixth consecutive victory for Phoenix over New Orleans.

The Pelicans' bench provided quite the challenge, scoring 67 points and stealing the narrative the Suns typically own with the production of their second unit. Three New Orleans reserves scored in double figures, with Jordan Poole leading the way with 28 points on 9-of-12 shooting, including 6-of-8 from beyond the arc. Zion Williamson was the only Pelicans starter to reach double figures, scoring 25 points.

Meanwhile, four Suns starters scored in double figures, with Collin Gillespie providing 18 points off the bench on 7-of-11 shooting, including 3-of-6 from deep. He played the entire fourth quarter and overtime after exiting the game earlier following a scary sequence in which Ryan Dunn appeared to roll up on his leg.

It was a tight game throughout, featuring 19 lead changes and 12 ties. Ultimately, the Suns pulled it out in overtime, fueled by five points from Gillespie and four from Miles Bridges.

The Suns improve to 4-0 on their current road trip and will look to complete a clean sweep when they wrap things up Wednesday night in Houston.

Jalen Green was the offensive catalyst early for Phoenix, at least from a shot volume perspective. He accounted for four of the Suns' first seven field goal attempts, connecting on two of them.

Meanwhile, the Pelicans employed a strategy we've seen plenty of times before. Give Zion Williamson the ball on the perimeter, let him get downhill and attack the basket, drawing fouls and creating and-one opportunities along the way.

The Pelicans aren't a particularly good rebounding team, and neither are the Suns. In the first five minutes alone, Phoenix surrendered five offensive rebounds, which New Orleans converted into five second-chance points.

The first round of substitutions included Khaman Maluach, who immediately made his presence felt on the glass and recorded an impressive block on the physical Zion Williamson. His insertion into the game helped spark a 13-3 run for the Suns.

Ultimately, it was the New Orleans second unit that brought the most energy in the opening quarter. The Pelicans' bench accounted for 22 of their 35 first-quarter points, fueling a 14-3 run in response to Phoenix's earlier surge, including a buzzer-beating three by Jordan Poole to end the quarter.

The Pelicans carried that energy into the second quarter, playing with more physicality and pushing the pace, which was to be expected from a team that ranks in the top five in the NBA in pace.

Jordan Poole was feeling it, and I think we all know what happens when Jordan Poole starts feeling it. Either grab the fire extinguisher or body him up. Poole started his night 6-of-6 from the field for 17 points, including 4-of-4 from beyond the arc.

Still, the Suns had an answer for everything the Pelicans threw at them during Poole's heater. Halfway through the second quarter, Phoenix found itself with a 48-47 lead.

With 3:28 remaining in the quarter, Yves Missi went up for a dunk, and Khaman Maluach elevated to deny him. A foul was called on Maluach, prompting Jordan Ott to challenge the play. The challenge was successful, overturning the call to an offensive foul on Missi.

The minutes Maluach provided in the first half were spectacular. He was playing with physicality, and his hands have been looking much better as of late. Twice the Suns penetrated the defense and found him in the paint. Twice he caught it. Twice he dunked it.

The Suns outscored the Pelicans 25-19 in the second quarter. New Orleans shot 8-of-16 from deep in the first half, but their 11 turnovers allowed Phoenix to hang around.

The Suns surrendered only three more offensive rebounds than they collected and were outrebounded by two overall, although it certainly felt much different watching the game unfold.

Jordan Poole led all scorers with 17 points off the bench, while Dillon Brooks and Jalen Green each had 10 points. Khaman Maluach contributed six points and five rebounds in 10 minutes of action.

Heading into halftime, the Suns held a 56-54 lead.

The Suns opened the second half on a 5-0 run, and Pelicans head coach Jamahl Mosley opted to use a challenge after Herb Jones was called for fouling Dillon Brooks on a jump shot. The challenge was unsuccessful, although Brooks proceeded to miss both free throws.

Shortly thereafter, with the Suns leading 61-58, Jordan Ott utilized his second challenge of the night, disputing whether the ball went out of bounds off Jeremiah Fears following a Devin Booker block. The challenge was successful, and Phoenix retained possession. Both teams, however, were now out of challenges.

The third quarter was a back-and-forth affair, but one of the more painful moments came following a collision between Ryan Dunn and Collin Gillespie as they pursued a rebound after a missed transition opportunity by Zion Williamson.

Dunn was pushed into Gillespie, taking his legs out from underneath him. Gillespie exited the game and immediately headed to the locker room.

Jordan Poole returned to the game and continued to cook. After falling behind by seven points, the Pelicans rattled off a 16-2 run that included another Poole three-pointer, this one coming with a foul that was upgraded to a Flagrant 1.

It certainly didn't help that during a 10-0 stretch of that run, Jalen Green opted to go into isolation and chuck up an airball from beyond the arc.

The Pelicans outscored the Suns 29-23 in the third quarter, fueled by another 11 points off the bench from Jordan Poole and eight from Zion Williamson. Devin Booker scored 10 points in the quarter, but Phoenix trailed 83-79 heading into the fourth.

The fourth quarter started with a welcome site: Gillespie returned to the floor.

We once again saw a lineup featuring Dillon Brooks alongside the second unit, although Oso Ighodaro was out there as well. The Suns quickly tied the game, but the Pelicans continued to use their size to their advantage, continually attacking the interior and getting to the line. Derik Queen benefitted from this aggression, shooting 5 free throws through the halfway mark of the fourth. He hit one.

The Suns, after being down by as much as 6, tied the game on an impressive Jalen Green 13-footer and took a 100-98 lead on a Miles Bridges reverse. Housekeeping note: Upon review, a Luke Kennard three-pointer was ruled a two-pointer, so it was a one-point lead for Phoenix, 99-98.

Jalen Green threw down a beautiful transition dunk and buried a three to put Phoenix up 104-100. Then came the carelessness. Green tried to do too much, Bridges followed with a poor pass, and consecutive Suns turnovers fueled a quick 6-0 Pelicans run.

The Suns regained the lead courtesy of a Miles Bridges free throw and a Collin Gillespie layup. Saddiq Bey tied it at 107 with 46.3 seconds remaining. The next possession? A patented Devin Booker turnaround 13-footer. Splash. Jeremiah Fears tied it at 109 with 22.8 seconds left. Booker passed out of a double team, got the ball back, and had his pass intended for Gillespie intercepted by Herb Jones. The Suns never got a shot off. Jones missed his half-court heave.

The unit out there to start overtime? Collin Gillespie, Jalen Green, Devin Booker, Miles Bridges, and Dillon Brooks. Yes, the Suns were going small. On the opening defensive possession, Gillespie did a stellar job of staying in front of Zion Williamson, forcing him into a fadeaway rather than allowing him to get to the rim.

He then converted an and-one opportunity on the other end to give Phoenix a three-point lead. Miles Bridges canned a three-pointer on the ensuing Suns possession, extending the lead to four.

The Suns looked much more locked in defensively during overtime. Their closeouts were spectacular, and it felt like they were everywhere. Even though they continued to get owned on the offensive glass, the defensive intensity was there, highlighted by a possession in which Devin Booker ripped the ball away from Zion Williamson.

Gillespie then knocked down a 19-footer as part of a 10-2 Phoenix run in overtime to give Phoenix the 6-point lead.

New Orleans continued to chip away as overtime progressed, and Saddiq Bey threw up a Hail Mary three-pointer on a play in which it appeared he traveled, pulling the Pelicans within three points with 25 seconds remaining.

The Suns inbounded the ball, and New Orleans began playing the foul game, sending Miles Bridges to the line. He once again went 1-of-2, giving Phoenix a four-point lead. The Pelicans chucked up another three, missed, and fouled the Suns with 2.6 seconds remaining.

Phoenix secured its fourth consecutive road victory, defeating New Orleans 123-119.

The road trip ends on Wednesday night with a Thanksgiving Eve battle against the Rockets in Houston.
