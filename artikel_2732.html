Packers should lean on running back Kaleb Johnson after their bye

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The bright spots of the Packers' Week 10 loss to the Vikings are few and far between, but running back Kaleb Johnson was surely among them.

"I thought he did a nice job," LaFleur said of Johnson's 10-carry, 42-yard outing. "I liked how he was hitting the hole, running with good pad level, pressing the line of scrimmage. I thought that was a positive performance."

If there's a big takeaway to be had here, it's that Johnson should be getting the lion's share of carries going forward, and probably deserves more snaps, too.

Josh Jacobs remains a mystery. He's on track to return in relatively short order from finger surgery, but a potential suspension still looms like the sword of Damocles. He wasn't overly effective as a runner in his brief return to the lineup, either. Across three games, Jacobs carried 36 times for just 80 yards, an abysmal 2.2 yards per carry. Even the perpetually disappointing MarShawn Lloyd has managed 2.7 yards per attempt this year.

Speaking of Lloyd, even if he's better than Jacobs this year, he's obviously not the future or the present at the position. Kudos to him for making it all the way back from his various injury issues over the last couple of seasons, but it's abundantly clear he can't get the job done. It's hard to foresee him improving much even if the Packers make major strides on the offensive line.

Chris Brooks, meanwhile, is a solid but limited player. It's probably better to think of him as a fullback at this point, a latter day John Kuhn who mostly finds his way to the field because he's not going to screw it up for the Packers in pass protection.

And since Pierre Strong Jr. has now been released, that leaves us with Johnson as the last remaining back on the roster, and perhaps the most viable.

The big problem with Johnson to this point has been his limited value in the passing game. You can see what the Packers think of him there by just look at his snap usage. He was on the field for 13 snaps against the Vikings and carried the ball on 10 of them. As a rule, they don't want him catching passes or blocking for Jordan Love.

But LaFleur did call out Johnson's pass blocking as his most notable contribution on Sunday. Johnson stonewalled a blitzing linebacker on a 19-yard completion.

"The thing I was most pleased with was one of the protections when we hit [Tucker Kraft], he came across and picked up Eric Wilson on the blitz right in the B gap," LaFleur said.

You don't have to be a tape grinder to see what LaFleur is talking about. Johnson lines up to Jordan Love's left on the play, but alertly comes across the formation to pick off Wilson just as he jets past Travis Glover. Love completes the pass to Kraft, and the Packers would go on to score three points on the drive, cutting the Vikings' lead to just 10 points. In theory, it could have been a pivotal play.

The fact is that Johnson is the only back doing anything even close to making pivotal plays this season. The Packers have only had a back rush for 40 or more yards in a game three times this season, and Johnson is responsible for all three of those occurrences. The Packers will probably never have a good running game this season, but if they're going to do anything at all on the ground, it's probably going to be because Johnson is getting the work done.
