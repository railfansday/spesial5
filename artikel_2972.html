Grand Theft Auto VI Is Finally Here. Is Take-Two Stock a Buy?

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Take-Two just launched the most anticipated game in years, but a better reason to own the stock is how these hit releases can generate revenue for years after launch.

By John Ballard – Nov 24, 2026 at 10:45AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Take-Two Interactive Software ›

After a long wait, Grand Theft Auto VI is finally here. Take-Two Interactive Software's (TTWO +6.18%) Rockstar Games released it on Thursday, Nov. 19, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S.

But for Take-Two shareholders, first-week sales aren't what matters most. Take-Two is the publisher behind other hit titles, including NBA 2K and a big lineup of mobile games. The stock is already pricing in a lot of growth, but what could make it worth buying is Take-Two's ability to keep earning revenue from its games for years after release.

Take-Two's biggest revenue engine is what it calls recurrent consumer spending: The money players spend after they already own a game, such as virtual currency, add-on content, in-game purchases, and in-game ads. Rockstar also sells a paid membership called GTA+.

This spending made up about 26% of Take-Two's revenue in fiscal 2017. By fiscal 2026, which ended in March, it was 78%. Part of that growth came from the 2022 purchase of mobile game maker Zynga, which added more revenue to the mix in fiscal 2023. In the most recent quarter, which ended Sept. 30, recurrent spending reached 81% of revenue.

GTA V launched in 2013, becoming one of the most played video games in recent years. In fiscal 2023, management said the game had sold more than 180 million units. A year later, that number exceeded 200 million, and CEO Strauss Zelnick noted that GTA V and GTA Online were still growing their audiences more than a decade after their release. On the recent Nov. 9 earnings call, he said GTA V has sold "nearly 235 million units worldwide."

The money didn't stop at the first purchase. GTA Online players kept buying virtual currency, and in the latest quarter, the GTA series grew both recurrent spending and average monthly GTA+ members by double digits. That's the playbook GTA VI could follow for years to come.

Management expects record net bookings, its measure of sales during a period from both digital downloads and physical products, of $8.0 billion to $8.2 billion in fiscal 2027. Zelnick has said record bookings will establish a new baseline for the business and put it on a path of enhanced profitability.

But Take-Two is also trying to convert that higher revenue into higher margins. It delivered $367 million in operating cash flow over the last year, but management's guidance calls for more than $1 billion in fiscal 2027.

Recurring spending helps margins and cash flow by extending the life of games that Take-Two has already paid to create and publish. Management has also executed a cost-cutting program to lift profits over the last few years. CFO Lainie Goldstein said the company aims to enhance its margin profile over time.

This fiscal year, recurrent spending is expected to be in line with fiscal 2026 levels, at about 65% of net bookings, as the initial wave of GTA VI sales shifts the revenue mix. Once those initial sales fade, the long tail of in-game spending should again make up a larger share of the business, which could lift margins, earnings, and cash flow. As Goldstein put it, GTA VI's launch "is just the beginning."

Take-Two shares closed at $216.61 on Nov. 18, up about 355% over the past 10 years. But they're down 9% over the past year and 19% below their July 7 high.

At about 6.3 times sales and 31 times analysts' fiscal 2027 earnings estimates, the stock is fairly valued for a video game stock. Analysts expect earnings to grow about 26% a year over the long term. That's solid growth that could justify the stock's valuation.

I believe it's worth buying a starter position in Take-Two stock today. GTA VI is a major catalyst, and the GTA V track record suggests it could keep generating revenue for years.

But you might want to wait for Take-Two's official holiday sales report before taking your position. That way, you avoid any negative surprises that might cause a sell-off in the stock, such as initial sales that don't quite meet the high expectations implied by the stock's premium.
