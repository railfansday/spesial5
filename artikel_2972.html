Qualcomm vs. Sandisk: Which Technology Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Sandisk's revenue surged over 270%, driven by demand for cloud memory, while Qualcomm is branching into automotive and satellite connectivity. Which chip stock holds up better?

By Brendan Coffey – Updated Nov 25, 2026 at 4:00PM EST

As mobile markets mature and artificial intelligence expands, investors are looking for the next big chip winner. Is Qualcomm (QCOM +2.24%) or Sandisk (SNDK -5.57%) the better choice for your 2027 portfolio?

Qualcomm provides the wireless connectivity and processors that power modern smartphones and connected vehicles. Sandisk specializes in the flash storage and memory solutions required for high-speed data processing in cloud environments. Both companies are essential to the global technology infrastructure, but they offer vastly different financial profiles for investors to consider.

As one of the most prominent semiconductor stocks, Qualcomm designs the high-performance computing and connectivity platforms found in premium handsets and industrial devices. The company relies on a small group of mobile handset customers, including Apple Inc. (AAPL +0.43%) and Samsung, for a significant portion of its revenue. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business, particularly as Apple develops its own internal modem technology. To offset this, Qualcomm is expanding into automotive and data center markets while partnering with Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +1.30%) to provide radio platforms for the Starlink Mobile 5G network.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 27, 2026, revenue reached about $43.1 billion, representing a decrease of approximately 2.6% compared to the previous twelve-month period. Despite the slight revenue dip, the company reported a net income of roughly $13.6 billion for that time frame. Net margin for the year was about 32%, showing the company's ability to maintain high profitability even during a period of slowing top-line growth.

The company maintains a solid balance sheet, with a debt-to-equity ratio of approximately 0.5x as of its September 2026 balance sheet. This ratio compares total debt to shareholder equity, indicating the company maintains a manageable level of borrowing. Its current ratio, which measures the ability to cover short-term debts with short-term assets, stands at nearly 1.9x. For the fiscal year ended Sept. 27, 2026, free cash flow was nearly $8.7 billion. Note that stock-based compensation (SBC) represented roughly 31% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Sandisk operates as a major force in the flash memory industry, serving the massive data center, PC, and mobile device markets. The company provides the hardware necessary for storing and moving the vast amounts of data used by modern artificial intelligence systems. Unlike many chip peers, its sales are highly diverse, with no single customer accounting for more than 10% of revenue in the most recent fiscal year. This broad base of OEM and channel customers helps reduce the impact of any single partnership ending.

In the 12 months ended Oct. 2, 2026, revenue reached about $29 billion, a significant growth of approximately 272% over the prior year. This rapid expansion was accompanied by a net income of roughly $19.4 billion. The surge in sales and earnings reflects the intense demand for high-capacity memory solutions in the cloud computing sector. The company achieved a net margin of nearly 67% during this period, highlighting the strong demand for its memory technology.

As of July 2026, the company reported a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.0x, indicating that total liabilities do not exceed shareholder equity and its reliance on debt is minimal. Its current ratio was nearly 2.3x, suggesting a comfortable cushion for meeting its short-term financial obligations. In the fiscal year ended July 3, 2026, free cash flow reached nearly $11.5 billion. This robust cash generation allows the company to reinvest heavily in manufacturing facilities and next-generation storage research.

Qualcomm faces significant risks from its revenue concentration among a small number of handset manufacturers. The company expects Apple will transition to its own internally developed modems in the near future, which could impact sales. Furthermore, heavy exposure to the Chinese market makes the company vulnerable to shifting trade policies and national security regulations. Its reliance on a fabless model also means it depends on third-party foundries like Taiwan Semiconductor and Samsung for leading-edge production capacity.

Sandisk carries operational risks due to its heavy reliance on joint ventures with Kioxia for memory production. Its manufacturing facilities are geographically concentrated in Japan and Malaysia, leaving the supply chain open to disruption from natural disasters or geopolitical conflict. The memory industry is also notoriously cyclical, with intense competition from Nvidia Corp. (NVDA -3.91%), Western Digital (WDC -0.55%), and Micron Technology (MU -4.53%). The company must also manage the complexities associated with its separation from Western Digital, including potential tax liabilities.

Sandisk appears significantly cheaper based on future earnings estimates, while Qualcomm maintains a slightly higher premium for its diversified connectivity portfolio.

Qualcomm's mobile phone business has been hammered this year, as weak Chinese demand and AI-driven memory shortages have caused handset-derived sales to fall below prior-year levels. Yet the AI boom has an upside for Qualcomm.

At the company's June 2026 investor day, Qualcomm raised its non-handset revenue target to more than $40 billion by 2029, nearly double the prior target of $22 billion. Qualcomm is reportedly partnering with OpenAI to develop smartphone processors with advanced AI capabilities. Analysts see this partnership as a positive for Qualcomm since it would enable advanced agentic AI on smartphone platforms. It would also diminish the business risk of Apple developing its own chips and dropping Qualcomm as a supplier.

Meanwhile, Sandisk shares have undergone remarkable appreciation since its spin-off from Western Digital created the firm as a stand-alone entity in February 2025. Shares went from $49 to more than $2,300 by the middle of 2026. Shares have since undergone an 8-for-1 split and seen a significant retracement of gains.

But the share strength shows just how strong the flash memory market has been for Sandisk. During periods of tight supply and strong market pricing, the company's profit margins can be robust, as they have been during this AI-driven market. Historically, though, high memory prices and low supply are followed by periods of high supply and low prices, which are a risk to Sandisk investors in the long run.

Still, the growth is impressive. Revenue should get near $50 billion 2027, more than double 2026, with Wall Street seeing more than $60 billion in sales in 2028. Net income should triple in 2027, to $34.9 billion, and grow more modestly in 2028.

The choice here is simply: Sandisk is booming and comes at a bargain-basement valuation while Qualcomm's expected improvement is still speculative at the moment. There is risk to Sandisk that one may be buying at the peak of the memory cycle, but it's clearly the better bet for 2027.
