Lumentum CEO defends Marvell deal after stock plunge: 'We've got work to do' with shareholders

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Lumentum CEO Michael Hurlston defended the company's planned merger with chipmaker Marvell Technology on Thursday, acknowledging that the deal has received a disappointing reception.

"Their numbers are phenomenal. Our numbers are phenomenal, and we feel the combination is even more powerful," Hurlston told CNBC's Jim Cramer on "Mad Money." Nevertheless, Hurlston said, "Shareholders are not happy ... It's been a tough couple of weeks."

Shares of Marvell and Lumentum have tumbled 45% and 42%, respectively, since the close on Oct. 28. That night, Bloomberg News reported Marvell was nearing a megadeal to buy Lumentum, sending shockwaves through the market and beginning the wild ride that both stocks have been on. The tie-up was officially announced on the morning of Nov. 2 with the deal expected to close in the first half of 2028. At the time, the all-stock deal implied an enterprise value of $115 billion for Lumentum.

Beginning on Oct. 29, Marvell shares experienced three straight days of double-digit percentage declines. In total, they've advanced in just three sessions since the Oct. 28 close. For its part, Lumentum rose modestly on Oct. 29 following the initial M&A rumor. Then the selling pressure arrived, as investors questioned the merits of the deal. The stock has been green in five sessions since the Bloomberg report.

The merger would combine Marvell's semiconductor and networking technology with Lumentum's optical components as AI data centers increasingly shift from copper wiring to fiber-optic connections to handle faster data transmission. Hurlston said that transition, rather than simply the construction of more data centers, will be a major growth driver for the planned combined entity.

"We've got work to do with our shareholders," Hurlston said, though he sees catalysts on the horizon that could help change their minds. He pointed to Marvell's earnings report scheduled for Dec. 1 and the upcoming release of a securities filing, known as an S-4, required as part of the merger process.

"As people see [Marvell CEO Matt Murphy's] earnings he's going to announce in early December, we've got the S-4 coming out that's going to describe how both businesses are performing, we've got our earnings … I think those things together will give the shareholders confidence that, look, there's nothing wrong here. I mean, these businesses are great, and they're even better together," said Hurlston, who has led Lumentum since February 2025.

To be sure, the dramatic decline in both Marvell and Lumentum shares isn't entirely due to the investor displeasure with the planned transaction. In recent weeks, the market has soured on the AI trade more broadly, amid questions about the sustainability of the AI buildout due to higher borrowing costs and political backlash on data center construction, which was exemplified by the midterm election results. Though Hurlston acknowledged these other forces affecting the stocks and leading to compressed valuation multiples, he emphasized the work that he needs to do to win shareholder support.

Despite concerns about the broader AI buildout, Hurlston said Lumentum has seen no slowdown in demand. The company reported better-than-expected quarterly results on the day the merger was announced, with revenue more than doubling from a year earlier.

"I think we're going to get that multiple back once we can show our numbers and show how strong the two businesses are doing," he said.
