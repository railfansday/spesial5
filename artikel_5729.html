What Other Toyota Models Deserve The GRMN Treatment?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Toyota's Corolla hatchback is already a pretty lovely car, but I adored driving the all-wheel drive turbocharged Corolla GR. You can imagine how great it must be with more power and less weight, as found in the GRMN. That initialism isn't just the stock ticker symbol for Garmin, it's an extremely fancy $64,000 Toyota hot hatch, and it allegedly stands for Gazoo Racing Masters of the Nürburgring. Maybe the hottest hatch of all time? I haven't yet had the pleasure of slipping behind the wheel, but my delightful (and extremely tall) colleague Logan absolutely loved their time driving it, and recommended buying one.

The Corolla isn't the first GRMN product in the world, as that honor belongs to the Yaris GRMN which isn't sold here. That said, this definitely got me thinking about what else Toyota could build if it wanted to get super serious about performance cars. Don't get me wrong, I think Toyota is building more cars for enthusiasts today than any other automaker, and there are more in the pipeline, but what's the harm in offering pumped up versions?

Given my druthers, I'd absolutely love to see an amped up Prius GRMN. The all-wheel drive Prius is already quite quick and powerful, given its economy car bent. The current generation is also quite a fetching design. I want something economical that looks good, but when I put my foot down, I want something a little quicker than the current 7-ish second 0-60 time. The AWD Prius currently uses a 150-horsepower 2-liter four-cylinder engine mated to a 111 horsepower electric motor at the front, plus a tiny 40 horsepower electric motor at the rear axle. What if Toyota wanted to get serious about acceleration and ditched the gas motor for the GRMN Corolla's 300-pony 1.6-liter three-cylinder instead? And maybe that puny rear motor could be supplanted by the 165 horse electric motor found in the base model bZ electric. Keep the same electric motor that the current Prius has in the front, you're still looking to at least double the power output from standard Prius to theoretical GRMN Prius. I like it, how about you?

Toyota makes a whole lot of different cars. I want to hear what your favorite hypothetical GRMN silk purse from a base model sow's ear might be. Pick a model that Toyota currently makes and give us your idea for how it could be GRMN-ified. Given that the GRMN Corolla is a two-seater with the rear seats stripped out, you can kind of go hog wild with it. Do you want to see a Sienna with aggressive track-focused aerodynamic bodywork? How about a Toyota Sequoia V8 stuffed under the hood of a GRMN-86?

This is your dream workshop, and you're playing with whatever toys are already in the Toyota (or Lexus) arsenal. Because GRMN is a track-focused operation, these creations should also be intended with the dual purpose of fun driving dynamics and fast lap times. Try to keep it to a parts bin special, if you please, because there's no good game in the world without a few rules to it. Maybe you want to go hog wild with it and bring the Lexus LFA's V10 out of retirement to shove under the hood of a track-oriented Tundra sport truck? Now that would be a whole lot of street style. It's Saturday, you don't have to leave dream world just yet, so you have the entire Toyota research and development team at your beck and call, what wild track-oriented automotive monstrosity would you make them create?

I don't know about you, but I'm still thinking about that time attack bZ that Toyota brought to SEMA last year. That could make a pretty cool GRMN EV.

Let's chat about it. Sound off in the comments below with your thoughts on engine and drivetrain swaps, and we'll discuss it. Then, I'll pick a few of my favorite responses to recap for everyone else next week.
