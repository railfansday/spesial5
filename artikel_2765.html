Game Preview: Knicks at Pacers, November 13, 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

There are sports movies, there are even some good sports movies. And then there is Hoosiers.

As a basketball romantic you are definitely not flipping the channel when old ball coach Norman Dale a.k.a. Gene Hackman drives his 1951 Chevy Styleline Deluxe through the backwoods of Indiana all the way to the little town of Hickory, where he hopes to find a new life, new love and redemption. You let yourself be mesmerized by Angelo Pizzo's and David Anspaugh's timeless fairytale, by the very essence of "Hoosier Hysteria," the beautiful yet tragic tribulations of high school sports, the rough, simple charm of small town America. And you enjoy every second of it, despite a rather predictable underdog story unfolding in front of your eyes that Hollywood has repeated many times over.

The current Indiana Pacers would kill for some of that predictability, the kind they expected going into this season. After a surprising Finals berth in 2025, their following year was doomed by Tyrese Haliburton's ruptured Achilles. They punted on the next campaign and thought, with their point guard's return, everything would return normal; that the half-court offense would run smooth as a "picket fence;" and that the jumpers would automatically fall like the ones by Jimmy Chitwood. But Haliburton (unfortunately) isn't as quiet nor as down to earth as Mr. Chitwood, nor is an NBA season comparable to a Hollywood movie. It's a lot more complicated than that.

The Pacers are finding that out the hard way. It only took testy Tyrese a handful of games until he got injured again (it's a hamstring now) and his team sorely misses the All Star as their 4-8 record puts them in the lower region of the Eastern Conference. On Friday against the New York Knicks, Haliburton will also be reduced to trolling from the sidelines, and he won't be able to right the wrongs that have plagued his squad so far this season. It's a huge advantage for Mike Brown's men as Haliburton is the straw that stirs the Apple Cider for Indiana. Most of their strategy revolves around his talent, his scoring and his distribution.

But it's not like the Pacers are just struggling out of the gate because Hali has only played four games. Even with him they lost to Milwaukee, barely got by New Orleans and were annihilated by Dallas. Indiana can't shoot threes (just over 33 percent), despite a traditionally fast pace their offense isn't even close to functioning well (20th in offensive rating), and their D is nothing to write home about either (15th in the league). The Pacers are an atrocious rebounding team (29th in rebounding percentage) and they struggle mightily to hit their free throws.

Do they do anything well? They do, in fact, take good care of the ball and have a one of the best assist-to-turnover-ratios in the entire Association. Individually Pascal Siakam is enjoying a strong start to the season (22.7 points) and Andrew Nembhard has come firing out of the gates as well (17.1 points, 42.9 percent from downtown). A lot of guys are still left from that Finals team two years ago, so in theory they should know what it takes. And Rick Carlisle is still a tremendously respected coach, at least when he isn't whining like a five year old who didn't get his wish at Toys-R-Us.

The skipper really has his work cut out for him early in the year. New center Ivica Zubac, a classic, plodding back-to-basket big man with considerable post skills, hasn't really found his place in an adjusted Pacers system that once was predicated on playing five-out at all costs. Continuity has also been a big issue as Carlisle has already started 11 different players, at times making it seem like the Pacers are still on their tanking ways from last year. They obviously aren't and it should be noted that slow starts are nothing new for Indiana under Carlisle. Consider yourself warned.

With Friday's game being another Cup contest you can bet that the Pacers will be highly motivated and our beloved Knicks better be ready for a hostile environment. Keep calm if Haliburton tries some stupid sideline voodoo as he certainly will when he senses a camera being on him. Feed Karl-Anthony Towns in a favorable matchup for him, let Brunson do his thing against Indiana's supposed Brunson stoppers who always seem to just limit him to 35. Make use of your more talented depth, outshoot the struggling shooting team and control the tempo. Just show that you are the better team – which the Knicks, even with their current ups and downs, are at this point in the season.

And please leave the Indiana underdog stories where they belong and where they are actually fun. Leave them to Hoosiers.
