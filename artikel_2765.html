Turner reignites trade discourse after Raptors lose to Bucks

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The Toronto Raptors fell to the Milwaukee Bucks 116-111 on Monday.

After a potentially season-defining win in front of a national audience against the Detroit Pistons, this loss served as quite the momentum-killer, particularly against an incredibly weak Bucks squad.

Record aside, Raptors head coach Darko Rajakovic believes the Bucks are better than their current place in the Eastern Conference

"I think they're a good team. Tyler Herro just scored 50 points the other night," said Rajakovic. "They lost on the last possession to Philly. They're an NBA team."

Here are four more takeaways from the game:

Adding Myles Turner is certainly a tired take among Raptors fans, but with the Bucks centre in town, it was an unofficial audition. If anything, it was an opportunity to erase his zero points (on seven shot attempts) and seven-rebound performance against Toronto on Nov. 6.

Turner made his presence felt right from the opening tip, scoring Milwaukee's first nine points.

By default, it also turned into a duel between Turner and Jakob Poeltl. In their meeting earlier this month, it was Poeltl who dominated, finishing with 19 points, 11 rebounds, three steals and one block. This time, Turner more than evened the score, recording 18 points and 10 rebounds by halftime.

Turner ultimately finished with 27 points and 11 rebounds on 75 per cent shooting. He also knocked down four triples on five attempts.

Beyond the box score, there was a litany of fodder for basketball pundits online. After Turner knocked down a signature three from the top of the arc, Poeltl was called for an illegal screen on the ensuing possession. Late in the half, Poeltl hesitated to take a clean look. He then drove to the rim before getting emphatically blocked by Turner. Late in the game, Turner knocked down a three on a pick-and-pop, and then blocked Poeltl again on the next possession. Going back up the court, Turner drove to the basket and drew a foul on Poeltl, sending the Raptors centre to the bench with his sixth personal foul.

As a note, Turner also fouled out moments later.

The Raptors next play the Bucks on Feb. 6, which is scheduled six days before the NBA's trade deadline. That matchup could serve as the tipping point for a potential trade. Regardless, Turner looked like an intriguing piece to complement Scottie Barnes and Kawhi Leonard, especially when it comes to spacing.

Without Leonard, the Raptors' offence must consist of a healthy dose of Barnes, supported by a balanced attack from the tertiary options. Against the Bucks, at least for most of the game, the offence was exactly that. Less than six minutes into the contest, all five starters had contributed to the Raptors' first 18 points.

"This team, the way we're playing right now, we need every single player to contribute," said Rajakovic.

In the second half, Barnes came out with an elevated aggression level. The All-Star took Toronto's first five field goal attempts to score seven points. On the team's sixth shot attempt, it was Barnes who assisted Poeltl's first basket of the half.

Barnes finished with 31 points, five rebounds, and eight assists. In his sixth year in the league, Barnes is consistently hitting a level where he's arguably the best player on the court, and he's doing so without the free-throw volume. Barnes went 5-for-6 from the charity stripe.

Barnes' 31 points also moved him into seventh for all-time Raptors scorers, passing headband magician Morris Peterson. He now sets his sights on Il Mago himself, Andrea Bargnani.

Ja'Kobe Walter has been the main beneficiary of Leonard's absence. Walter has already made 14 starts after a career-high 19 appearances in the starting lineup in 2025-26. Walter finished with 15 points while shooting 5-for-12 from the field. After dropping 25 points against the New York Knicks (Nov. 17) and 18 points vs. the Washington Wizards (Nov. 18), Walter had a forgettable 2-for-11 shooting night against the Detroit Pistons on Nov. 20.

"We're learning, we're getting better, we're improving but we're not consistent," said Rajakovic. "That's what we have to find."

An encouraging aspect of Walter's development has been his willingness to get on the glass. He finished with six rebounds, matching a total he's hit three other times this season.

On defence, he's been reliable next to Barnes. There were a few instances where he poked a ball loose as the point-of-attack defender, forcing an opposing guard to backtrack past halfcourt. Walter recorded one steal and one block. It was Walter's fourth consecutive game with at least one takeaway

Despite this, Herro finished with a game-high 32 points. Walter and the rest of the defence had contained Herro in the first half, but the Bucks guard ultimately found his rhythm.

"We held him to three points, so we're definitely capable," said Walter. "We definitely let him get comfortable, we let him get to what he does. When a good player gets confidence, it's hard to stop them."

"We need to get more stops and rebound the ball and really dominate on the defensive end so we have some leeway on the offensive side," said Rajakovic.
