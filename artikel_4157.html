10 Games That Made Me Weirdly Passionate About Jobs I'd Never Want

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Contributor at DualShockers, former Esports Insider DACH content lead, former Escapist Magazine Germany content lead, software engineering background.

Some of my favourite games are, when you really think about it, just jobs. I have pressure-washed patios, stamped passports, hauled cargo across half of Europe and sold second-hand books out of a trailer, all of it voluntarily and in my free time. And boy, did I take every single one of them seriously!

The Best Cozy Games That You Need to Play in Fall 2026 feature autumnal settings and the distinctive vibes of the season.

The funny thing is that I wouldn't actually want any of these jobs. Some of them look like far too much hard work, others require a kind of patience I most definitely do not possess, and with one or two, I'm fairly certain I'd end up in prison. That is probably exactly what makes them so enjoyable from the safety of my sofa, so let's take a look at the ten games that made me weirdly passionate about jobs I'd never want.

I had sunk many hours into both PowerWash Simulator games long before I ever held an actual pressure washer. That finally changed earlier this year, and I'm pleased to report that watching grime disappear is just as satisfying in real life as it is on screen. On a warm summer's day, it's even quite fun.

Still, there's a lot to be said for staying warm, dry and cozied up on the sofa whilst doing it. The game has an almost meditative quality that I couldn't quite find with a real hose in my hands, and that's no accident. Lead designer Nick McCarthy has said that the team left out certain real-world inconveniences on purpose, and I'm grateful for every single one of them.

FuturLab released the sequel on 23 October 2025, bringing 38 new jobs to Muckingham, online co-op for up to four players and new tools like an abseiling rig and a cherry picker. There are also two kittens named Bubbles and Squeak, which was frankly all the convincing I needed. As an actual career, though, it looks like an awful lot of work, so I'll stick to the version without the wet socks.

Papers, Please's premise is incredibly dark and difficult: you are manning a booth at the border of the fictional Eastern Bloc country of Arstotzka. It's 1982, and as a border inspector, it is your responsibility to check passports, permits and anything else that gets slid across your desk. Make a mistake and you get a wage deduction. Your family, meanwhile, desperately needs that money. The game is, however, also tense and brilliant, and it also taught me that I would be entirely unsuited for the job.

Quite frankly, I'm just not enough of a bootlicker for it. I'm far too nice, and my first instinct is to wave everybody through, which is not something Papers, Please looks kindly upon. Being generous with your stamp sooner or later gets you killed by your own government, and it's hard to build a career on that.

Note: Lucas Pope worked on the Uncharted series at Naughty Dog before making a game that's almost entirely about paperwork.

By its tenth anniversary in 2023, the game had sold over five million copies, so there are clearly plenty of people out there who are much better at saying no than I am. I have a lot of respect for them. I'd just rather not meet them at passport control.

Running a late-night coffee shop in an alternative version of Seattle sounds like a dream. Even more so when you consider that your visitors will consist of elves, orcs and mermaids that are living alongside humans. Coffee is the great connector here, since the need for a warm drink and someone to talk to is universal across every race. Toge Productions chose Seattle because Starbucks first opened there, and also because the rain sets the mood so well. It's easily one of the coziest games I own.

Parts of this job would actually suit me extremely well: I enjoy good banter, I'm perfectly happy with small talk, and I seem to have the kind of approachable face that makes strangers trauma dump on me far more often than you'd expect. If being a barista was only about listening, I'd be employee of the month.

Unfortunately, you also have to make the coffee, and Coffee Talk is the game that showed me just how hilariously bad I am at latte art. Nothing I produce looks like it was made on purpose. So as much as I love the idea, I could never run a coffee shop of my own.

Note: Creator Mohammad Fahmi sadly passed away in March 2022. He pitched the game as holding a warm cup of coffee by a window whilst it rains outside and lo-fi music plays in the background, which is exactly how it feels.

If you're looking for relaxing experiences with guaranteed quality, I recommend these ten little-known cozy games from famous developers.

Stardew Valley is meant to be about running a cute little farm with cute little animals on it. The way I play it, with spreadsheets at the ready and more wiki tabs open than I'd like to admit, has very little to do with that. My approach is a lot closer to a general planning a war than a farmer enjoying the countryside.

The forensic intensity I bring to this game does scare me a little. Every day is planned, every crop has to earn its place, and my poor character hasn't had a quiet afternoon in years. If I ran a real farm like that, I'd work myself into a burnout before the first harvest.

Note: Eric Barone, better known as ConcernedApe, made Stardew Valley on his own over roughly four years.

The game turned ten on 26 February 2026 and has sold around 50 million copies. I'd love to know how many of those players are peacefully watering their parsnips and enjoying the sunset, and how many are like me. I have my suspicions.

Something happens to me whenever I open Euro Truck Simulator 2. Within minutes, I turn into a 60-year-old trucker who has been doing this for decades, completely locked in, with nothing on my mind except my cargo and the motorway ahead. I can't explain it, but it awakens something in me.

There are two wolves inside of me: One is a sweet softie, and the other one is a fourth-generation trucker that graduated from the school of hard knocks, living and breathing 18-wheelers. Sadly for the second wolf, I'm really, really bad at parking, and truth be told, I'm no better at it in the game. That feels like a bit of a dealbreaker for a job that ends with reversing a trailer into a loading bay.

SCS Software first released the game at retail in autumn 2012, before it arrived on Steam on 16 January 2013. By its tenth birthday, it had sold over 13 million copies, and players had bought DLC more than 80 million times. Clearly, I'm not the only one with a trucker wolf, so at least I'm in good company.

Exterminauts is an upcoming co-op shooter from Behaviour Interactive, the studio behind Dead by Daylight, and it hands you several jobs at once. As part of a crew of three, you clock in, take on MegaCorp contracts, repair what's broken and exterminate alien pests along the way. It was revealed at gamescom Opening Night Live in August 2026 and is planned for 2027.

Anyone who has watched me deal with a woodlouse or a spider knows that pest control isn't my calling. I'm quite a caring person, so I had a lot of fun with the healing side of things. I've also discovered that I'm alarmingly quick to shoot my own teammates, sometimes because they were being annoying, and sometimes entirely by accident.

Note: Friendly fire is on in Exterminauts, and swinging one of the bigger weapons around carelessly is enough to take a teammate down.

A colleague who keeps taking out the rest of the crew isn't much use to anyone, no matter how good her healing is. I can't imagine it going down well in a performance review, either. It's better for everyone if I stay off the payroll.

Subnautica is one of my favourite games of all time, which means I approach the deep ocean with a healthy dose of suspicion. Dave the Diver has done nothing but confirm that this is the correct attitude. You spend your days diving into the Blue Hole to catch fish, and the further down you go, the more things would like to eat you instead.

Once the sun sets, the second job begins, and you help chef Bancho run the sushi restaurant, serving customers and keeping the drinks coming. All of that after a full day of nearly being eaten by sharks! I’d be a horrible sushi tester on the basis of not liking fish, and I could also never be the waiter on the basis of lacking a good sense of balance.

Note: The restaurant is the idea of Dave's friend Cobra, a former arms dealer who now handles the business side of things. Naturally.

By September 2026, Dave the Diver had sold more than 10 million copies across all platforms, and I completely understand why. It's a wonderful game. It's also the best argument I've ever seen for staying on dry land and ordering takeaway.

A review of over the hill, a cozy exploration-focused driving game.

Kerbal Space Program puts you in charge of a space program run by the Kerbals, little green aliens who are far braver than they have any right to be. You build rockets, launch them and watch most of them explode. I was oddly passionate about all of it, right up until the moment I finally made it into orbit.

I'm very aware that the game doesn't end there. It had taken me so long to reach that one milestone, though, that my passion fizzled out on the spot, and I decided that my work here was done. That's quite unusual for me, as I'm normally someone who needs to tick off every last goal.

A real head of a space program with that attitude wouldn't bode well for anyone. NASA worked with developer Squad on an asteroid mission update in 2014, and ESA followed with missions of its own in 2020, so the people who do this for a living are clearly made of sterner stuff than I am. I have a lot of respect for them, and I'm more than happy to watch from the ground.

Barbie Rewind was developed by Digital Eclipse, published by Atari and released on 12 November 2026. It is unfathomably fun. You get to decorate the DreamHouse with over 250 pieces of furniture and decor inspired by real Barbie playsets, from a classic pink vanity to a '90s inflatable chair. There are also 16 classic Barbie games from 1991 to 2007 included, one of which, Barbie: Vacation Adventure, had never been released before.

Truth be told, I think I'd make a good interior designer, and I think I'd enjoy it, too. In Barbie Rewind, you get to design to your heart's content, and nobody questions your choices. Ken, Teresa and Christie do pop in to ask for help, but none of them will ever tell you that your sofa is ugly.

Real clients are a different story. Even when something looks questionable at best, you have to go with the flow and keep them happy, and whilst you'd hope they trust your design decisions, there's absolutely no guarantee that they will. I don't think I have the temperament for that, and I'm fairly sure someone would end up wounded.

In Tiny Bookshop, you leave everything behind to sell second-hand books out of a little trailer in the seaside town of Bookstonbury-by-the-Sea. Each morning, you pick a spot, stock your shelves and wait for the locals to wander in. Many of the books are real ones, which I love.

I've worked in retail before, I'm quite a good salesperson, and I enjoy helping people find what they're looking for. The customers in Tiny Bookshop put that to the test, because a fair few of them behave as if it's their very first day on Earth. Not all of them, mind you, but enough of them describe the book they want so vaguely that it borders on nonsensical.

On top of that, everyone in town seems to need a favour. Community is important, of course, but I'm trying to run a bookshop here, and I'd quite like to be left alone to do it! Over half a million players have fallen in love with Tiny Bookshop, and it won three German Developer Awards in 2025, so this one might just be a me problem. I'd still end up scaring all of my customers away.
