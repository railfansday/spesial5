Climate change won’t just bring more extreme weather. It will shorten the seasons and change the activities many destinations are built on. Operators should be planning now for shoulder seasons and cooler-hour itineraries, not waiting for the next heat wave.

Climate change is coming for the tourist season.

More than a third of destinations could see conditions for sightseeing, swimming and hiking worsen by 2050, according to a new report, and some of travel’s biggest beach regions could lose months of good weather.

The report, by NGO Travel Forward, risk modeling firm Risklayer, and the Dutch public-private partnership Centre of Expertise Leisure, Tourism and Hospitality (CELTH), models climate hazards and conditions for common tourist activities across 2,588 destinations in 263 countries and territories.

Called Destination Climate RiskScan, it found that 36% of destinations would be “squeezed” by 2050 under a moderate emissions scenario, facing more extreme weather and a climate less suited to common tourism activities. That  percentage soars to 62% by 2050 under a high emission scenario.

A new report modeling climate conditions across 2,588 destinations in 263 countries finds that 36% of them could be "squeezed" by 2050 under a moderate emissions scenario — facing more climate hazards alongside worse conditions for sightseeing, swimming, hiking, and camping — with that figure rising to 62% under high emissions. The Destination Climate RiskScan, produced by NGO Travel Forward, risk modeling firm Risklayer, and Dutch partnership CELTH, scores hazard exposure and activity suitability on a 0–100 index. The steepest declines land in the hottest regions: Caribbean hiking and camping could fall 53 points by 2080 under high emissions, Southeast Asian swimming 48 points, and Mediterranean hiking and camping 48 points. Southeast Asia's typical beach and swimming season could shrink from eight months in 2010 to three by 2080, and the Caribbean's from 10 to six. Northern Europe and the Nordics are projected to become more comfortable for every activity assessed except skiing, though the ski model excluded artificial snowmaking. The authors stress these are measures of climatic suitability, not forecasts of visitor demand or commercial viability. The report lands as policymakers meet at COP31 in Antalya, Türkiye, where tourism is a thematic focus.
