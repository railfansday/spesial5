Sam Altman's OpenAI Took a $30 Billion Investment From Nvidia Earlier in 2026. Here's Why That Deal Matters for Both Companies' Investors.

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Circular financing in the AI industry has increasingly come under the microscope.

By Keith Noonan – Nov 24, 2026 at 11:33AM EST

Earlier this year, OpenAI received a $30 billion investment from Nvidia (NVDA -8.73%). While OpenAI has yet to go public, it stands as one of Nvidia's largest investment positions -- and the two companies have an important relationship.

OpenAI's frontier artificial intelligence (AI) model ChatGPT and other technologies rely on Nvidia's advanced graphics processing units (GPUs) as the foundational hardware that enables the incredible things they do. On the other hand, CEO Sam Altman's company is one of Nvidia's most important partners, accounting for both direct GPU purchases and compute contracts with third parties that have built data centers around Nvidia's GPUs.

With recent shake-ups and uncertainties in the AI space in mind, here's what the big investment deal means for Nvidia shareholders and prospective OpenAI investors.

Nvidia has taken to investing directly in some leading players in the AI industry in recent years. In some cases, the investments translate into meaningful sales and infrastructure boosts for the processing leader. In other words, Nvidia invests a large amount in a company -- and that company can then use that capital to purchase Nvidia's hardware.

While this circular financing has attracted criticism from high-profile investors, including Michael Burry and other analysts, it has also helped facilitate growth in the AI industry. On the other hand, it looks like Nvidia will be pulling back, at least somewhat, in providing easy access to capital for its partners in the AI space.

Even before the resignation of longtime CEO Jensen Huang, the company was reportedly planning to shift its approach to funding companies that would be major purchasers of its chips. Following Huang's departure, reports suggest that the AI hardware leader will be far more selective in providing investment capital.

On balance, Nvidia's $30 billion investment in OpenAI has probably been a good thing for both companies and their shareholders thus far. With Nvidia being the leading provider of AI processing hardware and OpenAI being a leading provider of advanced artificial intelligence software, the two companies naturally have a somewhat symbiotic relationship.

Nvidia's investment helped fund OpenAI's technological advancement and market expansion, and much of that capital went directly toward purchasing Nvidia's high-end GPUs along with related hardware and services.

On the other hand, these kinds of investment and purchasing relationships look poised to continue attracting scrutiny. For Nvidia, this could mean further downward pressure on its share price and earnings multiple in the near term as investors assess the implications of new investigations and shifts in regulatory dynamics.

For OpenAI, recent controversies surrounding Nvidia and circular AI financing could cause the company to postpone its initial public offering until the outlook on these fronts becomes clearer.
