Thinking About Investing in Anthropic? Make This 1 Portfolio Move First

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

There's nothing wrong with buying Anthropic, as long as you understand and are prepared for the risks.

By Reuben Gregg Brewer – Nov 26, 2026 at 8:15PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Anthropic ›

Anthropic (ANT +1.08%) raised a record $93.5 billion when it held its initial public offering (IPO) in late November 2026. That instantly made it one of the world's largest companies, one of the largest technology companies, and one of the biggest direct plays on artificial intelligence (AI). If you have set your sights on buying the stock, you aren't alone. But before you jump aboard, make sure you make this one portfolio move (and this one mindset move that goes with it).

At this point, it seems like everyone agrees that artificial intelligence will change the world. So it is completely understandable that you would want to buy one of the biggest companies in the AI space after it goes public. However, you need to step back and think about the risks. And not the risks that AI may pose to humanity. The risks inherent in new technologies.

Going back to the last big technology, the internet changed the world, but not every internet company turned out to be a big winner. For example, Yahoo! was once an industry giant, but it is now just a shadow of its former self. But the fact that not all companies involved in a technological revolution survive is nothing new. There are only a handful of major automobile manufacturers around today because many more went bankrupt or got swallowed up by larger competitors. Trains went through the same cycle.

Look at any technological advance, and you'll see something similar. Lots of companies rush in, but only a small number survive and thrive. Anthropic is a leader today, but it may not be one of the survivors over the long term. Which is why the one thing you need to do before buying it is make sure your portfolio is diversified. If all you own is technology stocks, even if you own 100 of them, you are not diversified.

To be diversified, you should own stocks of as many as 50 companies across various sectors. No single investment should make up a disproportionate share of your portfolio. While there's no exact number here, 50 stocks suggests that each stock should be around 2% of your portfolio. From a sector allocation perspective, tech makes up around 37% of the S&P 500 index (^GSPC -0.25%), which should probably be the high end of your range.

The key is that you are trying to limit the risk that Anthropic flames out in a blaze of glory. Notably, the company's IPO prospectus highlights that it is losing money and will likely continue to do so. It already has substantial indebtedness. And it may need to raise even more capital in the future. That's a short list of the risks that don't even consider the actual technology the company produces. In fact, the stock has already been highly volatile, dipping below its IPO price.

Owning Anthropic is fine, but the best place for it is in a well-diversified portfolio, where the investment's inherent risks can be offset by other holdings. This brings up another important consideration: thinking long term. Anthropic is at the forefront of AI, a new technology that is still evolving. Nobody knows what that technology will look like in a year, let alone a decade. However, that's exactly why you should go in thinking that Anthropic will be a long-term holding. Trying to time its ups and downs will be just as difficult as timing any other investment, and the failure rate is likely to be high.

It is exciting to own new companies in new sectors, but it comes with risks. You have to understand those risks and make accommodations for them. Which is why the number one portfolio move here is diversification, backed by a long-term investment mentality. It is perfectly fine to buy Anthropic, but only if you go in fully understanding and preparing for the risks that it entails.
