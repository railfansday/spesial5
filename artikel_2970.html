NBA hot seat rankings for 6 head coaches who need to pick it up quick

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

NBA head coaches often seem like they are hired to get fired. Only seven head coaches league-wide started their current jobs before 2023, and just Erik Spoelstra (2008) and Steve Kerr (2014) were hired before the dawn of the 2020s. The coaching carousel never pauses for long, and that will likely be no exception this year.

Teams can no longer hide their failures behind the thin veil of tanking after new lottery reform rules went into effect for this season. When everyone is trying to win, some teams are destined to become disappointed, and that usually means a head coach could quickly be out of a job.

Six teams made head coaching changes for the 2026–27 season, and none of them are in danger of getting fired after just one month of play. Some of the others won't be so lucky. Here's an early look at the NBA hot seat landscape, and what these coaches need to do to buy themselves a little more time.

I'm a long-time believer in Fernandez's coaching ability. He made Cam Johnson look so good that the Nets got a first-round pick back for him from Denver, then he immediately helped Michael Porter Jr. enjoy the most productive year of his career. It's hard to hold Brooklyn's 46-118 record the last two years against the head coach when the organization was actively trying to tank. Unfortunately, the Nets didn't get any lottery luck, and now they are miserable again this season (3-11) in a year when they owe their first-round pick to the Houston Rockets. Fernandez signed a contract extension back in April, but it's at least a little unnerving that the Nets seem ready to fire his boss, Sean Marks, after 11 years at general manager. If and when Marks gets canned, a new GM will probably want to hire their own head coach, and that puts a lot of pressure on Fernandez to turn things around for the Nets this season — which is starting to feel like an impossible task.

The Jazz's grand tanking plan was finally supposed to be over when they landed the No. 2 overall pick and Darryn Peterson in the 2026 NBA Draft. So much for that: Utah has been decimated by injuries to their three best front court players — Lauri Markkanen, Jaren Jackson Jr., and Jusuf Nurkic — while stumbling their way to a brutal 3-13 start. Like Fernandez, Hardy's previous losing seasons were excused by the organization's long-term tanking plans, and it's possible the injuries will be enough to excuse this one. Hardy signed an extension through the 2030–31 season just over a year ago. I thought the Jazz had enough to sneak into the Western Conference playoffs this season, partially because I believed in Hardy's ability to coach up his group. If Utah stays one of the worst teams in basketball all year, though, there will probably be some degree of heat on his seat.

Credit the Nuggets for hanging tough so far this season when it feels like they're no longer at their best. Denver is only No. 15 in the league in net-rating, but they've out-performed that mark with a 8-5 record, including pulling out a tight win over the mighty Boston Celtics recently. I just wonder what a few bad weeks could do to David Adelman's job stability. There are only so many levers for Denver to pull with an older, expensive roster at this point, and the easier response to a downturn might be firing the head coach. Adelman has had the respect of a veteran team since being promoted from assistant ahead of the 2025 playoffs, but the Nuggets' pathetic showing in the playoffs last year — a six-game first-round loss to the Wolves — probably didn't do him any favors. I don't think Adelman is the problem in Denver, but the team will need to keep winning to avoid the temptation of trying out a new coach.

It feels like the Cavs are trending in a poor direction for Atkinson's job security. Cleveland went from 64 wins to 52 wins (and an extremely unconvincing conference finals berth) last season while making a win-now trade for James Harden. The Cavs are just .500 to start this year, and they still haven't answered most of the big questions that have dogged this team for years. Evan Mobley has never ascended to superstardom like I thought he might, and we still don't know if he can play the five full-time. Donovan Mitchell and James Harden will always be a liability defensively, and Harden's offense is finally slipping a bit at age-37. I love what the younger bench pieces have given the Cavs this year — Tyrese Proctor and rookie Meleek Thomas have been great — and it might be enough to save Atkinson for now. Still, with an owner who spends a ton of money above him, the Cavs could be looking for a new coach if they don't take off on a winning streak soon.

The Kings' decision to fire Mike Brown was asinine in the moment, and only looks worse a couple years later after Brown led the Knicks to an NBA championship. It's not all Doug Christie's fault that Sacramento has so quickly reverted to its ordinary form as the worst team in the league after Brown led the franchise to its only playoff appearance in 20 years, but he hasn't done much to help the situation either. The Kings are atrocious again, and it doesn't feel like a stretch to think they could challenge for one of the worst records in league history after a 1-13 start. Everything about this franchise needs to be burned save for Darius Acuff and some savvy moves on the margins of the roster, and ultimately Christie has no chance to survive. Hopefully the next Kings coach will be dealt a better hand, because I'm not sure anyone could have saved this roster.

The Wizards don't have any excuses this year. They landed the No. 1 overall pick and A.J. Dybantsa. They traded for veterans with All-NBA pedigrees in Anthony Davis and Trae Young. It feels like they have a thousand first-round picks on this roster with multiple years of experience. Brian Keefe has to win with this team after combining for only 35 victories over the last two years, and it's not happening yet. While the disastrous Trae Young contract isn't Keefe's fault, he also hasn't been able to unlock him yet. Former top-10 picks like Alex Sarr and Tre Johnson aren't showing much in the way of real progress. Unlike Fernandez and Hardy who have shown real flashes of being good coaches, Keefe just has nothing to really hang his hat on as he comes out of the tanking era with more losses. I don't think he's surviving the season unless the Wizards turn this thing around quickly, and I wouldn't be surprised if he doesn't even make it to Christmas.
