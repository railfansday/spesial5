Innodata's 2027 Outlook: AI Data Engineering Pivot Drives High-Value Lifecycle Contracts

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

This under-the-radar AI services stock boasts a Superscore of 82 from our Hidden Gems Primary database, part of The Motley Fool's Moneyball Database system. Here's why.

By Robert Izquierdo – Updated Nov 23, 2026 at 12:26AM EST

In the quiet town of Ridgefield Park, New Jersey, sits a company that has spent 35 years building the infrastructure for other people's technology. For most of its history, Innodata (INOD -8.91%) was a back-office service provider, the kind of company that handled mundane data tasks that big tech companies couldn't be bothered with.

Then came the generative artificial intelligence boom, and the company found itself sitting on a pile of human-annotated data that frontier model labs desperately needed. The stock, trading at $28.12 as of the week ending Nov. 20, 2026, has seen its price decline more than 40% over the past year, reflecting the market's ongoing reassessment of the company's growth trajectory and project-based revenue volatility.

Our proprietary Hidden Gems scoring system assigns Innodata an overall Superscore of 82 out of 100, placing it in the Strong category. The Superscore is an AI-powered score that evaluates a company's overall strength by combining financial performance, product market position, technological capabilities, leadership quality, and relative valuation. It represents the unification of all our scores into a single score for public companies, with five rating bands: Exceptional (90-100), Strong (75-89), Above Average (60-74), Average (40-59), and Cautious (0-39).

An 82 places Innodata in the Top ~7% of every company we score, ahead of roughly 93 out of every 100 companies we evaluate. This score is a single data-driven signal, and this research pairs the strengths with the risks so you can weigh both sides before doing more work.

Innodata maintains a high return on net tangible assets, which means the business earns outsized profits on a small base of hard physical equipment. This efficiency allows the company to turn each point of revenue growth into meaningful returns, which helps explain why investors might pay a premium for the shares despite the underlying execution risks.

Thanks to the massive AI tailwind, Innodata is poised to see sales growth in the coming years. That's because the organizations building AI models need the software to deliver results that are accurate, safe, and reliable. Innodata is a key enabler of that outcome.

Consequently, the enterprise is now building a business pipeline with the U.S. government, including work with the Department of War and Navy. Its quality of work is of such high caliber that when AI researchers switch companies, they call Innodata to help them at the new organization.

Revenue concentration among a subset of customers is a concern, but Innodata is working to diversify. As an example of its progress here, even though it lost a project with one of its traditionally largest customers, the company still raised its full-year 2026 revenue growth guidance to 42% or more year over year, up from 40%, as new projects come online.

Innodata is also benefiting from the rise of physical AI, where it is using motion capture of human movement to train humanoid robots how to move like people. Through these numerous AI-fueled opportunities, Innodata is well positioned to see its business grow in the years ahead.
