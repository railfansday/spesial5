Mamdani takes swipe at Trump administration's response to tech outages: 'Far from what many wanted to hear'

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani takes a swipe at the Trump administration following multiple technical issues that halted flights, email services and online banking platforms across the U.S.

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani took a swipe at the Trump administration Tuesday following days of tech outages sweeping the nation, saying that "the response that we heard from federal officials was far from what many wanted to hear."

Mamdani spoke after the U.S. experienced a slew of technology disruptions that halted flights, email services and online banking platforms across the country.

"What I will say to New Yorkers is both an understanding of their frustration, we are speaking about New Yorkers who had made plans for months, some of them years, about going on a trip with their family or coming home from a trip with their family. And frankly, the response that we heard from federal officials was far from what many wanted to hear," Mamdani said.

"And it did not leave many New Yorkers with any greater sense of confidence about our national capabilities in case of this occurring again," he continued. "And I'm looking forward to seeing Democratic majorities in the House and the Senate utilize that power to also ensure that we're delivering more answers at a national level, because what we were hearing from some of the secretaries that day was in no way confidence building."

The White House, when asked by Fox News Digital for comment, referred to remarks that Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton made to FOX Business on Tuesday.

"We do not see the indicia of a coordinated cyberattack — let me just be clear about that — and we do not see the indicia of state-sponsored activity here," Clayton said on "The Claman Countdown."

"And we can be fairly confident in that because we know those things exist and we have been looking for them in the past," Clayton continued.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said two routers from Federal Aviation Administration facilities in Texas and Arkansas are undergoing extensive testing as investigators examine whether faulty equipment or an installation problem triggered the nation's longest nationwide ground stop in 25 years this past Friday.

The shutdown occurred as contractor L3Harris was replacing aging copper wiring with fiber-optic technology in the FAA's communications system.

Duffy then said during an appearance on Fox News' "Hannity" Monday that he plans to modernize America’s aging air traffic control system and build new architecture that is "worthy of America." The secretary also said intelligence agencies found no evidence of a cyberattack.

The roughly five-hour ground stop affected more than 1.5 million passengers whose flights were scheduled to depart on Friday afternoon and evening. More than 12,000 flights were canceled that day, according to the FAA, while delays and cancellations continued through the weekend.

Department of Homeland Security (DHS) Secretary Markwayne Mullin also said Monday on "Hannity" that "the homeland is secure.

"Our banking system is secure and when something like this happens, we’re able to react and react fast," Mullin continued.

Mullin’s remarks came as customers Monday reported trouble accessing online services of major U.S. banking platforms, including Wells Fargo, Chase, U.S. Bank and Bank of America. United Airlines also faced a widespread system outage on Monday that briefly affected departing flights.

Mullin maintained that the incidents were not a result of cyberattacks and described the disruptions as "individually unrelated."

Fox News Digital's Nora Moriarty, Stephen Sorace and Landon Mion contributed to this report.
