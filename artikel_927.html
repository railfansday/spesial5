Why are Manchester United fans wearing green and gold?

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Green and gold balloons have been a regular feature at United's recent games

Recent Manchester United matches have seen some fans decked in gold and green scarves, kits and hats, often accompanied by hundreds of gold and green balloons.

A stark contrast from United's famous red tones, fans have been using the colours to protest against their club's current ownership.

Six fan groups encouraged members to bring green and gold 'balloons, scarves and shirts' to United's home game against Aston Villa on Sunday, with a similar display present at Old Trafford in their Champions League fixture against Roma, in a bid to increase the pressure on the club's owners.

The Glazer family took over Manchester United in 2005, with Sir Jim Ratcliffe buying a 27.7% stake in 2024.

So why have fans chosen these colours been chosen for ownership protests?

United's 1992-94 away kit paid tribute to their past colour schemes

Manchester United were originally founded as Newton Heath LYR FC in 1878.

The 'LYR' element stands for Lancashire and Yorkshire Railway, as it was the carriage and wagon department of the company that formed the team at the Newton Heath depot.

The team mostly played against other departments' rail companies, until 1880 when they recorded their first match against Bolton Wanderers reserves.

Urban legend suggests that LYR's company colours were green and gold, and so that is the reason the colours were incorporated into early kits, but United say there isn't definitive proof this was the reason.

By 1902, Newton Heath had become Manchester United, and the famous red shirts were adopted.

United's 1992-94 away kit made reference to these original colours and has become a Premier League design classic and retro favourite among fans.

By referencing these original kit colours, fans are hoping to signal their unhappiness at the direction of the club under the current owners.
