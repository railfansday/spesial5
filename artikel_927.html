Kim hangs on to lead at CME Group Tour Championship

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

A Lim Kim's lead was reduced by two bogeys down the stretch including one on the 18th at the CME Group Tour Championship where she remains the leader, one shot ahead of Jeeno Thitikul and Yuri Yoshida; live coverage continues on Sunday from 6pm on Sky Sports+

A Lim Kim held on to the lead, reduced to one shot, going into the final round of the CME Group Tour Championship.

Kim made a pair of bogeys down the stretch on Saturday and had to settle for a three-under 69 at the season-ending event with $4m at stake and Nelly Korda suddenly very much in the mix.

Kim holed a birdie putt off the fringe on the par-three 12th, added another birdie at the 13th and looked to be in control with a three-shot lead over two-time defending champion Jeeno Thitikul and Yuri Yoshida at a rain-soaked Tiburon Golf Club.

Korda, who can qualify for the LPGA Hall of Fame with her fifth win of the year, had a season-high nine birdies and pulled within two shots until a three-putt bogey from long range on the par-four 18th.

Korda, who started the third round six shots behind, shot 65 and wound up two back.

On the 18th Kim missed left and the ball rolled down the slope into the water. She did well to get up-and-down for bogey.

"When I was in the second shot... little switch in the wind. That's all," Kim said. "That's golf."

She was at 18 under but her lead was down to one over Thitikul (67) and Yoshida (66), who both had birdie chances on the 18th to share the lead.

It leads to a final day with 10 players separated by four shots.

"Trending every single day, which is what you kind of want going into Sunday," said Korda, who has posted rounds of 68, 67 and 65. "Hopefully I can pull it all together and have a great Sunday."

Thitikul takes a 27-hole streak of no bogeys into the final round, along with the form of having won the CME Group Tour Championship the last two years, giving her $8m in two events.

"I don't think I'm going to change any strategy. I feel like what I have been doing for the last three days is pretty good. It's on point. What I have to do more, it's just I have to make putts," Thitikul said.

"You need to make birdies as much as you can because everyone is hunting for it."

As much attention as there has been on Korda this week, Thitikul also had a chance to surpass Annika Sorenstam on the LPGA's career money list.

Yoshida also played bogey-free and had three straight birdies to close out the front nine.

Auston Kim made a late charge with three birdies in a four-hole stretch, only to hit her tee shot left and into the water on the 18th for a bogey and a 67, leaving her tied with Korda two behind.

Korda has not won since her signature US Women's Open victory at Riviera in early June. On Saturday she made nine birdies, one of them on the 16th when she was inches away from a hole-in-one.

Her only three bogeys this week have come on three-putts, two of them on Saturday.

"I found something, for sure," Korda said. "I mean, I still left maybe a couple shots out right. But overall, really happy with what I found on the range. It was more of a takeaway thought versus an actual position thought. That really helps me on the golf course. When I get really technical, my mind goes a million miles a minute, and that's never good on a golf course."

England's Charley Hull could also get in the reckoning, four shots off the lead at 14 under.
