Millwall punished with partial stadium closure over ‘ableist and homophobic chanting’

Millwall have been ordered to partially close their stadium after being found guilty of discriminatory chanting by supporters.

A regulatory commission proved two charges relating to disability and sexual orientation discrimination during January’s fixture against Charlton. Consequently, the Sky Bet Championship team will host Sheffield United with a reduced capacity at The Den on 8 December.

Four blocks inside the Dockers Stand will be shut for the visit of the Blades.

The club have also been fined £70,000, issued an extended FA Action Plan, and handed another partial stadium closure, suspended until the end of next season.

A club statement read: "Both chants are discriminatory and offensive, and the club condemns them in the strongest possible terms.

"Ableist and homophobic chanting, as well as any other form of discriminatory chanting, has no place in football or society.

"Any individual identified as engaging in such abuse will face the consequences.

"The club will continue the significant work already achieved under its dedicated anti-discrimination campaign, ‘All ’Wall’, while continuously reviewing and enhancing best practice to ensure The Den is always a welcoming environment."

Anti-discrimination group Kick It Out said it “welcomes greater accountability for discriminatory mass chanting and supports greater collaboration between clubs and governing bodies to tackle this serious issue”.

It added: “Last season, we received a record number of mass chanting reports across football, including racist, sexist and misogynistic, homophobic, ableist and faith-based abuse. This is why we’re conducting further research and campaigning to show its impact on communities and ensure more consistent action from clubs and authorities.

“Education has a key role here, both for fans, so that they know the impact and consequences of discriminatory chanting, and clubs, so they can proactively help to reduce its prevalence. We stand ready to support football in helping eradicate this problem.”

Last month, Millwall’s match against Swansea was halted for seven minutes after an allegation of racist abuse from the crowd towards Swans substitute Eom Ji Sung was reported by the Welsh club to officials.

Referee Edward Duckworth halted the match in the 65th minute and walked to the dug-outs to speak with Lions boss Alex Neil, Swans counterpart Vitor Matos and fourth official Ruebyn Ricardo.

When play eventually resumed it was announced in the stadium that there had been a report of racist chanting, adding that police were working to identify any offenders using CCTV.
