7 Underrated JRPGs Released Against Huge AAA Launches

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Murillo Zerbinatto is a JRPG enthusiast, writer, and editor. He has played over 300 RPGs during his gaming career, with 90% of them being JRPGs.

When Grand Theft Auto VI finally announced its release date, it was quite amusing to watch a flurry of other games sprint away from the November window, pushing their launches to either September or October. It packed those two months to the brim, but the move was completely understandable. Unless you are a colossal, iconic title like The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time, steering clear of a GTA release is simply logical.

With that in mind, I looked into a few games that lacked the marketing foresight to avoid massive AAA releases and ended up buried beneath mainstream hype. To keep things focused, I zeroed in on JRPGs. Today, I am highlighting several underrated JRPGs that never achieved the success they deserved, largely because all eyes were on massive blockbusters landing right alongside them.

Nothing beats casually picking up a title with no expectations, only to be completely blown away by the end.

While this list doesn't claim deep theoretical research connecting the sales data of every paired release, the comparison makes for a fascinating exercise.

The World Ends with You feels like one of those experimental projects from an era when Square Enix loved to innovate for its own sake. It was stylish, set in Shibuya, and had every ingredient to become a Nintendo DS hallmark, complete with character designs from peak-era Tetsuya Nomura. The game launched in Japan in 2007, but it only reached Western shores in April 2008, specifically on April 22.

Do you know what launched on April 29, just one week later? Grand Theft Auto IV, which was already one of the most anticipated games in industry history. Rockstar's powerhouse moved 3.6 million units on day one and 6 million in its first week, staggering figures for that generation. Naturally, the core audience buying GTA IV doesn't always overlap with players hunting down obscure JRPGs like The World Ends with You. Still, the real issue was media attention and marketing bandwidth. With the entire world talking about Liberty City, poor Neku barely stood a chance at making headlines.

This entry cuts a little too close to home. When I bought my PS3, I could only afford two games, and my final choice came down to Valkyria Chronicles versus Grand Theft Auto IV. Since I knew very little about Valkyria Chronicles and PS3 games were expensive, I took the safer bet, a decision I regret to this day. GTA IV was fantastic, but my JRPG-loving heart should have given Valkyria Chronicles the home it deserved.

The game did spawn several sequels over time, yet it remained criminally underrated and underappreciated in general gaming discourse. It performed well in Japan and exceeded SEGA's internal expectations, but Western sales were a different story. Valkyria Chronicles launched on November 4 in North America, right alongside Fallout 3 and LittleBigPlanet. While LittleBigPlanet might not sound like direct competition on paper, when forced to choose between an unproven new IP and safer mainstream hits, most players made the exact choice I did.

I know The Last Remnant doesn't boast a massive fanbase, but I consider it deeply underrated, especially for SaGa fans. It is atypical, a bit weird, and carries its fair share of flaws, but it remains a fascinating JRPG. Even its remaster failed to garner the accolades it deserved. I understand the game was part of Microsoft's broader effort to make the Xbox 360 a viable console in Japan, but the Western release strategy could have used serious fine-tuning.

The Last Remnant arrived on November 20, 2008. Just one week earlier, the gaming world received Call of Duty: World at War, and on Xbox, Call of Duty was king. Players were so deeply hooked on multiplayer matchmaking that everything else disappeared from their radar for a month. To make matters worse, Left 4 Dead landed right around the same time. Even as a new IP, it carried Valve's pedigree and Xbox 360 console exclusivity. The poor team behind The Last Remnant should have delayed the game just to give it a fighting chance.

Eternal Sonata was another JRPG born on the Xbox 360 to captivate the Japanese audience, launching there on June 14, 2007. In North America, the title landed on September 17, 2007, roughly a week before Halo 3. If Microsoft genuinely wanted to draw attention to Eternal Sonata, launching it right next to their biggest flagship IP was far from ideal.

These forgotten Xbox 360 RPGs came from some of gaming's big developers, including a Monster Hunter game no one remembers!

In my eyes, Eternal Sonata remains one of the most underrated JRPGs of all time. It is magnificent, deeply musical, features an impressive combat system, and looks gorgeous. Unfortunately, even when it was eventually ported to the PS3, it never achieved widespread commercial success. As a side note, Eternal Sonata sits on the GOG Dreamlist, so if you want a chance to play it on modern platforms, I highly recommend voting for it.

Claiming a Wii U game failed to reach its full potential because of competing software can feel a bit far-fetched, given that the console itself struggled globally. Still, examining who ate up the market share around Xenoblade Chronicles X remains a worthy exercise. As someone who has played JRPGs for three decades, I didn't even realize Chronicles X existed when it launched.

Xenoblade Chronicles X released in Japan on April 29, enjoying a modest run of around 100,000 copies sold that year. In North America, it launched on December 4. While Nintendo avoided direct clashes with a few mega-releases, the game still landed in the middle of the crowded holiday shopping season. November 2015 saw heavy hitters like Fallout 4, Call of Duty: Black Ops III, and Star Wars Battlefront. Surprisingly, the title actually performed better in North America than in Japan, which was a welcome silver lining.

This comparison might seem a little skewed on the surface, since owning a Switch in 2022 meant you weren't playing Elden Ring or Gran Turismo 7 on that specific hardware anyway. But the core question remains: would a player invest in a Switch title, or put their money toward the major releases on other platforms? As someone who doesn't care for racing games or FromSoftware's punishing difficulty, I personally favored Triangle Strategy, though I recognize that put me in the minority.

Triangle Strategy launched on March 4, 2022, just a week after Elden Ring, and we all know how that went. The entire internet entered full Elden Ring mode, dissecting its secrets and marveling at Hidetaka Miyazaki's world design. To top it off, Gran Turismo 7 released on the exact same day as Triangle Strategy. Even if you are not a racing fan, never underestimate GT's commercial power, as the seventh entry is rapidly approaching 13 million copies sold.

I vividly remember when Square Enix announced Star Ocean: The Divine Force, because a friend and I debated whether to buy it or Valkyrie Elysium, which Square Enix had launched just a month earlier. Buying games here in Brazil is practically a second job because of the high prices, so we have to study our options carefully before picking our poison. Luckily, I opted for The Divine Force upon its October 27, 2022 release, and it proved to be a great JRPG, especially compared to Valkyrie Elysium.

If I were torn between two niche JRPGs, imagine the broader audience looking at Call of Duty: Modern Warfare II, which launched the very next day, or God of War Ragnarök, which dropped two weeks later. Faced with a choice between Star Ocean and two of the industry's biggest franchises, it is easy to see which path the casual gamer took. You can hardly blame them either: time and money are limited resources.

While some JRPGs disappear, others return from oblivion after years without new releases.
