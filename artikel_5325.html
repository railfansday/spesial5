LIVE – Bitcoin ETF Bleeds: Anthropic’s Record-Breaking IPO Fails to Save the Market

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Bitcoin ETF outflows accelerated as Wall Street extended its losing streak, with Asia’s semiconductor markets suffering their worst weekly declines in decades. Anthropic’s record-breaking $81 billion IPO failed to restore tech stocks. Nvidia also continued falling despite reporting another quarter of extraordinary growth.

US stocks suffered a selloff last night, with the S&P 500 dropping 2.02% to 7,175.83. The Nasdaq Composite lost 2.25%, while the Dow Jones fell 2.19%. More than 90% of S&P 500 companies finished lower.

The losses extended a six-session losing streak for the S&P 500, its longest since 2024. The index now sits 8.2% below its October record. Anthropic’s Nasdaq debut started with its shares jumping as high as $115 against an $83 IPO price, but the excitement faded quickly, with shares closing at $97.17.

Nvidia’s latest results should have given investors plenty of reasons to remain optimistic, yet its shares continued falling as Wall Street questioned the sustainability of the AI investment boom. Jensen Huang’s sudden departure was just one layer of uncertainty.

The company reported third-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% year over year. Data center revenue climbed 110% to $107.6 billion, displaying a huge demand for its AI processors. Nvidia also forecast fourth-quarter revenue of $130 billion. Yet its shares dropped another 3.3% at the market close, extending their losing streak to six sessions.

The financial commitments behind the AI boom are also attracting greater scrutiny. Major technology companies are increasingly relying on debt markets to finance infrastructure expansion.

The European Central Bank’s November Financial Stability Review underscored growing interconnections between AI companies, including circular financing arrangements. It also warned that changing expectations around AI profitability are triggering a sharp repricing across financial markets.

With US hyperscalers planning enormous infrastructure investments, investors are becoming less willing to accept expensive valuations without clearer evidence of future returns.

The pressure has now spread well beyond Wall Street, hitting the semiconductor companies powering the global AI industry. Asia’s markets suffered heavy losses on Friday. The MSCI Asia-Pacific index excluding Japan dropped 3.3%, putting it on course for its worst weekly performance since March 2020.

Taiwan’s benchmark plunged 17.5% for the week, its steepest weekly decline since 1990. Japan’s Nikkei and South Korea’s KOSPI recorded weekly losses of 8%. The selling also hit some of the industry’s biggest AI suppliers, including Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company, Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix.

The concentration of losses across these markets shows how deeply the AI boom has become embedded in global equities. Taiwan supplies advanced chips, South Korea dominates critical memory production, and Japan remains an important supplier of semiconductor equipment and materials.

A slowdown in AI infrastructure spending is affecting companies across the entire supply chain, not just American technology giants.

US spot Bitcoin ETFs are still recording heavy outflows. Another $306.5 million in net outflows yesterday, with seven finalized trading sessions producing $1.4 billion in withdrawals.

Bitcoin ETF outflows now coincide with a reassessment of expensive growth assets, weakening institutional demand just as the crypto market faces additional selling pressure. The AI boom has not disappeared, and Nvidia’s latest results demonstrate that demand remains enormous. But investors are no longer rewarding growth at any price.
