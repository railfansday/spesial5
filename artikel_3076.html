As a personal trainer, I plan my sessions the same way every time: a mobility-focused warm-up, the session plan itself, then a cool-down full of deep holds and static stretches.

The idea is to prepare your joints and muscles for movement and encourage blood flow around the body, which you won't really do by folding over your legs to stretch your hamstrings.

Building flexible muscles and mobile joints isn't just about sitting cross-legged or touching your toes; it's about combining strength, balance, control and stability, and increasing range of motion.

The next time you warm up before a run, try these three dynamic exercises instead. Below, I show you how to do them and why.

If you experience pain at any time, stop and rest. If you're working with an injury or health condition, or you're currently pregnant or postnatal, I recommend seeking advice before starting these exercises.

The routine: 50 seconds on, 10 seconds off, 3 rounds. Add or reduce the number of rounds if you prefer.

If you run often or you watch a lot of soccer, you know how nasty a hamstring injury can be, and how long it takes to recover. It can come out of nowhere, too, sending you into a slow, painful hobble. We want to avoid that by warming these tender muscle groups properly before a workout, and to do that, I encourage you to add gentle motion to your warm-ups.

Really, these three moves target more than just your hamstrings, but I highly recommend them as part of a wider routine or as a standalone session. If you have very tight muscles, a soft knee bend will help in downward dog, and during the Cossack squat, reducing your squat depth will be helpful.

Keeping active and moving around often will also move blood around the body, delivering more oxygen to the working muscle groups. Equally, staying on your feet longer means activating your calves, which are known as the second heart, as contracting the calf muscles pumps deoxygenated blood back toward the heart.

The moral of the story? Walk (and practice mobility) before you run.
