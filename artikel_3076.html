Prediction: Here's What $10,000 in Coca-Cola Could Pay You in Dividends by 2036

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Coca-Cola's dividend could grow nearly 50% by 2036.

By Jeff Siegel – Nov 24, 2026 at 5:13PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Coca-Cola ›

Coca-Cola (KO +1.81%) has increased its dividend for 64 consecutive years. In February 2026, the beverage giant raised its quarterly payout by roughly 4%, from $0.51 to $0.53 per share. That brings its annual dividend to $2.12 per share.

So what could a $10,000 investment in Coca-Cola generate in dividend income over the next decade?

At Coca-Cola's Nov. 20 closing price of $96.99, a $10,000 investment would purchase around 103.1 shares. With an annual dividend of $2.12 per share, those shares would generate approximately $219 in annual income today, representing a dividend yield of roughly 2.2%.

But Coca-Cola has a long history of increasing its payout. Assuming the company continues raising its dividend by 4% annually, its annual dividend could reach $3.14 per share by 2036. That would produce $324 in annual dividend income on the original investment, without purchasing additional shares. And over the entire decade, you would also collect around $2,600 in cumulative dividends.

Coca-Cola benefits from a portfolio of globally recognized beverage brands, including Sprite, Fanta, and Coca-Cola Zero Sugar. Its established distribution network, recurring consumer demand, and ability to raise prices help support consistent cash generation.

That said, the company faces challenges, including shifting consumer preferences, rising operating costs, and currency fluctuations. And while Coca-Cola's dividend history is impressive, future increases aren't guaranteed.

I wouldn't expect Coca-Cola to deliver explosive growth. At nearly $97 per share, the stock isn't particularly cheap either. But if you're seeking dependable income, Coca-Cola remains an attractive long-term candidate.

My prediction is that a $10,000 investment today could generate approximately $324 in annual dividends by 2036, assuming 4% annual dividend growth and no reinvestment. That's a roughly 48% increase in annual income from the original investment, excluding any potential stock price appreciation. That's not bad.
