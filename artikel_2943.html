BrightSpring: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Even after absorbing a $200 million revenue headwind from federal healthcare policy, the company grew EBITDA double digits in its pharmacy segment.

By The Motley Fool Team – Nov 25, 2026 at 11:05AM EST

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings c(BTSG -2.78%)all, held on Oct. 30, 2026, BrightSpring announced total company revenue increased 26% year over year to $4.2 billion while raising its full-year guidance for adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA), a measure of core operating profitability. The healthcare services provider continues to scale its dual model of pharmacy solutions and provider services, showing that lower-cost care in patients' homes creates durable financial compounding.

Three key takeaways from management's comments highlight the long-term thesis.

Specialty pharmacy revenue jumped 34% year over year, driven by limited distribution drugs (LDDs), which are medications restricted by manufacturers to select specialty pharmacies. It launched 19 branded LDDs through nine months of 2026, and they're producing more revenue in year one than last year's cohort.

BrightSpring brought its net debt leverage ratio down to 1.79 times adjusted EBITDA while generating $204 million in quarterly operating cash flow. It doesn't need massive acquisitions to compound, focusing instead on tuck-in mergers and acquisitions (M&A), which is the purchase or combination of smaller regional healthcare businesses.

The company absorbed a $200 million annual revenue drag from the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA), which is federal healthcare legislation modifying prescription drug reimbursement, in its home and community pharmacy segment. It didn't let that statutory headwind stall momentum, relying on technology upgrades in end markets like assisted living facilities (ALF) to restore profit growth.

Patient care continues shifting toward lower-cost home settings, and management is positioning its expanded network to capture that growing national demand. The company leverages scale across procurement, proprietary care coordination models, and automated administrative workflows to widen its operational advantage over smaller regional providers.

With leverage down and full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance raised to between $850 million and $860 million, management expects its compound growth momentum to carry forward into next year.
