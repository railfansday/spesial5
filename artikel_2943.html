Vanguard's Growth ETF Has 3 Stocks Making Up More Than a Third of It. Only 1 Has Held Up in the Tech Sell-Off.

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The stock that's held up best belongs to the slowest grower of the three.

By Daniel Sparks – Nov 27, 2026 at 7:49PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Vanguard Morningstar Growth ETF ›

A big part of the Vanguard Morningstar Growth ETF (VUG +2.06%) comes down to three companies. At the end of October, Apple (AAPL +0.83%) made up 13.7% of the fund's assets, Nvidia (NVDA +0.80%) 12.8%, and Microsoft (MSFT +0.60%) 10.5%. Together, they were around 37% of a fund that owns over 130 stocks.

And the past seven weeks have been tough for the fund. Its record close was on Oct. 6, at $92.64. By Nov. 25, it had lost around 15%, to $79.10. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite has also slid around 15% from its Oct. 6 high. The S&P 500 is down around 8% over the same stretch.

But the fund's three giants haven't fallen together. Apple is the only one of them that's climbed since Oct. 6. Its stock has gained around 7% over that time, closing Nov. 25 at $355.58, within a dollar of its Nov. 13 record.

Microsoft has dipped around 7%, a milder fall than the fund's.

Nvidia, meanwhile, has plunged about 38%.

Nvidia's slump started in October. Shares were down around 15% from their record by Oct. 30, and dropped further after Nvidia announced Jensen Huang's resignation as CEO on Nov. 12.

Nvidia announced his reinstatement on Nov. 23. Still, Nvidia closed Nov. 25 at $149.30, well below its $239.24 record on Oct. 6.

But the chipmaker's sales are still booming. For Nvidia's fiscal third quarter of 2027, which ended Oct. 25, 2026, revenue grew 103% year over year to $115.8 billion. That was also a gain of 20% from the quarter before. And management guided for around $130 billion in sales for the fiscal fourth quarter, which includes an extra week.

Microsoft's stock hit a record on Nov. 4, closing at $555.80, and it finished Nov. 25 around 12% under that peak. But in the software giant's first quarter of fiscal 2027 (the period ended Sept. 30, 2026), revenue climbed 19% to $92.3 billion, and revenue from its Azure cloud platform surged 48%.

Apple's latest numbers were solid, too. Revenue in its fiscal fourth quarter of 2026, which ended Sept. 26, rose 12% year over year to $114.5 billion, a record for a September quarter. iPhone revenue grew 17% to $57.6 billion, and services revenue hit an all-time high of $31.6 billion.

The iPhone maker's growth, though, is cooling. For the June quarter (Apple's fiscal third quarter), revenue rose 16%. Then growth eased to 12% in the September quarter. This leaves Apple the slowest grower of the three, trailing Microsoft's 19% and Nvidia's 103%.

On the earnings call, CFO Kevan Parekh guided for revenue growth of 7% to 10% for the December quarter. He said the outlook reflects the company's best view of tight supply. By his account, then, the limit is supply, not demand.

He also expects gross margin to be between 45% and 46%, and he pointed mostly to higher memory costs. That compares with 47.9% for the September quarter, which included about a percentage point of lift from tariff refunds.

"What we're seeing with memory pricing right now is unprecedented," CEO John Ternus said during the call.

The fund owns every stock in around the same proportion as its weighting in the index it tracks, and these weights mostly shift with share prices. It doesn't sell what held up to buy more of what dropped.

By my rough math, using Nov. 25 closing prices, Apple's weight has probably risen to around 15%, while Microsoft stands near 11% and Nvidia near 10%. The three still add up to more than a third of the fund. But the fund now leans harder on Apple than it did at the end of October.

This matters because Apple is also the priciest of the three. Its stock sells for around 40 times earnings, compared with about 16 times earnings for Nvidia. In other words, investors pay about two and a half times as much for every dollar of Apple's profit.

To the company's credit, Apple's revenue reached a record $479 billion for fiscal 2026, and its iPhone sales are still rising. But for those holding the fund into 2027, I think the bigger risk now is with Apple, not Nvidia. If Apple's growth keeps slowing while its memory costs rise, a stock at 40 times earnings might have room to fall. And the fund has more of it than it did in October.
