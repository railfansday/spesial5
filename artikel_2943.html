OMS Energy (OMSE) Q2 2027 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Saudi order delays, record cash and a services shift shape this quarter's results.

By Motley Fool Transcribing – Nov 26, 2026 at 9:53AM EST

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

OMS Energy (OMSE +6.67%) reported a decrease in revenue for the first half of fiscal year 2027 but expanded its gross margin while maintaining a debt-free balance sheet with record cash reserves. Management attributed the revenue decline primarily to procurement delays in Saudi Arabia, where orders under a long-term supply agreement are being deferred instead of canceled. The company is actively diversifying its geographic footprint with new orders in Malaysia, Kuwait, and Pakistan while shifting toward a higher-margin services revenue mix. Strategic efforts focus on cost discipline and cash generation to manage through extended customer decision cycles.

The company's financial and operating results for the 6 months ended September 30, 2026, have been released and are available on the OMS Investor Relations website at ir.omsos.com. Meng Hock How: Thank you, operator, and thank you to everyone joining us today. I am Meng Hock How, CEO of OMS Energy Technologies. I'll discuss our first half performance, what we're seeing in customer orders and the progress we're making across our business. I will then address several questions that we expect investors to have about the results. For investors newer to OMS, we are Singapore headquartered equipment manufacturer and engineered solutions supplier serving upstream oil and gas operators. Our core products include Surface Wellhead and Christmas Tree Systems, Specialty Connectors and Pipe. We also provide premium threading, repair, and other engineering services through 11 manufacturing facilities across 6 markets in Asia Pacific, Middle East, and North Africa. Turning to our results.

I'm pleased to report that we expanded our gross margin, generated strong cash flow and further strengthened our debt-free balance sheet in the first half. Gross margin increased to 31.4% from 28.2% in the prior year period, and net profit margin improved to 18.8% from 17.6%. We generated $25.3 million in operating cash flow and approximately twice our net profit and ended September with a record $172.3 million in cash, cash equivalents, and restricted cash or approximately $4.06 per share. We delivered these results while macro conditions remain challenging given ongoing geopolitical uncertainty and supply chain disruption in our major markets. Our facilities remained operational throughout the period.

Customers continued to tender and place orders with OMS during the half, but took longer to commit and schedule deliveries, weighing on revenue. First half revenue was $66.7 million compared with $82.8 million a year ago, largely due to the slower pace of Saudi Aramco call-off orders. This was partially offset by increased activity in Malaysia and Thailand, showing the value of our diversified footprint. In the current environment, the challenge for OMS lies not in lack of opportunities, but in reduced visibility, extended decision cycle and execution timing. You will see that pattern throughout this period's result.

With order timing harder to predict, we are concentrating on factors that we can control, including careful management of inventory, costs, and customer deliveries. By keeping inventory, purchasing and costs in line with the orders we actually received, we were able to protect profitability and generate strong cash flow despite fluctuating order volumes. Saudi Arabia is where reduced visibility and order timing have affected us most. Revenue from Specialty Connectors and Pipe was $37.1 million compared with $51.1 million a year ago as the timing of the order placed under our long-term supply agreement with Saudi Aramco continued to slow. I would like to emphasize, as previously mentioned, that these are deferred orders, not lost orders.

Our agreement with Saudi Aramco remains intact. Our relationship remains strong, and there has been no cancellation. To that end, we continue to receive business from Aramco. In September, we announced a $7.1 million order for specialty connectors and pipe won through an open tender outside our long-term agreement. Since that award, we have received a call-off order of approximately $3 million under the long-term agreement as well as a separate $500,000 order for loose connectors for Aramco's float equipment. We view these awards as a reflection of Aramco's confidence in our product and the delivery record we have built in the Kingdom over 15 years.

In Indonesia, our operation in Bogor just received an order for specialty pipe and connectors valued at approximately $396,000 for Pertamina North West Java. This is a follow-on from the order we announced in January 2026. Furthermore, our operations in Singapore and Malaysia secured their first order for specialty connectors and pipe for Petronas in Malaysia, valued at approximately $687,000. Of that amount, $367,000 was delivered during the first half of fiscal 2027. We also continue to bid for supply contracts with Kuwait Oil Company, KOC, and are actively engaged in the tender process we have entered after we qualified for the approved vendor list in July.

Also following that qualification, we received a $458,000 order from Tenaris for OMS connectors that we welded onto Tenaris pipe and supplied to Kuwait Oil Company as part of Tenaris OCTG package. While modest in size, the Petronas and Tenaris order extend our specialty connectors and pipe business to 2 more national oil companies, which we believe can lead to additional business over time. We've also recently bid to supply specialty connectors and Pipe for a development in the South China Sea with results expected next month. If we are successful, this will mark our reentry into China after more than 10 years and represent further progress in our ongoing geographic diversification efforts.

In surface wellhead and Christmas trees, we continue to win orders while revenue follows customer and project schedules. First half revenue from Surface Wellhead and Christmas Tree was $3.5 million compared to $6.2 million a year ago. Indonesia is the main market for this business and upstream activities there has been slow over the past year as new oil and gas projects have taken longer to come online. In September, we announced $2.3 million order from Pertamina Hulu Rokan under our existing 3-year supply contract following the contract extension we announced in March. We also recently received $766,000 order from Pertamina for choke valves, a component of Christmas tree systems.

Separately, we are awaiting a call-off of approximately $1 million from Pertamina under an existing contract. We also completed the stackup and function test of a new compact surface wellhead and Christmas tree system for Pertamina, a milestone in developing our wellhead product offering with an established customer. The equipment has been delivered and is being installed by Pertamina on its Tamadun-001 field, where it is carrying out an unconventional drilling program. Indonesia holds vast unconventional oil and gas resources, including shale gas, coal-bed methane, methane hydrate, and shale oil, and commercial development is still at an early stage. We believe this installation position us well as that activity develops.

OMS Singapore has also just delivered a complete 10,000 psi wellhead and Christmas tree system to Orient Petroleum in Pakistan, marking OMS Singapore's first delivery of such a system to the country. Orient Petroleum is one of Pakistan's largest private exploration and production company and has been operating since 1979. OMS Singapore has also received a supply contract for 2 sets of production tree for a Bleiswijk geothermal cluster in Netherlands. While the value is small at approximately $130,000, it is a step into the European energy sector that we want to build on. Across our service businesses, customers also continued to place orders during the half.

Other ancillary services revenue rose to $9.4 million from $7.5 million, an increase of approximately 25%, supported by repair, refurbishment, and engineering work in Thailand and Malaysia. This maintenance work depends less on new project approval than equipment sales do. Premium threading services revenue was $16.7 million compared to $18 million. Together, our services business accounted for approximately 39% of first half revenue, up from approximately 31% a year ago. In Singapore, we've also secured an approximately $0.55 million of repair work on production risers for a large oilfield services company for the upcoming Kikeh 3B Phase 2 subsea project in Malaysia. The Kikeh field is operated by PTTEP Malaysia. Our backlog reflects the period's slower order timing pattern.

At September 30, it stood at $40.9 million compared to $60.7 million at March 31, primarily due to call-off timing under the Saudi agreement. Our backlog comprises confirmed orders that have not yet been recognized as revenue and are expected to be delivered within the following 12 months. It does not include orders received after September 30, including the additional Saudi Aramco order I mentioned earlier. A framework agreement, a tender opportunity, and a confirmed call-off each represent different stages of the customer's procurement process and the backlog captures only the last of those stages. Today, opportunities are taking longer to move from tender to award and from framework agreement to call-off.

To put the backlog figure in context, Saudi Aramco maintained its 2026 capital spending guidance of $50 billion to $55 billion when it reported third quarter's results this month. Aramco attributed lower upstream spending in the quarter, mainly to the timing of crude oil increment projects and several of its major upstream developments, including the Dammam development and the second phase of Jafurah are scheduled to come on stream in 2027. Across the wider region, BMI expects oil and gas capital spending to grow about 4% this year and to average approximately 6% a year from 2027 to 2030. Now let me walk through our financial results in more detail.

First half revenue was $66.7 million compared to $82.8 million in the prior year period. Gross profit was $21 million compared to $23.3 million. Gross margin increased from 28.2% to 31.4%, supported by a more favorable mix in Saudi Specialty connectors and pipe and a greater contribution from higher-margin ancillary services in Thailand. Selling and general and administrative expenses were $7.1 million compared to $5.4 million in the prior year period. The increase primarily reflected personnel costs and professional services, governance, and compliance-related fees. Operating profit was $13.9 million compared to $17.9 million, and operating margin was 20.8% compared to 21.6%. Finance income increased to $2.4 million from $1.6 million, reflecting more cash placed in interest-earning deposits.

Income tax expense was $3.6 million compared with $4.6 million, and our effective tax rate was 22.3% compared to 24%. Net profit was $12.6 million compared with $14.6 million, and net profit margin increased to 18.8% from 17.6%. Basic and diluted earnings per share were $0.28 compared with $0.33, reflecting lower profit attributable to shareholders and a higher weighted average share count. Adjusted EBITDA was $17.3 million compared to $21.8 million. Adjusted EBITDA margin held relatively steady at 25.9% compared with 26.4% despite the 19% decline in revenue. We continue to use this supplement measure alongside the IFRS results. The earnings release sets out the adjustment and the reconciliation from net profit. Cash generation remained an area of strength.

Operating cash flow was $25.3 million compared with $26.4 million. Adjusted free cash flow was $19.9 million compared with $25.4 million. As described in our earnings release, this measure now deducts cash placed in fixed rate structured deposits, and the change from last year mainly reflects the $5 million we placed in these deposits during the period. Part of our cash generation came from a reduction in inventory, which contributed $9.5 million to operating cash flow compared to $12.2 million a year ago. Inventory ended September at $7.7 million compared to $17.2 million at March 31. The level of inventory and the timing of replenishment will affect cash flow as order activity changes.

We will continue to manage purchasing and production against the equipment needed for customer deliveries. Net cash used in investing activities was $5.4 million. This included $5 million placed in fixed rate structured deposits and approximately $0.4 million for property, plant, and equipment and software. These deposits are not included in our reported cash balance of $172.3 million. Cash and cash equivalents and restricted cash increased to a record $172.3 million at September 30 compared with $104.3 million at March 31. Our balance sheet remains free of bank borrowings. We continue to refine our capital allocation plan and assess investment opportunities while maintaining the financial flexibility to meet delivery commitments.

Looking ahead, our immediate planning reflects the slow pace of Saudi call-off and the orders we have in hand across the business. The September Aramco tender win and the Pertamina order were announced with expected delivery dates in January and March 2027, respectively. We are focused on execution against the schedule and continued engagement with our customers. We remain cautious about the timing of a wider recovery in procurement. At the same time, we are encouraged by our recent order activity, the growing contribution from our services business and the opportunities we are pursuing across the Middle East and Asia.

We have long-term agreement with leading national oil companies, 11 manufacturing facilities close to our customers and more than $172 million in cash with no debt. We believe this strength positions OMS well to capture future opportunities. Before we close, I would like to address questions investors are likely to have in the half, including how current visibility compares with the expectation that we discussed in June. Question one, how much of the earnings decline is attributable to geopolitical tensions and have projects been canceled? We have not observed material projects cancellation directly attributable to geopolitical development. The effect has been indirect primarily through longer customer decision cycle, deferred projects award and increased caution in customer capital spending decisions.

For OMS, the main operating issue has been the slower pace of Saudi call-off orders. Some customers have taken longer to finalize investment decisions or have adjusted project time lines, and it is difficult to isolate a specific financial impact. Opportunities remain in place, but decision and awards are taking longer. We are actively pursuing work in Malaysia, Indonesia, the UAE, Kuwait and China. Question two, is demand weakening, particularly in the Middle East? We would characterize the environment as one of increased caution rather than broad-based demand weakness. Activity levels vary by customers and markets. Customers continue to evaluate opportunities and pursue strategic projects, although decision-making and approval processes have become more measured.

In certain cases, this has translated into longer approval and procurement cycles. For OMS, that shows up as a slower Saudi call-off and a longer gap between tender and award. Several of the largest oilfield services have also pointed to the limited near-term visibility in the Middle East in their recent results. Even so, Saudi Aramco has maintained its 2026 capital spending guidance and has continued to award us business, including the $7.1 million tender award in September and additional orders since then. Our focus remains on supporting customers while adapting to changing market conditions. Third question, have supply chain disruption become a major concern? Supply chain conditions remain manageable, but less predictable than in previous period.

We have experienced longer lead time and occasional logistic challenges affecting certain projects. Management continues to work closely with suppliers and customers to mitigate these impacts where possible. The fourth question, how are margins holding up? And can they be sustained? Well, first half gross margin increased to 31.4% from 28.2%, supported by the mix of equipment specification and services. Operating margin was 20.8%. We continue to manage procurement, project execution and operating costs carefully. Our priority is maintaining financial discipline while remaining competitive in the markets we serve. However, if activity slows further in the second half, margins could be affected. We will continue to manage costs carefully as conditions evolve.

Question five, do we expect conditions to improve in the second half? Given the uncertain geopolitical and macroeconomic environment, we do not believe it will be appropriate to speculate on the timing or extent of any improvement. Customers continue to tender and place orders with OMS. Improvement depends on visibility returning and decision cycles shortening, and we do not control that timing. Continued uncertainty could further influence customer spending, project timing and supply chain performance. We continue to maintain long-standing customer relationships, technical capabilities and a diversified market presence. Management remains focused on factors within our control, including customer engagement, operational execution, cost management, and cash generation.

Sixth question is, does the expectation of modest FY 2027 revenue growth discussed in June still hold? Well, in June, we expected fiscal 2027 revenue to be modestly above fiscal 2026, assuming no further material geopolitical disruption. Since then, the Saudi call-off activity has remained slow and the timing of a broader pickup remains uncertain. Given the first half revenue of $66.7 million and the continued slow pace of call-off, we no longer expect fiscal 2027 revenue to exceed fiscal 2026. We're not providing a specific range today, and we will update investors as visibility on order timing improves. The last point or question 7, how do you plan to use your cash?

Well, our priorities are consistent with what we shared in June. First, we are reinvesting in core businesses, including capacity, certification, and in-region capabilities that support our growth. Second, we continue to review bolt-on acquisition opportunities with discipline and our cash position allows us to pursue them without raising new capital or taking on debt. We do not currently have a dividend and buyback program in place, but our balance sheet gives us the flexibility to introduce one when timing and conditions are right. We continue to refine our capital allocation plan, and we'll update investors as it develops. With this, we conclude our prepared remarks. Thank you very much for joining us today.

Operator: This concludes today's conference call. Thank you all for participating. You may now disconnect your lines. Thank you.
