Trevor Lawrence, Jaguars showcase potential fatal flaw in loss to Giants

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The Jacksonville Jaguars entered their Week 11 game against the New York Giants receiving consideration as the best team in the NFL.

The case for the Jaguars relied on the argument that the team was one of the NFL's most balanced. This was proven by their elite, plus-125 point differential, the improvement of Trevor Lawrence during his second season in Liam Coen's offense and the team's improvement of its defensive weaknesses with trades for Jessie Bates and Jordan Davis.

But in Week 11, the Jaguars showed any coronation is still a ways away, losing 23-18 to the Giants in a game that highlighted one of the team's biggest shortcomings.

Lawrence has struggled badly under pressure throughout the 2026 NFL season. He entered Sunday's contest sporting a passer rating of 69.5 under pressure compared to 119 when kept clean, per Pro Football Focus. He has endured similar issues against the blitz, where he has posted an 81.7 passer rating when blitzed compared to a mark of 117.8 when not.

Clearly, John Harbaugh and the Giants' Dennard Wilson-led defensive staff were aware of these numbers. New York dialed up a litany of pressures against Jacksonville, which clearly rattled Jacksonville and kept the Jaguars off-schedule throughout the contest.

Lawrence occasionally made some impressive plays against the relentless pressure. Several times he was able to scramble out of sacks and avoid massive, negative plays.

But more often, Lawrence was hurried into risky throws, leading to his first multi-interception game of the year and many other turnover-worthy passes during the loss.

A microcosm of Lawrence's struggles against pressure came on the Jaguars' final offensive drive of the game. Jacksonville was able to get a goal-to-go opportunity trailing 23-15 in the final four minutes of the fourth quarter. The Giants brought heavy blitzes on each of those goal-to-go snaps.

Lawrence failed to get the ball out of his hands quickly, denying the Jaguars a realistic chance at logging a potential game-tying score to cap off Jacksonville's below-average offensive performance.

It's abundantly clear that the Jaguars will need to do a better job keeping Lawrence clean moving forward to continue their winning ways. Entering Week 11, Lawrence had been pressured on just 31.2% of his dropbacks, which ranked 25th among 31 qualified quarterbacks to open the season, per the NFL's Next Gen Stats.

Failure to achieve that goal could lead the Jaguars to endure more offensive hiccups moving forward, which would threaten the balance they enjoyed during the first half of the 2026 NFL season.

Of course, part of the reason the Jaguars enjoyed better success keeping Lawrence clean early in the season was the team's weaker schedule. Jacksonville entered Week 11 having faced the 25th-hardest schedule in the NFL, per ESPN's Football Power Index, and only three of those opponents – the Denver Broncos, Houston Texans and Cleveland Browns – ranked top-10 in pressure rate during the 2026 season.

The Jaguars went 1-2 across those games, putting a beatdown on the Browns in Week 1 before losing to the Broncos and Texans.

Lawrence actually performed well against the Browns and Texans, logging four touchdowns in each game. But as his season-worst performances against the Broncos and Giants showed, there is a blueprint for getting Jacksonville's offense off-schedule.

The Jaguars' schedule will only get harder from here. Their Week 11 game against the Giants kicked off a stretch during which Jacksonville will face a trove of teams with well-regarded defenses, including the Chicago Bears, Pittsburgh Steelers and Texans.

If those teams can generate pressure against Lawrence in a manner similar to the Giants, that could cause the Jaguars to endure some bumps in what was supposed to be a relatively clear path toward winning the AFC South.

But if Lawrence and the Jaguars can bow up and find a way to better deal with the pressure, that could go a long way toward quelling concerns about this current weakness and establishing that discussion of them being among the best NFL teams was not premature.
