Green Bay Packers analyst identifies potential trade chip in Jonathan Taylor pursuit

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

The Green Bay Packers have been linked to several potential running back additions ahead of the NFL trade deadline, with Jonathan Taylor emerging as the biggest name in the discussion. However, pursuing a deal for the Colts star could be difficult given Green Bay's limited salary cap flexibility and lack of draft capital. Making such a move without sacrificing valuable future assets could prove challenging.

Tom Silverstein of the Milwaukee Journal Sentinel suggested that Green Bay could use edge rusher Lukas Van Ness as a trade asset. Although Van Ness still holds value, Silverstein believes the Packers have enough depth on defense to consider moving him in exchange for help addressing a weakness on the offensive side.

"The first player that comes to mind is edge rusher Lukas Van Ness," wrote the NFL analyst.

"He currently leads the team with 4½ sacks, six quarterback hits and 17 pressures, and can play the run from both an edge and inside position. The Packers picked up his fifth-year option in '27, which will cost them a guaranteed $13.75 million. Trading him would clear that total from the team's '27 salary cap and make Taylor's contract easier to fit in."

With Indianapolis reportedly seeking more than a second-round pick in return for Taylor, Van Ness could help bridge the gap in a potential deal and reduce the draft capital Green Bay would need to surrender. His upside and the Packers' ability to retain control of his contract through 2027 could also make him an appealing asset in trade discussions.

The Packers may soon have to decide whether Van Ness is worth a long-term investment. While his development has been encouraging, his performance so far could make it difficult to justify paying him the kind of money teams usually offer rising edge defenders.

Green Bay's decision to acquire Micah Parsons and absorb his sizable contract, despite already having Van Ness on the roster, could signal a shift in its long-term defensive plans. The move suggests the Packers may not view Van Ness as a central part of their future pass rush.

As the November 10 deadline draws closer, the possibility of Indianapolis accepting Van Ness in a deal for Taylor remains an open question. The Colts would need to decide whether adding a promising edge rusher is worth parting with their starting running back.
