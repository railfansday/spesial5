Top MS NOW Hosts Sign Letter Urging Versant to Ink Deal With Writers Guild East Members (Exclusive)

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Rachel Maddow, Chris Hayes, Jen Psaki and Lawrence O'Donnell are among the hosts that have signed the letter, which calls on the company to cut a deal 'without division or delay.'

Some of MS NOW‘s top on-air hosts and journalists have signed an open letter calling on parent company Versant to come to terms on a new collective bargaining agreement with employees who are members of the Writers Guild of America East.

The current contract with the union is set to expire Dec. 3.

The letter, which was delivered to management at the bargaining table, begins: “As hosts and guests of MS NOW programs, we stand in solidarity with the MS NOW Union as they fight for a contract that addresses inflation, AI protections, and retirement security. As one of the most trusted names in news, we are proud of our programming and the MS NOW producers that make it possible.”

Among the signers of the letter are Rachel Maddow, Jen Psaki, Chris Hayes, Lawrence O’Donnell, Ali Velshi, Alicia Menendez, Symone Sanders Townsend, Jacob Soboroff, Chris Jansing, and Katie Phang.

“We’re disappointed that Versant Media’s decision makers expect unit members to work extra hours creating new digital content, without treating it as extra work or paying for it. On top of that, we are disappointed that Versant Media will not agree to the Union’s demands for annual and minimum wage increases that keep pace with inflation,” the letter continues. “We are calling on the Company to agree to a quick and fair process to recognize all employees’ rightful place in the MS NOW Union without division or delay. Additionally, we call on the Company to agree to a contract that will protect wages, benefits, and security at work.”

In addition to cutting a new deal with the 300 or so existing WGAE members, the union is also seeking union recognition for another 50 employees across Digital, Audio and Longform, Newsgathering, Planning, and the DC Bureau.

The first contract with what was then MSNBC was ratified in 2023, and included annual pay increases and policies around overnight and extra work. The MSNBC union was formed in 2021. At the time of that first contract, the WGAE leaned on celebrity writer support from the likes of Tina Fey, David Simon and Adam McKay to apply pressure on NBCUniversal to cut a deal.

The letter from top talent however ramps up pressure on Versant to cut a deal with the WGAE, given the high-profile of the letter’s signees and the left-leaning politics of the channel’s talent and audience.

The WGAE has a robust membership across broadcast and cable news, including groups at ABC News, CBS News, the NBC promo writers, and stations like CBS 2, WNET, Fox 5 and 1010 WINS.
