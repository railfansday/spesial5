Clavicular allegedly told 17-year-old to call vodka ‘water’ to avoid Twitch ban before assault

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Streamer Clavicular is accused of instructing a 17-year-old girl to lie about her age and pretend she was drinking water instead of vodka to avoid getting his Twitch livestream banned before an alleged sexual assault.

The 20-year-old influencer, whose real name is Braden Peters, has been indicted on two counts of rape and one count of providing alcohol to a minor in Massachusetts.

According to a police report detailed by Fox News, the allegations stem from an encounter on May 23, 2025, at his family’s home in Chatham, Cape Cod.

The teenager told investigators that she had connected with Peters through Instagram before he invited her to the property to create looksmaxxing content.

Peters allegedly arranged an Uber to bring her to the home, where he was livestreaming on Twitch.

According to investigators, Peters knew the girl was 17 but instructed her to tell viewers she was 18. He also allegedly told her to refer to the vodka she was drinking as “water” to prevent his Twitch account or livestream from being banned.

The teenager claimed Peters supplied her with vodka throughout the evening, leaving her increasingly intoxicated.

Police subsequently reviewed footage from the Twitch broadcast, including a moment when the girl said, “Wait, I’m so drunk though,” before the two kissed.

She alleged that Peters later had sex with her while she was heavily intoxicated. She also told investigators that she woke up the following morning to Peters having sex with her without her consent.

On November 18, Boston 25 News reported that Peters had been indicted on two rape counts and one count of providing alcohol to a minor.

Peters pleaded not guilty to the original charges at an October 22 court appearance and was released after posting $10,000 cash bail.

His representative, Mitchell P. Jackson, told Fox News on November 18 that the influencer denies all charges and allegations against him.

The criminal proceedings are separate from a Florida civil lawsuit filed by his accuser. As Dexerto previously reported, she removed the sexual assault claim from that lawsuit in October, with her attorney explaining that the decision was made to avoid interfering with the ongoing criminal case.
