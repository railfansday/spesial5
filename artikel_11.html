Congo Ebola Outbreak: How Fear and Mistrust Drive Community Spread

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

As the Ebola virus spreads in eastern Congo, health teams struggle to curb fatal delays in seeking medical care and the widespread desire to treat sick relatives at home.

Fabrice Koli, 5, at an Ebola treatment center in the town of Nizi, Democratic Republic of Congo, this summer after his 17-year-old brother died.Credit...

Matthew Mpoke Bigg and Arlette Bashizi spent more than a week reporting on the Ebola outbreak from the town of Nizi, in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo.

Gorette Mbusi stood on her doorstep and wrestled with a question. Should she let her grandson be taken to the hospital?

Health workers had come to her village, in the eastern part of the Democratic Republic of Congo, to implore her to let them take the young man to a specialized Ebola facility in the nearby town of Nizi. But she was wary. She had already lost two family members, including her daughter, who had been transported to the hospital and then died.

“I don’t know where this sickness is coming from that is killing my family,” she said. “I feel abandoned.”

Ms. Mbusi’s dilemma reflects a central challenge for health workers as they struggle to contain the fastest-growing Ebola outbreak on record — how to stop people from passing the disease to loved ones at home.

Community transmission happens when family members care for Ebola patients at home and then become ill themselves, spreading the disease and making it more difficult for medical teams to identify and isolate people who have been in contact with those infected. Tracking and tracing the contacts of Ebola patients and educating people about the virus are crucial to ending community transmission.

Health workers must first get more people to seek medical treatment in hospitals sooner and overcome deep suspicions about Ebola in villages like this one, known as Nizi 4.
