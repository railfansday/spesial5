Best meditation apps of 2027 and how to choose the right one

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

You open the app store to look for a meditation app and find dozens of options promising calmer days, deeper sleep, less stress, and sharper focus. It’s hard to know where to start or which claims to believe.

Below, we cover three well-known meditation apps: Calm, Headspace, and Insight Timer. We've looked at what each one offers and named what we like about it, so you can decide which one to try.

Before we dive in, a quick note on where we’re coming from. We make Calm, and we love it. We hope you will too, but another option might suit you better.

The best meditation app for you depends on what you’re looking for. To narrow it down, ask yourself these questions:

What do you want help with? Maybe it’s stress relief, better sleep, sharper focus, or a guided start as a beginner.

How much variety do you need? Some people want an extensive library to explore, while others prefer a smaller collection built around a specific goal.

How personal do you want it to feel? You might prefer an app that tailors recommendations to your mood and goals, or one that leaves the choosing to you.

What's your budget? Compare what the free and paid tiers include, since a generous free version may be all you need.

Calm is one of the most widely used meditation apps, and it also offers sleep content, music, gentle movement sessions, and more. People use Calm at night for sleep and during the day for stress.

Content library: The app features Sleep Stories, bedtime tales read by well-known voices, alongside daily meditation programs including the flagship Daily Calm. You'll also find breathwork exercises, mindful tools, soundscapes, intro courses, and longer programs on themes like anxiety and self-kindness.

Personalization: Calm’s Check-Ins let you log your mood, sleep, and gratitude, so you can spot patterns over time. You can set reminders and follow a series of sessions to build a routine.

Standout feature: For us, it's the sleep content. Sleep Stories are the first thing we’d recommend to a friend who’s struggling with sleep.

Headspace is another widely-used meditation app, and it leans into teaching. Many sessions are organized into courses that walk you through a topic step by step.

Content library: The app features beginner courses and SOS meditations, short sessions for when stress spikes. It also has themed collections for focus and movement. For sleep, there are Sleepcasts.

Personalization: Headspace offers personalized recommendations and its AI companion, Ebb, lets you talk through how you’re feeling and suggests app content in response.

Standout feature: We like how Headspace gives beginners a clear path to follow, starting with its 10-session Basics course.

Insight Timer started as a meditation timer and grew into a large library of guided meditations, with plenty of free content and a paid tier called MemberPlus.

Content library: The app hosts a catalog of meditations, talks, and music tracks, plus a meditation timer for people who prefer to sit in silence.

Personalization: You can join teacher-led challenges to help you stay on track, and the streak feature tracks how many days in a row you’ve meditated.

Standout feature: We like how much the app offers for free. With so much available, you can try a range of teachers before paying for anything.

Calm won’t be right for everyone, but here’s why we love it. (Keep in mind that this section is our point of view, not a neutral comparison.)

The daily meditations are a great place to start. There are three to choose from, and each has its own feel:

The Daily Calm, with Tamara Levitt, leans toward gentle reflection

The Daily Jay, with Jay Shetty, draws on psychology and timeless wisdom

The Daily Trip, with Jeff Warren, suits curious meditators who like to experiment

You may click with one voice more than the others, so it’s worth trying all three to find out whether you’re a Tamara, Jay, or Jeff person. (There’s a fourth daily too, the Daily Move, a short mindful movement session — a great way to get your body ready for the day.)

We’ve also produced a lot of quick meditations over the past few years, all under five minutes. So even the busiest person, or the most skeptical first-timer, has something small to try.

Sleep is the other main reason we keep coming back. With our Sleep Stories and nighttime meditations, we’ve unlocked a delightful way to let go of the worries of the day, relax your mind and body, and allow yourself to fall (and stay) asleep easily and naturally. Our 500+ Sleep Stories include some of the world’s most celebrated and soothing voices, like Harry Styles, Cynthia Erivo, Idris Elba, Rosé, and Greta Lee. With plenty more to choose from, there’s always something fresh to help you wind down and drift off.

Then there’s how the app looks and feels. The visuals lean into calming nature—think misty forests, rain on a window, slow ocean waves—so opening the app itself sets the scene for calm. And if you just need to tune out the world for a while, our Rain on Leaves soundscape is a legit vibe.

The numbers back up the impact. More than 180 million people worldwide have downloaded Calm, and its content has been studied in peer-reviewed research. For example, in one randomized trial of 88 stressed college students, those who used Calm for eight weeks reported lower perceived stress than a wait-list group.

They may help, though the research is still young. An updated review of 45 randomized trials found improvements in anxiety and depression symptoms among people using mindfulness apps, compared with control groups. The effects were modest, and the authors note we still need larger, higher-quality studies to say how much apps help and for whom.

An app also isn’t a substitute for professional care. If you’re struggling with your mental health, it’s worth talking to a doctor, who can help you find the right support.

To choose the right meditation app, start with your main goal, whether that’s stress relief, better sleep, or a guided start as a beginner. From there, check prices and any features you care about, like offline listening or language options. Then take advantage of the free trials. Most apps offer one, and it’s a good way to see how an app fits before you pay. While you’re trying it out, sample a few different narrators. A lot of meditation comes down to the voice guiding you, and finding someone you connect with can turn the practice into something you look forward to.

Calm is worth it if you want to ease stress and anxiety, sleep better, focus when your mind wanders, or learn the basics of meditation. The app may be a helpful way to build a daily mental health habit, with support for moments from a racing mind at bedtime to a hard day at work. Whether you need quick relief in the moment or ongoing support through a life change, Calm is for you.

You can try a 7-day free trial to see how it fits your flow, and you don’t need to know which content format you prefer ahead of time.

Calm and Headspace have a lot in common. Both offer guided meditation, beginner-friendly courses, short sessions for when stress spikes, sleep content, and mindful movement. The difference is in the mix and the overall feel. On Calm, you’ll find hundreds of Sleep Stories read by well-known voices, daily meditations from a rotating cast of instructors, music, soundscapes, and Emergency Calm sessions for high-stress moments. On Headspace, you’ll find a more course-led, step-by-step approach and an AI companion called Ebb. Both offer free content and a free trial, so the best way to decide is to try each and see which feels right for you.
