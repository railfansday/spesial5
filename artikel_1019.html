Piastri to serve grid penalty at Las Vegas GP

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Oscar Piastri will serve a grid penalty in Las Vegas; McLaren confirm upgraded floor to be added to MCL40 at late stage of season; watch the Las Vegas GP weekend on Sky Sports F1, with qualifying at 4am on Saturday and the race at 4am on Sunday

McLaren have confirmed that Oscar Piastri will serve a 10-place grid penalty at this weekend's Las Vegas Grand Prix as he will be forced to exceed his engine allowance for the season.

Following an unspecified reliability issue, Piastri will need to use his fifth Internal Combustion Engine (ICE) of the season, taking him beyond the limit of four that each driver has for the season.

A team statement said: "Oscar will take a new Internal Combustion Engine in Las Vegas after a reliability issue.

"As it is his fifth of the season, he will take a 10-place grid penalty, but he has a good chance to recover positions with overtaking in Saturday's race."

The Las Vegas Strip Circuit features a 1.2-mile straight that drivers take flat out, which should present a good opportunity for Piastri to surge back through the field.

McLaren also confirmed that both Piastri and his team-mate Lando Norris will have their cars fitted with a new turbo specification, which has been made available by the power unit supplier Mercedes following an engine upgrade.

"Both cars, as with the other HPP-powered cars, will be fitted with a new ADUO-enabled turbo specification," the team added. "The expected performance benefit is relatively small."

Despite there being just three rounds remaining of the 2026 season, McLaren will continue to upgrade their car with a new floor arriving in time for Las Vegas.

Having officially been dethroned as constructors' champions by Mercedes last time out in Brazil, McLaren are attempting to close down a 17-point deficit to Red Bull in the battle for third in this year's standings.

A McLaren statement said: "The team will bring further developments to the MCL40 as they continue to push to unlock more performance in the remaining races, primarily a new floor.

"We will also have some solutions to improve drag during the Free Practice sessions, which we will then review before making a decision on whether to run them on Friday.

"With plenty of opportunities to exploit the car's potential, both through evaluating the new upgrades and refining how to operate the package at the maximum potential available, the priority is to turn those learnings into performance on track and valuable points in the immediate term."

McLaren were the only member of F1's 'big four' teams to bring upgrades to Brazil, where the new parts helped Norris win the Sao Paulo Grand Prix.

Ross Hood, McLaren executive director of programme delivery, said: "Our programme delivery has evolved throughout this year and continues to do so.

"The new regulations, combined with the volume and pace of performance coming from the tunnel and engineering through to the circuit, have made it an intense year. That galvanises the team and brings us closer together, so we get the right performance to the track while challenging each other and our ideas.

"A new floor is arriving in Las Vegas this week, and it's a great example of our programme delivery. The collaboration between Aerodynamics, Design, Performance, Planning and Manufacturing helped ensure the team could maximise the output of the last tunnel runs for the 40A. It's a big push, but by making decisions closely together, we hit the targets we set ourselves."
