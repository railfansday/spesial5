School specialist’s time spent on IEPs after hours may count toward FMLA eligibility

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The finding comes in a recent U.S. Department of Labor opinion letter involving a salaried occupational therapist.

DOL’s Nov. 17 FMLA opinion letter could affect how school districts and other employers determine whether an employee is eligible for leave.

Generally, employees are eligible for leave for a serious health condition or other covered reason if they work for an employer that is subject to the law for at least 12 months, have at least 1,250 hours of service with the employer during the 12 months before their FMLA leave starts, and work at a location where the employer has at least 50 employees within 75 miles.

Without accurate timekeeping, it’s up to the employer to demonstrate that an employee does not qualify for FMLA leave, DOL said. And if that lack of recordkeeping is because the employee is exempt from the Fair Labor Standards Act’s recordkeeping requirements for minimum wage and overtime purposes, “eligibility for FMLA leave is presumed for any employee who has been employed for at least 12 months,” according to FMLA regulations.

FMLA requires employers to provide up to 12 workweeks of unpaid, job-protected leave annually for qualifying family and medical reasons.

For the occupational therapist in question, DOL said, their after-hours tasks must be counted as hours of service. And while the agency said it didn’t have enough information to determine that the individual’s travel time must definitively count toward their service hours, it appeared likely to be the case.
