I've Had This Modular Sofa for Four Years, and Its Secret Twin "Bed" Is a Hosting Game-Changer

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Every item on this page was hand-picked by a House Beautiful editor. We may earn commission on some of the items you choose to buy.

The first couch I ordered for my basement was a bust. No matter how hard they tried, the delivery men couldn’t angle it to get it down the narrow stairs. Because I live in a 1930s home, all the charm turns to chaos when it comes to fitting new furniture. I was literally stuck because a family of five was visiting for the holidays and they had nothing to sit (or sleep) on. Plus, what even is a basement without a comfy couch?

Ideally, I wanted a sleeper sofa to accommodate my guests and let my kids have sleepovers. But there was absolutely no way I would risk buying another couch that couldn’t come apart and was stuck in a corner. For round two, I knew that I needed a modular sectional. After a bit of research, I came across the Burrow Range sectional and was elated that it could be assembled like a building-block puzzle that can grow over time if I wanted to add more sections.

Since the basement isn’t huge, my selection was a four-piece model that included two arm pieces as well as an ottoman. Because my dreams of a sleeper were no more, I liked that the Range has flippable top cushions that, when pulled back, could make a twin-size bed. Happily, four years later, the sectional has survived many, many sleepovers and now resides in my bedroom. Read on to see how it transitioned from child-hopping to a peaceful nook two floors up.

Best For: A growing household or someone who likes to change the layout of their space often.

Not ideal for: Someone who is very tall. If you are taller than 6 feet, the sofa may sit too low for you.

Price at review (4-piece sectional lounger): $1,271 (originally $2,119)

Bottom Line: The modular sectional is comfortable and won’t sag or end up with flat cushions, even after years of use.

Because I needed the sectional before the winter holidays, I was glad that I could order by early December and still get my sofa before Santa came to town. The four big boxes for the cushions and a little box for legs came to my front door within a week.

My husband and I brought down all the boxes to the basement where we started to unbox and assemble. The overall process took no more than 30 minutes. In a nutshell, you need to line up the holes with the metal braces and secure those with large plastic knob screws. The cushions have two strips of Velcro that stick to the black fabric at the base to keep them from sliding out from beneath.

The sofa pieces are easy to secure together.

Compared to my living room couch, which is a mid-century modern style West Elm reversible sectional, the Burrow has a bit more bounce to the cushion. While the seats are softer, I would still describe it as medium-firm. As soon as I sit down, it feels relaxing. Because it is only 28 inches high, it is a little lower than I am used to. (This still works out because I can slouch down and stretch my feet up on the ottoman when I watch a movie.)

Later, when I moved the sectional up to my bedroom, I lie across all three seat cushions, with my head resting on the arm cushion, as if I am reading in bed. The arm cushions are slightly angled, with more padding at the bottom to create a gentle incline that supports my head.

I chose the Stone Gray upholstery, an almost salt-and-pepper look, in performance flatweave. This is my third woven upholstery sofa, and it is the softest. It feels similar to a worn-in tweed jacket. I think it actually got a bit softer with age. I am happy to report there is absolutely no pilling or loose threads.

My biggest pet peeve is saggy couch cushions. I cannot stand it when the fabric looks crumpled on the seats, and there are sloppy valleys on the back cushions. After having this sectional for four years, I can vouch that it still looks the same as the day we assembled it. Which is a small miracle because when the Range lived in the basement, it was base camp for elementary-age boys playing video games and jumping around. If for some reason you wanted to fluff, you can unzip the underside of the back cushions and take out the filling to give it more shape.

In terms of stains and spills, if any kids spilled something on the sofa, I don’t know about it because it has no markings. Come to think of it, I don’t think I’ve ever had to clean the sofa beyond brushing off crumbs.

I did catch my cat trying to stretch (scratch) on the back of the sectional a few times, but I shooed him away, and he did not make it a habit. When I checked, there were never any loops or snags. (I cannot say the same about another woven sofa that is a victim of his claws.)

The cushions are still in terrific shape.

The flexibility of the Burrow Range sectional is everything. That’s the main reason I would highly recommend this couch. Not only do the pieces come in individual boxes that can fit through narrow doorways of older homes, but you can customize the sectional to your exact needs.

Originally, I needed something to help with hosting my out-of-town family, but this sectional has proven again and again that it’s a high-quality piece that can hold up for years to come. I still can’t believe that the cushions are in tip-top shape.

Pricewise, it’s a solid buy for mid-range furniture, especially because I don’t see a reason to replace it any time soon. Even when I ended up getting a new green modular sectional to go with the new wallpaper that I installed during my basement makeover, I found a spot for it in my bedroom. The Range now serves as a reading nook or a makeshift WFH station when I don’t want to stay in bed but feel too lazy to walk down the stairs to my home office. I added a Häti pouf with a scalloped EtúHome board next to the Burrow ottoman, and it serves as a perch for my coffee.

All in all, I am very happy with this purchase. It is durable, comfortable, and boasts clean lines and a silhouette that is approachable, but a bit more interesting than a standard traditional sofa.
