'That's An Error' - Xbox Engineer Denies 360 Games Are Being Teased For Disc To Digital

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Update [Fri 13th Nov 2026, 5:45pm]: Well, unfortunately we have answer - Xbox engineer Eden Marie has confirmed over on BlueSky that the inclusion of Fable III in this image was an "error".

While the post itself is private, it reads as follows (and thanks to Pure Xbox user TheBillConiglio for alerting us to this):

"Bill: Seeing an image of a library with Fable 3 having a disc to digital badge. Error, or......"

Original Story: Yesterday, Xbox starting rolling out three new console features for Xbox Insiders to try out: rotating home backgrounds, the ability to choose which side the Xbox guide pops open on, and new ways to organise disc to digital games.

They're all cool features that we're definitely keen to play around with, but what got our attention most was the image Xbox used to show off the new disc to digital update. Particularly, the games featured with the D2D licence icon plopped in the bottom-left corner of each game tile.

Did Xbox just tease the inclusion of Xbox 360 games in the D2D programme? Well, that would be true if the image depicting Fable III is to be taken at face value. At a glance, you'd probably see the image above and think, 'great, that's awesome', but the more you look at it, the weirder it gets.

Avowed famously launched on Xbox without a physical disc. So, if the theory about Xbox 360 games joining the programme is on the money, does that mean we're getting a physical copy of Avowed too? And what about Age of Empires IV? That never received a physical release either. Then If we really want to go Enola Holmes on this image, Forza games aren't D2D compatible.

We're thinking someone at Xbox might've messed up with this image. Announcements of this magnitude shouldn't be buried in an Xbox Insider update. Xbox should be shouting about it on the front page. So, why do an Xbox 360 game and two digital-only titles have D2D icons attached to them? I could think of a lazy reason.

Only time will tell, but consider us sceptical for now. We've chalked this one up to a blunder from Xbox, although it wouldn't surprise us if Xbox 360 games did eventually join the D2D programme. And hey, it's Xbox's birthday this Sunday. Maybe there's more to this than meets the eye.

@BacklogBrad the issue isn't with converting 360 or OG XBox game discs to digital versions, the issue is that those discs don't have a unique identifier assigned to them (this started with the XBox One generation, and not all of the early XBox One games have them).

Without the UID there's no way to assign a specific disc to the digital license, meaning one disc could be used to grant anyone digital access to that game. The UID prevents this as you need to revalidate the disc licence periodically, and if you sell the disc / lend it to someone, it will validate on their device, which will invalidate your license the next time your console does an online check in.
