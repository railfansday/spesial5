Bitcoin’s 16% slide cost Yi He $97M, but her message to investors remains — HODL!

Two developments have caught the market’s attention.

Yi He, Co-CEO of Binance, revealed that her portfolio lost a total of $97.32 million over the last 30 days (17 October – 16 November). After adding in all transfers, this amounted to a total loss of $101.31 million. This, after Bitcoin lost over 16% of its value over the same timeframe — Its largest monthly loss in over four months.

On the institutional front, JPMorgan has partnered with Singapore’s DBS Bank to launch a blockchain-based payment solution that allows institutional clients to transfer tokenized U.S dollar deposits between Singapore and the United States, 24/7.

Together, these developments show two different sides of the market.

On one hand, Yi He’s disclosure demonstrates just how deep the ongoing correction has been, with even major players taking a hit as selling pressure builds. On the other, JPMorgan’s partnership with DBS shows that institutional interest in blockchain is still holding up despite the FUD and broader market weakness.

This ties back to Yi He’s thesis that the ongoing pullback is only a short-term correction and there really isn’t any reason to panic. While prices continue to struggle, institutional activity suggests that long-term interest hasn’t disappeared.

Hence, the question is whether this conviction can eventually translate into a shift in sentiment.

According to the Bitcoin [BTC] Fear and Greed Index, sentiment levels fell to 10, indicating that the market was firmly in the “extreme fear” territory. The monthly average also seemed to be getting closer to this zone, with the same sitting at 28.2 at press time.

This suggests that fear is becoming more “persistent” rather than just a short-term reaction to market volatility. In fact, sentiment might soon be approaching levels last seen in February and June 2026, when Bitcoin faced deeper price declines.

And yet, Bitcoin’s Open Interest data showed that BTC was still accumulating new leveraged positions as it moved down from $83K to $74k. This may be evidence that while the market is becoming increasingly fearful, but it is yet to see leverage fully reset. This would leave BTC exposed to further volatility, especially if there isn’t fresh buying demand.

Against this backdrop, the significance of the two developments becomes clearer.

Extreme fear, Bitcoin’s 16% correction, and bearish on-chain signals could keep BTC under pressure in the near term. However, Yi He’s $97 million loss indicates not only the amount of stress on the market, but also that large institutions are continuing to increase/HODL their exposure to the blockchain space.

This might also mean that short-term market weakness has not necessarily changed the longer-term institutional outlook.

In this context, the 16% correction is more a general market correction rather than a loss of confidence in Bitcoin’s long-term potential.

Bitcoin’s 16% drop and high leverage point to further volatility.

JPMorgan’s partnership with DBS shows that blockchain adoption is on the right track, despite the downturn.
