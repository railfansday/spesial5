The XRP Ledger's native lending protocol entered a 14-day activation countdown on Friday after its amendments gained 29 of 35 validator votes, according to XRPScan. If support holds, the ledger will get native lending and borrowing as early as Dec. 11, for the first time since its launch in 2012.

Hussein Zangana, director of community at the XRP Ledger Foundation, cast the 29th vote with his validator and announced it in an X post on Friday. "I vote now for the Lending Protocol on the XRP Ledger and it's now in activation with 29 Yes votes," Zangana wrote.

I appreciate the efforts exercised by the community to give this amendment a good screening! For validators this is important assistance and help in making educated decisions. Let's dive into why i voted Yes for Lending on XRP🧵.

According to XRPScan, LendingProtocol and its update LendingProtocolV1_1 both reached 29 votes on Friday. The earliest activation date for LendingProtocolV1_1 is Dec. 11, and its support has to stay above 80% until then. SingleAssetVault, the third amendment in the package, has 30 votes and can activate as early as Dec. 9.

"Lending and borrowing unlock capital and liquidity on the XRP Ledger, provide yield and make XRP and XRPL assets usable as collateral," Zangana added in the same post.

Ripple engineer Vito Tumas first proposed the protocol in April 2024. According to the XLS-65 standard, each Single Asset Vault holds only one asset, such as XRP or RLUSD, and depositors get vault shares in the form of Multi-Purpose Tokens.

Under XLS-66, a loan broker uses that capital to issue loans with a fixed term and a fixed rate. The broker checks borrowers off-chain and can post its own first-loss capital to cover defaults. Interest payments go back into the vault, and the protocol has no on-chain liquidations.

"Represent value. Move value. Trade value. Now: finance value," Jazzi Cooper, head of product at RippleX, wrote on X about the vote. According to Cooper, the team made "deliberate design decisions: protocol native, built-in compliance, and off-chain underwriting."

J. Ayo Akinyele, head of engineering at RippleX, wrote that the vaults and the protocol went through "multiple independent audits, a public Attackathon, formal verification, AI-assisted reviews, community testing, and validator review." Ripple and Common Prefix did the formal verification in the Lean 4 theorem prover. "Traditional testing isn't enough when you're building DeFi directly into Layer-1," Tumas wrote.

Clearpool said that it would bring "real-world, institutional-ready lending infrastructure, built natively on XRPL." The platform plans a credit fund on the ledger with Ripple and Cicada Partners, and it has already tested a full loan cycle on Devnet. "We want to make real loans to real businesses," Tom Lombardi of Cicada Partners said.

Evernorth CEO Ashish Birla said that his company "is exploring how XLS-66 could support yield generation as part of our broader treasury strategy."

According to XRPScan, SingleAssetVault can go live on Dec. 9, and the two lending amendments can follow on Dec. 11 if their support holds.
