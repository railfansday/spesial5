Roads Less Traveled

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

I explore some alternative routes through the mountains to finish my hike in Pennsylvania. One works out well while the other does not.

As predicted, I was all set on sleep around 5:00AM. Less predictable was the state of my left hip, which I had been sleeping on throughout most of the night and which was experiencing some discomfort. Counterintuitively, my “good” night of sleep with less waking during the night meant I spent too much time sleeping on one side of my body. Normally I wake up a handful of times and roll around to change my sleeping position. Time would tell if that would spill over into my hike. I opened my tent to hang up my water filter to get some water ready for breakfast and noticed that my interior mesh was ever so slightly damp. Indeed, the inside of my rain fly was wet. It hadn’t rained, I figured we must have had an unusually humid night. Not ideal. I was determined to get an early start on the day, I managed to have breakfast in my tent and get packing underway before sunrise. I scrambled onto Table Rock to watch the sun rise over Third Mountain. It was somewhere in the mid 30s this morning, with the humidity the cold was piercing through my shoulder-season apparel like it was barely there. Movement is the best cure so after snapping a few pictures of the sunrise I headed back to my tent, finished packing, and at 7:15AM was heading north on the trail eager to move Pennsylvania into the “Done” column.

It didn’t take long to notice that my left leg was feeling a little goofy. There was a sensation in my hip that made me feel like I was stepping too narrow. I try swinging my leg around to loosen it up, but it didn’t help. My only choice was to hope it would go away once I warmed up.

A darn good spot to camp for the night if I say so myself.

Last evening I studied the topographic map on FarOut and found that the gravel road between Second and Third Mountain is a viable alternative route back to my van. It would be roughly the same distance as the AT, but flatter and with less rocks, assuming gravel doesn’t count as rocks. I love a good alternate route that let’s me get a different perspective on my return leg so it felt like a no-brainer.

The day began to slowly warm up as I hiked along Peters Mountain. By the time I was descending back into the valley the sun was shining directly on the south side of the ridge and providing plenty of warmth. Ahead was the climb up Third Mountain, knowing that I was about to get warm awfully I fast I stopped to shed my sleeping layers and load them into my pack. To connect to the gravel road, I needed to take the Knauber Trail up Third Mountain. It proved a tough, direct route up the mountain. The trail didn’t seem to get nearly as many visitors as the AT. There were leaves piled up several inches thick and I was having to heave myself up the trail with my trekking poles since my shoes were prone to slipping. I arrived at the crest of the ridge in a huff, and promptly felt the temperature drop now that I was between mountain ridges, out of the sun, and no longer climbing. My gloves went back on, and I decided to tough it out in my single layer sun hoodie rather than dig out my other layers.

Sunlight beams onto the trail toward Third Mountain.

The Knauber Trail proves to be a steep, leafy challenge.

The gravel road passed through a mountain gap and descended into the Stony Creek Valley. On the way, I passed an upright stone marker noting the spot where members of the Kabob Hiking Club have been gathering annually to commemorate the founding of their club. Apparently it began in 1934. This location is well off the beaten track. I suppose you could drive down the gravel road easily enough, but be prepared for a long drive.

Once the road settled between Second and Third Mountain it followed Stony Creek for what I imagine is somewhere between 8 and 10 miles. There are stretches of the road where you can see a full mile ahead, maybe more. It was honestly a little unsettling. Imagine hiking 10 minutes and the view ahead hardly looks any different. I found it more comforting to focus on the ground below me, at least that was moving. I enjoyed the steady progress, though there wasn’t much to see or report on, just pine trees and not a soul to be found.

The gravel road descends sharply to the Stony Creek Valley.

Once I had made my way down the gravel road I took a moment to visit a few other markers at Rausch Creek. One sign explained the structures next to the creek were indeed filters as I had guessed yesterday. They use limestone to clean the water, which is why it looked so clear. I suspect the light gray color of the limestone settling on the bottom of the creek also helps the water to look brighter and more clear than if the creek bed were covered in natural rock.

As I made my way back toward Second Mountain I passed back through a flat section of trail that seems to flood regularly. Beavers had dammed some of the creeks and the soft soil caused many of the trees to fall, creating a relatively open area. Oddly, a plaid shirt was hung in the middle of the bog, one can only assume it belonged to one of the beaver’s victims.

With one final push I made my way up to Second Mountain where I made a miscalculation. High on the success of my gravel road alternate route, I decided to double down and take what appeared to be a shortcut. The AT crosses an abandoned dirt road which seemed to lead back toward the road not far from the trailhead where my van was parked. I found it odd that the road bent at a 90 degree angle just ahead on the map, but thought little of it. I forged along the road and did see a turn, but it was slightly too early to be the 90 degree turn on the map. Continuing on, the road continued straight ahead, which wasn’t shown on the map. There was also no 90 degree turn where it should have been. I decided to stick with the road that I was on even though I didn’t know exactly where it would go. Really, there were only two options, it would either end up near the road I was looking for, or it would turn a bit and eventually rejoin the AT.

It turned out to be the latter, albeit after some unwelcome elevation gain that meant I probably lost a bit of time by taking this route. My hip, which had gotten better after the first mile or so, acted up again around mile 20. It is a good thing I have a day off tomorrow for it to bounce back. It was nearly 4:00PM when I was back on the road making my way back to my van. I decided to use the afternoon to drive down to Hagerstown, Maryland so I could spend all day tomorrow focusing on tying up any remaining loose ends before starting off on my final section of the trail.
