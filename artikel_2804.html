The Lakers may soon experiment with a very interesting lineup

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The benefit of having two elite ballhandlers is that one of them should always be on the floor. For the Lakers, when both Luka Dončić and Austin Reaves have been healthy this season, they have been staggered so that one of them is always playing.

But head coach JJ Redick and his staff are open to the idea of mixing things up where that's not the case in the future.

Ahead of the team's recent game against the Jazz, Redick was asked about Grimes' role this season and whether the team might keep him in the starting lineup even when Reaves returns from injury to help him keep his rhythm. While Redick was noncommittal on a starting lineup change, he did offer insight into another way the Lakers are looking to help the non-Luka and Reaves players find a rhythm.

"We're kind of talking about as a staff," Redick said, "but we just haven't had a stretch of time, a stretch of games to do this, is trying to find more minutes with, when we're healthy, with no Luka, no AR and and see if that can sort of get those guys going on their own. So then, second half when we kind of make those decisions in the fourth quarter about who we want to finish the game with, those guys are in a rhythm.

"I think that's probably what we're focused on once we're fully healthy rather than long term starting lineups because, again, I think with this group and the way this group, it's just an egoless group. We're going to be able to be flexible with the starting lineup and flexible with matchups."

There's not a great deal of harm in experimenting, even if it's an idea that feels a little far-fetched. The Lakers have struggled in the non-Luka minutes with Austin out, so I'm not sure it makes sense to go to those minutes when they are healthy.

The idea might make sense on paper. It gives other players more freedom. But it comes at the risk of not having elite players on the court.

We'll see whether Redick and his staff ever implement this idea. It feels like a pretty risky proposition. Is it worth potentially losing a couple games to try out this theory, especially if it doesn't work?
