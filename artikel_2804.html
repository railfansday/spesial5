2027 Bears mock draft: Finding a running mate for Jeffery Simmons

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Bears have a 7-2 record coming out of their bye week, and as their Week 11 matchup approaches against the Saints, their focus is heavy on the rest of the regular season and an expected postseason appearance, more so than what pick they'll have in the 2027 NFL Draft. That said, preparing for the draft is a year-round process, and for those interested in the upcoming class and which positions Chicago could focus on, I have you covered with another mock draft.

As always, I made these selections and simulated the picks of the 31 teams around me using PFN's free mock draft simulator, which recently underwent a fresh interface update. The picks are as follows:

The Bears made a massive splash trading for Jeffery Simmons a few weeks before the trade deadline, but he could use another upgrade alongside him on the defensive interior. Grady Jarrett is a potential cap casualty this offseason, and they don't have another else who's proven as even a high-level backup defender up the middle.

Ahmad Moten Sr. is a defensive tackle who can get it done on all three downs. He's quick off the snap, low to the ground, powerful in his lower half, and has a diverse arsenal of moves he can use to stack and shed blocks. He has the explosiveness to stress out interior defensive linemen as an immediate backfield penetrator, too. Though his pass-rushing efficiency has dipped in 2026 compared to 2025, he's a pro-ready run defender with the athleticism to project as a true three-down defender.

As things stand, the Bears will likely need to find a new starting cornerback in the 2027 offseason. Kyler Gordon and Malik Muhammad project as safe bets to return next year, but Jaylon Johnson is a potential cap casualty, and Tyrique Stevenson is in the last year of his rookie deal. With their play so far this season, neither can be relied upon too heavily as long-term fixtures in the starting lineup.

At 6'1" and 190 pounds, DJ McKinney is a lengthy cornerback with the physical profile to project as a future NFL starter along the boundary. A three-year full-time starter in college who shined at Colorado before transferring to Notre Dame for his 2026 season, McKinney is a competitive cornerback with a large catch radius, and he's super twitched-up for his size, changing direction well and sinking his hips into his transitions. Though his tackling form can be inconsistent, he shows willingness in run support, and with some better ball-tracking skills, he has some serious potential on Day 2.

All things considered, the Bears have a strong offensive line with solid depth, but Joe Thuney won't be around forever. He turned 34 years old on Thursday and has more football behind him than ahead of him. Luke Montgomery is a player I think would benefit from backing up as a rookie but has NFL starting-caliber tools. He's a savvy, refined lineman who lands his strikes accurately, buries defenders in the run game, and keeps his head on a swivel in pass protection. It also helps that he has taken reps at both guard spots and both tackle spots during his time at Ohio State, giving him key versatility that should help him get playing time.

An All-SEC freshman in 2023 with two years of starting tape at the SEC level, Raylen Wilson moves with a calculated, yet aggressive edge in pursuit. He has sharp pre-snap instincts and takes precise angles to the ball carrier in run support, and his straight-line speed helps him chase down running backs with ease. Though he won't wow you taking on blocks, I think Wilson is a perfectly capable linebacker with the potential to be a good LB3 in a defensive rotation as either a backup WILL or MIKE.

Melkart Abou Jaoude was a second-team All-ACC member for North Carolina in 2025, leading the conference with 10.5 sacks. Though his production has dipped this year, he's also battled through an injury, playing with a club on his hand. The 6'5", 260-pounder from Lebanon has impressive pop in his hands and good length, which helps him keep offensive tackles at bay. He has a high pass-rushing IQ and good anchor strength, and his ability to win the leverage battle is reflective of his district championship-winning days as a high school wrestler. Abou Jaoude doesn't have the elite burst and flexibility to project as an NFL starter, but I like him a lot as a depth addition late on Day 3.
