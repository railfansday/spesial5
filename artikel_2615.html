The Fearsome Foursome set the standard. Can the Rams’ new front four live up to it?

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The Los Angeles Rams are bringing back the Fearsome Foursome on Wednesday night.

Not the one that terrorized NFL quarterbacks in the 1960s. That group belongs in football immortality.

This one has Aaron Donald, Myles Garrett, Kobie Turner, and Byron Young. Four defensive linemen with enough talent to make opposing quarterbacks think twice about hanging on to the football.

They haven't all been healthy enough to wreak havoc together.

Byron Young (left), Kobie Turner (left-middle), Braden Fiske (right-middle) and Myles Garrett (right) wearing the Rams' inaugural "Fearsome White" jerseys that will be unveiled during the Thanksgiving Eve game between the Green Bay Packers and Los Angeles Rams on Netflix (Credit: Los Angeles Rams)

With Turner returning from a groin injury and Garrett finally finding his footing after knee surgery, the Rams could have their entire defensive front available for the first time this season when they host the Green Bay Packers on Thanksgiving Eve.

And the timing couldn't be more appropriate.

The Rams will unveil their Fearsome White alternate uniforms Wednesday, paying tribute to the legendary defensive line of Deacon Jones, Merlin Olsen, Rosey Grier and Lamar Lundy.

Grier will be honored on the field alongside family members of the original Fearsome Foursome, while the Rams will wear throwback-inspired uniforms celebrating the franchise's defensive dominance of the 1960s.

But the current group doesn't want to spend the evening playing dress-up.

They want to make some history of their own.

"When we go out there and play to the best of our ability, and we rush four as one, five as one, we're a very, very scary unit," Turner said Monday. "I don't want to put too much out there up front. I want our work to be able to show itself, and I think it's going to show itself on Wednesday."

The Los Angeles Rams defensive front four, Lamar Lundy, left, Merlin Olsen, Rosey Grier, and Deacon Jones, pose in a 1964 photo. AP

That's a fairly bold statement from a player who hasn't shared the field with the Rams' complete collection of pass rushers this season.

Donald is a three-time NFL Defensive Player of the Year. Garrett is the reigning champ and has won the award twice. Young has developed into a dangerous edge rusher, while Turner provides the kind of interior disruption that can collapse a pocket before a quarterback finishes his drop.

Before the season, this group looked like a dream for defensive coordinator Chris Shula.

But on the field, it has largely remained an unfinished experiment.

Los Angeles Rams defensive tackle Aaron Donald (99) greets defensive end Myles Garrett (95) during an NFL game in Landover, Md., Sunday, Nov. 8, 2026. AP Photo/Stephanie Scarbrough

Garrett understands the history he's being asked to honor.

The Rams linebacker revealed Monday that he studied Jones while growing up, watching footage of the Hall of Fame pass rusher who helped popularize the quarterback sack as an official measure of defensive dominance.

"I was watching Deacon Jones in high school, watching 'Top 100,' listening to him speak, him coining the term 'sack,' how much he's done for this game," Garrett said.

But Garrett's admiration apparently went beyond watching old highlights.

Asked whether he ever tried Jones' infamous head slap, a technique that was eventually outlawed by the NFL, Garrett couldn't help laughing.

"In high school I did, for sure," Garrett admitted. "I got away with it for a little while too."

Somewhere, Jones would probably appreciate that confession.

Donald, meanwhile, said he has spent his Rams career trying to uphold the standard established by the franchise's original defensive giants.

"Coming here as a rookie, you heard about it," Donald said. "I always try to live up to their success and everything they were able to do."

The challenge Wednesday isn't simply honoring history. It's stopping a Green Bay offense capable of turning a broken play into a 50-yard touchdown.

Packers quarterback Jordan Love has developed a dangerous connection with his deep threats, including Christian Watson and Matthew Golden.

But throwing deep requires time, and that's precisely what the Rams hope to take away.

"They're going to take their shots. They're going to test our back end," Turner said. "But for them to even have that opportunity, they have to hold the ball a little bit longer. And that's where this defensive front comes into play."

Braden Fiske, Kobie Turner and Byron Young of the Los Angeles Rams celebrate a sack during a game against the New Orleans Saints in Inglewood, Calif., Nov. 2, 2025. Getty Images

Defensive coordinator Chris Shula has resisted comparisons between his current group and the original Fearsome Foursome, preferring to emphasize chemistry, preparation and getting his best players healthy.

It should be pointed out that the original four earned their reputation over years of dominance, not one nationally televised game.

But there's something fitting about the Rams finally unleashing their complete defensive front on a night dedicated to the men who helped redefine the position.

"It's our job to go out there on Wednesday night in front of the entire world and show that legacy, and make sure that they're proud," Turner said.

The Rams will provide the white uniforms, the vintage graphics and the historical tribute.

The rest is up to Donald, Garrett and company. After all, the best way to honor the Fearsome Foursome isn't by looking like them. It's by making Jordan Love feel what opposing quarterbacks felt 60 years ago.
