Takeaways coming off a Mile High loss

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

In an absolute ref show, the Celtics lost 105-101 to the Nuggets in a game marred by poor officiating and a free throw parade.

The only time in Joe Mazzulla's tenure that the Celtics have beat the Nuggets in Denver, Jokic didn't play in the game. Well, Jokic played on Wednesday night.

For the last few days, there's been a bit of a referendum on Boston's center position. Mitchell Robinson started the first five games at center and to his credit, that lineup has a +19.5 net rating. Neemias Queta had a run of nine before sitting out in favor of Luka Garza Sunday night in a loss against the Magic. In that game, Orlando hit 11-of-13 from inside the arc in the fourth quarter en route to outscoring Boston 54-32 in the paint. Losing that interior margin highlighted just how important that center position is, so much so that Mazzulla has said that it's the most important position on both ends of the court.

Queta was incredible in his return. His 17 points didn't come off alley-oops and dunker spot gimmes. Neemy grabbed seven offensive rebounds and on a night when the Celtics shot 11-of-44 from behind the arc, all of Queta's dirty work against the three-time MVP and inarguably the greatest center of his generation was absolutely needed in that slugfest.

The Celtics won the battle in trenches, outscoring the Nuggets in the paint 48-34 and grabbing four more offensive rebounds. Jokic didn't even score a point in the key until midway through the third quarter. Alas, it wasn't enough.

In the second quarter, ESPN's broadcast highlighted that the Celtics average the most bench minutes in the league (45.7%). Sure, two blowout wins in the NBA Cup helps this early in the season, but Boston's depth was always going to be their strength.

It helps to have a former Sixth Man of the Year anchoring your second unit. Payton Pritchard extended double-digit scoring streak to eight games with 15. points

There's also the underrated Sam Hauser who hit 3-of-6 from behind the arc. Here's CelticsBlog's Nate Moskowitz on the third longest tenured Celtic and calming force off the bench.

With a very fluid offensive approach for Boston this year, Hauser has played a big part in their on and off-ball screening game. He's been the recipient, flying around stagger screens and knocking shots down on the move. He's also brought his usual ghost screen and pick-and-pop ability, creating advantages for himself or the ballhandler.

And out of nowhere, Chris Cenac Jr. has worked his way into a spark plug role. After showing signs in the blowout win over the Mavericks, he's earned consequential playing time against Orlando and Denver, including being part of the closing lineup last night and finishing with six points and six rebounds.

The Celtics subs are sixth in the league in points (43.8) and conversely, the Nuggets are 26th (29.1). Last night was no different; they outscored the Nuggets' second unit by 14, despite 18 from the elder statesman and Celtics killer DeMar DeRozan.

Unfortunately, that was negated at the free throw line on a night when both teams were miserable from 3. Boston registered 30 fouls — their highest total of the season — including 20 in the second half. That all resulted in a 29-12 edge in free throw attempts in favor of the hosts.

If you can stomach missing two wide open threes by Tatum and White and a bunny hop floater, you'll be rewarded at the end by Queta's unrelenting hustle around the rim, great hands, and dunk to take the lead.

"He has a level of talent and athleticism and has the ability to make plays on both ends of the floor…he's got the versatility, he's got the toughness, he's got the athleticism, he's got the talent. He just needs the reps to get better and better. I thought that was the bright spot tonight: his development from game 1 to game 2. He's just got to keep it up." — Joe Mazzulla on Chris Cenac Jr.

The first-round pick spent much of the first month of the season with the Maine Celtics with some mixed results, but he's sparkled in his call-up so far. Mazzulla has been cycling through Queta, Robinson, and Garza at the five and mixing in some double big looks, too. With CCJ showing some potential, it's becoming more and more evident that Cenac Jr. should be part of that conversation as well.

The Celtics will continue this four-game road trip with back-to-back games over the weekend in Sacramento and Golden State before returning to the East Coast with an NBA Cup game in the nation's capital.
