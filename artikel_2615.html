Kyle Shanahan on the Brian Flores defense: &quot;It's not just a fad&quot;

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

49ers coach Kyle Shanahan has never beaten a Brian Flores defense. Which might be the most impressive thing that can be said about the Brian Flores defense.

They meet again on Sunday night in Mexico City, when Shanahan's 49ers play the Vikings, whose defense is coordinated by Flores.

"He's more unique than all the other 31 guys," Shanahan told reporters on Wednesday regarding Flores. "He's the one guy who commits to this and it's not just a fad for these nine games or whatever. He's been doing it his whole time in Minnesota. He did the same thing in Miami. He's as good as it gets at that stuff.

"Anybody who lives in that world as much as he does, it's usually a matter of time. I think offensive coaches used to like to say that early on, but it's too many years of it and it's too many games in this year that they're sound. They put so much pressure on the quarterback, the play-caller, the O-Line, the receivers, on everybody. A lot of times when you go into an NFL game, the pressure's really on people on third down and stuff like that because those are things where everything can come and when you play against Brian it's 70-straight plays of that."

It's not easy to beat in part because it's almost impossible to simulate in practice. As Devin McCourty has said, you think you have a plan and then the game starts and everything changes.

It changes in part because Flores seems to be constantly self-scouting and adjusting, defying the films to create pressure in new ways.

The goal is to eliminate the offense's built-in advantage that comes from knowing what the play will be by creating confusion for the entire offense. Who's rushing? Who's dropping into coverage? Who do I block? If that guy doesn't rush, what do I do?

It makes for a compelling chess match, with the 49ers needing to find a way to get the Minnesota defense on its heels.

A strong running game is a must. Wearing down the defense physically is required. When inevitable passing situations arise, the quarterback needs to quickly find the open man (if there is one) and get the ball out before the walls close in.

Sometimes, they close quickly. The strategy results in plenty of guys getting a free run at the quarterback, forcing a deer-in-the-headlights response — right before impact.
