Greenwich holds off Ridgefield for 4th straight Class LL girls swimming title, 33rd overall

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

WEST HARTFORD - The Greenwich and Ridgefield girls swimming and diving programs have engaged in plenty of battles over the years, and the CIAC Class LL championship meet on Tuesday night at the Cornerstone Aquatics Center was no different.

For the Cardinals, depth turned out to be key as they captured their fourth straight Class LL title and 33rd overall in program history. Greenwich scored 924 points to second-place Ridgefield's 878.5. Amity was third with 510.5 points.

"I felt pretty confident going into this because I knew that everyone on the team has been working super hard, and I had a lot of faith in us," Greenwich junior Olivia DeVries said.

With sophomore Alden Charette's first-place finish in Class LL diving, the Cardinals won three individual events and one relay. Seniors Kensie Worden (50 freestyle, 23.59) and Julia Kozma (100 butterfly, 57.70) won gold medals, as did the opening 200 medley relay team of Charette, junior Sara Jee, Kozma and Worden (1:47.96).

Greenwich claimed 18 individual medals, including three golds, three silvers and three bronzes. DeVries claimed bronze medals in the 200 freestyle and 500 freestyle.

"I'm super proud of the team because we all have worked so hard in practice. The captains have done such a good job to keep us motivated and I'm just very proud of how hard we worked," DeVries said. "Every single swimmer made a difference, every single spot. If you were in the C final, the A final, everyone makes a difference. We are all cheering for each other, and I think that's what makes Greenwich special."

"We had some really great swims from all the girls, but especially our senior class," Greenwich coach Megan Kelly said. "I think it really means a lot to them, and to see them swim with such heart was really great."

Senior Ellison Charette was second in the 200 IM and 500 freestyle, and she was part of the second-place 200 and 400 freestyle relay teams.

"Ellison, she fought hard in both of her races. She had some really good swims. Overall, I was really, really pleased," Kelly said. "They didn't win the (200) free relay, but they went 1:35, which is All-American consideration time. That was pretty impressive."

Greenwich's second-place 200 freestyle relay finished in 1:35.79. Ridgefield's first-place relay of sophomore Zsofia Milak, senior Dalia Katra, sophomore Mary Kelly and junior Ellie Griffin swam the race in 1:35.21, which broke Ridgefield's 2022 state record of 1:35.48.

Milak had a terrific night for Ridgefield, winning individual gold medals in the 200 freestyle (1:50.20) and 100 freestyle (50.95). She was also part of the first-place 400 freestyle relay (3:32.60), along with Kelly, junior Chloe Archibald and sophomore Charlie Palmer. Kelly won the 500 freestyle in 4:59.44.

Norwalk/McMahon junior standout Emma Giafardino won gold medals in the 200 IM (2:05.71) and 100 breaststroke (1:04.71). Trumbull sophomore Noelle Ivanovich won the 100 backstroke in 57.47.

Greenwich and Ridgefield will likely be the top contenders Sunday for the State Open at Yale.

"I think across the board there were some really great swims. It was a battle though, and it's always fun to have a battle," Megan Kelly said. "We had it at FCIACs, now we've had it here, and Opens is going to be another battle. It makes it a lot more fun this way."
