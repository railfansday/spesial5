Grading the Mavericks: Dallas’ accountability helped prevent a serious fall

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The Mavericks went 4-2 over the last two weeks and sit at 11-7, in eighth place in the West. They lost back-to-back road games to Cleveland (127-117) and Boston (131-94), but rebounded with four straight wins against Golden State (116-94), the Clippers (110-100), Utah (116-114), and Denver (120-101). Cooper Flagg led the team in scoring with 23.7 points per game.

The Mavericks did not start the last two weeks well. Losing back-to-back road games in embarrassing fashion, then giving a suboptimal performance against a really hurt Golden State team was pretty dire, despite the large margin of victory. The three games since have been pretty convincing; the Mavericks are a good team somewhere in the realm of "better than bad, not elite, but can win on any night".

We have seen the emergence, or more accurately, the rebirth of Caleb Martin in the last six games. He is scoring over 10 points per game, shooting 56.5 percent from three, and grabbing 2.3 steals per game over that stretch. Martin is the true definition of a glue guy and has held the team together in their four-game win streak.

The win against Denver was incredible. It was an utterly dominant defensive performance. It is the type of win you have to build off of and refuse to let the train slow down. It was also Dallas' third NBA Cup victory, meaning if they beat Phoenix on Friday, they will win their group.

Dallas' two losses that applied to this round's grades were part of a three-game skid that lacked effort in every sense of the word. Losing to a Cleveland team that sat all of their starters after travel issues was bad, but going into Boston the next day and losing the game in roughly six minutes (after Dallas went down 24-4) was a true wake-up call. Cooper Flagg said after the loss: "I don't think we competed at a high enough level, you know, throughout the three games".

Every viewer noticed that, but having a guy in the fire acknowledge their shortcomings showed they could get through the adversity. And they did, to the tune of four straight wins. Even after the third victory in Utah, where they squandered a 17-point lead in three-and-a-half minutes, Kyrie Irving held the team, including the coaches, after practice to run in preparation for their game in Denver's altitude. It paid off with a 19-point road victory.

That is the sign of a good team. Being able to have honest conversations about your play and notice when things need to change is a key component of success. The Mavericks clearly have that. There will be more adversity and plenty more losses to come this season. If the Mavericks can lean on this framework, they will be solid, no matter what happens.

The Mavericks already have a weird roster, but the player that sticks out the most is Naji Marshall. He is not a good enough defender to justify the offensive dominance, and he turns the ball over far too much to be handling the ball at the rate he does. Part of his 20 percent usage rate is the fact that the roster is lacking ball handlers, but some of it, and perhaps the most frustrating part, is that he simply chooses to play hero ball far too often.

It's not clear when you look at his 10.9 points in 24.1 minutes per game. And sometimes he does play well enough to matter. But it's the choice to keep a dribble handoff, or the pass that he forces in transition, or the insistence on taking floaters and shrinking the floor that really hurts the team. The extension Dallas signed him to already feels cumbersome, and I expect the best version of this team to not have him on the floor. Whether that is because he is benched or traded away, I am not sure.

Maybe we have been spoiled by the likes of Luka Doncic and Cooper Flagg, or it's the guys picked behind him playing well, but Johnson Jr., the ninth overall pick in this past draft, has felt overwhelmingly underwhelming. His athleticism does not jump off the screen a lot of the time, his offensive awareness is sketchy, and he fouls a bit too much to stay in the game consistently. That being said, he has shown flashes of being a really good glue piece to a great defensive team.

One of these came against Dallas' statement win in Denver, where he scored nine points and had a team-high 10 rebounds. He played excellent defense, had a monster dunk, moved his feet incredibly well, and impacted winning on all fronts. It is this type of game that makes you wonder why he can't do this all the time. Some of it may be physical, with the shin splints still affecting him. A lot of it is probably mental. But either way, although the consistency isn't there yet, you can see the vision finally.
