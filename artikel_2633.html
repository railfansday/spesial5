HPU women sink Hampton, improve to 6-0

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

HIGH POINT — High Point University continued its strong start while Hampton struggled mightily.

The Panthers gained momentum offensively as the game went along, held the Pirates to single-digit scoring in every quarter, and won 81-21 in nonconference women's basketball Monday evening in the Qubein Center.

"I thought tonight was just a combination of we were at an elite level of execution defensively from a focus standpoint," High Point coach Wyatt Foust said. "Everybody that came in executed the defensive game plan great. They knew where we could take chances, where we didn't need to.

"It was a team effort to do a really good job on their best player, who's a first team all-conference level player in their league. And they just had a bad night. We're grateful we got to play a lot of guys. And just to see everybody celebrate each other, it's good to get everybody in there."

Meredith Tippner had 16 points, 11 rebounds and five assists to lead the Panthers (6-0). It was her third straight double-double.

"My coaches and my teammates have empowered me — that's got to be a role that I take for this team," Tippner said. "Part of it is like just go be a dog. I have people around me that are just like, 'You can do that.' I don't think I could be kind of that mentality without the people surrounding me. So it's been pretty cool."

Taylor Feldman added 13 points, eight rebounds and five assists. Juliann Woodard had 12 points and nine rebounds, while Rylee Bess had 11 points. Both Woodard and Bess made three 3s as High Point totaled nine for the game.

On offense, the Panthers, who tallied 17 assists, shot 48% from the field for the game — improving each quarter from 35% in the first to 63% in the fourth as their lead peaked at 61 in the final minute.

On defense, High Point held former Big South foe Hampton (1-4), led by Kennedy Hall with six points, to just 11.9% on 7-of-59 shooting — the lowest percentage and fewest made field goals by an opponent in program history.

The result was a 26-point advantage in the first half, a 33-10 lead at halftime and a 37-point difference in the second half (the second-largest margin in program history). The win was the 14th-largest in program history.

All 11 available players played — 10 scored while all 11 had at least one rebound. And High Point won for the sixth straight time to start the season for the first time in its Division I history.

The Panthers will play their next three on the road — Thursday at Vermont, plus Nov. 23 against Grand Canyon and Nov. 24 against Arkansas in the San Diego Classic. They will next play at home Nov. 29 against Army.

"They've worked so hard," Foust said. "I would put the work they've put in against any team in the entire country, so they deserve that recognition. They deserve to enjoy that and carry that through today. But we didn't come here to just be 6-0.

"We want to keep pressing and keep pushing. I think that's the expectation. And I think that we're going to win them all. We are excited and we'll celebrate today and then we'll turn the page and get ready to go. We've got a really, really tough Vermont team at their place coming up."
