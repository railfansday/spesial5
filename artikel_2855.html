Division games a true barometer of Raiders progress, or lack of it

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Divisional games just hit different in the NFL.

Featuring the twice-a-year meetings which lead to some heated rematches, the weight division games carry are much heavier than the standard affair of matchups league schedule makers put forth amongst the 32 teams. When the AFC and NFC teams in the East, North, South, and West tango, the playoff stakes are higher (I'll explain this more below) alongside familiarity creating either nail-biter meetings lopsided beatdowns.

It'll be intriguing to see which way the Las Vegas Raiders road trip to the Denver Broncos this Sunday plays out. Will it be an AFC West slugfest that's close? Or will the bucking Broncos trample the visiting Raiders?

With four of the final seven games on Las Vegas' slate being AFC West tilts — at Denver this Sunday; hosting the Los Angeles Chargers (Week 14, Dec. 13); hosting the Broncos (Week 15, Dec. 20); and at the Kansas City Chiefs (Week 18, Jan. 10) — we're going to see how much progress Klint Kubiak's Raiders have made — or the lack of it.

Kubiak, the heralded offensive mind who the Silver & Black tabbed as its 27th head coach in franchise history, has a storied history with Denver as he grew up around the team thanks to his father Gary, who spent decades as a Broncos player, offensive coordinator, head coach, and senior personnel advisory.

"Yeah, many memories. Looking forward to going back to the stadium and being a part of this game. Extremely excited for our team," the stoic younger Klint Kubiak said when asked about a memory that stands out as he heads back to Denver, this time, as a rookie head coach. "I think when I was blessed enough to get hired here to coach the Raiders, I thought about games in Denver and games in Kansas City and being a part of this division, so it's something that I've definitely thought about before. And now that we're in it, we're in the season. It's just next practice."

Kubiak is set to lead his 4-6 Raiders against Sean Peyton's 7-2 Broncos highlighting the different spectrums of success the two division foes are currently at. A win keeps Denver in-line with the 7-2 Chiefs and an all-important AFC West title and high seed in the postseason race. A victory by Las Vegas puts a nice shiner on Denver's division title hunt.

As Broncos wide receiver Courtland Sutton aptly mentioned above, the division games often lead to close, hard-fought one-score contests because teams know each other so well that tendencies are identified and counters properly installed. Even when one team is the cellar desperately trying to climb out, it can give a team atop the mountain with lofty goals in their eyes a difficult time gameday. This is a direct result of that deep familiarity and competitive drive amongst players and coaches.

As they say, familiarity breeds contempt and there's no love lost between the Raiders and Broncos — heck, the entire AFC West.

Las Vegas intends to be in the spot Denver is now — fighting for the division crown, a ticket to the postseason dance, and a legitimate chase for another Lombardi Trophy — and the future is now as the team traded trusty veteran Kirk Cousins and are now on the Fernando Mendoza era at quarterback. The rookie and No. 1 overall pick in the 2026 NFL Draft is well aware of what Week 11 presents for the Silver & Black.

"It's a great challenge. It's a rivalry game. If you can't get excited for a rivalry game, then there might be something wrong with you," Mendoza said. "So, I'm incredibly excited to take on the challenge. They're a great opponent. They have a great team. They have a great coach and a great quarterback. So, I'm looking forward to taking it on with my teammates."

Through the 10 games the Raiders have played this season, the team is 1-1 in AFC West play (beat the Chargers 26-14 in Week 2; lost to the Chiefs 30-27 in Week 4) and are 3-3 against AFC foes. The Broncos, on the other hand, are coming off their bye week and are 2-1 in division games played (lost the Chiefs 31-10 in Week 1; beat the Chargers 23-17 in Week 5, topped the Chiefs 28-24 in Week 8) and are 3-1 in AFC play through their nine games on the year.

Kubiak needs to get complementary football from his Raiders where his offense combines with Rob Leonard's defense, and Joe DeCamillis' special teams unit to lift each unit up against a Broncos team eyeing back-to-back AFC West titles, something the Chiefs won nine-straight seasons from 2016-2024.

"Yeah, absolutely. Obviously, I've learned how big of an operation this is, and how it takes a team to go win a football game," Kubiak said on what he's learned about himself 10 games into his head coaching career. "It's every week I'm learning new things. But the great thing about this whole experience is surrounding yourself with excellent people, and from our personnel staff to our coaching staff to our players, I could not have come into a better situation with the type of people."

"Yeah, it's going to be very loud. They have a great fan base, and that's something we've been practicing at practice, whether it's the crowd noise, to make sure we get adjusted to that. It's going to be a great environment," the rookie said. "But it is new. I've never started on the road before, so I'm looking forward to the challenge."

"Protection is an all 11 thing. It's everybody. The run game is an all 11 thing. That's the greatest thing about football; is every play all 11 people are involved. It's not just one person making a play. It's all 11 coming together for success. So, everybody has a role in a touchdown play. Everybody has a role in a sack. Everybody has a role in an explosive run. Everybody has a role in a tackle for loss. So, specifically, you look at each individual play and you coach every single position on how can we get better in that circumstance to make that play successful."

—Las Vegas Raiders offensive coordinator Andrew Janocko on the shared responsibility of accounting for cornerback blitzes

"Yeah, at times. We're not playing well enough. We're not playing up to the standard we need to be. So, effort covers up a lot. So, there's definitely room for improvement there. And to say it's division from the start, no, we're not playing at a high enough or good enough level right now. So, there's definitely room for improvement."

—Las Vegas Raiders defensive coordinator Rob Leonard on how the fast and aggressive play style he wants from his defenders has translated into game days
