Knicks Bulletin: ‘The cardiac Knicks find a way’

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The same we thought we'd never see GTA VI hit the proverbial shelves…

…I'm sure there'll be one day the Raptors beat New York again. Not yesterday, though.

Here's a bunch of quotes and comments coming off another Knicks dub in Toronto.

On Darko Rajaković's coaching and the Raptors' defensive intensity:

"Darko does a heckuva job… those guys play hard, really good defensively, give us fits."

On Jalen Brunson's late four-point play creating the separation needed to beat Toronto:

"That created the separation that we needed to be able to put them away. So just a big shot. It was one of those things where the play broke down, and we kept looking for an opportunity while spacing the floor. And Jalen found one and let it fly."

On the Knicks' late-game execution and contributions from Towns, Anunoby, Bridges and Shamet:

"I thought our execution, especially down the stretch, the last few minutes of the game, was at a pretty high level, on both ends of the floor, but particularly offensively. Our pacing, our spacing, our screening — we took care of the basketball down the stretch.

"We got a lot of different guys that produced in a lot of different ways tonight. Obviously, KAT continues to be a double-double machine. OG [Anunoby] was our defensive player of the game. … Mikal was good for us, especially down the stretch. And Landry Shamet was huge."

On his improved relationship with Karl-Anthony Towns and the importance of adapting his coaching philosophy:

"It's just part of being around each other. You hope your relationship with everybody grows. That's not gonna always happen but you hope it does. KAT's got a really good feel of how to play the game. Obviously he's a perennial All-Star. So those things you've always got to take into account when it comes to trying to listen or try to adapt more to what their style of play is.

"As a head coach — come in, it's your first year, and you have an idea what you want to do. Sometimes it doesn't fit. Offensively, or defensively. And that's when you've got to pivot. And so we kept pivoting and pivoting and building until we found a nice little groove. We feel pretty good about each other right now."

On recovering from the ankle injury scare after his four-point play:

"Adrenaline was pumping, and went back out there."

On Josh Hart and Mikal Bridges regaining confidence and contributing to the Knicks' success:

"This is a huge night for them, the way they played. They're gaining confidence every single night. They're going to continue to do great things for us and they're only going to get better."

On the Knicks rediscovering their ability to win close games:

"A lot of times when we get in games like that, especially last year, the cardiac Knicks find a way."

On gaining Mike Brown's trust and having more input in the team's strategy:

"It's more familiarity with the system and building that trust with Mike where he can trust me with things I see and say, which started in the playoffs. So building off that this year. Having more input and him hearing me out."

On acting as a second coach and collaborating with Brown during games:

"Him trusting me like a second coach. Me and him are always coaching with each other in the game, looking at game plans and plays and maybe ideologies and things I see that can help our team win, especially in close games. Working with him and coaching on the floor. It's been great. It's been fun."

On how his relationship with Brown has improved since the championship run:

"We just grew. We've grown with each other. And obviously there's trust that has to be established and both of us trust each other a lot. And the last playoff run showed it. So just building off that trust and the relationship that was built in the playoffs and having it make our team better this year."

On Jose Alvarado's professionalism and importance to the Knicks' locker room:

"Jose is a consummate professional. He's probably one of the best locker room guys we have, and one of the best people we have on the bench. His energy, just his presence, with a jersey on or a warmup on, it means more for this team than what the stats show."

"Has been kicking my ass for a couple weeks."

On stepping up his game on Tuesday after struggling through a few games:

"I think it was a step in the right direction. I think I'm obviously getting into the flow, into the rhythm of things, but today was a step in the right direction. Still a lot to improve on, but you got to start stacking days at some point."

On how his contract situation can amplify the pressure of poor performances:

"That's a low-hanging fruit. Sometimes some of those things amplify because you're in a contract year, essentially. It's human nature.

"It's in the back of your mind, and when you start focusing on those things, negative outcomes happen."

On trying to overcome his struggles and improve his mindset:

"When you're in the middle of it, there's not much that can really help. At the end of the day, you just have to go out there. And I think today I had a little bit of a better spirit."

On the negative thought patterns that have affected his performances:

"I'm struggling to shoot the ball, but it's one of those things where you start looking at the negatives and that's all you see. You don't see some of the positives. Then it's a snowball effect. One thing happens and you're trying to get yourself out of a hole that you're not giving yourself the ability to get out of. It's about playing with that joyful spirit, looking at things half full instead of half empty."

On dealing with physical and mental challenges simultaneously:

"For me, this (rough patch) is a little harder because I'm trying to get the mental aspect right and the physical aspect right. It can be easier when you're just dealing with one of those things. … I'm battling both of those things. I'm trying to give myself a little bit of grace with that."

On maintaining a positive attitude despite losing his place in the Knicks' rotation:

"I think it's more how I was raised [by] my parents. Treat people how you want to be treated. Sometimes the situation isn't going to be what you want. You can't control certain things and control what you can and that's your attitude, your energy every day. So I try to do that.

"I'm human, I have days where it's not going to be all lovey-dovey. But I always tell myself, 'Whether I'm not playing or not, I got the best seats in the house.' And I get to support my teammates. So I try to go with that mindset as best I can and my teammates allow me to be myself."

On the viral clip showing him comforting Mikal Bridges on the bench:

"[Mikal] is fine. We are all human. The camera caught us that time, but I probably do stuff like that 100 times during a game."

On staying prepared for opportunities after falling out of the rotation:

"The season is long. I've been on the other side. When I first got in the league [in 2021], I wasn't even touching the court. Then eventually I got to touch the court and get some reps in and played a huge role in the rotation.

"Sometimes you get out of it. The only way to get back is stay ready because an opportunity is going to come, you just don't know when. So that's the hardest thing is the guessing game of when that will happen. But you work, work and work and trust that it will come in the end of the day."

On how close the Raptors came to beating the Knicks in their previous meeting:

"After rewatching and rewatching and rewatching film, we played against the NBA champions and, in my three-and-a-half years here as coach, that's when we came the closest to beating them. We're getting better. Just one free throw and the game is completely different."
