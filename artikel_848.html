COP31 went up in flames over fossil fuels — it sends an important message

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Anyone hoping for bold action on climate change has many reasons to be disappointed by the latest United Nations climate summit, COP31, which concluded in a dramatic overtime session on 22 November. After gathering for nearly two weeks in the beach resort city of Antalya, Turkey, in what will probably be the hottest year on record, representatives from nearly all the world’s countries failed to agree on a plan to tackle climate change. Almost 100 nations walked out without signing a ‘cover decision’ — a non-binding statement meant to push for ambitious action — because the document did not call on the world to transition away from fossil fuels.

The last COP climate talk to break down this badly was the Copenhagen summit in 2009. After that meeting, the late geoscientist James Lovelock commented: “I don’t think we’re yet evolved to the point where we’re clever enough to handle as complex a situation as climate change.” Seventeen years and an increase in atmospheric carbon dioxide levels of almost 40 parts per million later, and governments still seem unable to overcome national differences for the benefit of the planet.

The COP31 outcome wasn’t inevitable. There had been some optimism among climate watchers ahead of the summit that, despite ongoing geopolitical conflicts, rising energy prices and energy shortages would spur countries to support a transition away from burning coal, oil and gas — the biggest drivers of climate change. But when language targeting fossil fuels disappeared from the draft cover decision after lobbying by oil-rich nations, a coalition of around 90 countries, led by the European Union and small island states, rejected the agreement.
