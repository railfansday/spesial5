Thirty-one years is a pretty long snapshot. For what it's worth, looking at 1/1/2004 until today, 100% S&P 500 had a CAGR of 10.34% and a maximum drawdown of 55.21%, and a 60/40 portfolio had 7.7% and 33.82% respectively. 100k would have turned into $951,040 versus $554,580.
https://testfol.io/?s=7zPa8F42OZF
This is a perfect example of how analysis of PAST returns of equities and fixed income investments strongly suggests that 100% equities is sensible throughout the investing lifecycle, even in retirement. The reason is that historically, equities have always outperformed fixed income over long periods, and retirement can be a very long period (particularly if future generations and estate planning is considered). The updated trinity study found that a 100% equities portfolio had lower failure rate than a 70% equities portfolio.

source: https://desjansaar.com/wp-content/uploa ... _study.pdf

The bonds actually added risk and lowered returns.

However, this does not guarantee that equities will outperform fixed income for the next 100 years. Maybe high growth projections will fall short for any number of reasons:

1) failure of adoption of new technologies
2) wars
3) demographic shifts (more older/retired people and fewer young/working people)
4) environmental changes
5) global pandemics
6) culture change (less people willing to work 60 hours a week)

Meanwhile, bonds may continue to pay in full with low default rates as companies/governments survive even if growth is slow.

I personally am at 90% equities (middle aged, mid career) and will shift to 70% as I approach retirement)
