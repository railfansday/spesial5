Thru-Hikers, I Am Begging You: Stop Drinking Beer Out of Your Disgusting Hiking Shoes

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Working at The Trek means that every year around this time, I get to look through a lot of thru-hiker summit photos. I always look forward to this season because of those pictures. Every one is so full of emotion: joy, relief, heartbreak, and triumph over countless unspoken obstacles.

I’ve seen hikers striking silly poses, hikers high-fiving themselves from either end of a long trail, hikers collapsed in tears before the terminus. I’ve also seen a disturbing number of photos of thru-hikers chugging beer out of their own disgusting, beat-up trail runners.

I certainly do not remember the genteel thru-hikers of my youth drinking alcoholic beverages out of their footwear, though perhaps I was a sheltered child in the world of thru-hiking and simply missed the memo. But the tradition has persisted and even seems (to me) to have become more popular in recent years, especially on the CDT.

I have attempted to rationalize this behavior in many ways. Was it simply the result of an internet trend? Were the hikers, perhaps, thinking ahead and packing out specially designated, clean shoes for this odd terminus celebration? Were they using some sort of sterile liner to keep the shoe beer free of dirt, debris, and foot flavors? I even considered that the hikers might simply be pretending to drink from beer-filled shoes for the camera.

However, after scrutinizing dozens of photos of this behavior, I have been forced to conclude that the answer to all of the above questions is no, and the answer to the original question — Why? — is, “why not?”

Only thru-hikers, I thought, could come up with such an unhinged tradition. It does seem the sort of thing we like to do: quirky, unexplainable, and gross, but in that oddly charming way only thru-hikers can pull off.

But I recently learned that this tradition is far older than America’s oldest long-distance hiking trails and that it crops up in all sorts of endurance sports. It even has a name: the “shoey.”

The best resource for learning more about shoeys is the highly informative and surprisingly detailed Wikipedia article Drinking From Shoes, which traces the tradition back at least to the Middle Ages, when drinking out of shoes was, for some reason, apparently thought to bring good luck. Based on the presumptive bacterial load of a typical thru-hiker’s well-worn shoes, I am thinking the shoey tradition could bring on a lot more than just good luck.

My dear thru-hikers, you are tough, strong, and competent, so I’m sure you know what you’re doing as you crack that can of refreshing, delicious beer and dump the contents into your sweaty, mildewy, sand-encrusted, and highly porous trail runner. So it’s probably fine, right? Right?

A shoey to celebrate the completion of the Triple Crown. Photo: Kelly “Toddler Snacks”

Blogger Kelly “Toddler Snacks” captures the magic (?) in her account of finishing the Triple Crown of hiking with a Coke shoey at the CDT southern terminus. “A shoey is a very hikertrash thing to do, and is when you pour a beverage (usually a beer) into your shoe and then chug it,” she writes. “We cheers’d shoes and put ’em treads up. I expected it to taste a lot nastier coming from a shoe that had 500 miles of foot stank, but it surprisingly just tasted like soda.”

A quick Google search reveals numerous accounts of shoey behavior right here on The Trek and on Backpacker Radio. Am I the only person who did not know about shoeys? I have never drunk beer or any other liquid out of my shoes. I must confess I do not understand the appeal. I am ashamed and can no longer face any of my colleagues.

Thru-hikers have many weird and wonderful traditions, but at least for me, this one might be the most mystifying.

So, hikers, please help me to understand the shoey tradition. Have you done one? What was it like? (Most pressingly: did it taste like feet, and did it not have bits of dirt and lint and shit floating around in it?) Did you finish your entire drink? Did you then have to put your beer- or soda-soaked shoe back onto your foot to hike out? Would you do it again?

Please tell me. I have so many questions and so few answers.
