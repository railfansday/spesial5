Fund browser-grade certificate checking for every TLS client

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Other software leaves users at risk while operators are responsible for timely remediation efforts across numerous platforms and application runtimes. upki brings platform-independent browser-grade protection to any TLS library, so everyone can benefit from better security and agility with minimal changes.

Help sponsor upki to make your TLS clients more secure, now and in the future.

Non-browser applications have historically relied on a slowly changing set of trust roots maintained by the operating system or platform vendors. These trust roots are often based on browser root programs, but lack the data that allow browsers to make fine-grained trust decisions: revocation data for end entities, intermediates and roots (like CRLite and OneCRL), distrust dates and Certificate Transparency policy.

The October 2026 eMudhra incident showed the cost. Browsers blocked the affected intermediates quickly, while other clients were unable to restrict only the affected intermediates. Instead, they had to wait for platform trust store updates to remove the parent root certificates and spent significant time and effort to mitigate the impact on their applications across different platforms and environments.

upki ships these signals as a compact local cache that's updated automatically, independently of applications, so every TLS stack on a machine can share one copy. Its checks only ever narrow what a client accepts: they never make it trust a certificate it would otherwise reject.

Sponsorships are annual and pay for maintainer time on the roadmap and on ongoing maintenance. We don't sell individual features; priorities are open for discussion with sponsors.

Clients outside the browser accept revoked end-entity certificates.

upki syncs compact CRLite filters several times a day and checks revocation offline.

When root programs distrust a CA or intermediate, other clients keep trusting it until a trust store package update arrives, as the eMudhra incident showed.

upki will ship OneCRL data, so distrust takes effect at the next cache update.

Post-quantum signatures would add kilobytes to every TLS handshake. Merkle Tree Certificates can bring down the costs, but clients need landmarks delivered ahead of time to minimize handshake overhead.

upki will distribute landmarks alongside its other data.

Clients that don't enforce Certificate Transparency accept certificates that were never publicly logged, so misissuance can go unnoticed. Clients also don't benefit from certificate transparency logs blocking compromised/distrusted roots from obtaining proof of inclusion.

upki will deliver and maintain an up-to-date log list and policy inputs, so applications don't have to configure logs manually.

Servers that send an incomplete certificate chain work in browsers but fail verification in other clients.

upki will ship the known intermediate certificates, so clients can complete the chain as browsers do, without expensive network fetching.

Root changes, including private PKI roots, reach machines through OS packages or configuration management, which can be slow and uneven across a fleet.

upki's sync mechanism could distribute root sets to every machine.

The checks only protect applications whose TLS stack uses upki.

upki could gain native support in OpenSSL upstream, GnuTLS, NSS or in language runtimes.

Every check depends on data that's refreshed several times a day, on upstream sources and formats that keep changing, and on prompt fixes when security issues come up.

Sponsorship pays for releases, security response, dependency updates, keeping the data pipeline running and in step with upstream changes, supporting distribution packagers and maintaining C2SP specifications.

upki lives in the rustls organization on GitHub and is dual-licensed under Apache-2.0 and MIT.
