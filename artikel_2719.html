Matthew Stafford gets emotional about Puka Nacua’s surgery and human cost of football

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Matthew Stafford has spent enough time in NFL training rooms to know there's a difference between playing through pain and gambling with your future.

So when the Rams quarterback learned that his favorite target, Puka Nacua, needed surgery to repair his injured right MCL, Stafford wasn't thinking about the passes they wouldn't complete together or the touchdowns left on the table.

He was thinking about his teammate's life and career moving forward.

Matthew Stafford (9) of the Los Angeles Rams interacts with teammate Puka Nacua (12) before an NFL football game against the Las Vegas Raiders on Oct. 25, 2026. Getty Images

"I obviously hope all that is in the best interest of his long-term health because that's what I care about as a human being, and his future," Stafford said Saturday.

Nacua underwent surgery Tuesday, effectively ending his 2026 season, including any potential postseason run. His recovery is expected to take four to five months, with doctors anticipating he'll be healthy for the 2027 season.

For a Rams team already decimated by injuries, losing Nacua is another crushing blow. But Stafford understands the bigger picture.

"For a guy that puts a ton of effort into trying to be out there every single Sunday for us, he plays, you guys know, as violently as anybody in the NFL, especially at his position," Stafford said. "He puts his body on the line for us, and unfortunately, he had to get some things fixed and cleaned up."

Matthew Stafford #9 of the Los Angeles Rams celebrates with Puka Nacua #12 after a touchdown pass against the Carolina Panthers on Jan. 10, 2026, in Charlotte. Getty Images

At 38, Stafford has endured enough injuries and medical consultations to understand that deciding whether to undergo surgery isn't always as simple as listening to one doctor.

"There's a lot of really smart people out there that have opinions on how things should go," Stafford said. "But in the end, you've got to find people who you trust, go with those people and make a decision."

Stafford acknowledged that some of his own medical decisions were straightforward. Others weren't.

"Luckily, a lot of the stuff I've had has been pretty cut and dry. Either you can or you can't," he said. "But sometimes there are those ones that can go either way."

Los Angeles Rams quarterback Matthew Stafford (9) and Puka Nacua (12) look on against the Philadelphia Eagles on Oct. 4, 2026, at Lincoln Financial Field. Getty Images

That's the cruel reality of professional football. Teams need their stars on Sundays, but players are the ones who must live with the consequences long after the stadium lights go dark.

"We're human beings, too, trying to get our bodies back to do what we love to do," Stafford said.

Stafford said he's remained in contact with Nacua throughout the ordeal, offering encouragement as his teammate begins the long road back.

"He's been a great teammate of mine, and I just want him to feel good again," Stafford said.
