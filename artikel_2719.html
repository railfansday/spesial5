The NFL's Week 12 flex decision is looming

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Thanksgiving week will entail a smorgasbord of football. But not every course will be particularly appetizing.

The NFL’s brand-new embrace of the previously quiet night before Thanksgiving, only a few years after Black Friday was invaded, creates nine separate windows for the weekend — and seven standalone games.

The Wednesday night clash, pitting the 6-4 Packers against the 6-4 Rams, has huge implications for the slow-moving game of wild-card musical chairs in the NFC. The early Thanksgiving contest has the 7-2 Bears visiting the 7-2 Lions in a massive NFC North battle.

The Eagles-Cowboys game at 4:25 p.m. ET is becoming increasingly irrelevant — and it’ll still get more than 40 million viewers because any game in that window would generate a huge audience. The Thursday night game, between the 7-2 Chiefs and the 8-1 Bills, will be very relevant to the AFC West championship and the No. 1 seed in the conference.

On Friday, it’s the 7-2 Broncos at the 6-3 Steelers, which also has major playoff implications.

Then comes the late-afternoon Sunday window. At 4:25 p.m. ET, the 8-1 49ers host the 6-4 Seahawks.

Unfortunately, it doesn’t end there. On Sunday night, the 5-5 Patriots (who likely won’t have quarterback Drake Maye) visit the 0-8 Chargers.

If the NFL is going to slide that game out of prime time, Tuesday is the deadline for doing it. For the Eagles-Commanders game in Week 8, the league opted not to move a more consequential game into the Sunday night spot. Will the NFL again stand pat, keeping the Pats and Chargers as, to borrow a classic line from John Madden, the last shot out of a Roman candle?

The problem is that the playing of five games on Wednesday through Sunday leaves few options for Sunday night. In the 1:00 p.m. ET window, the games are Saints-Bengals, Raiders-Browns, Ravens-Texans, Giants-Colts, Jets-Dolphins, and Falcons-Vikings. Titans-Jaguars is due to begin at 4:05 p.m. ET, with Commanders-Cardinals being the other 4:25 p.m. ET game.

There’s no Sunday afternoon game (other than Seahawks-49ers) that carries huge meaning for both teams. The best option for a flex would be Falcons-Vikings, since it’s the first home game of Kirk Cousins 2.0 — and it comes against the team that signed him to a deal with multiple years of full guarantees before shocking everyone (including Cousins) the following month by selecting Michael Penix Jr. with the eighth overall pick in the draft.

The good news is that the Monday night game between the 4-5 Panthers and the 3-6 Buccaneers has direct relevance to the NFC South title, and the guaranteed postseason home game that comes with it. Still, the current Sunday night game stands out as being something that few will be interested in watching, especially after a five-day turkey-and-pigskin-induced coma.

In the end, it’s always the league’s decision. Under the rules the NFL has put in place, that decision is due to be made tomorrow.
