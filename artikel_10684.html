Binding of Isaac fans review bomb DLC after discovering GTA 6 mode songs are AI

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Players have found that music in The Binding of Isaac: Repentance+’s new GTA 6-themed secret run was made with Suno, as the free DLC sits at Overwhelmingly Negative recent reviews on Steam.

Creator Edmund McMillen has since said he had no idea the music was AI-generated, with publisher Nicalis now patching it out of the game.

The run arrived in the same update that brought Repentance+ to PS5, Xbox, and Switch 2 on November 19, a release date publisher Nicalis picked to go head-to-head with GTA 6.

Entering the seed V1CE BSMT unlocks a Vice City-inspired daily run with its own soundtrack, made up of seven songs plus a handful of intros and outros.

A player on the Binding of Isaac subreddit extracted the run’s music from the game files and found several tracks still carrying metadata reading “made with suno,” along with links to the songs on Suno’s site.

The metadata on one track shows it was created on October 30, 2026, less than three weeks before the update went live. Other players have since checked the files and confirmed the find.

The run itself is credited to a modder known as Mr. Headcrab, who the original poster says is “staunchly anti-AI” and had no idea the music was generated.

Valve requires developers to disclose AI-generated content on a game’s Steam page, but Repentance+’s store listing makes no mention of it.

The DLC currently sits at Overwhelmingly Negative recent reviews, with only 10% of 908 reviews from the past 30 days positive, though the same update has also been blamed for breaking mods and causing crashes.

For some fans, the AI music is the final straw. “They used AI when synthwave is like… the easiest genre to make,” one player wrote, in a comment with dozens of upvotes.

The community-run Isaac wiki has since added a note flagging the AI-generated tracks. Nicalis has yet to comment on the music or add a disclosure to the Steam page.

McMillen addressed the backlash on X, making it clear the music had been added without his knowledge.

“Woke up to a sh*t show, obviously I had no idea it featured generated music in the daily run,” he wrote. “Looking into how that happened and getting it removed, stay tuned.”

Within hours, Nicalis pushed a new build of Repentance+, listing the “removal of generated music files” at the top of its patch notes, alongside fixes for mods broken by the launch update.

The patch is already live on PC, with the console versions submitted to Microsoft, Nintendo, and Sony for approval.
