Lobos backcourt dominates Northern Arizona, improves to 2-0

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Broadcast: Streamed on MW+; Radio broadcast on 96.3 FM/770 AM

Only one thing seemed to catch the Lobos off guard on Saturday night.

After a dominating 94-65 beating of Northern Arizona in the Pit — the second blowout win of the opening week of the season for UNM — guards Uriah Tenette and Chris Howell were masterfully handling questions from local media in the postgame press conference when they got stumped.

Asked to comment on the school record-tying two turnover performance they just put together — UNM had just two turnovers while NAU had 16, leading the Lobos to a one-sided 23-0 margin in points off turnovers — the both players seemed caught off guard.

"I didn't even …" Tenette said while quickly looking at the stat sheet sitting in front of him.

"I didn't even notice that," Chris Howell said, reaching for his stat sheet as well. "That's pretty elite. I didn't even notice."

So caught up in the process-oriented, forget the final score and stats approach being instilled in them by their head coach, neither guard even realized just how efficient they played and how impressive that stat sheet in front of them was on Saturday.

"I told them they did a good job of taking care of the ball. I did mention that, I think," Lobos coach Eric Olen said when asked how Tenette and Howell didn't even realize they had just had a program-record performance in turnovers.

"But you know, I think our focus is on getting better, and I just want to make sure that we have an urgency to do that."

The Lobos' win improved them to 2-0 on the young season and was powered by a 57-point performance from the starting backcourt trio of Jake Hall, Tenette and Hudson Mayes.

Hall scored 20 of his game-high 23 points in the first half and had a highly efficient scoring night — 4-of-6 on 2-point shots; 2-of-4 on 3-pointers; and a perfect 9-of-9 on free throws.

For the second straight game, he went off early (he had 19 of his 30 points in the first half of Monday's win) before the defense was so focused on him that others ate their lunch in the second half.

Tenette scored 14 of his 18 points in the second half after picking up three first half fouls. Mayes, meanwhile, was balanced and pretty dominant in his own right with a 16 point, seven rebound, four assist, no turnover and one steal stat line.

Howell, the veteran off the bench, had nine assists and no turnovers. That he had just five points and took three shots in a game his team scored 94 points didn't bother him.

"I say it all the time, I'll say it again. I'm gonna do whatever it takes to win," Howell said. "Each game might be different … but I love to win. Whatever that takes. That's what I'm here for."

The Lobos used an 11-0 run midway through the first half to end early in the night any thoughts of an upset in front of the announced Pit crowd of 11,861.

UNM's bench outscored the Lumberjack reserves 23-8. Most notable for Olen was how reserves Howell, Benjamin Schuch and Dax Hall had nearly as many assists (14 combined) as points (15). That trio also has six steals and just one turnover.

Rytis Petraitis, the Cal transfer forward who also missed Monday's opener against Utah Tech, was held out of Saturday's game with an ankle sprain.

Olen said Friday it is absolutely progressing and Petraitis has been practicing. On that note, Petraitis hit a half court shot as the players were finishing warmups before the game and then sprinted up the Pit ramp to the locker room in celebration.

As Jason and Kimberly Eck walked down the steps to their seats in the Pit midway through the first half, fans started to take notice and by the time he was sitting — about five rows up from courtside — the standing ovation and Pit roar was at its loudest of the first half.

The head coach of the No. 22 ranked Lobo football team which won Friday night in Reno — 31-3 over Nevada — promptly protected some beers from an errant NAU pass into the stands, again receiving a huge ovation.
