Top 25 EFL Championship goalscorers since the 2004 rebrand

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Goalscorers are often the difference between a Championship side merely competing and one capable of going up, which makes them among the most valuable commodities in the game. Football League World have looked at the top 25 since the inception of the division in 2004.

Of course, a reliable striker can turn draws into wins, settle tight games, and provide the consistency required over a gruelling 46-game season. However, the problem for Championship clubs is that when one of those forwards hits the ground running, they do not always get to enjoy the benefits for very long.

A prolific season in the second tier can put a player firmly on the radar of top flight clubs looking for a proven goalscorer, while the alternative is often just as painful for the Championship club when the striker scores enough goals to help fire them to promotion and then faces a new challenge in the top flight.

It is little surprise, then, that centre-forwards regularly command some of the biggest transfer fees and salaries in football. There have been plenty of prolific names in the Championship since the division was rebranded ahead of the 2004/05 season, with some strikers establishing themselves as second-tier specialists and others using it as a springboard towards the Premier League.

Some of the division's most exciting modern goalscorers are not yet eligible for this particular list because they simply have not spent long enough in the Championship. Middlesbrough's Will Lankshear, Wolverhampton Wanderers' Raul Jimenez, West Ham's Taty Castellanos, and Southampton's Cyle Larin are among the forwards currently capable of making their mark, but their Championship stories are only just beginning.

But who has actually scored the most goals since the Championship era began? Including play-off goals as well as goals scored during the regular league season? We have taken a look and ranked the top 25, here.

His Championship career took Martyn Waghorn to a number of clubs, including Leicester City, Ipswich Town, Derby County, and Coventry City. He was particularly effective during his time at Ipswich, where his goals made him one of the division's leading forwards. His 78 goals put him just ahead of Britt Assombalonga and David McGoldrick below him in the standings, but there are the likes of Kieffer Moore and Ben Brereton-Diaz closing in on his tally from the crop of currently active players.

Joel Piroe has established himself as one of the Championship's most reliable modern goalscorers, reaching 78 goals during spells with Swansea City, Leeds United, and now West Ham United. The Dutchman has been particularly prolific in the second tier and remains an active player capable of climbing this list further. Piroe will hope more goals can help the Hammers achieve their ambitions this season, with his 78 goals just ahead of Waghorn on a goal-per-game basis.

Across England's top two divisions, Chris Wood has enjoyed an outstanding career, with his Championship goals coming for the likes of Leicester, Leeds, Millwall, Ipswich, and Birmingham City. His 78 goals match Waghorn's tally as well as Piroe's, but his superior goals-per-minute ratio means he sits higher than both. Wood has not played a minute of Championship football since leaving Leeds for Burnley and has gone on to score 92 goals in the Premier League as well.

One of the Championship's most experienced forwards during the 2000s and early 2010s, Marlon King scored regularly for clubs including Watford, Coventry, and Birmingham. He eventually reached 80 goals in the division, underlining his consistency across a lengthy second-tier career which began prior to the Championship's rebranding. King's physical presence and eye for goal made him a valuable Championship striker for many a year.

Premier League pedigree did not prevent Kevin Phillips from continuing to score at an impressive rate in the Championship. After his famous goalscoring exploits with Sunderland, Phillips found the net regularly for clubs including West Bromwich Albion, Birmingham, Blackpool, Crystal Palace, and Leicester. His 82 Championship goals highlight that his finishing ability remained intact well beyond his top-flight peak and well into his 30s.

During spells with the likes of Watford, Middlesbrough, and Blackburn Rovers, Danny Graham became one of the Championship's most dependable goalscorers. His movement and finishing made him a consistent threat for years, while his goals helped earn him opportunities in the Premier League. Graham's 83 goals leave him inside the division's top 20 scorers since the 2004/05 rebrand.

Peter Whittingham was one of the defining Championship players of his generation, spending much of his career with Cardiff City after an earlier spell at Aston Villa. His left foot, set-piece ability, and eye for goal made him an influential attacking midfielder or winger rather than a conventional striker. His 84 goals are an impressive return for a player operating primarily from midfield. Although he was an attacking player, he is one of only two midfielders on this list.

Dexter Blackstock never scored more than 13 in a season and only hit double figures three times as well, but consistency rather than one spectacular scoring season was the hallmark of his Championship career. He represented clubs including QPR and Nottingham Forest, becoming a familiar figure across the division. Blackstock's 84 goals put him level with Whittingham, although his goals-per-minute record places him slightly better on this particular ranking.

After scoring a few for Newcastle United and then joining Fulham, Aleksandar Mitrovic wasted little time establishing himself as one of the Championship's most fearsome strikers. He scored for Newcastle before his remarkable Fulham spell produced a record-breaking individual season and helped secure promotion. His 85 goals are a remarkable total considering how much of his career has been spent outside the Championship. He has the best goal-per-minute ratio of any player on the list.

A remarkably long Championship career saw Cameron Jerome represent clubs including Cardiff, Birmingham, Norwich City, and Derby County. His pace and power made him difficult to contain, while his longevity saw him accumulate 86 goals. Jerome's ability to contribute across multiple clubs, as well as many generations of Championship football, makes his tally particularly impressive.
