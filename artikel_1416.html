Formidable and relentless Bournemouth's astonishing form is no mere hot streak, writes JAMES SHARPE, as dominant Cherries beat Forest to claim NINTH win in a row

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Published: 12:38 EST, 21 November 2026 | Updated: 14:28 EST, 21 November 2026

This is all becoming rather inevitable at Bournemouth. Will they ever stop winning? Will Evanilson ever stop scoring?

The numbers alone are astonishing. Nine wins on the bounce for the first time in their history, their victory here against Nottingham Forest making it six on the spin at home at an aggregate score of 15 to nil. Five wins in a row in the Premier League for the first time, too, a result that moved them into the top four.

This is Bournemouth, remember. Only this is Bournemouth now.

Make no mistake: this is no hot streak. This is not a team under Marco Rose carried to win after win after win on a wave of momentum and vibes. This is relentless, breathtaking stuff. This is a team that, week after week, outplays every team that stands in front of them. It doesn’t matter if it’s Forest, or Manchester United, or AC Milan. Bournemouth turn up and they blow their opponents away.

They move it quicker and sharper, they win it back faster and higher. They give teams no time to think or breathe, so it’s little wonder they have no answer.

‘We never really struggled, said Rose. 'We suffered a little bit at the end, but we defended quite well together, and that's why it's a deserved win.

Bournemouth racked up their sixth successive league victory and ninth in all competitions

'If we use the right approach every game – and this is really, really demanding – we win games. Next game, we have to prove it again. If we do it, then we can keep the level up. But as I said, this is a tough job.

‘The collective is strong, but even this we have to maintain. Because if you win games…I mean, some of our players right now are in a lot of noise and rumours.

'If we are able to keep these kinds of things away from the locker room, and if everyone comes into training every day, works hard and gives everything for the club and the team, then we are really, really good.'

The only real surprise was that Evanilson, Bournemouth’s talismanic Brazilian, didn’t leave with more than just his one, now customary, goal.

That itself was another fine finish after Tyler Adams won the ball high up the pitch – obviously – dinked it across to James Hill, back after his first England call-up, who flicked a header into Evanilson’s path. He thumped it past Matz Sels to do the rest.

Rose, who called him a ‘real No9’ the other week, had been urging him to get into the box more to finish off chances. He did that, all right. Twice earlier in the game, he raced on to through balls only to be denied by Sels and a last-ditch tackle from Jair Cunha.

His opening strike, his eighth goal in his last nine games, means he is the first Premier League player to reach double figures for goal contributions with six goals alongside his four other assists. He leads the press, he creates chances, he scores goals. He does the lot.

That’s now six league games in a row he’s found the net, halfway to breaking Jamie Vardy’s record of 11 in a row. You wouldn’t put it past him.

Bournemouth’s talismanic Brazilian Evanilson scored for the sixth successive league match

Forest came into this, after all, off the back of a huge, much-needed victory against Manchester City. After just one win in Oliver Glasner’s opening nine league games, that was the performance those around Forest felt that could spark the season into life.

It was also meant to be the one where Liam Delap’s Forest career burst into life with two thunderous goals in a bullying, battering-ram showing.

Alas, they both ran into a proper team this week.

Forest tried to go direct to Delap, partly to try to bypass the intense Bournemouth press, but nothing stuck. Delap lost possession with 23 of his 39 touches. He committed more fouls than he had shots and found his way into the referee’s book for clumsily bundling over Adam Smith.

The visitors huffed and puffed in the second half but never really looked like scoring. The closest they got was a couple of speculative long-range efforts from Morgan Gibbs-White and James McAtee before a late goalmouth scramble nearly provided an opening through chaos more than creativity.

It was only the busy hands of Sels that kept the scoring respectable.

’The defeat was deserved,’ said Glasner. ‘City and today were completely different. It's like a different sport. This is football, but it's different.

'When teams play like City, when it's more of a tactical game, we are doing well. When it's more of a physical, intense game, we're struggling. Today our weaknesses came to the surface.’

When asked if he had the players to play this way, Glasner responded: 'Right now, I have to say no. If not, we will add players. If we believe we need somebody to add this physicality, these quick solutions, we will do it.'

Did they expect much else, though? Forest never beat Bournemouth. That’s 13 meetings in a row now they have failed to win.

‘Nottingham Forest, it’s happened again,’ sang the home fans in the final few minutes.

Liam Delap and Nottingham Forest crashed into a proper team as they came back down to earth after their win over Man City before the international break

Marcus Tavernier's performances could get a chance with England if he maintains his form

Bournemouth have already had a couple of deserved England call-ups this season in Hill and midfielder Alex Scott. How long until Marcus Tavernier gets his chance?

No English player has scored more Premier League goals this season than the Bournemouth winger, level with Morgan Rogers and Dominic Calvert-Lewin, and once again he was at the heart of everything good the Cherries do.

His superb cross behind the Forest defence found Justin Kluivert for Bournemouth’s second of the afternoon. He danced through the Forest defence to set up Rayan for a late chance.

'It was my 11th Premier League game today, and I couldn't see better offensive midfielders so far this season,’ said Rose.

‘If you see how he works and how he plays, the quality he has, he scores goals. He's 27, he's at the best age.

‘If he plays well, if he stays healthy, I think, like with Hilly, these things come. They're just around the corner.'
