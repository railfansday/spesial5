Former United CEO Warns Airline’s AI Ban Could Drive Flyers Away—Its New Job Ad Shows What They Really Want

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Former United Airlines CEO Oscar Munoz says customers who want an AI assistant to book their flights will choose an airline that lets them. That’s a warning to the airline he led, which just moved to ban those bookings. But the carrier’s own recruiting ad offers a revealing look at the future they’re preparing for: AI agents with access to shopping systems, under United’s control. Speaking with Scott McCartney on the November 15 special edition of Airlines Confidential, Munoz said: If travelers want an agent booking their trips, they’re going to find the airline that lets them do that.

Speaking with Scott McCartney on the November 15 special edition of Airlines Confidential, Munoz said:

If travelers want an agent booking their trips, they’re going to find the airline that lets them do that.

Munoz expects successful airlines to accommodate agents with safeguards and sees airlines negotiating terms of access, while recognizing that customers get a say in how they buy. That seems to focus on major labs, not offshore ones or open source.

United’s November 11 announcement cited account security, unintended purchases, misuse of miles and regulatory requirements. They said the restrictions would reach outside agents using United’s own systems as well as travel agencies and aggregators. As I wrote when United announced the ban, their contract language reaches even agents whose actions a customer supervises or approves. And current CEO Scott Kirby gave the real story, which wasn’t about security: To be honest, I don’t want to create another middleman.

An assistant that becomes the starting point for travel shopping can gain enormous bargaining power. It decides which options to recommend. Eventually, its owner might demand payment for access to customers. United would rather customers start with United where they can sell ancillaries as well as the ticket, control messaging to the customer, and manage costs.

United is banning agents at the same time they want to manage how they work with agents. A United Principal Developer – AI job listing describes connecting agents to flight shopping and shopping carts. Build reusable tools and APIs that expose existing business capabilities to authorized AI agents and applications.

Human approval where appropriate, and recovery when something goes wrong. Controls over access, protection of customer information and safeguards for transactions. Testing and ongoing support for working systems.

A United assistant could be excellent at finding a United flight, explaining the seats and handling a schedule change. That would be valuable. But I wouldn’t expect it to suggest that I should buy a competing airline’s nonstop instead. An assistant working for me should know whether I’d pay more to avoid a connection, whether a better seat is worth the difference, and whether spending miles makes sense for this particular trip. It finds the best trip on the right airline to match my preferences and tradeoffs. I’ve made the same argument about rewards credit cards: companies are excited about using AI to sell more, but consumers get better tools, too. An offer that sounds appealing in an airline’s sales pitch may look much worse once software compares it with everything else available. Of course, the outside assistant might have its own conflicts. A service paid by airlines to produce bookings could favor whoever pays it the most. OpenAI’s Sam Altman says “If ChatGPT were accepting payment to put a worse hotel above a better hotel, that’s probably catastrophic for your relationship with ChatGPT.” He argued that a booking transaction fee would be acceptable if ChatGPT recommended the best choice independently and took the same cut regardless of what choice the customer books. My bet is that United eventually accepts more outside agents after negotiating terms, because customers will increasingly expect someone else to do this work. And they won’t be able to stop agents that book United tickets without paying them a separate fee or accepting their rules. There’s no world where passengers permanently abandon their preferred assistant to use an airline’s sales tool, and actually succeeding in blocking agents would cost United too much business.
