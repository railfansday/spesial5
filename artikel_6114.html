Turkey is making more money from fewer visitors, but the gains come from longer stays, not pricier rooms: hotel rates fell in dollar terms. The lowered $65 billion target can be met even if fourth-quarter revenue drops 9%, so clearing it says little about a recovery.

Turkey earned 5% more from tourism in the third quarter from 1% fewer visitors, but hotels aren’t the ones capturing the gain.

Culture and tourism minister Mehmet Ersoy credited the rise in tourism revenue, captured by the Turkish Statistical Institute, to “longer stays and higher overnight spending.”

Average visitor stays stood at 10.7 nights in the third quarter, up from 10.2 a year earlier. Spending per night rose 6.7% to $107, lifting total spending per visitor by about 12% to $1,145.

But hotel rates don’t explain the gain in tourist spending. In dollar terms, average rates fell fractionally to $148 a night in Q3, down from $149 last year, according to Lighthouse.

Package tour expenditures accounted for 32.3% of total tourism income, followed by food and beverage spending at 20.5% and accommodation at 12.5%, the Turkish Statistical Institute data showed.
