'Tis the season to get big discounts on Disney+, HBO Max, Hulu, Apple TV and more

When you purchase through links on our site, we may earn an affiliate commission. Here’s how it works.

Every November, I commit an act of subscription arson. Netflix? Cancelled. HBO Max? Gone. Disney+, Hulu, Apple TV? Cleared out like a fridge before vacation. Then, just as the Black Friday streaming deals start rolling in, I sign right back up, at a fraction of the price. It's the closest thing to a financial magic trick that doesn't involve a crypto wallet, and it works every single year.

The problem it solves is real. A Tom's Guide survey about streaming habits found that readers are spending more than $60 a month on seven or more streaming services. That's a lot of money to spend on a pile of apps you open just to scroll for 20 minutes before rewatching "The Office." Prices keep creeping up, and your watchlist isn't getting any shorter.

I prefer to play musical subscriptions instead: Cancel the ones I'm not using, hop back in when a sale comes along. Below, I'll walk you through exactly how I do it and round up seven streaming deals worth grabbing before they vanish.

At $4.99 a month for an entire year, this Disney+/Hulu bundle is almost absurdly cheap. One subscription gets you two major streaming libraries, from Hulu hits like “Only Murders in the Building” to Disney+ movies, series and family favorites. Frankly, it's hard to argue with that kind of entertainment bargain.

HBO Max sits at No. 1 in our best streaming services rankings, and for good reason. Its lineup is stacked with acclaimed shows like "The Pitt" and "A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms" and all-time great dramas including "The Sopranos" and "Veep," plus a deep bench of movies from Warner Bros. It's a one-stop shop for seriously good TV and film.

Apple TV is getting a serious Black Friday price cut, dropping to just $6.99 per month instead of its regular $14.99. It's a great, cheaper way to catch buzzy series like "Slow Horses," "The Studio" and "Silo."

Peacock is finally getting in on the Black Friday action again after sitting out last year's sale. The entry-level Select plan will set you back just $2.99, but my money's on Premium for $8.99. That's where you'll find the good stuff: Peacock's full lineup of hit shows, blockbuster movies, original series and live sports. Sure, you can find steeper discounts elsewhere, but this is a worthwhile chance to get more bang for your streaming buck.

Paramount+ is discounting both subscription tiers for Black Friday, but the Premium plan is the one worth splurging on. It includes everything in Essential, with the added bonus of Showtime's lineup of movies and series, a live feed of your local CBS station and mostly ad-free streaming. That's a lot of extra bang for your subscription buck.

If you're a "Walking Dead" completist, AMC+ is practically essential. The service has the original zombie saga alongside spinoffs like "Daryl Dixon." This Prime Video channel deal makes it a perfect excuse to revisit the franchise or finally catch up on all the undead drama.

BritBox is a streaming paradise packed with British television from the BBC and ITV. Its library spans generations of acclaimed mysteries, juicy dramas, offbeat comedies and fascinating documentaries. This is the perfect deal if you want to catch the upcoming "The Other Bennet Sister" three-part Christmas special.

The workaround is almost insultingly simple: Sign up with a different email address.

You likely have a spare lying around already, probably the one you made in 2011 and now use exclusively for receipts. If not, any of the many email services with a free tier will do the trick, whether that's Gmail, Yahoo, Outlook or AOL.

Here's my annual routine. Last year, Past Me, a surprisingly responsible person, set calendar reminders to cancel everything I signed up for during Black Friday week. Then I take my fresh email address and go deal hunting. The ones I think are worth your money every year are Peacock, HBO Max and Paramount+.

A word of warning: Some of the best deals out there are for annual plans, which means you can't churn them month to month. But the discounts are steep enough that paying upfront is still the smarter play.

And whatever you do, set a reminder to cancel next year. Future You will thank you.

In a word: no. Netflix doesn't do Black Friday. I check every year, and every year I'm met with the same cold, red-logoed silence.

Netflix has rarely discounted its plans, and Black Friday and Cyber Monday are no exception. So the usual monthly prices stand: $8.99 for Standard with ads, $19.99 for Standard ad-free, and $26.99 for Premium with 4K Ultra HD.

The upside of having no deal to chase is that you're not locked into a discounted annual plan, which makes Netflix the easiest service on the list to churn. Netflix knows this, of course, and has gotten sneaky about it. Its biggest shows now tend to arrive in installments, a neat little trick for making sure you stick around for the next drop.
