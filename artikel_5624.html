Hepatitis B Shot at Birth Dips Among Babies of Infected Moms

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

— "Birth dose vaccination serves as a critical safety net," researchers say

Fewer newborns are receiving the birth dose of the hepatitis B virus (HBV) vaccine, including those born to infected mothers, according to a national retrospective review.

For over 1.3 million U.S. infants born between January 2018 and June 2026, vaccination rates were 76.4% in 2018 and 79.8% in 2022 before beginning to fall in mid-2023, landing at 65.6% in June of this year, reported Nina Masters, PhD, MPH, of Truveta in Bellevue, Washington, and colleagues in NEJM Evidence.

From 2018 through 2025, a positive maternal HBV test led to vaccination in 97%-98% of newborns born to those mothers -- but that percentage dropped to 93.9% during January through June 2026.

Overall, as newborn HBV vaccination rates fell, maternal HBV screening rates rose. Over the study period, 90.5% of mothers had evidence of an HBV test, with 0.18% testing positive for the virus. The proportion of moms who had no recorded HBV test during pregnancy dropped from 15.6% in 2018 to 7.6% in 2026.

"Hepatitis B birth dose vaccination serves as a critical safety net against gaps in protection against perinatal HBV infection," Masters and team wrote.

"Applying the 90% screening rate found in this study to the approximately 3.6 million births per year, mothers without evidence of HBV screening during pregnancy may give birth to an estimated 360,000 infants per year," they noted. "Applying the 0.18% ... positivity rate in this study could translate to more than 640 infants a year born to mothers with HBV infection who are untested."

Universal HBV vaccination of newborns at birth has been shown to be safe and effective. However, the CDC's revamped Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices last December abandoned more than three decades of U.S. vaccine policy and voted to stop recommending a universal birth dose.

The panel recommended instead a shared clinical decision-making process for babies born to mothers who test negative for HBV. Under that approach, parents and clinicians would decide "when or if" to administer the vaccine. For families that opt to not give the birth dose, the panel suggested delaying the first dose until a child is 2 months old. The CDC's proposed childhood immunization schedule would move HBV vaccination to shared clinical decision-making except in high-risk groups.

The steady declines in newborn HBV vaccination rates across all maternal groups "potentially indicate distrust of medical interventions even beyond recommendations," Masters and team cautioned. "These results reinforce previous reports, likely reflecting continuing vaccine hesitancy and confusion among parents after recent changes in recommendations for newborn HBV guidelines."

The trends seen in this study could lead to more chronic HBV infections in the years ahead, according to a recent modeling study. That research predicted that moving from a universal birth dose to deferred vaccination or a shared decision-making approach could increase chronic HBV infections by 19% with delayed vaccination and 83% with shared decision-making. Other research has suggested that unvaccinated infants may face unexpected HBV household exposure risks even when moms test negative.

For this study, Masters and colleagues reviewed electronic health records from the nationwide Truveta Data database. The analysis included 1,338,961 infants born to 1,075,767 mothers who were ages 15-49 years at delivery.

Over the study period, 75.9% of newborns received HBV vaccination within 1 day of birth.

Vaccination rates were highest among babies born to moms who tested positive for HBV, at 96.9%. Vaccination rates were 75.9% among those with mothers who tested negative, 76.8% among those with moms with unknown infection status, and 75.4% among those with moms who had no evidence of a test in the database.

Among the 2,231 mothers who tested positive for HBV infection during pregnancy, 92.8% received both hepatitis B immunoglobulin and HBV vaccination.
