Hello there from STAT’s London bureau, with reporter Andrew Joseph here filling in for Mr. Pharmalot for a few days. It may be a holiday week stateside, but the news, at least for now, has yet to let up. So grab your choice cup of stimulation and settle in. …

Sanofi is buying the biotech Insmed for $41 billion, the largest biopharma acquisition in years and one that will give the French firm two new approved drugs, STAT tells us. The purchase, which includes another drug in Phase 3 trials, reflects the pressure Sanofi has been under as it faces looming revenue losses from its blockbuster inflammatory drug Dupixent. Sanofi has been upfront that it was searching for takeover targets with late-stage or clinical assets, and with the deal, it will get New Jersey-based Insmed’s two lung disease drugs.

Roche touted the blockbuster potential of a new breast cancer pill after winning two U.S. regulatory approvals within a week, Bloomberg shares. The new medicine, called Wilserdly, is central to the drugmaker’s plan to regain its footing in the fast-growing cancer market. The company is positioning the pill to replace decades-old hormone therapies that are taken for years in an effort to reduce the risk the cancer will return. Estimates from analysts placing its peak annual sales potential at $9 billion are roughly in line with its own expectations, the Swiss drugmaker said.
