The Jeep Wrangler Scrambler SRT Concept Is a High-Speed Desert-Shredding Machine

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

But don’t be too surprised to see something like this concept reach showrooms in the next few years.

The Jeep CJ-8 Scrambler of the 1980s took the basic CJ-7, stretched it, and added a pickup bed. Jeep’s latest concept, the Wrangler Scrambler SRT, takes that same formula and ups the ante. Unveiled at the 2026 Specialty Equipment Market Association (SEMA) Show, the Wrangler Scrambler SRT Concept is not just a body kit on top of a Wrangler frame. Instead, the Wrangler-based concept is the beginning of new developments for the off-road brand, particularly in suspension technology.

The highlight of the concept model is a bespoke high-travel independent front suspension, developed with off-road racing champion Casey Currie. The all-new suspension expands the concept’s performance envelope with enhanced steering precision, wheel control, and high-speed stability. Jeep also added a trophy-truck-inspired rear axle, heavy-duty wheel package, and desert-racing-informed hardware to ensure long-term durability. The suspension architecture first appeared on the Jeep Striker Concept, and was validated by Currie in a custom Jeep Gladiator “Striker” at the NORRA 500.

“The original Scrambler became an icon because it broke the rules. It looked different, worked differently and represented a certain kind of freedom that resonated with enthusiasts then and still does today,” said Tim Kuniskis, head of American brands, SRT Performance, and Direct Connection, Stellantis North America. “We asked ourselves a more provocative question: what happens when SRT stops treating the trail as the limit and builds the ultimate open-air desert weapon? The Wrangler Scrambler SRT Concept is our answer.”

Jeep may have started with a Wrangler chassis, but the final product is not dimensionally similar to a factory JL-generation model. Behind the A-pillar, Jeep added longer doors, custom rear bodywork, an integrated pickup bed, a redesigned tailgate, unique fender flares, and a structural roll cage. Up front, the Wrangler Scrambler features a sharknose design like the Gladiator. (We can’t help but smile at the Punk’n orange paint and bronze-painted beadlock wheels.)

Inside the Wrangler Scrambler, Jeep focused on adaptability. That’s why the rear seats clip securely into floor-mounted hooks and can be removed entirely or rotated to create rear-facing seating. Removing the rear seats allows for storing camping gear, motorcycles, and other adventure equipment without compromise. Dark Graphite leather, orange inserts, contrast stitching, bronze accents, and SRT embroidery adorn the touchpoints of the interior.

Jeep didn’t specify which engine the Scrambler Wrangler uses or detail power figures, but the automaker did leave us some clues about its powertrain. The concept model “honors the original Scrambler” by “imagining how far its rule-breaking formula could be pushed when V-8 power, desert-racing hardware, and SRT performance development are allowed to run without constraint.” That sure sounds like a V-8 engine is under the hood. We’ll cross our fingers that it’s the supercharged 6.2-liter unit out of the Hellcat lineup.

“The Wrangler Scrambler SRT Concept combines everything enthusiasts love about Jeep vehicles—capability, open-air freedom and authenticity—with the power, control and relentless performance mindset that define SRT,” Kuniskis said. “It is overbuilt and engineered around the idea that the desert can be every bit as demanding as a racetrack. It is unapologetic by design.”

And while this may seem like a flight of SEMA fancy, we suspect it may be a tease for a production car to come. After all, Stellantis’s wide-ranging brand presentation back in May said the carmaker was planning a 2-door Wrangler Scrambler pickup, and that said vehicle would also have an SRT version...
