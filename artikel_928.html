T20 the focus as batters bowl and 'Sandpapergate' saga rumbles on

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

England begin five-match T20 series against beleaguered Australia in Melbourne on Saturday (8.15am UK); Harry Brook's side looking to build-on ODI series win as Adil Rashid eyes recall after being omitted for 50-over matches; Mitchell Marsh does not appear at pre-series press conference

"You could see it going horribly wrong, couldn't you? You could see things imploding."

That was Michael Atherton's take on Australia before the 'Sandpapergate' scandal spectacularly hit the headlines once again following Usman Khawaja's claims in his autobiography.

Three weeks ago, the biggest problems for the Baggy Greens were Test batting woes, including a bang-out-of-form Marnus Labuschagne, and some odd selection decisions.

That "implosion" Athers predicted on the field has come off it, with David Warner and Cameron Bancroft supporting Khawaja's account that all members of the team were aware of the sandpaper scheme in 2018, and Pat Cummins strenuously denying it.

Australian cricket is at civil war and there is no obvious end in sight.

Questions abound (will Cummins survive as captain? Will Steve Smith, Nathan Lyon and Josh Hazlewood have their say? Does Cricket Australia have any choice but to launch a fresh investigation and not just an open forum?) but answers are in short supply.

Runs were also in short supply for Australia in the final two ODIs against England as a side helmed by Mitchell Marsh in the absence of the rested (but surely mentally tired) Cummins were twice bundled out in the low 200s chasing targets of 320-plus.

That meant Harry Brook's tourists were able to bounce back from being pummelled in Perth in the opening fixture - a result which brought back horrible memories of their Ashes thrashing at the venue a year ago - to claim a come-from-behind 2-1 series win.

They will now look to pile the misery on their beleaguered hosts with victory in a five-match T20 international series, starting in Melbourne on Saturday (first ball at 8.15am UK time).

At times in the ODIs, it felt like England were toying with an Australia team whose minds were perhaps understandably elsewhere. Jofra Archer sent out to bat at No 6 in Hobart, for example, and also Brook and Tom Banton being asked to deliver their part-time off-spin.

If Archer's elevation was a one-off - it certainly won't be seen in the T20s with the England quick rested as he prepares for the three-Test assignment in South Africa from December 17 - then the use of Brook and Banton with the ball may be a more regular occurrence, especially in 50-over cricket as Brendon McCullum's side laser in on the 2027 World Cup in South Africa, Zimbabwe and Namibia.

Head coach McCullum said: "These guys are talented, there is something there.

"We are looking at a World Cup in 12 months' time, and no one knows what the conditions are going to be like. You could end up on a pitch that is exploding and need some options.

"We will keep encouraging them to do that. That World Cup is potentially 13 games in three different countries, fresh pitches, there's not much data on how those pitches will play."

Brook and Banton bowled 2.5 overs between them in the ODI series, which is 2.5 overs more than Adil Rashid. The frontline spinner had ample time to pour over the sandpaper storm after being left out for all three games, with Liam Dawson once again favoured.

Rashid will hope to be recalled for the T20s, starting at the weekend on a ground where he helped England beat Pakistan in the 2022 T20 World Cup final. The 38-year-old has played in all 19 of his side's T20Is in 2026, appearing alongside Dawson in 18 of them.

Brook, like Rashid, has fond memories of the MCG. He, too, was part of the T20 World Cup-winning squad four years ago, while he top-scored in a blink-and-you'll-miss-it, two-day Ashes Test there last winter as England claimed their only win of a sobering tour.

England sit top of the T20I rankings - Australia are third, behind India - after winning 23 of their last 26 completed matches in the format. This series will serve as good preparation for the next T20 World Cup, which will be held in Australia and New Zealand in 2028.

Brook was all smiles during his pre-series media duties in Melbourne on Friday, saying he wanted to guide his side to a 4-0 lead over Australia before he departs for the South Africa tour ahead of the series finale in Canberra on December 2.

He also spoke of his excitement that the uncapped James Coles, Henry Crocombe, Eddie Jack and Thomas Rew were able to experience being in an England squad in Australia, albeit without confirming if or when they would make their T20I debuts.

We do not know how Australia skipper Marsh is feeling, though, with the 34-year-old not expected to engage in any media activity with the written press throughout the T20 leg following Bancroft's explosive claims in that detail-filled account in the West Australian.

Marsh was part of the XI for the now infamous Cape Town Test eight years ago.

Cummins, Hazlewood and Mitchell Starc will not feature in the series at all as chat regarding what they did or did not know about the sandpaper use runs rampant.

Cummins and Hazlewood are rested, like they were for the ODIs, while Starc, who also sat out the 50-over encounters, has retired from international 20-over cricket.

Make no mistake, though. The heat is very much on them.

On the field, the heat will be on Marsh and an inexperienced bowling attack - weakened further by injuries to Mahli Beardman (quad) and Xavier Bartlett (knee) - to contain an England side that, despite missing the injured Jacob Bethell and Jos Buttler, is formidable.

Australia will also be out to avoid the top-order collapses of the final two ODIs, a goal that could be made easier by Gus Atkinson and Sonny Baker joining Archer in being rested ahead of the red-ball trip to South Africa, which you can watch live on Sky Sports.

What the home side want most, however, is a series win over England and for all this sandpaper chat to stop. The first of those objectives looks tough. The second impossible.
