A medical tribunal has heard that a doctor who helped save the life of Premier League footballer Fabrice Muamba engaged in sexual activity with a "vulnerable" patient on practice premises during working hours.

Dr Jonathan Tobin, based at Kirkham Health Centre in Preston, was among four men who assisted Muamba when the midfielder suffered a cardiac arrest on the pitch while playing for Bolton at Tottenham in an FA Cup match in March 2012.

Muamba’s heart stopped for 78 minutes, but he was able to make a full recovery before announcing his retirement from football five months after the incident.

Mr Tobin received further praise in November 2021 when Wigan Athletic striker Charlie Wyke thanked him for saving his life following a cardiac arrest during training.

However, Mr Tobin has now been struck off the medical register after scheduling unnecessary medical appointments to meet a "vulnerable" woman and subsequently falsifying her medical records to cover up their relationship, a Medical Practitioners Tribunal panel wrote.

The panel heard that their relationship initiated after they kissed inside his consulting room in July 2024 and persisted until November of that year. Mr Tobin admitted to engaging in sexual activity at the surgery during working hours, and acknowledged dishonestly altering her records twice to state she failed to attend appointments.

Fully aware of the woman’s vulnerability, Mr Tobin acknowledged that she needed "a good empathic GP", adding: "What she got was incredibly harmful." Stephen Brassington, his legal representative, stated that the doctor accepted complete responsibility, noting that his shame and contrition "could not be more obvious".

The tribunal observed that Mr Tobin’s professional style was "informal, friendly, and made use of humour", adding: "He enjoyed being popular with his patients, rather than being formal."

The panel further wrote: "Dr Tobin allowed himself to form a sexual relationship with this patient in his surgery when all his professional training and experience will have told him that behaviour of that nature was unacceptable."

Despite acknowledging that Mr Tobin had been a "fine doctor", the panel concluded that maintaining his registration would undermine public trust and confidence in the profession, issuing an immediate order of suspension.
