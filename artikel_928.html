More than F1 - Monza's captivating football story

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Monza were formed in 1912 but did not play in Serie A for the first time until 2022

Think Monza, and the mind conjures up famous winning drives by Lewis Hamilton and Michael Schumacher, and by Kimi Antonelli this year on the way to his first Formula 1 title.

Sporting theatre on four wheels is not all that this northern Italian city has to offer, though.

Just under four miles from the historic home of motor racing's Italian Grand Prix sits the 17,000-capacity Stadio Brianteo, home to Serie A football club AC Monza.

It's not easy for the club to capture attention, given European giants AC Milan and Inter Milan play at the San Siro, a 20-minute drive away, while the romantic lure of Como is less than an hour from here by train.

But Monza have their own captivating story to tell.

Founded in 1912, the Biancorossi - white and reds - did not even play in Serie B for the first time until 1951. As recently as 2017, they were playing part-time in Serie D.

Under the guidance of former player Giovanni Stroppa, Monza were promoted to Serie A for the first time in 2022. Stroppa was sacked after a slow start in the top flight, but Monza survived for three years before being relegated.

They then bounced back immediately, earning their place among the elite again in May of this year.

Now owned by American venture capitalists Beckett Layne Ventures (BLV), and with former Southampton boss Ivan Juric in the manager's chair, Monza have a clear vision for how they can make an impact in their crowded geographical market.

AC Monza have played at the 17,000-capacity Stadio Brianteo since 1988

I am speaking to Monza chief executive Mauro Baldissoni in an office at the Stadio Brianteo, having been given a brief tour of the club's training facilities at Monzello, less than a five-minute drive away.

The atmosphere is positive after a 4-0 win over Cagliari the previous night - the club's biggest ever Serie A victory.

Monza sit third bottom at the time of writing, a point from safety, but the club feel so much is in their favour.

"First off, Monza is on the outskirts of Milan, so the location is attractive for the players," says Baldissoni. "It's a good point of visibility for what we do.

"There is a point of attraction and point of awareness about Monza, because it is already known in the world for luxury sports through the association with Formula 1.

"The infrastructure at the club is larger and probably more efficient than Milanello, the sporting centre of Milan, because they cannot have all the teams from the youngest in the academy to the first team like we can. This province specifically is one of the richest in terms of GDP in Europe.

"So, you have all the premises to create a good project. And there is always people coming to watch.

"Yesterday there were people from Newcastle, Fulham, Southampton, Sunderland, Liverpool and Borussia Dortmund at the game, and there were others. If you come here, you can watch AC Milan, Como, [an] Atalanta game, Inter game, and Monza.

"Scouts and sporting directors can come for two days and watch different games at a high level."

England youth international Jay Robinson (left) celebrates with striker Gustavo Varela during Monza's 4-0 win against Cagliari in October

Monza president Lauren Crampsie was previously the chief marketing officer at Chelsea.

Crampsie, 46, watched the pre-match build-up from the pitch before the Cagliari win, making sure all the details were in order. Afterwards, she is mindful to let the players and coaching staff enjoy their victory.

Young striker Gustavo Varela is the star of the night, scoring two of his 11 goals in all competitions so far this season, earning himself a first senior call-up to the Portugal squad.

Monza paid Benfica Â£2.5m for the 21-year-old, who is currently top scorer in Serie A, with nine in nine games.

Already there are big-name suitors sniffing around, doubtless willing to pay a fee significantly higher than the one Monza forked out.

It is a perfect illustration of the business model the club are trying to create.

"We always think the club's mission is to win games, but it is also about forming players that are part of their academy first and also trying to identify talents that can fit with the purposes and the style of the club," says Baldissoni, who spent 10 years with Roma before leaving in 2020.

"In Rome we were able to identify young talents like Marquinhos, who was 18 years old and able to play as a starter before we sold him to Paris St-Germain.

"There were others like Erik Lamela and Mo Salah. But in Rome it is more difficult because expectations are higher.

"In Monza the idea is the same. The pressure is bigger because the impact of relegation on revenues is worse. But the environment and the sporting expectations are more manageable for a young player who has more time to form his emotional background."

Monza fought off competition from League One club Leicester to sign Southampton's England youth international Jay Robinson on loan this season, with Juric's presence being key.

The 19-year-old has already played 10 times in all competitions, scoring twice.

The main entrance of Monza's training ground

Fiorentina, Roma, Como and Juventus provide a stiff run of fixtures for Monza heading towards Christmas, before an equally important match against Venezia, who are currently one place and one point worse off in the table.

Baldissoni is optimistic. His wider view of the football landscape is not altered by the knowledge that promoted Premier League trio Coventry, Hull and Ipswich all spent in excess of Â£120m in the summer, a figure Monza can only dream of.

Plans are under way to refurbish Stadio Brianteo, which has covering over a single stand.

"Infrastructure is the weakest point in Italy under the competition point of view, to compete with other global markets," Baldissoni says.

"But there are costs and there are investments."

He says the stadium is "an investment meant to generate more revenues".

"Of course, the Premier League's revenues are much higher than all the other leagues, so those clubs have a competitive advantage.

"But they also overpay players because it's Premier League. Maybe they get a player that I would like to have because they can pay more, but maybe I can find another player that is not that far away. Then you have to be able to form the player.

"So, you identify a talent early, and then you keep forming him to bring him to a level where some other players already are. Maybe then we sell him to Premier League?"

It's a delicate balancing act, but Monza are sure it can be done.

"Sassuolo made it to Serie A 13 years ago for the first time and until last season, spent all that time in Serie A. Atalanta was going up and down and now it's one of the prominent teams, targeting European competitions every year," Baldissoni says.

"This is out of the work they've been doing in terms of identifying talents, forming talents, and remaining competitive.

"That is the target. It is possible. Let's see if we can make it."
