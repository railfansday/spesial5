Phillips Names Senior Leadership in Asia, Clark Art Institute Hires New Chief Curator, and More: Industry Moves for November 19, 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Editor’s Note: This story originally appeared in On Balance, the ARTnews newsletter about the art market and beyond. Sign up here to receive it every Wednesday.

Phillips Names Senior Leadership in Asia: The house confirmed the promotion of Cherry Lam to managing director of Asia; she was promoted earlier this year to general manager of Asia. Crystal Wong has joined as head of handbags for Asia.

Clark Art Institute Names New Chief Curator: The western Massachusetts museumhas hired Caroline Shields for the top curatorial role, beginning in January. She joins from the Art Gallery of Ontario in Toronto, where she is currently curator of European art and head of the department. Joyce Foundation Names Winners of Inaugural Batch of Revamped Awards: As ARTnews reported in April, the Chicago-based nonprofit decided to change its $100,000 project-based grant to an unrestricted grant of the same amount after a short hiatus. Now, it has revealed the first winners: poet Heid E. Erdrich (based in Minneapolis), bassist and composer Marion Hayden (Highland Park, Michigan), social practice artist Ck Ledesma Borrero (Milwaukee), and traditional Native American culture artist Ronald Paquin (St. Ignace, Michigan).

Beatriz Cortez Wins California Artist Residency: A partnership between Los Angeles Nomadic Division (LAND) and Presqu’ile Winery in the Central Coast city of Santa Maria, the residency will see Cortez, who is based between Los Angeles and Davis, California, create a new project for the vineyard’s 13,000-square-foot concrete exhibition space, opening next spring.

Nara Roesler and Andrew Kreps Announce Joint Representation of Maria Dávila and Eduardo Portillo: Based in Mérida, in the Venezuelan Andes, the two textile artists have been working together since 1983. They will show new work at both galleries’ booths at Art Basel Miami Beach, ahead of a two-venue solo across the galleries’ New York spaces, curated by Roesler’s senior curatorial director Luis Pérez-Oramas.

Ryan Lee Takes on Brian Bress: Working across performance, painting, sculpture, and drawing, the Los Angeles–based artist will have a solo show at the New York gallery in January.

Galvak Now Represents Rachel Lee Hovnanian: The sculptor, who splits her time Miami, New York, and Italy, will have work in the West Palm Beach gallery’s booth at Art Basel Miami Beach, to coincide with a solo exhibition at The Ritz-Carlton in South Beach.

Lisu Vega Joins Gladwell Projects: The New York–based gallery founded by dealer Christiana Ine-Kimba Boyle will have a solo presentation for the artist at NADA Miami next month.

Deutsche Bank Names 2027 Artist of the Year: Mexico City–based Tania Franco Klein will present work in the bank’s VIP lounge at Frieze Los Angeles next February.

That’s the running total, inclusive of buyer’s fees, that Christie’s, Sotheby’s, and Phillips have sold at the three evening sales held in New York this week. At Sotheby’s on Monday night, a double header sale generated a total of $378.7 million, with the white-glove Baum collection bringing in $110 million and the contemporary sale realized $268.8 million. The top lots, respectively, were Joan Mitchell’s Green Book of Barbara Guest (1960) for $19.3 million and Roy Lichtenstein’s Reflections on “Painter and Model” (1990) for $24.7 million. Tuesday evening saw first Phillips sell $104.4 million in art and then Christie’s having a haul of $626.6 million. Amedeo Modigliani’s Tête (1911–12) led the Phillips auction, selling for $44.6 million, while a short bidding war saw Wassily Kandinsky’s Betonte Ecken (1922–23) go for $85.8 million, a new record for the artist. Christie’s set records for four other artists (Wifredo Lam, Cornelius Cornelisz. van Haarlem, Fernando Botero, and Hans Bellmer), while Sotheby’s also saw five records on Monday (for Yayoi Kusama, Sam Francis, Jonas Wood, Firelei Báez, and Tomma Abts.)

While we’re on the topic of Sotheby’s, Christie’s, and Phillips, the New York Times published this week a feature looking at how Phillips’s recent decision not to compete with the other two houses. Calling Phillips “always an underdog in the auction world,” reporters Zachary Small and Julia Halperin got the house’s chief executive, Martin Wilson, to say, “One client described us as scrappers and I loved that description.” At least to my mind, that doesn’t come off as intended. Though it would seem that Phillips has admitted defeat and retreated like a dog with its tail between its legs, Wilson said that the house no longer believes in growing for the sake of growing, especially at the expense of profitability. It’s been a year of change for Phillips’s top brass, with several executives departing, as ARTnews previously reported. One of them, former Asia chairman Jonathan Crockett, told the Times that his decision to leave was based on the fact that this “strategy no longer resonated with me.”

For more auction-related coverage, might I suggest reading three pieces from ARTnews regarding controversies over whether or not a work would actually make it to the block. There’s the $180 million Vincent van Gogh painting that will be auctioned by Sotheby’s tonight after a last-minute settlement. Two Gothic paintings are to be sold on Saturday in Germany despite claims to stop the sales. And there’s a Frida Kahlo painting that was pulled from last night’s Christie’s evening sale. But, if I were you, I’d skip Jerry Saltz’s latest, an overly “sentimental” column, bemoaning last night’s sale of works owned by the reportedly cash-strapped artist Jeff Koons. Despite his objections, the works sold for $104 million, or the sale’s low estimate.
