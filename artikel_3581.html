Sam Rockwell Breaks Down Scenes From His Career, Including ‘Charlie’s Angels’ and ‘The White Lotus’

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

“Thank God for Marvin Gaye and this song,” Sam Rockwell says while watching a younger version of himself dance opposite a stunned Drew Barrymore in the 2000 action-comedy Charlie’s Angels. “I mean, this song really makes the scene.”

While telling stories from his more than 35-year career across film and television for Vanity Fair’s Scene Selection, Rockwell says people tried to stop him from appearing in the remake of the 1970s TV series starring Barrymore, Cameron Diaz, and Lucy Liu. When he joined the project as secretly evil software engineer Eric Knox—who starts the film as Barrymore’s love interest—the script was “a work in progress,” Rockwell recalls. “A lot of people told me not to do this movie. And then, they all ate their words. It had a very specific kind of popcorn vibe to it, very unique, and I don’t think I’ve seen [it] since.”

Rockwell originally envisioned a more “punk rock” direction for his character, but changed his approach after speaking with friend and former Galaxy Quest costar Alan Rickman. “You know, Sam, keep it simple,” Rockwell recalled the late actor telling him. “When I did Die Hard, I killed someone and ate a sandwich.”

Over the decades, Rockwell tried his hand at a mix of projects—somber indies like sci-fi drama Moon (2009), studio awards contenders including The Green Mile (1999), and mainstream fare like Iron Man 2 (2010). “I was dating Leslie Bibb when Iron Man one came out…. It sort of changed superhero movies,” says Rockwell, whose now real-life partner of nearly 20 years appeared in the first film.

After being cast in the sequel as fast-talking arms dealer Justin Hammer, Rockwell learned that he would share most of his scenes with Mickey Rourke as fellow fictional baddie and Russian physicist Ivan Vanko. “Mickey didn’t want to meet me until we were on camera,” Rockwell remembers. “It was a wild experience…. Because my scenes were dialogue-driven, whereas a lot of [the rest of] it was special effects…it was a surprisingly actor-friendly experience being that it was a studio movie for me.”

Bibb and Rockwell, who also shared scenes in Iron Man 2, reunited onscreen in 2025 for season three of The White Lotus as it was filmed in Thailand. He joined the series partway through production, tasked with performing Mike White’s “brilliant monologue” about sex addiction and gender identity opposite Walton Goggins. “Leslie helped me get off-book in South Africa; we were on safari,” says Rockwell, who says he based his searching Buddhist character, Frank, on Martin Sheen in Apocalypse Now.

The actor also detailed the past screen inspiration behind his Oscar-winning performance in Martin McDonagh’s drama, Three Billboards Outside Ebbing, Missouri (2017), calling the trajectory of his role as a bigoted local cop, “[The Andy Griffith Show’s] Barney Fife becomes [Taxi Driver’s] Travis Bickle,” says Rockwell. He joins forces with McDonagh once more for the new awards-season contender Wild Horse Nine, in which he acts with teenage idol John Malkovich. “I love characters who change and are underestimated by the audience,” says Rockwell of Three Billboards. “And this is really that.”
