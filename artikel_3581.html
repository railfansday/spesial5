What Happened to Saylor Hayes? Girl's Parents Arrested for Her Death

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

More than two months after 5-year-old Saylor Hayes was found dead in a South Carolina waterway during a family vacation, authorities have revealed her cause of death — and as the criminal investigation into her parents continues.

Saylor’s father, John Herb Hayes III, and her mother, Geordyn Nichole Hayes, were arrested in Tennessee on September 1, each charged with unlawfully placing a child at risk/wilfully abandoning a child. The kindergartner — who is both nonverbal and autistic — was discovered in the water near her family’s Pawleys Island, South Carolina, rental home on August 31, 2026, following a massive search involving hundreds of people and multiple agencies.

Here’s everything to know about the case, the cause of death ruling and the allegations against Saylor’s parents.

On November 9, 2026, the Washington County, Virginia, Sheriff’s Office shared findings from Georgetown County, South Carolina, coroners, who concluded that Saylor died from asphyxia due to drowning, with child neglect listed as a contributing factor.

The manner of death, however, remains undetermined while the criminal investigation into the circumstances surrounding Saylor’s death continues.

“This classification reflects the inability to conclusively determine whether Saylor’s death was accidental or the result of a homicidal act,” a Georgetown County Coroner’s Office spokesperson told WCBC.

Saylor’s body was found in the waters near Hagley Landing on the morning of August 31, 2026, one day after locals and law enforcement officials launched a large-scale search of the area surrounding the family’s rental property.

John and Geordyn were taken into custody in Tennessee on September 1, 2026, and each charged with unlawfully placing a child at risk/wilfully abandoning a child, the Georgetown County Sheriff’s Office announced that same day. The couple have yet to enter a plea.

“The body of the missing girl, who had been the subject of an intensive search involving hundreds of people and multiple agencies, was found yesterday morning in the waters near Hagley Landing,” police said in the statement. “Once extradition proceedings are complete, the couple will be transported back to Georgetown County.”

According to the arrest affidavit, John and Geordyn allegedly put their daughter “at an unreasonable risk of harm affecting her life and safety by failing to provide supervision and safeguards appropriate for a severely nonverbal, autistic, 5-year-old child with a known affinity to water and a history of elopement.”

Investigators determined that Saylor was not wearing her Project Lifesaver transmitter when she disappeared. The young girl typically wore the device because she had a tendency to wander off when she wasn’t supervised by an adult.

Saylor began wearing the monitoring device in 2024, but it was allegedly removed before the family left their Virginia home for the South Carolina trip. It’s not clear why the transmitter was taken off.

According to authorities, John and Geordyn further complicated search efforts by directing law enforcement to look for Saylor using the very tracking technology she wasn’t wearing.

“[They] encouraged search efforts to be directed toward the use of tracking technology, despite knowing that the child was not wearing the monitoring device,” a warrant stated. “[They] further directed search efforts away from the nearby body of water.”

In a statement issued on September 1, 2026, Georgetown County Sheriff Carter Weaver suggested that the Hayeses may have violated a “fundamental responsibility to protect children who cannot protect themselves.”

“When the evidence establishes probable cause to believe that a child has been unlawfully placed in circumstances that endanger that child’s safety, law enforcement has a responsibility to act,” Weaver said.

The sheriff emphasized that Saylor was a “vulnerable five-year-old child who depended upon the adults responsible for her care and protection.”

“She deserved to be safe. She deserved to be protected,” Weaver continued. “These arrests are based upon the evidence developed during this investigation and the applicable law. They should not be interpreted as a determination of guilt. The defendants are presumed innocent unless and until proven guilty in a court of law.”

Following Saylor’s death, reports emerged that Geordyn had allegedly been charged with child abuse over a separate matter six months before her daughter’s death.

That earlier case reportedly involved accusations of child abuse/neglect, along with reckless disregard for life, stemming from an alleged incident involving a juvenile male.

Us has reached out to representatives for the Hayes family for comment.

The investigation into Saylor’s death remains active, and the manner of death — whether accidental or homicidal — has yet to be formally classified by authorities. John and Geordyn are expected to be returned to Georgetown County to face their charges once extradition proceedings are complete.

For now, the questions surrounding why Saylor’s Project Lifesaver tracker was removed before the family’s trip and why her parents allegedly misdirected searchers continue to drive the investigation forward.
