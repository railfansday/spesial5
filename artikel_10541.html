Pixel Tag is a brilliant Band-Aid on a Find Hub network Google can’t easily fix

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Google Pixel Tag is here and, at least so far, it’s really good. But fresh hardware and some new features can’t fix the core problem. Google’s Find Hub network has been restricted from the very start, and it’s not something Google can easily fix.

The good news on Pixel Tag: this is a well-built tracker that, unlike others, is the clear product of the hardware having direct access to the software. Little touches like being able to adjust ringer volume on the fly combine nicely with UWB – the latter something we rarely see on third-party devices largely due to Apple – all things that elevate the Find Hub tracker to what we called the new “gold standard.”

The bad news: this is still a Find Hub tracker.

Google’s Find Hub network has improved dramatically over the two years since its launch. Where Google originally had to admit that tracking performance was lacking (a nice way of putting it, frankly), improvements started arriving in 2025. We noticed that the network seemed to be working much better a few months after its wide rollout, and Google later confirmed speed improvements.

But, ultimately, the Find Hub network is still reeling from how it debuted.

Again, it’s gotten better since then, but it’s still not as good as it should be. Samsung’s network, limited solely to Galaxy phones, shouldn’t outperform Google’s. Find Hub trackers should be just as good as AirTags and the third-party trackers that work with Apple’s network. They’re just not, and I truly do believe it all boils down to this initial choice. One of the reasons this is getting better is that Google has been pushing users to the “all areas” setting during the setup of a new device – I’ve noticed this on some recent devices I set up, but it’s not been completely consistent just yet. That’s a good step forward but, even if it is the new default, that still requires millions of users to upgrade their devices, and we’ve never been in a better time to not upgrade your phone.

All of that is to say, the Pixel Tag isn’t fixing this problem. Surely, it’ll give Google some needed fire to fix the problem, but new hardware isn’t the solution. That’s something I was discussing with 9to5Google readers on our review. UWB is a tentpole feature of the Pixel Tag, but it doesn’t change the network problems, it just improves precision finding. We needed a first-party tracker like this regardless of the state of the network, something that really takes advantage of the platform and pushes it forward, but it’s just a quick fix, a Band-Aid.
