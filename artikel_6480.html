Why Weed Is Now a Family Thanksgiving Tradition

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Getting stoned in America has never been more socially acceptable. In the 14 years since Washington and Colorado first legalized recreational cannabis, a majority of states have followed suit by relaxing restrictions as well. While policy has a ways to go, the social stigma attached to the substance has dissipated into a skunky haze. If you’re not convinced, just look at the sheer number of Americans lining up to buy weed each Thanksgiving.

The day before Turkey day—dubbed Green Wednesday by the cannabis industry—might soon outpace 4/20 as the busiest day of the year for dispensaries. News outlets have covered this phenomenon in recent years with varying degrees of sincerity and snark, but the through line is always the same: People are getting high on Thanksgiving not only to improve the meal, but to make spending time with family more bearable—and apparently it’s kind of working.

It makes sense: For many Americans, the fourth Thursday in November is for reuniting with relatives who, were it not for blood, might be the last people you’d ever choose to sit next to on a public bus. Thanksgiving is precision engineered for passive-aggressive ideological bickering and wrenching open old emotional wounds. It’s the day when adult siblings remind each other of their deepest insecurities while loading the dishwasher together, and when drunk aunts across the country engage in the time-honored tradition of casually outing their gay nephews to the table.

While this aspect of Thanksgiving provides plenty of material for movies, comedy specials, and sitcom bottle episodes, the lived reality is often less entertaining. The Reddit threads and advice column queries that crop up around this time each year tend to frame the holiday as a problem to be dealt with—a time for wielding the skills gleaned from cognitive behavioral therapy. Sometimes, in more dire terms, it’s a day one must try to survive. No wonder so many people are opting for a predinner edible.

But getting high merely as a means to dissociate through it all isn’t what all of these people seem to be stepping outside for. When Lisa Bonos reported on the phenomenon back in 2019, she spoke with several people who found that getting stoned with family members had a transformative impact on their relationships. Weed helped high-strung parents chill out, impart a “cozy atmosphere” for the stoned and sober alike, and most significantly, it dissolved intergenerational communication barriers. As Bonos pointed out, “getting high together can help equalize a historically unequal relationship—at any age.”

This is all anecdotal, of course, but there’s research to back it up. Humans have used cannabis for thousands of years socially through ritual and celebration, and the endocannabinoid signaling system plays an important role in how humans process social interactions by easing anxiety and fostering feelings of connectedness. Granted, this depends on dose and context.

I don’t mean to suggest that cannabis is a miracle substance that will heal the American family. It goes without saying that a whole lot of stuff is far too painful, complex, or irreconcilable to resolve by passing a joint. Opting for an alternative celebration, such as Friendsgiving, chosen family’s-giving, or DINKs-go-out-to-a-nice-restaurant-instead’s-giving, is for many, the safest way to go.

But for the rest of Americans already imbibing this holiday to deal with more casually dysfunctional matters, it might be time to take the “cousin walk” out of the shadows and invite other cautiously interested members to partake. Since dosage really matters, I’d forgo slathering the Turkey in cannabutter and instead opt for a tin of one of those little low-THC, high-CBD edibles as an option for the inexperienced. Lest we forget when famous New York Times columnist Maureen Dowd ate too much of a THC infused chocolate bar, causing her to hide in a Denver hotel room in fear that she was both dead and wanted by the police.

Smoking weed with your family may not lead to a miracle, but at the very least, the odds of getting into a heated debate with your uncle regarding the directional shift of the Overton window are less likely if he’s zooted on the couch, reading the Wikipedia page on pecans. And maybe, just maybe, as Thanksgiving continues to get greener, it might become—for some—a day to look forward to rather than a day to endure.
