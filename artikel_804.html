Westlife Sets 2027 North America Tour: Exclusive

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

"This is probably one of the most exciting moments of our whole career," Kian Egan tells Billboard.

The beloved Irish pop group will extend their Westlife 25: The Anniversary World Tour with a 12-show run across the United States and Canada next March, Billboard can exclusively reveal.

Announced today, the North America trek will get underway March 1, 2027 at YouTube Theater in Inglewood, CA, then visits San Francisco, Edmonton, Calgary, Vancouver, Uncasville, Boston, Orlando, Irving, Toronto, Rosemont, and comes to a halt March 19 at Madison Square Garden in New York City. It’s just the second tour of North America for the Irish lads, following a sold-out trek in 2024 which included Manhattan’s Radio City Music Hall. This next trek, however, promises to dwarf what came before it.

“This is probably one of the most exciting moments of our whole career,” Kian Egan tells Billboard over a Zoom from Paris on Sunday (Nov. 14), just prior to their first-ever performance at the Zenith. Indeed, their first ever performance in the French capital. “Madison Square Garden was always a dream that we thought was probably not in reach for us, because we never really broke the States.” Because of the success of the last tour, the lads dared to dream. “It’s only starting to become real in our minds now,” Egan enthuses.

The date at Allstate Arena in Rosemont, IL will be a particularly special one, landing as it does on St. Patrick’s Day, Wednesday March, 17. “To be Irish boys in our late 40s, bringing people together in Chicago,” says Shane Filan, “that’s just so special to us.” As for playing the Garden, “honestly, it’s mind-blowing. Absolutely mind-blowing. To see the poster with the words Madison Square Garden, I literally started crying the other day when I heard like the announcement. When you think of the artists that have walked on that stage. It’s the most famous arena in the world, without a doubt. And we’re going to have Westlife’s name over.”

Westlife are, based on this ongoing world tour, bigger than ever. Produced by Live Nation, the jaunt is group’s most ambitious yet with 90 concerts, including a sold-out 42-date run of stadiums and arenas in the United Kingdom and Ireland. As it stands, the itinerary will motor into the second half of 2027, rounding out with arena shows in Australia and New Zealand, for their first trip down under in 20 years.

“This whole thing is so emotional for us. It’s just so incredibly emotional from beginning to end, you know?,” Nicky Byrne explains. “We can sit here and talk about the stadiums, like Wembley Stadium and Croke Park, which all means so much to us, but we genuinely sit down as a band and pinch yourselves and say, you know, you’re here for 80 years on this earth if you’re lucky, what else can we do while we still are young enough to do it? MSG was always out of reach. We’re just counting ourselves very lucky. The whole tour, we cannot wait to do America, and then Australia and New Zealand next. It’s gonna be quite the ride, to be honest with you.”

The luck of the Irish has nothing to do with it.

Formed in 1998, Westlife hold the record for most consecutive No. 1s in the United States (seven) and most singles to debut at the summit. All told, they’ve scored eight No. 1 albums in the U.K. and have an impressive haul of 14 No. 1 singles, a tally surpassed only by the Beatles and Elvis Presley. Twenty-five Westlife singles have crashed the U.K. top 10.

According to Warner Music’s EastWest division, which signed the act in 2021 on St. Patrick’s Day, the group has sold over 55 million records worldwide. And according to Live Nation, they’ve shifted upwards of 6 million concert tickets around the world.

They’re the U.K. & Ireland’s top-selling album group of the 21st century.

In the United States, the band landed a top 20 hit on the Billboard Hot 100 with 2000’s “Swear It Again,” peaking at No. 20, and their self-titled debut album from 1999 cracked the Billboard 200, hitting No. 129.

“I don’t think any of us ever thought we’d be sitting here doing a 25th anniversary tour,” says Egan. “The fact that we were a boyband when we started, it was always like, oh, boy bands last for five years at most, and then you have a member leave and it falls apart and, you know, all these different things happen.”

Recounting the departure of Brian McFadden in 2004, “we did break up for a period of time. We got back together again and realized, you know, there’s still so much love out there for us.”

The start of the tour coincided with the release of Westlife’s Ultimate Collection, which included new material such as the lead single “Chariot” (written by Ed Sheeran and produced by Steve Mac), along with “Your Love Amazes Me,” “First Time Feeling,” and “Beautiful Life.” New recordings aren’t on their collective mind. Right now, it’s all about the tour and soaking it all up with fans.

“I think what’s happening is people are transforming back to that childhood that they had, and they’re acting like that as well in the room. It’s been a bit of a bonkers kind of moment,” Byrne says. “It’s all been very much pinch-yourself moments on this tour.”
