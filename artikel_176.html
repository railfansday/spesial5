Today, House Democrats are set to begin their closed-door leadership elections. One particular race to watch: minority leader Hakeem Jeffries is expected to seek the caucus’ formal backing as its candidate for Speaker in the next Congress through a recorded vote, according to several reports.

Jeffries is widely expected to win and, with Democrats poised to hold a sizeable House majority, secure a glide path to the speakership when the full chamber votes in January. If elected Speaker, Jeffries would become the first Black person to hold the gavel in US history.

Jeffries is not facing a challenger, and despite the possibility of some defections from the incoming progressives, he is still expected to have the 218 votes needed to win on the House floor.

Democrats will cast votes for House majority leader and whip, and the caucus chair and vice chair, among other positions.

Doctors and public health leaders expressed grief and alarm at the loss of US measles-free status, a hard-won public health victory that once made the US a leader in infectious disease elimination.

Measles was declared resurgent in the United States, Bolivia and Mexico by the Pan American Health Organization (Paho) on Tuesday, meaning the virus has spread continuously for 12 months.

The announcement formally ends a 26-year record of measles elimination in the US, and comes amid the worst measles outbreak since 1991, making the loss of measles elimination status widely predicted. The Americas were once the only measles-free World Health Organization (WHO) region on the globe.

“There’s no question it was emotional, because we’ve been working on this initiative for decades,” said Dr Jon Andrus, the chair of the Paho advisory committee that recommended the US lose its measles elimination status.

“To see the US, once a global leader, lose its status is of course sad,” Andrus continued, citing the work of friends and colleagues, “and it was also sad to see our Mexican colleagues lose their status, as well as Bolivia.”

That grief was in sharp contrast to the reaction of the Trump administration. Health secretary Robert F Kennedy Jr, who has spent years spreading misinformation about the measles, mumps and rubella combination vaccine (MMR) and downplaying the disease, had his agency promote an appearance with a Texas-based chef and posted a picture of himself as James Bond this week. After devastating midterm losses, Trump appointed Maha advocate Stephen Miller as White House chief of staff.

The US is in the midst of the worst measles outbreak in decades, with more than 5,400 cases and 10 measles-associated deaths reported by state health authorities. Measles is considered one of the most infectious diseases known to medicine. The infection kills one to three unvaccinated people per 1,000 and hospitalizes up to one in five.

Late into the night on 3 November, while the lower 48 states were reeling from the blue wave crashing across the US, Mary Peltola took to the stage in a Sheraton hotel ballroom in Anchorage, Alaska, to greet her supporters.

It wasn’t a victory speech as such – there would still be long days ahead before all votes could be counted across this vast arctic state. But that didn’t prevent the Democratic Senate candidate from expressing her elation, bouncing up and down on stage, clapping her hands and clicking her fingers.

Before she could speak, a volunteer shouted out from the back of the hall: “Aarigaa!” Peltola picked up the refrain, and threw it right back:

“Aarigaa! Can we just chant aarigaa? Aarigaa! Aarigaa!”

It was just one word. But it encapsulated something special about Peltola and her insurgent’s campaign to unseat the incumbent Republican US senator, Dan Sullivan.

“Aarigaa” is an Iñupiaq word that is commonly understood across Alaska’s 229 federally recognised tribes. It is a word of celebration, of relief almost, that roughly translates to: “Way to go!” or “Attaboy!”

Now, Peltola genuinely has a reason for exclaiming it. On Tuesday – two weeks after the midterm elections – the tortuous count was finally completed, and she was declared winner.

In prevailing, she becomes the first Alaska Native to take a seat in the US Senate.

Mary Peltola’s win in Alaska to give the Democrats a commanding Senate majority of 56-44 seats has been hailed as “a gift” by the party’s leader.

Peltola will be Alaska’s next senator after beating the Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan, with the result called two weeks on from midterm elections which saw Donald Trump’s party lose ground across the country.

Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer, who enlisted Peltola to challenge Sullivan, wrote on social media that her victory is a “gift to Alaska, the Senate, and all of America”.

“It’s official: 56 Democratic Senate seats!” Schumer added. “Her knowledge and love for Alaska are unparalleled, and she ran a pitch-perfect campaign devoted to the issues Alaskans care most about – fish, family, and freedom.”

Peltola, a former US House lawmaker, is the first Democrat the state has elected to the Senate since 2008. Her victory comes just two years after Alaska voters unseated her while supporting Trump by a double-digit margin.

“From Juneau to Utqiagvik, the message from Alaskans is clear: the status quo in DC is not working for Alaska,” Peltola said in a statement on Tuesday.

“As a lifelong Alaskan who has dedicated my life to serving our state, there is no greater honor than having Alaskans’ support in our fight to take on corporate greed, lower costs, and put Alaska first,” she said. “As our next United States Senator, I will stand up to anyone to take on the rigged system in DC and deliver results for Alaska’s working families.”

However, Peltola’s success has not been without controversy. Last month the New York Times reported that her staffers were involved in a scheme to recruit another Republican challenger, also named Dan Sullivan, to appear on the ballot.

Peltola’s campaign had denied any involvement in encouraging the second Sullivan’s run, and after state election officials tried to remove him, Alaska’s supreme court in June ruled he be allowed to appear on the ballot.

Trump called for Peltola to be prosecuted over the apparent ruse, and Alaska’s top federal prosecutor announced that criminal and civil inquiries were under way, while the state’s governor separately announced plans to open a criminal investigation.
