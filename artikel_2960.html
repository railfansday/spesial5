Datadog vs. Figma: Which Technology Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Datadog and Figma pull in opposite directions, one with real profits and one with rapid growth, and the valuation gap may be the deciding factor.

By Brendan Coffey – Updated Nov 18, 2026 at 9:20AM EST

As cloud complexity grows and digital design becomes more collaborative, choosing the right software platform is essential. Investors must weigh whether Datadog Inc. (DDOG -1.33%) or Figma Inc. (FIG -1.18%) provides the better long-term opportunity.

Datadog focuses on observability and security, helping companies monitor their cloud infrastructure and applications. Figma provides a collaborative platform for teams to design and build digital products together. Both companies have become staples in the software ecosystem, but they navigate different market dynamics and financial stages as they look toward 2027.

Datadog sells an AI-powered observability and security platform to organizations of all sizes. The company is a key player among tech stocks, offering tools that monitor infrastructure, logs, and data. It serves approximately 32,700 customers across more than 160 countries and maintains over 1,000 integrations to support various IT environments.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached about $4.3 billion, representing growth of approximately 33.2%. The company reported net income of roughly $262.0 million during this time, resulting in a net margin of about 6.1%. This profitability marks a steady progression as the business scales its suite of cloud-native services.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is 0.4x. This measures the company's total debt against its shareholder equity. The current ratio is 3.4x, indicating the company can comfortably cover its short-term obligations. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow reached about $1.0 billion. Note that stock-based compensation (SBC) represented roughly 71.5% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Figma provides a collaborative design platform used by global product teams to build digital interfaces. Roughly 85% of its monthly active users are outside the U.S., with India serving as its second-largest market. In its latest annual report, the company noted it moved to an administrator-controlled provisioning model to streamline enterprise adoption and manage complex procurement requirements.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached about $1.4 billion, reflecting growth of roughly 44.4%. However, the company reported a net loss of about $562.5 million for the period, with a net margin of approximately -40.2%. These figures show a company prioritizing rapid expansion and AI investment over immediate profitability.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is 0.1x. The current ratio is 2.6x, demonstrating a healthy ability to meet its near-term financial commitments. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was approximately $246.2 million. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 544.2% of operating cash flow, meaning reported cash generation is heavily inflated by this non-cash add-back.

Datadog faces intense competition from established technology companies like Microsoft Corp. (MSFT -0.69%), open-source solutions, and native tools from cloud providers. It also competes with specialized rivals such as Dynatrace (DT +1.25%) and Cisco Systems (CSCO -0.49%). The company's reputation depends on protecting against cyber-attacks and data breaches. Rapid expansion into international markets adds risks from complex local regulations, while its reliance on Amazon.com Inc. (AMZN +1.61%) for cloud infrastructure presents ongoing operational challenges.

Figma relies heavily on the successful adoption of its AI-powered features, though these investments currently pressure operating margins. The company faces stiff competition from design suites like Adobe Systems Inc. (ADBE -2.23%) and emerging AI-native tools. Its reliance on Amazon for hosting its platform exposes it to risks of service outages. Additionally, recent shifts in billing models, including the transition to AI credit-based pricing, risk customer dissatisfaction and create challenges in maintaining high retention rates.

Figma appears cheaper than Datadog based on its P/S ratio, measuring value against sales over the past twelve months, and its Forward P/E.

Valuation metrics include those sourced from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from those of other data providers.

Datadog is fast-growing, with revenue expected by Wall Street analysts to jump more than 31% to $4.47 billion in fiscal 2026, and net income to nearly double to more than $203 million. Yet there is some customer concentration risk. It is believed OpenAI is Datadog's largest customer, accounting for more than $100 million--perhaps well more--according to various analyst reports. There are also vague concerns that AI could develop tools that can replace Datadog's services, but that is a general concern for just about any software provider these days.

The good news is Datadog usage continues to increase with both new and established customers. Spending by its largest customer group is rising, with the number of customers providing annual recurring revenue of more than $100,000 accelerating, up about 20% in the third quarter compared to the year-ago period. That cohort rose 23% in the second quarter. Demand seems broad-based across both its AI and non-AI customers. Revenue growth from its non-AI customers climbed meaningfully, too, signaling a ramp in its enterprise segment.

Figma, meanwhile, has seen its user experience design solution become the industry standard for digital asset and mobile application development. The company uses this niche dominance to introduce additional modules, helping transform it from a single-point solution into a full design platform.

One differentiator from competitors is that Figma has done an excellent job not only presenting itself to developers, but to marketers and product managers who have to collaborate on designs. That gives the company more champions in an organization for its products.

For the current fiscal year, revenue should grow quickly, at about a 40% rate, to $1.49 billion. Look for a dramatic reduction in its net loss, from nearly $1.3 billion to about $490 million. Wall Street still sees similarly sized losses ahead for the foreseeable future, however.

So which cloud tech stock is the better buy? Datadog registers profit, which can sometimes be an overlooked metric for tech stocks. It comes at a growth stock premium, but rising sales and net income suggest Datadog can keep the momentum going through 2027 and beyond.
