Strategy's Michael Saylor Says Bitcoin Is Now a Bipartisan Issue That Crosses Party Lines. Here's Why He's Right.

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Even with a Democratic majority in Congress, Bitcoin can still soar higher over the next two years.

After the Democrats swept both the House and Senate in the midterm elections, investor sentiment in the crypto market quickly turned bearish. The crypto industry spent a reported $176 million on over 100 midterm election races, with very little to show for it.

But not all is lost. According to Michael Saylor, founder and executive chairman of Strategy (MSTR -2.44%), formerly known as MicroStrategy, Bitcoin (BTC -3.12%) is now a bipartisan issue, saying, "The case for holding Bitcoin crosses party lines."

He's right. Even with a Democratic majority in both the House and Senate, Bitcoin can still go higher.

Bitcoin is primarily thought of as a financial asset. But it is much more than just that. It is also a technological innovation and a commitment to sound economic policies. On both of these issues,  Republicans and Democrats can find common ground, even if they have different political ideologies.

For example, in an era of runaway government spending, mounting deficits, rising inflation, currency debasement, and a $40 trillion national debt, Bitcoin has tremendous appeal as a potential solution to at least some of those problems.

Remember: Bitcoin launched in 2008 in response to the global economic crisis at the time. Satoshi Nakamoto designed Bitcoin to be a new form of "sound money" that could not be inflated away by governments or central banks. Bitcoin has a finite lifetime supply capped at 21 million coins, of which 20.1 million are already in circulation.

The Bitcoin-related political issue I'm watching most carefully in 2027 is the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve. This was established by a White House executive order back in March 2025 but has never lived up to its initial billing as a potential way for the U.S. government to buy Bitcoin in size.

That's what has me concerned. The Democrats could decide to gut the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve entirely, or at least make it close to impossible to ever spend a single taxpayer dollar on Bitcoin. Current plans to codify the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve into law could go nowhere over the next two years.

The good news is that some top lawmakers elected in the midterm elections are now very much behind the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve. As Saylor noted, "Florida elected a Republican governor who backed a U.S. Bitcoin reserve. Texas elected a Democratic senator who voted to create one for his state."

Going forward, I'm expecting a contentious national debate over the future role of Bitcoin. If it is viewed simply as a new kind of speculative digital asset, it may not have much upside potential. But if support for Bitcoin is viewed as support for financial freedom, technological innovation, economic competitiveness, and fiscal responsibility, then it could continue to soar in value, regardless of who's in power.
