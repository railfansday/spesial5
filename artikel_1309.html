Harry Maguire - the coach! Man United star spearheading innovative new AI training app Feedz - with Man United, Chelsea and Sunderland among those signing up for platform

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Published: 03:30 EST, 26 November 2026 | Updated: 05:12 EST, 26 November 2026

When England and Manchester United defender Harry Maguire finally decides to hang up his boots, he may have an alternative to the pundits’ studio.

Maguire, 33, is co-founder of a new AI coaching app, Feedz, that is already transforming the way elite sports teams are instructing their talent with their cutting-edge voice-to-report technology.

The Manchester United centre-half started the company alongside British tech entrepreneur Matt White in May and the app is already being used by sporting outfits, including Maguire's club United, Chelsea's women's team and Sunderland.

Having bounced back from setbacks and criticism throughout his career, Maguire's resilience will stand him in good stead if he decides to transition from the backline to the touchline.

And while it is not a traditional managerial role his decision demonstrates a keen eye on the future of coaching and the development of young athletes.

A lot of new AI developments are mind-boggling on the face of it, but what sets Feedz apart is just how easy it is to use.

Harry Maguire has co-founded the revolutionary new AI coaching app, Feedz

All a coach needs to do is dictate a messy audio message giving instructions to a player and within seconds Feedz will provide them with a PDF outlining exactly where their player is succeeding, where they must improve and how to do it.

Even more impressive, the use of this app is not limited to football and indeed has fast become a tool used all over the globe in 19 different sports.

Feedz has also been able to cross the Atlantic, with the technology being used by a plethora of elite institutions across the pond – most notably Sporting Jacksonville, who, with more than 150 coaches training 10,000 young players, are the largest soccer academy in the States and something of a feeder club for top MLS teams.

Co-founder Matt White tells Daily Mail Sport: 'The purpose of Feedz is to develop youth sports. There is no downside to feedback, transparency between coach, player and parent is the flywheel.

'The issue is time. Coaches don’t have the time to give feedback consistently, the technology now exists to solve that problem of time. Coaches can now give feedback in seconds.

'This is across all sports. Caroline Elite, a US soccer academy in the US, have over 2,000 players... it used to take them two weeks to give feedback to these players and  now it takes two hours.'

White then emphasised the vast number of sports in which Feedz can be used, and outlined the elite outfits already using it.

'Feedz is for all sports,' he says. 'Multiple schools, cricket counties like Surrey, Sussex, Kent, Glamorgan, Middlesex. Table Tennis England, Sale Sharks rugby club, Surbiton hockey club, Sunderland AFC, Manchester United, West Riding Oxfordshire (riding club), Louisiana Fire, Arizona Arsenal, Sporting Iowa (US soccer teams), Soccer Indiana. They all use Feedz.

'Good feedback helps a young player understand what they’re doing well and what to work on. It also helps parents feel involved and gives coaches a way to track progress over time.'

Another encouraging aspect of Feedz is that the company aims to help young sportspeople at all levels of the game.

White explains that when he started the company he went straight to the FA's head of grassroots football to ensure that coaches who simply don't have the time or resources to relay meaningful feedback to their players could do so.

His savvy investment may point to Maguire's plans post-retirement

He went on to explain that at the highest level professional athletes have nutritionists, personal physios and all of the available data handed to them to ensure they can perform at their best but young talent are often not given the same chance to reach their potential.

Maguire told White: 'This is the exact kind of thing I would have loved to have as a young player coming through.'

In a world where the majority of stories on AI are negative and inspire fear, this is a use of the technology that can keep grassroots football alive and even help it thrive.

And, just as he approaches the twilight of his own career, Maguire clearly agrees.
