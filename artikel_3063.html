Where Will XRP Be in 2030?

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Future price targets for XRP need to take into account the growing importance of stablecoins.

For the year, XRP (XRP -5.20%) is down a dismal 37% and currently trades for just north of $1. At one time, this popular altcoin looked set to go stratospheric, and some investors predicted it might go to $10, $100, or even $1,000.

But that simply hasn't been the case. One potential catalyst for XRP in 2026 -- passage of the Clarity Act -- did not materialize. And now, with the Democrats having swept both the House and the Senate in the midterm elections, the brief era of pro-crypto regulation may soon be coming to an end.

That puts XRP's prospects very much in doubt. Here are three potential scenarios for XRP in 2030.

In the most favorable scenario, institutional demand for XRP takes off, and new use cases continue to emerge. To make that a reality, Ripple, the company behind the XRP token, has spent nearly $3 billion to create an end-to-end payment and liquidity network featuring XRP.

Some analysts have suggested that the Ripple payment network could eventually replace the SWIFT payment network. Wouldn't it be cheaper, easier, and faster to run everything on blockchain rails rather than a legacy network dating back to the 1970s?

That type of thinking led to some truly eye-popping price targets for XRP. Even Ripple got into the act last year, suggesting that as much as 14% of SWIFT's annual payment volume (roughly $150 trillion) would eventually run through the XRP blockchain ledger.

If that happens, then demand for the XRP token would be off the charts. According to Standard Chartered, the price of the XRP token could hit $28 by the end of 2030.

However, there are a number of flawed assumptions in the preceding scenario. For one thing, SWIFT is implementing blockchain technology of its own. That dramatically reduces Ripple's potential growth opportunities.

Secondly, stablecoins continue to eat XRP's lunch. Stablecoins have become a $300 billion industry and a popular means of moving money around the world. Even Ripple has seen the writing on the wall. In December 2024, it launched a stablecoin of its own, Ripple USD. In less than two years, Ripple USD has attained a market cap of $2.7 billion.

Given those two facts, the long-run price of XRP might turn out to be $1, which is almost exactly where it's trading right now. In short, XRP will become nothing more than a glorified stablecoin.

In early November, Ripple CEO Brad Garlinghouse delivered some fantastic news for XRP investors. He proclaimed that XRP and stablecoins are "complementary" and not "competitive."

With that in mind, I'm predicting that XRP will be able to grow at a 20% compound annual growth rate (CAGR), about on par with what you'd expect from an established tech stock. Provided that Ripple continues to support it, XRP could hit a price of $2.50 by the end of 2030.
