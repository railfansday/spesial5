Jensen Huang faces Senate hearing as both parties attack his return

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Chuck Schumer promised investigations and John Cornyn asked what the board based its security call on. Brian Mast says Huang still owes answers under oath.

Senate Banking Committee chair Tim Scott has called Jensen Huang to a public hearing at 10am on Thursday, 3 December, according to a notice. The hearing will come less than two weeks after Nvidia’s board reinstated him as chief executive, Politico reported.

Huang had agreed to testify before his return. A spokesperson for Huang declined to comment on whether his plans have changed, Politico’s Jasper Goodman reported.

Lawmakers from both parties criticised the board’s decision on Monday. Several said it made it more urgent to pass tighter controls on chip exports to China.

Nvidia’s board said in its announcement that it relied on input from senior levels of the US government. On that basis, it said Huang’s return poses no national security risk. Senator John Cornyn of Texas asked what that was based on.

Brian Mast, who chairs the House Foreign Affairs Committee, called the reports about Huang “extremely concerning” on X. In a video posted minutes after the announcement, he said Huang still owes Congress answers under oath. A committee spokesperson told Politico the committee still expects Huang’s closed-door interview with three House committees on 1 December to go ahead.

“The American people deserve answers on Mr. Huang’s relationship with this potential Chinese intelligence asset,” Mast said.

As Chairman of the @HouseForeignGOP, export controls on our most advanced AI chips has been a top priority. The reports of Jensen Huang’s alleged affair with a Chinese intelligence asset and the smuggling of our most advanced technology to China is extremely concerning.

— Rep. Brian Mast (@RepBrianMast) November 23, 2026

Representative Don Bacon of Nebraska said Nvidia should have moved on without Huang, Reuters reported.

“Huang was compromised and exploited by a Chinese spy,” Bacon said.

Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer said in a statement that the administration’s role in Huang’s return showed Congress must act.

“Democrats will wield their full power to investigate any wrongdoing by the Trump Administration, Jensen Huang, other NVIDIA executives, and anyone selling out our national security for financial gain,” Schumer said.

Representative Raja Krishnamoorthi called the decision “outrageous” on X. He said Trump’s assurances could not be taken at face value, citing a $25m donation from Nvidia to Trump’s ballroom. Huang must testify at a full public hearing, he wrote.

Nvidia’s decision to reinstate Jensen Huang is outrageous. We still don’t know what information Chinese intelligence may have obtained from him, and neither the government nor Nvidia’s own board has completed its investigation.

Trump’s assurances cannot be taken at face value, especially after Nvidia’s $25 million donation to his ballroom.

Representative Debbie Dingell called the decision irresponsible, as Congress has not yet heard from Huang. Representative Richard Neal said the board’s reference to “input from senior levels” was one of many reasons Congress needs answers, Reuters reported. Senator Elizabeth Warren had already called the move “reckless”.

Senator Ruben Gallego went further. He wrote on X that Trump was selling out national security to keep the stock market high.

The President is selling out our national security to keep the stock market high and probably for some fucking donation to his ballroom.

— Ruben Gallego (@RubenGallego) November 23, 2026

Huang’s return is likely to affect his legal representation before Congress, Semafor reported. The administration might also push back on the oversight plans, it said.

Leaders in both parties are leaning towards using the coming defence bill to close gaps in chip export policy, Semafor reported. Mast is among those pushing to attach export control bills to the National Defense Authorization Act, according to Politico.

Trump told CNBC on Monday that he did not think the board would have brought Huang back without him. The White House did not respond to Semafor’s request for comment.
