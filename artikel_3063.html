iFixit tears down Apple HomeView, finds lots of glue and endless frustration

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

iFixit’s teardown of the new Apple HomeView is now up, revealing a device that seems nearly impossible to take apart and repair. Watch it below.

Following last week’s launch of the Apple HomeView, iFixit today published its teardown of the device.

Contrary to recent teardowns, which found significant repairability improvements with greater component modularity and easier access to internal components, the HomeView put up a fight nearly all the way through.

With heavily glued or adhesive-bonded components, including the display, speakers, antennas, and logic board, the teardown proved particularly challenging, even after the adhesive securing the display’s speakers was soaked in isopropyl alcohol.

At one point, one of them broke apart during removal, while the logic board required considerable force to separate from the chassis due to the thermal adhesive holding it in place.

iFixit’s teardown covered the two HomeView variants: the tabletop model and the wall-mounted version. Interestingly, there are some internal differences between them, as the wall-mounted model has a slightly wider logic board to accommodate capacitors that are housed in the base of the tabletop version.

As a result, the wall-mounted HomeView also has smaller internal speakers, which helps explain the differences in their audio systems that Apple had already acknowledged at launch.

Back to the tabletop model, iFixit’s teardown also showed that separating the display from the base requires either specialized tools or extensive, potentially destructive disassembly of the display assembly, thanks to the way Apple routed the connecting cables through the pivot mechanism.

Once iFixit was in the base, however, there was some good news: the USB-C port is modular and relatively easy to access, as are the passive radiators and the board powering the lower assembly.

Still, an extremely short cable connecting the woofer to the board underneath made disassembly difficult, with iFixit questioning how anyone would be able to reconnect it during reassembly.

Given all these repairability challenges, Shahram Mokhtari joked that while iFixit doesn’t have an official repairability scorecard for this category of device, he would give the Apple HomeView a “BEEP this thing” out of 10.
