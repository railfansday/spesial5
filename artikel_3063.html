Alphabet vs. Apple: Which Warren Buffett Stock Is the Better Buy?

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Both are major positions in the Berkshire Hathaway portfolio.

By Adria Cimino – Nov 18, 2026 at 6:25PM EST

Warren Buffett is known for selecting quality stocks and holding on to them for many years. And he's proven that this strategy works: For 60 years as chief executive officer at Berkshire Hathaway, Buffett helped deliver market-beating returns. Buffett retired at the beginning of this year, but he remains involved at Berkshire Hathaway and now holds the title of chairman emeritus.

Greg Abel, who took over from Buffett as CEO, has maintained the investing giant's principles and continues to hold many Buffett favorites. Two in particular remain among the company's top holdings: Apple (AAPL +0.15%) and Alphabet (GOOG +0.58%) (GOOGL +0.80%), in the No. 1 and No. 4 positions, respectively.

Which of these Warren Buffett stocks is the better buy today? Let's find out.

Warren Buffett. Image source: Getty Images.

This year marks a 10-year anniversary for Apple and Berkshire Hathaway: Buffett initially bought Apple shares in 2016. And over the years, it's become a cherished position, with Buffett on multiple occasions praising Tim Cook, who held the CEO role until September of this year.

Cook, who recently retired, helped the company's market value soar 1,000% to $4 trillion and revenue almost quadruple to $416 billion in the 2025 fiscal year. Now, with John Ternus at the helm, there's reason to be optimistic about more growth ahead. Ternus, formerly the company's senior vice president of hardware engineering, has the product experience and knowledge to lead Apple through new waves of innovation. Products are Apple's strength, with the iPhone the global smartphone leader, so a product focus is key for future growth.

Apple stock has climbed more than that of fellow tech giants this year, as it isn't as heavily linked to artificial intelligence (AI) -- and therefore is less sensitive to AI-related risks. Though Apple stock isn't cheap, it still could have room to run.

Buffett opened a position in Alphabet last year, and Abel added to it this year, showing clear commitment to this Buffett selection. Alphabet climbed earlier this year but has been under pressure in recent times, along with other tech giants that are investing heavily in AI. Some investors worry that these players are overspending and that the revenue opportunity will be disappointing.

Still, Alphabet's Google Cloud business hasn't faltered; in fact, that unit's revenue has been soaring amid strong demand for its AI products and services. In the recent quarter, Google Cloud revenue jumped 103% to $30 billion. The company has also begun selling its own chips, known as TPUs, to certain customers for use in their data centers. Though this is a small source of revenue right now, it could grow in time.

Meanwhile, Google's search business continues to hold 90% of the search market worldwide, and this has been its ticket to enormous revenue year after year. This is through advertising, with advertisers eager to place ads across the platform to reach potential customers. In the recent quarter, Google ads brought in $82 billion in revenue for the company.

Alphabet stock today looks cheap, particularly compared with that of Apple.

There's a clear reason for this, however, and this goes back to the point I made above: Some investors are turning away from AI big spenders. But this opens up an opportunity to buy Alphabet at a fantastic price.

This depends on your investment style and priorities. From a valuation perspective, Alphabet clearly stands out. Today, this tech giant is a great deal for growth investors looking for a bargain. Though current market sentiment may continue to weigh on Alphabet stock, this doesn't change the company's long-term story.

Alphabet's search business remains solid, and the company's cloud business is well-established and growing -- and doesn't rely uniquely on AI. So investors may benefit if they buy now and hold on as the storm passes.

However, Apple, less linked to AI spending and a long-term Buffett favorite, may be a better buy for the cautious investor. Though the stock has climbed, the release of new products just recently could boost revenue and lead to more gains ahead.
