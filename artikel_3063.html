Uber Adds Zoox’s Robotaxis to Its App. Everything to Know

You could be paired with the boxy self-driving vehicles when hailing a ride in Las Vegas.

Starting this week, customers booking an Uber ride in Las Vegas could be matched with a Zoox autonomous vehicle.

Zoox’s boxy, purpose-built vehicles have been operating for the general public in Sin City for a little over a year via the company’s own app. But this is the first time Zoox, which is owned by Amazon, is teaming up with a third-party platform like Uber. (You can still hail a ride in Vegas through the Zoox app; the company began charging passengers in August.)

Now, riders who request an UberX, Uber Comfort, Uber Electric, Uber Comfort Electric or Wait & Save could be paired with a Zoox robotaxi, and they won’t have to pay extra. You can boost your chances of being matched with the AV by going to Ride Preferences in the Uber app’s settings. Then tap Autonomous vehicles, and hit the toggle next to Get more autonomous rides.

After you’ve been matched with a Zoox vehicle, you can choose to accept the autonomous ride or opt for a non-AV. Passengers can access 24/7 human support from both the Uber app and inside the Zoox vehicle.

Unlike other autonomous vehicles from companies like Waymo and Wayve, which retrofit cars from third-party manufacturers, Zoox’s purpose-built robotaxis don’t have a steering wheel, driver’s seat or pedals. Instead, there are two rows of seats facing each other, so you can chat more easily with your fellow passengers. The vehicles are bidirectional, making it a little easier to get out of a parking space, for instance. I’ve taken a few Zoox rides in Las Vegas and San Francisco, and despite the robotaxi’s unique design, the experience felt remarkably ordinary, with the vehicle seamlessly navigating through traffic.

Uber and Zoox announced their multiyear partnership in March. The companies also plan to hit the road together in Los Angeles in 2027, after Zoox launches there via its own app.

Teaming up with Uber could expose Zoox to more customers, since many people already have the ride-hailing app downloaded on their phones. A tourist hub like Las Vegas has become a hotspot for AV companies to test and launch their services, with many people willing to try something new as they get around the city.

“Zoox has built something truly unique, and by bringing its purpose-built robotaxis to Uber, we’re introducing an entirely new riding experience to more people in Las Vegas, with Los Angeles coming next,” Samarth Kejriwal, global head of autonomous mobility at Uber, said in a statement.

This comes after the dissolution of Uber’s exclusive partnership with Alphabet-owned Waymo in Austin and Atlanta, which Bloomberg reports will end starting early next year. Instead, Waymo reportedly plans to offer rides directly within its own app, as it does in several other locations. Meanwhile, Uber has partnered with dozens of other AV companies, including Aurora, Wayve, Avride, Nuro and May Mobility.

While Waymo is the leader in the self-driving space, Zoox has been ramping up its operations in recent months. Along with Las Vegas, riders can join a waitlist to ride in San Francisco, with plans to roll out its robotaxi service in Miami and Austin. Zoox is also testing its self-driving technology in Seattle, Atlanta, Los Angeles, Dallas, Phoenix, Houston, San Diego and Washington, DC.
