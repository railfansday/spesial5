Kraft Heinz vs. On: Which Consumer Goods Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Kraft Heinz trades at a lower forward multiple, while On's revenue is growing at a far faster clip, so the right pick depends on how much risk you're willing to carry.

By Sarah Sidlow – Updated Nov 24, 2026 at 5:23PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Kraft Heinz ›

Investors often weigh the safety of household staples against the excitement of growth brands. Let's see if Kraft Heinz (KHC +1.87%) or On (ONON +1.58%) is the better buy for your portfolio.

Kraft Heinz operates as a global food giant with iconic household brands, while On is a Swiss athletic brand disrupting the footwear market. While they serve different consumer needs, both companies are vying for space in diversified portfolios, making them an interesting pair to compare for long-term growth and stability.

The company focuses on its North America Retail and International Developed Markets segments, moving brands like Heinz and Philadelphia through a vast distribution network. Walmart (WMT +2.11%) is the largest customer, representing nearly 21% of net sales, and customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business. Kraft Heinz also provides products for many foodservice distributors, reaching consumers in restaurants and on the go.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 26, 2026, revenue reached nearly $24.8 billion, representing a decrease of approximately 1.5% compared to the prior year. The company reported a net loss of close to $3.5 billion during this same period. This decline in revenue suggests a challenging environment for top-line growth as consumer spending patterns shift.

The debt-to-equity ratio was roughly 0.5x as of its December 2025 balance sheet. This ratio measures total debt against shareholder equity, where a lower number indicates less reliance on borrowed money. The current ratio, which measures a company's ability to pay short-term obligations with short-term assets, was approximately 1.2x. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 27, 2025, free cash flow was close to $3.7 billion.

On focuses on a premium performance strategy, selling high-end running shoes and apparel through a mix of wholesale partners and its own retail stores. The brand has expanded rapidly across the Americas and Asia-Pacific, relying on a premium image to attract athletes and lifestyle consumers alike. By controlling its direct-to-consumer channels, the company maintains higher oversight of its brand presentation and customer relationships.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached approximately $4.0 billion, a significant increase of roughly 17.4% year over year. Net income for this period was close to $527.4 million. This strong growth in both revenue and net income highlights the brand's momentum in the global footwear and apparel market.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was nearly 0.4x, indicating that the company has a conservative amount of debt relative to its equity. The current ratio was approximately 2.7x, suggesting a robust ability to cover its immediate financial liabilities. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was close to $301.8 million.

Kraft Heinz faces significant risks from its dependence on large retail customers like Walmart, which can exert pricing pressure. The company also faces competition from other packaged food giants like J. M. Smucker (SJM -1.45%) as private-label brands gain market share. Additionally, failure to adapt to shifting consumer health preferences or volatility in commodity prices for meat and dairy can negatively impact profitability.

On must navigate intense competition in the premium footwear market from large established players. As a brand that relies on a premium reputation, any failure to maintain its brand image or keep pace with technological innovations could harm its growth. Additionally, expansion into new international markets carries execution risks, including varying consumer tastes and complex supply chain logistics.

Kraft Heinz appears to be more conservatively valued based on its Forward P/E, while On carries a higher P/S ratio relative to sales over the past 12 months.

Kraft Heinz has been a stalwart on the stock market and store shelves for decades, and its ubiquity in both areas is part of its appeal. Value investors will likely be drawn to its lower valuation multiples, while income investors may praise its 6.26% dividend yield. But it faces some real challenges: Sales have remained sluggish, it continues to record significant brand impairments, and its stock price has dipped more than 25% over the last three years. Even with its generous dividend reinvested, investors would have lost more than 15% on KHC stock since Nov. 24, 2023.

But Kraft Heinz also offers something On can't: Its products are inexpensive consumer staples, and in uncertain economic conditions, packaged foods products have historically held up well. It's arguably easier to forgo expensive running shoes than the staples for at-home meals.

New kid on the block On continues to grow revenue and net income. Its premium brand and direct-to-consumer business model has allowed it to maintain pricing power. Betting on On means wagering that its clientele is affluent enough to keep products moving regardless of market conditions. That's the bet I'd make in this matchup. And with the stock more than 50% off its all-time high, it may offer an attractive entry point for growth-oriented investors willing to hold the premium apparel company for several years.
