Kane bends game to his will on record-breaking night

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

One day it will have to end for Harry Kane, but for now, almost 12 years into his England life, there is still no matching the wisdom and influence he has accumulated over all those games and all those goals for his country.

This would have been a great night by any measure for a man collecting his record-breaking 126th England cap. There was a handshake from Peter Shilton, now deposed as king of caps, and a flourish by Kane of his recently acquired Ballon d’Or, once his four children had accompanied him on to the pitch. These are the nights of which dreams are made, and yet – given the defining feature of Kane’s career – there was one more thing to accomplish.

That was a goal from the man who has ploughed relentlessly past the two great England individual landmarks of caps and goals. His second-half penalty won the game and, although he is not infallible from the spot, he is as close to that status as England have ever had. Age has not yet run him down, he can still bend the game to his will, and when it comes to the pressure moments, his longevity seems to offer the upper hand.

This was always going to be Kane’s night, with a great banner on the west side of the stadium paying tribute to the England captain. The Prince of Wales was in attendance for the occasion, too. Luka Modric presented his former Tottenham Hotspur team-mate with a keepsake from the Croatian football federation. Kane had to wait for the second half for the most valuable offering of all.

After the hour, the Danish referee Morten Krogh was advised by his VAR Bastian Dankert to take a second look at a corner tangle involving Jarell Quansah, and decided to award the second-half penalty from which Kane scored. It was certainly clumsy, but these grapples have been overlooked by referees in the past. The 27th successfully converted penalty of the Kane England era followed, and the man himself played the full game.

Afterwards, Kane made a point of thanking the Football Association for organising it all, although it was also clear that this was no less than he expected. “It’s important to recognise the great players,” he said, “and it’s not something we have always done over the years.”

If the sentiment was a little awkwardly articulated, he is certainly right on the key point: so many England careers have ended abruptly at tournament elimination or elsewhere. Yet here was one being celebrated with the man himself, in what might even be argued to be his prime. Kane, 33, was asked again as to how long he might continue and did not rule out going on to 40, “if I’m performing at the highest level”.

That was a nod to Modric, a second-half substitute at the age of 41 and now on 207 caps for Croatia and almost 1,200 senior games. This Croatia team still seem unable to part company with their greatest player, despite his struggles against England in the seven-goal defeat in Rijeka. On this occasion, Croatia were much better.

It should have been more straightforward for England. They had taken the lead through Morgan Rogers midway through the first half and had dominated the first hour. But Quansah’s mistake gave Croatia an equaliser, and after Kane’s penalty England were fortunate to survive on occasions.

“It was a tight match – a tough one,” Thomas Tuchel, the England manager, said. “We didn’t take our chances when we had chances, so it stayed a tight match. It cannot be glamorous and super-exciting [all the time]. It has to be serious and determined. We wanted a final on Sunday in Spain and we have that.”

The win means that England’s game in Madrid on Sunday against Spain, who beat the Czech Republic and lead the Nations League group by three points, has some bite to it. Another England win would mean they would overtake Spain on goal difference and win the group ahead of the Nations League quarter-finals in March.

This was one game when the players might have wondered if they were due more from a Wembley crowd that went through the motions in the first half and scarcely returned for the second. For the first hour, England dominated a Croatia side who often invited six blue shirts into their back line. Tuchel said his decision to select Dan Burn in the left-sided centre-half position over the left-footed Jarrad Branthwaite was almost split – a “51 per cent decision”.

England would break through just once, when Rogers got enough of his laces on an awkward Jude Bellingham cross deflected behind him and the shot brushed Josko Gvardiol on the way in.

Trent Alexander-Arnold started his third England game of the autumn and the right-back spent most of it in midfield. Not just central midfield, or right midfield, but he also emerged in the inside-left position at times. Kane stepped into the No 10 role when he saw fit and his passing was reliably excellent. From the left it was his pass to Bellingham on the right wing that prefaced the cross for the Rogers goal.

The catastrophe of the defeat last month for Croatia meant they were naturally conservative. Nevertheless, in Martin Baturina, the 23-year-old Como attacking midfielder, they have another fine talent. When Modric did come on, he was applauded on all sides of Wembley. The home fans would later boo the arrival of Ben White, still not forgiven for his 2022 mid-tournament World Cup departure.

The equaliser was a Quansah error when the defender tried to head a loose corner back to Jordan Pickford and did not make the distance. Petar Sucic intervened and pushed it home with a right foot. Three minutes later it was judged a foul on Quansah by Ivan Smolcic when he pulled the Englishman down and the referee Krogh eventually gave the penalty.

Kane scored that and the substitute James Garner had a goal disallowed in the closing stages of the game, which were much less comfortable for England.

England manager Thomas Tuchel, speaking to ITV: “Good win. We wanted this win desperately and I think we deserved it. I think it was hard work against a good opponent. It took us a while to grow into the game but after 15 minutes I think we did OK, got the goal and got better and more confident and had some good actions and good chances, good half-chances.

“Got a little bit sloppy at the very end of the first half, started the second half very good, didn’t score the second goal, conceded from a set piece, and then showed a good reaction.

“We got the job done and it was important because it was a special occasion for Harry [Kane] and he scored the winner, so perfect evening.”

On whether England were sloppy when they conceded: “It was maybe only three or four minutes before we gave passes away and especially in a moment when we were clearly the better team, we came with a very strong reaction after the half-time, had a beautiful chance created from Declan Rice and they blocked it on the line. “An awkward moment in our box can always happen. It’s important to react and we did it.”

On his defenders: “Good. It was demanding for Dan Burn and also for Jarell [Quansah] because they [Croatia] play with a back five and have two number 10s in between the lines.

“It was a lot of work to do, a lot of communication but they did very well.”

On what will be better against Spain on Sunday: “I don’t know. Everything will be different. Completely different scenario, completely different setup, but I’m very positive that we will get what we want.”
