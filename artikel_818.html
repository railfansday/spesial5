Former world No 1 Jannik Sinner is set to undergo surgery on his troublesome knee which is likely to see him miss the Australian Open in January, according to Sky in Italy.

The surgery, performed by Lasse Lempainen, one of the world's leading specialists in tendon and muscle injuries, could sideline the Italian for three to four months.

Sinner last played at Wimbledon, where he defeated Alexander Zverev in the final to claim his fifth career Grand Slam title.

The 25-year-old stated earlier this month that he expects to make his official return at the Australian Open following a planned appearance at the Six Kings Slam in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, in late December.

"The goal is to go to ​Australia; I hope to get there, and perhaps play a few tournaments beforehand," Sinner told media in Milan.

Despite playing only nine tournaments, Sinner compiled an astonishing 44-3 match record (93.6 per cent win rate), where he captured six titles and reclaimed the world No 1 spot during a 30-match winning streak.

He sealed maiden titles in Indian Wells, Monte-Carlo, and Madrid to become only the second man in history [after Novak Djokovic] to win all nine ATP Masters 1000 tournaments.

However, Sinner did not feature in another competitive match after beating Alexander Zverev in four sets to secure his second consecutive Wimbledon title in July, with persistent inflammation in his right knee forcing him to miss the North American hard-court swing, including the US Open.

Due to an extended absence from the tour, Sinner is likely to apply for a protected ranking which would mean he would be able to play in tournaments without having to go through qualifying.
