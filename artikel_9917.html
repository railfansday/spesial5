Europe's AI sovereignty push: Lots of cloud, not much silver lining

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Datacenter spending is booming but the models, the chips, and the clout all live in US and Asia

Europe's IT spending is growing more slowly than the global average, according to Gartner, whose analysts warn that the region's push for tech sovereignty faces commercial and technological obstacles.

Gartner forecasts European IT spending of $1.57 trillion in 2027, up 9.9 percent from 2026, compared with worldwide growth of 15 percent to $7.35 trillion. For 2026, it estimates spending growth of 20.7 percent in North America and 13.7 percent in China.

"The world seems to be racing towards this AI goal of where the large language model provider is going to be, and the race seems to be between North American-based providers and some Asia Pacific providers," John-David Lovelock, Distinguished VP Analyst at Gartner, told The Register.

He said Europe had traditionally trailed in IT uptake. "We saw that happen when global sourcing, cloud, and software as a service all came along. It's down to the pushback against the idea of creative destruction," where new technologies replace old ones.

Europe's fastest growing categories are datacenter systems, forecast to grow 29.7 percent in 2027, and infrastructure as a service, at 26.7 percent.

Even so, Lovelock does not expect Europe to compete with North America and China on AI investment.

"For AI provision, Europe is going to be behind, and there won't be a way to catch up," he said. "The AI datacenters, the large language models, the foundational models, the tools and techniques for building out AI are all being centered outside of Europe, but enterprises don't need to do that. So the question becomes: regardless of where the source of the AI is coming from, can the enterprises actually adopt and use it? In one sense, Europe will be behind in the provision of AI, but it need not be behind in the use."

The gap nonetheless complicates Europe's push to reduce its dependence on US technology suppliers. The European Union has introduced new rules to encourage tech buyers to favor sovereign tech over the dominant tech vendors, but Lovelock doubts they will move spending much.

"Sovereignty is a wonderful flag that we fly on the front of a building. IT is running in the basement. CIOs are not going to buy a second-best solution that costs more simply because it's sovereign.

"They are not going to break any laws, but they are not going to make a decision that puts their company in a worse position at a higher cost. While sovereignty gets [vendors] on the list, it doesn't assure that they get purchased," he told us.

Earlier this month, Gartner predicted that by 2029, more than 90 percent of EU organizations will have a digital sovereignty strategy, but argued that a fully sovereign technology stack remains out of reach.

"The global technology supply chain is too interconnected, and many critical dependencies cannot be replaced at a commercially viable scale," said Bettina Tratz-Ryan, VP analyst at Gartner. ®
