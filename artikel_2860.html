Vanderbilt basketball beats UCF despite nation's leading scorer's 30 points

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

UCF's Dior Johnson, the nation's leading scorer, surpassed his scoring average against Vanderbilt basketball on Nov. 20, but Commodores coach Mark Byington wasn't concerned because his defense made Johnson work hard for every point.

Ultimately, Johnson's 30 points were not enough as No. 16 Vanderbilt held off the Knights at the end of each half for an 82-74 win at Memorial Gym. Johnson came into the game averaging 28.8 points.

The Commodores, who have played all their games at home so far this season, improved to 6-0 while UCF dropped to 4-2.

Vanderbilt was up by 20 points twice in the first half before Johnson, who missed his first five shots, warmed up and scored seven straight points to trim the Commodores' lead to 38-26 late.

After Vanderbilt went up 71-59 in the second half, Johnson scored six straight points and cut the Commodores' lead to 73-66. But he missed a deep 3-point attempt from the corner, Vandy's Tyler Tanner made a couple of free throws and the Commodores coasted in the final minutes.

Johnson, who came into the game shooting 61% from the field (47-for-77), only shot 34.8% after making 8-of-23 field goals, with the Commodores' Chandler Bing sticking with him primarily on defense with some help from T.O. Barrett.

"I thought Chandler Bing did a good job on Johnson and some other guys," Byington said. "He was scoring a lot of points coming into the game, but he was also really efficient. To make him work and shoot 23 shots to get 30 points, we'll take that. I didn't want him to get 30 points on 16 shots or 15 shots, which a lot of games that's what he was doing."

Bing finished with a career-high 19 points on 5-for-9 shooting, but was more proud of the job he did on Johnson, who never got into a scoring groove as he has in most games this season.

Bing said the key was to let Johnson know early his shots would not be easy.

"Definitely just be aggressive from the jump," Bing said. "Make his shots contested. It's going to be a long game, so kind of wear him down. Get him to our help side and force him into tough shots. That was the biggest thing."

Many of Bing's points, meanwhile, came at key times. After UCF cut the deficit in the latter part of the first half, Bing scored a bucket to put Vanderbilt back up 38-24. Bing made only one 3-pointer, but it came with 9:06 remaining and stretched Vanderbilt's lead to 64-48. His free throw with 4:32 left gave Vandy a 10-point lead at 73-63.

Vanderbilt's offense has had better games. The Commodores were only 2-for-21 on 3-pointers and 40% on field goals (23-for-58).

The exception was Tanner. The junior from Brentwood Academy scored 23 points, giving him 1,000 for his career. He also grabbed a career-high 11 rebounds.

Byington credited Tanner for making winning plays down the stretch and added that he has to find a way to get some of his other players to do the same.

Tanner was especially pleased to have the statistical game that he had, considering UCF's zone defense, which bothered the Commodores at times.

"It was a little awkward at first," Tanner said. "We knew they would play a zone. It's kind of a weird zone like you've never really seen. It's kind of like a 4-1; they're kind of all above the free-throw line. At first we were kind of hesitant with it, but Coach said we just need to be aggressive, stick to what we do and that defense would break down."
