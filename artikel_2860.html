What's next for Arch Manning's future as Texas QB partakes in Senior Day?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

"Closing Time" by Semisonic may soon be playing in the background for Arch Manning, as the possibility of his college football career coming to an end is looming, though that outcome is far from certain.

One milestone, however, is guaranteed for the Texas quarterback in Week 12: Senior Day.

Manning is set to take part in the senior day festivities ahead of Texas' Week 12 matchup against Arkansas at DKR-Texas Memorial Stadium on Saturday, joining the rest of the Longhorns' senior class as they are recognized for their contributions to the program during their time on the Forty Acres.

Manning has endured another up-and-down season for the Longhorns with the highs including a come-from-behind win against then-No. 1 Ohio State and wins against Tennessee and Ole Miss, while lows include turning the ball over multiple times in the fourth quarter in back-to-back losses to Oklahoma and Florida.

Despite all that, Manning still holds a strong 18-5 record while serving as Texas' full-time starter, which includes a 9-2 mark against top-ranked opponents.

That all said, there is a possibility of Manning prolonging his career. Once viewed as a near lock for the No. 1 overall pick in the NFL draft, Manning's draft stock is no longer as clear-cut as it once appeared, and he has another year of eligibility remaining, so a return to college football is far from an unrealistic scenario.

Here's what to know about his future plans:

Yes, Manning will partake in Texas' senior day festivities on Saturday ahead of its final home game of the season against Arkansas.

"I'm a senior, so I'm doing it," Manning said earlier this week.

Manning's decision to return to the Longhorns for the 2026 season followed in line with what each of his uncles, Eli and Peyton Manning, and his grandfather, Archie Manning, did by exhausting their eligibility before moving on to play in the NFL. Peyton and Eli Manning were both the No. 1 picks of their respective drafts in 1998 and 2004, while Archie Manning was the No. 2 overall pick in the 1971 NFL Draft.

While Manning is a senior and will be part of senior day on Saturday, it doesn't necessarily mean he'll be moving on from college or the Longhorns after this season. How come? Well, Manning has an extra year of eligibility — as he redshirted his true freshman season after appearing in just three games — so he could return to Texas for another season or transfer somewhere else. Or he could declare for the NFL draft.

Yes, Manning is eligible for the 2027 NFL Draft, which is the second straight draft he's eligible to declare.

It remains unclear whether Manning will declare after the Longhorns' season for the NFL draft or return for a fifth season. The Longhorns have KJ Lacey Jr. and Dia Bell already on their roster as Manning's backups, and have three-star quarterback Ty Knuster verbally committed to their 2027 recruiting class.

According to NFL Operations, to be eligible for the draft, a player must have been removed from high school for three years to be eligible.

"To be eligible for the draft, players must have been out of high school for at least three years and must have used up their college eligibility before the start of the next college football season. Underclassmen and players who have graduated before using all their college eligibility may request the league's approval to enter the draft early.

"Players are draft-eligible only in the year after the end of their college eligibility."

Manning is listed as the No. 22-ranked quarterback by ESPN's Mel Kiper Jr. in the 2026 NFL Draft class.

Polling NFL mock drafts from several major outlets — such as USA TODAY, CBS Sports and Sports Illustrated The 6-foot-4 quarterback is not in any first-round projections as of right now.

Back in late October, in the midst of a stretch of back-to-back losses to Oklahoma and Florida, an NFL scout told USA TODAY Sports' Matt Hayes that Manning's draft stock hasn't exactly been elevated by his then-19 games as a full-time starter at Texas despite having some notable top-ranked wins on his resume.

"Someone might take him in the third or fourth round right now. But a 1? No chance," the NFL scout told USA TODAY Sports. "There's any number of issues. Decision-making and arm strength at the top of the list."
