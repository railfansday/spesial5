For Cowboys dreadful defense, progress against lowly Titans is welcome | Opinion

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

For one day, at least, the Dallas Cowboys can feel somewhat comfortable with the team's defense.

That's seldom been the case this season, with the unit's breakdowns showing no sign of abating. After a 46-40 loss to the San Francisco 49ers last week, the team took the drastic step of stripping defensive coordinator Christian Parker of his play-calling responsibilities, with midseason hire Mike Pettine taking them over.

The first performance after the switch was mostly encouraging if not revolutionary. In the 51-30 rout of the Titans, the Cowboys managed to engineer enough key stops to keep their distance.

For once, however, Dallas' defense wasn't in the spotlight.

The driving force behind Sunday's blowout was the Cowboys' offense, which never punted, scored touchdowns on its first six possessions and reached its season high for scoring by the third quarter. Or maybe it was the Titans' defense, which allowed 40 or more points for the third consecutive week.

Either way, Pettine had considerable comfort room to sort through the issues that plagued the team. But even with another offensively limited opponent waiting in the Thanksgiving matchup against the Philadelphia Eagles, that advantageous dynamic might be short-lived.

Here are three key takeaways on the Cowboys' defense:

Throughout Sunday, the Cowboys' defense was at its best when it was able to unleash its pass rush.

Dallas sacked Cam Ward five times, with Quinnen Williams and James Houston each taking down the quarterback twice. Williams seized the NFL lead in tackles for loss, adding three for a total of 18 on the season. His fourth-quarter strip sack was mostly ornamental, with safety Markquese Bell's 70-yard runback helping set up the Cowboys' seventh and final touchdown of the day. Still, it was the kind of big swing the unit hasn't been able to count on this season and likely will need more of to stay afloat down the stretch.

Getting Tennessee in obvious passing downs helped Dallas finish with a 3-of-10 mark on third downs. That was a substantial step for a group that had more difficulty than anyone in getting opponents off the field with a league-worst third-down conversion allowed rate of 48.84%.

Of course, the Cowboys managed five sacks in the loss to the 49ers. Disruption alone can't sustain any group, much less one with this many leaks.

Other than Chimere Dike hauling in a tipped pass for a fluke 22-yard gain on the first drive, a defense that had been the NFL's most susceptible to explosive plays generally kept things under wraps in the second half.

Then, two regrettable miscues hit on the first two drives of the third quarter, marring what could have been Dallas' first game holding an opponent under 20 points this season.

Rookie safety Caleb Downs couldn't keep up with former Ohio State teammate Carnell Tate, who raced down the left sideline and drew a 38-yard pass interference penalty on an overthrown ball to the end zone. That set up Ward with an easy 1-yard touchdown dive.

The clear lapse of the day, however, came when Tate hauled in a pass while on the ground on the following drive. Cornerback Cobie Durant thought he could touch down the receiver, but the No. 4 overall pick managed to get his body off the turf before contact, allowing him to race for a 59-yard touchdown.

Those are the kind of meltdowns that Parker was brought in to cut down on after they repeatedly doomed Matt Eberflus' group last year. Pettine's half-season stint might be defined by whether he can do more to curb the errors on the back end.

A blowout should have rendered the Titans' run game moot. Tennessee, however, remained committed to a balanced approach.

The good news for Dallas: It prevented the kind of breakaway runs that have dogged the defense throughout the year, with Tony Pollard's longest gain of the day going for 14 yards.

The bad news: Pollard still found a fair amount of room to run in taking 21 carries for 110 yards. The Cowboys continued to be particularly soft on the perimeter, where Pollard was able to pick up yards in chunks.

A more complete picture of the team's run-stopping efforts might emerge Thursday, when containing Saquon Barkley is sure to be central to the defensive game plan.
