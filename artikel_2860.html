Another trade! Jaguars land their defensive game-changer in Jordan Davis

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

If there was any lingering doubt about the Jacksonville Jaguars' ambitions this season, James Gladstone has just put it to bed.

The Jaguars have agreed a blockbuster trade with the Philadelphia Eagles for defensive tackle Jordan Davis, sending a 2027 first-round pick and a 2029 second-round pick to Philadelphia in exchange for the former first-round selection.

It's the third defensive addition Jacksonville has made in a remarkable day of activity, following the acquisitions of Davon Godchaux from New Orleans and Jessie Bates III from Atlanta.

But this is a different level of investment. Jacksonville hasn't simply dipped into its draft capital to patch up a few holes. It has surrendered two premium selections to acquire a 26-year-old defensive lineman who could become a cornerstone of its defense for years to come.

The Jaguars aren't just buying to secure a place in the playoffs. They're buying for a Super Bowl.

There aren't many players in the NFL who can physically dominate the interior of an offensive line quite like Jordan Davis.

Standing at 6-foot-6 and weighing 336 pounds, Davis is an imposing presence who brings an entirely different dimension to Jacksonville's defensive front.

Selected 13th overall in the 2022 NFL Draft, Davis arrived in Philadelphia with enormous expectations. His development has seen him become an important component of one of the league's most formidable defensive fronts, earning a Super Bowl ring along the way.

His 2025 campaign was arguably his best yet. Davis recorded career highs of 72 tackles, 4.5 sacks and nine tackles for loss, demonstrating that he could offer more than simply occupying blockers and clogging running lanes.

He's registered 31 tackles, 1.5 sacks and two tackles for loss through nine starts this season. Those numbers don't necessarily jump off the page, but Davis' value extends beyond the traditional box score. His ability to occupy multiple blockers, control the line of scrimmage and create opportunities for the players around him makes him an incredibly valuable defensive asset.

For defensive coordinator Anthony Campanile, adding a player of Davis' physical profile could be transformational. The Jaguars have already lost Davon Hamilton for the season, creating an obvious need in the middle of their defensive line. Godchaux provides an experienced replacement, but Davis offers something considerably more exciting: a potential long-term answer at the position.

And there's a further attraction. Davis has spent his entire professional career playing in Philadelphia's defensive system under Vic Fangio, giving Jacksonville another player with experience in a defensive environment that shares some philosophical similarities with Campanile's approach.

Let's not pretend this is a cheap acquisition.

A first-round pick in 2027 and a second-round selection in 2029 is an enormous amount of draft capital to surrender for one defensive player. Especially when you consider that Chicago recently paid two first-round picks to acquire Tennessee Titans defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons.

But context matters. Jacksonville is 6-2, sitting atop the AFC South and playing some outstanding football. Its offense is among the league's best, while Campanile has developed a defense capable of competing with anybody.

The opportunity is there. The Jaguars have recognised it, and they're acting accordingly.

There's also a longer-term consideration. Davis is only 26 and was signed to a three-year, $78 million contract extension by Philadelphia in March. This isn't a rental player brought in for a desperate postseason push. Jacksonville is acquiring a player who can help them now and remain an important part of the defensive front beyond this season.

The first-round pick could ultimately be a relatively late selection if the Jaguars continue on their current trajectory. That doesn't make it insignificant, but it does change the calculation. At some point, teams have to stop treating draft picks as lottery tickets and start using them to improve a roster capable of winning a championship.

This is a question that needs answering. Whenever a blue-chip player gets moved from one roster to another, there has to be a reason, right?

The Eagles have invested heavily in Davis, handing him a three-year, $78 million extension earlier this year. He's also been an important leader in their locker room, making his departure all the more surprising.

However, Philadelphia's season has taken a significant turn for the worse. The Eagles have been struggling, and moving a player of Davis' value for two premium draft selections gives Howie Roseman an opportunity to reshape the roster.

The return is substantial. Philadelphia now has an additional first-round pick in 2027 and another second-round selection in 2029, giving Roseman even more ammunition to replenish a roster that has some significant questions to answer.

There is also the situation surrounding Moro Ojomo. Philadelphia had reportedly been reluctant to move him, and Davis' departure potentially creates a clearer path towards retaining the younger defensive tackle.

It's a significant financial and football decision, particularly after extending Davis just eight months ago. But Roseman has built his reputation on knowing when to sell, and he's extracted a substantial return here.

The Eagles may ultimately be comfortable with the decision. Jacksonville certainly will be hoping they regret it.

There's always a danger in handing out top grades for blockbuster trades. Draft capital matters, and surrendering a first and second-round pick is a considerable commitment. But the Jaguars have a genuine opportunity to do something special this season.

They've lost important defensive starters to injury, identified the positions that need strengthening and gone out to acquire established NFL talent. Godchaux brings experience. Bates brings leadership and playmaking ability. Davis brings size, power, youth and the potential to anchor the defensive interior for the foreseeable future.

Most importantly, these additions complement a roster that was already performing at an exceptionally high level. James Gladstone and Liam Coen clearly believe this team is ready to compete for the biggest prize in football. Rather than sitting back and hoping their existing roster is good enough, they've invested heavily in making sure it is.

This is what going ALL IN looks like. The Jaguars have made their intentions abundantly clear, and the rest of the league have just been put on notice.
