Lucid Needs More Buyers. This Strategy Could Help It Reach Them.

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Only time will tell whether Lucid's plan to bring on partners for expanding showroom and service reach will pay off.

By Daniel Miller – Nov 23, 2026 at 12:00PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Lucid Group ›

Lucid Group (LCID +6.79%) has spent plenty of time convincing investors its vehicles deserve attention. But building an impressive electric vehicle (EV) is one thing. Convincing people to pull the trigger on a big-ticket purchase can prove more challenging.

A customer might love the range, performance, and design, then wonder where they'll get it serviced in a pinch. Admiring a car online is easy, but signing the paperwork when support feels far away, combined with Lucid's financial uncertainty? That's a tougher sell, especially for a young EV maker such as Lucid.

Lucid is introducing another twist within its European strategy, and it's possible the move could help Lucid convince more buyers to jump on board without needing to operate more locations itself.

In August, Lucid named Munsterhuis Autobedrijven its first retail partner in the Netherlands. The partnership calls for a dedicated Lucid sales location in the city of Hengelo, with authorized after-sales and service at the same site. Essentially, it complements Lucid's existing Studio and Service Center in Hilversum.

This continues a hybrid retail strategy that already included a partnership with Germany's Wackenhut. Lucid is adding independent partners alongside its own locations. The upside is worth noting for investors because an established retailer brings local relationships and experience selling and servicing cars. Lucid brings the vehicles and enjoys its expanding reach.

For buyers, nearby sales and service could remove a reason to hesitate before making the big-ticket purchase. For shareholders, partners could provide a way to expand customer access while sharing the work of building a retail presence. All in all, it's really an underrated move by Lucid if it can expand on the idea and execute.

Lucid's strategic twist isn't completely unheard of before. Chinese EV maker Nio is pursuing a related approach. In June 2025, it announced European expansion through local distribution partners, including Hedin Mobility Group for Belgium and Luxembourg.

The agreements aren't exactly identical, but the logic and strategy are similar: An automaker doesn't have to build every part of its overseas sales network from scratch. Of course, the middleman needs to make money, too. Sharing the economics with a retail partner could leave Lucid keeping less from each sale.

But reaching more buyers while committing less capital to its own retail network could be a worthwhile trade-off for a company trying to conserve cash.

The question facing investors is whether better access produces additional sales without an equally large increase in Lucid's own spending. Investors should cheer more partner appointments, broader service coverage, and evidence that the expanded network attracts customers. That said, a longer location list alone won't guarantee a spike in deliveries, demand, or revenue.

This won't fix Lucid's business overnight, and to be fair, the young automaker has a number of problems to address. However, making its vehicles easier to buy and maintain for more potential consumers could help turn product credibility into more sales.

Lucid doesn't need to own every showroom. It needs more customers walking into one – and driving home in its cars.
