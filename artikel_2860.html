The Chicago Bears are on top of the world

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Down by 11 points with less than eight minutes to go in the fourth quarter, the Chicago Bears had their backs against the wall in their Thanksgiving showdown against the Detroit Lions on Thursday. But somehow, someway, they found a way to win, such has been the case for much of Ben Johnson's tenure as their head coach.

Thanks to a last-second touchdown pass from Caleb Williams to Rome Odunze, the Bears picked up the 39-35 win over the Lions, marking their sixth win in a row and improving their record to 9-2 for the year. They were in search of their first divisional win of the year, and after falling in each of their first two NFC North games, they came away with a massive win, granting them sole possession of first place in the division.

In between bites of turkey and second helpings of mashed potatoes, Bears fans had plenty to be thankful for watching this game. Here are some of my takeaways from Thursday's victory.

They don't call him "Iceman" for nothing. Caleb Williams was lights out in the fourth quarter, throwing for 135 passing yards and two touchdowns in the final stretch for the Bears. Both of his touchdown passes ended up going to Rome Odunze, who himself deserves significant credit for his toe-tapping touchdown grab in the back corner of the end zone. The Bears' offense climbed out of the hole it made for itself and utterly dominated down the stretch, controlling the last stretch of the game.

Chicago utilized a pass-heavy approach on Thursday, and that worked out well for them. Odunze finished the game with six catches for 87 yards, on top of his aforementioned two touchdowns. D'Andre Swift added seven catches for 95 yards, on top of his 65 rushing yards and a touchdown on nine carries. When Colston Loveland went down with an injury, the likes of Sam Roush and Stephen Carlson contributed with a few plays. Luther Burden III had an impressive 25-yard gain, to boot.

The Bears have such a deep arsenal of weapons in the passing game, and though the absences of Loveland and Cole Kmet loom large, head coach Ben Johnson did a great job of adjusting, and his players stepped up when it mattered most.

Of course, no game in the NFL is ever going to be perfect. For the Bears to have been trailing by 11 points to begin with, they had to have made some mistakes. Ball security remains an issue, as both Loveland and Williams turned the ball over with fumbles, with the latter's fumble resulting in a safety to add to the Lions' lead. Offensively, the run game was inconsistent, as D'Andre Swift's occasional big run was dragged down by a poor offensive line performance and Kyle Monangai's inefficiency, having tallied just 1.9 yards per carry on Thursday, despite rushing for two touchdowns.

As the scoreboard would indicate, Chicago's defense had some issues against the Lions. Detroit has an elite offense — it ranks No. 3 in the NFL with an 88.4 PFN Offense Impact grade — but the Bears had some discouraging lapses on Thursday. The defensive line failed to generate pressure on a consistent basis, and even with Jeffery Simmons often being double-teamed or schemed around, the rest of the defensive line couldn't throw Jared Goff off his rhythm much. The cornerbacks got beat in coverage a handful of times, and Jahmyr Gibbs managed to run for 205 yards and three touchdowns. That's never a great day at the office.

Obviously, there's much more positive to take from a win like this than there is negative. Rather than lamenting the weaknesses, I like to think of these as reasons why Chicago hasn't even reached its ceiling yet, and considering how good they already are, that's a scary thought for the rest of the NFL.

I feel confident in saying that the Bears currently have their core in place. I also feel confident in saying that this core has an extremely high ceiling that could end in this team winning the Super Bowl.

Maybe that will be this year, maybe it will come in a different year, and maybe it won't come at all. But there's so much to like about this team. They have their franchise quarterback, one of the best head coaches in the NFL, and a deep group of weapons in the passing game. Chicago also has superstar players at positions like offensive tackle and defensive tackle, and its defense has shown that it can step up when it matters most and still generate turnovers at a high level.

The Bears have now beaten several top teams across the NFL. They defeated each of last year's Super Bowl competitors in back-to-back weeks, notably marching into Seattle to beat the Seahawks in a difficult road environment. Their win on Thursday against the Lions might've been the most impressive of them all, because it looked like they were being outplayed by one of the NFL's best teams, but they found a way to pull it all together and achieve the comeback victory. That's a testament to the Bears' coaching staff and the heart that this current roster brings to the table.

Time will tell how far this Bears team ends up going. But I believe that the primary pieces are in place for this group to do some special things.
