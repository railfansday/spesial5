After two-week hangover, Warriors' slumbering offense wakes up in win over Kings

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

After two-week hangover, Warriors' slumbering offense wakes up in win over Kings originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

SAN FRANCISCO – De'Anthony Melton was raining 3-pointers again. Draymond Green was flipping dimes for fun. Gui Santos was wearing out his battery pack. Brandin Podziemski couldn't miss and Brandon Williams was back and in 2K mode.

The Warriors on Wednesday night played offense with a fury that suggests they finally have gotten past the two-week hangover following Stephen Curry going down with an injury.

Their 129-110 victory over the Sacramento Kings reminds them that, after living below the 100-point mark for five consecutive games in Curry's absence, they can find buckets without him.

"Two good practices in a row," coach Steve Kerr said. "Building rhythm, getting better with our recognition of what we're trying to do. We were due. It's been a tough five- or six-game stretch shooting the ball."

The scoring outburst was an exploitation of Sacramento's damp-cardboard defense, but it still was significant that Golden State's offense did what it should be able to do against a beatable opponent – even with Curry out with a right oblique tear.

On this night, the Warriors came out smoking, building a 28-12 lead in less than eight minutes mostly behind 12 points each from Melton and Santos, and ending the quarter with a 44-19 lead. Williams grabbed the baton in the second quarter, blasting in for two layups on back-to-back possessions in the first 55 seconds.

The return of Williams, Melton's former high-school teammate, brought the kind of dynamic effect that filled a vacuum that limited Golden State's offense.

"He just gives us that element of speed that can break down the defense, and you don't even run any offense," Kerr said. "You're not calling any plays. You just get a stop, and you throw him the ball, and he's just gone."

While Williams' strained hamstring was healing, he only could watch as Golden State's offense wheezed and sputtered, averaging 94.5 points, over six games he missed. He was thrilled to be back in his element.

"It's been a while since I've been out there, and just watching ain't been the same," said Williams, who scored 14 points in 13 minutes off the bench. "Being out there, being with the team, all that stuff was fun.

"That's what they brought me in to do. Keep that offense running when a certain point guard comes out. So, the offense won't look stagnant or anything like that. I know my job. I know my role."

Maybe it was coincidental, but Melton's revival came on the same night his former teammate at Crespi Carmelite High (Encino) returned to the lineup. His team-high 24 points, all on 8-of-12 shooting from beyond the arc, were a welcome sight.

It was quite the divergence from his previous 10 games during which he averaged 6.5 points on 26.7 percent from the field, including 25.5 percent from deep.

"Melt hasn't shot it well the last few games, so you kind of knew that it was it was time," Kerr said.

"It's constant reps," Melton said. "I think constantly just repping it out, just having a confidence, and just putting yourself in certain game situations that and you put your mindset on that. I feel like I passed up a couple 3s last game, so it's just about taking them now.

"Steve was just telling us just to be confident, let it fly. We tweaked a couple things on offense, which it's a lot more shooting threes and just be ready to shoot."

The most encouraging aspect for the Warriors was the collective effort and production. Santos' 8-of-11 shooting from the field. Green's 11 assists and only two turnovers.  Podziemski making his first five shots and recording six assists without a turnover. Yaxel Lendeborg's dutiful 15 points and eight rebounds.

After six games in trudging in quicksand, Golden State's offense looked alive.

"We started out the season shooting the ball so well that was unsustainable." Kerr said. "But then so was the shooting from the last six games. This was obviously a hot night, and it's not going to be like this all the time, but I think over the course of the season we will show that we're an excellent 3-point shooting team."

Thrashing the lowly Kings should not register as magnificent. Not with their dreadful defense. But it was the kind of performance the Warriors could not come close to summoning 16 days ago in New Orleans against the reeling Pelicans.

Recovery begins with one step, and that's what this victory represents.
