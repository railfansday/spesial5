Judge Blocks FCC Move Against ABC Station Licenses: ‘This Is Not a Close Case’

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

A judge on Wednesday blocked the Federal Communications Commission from seeking early license renewals from ABC stations, finding that the network has faced a “campaign of retaliation” for its protected speech.

The FCC ordered Disney-owned ABC in April to file renewal applications for its eight owned stations. The move came a few days after Jimmy Kimmel, the network’s late night host, joked that the First Lady had “a glow like an expectant widow,” which prompted President Trump to call for Kimmel’s firing. In a ruling on Wednesday, Judge Loren AliKhan ruled that ABC was likely to prevail on its claim that the renewal process was forbidden by the First Amendment.

“No one doubts that the Commission may enforce the law in the manner it sees fit,” the judge wrote, “But in doing so, Defendants may not retaliate against or punish Plaintiffs and other broadcast license holders for disfavored speech.”

The judge noted that Trump has routinely called for ABC’s licenses to be revoked in response to editorial decisions. Under Brendan Carr, Trump’s appointee to chair the FCC, the agency has opened an investigation of “The View” and its candidate interviews. Carr also threatened ABC over a Kimmel monologue on September 2025, saying “We can do this the easy way or the hard way.”

AliKhan found that ABC has engaged in self-censorship since the FCC actions began. “The View” has avoided candidate interviews, for fear of running afoul of the “equal time” rule, while Kimmel put an interview with Texas Senate candidate James Talarico on YouTube, rather than risk airing it on broadcast.

“This is not a close case,” the judge wrote.

ABC sought an injunction blocking the early renewal process, as well as an order barring “any actions, formal or informal, to coerce or threaten Plaintiffs with sanctions in an effort to alter their exercise of editorial discretion.” The judge granted the former but denied the latter, saying the request was too vague.

The FCC also sought to force ABC to post an “appropriate bond” as a condition of maintaining the injunction. The judge ordered the company to post a bond in the amount of $1.
