Smartphone shipments face another bleak year before modest 2028 comeback

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Counterpoint forecasts another 6% shipment drop in 2027, with volumes still below 2025 levels by decade's end

Global smartphone shipments are forecast to fall 14 percent this year and another 6 percent in 2027, with a recovery not expected until 2028.

That's the outlook from Counterpoint Research's latest Smartphone Market Outlook Tracker, which points to rising memory costs and constrained supplies as continuing pressure on handset makers and buyers.

The downturn was particularly sharp in the third quarter: IDC and Omdia estimated year-on-year shipment declines of 22 percent and 20 percent respectively, as rising prices and component shortages squeezed the market.

Don't expect things to get better in the short term, as Counterpoint forecasts another 6 percent drop during 2027. But it believes that the market may see a 6 percent rebound in 2028, as memory supplies improve, pricing begins to stabilize, and pent-up demand for replacement phones returns.

The 2026 outlook has been shaped by sharply rising component costs, particularly for DRAM and NAND flash. According to IDC, memory costs have risen approximately 350 percent since last year and are still climbing.

As Reg readers will be well aware by now, this has been blamed on the memory manufacturers prioritizing output of high-margin chips to go into AI servers and GPU accelerators, creating a shortage of the mainstream components that are needed for devices like PCs and phones.

Vendors have responded by raising prices, reducing memory configurations, and withdrawing lower-cost models. As The Register previously reported, memory costs accounted for almost 60 percent of the total bill of materials in sub-$400 smartphones during Q1 of 2026.

For 2027, Counterpoint warns the outlook is for continued tight mobile DRAM supply, further price increases for low-power DDR (LPDDR) memory, and limited evidence to suggest short-term capacity increases.

"The 2026 decline was initially expected to mark the low point of the cycle, but the memory situation has remained more restrictive than anticipated," said principal analyst Yang Wang.

"Mobile memory allocation is still being displaced by AI and server demand, while higher costs are taking longer to move through handset portfolios and retail channels. The result is a much weaker 2027, particularly for lower priced devices and price sensitive markets."

Global shipments are forecast to rebound by 6 percent in 2028, but that would bring shipments only roughly back to this year's already shrunken level, rather than hitting the peak of previous years.

Counterpoint cautions that the scale of the rebound will depend on how quickly lower component costs translate into retail pricing.

A speedy improvement in mobile memory allocation during the second half of 2027 could bring replacement demand forward and swell shipments, but continued competition from the AI market or another round of handset price increases would hinder recovery.

"The unusually low 2027 base creates the potential for a robust rebound in 2028 once the memory bottleneck begins to ease. But this should not be interpreted as a return to the old market trajectory," Wang said.

"Some low-end demand has been permanently lost, and the 2028 market will still be about 14 percent below 2025 shipments."

Potential availability of 6G-capable smartphones toward the end of the decade could give the market a boost, but Counterpoint does not expect it to regain its 2025 shipment levels by 2030. ®
