Bucks vs. Trail Blazers Player Grades: Ament drops career high, Rollins returns with a vengeance

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

On yet another night when the Milwaukee Bucks matched up against a high-profile former player (or two), they couldn't get it done, losing to the Portland Trail Blazers 124-114. The Blazers gave the Bucks chances early, looking like they were the ones on the second night of a back-to-back, not the Bucks. Unfortunately, the Bucks failed to capitalise on those chances and fell at the mercy of two former star Milwaukee point guards, Damian Lillard and Jrue Holiday, who taught the young Bucks a lesson down the stretch. Read our full summary of the game here and catch a six-minute audio recap on the Bucks+ podcast, Bucks In Six Minutes, below.

31 minutes, 19 points, 4 rebounds, 3 assists, 1 steal, 4 turnovers, 7/13 FG, 4/7 3P, 1/3 FT, -16

Herro's counting stats look fine, but it felt like he played a lot worse. Herro started the game on autopilot, and his turnovers all felt like pick-sixes, especially the one in the fourth quarter where he got ripped trying to bring the ball up against Holiday. I generally don't lean too much into single-game plus/minus, but this one feels dead on. All things being equal—which they may not have been—Herro should not have closed the game.

21 minutes, 15 points, 3 rebounds, 6 assists, 4 steals, 3 turnovers, 5/11 FG, 1/5 3P, 4/6 FT, +5

You can basically take everything I said about Herro and flip it for Rollins. His impact on the game was monumental, and most of it came on defence. Even more impressive, he did this while coming off an illness. Rollins deserved more minutes than he got (assuming he wasn't on a minutes restriction).

22 minutes, 9 points, 1 rebounds, 2 assists, 1 steal, 1 turnover, 3/11 FG, 1/4 3P, -5

Jaquez was bad. He contributed virtually nothing on offence, and with Avdija out, there wasn't really a defensive matchup for him. I especially hate the possessions where Jaquez dribbles aimlessly without gaining any advantage on his defender; it feels like they always end in a turnover.

25 minutes, 11 points, 5 rebounds, 2 steals, 4/8 FG, 2/4 3P, -5

Ware was… decent? Like, the three-ball is coming back, and he continues to show stuff defensively, but you know what I loved? That monster offensive rebound and putback he had in the fourth quarter. That showed a level of emotion and care factor we have rarely seen from Ware. Inject it into my veins!

28 minutes, 14 points, 3 rebounds, 3 assists, 2 steals, 3 turnovers, 5/13 FG, 2/5 3P, -11

A bit of a rookie game for Burries, with a bunch of good stuff and some not-so-good stuff. He's got that floater going left on lock, so that's good. His defence felt really impactful, and the play where he prevented Scoot Henderson from even crossing half court was stupendous. I will say that Burries' release is going to have to speed up in the future; guys are getting to his shots starting from far away. All in all, I thought the rook played better than what his stat line would suggest.

22 minutes, 18 points, 3 rebounds, 2 assists, 1 steal, 2 turnovers, 6/11 FG, 3/6 3P, 3/5 FT, -6

A third really solid game in a row from Jakucionis, who should have closed over Herro. At this point, it's basically become a theme that he comes into the game, makes stuff happen, and, most importantly, brings energy. Now, some silly stuff is going to come with that, but you take the good with the bad, because there is undoubtedly more good. Finally, love that he's not even hesitating on his three-ball right now.

35 minutes, 18 points, 9 rebounds, 0 turnovers, 6/11 FG, 2/5 3P, 4/4 FT, -7

On balance, this was Ament's best game of his young NBA career, and it showed in the number of minutes he played. I truly think his shot has a high chance of going in every time he shoots, and that's a great sign for the future. I also love that Ament is attacking rebounds more and more, especially on the offensive glass; his game is becoming more multifaceted with each passing week. Plus, no turnovers! My only negative—and it is a big one—is that his off-ball defence remains a wreck; overhelping was a major issue last night.

27 minutes, 7 points, 3 rebounds, 3 assists, 1 block, 0 turnovers, 3/8 FG, 1/5 3P, +8

I know it's become a bit of a cliché to say that "good things happen when Nance is on the floor," but it's true! His line isn't impressive, but the offence just flows when the two-way big is on the court in a way it doesn't necessarily with other players. Just wait until that three-ball starts dropping. More please!

11 minutes, 3 points, 3 rebounds, 1/2 FG, 0

I considered leaving LeVert off, but I couldn't ignore his impact as a rebounder in the fourth. He obviously had that ridiculous and-one over Clingan, but LeVert grabbed like three O-boards in one possession; unfortunately, the Bucks couldn't pay it off with a bucket.

I've already alluded to Jenkins making what I believed was the wrong call closing with Herro over Jakucionis, but he also brought back Rollins—who was making a wildly positive impact—too late. Then there are the tough decisions that aren't entirely based on the now, like closing with Ament. You had to close with the rookie given the game he had played, but Lillard and Holiday kinda feasted on him late. That's the push-and-pull of coaching a rebuilding side. On the other hand, I loved the move to start Nance.

DNP-CD: AJ Green, Kam Jones, Bogoljub Markovic, Cormac Ryan

Inactive: Myles Turner, Kyle Kuzma, Ousmane Dieng

The veterans are not necessarily needed for this team to compete.

I found it ironic that Dame complained about a foul call where Ament hooked his arm and then drew a shooting foul. That's Dame's signature move! LOL.

Jrue was coaching up Brayden after the game. What a guy.

Speaking of sneaky foul-drawing, Jakucionis has the "drive hard at the hip, throw it up, and pray to the gods they call a foul" move on lock. What worries me is that, like it or not, those calls are generally reserved for guys with stature. Therefore, there will be—and have been—times when KJ does that, it doesn't get called, and he throws up a shot that is basically a turnover.

Gary Trent Jr.'s stint was absolutely diabolical. Exactly why I don't necessarily buy the idea that high steal numbers are a sign of a good defender.

Former Wisconsin Badger John Tonje was in the Blazers rotation.

Nate Ament spoke glowingly of Jrue Holiday in his postgame presser, highlighting his two-way impact.
