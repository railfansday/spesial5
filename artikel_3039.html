What we learned as Darius Acuff Jr. cools off in Kings' loss to Trail Blazers

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

What we learned as Darius Acuff Jr. cools off in Kings' loss to Trail Blazers originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

SACRAMENTO — The losing streak continued for the hapless Sacramento Kings.

The Kings dropped their second game of the Emirates NBA Cup on Friday against the Portland Trail Blazers, 120-107, and their fifth straight overall to drop to 1-11. Only the 1-12 Utah Jazz have a worse record.

The Blazers opened a 20-point lead early in the fourth quarter which drew boos from the home crowd at Golden 1 Center. Rookie guard Darius Acuff Jr. cut the lead to nine midway through the frame with a 3-pointer to give the crowd some life down the stretch.

But Portland received 34 points from forward Deni Avdija, including 14 in the fourth quarter, and 29 from star point guard Damian Lillard as the two combined to shoot 9 of 18 from 3-point range to improve to 9-3 on the season.

Forward De'Andre Hunter led the Kings with 24 points on 7-of-13 shooting. He had 20-plus points for the first time since posting back-to-back 24-point outings Oct. 27 and 29.

Hunter played a team-high 41:31 and made 8 of 9 free throws. He's been one of Sacramento's steadiest players throughout the season and could garner interest on the NBA trade market. He has a $24.9 million expiring salary that could be of interest to a contender needing size and on the wing.

He finished Friday 2 of 5 from distance, improving to 37 percent on the season.

Domantas Sabonis was coming off one of the worst statistical games of his NBA career Tuesday when he went scoreless in 24 minutes during the 107-100 loss to the Orlando Magic. He went 0-for-2 from the floor with his first shot not coming until the fourth quarter.

But he came out aggressive early on Friday, finishing the first quarter with eight points, six rebounds and three assists, including a nifty behind-the-head pass to Keegan Murray for a fast-break layup. He was aggressive throughout, finishing with 21 points, 12 rebounds and five assists.

It was Sabonis' eighth double-double of the season, highlighted by a dunk in traffic and 3-pointer in the fourth quarter. Sabonis battled Blazers big man Donovan Clingan, who scored 13 points with 11 rebounds.

Forward Keegan Murray was back in the starting five for the first time since the second game of the season before missing seven games with left ankle capsulitis.

Murray had his best game of the young season with a season-high 16 points, while adding seven rebounds and four assists.

The fifth-year forward has gotten off to an uneven start to the season due to his injury after only appearing in four games all of last season, when he signed a five-year, $140 million contract.

"Keegan's a little bit stoic," Kings coach Doug Christie said. "You know he's just going to be a fantastic warrior for you through whatever it is. But for him, what I want is, I want joy. I want him to go out and have fun and play free. We've seen it a couple of times, we've seen it in flashes. But he deserves that. He's a great kid, he works extremely hard.

"… I know that sometimes it's difficult when you start to get injuries and different things. Sometimes you question yourself a little bit, but he doesn't need to question himself. He's a damn good player. We value him very highly and he'll see his way through it. It's just those things unfortunately take time, but having him back in uniform is, like I told you guys previously, it makes me smile because I like to see him out there."

Acuff entered the fourth quarter with just seven points and was in danger of snapping his streak of 20-plus points at seven games. But he scored 10 points in the fourth, including a fast-break dunk, and two made threes.

Acuff entered the fourth having played just 23 minutes with Christie tapping Malik Monk for 18 minutes throughout the game after Monk played just 3 on Tuesday. Acuff came into the game leading the NBA in minutes, with Christie noting recently he wanted to cut the rookie's playing time down to ease his workload.

The Blazers' lengthy perimeter defenders, including Toumani Camara, Sidy Cissoko and Jrue Holiday, made things tough on the rookie, who shot just 6 of 19 on the night.
