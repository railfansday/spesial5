XP: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Offshore accounts reach about a third of XP's clients yet hold under 8% of assets, leaving room the company is betting on to grow.

By The Motley Fool Team – Nov 27, 2026 at 7:58AM EST

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 16, 2026, XP (XP +19.56%) announced a new BRL 1.5 billion share buyback program and declared BRL 1.3 billion in dividends. Total client assets expanded to BRL 2.2 trillion as the business diversified its revenue sources into corporate banking and global product offerings. Three things management said stand out for long-term investors.

XP completed its prior share buyback program and immediately authorized a fresh allocation while declaring direct dividend distributions. Returning capital to shareholders reduces outstanding share count and boosts earnings-per-share growth over time.

International accounts provide attractive fee structures and deepen client retention across market cycles. While one-third of clients hold an offshore account, those balances represent less than 8% of total assets under custody (AUC), leaving substantial room for balance growth.

Expanding corporate credit and financial planning services cushions revenue against fluctuating retail trading volumes. Management targets annual revenue growth around 10% while controlling selling, general, and administrative (SG&A) expenses to expand earnings before taxes (EBT).

Total client assets reached BRL 2.2 trillion as retail net inflows resumed. Corporate banking solutions and international account offerings provide structural protection against interest rate volatility. Management tracks operational discipline alongside efficiency ratios.

Ongoing share repurchases and disciplined capital allocation position XP to compound per-share earnings across economic cycles.
