Jim Cramer Isn't Buying SpaceX Stock Yet. Cathie Wood Keeps Buying. Here’s Who’s Right.

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Your investing goals are more important than who is right.

By Jack Delaney – Nov 19, 2026 at 6:15AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Space Exploration Technologies ›

CNBC stock analyst and TV personality Jim Cramer is cautious about investing in Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -0.20%), while Ark Invest CEO and investing head Cathie Wood said in July, "We think this could become the most important company in history, and I mean in global history."

Each of these stock prognosticators has their own following and detractors. Which camp do you fall into?

Deciding whether to invest in SpaceX comes down to what makes sense for an individual investor's situation.

Cramer has told his audience that he has not bought shares for his charitable trust. He's been complimentary toward SpaceX, but he also raised a range of issues in one of his recent Mad Money shows. He has highlighted SpaceX’s spending costs as well as the complications surrounding a potential merger between SpaceX and Tesla (TSLA +0.51%).

Meanwhile, the second-largest position in Wood's ARK Innovation ETF (ARKK +1.53%) at the moment is SpaceX (portfolio weight of 8.2%), only trailing Elon Musk's other company, Tesla.

The investment actions differ, but that speaks to each having their own goals, which is the bigger takeaway from this little thought exercise than who is "right."

Retirees, for example, may be more concerned with stability in their investments than with high-risk, high-reward results that could take multiple years (or decades) to pay off, if they do at all. They may then lean more toward Cramer's actions of sitting on the sidelines.

In the third quarter of 2026, SpaceX's capital expenditures climbed 186% from the prior year to $19.5 billion, with spending only likely to keep climbing. It's also not a profitable company, reporting a net loss of $413 million for the first nine months of the year. In a retiree's situation, it may make more sense to own shares of a company like JPMorgan Chase (JPM +0.92%), which is profitable and can use earnings for dividend payments and stock buybacks.

A younger investor with a longer time horizon may find the risk-to-reward framework worthwhile and lean toward mimicking Wood's moves. In Q3, SpaceX's revenue grew 161%; sales from what it calls its Super Intelligence segment increased from $813 million to $7.5 billion; and it has financial flexibility, with approximately $90 billion in cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities.

All the numbers suggest that SpaceX is likely a better fit for investors seeking aggressive growth than for those looking for predictable returns. Keep that in mind as you do further research on this stock.
