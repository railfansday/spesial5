Anthropic’s IPO nets Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley $113 million apiece in fees, eclipsing their SpaceX paydays

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Anthropic’s blockbuster IPO on Thursday delivered one of Wall Street’s largest underwriting paydays in history, with the banks behind the listing raking in at least $550 million in fees, eclipsing the $500 million that was earned from Elon Musk–led SpaceX’s debut in June.

Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley, the lead underwriters on the deal, each garnered fees of $113 million, according to Anthropic’s prospectus and confirmed by a source familiar with the matter. That’s because they both took on underwriting commitments of 20.5% of the $81.3 billion IPO. It’s also more than the roughly $100 million Goldman and Morgan Stanley each raked in from fees on the SpaceX deal over the summer.

JPMorgan and Citigroup each agreed to underwrite 10% of the Claude maker’s offering, putting their estimated fees at roughly $55 million apiece if allocated in proportion to their commitments. Bank of America, which committed to underwriting 3%, would receive an estimated $16.5 million in fees.

The last IPO to generate more than Anthropic’s in underwriting commissions was Visa’s 2008 debut, which delivered a $550.2 million fee pool. Anthropic could surpass that figure if underwriters exercise the offering’s over-allotment option, which allows banks to sell up to 15% more shares than initially planned.

Anthropic shares, trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT, surged 17% in their Thursday debut, giving the company a fully diluted market capitalization of $1.27 trillion at the close. The $81.3 billion offering was the largest IPO in history by deal size.

Anthropic’s listing generated roughly 30 to 40 times the commissions of a typical IPO owing to its massive size, according to Matt Kennedy, senior IPO market strategist at Renaissance Capital. “By deal count, 2026 wasn’t the blowout year many were hoping for,” Kennedy said in a statement to Fortune. “So, mega IPOs like Anthropic have done a lot of the heavy lifting to make this a standout year for investment banks.”

Morgan Chittum is a reporter at Fortune covering finance.
