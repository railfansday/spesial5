Bridgewater says China's open-source AI model release coincided with a cyberattack surge

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The hedge fund is managing money on a one-in-three chance of an economically destabilizing cyberattack within the next year

Bridgewater Associates said the release of GLM 5.3, a model from China's Z.ai, coincided with a surge in cyberattacks, according to MarketWatch.

The Westport, Connecticut, hedge fund developed a time series of cyber incidents and paired it with a framework that ties each episode to its underlying causes, plus a live pipeline that follows new exploits, according to its Nov. 13 Daily Observations note. Bridgewater found that incidents are being logged far more often and that a growing portion of them is blamed on AI, with exponential growth continuing.

In that note, Bridgewater said it is positioning its portfolio on the assumption that there is a one-in-three probability of an attack that could destabilize the economy over the coming year. It described AI-enabled cyber warfare as growing at an exponential pace with few checks on it, much as a virus would.

"We are in the middle of an arms race where malicious actors are using agents to find exploitable bugs and defenders are using agents to preemptively find and patch vulnerabilities," said an analysis written by Bridgewater's Hudson Attar and Arham Habib, according to MarketWatch. "While models have significantly accelerated work on both sides of the ledger, the defenders have the much heavier burden of patching all bugs before they can be exploited."

Bridgewater said zero-day exploits, meaning attacks that give defenders no advance warning, have approximately doubled in number since open-source models with real hacking ability first came out. The typical lag from vulnerability disclosure to exploitation has shrunk to 11 days, compared with about 30 previously, the firm said.

The analysis did not focus on the reasons GLM is favored, according to MarketWatch. Pricing on OpenRouter shows GLM costs $1.40 per million tokens, while models from OpenAI and Anthropic can run up to $10. Amazon $AMZN, Google $GOOGL and Microsoft $MSFT host GLM models, and Hugging Face used GLM 5.2 to defend against a breach by an OpenAI rogue agent.

Open-source agent frameworks have drawn separate warnings. The FBI warned on Nov. 13 that hackers relied on a framework called ARTEX to hit organizations around the globe, and that in some cases they needed under three hours to go from first targeting to privileged access, according to Axios.

"We're seeing more active attacks than ever right now," Sam Rubin, senior vice president of Unit 42, the threat intelligence and consulting arm of Palo Alto Networks $PANW, told Axios.

The Amplify Cybersecurity ETF jumped 6.1% on Wednesday. Palo Alto Networks raised its annual outlook the same day.

More than 100 technology companies, including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Microsoft, signed an open letter in August urging a unified defense against AI-enabled cyberattacks. The letter warned that such attacks would become more widespread and sophisticated as models become more capable.
