The Gear Rémi Bonnet Uses to Run up Mountains—and the Tudor Watch He Wears

Inside the shoes and layers the endurance athlete relies on for steep climbs.

RÉMI BONNET MAKES running up mountains look easy. The Swiss endurance athlete holds the world record for the vertical kilometer, having climbed more than 3,200 feet in just 27 minutes and 21 seconds. It’s the equivalent of running nearly three Empire State Buildings straight up in less than half an hour.

That's why Tudor, Rolex's adventurous younger brother and one of our favorite watch brands, tapped the 31-year-old athlete for its “Born to Dare” campaign, which celebrates high-achieving risk-takers. For Bonnet, that partnership means "daring to take risks, daring to try new things, and daring to push the limit a bit further."

When Men's Health caught up with Bonnet in Chamonix, France, during UTMB Mont-Blanc, one of the world's most prestigious trail-running events, he shared how his gear has evolved over the years. While he once obsessed over shaving every possible gram off his equipment, he's learned that lighter isn't always better, especially when it comes to performance.

We asked Bonnet to share the six pieces of gear he swears by, from his favorite trail-running shoes to a blister-prevention product.

As a Tudor ambassador, Bonnet has his pick of some seriously handsome watches, but the Ranger is his favorite. We're also big fans. Inspired by Tudor’s history of exploration, this rugged field watch features a stainless steel case, scratch-resistant sapphire crystal, and water resistance up to 100 meters, so it can take a licking. With multiple case sizes and strap options (stainless steel bracelet! Fabric strap! Rubber!), you can find one that fits your vibe, whether you're dressing up for dinner or heading outdoors.

Bonnet relies on Normatec compression boots after grueling races and long flights. The latest Elite model is completely wireless, with up to four hours of battery life, making it easy to squeeze in a leg massage whether you're at home or on the road. It also offers seven pressure settings to customize the intensity. A Men’s Health tester found the boots helped relieve his tired legs after a tough workout, and he even fell asleep during a 60-minute session.

Before races, Bonnet puts on this anti-chafing cream. A blend of shea butter forms a thin, sweat-resistant barrier to help prevent blisters and friction mile after mile.

Bonnet loves these trail-running socks, which feature targeted cushioning in high-pressure areas like the heels and balls of the feet, plus moisture-wicking fabric to keep his feet dry mile after mile. They're also designed with extra support around the ankles to help lock the socks in place on uneven terrain.

As a Salomon-sponsored athlete, Bonnet is already running in the upcoming S/Lab Pulsar 5. "It is a mix between lightness and the performance of a road shoe," he says. Until that model hits shelves, the Pulsar 4 is the next best thing. It weighs just 9 ounces and features a dual-foam midsole, abrasion-resistant Matryx upper, and 3.5-millimeter rubber lugs for traction on rocky trails.

Bonnet uses an Insta360 camera to document his mountain adventures for Instagram. We're also fans of the brand, having awarded its GO Ultra a Men’s Health Tech Award in 2026. The latest X6 shoots sharp 8K video, with impressive image stabilization that keeps footage smooth even when you're shredding down a mountain. Its invisible selfie stick disappears from your footage for drone-like shots without needing someone else to film you.
