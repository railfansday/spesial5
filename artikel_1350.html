Record For Most Red Cards Given in a Single Football Match Shattered in Brazil

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

The record for most red cards given in a single football match was broken on November 1, 2026, when it all kicked off in the Minas Gerais Under-20 Championship Second Division clash between Uberlândia and Mamoré.

The match had to be abandoned after absolute carnage broke out in the second half, according to Ge.Globo

Eight minutes after the restart, Uberlândia were reduced to 10 men after Luike Bispo dos Santos was sent off for 'recklessly striking an opponent in the abdominal area' off the ball.

Uberlândia's football manager, Willer Francisco Silva, their youth team manager, André Luiz Nascimento, and youth coordinator,

Junior Caetano, stormed the field to confront Fernando Patrick, who was officiating the match. They're reported to have told him: "You're not leaving here today, you think you can get out of town? We're going to get you, you came here to screw us over!"

Uberlandia official Fábio Mineiro is also reported to have told the referee: “You will never referee again. I will do everything I can to ensure you don't work anymore. I can stop, but you will stop too", while Silva said: “I’m going to punch you, you’re not leaving here. I want to see if you’ll leave with an escort. Come at me, I’ll get you outside, it’ll just be you and me, you son of a b****, I want to k*** you, you piece of s***.”

Tensions were eased, and the match was able to restart. But sheer chaos erupted in stoppage-time after Davi Fernandes Bittencourt put Mamore 3-1 up.

Davi is said to have made provocative and inflammatory gestures towards the Uberlândia team's bench after his goal, shouting: "This is Mamoré, go f*** yourself! Suck it! Go f*** yourself!"

Those actions did not go down well with Uberlândia. Their goalkeeping coach, Paulo Sérgio Dias dos Santos, sprinted onto the pitch and pushed the Mamoré player. That sparked a brawl, with punches and kicks thrown, while stretchers and chairs were also launched. Six ball boys were also said to have got involved.

Patrick called a premature end to the match and the 17th Battalion of the Military Police were called. According to OTempo, The military found roughly 14 doors destroyed, as well as damage to sinks, faucets, toilets, showers, electrical installations, and furniture. Two hot tubs were also reportedly damaged.

51 red cards were dished out following the brawl, with 22 players from Uberlândia sent off, as well as 21 players from Mamoré. Four members of staff from both sides were also shown red cards.

The previous record for most red cards given in a football match came in 2011 in the Argentine Primera D game between Club Atlético Claypole and Victoriano Arenas. 36 red cards were handed out by referee Damián Rubino.

In March 2026, 23 red cards were handed out in the Campeonato Mineiro final between Cruzeiro and Atletico Mineiro.

Former Porto winger Hulk was one player that was given his marching orders. He said after the game: “I don’t recall ever participating in violence like that at a football match I attended. I don’t. It’s regrettable, and I will never tire of apologising. Of course, we are defending our colors, we will defend them to the death. We try to calm things down, but our blood runs hot, we see a teammate being attacked, and we automatically react. We have to defend our teammate and the colours of our team.”

"It could have been avoided, it could have been avoided. I never tire of saying that the main culprit in everything that happened is Matheus, the referee. I had spoken to him at the beginning of the second half, 'if you don't have control of the game, forget it.' There started being slaps in the face, pushing, and he did nothing.

"It was a very poorly played game, very disjointed. It looked like the friendly in Orlando, all fighting and the referee doing nothing. It's ugly, it's ugly for more than 50,000 fans watching, ugly for those watching at home, ugly for those who like to play football. If there's a fight, and the referee lets it go, it's ugly. There was no game."
