What Is A Lifestyle Hotel? The “Cool” Hotel Trend That Means Nothing

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

It goes without saying that the hospitality industry has a lot of trends. At times there might not actually be much substance to these trends, but the idea is that executives in the industry think certain things are “hot,” and they perceive it to reflect consumer demand.

Along those lines, if you look at trends among the major hotel groups, the concept of “lifestyle hotels” has become incredibly popular. There are an endless number of brands like these that have emerged. I think one logical question is what the term actually means. What makes a property a lifestyle hotel, rather than an… umm, not lifestyle hotel?

I know I’m generally pretty verbose and use a fairly limited vocabulary when it comes to describing things, but that’s intentional. In part, my issue is that many of the descriptors used in marketing, particularly in the travel industry, are so beyond meaningless:

My point is, we’re completely desensitized to useless descriptions of every aspect of the travel experience… unless you’re currently enjoying a seasonal fruit plate served by a private Michelin star chef on a plane, in which case I can’t save you. 😉

Anyway, that brings us to lifestyle hotels, one of the hottest trends in the hotel industry. It’s easy to name brands that claim to be lifestyle hotels — we’re talking Hilton’s Canopy and Curio Collection, Hyatt’s Andaz and Thompson, IHG’s Kimpton and voco, Marriott’s W Hotels and Autograph Collection, etc.

I guess the best way to define a lifestyle hotel would be to step back for a moment, and separate out the two words. Generally a lifestyle brand is a company that sells products by associating them with a specific way of living, set of values, or identities, that customers want to adopt.

A lifestyle hotel is generally defined as a property offering the personalization of an independent boutique hotel, with the attributes of a chain hotel (meaning loyalty, distribution power, etc.). Generally (but not always) these properties have a strong food & beverage or social concept as a core pillar, since that’s a large attribute that can make a hotel popular with a certain guest profile.

We can of course create all kinds of more specific distinctions, but I think it’s unnecessary. I’ve already written in the past about the concept of “boutique” hotels, another term that means so little. As far as I’m concerned, a lifestyle hotel could be small or big, as the big question is how bustling it is relative to its size.

But this also gets at the issue of how hotel groups choose to label their brands. For example, Marriott labels AC Hotels, Aloft, Autograph Collection, Element, Moxy, Tribute Portfolio, and W Hotels, all as lifestyle brands. From my perspective:

I mean, I live pretty close to a Moxy, and I’ve never thought to myself “hey family, maybe we should go to the Moxy for dinner tonight” (as much as my older son would probably love going somewhere and being able to microwave his own dinner).

So to me, these are the hotels that very much fit into the “fake it until you make it” camp. Like, the major hotel groups want to convince investors and customers that these are somehow something they aren’t. I mean, I’ve also had pleasant stays at AC Hotels properties, and that’s described as “a lifestyle brand that celebrates a new modern aesthetic complemented by a European soul and Spanish roots.” I mean… really?

Hotel groups love to grow and market their lifestyle hotel brands, but I feel like they take a lot of liberties with how they define these. On the most basic level, the idea is supposed to be that lifestyle hotels offer a boutique hotel-style experience, while being associated with one of the major brands.

However, I’d say more accurately, it’s about how much of a “destination” a property is, as that also determines the vibe of the place. I’d love if someone could help me understand just how hotel groups can in good conscience categorize some brands as being lifestyle hotels. Like, how does Marriott include AC Hotels, Autograph Collection, EDITION, and Moxy, all under that same umbrella?

How do you define the lifestyle hotel concept?
