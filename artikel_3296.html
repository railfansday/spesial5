Deal (Nearly) Done? UK Actors Union Equity Urges Members To Vote “Yes” On Final Offer From Pact Including Streamer Compensation, Pay Bumps & “Groundbreaking” AI Provisions

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

EXCLUSIVE: Nearly three years after they first sat down, British actors union Equity is finally taking a deal to its members from producer trade body Pact.

The new deal for actors on the majority of British shows and movies, starting January 1 2027 through December 31 2029, includes everything from pay to streamer compensation to self-tapes, but it is artificial intelligence that has delayed things time and time again.

That offer features a “groundbreaking agreement on protections for artists over their digital likeness alongside vital safeguards that prioritize human creativity in the industry,” Equity said.

After much tussling, Equity said the AI element of the Pact offer is “significantly improved” and is four-pronged, with protections for the creation and use of digital replicas, protections relating to digital alterations of your performance, protections against “Synthetics” and obligations around AI training licensing.

On digital replicas, an area of controversy that led the vast majority of Equity’s member base to say they would refuse to be digitally scanned on-set, actors will now “have the right to be paid when your digital replica is used” and “have the right to consent to whether a digital replica of you is used and the right to information about the specific use prior to granting consent.”

Furthermore, a producer “cannot obtain blanket consent to your digital replica” but only for specific projects. Use of a digital replica or digital alteration will also “require an actor’s approval if there are any substantive changes to scenes that are agreed as part of a nudity rider.”

Despite “our best efforts,” Equity noted it had failed to negotiate contractual protections for “digital replica deletion” or “digital scanning that is traditionally and/or customarily used in production for CGI, VFX and SFX and other digital technologies.”

Equity’s deal includes a 24% rise in pay rates for actors on movies budgeted over £50M ($66M), which will take effect across the next three years (17% year 1), a 13% (7% year 1) uplift for movies budgeted between £30M and £50M, and a 9% increase on TV shows (3% per year).

In the thorny area of streamer compensation, which dominated the 2023 U.S. labor strikes, Equity has negotiated a 3 percentage point uplift to “streaming platform use fees,” effectively when a show is given a second window on a streamer. This could equate to a real term rise of as much as 50%, Equity said. The period during which these apply has been reduced from 15 years to 10, meaning actors will get secondary payments more quickly.

There are also “world-leading provisions” in another knotty area, casting self-tapes, with Equity including a 72-hour notice period for producers submitting scripts for self-tapes.

And coming amid the Russell Brand and David Walliams scandals, the deal introduces bullying and harassment principles commiting producers to safe casting and audition spaces, with training and reporting on sexual harassment and other workplace safety issues. For the first time ever, there are also sustainability provisions included in contracts, adopting a Green Rider to help improve the environmental impact of film and TV.

Equity’s initial demands from way back in summer 2024 also centered on the end of special stipulations from the streamers. We understand these will likely be addressed in Equity’s upcoming negotiations with the American SVoDs once the Pact deal is finalized.

Members are being asked to vote on the deal for the first time and are being recommended to say “yes.”

Equity General Secretary Paul W Fleming labeled the agreement “groundbreaking.”

“We set about to radically improve and modernise the terms and conditions for performers across UK TV and film,” he added. “The result is groundbreaking. We’ve achieved the best AI provisions in the business – protecting performers and ensuring that human performance is valued and prioritised across the industry.”

Unlike in the U.S., there is no cliff edge for these deals once previous contracts run down but they are instead constantly updated and tinkered with. So the stalemate has not led to what would have been an unprecedented strike, although it has certainly been discussed by Equity. Equity was a firm backer of SAG’s cause during those headline-grabbing 2023 strikes.
