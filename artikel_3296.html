Mistral opens Asia Pacific headquarters in Singapore to build sovereign AI

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The French AI lab plans to double regional headcount to 200, building custom models for governments, manufacturing, and robotics.

The ribbon cutting at Mistral’s new Asia Pacific headquarters in Singapore

Mistral AI is expanding its presence in Asia Pacific, opening a regional headquarters in Singapore that will double as a research hub for developing AI models tailored to local industries.

The French AI company plans to build custom frontier models for three areas: public services, robotics, and high-tech manufacturing. It also wants to more than double its regional workforce, bringing the team to 200 people by 2027. New hires will work across research, applied AI, engineering, and robotics.

For Mistral, the Singapore office is about more than having a local presence. The company wants to give governments and businesses in the region greater control over the AI systems they use.

“Our ambition is to establish Singapore as a strategic location for empowering governments and enterprises across Asia Pacific to retain ownership of their infrastructure and intelligence,” said Arthur Mensch, Mistral’s co-founder and chief executive.

It’s an argument Mistral has made repeatedly in Europe, where concerns about dependence on American technology have helped fuel interest in sovereign AI. Its open-weight models can be adapted and deployed within an organisation’s own infrastructure, allowing customers to retain control over their data and how the systems operate.

Singapore is a natural place to take that proposition further, as Mistral already has several partnerships in the city-state, including work with HTX, the Home Team Science and Technology Agency responsible for developing technology for Singapore’s public safety agencies.

In May 2025, HTX signed an agreement with Mistral and Microsoft to develop AI models for public safety applications. A strategic partnership with HTX followed, alongside collaborations with Singtel, NCS, and ST Engineering.

The company says its projects with HTX and DSO National Laboratories, Singapore’s defence research organisation, were among its first efforts to fine-tune models for individual customers. The experience also helped inform the development of its broader product portfolio.

For HTX, the collaboration has brought AI development closer to the operational needs of public safety agencies. Its chief AI officer, Ang Chee Wee, said the partnership has allowed the organisation to ground its work in real-world operations.

Robotics is another area Mistral wants to develop from Singapore, as in July, the company released Robostral Navigate, an 8-billion-parameter model designed to help robots move through unfamiliar buildings using a single RGB camera. Mistral trained it entirely in simulation.

The new hub will build on that work, with research also covering physical AI and engineering simulations for manufacturing environments. These are areas where AI systems need to interact with the physical world, rather than process information or generate content.

Founded in Paris in 2023, Mistral has become one of Europe’s most heavily funded AI companies, attracting investors as governments and businesses look for alternatives to US technology providers. Chip equipment maker ASML led its €1.7 billion Series C in September 2025, valuing the company at approximately €11.7 billion.

Singapore’s Economic Development Board welcomed the expansion, saying it would strengthen the country’s position in frontier AI and create opportunities for local talent to work on advanced AI research and development.

For Mistral, the move also gives it a stronger base in a region where governments are investing in their own AI capabilities. It also gives its European sovereignty pitch another market in which to prove itself.
