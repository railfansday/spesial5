"I essentially wanna get rid of [best in slot gear (BiS)]. I think that BiS is not a good thing for a videogame that's supposed to be about roleplaying aspects of it—that's why I've introduced the creature subtype system (where things are more effective against [for example] murlocs), I've introduced a biome system."

"In the raid, it's different … In the raid, if you go to the left wing, that's snowy, if you go to the right wing, that's haunted, if you go down below, that's cavernous. Different parts of the raid will have different biomes. I think your BiS should be different every single boss if you're actually making a theoretical BiS list," via the official WoW Forever podcast.

I might be going against the grain here, but I'm honestly all for this—my one major hangup, the inventory management, is also swiftly addressed in the podcast, with principal game designer Kris Zierhut commenting that "we've thought about that a lot—it's not something we need right now, but we're going to give you bigger bags and better ways of organising and searching to find those things."

That's my worries solved, then! Moon also chimes in and notes that "Back in Season of Discovery, I think the biggest bags we could give you were 32 slots … now you can make 98-slot bags. I open the bag and my screen just [explodes]."

The major hullabaloo from player communities seems to be this idea that we're just doing resistance gear again. Back in Vanilla WoW, you'd have to farm a bunch of specific elemental resistance gear for specific raids, and it's something that nobody quite like that much.

The way I see this being different, however, is that Moon is very specifically talking about a "theoretical BiS list". In other words, this biome and creature-matching only has to be completely aligned if you want to be maximally sweaty and pump the highest numbers you possibly can.

It does not seem like this gear is mandatory, and I think the actual motivation behind it seems better. Trading off a ton of damage just to get crappy gear pieces so you can survive feels bad, but realising you have the perfect-shaped peg to put into a hole (stop giggling) actually sounds satisfying.

Not that there won't be problems, but I would genuinely rather WoW: Forever try and experiment properly with its gear systems, instead of following the same formula that the main game's been trundling out for the past 20+ years. I am, bare minimum, willing to wait and see how it pans out.
