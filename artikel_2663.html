Penn State notes: Tony Rojas denies he’s made a decision regarding his future

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

STATE COLLEGE – Tony Rojas stood outside the Penn State locker room for several minutes before he entered Saturday.

The Nittany Lions had just squandered a 16-point lead to prohibitive underdog Rutgers, which scored a touchdown and a 2-point conversion with no time left to win 25-24 at Beaver Stadium.

Rojas was asked what was going through his mind.

"A lot of emotions," he said. "Last game here. Just missing everything. Trying to soak it all in. Not coming out with the W."

Rojas said he hadn't decided whether he will declare for the NFL draft, but it sounded like he had already made up his mind.

If it was his final game at Beaver Stadium, he put in maximum effort. He made a game-high 15 tackles, including 2.5 stops in the backfield and 1.5 sacks, and broke up a pass.

"The game shouldn't have been left up in the air," Rojas said. "We put ourselves in that position. Obviously it hurts. It's the last one at Beaver. It's obviously (one of his worst losses)."

He was asked if he had played his final game.

"We still have another game," he said, referring to next week's regular season finale at Maryland.

Penn State began the game without running back Carson Hansen, cornerback Jahmir Joseph and safety Zion Tracy.

Hansen and Joseph were listed as out on the Big Ten pregame availability report, but Tracy was not listed at all. He suffered an unspecified injury before the game.

"Zion tweaked himself in pregame warmups," Lions coach Matt Campbell said. "I thought he was going to be able to go. When Zion went down, that was a big hit for us."

Tracy can play multiple positions in the secondary. He moved from cornerback to safety earlier this season because of Penn State's problems at that position.

"He's obviously very versatile," Rojas said about Tracy. "He's able to do a lot of things and play a lot of positions. Not having that playmaker in the back obviously sucks. But it's the next man up mentality."

Hansen, the Lions' top rusher, suffered an apparent ankle injury late in the second quarter of a 42-17 win over Minnesota a week earlier. Then James Peoples, who played behind starter Cam Wallace, sustained an injury to his left leg on a 15-yard gain in the first quarter. He did not return.

"We're down to a limited number of running backs," quarterback Rocco Becht said. "He definitely brings a spark to the offense, but we have guys in that room who have made plays in the past. There's total confidence in the other guys to make plays."

In addition, safety Jamison Patton left the game in the second quarter with an injury and did not return. He was listed as a game-time decision on the availability report. Wide receiver Brett Eskildsen hit the back of his head on the ground while trying to make a catch in the first quarter and did not return.

In addition, center Brock Riker and tight end Andrew Rappleyea were hurt and returned to the game.

Rutgers coach Greg Schiano worked at Penn State as an assistant coach from 1990-95, so leading the Scarlet Knights to their first win over the Nittany Lions since 1988 was momentous.

"That was something special," Schiano said. "Really proud of this team, their faith and their belief in what we do. It would have been easy to say this isn't our year and let's get to next year. The whole season hasn't always been pretty. I know the fans, the coaches, the players, everybody's frustrated.

"But it's a good lesson for our guys that this is what life is about. It doesn't always come when you want it, but you have to keep chopping, you have to keep fighting."

Rutgers had lost four straight, including a 31-0 decision to Nebraska last week at home.

"There were a lot of thoughts while driving here last night," Schiano said, "and a lot of special moments here, but I don't know if any are more special than this."
