The "carnival" came back to town on Thursday, and Joe Milton III won his "teddy bear"

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Fifty-two years after the Thanksgiving legend of Clint Longley was born, a guy who couldn’t convince the Cowboys to give him a spot on the 53-man roster pulled a Reverse Longley on America’s Team.

On Thursday, former Cowboys practice-squad quarterback Joe Milton III returned to Dallas and made them regret not putting him on the roster when they had the chance.

The Cowboys acquired Milton in April 2025, sending a fifth-round pick to New England for Milton and a seventh-round pick. He appeared in four games last season before losing the training-camp and preseason competition with Sam Howell to be the backup to Dak Prescott.

Milton was released in late August. He cleared waivers. He joined the Dallas practice squad. After Eagles quarterback Jalen Hurts landed on injured reserve due to blood clots, the Eagles came calling with a spot on the active roster. The Cowboys could have attempted to add Milton to their own active roster, but they didn’t.

Milton’s impressive performance has rekindled the comments made by Cowboys coach Brian Schottenheimer in the days before the 2026 rosters were cut from 90 to 53.

“I first remember hearing about Joe Milton he was at [the] University of Michigan, and everyone applauded him for how hard and how far he could throw the ball,” Schottenheimer said at the time, via Jane Slater of NFL Network. “Cool. That’s awesome. You know, go be in a carnival somewhere.  Like, that’s not what playing NFL quarterback is. It’s not, ‘Yeah, I can throw the ball 85 yards.’ You’re throwing through small windows, you’re throwing over people, you’re changing arm slots.  So, like, to me, arm strength is cool but, like, you know, go to the fair and win a teddy bear.”

In fairness to Schottenheimer, he then praised Milton’s development.

“But you got to play quarterback, you know, and that’s what I think you see Joe learning is he’s playing quarterback,” Schottenheimer said at the time. “And he’s playing quarterback at a high level.  He’s checking the ball down. He’s making blitz adjustments. He’s solving problems. He’s reading, you know, from his first, second, to third and even fourth progression at times. And it takes time. I mean, it, you know, you don’t stop. I mean, you don’t stop learning as a quarterback and the only way to do it is to play and I’m really, really proud of him.”

Still, the Cowboys weren’t sufficiently proud of Milton to give him a roster spot. And owner/G.M. Jerry Jones refused to admit that they made a mistake.

“That is the best I’ve seen him play,” Jones told reporters after the 39-38 loss. “And he picked a bad time to do it, from my standpoint.”

From Milton’s standpoint, he picked the ideal time to do it. He’ll now become a factor in the Eagles’ future plans at quarterback. How could he not be after what he accomplished on Thursday, engineering a comeback in a game that saw the Eagles down by 21 points late in the third quarter?

Still, it’s just getting started for Milton. He needs to build on what he did. If he does, he’ll become a long-time NFL starter. If he doesn’t, he’ll become another Clint Longley — whose Thanksgiving Day performance in 1974 wasn’t the beginning but the end.
