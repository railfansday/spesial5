The Good, The Bad and The Ugly: LSU vs. Texas

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Another home loss in November, and the chances of securing a spot in the College Football Playoff are now gone.

Here is some of the good, bad, and ugly that came from the LSU game against Texas on Saturday night.

This honestly could have been the great. The defense showed up, kept this team in the game, and held a lead until the final two seconds of the game. The defense finished the game with 4 sacks, 8 TFLs, 6 pass deflections, and 3 INTs. In a game where your QB could not help but give the ball to the other team with 3 INTs of his own, the defense prevented Texas from scoring off any of those turnovers.

This game had the opportunity to be a blowout for LSU because of how great the defense was playing. LSU had an eight-point lead late into the third quarter and held the lead for nearly the entire fourth quarter. Blake Baker utilized the defense to its strengths and had another game in which his unit held a top-10 team's offense to under 280 yards of total offense. The defense also held the QB to just 35 rushing yards, which tied him for the team lead in rushing for Texas. The defense did everything possible to get LSU a win.

LSU's special teams have been a phase of the team that has not been highlighted enough throughout the season. Kicker Scott Starzyck missed a kick for the second week in a row. The missed kick last week gave Alabama a short field and allowed them to score on the drive following that miss. This week, you lose a game by one point after he misses a kick from inside 40 yards.

On top of that, Grant Chadwick has not been good either. Four bad punts on four consecutive drives gave Texas short fields and multiple opportunities to take the lead and ultimately win the game. In order to win football games, you have to be good in all three phases. LSU's special teams unit has been anything but special.

The offense, specifically the QB and the coaching. This game saw Dilin Jones run for 125 yards and average 6.6 yards per carry, Tre Wilson have 7 receptions for 96 yards, Jackson Harris have 8 receptions for 64 yards and 2 TDs, and Sam Leavitt have another game in which he completed over 70% of his passes.

If you look at the stats, you ask yourself: How did LSU not blow this team out? Well, three horrible interceptions, two of them coming on passes into double coverage that should have never been thrown, and two of the three coming in the red zone, costing the Tigers valuable opportunities to score much-needed points.

These plays were not the result of the defense making great plays; they were simply the result of Sam Leavitt making poor decisions and putting the ball in the hands of the defense. There were several other passes the defense could have intercepted as well, but fortunately, the defenders were not able to complete the catches.

The inability to play consistently at QB forced the offensive play-calling to shrink at the most critical moments in the game. Then the coaching issues started coming back, as the play-calling just got sideways.

This team should have been better than 6-4, but constant turnovers, constant red-zone issues, and constant poor coaching decisions have caused this team, which has the talent to be undefeated, to be facing down the likelihood of finishing 7-5 or, even worse, 6-6.

Not too much can change at this point in the season, and this team is who they are.
