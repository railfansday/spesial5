The Luxury Business of Las Vegas F1 Is Booming

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

If Formula 1 circuits were Mario Kart race tracks, the Las Vegas Grand Prix is as spiritually close to Rainbow Road as it gets.

This year, newly hybrid-engined F1 cars zoomed along an LED-lit Vegas Strip that had been closed off to civilian traffic each night of the event, much to the chagrin of every ride-share driver and/or tourist trying to cross Las Vegas Boulevard. They whipped past the Paris hotel casino’s glittering facsimile of the Eiffel Tower and the cartoonishly massive Sphere. They skirted under banners advertising Louis Vuitton and Qatar Airways. At the end, winner Max Verstappen was driven in a racing-striped stretch limousine, operated by a dashingly bedraggled Keanu Reeves, to the Fountains of Bellagio—inspired, like so many Vegas landmarks, by the architectural grandeur of Europe.

Max Verstappen, shortly after his win on Saturday night.

Motorsports enthusiast Keanu Reeves, center, drove the winners in a limo.

Since the Vegas GP was first revived in 2023, it has been a polarizing stop on the globe-trotting F1 calendar. (Back then, Verstappen called it “99 percent show and 1 percent sporting event.”) Even for a multibillion-dollar sport, was this too much glitz? Surrounded by sandy desert and snow-capped mountains, the gaudiest track in Formula 1 is nevertheless a sight to behold—and this year, it produced one of the most thrilling races in recent memory. Plus, as far as metropolises go, there are certainly more boring places to spend a few days than Sin City.

The winners’ podium: Red Bull’s Verstappen in first, followed by McLaren pair Lando Norris, left, and Oscar Piastri.

Well, unless you’re Kimi Antonelli, F1’s baby-faced world champ.

“The thing I like the most here is the track, because I’m not allowed to do anything else other than drive around,” Antonelli told me, with a smirk, ahead of the race last week. “Because I’m not 21.”

(The Italian, who turned 20 in August, became Formula 1’s youngest-ever world champion at the Mexico Grand Prix earlier this month. In Vegas, he and his Mercedes teammate George Russell just missed the podium, finishing fourth and ninth respectively after more than a few late-race heartbreaks.)

Mercedes drivers Kimi Antonelli and George Russell.

But if you’re too old to wait in the line outside Marquee, there were plenty of other desirable libations afoot. Vegas has always been a place where fine cuisine (or at least, high-roller cuisine) means bumping up prices by dumping truffles and caviar on everything. The OG greats of modern dining—the Jean-Georges Vongerichtens and Wolfgang Pucks—have been here since the ’90s. But since COVID, a new generation of big-brass restaurateurs have set up shop on the Strip: Carbone Riviera and the Corner Store at the Bellagio’s properties; Sartiano’s and Delilah at the Wynn; Mother Wolf and Contramar at the Fontainebleau. (There are elite members’ clubs, too: Zero Bond opened at the Wynn in March.)

Brunch at Sadelle's, a popular Major Food Group spot, inside the Bellagio.

During Friday’s qualifying run, former F1 star Daniel Ricciardo stopped by the Corner Store at the Cosmopolitan, a wood-paneled outpost of the New York hotspot that opened a few weeks earlier. In the moody, multi-room space, the kitchen sends out platters of dry-aged wagyu and pepperoni pizza rolls while bartenders shake up sour-cream-and-onion martinis served with potato-chip sidecars. (The cocktail is purportedly Taylor Swift’s favorite, though executive chef Michael Vignola will neither confirm nor deny this: “I don’t know what you’re talking about,” said Vignola, grinning. “You can also ask Travis.”)

A day earlier, Ricciardo—who retired from F1 last year but came to town for a self-branded clothing pop-up and bar at the Bellagio—reflected on his own experience at the first-reprised Vegas GP in 2023. “It feels like a video game,” he said. “Whether the track is your favorite or not your favorite in terms of the actual layout, it’s still pretty surreal to be here and be the main event in a city that revolves around entertainment.”

According to Vignola, Ricciardo is a big fan of the Corner Store’s steak program as well as the chicken paillard, “a sleeper on the menu and a great mid-course this year.” Manning the kitchen at the Corner Store in Vegas, which has 100 more seats than the original NYC location, the chef said they’re selling about as much caviar here as they do in Manhattan.

“Because of the volume here, we are slightly selling a little bit more caviar, slightly, you know, maybe 2 to 1,” Vignola said with a laugh. Lately, they’ve started R&D’ing some Norwegian king crab legs, including a prototype for a “crab appletini.” They’re leaning into menu items that the New York-born chef described as “a little bit more Vegas, flashy kind of bespoke things. There are some high-roller dishes that pop off.”

An Elvis impersonator at F1's Paddock Club.

The likes of Vignola and Mario Carbone, as well as the actual Wolfgang Puck and Jean-Georges, hosted caviar-filled pop-up stands at the Bellagio Fountain Club suites during the F1 festivities. The starriest celebrities, including Reeves and Kim Kardashian, who’s dating driver Lewis Hamilton, watched the big race from the paddock, with a good view of the Elvis impersonator and humanoid robots who roamed the asphalt.

Over my few days in Vegas, multiple locals—including chefs, service and casino employees, and Uber and Lyft drivers—described how the city’s changed over the last 15 years. On the ride from the airport, my driver pointed to a new landmark on the horizon: a massive mirrored edifice shaped like the body of a guitar (the new Hard Rock Las Vegas hotel) that’s in the midst of being erected from the sand.

“I think Vegas and Formula 1, they were meant for each other,” said José Andrés, the Spanish-born chef who opened his first Las Vegas restaurants inside the then-freshly built Cosmopolitan in 2010. “I came here three weeks before the casino opened. And just to watch a casino open is the closest thing to watching a little egg hatch, or my daughter [being] born. For me to watch an entire casino from nothing to becoming a tower, it brings good memories.”

One can only imagine how we’ll look back on the Las Vegas Grand Prix—only in its fourth year—a decade or two from now, when all the cars are self-driving and the Strip has been converted into an actual rainbow speedway floating in deep space. Will there still be Champagne?
