Xbox Project Helix Was Reportedly Designed To Make Online Multiplayer Free

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

For years, most multiplayer games on Xbox consoles required a subscription to go online. From Xbox Live to Xbox Game Pass, Microsoft paywalled multiplayer on its platforms, but the company reportedly aimed to break down that barrier for its next-gen console, Project Helix.

According to Windows Central, Project Helix was conceived under the previous Xbox leadership team as the device that would bridge the divide between PC and console gaming. Like the Asus Xbox ROG Ally PC handhelds, the console is said to have an Xbox-inspired interface and full PC game support through storefronts like Steam. Because of that design decision, it's claimed it didn't make sense to continue with paid multiplayer, since that service is free for games purchased on PC platforms like Steam and the Epic Games Store.

As the Windows Central report noted, that was reportedly the plan before Xbox leadership was shaken up and gaming hardware prices rose during the AI boom. With Asha Sharma now in charge of Xbox, the gaming division is more focused on profitability, and Microsoft's gaming arm has seen sweeping changes.

Mass layoffs and studio divestitures occurred throughout 2026, and Project Helix could be a very expensive family of devices. If Microsoft ditches paid multiplayer, it loses a revenue stream for its next-gen push at a time when component prices have risen steeply.

As part of its 25th anniversary celebrations, Microsoft also teased a few more details about Project Helix, calling it specifically "a console" that plays games from your PC, not "a PC that can also play console games."

"When we think about Project Helix, it’s really designed to be the ultimate gaming machine--one that removes all the barriers of play that have historically existed," VP of next-gen Jason Ronald said.
