Man admits to cheating on job exam after getting top score and sparking national investigation

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

A man in South Korea has apologized after admitting to cheating to get the top score on his certified tax accountancy exam, all because he wanted to make his parents proud again.

Over the years, plenty of people have found ways to cheat on their exams and get away with it. However, in the case of Han Won-seok, he has become the target of a viral investigation into South Korea’s certified tax accountancy exam.

The 34-year-old, from Seoul, came out as the top scorer in the test’s first round, only getting one of the 160 questions wrong. He scored 397.5 out of a possible 400 points, raising suspicions about the result.

After some backlash, Won-seok joined his lawyer, Lee Don-Ho, on his YouTube channel, admitting to the cheating after using a mini-camera and generative AI.

“I cheated in both the first and second rounds of the tax account exam by using electronic devices and generative AI,” Won-seok said.

“I used a microcamera to photograph the test questions and sent them to an AI then checked my answers on my smartphone to commit the cheating.”

The 34-year-old apologised to other exam takers who have now come under investigation because of him. He also added that he wanted a high score in order to make his parents proud of him again by getting a well-paid job.

As per Yonhap in Korea, Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency have also placed a number of it’s investiatiors onto the case after receiving a “written confession” from Won-seok and his lawyer about the incident.

The 34-year-old’s certificate from the exam has been revoked, and he may face criminal charges over it. It wouldn’t be the first time either as, back in July, South Korea filed its first criminal case over the use of AI-powered smart glasses as a man was caught cheating.

The rise in popularity of smart glasses has given students additional power in cheating on exams. Over in China, services are being run so that test takers can rent smart glasses solely for their exams.

However, in the United States, one university professor is now refusing to grade work completed by students due to the fear of AI assistance. “Grading for correctness just adds a huge incentive for them to use AI for homework instead of doing it themselves, and penalizes students for honesty,” professor Richard Borcherds said.
