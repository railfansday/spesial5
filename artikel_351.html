Nubia Redmagic 12 Pro Review: Furiously Fast but Flawed

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Mobile gamers don’t have many dedicated gaming smartphones to choose from, so Nubia’s Redmagic series stands out. This big, boxy phone offers cutting-edge performance, long battery life, and a large display. You also get touch-sensitive shoulder buttons, RGB lighting, and gaming-focused software features.

Sadly, Nubia has followed every other phone manufacturer this year, offering minimal iterative improvements while jacking up the price significantly. While this series has always been a clear bargain for gamers seeking the best chipset, it’s harder to swallow the compromises lurking here (camera, software, update commitment) when the price starts at $949.

The Nubia Redmagic 12 Pro sports the same boxy design as its predecessors, just a teeny bit slimmer and lighter. I’m a fan of the translucent industrial aesthetic and RGB lighting, but it’s not something I really want in my everyday phone. I had exactly the same issues with the 12 Pro as I had with the 10 Pro I reviewed two years ago. It’s very big, so you really feel it in your pocket, and it can be awkward to handle, especially when you’re trying to scroll one-handed. It’s also the most slippery phone ever. Every time I put it down, the 12 Pro slowly starts to slide away.

The big positive of this design is that it feels great in landscape orientation for gaming. The thick frame is easy to hold. There are touch-sensitive shoulder buttons on the top edge, and Nubia has a dedicated Magic Key for gaming mode, though you can customize it to launch the camera. This used to be a switch, but is now a red button. It’s still flanked by the round power button. Sadly, there’s no headphone port.

It’s refreshing not to have a camera bump on the back, though this inevitably adds to the slipperiness, and this glass sandwich quickly gets covered in finger smudges. Nubia has toned down the lighting a little, but you can still customize it for all sorts of activities. There’s a powerful cooling fan inside, with two vents to expel hot air, but the 12 Pro still somehow scores an IP68/69 rating for dust and water resistance.

The 6.85-inch OLED display is completely uninterrupted, with no notch or cutout camera to break it up. There’s an effective fingerprint sensor at the bottom and a less effective selfie camera under the top of the screen. The screen can hit a 185-Hz refresh rate, but only a handful of games actually support it. Most of the time you’ll be capped at 120 Hz. It supports HDR with 5,000-nit local peak brightness for highlights, but overall brightness is capped at 1,900 nits. Overall, it’s a lovely display, and a pleasure to play games or watch movies on. The Dolby Atmos–certified stereo speakers are decent for a phone.

Perhaps the biggest enticement is Qualcomm’s Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6 chipset, paired with Redmagic’s RedCore R5 chipset and 12 or 16 GB of RAM. You won’t find a faster Android phone right now, and the Redmagic 12 Pro can even give the iPhone 18 Pro a run for its money. Benchmark scores topped the Android charts, above phones like Samsung’s Galaxy S26 Ultra.

I had no issues playing Asphalt Legends and Wuthering Waves with settings cranked to the max. I also tried PUBG Mobile, one of the titles that can support that 185-Hz refresh rate, and it was smooth sailing. The 12 Pro keeps its cool, even after an hour or so of challenging gaming. Aside from the fan and vapor chamber for cooling, Nubia added dual-loop liquid cooling. You do get some fan noise and thermal throttling, but the 12 Pro has enough grunt to satisfy even the most hardcore of gamers.

Nubia includes a dedicated game space called Gravity X, triggered by that red button, where you can map those pressure-sensitive shoulder buttons and optimize the settings for your favorite games. It’s packed with options and even includes an anime companion and AI assistant called Mora. Even more exciting, it has an embedded Steam emulator, so you can log into your account and potentially play Steam games on the 12 Pro, though not everything works. It’s also easy to connect a gamepad, mouse, and keyboard, or a second screen.

To keep you gaming for longer, the 12 Pro boasts an enormous 8,400-mAh battery. Even with hours of gaming, I made it to the end of every day with something left in the tank. With careful use, you could easily push this phone to two days. There’s no charger in the box, but it supports up to 80-watt wired or wireless charging if you spring for a Redmagic charger. It took me an hour and 17 minutes to charge it from dead, but you can hit around 50 percent in half an hour. Sadly, it doesn’t support Qi2 magnets, so you’ll need a flat wireless charging pad.

To get top-notch performance at this price, you’ll have to accept several compromises. Let’s start with the camera. While the main 50-megapixel camera is quite good, and there’s a 50-megapixel ultrawide, the flat design doesn’t leave room for a telephoto lens, so you can only get 2X zoom by cropping. The main camera will satisfy folks who only snap the odd photo, and it does well in most conditions. The ultrawide is fine for group shots, but its smaller sensor means performance suffers in low light. The 16-megapixel front-facing camera is hidden under the screen, and it’s fine for video calls or well-lit selfies, though shots often look a bit muddy.

The real problem with the 12 Pro’s camera is the processing. Use the terrible portrait mode or zoom in too far and you’ll see how badly it handles image processing. Nubia lacks Google’s expertise in this space, so a camera system that sounds similar on paper produces far worse photos in reality. Nubia’s colors often look a bit off, it really struggles with mixed lighting, and shots tend to be noisy or oversharpened. Video recording is quite good with the main camera, but stabilization was a little poor in some of the videos I shot.

Another big turnoff with the 12 Pro is the software. Redmagic OS 12 sits atop Android 17, and it’s mostly OK, though I wish Chinese manufacturers didn’t feel the need to imitate things like Apple’s Liquid Glass. The interface also has some bad translations. What’s worse is the prevalence of absolute junk bloatware and the subpar update commitment. Nubia is only promising two years of platform updates and three years of security software updates in the US (five years in the UK).

Our best Android phones guide includes many more well-rounded options than the 12 Pro. You can even buy a Pixel 11 Pro XL on sale for the same price right now. But the 12 Pro is all about gaming, and to that end, it offers the best performance-and-screen combo you can get today. Whether the gaming features, cooling tech, and overall style add enough value depends on you. That same chipset will roll into more phones soon, and most of them will have a better camera and longer software support, but they’re likely to be more expensive, like the forthcoming Motorola Signature 27 at $1,600.

For buyers in the US, check your carrier bands. The 12 Pro should be fine with T-Mobile and its associated MVNOs, but it may not work well, or at all, with some other carriers. It also doesn’t support eSIM, so you need a physical SIM card. The 12-GB RAM and 256-GB storage model is $949; upgrading to 16 GB and 512 GB costs $1,049, and the 1-TB model is $1,199.
