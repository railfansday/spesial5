This Creative Couple Hosted a New Orleans Wedding Complete With a Crawfish Boil and a Masquerade After-Party

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Lily Monbouquette’s first-ever date on Raya was also her last. “The algorithm really worked overtime for me,” the jewelry brand co-founder says. Creative strategist and Good Boy Wine co-founder Eric Bach invited her out for a picnic at Echo Park Lake with his dog, Ty, in June 2022, and the pair hit it off. “Eric asked me a lot of questions, which was honestly a revelation; men, take note,” says Lily. “Eventually, I got to flip the script and hear about his travels, his wins, his losses, and I remember thinking, ‘This is a very special person.’” Lunch led to dinner, and the two had a feeling there were more dates to come. Says Eric, “Pretty early on, I could tell there was something incredibly solid about her, and being with her just made me feel more like myself.”

Their relationship bloomed over the next few years, and on February 24, 2025, Eric popped the question. It was during Frieze in Los Angeles, and Eric told Lily there was a special event at the Wolford House, which was transformed into a gallery for the week. “On the drive over, I told Eric about a conversation I’d had that day with my friend and mentor Bobby Hundreds, who asked about my boyfriend. I’d said, ‘It’s funny hearing you say ‘boyfriend,’ because Eric is so much more than that. He’s my person, for life, I hope.’ I had no idea how perfectly timed that was.”

When they arrived at golden hour, the house was completely empty. “Then, I looked at Eric, and he was tearing up. He couldn’t get a word out,” remembers Lily. “He pulled out a box, dropped to one knee, and opened it to reveal his mother’s diamond ring and necklace. He told me he wanted to spend the rest of his life with me, and I said, ‘Yes! Put it on me!’” His mother had also given her blessing for Lily to take the stones and redesign them into something of her own. “I get to wear something charged with her love and legacy every day,” the jewelry designer says.

The bride and groom decided to host their wedding over the weekend of April 3, 2026, in New Orleans. “Eric’s family has deep roots there—four generations—and it was the first trip we ever took together,” says Lily of their destination choice. “We wanted to share the best the city has to offer with our guests.” The festivities would kick off with a crawfish boil welcome party at Industrial Garden, their wedding ceremony in the Sydney and Walda Besthoff Sculpture Garden, a candlelit reception in the church of Hotel Peter and Paul, a second-line parade, and an after-party at DBA on Frenchmen Street. “We wanted people to experience the city through its food, music, art, rituals, and sense of chaos, but filtered through us and the things we love,” says Eric. “Every part of it was meant to engage your senses and keep changing shape as the weekend went on. And above all, we wanted it to be insanely fun.”

The couple approached wedding planning like any other creative project: “Story arc first, details second,” Lily explains. “With Eric’s long-time background in experiential, we wanted to spearhead the planning ourselves, with Michelle Norwood and Sarah Schreiber consulting along the way.” As collectors of contemporary art, getting married in a sculpture garden felt like the perfect complement to their relationship. “When we found out the garden had Jean-Michel Othoniel’s L’Arbre aux Colliers—an oak hung with strands of his glass necklaces—we knew exactly where we’d be standing,” says Lily. “A jeweler getting married under a tree of necklaces… I couldn’t have designed that better if I’d tried.” Guests were also encouraged to have fun with their wardrobes. “We kept it loose and wanted our friends to have the freedom to express themselves: jewel tones with a New Orleans, formal flair,” says Eric.

Other intentional details were folded in throughout the weekend. “Instead of a traditional invitation, we wrote our guests a poem, framed in a hand-painted border by our dear friend, the illustrator Sarah Kersting, and inspired by vintage New Orleans carnival ephemera,” says the bride. The bride’s mother calligraphed all the invitations and even hand-made masquerade masks for every guest. The couple also wanted to highlight their own creative projects within the celebration. Eric made a custom Good Boy wine to be served just for the weekend, and Lily debuted Monbouquette’s bridal collection across all three days.

The bride’s redesigned engagement ring actually served as the inspiration for her wedding dress. On the ring, her mother-in-law’s diamond is surrounded by a spinning ballerina halo of custom-cut emerald and diamond baguettes. “Like the twirl of a tutu!” Lily says. While shopping at Loho Bride with owner Christy Baird, she fell for a dress from Wed Studio with a similar ethos of shape and movement. “The tiered gazar skirt with its ruffled edge is pure ballerina, like a Degas painting, and a whisper-thin tulle overlay drapes the entire dress, but the V-shaped boning and sheer side panels of the corset give it an edge,” she describes. “Christy worked closely with my mother and I to add a few customized touches to my entire look.”

The bride curated the rest of her wardrobe to mirror the essence of her ceremony gown, which she describes as “playful, a little edgy, feminine, and timeless.” For the welcome party, she donned a Wiederhoeft slip dress beaded like a tattooed retelling of the Eurydice myth. “It’s genuinely a work of art,” Lily says. She also wore a beaded mini skirt and corset from the designer for the after-party. “I plan to wear both for the rest of my life,” the bride adds. As for beauty, Lily had makeup artist Nicole Costanza do a look inspired by the work of Katie Jane Hughes.

A longtime fan of the brand Bode, Eric chose to create a custom suit with the designer. “I wanted something a little unexpected from them while leaning into what makes them great: vintage nods, very current tailoring,” he says. The look included a cream jacket with a black lapel, which was layered over a black shirt and wide-leg pants. “I loved how the black shirt underneath almost seamlessly blended into the lapel and the unstructured satin bow tie, which read more like a neck scarf,” the groom says. “And then, of course, there was the vase brooch Lily made for me, which made it feel completely personal.”

The weekend kicked off in classic New Orleans style with a crawfish boil. “Most of our LA friends had never been to a boil, and there’s no better introduction to the city: the Willie Green Project—Bacchanal’s house band—playing live, bibs for everyone, and a king cake that crowned the king and queen of the wedding weekend,” says the groom. “It immediately set the tone for everything that followed.”

The wedding day arrived and the bride and groom connected on the grounds of the Besthoff Sculpture Garden for their first look. While a stormy forecast threatened the proceedings, the sun broke through just as Lily was about to head down the aisle. “Eric’s mother passed away earlier this year, and we all felt her shining down on us at that moment,” she says. The couple’s friend Chris Swanson officiated the ceremony. “He put so much thought into what he wrote, and he delivered it with such grace,” says Lily. “Having someone who truly knows us standing up there is what made the ceremony feel like ours.”

Eric describes the ceremony as surreal. “We were surrounded by incredible sculpture, beneath these beautiful draping mossy oaks, with all the people we love around us. Lily looked absolutely stunning,” he says. “Nothing had ever felt so right.” The couple also recited their own vows. “Just to add, there really wasn’t a dry eye in sight,” says the groom.

After the newlyweds said “I do,” cocktail hour was held throughout the sculpture garden so guests could experience the collection. “We did a champagne toast, Eric sabered the bottle with his uncle’s Mardi Gras sword, and then led everyone along a path through the grounds so they could wander amongst the art and the gardens,” says the bride. “An alligator slid through the pond right as we were walking along the floating boardwalk. So rare and so New Orleans!”

The party then moved back to Hotel Peter and Paul, where the old church was lit by close to 1,000 candles. “There was classic New Orleans cuisine, there were epic speeches, there was a performance by the whistler Molly Lewis, and then I gave everyone a surprise: I sang ‘La Vie en Rose’ in the church,” says Lily. Most guests were unaware the bride could sing and were floored by the performance. “I knew she could sing, but damn, I had no idea how emotional it would make me,” reflects Eric. “Watching her up there was pretty unreal.”

As dinner was complete, guests put on their hand-made masks and made their way through the city in a second line. “Instead of circling the block, we paraded all the way down Frenchmen Street,” says Lily. “It was the most exhilarating thing: people came out onto the balconies of the jazz bars to watch us, and strangers joined in dancing in the street. We ended at DBA, where our dear friend DSD DJ-ed.” Even the groom popped on the decks for a set during the night. Adds the bride, “He absolutely crushed it!”

The next day, guests enjoyed beignets from Café du Monde and a pool party at The Country Club, a pool, bar, and garden in the Bywater neighborhood. The groom says this was secretly one of his favorite parts. “Everyone was a little destroyed from the night before and completely relaxed,” he says. “And the drag queens were next level! It felt like the perfect comedown from the weekend.”

Looking back, the couple say they have yet to get the wedding blues as they continue to celebrate. Says Eric, “It feels like the wedding was less of an ending and more like the beginning of this really wild, exciting new chapter.” Adds Lily, “I’m too lucky to be married to my soulmate, and I wouldn’t change a thing.”
