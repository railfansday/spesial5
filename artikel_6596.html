Cider Braised Pork Shoulder

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Pork and apples have been partners in crime since the beginning of time. Or at least, since the beginning of recorded recipe history. There’s something magical about how an apple’s natural sweetness and tartness can cut through a fatty, savory piece of pork, especially on a chilly fall day. Braising is by far one of my favorite fall cooking techniques, so after trying Food Editor Makinze’s apple cider braised brisket, I knew it was time to put my own spin on it with the apple’s natural bestie—pork shoulder.

The braise:Just like any good braise, this one starts with a quick sear in a Dutch oven to develop color and flavor on the exterior of the meat and activate the heat in the cayenne-pepper-dusted surface. Then, sliced red onions are seared in the pork fat along with garlic and my secret ingredient: fresh chopped ginger. The ginger brings a floral heat that complements both pork and the apples without overpowering the braising liquid. Once the onions cook down a little bit, the pork returns to the pot with apple cider and braises in a hot oven with fresh Granny Smith apple slices until it's practically falling apart.

How to serve:Shred the pork and serve it with cheesy grits, roasted potatoes, or spiced rice pilaf to make your new favorite, shareable fall dinner.

Make ahead & storage:Pork can be made 3 days ahead. Store in an airtight container and refrigerate.
