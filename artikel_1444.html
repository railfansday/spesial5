Arsenal teach Chelsea a lesson in how to be champions

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

This is how champions are supposed to play and, although the Arsenal home support have not seen a team defend their place at the apex of English league football for two decades, they knew it when they saw it.

The chase was on for Mikel Arteta’s team within two minutes when Morgan Rogers, the man who might have moved to Arsenal this summer, put his new club Chelsea in the lead. So unfolded a thrilling 100 minutes of Premier League competition. Arsenal were the better team in terms of ambition and attack even if one could not discount Chelsea until the very last moment.

Others might have crumbled. That Chelsea did not will give Xabi Alonso hope for a team that did not feature the injured Moisés Caicedo and is now without the departed Enzo Fernández. Yet he will also recognise that his childhood friend from San Sebastián in the other dugout has created a side that treated the game, and their place in it, with an urgency that looks different. The first two minutes might have been ragged for Arsenal but after that they did not let the game happen to them.

Morgan Rogers fires Chelsea in front inside TWO minutes! ⚡️ pic.twitter.com/ca8cqnT2Wm

Instead, Arteta’s players drove this contest, and the sequence of chances that came and went was dizzying at times. Their best players scored the two goals. First Kai Havertz, who now has three in four games this season, and then the captain Martin Ødegaard who dispatched what would be the winner.

"A spear through the heart of Chelsea!" Martin Odegaard puts Arsenal in front at the Emirates 🎯 pic.twitter.com/5deEQykjck

Ødegaard looked like the veteran of a title-winning season, and a deep run in the World Cup – an accomplished, shrewd performer in a midfield that was fought over ferociously all afternoon.

As for the game’s defining moment – it would be hard to overlook the feint from Havertz for the winning goal. It deceived the Chelsea players around him and created the channel for Christos Tzolis’s cross to reach Ødegaard. His goal was a close copy, in style and personnel, of the one he scored against Manchester City in the Community Shield but it was crowned by Havertz’s bluff. He shaped to take the ball and let it run under his foot at the last moment.

This was Chelsea’s first defeat in three – leaving only Arsenal and City with nine points from their first three games – although the seven goals conceded is the standout. For all the jet fuel in their attack they keep conceding. This was a much more exacting test than Fulham and Brighton previously, but even so. Alonso has introduced his preferred back three, often a back five, and it is a work in progress.

Arsenal have set the standard in that regard and they were uncompromising again. They did not replicate the errors that led to the Rogers goal and they pushed the limits at all times. Gabriel Magalhães kicked Emi Martínez in the head competing for a ball in the Chelsea area. Myles Lewis-Skelly went after João Pedro. Ben White and Roméo Lavia saw things differently in the first half. This Arsenal team does not shy away from those confrontations in the way their predecessors often did.

In fact, it was notable just how furiously Rogers reacted to Declan Rice when the two England team-mates converged as the Chelsea man charged off to join a late counter-attack. The bad feeling lingered even after the game when Rogers seemed unwilling to forgive. Rice had applied the minimum contact and the maximum cynicism to stop Rogers while the attention of referee Chris Kavanagh was elsewhere.

"He's FUMING Morgan Rogers!" 😳 pic.twitter.com/FciivEedvh

All that ringcraft was one thing, but it should be said that Arsenal were excellent in setting an attacking tempo that will be hell for any team to live with – especially at the Emirates. Havertz is now Arteta’s centre-forward of choice in the absence of a big summer acquisition. Viktor Gyökeres made a late substitute’s appearance – his first minutes of the season. The hierarchy is clear: last season’s great goalscoring hope is now back-up.

“Exceptional” was how Arteta described his team’s response to the early Rogers goal. They had twice failed to clear the ball in their own area when first Maxence Lacroix and then Jorrel Hato created the chance. Rogers’s right foot half-volley was not struck on the full, but it was placed perfectly inside the post with the necessary bounce and skip to mean David Raya never got behind it.

Arteta’s squad runs deep enough that in the closing stages he could relieve his midfield with two Spanish World Cup-winning substitutes – Martín Zubimendi and Mikel Merino. Then there was Noni Madueke on for Bukayo Saka, understudy and first-choice for club and country. This was not Saka’s day in the way it has been in the past, although a very promising shot of his was stopped by Martinez in the first half.

In the pounding that Chelsea took after they went into the lead, Riccardo Calafiori had an equaliser within minutes that was disallowed by VAR on an offside call. The left-back was the third in sequence to try to force the ball over the line from a Rice free-kick. Calafiori was successful, but the second of his team-mates to try, Ezri Konsa, had been offside when he connected.

For the equaliser it was Havertz, accelerating cautiously at first and then full-on with his left foot from the right-side. Ødegaard was well-placed to prevent Lacroix getting close but in truth the challenge never had the force required. Havertz’s shot was not totally convincing but then neither was Martinez’s attempt to save it – every bit as uncertain as Raya for the Chelsea goal.

A precise finish from Kai Havertz and Arsenal are level! 🔴 pic.twitter.com/DqL1MEX4zN

Chelsea had profited from the quick ball out to their right where Calafiori was often out of position in the left-back slot and Pedro Neto could cut in on his left foot. That seemed to be the sacrifice that Arteta was prepared to make in keeping the hammer on Chelsea. If Neto had not struck the post in one such escape in time added on at the end of the first half it might have been different.

Ødegaard struck what would be the winner early in the first half, and although Chelsea fizzed enough in the later stages it always felt as though it was Arsenal’s game. Raya came into his own in the 89th minute when Estêvão, a substitute, cut in from the right and hit a fierce shot that the goalkeeper pushed over. It was close in some ways – and in others not at all.

“It hurts. It hurts because a defeat has to hurt. It has been a very intense game with different moments for everything. We had chances to equalise. In the first half as well a couple of chances. Never happy if you lose.

“After we scored, there were 15-20 minutes where we had to defend too much and we were struggling a bit to get control. We then had better spells with the ball. Overall, it was a very tight, very intense game. We could do better with the second goal to conceded too early. The guys gave everything, tried everything. It has to hurt.

“It is about preparing the best possible way to compete the best possible way. You need to control the overloads on the right side. It is part of the game to have these actions. We continue. It is game three and this is going to be a long, long ride.”

It is a relationship that Arteta has previously described as “deeper than just a friendship”. Mikel Arteta insists Arsenal are “better equipped” for success this season, despite falling short in their attempts to add a top-class attacker. Arsenal have allowed four forward players to leave this summer and brought in one, Greece winger Christos Tzolis.

The departures of Gabriel Martinelli, Leandro Trossard, Gabriel Jesus and Ethan Nwaneri (on loan) have raised concerns among supporters that the champions lack firepower. Moves for Morgan Rogers, Vinicius Junior and Julian Alvarez did not come to fruition. Arteta, though, feels Arsenal are stronger now than at the end of last season following a summer in which they added midfielder Bruno Guimarães and defender Ezri Konsa.

“We are better equipped,” said Arteta, who faces Rogers and close friend Xabi Alonso. “We have more players available than the start of last season. We have increased the quality and we have increased the leadership, which is very important.”

Asked specifically about the attack, he added: “Yeah, but that’s another question. We have to prove in that particular position whether we are better or not. Let’s see. We need to play. I don’t like to make assumptions.

“It’s not just the responsibility of those players, it’s the team and the dynamic. We have multiple options to play there with different profiles. It’s human nature. When you talk to people and you ask: ‘Ok, what is on your bucket list?’ Sometimes we forget the reverse bucket list, which are the things you already have that are incredible. It’s very important to value what we have, what we have created together. The ambition is great, but [don’t] lose perspective to what we already have which is amazing.”

Two sides with 100% records meet in a London derby in north London as defending champions Arsenal host Chelsea at the Emirates. Arsenal have wins over Coventry and Aston Villa to their name whilst Chelsea have beaten Fulham and Brighton.

Arsenal could make it three wins from three to start the season, a feat they have only achieved once in the last 21 seasons. They have lost just one of their last 29 Premier League London derbies. Mikel Arteta comes up against his childhood friend Xabi Alonso today.

“Really happy for him [Alonso],” Arteta said. “It is incredible because obviously we have the history we have together, the same with Andoni [Iraola]. To be facing each other at such a level and with the responsibility we have managing some of the top clubs in the country is a really beautiful thing to experience. Now it will be tougher to meet because of the schedule we both have but that friendship will go for many years. We shared some great moments but now it is the competition and when you face each other, you know what happens.”

Chelsea were involved in late drama on transfer deadline day. They were on the verge of signing midfielder Lamine Camara from Monaco only to see that deal fall through at the last minute. After being linked with a move away throughout the summer, Chelsea did in the end sell Enzo Fernandez to Manchester City for a joint Premier League transfer record fee of £125m.

“In football, many things happen and you need to be prepared for everything,” Chelsea boss Alonso said. “With Enzo, we have had conversations, but always with respect for what he has done for the club. It has been great, we are thankful, but it is time to move forward. We take all the decisions together. We are responsible for them, we work together with every decision, big and small.

“We knew that, ultimately, the last goal is to have the best for the team, the best for the squad, the best for daily work here. We want to build a good spirit, a good team, and we have always taken decisions thinking we want to reach that. We have the squad we have. We have a lot of big personalities, players with a lot of quality in all the different positions, different lines and now we are ready to go.”

Arsenal won this fixture 2-1 last season, with William Saliba and Jurrien Timber earning Mikel Arteta’s side three points on their way to the title. Arsenal are unbeaten in their last nine Premier League matches against Chelsea.
