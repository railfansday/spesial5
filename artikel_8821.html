How did Matt James get into marathon running?

“I don’t think it’s a secret that I love food,” he says, smiling. “When you’re training for a race and running a lot, you need fuel. The more I was able to train, the more I was able to explore the culinary scene without a guilty conscience. It’s part of the job.”

This makes sense to anyone who follows James on Instagram, where he’s shared videos eating a $225 bagel, housing a five-pound hot dog, and spending a month eating nothing but pasta and gelato. While James is perhaps best known for appearing on reality TV shows like The Bachelor and Dancing With the Stars, he’s now built a social media following of like-minded runners/eaters.

James’s marathon and carb-loading journeys actually predate his time as a celebrity. He raced his first marathon in Chicago in 2019, and has run a dozen since. On November 1, he’ll line up for his sixth consecutive New York City Marathon, but this time, he’s more focused on eating than on his time.

“I’ve got a lot of eating planned,” he says. “I’m going to a family-style dinner at Little Ruby’s on Thursday. It’s probably my favorite restaurant in the city. I eat there all the time. Then, I’ll go to Lavagna and have pasta Friday.”

In fact, James takes his marathon-week dining so seriously, he’s partnered with Yelp to create a marathon weekend dining guide for out-of-towners. The list, which includes Melba’s Restaurant, Chrissy’s Pizza, and Winson Bakery, looks beyond the typical tourist fare in Manhattan to give marathon runners a true tour of New York’s best food.

James’s fueling expertise is hard-won. He ran his second NYC Marathon in 2022 alongside former NYC Marathon winner Shalane Flanagan. On race day, he ambitiously told her he was trying to run a sub-three-hour marathon, despite not training properly for the task.

“I just hit the wall,” he remembers. “We’re walking up First Avenue, and I’m like, ‘Oh my gosh, I have 10 more miles to walk with a former marathon champion.’” While he tried to send Flanagan on ahead, she stuck by him and coached him to a 3:46:45.

“Running with her inspired me to take the sport seriously,” he says. “It’s really been a complete lifestyle change after that in terms of getting adequate miles and workouts in. Then when it comes to race day, it’s a celebration and not a death march.”

When the two reunited at the same race in 2023, he finished in 3:04:50.

Besides carb-loading and a strong training regimen, a great playlist is a part of a successful marathon strategy, and James crafted one in honor of the New York City Marathon.

“I like to take myself through a musical journey,” he says of his typical running playlists. “I’ll start with some pop punk, something that’s upbeat. Then I’ll go into some R&B vibes, and then I’ll pick it up at the end with something that’ll get the heart going as a reward.”

His Runner’s World playlist, which features blink-182, Wale, and Gucci Mane, does just that. And while he doesn’t recommend running a full marathon with headphones in, he does recognize that a good playlist can help you push through a “pain cave” if you don’t have Shalane Flanagan running beside you.

“If you see me and I have my headphones on during a race, know I’m battling demons to keep my legs moving,” James laughs.

With a running playlist, a restaurant guide, and his training done, James is ready for the marathon. “It’s the best day of the year in the city,” he says. “I’d have too much FOMO if I didn’t run.”
