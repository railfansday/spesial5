In 2024, Atlus released Persona 3 Reload, giving the game far more than a fresh coat of paint. New character models, fully voiced Social Links, and a whole new battle system headlined a huge roster of changes. The same looks set to be said for Persona 4 Revival, which is due for release early next year.

Atlus has been slowly breaking down the changes with news drops and character introduction videos, and the game's development team occasionally speaks to the press about what makes 4 Revival special.

A new interview has just surfaced with Persona general producer Kazuhisa Wada, Persona 4 Revival's director Daisuke Yajima, and sound composer Ryota Kozuka. The trio discussed the challenges they've faced during the development of the upcoming reimagining, and surprisingly, one of them was the design of Chie Satonaka's thighs.

Chie Satonaka is one of Persona 4's main party members. She goes to the same high school as the game's protagonist and is also a lover of Kung-Fu. This juxtaposition of character proved difficult for the team at Atlus.

In an interview published on the Famitsu website today, director Yamija expressed his desire to "maintain the same level of quality" throughout Persona 4 Revival as was found in Persona 3 Reload. Despite being full of enthusiasm, Yajima revealed that this was "quite challenging," especially as the team had to "adjust the CG models and illustrations" after it had begun creating cutscenes.

When asked for specifics, Yajima said, "There was a discussion about Chie's thighs. The CG model was made with a 'thickness' that would make kung fu-like motions look good, but the illustration was drawn with a 'thinness' that was faithful to the original character design, so there was a slight gap between the two."

It was a debate that, according to Yajima, continued for as long as the game was in production.

"We eventually managed to find a compromise, but even with the bust-up illustration, each staff member's attention to detail never ceased, with comments like, 'She looks a little too young, so I want to redraw her to look more like a high school student,' or 'Wouldn't it be more 'like 4' if we deliberately made the coloring more anime-like?' The event cutscenes were polished relentlessly right up until the end of development."
