STATE OF PLAY: Blue wave sweeps Democrats into power, with key races still hanging in balance

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

The ‘FOX News Democracy 26’ panel discusses what the GOP takeaways should be and how Democrats might work with President Donald Trump.

Democrats decisively recaptured majorities in the House and Senate in the midterm elections, riding a massive blue wave that exceeded expectations.

But with Tuesday turning into Wednesday and a handful of high-profile races still to be called, the big question remaining is whether the already formidable Democratic congressional majorities keep growing.

In the battle for the Senate, Democrats stormed to the majority by flipping GOP-held seats in Maine, Ohio, Kansas, Iowa, Texas, North Carolina and South Carolina, while successfully defending open seats in Michigan and New Hampshire.

Meanwhile, Senate races for two GOP-held seats were still too close to call.

In Mississippi, Republican incumbent Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith and Democratic challenger Scott Colom appeared deadlocked. In Alaska, GOP incumbent Sen. Dan Sullivan had a slight edge over former Rep. Mary Peltola with votes still being counted.

In the battle for the House, thanks to the stunning blue tsunami, Democrats easily cleared the 218-seat threshold needed to recapture the chamber.

But with plenty of races still outstanding, the big question is just how large of a majority will Democrats end up with when all the votes are counted.

As of early Wednesday, Democrats had won 228 seats to 139 for the GOP, with 68 races yet to be called.

In gubernatorial showdowns, competitive races in Alaska and Kansas had yet to be called.

The results amounted to a stunning rebuke of President Donald Trump and the GOP.

"The American people reduced the red brick wall to a pile of bricks," Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, D-N.Y., told reporters.

And looking ahead to January, when the new Congress gets to work, Schumer said, "We have five words for the American people. Relief is on the way."

But Senate Majority Leader John Thune, R-S.D., said, "As we head into 2027, Senate Republicans will not stand idly by as the far-left, socialist wing of the Democratic Party attempts to unwind everything we’ve done."

Thune said that Senate Republicans will work with Trump "to stop the worst impulses of Democrat extremism. We cannot let their fringe positions become the mainstream."

Trump spent the past two months repeatedly stressing to supporters to "pretend I’m on the ballot," as he tried to energize the Republican base.

But, as the results were pouring in on Tuesday night, the president took to social media to argue, "I wasn’t on the Ballot and if I was, we would have won IN A LANDSLIDE!"

Minutes later, the president, in an interview on Fox News with anchors Bret Baier and Martha MacCallum, complimented the opposition party, saying, "I think the Democrats did a good job."

And pointing to their incoming congressional majorities, Trump added, "I can work with them on many things."
