Trump's massive DC triumphal arch project dealt another blow by federal judge

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

A federal judge extended the construction halt on President Donald Trump's proposed 250-foot triumphal arch in Washington, D.C., until at least Dec. 3, prompting criticism from the White House amid ongoing legal challenges.

President Donald Trump's proposed 250-foot triumphal arch near Arlington in Washington, D.C., was dealt another blow Wednesday when a federal judge extended an order blocking construction until at least early December.

U.S. District Judge Tanya Chutkan extended her initial Nov. 5 temporary restraining order blocking construction of the arch for another 14 days, delaying progress on the structure until at least Dec. 3.

The extension comes as Chutkan's original order was set to expire Thursday.

The judge said the extension would give her additional time to consider lawsuits filed by the District of Columbia and Virginia seeking a longer-term injunction against the project.

The order marks the latest setback for one of the president's major projects in the nation's capital. Inspired by Paris' Arc de Triomphe, the proposed monument would stand near the Memorial Bridge, which connects Arlington National Cemetery to Washington, D.C.

In a statement to Fox News Digital, the White House criticized the order, saying the project was being undercut by an "activist district court."

"The judiciary should not be abused to politicize a structure designed to unite our nation," White House spokesman Davis Ingle said.

"We will continue making the case for this beautiful and fitting monument and are confident we will be fully vindicated as this litigation proceeds," he added.

Chutkan issued the temporary restraining order earlier this month just hours after the National Capital Planning Commission approved the project.

The approval had cleared the way for construction to begin before the court intervened.

Trump has previously said the triumphal arch would be converted into a military complex capable of rapidly deploying large numbers of drones, with snipers positioned on the roof and plaza areas.

He also said the complex would store "large quantities of sniper ammunition."

The project has drawn criticism from preservationists, lawmakers and veterans, who argue that it could disrupt views of the Lincoln Memorial and Arlington National Cemetery.

A view of Columbia Island, the site of President Donald Trump's proposed 250-foot triumphal arch between the Lincoln Memorial and Arlington National Cemetery. (Aaron Schwartz/Getty Images)

Last week, nine members of the Kennedy family also sought to stop the project, saying it would obstruct views and cast shadows over their relatives' grave sites at Arlington National Cemetery.
