CBS Is Setting Up The Perfect Way To Revive FBI: Most Wanted

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Cher Thompson is a Senior Staff Writer at Screen Rant, where she covers everything from explosive reality TV moments to cast interviews about the twists and turns of scripted dramas. With a background in creative writing, marketing, and teaching, Cher knows how to spot both the storytelling genius and the guilty pleasures in pop culture. Her work blends wit with insight, making readers feel like they’re chatting with a friend who just happens to know way too much about TV.

The FBI franchise may be bracing for change once again after the most recent episodes explored one of its longest standing characters leaving New York, but the CBS series is creating the perfect conditions to revive one of its most intense franchise shows. Throughout the run of FBI, the Dick Wolf series has spun off into several different shows, building an expanded universe that has been scaled back in recent years. FBI has spun off into FBI: Most Wanted in 2020, FBI: International in 2021, and most recently, CIA in early 2026, bringing viewers into several different teams that have crossover potential.

FBI: Most Wanted, which was led by the late Julian McMahon for its first three seasons before Dylan McDermott took over in the final three years, had a tough time getting on its feet, but managed to be one of the most interesting spinoffs the franchise ever had. Exploring the work of the Fugitive Task Force, Most Wanted saw its team searching out criminals on the Most Wanted list who were intense, dangerous, and at times, terrifying. Eventually, including Shantel VanSanten's Nina Chase, FBI and FBI: Most Wanted connected in an interesting way when Chase and FBI's Stuart Scola's (John Boyd) crossover romance came into play.

When Most Wanted came to a close, one of the biggest questions was about whether Nina and Scola's relationship would be able to continue, especially considering how deeply they'd connected. Now, with the pair married and parents to a young son, Dougie, Nina's move to San Diego to lead a brand new task force sets up the perfect conditions for the return of FBI: Most Wanted.

With Nina and Scola working through her move to San Diego in the past few episodes of the series, the pair appeared together during FBI season 9 episode 7, "Posterity," and made it clear that their priority was their family. While both agents are dedicated to their work and want to explore further opportunities with the FBI, Scola began to realize he wasn't interested in living fully across the country from his wife and son. Sharing that he put in for a transfer that won't go through for some time, Scola's likely to find himself leaving the New York FBI office to join his family.

While Scola's departure hasn't been set in stone or given a time frame yet, it's clear that he's serious about moving away from the city to be with Nina and Dougie, which sets up the perfect conditions for a new FBI spinoff. With CIA having joined the FBI family in 2026, the lack of spinoffs currently airing within the franchise leaves some space for what's to come on, and with minimal coverage on the West Coast, an FBI spinoff anchored by two well-known figures in the franchise could be a huge swing for the CBS hit.

While there hasn't been any movement on what could happen with a Scola and Nina FBI spinoff, the fact that they're moving away from the original series in the future opens up something vastly different for the franchise. Branching out to San Diego and following Nina's task force, Scola's transition into a new place, or both could allow FBI to shift its scope within one of the franchise's shows. A West Coast series could combine the approaches of FBI and FBI: Most Wanted, creating something that looks and feels like a franchise series, but has different, unexpected beats for viewers to follow.

Though Nina and Scola would be the anchoring characters for a spinoff, a series that follows them to the West Coast would have to avoid becoming too consistently about their relationship. Regardless of the potential pitfalls, another FBI franchise spinoff that brings back the higher stakes of Most Wanted while being able to keep two of the franchise's best agents in the spotlight feels like a no-brainer. Ultimately, CBS has the chance to chase the appeal of what made FBI: Most Wanted work throughout its six-season run, and at the same time, keep a wonderful FBI pair together in a new setting.
