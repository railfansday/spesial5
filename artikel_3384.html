More than four years after airing its final season, a hit historical epic series is slated to be removed from Netflix this December. It joins other shows superficially marketed as Netflix Originals — for example, The End of the F***ing World and Gilmore Girls: A Year in the Life — to be removed from the platform this year. These removals happen because Netflix doesn't own distribution rights in perpetuity. According to What's on Netflix, the streamer retains the option to renew them, but in the case of these shows, it chose not to.

Netflix handled international distribution on The End of the F***ing World, which aired from 2017 to 2019 and was removed from the streamer on November 5. Netflix acquired distribution rights to the historical epic in 2018, three years after its debut on BBC Two in the United Kingdom. Netflix began co-producing the show after Season 1, not long after it entered the original programming business. This coincided with the peak of Game of Thrones' success, prompting other platforms to seek their own replacements. In many ways, Netflix is still trying to produce a Game of Thrones-level fantasy hit, despite having poached that show's creative duo.

Netflix entered into a similar agreement for the History Channel series Vikings, which debuted in 2013. Netflix aired the show's spin-off, Vikings: Valhalla, between 2022 and 2024. The historical epic slated to be removed from the platform is based on the novels by Bernard Cornwell, and stars Alexander Dreymon and Tobias Santelmann in the lead roles.

You belong to Highgarden, where nobody raises their voice and everybody gets exactly what they came for. The Tyrells feed the realm, charm the realm and quietly steer the realm, and they have never once needed to win a battle to get their way.

We're talking, of course, about The Last Kingdom. The show holds a 91% critics' score and a 95% audience score on the aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes. The show was evidently quite successful for the streamer; according to Netflix's own viewership reports, the show amassed around 155 million views between 2023 and 2025. As with Vikings, Netflix produced a follow-up title for The Last Kingdom as well.

Titled The Last Kingdom: Seven Kings Must Die, the spin-off movie holds an 82% score on Rotten Tomatoes. It premiered in 2023 and will remain on the streamer until 2033. The main series will be removed from Netflix in the United States on December 9, after being removed from the platform's Canadian library earlier this year. The series will, however, remain on Netflix in the United Kingdom and Australia until 2028. Stay tuned to Collider for more updates.
