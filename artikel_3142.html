Kornit (KRNT) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Screen printers are switching to digital and recurring revenue is rising

By Motley Fool Transcribing – Nov 18, 2026 at 4:11PM EST

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

Kornit Digital (KRNT -22.89%) reported third quarter results that met the upper end of its revenue guidance and outperformed adjusted EBITDA margin targets. Management reported that the business is undergoing a strategic shift as digital production moves into core apparel manufacturing, specifically capturing longer, higher-volume runs traditionally managed by analog screen printers. The company stated that approximately 80% of its total revenue is now recurring or highly recurring, supported by the rapid scaling of its all-inclusive click model and steady impression growth. Management indicated it expects to maintain this momentum into 2027 through upcoming product introductions and participation in major industry events such as ITMA.

Revenue reached $58.3 million, up nearly 10% year-over-year and towards the high end of our guidance range. Adjusted EBITDA was $2.7 million, above the high end of our margin guidance, and we generated positive operating cash flow for the 12th consecutive quarter. AIC revenue grew approximately 70% and approximately 70% of systems shipped in the quarter were sold through AIC compared with 40% a year ago.

ARR increased by $5.6 million during the quarter to $39.4 million, up 83% year-over-year. TCV increased approximately 70% to $162.4 million, representing the remaining value of our active AIC agreements. Trailing 12 months impressions grew approximately 10% year-over-year. Based on what we see today, we expect full year impression growth to be around 10%, reflecting rising production and utilization across our installed base. We continue to see healthy growth in system deliveries expanding our production footprint and customer base. Approximately 40% of our system sales during the quarter came from new customers. The largest opportunity in front of us is transforming the massive screen printing market into digital era.

This market has historically been difficult to address with digital technology, but that is changing. Kornit solutions now enable digital to compete at industrial scale, including on longer production runs. We have identified hundreds of traditional screen printers ready to make this move, and the momentum is already clear. Traditional screen printers accounted for around 60% of our system deliveries year-to-date, while long-run jobs drove approximately 84% of year-to-date impression growth. This is more than a shift in market share, digital now addressing production that was beyond its reach just a few years ago, and Kornit is positioned to lead that transformation. The clearest evidence is what customers are doing.

Screen printers are bringing more core production in-house and many are adding capacity within months of their first installation. Let me share a few examples of how opportunity is taking shape. For example, Fast Life Brands, a California-based screen printer and Real Thread, a Florida-based custom apparel producer, each added a second Kornit system within months of their first. Both are bringing more of their core production on to digital. KDS, a leading U.K.-based screen printer specializing in music merchandise is integrating Apollo, Atlas MAX systems, PrintFactory and KornitX workflow automation into end-to-end web-to-production operation just a few months after installing their first Atlas MAX.

We are also bringing new customers into Kornit across a wider range of production geographies, including offshore markets. In Sri Lanka, Antler, a new Kornit customer and leading screen printer for brands such as Victoria's Secret, Nike, Tommy Hilfiger and Calvin Klein signed on four Atlas MAX systems through AIC to modernize its operation around a more agile on-demand model with plans to add multiple Apollos next year. We are seeing established customers in the customized design market expand their Kornit production as well.

Print Dash, a Texas-based apparel producer operating under our AIC model, added a second Apollo and an Atlas MAX, moving more production from direct to film onto Kornit to improve print quality and hand feel while reducing labor and production complexity. Shirt Monkey, a long-standing U.K. on-demand apparel producer, added an Apollo system and two MATRIX systems increasing production capacity by more than 50%. The added capacity supports higher volumes, faster turnaround and a greater flexibility and is opening new opportunities in licensed apparel. Cimpress, another long-standing Kornit customer, is growing its digital footprint globally, standardizing on Apollo and Atlas MAX across multiple production hubs to improve consistency, scalability and operating efficiency. Agile, a leading Italian provider of printing solutions for footwear, fashion and leather goods is expanding production with Kornit's roll-to-roll technology to serve leading footwear brands. As customers bring more production onto our platform, we grew with them. Approximately 80% of our revenue is recurring or highly recurring, supported by ARR, ink, service and software. AIC strengthens this model by helping customers adopt Kornit and scale over time, giving us greater visibility and making our business more predictable and resilient. As we enter peak season, our focus is clear: execute with discipline, finish 2026 strong and carry our momentum into 2027.
