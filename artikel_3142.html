Palantir Just Delivered a Monster Quarter, but the Stock Is Plummeting. The Reason Why Is Obvious.

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Palantir's third-quarter earnings report was impressive across the board.

Earlier this week, Palantir Technologies (PLTR -2.11%) delivered an exceptional third-quarter earnings report. The artificial intelligence (AI) software company nearly doubled its revenue, generated more than $1 billion in profit, and raised its full-year financial outlook. Even so, Wall Street responded by selling the stock, extending a broader decline from Palantir's high back in October.

At first glance, this reaction looks puzzling. However, a closer analysis of Palantir's earnings report suggests investors may be concerned about the company's contract bookings and future revenue visibility.

I think these concerns overlook an important change in how customers are actually adopting Palantir's Artificial Intelligence Platform (AIP).

During the third quarter, Palantir generated $2.3 billion in revenue, representing 96% year-over-year growth. Generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) net income reached $1.1 billion, while adjusted operating income climbed to $1.4 billion, translating into an impressive 62% adjusted operating margin. The company also generated $1.2 billion in adjusted free cash flow. These figures underscore that Palantir isn't simply accelerating its top line but converting that growth into meaningful profitability and cash generation.

Perhaps the most important milestone came from the company's U.S. commercial business. Revenue from Palantir's U.S. commercial customers surged 148% year over year to $984 million, surpassing the company's U.S. government revenue for the first time in its history. Importantly, U.S. government revenue also increased 96%, showcasing that commercial expansion is not coming at the expense of Palantir's legacy public sector base.

Management raised the company's full-year revenue guidance to $8.53 billion, representing roughly 91% annual growth. Nevertheless, investors focused on a different set of numbers.

During the third quarter, Palantir closed $2.9 billion in total contract value (TCV), representing just 6% year-over-year growth. More concerning, U.S. commercial TCV bookings increased only 11%, compared with 153% growth during the second quarter. TCV measures the potential lifetime value of contracts signed during a particular period, providing investors with an indication of future business commitments.

Another closely watched metric is remaining performance obligations (RPO), which measures contracted revenue that has not yet been recognized and excludes short-duration arrangements. Palantir finished the quarter with $4.9 billion in RPO, representing 88% year-over-year growth. Investors took note that this figure was essentially flat compared to the previous quarter. Meanwhile, total remaining deal value (RDV) reached $13.4 billion, increasing 55% annually but only 2% sequentially.

The divergence between Palantir's rapid revenue expansion and slower bookings growth raises an obvious question: Is demand beginning to soften?

Management's commentary provides a compelling explanation. During the earnings call, Palantir CFO Dave Glazer explained:

We signed multiple 8-figure deals in the quarter that were shorter in duration than usual, as customers rapidly adopted initial Sovereign AI capabilities in their enterprise. It is important to remember that the Sovereign AI revolution is only 1 quarter old. As we continue to deliver value with Sovereign AI, over time, we expect both the duration and size of these contracts to increase.

Consider two hypothetical customer scenarios. One signs a five-year software agreement worth $50 million, while another signs a one-year agreement worth $10 million.

Both generate $10 million in annual revenue, assuming the services are delivered evenly. But the first contract contributes five times as much initial TCV. Similarly, the shorter agreement contributes less future contracted revenue to RPO, even though both customers could be generating identical sales on an annual basis.

In other words, shorter contract durations can give the appearance of depressed forward-looking bookings metrics without necessarily indicating weaker underlying demand.

Palantir's 165% net dollar retention provides additional evidence of strong customer spending. This metric indicates that revenue from existing customers has increased over the last year, with periods of expansion and contraction.

I would not interpret one quarter of slower contract bookings as evidence that Palantir's AI opportunity is deteriorating. In fact, the company's U.S. commercial TCV bookings grew 62% over the last year to $6.1 billion. This longer-term view suggests the disappointing quarterly comparison doesn't tell the entire story.

On a deeper level, rather than waiting years for large, multiyear contracts to materialize, customers appear to be deploying Palantir's AIP software through shorter initial agreements. If these deployments demonstrate profitable returns, customers could subsequently increase their spending and commit to longer contracts.

This creates a unique opportunity for Palantir to build customer relationships more quickly while generating larger recurring revenue streams in the long run. For investors with a long-term time horizon, I think the recent sell-off in Palantir stock is an opportunity to buy the dip in one of the AI industry's fastest-growing businesses.
