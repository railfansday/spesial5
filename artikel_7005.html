Movado’s New Tempo-Matic Watch Is Inspired by an Iconic Piece from the ’70s

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Proof once again that it’s hip to be square.

Movado was founded in 1881 in La Chaux-de-Fonds, Switzerland, which isn’t a particularly odd backstory for a heritage Swiss watch brand. But it has been American owned since 1983 and has a distinct quality tied deeply to its long history in the U.S. market—which puts it slightly outside the predictable purview of many watch collectors.

Right now, a growing band of horological nerds is finding rich pickings in the Movado back catalog and pushing up vintage prices considerably—especially for its mid-century chronographs and its legendary Museum Watch (which celebrates 80 years in 2027; more of that to come) in the process.

It’s not only the collectors who are digging into Movado’s past, however. Last month, the brand released the Heritage Tempo-Matic ($995–$1,095), a re-creation of a watch that merges sporty and retro design in a neat and slinky package. The Tempo-Matic, Movado’s first automatic movement, was launched in 1945 and went on to power a broad range of Movado’s wristwatches. But the rounded yet boxy “TV” shape of the new Heritage Tempo-Matic is unmistakably 1970s, when watch design took a giant leap forward into sexy new territory.

The 2026 version is available in three executions, each powered by the reliable Sellita SW200-1B movement and fitted with integrated steel bracelets that positively scream seventies. Two of them come in brushed steel with vertically brushed burgundy or blue dials. A third—which, we must admit, is our favorite—is gold-tone PVD with a warm gray dial. You’d be hard-pressed to find such an elegant, authentic, and affordable homage to the 1970s—a decade that’s currently a very hot motif in watch-collecting circles—anywhere else.

For further Movado inspiration, you might enjoy a glimpse into the brand’s rich watchmaking history at the Horological Society of New York, a fascinating nonprofit organization—effectively a guild created to support immigrant German watchmakers—that since 1866 has promoted the art and science of watchmaking in America. In the guild’s expansive watchmaking library, until July 2027, a dedicated display charts the Movado brand’s achievements in watchmaking, advertising, and design from as early as the ahead-of-its-time, art-deco-style Polyplan of 1912. Visits to the Movado exhibit are free, but we advise you to call ahead to check availability. And if you really get the bug while you’re there, you might like to sign up for membership, regular lectures, and even a series of watchmaking workshops where you get hands-on watchmaking experience under expert tutorship.
