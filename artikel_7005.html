We are looking for a Product Builder to join the IDP & RPA pod. You will take real problems from customers, decide what is worth solving, and build and ship the solution yourself, using AI tools to get from idea to production quickly without lowering the bar.

Product Building is how Camunda's Experience organization builds the applications people use on top of Camunda 8, such as Hub, Operate, and ProcessOS. Small pods of builders own a product area from customer problem to shipped change, with no hand-offs between product management, design, and engineering. We are setting this model up now, so you will help shape how it works.

The IDP & RPA pod builds how Camunda processes work with documents and legacy applications. With intelligent document processing (IDP), customers create extraction and classification templates in Camunda Hub and publish them so a process can use AI to turn invoices and other documents into structured data. With robotic process automation (RPA), customers integrate legacy applications that have no APIs, using Robot Framework scripts on a standalone RPA worker. The hard problems come with putting LLMs into customer processes: extraction quality, model and prompt choices, testing and trust, and working for AI agents as well as for people.

Help shape product direction for IDP & RPA by combining customer input, usage data, and field feedback into clear priorities.

Partner with the other builders and product strategy to turn problems into shipped features, using short written proposals and prototypes to align early.

Make judgment calls with incomplete information within your scope and own the outcome.

Communicate trade-offs and decisions clearly to stakeholders who don't live in the details the way you do.

Use AI tools across the whole loop, from specs to prototypes to production-ready code, and hold a high bar for what you ship by reviewing and improving the output rather than accepting it.

Build, test, and ship improvements to the IDP & RPA experience, reading errors and tracing issues far enough to give your pod useful context.

Talk to customers and to support and customer success colleagues about how IDP is used and where it breaks, and turn what you learn into decisions about what to build next.

Pick up customer incidents and trace them across Hub, the connectors, and the deployed process of the customer.

Track what you ship against real outcomes and adjust when the data tells you to.
