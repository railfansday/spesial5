Could hiking BE any hotter ? – Stage 4 of the Hexatrek

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

The fourth stage of the Hexatrek takes you through gorges and valleys. With 14,389 meters (47,200 feet) of elevation gain over 500+ kilometres, it is the flattest of the six sections that make up this trail, but it is also by far the driest. Many times over the last few months, we have been warned by other hikers about the upcoming conditions. Quite a few fellow thruhikers have confided in us that they will either skip this section entirely, or flip-flop and do the Pyrenees first before (maybe) returning to complete Stage 4 in more manageable weather conditions in the autumn. We also considered this option but, after spending a week at home and having had some time to rest, we decide to continue with our straight-through SOBO hike after all.

We restart in Châteauneuf-du-Rhône, where we got off trail a week ago, and we stay at a wonderful little campground with lovely hosts. It doesn’t hurt that it is also the cheapest campground on trail so far at €13 for two people. Early on, we realise how different the hiking experience will be here compared to the Alps. The first day mostly consists of roads, wide forest paths, and rolling hills rather than lengthy climbs. Another new development is that we seem to have these routes mostly to ourselves. Well, apart from the herds of wild boars that frequently rummage around in the bushes at dusk and make us reconsider our planned sleeping places at night. Instead, we start a practice that will soon become quite normal – we nighthike. While this is not our favourite way to thruhike, it has its advantages since it is the only time of day the sun isn’t burning our skin, the air isn’t stifling, and the heat is not exhausting us.

Also early into this section we cross the Gorges de l’Ardèche, a natural reserve with a river surrounded by cliffs, forest, and scrubland. A signpost tells us that the temperature can reach 60°C here…and we can believe this easily. In general, this gorge turns out to be a surprisingly difficult part of the trail. We hadn’t anticipated the repeated technical parts along this path. It feels like the trail changes every half kilometer, from scrambling over boulders to trekking across sand, from paths that are overgrown to sheer rock faces with ladders or chains to hold on to for stability. We even had to squeeze through a tight hole in the cliffs at one point.

While most of these paths are fun and feel like doing parcour, we can’t help but feel a bit annoyed too because we didn’t anticipate our pace to slow down to 2km/h, and we certainly hadn’t planned on staying inside the reserve for the night. It’s mandatory to stay at an official campsite here and these charge €15 per person. But we don’t have another choice and at least we get to enjoy some amenities like showers and bathrooms.

When I say hiking here is always different and keeps surprising us, I do mean it. For example, we hadn’t realised that at one point we would walk right through a nudist campground, or that we would do two thigh-high river crossings in one day. This gorge keeps us on our toes. I really should mention that this particular section only took two days to cross, but those days certainly left an impression.

After the gorge, most of this stage consists of good paths and little elevation gain as promised. We are consistently able to cover 30-40km on any given day. Twice we manage to do a marathon day (42+km) which we are, given the circumstances, immensly happy and proud with. During the day,  it is usually punishingly hot. Our clothes cling to our skin, the sweat is rolling off us and burning in our eyes, and our bodies often feel sluggish and tired from heat exhaustion. But after nightfall we feel as if we are able to fly through this section.

Apart from the temperatures, some overgrown paths usually present the biggest challenge, since the water situation is thankfully easier than expected. Although we are drinking around 5 litres each per day, we get away with carrying out up to 3.5 litres per person. After some careful planning, we never get into the trouble of running out and a major concern we had prior to starting this section is resolved.

On the fun side of this stage is definitely the Gorges du Tarn. It is possible, and often recommended, to canoe on the river of this canyon. This alternate follows the trail for 21km, just on water rather than on land. The canoe rental companies have adapted to thruhikers and offer luggage transportation to the end point where they pick up the boats again. It is a gorgeous day out on the water and a fun way to make the miles. It is still strenuous muscle work and we did get stuck here and there due to low water levels and had to paddle quite a bit over the course of the journey.

Stage 4 has a multitude of suprises for us, one of which came as a bit of a shock…

Who would have thought that there are scorpions in southern France?

The natural surroundings here are truly fascinating, as are the urban areas, which I will be covering in the next post.
