Shanghai police bust counterfeit Final Fantasy 7 Tifa figure operation

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Police in China have arrested 13 people accused of selling counterfeit Final Fantasy 7 Tifa Lockhart figures, with the operation generating more than 2 million yuan (around $300,000 USD) in sales.

The arrests come after multiple customers complained about the quality of Tifa figures purchased online, claiming the materials used were noticeably different from the official products.

According to Chinese outlet Shangguan News, Shanghai police began receiving complaints in May 2026 and subsequently contacted the rights holder, confirming that the seller had no authorization to produce or distribute the figures.

Investigators eventually identified an alleged counterfeiting operation that had been running since 2024.

Police claim the operation was led by a suspect identified by the surname Qian, who allegedly commissioned a factory in Guangzhou to manufacture unauthorized figures.

The suspect reportedly employed more than ten people across departments responsible for customer service, wholesale distribution, inventory, and online sales.

The group allegedly promoted the figures through online marketplaces, QQ groups, and WeChat, offering different packages priced at 1,280 yuan, 1,580 yuan, and 2,580 yuan.

Some of these counterfeit figures were actually more expensive than official Final Fantasy 7 Remake Tifa merchandise, which investigators said could cost around 1,000 yuan.

According to police, the unauthorized sellers attempted to justify their higher prices by including additional interchangeable faces, hands, and accessories that weren’t available with the official products.

Following their investigation, authorities conducted coordinated raids in Shanghai and Guangzhou, arresting 13 suspects and dismantling two locations used to store and distribute counterfeit merchandise.

All 13 suspects are facing allegations related to selling infringing reproductions, with the investigation still ongoing.

Shanghai authorities say they have investigated more than 30 criminal cases involving intellectual property violations across collectible figures, entertainment, and gaming merchandise in 2026, involving over 140 suspects and more than 70 million yuan in total case value.
