Florida’s new Republican governor wants to bring NASA headquarters to the Sunshine State. Could it happen?

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Republican Congressman Byron Donalds, Florida’s first Black governor-elect, wants space and tech at the forefront of job creation in Florida. He also wants NASA to move its headquarters there. 

The Kennedy Space Center in Brevard County, Florida, was central to the Apollo missions, including the first human moon landing in 1969. NASA’s rockets were assembled in Florida, and launched from the center.

NASA’s headquarters have been stationed in Washington, D.C., since President Dwight D. Eisenhower signed the National Aeronautics and Space Act on July 29, 1958.

Nearly 68 years later, Donalds hopes to recenter Florida in the aerospace ecosystem and generate jobs through launches. 

Flying south?

NASA’s forthcoming move follows an executive order signed by President Trump in February 2025, launching his administration’s federal workforce optimization initiative. In March of that year, NASA considered closing its headquarters and distributing workers to its other field locations.

NASA’s lease on its current headquarters in D.C. expires August 2028, and the administration announced that it is looking for a new headquarters in a 2024 press release.

“NASA Headquarters issued a solicitation for the sale or lease of commercial property in Washington, D.C., earlier this year. Responses were due in September, and our evaluation is ongoing,” NASA news chief Cheryl Warner told Fast Company.

The push toward Florida is not random

Donalds first made space a big part of his campaign when he and other representatives co-led bipartisan legislation called the Consolidating Aerospace Programs Efficiently at Canaveral Act (or the CAPE Canaveral Act) in March 2025, alongside members of Florida’s congressional delegation.

It requires NASA to relocate to Brevard County, and was referred to the House Science, Space, and Technology Committee, but has not become a law. 

Donalds positioned himself even closer to the idea of space in September during his campaign for governor.

He introduced the STARS Act, proposing that Florida’s Space Coast become the permanent home of the United States Space Academy, a proposed, NASA-led federal academy established by President Donald Trump via an executive order signed in August.

The academy would train future military, civilian, and commercial space professionals, and the order suggests that the location be within reasonable distance to the Kennedy Space Center and Cape Canaveral.

It is unclear how many jobs Florida has to offer for a NASA headquarters and hosting the United States Space Academy, beyond the state’s historical significance. But space exploration and national security have become prominent priorities within the Republican Party in recent years, particularly under President Donald Trump.

In March 2025, Florida Governor Ron DeSantis said he had discussed moving NASA headquarters with Elon Musk, CEO of SpaceX, and believed that Musk supported relocating it to Cape Canaveral, according to DeSantis.

While Musk has not publicly supported this proposed relocation, he congratulated and endorsed Donalds in 2025 when he announced his campaign for governor. 

The move has support from Floridians

In a survey of 1,000 Florida voters and 500 Americans, Sachs Media found that 82% of Florida voters preferred Florida for the headquarters move, compared to 40% of U.S. adults with the same opinion.

Additionally, 75% of Florida voters thought the Space Coast/Cape Canaveral is the ideal location for the headquarters, if moved to Florida.

“To ensure American space superiority in an increasingly competitive domain, our future leaders must be educated at the very nexus of global space operations,” Donalds said when he introduced the STARS Act.

The NASA headquarters workforce, an estimated 1,841 people last year, is much smaller than the national NASA workforce of over 17,000. Relocating NASA’s headquarters would not necessarily mean relocating the majority of its employees or its scientific operations.

However, Donalds wants to generate jobs in Florida, beyond those transferred from Washington.
