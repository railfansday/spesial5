Michael Saylor’s Strategy starts this week on the back foot. MSTR stock fell about -3% in premarket trading today, slipping to around the $125 support level. That follows a strong third quarter, when MSTR rose +76% versus Bitcoin’s +43% gain. Claude Fable 5.2 predicts where the MSTR stock will go over the next few months.

The latest headlines are mostly about capital management. Q3 earnings landed on 29 October, and EPS of $44.75 beat the $21.50 consensus, though revenue of $125.4M missed.

The profit is mainly a mark-to-market Bitcoin gain. Last week’s filing was more unusual. The company sold about 2.05M MSTR shares for $283.8M in net proceeds, and only $81.9M went to buying BTC USD, while $188.3M funded repurchases of its STRC preferred stock.

Holdings now stand at 850,364 BTC, with an average cost of $75,456 per coin. Bitcoin is trading just above that, so the margin is thin. Management also admits that Bitcoin per share is 3.7% lower year to date.

The chart is weak in the short term. MSTR sits below all four major EMAs, with the daily RSI near 41, signaling soft momentum. Other data show a negative MACD.

The 200-day average, around $133, has acted as a floor so far, but the stock is below its 20-day ($139) and 50-day ($149) averages. These are the key levels to watch:

Support at $120 is the next downside level if $125 fails. Over the past six months, the stock has found support near $87. Resistance sits between $136 and $140, the first major hurdle where the moving averages cluster.

Above that, resistance has formed near $196. The trend is bearish until MSTR reclaims the $136–140 zone on a closing basis. A failure at $120 would put the lower range in play.

The bull case rests on a Bitcoin recovery, since MSTR has behaved like a leveraged bet on it. Its CEO, Michael Saylor, argues that “we’re at the beginning of the next Bitcoin bull market,” pointing to renewed institutional demand and a Bitcoin price 21% above its 200-week moving average.

If that holds, Claude sees several catalysts. Bitcoin is down roughly 35% from its all-time high, while Strategy’s holdings have grown by about a third. A move back toward six figures would hit MSTR’s share price disproportionately hard, because the company’s holdings are large relative to its market value.

Net leverage has fallen to 2.4% from 8.4% in Q2, and management says bitcoin could fall 78% before liabilities are no longer covered. The $4.77Bn reserve covers preferred dividends and reduces fears of forced selling. Buying back STRC below par can also raise Bitcoin per share.

The company has an unused $1Bn common-stock buyback authorization. Several quarters of huge GAAP profits could also revive talk of S&P 500 eligibility, though that is speculative.

In a bullish scenario, MSTR first reclaims $136–140 and then the 200-day average by year-end. Strong bitcoin follow-through could carry it to the $170–196 resistance band in the first half of 2027.

Analyst targets such as BTIG’s $250 would require Bitcoin to make new highs. The mirror-image risks are real: continued share issuance dilutes holders, and a Bitcoin drop below the ~$75k cost basis would quickly pressure sentiment.
