Solana Lands Major Wall Street Boost as DTCC Targets 2027 Launch of Tokenized Assets: What It Means for SOL

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Wall Street is taking another major step toward blockchain adoption, and Solana (SOL) is emerging as one of the networks positioned to benefit. The Depository Trust & Clearing Corporation (DTCC), the financial infrastructure giant that clears and settles much of the U.S. securities market, plans to bring its tokenization services to Solana in 2027.

The move could open the door to tokenized stocks, exchange-traded funds (ETFs), and U.S. Treasuries on the blockchain, marking a significant institutional milestone for Solana. But beyond the headline announcement, the obvious question is what this development could mean for the price of SOL and its long-term investment appeal.

DTCC announced Thursday that it plans to extend its tokenization services to Solana in the second half of 2027. The initiative will connect its DTCC Tokenization Service to the Layer 1 blockchain, enabling eligible securities held by its Depository Trust Company subsidiary to be represented as digital tokens.

“DTC-tokenized assets made available on Solana – and all blockchain networks integrated with the service – will retain the same investor protections, ownership rights and regulatory safeguards that apply to traditionally held securities,” Brian Steele, president of DTCC Clearing & Securities Services, said in the announcement.

DTCC’s Tokenization Service went live on October 28, initially operating on the Canton Network and the corporation’s own private blockchain, which is built on the Besu framework. The platform allows eligible participants to convert traditional securities into tokenized assets and redeem them back into their original form, with the digital tokens delivered directly to wallets of their choice. At launch, DTCC also outlined plans to expand its tokenization service to additional blockchain networks in 2027, with Stellar and Circle’s Arc among the platforms also slated for integration.

Solana has been stepping up efforts to attract traditional financial institutions, positioning itself as a blockchain capable of supporting large-scale financial activity. Nick Ducoff, head of institutional growth at the Solana Foundation, recently highlighted this appeal, stating that institutions choose Solana because it already “runs real financial activity at scale.” He added that firms are already issuing assets, settling transactions, and deploying capital on the network, according to a post shared by Solana’s official X account.

The potential opportunity becomes clearer when considering DTCC’s position in the U.S. financial system. Through its Depository Trust Company (DTC) subsidiary, the financial infrastructure giant holds approximately $114 trillion in securities on behalf of banks and brokerage firms. Its newly launched tokenization service initially covered 34 eligible securities representing an estimated $30 trillion in potential tokenized assets. However, this figure reflects the value of securities eligible for tokenization, not assets already issued or circulating on blockchain networks.

Bringing even a fraction of this enormous securities market onto Solana could translate into substantial institutional activity and settlement volumes over time. This could create additional opportunities for SOL demand if the token is required for relevant network transactions. Nevertheless, the integration is not expected until the second half of 2027, making the development a potential long-term growth driver rather than an immediate price catalyst. Its ultimate impact will depend on institutional adoption, actual transaction volumes, and whether increased network activity translates into meaningful demand for SOL.
