Former chief of staff to Pentagon chief Hegseth says 'it’s time for Pete to call it quits': report

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Joe Kasper, who served as chief of staff to Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth during the early days of the Trump administration last year, has now said that Hegseth should resign.

"Pete's ability to be effective has been constrained and very soon he’ll have lost any ability to be effective," Kasper said, according to Politico. "For that reason and others, it’s time for Pete to call it quits."

"Pete's been belligerent in his stewardship of an office that will outlive him or any memory of his service," Kasper asserted, the report states.

The outlet reported that Kasper suggested that Hegseth would not be able to work with the nation's legislature or obtain appropriate funds for department priorities.

"He’s not just burned bridges up on the Hill," Kasper reportedly said. "He’s napalmed them. … He’s going to be suffocated, and it’s not like it’s going to be a slow suffocation. It’s going to be a quick asphyxiation."

U.S. Secretary of War Pete Hegseth attends a Halloween event hosted by first lady Melania Trump at the White House on Oct. 29, 2026 in Washington, D.C. (Finn Gomez/Getty Images)

According to Politico, Kasper noted that he understood within a week of joining the Pentagon that Hegseth's focus was "all about Pete rather than the institution and the men and women who support it."

"There’s quite an emphasis on the showmanship," Kasper reportedly said. "The virtues of humility and basically service before self and those things have disappeared. I didn’t see those at the start, and see them ever less so now."

The Pentagon chief "overindulged in ego, in attitude, and in entitlement," Kasper said, according to the report.

He noted that he would rate Hegseth near the bottom 10% of defense secretaries in U.S. history, Politico reported. "I think history will be much crueler to him than I could be," he said.

Due to an executive order that was issued last year, the administration now refers to the Defense Department as the Department of War and to Hegseth as the secretary of war.

A senior department official said in a statement obtained by Fox News Digital, "It’s no surprise that a disgruntled former staffer—someone who could barely survive ninety days in the building—is now peddling made up gossip to the media. Secretary Hegseth is laser focused on restoring lethality to the force and supporting our warfighters, while Mr. Kasper is more focused on rewriting history to cover for his own failed tenure."
