'Grave concerns' mount as Army moves to restart DC helicopter training after deadly 2025 crash

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

President Donald Trump addresses the FAA ground stop and broader technology disruptions, calling the nation’s air traffic control system outdated. Responding to Fox News' Aishah Hasnie, Trump says air travel remains safe and outlines his request for $50 billion from the Senate to upgrade the system.

Criticism is mounting among experts concerned with the U.S. Army's "risky" plan to resume helicopter training flights near Washington National Airport following a deadly midair collision in 2025.

The Army released a plan and told select members of Congress it wants to restart helicopter test flights near DCA — just a few miles from the Pentagon — as soon as Dec. 1, 2026. Backlash immediately ensued.

Rep. Don Beyer, D-Va., asserted on X he has "grave concerns about the decision to resume training flights before the NTSB’s safety recommendations have been fully implemented."

A spokesperson for the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) told Fox News Digital that it "has not received a formal request to resume military helicopter flights around Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport (DCA)."

"Approval from the agency is required before any training in the area can restart," the spokesperson added.

A U.S. Army Black Hawk helicopter collided with American Airlines Flight 5342 in January 2025, killing all 67 people aboard both aircrafts. The FAA subsequently halted military helicopter training flights in the area in May 2025 after another Black Hawk helicopter flew too close to DCA, forcing air traffic controllers to reroute some commercial flights.

In a March 14, 2025, statement the FAA said it was "permanently closing [helicopter] Route 4 between Hains Point and the Wilson Bridge and evaluating alternative helicopter routes as recommended by the NTSB."

Rep. Don Beyer, D-Va., on Monday wrote in an X post that the Army told his office it had reached out to the FAA regarding its request to resume training flights and is currently "awaiting a response."

"Army officials assured my office that no flights will take place unless they are approved by the FAA," Beyer stated.

His office did not expand on specific concerns when reached by Fox News Digital on Tuesday.

An anonymous Army spokesperson told Politico on Monday "there is intent to resume flights," acknowledging that the move would require approval from the FAA.

The DOT referred Fox News Digital to the FAA when reached for comment.

"Members of the investigative team and I were briefed on the Army’s plans to begin the initial phase of a return to helicopter flights to the Pentagon through airspace near DCA as soon as December 1st," NTSB Chairwoman Jennifer Homendy told Fox News Digital.

"It is our understanding that the FAA has yet to receive a formal request for these flights. We have not seen the Army’s concept of operations and look forward to reviewing it once it is formally submitted to the FAA for evaluation."

When reached for comment on Tuesday, the Department of War referred Fox News Digital to the Army. The Army did not respond to Fox News Digital’s request for comment.

Resuming helicopter training flights near DCA could pose "major" safety concerns, according to Randy Klatt, flight safety officer at the Foundation for Aviation Safety (FAS).

"Yes, [there are] major concerns considering that no entity — the airlines, DCA, FAA, or the Army — took any proactive action on the thousands of loss-of-separation incidents that preceded the midair collision. Helicopter Route 4 was a trap as designed, yet it was in use regularly until disaster struck," Klatt told Fox News Digital on Tuesday. "Knowing this does not instill any confidence that the resumption of Army training flights will be done safely and in close coordination with the FAA."

"There has been no mention of reopening Helicopter Route 4 but for the above reasons FAS would strongly oppose such an action," he added.

The air traffic control tower after the American Airlines crash at the Reagan National Airport on Feb. 3, 2025, in Arlington, Virginia. (Kayla Bartkowski/Getty Images)

Heritage Foundation senior research fellow and retired U.S. Navy Capt. Brent Sadler warned Fox News Digital that there is little justification to restart training flights "or even routine flights" in the DCA area.

"Interference to commercial [air] traffic remains a risk," Sadler explained. "Training flights would be better served in a lower activity area at least from commercial civilian air traffic."

"There is little reason to rationalize training flights or even routine flights in proximity to DCA," Sadler continued.
