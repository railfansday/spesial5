Out-of-Network ATMs Often Charge You Twice – Use Your Bank’s Network or Get Refunds

Using an ATM outside your bank’s network often means one fee to your bank and another to the machine’s owner. Avoid both at the free ATMs your bank’s app shows, or get cash back with a debit purchase at a store. If you rely on other banks’ machines, move to an account that refunds ATM fees.

The two fees averaged $4.86 a withdrawal in Bankrate’s 2025 Checking Account and ATM Fee Study, a $3.22 surcharge from the machine’s owner plus $1.64 from the customer’s own bank. Pay $5 a withdrawal once a week, and that adds up to $260 a year.

Neither fee applies at an ATM in your bank’s network, which can include machines the bank doesn’t own. Many banks and credit unions belong to a surcharge-free network such as Allpoint or MoneyPass, and Allpoint alone has over 55,000 ATMs worldwide, in stores like Target, CVS, and Walgreens.

At a store register, ask for cash back when you pay with your debit card. In a 2024 review of eight big chains, the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau found no cash-back fee at Walmart, Target, CVS, Walgreens, or Albertsons. Their cash-back limits ran from $20 at Walgreens to $200 at Albertsons. Kroger’s stores charged 50 or 75 cents on $100 or less, and Dollar General, Dollar Tree, and Family Dollar charged $1 to $2.50.

At another bank’s machine, federal rules make the owner show its fee on the screen or on paper before you’re committed to paying it, and you can still back out.

If you use other banks’ ATMs every week, choose a checking account that refunds their fees, and check its limit. Alliant Credit Union refunds checking customers up to $20 a month in ATM fees, while Schwab Bank Investor Checking refunds ATM withdrawal fees worldwide with no limit, though it’s available only with a linked Schwab One brokerage account.

Before your next withdrawal, open the ATM finder in your bank’s app and save the free machines nearest your home and work.
