Investing - Theory, News &amp; General • Re: Market-cap weighting and company-specific risk

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

It's been quite a while since I've posted here, but there's something I've been wondering about for a long time and I'd be interested in hearing your opinions.

One concern I have about market-cap-weighted index funds is that they might create a rather unusual concentration of company-specific risk, particularly when it comes to the role of CEOs.

Consider what's happening with Nvidia at the moment. A controversy involving just one CEO could potentially have an impact on 7-8% of the S&P 500, given the company's enormous weight in the index.

In an equal-weighted index, the impact of a similar event involving a single company would obviously be much smaller.

And if you look at small-cap indexes, it would take dozens of CEOs facing similar problems at roughly the same time to produce anything close to the same overall impact.

I understand the usual arguments in favour of market-cap weighting. It's simple, inexpensive, market-neutral, and avoids making active bets on individual companies.

But I wonder how we should think about this particular aspect of risk. Doesn't market-cap weighting also mean that we become disproportionately exposed to the fortunes, and sometimes even the personal conduct, of a relatively small number of individuals?

Is this simply part of the concentration risk we have to accept when investing in the market portfolio, or is there a genuine diversification advantage to equal-weighted indexes that tends to be overlooked in the usual discussion?

I'd be interested to hear how other Bogleheads see this.
