Sober Glory storms to victory over Old Park Star on chase debut

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

A dramatic afternoon at Ffos Las saw Sober Glory soar to success on chase debut for the Hobbs and White team with old rival Old Park Star fading into third with a broken blood vessel.

Sober Glory turned the tables on Supreme Novices’ Hurdle conqueror Old Park Star to make a faultless chasing bow in the Golwg Gwendraeth Beginners’ Chase at Ffos Las.

One of the most eagerly-anticipated showdowns of the early season as the dry spell forced the exciting duo to west Wales, the main protagonists were immediately to the fore with Philip Hobbs and Johnson White's Sober Glory the more sure-footed of the duo in the early exchanges.

There was little to separate the pair as a fine leap four out brought the Nico de Boinville-ridden Old Park Star alongside, but Ben Jones soon reclaimed the advantage in the home straight aboard the 5-4 second favourite.

With the gap growing all the way up the home straight, the Cheltenham Festival runner-up gained revenge over Nicky Henderson's 4-5 favourite, sauntering to an impressive nine-and-a-half length opening victory over fences.

Co-trainer White said: "It was a very pleasing performance jumping wise and there was a few novicey mistakes, but that is understandable for a first run over the bigger obstacles.

"I was delighted with the authority in which he won the race and he had a very good horse a good few lengths behind.

"His schooling has been very good all autumn, but you still have to go out and put that into practice on the course. Ben Jones says he's only going to get better with experience, but that has to be a very nice starting point.

"There is very few parts you could be disappointed with there. He's jumped well, travelled nicely and he's beaten good opposition so it's a very encouraging start to the season."

BOYLE Sports cut Sober Glory to 7-2 joint-favourite for the Arkle alongside Dan Skelton's Kabral du Mathan and while Hobbs and White will work a path back from that Cheltenham Festival event, they could clash early next month at Sandown in the Henry VIII Chase.

White added: "We'll get him home and reassess, but I think you have to aim at the Arkle and work back from there. Sandown would certainly be one of the races on the agenda along the way."

One horse who will not be in Esher on December 5 is Old Park Star after he faded into third, with Henderson left in "shock" after the six-year-old bled as his four-race winning streak came to an end.

"He's broken a blood vessel and bled so that explains it and it's a not just a bit of a shock, but a huge shock," said the Seven Barrows handler. "These things do happen, but we always just pray to God they don't but it has and it is as simple as that.

"It explains things but does create a massive headache. It's a blow when it happens to one like him and at the moment it is just a massive shock really.

"We'll get him back but he obviously won't be going to the Henry VIII which was the race we were trying to get him ready for. We've got the problem and now we have to try and resolve it - we will do our best."
