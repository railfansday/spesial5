Frick enjoys winning start with Aberdeen with victory at Kilmarnock

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Kilmarnock vs Aberdeen. Scottish Premiership.

Report as Mario Frick makes a winning start as Aberdeen head coach; Kevin Nisbet scored a double in their 4-2 win at Kilmarnock; the Dons are up to eighth in the Scottish Premiership; Killie remain bottom

Mario Frick enjoyed a dream start to life as Aberdeen boss by edging a six-goal thriller with a 4-2 win over Kilmarnock.

Kevin Nisbet's double, coupled with Brad Lyons and Findlay Marshall strikes, secured three priceless Scottish Premiership points to leave the Rugby Park side rooted to the bottom.

The visitors climbed to eighth spot after a remarkable afternoon where they hit a much-needed scoring streak.

The Dons opted to dispense with the services of boss Stephen Robinson after a run of only one win from seven which saw them sink to second-bottom and Frick was appointed as his replacement last Sunday.

It should have been advantage to the hosts in the seventh minute as Greg Kiltie picked out Joe Hugill and the striker went one-on-one with Marius Muller but could only rattle his shot into the body of the Dons 'keeper. They were made to pay moments later.

Another slick Aberdeen attack had skipper Nisbet taking full advantage of some familiar hesitancy in the Killie defence to sidefoot a shot into Nico Mantl's bottom left-hand corner and Frick's reign was off and running in emphatic style.

It would get better in the 10th minute as Ieuan Owen was booked for a cynical foul on Nicky Devlin and Nisbet stepped up to brilliantly curl his 20-yard free-kick low past Mantl via post for his 15th goal of the season.

As the Red Army celebrated an astonishing reversal of fortunes, Hicham Mahou rattled a shot in from distance which sailed inches wide of the target as Killie struggled to regroup from the double-whammy but the pulled one back in the 20th minute.

It was a much-needed response as Greg Kiltie reacted first to a loose ball to drive a low shot past Muller and at a stroke the contest was back in the balance.

That would shift again on the half-hour mark as the Dons made it three with Lyons running onto a Nesta Guinness-Walker cross from the left to bury a header past Mantl as the goal-fest continued.

Killie almost reduced the deficit moments after the restart as Michael Schjonning-Larsen's strike was superbly turned away by Muller as the Rugby Park side looked to launch a rescue act.

Manti then blocked a Nisbet shot with his legs as the Scotland international went in search of his hat-trick in the 52nd minute but Kilie were still in the fight and they threw themselves a lifeline five minutes later as Muller gifted possession to Tyreece John-Jules who gratefully accepted by clipping his finish high into the net.

Jamie Brandon squandered a terrific chance to square the game in the 73rd when he blazed a shot over the bar from 16 yards with the goal gaping and with that the Dons held on to see out a contest which allows Frisk to get off to a winning start as the last word went to Marshall who drilled in a fourth three minutes from time.

Mario Frick predicted Kevin Nisbet would get his name on the scoresheet:

"I spoke to Kevin during the week and told him he would score and he scored twice, so he showed how dangerous he is inside the box. He could have scored three but I was very happy for him.

"I can enjoy it now but during the game it was difficult. We were lucky as well as Killie played very well, especially in the second half.

"We had some difficulties but in the end it was a deserved victory. We can defend better as we were lucky, We struggled in the first five or six minutes but after taking the lead we played well. But it was a bit chaotic and I was surprised.

"I told the players to play the way they did when they were young and they didn't think about mistakes or any negatives and played only to win for fun. I told them to play as they did as young players. Watching the third goal was beautiful, it's only the start and a beginning as we need to improve."

Killie boss Neil McCann admitted their winless run has to end, quickly:

"We are just conceding cheap goals and the boys are working so hard in training but when it comes into a game then that's when you really need to execute as that's when it matters most.

"When we managed to get ourselves back into the game at 2-1 I felt that we would go on and create more chances and then we conceded a third.

"In the second half we changed things around a bit as well as some personnel and adjusted how we were playing and I felt from the start of the second half onwards we were the better side.

"We were done on the counter-attack for the fourth goal and when you strip it all down I probably sound like a broken record. but the amount of chances we created and didn't take them....

"It's a sore one as we have worked so hard but it's in games where it matters most and every game is a chance to arrest that."
