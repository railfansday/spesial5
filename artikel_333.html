In Argentina, Pope Leo Walks in the Giant Shadow of Francis

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Francis never returned to his home country after becoming pope, but the new pontiff will still be confronted with the overwhelming presence of his predecessor.

A swimming pool named after Pope Francis in La Matanza, outside Buenos Aires.Credit...

When the Argentine priest was reminded that the Roman Catholic Church no longer had an Argentine pope, he let out an exasperated chuckle.

“We are already mourning Messi,” said the priest, the Rev. Lorenzo de Vedia, referring to Lionel Messi’s departure from the Argentine national soccer team. “Two is too much.”

Though Pope Francis, the first Argentine pope, died over a year ago and was succeeded by Pope Leo shortly after, Argentines have hardly moved on. Francis’ photo still adorns public schools, parishes and trade unions. Murals and mosaics of the Argentine pope dominate public spaces, street vendors still sell his figurines, parish priests quote him in their homilies.

With Leo visiting the country, Argentines are finally confronting the reality that the leader of the Catholic Church no longer speaks Porteño, the Buenos Aires dialect; roots for the city’s beloved San Lorenzo team; or shares their ritual of mate — the national drink.

As Argentines face this new reality, their reception is permeated with the pressing question of whether Leo represents an expression of Francis’ continuity. And while the answer is important to the former pope’s compatriots, it is a question much of the world has been asking.
