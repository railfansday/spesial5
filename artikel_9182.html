Hockney and His Beverly Hills Patron

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

A review of James Cahill’s book on the painter and the patron in “Beverly Hills Housewife,” the state of arts journalism, and why Tribal Nations are opposing a Congressional bill.

It’s a beautiful day to support indie publications! Last week, a survey conducted by Critical Minded and the Rabkin Foundation quantified the precarity of independent US-based art magazines amid a climate of censorship, corporate takeovers, and economic instability. It’s no wonder, when media companies decide to partner with Amazon on art initiatives (cough, cough, Penske). Senior Editor Valentina Di Liscia breaks down the survey results and what they can teach us about the future of independent, critical arts journalism.

Speaking of which, Staff Writer Anna Lee reports on a coalition of Tribal Nations opposing a bill that would threaten Native sites if Congress passes it. Longtime Angeleno Matt Stromberg considers an engrossing if occasionally overwrought book by James Cahill that explores the influential friendship between David Hockney and Betty Freeman, the arts patron at the center of one of his most recognizable paintings. And if that isn’t enough, Aaron Short shares a wry dispatch from the preview of the Art Dealers Association of America’s art fair in New York City — the tickets may have been $250 a pop, but at least there were unlimited dumplings and champagne to sweeten the deal?

—Lakshmi Rivera Amin, associate editor, books
