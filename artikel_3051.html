Strategic Education: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Australia's visa crackdown forced a $134 million writedown, yet management now expects flat 2027 revenue and is redirecting capital.

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 5, 2026, Strategic Education (STRA -0.99%) announced flat year-over-year third-quarter revenue of $320 million as growth in software and healthcare offset regulatory headwinds in Australia. The company's performance shows how targeted expansion in high-demand degree programs can cushion macro disruptions.

Education Technology Services (ETS), the division housing digital learning platform Sophia Learning and tuition-benefit software Workforce Edge, remains a prime engine of organic sales growth. Management plans to increase segment investments next year to protect market leadership and speed up product rollouts.

Healthcare courses now account for 54% of total U.S. Higher Education enrollment, up from 49% last year, driven by corporate partnerships and clinical training offerings. Strategic Education is committing extra capital to expand nursing faculty and campus instructional capacity to meet student demand.

Government restrictions in Australia sharply curtailed international student visa approvals, leading Strategic Education to record a $134 million non-cash impairment of goodwill and intangible assets, which is an accounting writedown of asset values. The company is pivoting capital toward domestic Australian enrollment, which grew 9% during the quarter.

Management views growth investments in digital learning and healthcare as levers for expanding market share. Cost reductions will absorb nearly half of next year's $50 million funding expansion.

Executive leadership projects stable sales while international visa restrictions run their course.
