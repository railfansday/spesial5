Personal Finance (Not Investing) • Can we comfortably retire with $5.45M invested and $185K annual spending?

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

I'm looking for feedback from the Bogleheads community on whether our retirement plan is financially sound and whether we are being too conservative or optimistic.

- Married couple living in Texas in mid 50s. 2 kids with a small 529 to support their UG; PG support if we can.
- Approximately $5.45 million in investment assets across taxable brokerage, 401(k), IRA and Roth accounts.
- Estimated retirement spending of $185,000 annually in today's dollars, including housing, healthcare, travel and discretionary spending.
- Initial spending represents approximately 3.4% of current investment assets.
- We expect Social Security benefits later in retirement around 70, about 3000 per month if all stays the same.
- We are willing to reduce discretionary spending during significant market declines by 30K.


Retirement planning:

We have built Monte Carlo simulations incorporating retirement spending, taxes, Social Security, healthcare expenses and potential long-term care costs in various tools.

The simulations suggest approximately 94%–95% retirement success under our current assumptions, with flexible discretionary spending.

We are also evaluating:

- Roth conversions before and during retirement.
- ACA marketplace insurance and likelyhood
- A TIPS ladder covering some spending needs from now through social security.
- Tax-efficient withdrawals from different account types.

Our main concerns:

1. Is $185K annual spending sustainable with approximately $5.45M invested, especially for a potentially long retirement?
2. In a Bogleheads portfolio what % shd be stocks.
3. Is allocating 20% to TIPS, short-term Treasuries and other safety reserves reasonable?
4. Should we prioritize protecting against sequence-of-returns risk over maximizing investment returns?
5. How should we approach Roth conversions and ACA income management? is up to 22% tax roth conversion better?
6. What are the biggest weaknesses or unrealistic assumptions in our retirement plan?

What we're trying to determine:

We are not necessarily trying to maximize our ending portfolio, but like leaving some for kids. Our priority is maintaining a comfortable retirement lifestyle without excessive investment risk.

I'd particularly appreciate feedback from people who have retired with similar assets and spending levels.

Would you feel comfortable retiring under these circumstances? What would you change?
