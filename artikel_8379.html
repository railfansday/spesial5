Te Araroa day 16: Walking to paradise in Ngunguru

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

I finally fell asleep perhaps a couple of hours before my alarm, and was rudely awakened at 6am. I slowly got myself together and got moving. My tent had left a perfect dry rectangle on the ground – good to know it’s waterproof, I guess! Cody and Eve left first, and I think I was next. Everyone else was planning to hitch the road parts, but I was still keen to walk for the time being.

Longest footbridge in the Southern Hemisphere

Straight out of the gate, the trail hit Whananaki’s claim to fame; the longest footbridge in the Southern Hemisphere. To be honest, it didn’t feel that long (or that sturdy!) but it got me over the water. On the other side, the low-tide route ran along the edge of the estuary, but since the tide was in I took a gravel road. This road ran alongside some paddocks, where I saw some Paradise ducklings! To be fair, they were maybe more like adolescents, but they were still cute and fluffy.

The coast track from then on was pretty nice. It wound its way up through some farmland and past baches (holiday homes), with some nice coastal views along the way. There was a bit of rain on and off, but then it got quite sunny! I caught up to Eve and Cody, then later they overtook me when I stopped to apply sunscreen. All too soon the nice track ran out and I was back on the inevitable road. At first just a gravel road, then a tarmacked one. At the sealed road, Cody and Eve stopped to try and hitch. I put on my hi-viz vest and carried on. The road went up a substantial hill, and lots of classic cars were passing me from behind. I guess there must have been some meet up or something going on. After a few kilometres, I was walking towards the junction where the trail turned and could see a couple of hikers standing there. I assumed it must be Eve and Cody, but actually it was Lindsey and Sven. They had got a ride more easily and passed the others on the road – apparently Sven has unreasonably good luck with hitching. I turned up the next road, while Lindsey and Sven debated how long to wait for a hitch. It was a quieter road and they had been there a little while at least. I thought it would be pretty funny if I beat all the hitchers by walking!

The road went on for a few more kilometres. I was pretty over the road walking by this point. I stopped for lunch in a shaded layby, and an older man came down from his house to talk to me. I explained what I was doing and he seemed pretty interested, and I might have even talked him into giving thru-hiking a go himself! He seemed particularly interested in the E1 route, which goes from the top of Norway to the bottom of Italy. I haven’t looked into it much myself, but I think that would be an awesome long-term section hiking goal for when I’m back in Europe.

A short while later the trail turned onto a gravel road, which became a bike track. I decided I needed some musical motivation for the afternoon and got my tunes going. The bike track was quite nice, but still road-adjacent, and I knew I had yet more road at the end of it.

As I popped out at the bottom of the bike track, I saw two things that increased my motivation no end. First, I saw some horses by the road, although they weren’t that keen to talk to me. Then, I saw a hiker up ahead. I couldn’t quite tell who it was, but I was determined to catch up and find out. I turned on my speed and zoomed up the road.

The hiker turned out to be Lucy, and we chatted for a bit before I left her behind. The Ngunguru (which I think is pronounced nu-nu-ru?) shops were so close now, and I could practically taste the ice cream and cold fizzy drink I would be buying myself. Eventually, I made it to the food store. Cody and Eve’s packs were already positioned outside the door, and I found the two of them perusing the shelves inside. The shop was surprisingly well stocked, and I wished I’d saved the stuff I had bought at Whananaki to buy here instead. I was able to get the things I wanted and needed, and headed outside to enjoy my pistachio Magnum and orange Fanta. Lucy had also arrived at this point, and she and I headed onwards while Cody and Eve had lunch at a takeaway.

To get to our next campsite at Nikau Bay, we needed a boat ride across the river. We let the host James know that we were close, and Lucy went for a swim while we waited for everyone else. The others all arrived in due course, and we headed down to the water’s edge. There were 8 of us, and apart from Riv and I everyone decided to swim across. The tide was quite far out, and I definitely could have done it if I needed to, but I’m not a particularly strong or enthusiastic swimmer. James arrived in his boat at some speed, and we loaded up the packs. Riv and I stayed behind while James ferried the packs over, then got a ride over afterwards. I think the boat might actually have been more adrenaline-inducing than the swim!

The campsite was amazing. Loads of flat green grass to pitch our tents on, a beautiful open-air communal kitchen and lounge area, great hot showers… a hiker’s paradise! We agreed that we would love to take a rest day there, and it turned out that an American couple had done just that. They came back with a kayak they had borrowed as I was pitching my tent. I was so glad I ended up coming here. I almost took the detour to skip out some silly wiggliness, but ended up tagging along with all the others. And it was a great choice!

As we were all making and eating dinner, James brought us a big pot of rice and some delicious greens from his garden. Fresh food!! Then he gave a briefing on the next section of track. Earlier we had been discussing the estuary crossing that was coming up. I hadn’t looked that far ahead yet, so it was a good thing that the others were on it. The Taiharuru estuary, 26km from where we were, can be safely crossed two hours either side of low tide, but low tide for the next couple of days was at quite inconvenient times. We hatched a plan to set off early the next day and hitch as much as we could to get to the estuary in time to cross that afternoon. The alternative was to walk to the next campsite, and have to wait around all of the next morning for low tide. James reassured us that we should be able to get a hitch – the road was a dead-end, but it leads to a beach and it was going to be a sunny Sunday.

After eating and talking, the others lit a campfire. But I was tired, and I headed to my tent to be ready for the early start the next morning.
