Susan’s Minnesota Garden, Part 2: Spring and Summer

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Hi GPODers! We’re back in St. Paul, Minnesota (Zone 5a), for Part 2 of Susan Warde’s garden tour! Earlier this week, Susan shared a look at her garden from late July through October. Today we’re heading back to spring to see what was growing earlier in the season. Let’s take a look!

Here are some photos from late May through July.

The back garden. Wild cranesbill (Geranium maculatum, Zones 3–8) in the upper left corner is the only plant in flower at this time (late May), but the foliage is pretty, I think. The dominant hosta is ‘Blue Ivory’ (Hosta ‘Blue Ivory’, Zones 3–9).

Front garden (late May). I think the azalea is ‘Rosy Lights’ (Rhododendron ‘Rosy Lights’, Zones 3–7) from the Northern Lights azalea series.

Tall bearded iris (Iris ‘Cherished’, Zones 3–9), late May.

Tall bearded iris ‘Sultry Mood’, late May.

A Siberian iris (Iris sibirica cvs., Zones 3–9); I don’t know the cultivar name (late May).

My earliest clematis (Clematis ‘Acropolis’, Zones 4–9), with ‘Kansas’ peony (Paeonia lactiflora ‘Kansas’, Zones 3–8) in the background (early June).

This Asiatic lily (Lilium ‘Stracciatella Event’, Zones 3–8) was labeled ‘Stracciatella Event’, but it doesn’t look like the photos of that cultivar. Within a couple of days of this photo, taken July 6, the lily was demolished by scarlet lily beetles, which ultimately made mincemeat of all my lilies. The foliage looked so terrible that I had to cut them all down.

‘Lavender Moonchild’ daylily (Hemerocallis ‘Lavender Moonchild’, Zones 3–9), mid-July. Deer are a very rare occurrence in the city. I hadn’t seen one for years, but this summer two young bucks roamed the neighborhood, and every night they snacked on daylily buds and blossoms. They were not deterred by either of the sprays I tried.

This daylily is ‘Marquee Moon’. Set farther back in the garden, it was spared by the deer (mid-July).

A view of the side yard (July 25). Daylilies, garden phlox (Phlox paniculata cvs., Zones 4–8), coneflowers (Echinacea cvs., Zones 3–9), black-eyed Susans (Rudbeckia cvs., Zones 3–9), and blazing stars (Liatris cvs., Zones 3–9) are all in bloom.

Thank you for sharing another look at your garden, Susan! I especially enjoyed seeing your collection of irises and daylilies, although I’m sorry the deer and lily beetles caused so much trouble this year.
