Roblox wants players to celebrate Thanksgiving by building a “ridiculously tall tower”

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Last month, Roblox ushered in Halloween with a platform-wide horror event stocked with mazes, monsters, and general mayhem. That nine-night event—called The Hollow—featured creepy creatures from a variety of well-known Roblox experiences, including 99 Nights in the Forest, DOORS, Dandy’s World, Scary Shawarma Kiosk, and more.

Now, Roblox is switching gears from horror to family-friendly holiday fun. Ahead of Thanksgiving, the platform is inviting players to express their gratitude by building something tall—“like, really tall.”

Starting tomorrow, the sandbox platform will kick off The Great Build: a global community event culminating in the creation of “a massive tower of gratitude.” According to a press release, the event will run through Nov. 30 and is designed to encourage players around the world to “share what they’re thankful for and work together toward a shared goal.”

The process is as simple as it is wholesome. Every hour, players will receive a free block that they can personalize with a message “inspired by gratitude traditions from around the world” and add to the tower, Roblox says.

Players can also climb The Great Build as it grows, and will unlock free avatar items as different community milestones are achieved.

A gratitude-themed event aimed at global unity is exactly the kind of positive PR Roblox needs right now.

According to Latin Times, the sandbox platform is currently facing almost 200 consolidated federal lawsuits, with states and parents alike pushing back against the platform’s approach to child safety.

Legislators are bearing down on Roblox, too. Earlier this month, Senators Josh Hawley and Dick Durbin proposed the “No Forced Arbitration for Child Sexual Exploitation and Cyber Injuries Act,” which would allow families to take Roblox to court regardless of forced arbitration terms.

The “first-ever congressional hearing on Roblox safety” followed the bill’s introduction and included appearances from both legal experts and survivors of child sexual exploitation.

Roblox CEO David Baszucki, on the other hand, reportedly sent a “last-minute letter instead of testifying before the Senate subcommittee.” We’re guessing the platform’s Thanksgiving tower of gratitude won’t be quite tall enough to overshadow that absence.
