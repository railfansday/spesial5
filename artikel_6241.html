It's Official: Lufthansa Signs $1.2 Billion Deal To Purchase 44.9% Of TAP Air Portugal

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Lufthansa Group and the Portuguese government have officially signed an agreement for the German aviation giant to acquire a 44.9% stake in TAP Air Portugal for €1.025 billion (approximately $1.2 billion). The signing took place in Lisbon earlier today, nearly three weeks after Portugal selected Lufthansa ahead of rival Air France-KLM. The transaction remains subject to regulatory approval.

The agreement represents another milestone in Lufthansa's European airline consolidation strategy. It could add Lisbon as the group's seventh hub while significantly strengthening its position in Latin America. However, the deal also raises two important questions: how did Lufthansa beat Air France-KLM, the airline group that many experts favored, and can it replicate IAG's success with Iberia?

The agreement was officially signed at Lisbon Airport (LIS), with Lufthansa funding the acquisition entirely from existing liquidity. Euronews reports that the group could increase its holding to 49.9% by purchasing shares initially reserved for TAP employees, potentially lifting total consideration to €1.2 billion. Portugal will initially retain majority ownership, while European Commission approval is still required before completion.

Lufthansa CEO Carsten Spohr emphasized that the airlines' networks are complementary, particularly in South America and Brazil. Lisbon will become Lufthansa Group's seventh hub, with further growth planned across Portuguese-speaking Africa and North America. The group also expects up to €450 million in synergies, with most potentially achievable before acquiring majority ownership.

“Together with TAP's employees, we want to continue the airline's success story, preserve its Portuguese identity, and develop Lisbon into a Lufthansa Group hub.”

Lufthansa has also welcomed the possibility of TAP's full privatization, while its commitments include maintaining the airline's Portuguese identity, strengthening regional connectivity, and expanding investment in Portugal. The government has outlined potential fleet growth to 130 aircraft, while Lufthansa Technik's new facility near Porto should help increase the group's Portuguese workforce to more than 1,000 by 2030.

Air France-KLM initially appeared to be the more attractive buyer. While Lufthansa enjoys an existing relationship with TAP through Star Alliance, potentially simplifying commercial cooperation, the Franco-Dutch group seemed the more complementary partner with its hubs at Paris and Amsterdam. Most crucially, Air France-KLM wasn't already entangled with the integration of a prior acquisition, as Lufthansa is with ITA Airways.

But ultimately, the financial offer proved decisive. Portugal revealed that Lufthansa's proposal was up to 16% higher than Air France-KLM's, with less uncertainty. Both bidders had improved their proposals during final negotiations, and their strategic commitments had become broadly comparable. Prime Minister Luís Montenegro described Lufthansa as "the choice that best protected Portugal's national interests."

Lufthansa's experience integrating SWISS, Austrian Airlines, Brussels Airlines, and ITA Airways provides another potential advantage. Its multi-brand model allows carriers to retain their identities while sharing commercial and operational resources. However, TAP's minority ownership structure presents challenges: Lufthansa must deliver substantial synergies without initially enjoying the control associated with its other acquisitions.

The $1.2 billion investment is more than three times what it paid for 41% of ITA back in 2025. So why is it splurging on a smaller European carrier? Brazil provides the answer. The group estimates its share of Europe–Latin America traffic at approximately 9%, rising to a calculated 20% when TAP is included. More specifically, Lisbon's established connections to Brazil offer access to markets where Lufthansa's existing network is comparatively limited, helping it compete more effectively with IAG and Air France-KLM.

The obvious comparison is the creation of IAG and the transformation of Iberia following its 2011 merger with British Airways. After restructuring, Iberia developed into one of Europe's most profitable airlines, recording a 16.2% operating margin and €1.31 billion in operating profit in 2025. Its Europe–Latin America and Caribbean capacity reached a record 5.5 million seats, demonstrating the financial potential of a strong transatlantic network.

Lufthansa hopes to replicate that success through coordinated schedules, purchasing, distribution, and stronger connecting traffic. However, Iberia's turnaround occurred under IAG's full ownership, whereas Lufthansa must initially work alongside the Portuguese government. With TAP also facing profitability pressures, the €1 billion investment is a significant bet that its valuable Brazilian network can deliver an Iberia-style transformation.
