Carolina Herrera Takes Miami with a New Art-Filled Boutique

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The store brings Herrera’s signature color and feminine spirit to the Design District, with works by women artists throughout.

Above: Inside Carolina Herrera’s Miami flagship, a sculptural chandelier by artist Liz Oliver hangs suspended above a soft pink couch.

In September, Carolina Herrera held an extravaganza of a runway show during New York Fashion Week, celebrating the brand’s 45th anniversary. This past week, the festivities went further south, to Miami. Last Thursday night, creative director Wes Gordon and members of the city’s fashion and arts community gathered beneath a cherry-red awning to toast the brand’s newest boutique—and sixth official location in the U.S.

Opened on August 15, the 3,000-square-foot shop sits in the middle of the Miami Design District, with a dramatic arched entrance welcoming guests into the limestone-finished structure. Designed in collaboration with interior designers Brandi Howe and Jessica Hyland of Hyland Studio, the store is bursting with color and feminine details—soft pink travertine covers the floor while furnishings feature red and pink accents. In the center of the main salon, a custom pink sofa with graceful curvature offers an elegant seat; in another area, the same red awning from the boutique’s entrance reappears suspended over a pair of regal armchairs.

“The Carolina Herrera woman is bold, elegant, and joyful. She embraces color, expresses herself with confidence, and never shies away from making an entrance,” says Gordon.

But the new store is not only an ode to the Carolina Herrera brand but also to other female creatives. On view throughout the space are artworks commissioned specifically for the boutique as a part of the Carolina Herrera for Women in the Arts initiative. In the main salon, Los Angeles-based artist Liz Oliver designed a rose-colored textile installation that doubles as a sculptural chandelier. Elsewhere, ceramic planters made by the women-owned Choplet Ceramics are on display. Throughout the boutique, a recurring custom floral motif by Argentine artist Constanza Vallese appears on vases and tables.

In raising a glass to the boutique, guests at last Thursday’s event honored both the Carolina Herrera woman and her new city. “Her energy is magnetic—much like the spirit of Miami,” says Gordon. “It feels entirely natural that we should open our next home here.”
