Zohran Mamdani’s Mustache Is Singlehandedly Bringing Movember Back

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

When New York City mayor Zohran Mamdani passed out water bottles to marathoners on Nov. 1, few clocked the newly mustachioned man as the city’s head honcho. After all, he’s the first bearded NYC mayor in over a century after, and his constituents had grown familiar with his signature look. When images of his new facial hair circulated, people wondered what inspired the change. Had the mayor been influenced by his hip Brooklyn constituents enough to grow his own on-trend mustache? As we learned the day after the marathon in an online video, the mustache was in support of a somewhat-ancient tradition: Movember.

If it feels like no one’s talked about Movember for a while, that’s because the non-profit charity’s heyday online peaked in 2012. That year, according to the Australian-based foundation, over 1.1 million people participated in Movember—a campaign that encourages men to grow mustaches to raise awareness and funds for men’s mental health, prostate cancer, and testicular cancer—collecting more than $126 million in charity. But like planking, “Gangnam Style,” and flash mobs, Movember eventually faded from the cultural conversation. Google Trends recorded a 10% drop in searches for the cause the following year, and by 2026, search interest had fallen 90% from its 2012 peak. Now, after Mamdani, alongside Council Member Rita Joseph, announced a $4 million investment in a new prostate cancer center at Brooklyn’s Kings County Hospital in honor of the hairy month-long social movement, interest in Movember has since climbed to its highest level since 2019, according to Google Trends.

While the mayor’s stubble has come and gone throughout the month, the heavy mustache has remained constant.

The stubble around the mustache has come and gone during the month, but that thick mustache has remained a staple. He had it at a Veterans Day reception, during a plethora of press conferences, and when he announced the disbanding of the New York Police Department’s controversial Strategic Response Group.

Politicians have long been defined, at least in part, by their hair: Donald Trump’s comb-over, Bernie Sanders’ horseshoe-shaped hairline, Gavin Newsom’s slicked-back locks. But facial hair is increasingly becoming part of the political image, too, as more men embrace letting their beards grow. Pete Buttigieg, once clean-shaven, grew a beard after leaving his post as Transportation Secretary; JD Vance became the first bearded vice president in decades; and Mamdani ended more than a century’s worth of clean-shaven NYC mayors.

And hey, what better time to revive an early 2010s tradition than following the Democrats’ historic victories in the midterm elections. Millennial woke optimism is back, baby, and so is growing facial hair for a charitable cause. If the mayor’s facial hair can turn a bygone internet fad into renewed attention for men’s health, it may be his most consequential grooming decision yet.
