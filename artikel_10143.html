Japan's Fugaku supercomputer has decidedly un-super security

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Attackers try logging in to Earth’s ninth most powerful machine after status page hack exposed credentials

The RIKEN Center for Computational Science, the home of the Fugaku supercomputer, has revealed unauthorized attempts to access the machine after a data leak.

According to a statement issued by RIKEN yesterday, between November 10 and 13 an unknown entity attacked a web dashboard that displays operational status data for Fugaku.

It sounds like whatever the attackers did to that dashboard allowed them to move across the RIKEN network, because they were able to reach several servers that store data belonging to authorized users, and install malware.

The servers stored users’ names and email addresses, plus credentials used to access supercomputers and services not operated by RIKEN.

The attacker has tried to use those credentials. RIKEN is working with the organizations that host those other supercomputers to investigate whether the attackers succeeded.

The research outfit doesn’t know how the attack happened and can’t say with certainty what information attackers were able to access and/or steal, beyond saying that 4,222 registered users of Fugaku are at risk, along with 375 registered users of its smaller Rikyu supercomputer.

RIKEN does know that on November 13, someone attempted to log onto Fugaku. RIKEN quickly took the machine offline.

Fugaku comprises 158,976 nodes, each packing a 48-core processor and 32GiB of available RAM, for a total of 7,630,848 cores and over five petabytes of memory. Those specs saw Fugaku lead the Top 500 lists of Earth’s mightiest supercomputers for two years between 2020 and 2022. It ranks ninth on the update of the list released on Tuesday. RIKEN’s other super, Rikyu, operates 288,000 cores.

If attackers managed to run a workload on either machine, they could do a lot of damage.

The intrusion has already had serious consequences because Fugaku is currently offline. As the machine is used for scientific research, and RIKEN schedules use of the machine, the outage will disrupt researchers who were waiting for their turn to use the supercomputer.

At the time of writing, RIKEN has found no evidence that users’ research data has leaked but isn’t yet sure what info did make it out of the building and whether it contains any data describing third parties.

The research org has promised to conduct a detailed investigation into the incident, which is the latest in a long string of significant cyberattacks that have struck Japanese clouds, telcos, retailers and government agencies, leading Tokyo to release emergency funds in the hope that extra money can quickly shore up the nation’s cyber-defenses. ®
