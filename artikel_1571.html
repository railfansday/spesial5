Diet at disease onset determines tissue inflammatory memory

Obesity, which is often associated with prolonged exposure to an obesogenic diet, substantially alters immunity1,2,3,4,5,6,7, but whether brief dietary changes shape acute inflammatory responses and long-term tissue memory remains unclear. Here we show that transient high-fat diet (HFD) exposure at the onset of acute type 17 skin inflammation exacerbates disease in lean mice to levels comparable to those associated with chronic obesity. This effect is restricted to a discrete 4-day peri-initiation window; HFD outside this window has no impact, whereas switching obese mice to a low-fat diet (LFD) during a similar window fully blunts disease amplification. Mechanistically, HFD rapidly recruits neutrophils into the skin, and neutrophils in challenged skin express high levels of Nlrp3 (ref. 8) and Il1b. Neutrophil depletion, genetic Nlrp3 deficiency and pharmacological NLRP3 inhibition each blunt the HFD-induced inflammatory amplification. These findings nominate a neutrophil–NLRP3–IL-1β axis that licences pathogenic IL-1R1+CD4+ T helper type 17 (TH17) cells9,10. After recovery, a chromatin-poised tissue-resident memory T cell population persists. Consequently, mice exposed to HFD at disease onset develop exaggerated inflammation upon recurrence, despite recovery and maintenance on LFD. Transient HFD similarly amplifies lung TH17 inflammation through neutrophil- and IL-1R1-dependent mechanisms. Together, these findings show that dietary state during a narrow temporal window can durably reprogram disease trajectory, suggesting that short-lived physiological states may become embedded in local immune memory11 when they coincide with tissue inflammation.

Obesity alters immune function across multiple contexts, including infection, autoimmunity and cancer7,12,13,14,15,16,17. This effect is especially pronounced in the skin, where obesity exacerbates inflammation and complicates therapeutic responses6,18. Although chronic obesity and weeks to months of obesogenic diet exposure are known to remodel cutaneous immunity, these longer interventions also produce cumulative metabolic, endocrine, microbial and tissue remodelling across multiple organ systems7. It remains unclear whether comparatively brief, days-scale dietary exposures, before such systemic remodelling is established, can acutely modify triggered inflammatory responses and durably imprint long-term tissue inflammatory memory. Experimental models of acute type 17 skin inflammation provide an opportunity to examine these effects, as psoriasis, a type 17 inflammatory disease tightly linked to obesity4,15, has also been associated with dietary patterns and other nutritional exposures19,20,21. We therefore examined whether transient dietary state could bidirectionally modify the response to a synchronized inflammatory trigger, such that HFD amplifies inflammation in lean mice whereas LFD restrains inflammation in obese mice, and whether this acute dietary window could alter subsequent tissue inflammatory recall.

To distinguish the impact of diet itself from the consequences of established obesity, we used topical imiquimod (IMQ) challenge as a synchronized model of acute type 17 skin inflammation in which age-matched mice were fed either a short-term (1 week) or long-term (10 weeks) HFD (60% kcal from fat), or a low-fat (control) diet (LFD; 10% kcal from fat), before topical IMQ challenge22 (Fig. 1a). Mice remained on their assigned diets throughout IMQ challenge. Measurements of serum cytokine levels before application of IMQ did not reveal significant differences between the three groups (Extended Data Fig. 1a), although we did observe modestly increased numbers of TH17 cells and γδ T cells in the skin of mice fed a short- and long-term HFD, relative to the mice fed LFD (Extended Data Fig. 1b–d). Both short- and long-term HFD also reduced glucose tolerance, although these effects were more pronounced in obese mice that were fed a long-term HFD (Extended Data Fig. 2a,b).

As expected, 10 weeks of HFD caused marked obesity, whereas HFD for 1 week did not (Fig. 1b). Despite this, both regimens exacerbated IMQ-induced skin inflammation comparably, with approximately 2.1-fold (1 week) and 2.3-fold (10 week) increases in ear thickness relative to the LFD-fed mice (Fig. 1c). Gross examination revealed equivalent levels of ear shortening (Fig. 1d), and histological analysis showed dermal and epidermal expansion in both groups (Fig. 1e). Notably, 1 week of feeding on a medium-fat diet (MFD; 45% kcal from fat) was sufficient to exacerbate disease to a similar extent to the HFD (60% kcal from fat) (Extended Data Fig. 2c,d), indicating that neither extreme fat exposure nor established obesity is required to amplify inflammation. Consistently, western diet (WD; 40 kcal% fat; 29 kcal% sucrose) feeding amplified inflammation after IMQ treatment to the same extent as MFD feeding (Extended Data Fig. 2e). Further, WD or HFD feeding alone did not induce overt ear inflammation or increased ear thickness in the absence of IMQ challenge across the time periods examined (Extended Data Fig. 2f,g), indicating that diet-sensitive amplification of IMQ-induced inflammation is experimentally separable from spontaneous diet-associated dermatitis as reported in some prolonged diet-feeding models23,24.

To determine whether systemic metabolic correction was sufficient to blunt diet-amplified inflammation, we treated HFD-fed mice with semaglutide during the acute dietary exposure and IMQ challenge. Semaglutide was pharmacologically active, improving glucose excursion, reducing body weight, lowering circulating insulin and leptin, increasing GLP-1 immunoreactivity in an assay that recognizes semaglutide, and reducing white adipose tissue mass (Extended Data Fig. 3a–h). However, semaglutide did not broadly reduce baseline inflammatory cytokines, alter gross ear or spleen morphology or substantially remodel splenic conventional T (Tconv) cell subsets (Extended Data Fig. 3i–l). After IMQ challenge, semaglutide did not attenuate HFD-amplified ear thickening or lesional TH17 inflammation (Extended Data Fig. 3m–p). Thus, HFD-conditioned inflammatory susceptibility can persist despite GLP-1 receptor agonist-mediated improvement in systemic metabolic parameters.

IMQ-induced inflammation predominantly stimulates type 17 immune responses, characterized by the production of IL-17A, IL-17F and IL-22 by TH17 cells, γδ T cells and other immune cells22,25. Flow cytometry revealed that acute and chronic HFD similarly increased levels of CD4+ Tconv cells expressing RORγt, the lineage-defining TH17 transcription factor, as well as CD4+ Tconv cells competent for IL-17A, IL-17F and IL-22 in lesional skin and draining lymph nodes (Extended Data Figs. 1b and 4a–d). TH22 cells (IL-17A−IL-22+FOXP3−CD4+ T cells) were also increased, although to a lesser extent than TH17 cells (Extended Data Fig. 4e). CD4+ T cell proliferation (Ki-67+) was increased (Extended Data Fig. 4f), whereas the proportion of Treg cells was reduced under both conditions (Extended Data Fig. 4g). Neutrophils, which are prominent cellular effectors of type 17 inflammation26, were elevated after just 1 week of HFD, mirroring the increase observed with chronic HFD (Extended Data Fig. 4h). By contrast, the proportion of cytokine-producing γδ T cells was unchanged, although total γδ T cell numbers increased in HFD-fed mice (Extended Data Fig. 4i,j).

Finally, we examined whether brief withdrawal of dietary fat could mitigate disease in obese mice. Mice fed HFD for 13 weeks were switched to LFD for 1 week before IMQ challenge, and were compared with obese mice maintained on HFD and with lean LFD-fed control mice (Fig. 1f). Despite persistent obesity (>40 g), the mice that were switched to LFD did not develop the enhanced ear thickness observed in obese mice maintained on HFD (Fig. 1g–j). Thus, dietary state at the onset of inflammation, rather than body mass alone, dictates the magnitude of inflammatory responses after acute challenge with IMQ. This result prompted us to test whether dietary exposure within a specific temporal window is sufficient to drive this effect.

To define the temporal dynamics of this response, we examined how the timing of HFD feeding influenced IMQ-induced inflammation. Four days of HFD coincident with disease initiation (HFDin) were sufficient to markedly exacerbate inflammation (Fig. 1k,l and Extended Data Fig. 4k,l). Short-term exposure to 15% (w/v) glucose in water during the same peri-initiation window did not enhance IMQ-induced inflammation (Extended Data Fig. 2h–k), suggesting that increased simple sugar exposure is insufficient to recapitulate the acute inflammatory amplification observed with fat-enriched obesogenic diets. By contrast, HFD provided outside this window (HFDout) did not alter disease severity compared with continuous LFD (Extended Data Fig. 2l). Conversely, switching obese mice to LFD during a similar 4-day window (LFDin) protected them from the heightened inflammation observed in obese mice maintained on HFD (Fig. 1m,n), whereas LFD given outside this window (LFDout) offered no protection (Extended Data Fig. 2m). In a complementary experiment, mice that were fed HFD for 1 week and switched to LFD on the day of IMQ initiation (HFDbefore) developed disease severity comparable to mice maintained on HFD, further narrowing the critical dietary window to the onset of inflammation (Extended Data Fig. 2n). Together, these results establish that dietary state during the peri-initiation window, rather than obesity per se, determines disease severity, and raise the question of which immune compartments translate coincident dietary cues into amplified inflammatory responses.

We next sought to identify the critical immune cell types that enhance disease severity and type 3 inflammation after HFD feeding. To this end, we first used Rag1−/− mice, which lack adaptive immunity but retain innate lymphoid cells27. These mice were resistant to HFD-induced exacerbation of disease severity, suggesting that adaptive lymphocytes are required for this effect (Fig. 2a,b). Given the known role of γδ T cells as major IL-17 producers in the skin and their significant expansion after long-term HFD feeding18, we examined the effects of short-term HFD on Tcrd−/− mice. Notably, HFD-fed Tcrd−/− mice also developed significantly greater disease compared with LFD-fed controls (Fig. 2c), suggesting that γδ T cells are dispensable for the short-term HFD-induced inflammatory exacerbation. By contrast, HFD-fed Tcra−/− mice, which do not develop αβ T cells, did not develop greater disease compared with their LFD counterparts, suggesting that αβ T cells are required for short-term HFD-induced disease exacerbation (Fig. 2d). To further identify the subset within the αβ T cell compartment required for the HFD-enhanced disease severity, we used B2m−/− mice, which are deficient in CD8+ T cells, and MHCIIΔ/Δ (H2-Ab1, H2-Aa, H2-Eb1, H2-Eb2, H2-Ea null) mice, which lack CD4+ T cells. Notably, only CD4+ T cell deficiency resulted in the loss of the exacerbated disease and inflammatory response caused by HFD in wild-type (WT) mice (Fig. 2e,f). To further confirm the essential role of CD4+ T cells in altering disease trajectory in response to HFD, we treated WT mice with IgG or CD4-depleting antibody. CD4 depletion completely abolished the diet-induced difference in disease severity between mice fed LFD and those fed HFD (Extended Data Fig. 5a–d). Notably, in this setting, LFD-fed mice displayed modest ear thickening accompanied by an increase in cutaneous γδ T cells (Extended Data Fig. 5e). We then sought to identify whether the presence of CD4+ T cells, in the absence of other adaptive immune cells, could produce an exaggerated immune response to HFD. We adoptively transferred bulk CD4+ T cells isolated from the spleen of WT mice into Rag1−/− mice and, after homeostatic expansion, provided HFD or LFD for 1 week, followed by IMQ treatment (Fig. 2g). Rag1−/− mice that were transferred with CD4+ T cells had a robust enhancement of IMQ-induced disease after HFD feeding, with elevated numbers of TH17 cells in the skin (Fig. 2h–k). Taken together, these results demonstrate that CD4+ T cells are critical immunological mediators in translating dietary influences into enhanced inflammatory responses in this model of skin inflammation.

To further elucidate the mechanism by which short-term HFD feeding influences CD4+ T cells, we systematically profiled the lesional CD4+ T cells from LFD- and HFD-fed mice after IMQ challenge using single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq). Exploratory analysis revealed two Tconv cell clusters (TH1 and TH17), four distinct Treg cell clusters (proliferating, resident, central and Penk+)28 and a naive cluster (Fig. 2l). The gene-expression heat maps of effector cytokines (Il17a, Il17f, Il22, Ifng and Fasl)29, key transcription factors (Rorc, Tbx21, Foxp3 and Ikzf2)10,30,31, cytokine receptors (Il23r, Il1rl1, Il1r1 and Il2ra)30, markers of proliferation (Mki67, Cdca3, Spc24 and Pclaf), prominent markers of activation, quiescence, tissue residency and memory (Ccr7, Sell, Itgae, Klf2 and Bcl2)6 and Penk28 validated these population assignments (Extended Data Fig. 5f).

Cells from mice that were fed HFD showed marked differences in distribution across the cell populations, particularly in the TH17 cluster (Extended Data Fig. 6a). In TH17 cells, TH17 effector genes such as Il17a, Il17f, Il22, Il23r and Rorc were significantly upregulated in CD4+ T cells from HFD-fed mice (Extended Data Fig. 6b). Furthermore, leveraging published gene signatures associated with TH17 pathogenicity9,31, we found that short-term HFD feeding markedly enhances the pathogenicity of TH17 cells in the skin (Extended Data Fig. 6c). We performed differential gene expression analysis (Extended Data Fig. 6d and Supplementary Table 1) and found that Il1r1 was the most strongly upregulated gene from TH17 cells taken from mice fed HFD, as measured by fold change (Fig. 2m and Extended Data Fig. 6d). These findings were further validated by flow cytometry, which confirmed that HFD feeding significantly increases IL-1R1 expression in CD4+ T cells from lesional skin (Extended Data Fig. 6e). IL-1R1 signalling has been shown to be critical for stabilizing and enhancing type 3 inflammation10,32. CD4+ T cells expressing Il1r1 exhibited increased expression of TH17 effector genes, along with a higher TH17 pathogenicity score (Extended Data Fig. 6f,g). TH17 differentiation involves multiple inflammatory cues, of which the circuitry is well defined in vitro but less well understood in vivo33,34. These findings suggest that IL-1R1 has a specific and critical role in driving TH17 differentiation in mice being fed HFD.

To directly test this hypothesis, we subjected mice with T cell-specific ablation of IL-1R1 (Cd4creIl1r1fl/fl) to 1 week of LFD or HFD feeding followed by IMQ challenge. Notably, T cell-specific IL-1R1-deficient mice were completely protected from HFD-induced amplification of IMQ-induced skin inflammation, maintaining disease severity levels similar to LFD-fed controls (Fig. 2n and Extended Data Fig. 6h). We did not observe an increase in TH22 cells in T cell-specific IL-1R1-deficient mice (Extended Data Fig. 6i). Collectively, these results demonstrate that short-term HFD exacerbates IMQ-induced type 17 inflammation through IL-1R1+ TH17 cells in the skin lesion.

We next examined the mechanistic basis underlying CD4+ T cell induction during IMQ challenge with short-term HFD-feeding. As IL-1α and IL-1β signal through IL-1R135, we investigated whether HFD feeding combined with IMQ challenge increases their expression in the skin. We performed bulk RNA-seq analysis of whole ear tissues from mice that were fed either LFD or HFD and subsequently challenged with IMQ (Supplementary Table 2). Consistent with our earlier findings (Extended Data Fig. 4a,b), the ears of mice fed HFD exhibited a marked upregulation of type 3 cytokine transcripts, including Il17a and Il17f, alongside genes involved in chemotaxis signalling such as Cxcl2, Cxcl3, Ccl3 and Fpr1 (Fig. 3a and Extended Data Fig. 7a). Although both Il1a and Il1b were upregulated in lesional ear skin from HFD-fed mice, the diet-associated increase was more pronounced for Il1b, which increased approximately 3.9-fold (Extended Data Fig. 7b). This pronounced increase in Il1b coincided with a significant upregulation of Nlrp3 (Fig. 3a and Extended Data Fig. 7b). Gene expression analyses of immune cells sorted from the lesions of 15 humans with psoriasis also revealed expression of NLRP3 and IL1B (with specific enrichment in myeloid cells) (Extended Data Fig. 7m–o).

Assembly of the canonical NLRP3-dependent inflammasome leads to the proteolytic cleavage of pro-caspase-1 into active caspase-1, which subsequently processes pro-IL-1β into its mature, active form2,8. We therefore hypothesized that the NLRP3-dependent inflammasome may be important in driving differentiation of IL-1R1+ TH17 cells in the skin of mice fed HFD during disease development. Consistent with this, genetic ablation of NLRP3 abolished this response: HFD-fed Nlrp3–/– mice did not develop exacerbated disease, in contrast to WT controls (Fig. 3b–d), and Nlrp3 deficiency similarly prevented WD-enhanced inflammation relative to CD (Extended Data Fig. 7c–e). Furthermore, Nlrp3 deficiency prevented the HFD-induced increase in IL-1R1+ TH17 cells and IL-1β protein levels observed in WT mice (Fig. 3e–g). Importantly, Nlrp3 deficiency did not confer metabolic protection after short-term HFD feeding. HFD-fed Nlrp3–/– mice exhibited glucose excursion curves and area under the curve (AUC) values comparable to those of the WT controls (Extended Data Fig. 7f,g), indicating that the attenuation of skin inflammation in Nlrp3–/– mice occurs despite modestly increased glucose intolerance. To independently confirm the role of NLRP3, we topically treated WT mice with MCC950, a selective NLRP3 inhibitor, or vehicle. MCC950 treatment protected against HFD-amplified exacerbation of IMQ-induced inflammation (Extended Data Fig. 7h,i), with marked reductions in lesional TH17 cells and neutrophils (Extended Data Fig. 7j,k). Together, these findings establish NLRP3 as a critical amplifier of inflammation during IMQ challenge under brief HFD feeding.

To identify the cellular source of Nlrp3 and Il1b, we sorted lesional CD45.2+ cells from LFD- or HFD-fed mice after IMQ challenge and performed scRNA-seq (Fig. 3h–j). Clustering revealed diverse myeloid and lymphoid populations, confirmed by cell-type-defining marker expression (Extended Data Fig. 5g). Nlrp3 and Il1b were most strongly expressed in neutrophils (Fig. 3i,j). Quantification showed that neutrophil numbers increased with HFD feeding alone, even without IMQ challenge (Fig. 3k); neutrophils rose further after IMQ challenge (Fig. 3l). Notably, semaglutide did not alter HFD-induced neutrophil accumulation in the skin under the baseline conditions (Extended Data Fig. 3q). Consistent with transcriptional priming of the NLRP3 inflammasome, HFD-fed ear skin before IMQ challenge showed increased expression of Il1b and Nlrp3 (Extended Data Fig. 7l). Thus, short-term HFD establishes a neutrophil-enriched and inflammasome-primed tissue state, whereas IMQ provides the acute inflammatory signal required for productive IL-1β protein accumulation and downstream IL-1R1-dependent TH17 amplification. We next examined how rapidly neutrophil accumulation in skin responds to dietary change. Flow cytometry profiling revealed that neutrophil numbers, but not CD4+ or CD8+ T cells, γδ T cells, B cells or monocytes, showed the largest early increase after short-term HFD feeding and returned to near the baseline 7 days after switching back to the LFD (Fig. 3m). Finally, depletion of neutrophils with anti-Ly6G antibodies abrogated HFD-induced ear thickening during IMQ treatment (Fig. 3n), establishing neutrophils as required mediators of the diet-amplified inflammatory response.

We wondered whether this effect of coincident dietary state extended beyond the skin. To test this, we examined the lung in an experiment in which mice were fed HFD or LFD for 1 week and then challenged intranasally with LPS. We observed a significant increase (around fivefold) in neutrophil counts just after HFD feeding (before LPS treatment) (Extended Data Fig. 8a). After challenge with LPS, HFD-fed mice showed an increased change in lung weight and heightened TH17 inflammation relative to the LFD-fed controls (Extended Data Fig. 8b–d). This response was abolished by neutrophil depletion or T-cell-specific loss of Il1r1 (Extended Data Fig. 8e,f). Thus, coincident dietary state amplifies acute inflammatory responses in a mechanistically similar manner across tissues, indicating that this effect is not skin restricted.

Psoriasis is a chronic inflammatory disease characterized by onset, recovery and repeated recurrence36. Inflammatory memory is increasingly recognized as a tissue-retained property that may facilitate relapse at sites of previous inflammation11,37,38. To test whether diet over disease onset (DODO) shapes disease trajectory beyond the initial flare, we developed an IMQ-induced inflammation recurrence model (Fig. 4a). Mice were placed on LFD or HFD for 1 week, then subjected to daily IMQ challenge for 6 days while continuing the assigned diet. As expected, the HFD-fed mice exhibited roughly twofold greater ear thickening compared with the LFD-fed control mice during the acute phase. Thereafter, all mice were returned to LFD and IMQ treatment was discontinued. After approximately 25 days of recovery, both groups appeared fully resolved by gross examination, ear thickness measurement, histology, bulk RNA-seq and serum cytokine profiling (Fig. 4b,c and Extended Data Fig. 9a–d).

On secondary IMQ challenge, however, HFDDODO mice developed a markedly more rapid and severe disease recurrence, with around twofold greater ear thickening, elevated TH17 cell numbers, enhanced neutrophil accumulation and sustained IL-1R1 expression in conventional CD4+ T cells compared with the LFD-fed control mice (Fig. 4b–f). This enhanced response in HFDDODO mice was durable: mice that were allowed to recover for 6.5 months after the initial flare continued to exhibit exaggerated ear thickening after rechallenge (Extended Data Fig. 9e). Importantly, transient HFD in the absence of IMQ did not confer durable effects: such mice responded like the LFD-fed control mice on rechallenge (Extended Data Fig. 9f). Moreover, when mice that had recovered from initial disease were rechallenged, only the previously inflamed ears—not contralateral naive ears—exhibited exaggerated recurrence in HFDDODO animals (Extended Data Fig. 9g), indicating that the long-term immunomodulatory imprint of DODO is localized and tissue specific. We further found that obese mice maintained on HFD at disease onset developed recurrence equivalent to lean mice that were exposed to HFD at disease onset, underscoring that dietary context coincident with the inflammatory trigger, rather than previous obesity or dietary history, dictates the establishment of diet-induced inflammatory memory (Extended Data Fig. 9h–j). In addition, blocking lymphocyte egress with FTY72039 during the second flare did not prevent HFDDODO-induced exaggerated inflammation, further suggesting a tissue-resident immune response (Extended Data Fig. 9k,l).

We next examined whether skin-resident immune cells mediated these durable effects. To address this, we profiled previously inflamed (left) and unchallenged (right) ears of recovered mice before secondary challenge. In HFDDODO mice, previously inflamed ears contained around 2.3-fold more CD4+ tissue-resident memory (TRM) cells40 compared with LFD-fed controls, whereas unchallenged ears showed no differences (Fig. 4g and Extended Data Fig. 9m). Consistent with the flow cytometry findings, thick-section imaging of back skin 45 days after IMQ withdrawal revealed a greater accumulation of CD4+ T cells (red) in HFDDODO mice (Fig. 4h), concentrated around the hair follicle near the sebaceous gland—a region where follicle-associated immune niches have previously been described41,42. To further resolve the CD4+ T cell compartment, we sorted CD4+ T cells from recovered ears and performed single-nucleus assay for transposase-accessible chromatin (snATAC–seq; Fig. 4i). Dimensionality reduction revealed marked chromatin heterogeneity, including multiple clusters with features consistent with TRM cells (Extended Data Fig. 10a). Notably, one population (cluster 8) was selectively enriched in HFDDODO mice relative to LFD-fed control mice (Fig. 4j). This cluster exhibited increased chromatin accessibility at loci encoding key inflammatory signalling receptors, including Il1r1 and Il23r (Fig. 4k), alongside accessibility at Hlf, a transcription factor that was recently implicated in CD4+ TRM programming43, Il21, Ccl20 and Tnfsf4 (Extended Data Fig. 10b). By contrast, cluster 8 showed reduced accessibility at Ikzf2, a regulator associated with regulatory T cell identity, as well as Tox and Il6st (Extended Data Fig. 10c). Together, these features define cluster 8 as a distinct CD4+ TRM-like population epigenetically poised for IL-1 and IL-23 responsiveness. These findings indicate that transient HFD exposure durably reshapes the chromatin landscape of skin-resident CD4+ T cells, biasing inflammatory recall responses long after dietary normalization.

To formally establish the functional significance of CD4+ T cells in mediating dietary inflammatory memory, we administered CD4-depleting antibodies or isotype controls to recovered mice and then rechallenged them with IMQ (Fig. 4l). HFDDODO mice treated with isotype control antibodies developed exacerbated recurrent disease, whereas anti-CD4 treatment abolished the difference between the HFDDODO and LFD groups (Fig. 4m). Thus, the CD4+ cell compartment is required for the expression of durable inflammatory memory induced by transient HFD feeding. Further supporting this mechanism, topical administration of the NLRP3 inhibitor MCC950 during the initial challenge reduced the exaggerated recurrence observed in HFDDODO mice, whereas identical treatment during recurrence conferred no benefit (Extended Data Fig. 9n). These results indicate that local NLRP3 activation during the initial inflammatory episode is required to establish diet-induced inflammatory memory, which is subsequently mediated by CD4+ T cells.

A defining feature of tissue-resident memory is the persistence of the memory response after transplantation. To test whether diet-induced inflammatory memory was similarly tissue intrinsic, we transplanted previously inflamed back skin from recovered HFDDODO or LFD mice—pretreated with either IgG isotype control or anti-CD4 antibodies—onto naive recipients and subjected them to IMQ challenge (Fig. 4n). Grafts from IgG-treated HFDDODO donors displayed markedly increased pathology compared with the IgG-treated LFD controls, including prominent scaling and surface roughening (Fig. 4o) as well as epidermal thickening and elevated leukocytic infiltration in the dermis (Fig. 4p). Thus, transient HFD exposure during disease onset is sufficient to imprint a CD4+ T-cell-dependent, tissue-intrinsic program that amplifies inflammatory recall responses after recovery.

Transient exposure to HFD during a narrowly defined peri-initiation window durably reshapes tissue immunity without requiring obesity. The consequence is not merely heightened acute pathology but a lasting change in local immune responsiveness that becomes evident months later after rechallenge, after return to LFD. These experiments identify dietary state, rather than accumulated adiposity, as the proximate determinant of disease amplification. Three compositionally distinct fat-enriched diets—HFD, MFD and WD—each amplified IMQ-induced type 17 skin inflammation, whereas glucose water alone did not. Conversely, transiently switching persistently obese mice from HFD to LFD during a corresponding four-day peri-initiation window attenuated disease. Semaglutide further sharpened this dissociation: GLP-1 receptor agonism improved glucose handling, reduced body weight and adiposity, and lowered circulating insulin and leptin, but did not attenuate HFD-amplified inflammatory responses. Although longer-term treatment with GLP-1 receptor agonists and related incretin-based therapies may improve established psoriasis in patients with obesity or type 2 diabetes44,45,46,47, our experiment addressed the distinct question of whether short-term semaglutide treatment blocks the additional amplification imposed by coincident HFD exposure. It did not, indicating that systemic metabolic improvement and diet-primed inflammatory susceptibility are separable. Whereas prolonged high-fat or Western-style feeding has previously been shown to exacerbate IMQ-induced and IL-23-driven inflammation48,49,50 and, with sustained exposure, promote spontaneous dermatitis23,24, as little as 4 days at inflammatory onset was sufficient here. HFD alone did not confer durable susceptibility to a later inflammatory challenge, indicating that dietary exposure must intersect with tissue inflammation to imprint the long-term phenotype. Our findings may also help explain a previous observation that leptin-deficient ob/ob mice did not exhibit increased IMQ susceptibility despite profound adiposity49. As leptin supports optimal TH17 differentiation51, its absence may limit engagement of the IL-1β–TH17 axis. Consistent with this possibility, transient peri-initiation HFD amplified IMQ-induced inflammation in WT but not ob/ob mice (Extended Data Fig. 2o).

Mechanistically, the data support a model linking neutrophil-dependent inflammatory signalling to durable tissue immune memory (Extended Data Fig. 10d). Brief HFD increased neutrophil accumulation in skin before overt disease; after inflammatory challenge, Nlrp3 and Il1b were expressed predominantly in neutrophils, and NLRP3–IL-1β signalling licensed IL-1R1+CD4+ T cell responses. NLRP3 inhibition was effective during the initial challenge but not at rechallenge, whereas tissue-resident CD4+ T cells mediated recall, distinguishing memory establishment from memory expression. Skin transplantation further showed that a systemic dietary exposure can be encoded as spatially restricted memory within previously inflamed tissue. How brief HFD drives neutrophil accumulation before overt disease remains unresolved. Targeted lipidomics of skin before IMQ challenge revealed rapid remodelling of the cutaneous lipid environment (Extended Data Fig. 10e), while bulk RNA-seq identified increased expression of genes involved in fatty acid transport, lipid droplet formation and lipid storage (Extended Data Fig. 10f). Although these data do not identify the causal lipid species, receptor or stromal compartment, they nominate local lipid metabolism as an upstream process linking short-lived dietary change to neutrophil accumulation and transcriptional inflammasome priming. This interpretation is consistent with reports of rapid neutrophil recruitment and neutrophil-dependent inflammation after HFD52,53, lipid flux-dependent neutrophil recruitment in adipose tissue54, and FABP-dependent inflammasome and type 17 signalling in skin24,55. The dependence of HFD-amplified LPS-induced lung inflammation on neutrophils and T-cell-intrinsic IL-1R1 signalling suggests that a related acute diet-sensitive mechanism operates beyond skin, although durable inflammatory memory was established here only in skin.

The distinct requirements for memory establishment and recall suggest opportunities for intervention at different stages of disease. Dietary modulation around inflammatory onset may exert disproportionate effects relative to changes during quiescence. Targeting NLRP3 during the initial challenge or interrupting IL-1R1 signalling may prevent the establishment of a maladaptive resident memory program that amplifies recurrence. More broadly, transient physiological states may become embedded in local immune memory when they coincide with tissue inflammation.

Measurements were taken from distinct biological samples, except for ear thickness, which was measured longitudinally in the same mice at multiple time points. The investigators were not blinded in the studies unless otherwise stated. No estimate of variance was made between each group. All in vivo experiments were conducted with at least two independent cohorts unless otherwise specified. Unless otherwise noted, data from one instance of the repeated experiments are represented in this Article. The recurrence experiment in which mice were allowed to recover for 6.5 months before rechallenge with IMQ was only conducted once. The targeted lipidomics analyses were conducted once. Bulk RNA-seq, scRNA-seq and snATAC–seq experiments were conducted once using multiple biological samples per group (as indicated in the figure legends). Histological analyses were performed on tissues from a single experimental cohort for each comparison. Unless otherwise indicated, at least five mice per group were examined; for each mouse, three serial sections were assessed at each of at least two separately cut tissue levels. Thick-section confocal imaging was performed once using two mice per group, with three 200 µm sections imaged per mouse.
