Wadephul: 'Coalition of the Willing' talks in Berlin send strong signal to Moscow

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

German Foreign Minister Wadephul has said the Coalition of the Willing meeting in Berlin sends a clear message to Moscow to take peace talks seriously. He also warned Ukraine should be prepared for peace talks to fail.

German Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul said Thursday's meeting of the "Coalition of the Willing" in Berlin sent a strong signal to Moscow that it was time to take negotiations to end the war in Ukraine seriously.

Speaking to DW during a visit to the South Caucasus, Wadephul described the summit as the largest meeting of Ukraine's supporters so far. The meeting represents the 18th set of high-level talks among the coalition's members.

He said it demonstrated that allies remained firmly behind Kyiv in the fifth year of Russia's war and were ready to do "what was necessary to ensure Ukraine could negotiate from a position of strength."

"The summit itself is already a success," he told DW.

Wadephul also welcomed the US initiative to restart peace negotiations. After nine months without progress, an initial meeting had taken place in Abu Dhabi, with a follow-up round planned and European participation expected.

"We are also preparing for the possibility that the negotiations will not be successful and that, over the coming winter, we will have to continue supplying Ukraine with heating, electricity and all essential supplies, including water," he said.

"And we will continue to stand by Ukraine militarily."

He stressed that any peace agreement must include security guarantees to protect Ukraine against future Russian attacks.

In earlier comments during his trip to the South Caucasus, Wadephul said that Ukraine and Europe must be involved in any negotiations over the future of Ukraine.

Wadephul did, however, welcome the trilateral meeting between US President Donald Trump, Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping in Shenzhen.

"Every conversation that takes place is a good conversation," he told DW during a visit to Azerbaijan on Wednesday.

Later on Thursday, Russian President Vladimir Putin said he had invited Europeans to Moscow for talks on the potential structure of future talks to end the war.
