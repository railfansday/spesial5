Borussia Dortmund bows to fan pressure and end Rheinmetall deal

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Borussia Dortmund has reluctantly ended its association with a major sponsor after long-term fan protests. They are not the first Bundesliga club to u-turn, as commercial interests clash with politics and fan culture.

Two years of fan pressure and criticism has forced Borussia Dortmund to drop a sponsorship deal with Germany's largest arms manufacturer, Rheinmetall.

The Bundesliga club will not renew its deal with Rheinmetall beyond its expiry in June 2027, at the end of this season. Dortmund chairman Carsten Cramer confirmed the news on the club's website ahead of its AGM on Sunday, which may have proved a potential flashpoint. He acknowledged that "our partnership has been controversial from the outset" but lamented the loss of income.

"It reduces our financial room for manoeuvre. We will have to reprioritize planned investments and carefully examine how we can find viable solutions."

Though he welcomed the news, one of the leaders of the fan revolt against the partnership, Michael Schulze von Glaser, warned this is likely not the end of the road.

"I think in the end it was a victory for the fans. But I'm also a bit skeptical about  what the future will bring us," the Dortmund fan and former head of the German Peace Society told DW. "Football fans in Germany always must be skeptical and always must watch what the club is doing.”

Many Dortmund fans have opposed the deal — which has seen Rheinmetall's logo on advertising boards and other surfaces at locations such as Dortmund's 83,000-capacity Westfalenstadion — from its beginnings in 2024. It was estimated to be worth between €7 ($7.68 million) and €9 million per year.

At the club's AGM that year, 556 of 855 members present voted against the deal in a non-binding vote. There were 247 votes in favour, with 52 abstentions. This, said Cramer, informed the eventual outcome.

Rheinmetall sells arms mostly to Germany, Ukraine and NATO but fans and critics feel that platforming any arms manufacturer goes against the club's statement of its core values. In that, Dortmund states its desire for "a club and a society that operate free of racism, anti-Semitism, LGBTQIA+ hostility, sexism, violence and discrimination."

It has always been clear, said Schulze von Glaser, that this deal did not chime with that.

"In the end, they kill people. They are for war," he said of Rheinmetall. "The slogan of Borussia Dortmund is true love (Echte Liebe). But for me and many others, our love ends there…. I know some fans who have put fan merchandise in the trash because they were so shocked.”

In the Q and A on the club's website, Cramer said he was still a "personal advocate" of the deal but that Dortmund's commitment to the 50+1 model, which means German clubs are majority controlled by the members, meant they had to listen to fan voices. But he also warned that Rheinmetall must be an "absolute exception and an isolated case."

That set off some alarm bells for Schulze von Glaser.

"It's a bit concerning that there are constantly these attacks on professional football — a new sponsorship, investment deals," he said.

Indeed, Dortmund's climbdown is not the first of its kind in Germany's top flight. Last year, after fan and media pressure, Bayern Munich ended its commercial deal with the tourism arm of the Rwandan government before its conclusion.

Fans had objected to the promotion of Rwanda, which many felt sought to divert attention from its poor human rights record and actions in the war with DR Congo, through sporting partnerships. Two years earlier, Bayern withdrew from a similarly controversial deal with Qatar Airways.

Elsewhere in the league, Werder Bremen fans objected to their long time shirt sponsor, Wiesenhof. Many supporters took issue with the factory farming practices  of Germany's largest poultry producer. Though it was not given as a direct reason for the end of a shirt sponsorship deal in 2023, it likely played a part.

Union Berlin fans have also made their voices heard. When the club signed a new shirt-sponsorship deal with Luxembourg real estate company, Aroundtown, upon promotion to the top flight in 2019, many fans were frustrated at the tie up in a city where rent prices were skyrocketing.

"It's a problem for a lot of people," fan Sven Dobberstein told DW at the time.

"We have to try to find a way between commercialization and what we have at the moment. It isn't easy."

Striking that balance can often be a source of tension for Bundesliga clubs. 50+1 ensures fans have a voice, preventing rich owners, or nation states, from taking control as they do in England's lucrative Premier League or in France or Spain. Indeed fan protests have also stopped the league signing a €1billion private equity partner and seen Monday night matches scrapped. For Schulze von Glaser, this needs to be protected.

"In Germany, the fan culture has an impact," he said.

”If there is something bad or terrible happening in your club and in the Bundesliga, there are the organized fans and they can have an impact if they want, if they stand together and, yes, they can stop the Rheinmetall deal."
