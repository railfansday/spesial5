Docusign vs. Monday.com: Which Technology Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Docusign's eSignature empire faces a new test as Monday.com's double-digit growth challenges it on valuation and momentum.

Deciding between an established market leader and a high-growth challenger is a common challenge for investors. Today, we compare whether Docusign Inc. (DOCU -1.96%) or Monday.com (MNDY -3.06%) is the better long-term choice.

Docusign pioneered the e-signature category and now seeks to automate the entire contract lifecycle for businesses of all sizes. Meanwhile, Monday.com provides a highly customizable work management platform that helps teams streamline complex workflows. Both companies compete for enterprise budget in the software space, though they currently occupy very different stages of business maturity.

Docusign provides Intelligent Agreement Management, including its eSignature and contract lifecycle management solutions. The company operates in the broader tech stocks landscape, serving over 1.8 million customers globally through various digital tools. Major strategic technology partners include Microsoft Corp. (MSFT -3.58%), Salesforce.com Inc. (CRM -2.07%), SAP SE (SAP +1.45%), ServiceNow Inc. (NOW -5.93%), Workday Inc. (WDAY -0.83%), and Alphabet Inc. (GOOG -1.93%).

According to its latest annual report, filed for the fiscal year ended Jan. 31, 2026, revenue reached $3.2 billion. This represents an 8.2% increase compared with the prior fiscal year, showing steady expansion in its core markets. Net income for the period was $309.1 million, resulting in a net margin of 9.6% for the latest fiscal year.

As of its January 2026 balance sheet, the current ratio is 0.7x and the debt-to-equity ratio is 0.1x, which measure short-term liquidity and total debt levels. Free cash flow for the fiscal year ended Jan. 31, 2026, reached $1.1 billion. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 53.4% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Monday.com offers an AI work platform designed to manage and automate work across CRM, service, and development segments. The platform is currently used by approximately 250,000 customers worldwide who utilize its flexible Work OS to build custom applications. By focusing on a no-code interface, the company targets a wide range of departments that need to consolidate disparate work processes.

According to its latest annual report, filed for the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, revenue reached $1.2 billion. This marked a significant increase of 26.7% compared with the prior fiscal year. Net income for the period was $118.7 million, while the net margin improved to 9.6% as the company scaled its operations.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the current ratio is 2.5x, and the debt-to-equity ratio is 0.3x, tracking liquidity and total debt relative to shareholder equity. Free cash flow for the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, reached $309.9 million. Note that stock-based compensation (SBC for short) represented roughly 53% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Docusign faces significant revenue concentration, as it relies on its eSignature product for the majority of its total sales. Rapid advances in artificial intelligence could lead to increased competition from general-purpose providers or disrupt the core agreement market. The company also depends on third-party cloud infrastructure from Microsoft and partner platforms to deliver its various services to users.

Monday.com faces intense competition in the productivity and collaboration market from established giants like Microsoft and Adobe Systems Inc. (ADBE -0.82%). It must also compete with specialized players like Atlassian Corp. (TEAM -3.47%), Asana Inc (ASAN -4.04%), and Smartsheet for limited enterprise software budgets. The future success of the company depends on its ability to maintain high growth rates while successfully cross-selling its newer customer relationship management (CRM) and service products.

Based on current Forward P/E and P/S ratio metrics, Monday.com appears more attractively priced relative to its growth profile. The P/S ratio measures market capitalization against sales over the past twelve months.

Monday.com is undergoing a bit of a reset. In July, it fired 20% of its workforce. It's part of an effort to redirect more capital to AI services for its platform.

In its second quarter, revenue grew 22% year-over-year to nearly $365 million, but net income was $3.64 million, representing a net profit margin of barely 1%.

Still, there were some bright spots. Management touted a record net addition of $100,000-plus and $500,000-plus (client spending) customers, saying they reflect the continued strength of Monday.com's push to go upmarket. In July, the business crossed $1.5 billion in annual recurring revenue (ARR), a milestone the company says reflects the durability of its core business even as it reshapes the company around AI.

For the full year, sales should rise nearly 20% to $1.47 billion, but net income should weaken to about $68 million.

For Docusign, sales in its current fiscal year are seen growing by nearly 9% to $3.5 billion, with a slightly better percentage gain in net income to $346 million.

Docusign's launch and global expansion of the IAM platform with the introduction of AI-powered tools should position the company for future growth. However, the not-so-great projected revenue growth rates suggest that Docusign may be entering a phase of more mature growth and a landscape of increasingly competitive digital agreement and contract management software. In particular, news reports OpenAI would launch a ChatGPT product that would compete head-on with Docusign in the document summary field caused some investor fears. Right now, however, the signature and identity verification part of Docusign's business appears unthreatened by ChatGPT.

The choice here is to go with the faster-growing business, sales-wise. In the long term, sales growth is the basis on which businesses can generate profits for investors, so given the two stocks' roughly comparable valuation ratios, Monday.com is the choice.
