Shopify vs. Uber Technologies: Which Technology Stock Is a Better Buy?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Shopify's revenue surge and Uber's free cash flow make for a sharp contrast, but one future opportunity may settle which stock to own.

By Jack Delaney – Updated Nov 21, 2026 at 5:20PM EST

Choosing between Shopify (SHOP -6.25%) and Uber Technologies (UBER -3.25%) involves weighing two different tech stocks. Both companies have become essential to how we shop and move.

Shopify acts as the digital backbone for millions of online merchants, while Uber manages a massive logistics and transportation network. This comparison evaluates their financials and business models to see which is a better pick for 2027 and beyond.

Shopify provides the essential digital tools that allow companies to build their own online storefronts and manage physical retail locations. The company serves millions of merchants in multiple countries, providing an all-in-one ecosystem for everything from inventory management to payment processing. By focusing on giving brands total control over their customer relationships, it has become a central player among tech stocks that support the broader e-commerce market.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $14.2 billion, representing an increase of approximately 32.5% compared to the prior twelve-month period. This robust growth was accompanied by net income of $2.1 billion.

Uber Technologies operates a massive technology platform that connects people to transportation, food delivery, and logistics services. Its network spans over 70 countries, serving a growing number of monthly active consumers who use the app for ridesharing or ordering from local merchants. The company leverages partnerships with major retailers like Best Buy (BBY -1.33%) and Tractor Supply (TSCO -0.77%) to expand its reach beyond traditional restaurant delivery into a broader marketplace.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $56.5 billion, which is a growth rate of approximately 13.9% over the previous twelve months. The company generated net income of around $3.5 billion during this period.

Shopify faces intense market competition from established platforms and niche providers that could limit its growth or force pricing changes. The company depends heavily on third-party cloud infrastructure from Alphabet (GOOGL -2.70%) and payment processors like PayPal Holdings (PYPL -5.46%). New risks involve the use of artificial intelligence, which could lead to inaccurate outputs or regulatory challenges.

Uber Technologies deals with ongoing legal and regulatory challenges regarding how it classifies its drivers, which could fundamentally change its business model. It competes globally in mobility and delivery against well-capitalized rivals such as Lyft (LYFT -2.54%) and DoorDash (DASH -2.88%). The platform is also vulnerable to macroeconomic shifts, as inflation or rising fuel prices can impact consumer spending on rides and deliveries. Finally, the company relies on the mobile marketplaces of Apple (AAPL -1.90%) and Alphabet to reach its vast user base.

Uber Technologies appears more reasonably priced as it trades at a much lower forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio and price-to-sales (P/S) ratio than Shopify.

Both are well-run companies. But if I had to pick only one stock, it would be Uber. It's not so much about anything Shopify is doing wrong; it's more about Uber growing its current business and the opportunities ahead.

Despite Uber being a well-established brand, its recent 2026 third-quarter earnings results showed that monthly active users increased by 14% to 215 million. Trips also increased by 16% to 4.1 billion, and gross bookings climbed 20% to roughly $60 billion. That shows not only that more people are signing up to use its platform, but that users are also booking more rides and deliveries.

For its sales growth opportunities, the focus should be on robotaxis, which could be a huge market. According to research from Goldman Sachs (GS -6.71%), the global robotaxi market size is expected to reach $415 billion by 2035. By the end of 2026, Uber expects to have 700 fully driverless vehicles on its platform, with more than 2,000 in 2027.

Shopify will continue to benefit from the growth of e-commerce sales. But for long-term investors, understanding that the roads over the next decade will be filled with more autonomous vehicles, Uber can be a centerpiece in that market, which should only grow in value.
