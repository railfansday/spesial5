Tesla's FSD (Supervised) Attach Rate Hit 75% on New Vehicles. Here's Why That Single Number Could Matter More Than Robotaxi for the Stock in 2027.

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Tesla (TSLA +4.23%) reported that more than 75% of its new vehicle deliveries in North America included a paid Full Self-Driving (Supervised) subscription sign-up in the third quarter of 2026. That's an increase of nearly 20 percentage points from the previous quarter's attach rate.

For a company spending billions developing autonomous vehicles, artificial intelligence, and humanoid robots, this might be one of the most encouraging numbers in its latest earnings report. And unlike the robotaxi, FSD (Supervised) is already generating revenue from customers driving vehicles they own.

Tesla ended the third quarter with approximately 1.74 million active paid FSD customers worldwide, up from 1.48 million in the second quarter and 1.04 million a year earlier. That's roughly 67% annual growth.

Tesla's reported attach rate measures sign-ups at delivery. It doesn't tell investors how many customers continue paying after the initial period. In fact, management acknowledged during its earnings call that new vehicles come with a free FSD trial and that the company isn't yet providing detailed customer retention or cancellation rates.

Yes, a 75% sign-up rate is impressive, but a 75% long-term retention rate for paying customers would be far more valuable. FSD also remains a supervised driver-assistance system and is not to be seen as autonomous. Drivers must pay attention and be prepared to intervene. It isn't the same as the fully autonomous service Tesla hopes to expand through its robotaxi business. That said, there's been no indication that supervised FSD has presented much of an obstacle for customers who have opted for the subscription.

Tesla's robotaxi operation is expanding, but the company still faces uncertainty surrounding regulatory approvals, operating costs, geographic expansion, and eventual profitability. Indeed, FSD subscriptions offer something more measurable.

Tesla already has millions of customers paying for the technology, and its installed vehicle base provides opportunities to sell subscriptions beyond new-car buyers. So for 2027, I'd actually watch three numbers: total paid FSD customers, the percentage paying monthly, and customer retention.

If subscriptions continue growing while cancellations remain manageable, Tesla could build a multibillion-dollar software revenue stream without waiting for robotaxis to reach nationwide scale.

While that wouldn't necessarily justify Tesla's valuation on its own, it would give investors a growing, identifiable source of revenue to evaluate as the company's more ambitious autonomous driving plans continue to develop.
