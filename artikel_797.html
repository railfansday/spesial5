Henman: While Sinner is out of equation, Alcaraz is best in the world

Carlos Alcaraz is the "toughest player to play against" right now with Jannik Sinner out of the equation, says Tim Henman.

Alcaraz began the year facing questions over his split with long-time coach Juan Carlos Ferrero, but the ‌Spaniard has answered them emphatically by completing the career Grand Slam and adding the elusive ATP Finals title to his collection.

Since returning at the US Open ​in late August, the 23-year-old has made up for lost ‌time in a superb late-season charge that culminated in Sunday's triumph in Turin, where he beat top-ranked Alexander Zverev ​in the final.

By winning the ATP Tour's season-ender, Alcaraz joined Andre Agassi, Roger Federer and Novak Djokovic as the only men to secure the career Grand Slam and ATP Finals title.

With former world No 1 Jannik Sinner set to undergo surgery on a persistent knee injury in Finland this ‌week, casting doubt over ​his participation in the Australian Open, Alcaraz looks set to dominate tennis in 2027.

Speaking at the ATP Finals in Turin, former British No 1 Henman told Sky Sports Tennis: "We can obviously have a deep dive into the five matches here, but I think in the context of being out of the game for four and a half months, that is not easy then to come back into a Grand Slam.

"He played well in New York, getting through to the quarter-finals, but then we just have to look at this last third of the season.

"For him to not lose a match, play a couple of matches at Laver Cup, and then he's gone on this tear where every aspect of his game has been so impressive. The physical side of things, the wrist has obviously been perfect.

"Yes, he had a challenge with the hamstring, but mentally and technically his game has been so good. In Paris, he didn't have it all his own way, and I think that was such a good sign that he can play and win with this sort of B or C game.

"He's now come into the Tour-ending championships, the best against the best, an event that he's never won, and he's taken the title."

Four ​of the Spaniard's six titles this year have come since he returned from injury, with triumphs ⁠in Tokyo and Vienna followed by victories at the Paris Masters and the ATP Finals - all under the guidance of head coach Samuel Lopez and brother Alvaro.

"He started the year off with a bang down in Australia, that was the one Grand Slam that he was missing from his CV," Henman said.

"To be able to achieve the career Grand Slam at such a young age was incredible.

"Then obviously on to the clay, getting injured in Barcelona, missing a big chunk, missing two slams at Roland-Garros and Wimbledon, coming back in New York.

"Zverev's got a better serve than Alcaraz, and he hadn't lost his serve. This is the skill set and the challenge of playing Alcaraz, and I think he's in Zverev's head, obviously, because it was just a completely different level.

"Zverev couldn't find the spots, and then his serve has been unbreakable by everyone else in the tournament, and Alcaraz has dismantled him."

Former doubles No 1 Jamie Murray said: "He [Alcaraz] is amazing. He is the best. He plays in a completely different way to anything that we see these days on the ATP Tour.

"He obviously came back at the US Open, it's not an easy thing to do. I'm sure he was disappointed losing to [Ben] Shelton, the way that he went out there, but it definitely helped him after that for the rest of the year.

"He was up to speed, he had his rhythm. It's not a huge surprise, because he is one of the two best players in the world, and his level is so much better than everybody else when he's in full flow.

"It's an amazing run that he's won these last three tournaments, but it's not out of the realm of possibility for Carlos Alcaraz to do that."
