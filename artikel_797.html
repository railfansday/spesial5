Hull should be in League One, says xG - so how do they keep winning?

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Hull City are sitting comfortably in the middle of the Premier League table - when the Expected Goals underlying data says they should be two divisions below; watch Crystal Palace vs Hull live on Sky Sports this Sunday from 1pm, kick-off 2pm

Football Journalist @SamBIitz

Hull aren't doing much wrong as a newly promoted club so far. A great start to the campaign has them middle of the Premier League table, clear of the relegation zone and unbeaten at home all season.

These are all the right ingredients for survival. But the underlying data says otherwise.

If Expected Goals (xG) decided football matches - yes, yes, we know they don't - then Hull would be bottom of the Premier League, with a similar record to the actual bottom club Crystal Palace, who they play live on Sky Sports this Sunday.

The reason? Hull have only won one game this season on xG since their promotion to the top flight.On its own, this underlying data should be taken with a pinch of salt. xG numbers in a match require context.

For example, a team that loses a game can often end up having a higher xG than the opponent, as they were chasing the game and attacking for longer periods.

The same xG table also has Chelsea in 14th place at the moment. Bournemouth should be third and Nottingham Forest should be sixth. After 11 games, there is a lot of differentiation between the Expected Table and the actual table across the board.

But this isn't a new phenomenon when it comes to Hull. The fascinating thing about the Tigers is that this has been happening for 18 months.

The same xG data says Hull should also be playing in League One this season. That's because last term, they should have finished second-bottom of the Championship after 46 games, according to xG. Instead, they were promoted to the Premier League via the play-offs.

As Aston Villa showed last season, you can get away with overperforming this way for some time but things eventually regress to the mean. To do this over a long period of time is extraordinary.

So why are Hull where they are? Style has something to do with it.

Set up in a 5-4-1 formation, Hull have the lowest possession in the Premier League this season by an absolute country mile. The same applies for the number of shots they have and how high they start pressing up the pitch.

"It is not about possession, it is what you do with it," said manager Sergej Jakirovic told Sky Sports in an interview last month. Hull are comfortable in a low block, which ultimately leads to losing the 'stats side' of games.

So how do Hull actually win games? As their manager says, in the little time Hull get hold of the ball, they use it to good effect - the Tigers move the ball up the pitch quicker than any other team.

There is also an efficiency in attack. In the Championship last season, Hull had two of the most clinical forwards in Ollie McBurnie and Joe Gelhardt. They were given few opportunities, but when they got them, they took them.

The same culture applies here. "No matter who is the opponent, we will have our chances," added Jakirovic.

The aim of the game is not only to progress the ball as quickly as possible, but also choosing the right battles. That can be seen in Hull's pressing.

Hull barely press - they have the lowest number of high turnovers in the Premier League this season. However, they have the second-most goals from high turnovers this season and rank fourth for high pressures that end up in a shot. It's a selective, patient press.

Take their recent draw with Brentford as an example. Their only chance of the first half came from Hull pressing out-of-position midfielder Aaron Hickey to win a penalty which they scored from. Then they relied on the defence to hold out for a result. They conceded late, but got what many would say is a good point.

But this game plan of little possession and reliance on taking advantage of the few attacks that come their way is risky. You need a lot of luck to go your way, and Hull have had their fair share of that.

In the Championship last season, Hull had the most let-offs out of any second-tier team in terms of big chances missed by their opponents. A similar theme has continued in the Premier League.

In Hull's first two home games of the season, Man Utd and Aston Villa should have scored around two goals each, according to xG. Neither of those teams found the net, as the Tigers picked up four points instead.

But it is not just luck. In their most recent home game against Brighton, the performance of Konstantinos Tzolakis caught the eye, which confirms skills matters as well.

The Greek goalkeeper - or 'Greek God' as many Hull fans were claiming last weekend - has the best save record out of any Premier League goalkeeper this season. He alone has prevented nearly 2.0 worth of xG going in.

Without him, and without the game plan from their experienced manager, Hull would be closer to the bottom of the table. Instead, they go to the Premier League's basement club knowing they can still get a result.
