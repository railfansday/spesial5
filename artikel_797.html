A 3-2 home defeat by Austrian side LASK is the only blot on the Portuguese giants' record in Europe this season, with wins over Galatasaray, Lens and Shakhtar Donetsk all-but ensuring they will finish in the top 24 at the very least.

Despite their strong continental form, Sporting's below-par start to the domestic season cost former head coach Rui Borges his job earlier this month, with ex-Lyon boss Paulo Fonseca taking over.

He will look to striker Luis Suarez (not that one) to provide Sporting's principal goal threat, with the Colombian scoring 13 times in all competitions already this season. Fellow forward Fotis Ioannidis and winger Flavio Goncalves could also return from injury.

Mauricio Pochettino's start to life as United manager could hardly have gone much worse, with the Argentinian finding his side two goals and one man down against arch-rivals Liverpool just 13 minutes into his tenure.

The one positive from the 4-0 defeat at Anfield is that surely the only way is up for Pochettino, and a positive result on the road in Europe would be a step in the right direction.

United's 3-2 comeback success over Roma three weeks ago, which proved to be Michael Carrick's last victory in charge, put them on six points at the halfway point of the league phase, so they will likely need one more win to finish in the top 24 and qualify for the knockout phase play-offs.

To do that, Pochettino has to sort out a defence that has conceded more goals (34) than any other in Europe's top five leagues this season. Left-back Luke Shaw is closing in on his return from injury, while captain Bruno Fernandes will face his former club for the first time since leaving Sporting in 2020.

United can't stop conceding goals at the moment, but they should be able to hurt Sporting — and that could save them here.
