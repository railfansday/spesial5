Bitcoin’s $60,000 recovery masks a growing threat that could push its price as low as $29,000

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Galaxy Research says Bitcoin has exceeded historical bear market timelines while failing to meet 11 of its 13 historical bottom indicators.

Bitcoin recovered above $60,000 after slipping below that mark on Nov. 25, in a volatile week in which Bitcoin fell to around $55,000, its lowest level since September 2024.

BTC rebounded to $63,000 on Monday, but subsequently surrendered much of those gains.

According to Talos, Bitcoin lost 7.9% over the week, compared with declines of 2.9% for the S&P 500, 4.5% for the Nasdaq 100 and 7.8% for gold. Over 30 days, Bitcoin has fallen 25.3%, approximately 2.6 times the Nasdaq 100's decline.

Bitcoin's correlation with traditional risk assets has also strengthened. Its 30-day correlations with the S&P 500, Nasdaq 100 and Nvidia climbed to 0.78, 0.77 and 0.68, respectively, roughly 1.4 to twice their one-year medians.

Galaxy Research noted that Bitcoin's recent struggles are particularly unusual given its September recovery above the 50-week moving average, which has historically helped identify the end of bear markets.

Bitcoin has since fallen below both its 50-week and 200-week moving averages, breaking a recovery pattern that had held since 2011.

Bitcoin reclaimed its 50-week moving average in September after spending 44 consecutive weeks below it. It held above the indicator for seven weeks, reaching a daily close of $86,521 on Oct. 4, nearly 48% above its June low.

Galaxy found that none of the seven previous recoveries that sustained the moving average for at least seven weeks later produced a new bear-market low, even intraday.

September's recovery differed from earlier instances in one significant respect. Previous recoveries after prolonged periods below the moving average followed declines of 76.7% to 92.7%, whereas September's followed a comparatively shallow 53% drawdown on a closing basis.

Bitcoin's subsequent decline to $55,100 on Nov. 21 prompted Galaxy to withdraw its October assessment that the bear market ended in June.

The breakdown shifts attention to Bitcoin's June 30 closing low of $58,525.

Galaxy identified seven completed instances in which Bitcoin closed below a bear market low that had held for at least 60 days and from which the cryptocurrency had rebounded at least 20%.

All seven resulted in further declines of at least 17.1%, with a median loss of 51.8%. Applied to Bitcoin's June closing low, those historical outcomes would put the cryptocurrency at approximately $48,500 and $28,200, respectively.

However, another closing low would push Bitcoin beyond the historical duration of its bear markets.

As of Nov. 22, 412 days had elapsed since Bitcoin reached its record closing price of $124,824 in October 2025. Its four previous bear markets took between 163 and 406 days to bottom.

The current downturn has also exceeded historical halving-to-bottom timelines. The three previous cycle lows occurred between 777 and 912 days after their respective halvings, compared with the 946 days since Bitcoin's April 2024 halving.

While those timelines suggest Bitcoin is overdue for a recovery, its valuation and investor behavior point toward an unfinished bear market.

Bitcoin's previous four bear markets recorded peak-to-trough closing-price declines of approximately 93%, 85%, 84% and 77%, respectively.

Successive downturns have become shallower, but the largest reduction between previous cycles was eight percentage points. A bottom at the June closing low would represent a 24% reduction.

The divergence extends to Bitcoin's realized price, which estimates the aggregate cost basis of its circulating supply.

During previous bear markets, Bitcoin spent between 140 and 304 days below its realized price. It has yet to register a single daily close below that level, which stood at $53,205 on Nov. 22.

Meanwhile, the proportion of Bitcoin's supply held at a loss reached 57% on Nov. 20, within the 55% to 61% range recorded at previous cycle bottoms.

Despite that financial distress, Bitcoin's 30-day realized losses peaked at just 3.6% of its realized capitalization in February, compared with peaks of 7.3% to 16.7% during previous bear markets.

Investor sentiment has deteriorated more sharply. Galaxy's proprietary Bitcoin Fear & Greed Index fell to 5.2 on Nov. 21, its lowest reading since June 2022 and among the lowest 0.5% of historical readings.

Yet the index has spent just six days at or below 10 during the current downturn, compared with between 12 and 57 days during previous bear markets, including the month following their bottoms.

Derivatives traders have also maintained relatively resilient positioning. Bitcoin perpetual futures funding averaged a positive annualized rate of 4.3% over the seven days through Nov. 22, compared with negative 24.5% shortly after the November 2022 bottom.

As of Nov. 22, Bitcoin satisfied just two of the 13 historical bottom indicators Galaxy tracks.

The conflict between BTC's historical cycle duration and the depth of its losses leaves Galaxy examining two potential scenarios.

If cycle timelines prevail, several valuation measures point to potential support between $48,400 and $53,200. These include Bitcoin's realized price, its long-term holder cost basis, and the smallest historical decline following the breakdown of an established bear market low.

A bottom within that range would still represent Bitcoin's shallowest bear market on record.

However, a return to the valuation extremes that characterized previous downturns could push Bitcoin toward levels between $29,100 and $46,100.

Galaxy is also monitoring US spot Bitcoin exchange-traded products, which have shed 71,339 BTC worth $4.95 billion since October. Their holdings have declined to approximately 1.24 million BTC, just 1.5% above their July trough.

A break below that 1.22 million BTC trough, alongside a weekly close below Bitcoin's realized price, would strengthen the case for a deeper downturn.

Conversely, reclaiming the 200-week moving average at $68,159 and subsequently the 50-week average at $74,401 would move Galaxy back toward its October recovery thesis.
