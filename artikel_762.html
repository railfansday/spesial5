Cantwell penalty steers Blackburn past Watford

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Report and free match highlights from the Sky Bet Championship match between Watford and Blackburn Rovers at Vicarage Road on Wednesday night; Todd Cantwell's penalty proves enough as Hornets end with 10 men.

New head coach Edward Iordanescu watched from the stands as Todd Cantwell's penalty condemned struggling Watford to a 1-0 defeat against resilient Blackburn.

The former Romania national team coach, who had just flown in after signing a contract of undisclosed length, was introduced to a sparsely-populated Vicarage Road, to polite applause, 10 minutes before kick-off.

On the evidence of Watford's third home defeat of the season, he will have his work cut out to steer them away from the Championship's relegation quicksands.

By the time Iordanescu slips into the cockpit at Derby on Saturday, he will be the sixth different head coach, including interims, to take charge of Watford in 2026 after Javi Gracia, Charlie Daniels, Ed Still, Alessio Dionisi and Adrian Mariappa, who was holding the fort here.

After a damaging night, he will start his reign in the dugout without two key defenders after Jordan Zemura's red card and captain Mattie Pollock's fifth booking of the campaign.

Mariappa revealed Iordanescu had given the players a "great speech and showed his passion" when meeting his new players in the dressing room.

But with manager Tony Mowbray having stepped back to address health issues last week, for long periods Rovers were assured and they deserved the points.

In an even first half, skipper Cantwell whipped a free-kick over the top from 25 yards and centre-back Hayden Carter was foiled by Pollock's last-ditch tackle after a dazzling, mazy run.

Iker Bravo's effort flew just wide as the home side replied, and on the stroke of half-time the Hornets wasted a golden opportunity to go in front.

Jayden Fevrier tripped Zemura, but from the resulting penalty Rovers goalkeeper Aynsley Pears sprawled low to his right to turn Bravo's spot-kick round a post.

Barely 60 seconds after the resumption, Pears' opposite number Federico Ravaglia made a sharp save to deny Oladapo Afolayan and the Watford goalkeeper also did well to smother Cantwell's stooping header.

But Rovers went in front from the game's second penalty after 57 minutes when Pollock brought down Afolayan - referee Steve Martin's decision was a formality - and Cantwell made no mistake.

Pollock's fifth yellow card of the season rules him out of Iordanescu's baptism in the dugout at Pride Park, and worse was to follow for the home side five minutes later.

Zemura, already cautioned in the first half for kicking the ball away, was sent off for a second booking when he appealed for another penalty but was punished for alleged simulation.

Blackburn dominated the last half-hour and only Ravaglia's series of saves denied them a more comfortable win.

Watford's acting head coach Adrian Mariappa:

"The boys have to keep believing. They showed determination, they didn't roll over. These are the fine margins of football and at the moment we are falling the wrong side of those margins.

"We've got to keep working on the training pitch - that's how you get the rub of the green. We've got some big players to come back and it's just a question of putting the squad together and pushing on.

"The new coach has a great track record, a lot of experience and now everyone has to dig in together."

"We want Tony to take comfort and pleasure iin results like that but we also hope he's getting the time and space he needs.

"Even when the game was 11 v 11, I thought we were the better side. This is the thing Tony has created - he has asked the guys to take ownership, and take more responsibility for their performances, so we didn't have too say much to the players at half-time.

"The only frustration was probably that we didn't capitalise on the opportunities we had to win by more, but it's not always easy when you are playing against 10 men. In the end it felt quite comfortable."
