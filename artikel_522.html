OpenAI launches tool for finding child sexual abuse material

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

OpenAI announced Monday the release of a new tool to detect child sexual abuse material (CSAM), including content generated by artificial intelligence.

The tool is a classifier that looks for visual signs of abuse in images. It then scores images to help human reviewers prioritize which of them warrant attention. OpenAI said its CSAM classifier is the first broadly available tool of its kind to include a specific signal indicating the image may be generated with AI.

This capability is becoming critical as AI-generated CSAM skyrockets.

A new analysis conducted by Childlight — Global Child Safety Institute at the University of Edinburgh found AI-related reports of CSAM increased globally by more than 8,400 percent from 2023 to 2025. Last year, the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children received more than 400,000 of these reports. In 2023, that number was roughly 4,700.

"Individual companies like OpenAI have a responsibility to prevent our technology from being exploited by predators to harm children, and we also must work together with the broader industry, child safety organizations and law enforcement to advance our collective efforts to protect children online," OpenAI said in its blog post about the announcement.

The company's new tool can be used by organizations required or authorized to detect CSAM, including certain developers that use OpenAI's application programming interface, or API. OpenAI also launched the tool to work with open source detection-and-reporting software called Coop. The software, which is offered by the nonprofit organization ROOST, allows users to both flag and review content and report it to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children.

In addition to launching the classifier, OpenAI also created a model card for it. OpenAI routinely uses model cards to provide detailed information about how its ChatGPT products have been refined and tested.

Camille François, president of ROOST, told Mashable the CSAM classifier model card is the first in its field.

"OpenAI is setting an important precedent for transparency in child safety," François said in an email. "Builders need to know how well detection tools work and where they fall short. Publishing that evidence helps teams build better safeguards and gives the whole field a clearer view of where to focus research."

The model card explains that early testing led to reduced reviewing of false positives. OpenAI estimates that it could flag nearly half as many images while finding roughly the equivalent number of abusive images as the company's existing tool.

If you are a child being sexually exploited online, or you know a child who is being sexually exploited online, or you witnessed exploitation of a child occur online, you can report it to the CyberTipline, which is operated by the National Center for Missing Exploited & Children.
