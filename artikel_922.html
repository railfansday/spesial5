How 'complacent' Arsenal unravelled at Newcastle - and their key lesson

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Arsenal were completely dominant in the first half at Newcastle but saw their 1-0 lead turn into a 2-1 deficit before Noni Madueke rescued a point against their re-energised hosts; Mikel Arteta called the result "two points dropped" and revealed key lesson required to take "next step"

Senior Football Journalist @psmithXI

From "exceptional" to "complacent" - Arsenal's drop-off in the second half at Newcastle was extraordinary.

Noni Madueke's equaliser at least rescued a point, but after completely dominating the first half at St James' Park Mikel Arteta says his team have learnt a harsh lesson about the need to deliver a complete performance in the Premier League.

Credit has to go to Newcastle, who were energised by Matthias Jaissle's triple substitution at half-time, with Tino Livramento, Jacob Ramsey and Anthony Elanga bringing intensity. Harvey Barnes and Malick Thiaw brought the goals. But the Premier League champions were their own worst enemy and, in the end, were fortunate to get away with their lead at the top of the table improved - for now.

With Man City dropping points again on Saturday and surprise-package Brighton still to play on Sunday, Arsenal remain firmly in the box seat. But Arteta is eager to drive even higher standards in his squad this season as he builds on last year's success - and he was far from impressed with how his team allowed Newcastle back into the game.

"My feeling is that we lost two points today," he told Sky Sports. "We were exceptional in the first half. At every level. Our intensity, the clarity that we have, how to attack the jumps of Newcastle every time they wanted to go man-to-man, the composure that we have, the way we use our relationship to break their press...

"It should have been 2-0, 3-0 or 4-0 up. But it wasn't. And when it wasn't, then we expected in the second half that we have to again raise the level because the opponent is going to have a reaction. The opponent did have a reaction and we were very below the level that is required to stretch the gap in a game like this in this stadium. And then we gave them belief.

"I say we give them belief because we give them a goal, we give them a throw-in that we just give them the ball. We have no structure to control the opposite winger. We concede a goal and it's game on.

"And from that it became a very chaotic game. Every set-piece, we know that they are exceptional at it and we concede from a set-piece.

"And then you have to react. Then we went back to the exceptional team that we were in the first half again for another 25 minutes and we scored a great goal. We create infinity of openings to create and score the third goal, but we lack some time or to be precise, to make that count."

The stats paint a clear picture of how different the two halves from Arsenal's perspective were. Attacking stats and, significantly, duel success numbers shifted.

Sky Sports pundit Jamie Redknapp said: "I think in the second half Arsenal were just complacent. I think they thought this was so easy and that can happen sometimes in a game.

"You're getting so much time on the ball. Declan Rice was able to turn. Bruno Guimaraes had so much time in the first half and then all of a sudden [Jacob] Ramsey comes on and he's all over you. He's closing you down. Myles Lewis-Skelly's getting under pressure and that was a full credit to Newcastle.

"The fundamentals that you have to do to win a football match by winning second balls, by putting teams under pressure, by getting in people's faces, Newcastle did well in the second half."

For former Arsenal striker Ian Wright, there is also an issue in the construction of Arsenal's midfield.

"What's worrying for me and probably for Arsenal fans is that that period where we seem to can't get it back, can't grasp it back," he said.

"I think [Newcastle] could have maybe nicked another one while it was 2-1, but they didn't. I'm very concerned with the Declan Rice and Bruno Guimaraes midfield. It just didn't feel in that moment that we were able to keep them off us."

He added: "It was worrying to see Arsenal collapse like they did, for the time that they did."

An injury to goalkeeper David Raya presents another problem.

Goalscorer Madueke made a point of noting the "character" Arsenal showed to get back into the game after the turnaround from 1-0 to 2-1. On reasons for the poor start to the second half he said: "Not sticking by our own principles. I don't know whether we came out too relaxed after the first half performance."

The quality of that first half performance shouldn't be overlooked. Nor should Newcastle's role in changing the game.

But clearly Arteta will zone in on that second 45 and use it as an example of how he can raise further the levels of the English champions.

"I think individually we have some top performances for 70 minutes," said Arteta. "But at this level it's not enough. At this level requires 96 [minutes] unless you score three or four goals and then you can allow yourself for 15 or 20 minutes not to be at your best.

"That's the next step this team has to make."

"I think first half for any player in that Newcastle team, even in that dressing room, that would have been a lonely place because you were getting played off the park, the spaces seemed so big, your home crowd have been booing you. You're like, 'this is going to be a really tough half'.

"He made three great changes. Elanga added pace, Livramento good energy down that right-hand side, and Ramsey was one of the best players on the pitch.

"Newcastle, full respect from me, and I thought they showed a great deal of effort and they played against a very difficult side in Arsenal, but they got after them. It just shows you what a bit of energy can do.

"And once that crowd get up and you get that first goal, anything was possible. And they'll look at that second half thinking, actually, we could have won this game. Really good performance from Newcastle.

"I would give Jaissle more credit because he's taken a lot of stick. It's been really hard for him, but he's definitely got some money in the bank tonight.

"He'll be proud of his team. The fans will be proud of their team. And they made it very difficult for Arsenal that second half."
