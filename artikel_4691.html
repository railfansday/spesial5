Mate Rimac’s Bugatti Chiron Was Stolen From the Factory After He Left the Keys Inside

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

The car was quickly tracked down, and the CEO now says he’ll stop leaving his keys inside his multi-million dollar hypercar.

Mate Rimac, by just about any definition, is a very smart man. After all, this is a man who arguably revolutionized the electric car as much as that fellow from South Africa who gets all the press; a man who created his own EV racer as a teenager, his own electric hypercar company before he was 22 years old, and explored the bleeding edge of high-end EVs so impressively that Porsche and the VW Group gave him the reins of Bugatti at the age of 33. (He is also a very polite, patient, and fun man, as I found while interviewing him at the Geneva Motor Show and he was game to do a Vanna White impression for camera to show off the Rimac Nevera.)

But we all have blind spots. One of his, apparently, involves occasionally leaving the keys to his own personal Bugatti Chiron in the car—and on Wednesday, that led to the in-hindsight-perhaps-obvious occurrence of someone stealing his multi-million dollar speed machine from the Rimac factory.

Rimac spilled the beans himself in a set of social media posts on Thursday, explaining exactly what happened. Croatia, he stresses, is a very safe place, so for years now, he’s been leaving his keys in his cars, be they his Nevera or his Chiron or whatever else. But while the buildings are secured, the compound is fairly open, which the CEO says enabled a stranger to wander in and take the car.

After some brief confusion as to whether someone authorized to take the car had borrowed it, Rimac says, he and his crew realized the Bugatti had been boosted, and called the police. As officers arrived, Rimac says he was tracking the Chiron from its on-board telemetry, and realized it was heading towards the Slovenian border. So, being awesome, the CEO gave chase in the Porsche 911 he happened to have on hand.

Rimac says the driver made it all the way to Austria before authorities there, aided by real-time updates from the factory, were able to stop the Bugatti. At the police station there, the CEO says the thief claimed he had actually come to the factory looking for a job, then saw the Chiron with the key inside— “Like it was my fault!”, Rimac says with astonishment—and then took off with the car. (Rimac says the man was planning to go to Germany “for whatever reason,” but I think we can all make a guess as to why.)

The second, perhaps even more dramatic post, showcased the security camera footage of the theft itself. In it, the apparently cigarette-smoking thief ever-so-slowly sidles up to the blue Bugatti, glancing around before eventually taking one final drag and clambering into the car. The CEO wisely went for “The Pink Panther Theme” as a soundtrack here, but the Parks & Recreation “Don’t Be Suspicious” song would work just as well.

Suffice it to say, Rimac says he will not be keeping the keys in the cars from now on. “Don’t try your luck—it will not happen again,” he says.
