Ubisoft says Assassin's Creed Jade is still alive as it merges two of its Creative Houses

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Ubisoft has confirmed that while Assassin's Creed Jade - its mobile entry in the series - is still in development, its focus remains with console and PC.

Speaking during the earnings call for Ubisoft's first-half results, CFO Frédérick Duguet said mobile was "still an opportunity for the medium to longer term, with games such as AC Jade on Assassin’s Creed", according to a transcript of the call published by Investing.com.

"Looking at what we can see in terms of long-term financial return, in the mindset of having a stronger focus in terms of capital allocation and financial returns, we consider that we would rather put our resources on bigger opportunities," Duguet said. "Today, we can say that console and PC remain our core focus for our biggest franchises."

He added: "The focus is really to make sure that we come with strong growth on console and PC, and notably PC, which is a more dynamic platform than ever."

Ubisoft last confirmed its China-set mobile game was still in the works in March, when it was mentioned in the publisher’s Assassin's Creed: Into 2026 blog. Assassin's Creed now sits within Vantage Studios, Ubisoft's joint venture with Tencent, which launched last year.

Ubisoft's results also confirmed some more changes to the way the company is run, with the publisher saying its new operating model is "now fully in place". Creative Houses 3 and 5 have been combined under the leadership of Julien Bares "to leverage synergies between Live experiences across platforms".

All leadership roles across the Creative Houses are now filled, too. An external general manager has been appointed for Creative House 4 and is set to join in January 2027, though Ubisoft hasn't yet confirmed who. That follows September's appointment of 2K and Amazon Games veteran Christoph Hartmann to lead Creative House 2, home to The Division, Ghost Recon, and Splinter Cell, which was renamed Massive Entertainment at the same time.

Outside the Creative Houses, Motherbrain, the team behind Ubisoft's generative AI experiment Teammates, is now a subsidiary in its own right. It's working on "smart NPCs with memory and AI game masters that manage large-scale systemic worlds". A separate unit has also been set up to look after brands that don't belong to any of the Creative Houses.

Ubisoft says the new structure is already paying off, pointing to the commercial success of Assassin's Creed Black Flag Resynced and renewed momentum for Rainbow Six Siege, which it says ranked among the top 10 games across Xbox and PlayStation for monthly active users over the half.

That said, the publisher nonetheless posted a net loss of €254.5 million for the first half of the year, with net bookings down 16.5 percent to €644.7 million.

The news comes just hours after Ubisoft confirmed 166 job cuts at its Montréal and Québec City studios, the latest in a year of layoffs. They're part of the "major organisational, operational and portfolio reset" that Ubisoft announced in January. That initial announcement was accompanied by the news of two studio closures - affecting its Halifax mobile and Stockholm teams - plus the cancellation of six projects, including the much-delayed Prince of Persia: The Sands of Time remake.

Since then, 105 jobs have been cut at Red Storm Entertainment, the Ubisoft studio behind the Ghost Recon series, and around 380 employees were reportedly laid off when the publisher's Winnipeg and Belgrade teams shut in June. In October, Ubisoft closed Trials developer RedLynx and downsized its Singapore Assassin's Creed Black Flag Resynced team, impacting a total of approximately 120 employees. Amid all this, Ubisoft developers have been on strike in response to the publisher's sweeping layoffs.

On the weekend, we learned that Ubisoft is reportedly working on a new Rainbow Six Vegas game 18 years after the last entry in the series.
