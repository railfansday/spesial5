Have you ever butted heads with a partner because you wanted to plan a jam-packed vacation itinerary and they preferred to lounge on the beach? Have you ever argued with family or friends about whether to book a local Airbnb or an all-inclusive resort? When flying solo, you don't need to worry about these dilemmas — and that's far from the only benefit of solo travel.

However, planning a trip on your own can feel daunting, especially if it's your first time. That's why I've rounded up some of TPG's favorite destinations for first-time solo travelers. No matter what kind of traveler you are and what you're looking for on your maiden voyage, this list is sure to inspire you.

While Mexico often conjures images of all-inclusive hotels and long beach days, the cultural and culinary hub of Oaxaca goes deeper. TPG managing editor Lyndsey Matthews just got back from a solo trip here and raves about the travel-worthy food scene and captivating art and history.

“The historic center of Oaxaca is easily walkable and full of coffee shops, art galleries and ceramic stores to pop in and out of at your own pace and convenience,” she says. “It was also super easy to make friends on the few tours I booked — I did a nighttime street food tour with Culinary Backstreets and a long day trip to Hierve del Agua that I booked on Viator, and I ended up running into the people I met at bakeries and markets the rest of the week.”

Whatever type of solo trip you’re dreaming of, France is worth considering.

TPG principal spokesperson Clint Henderson recommends Paris. It may be known as the City of Love, but that doesn’t mean you can’t enjoy it on your own — especially if you’re a foodie or art lover. Snack on fresh-baked croissants in between museum visits (the Musee du quai Branly, which features non-Western art from around the world, is a must-see).

If you’d prefer a slower pace, Lauren Wassum raves about Provence: “There are plenty of villages to explore where you can get in touch with yourself through slow and intentional living.” But this isn’t to say there is nothing to do. “You can walk Van Gogh’s footsteps in Arles and Saint-Rémy, take a provencal cooking class or even tour the lavender fields when in they are in bloom in the Luberon Valley.”

To really push yourself out of your comfort zone, try Japan for your first solo trip. Japan’s culture and traditions are so different from the U.S., it will catapult you out of your day-to-day life a little faster than most destinations.

TPG senior writer Tanner Saunders loves the Land of the Rising Sun as a solo destination. “Tokyo is massive, sure, but it's relatively easy to figure out how to get around, the people are mostly so kind, it's super safe and dining alone is so common.” And don’t worry if you can’t read Japanese; English is common in most tourist areas.

Japan may seem small, but its islands are incredibly diverse. There’s something for every type of traveler — from the snow-dusted ski capital of Hokkaido to the southernmost island of Okinawa, sometimes known as “the Hawaii of Japan.”

Senior editor Christine Gallipeau recommends New Zealand for first-time solo travelers, citing the country’s safety record and the Kiwis’ trademark friendliness.

“I was there during the Rugby World Cup and had a last-minute issue with lodging,” she says. “One of the local airports let me and my friends just crash in the rental car office for the night, and later in Wellington, a stranger opened their home to us since hostels and hotels were sold out.”

It takes a while to get here, but you’ll be rewarded with peace, quiet and the sense of being truly alone.

Most visitors agree road tripping is the best way to see New Zealand, so you’ll need to rent a car (and be prepared to drive on the left side). You can see the highlights in just a few days, or spend weeks exploring and not run out of things to do.

If you’d like to stay a little closer to home for your first solo trip, Costa Rica is relatively easy to reach from the U.S. while still feeling a world away.

It’s among Latin America’s most popular solo travel destinations thanks, in part, to its friendly locals. “Everyone is sooo welcoming,” says TPG social media manager Maren Gimnes, “plus it’s safe and easy to get around.”

With beaches on one side and wildlife-rich rainforests on the other, not to mention volcanoes and hot springs, solo travelers can get their fill of both relaxation and adventure.

Try basing yourself in Tamarindo if you’re after sun, surf and sand. If wildlife is your main goal, head to Monteverde Cloud Forest or Manuel Antonio National Park. Thrill seekers will want to focus on La Fortuna, the country’s adventure capital.

Many European cities are great candidates for first-time solo trips, but Ireland’s lively capital city stands out for its welcoming pub culture and manageable size. TPG social media manager Annie Black loves it because Dublin is “very easy to navigate and has lots of fun free things to do.”

If you’re hoping to socialize with locals and fellow travelers, Dublin’s welcoming pub culture is a must. Head to the bustling Temple Bar neighborhood (or any pub, really) for a pint of Guinness or Bulmer’s and some foot-stomping trad music.

Plus, plentiful day trips and group tours make it easy for solo travelers to explore nearby attractions like the gorgeous Wicklow Mountains and the jaw-dropping Giant’s Causeway without the hassle of renting a car.

If you’re ready to venture a little farther afield, TPG principal Eric Rosen highlights Cape Town as a great spot for first-time visitors, saying it’s “very navigable and safe for solo travelers.”

Cape Town has all the benefits of a city trip plus easy access to outdoor pursuits like hiking, mountain bike trails, beaches and even penguins. Rideshares are readily available and affordable for getting around town, or you can easily explore outside the city by renting a car, hiring a driver or joining an organized group tour.

Sea Point and De Waterkant/Green Point neighborhoods are great choices for solo travelers wanting a safe, walkable base close to the beach. If you’d rather be within easy reach of the restaurant and bar scene on Kloof Street, try Tamboerskloof.

Vietnam is a Southeast Asian sliver of adventure, culture and more. Plus, it’s quite affordable — even if you’re not splitting the cost of your hotel with a travel companion. Most meals will run you between $5 and $10.

There’s truly something for everyone: The UNESCO World Heritage Site of Ha Long Bay; the delightfully chaotic capital of Hanoi; and the hip beach town of Da Nang, where expats from all over the world relax.

You won’t want to leave without sampling some of the local cuisine. There’s pho and banh mi, of course, but don’t miss other local dishes like bun cha (pork with dipping broth) and cha ca (turmeric-marinated fish). Wash it all down with a beer at Hanoi’s Train Street, where you can eat while watching an active railway run back and forth inches from your trackside café.

There’s a reason (several, in fact) that Iceland is a perennial favorite for solo travelers. English is nearly ubiquitous, and the country’s manageable size and well-developed infrastructure make it easy to get around. It’s also one of the safest countries on the planet — so safe, it’s said Iceland has more horses than police officers.

Nature lovers can bask in a huge variety of landscapes, from hiking across glaciers to soaking in geothermal hot springs to snorkeling between tectonic plates. Meanwhile, travelers seeking nightlife and connection can revel in downtown Reykjavik, which also has a booming LGBTQIA+ scene.

“You really can’t go wrong with any Nordic country,” TPG managing editor Lyndsey Matthews says. “English is widely spoken, plus they’re all very safe countries, so I’ve always been comfortable whether it’s a super remote destination or a big city.”

Whether you’re looking for epic underwater adventures or a few days of beach bumming, look no further than the Central American oasis of Belize.

Despite its small size, Belize has a big reputation for some of the world’s best snorkeling and scuba diving. Check some adventures off your bucket list by diving the Great Blue Hole or swimming with nurse sharks in Shark Ray Alley.

If you’d rather spend your vacation on land — perhaps in a hammock with a good book and a cold drink — head out on a ferry to one of the many islands, or cays, like car-free Caye Caulker. The island's motto is “Go Slow,” and that’s all you need to know.

When traveling solo, you can go, do, see and eat exactly what you choose — whether that's a culture-filled city trip, an epic outdoor adventure, a relaxing beach break or a little of everything. This list has all the above so you can choose your dream solo destination.
