Aave’s revenue falls $10M in Q3, but one metric tells a new story

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Aave's quarterly revenue fell 36.6% as liquidation fees plunged and borrow-interest income declined despite rising deposits.

Aave’s deposits grew during the third quarter of 2026, but the lending protocol’s quarterly borrow-interest revenue remained below the second-quarter levels, according to an Aave Labs report published Nov. 13.

The report puts supplied assets up about 40% between July 1 and Oct. 1, while its borrow-interest revenue line fell roughly 23% from the previous quarter.

Lower liquidation-related receipts explain about 39% of the overall revenue decline, but a smaller borrow-interest line also contributed substantially. Monthly revenue improved through the third quarter, and Aave’s separate official dashboard shows net income rising, so the figures do not establish a fall in profit.

The report lists total protocol revenue of roughly $27.3 million in the second quarter and $17.3 million in the third quarter, a decline of almost $10 million (36.6%). Its named borrow-interest line fell from $16.2 million to $12.5 million, a reduction of $3.7 million.

Combining ordinary liquidation fees with Smart Value Recapture (SVR) liquidation fees gives $4.3 million in the second quarter against about $402,429 in the third quarter. That $3.9 million reduction accounts for 39.1% of the total revenue decrease.

The borrow-interest line explains another 37.1% of the decrease. Other revenue lines, net of increases, account for the remaining 23.8%. Staking income fell from nearly $1.3 million to zero, special income from about $684,000 to zero, and the separate aToken interest line declined by roughly $708,000.

Labs reports monthly protocol revenue of $5.4 million in July, $5.7 million in August and $6.2 million in September. Its borrow-interest line also rose each month, from $3.9 million to $4.1 million, then to $4.4 million.

Meanwhile, the report’s all-version supplied balance rose from $24.1 billion on July 1 to $33.8 billion on Oct. 1. Borrowed assets increased from $10.2 billion to $13.3 billion.

Asset-price changes can affect dollar balances, so without a matching average lending balance, the tables cannot establish that Aave earned less per dollar lent. They show an endpoint balance rebound alongside a smaller quarterly revenue total.

TokenLogic's Aave Insights dashboard reports revenue for the second and third quarters of $23.4 million and $17.8 million, respectively, different from the Labs report.

It shows net income rising from $197,930 to $1.7 million as expenses fell from $23.2 million to $16.1 million. The sources provide no numerical reconciliation between the two revenue series.

DefiLlama’s monthly revenue figures total approximately $21.3 million for April through June and $13.4 million for July through September.

DefiLlama’s methodology separates borrower fees, revenue retained by the protocol, supplier income, and tokenholder revenue. These measures cannot be substituted for Aave Labs’ profit, DAO net income or returns to AAVE holders.

Labs describes its operational figures as unaudited and subject to revision, and says they are not financial statements of Aave Labs or the DAO. The breakdown above uses that report’s revenue series throughout.

A separate development is TokenLogic’s Nov. 12 reserve-factor proposal, which changes the reserve factor across 54 asset reserves in 12 Aave V3 markets and the older V2 Ethereum market, projecting about $3.5 million in additional annualized DAO revenue at Nov. 9 balances and rates.

The proposal would lift the blended reserve factor from 12.0% to 12.7%. Its Plasma USDT0 component remains conditional on finalized terms, and the forum post lists community feedback and escalation to an on-chain proposal as next steps.

The reserve factor allocates borrower interest between suppliers and the DAO, but retaining a larger share is a different mechanism from attracting more deposits.
