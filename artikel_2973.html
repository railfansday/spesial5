Paysign: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Plasma donor card revenue is shrinking as Paysign shifts toward patient affordability, so the margin story depends on how fast that pivot plays out.

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 9, 2026, Paysign (PAYS +0.32%) announced a 43% year-over-year revenue increase to $30.8 million, driven by expansion in its pharmaceutical copay assistance business. The shift from transactional plasma donor card processing toward higher-margin patient affordability solutions is accelerating overall profitability and lifting gross margins. Three things management said stand out.

Pharma patient affordability revenue, which provides financial assistance cards for prescription medications, grew 110% year-over-year to $16.6 million as the number of active programs expanded. This higher-margin segment now generates more revenue than legacy payment processing for plasma collection centers.

A plasma client is transitioning 113 collection centers to an existing banking partner by year-end, which will reduce fourth-quarter revenue by $1.5 million and gross profit by $700,000. Management expects to offset these losses over subsequent quarters through existing client expansion and new customer acquisition.

Paysign uses dynamic business rules, a software feature that prevents health insurance co-pay accumulators from absorbing pharmaceutical manufacturer assistance funds. By protecting client budgets, the software increases product stickiness and boosts average quarterly revenue per program.

Expanding margins and cash flow from legacy processing give Paysign flexibility to scale its software platform across new medical verticals. Management is watching program onboarding schedules and regulatory approval for its Apherion donor management software, which automates plasma collection operations.

With zero bank debt and $34.5 million in cash, the company is allocating capital to high-margin patient-assistance features while expanding its core processing operations.
