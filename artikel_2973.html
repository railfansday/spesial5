Only 11% of the Power Plants Vistra Just Bought Are in Texas. Here's Why That Matters After the Election.

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Independent power producers like Vistra have faced scrutiny from regulators over data center deals, but the recent dip in the stock price could be a buying opportunity for patient investors.

The recent midterm elections cast a shadow over the ongoing artificial intelligence (AI) data center build-out. Many viewed the results as a referendum on data centers, with voters electing candidates critical of their rapid expansion and its impacts on communities and the power grid.

Vistra (VST -2.31%) is an independent power producer and AI power stock that has found itself in the middle of all this debate. The company has a large presence in Texas, which represents over one-quarter of planned U.S. data center capacity growth.

The issue is a bipartisan one. Outgoing Texas governor Greg Abbott paused state permits for data centers to evaluate their impact on the ERCOT electric grid, while governor-elect Gina Hinojosa has campaigned to extend the moratorium until lawmakers establish stronger AI regulations.

Vistra's recent acquisition of Cogentrix added 10 natural gas power plants with nearly 5.5 gigawatts (GW) of capacity, with only roughly 11% of that in Texas. Here's why this matters after the recent election.

Before it acquired Cogentrix, roughly 42% of Vistra's power generation capacity operated in Texas. This position and its merchant-power business model put Vistra in an excellent position to benefit from growing electricity demand as data centers move to Texas.

With its recent Cogentrix acquisition, Vistra added power capacity across the Mid-Atlantic, New England, and Texas, but only 11% of that capacity is in Texas, reducing its overall exposure to the region to 40% of its total power generation capacity.

At one point last year, Vistra stock touched nearly $220 per share as investors priced in optimistic growth projections. However, the stock has fallen nearly 41% from that peak, with the weakness beginning well before the 2026 midterm elections. Regulators across the board have been pushing back on data center developers over soaring electricity rates, strained power grids, and heavy water use for most of this year.

To address these concerns, Vistra has signed power purchase agreements (PPAs) with major hyperscalers, Meta Platforms and Amazon Web Services, that lock in long-term deals and add new capacity to the grid through upgrades and uprates. The company is also aggressively adding capacity, including 3.1 GW in Texas since 2020, to maintain grid reliability and meet growing demand.

The AI trade faces big questions following the most recent midterm results. That said, Vistra stock is down significantly from its all-time high, and much of the premium has been removed; today, the stock is priced right around 15 times its projected earnings for the year.

For investors bullish on the long-term AI build-out and electricity demand, I believe the recent dip in Vistra is an excellent opportunity to scoop up shares to hold for the long haul.
