Live Service Fatigue Claims One More Popular Shooter

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Covering the video games industry since 2017, with experience in news, articles, and reviews. Currently a writer at DualShockers and gaming magazines.

Another ambitious online shooter is biting the dust, and this time, it seems that players won't even get the chance to return for one last update.

GFA Games has officially announced today that PIONER, its post-apocalyptic MMO inspired by titles like STALKER and Fallout, is shutting down. The studio is ending development, canceling future updates, and preparing to take the game's servers offline after failing to secure the funding necessary to keep the project running.

In an announcement shared on social media, the development team explained that maintaining PIONER required additional financial support, but they couldn’t find any. The studio had spent the past few months negotiating with existing and potential partners, hoping to find an investment deal that would allow development to continue and preserve the team.

Unfortunately, those discussions didn't lead anywhere. According to the developers, GFA Games no longer has enough money of its own to continue working on the game or maintain its server infrastructure.

“We were unable to agree on the funding needed to continue the project. The studio no longer has sufficient funds of its own to continue development and keep the servers running.”

Aleksandr Nikitin, CEO of GFA Games, even addressed the community, expressing his disappointment over the decision and apologizing to players who purchased PIONER expecting more updates. He explained that the team had envisioned a long future for the project and had been trying to find a way to keep both the game and its developers together.

The recent announcement effectively puts an end to those plans. Since PIONER is an online-only experience, shutting down its servers also means players will no longer be able to access the game once the infrastructure is taken offline.

It's quite an unfortunate outcome for a game that spent years building up expectations before finally arriving on Steam Early Access in December 2025. The post-apocalyptic MMO promised an enormous world filled with dangerous mutants, exploration, survival mechanics, and multiplayer activities, which initially attracted some attention on Steam, reaching a peak of more than 1,600 concurrent players around launch. However, that interest quickly faded. By September, its 24-hour peak had dropped to just 11 players, according to SteamDB.
