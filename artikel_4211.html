Ocarina of Time Remake Finally Gives Zelda’s Saddest Story the Attention It Deserves

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Covering the video games industry since 2017, with experience in news, articles, and reviews. Currently a writer at DualShockers and gaming magazines.

After nearly 30 years, the remake of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time is finally here, and it's already winning over critics and fans alike – myself included. As I highlighted in my review, this new version embodies what a modern remake should be: an experience that embraces contemporary improvements without losing the old soul of the classic we all love!

And one of the things I've always loved about Ocarina of Time is how many curious characters are scattered throughout Hyrule. Some have surprisingly elaborate backstories, while others barely give you enough information to understand what's going on. More often than not, you need to be genuinely curious to piece together their stories and figure out what they're doing in the world.

My favorite example, by far, is Grog, a rather strange-looking punk who barely appeared in the original game but arguably had one of its saddest stories. Much of what made his character so fascinating came from the lovely fan theories surrounding his family and his mysterious disappearance.

To my surprise, the Switch 2 remake doesn't just subtly address some of those theories, but it also expands Grog's story in ways that make his already tragic arc considerably more meaningful. And to appreciate just how much has changed, we first need to revisit what made this little side story so memorable in the first place.

First things first, let's remember how Grog's story unfolded in the original Ocarina of Time. The character only appeared in two locations throughout the entire adventure: beneath a tree in Kakariko Village at night as Child Link, and later in the Lost Woods as Adult Link.

In Kakariko Village, you'll find him sitting beneath the tree in the center of town, looking rather miserable. Talk to him, and you'll quickly discover that he's not exactly the friendliest person around. He's withdrawn, bitter, and apparently hates just about everyone – including Link, simply for daring to approach him! And that's pretty much all you get from this first encounter.

To my surprise, the Switch 2 remake doesn't just subtly address some of those theories, but it also expands Grog's story in ways that make his already tragic arc considerably more meaningful.

His second appearance, however, is where things get interesting. As Adult Link, you can find Grog in the Lost Woods, but getting him to appear requires a little detour. First, you need to talk to the Cucco Lady in Kakariko Village, who turns out to be Grog's sister. She explains that he had a falling-out with their father, the village's Master Craftsman, and eventually left home. She's worried about him, though, so she gives you an egg that eventually hatches into Cojiro, Grog's beloved blue Cucco.

After using Cojiro to wake up Talon in Kakariko Village, you can take the Cucco into the Lost Woods. That's when you'll finally find Grog again, now seven years later, sitting in a rather strange, almost lifeless state. However, showing him Cojiro seems to snap him out of it, and he asks you to take an Odd Mushroom to the Granny's Potion Shop so she can prepare a medicine for him.

There's just one problem: when you return with the medicine, Grog is nowhere to be found. Instead, you're greeted by Fado, one of the Kokiri children, who explains that he disappeared into the Lost Woods. She also tells you that adults who get lost in the forest turn into Stalfos – those skeletal enemies that have been giving Link a hard time throughout the adventure.

And just like that, Grog's story appears to come to an end. The implication is pretty heartbreaking: the troubled young man who left home after a disagreement with his father has seemingly lost himself in the forest, never to return.

But even that brief story leaves plenty of unanswered questions. What exactly happened between Grog and his father? Why was he so unhappy? And what was the medicine supposed to cure? The original game never gave us definitive answers, leaving players to connect the dots through scattered dialogue and a handful of subtle clues. Thankfully, the remake finally gives us a much closer look at what was going on!

Some of the original's most charming quirks from Zelda: Ocarina of Time didn't make the journey.

One of the most interesting additions in the remake is a new time system that allows the day-night cycle to progress naturally even while you're exploring towns and villages. Outside of more contained areas, such as dungeons and minigame shops, NPCs now follow their own schedules, appearing in different locations and going about their daily routines.

It's a relatively small change, but it makes Hyrule feel considerably more alive – and if you're a fan of Majora's Mask, you'll probably appreciate the familiar feeling of watching characters follow their own little agendas instead of simply standing around waiting for Link to show up!

More importantly, this new system gives us additional opportunities to learn about Grog when playing as Child Link. For starters, you can now find him during the day near the entrance to Kakariko Village's graveyard. He's officially identified as the Master Craftsman's Son, making his connection to the village's grumpy carpenter much clearer from the beginning. Talk to him (or simply get close enough to hear him mumbling to himself) and you'll discover that his hatred for practically everything is closely tied to his strained relationship with his father.

And speaking of the Master Craftsman, the remake also gives us a better look at his daily routine. He and his assistants no longer spend all their time repeating the same actions in the same spots. Instead, they're spread throughout the village, and they even gather in front of the central tree for a meeting as the day comes to an end.

Talking to the assistants reveals that they also find their boss rather difficult to deal with. He's a grumpy, demanding man who seems to have a complaint about just about everything. Yet, interestingly enough, none of those complaints ever seem to involve Grog. While his son is clearly consumed by their strained relationship, the Master Craftsman barely acknowledges that anything is wrong – or just wants to ignore it.

But my favorite addition is that you can actually follow Grog around Kakariko Village throughout the day. He eventually gets up from his spot and sneaks behind a pile of bricks near the construction site overseen by his father. There, he quietly mutters that he needs to wait for the Master Craftsman to leave before he can go inside.

And when night finally falls, Grog makes his way back to the central tree, recreating the exact scene we remember from the original game. It's such a small sequence, but it adds an entirely new layer to his character. We no longer just see a miserable young man sitting beneath a tree. We see someone who is actively avoiding his father, seemingly unable to find a way to repair their relationship. And considering what we eventually learn about Grog, that little detail becomes even more heartbreaking.

The remake also expands Grog's story when playing as Adult Link, particularly through additional dialogue with his sister and mother. As in the original, you'll only encounter Grog in the Lost Woods once you reach the point in the Biggoron Sword side quest where Cojiro has turned blue. However, the Cucco Lady now has considerably more information to share about why her brother left home in the first place.

According to her, Grog didn't want to follow the family business. While their father expected him to become a carpenter, Grog wanted to work with animals, something he was particularly passionate about and apparently quite good at. The Master Craftsman strongly disapproved of this decision, and their disagreement eventually caused a complete breakdown in their relationship.

This is a pretty significant addition because it finally gives us a clearer picture of what happened between the two. Grog wasn't simply a rebellious young man who decided to leave home. He had his own dreams and interests, but his father couldn't accept that his son wanted a different future.

After hearing this, you can head into the Lost Woods and follow the original sequence. Grog is still there, seemingly trapped in the same strange state, and showing him Cojiro produces the same reaction as before. He snaps out of it and asks you to take the Odd Mushroom to the Granny's Potion Shop.

However, Granny also has a few new things to say! She confirms that Grog has been acting foolishly by distancing himself from his family, adding another perspective to the conflict. It's a small exchange, but it helps establish that this isn't just a disagreement between a father and son. So yes, the entire family has been affected by Grog's departure.

Unfortunately, things take the same tragic turn when you return to the Lost Woods with the medicine. Grog is gone, and Fado is waiting in his place. This time, however, the encounter is considerably more unsettling. She immediately notices the medicine's smell and desperately asks you to hand it over, offering a tool Grog left behind in exchange. Then, she explains that adults who enter the forest and lose their way, just like Grog apparently did, eventually turn into Stalfos.

But the story doesn't quite end there. You can return to Kakariko Village and talk to the Cucco Lady again, who provides even more information about her brother. She explains that she now takes care of the Cuccos because Grog left home after refusing to become a carpenter. In other words, her own life has also been shaped by the same family conflict that drove him away. And she’s also still hopeful that her brother is okay and will be back someday.

Finally, you can take the tool Grog left behind to the Master Craftsman, who is now waiting near the entrance to Gerudo Valley. He recognizes it as something he had given his son and explains that he had asked Grog to return it personally. Instead, his son apparently chose to send someone else to do the job.

It's a rather dismissive reaction, especially considering everything we've learned about their relationship. Even after Grog has disappeared, his father seems more concerned about the way he returned a tool than the fact that he may never see his son again.

What always fascinated me about Grog's story in the original Ocarina of Time was how much the game managed to communicate without ever explicitly explaining anything. It gave us scattered clues, brief conversations, and seemingly unrelated characters who slowly revealed their connections to one another. The rest was left to the players, who spent years discussing theories about Grog, his family, and what might have happened to him.

The remake takes those little pieces and finally puts them together. We now know exactly why Grog left home, what caused the rift with his father, and how his decision affected the rest of his family. And while the game still leaves some things open to interpretation, his story feels much more complete.

What I particularly love about this addition is that it doesn't try to turn Grog into some incredibly important character or give him an elaborate questline that changes the entire adventure. Instead, it simply gives more attention to a person who was already part of Hyrule, allowing us to understand the struggles that were always hiding behind his grumpy attitude.

And there's something genuinely sad about realizing that the Stalfos we encounter throughout the game might have once been people with their own dreams, families, and reasons for entering the forest. Grog's story makes that possibility feel much more personal. After all, he wasn't some nameless traveler who got lost. He was a young man who wanted to follow his own path, but never managed to find a way back home.

I also love how this expanded story fits into what makes Ocarina of Time so special! Hyrule isn't just a collection of locations for Link to explore, but a world filled with people whose lives continue beyond the moments when we interact with them. Sometimes, all it takes is a few extra lines of dialogue or a character walking to a different spot at sunset to make that world feel considerably richer.

Of course, I still have one personal theory I'd rather believe. Maybe Grog didn't actually turn into a Stalfos. Maybe he discovered the secret entrance to Termina, found his way to Clock Town, and eventually became the Cucco-loving man we meet in Majora's Mask. After all, if there's anyone who deserves a happy ending surrounded by the animals he loves, it's Grog. And after everything this remake has revealed about his family, I think I'll keep believing that version of the story.
