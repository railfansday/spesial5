‘Absurd’: Delta CEO says it takes longer to fly from ATL to LGA today than in the 1950s

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Delta CEO Ed Bastian says one of his airline’s oldest routes is slower now than when it launched in the 1950s, and it’s “absurd.”

“Today, it takes more time to fly from Atlanta to LaGuardia than it did in the 1950s when we first launched that flight, which is absurd,” Bastian told Fortune’s Alyson Shontell at the Fortune 500 Innovation Forum on Monday.

His complaint lands days after the Federal Aviation Administration’s nationwide grounding of flights last week put the country’s congested air traffic control system back in the spotlight. Bastian said fixing it would bring ticket prices down by letting airlines add flights, but modernization can’t happen without investment, which doesn’t seem likely when the data-center buildout is diverting trillions in capital.

“We can’t even get $50 billion to fix our skies, which affects all of us at a much higher level than any individual data center,” Bastian said.

Airplanes have become more advanced since the one equipped for the world’s first scheduled commercial flight from St. Petersburg to Tampa over a century ago. By Bastian’s account, the system they fly through hasn’t kept up.

Delta is also grappling with airfare pricing after the Iran war’s pressure on jet fuel prices squeezed the airline industry, and it has passed much of that cost onto consumers. Bastian said Delta’s tickets are 15% to 20% higher year-over-year because of jet fuel, adding that this wouldn’t have been possible to do if there wasn’t sufficient demand.

“We would not be able to generate that type of pricing if we didn’t have a consumer that was willing to pay it, and so that tells you the core demand is really healthy, even if pricing is where it’s at,” Bastian said.

According to the Bureau of Labor Statistics’ October release, U.S. airfares climbed 22.4% year over year as airlines raised prices to offset the higher cost of jet fuel. That helped the five major airlines generate $8.8 billion more in Q3 2026 than in Q3 2025, but passenger traffic grew less than 1%. Bastian said that instead of competing on fares with other airlines, Delta is trying to draw more passengers by distinguishing itself from its competition through service.

“If you don’t have people that are there to serve our customers in a premium manner, the rest of the stuff doesn’t really matter, and that’s the one thing that our competitors cannot replicate,” he said.

He said 15 years of cultivating that premium customer service culture came through during the FAA flight shutdown. While he said he typically hears compliments about the service on most weekends, this one was special because of the disruption coming from outside of Delta’s control.

“Unlike most weekends, when there’s always something going wrong or excuses somewhere, the overwhelming sentiment I received from customers was, ‘You have amazing people’ because they saw the stress, the conditions [were] none of our doing,” he said.

That’s also why Bastian said AI won’t replace Delta employees. Instead, the company will redeploy affected employees to the extent possible because “there’s always something to do.” He said this is because when there’s a crisis like the FAA one, customers prefer human support over machines.

“Obviously this past weekend, they wanted individuals to tell them what was going on, and there’s a lack of trust the more you put technology in place of humans, so humans are always going to be at the center of Delta,” he said.
