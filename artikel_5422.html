42 state AGs are trying to block Character.AI from auctioning user chats

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The coalition asked a Delaware bankruptcy court to reject the sale of chats from 21 million U.S. accounts, set for auction Dec. 1

A coalition of 42 attorneys general is asking a Delaware bankruptcy court to reject Character.AI's plan to sell its platform along with the stored conversations tied to over 21 million accounts in the U.S. The states filed an objection on Nov. 20 to a sale covering nearly everything the company owns.

More than 2.1 million of those accounts are registered to minors, according to the filing. The chats and users' personal data, including IP addresses and other metadata, are set to be sold at auction on Dec. 1. A sale hearing is scheduled for Dec. 3 and 4.

Character.AI picked Smirks and Pangs Acquisition as its lead bidder on Nov. 14, the filing says. Smirks would pay $10 million in cash and assume liabilities. The Delaware company was formed Nov. 13, and the sale agreement names no parent company or sponsor.

California Attorney General Rob Bonta said in a statement that "bankruptcy should not be used to sidestep these consumer protections." Maryland Attorney General Anthony Brown urged the court to change the terms of the auction. Connecticut Attorney General William Tong called on the court to reject the sale. Colorado Attorney General Phil Weiser said Colorado, California and Connecticut are leading the effort.

The states ask the court to deny the proposed sale terms, reject Smirks as lead bidder and deny its proposed $350,000 break-up fee, which it would collect if Character.AI sells to someone else. At a minimum, they say, any sale must exclude minors' chats and other sensitive data and include conditions that protect users.
