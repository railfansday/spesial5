Jets’ main goal against putrid Chargers starts with getting to an elusive place

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

LOS ANGELES — The Jets head west with some extra baggage this week.

They face the Chargers on Sunday, carrying with them a touchdown drought of nine quarters. The Jets last reached the end zone Nov. 1 in the third quarter against the Raiders, when Braelon Allen scored on a 21-yard run. Since then, they have not crossed the goal line. They instead have settled for nine Jason Sanders field goals.

Now, the 2-8 Jets face a 1-8 Chargers team with the chance to breathe some life into whatever is left of their season, but it starts with scoring touchdowns.

"It all weighs on you," offensive coordinator Frank Reich said. "When your record is what it is and when you go nine quarters without scoring a touchdown, yeah, that's what we're here all hours of the day trying to get right.

"That starts with me; I'm the offensive coordinator. We're working hard to get that right. I'm confident we will. I believe we have a very competent staff that knows how to game plan, put players in position. I believe we've shown that at times this year. We just need to show it more consistently, and we need to execute and make the plays more consistently."

New York Jets quarterback Geno Smith (7) runs the ball against the Buffalo Bills at MetLife Stadium on Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. Charles Wenzelberg/NY Post

The offensive ineptitude has been particularly frustrating to watch because they do move the ball, but they find a way to stall in the opponent's territory, particularly the red zone. The Jets had 376 yards last week in a 31-12 loss to the Bills, but had to kick four field goals.

The red zone has been the dead zone. Their red zone touchdown percentage of 37.9 is last in the NFL.

The Jets are trying to fix their issues inside the 20 with extra work in practice.

Jets offensive coordinator Frank Reich speaks to the media before practice in Florham Park, N.J., Thursday, Nov. 19, 2026. Bill Kostroun for NYPost

"Well, we've already started a process on Wednesday," coach Aaron Glenn said, "the last period that we have, we have it as a red zone period, and I thought that process has been going good. … On Friday is really full blown red zone, and what we did this week is we continued to have our Wednesday practice in the red zone, and we had an actual competitive red zone period [Thursday]. Good on good [starting offense vs. starting defense], we ran pads, and it was the head-coach-call-it period, to where I called the [down and distance] and exactly where I want the ball to be placed, what yard line, and again, it was good on good. It was highly competitive, so I thought that was a really good period for us. … So, I feel good about the process and what we did this week when it came to that."

The Chargers come in after winning their first game of the season last week, beating the Ravens. Jim Harbaugh's team entered the year with playoff hopes but is now being talked about as a team that will be picking at the top of the draft. In fact, this game carries more draft implications than playoff implications, with the Jets and Chargers both potentially picking in the top five.

The Jets have lost five straight games but now have an opportunity against the Chargers and Dolphins in the next two weeks. They are both one-win teams that the Jets should have more opportunity to beat than the Bills and Chiefs, their past two opponents.

Baltimore Ravens linebacker Mike Green (15) sacks Los Angeles Chargers quarterback Justin Herbert (10) on Monday, Nov. 16, 2026, in Baltimore. Los Angeles won 23-20. David Tulis/UPI/Shutterstock

Though improvement may be hard to find with the way the Jets have been playing, Glenn pointed to how they have operated in the last two minutes of the first half recently as a positive. They kicked field goals at the end of the first half against the Raiders and Bills in the past three weeks. The team has been outscored 107-12 in the final two minutes of the first half under Glenn.

"I think the way that we've operated in our two-minute has improved," Glenn said. "I think the way that we've operated in managing the clock has improved, and I'm really happy about that and I really think that we want to continue to improve in that, and that's something that we talk about constantly."
