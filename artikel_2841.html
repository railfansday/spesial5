Celtics star Jayson Tatum accepts fault for derailment vs. Nuggets: ‘That’s on me’

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Jayson Tatum let the Boston Celtics down Wednesday night, as they suffered their first set of consecutive losses this season.

After outperforming three-time league MVP Nikola Jokić with a 20-point first half, Tatum went ghost. He played all 12 minutes of the third quarter but attempted just one shot and committed four turnovers. Denver mounted a 14-point comeback, capitalizing on Tatum's second-half disappearance.

In the fourth quarter, Tatum's ice-cold trend continued. He shot 1-for-5, missing his last three shots before delivering Boston one final costly blow by receiving a technical foul as the Celtics trailed, 103-101, over an incident with referee John Goble in the final 6.4 seconds of the game.

"I should've just probably gave the ball to the ref closest to me," Tatum admitted to reporters after Boston's 105-101 loss, per CLNS Media. "I thought that John was looking at our bench. I should've just found the ref closest to me and just gave them the ball. So that's on me."

Denver's Jamal Murray shot and made the technical free throw, sending Boston packing.

Tatum's incident with Gobble unfolded after Murray's go-ahead jumper put the Nuggets ahead three points. The Celtics called a timeout, and as Tatum retreated to the sideline, he tossed the ball toward Gobble, standing near midcourt. Referee Eric Dalen subsequently blew his whistle in response and assessed Tatum the game-sealing technical foul.

Understanding the gravity of the mistake, along with Tatum's remorse in hindsight, he also expressed some confusion, sharing his follow-up encounter with Dalen.

"I told Eric, I was like, I wasn't mad," Tatum said. "It wasn't a foul on me. We just called a timeout. John Goble called timeout and pointed at our bench. I thought he was looking this way, but they called a tech. So for me, just find the female ref that was there or the other ref, whoever was probably closest, and I should've just gave them the ball."

Tatum's technical foul was untimely, but it would've been salvageable had he not blown a flat tire coming out of the players' tunnel after halftime. As the undisputed superstar in the driver's seat this season, Tatum needs to give the Celtics much more. He painted half a masterpiece before completely defacing the canvas, piling up miscues and reverting to his passive self at moments that demanded a Tatum takeover instead.

Tied at 95-95, Tatum nearly traveled while trying to escape Jokić and Christian Braun. He handed the ball to Neemias Queta before demanding it back with Braun defending him in the post. But rather than exploit the mismatch he had drawn for a much-needed go-ahead bucket, Tatum allowed Braun to poke the ball away, resulting in a costly turnover.

The perfect storm led Tatum and the Celtics right to Gobble and Dalen.

That marked Tatum's third technical foul this season.

"I'll go back and watch it," Celtics head coach Joe Mazzulla told reporters. "But we just can't put ourselves in that position to even give the refs an opportunity to interpret a certain type of way. So we just gotta be better there."

His fourth-quarter run-in with the officials wasn't the only thing Tatum took responsibility for. He recognized the negative impact his underperformance had on his teammates, with every decision carrying added weight as Boston's No. 1 option. The Celtics waited and waited, but Tatum never returned to his earlier self that saw him shoot 8-for-16 with six rebounds in less than 19 minutes during the first half.

That's the Tatum the Celtics need for four quarters.

"The four turnovers or something I had in the third quarter really just put us out of sync," Tatum said. "And in those situations, I gotta communicate better, I gotta execute better, I gotta take care of the ball, and I think that really put us in a hole, and it was kind of the turning point of the game — the second half."
