Dylan Harper wills the Spurs to a twelfth straight victory

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

A win is not always pretty. A Sunday afternoon game against the Utah Jazz got weird quickly, but the San Antonio Spurs were able to pull out a 110-102 win – their 12th in a row.

It probably wouldn't have happened without the late-game heroics of Dylan Harper, who scored a career-high 31 points.

As usual, Harper was excellent in transition. All game long, he ran the break to score buckets through and around the Jazz. This play shows what makes Harper tough to cover in transition. He's so fast and has a big frame that blocks defenders from getting to the ball, and he finishes with soft touch at the rim, even under duress.

Oh, and Harper is incredibly strong. He's shooting an eye-popping 82.3% from 0-3 feet away from the hoop this season. Already he is one of the best rim finishers in the NBA.

San Antonio's offense was best when it could get out and run. The Spurs had 29 points in transition. The team kept pushing the ball up the floor to catch the Jazz backpedaling. Utah is a youthful, athletic team, but even they weren't able to keep up with San Antonio's uptempo offensive attack.

Harper was helped by Victor Wembanyama, who was unstoppable in the first half, but cooled down a bit in the second. Wembanyama had 26 points, 10 rebounds, 6 assists, and 5 blocks. The Jazz did a pretty good job cutting off lanes for lobs, but the Spurs were able to find Wembanyama in transition for looks at the rim. It helps when your 7-foot-5 center is willing to run the floor ahead of the opposing big.

Wembanyama battled with Mo Bamba all game. Utah is extremely banged up at center, missing three potentially starting big men. Bamba filled in admirably with 26 points on 10-for-13 shooting and hit all 6 of his three-pointers.

San Antonio got a big bench boost from last year's Sixth Man of the Year, Keldon Johnson. He had 11 points, 10 rebounds, and 2 steals. Johnson had 2 assists, building on his improved playmaking lately.

Sometimes an assist is generated by the passer, and others are made by the scorer. This one was driven by the scorer, as Luke Kornet got all the way up to tip this lob in.

Perhaps the best play of the game came in the final minute, when Stephon Castle grabbed a long rebound and turned it into a windmill dunk. The Spurs were already in control of the game, but this play put the exclamation point on it.

After the win, the Spurs will look to extend their winning streak to 13 against the Detroit Pistons on Tuesday.
