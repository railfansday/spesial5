Nolan Gorman is the wrong kind of third base bet for the Yankees

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The Cardinals made Nolan Gorman a free agent on Friday, and Brian Cashman is still looking at his third-base options. Put those facts together and it's easy to talk yourself into a power rebound in the Bronx. I wouldn't give Gorman a guaranteed job. He's a left-handed power project, and putting him next to Ryan McMahon doesn't give the Yankees the matchup flexibility they're missing.

That's my roster read, not a report of Yankees interest. If the choice is guaranteeing a spot to Gorman or opening the spring with McMahon and a right-handed alternative, I'd take the second option.

The 27 homers Gorman hit in 2023 are part of the case for him. So is making pitchers pay for leaving something in his swing path. But his current MLB record has a .184 average and .580 OPS in 2026. MLB.com's report on the non-tender puts the three-season figure at .199/.280/.365 across 1,106 trips to the plate. That's more than a slump in a utility player's first 80 at-bats.

Gorman's left-handed swing isn't a disqualification. It is an issue for this roster because McMahon also hits left-handed. Making room for Gorman doesn't give the manager a different look when a lefty starts. It means choosing which of two left-handed hitters gets the plate appearances, or giving one of them time at another position. The shorter porch can reward contact that gets in the air. It can't make a pitch off the plate easier to reach.

McMahon's own .213 average and .636 OPS last season aren't a performance to protect. Cashman told MLB.com's Bryan Hoch the Yankees were considering what the market offered at third, where McMahon is still under contract for 2027. That commitment isn't a reason to play him regardless of results. Filling another infield spot without adding a different option would make it harder to respond if he starts slowly again.

Caballero, a right-handed hitter who played 37 games at third for New York last season, can give the Yankees a different look without promising someone a new role. He would still have to cover other spots, so I'd prefer a right-handed addition who can defend third if the price makes sense. That's a better place to allocate a guaranteed job.

If Gorman is open to Triple-A and shows he can get to his power without the same swing-and-miss, that would change the decision. The Cardinals' transaction log says he's free to find that chance anywhere. I wouldn't bid against a team that has an open DH spot and can offer the at-bats New York doesn't have to spare.
