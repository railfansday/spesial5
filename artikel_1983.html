Majority of clubs vote for extra legal protection for Premier League chiefs

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Premier League board members have been given extra legal protection amid the threat of actions linked to the Manchester City case.

Eighteen clubs are reported to have supported changes to the league’s articles at a clubs meeting in central London on Thursday, which extend protections provided to individual board members to enable them to freely carry out their duties.

A 14-club majority is required for votes to be carried, and it was understood to have been comfortably above that.

The change comes after City sponsor Etihad said in September it was considering legal action over the “damaging” implications of the independent commission’s guilty verdict against the club.

The airline was not named in the verdict, which found City had benefited from “sham” arrangements to inflate revenue and disguise costs to avoid breaching Premier League and UEFA rules. City have appealed against the verdict. Another City sponsor, Etisalat, was reported by The Times to be considering similar action earlier this week.

The Press Association understands the issue was brought to a vote in order to bring the league’s individual legal cover in line with similar companies – including many of its clubs – and that these articles had not been looked at since the league’s inception in 1992.

There was no indication of any cap on the costs clubs could be exposed to as a result of the change to the league’s articles, and no wider discussion or questions raised on the City case despite this being the first shareholders’ meeting since the verdict was published on September 29.

Club executives also received a first update of the season from referees’ chief Howard Webb.

He is understood to have accepted the 10th round of matches immediately before the November international break had been especially difficult.

Six on-field errors were identified by the Key Match Incidents (KMI) panel which were not corrected by VAR intervention.

This is in part due to the high bar for intervention being operated this season, where referee’s on-field calls can only be overturned where it is felt the error was not a clear and obvious one.
