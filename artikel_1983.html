Kasper Hogh is 'lost' at Celtic and 'doesn't know what he's doing' - Scandinavian expert

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Kasper Hogh must respond at Celtic as concerns continue to emerge over his ability to perform in Scotland.

That is the opinion of former Tottenham, Everton and Aston Villa scout and Scandinavian football expert, Bryan King, speaking exclusively to Football Insider.

The 25-year-old joined the Hoops from Bodo/Glimt for a fee in the region of £11million in the summer transfer window, and got off to the perfect start when he scored a hat-trick against Kilmarnock in his second game.

But Hogh has not scored once in the 16 appearances he has made for Celtic since, seeing his goal against the same opponent on Sunday ruled out for an earlier foul in the build-up.

Faith in the Denmark international remains, but there will be growing concerns as his goal drought stretches further.

Sunday's win saw Celtic take a three point lead at the top of the Scottish Premiership for the international break, with Rangers holding a game in hand.

Hogh caught the eye during a brilliant Champions League campaign with Bodo/Glimt last season.

The Dane scored against Manchester City, Atletico Madrid, Inter Milan and Sporting CP as the Norwegian side made it to the knockout stages.

Hogh had enjoyed a superb league season as well, with Celtic fighting off the competition to bring him to Parkhead.

"He was great in Norway and he was always great against top European teams," King told Football Insider.

"He had a bright start, but it's all about confidence. The centre forward's got to be scoring goals, but he can only score goals if the service is right.

"And now he's getting into a situation where Celtic haven't exactly been setting the Scottish league alight.

"It's going to be interesting to see whether Kasper Hogh has got the ability to stamp his authority on the game and the position. But at the moment he looks as though he's lost.

"I'm not sure he knows exactly what he's doing. Big price tag, but he won't be the first or last player to have a big price tag and not fulfil it."

Speaking after Sunday's game, Martin O'Neill made sure to keep his confidence in Hogh.

"In front of goal it’s difficult, but he worked exceptionally hard, as he did on Thursday. Things will come," the Celtic boss said.

"He thought he’d broken the spell. His hold-up play is much better now. If you have the crowd behind you, they feel as if you’re working hard, then they’ll forgive other things.

"It won’t last forever, strikers live and die by their goals, but he feels we all believe in him in the dressing room, which is very encouraging.”
