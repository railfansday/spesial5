AFC North Roundup: Bengals cruise to division lead; Browns stay in playoff hunt

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Even with three teams still firmly in playoff contention, the AFC North race officially has a new favorite, while another team's playoff hopes are all but officially over.

With Cincinnati, Cleveland, and Pittsburgh all multiple games over .500 entering Week 11, there's a very real chance we'll see all three teams in the 2026-27 NFL Playoffs.

As for the Ravens, their latest defeat effectively eliminated them from postseason contention.

Here's a look at what took place in Week 10 for the AFC North.

Cincinnati brought the fireworks. Pittsburgh supplied the audience. With first place in the AFC North at stake Sunday night, the Bengals overwhelmed the Steelers, 38-18, improving to 7-2 with their fifth consecutive victory. Pittsburgh slipped to 6-3 after spending much of the evening trying to keep a divisional showdown from becoming an even bigger embarrassment. The final margin hardly captures how thoroughly Cincinnati controlled the game, which stood at 24-3 by halftime and 31-3 entering the fourth quarter.

Joe Burrow gave Pittsburgh almost no room to breathe. He completed 35 of 44 passes for 409 yards and four touchdowns without an interception, piling up 302 yards and three scoring passes before halftime. Against a defense that had been one of the best in the NFL over the past four weeks, Cincinnati's quarterback repeatedly delivered. The Bengals have now scored at least 30 points in five consecutive games, establishing a franchise record and giving their winning streak some serious offensive muscle.

Ja'Marr Chase was the problem Pittsburgh never solved. His 11 receptions produced 231 yards, including a 53-yard gain that helped set up Cincinnati's second touchdown. Chase Brown caught touchdown passes of two and four yards, while Drew Sample and Dohnte Meyers also scored through the air. That distribution made the Bengals especially difficult to contain: Chase could rip apart the coverage between the end zones, and Burrow had several other answers when Cincinnati reached scoring territory.

The defense made sure Burrow's production came with a comfortable cushion. Cincinnati limited Pittsburgh to 222 total yards and 17 first downs while generating three sacks. Jonathan Allen, Myles Murphy and Boye Mafe each brought down Aaron Rodgers, whose passing line finished at 16 completions in 32 attempts for 145 yards. Pittsburgh managed only a field goal through three quarters, leaving its offense with far too much ground to cover when the touchdowns finally arrived.

Rodgers connected with DK Metcalf and Pat Freiermuth for fourth-quarter touchdowns, but Cincinnati answered the first of those scores with a one-yard Samaje Perine touchdown run. The Bengals finished with a 484-222 advantage in total offense and held the ball for 35:09. Those numbers tell the story of a team that dictated the evening on both sides of the ball.

Deshaun Watson led Cleveland to its sixth come-from-behind victory of the season en route to a 27-24 win over Houston that pushed the Browns' record to 6-4. Cleveland spotted the Texans a 17-0 lead as C.J. Stroud threw early touchdown passes to Nico Collins and Xavier Hutchinson, but the Browns gradually erased the deficit.

Touchdown runs by Deshaun Watson and KC Concepcion, along with a pair of Andre Szmyt field goals, produced 20 consecutive points and a three-point Browns lead entering the fourth quarter.

Houston regained the advantage on Stroud's 11-yard touchdown pass to Dalton Schultz, forcing Cleveland to mount one more rally. The decisive opportunity came with fewer than two minutes remaining and the Browns starting at Houston's 49. Harold Fannin Jr. delivered the biggest gain, taking a reception 30 yards to the Texans' five-yard line. Quinshon Judkins finished the drive on the next snap, scoring with 53 seconds remaining to put Cleveland ahead for good.

The Browns still needed their defense to protect the lead, and Denzel Ward and Donovan McMillon supplied late pass breakups, with McMillon's coming on fourth-and-20 to end Houston's hopes. Cleveland remained unbeaten at home, while the Texans fell to 3-6. The victory kept the Browns within reach in the AFC North and served as a reminder that Cleveland remains part of the divisional conversation.

The Los Angeles Chargers snapped an 11-game losing streak Monday night, beating the Baltimore Ravens 23-20 for their first win of the season. Los Angeles outrushed Baltimore 160-16 and held Derrick Henry to 10 yards on 10 carries. Justin Herbert opened the scoring with a 22-yard touchdown run, and Omarion Hampton scored from the 1 on fourth down to give the Chargers a 17-7 lead late in the first half.

Jacoby Brissett threw for 340 yards in his Ravens debut with Lamar Jackson and Tyler Huntley both on injured reserve. Baltimore went three-and-out on its first three possessions, but Henry scored twice, cutting the deficit to 23-20 with 10:23 remaining. Herbert finished with 129 passing yards and 51 rushing yards and went without an interception for the first time this season.

The Ravens were approaching field-goal range with 2:03 left when Tarheeb Still stripped Zay Flowers after a catch near the Chargers' 45-yard line. Elijah Molden recovered, and Herbert found former Raven Charlie Kolar for a first down to seal the win. Baltimore dropped to 4-6 with its fifth straight loss that likely ends their slim postseason dreams.
