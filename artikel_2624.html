Trent Williams, 49ers silence Cowboys, fans on and off field

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The San Francisco 49ers were anticipating a hostile environment when they entered AT&T Stadium on Sunday.

A sea of 49ers fans took over the Cowboys' stadium on Sunday and gave San Francisco a rare home-field advantage for a road game.

49ers offensive tackle Trent Williams celebrating with George Kittle. IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect

49ers offensive tackle Trent Williams, a Longview, Texas, native, said Sunday's game "was like a dream come true."

"Walking down on that field, looking around, I'm like, 'I'm struggling to find blue jerseys. A Cowboys fan.' It literally looked like Levi's. It sounded like Levi's," Williams told KNBR radio about the environment.

Williams said the atmosphere was so pro-49ers that he reminded quarterback Brock Purdy that he didn't need to use a silent count.

For Dallas, it was the complete opposite.

During the game, the rambunctious 49ers fan base was so loud while the team was on defense that Dak Prescott was forced to use a silent count.

"We practiced all week going on a silent cadence, just assuming that the home crowd would have some impact," Williams said. "We had to look at [Purdy] in the huddle and be like, 'Brock, it's Kyle in your head, man. We can hear your voice.' … It was at that realization where I'm like, 'Yeah, the Faithful took over."

Brock Purdy greeting Cowboys quarterback Dak Prescott after San Francisco won 46-40 on Sunday. AP Photo/Julio Cortez

Williams wasn't the only 49er to become aware of the surroundings.

"I've been in this stadium a bunch, just coaching in the NFC East for a while," Kyle Shanahan said to California Post sports reporter Evan Webeck. "For us not to have to use silent counts at all — I don't even think we did on third down — was really cool."
