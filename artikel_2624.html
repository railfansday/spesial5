Chargers Week 10 PFF Grades: Del’Shawn Phillips lead spirited effort vs. Ravens rushing attack

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The Chargers beat the Ravens to move to 1-8 on the season. The 0-17 jokes and hypotheticals can all finally stop.

Justin Herbert and the offense took a step forward with zero turnovers and a dominating performance on the ground with 160 yards, spurred by Omarion Hampton's 105-yard night. Herbert also added 51 yards and a rushing score, as well.

Speaking of ground games, the Chargers held the Ravens to almost nothing on the ground. All-Pro back Derrick Henry was limited to just 10 yards on 10 carries. The zero yards on the ground for Baltimore during the first half was a historic low for the franchise.

You can't be much more dominant than that.

Let's go ahead and check out the grades from Monday night.

TE Charlie Kolar – 74.8OG Logan Taylor – 74.4QB Justin Herbert – 73.0WR Tre' Harris – 72.0FB Scott Matlock – 70.2

It was poetic for Kolar — the former Baltimore Raven — to catch the first-down pass that helped seal the win over his former team. He was also dyamite in the run game, posting the second-high run block grade with a 74.6. His 76.4 pass block grade was also fourth best on the night.

Monday was Taylor's best performance as a pro, finishing with zero pressures or sacks allowed on 27 dropbacks. His 81.1 pass block grade was tops on the team while his 68.9 in run blocking was fourth best.

For the first time all season, Herbert did not throw an interception and it led to his highest game grade of 2026.

Harris caught five passes for 86 yards all in the first half. Unfortunately he caught zero passes in the final 30 minutes of game time, but his yardage still gave him a new season and career high.

During the broadcast, it was shown that, at one point, the Chargers were averaging 5.1 yards per carry with Matlock on the field and 3.8 when he was not. He's done an excellent job as the team's starting fullback and I believe the switch from a zone-heavy run scheme to a gap scheme has benefitted both him and Hampton.

OT Travis Burke – 39.5WR Quentin Johnston – 52.4OG Cole Strange – 53.4WR Derius Davis – 53.6WR Theo Wease Jr. – 54.1

Unfortunately the team's first win of the year was also one of Burke's worst performances of the season. He allowed a team-high five pressures and had an abysmal 27.9 pass block grade to go with a penalty. The next worse grade among the offensive line was Strange with an above-average 64.0.

Johnston finished with one catch on one target for six yards. His season may be one of the biggest failures for Mike McDaniel in a season packed to the brim with them.

The aforementioned Strange allowed four pressures and was also called for a penalty. His 57.3 in run block was below average but still better than Joe Alt's 53.2.

LB Del'Shawn Phillips – 90.6DT Teair Tart – 82.8CB Donte Jackson – 77.4EDGE Tuli Tuipulotu – 73.7EDGE Khalil Mack – 73.0

What a night for Phillips, and also what a season it's turning into for the first-year NFL starter. Phillips was a menace against the run on Monday night, helping the Chargers hold Derrick Henry to just 10 yards on 10 carries. He also snagged the team's only interception off Jacoby Brissett when he sprinted under a deflected by Cam Hart. All night long, his high effort was apparent.

Tart had his highest-graded game of the season overall to go with his season-high 81.7 pass rush grade. He tied a season-high of three pressures, as well.

Jackson rebounded after a rough performance against the Texans to allow just one catch for six yards in three targets his way.

Mack and Tuli combined for 15 pressures on Brissett with the latter recording a nine by himself. His previous season high was six in Week Three.

S R.J. Mickens – 25.1CB Deane Leonard – 28.5S Elijah Molden – 40.9LB Daiyan Henley – 51.7DT Jamaree Caldwell – 53.9

Mickens luckily knocked down the Hail Mary attempt by the Ravens to seal the team's first win of the season, but it was otherwise a bad night for the second-year safety. His overall game grade was his worst of the year by far and his 26.6 coverage grade was very much the same. Mickens was also called for his third penalty of the season.

Molden was credited with allowing five receptions on six targets towards him for 65 yards, including 53 after the catch. Henley also was dinged a bunch in coverage. PFF has him credited with a whopping nine receptions allowed on 11 targets in his area.
