‘The World’s Strongest Dad’ – Keito Yamakita Heads Into World Title Fight With A New Reason To Win

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Life rarely delivers a man's two greatest desires in the exact same breath, but for Keito "Pocket Monk" Yamakita, destiny is converging on a single week.

The surging Japanese martial artist is currently staring down two massive, life-altering countdowns. One leads directly to a career-defining World Title fight, while the other ticks toward the arrival of his precious firstborn child.

That incredible convergence culminates at ONE SAMURAI 5 on Wednesday, November 18, when he officially challenges reigning ONE Strawweight MMA World Champion Joshua "The Passion" Pacio at EBARA WAVE Arena Ota in Tokyo.

While martial arts has entirely defined Yamakita's existence since his earliest days on the wrestling mats of Inabe in Mie Prefecture, fatherhood represents a profoundly different kind of unknown.

With his wife due to give birth the very day after the World Title match, his heart is already tethered to a far greater miracle waiting just outside the Circle.

Yet the 30-year-old candidly admits his sense of maturity has not fully caught up with the monumental shift ahead. Even so, he is eagerly looking forward to growing into the role the moment his son is born.

"We'd wanted a child for quite a while, so more than anything I felt relieved that it had actually happened. There were worries too.

"And the hundreds of days until the birth seemed so long. It felt endless, but before I knew it, it was almost here. So now I'm feeling a little restless and excited."

Fate may have perfectly aligned the dates, but "Pocket Monk" has spent a lifetime grinding toward this athletic peak.

An impressive 6-1 promotional record and a brilliant five-fight winning streak delivered Yamakita to the biggest night of his professional career. Lately, though, his quiet moments reach far beyond himself, extending toward the life that follows.

"I'll be a father while still competing, and I'd love my son to see me fight. That will probably be a little further down the road, but I'm really looking forward to him watching my fights."

That nervous anticipation sits alongside steady, unwavering support at home. His wife has never demanded he win a gold belt, only that he returns home in one piece.

Still, a profound internal shift has altered how he approaches the grueling work ahead, and the reason is rapidly approaching.

"I was chasing championship belts for myself. Even when I became Pancrase champion in Japan, I felt I was doing it for myself. Now I have my son to do it for as well, so that gives me even more motivation.

"I went into fights wanting to give one hundred percent of what I had. Now, with my child in the picture, I feel like I can give one hundred and twenty percent—something beyond what I normally have in me."

For a man standing on the precipice of fatherhood, a World Title is far more than a glittering line on a resume. It is a foundational story his child will grow up hearing.

A child carries the lessons learned from their father for the rest of their life, and for Keito Yamakita, those opening pages could be beautifully written on November 18.

"If I could welcome him as world champion, then when he's a bit older, I hope he might think, 'My dad was someone amazing.'

"I don't know how many years from now that will be, but it would give me one more thing to look forward to."

Yamakita also senses a distinct psychological edge in his current position. In his view, the hungry fighter actively chasing the title operates with a strength the defending champion simply cannot match.

Wednesday night in Tokyo will carry the immense weight of everything he has built and everything that is about to begin. And waiting just beyond the final bell is his newborn son.

The challenger fully intends to leave the Circle shouldering 26 pounds of undisputed gold. Soon after, he hopes to meet the boy who will make fatherhood feel real.

"If I win, I can become the world's strongest dad. This fight has come at a time when I absolutely cannot afford to lose. For myself, for everyone who always supports me, and for my son. I want to win that championship belt and become world champion."
