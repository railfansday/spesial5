Uncalled illegal formation (if there was one) wouldn't have resulted in 10-second runoff

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Yes, the officials missed an apparent issue with the clock last night, after the Commanders kicked a game-winning field goal. No, it was not preceded by the officials missing an illegal formation by the Commanders that would have ended the game against the Bengals, with Cincinnati winning, 22-20.

That’s not to say there wasn’t an illegal formation on the play that resulted in a spike that killed the clock with four seconds to play. There likely was. Even though the X receiver was holding his hand forward to signify he intended to be on the line of scrimmage, he seemed to be behind it. Which would have given the Commanders fewer than seven men on the line.

However, illegal formation isn’t one of the fouls that trigger a 10-second runoff. So it would have resulted in a five-yard penalty and nothing more.

The issue came up in 2014, in a Browns-Falcons game. Also, the NFL’s Approved Rulings confirm that there’s no runoff. We also confirmed the lack of a runoff with someone who knows the rules inside and out.

The reason is simple. Illegal formation doesn’t shut down a play, like a false start does.

Still, even without the runoff, the foul shouldn’t have been missed. The Commanders should have been pushed back by five yards, making the 26-yard field-goal try a 31-yard attempt.

As it was, the kick from Drew Stevens nearly hit the upright. If the ball had been struck the same way five yards farther from the target, the kick would have been no good. And the Commanders wouldn’t have walked off (when it shouldn’t have been a walk-off) with a win.

That’s the issue. Even without a runoff, a five-yard penalty may have resulted in a miss — just like it did last month for Packers kicker Trey Smack in Detroit, when a late-game kick that was good from 21 ended up being no good from 26.
