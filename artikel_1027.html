Banned Springboks coach Erasmus invites fans to watch along of Ireland game

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Springboks coach Rassie Erasmus was handed a six-match suspension for comments he made about referees following South Africa's Nations Championship defeat by France; South Africa Rugby have requested a full hearing into his ban

Rassie Erasmus has invited fans to join a live watch-along online of South Africa's clash against Ireland, after the Springboks coach was suspended for six matches for comments he made about match officials.

Erasmus had questioned whether there were double standards in the officiating of South Africa's 21-19 defeat to France during a press conference earlier in the week, prompting an investigation from World Rugby, who later said that the coach had "disrespected the authority of the officials".

He will now be unable to have any match-day involvement in his side's Nations Championship match against Ireland on Saturday, or their Nations Championship Finals match, commencing the following week.

South Africa have requested a full hearing in response to the sanction and on Wednesday, Erasmus broke his silence, inviting members of the public to join him to watch Saturday's game via a live stream.

"Can sit and moan or make the best of it," the Springboks coach wrote in a post on X.

"I'll be watching the match at the hotel. Not keen to watch [it] on my own.

"So, let's give it a go and hope I get the 'tech' sorted = TV on, My Laptop camera on, and you're welcome to join me.

"Sign up here (adults 18+): http://watchwithrassie.com. This is personal, not official."

On his streaming website, Erasmus has embedded a video of a flying South Africa flag, with a link to sign up to watch.

The 54-year-old, who guided South Africa to back-to-back Rugby World Cups in 2019 and 2023, provided some more details on the webpage about how the live stream would work.

"We each watch on our own TV, and I'll be on camera so you can see me watch it too. Send me a message and I'll read some out."

A disclaimer on the page read: "This is me personally, Rassie. It is not an official team or Springbok channel."

It is unclear when Erasmus will be able to return to coach South Africa, but he is expected to be available for the 2027 Rugby World Cup.

On Tuesday, World Rugby confirmed an independent panel had reviewed the comments made by Erasmus under World Rugby's Match Official Abuse Sanction Process, a framework that was introduced in July.

"The panel determined that the comments suggest that match officials acted with bias/prejudice or deliberately influenced the outcome of a match, and when the conduct is considered as a whole a reasonable person would conclude that the conduct constituted an unacceptable attack on the credibility and competence of the match officials," the governing body said in a statement.

"In respect to the sanction, the panel determined that the conduct clearly amounts to disrespecting the authority of the match officials by impugning their integrity."

On Monday, Erasmus claimed France should have been given three yellow cards for illegal tackles as he leapt into a rant criticising referees.

"Let's say 11 of those were at least a penalty and three I thought were at least three yellow cards. And not one was sanctioned and not one was shown out or cited," Erasmus said.

"I'll send you the playlist, but I'll get into trouble… So I can't send the playlist to anybody and we can only send five clips to World Rugby. So we'll probably query it, but the answer will probably be: 'Yes, it was a mistake; it should have been penalised'.

"On numerous times we mentioned to the referee 'we think there's a knee-drop tackle'. I'm saying to you, my feeling is that knee-drop tackles is out. They happened 17 times in our game. Not one was penalised.

"And if the TMO didn't see it, then maybe technology must be better. If he saw it and ignored it, what does that boil down to? Manipulation of the game or the result? Or players getting injured?

"So, all I'm asking is don't take the TMOs out and discourage them from getting involved. Then the wrong team will win and players will get injured. That's all I'm saying."

World Rugby has since received backlash on social media in the wake of Erasmus' ban, and South Africa Rugby has condemned the abuse.

"SA Rugby unequivocally condemns any form of intimidation, threats or personal attacks. Such behaviour has no place in rugby and is entirely contrary to the values upon which our game is founded."
