What’s Your Hiking EDC? Backpacker’s Editors Share Their ‘Every Day Carry’ Gear

Associate Editor Will Brendza's EDC requires three to four pockets, but prepares him with tools, fire-starter, light, and his favorite pair of sunglasses. (Photo: Will Brendza)

It seems like everywhere online people are sharing photos of their “Every Day Carry” (EDC) kits. Watches, pocket knives, key fobs, and other day-to-day, on-your-person gear are photographed in a spread and posted to socials with a quippy caption. Often, it’s tactical bros who just want to show off their knives and handguns. For others, it’s an opportunity to flash a Rolex or humble brag about their expensive whip. It’s an internet trend that’s exploded among gear nerds, collectors, survivalists, and preppers.

But what about hikers? We’ve all got a dedicated list of gear we wouldn’t hit the trail without. How would the EDC trend look if you shook people down on the trail to find out what was in their pockets?

We wanted to know. So we’re starting with Backpacker’s editors—to see what our trail EDC looks like and how it’s different from person to person. We’re curious what others’ might look like, too. Tag @backpackermag in a photo of your hiker EDC on Instagram, and we’ll check it out.

Here’s what our team is never leaving the house without. #hikerEDC

The most important piece of gear I keep on me is my Ombraz Armless Sunglasses. I’ve got sensitive eyes and a penchant for losing things—these dandies solve both of those problems. They stay tethered to my neck or head, even when I’m underwater or upside down, and offer fully polarized eye protection. Next is my Knives of Alaska folding knife, which is big enough for most camp tasks and still fits in a pocket. I also always keep a bandana handy, because it’s the single most versatile tool a backpacker can carry (serving as a headband, handkerchief, surface cleaner, hot mitt, face mask, etc.).

If you follow Backpacker’s Editor’s Gear Picks, you’ll recognize my Nocs Zero Tube Monocular, which is one of my favorite backpacking tools. For scoping routes, spotting wildlife, or just enhancing the details of a view, it’s an incredible companion. My no-frills Petzl headlamp is getting old, but still serves me well when I get caught on the trail after dark.

Then I usually keep tissues for when I get sniffly, a pen and paper for taking notes, my ID and money, the sunscreen and chapstick I apply on the go, and, of course, a lighter (preferably one with a recognizable graphic so people are less likely to “accidentally” snatch it).

Not pictured: My dog, a compass, and my Coros Pace 4 Pro watch.

I’m on a never-ending quest to see how many essential items I can cram into a waist pack, but one thing I usually leave at home during non-working hours is my smartphone; I’ve found that the more I disconnect, the happier I am. Instead, I’m generally packing an AT&T Cingular Flex 2 flip phone, which cost me all of $15 and, besides texting and calling, can display rudimentary hiking maps with the O.map app. I supplement it with a button compass, which is enough for me to rough out directions, and my Life Camera S168, a tiny digital camera capable of taking better photos and videos than my Walmart phone.

A cheap space blanket has saved my bacon at least once, and I usually add a lighter wrapped in a twist of duct tape and a travel-size tube of sunscreen. My (sadly discontinued) Leatherman Tread multitool bracelet keeps a TSA- and security-friendly cutting tool and a host of screwdriver heads at hand, and for dayhikes where the possibility of being caught after dark is remote, I toss in my (also sadly discontinued) 40-lumen Black Diamond Ion Keychain Light. My wallet-size first aid kit has antibiotic ointment and a couple of adhesive bandages in kid-friendly patterns for the inevitable skinned knees and elbows that happen on family hikes.

I carry a waterproof notepad for jotting things down. My pen of choice is a Pilot Precise Varsity disposable fountain pen; it’s probably not the lightest scribbler out there, but it’s fun to write with. I usually bring cheap sunglasses so I don’t stress when I break them—these are from last year’s Outside Days in Denver. My choice of snacks is cheap, too: fruit snacks, granola bars, or whatever I most recently bought in bulk for my kids. And the toothbrush? I’ve just found my quality of life is way higher if I can brush my teeth whenever I want.

Most days, I walk out my front door and hit my local trails with very little in my pockets save for my iPhone and one or two dog poop bags (whatever brand was on sale last time I ran out). If I’m carrying any snacks, gummy candy is the first thing I reach for, and Nerds Gummy Clusters have never let me down. On long, difficult efforts, I like Honey Stinger Organic Energy Chews. My Garmin Instinct Solar watch is always on my wrist to track mileage or elevation gain, and of course, if I’m going far, I’ll pack my inReach to contact help in an emergency. Whether I’m hitting the trail or exploring a new city, my Nalgene is along for the ride, too.

Not pictured: My Kula cloth, avalanche transceiver for winter adventures, collapsible dog bowl, and some Leukotape wrapped around my sunscreen stick.
