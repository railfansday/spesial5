2 of the Best Dividend Stocks to Buy With $5,000 in November

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

These two dividend stocks offer reliable raises year after year.

Investors with different goals and preferences will gravitate toward different companies, even if they have the same investment strategy. That's why it's so hard to come up with "the best" of any category of stocks. But we can still give it a try. Consider the following two companies: Johnson & Johnson (JNJ -1.36%) and Walmart (WMT -0.69%). Both are longtime market leaders with excellent dividend programs and are arguably among the top income stocks, given their impressive track records.

For those with $5,000 to spare (make sure it isn't set aside for emergencies), Johnson & Johnson and Walmart are two of the first dividend stocks I'd recommend buying this month. Here is why.

Johnson & Johnson has shown its resilience this year. Despite macroeconomic problems, a major U.S. patent cliff it experienced last year, and several of its medicines being targeted by government-led drug price negotiations, the healthcare giant has performed well. Third-quarter revenue increased by 5.9% year over year to $25.4 billion.

Johnson & Johnson's adjusted earnings per share declined 7.5% year over year to $2.59, primarily due to acquisition-related expenses. Overall, the business is healthy, and Johnson & Johnson once again increased its guidance for the full fiscal year 2026. The company now expects operational sales of $100.7 billion at the midpoint, representing a 6.9% year-over-year increase, versus midpoint sales and year-over-year growth of $100.6 billion and 6.8% year-over-year for its previous guidance.

Johnson & Johnson has also made meaningful clinical and regulatory progress this year, which should help the company boost sales and navigate patent cliffs over the medium term. In March, the company earned approval for Icotyde, a medicine for plaque psoriasis. It has had an impressive commercial ramp-up so far. As management noted, more than 20,000 patients have initiated treatment with Icotyde so far.

The company believes this drug can generate more than $5 billion in peak annual sales. And it boasts several other brand-new approvals or pipeline candidates that it believes can approach or even exceed the $5 billion annual sales milestone. We haven't even mentioned Johnson & Johnson's medtech business, where it has also made progress this year.

The company has earned clearance for the Ottava system, a robotic-assisted surgery device that will help it tap into an underpenetrated market. Johnson & Johnson looks unstoppable right now, and the company could continue delivering excellent returns for a while, especially once we factor in the dividend, which investors should seriously consider reinvesting.

Johnson & Johnson is a Dividend King, a company with a minimum of 50 consecutive annual dividend increases. Investors can get 17 shares of Johnson & Johnson with $5,000 right now. That'd be money well spent.

Walmart has also performed well this year, despite economic headwinds that have weighed on much of the retail industry. In the second quarter of its fiscal year 2027, which ended July 31, the company's revenue increased by 5.9% year over year to $187.9 billion. Adjusted EPS rose 19.1% year over year to $0.81. Importantly, membership revenue, e-commerce sales, and advertising revenue all grew much faster than retail sales.

These are three crucial, higher-margin businesses that should help lift the company's profits over the long run. Here's another important point to consider. While many legacy retailers went out of business -- or are currently struggling to stay in business -- because they failed to capitalize on the shift toward e-commerce, Walmart made no such mistake.

It currently stands as the second largest e-commerce player in the U.S. That highlights the company's ability to capitalize on new technologies, and it is doing the same with artificial intelligence (AI). Walmart is leveraging AI across much of its business, including in optimizing inventory management and improving supply chain efficiency. It is also using AI to personalize customers' shopping experiences, which can lead to higher sales volume.

Walmart's AI efforts could make a meaningful impact over the long run. Meanwhile, the company still has many of the same strengths that made it so successful. It is one of the largest retailers in the U.S., with a vast network of locations -- 90% of the U.S. population lives within 10 miles of one of the company's stores. Further, Walmart leverages its large retail footprint to negotiate attractive prices and discounts with wholesalers and passes those cost savings onto customers. The result: Walmart tends to offer lower prices for items.

These are important competitive advantages that could enable the company to perform well over the long run, while continuously raising its dividends. Walmart is also a Dividend King -- it has raised its payouts for 53 consecutive years. Investors can buy 38 shares of the company for $5,000 right now. For long-term dividend-seeking investors, that might be a great move.
