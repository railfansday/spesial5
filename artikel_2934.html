SpaceX’s December Lock-Up Frees Another 17% of Its Shares. Here’s What October’s Unlock Did to the Stock.

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

SpaceX's public float continues to balloon.

By Ryan Vanzo – Nov 18, 2026 at 9:35PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Space Exploration Technologies ›

Space Exploration Technologies' (SPCX +1.27%) initial public offering this summer was unique in several ways.

It was one of the biggest IPOs in history. It was also one of the largest public debuts ever for an unprofitable business. Finally, the company went public with less than 5% of its shares available for public purchase -- far below the industry average.

There are some benefits to going public with only a small fraction of your total share count available for trade. First, it limits initial selling pressure, as most of the shares are locked until future milestones are met. Second, it can stack the odds in favor of a successful debut, as buying pressure can easily overwhelm selling pressure.

There are downsides to SpaceX's approach, however. Over time, more SpaceX shares become available for trading, adding potential selling pressure. Some events call for large share unlocks, potentially flooding the market with tradable shares over a short period.

Several analysts were worried about a potential overhang from major share unlock events. Morningstar (MORN -0.65%) analysts, for example, were concerned not only with SpaceX's initial IPO valuation, but also the company's unusual staggered lockup schedule.

"We think long-term investors eager to participate in SpaceX's future endeavors and potential success will have opportunities to do so with more margin of safety than the initial offering is likely to provide," the firm concluded. SpaceX's lockup schedule, one Morningstar analyst warned, could create "regular bouts of selling pressure."

Next month, one of SpaceX's biggest share-unlock events will take place, freeing up roughly 17% of its total locked shares for sale. Will this create selling pressure as some experts predict?

The best way to answer this question is by analyzing SpaceX's October share price action. That month, several unlock events sent the company's public float soaring. The impact is critical for SpaceX investors to understand.

SpaceX experienced three share unlock events in October.

The first was on Oct. 9 when 328.4 million shares became eligible for trading. That represented around 7% of the shares covered by the lockup schedule, excluding founder shares, mostly owned by Elon Musk, which won't be eligible for sale until next summer. Shares barely responded to this even, moving higher by 1% on the day.

The next event on Oct. 22 triggered the release of 1.3 billion shares. That's around 28% of the locked-up pool. That trading session was rough, with SpaceX stock falling roughly 9%.

The final October unlock event began Oct. 24 with the release of 328.4 million shares valued at nearly $50 billion. Shares fell by just 0.4% the next day of trading.

First, major unlock events can affect a company's near-term stock price. SpaceX's smaller unlocks had little immediate effect. But its larger unlock events seemingly sent shares plunging. Despite strong third-quarter earnings and 11 successful rocket launches, shares fell roughly 9% in October.

Second, share unlock events can create buying opportunities. SpaceX's intrinsic value as a business does not change as more shares are made available for trade. The share price action is simply a reflection of short-term supply and demand dynamics. If you like SpaceX as a company but have been waiting for a better entry point, the next share unlock event on Dec. 9 could be your chance to jump in.

With a market cap of nearly $2 trillion, I'm still on the sidelines when it comes to SpaceX stock. As I explained recently, "SpaceX's current valuation is tough to justify based on launch services and Starlink potential alone. The company must execute on its AI roadmap to justify a valuation above $1 trillion."

SpaceX's AI roadmap, however, will be difficult to fully achieve. It relies on several high-risk, high-reward efforts, including the successful commercialization of Starship -- which would become the largest rocket to ever reach orbit -- and the successful deployments of orbital data centers, which some experts believe will never be economical.

December's unlock event could create additional selling pressure, providing investors with a more attractive entry point. But it still might not be enough for me to jump in.
