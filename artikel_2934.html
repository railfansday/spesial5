Nvidia Is the Worst-Performing "Magnificent Seven" Stock This Year. Is It a Buy Before 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Even with Jensen Huang back as CEO, Nvidia is drastically underperforming the S&P 500 and Nasdaq this year.

By Daniel Foelber – Nov 26, 2026 at 5:30PM EST

Nvidia (NVDA -3.91%) investors have been on a rather unenjoyable roller coaster in recent weeks. And every time the ride looks like it's nearing an end, there's another unexpected turn that unearths more questions than answers.

Founder and CEO for over 33 years, Jensen Huang, admitted on Nov. 8 to being unfaithful to his wife, then resigned on Nov. 12, only to be brought back as CEO less than two weeks later on Nov. 23.

That same day, President Trump was on a phone interview with CNBC and took credit for speaking with Nvidia's board and helping convince them to bring Huang back. Trump said, "I spoke to them. Let me just put it that way. And I convinced them. And I will tell you, it was a lot easier to convince them yesterday than it was a week and a half ago." Trump was referring to Nvidia's stock price and how it was easier to convince Nvidia to bring Huang back when the stock was cratering.

Nvidia has rallied substantially since Huang came back as CEO, but Huang and Nvidia still face an uphill climb of federal, state, and internal investigations linked to Nvidia misleading investors and potential national security violations regarding advanced Nvidia artificial intelligence (AI) chips leaking into China through backdoor channels.

The stock is still under $100 per share at the time of this writing and down 20% year to date -- making it the worst-performing "Magnificent Seven" stock in 2026. The Magnificent Seven consists of seven mega-cap tech-focused companies that include Nvidia, Apple (AAPL +0.43%), Microsoft (MSFT -1.25%), Alphabet (GOOG -2.02%) (GOOGL -2.13%), Amazon (AMZN -3.12%), Meta Platforms (META +3.40%), and Tesla (TSLA -1.18%).

Here's the simplest reason to buy Nvidia, and an argument for why it's best to keep the semiconductor stock on a watch list for now.

Heading into this year, some investors were concerned that Nvidia would be unable to maintain its breakneck revenue growth rate and high gross margins. But hyperscalers continued to ramp up AI capital expenditures. And Nvidia has broadened its customer base to other enterprises, AI start-ups, AI labs, and neoclouds.

As a result, Nvidia's earnings growth began outpacing its stock price growth, compressing its valuation. But then the unthinkable happened.

Nvidia's stock price has plummeted despite exceptional results and guidance for 75% revenue growth in fiscal 2028. Nvidia reported third-quarter fiscal 2027 results on Nov. 17, with revenue more than doubling from the third quarter of fiscal 2026. In the nine months ended Oct. 25, 2026, Nvidia generated $183.3 billion in net income compared to $77.11 billion for the nine months ended Oct. 26, 2025.

Nvidia is on track to surpass Alphabet as the world's most profitable company. It now trades at just 15.4 times forward earnings -- making it the cheapest Magnificent Seven stock despite having the highest gross margins and the fastest revenue growth rate.

For context, the forward P/E of the S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.25%) is 17.3 -- meaning Nvidia is now priced like a value stock.

Buying high-quality companies on sale is an excellent way to compound wealth over time. Nvidia doesn't have to deliver blowout results to impress investors. In fact, its growth rate could fall substantially, and it would still be a steal at current levels.

The issue isn't Nvidia's valuation -- it's investors' concerns about where the company could be headed over the next three to five years.

Jensen Huang's sudden resignation exposed Nvidia's extreme vulnerability to key-person risk, governance failures, and an incapable board of directors. Going forward, Nvidia will need to change its structure to be less dependent on Huang, which could make it more rigid and less innovative but also protect long-term investors.

In addition to Nvidia-specific issues, there's also the risk that AI spending could decline considerably due to higher interest rates, which have made borrowing more expensive. With democrats taking control of the Senate and the House, there may be higher regulatory hurdles to accelerating data center construction. And there's the ongoing risk that compute demand will taper off as AI models become more efficient.

Granted, Nvidia is incredibly well positioned to endure a slowdown because of its industry-leading position and rock solid balance sheet. But it could also lead to stagnating or declining earnings in the medium term, which would challenge Nvidia's valuation.

All told, Nvidia simply has too many unknowns right now. Until investors get more clarity on the findings of the investigations, the company's succession plan for Huang, and the extent of its governance overhaul, Nvidia remains a hold.

Heading into the new year, I'd rather buy high-quality Magnificent Seven stocks with a clear runway for future earnings growth like Alphabet, even if its valuation is more expensive than Nvidia's.
