Campaign Trail: Tiffany tells human holiday story with Hollywood talent

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The luxury brand and agency American Haiku leaned into real emotions with actor Amanda Seyfried and director Derek Cianfrance.

In adland, it has been a decidedly handmade holiday season, with brands including Levi’s and E.l.f. turning to techniques like stop-motion animation to connect with consumers who are growing weary of ads created by artificial intelligence.

For its annual holiday effort, Tiffany & Co. found the best way to resonate with consumers is through human storytelling. The luxury brand’s global campaign, “Love is a Gift,” was created with agency American Haiku and revolves around a two-minute film starring actor Amanda Seyfried and director Derek Cianfrance.

The short, “You & Me,” jumps from the present day to New York City in the 1990s to tell the story of a young girl and her mother, played by Seyfried. The girl watches her mother work as a choreographer and has a handful of holiday experiences before being gifted with a Tiffany ornament that she shares with her own daughter in the present.

“Moving between past and present, the film reflects the enduring place Tiffany & Co. holds in life’s most meaningful moments and the stories we carry forward,” said CMO Andrea Davey in a statement.

The film is soundtracked by a cover of The Wannadies’ “You & Me Song,” a tune best known for its appearance on the 1996 “Romeo + Juliet” soundtrack. The campaign will run across cinema, paid and organic social, digital out-of-home, owned channels and clienteling across global channels.

Along with being on-trend with regard to stories imbued with humanity, Tiffany’s holiday campaign also represents a pivot from the brand’s past holiday creative. The 2025 edition of the “Love is a Gift” campaign turned its iconic blue boxes into portals to love stories around the world. This year, Tiffany brought on American Haiku, which had worked on a previous campaign for the brand in the U.K., but didn’t offer a traditional brief.

“They came to us with an initial idea of a family spending the holidays in their home and [tapping] into the memory aspect,” said Ben Muckensturm, creative director at the shop. “Where we pushed the work was bringing in the importance of the mother-daughter relationship that I think is the core of the story.”

Keying into the mother-daughter story gave the brand and agency a larger insight around the memory of seeing a parent as a person in their own world that is relatable to consumers, whether or not they are wealthy residents of the Upper East Side like the characters in the film. In the film, the true gift is not the ornament but the memory that is associated with it.

“That opened us up to how we explore the past and then bring it back to the present and create a piece that feels essentially real, that anyone could connect with,” Muckensturm said. “Everything was shot in New York. It was definitely an intentional push to try to bring it to the real world and make it more of an emotional piece.”

“You & Me” also delivers nostalgia for a pre-digital ‘90s that has been a reliable way to engage millennial audiences. However, the nostalgia in the film is not the traditional touchstones, like technology and boy bands, used by marketers looking to tap into memories of the decade. Instead, Tiffany looked at its own archives and at the holidays of movies like “Home Alone” and “The Parent Trap” for inspiration.

“When we created the town house, we went back to old archival Tiffany imagery of how things were,” Muckensturm said of creating the main set for the ad. “Especially on Fifth Avenue, most of the references that we brought were from ‘Home Alone 2,’ like what the streets looked like at the time. It's great to be able to tap into things that people are familiar with.”

After creating the world of the ad, the agency populated it with brand ambassador Seyfried, whose character is the mother in the past, rather than the main character in the present-day framing device. The actor, an Academy Award nominee whose performance in one of the year’s biggest films has captivated audiences, brought her gifts to the production.

“She has such a maternal instinct that really played perfectly on set, and you could see really how she was a mother to that girl for the days that we shot,” Muckensturm said.

The choice of Cianfrance, a director best known for dramatic fare like “Blue Valentine” and “The Place Beyond the Pines,” helped the brand and agency get the emotional tone right, without rehearsal and without falling into familiar or even corny holiday tropes.

“There was a trusting relationship between production partners, us and the client to come into it with a really open mind to see what the scene gives us,” Muckensturm said. “We knew where we were going to shoot, and we were trying to get a range of emotion… but we really let him direct them and let this play naturally.”
