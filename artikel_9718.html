PWA vs native app: when a web app is the right choice

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Posted on Nov 18 • Originally published at tenlux.tech

Every client who says "we need an app" usually means one of three things: a website, a PWA, or a native app. The cheapest wrong decision is to build a native app before validating demand. Here is how we decide, and what it costs.

A PWA (progressive web app) is a website with a manifest and a service worker. It installs to the home screen, works offline, and feels like an app — without app store review.

Figures are ballpark for a typical scope; the real price depends on screens, integrations and design.

In these cases we say "native" before the project starts, not in the middle of it.

A car dealer needed vehicle inspections: the driver photographs 8 angles, the manager approves or marks a defect with a comment. Instead of a native app we built a PWA: form, camera, client-side compression, offline queue, simple PHP admin panel.

A PWA is still a website, so pages index normally — and often rank better thanks to speed and caching. Keep canonical URLs, sitemaps and meta tags in order; do not block the offline shell in robots.txt; measure installation as a separate analytics event.

Mostly "yes" → PWA. Heavy background work or a store requirement → native.
