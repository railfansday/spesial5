Bitcoin Reclaims $80K as Democrats’ Victory Lifts Markets. Could This Be a Bull Trap?

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Home » Bitcoin (BTC) » Bitcoin Reclaims $80K as Democrats’ Victory Lifts Markets. Could This Be a Bull Trap?

Bitcoin rallied as Democrats took Congress, yet traders worry the move might be a temporary pump.

The Bitcoin price jumped 3.7% to $80,570 after Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress in the November 4 US midterm elections. Optimism returned to financial markets as investors priced in lower political uncertainty, while falling oil and a weaker dollar boosted risk appetite across both stock and crypto markets.

However, despite improving market sentiment, a significant wave of short liquidations helped accelerate Bitcoin’s recovery, raising questions about whether the move reflects genuine buying demand or is simply the result of a temporary short squeeze.

The November 3 US midterm elections saw Democrats win control of both chambers of Congress, prompting investors to reassess the political outlook and the risks surrounding US policy.

Oil prices dropped sharply. Brent crude fell more than 8% as markets priced in the possibility of easing tensions surrounding Donald Trump’s Iran war.

Lower oil prices, a weaker US dollar, and easing geopolitical concerns boosted sentiment across financial markets. The Dow Jones rose 3.03%, its best post-midterm day in 44 years. The S&P 500 gained 1.58%, and the Nasdaq added 1.44%.

Crypto followed the broader market recovery. The total crypto market capitalization rose 2.7% over 24 hours to $2.78 trillion, according to CoinGecko. Ethereum gained 6% to $2,420, while Solana climbed 3.8% to $96.07.

Derivatives market activity may have played a significant role in Bitcoin’s latest move.

Roughly $778.93 million in crypto positions were liquidated in 24 hours, per Coinglass data. Short positions accounted for $491 million, including $273.12 million in Bitcoin shorts.

Institutional investment flows also showed signs of improvement. US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded $74.57 million in net inflows yesterday, ending three days of outflows. BlackRock’s IBIT was the only fund with net outflows, while Fidelity’s FBTC led inflows.

Falling geopolitical and inflation risks often push investors back into riskier assets. When short sellers close losing positions, they must buy back Bitcoin, adding further buying pressure and potentially accelerating the rally.

That dynamic is why some traders question the move’s durability. Prominent trader @astronomer_zero described the move above $80,000 as a potential bull trap, dismissing it as a temporary “funny little pump” rather than the start of a sustained uptrend.

The trader expects Bitcoin to revisit $75,000. He also outlined a potential pattern behind the move: “Pump → Range → Drop.” According to his assessment, Bitcoin is entering a consolidation phase that could precede another decline.

The rally shows how closely crypto still tracks macro and political sentiment alongside equities. Whether Bitcoin holds above $80,000 will indicate if the move reflects real demand or short-term positioning.
