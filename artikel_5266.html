Anthropic is targeting a valuation of up to $1.02 trillion, based on its proposed IPO price range of $70–$80 per share.

The company could raise $69.4 billion through its public offering and another $26 billion through a private placement.

Nvidia and Alphabet are each committing $10 billion, highlighting the financial exposure of major technology companies to Anthropic's growth.

Anthropic’s planned IPO is emerging as a major test for the AI industry, with the Claude developer targeting a valuation of approximately $1 trillion. Backed by Nvidia and other technology companies, the offering could raise more than $90 billion through public and private share sales. Its market debut comes as investors increasingly scrutinise the cost of developing artificial intelligence and the returns on infrastructure spending. Can Anthropic justify its ambitious valuation, and what could its listing mean for Nvidia and other AI stocks?

Anthropic has proposed selling 865 million shares at $70–$80 apiece, potentially raising up to $69.4 billion. Based on outstanding shares, the upper end of the range implies a valuation of approximately $1.02 trillion. Including options and other potential shares, the fully diluted valuation could reach around $1.4 trillion.

The company is also arranging a separate $26 billion private placement involving Nvidia, Alphabet, Microsoft, AMD, Broadcom, Micron and TSMC. Google and Nvidia are each committing approximately $10 billion, making them the largest participants in the arrangement.

Anthropic plans to list on Nasdaq under the ticker symbol ANT. The offering’s final price and trading date remain subject to the IPO process.

Nvidia’s $10 billion commitment to Anthropic highlights its growing financial exposure to the AI ecosystem. The company already benefits from demand for GPUs used to train and operate advanced AI models. Anthropic’s expansion could support further demand for computing capacity, particularly as enterprise adoption of Claude increases.

However, the investment also raises questions about interconnected financing in the AI industry. Nvidia is investing in a company that needs substantial computing infrastructure, including its own products. Investors will therefore examine whether AI infrastructure spending is generating sustainable customer demand and revenue rather than depending heavily on capital flowing between industry participants.

Anthropic’s growth has been accompanied by substantial infrastructure commitments. Its latest filing disclosed approximately $17 billion in third-quarter revenue and $33.2 billion in revenue for the first nine months of 2026. However, the company also reported a nine-month net loss of $104 billion and computing and infrastructure commitments of approximately $900.9 billion.

The figures highlight the financial challenge facing advanced AI developers. Revenue growth remains important, but investors will increasingly assess operating costs, capital requirements and the path towards profitability.

Anthropic’s public listing could provide an important indication of how much investors are willing to pay for large-scale AI businesses. Strong demand could support confidence across the technology sector, while a weaker reception could renew concerns about elevated AI valuations.

For Nvidia and its technology partners, the central question is whether the enormous investment in AI infrastructure can translate into sustainable returns. Anthropic’s IPO will put that question directly before public-market investors.
