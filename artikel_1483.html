£380m = zero points: Big-spending Spurs humbled by Newcastle

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

These remain the earliest of early days but the blunt facts are there for all to see. Tottenham Hotspur, the club that has spent around £380m this summer in search of instant improvements, have played twice and lost twice in the Premier League. After making eight new signings, they have so far scored zero goals and won zero points.

Not since 1974 has a Spurs team lost the first two games of a league campaign without finding the net. The summer enthusiasm in this corner of north London is therefore already drifting away, to the sound of an alarmingly familiar soundtrack: boos from the home supporters at the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium.

This meeting with Newcastle United was, in the minds of those Spurs fans, meant to be a clash between a team remade by the transfer market and a team broken by it. Yet it was Newcastle, still adjusting to the loss of three key players this summer, who looked the more unified group and more organised team.

It proved to be a truly glorious evening for the visiting supporters, who not only delighted in their team’s victory but also in the struggles of Sandro Tonali. The £100m Italian, who swapped Newcastle for Spurs a few weeks ago, never came close to offering the sort of class one would expect for such a fee. That price tag is not his fault but it is his burden, and in time he will need to shoulder it more impressively than he did here.

Roberto De Zerbi, the Spurs head coach, can rightly say that it will take time for his new group to develop the cohesion he needs. The worrying reality for Spurs, though, is that his opposite number had far less time to work with his players and far less money to reshape his squad. Matthias Jaissle appears to be a man with a defined plan, and an ability to convey it. Newcastle improved considerably after half-time, which spoke volumes of Jaissle’s work. “He is the best signing of Newcastle so far,” said Yoane Wissa of his new coach.

For Spurs, so much of it would have felt painfully similar to last season, when they won only three home games in the Premier League. They played some pretty football in bursts, but not at the crucial points of the game. The second half, especially, was pockmarked with moments of despair. There was Mathys Tel’s free-kick that almost flew into Islington, for example, and Mateus Fernandes falling over when running onto a pass in the penalty area.

De Zerbi said he was sorry for the fans and for the club, and suggested his team is not properly prepared for this campaign. “Maybe we are not ready yet,” he said. “For many, many reasons we are not ready yet.”

Newcastle’s two goals, much like Brentford’s three last weekend, exposed the enduring frailties in the Spurs defence. Anthony Elanga’s opener stemmed from a long pass over the top of the home side’s high line, while Wissa’s acrobatic effort came from a corner kick that Spurs could not handle. Neither strike, it would be safe to say, was the result of world-class attacking play.

“After the first goal we lost confidence,” said De Zerbi. “The reaction was not good enough, not what I want to see on the pitch. The solution is not to put the head down, but to react.”

To be clear, Spurs were not quite as bad as they had been against Brentford on the opening day. They did create chances here, taking 17 shots and forcing a few terrific saves from Lukas Hornicek, Newcastle’s impressive goalkeeper. But in the key moments they suffered from the same absence of clarity and execution as they did last season. Troublingly, this seemed to affect the new signings as much as it did the longer-serving players.

Of the five new arrivals who featured for Spurs, Tonali was the least effective. The Italian had been vigorously booed by the away fans before kick-off and the travelling Geordies waited with palpable excitement for his first mistake. Much to their delight, it arrived after only three minutes: an underhit pass, in his own half, which directly led to Micky van de Ven picking up a yellow card. Tonali was outshone by new Newcastle midfielder Nico Gonzalez, who excelled only a few days after completing his move from Manchester City.

Might it have been different for Spurs if £75m winger Savio had been available? The Brazilian was electrifying on his debut in midweek, when he scored against Charlton Athletic in the League Cup, yet was unavailable here due to a minor injury. It instead fell to another signing from City, Omar Marmoush, to lead the attack. He was busy and floaty but never truly hurt Newcastle.

There are more changes to come at Spurs before Tuesday’s deadline. De Zerbi revealed after the game that Richarlison, Kevin Danso and Pape Matar Sarr have all asked to leave the club.

At least the issues facing his players appear clear to De Zerbi. His list of areas to improve was lengthy – he spoke of strengthening the connection between the players, the quality of their play, their work in the defensive phase and the distances between them – but he also insisted he loves them and remains confident in the club’s project. At this rate, though, the fans might not share that confidence for much longer.

The Tottenham boss told BBC Sport: “Disappointed for the result because until the first goal we conceded I think we played better than Newcastle. We lost ball possession in the first half because we didn’t defend in the right way, the distance between the defenders and attackers was too big. We can improve in the defensive phases but today was an unfair result.

“At the beginning of the second half we had some chance to create the potential chances. We created many chances to score, we have to improve the last decision, because that will make a difference. At the end we are disappointed for the result and our fans. We are working to be a great team. When we become a team I think we can change the story of the last two seasons for Tottenham.

“We are working to be good on the pitch, if you believe in work and training then you have to improve day by day.”

Speaking to Sky Sports, Newcastle’s head coach said: “It was incredible and I’m really proud. We are really proud as a club, as a coaching staff to see a team on the pitch delivering the match plan at a high level already, but also seeing players fully committed to what we’re doing. This is a good start and a moment to enjoy.

“We also did well last week against Liverpool, therefore it was a step in the right direction again against a strong opponent with huge signings during the transfer window. This is something we were aware of, but how we handled that challenge was amazing.”
