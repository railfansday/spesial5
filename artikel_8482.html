Te Araroa day 14: Finally back on some track

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

I got up fairly slowly and left at around 8am. The trail notes mentioned being able to walk on some farm tracks to skip a bit of road walking, so I headed off. It wasn’t very clear where I was supposed to go, but I found my way back to the road and started walking along. That is, until something made me think of my groundsheet. I had hung it over a gate when I was packing up, and I suddenly didn’t remember picking it up. I checked my bag and it wasn’t there. So I turned around and started heading back. This did not put me in a fantastic mood! Luckily I hadn’t gone too far, although realistically I wouldn’t have gone back for it if I’d gone a long way. It’s just a sheet of plastic!

By the time I had angrily marched back to the campsite and picked up my groundsheet, I was also hungry. So I had a snack of some crackers with mayo, which felt like a real hiker snack! Eventually I was ready to leave, again, at around 9am. I plugged my podcast in and hit the road. It wasn’t rainy, but was very muggy again, and the road was up and down but not as hilly as the previous day.

A highlight of this road walk was all the horses I saw. Those that know me, know that I’m a horse girl at heart! Some of the horses were very friendly, others less so. While I was trying to persuade some shy horses to talk to me, some tourists drove past and asked me about my trip. The woman wanted to tell me I was very brave for travelling alone. I told her New Zealand is a pretty safe country to travel in. Of course, nowhere is without its risks, but I’ve really never felt uncomfortable alone here – although in general, I’ve never been particularly worried about being a woman alone.

This cheeky guy was very keen to eat my bag!

Finally, it was lunchtime and I had just reached Helena Bay. I decided to pop down the road to the beach and see if there was a nicer lunchspot than a roadside ditch. And there was! A bench, a bin and a nice view made for an excellent lunch spot. Hikers really are simple folk, and this made my day.

A short walk up a hill later, and I was finally getting back on some actual track! The Helena Ridge track had clearly had work done fairly recently, but a number of large slips had knocked out sections of track. They were easily passable though, and the track went steadily up with some steeper sections of steps. I huffed and puffed my way to the top (mostly irritation at the number of steps – they’re so annoying!) and was pleased to find a nice bench at the top, well positioned to take in the view.

The track wound through the bush for quite a while, going up a whole lot more steps. At one point I started to see a hiker ahead, and thought it might be someone I’d met at The Farm the previous night. But it turned out to be a hiker called Graham, who was at The Farm but who I didn’t meet. He made his own tent, which is pretty cool! We walked together up the rest of the hill and came out into a grassy field. I was planning to stay at a free campsite a bit later, which might or might not have water. I had seen a comment on the TA app about a tap on the way out of this field, so I found it and Graham and I both filled up. He was going to head all the way to the holiday park at Whananaki, but I guess he needed water too. As I was finishing up, the farmer appeared in her Gator with her dog. She warned us that she’d just put some cows into a field we were about to walk through, and then just kept talking! She wished us a nice walk several times and then launched into another conversation.

Eventually we managed to escape, and headed on down the track. The cows the farmer had mentioned looked a little bit excitable, but we got through without problems. After a bit more up and down, we popped out at the ‘campsite’ where I would stay the night. There was a shelter, a water tank, and a rather lumpy, not very flat bit of ground. I had already booked the holiday park in Whananaki for the next day, otherwise I would have been sorely tempted to continue straight there. It was another 18km away, but it was only 3pm and I was feeling fairly fresh. Graham decided to have some food before continuing, and I was half wishing he would hurry up and leave so I could be alone, and half wishing he would stay so I wouldn’t be alone. Although I am very comfortable walking by myself in the daytime, I haven’t camped alone much at night. The camping spot had a bit of an eerie vibe, but I think that was mostly the mist.

An orange triangle being swallowed by a tree

I love whoever is building all these benches

I found a flat-ish spot for my tent, and had some snacks before making dinner. My dinner was pretty great, actually, and felt like ‘real’ food – pasta with veg, TVP, spices and tomato puree. Then I settled in my tent and listened to a podcast for a while before making myself go outside into the dark and scary night to pee and brush my teeth. I had read that there were possums about, and based on my experience with possums last time, I decided to put earplugs in so that at least they wouldn’t wake me up!
