Still England's best? Bronze and Earps' performances analysed

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Digital journalist Laura Hunter returns for a second season with a WSL-dedicated column, analysing all the big talking points from across the weekend's action; this week's focus is on the heroic performances of two England legends Lucy Bronze and Mary Earps

Sky Sports columnist Laura Hunter analyses the big talking points from the sixth round of Women's Super League matches, bringing you closer to the key stories at the heart of the women's game.

Nothing makes Lucy Bronze prouder than a clean sheet. She absolutely thrives off the scrap and skirmish of it. Against Manchester City, her determination to put defensive duty above all else won Chelsea a priceless three points.

Not since November 2025 had the WSL's leading scorer Khadija Shaw failed to register a shot on target in a game. Bronze can pocket that achievement. She had help from other members of the backline - Veerle Buurman was standout too - but Chelsea's captain led the lockdown.

Shaw was isolated, limited in time and space, and restricted in her movement. "We had to be like dogs in this game," Bronze told BBC Sport afterwards. "We were very focused on that." They shackled Shaw in a way few defences across Europe are able to, Bronze acting as the technical and tactical lynchpin.

This, Chelsea's biggest win of the season so far, is one that sees them leapfrog their greatest rivals in the race for this year's title. It will be a campaign built on solidity as much as anything else. The Blues have given up a miserly 0.5 goals per 90 so far.

Sonia Bompastor had stressed the importance of winning duels pre-game and that emphasis ran through the spine of her team. She made a point of praising Bronze's leadership in the handling of City's threats post-match, capping a heroic display with a game-high 13 defensive contributions.

Collectively, Chelsea won the duel count 47-37 and were equally better in possession, with more systematic and sustained pressure in the opposition half. Bronze won that contest too. No Chelsea player created more chances (2) or completed more final third passes (9) than her. She also registered more touches than any team-mate (87).

Meanwhile, Shaw was reduced to taking 45 per cent of her open play touches in City's defensive half and received more passes from goalkeeper Ayaka Yamashita (6) than any outfield player. City's usual rhythm was heavily disrupted and meant the striker blanked in a league game for the first time this season.

Even in the second half, when Andree Jeglertz adjusted his setup to bring Vivianne Miedema into the pocket behind Shaw, Chelsea adapted to match up. Bronze smelt the danger, commanding others to follow her exemplary lead.

A situation in the 75th minute summed up Chelsea's application perfectly.

The ball is punched through the lines to Shaw, usually so reliable in her hold-up play. But on this occasion Buurman is encouraged to engage aggressively and the ball turns over. Aggie Beever-Jones and Maika Hamano exchange passes on halfway, before the latter opts to go long in search of Alyssa Thompson's far side run.

The sequence of events that follow spell the beginning of the end for City. Yamashita feels compelled to try and cut out the pass but is caught in no man's land, mistiming her intervention and gets sent off for denying Thompson a goalscoring opportunity. If City were struggling to get at Chelsea with 11, playing with 10 made the task impossible.

Bompastor got her tactical approach spot on at Stamford Bridge, balancing enough risk with sensible stability. But she was reliant on perfect execution of the game plan from her players. They delivered. None more so than evergreen Bronze.

Another to confess to "love defending" at the weekend was Mary Earps.

Her heroism in defending London City's goal at the Emirates earned her side a valiant point against Arsenal as they continue to hold on to third spot while also staying in touch with the top two. "This game sums up why I wanted to come back and play in the best league in the world," she told Sky Sports afterwards.

England fans will have fond memories of Earps in this vein, bent on denying Arsenal from any and every angle imagineable. Sarina Wiegman will too, and she was in the stands to witness one of the finest displays of goalkeeping from a player she once called her No 1.

"She has a very uniquely competitive mindset," Izzy Christiansen reflected from the studio in north London. "She's in her element. Some of the saves were world class." Earps made eight of them. Five from inside the box.

But the best was left until last. In the final throws of the game, as the Gunners pushed to turn another unwanted home draw into something more, the ball found Frida Maanum on the edge of the area. She had already scored the game's opener but Arsenal had been undone 98 seconds later and needed another moment of inspiration.

With almost no backlift at all, Maanum shaped a lovely strike towards the top corner and waited to see it nestle in the net. Except it never did. Earps was forced to backpedal but got enough purchase on her leap to claw the ball onto the crossbar and preserve the point London City deserved for their efforts. None more impressive than her own.

Claims of vintage Earps echoed around the Emirates. Social media buzzed with posts of 'Mary's back!' The WSL is certainly a better league with her in it.

Wiegman may no longer be able to call upon the 33-year-old's services, but Eder Maestre will be delighted he can. If London City are to turn their Champions League ambitions into reality, Earps' wealth of big-game experience and ability to handle pressure moments could prove invaluable.
