Jensen Huang’s Potentially Seismic Art Retreat

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Welcome to True Colors. I’m in Manhattan. Lots to get into this week. There’s the matter of what to do about a major new San Francisco arts institution that was set to be named after Jensen Huang and his wife, Lori Huang. Understandably, it’s a question whether that renaming will still happen given…everything. There’s my ode to the death of the Cybertruck. And there’s all the details from the star-studded MoMA Film Benefit honoring Steven Spielberg. Tom Cruise shocked the crowd with surprise remarks, and Bruce Springsteen roasted the director. It was a whole night.

MENTIONED IN THIS ISSUE: Donald Trump, Jason Calacanis, Brendan Dugan, Siniša Mačković, David Zwirner, Lorenzo Amos, Kate Capshaw, Zohran Mamdani, Jessica Craig-Martin, Dean & DeLuca, Indochine, Stanley Whitney, Marina Adams, Will Whitney, The Beverly Hills Hotel, Elon Musk, Lana Del Rey, Grimes, Travis Scott, Pharrell Williams, 21 Savage, Tyler, the Creator, Bad Bunny, Julia Wachtel, Todd von Ammon, Frieda Toranzo Jaeger.

The major arts institution named for Jensen Huang and his wife, Lori, appears to be—shocker!—very much in jeopardy.

Just months ago, Huang was getting a phone call from President Trump while onstage at the All-In Summit in Los Angeles. “I’m here with the besties, Mr. President,” a laughing Huang said, putting his phone up to the mic. “Tell him I say hi!” said All-In cohost Jason Calacanis.

But Huang’s recent weeks have not been marked by good times with the besties. First, the Democrats took over Congress, throttling all the goodwill he built with the administration. Then he admitted to having an extramarital affair with a woman suspected by the US to be a Chinese spy—and then news of another affair, this time allegedly with a subordinate, came via a shocking New York Times bombshell. Amid rumors that yet another affair would soon break, he stepped down as CEO and, according to Forbes, has watched nearly $20 billion of his estimated net worth vaporize since Monday.

What’s unknown is the current state of his relationship with his wife, Lori Huang—and their ambitious plans for an arts organization that was set to bear their name. Earlier this year, the Huangs pledged $75 million to Vanderbilt University in order to help transform the soon-to-be-closed legendary San Francisco art school California College of the Arts into part of Vanderbilt’s SF campus. Vanderbilt announced that it planned to name a portion of its SF campus the Jen-Hsun and Lori Huang College of Art, Architecture and Design.

Which is now a bit awkward, given the multiple alleged affairs, and the whole Chinese-spy thing. Plus, Huang is no longer affiliated with Nvidia, which teamed up with CCA in 2025 to form the CCA-Nvidia Incubator for Creative Intelligence. Will Jensen and Lori Huang still put their names together on arts organizations after all that’s happened? And if there is no Jen-Hsun and Lori Huang College of Art, Architecture and Design, what will happen to Vanderbilt’s SF campus when it opens next year?

The CCA was started in 1907, and Huang and his wife were expected to support its transition to a new chapter. It would be a shame if this is how it ends. Reps at Vanderbilt didn’t get back to me.

It’s a new era in Tribeca. Tonight, Karma, a burgeoning mega-gallery with spaces in New York’s Chelsea and LA’s Hollywood, opens its new gallery at 38 White Street. Founder Brendan Dugan and director Siniša Mačković took me on a tour earlier this week, and it’s easily one of the most generous spaces anywhere in the neighborhood. Plus, an outpost of Karma Bookstore—a much needed bookstore for a neighborhood shockingly lacking in one—is coming in right across the street.

This will be the latest big-tent blue chipper to take a space in the Triangle Below Canal, as they used to call this neighborhood. Something to consider: In the last few years, mega-galleries such as David Zwirner and Hauser & Wirth have shut their uptown townhouses and opened downtown.

Also opening tonight is a White Street outpost of Gratin gallery, which has a roster of incredibly exciting young stars, including Lorenzo Amos (whom we wrote about a year ago). I’m hearing about other younger dealers grabbing spaces in the neighborhood. The art market isn’t exactly on fire, but dealers are betting on one thing: the enduring power and capital of New York City.

Speaking of New York, guess who’s lived here full-time for almost a year? Steven Spielberg, that’s who. On New Year’s Day 2026, the GOAT American director—who is married to the artist Kate Capshaw—moved his official residence from California to New York, and has made the apartment in the San Remo his home base. You will not be surprised to hear that, post-move, Spielberg is doing extremely cool stuff in town. He’s walking around with a script and a Tracy Kidder book while slugging down an iced McCafé. He’s getting lunch at Benvenuto Cafe. He’s having Mayor Zohran Mamdani over to the crib.

The only thing left was for one of the city’s great museums to throw a big party for the Hollywood-royalty-gone-Gotham. Last night, Spielberg got just that: He was honored at the MoMA Film Benefit, sponsored by Chanel. I was there. Read the full report—with fun pictures!—here.

Also on VF this week: an exclusive excerpt from an uproarious new memoir by artist Jessica Craig-Martin, I Regret I am Able to Attend. Craig-Martin is a real treasure of the art world, a great photographer—and, as it turns out, a hell of a writer. The whole book is a compulsively readable blast. The chapter where Craig-Martin recalls working as an extremely inexperienced, occasionally larcenous teller at Dean & DeLuca is one of the funniest, most satisfying New York vignettes I’ve read this year. And of course, all the scuttlebutt from the ’80s art scene is fabulous. Read the excerpt, in which our narrator spills all the tea—and, um, stuff stronger than tea—about working as a waitress when Indochine opened.

Happy 80th birthday to the great painter Stanley Whitney. His wife, the artist Marina Adams, and son, dealer Will Whitney, threw Stanley a party to celebrate his octogenarian status on Nine Orchard’s roof, overlooking Dimes Square. Writers, artists, dealers, old heads, everyone was there. A fabulous Wednesday night.
