The Voice Behind The Pillsbury Doughboy’s Iconic Giggle Has Passed Away

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

A little giggle that left a big impression.

JoBe Cerny, the actor who gave the Pillsbury Doughboy his signature voice and unmistakable giggle, has died at age 78.

According to TMZ, Cerny died on October 30 in Illinois following a years-long battle with Alzheimer’s disease. His son, Tim Cerny, confirms the news to the outlet, explaining that his father was diagnosed around 2020 and eventually stepped away from the beloved role as his health declined.

For anyone who grew up watching Pillsbury commercials, Cerny’s voice is probably a lot more familiar than his face. He voiced the Pillsbury Doughboy, also known as Poppin’ Fresh, from 2014 until around 2020, bringing the cheerful baking mascot to life with the giggle that generations of viewers have come to recognize and adore.

Cerny took over the role, previously voiced by both Paul Frees and Jeff Bergman, becoming part of the character’s decades-long history in American advertising.

Following the news of his death, fans have been sharing memories of the Pillsbury Doughboy and the small moments of happiness Cerny’s work brought them.

“Nooo! Ugh I loved his voice would cheer me up when I was having a crap of a day!” one Instagram commenter wrote.

Another shared a particularly nostalgic reaction: “I loved the doughboy as a kid! Didn’t know he voiced him!”

Others reflected on how much the commercials meant to them growing up. “Childhood leaving us day by day. Back then commercials were actually good,” one person commented.

And one fan even has a special tribute planned: “Will be buying the rolls tonight in his honor 🙏🏻,” they wrote.

The reactions remind us how recognizable the Pillsbury Doughboy became over the years. Even if viewers didn’t know Cerny by name, they knew the character and the familiar laugh he helped bring into their homes.

In addition to his voice work as the iconic Doughboy, Cerny also did commercials for Cheer laundry detergent and made appearances in movies including My Best Friend’s Wedding and Legally Blonde 2.

Outside of his entertainment career, Cerny was an enthusiastic model train collector and racing fan who continued driving go-karts into his 60s, according to his obituary.

Although Cerny spent decades working in entertainment, his time as the voice of one of America’s most recognizable food mascots remains an especially memorable part of his career.

And judging by fans' reactions, that little giggle made a much bigger impression than he might have realized.
