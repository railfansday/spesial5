The Celtics, in all of their early-season glory, haven’t passed Brad Stevens’ test yet

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

We're about a fifth of the way through the 2026-27 Boston Celtics season.

So far, mostly so good. The Celtics are 11-4, which has them slotted second in the East behind the Philadelphia 76ers — whom they beat in comfortable fashion last week at Xfinity Mobile Arena, 123-101.

For you analytics heads out there, Boston ranks sixth in offensive rating, second in defensive rating, and second in net rating.

Paul George, whom the Cs bet on when they traded Jaylen Brown for him (and some nice picks) couldn't be playing much better. If you need any insight into how the PG-13 Experience has been, look no further than the fanbase collectively referring to him as "Paul God."

The other offseason addition hasn't panned out as well, but we won't jump on the pile here.

Through this first month, the only real nitpick about this team is the schedule that they've had. Multiple games against the putrid Milwaukee Bucks and Chicago Bulls, plus a handful against other struggling squads like the Charlotte Hornets, Dallas Mavericks, Toronto Raptors, and Brooklyn Nets make the 11-4 record feel a bit inflated.

Don't take my word for it; here's what Brad Stevens had to say about last season's 56-win team in his end-of-season press conference.

"We have to play well in the regular season to get to where we want to go," he told reporters back in May. "We don't want to undersell the regular season. That said, as I look at us honestly, I look at how we played against the best teams is relevant and should be taken into account as we look at our team moving forward."

Boston's record against the top three teams in each conference was 3-11. Of course, their first-round loss to the 76ers only amplified that issue. The Sixers weren't a "top team" but at full-strength (which they miraculously were in Games 4-7), they're not your typical Play-In team.

Anyway, there was a clear correlation in all the losses vs. that in the wins. Boston shot just 27.4% from three in the losses. In victories, that number jumped up to 41.7%.

This season, they haven't quite shown us that it'll be a different story just yet. The Cs have had five games against teams above .500 and have won just two. Their three losses came agains the Detroit Pistons, Houston Rockets, and Denver Nuggets.

The two wins have featured elite shooting performances from the perimeter. Boston sank 16-40 in their home opener against the New York Knicks, and then lit the Sixers up with a 22-49 performance in Philly.

Somehow, they lost the opener against Detroit despite a strong shooting day, but the other two losses have gone exactly how you'd guess.

I know how this all sounds, too. Like yeah, of course they're going to shoot better in wins than losses — you could probably pull up the splits for every team in the league and they'd look the same.

With the Celtics, though, it's #DifferentHere. A team that's relied so heavily on winning the mathematical margins over the past four seasons has seen shooting variance end three of them in disappointing fashion. The 2024 team was special, but also managed to win playoff games when they went cold — even they dropped a few headscratchers thanks to variance.

Does this all mean that this season's team sucks?

Probably not. But it's disappointing that they've come up short a few times in their limited amount of "prove it" games. Their lost to Houston did come on the second night of a back-to-back where Paul God was resting. So, there's some grace to be given there.

One optimistic takeaway so far has been the elite defense. Boston's 106.0 defensive rating has been built on sharp rotations, disruptive defensive playmaking, and a strong paint presence from Neemias Queta. All three of those things will remain under their control regardless of whether or not the shots are going in on a given night.

The Celtics have held opponents to under 110 points in 11 of their 15 games thus far. By doing so, they're at least giving themselves a chance to get across the finish line when the threes don't fall. We might be watching them develop their backup plan in real time.

It's going to be a while before another "test" game. Their NBA Cup group stage finale against the Atlanta Hawks will be a minor test before they head to San Antonio to take on the Spurs next Sunday. A win against Wemby and the reigning West champs would go along way in battling the "schedule merchants" narratives.
