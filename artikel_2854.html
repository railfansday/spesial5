Jacob Duffy gets Virat Kohli's wicket, teases RCB teammate as 'annoying' after Auckland ODI

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Jacob Duffy called Virat Kohli "annoying" after his 74 settled the first ODI in Auckland.

The two are RCB teammates, and Duffy said Kohli had done the same to him in the nets in Bengaluru.

Kohli passed Sachin Tendulkar for the most ODI runs in chases during India's chase.

Rain had reduced India's target from 186 in 50 overs to 143 in 32 overs, which is the kind of chase a bowling side can still make awkward.

Virat Kohli, however, removed that possibility in 51 balls by scoring a well-crafted 74. Jacob Duffy, who dismissed Kohli, was asked afterward what it had been like to watch.

"Yeah, it was a bit annoying, wasn't it?" the seamer said at the post-match press conference.

"Small targets like that. If you get a guy who comes out with that kind of intent to knock it off, it can just break the chase pretty easily." Duffy was more admiring than irritated by the end of the answer, and the detail he kept coming back to was how little the Eden Park bounce troubled Kohli.

"Bloody impressive, wasn't he? Didn't struggle with the bounce. He got inside those hook shots and pull shots, and then he could wake you down the ground," he said, before adding the line that explained his familiarity with the problem. "He's done it for a lot of years, and he did that to me in the nets a lot in Bangalore."

Pressed on how a bowling attack is supposed to contain him, Duffy declined to give much away while conceding there was not much to work with.

"I won't give away all those scouting tips, you know. I did get some intel in the nets over in Bangalore, but there's not many holes in his game to be fair," he said. "Small grounds like that, he uses the pace really well and then you can obviously, like I said, hit you over your head as well."

He did offer one caveat: the equation might look different with a larger total to defend. "If they're batting first or there's a bigger score on the board, it'll be interesting to see if he plays like that. I don't know if he will."

Duffy's own diagnosis of the defeat had little to do with Kohli. New Zealand were bowled out for 185 after Shubman Gill chose to field, and the pacer was blunt about what that meant at the ground.

"180 at Eden Park was never going to be enough. They bowled pretty well," he said, crediting India's seamers for the manner of it. "India bowled well, extracted the bounce very well. We are a good batting team."

This comes as a surprise because it was at the same ground where New Zealand had scored a record 259/7 in the fourth T20I.

Arshdeep Singh took four wickets in that innings, continuing a run of form that has made him India's most dependable ODI seamer since the 2023 World Cup. Kohli then made 74 from 51 with 13 fours and a six as India got home in 22.4 overs with five wickets standing.

Kohli moved past Sachin Tendulkar for the most ODI runs scored while chasing, taking his tally to 8,741 against Tendulkar's 8,720.

He became the first batter to reach 13,000 ODI runs at No. 3.

He equalled Ricky Ponting's record of 1,971 ODI runs against New Zealand.

Duffy, for his part, was generous about the opposition beyond the man who beat them.

"A lot of Indian players are patient, appreciate their fans. I look up to them. Impressive to watch," he said, and insisted New Zealand would not change their approach across the remaining four matches.

Chasing 143 in 32 overs, Kohli could attack from the first ball without consequence, and he did, striking at better than a run a ball against an attack that had nothing to bowl at.

Whether he plays that way with 300 on the board is a genuinely open question, and one New Zealand will get four more chances to answer.

Kohli's recent ODI returns against this opposition have come at both tempos, including a 93 in a chase of 301 in Vadodara in January and a century in a losing cause in Indore.

What Duffy is really conceding is that the planning problem has no clean solution at a ground this size. Eden Park rewards exactly the strokes Kohli played, and New Zealand's answer will have to be a total that makes those strokes a risk rather than a formality.
