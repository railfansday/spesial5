David Greaves Says ‘Once Upon a Time in Harlem’ Is “An Act of Resistance” Against Efforts to Erase Black History

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

When William Greaves died before completing his Harlem Renaissance documentary, his son David, who had helped to shoot it but had never directed a feature film of his own, stepped in to finish it: "I couldn't imagine anybody else doing it," he tells The Hollywood Reporter.

When the veteran newspaperman David Greaves told people about the film that his father, the legendary documentarian William Greaves, had been trying to complete for decades — the one built around footage of a 1972 reunion of Harlem Renaissance luminaries at Duke Ellington’s apartment — some asked him why anyone would want to watch a bunch of old people sitting around talking. David Greaves has been proven correct by the tremendous reception of that documentary, a one-of-a-kind chronicle of a critical chapter in African-American history, which finally premiered at the Sundance Film Festival in January, screened at the Cannes Film Festival in May back and was released by Neon in October under the title Once Upon a Time in Harlem. Sadly, William Greaves didn’t live to see the film’s completion — he died in 2014 — but his work was finished by his son, who had been a 26-year-old cameraman at the 1972 gathering, but had never directed a film before. “I couldn’t imagine anybody else doing it, frankly,” David explained during a post-screening Q&A at Savannah’s Lucas Theatre. (He also was one of 10 directors of outstanding 2026 docs who appeared on the fest’s Doc to Watch Panel.)

The fact that it took more than a half-century for the film to come to fruition was largely the result of the perfectionist tendencies of William and his second wife, Louise, David’s stepmother, who died in 2023. “They had continued shooting on through the decades,” further tracing the history of the Harlem Renaissance, David reports. While he’s sorry that his father and stepmother weren’t able to see the enthusiastic response to the film from audiences of all ages and races, he also feels that it all happened for a reason: “I think that this film was made to come out now, at a very important time in American history, when [some in positions of power] want to erase Black history,” he asserted. “This film is sort of an act of resistance against that.”
