Highest To Lowest: How To Get From JFK To Manhattan For Just $3

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

In the film Highest 2 Lowest (2025), starring Denzel Washington, legendary filmmaker Spike Lee portrays two distinct sides of New York City, depending on your altitude and bank account. Nowhere is that contrast more apparent than in the choices travelers face when commuting between Manhattan and John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK).

Whether you are a visiting traveler or an NYC resident, navigating to and from the city’s primary international hub is a perpetual balancing act between cost, comfort, and time. Previously, I detailed what is literally and figuratively the “highest” mode of airport transit: Blade’s helicopter transfers (starting at $195 and easily topping $300, though rides can cost you nothing out of pocket using Bilt Cash). Each Bilt Cash award expires 12 months after you earn it, on the last day of that month.

In this Reader Story, I’m taking you down, subterranean, to the lowest option available: the Metropolitan Transportation Authority’s (MTA) $3 bus-to-subway hack.

Duke Ellington was onto something: “Take the ‘A’ Train.” However, he didn’t specify which A train branch, nor did he mention that a quick bus transfer might be involved!

Even some locals may ask: “Wait, isn’t the AirTrain $8.75, plus a $3 subway fare?” That’s true if you exit the AirTrain at Jamaica or Howard Beach. However, there is a lesser-known and dramatically cheaper workaround: just $3 total from JFK to nearly anywhere in NYC with the MTA, provided you don’t mind adding a few extra minutes to your journey.

For the reverse trip, take any Lefferts Blvd-bound A train, ensuring the destination sign explicitly says “Lefferts Blvd” rather than Far Rockaway or Rockaway Park. Importantly, Far Rockaway A trains often display “JFK Airport” on their digital side displays because most travelers use the more expensive $8.75 Howard Beach transfer route, so do not board those if you want the $3-only route. Once on the correct train, get off at Liberty Ave / Lefferts Blvd, exit to the street, locate the bus stop, catch a JFK-bound Q10 or Q80 bus to the Lefferts AirTrain station, and take the free AirTrain directly to your terminal.

If you type “JFK Airport” into Google Maps or Apple Maps, the app will almost never display the $3-only hack. Navigation algorithms prioritize minimizing the number of transit transfers over saving money, automatically suppressing the bus transfer in favor of the standard $8.75 AirTrain connection at Jamaica or Howard Beach.

Even typing “Lefferts Blvd AirTrain Station” into navigation apps often causes confusion because the AirTrain station and the Ozone Park A-train terminal are located roughly 1.5 miles apart.

To force your map app to show the correct route, search for the street-level bus-to-subway transfer point at “Lefferts Blvd & Liberty Ave.” From JFK, this will display the Q10 Rush or Q80 bus taking you to the A train turnstiles; from Manhattan, it will guide you on the Lefferts-bound A train directly to your bus connection.

Other than extreme weather events, strikes, and major crises, all three legs of this route—the AirTrain, the Q10/Q80 bus, and the A Train—operate 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, though timing varies. During daytime and peak hours (6:00 AM – 10:00 PM), AirTrain cars run every 7–10 minutes, Q10 and Q80 buses depart roughly every 8–15 minutes, and A trains run frequently as express services through Brooklyn and Manhattan.

However, late-night transit frequency changes significantly in NYC, so keep that in mind for overnight or early-morning flights: AirTrain headways drop to 15–20 minute intervals, late-night Q10/Q80 bus departures stretch to every 20–30 minutes, and the A train may shift to local-only service (check MTA Service Status for possible re-routings).

Thanks to the flat-fare structure of the New York City Subway (as opposed to distance-based transit networks found in cities like London, Tokyo, or Washington, D.C.), that single $3 fare (and free transfer) unlocks access to nearly the entire city once you pass through the turnstiles at the Ozone Park–Lefferts Boulevard station.

Because there are no exit turnstiles or zone caps inside the subway network, you can ride from Queens through Brooklyn, up Manhattan, and deep into the Bronx, all on that same initial $3 tap—as long as you stay within the fare-paid zone. And, if you exit the subway system in Lower Manhattan, you can walk over to Whitehall Terminal and board the free Staten Island Ferry to reach the fifth and final borough. While attempting to tap onto a Staten Island bus or the Staten Island Railway (SIR) on the other side would consume a second $3 fare (and likely break the 2-hour transfer clock), at least JFK to Manhattan for $3 remains one of the greatest transit bargains in the world.

All told—AirTrain, bus, and subway—you can get between JFK and Manhattan in about 75 to 90 minutes. Depending on brutal rush-hour highway traffic, you might even tie or beat a $100+ rideshare. That said, I would recommend this route exclusively for those traveling light (backpacks and carry-ons), willing to spend the extra time, and comfortable navigating city transit. But for the right traveler, it is a relatively safe, clean, reliable, and wildly affordable hack.

For light packers on a budget, the Lefferts Boulevard Q10 transfer stands as the undefeated champion of pure value. Duke Ellington was right all along—if you want to get to Sugar Hill or anywhere else in the city without breaking the bank, simply take the “A” train!
