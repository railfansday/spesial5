Why Kirk Cousins made decision to return to Minnesota, reunite with Vikings

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

Maybe the most consequential trade of deadline day in the NFL wasn't actually made by a front office executive. Instead, the lone player in the deal gave it the final go-ahead.

In the final hours before Tuesday's 4 p.m. ET deadline, the Minnesota Vikings pulled off a trade for Kirk Cousins, sending the Las Vegas Raiders a 2027 third-round pick to reunite with their former quarterback. Before ultimately agreeing to the deal, however, the Raiders put the ball in Cousins' court, according to The Ringer's Tom Pelissero. Las Vegas gave Cousins the chance to decide whether he'd like to leave the organization and head back to Minnesota or stay with the club he had started the first nine games of the 2026 season for. A big reason he decided to head to the Vikings was head coach Kevin O'Connell.

"Kevin knows me. Kevin knows what I do well. Kevin knows what I don't do well," Cousins said on Wednesday. "Kevin knows what I can handle. Kevin knows what I can't handle. And I think that helps."

Cousins said he was eating breakfast with his Raiders teammates when he got a call from Las Vegas coach Klint Kubiak informing him he was being traded. After talking with his family, agent Mike McCartney, and O'Connell, Cousins made up his mind by midday Tuesday and ultimately informed Raiders GM John Spytek that he wanted to return to Minnesota. Thus, the trade was made.

Part of the calculus in Cousins' decision to opt for a return to the Vikings was the presence of Fernando Mendoza. Cousins seemed to know it was only a matter of time before the Raiders turned the keys to the franchise over to the 2026 No. 1 overall pick, so his status as QB1 was fleeting.

But Cousins has such a strong familiarity with O'Connell and the Vikings that made the move easier. He believes it will help them down the stretch.

"Not just that [O'Connell] knows your strengths, but that he knows your weaknesses," Cousins said. "And that as a result, he's going to try to protect you and not have you do things that he knows, 'You know what? That's not how Kirk functions. That's not his best chance of success.'

"I think the fact he knows me well, the good about me and the bad about me, I think that should help us."

Cousins was a name thrown around during the trade deadline, but he said he wasn't necessarily interested in just going to any team. The fit had to make sense for him and his success, and it made sense with the Vikings.

"I'm not too interested in completely starting over," Cousins said when asked about potentially being traded elsewhere. "I want to play with … you've got to play off reaction and instinct. And if you're thinking, I don't think you can quite be the player that you otherwise could be. So to me, it's important to be able to be at a place that you feel you at least have the potential to do that."

Even with that familiarity, joining a new team and starting for them in the same week is no easy feat, even for a veteran. O'Connell said the goal would be for Cousins to start in their high-stakes divisional game against the Packers on Sunday, but added that he wants to "be fair to Kirk and make sure he feels good about it going into Lambeau Field."

The last game Cousins played in a Vikings uniform was at Lambeau Field in 2023, when the QB tore his Achilles and his season was declared over.

"To be back there wearing purple and gold, it has meaning," Cousins said about the significance of his return with the Vikings happening in the same spot as his injury. "But you have to go make it mean something with the way you play."

When asked if he feels he has unfinished business, he said, "I think my whole career's felt like unfinished business."

With an opportunity to go back and help a Vikings team he spent six years with and won the NFC North with in 2022, the proper pieces fell into place to give the deal the green light. This is also a pretty unprecedented move, as Cousins has the most single-season passing touchdowns (20) — as well as career passing touchdowns (318) — by any quarterback changing teams midseason in NFL history.

During his first six-season stint with the Vikings, Cousins was second in the league in completion percentage (67.9%), fourth in passing yards (23,265), and third in passing touchdowns (171) among all quarterbacks over that span with at least 50 starts. He also reunites with wide receiver Justin Jefferson, who averaged 98.9 receiving yards per game with Cousins — an NFL record for most receiving yards by a quarterback-receiver duo over at least 50 games.

While the 15-year veteran is now returning at 38, the hope is that he can come close to that production and give an offense tied for the fewest offensive touchdowns (11) in the NFL this season a boost. Cousins is focused on maintaining "joy" in a job where comparisons can steal it. While that can be a challenge, he said he looks forward to embracing it in the coming weeks.
