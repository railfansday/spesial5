Chase Sapphire Reserve Business Bonus: 200K Points Welcome Offer

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

The Sapphire Reserve for Business℠ is Chase’s premium business card. While the card has a steep annual fee, it also has strong perks, and for many people, will prove to be more than worthwhile.

We’re currently seeing a highest-ever Sapphire Reserve Business bonus, which matches the best bonus we’ve seen on this card. I’d like to recap the details of the offer, including talking about eligibility, and why the card could be worth it. While this is a limited time welcome offer, the end date hasn’t been published.

Link: Learn more about the Sapphire Reserve for Business℠

The Chase Sapphire Reserve Business currently has a limited time highest-ever welcome offer of 200,000 Chase Ultimate Rewards after spending $30,000 within the first six months.

Based on my latest miles & points valuations, I consider Chase Ultimate Rewards points to be worth 1.7 cents each. That means to me, the welcome bonus on this card is worth $3,400, which is incredible, among the best welcome offers out there. Admittedly the spending requirement is big (you need to average $5,000 per month in spending over six months), but so is the bonus.

For context on the Sapphire Reserve Business bonus history, when the card launched in 2025, we saw the same offer that’s currently available, for 200,000 points. Then in early 2026 we saw an offer for 150,000 points, before the offer for 200,000 points returned. Initially the offer for 200,000 points was supposed to end in September 2026, but it was extended, though we don’t know the official end date.

When it comes to applying for the Chase Sapphire Reserve Business, eligibility is unrelated to that of any other Chase Sapphire or Chase Ink card. That means if you’re eligible for business credit cards, you should be eligible for the welcome offer on this card, making this offer pretty widely available.

Keep in mind the general rules of applying for Chase cards, including the 5/24 rule, whereby you may not be approved if you’ve opened five or more new card accounts in the past 24 months (though select business cards don’t count toward that total).

If you’re trying to understand general Chase business card restrictions, the Chase Ink cards probably give you the best sense of what you can expect in terms of eligibility. You don’t need a corporation to apply for the Sapphire Reserve Business, as a sole proprietorship would qualify as well. So even a side gig (like freelancing or having a rental property) would potentially qualify you for the card.

There’s not a specific credit score you need to get approved for the Sapphire Reserve Business, though I’d hope to have a score in the “good” to “excellent” range, ideally 700+ points. However, people with lower scores have been approved, and people with higher scores have been rejected, as it’s one of only many factors that goes into deciding on approvals.

The $795 annual fee Chase Sapphire Reserve Business is a pretty compelling business card. As is the case with many premium cards, the idea is that the annual fee can be justified based on a combination of the rewards structure, benefits, and airport lounge access, among other things.

While the Sapphire Reserve Business has a steep annual fee, it also offers strong perks, so I’m sure many people will have no issues justifying this annual fee. Either way, if you’re eligible for the card and can complete the spending requirement, this welcome offer is incredibly tempting, and a great reason to apply for the card.
