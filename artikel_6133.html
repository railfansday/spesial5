World Of Hyatt Business Credit Card Review: Worth It For The Bonus & Status?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The $199 annual fee World of Hyatt Business Credit Card is Hyatt’s only business credit card. While I’d argue that the personal World of Hyatt Credit Card is all-around more lucrative on an ongoing basis, there’s still value to be had in picking up the business version of the card as well, especially when it’s offering a big welcome bonus.

This post is my in-depth World of Hyatt Business Card review. So let’s talk about whether the card is worth applying for, whether it’s worth keeping in the long run, and how it compares to the personal card, among other things. I will say, I don’t consider this to be among the best hotel credit cards.

Understandably, when people apply for credit cards, they’re often considering the welcome bonus, the card eligibility, and the annual fee. So let’s talk a bit about that for the World of Hyatt Business Card.

The World of Hyatt Business Card is offering an elevated welcome bonus where you can earn 70,000 World of Hyatt bonus points after spending $7,000 within the first three months.

Based on my latest miles & points valuations, I consider World of Hyatt points to be worth 1.4 cents each. That means I value the 70,000 points at $980, making this a solid bonus. This is a better bonus than what’s typically offered on the personal World of Hyatt Credit Card, making it a compelling reason to pick up the business card.

When you pull up the World of Hyatt Business Card application, you’ll see the following terms:

This new Cardmember bonus offer is not available to either (i) current Cardmembers of this business credit card, or (ii) previous Cardmembers of this business credit card who received a new Cardmember bonus for this business credit card within the last 24 months.

As you can see, you’re eligible as long as you don’t currently have the card, and haven’t received a new cardmember bonus on the card in the past 24 months. This is good news, as eligibility is considered independently of any other Chase credit card, and it’s also considered independently of the World of Hyatt Credit Card (learn more).

You can expect that approval requirements on this card are similar to other Chase business cards. You’ll need to have a legitimate business to apply, though Chase’s definition of “business” is fairly broad. Sole proprietorships, freelancers, and side hustles generally qualify.

When applying, you’ll be asked about your business revenue and years in business. Be honest with your answers, as Chase may verify this information. Many people are approved instantly, though some applications go to manual review.

If you’re not instantly approved, you can call Chase’s reconsideration line to discuss your application. In many cases, they just need to verify some information or may ask you to move credit from an existing Chase card

Chase has what’s known as the 5/24 rule, whereby you typically won’t be approved for a card if you’ve opened five or more new card accounts in the past 24 months (though there are many reports lately of it no longer being consistently enforced).

While this card is theoretically subject to that rule, the good thing is that applying for the World of Hyatt Business Card won’t count as a further card toward that limit.

In other words, if you’re at 4/24 and you apply for a Chase business card, you’ll still be at 4/24 (under the limit). Meanwhile if you’re at 4/24 and you apply for a Chase personal card, you’ll be at 5/24 (which is the limit). Therefore I always recommend picking up Chase business cards before Chase personal cards.

The World of Hyatt Business Card has a $199 annual fee. However, the benefits can definitely help offset that, so you shouldn’t think of the card actually costing you that much to hold onto. You can also add authorized users to the card at no extra cost.

The World of Hyatt Business Card has no foreign transaction fees, so it’s an excellent card for purchases abroad. That’s especially true when staying with Hyatt abroad, or in one of the categories eligible for bonus points.

When it comes to the return on spending, the World of Hyatt Business Card offers bonus points for Hyatt stays, has a unique adaptive accelerator bonus category, and offers 1x points in non-bonused categories. However, the main reason you’ll actually want to spend on the card is to earn World of Hyatt elite status, as I’ll cover a bit later.

The World of Hyatt Business Card offers 4x points for spending at Hyatt hotels and resorts, including Mr & Mrs Smith properties. That’s a solid return on hotel spending, though it doesn’t beat the return you get on the Sapphire Reserve for Business℠ (learn more), which also offers 4x points on hotel purchases, and points can be moved to World of Hyatt.

Keep in mind that this is all in addition to the standard points earning at properties. World of Hyatt members ordinarily earn 5x points per dollar spent, plus elite bonuses of 10-30%. So the 4x points stacks on top of that.

Based on my valuation of Hyatt points, 4x points is like a 5.6% return on Hyatt spending.

The World of Hyatt Business Card has some useful 2x points bonus categories. For one, the card offers 2x points for fitness clubs and gym memberships, reflecting Hyatt’s focus on wellness.

On top of that, the card has a unique adaptive accelerator feature, whereby you earn 2x points in your top three spending categories each quarter. If you’re going to put spending on the card, I recommend concentrating as much spending as possible in bonus categories, so you can earn elite nights and maximize your points.

Based on my valuation of Hyatt points, 2x points is like a 2.8% return on Hyatt spending.

The World of Hyatt Business Card offers 1x points per dollar spent on everyday spending. Generally speaking, there are better cards for everyday spending, though if you’re pursuing World of Hyatt elite status, there could be value to this.

As is common with hotel credit cards, one of the main reasons you may want to consider applying is because of the ongoing benefits and credits the card offers. So what should you expect from the World of Hyatt Business Card? While I wouldn’t say the card has the strongest perks of any hotel credit card, for many people the math may still check out.

The World of Hyatt Business Card offers an up to $100 Hyatt credit every anniversary year. Just spend $50 or more at any Hyatt property, and you’ll receive a $50 statement credit, up to twice every anniversary year. Here are the key details to know about this credit:

This is one of the most straightforward hotel credits offered by any credit card. If you don’t value this credit at close to face value, then the World of Hyatt Business Card probably isn’t for you, since you likely don’t stay at Hyatts enough.

Pro tip: When you stay at a Hyatt, you can always split your bill between multiple cards at check-out. So if you want to use a different card for most of your Hyatt spending, you can still charge $50 to this card during a stay to unlock the credit.

The way I view it, the $100 Hyatt credit essentially slashes the card’s real annual fee in half, from $199 to around $99.

The World of Hyatt Business Card offers five World of Hyatt elite qualifying nights for every $10,000 spent on the card in a calendar year. In other words, you’re effectively earning one elite night for every $2,000 spent.

As a point of comparison, the personal version of the card offers two elite nights for every $5,000 spent. The rate of earning elite nights on the business version of the card is better (however, the business version doesn’t offer any elite nights just for being a cardmember, while the personal card offers five elite nights annually with no spending required).

Being able to earn elite nights through credit card spending can be valuable whether you’re going for World of Hyatt Globalist status, or are looking to reach the next Milestone Rewards threshold to earn suite upgrade awards, club access awards, Guest of Honor awards, etc.

The World of Hyatt Business Card offers Discoverist status not only for the primary cardmember, but also for up to five employees (they don’t even have to be authorized users on the card). While this is only entry-level status, it still offers some valuable perks, especially when you consider that you can gift this status to five others.

Honestly, this perk is pretty niche. For the average cardmember, it isn’t something I’d significantly consider when deciding on the overall value proposition of the card. However, if you’re someone who largely qualifies for Globalist status through credit card spending, there’s something to be said for getting that spending done early in the year to maximize the award rebate.

One unique perk of the World of Hyatt Business Card is access to Hyatt Leverage, Hyatt’s corporate travel program. This gives cardmembers access to discounted rates and perks at Hyatt properties worldwide, which can be useful for business travelers who may not otherwise have access to corporate rates.

I’d consider World of Hyatt points to be among the most valuable hotel points currencies out there, though admittedly they’ve been devalued a bit over time. The best value for redeeming World of Hyatt points is using the free night award chart, which you can find below.

While the World of Hyatt Business Card offers many perks, I’d argue the personal World of Hyatt Credit Card (learn more) is still the more valuable card on an ongoing basis for most people.

The personal card has an annual fee of just $95 and offers a Category 1-4 anniversary free night award, five elite nights toward status annually with no spending requirement, and more. The card is also worth spending $15,000 on annually, as you can earn a second Category 1-4 free night award that way.

That said, the business card has a potentially better welcome bonus, a faster elite night earning rate through spending (one per $2,000 vs. one per $2,500), and the ability to give Discoverist status to five others. The World of Hyatt Business Card has a welcome bonus of 70,000 points (worth ~$980). On an ongoing basis, the card isn’t quite the slam dunk that the World of Hyatt Credit Card is, at least in terms of being able to derive value from the card without putting significant spending on it.

Nonetheless, this is a great card with some valuable perks. The $199 annual fee is effectively reduced to ~$99 with the Hyatt credit, and the card is the best option for spending your way to Hyatt elite status — earning one elite night for every $2,000 spent, rather than every $2,500 on the personal card.

The ability to grant Discoverist status to up to five others, access to Hyatt Leverage, and the fact that it doesn’t count toward 5/24 make this a compelling card for Hyatt loyalists. If you don’t yet have the World of Hyatt Business Card and are eligible, I’d highly recommend applying — especially given the current bonus that’s available.
