Are Marriott Bonvoy Airline Mileage Transfers Worth It? They Could Be, But…

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Among hotel loyalty programs, one thing that makes Marriott Bonvoy unique is the ability to (fairly) efficiently convert hotel points into airline miles. While most major hotel loyalty programs allow some sort of opportunity to convert hotel points into airline miles, it typically represents a very bad value.

In this post, I’d like to take a look at the opportunity to convert Marriott points into airline miles. Which airline programs can you move your Marriott points to, what’s the transfer ratio, and is it worth it?

It’s possible to convert Bonvoy points into airline miles, with your choice of nearly 40 different frequent flyer programs. Let’s take a look at which partners Marriott Bonvoy has, what the transfer ratios are, etc.

Marriott points generally convert into airline miles at a 3:1 ratio, and with most partners you get 5,000 bonus miles for every 60,000 Marriott points you transfer.

In other words, 60,000 Bonvoy points will convert into 25,000 airline miles (including the 5,000 bonus miles). You can take advantage of that bonus of 5,000 miles multiple times, meaning that you can convert Bonvoy points into airline miles at a ratio of up to 2.4:1.

Note that you can transfer a minimum of 3,000 Bonvoy points and a maximum of 240,000 Bonvoy points per day. So if you want to transfer more than 240,000 Bonvoy points, you’ll want to do so over multiple days.

With the major transferable points currencies, converting points into airline miles is typically an instant process. So it’s worth being aware that Marriott points don’t convert into airline miles instantly. Rather, transfers generally take anywhere from a day to a couple of weeks, with transfers for most partners taking anywhere from two to five days.

I’d consider this to be a major weak point of this opportunity. I generally recommend converting points into airline miles in situations where you have a specific use in mind, and that could be a situation where you find award space on a particular flight.

Obviously that’s not exactly useful here, since it could take a couple of weeks for the transfer to happen, and odds are that whatever award availability you were looking at will be gone at that point.

The process of converting Marriott points into airline miles is really straightforward. Just go to the partner transfer page, click the “Transfer Points” button, and then you’ll be asked to enter the information for the account you want to move your points to. There you’ll be able to select how many points you want to transfer, and note that the amounts shown won’t reflect a bonus, if eligible.

It’s important to be sure that the names of the accounts match, as you can only transfer your Marriott points to an airline frequent flyer account that’s also in your name. Also keep in mind that once the miles are in your frequent flyer account, they’ll be subject to the standard airline mileage expiration policies of the program.

While I think converting Marriott points into airline miles could represent a good value, it’s not how I choose to redeem my Bonvoy points.

To briefly crunch some numbers, based on my latest miles & points valuations, I consider Bonvoy points to be worth 0.7 cents each. At a 2.4:1 transfer ratio (60,000 points per 25,000 miles), that’s like “paying” 1.68 cents per airline mile, which is a pretty decent cost.

Why do I choose not to convert Marriott points into airline miles?

I absolutely don’t think anyone is off base for choosing to convert Marriott points into airline miles. For that matter, this could be a good option if you’re in a crunch, and just need to top off an account for a high value redemption. However, it’s not how I choose to redeem my points, and I don’t think the value is there for most.

It’s more of a last resort redemption option, if you’re truly unable to get value elsewhere. In fairness, that might just be the direction the program is headed. While Bonvoy points have been significantly devalued over the years for hotel redemptions, the mileage conversion options have remained mostly consistent.

While the ability to convert Marriott points into airline miles has been around for a long time, we’re increasingly seeing two-way transfers, where you can convert airline miles into Marriott points.

The idea behind this is great — maybe someone has a lot of airline miles, but really wants to redeem for a hotel stay. Is this a good deal, though? Unfortunately the answer is almost always “no.”

While I suppose more flexibility is always a good thing, these transfers are almost never worth it. As a reminder, I value Bonvoy points at 0.7 cents each, and you’re getting at most one Bonvoy point per airline mile.

If you have the same valuation I do, at a 1:1 transfer ratio you’re getting up to 0.7 cents per mile, while at a 2:1 transfer ratio, you’re getting up to 0.35 cents per mile. That’s not a rate at which I’d be happy cashing in miles.

It’s possible to convert Marriott points into airline miles with over three dozen programs. While this is the best hotel points to airline miles transfer ratio that you’ll find with any program, it’s still not how I’d choose to redeem my Bonvoy points.

Yes, I appreciate the “insurance” that this redemption option provides, since it essentially creates a floor value for Bonvoy points. However, Bonvoy points are hard to efficiently earn through anything other than hotel stays, so I prefer to save them for hotel stays. I’d rather convert credit card points currencies into airline miles.
