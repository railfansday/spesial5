designboom’s guide to miami art week 2026: what to see in and out of the fairs

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

explore must-see highlights of art basel miami beach, design miami, and other standout shows and events across the city.

Miami Art Week returns from November 30th to December 6th, 2026, bringing a packed program of art fairs, outdoor installations, exhibitions, and unexpected encounters across Miami and Miami Beach. This year, the city becomes a setting for everything from giant mirrored disco balls and interactive robots to surreal theatrical environments and public sculptures built for play. Don’t miss our upcoming Miami Art Week LIVE feed, updated daily with on-the-ground discoveries, highlights, and real-time dispatches from across the city.

Art Basel Miami Beach returns with a reconfigured fair and an expanded digital art program, while Design Miami explores the stories behind collectible objects through its curatorial theme, Unveiling. Beyond the convention center, the Miami Design District welcomes a series of interactive sculptural installations by Objects of Common Interest, and Faena Beach becomes a dance floor beneath a monumental mirrored sphere by Christopher Joshua Benton.

Meanwhile, CJ Hendry invites visitors into a swimming-pool-sized pit of oversized pills, Marcel Dzama brings his dreamlike universe to Lincoln Road, and Miami’s galleries and museums open new exhibitions. From established fairs to temporary interventions along the beachfront, here’s designboom’s guide to what to see across Miami Art Week 2026.

Art Basel Miami Beach returns to the Miami Beach Convention Center with 272 exhibitors from more than 40 countries and territories. For 2026, the fair introduces a reconfigured floor plan with a smaller overall footprint, longer sightlines, and established international galleries distributed throughout the space alongside younger exhibitors.

The fair’s main sectors bring together contemporary work and historic presentations, with Nova focusing on recently created pieces, Positions supporting emerging artists through solo booths, and Survey revisiting overlooked artistic practices. More than 30 galleries will participate for the first time, with a strong presence from Latin America and the Caribbean.

Zero 10, Art Basel’s platform dedicated to art of the digital era, returns with Generation, an exhibition curated by Karen Archey and Eli Scheinman. Featuring 19 projects, the presentation explores how digital technology is changing the ways art is made and experienced, with works by artists including Refik Anadol and Sarah Meyohas.

what: Art Basel Miami Beach when: December 4th — 6th, 2026 (VIP previews December 2–3) where: Miami Beach Convention Center, 1901 Convention Center Drive, Miami Beach, FL 33139

Design Miami returns for its 22nd edition with more than 75 exhibitors bringing together historic and contemporary collectible design. Under the direction of curator Charlene Prempeh, the 2026 fair follows the theme Unveiling, inviting visitors to look more closely at the cultural histories and making processes behind the objects on display.

The program spans established galleries and emerging practices, with furniture, lighting, and sculptural works presented alongside experimental installations. The fair’s Curio section provides a platform for more focused design presentations, while special projects and collaborations extend the program beyond the traditional gallery booth.

As one of the main events of Miami Art Week, Design Miami brings together designers, collectors, and galleries working across different generations of material experimentation and craftsmanship.

what: Design Miami when: December 2nd — 6th, 2026 (invitation-only previews December 1st and 2nd) where: Convention Center Drive and 19th Street, Miami Beach, FL

Untitled Art celebrates its 15th Miami Beach edition with more than 170 galleries and nonprofit organizations representing 36 countries and territories. The fair returns to its beachfront pavilion along Ocean Drive, offering a curated selection of contemporary art with an emphasis on emerging practices and independent galleries.

The 2026 edition features five sectors: Main, Artist Spotlight, Nest, Non-Profit, and Special Projects. Artist Spotlight, curated by Agustina Ferreyra, brings together focused solo presentations, while Nest, curated by Willem Molesworth, explores collaboration and exchange across East Asia and the wider international art scene.

With 53 first-time exhibitors joining the program, the fair continues to create space for younger galleries alongside established international participants.

what: Untitled Art when: December 2nd — 6th, 2026 where: Ocean Drive and 12th Street, Miami Beach, FL

The New Art Dealers Alliance returns to Ice Palace Studios for the 24th edition of NADA Miami, bringing together 135 exhibitors from 63 cities and 27 countries.

Known for its focus on independent galleries and emerging artistic practices, NADA presents work from both established and younger art spaces. Its relatively informal setting offers an opportunity to discover artists working across painting, installation, sculpture, and experimental media, with presentations that often take a more exploratory approach than those found at the larger international fairs.

The event occupies Ice Palace Studios in downtown Miami, with exhibitions and programming extending into its outdoor areas.

what: NADA when: December 1st — 5th, 2026 where: Ice Palace Studios, 1400 North Miami Avenue, Miami, FL 33136

Art Miami returns to its waterfront pavilion at One Herald Plaza with an international selection of modern and contemporary art. One of Miami’s longest-running art fairs, the event brings together established galleries presenting works from the 20th and 21st centuries.

The fair spans painting, sculpture, photography, and installation, with a program that moves between historically significant artists and contemporary practices. Alongside conventional gallery presentations, visitors can expect individual artist projects and interactive installations, including a new felt environment by British artist Lucy Sparrow.

Located in downtown Miami, the fair takes place alongside its sister event CONTEXT Art Miami, allowing visitors to move between the two exhibitions.

what: Art Miami when: December 2nd — 6th, 2026 where: One Herald Plaza, NE 14th Street, Miami, FL 33132

Running alongside Art Miami, CONTEXT Art Miami returns to One Herald Plaza with a focus on emerging and mid-career contemporary artists. The sister fair brings together 75 international galleries presenting new work and curated solo exhibitions.

Established in 2012, CONTEXT offers a platform for galleries working with younger artists and those developing new approaches to familiar media. The fair also features installations and projects created specifically for the event, offering visitors a view of contemporary practices beyond the more established market represented at Art Miami.

what: CONTEXT Art Miami when: December 1st — 6th, 2026 where: CONTEXT Art Miami Pavilion, One Herald Plaza, NE 14th Street, Miami, FL 33132

Amsterdam-based Studio DRIFT brings Murmuring Minds to Art Basel Miami Beach’s Zero 10 sector, presented with the digital art platform TAEX. The installation transforms an arena of autonomous robotic blocks into a responsive environment, with their movement shifting according to the actions of visitors.

Entering the space, one person at a time can influence the robots as they move collectively, follow a lead, or behave according to patterns inspired by wind and natural swarms. The project draws on DRIFT’s long-running research into collective behavior, particularly the coordinated movements of birds and other living systems.

The presentation also includes Coded Nature, an interactive digital series in which algorithmically generated swarms respond to visitors’ gestures. Together, the works examine how individual actions influence the behavior of larger systems.

what: Studio DRIFT, Murmuring Minds and Coded Nature when: December 4th — 6th, 2026 (VIP previews December 2–3) where: Art Basel Miami Beach, Zero 10, Miami Beach Convention Center

At Art Basel’s Zero 10, Sarah Meyohas presents Phase Transition, a new installation of organic, sculptural forms that appear to communicate through movement and sound. Presented by Boesky Gallery, the work brings together magnetized liquid, algorithmic systems, and synthesizers to create a strange conversation among objects.

The forms rest on mesh pedestals or hang from suspended wires. Cutout windows reveal glass tanks filled with ferrofluid, a liquid containing magnetic particles that shift in response to programmed magnets. These movements are translated into sound, allowing each sculpture to participate in an ongoing exchange.

Their rounded, bodily shapes reference biological growth and transformation, while their mechanical conversations raise questions about intelligence and communication beyond human language. The installation extends Meyohas’ interest in the relationship between organic life and emerging technologies.

what: Sarah Meyohas: Phase Transition when: December 4th — 6th, 2026 (VIP previews December 2nd — 3rd) where: Art Basel Miami Beach, Zero 10, Miami Beach Convention Center

During Miami Art Week this December, artist Derrick Adams and the LEGO Group will bring a nine-foot unicorn pool float to Miami Beach. Built from LEGO bricks, the oversized figure will sit at the center of Pool Party, a colorful installation at Avant Gallery inside 1111 Lincoln Road. The project takes Adams’ long-running Floaters series, with its images of Black people at leisure, into an entirely different medium.

On December 3rd and 4th, visitors will have a hand in creating the artwork themselves. Adams and the LEGO team will invite the public to help assemble parts of the installation, ahead of its completed unveiling on December 5th. The two-day community build is part of The Art of Building, a participatory experience organized during Miami Art Week — RSVP here!

what: Derrick Adams x LEGO: The Art of Building when: December 3rd — 4th, 2026 (completed installation reveal December 5th) where: Avant Gallery, 1111 Lincoln Road, Miami Beach, Florida

Japanese artist Tadashi Kawamata brings a site-specific installation to Collins Park in collaboration with Maison Ruinart during Art Basel Miami Beach. Part of the champagne house’s ongoing Conversations with Nature program, the work draws on Kawamata’s observations of Ruinart’s historic vineyards in France.

Known for his temporary architectural constructions made from lightweight materials, Kawamata approaches installation as a process of responding to a place. His Miami commission continues that interest through a large-scale structure that invites visitors to reconsider their relationship with the surrounding environment.

what: Tadashi Kawamata x Maison Ruinart when: December 3rd — 6th, 2026 where: Collins Park, Miami Beach, FL

Dutch designer Iris van Herpen brings her collaboration with Maison Perrier-Jouët to Design Miami, following its presentation in Tokyo and Paris earlier this year. The project grew from her selection as the inaugural recipient of the Perrier-Jouët Design for Nature Award.

Drawing on cymatics, the study of how vibrations produce visible patterns, van Herpen translated signals associated with the living ecosystem of the champagne house’s vineyards into sculptural forms.

The resulting creations include Symphonic Bloom, a one-of-a-kind artwork made from recycled golden metal and silk surrounding a historic champagne bottle, and Symphonic Life, a limited series of sculptural sleeves adorned with gold. The pieces carry van Herpen’s interest in natural structures into the scale of collectible objects.

what: Iris van Herpen x Maison Perrier-Jouët when: December 2nd — 6th, 2026 where: Design Miami, Convention Center Drive and 19th Street, Miami Beach

British artist Lucy Sparrow returns to Art Miami with The Money Laundry, an interactive installation that transforms a familiar laundromat into a shop filled with handmade felt objects.

Among the colorful boxes of detergent and cleaning supplies, visitors will find painted felt banknotes, imitation luxury watches, and passports created by the artist. Sparrow will also exchange $100 in real money for a bundle of felt currency carrying a fictional value of $600.

An on-site photo booth allows guests to have their own felt passports painted live, extending the artist’s ongoing interest in consumer culture and the objects through which people assign value.

what: Lucy Sparrow: The Money Laundry when: December 1st — 6th, 2026 where: Art Miami, Booth AM207, One Herald Plaza, Miami

Artist Christopher Joshua Benton transforms Faena Beach into a temporary dance floor with A Lore Supreme, a monumental kinetic sculpture that reimagines the disco ball as a map of Miami’s cultural history.

The mirrored sphere rises 27 feet above the sand and stretches 44 feet across. Its surface is composed of around 7,000 reflective facets, which move with the wind and scatter images of the surrounding beach.

Partially embedded in the ground, the sculpture becomes both a gathering place and a setting for music and performance.

Drawing on research into Miami’s musical communities, Benton incorporates recorded conversations and sound from local DJs and musicians. The installation traces the influence of African, Caribbean, and Latin American cultures on the city’s music, turning the beach into an open-air space for listening and dancing.

what: Christopher Joshua Benton: A Lore Supreme, commissioned by Faena Art when: December 1st — 6th, 2026, noon-9 p.m. where: Faena Beach, 3201 Collins Avenue, Miami Beach, FL 33140

The Miami Design District’s 11th Annual Design Commission has been awarded to Objects of Common Interest, the New York- and Athens-based studio founded by Eleni Petaloti and Leonidas Trampoukis.

Titled Out of This World: A Field of Conditions, the installation places six abstract sculptures throughout the neighborhood. Some release clouds of vapor into the Miami air, while others rotate when touched or distort their surroundings through reflective surfaces. A marble-and-resin pyramid produces sound that becomes audible only when visitors approach.

Developed through the studio’s ongoing experiments with resin, marble, and movement, the commission extends into Design Miami with a companion installation, Out of This World: A Certain Attraction. There, a concealed sculptural object responds to the presence of visitors, shifting to reveal fragmented reflections of those who enter.

what: Objects of Common Interest, Out of This World: A Field of Conditions when: during Miami Art Week 2026 (exact opening date to be confirmed) where: various locations throughout the Miami Design District, Miami, FL

Artist Marcel Dzama brings his distinctive universe of masked figures and theatrical imagery to Miami Beach with Who Loves the Moon?, an immersive installation presented by Capital One and The Cultivist.

The experience begins with a giant Moon that opens its mouth to welcome visitors inside. From there, guests move through a series of environments incorporating illuminated optical works, puppetry, and imagery drawn from Dzama’s paintings and films.

The journey culminates in a theatrical landscape with checkerboard floors and towering palms. By day, visitors can play chess and explore tarot and astrological readings. Evening programming introduces music and performances by Miami City Ballet, bringing the artist’s imagined world into motion.

what: Marcel Dzama: Who Loves the Moon? when: December 1st — 4th, 2026 (public hours: December 3rd, 10:30a.m. — 4p.m. / December 4th, 10:30a.m. — 6p.m.) where: 434 Lincoln Road, Miami Beach, FL 33139

Australian artist CJ Hendry joins Salon 94 Design to bring PASTEL to the Miami Design District, transforming thousands of oversized pills into an immersive landscape of color.

The installation revisits Hendry’s earlier bead-filled environment, this time replacing the familiar spherical forms with sculptural vitamins, supplements, and prescription pills. Visitors can enter the swimming-pool-sized pit, sift through the colorful objects, and assemble their own playful prescriptions.

Through the oversized forms, Hendry turns familiar products associated with health and self-optimization into objects for interaction and play.

what: CJ Hendry x Salon 94 Design: PASTEL Miami when: November 30th — December 20th, 2026 where: 3925 North Miami Avenue, Miami, FL 33127

At The Ritz-Carlton, South Beach, American artist Rachel Lee Hovnanian presents The Perfect Trade-Off, a solo exhibition exploring how technology has changed the ways people spend time together.

In the hotel’s Art Deco lobby, Dinner for Ten stages an elaborate meal with ten places at the table. Nine chairs are occupied by monitors depicting individuals absorbed in their phones, leaving a single empty seat for visitors. As the feast deteriorates, the work considers what happens to shared rituals when attention turns toward digital devices.

The exhibition extends onto the beachfront with Poor Teddy in Memoriam, a monumental bronze teddy bear surrounded by flowers. Elsewhere, a vintage three-wheeled Piaggio Ape has been transformed into a functioning phone-charging station, bringing Hovnanian’s examination of our relationship with technology into a familiar, everyday encounter.

what: Rachel Lee Hovnanian: The Perfect Trade-Off when: December 1st — 6th, 2026 where: The Ritz-Carlton, South Beach, 1 Lincoln Road, Miami Beach, FL 33139

Argentine artist and designer Andrés Reisinger brings his dreamlike architectural imagery into physical space with Do You Dream In Color?, a property-wide exhibition at Andaz Miami Beach Resort & Spa.

Known for his surreal digital environments and all-pink architectural takeovers, Reisinger will transform parts of the hotel through immersive installations and digital artworks. At the entrance, one of his signature draped interventions will cover the architecture in flowing fabric-like forms, while more digital works appear throughout Bar Centro and the hotel’s screens.

The exhibition is presented with ArtRepublic, and brings Reisinger’s digitally conceived aesthetic into an environment that visitors can experience in-person.

what: Andrés Reisinger: Do You Dream In Color? when: December 1st — 6th, 2026 where: Andaz Miami Beach Resort & Spa, 4041 Collins Avenue, Miami Beach, FL 33140

Greek designer Natalia Triantafylli presents Under the Libra Sun at Villa Paula in Miami’s Little Haiti neighborhood, marking her first major exhibition and largest-scale work to date. Presented by The Future Perfect, the collection takes inspiration from the historic villa’s original tiles and gardens, with pieces that reinterpret Greek and Victorian ornament through bright colors and floral patterns.

Triantafylli hand-sculpts forms in clay before scanning them and combining them with 3D-printed counterparts. The collection includes two monumental porcelain chandeliers, decorative mirrors, column-capital tables and stools, and her first upholstered pieces. Several works are developed with her partner, artist Andrew Pierce Scott, whose blackened steel structures contrast with her richly patterned ceramics. The pieces are bolted together rather than glued to leave their construction visible.

what: Natalia Triantafylli: Under the Libra Sun, presented by The Future Perfect when: opening November 30th, 2026 where: Villa Paula, 5811 North Miami Avenue, Miami, FL 33127

Bosnian artist Radenko Milak presents Algorithms for the Future at Piero Atchugarry Gallery in Miami, his first solo exhibition in the city. Curated by Claire Breukel, the show brings together hyperrealistic black-and-white watercolors that examine how technology shapes our memories of the past and visions of the future. Drawing from archival photography, science fiction, and architecture, Milak combines familiar imagery with imagined scenarios, from Edward Hopper’s solitary figures to futuristic skyscrapers filled with surveillance equipment.

Among the works is White Noise (2026), which merges a warped urban tower with vintage recording devices, and Ghost Game, a series of 23 paintings tracing the history of artificial intelligence through mathematics, cinema, and popular culture. Each image is painted by hand with near-photographic precision, a deliberately slow process that contrasts with the speed of algorithmically generated pictures. The works question what happens when machines begin to determine how history is represented and remembered.

what: Radenko Milak: Algorithms for the Future, curated by Claire Breukel when: November 29th, 2026 — April 18, 2027 where: Piero Atchugarry Gallery, 5520 NE 4th Avenue, Miami, FL 33137

Design Hotels teams up with Play-Doh for Designed to Play, a five-day program at The Shelborne by Proper in Miami Beach during Miami Art Week 2026. At its center is an installation by Copenhagen-based Helle Mardahl Studio, created in collaboration with Hasbro’s new Blooms by Play-Doh collection. The project marks an unusual moment for the familiar modeling compound, which has introduced its first line developed specifically for adults in the brand’s 70-year history.

Throughout the week, visitors can experiment with the colorful material in hands-on workshops and create their own sculptural bouquets. The hotel will also host morning conversations on creativity and design, while New York’s Pawn Chess Club brings custom chessboards and open games to the space. The program takes a playful approach to the usual Miami Art Week schedule, with opportunities to make something, try a new game, or simply spend an afternoon experimenting.

what: Design Hotels x Play-Doh, Designed to Play, featuring Helle Mardahl Studio when: December 2nd — 6th, 2026 where: The Shelborne by Proper, 1801 Collins Avenue, Miami Beach, FL 33139

Miami’s Wynwood Arts District welcomes Grotto Baths on December 1, a 7,500-square-foot social bathhouse that brings contemporary art into the centuries-old tradition of communal bathing. Designed by Antoinette Marie Johnson in collaboration with Stokes Architecture + Design, the interiors draw from Brazilian modernism, with curved archways, geometric tile patterns, and bursts of orange and blue against natural stone and travertine. Visitors move between thermal pools and cold plunges, while a 30-person cedar sauna hosts guided rituals accompanied by aromatic steam.

Art plays a central role in the new space. Miami-based artist Emmett Moore has created site-specific sculptures and custom benches using reclaimed materials inspired by South Florida’s limestone and mid-century breeze-block architecture. Works by Papou appear in the lobby, and an exterior mural by Mandy Ann takes inspiration from water. The phone-free bathhouse also includes a garden café, with special food and drinks inspired by Moore’s installations planned for Miami Art Week.

what: Grotto Baths, featuring site-specific installations by Emmett Moore when: Opens December 1st, 2026, for members (day-pass access begins December 7th) where: Grotto Baths, 325 NW 28th Street, Miami, FL 33127

At Nobu Hotel Miami Beach, Japanese artist and ceramic conservator Naoko Fukumaru presents a collection of works exploring the centuries-old practice of kintsugi, the repair of broken ceramics using lacquer and gold.

Presented in collaboration with The Morikami Museum and Japanese Gardens, the lobby exhibition brings together ceramic objects that have been broken, discarded, or altered through firing. Fukumaru reconstructs their forms using natural urushi lacquer and 23.5-karat gold, leaving visible lines that trace the histories of the objects.

With more than three decades of experience in ceramic conservation, the Kyoto-born artist approaches repair as an opportunity to give damaged objects a new visual identity.

what: Naoko Fukumaru: The Kintsugi Art of Naoko Fukumaru when: November 25th — December 7th, 2026 where: Nobu Hotel Miami Beach, 4525 Collins Avenue, Miami Beach, FL 33140

Miami’s Little River neighborhood welcomes a new cultural space with the opening of Mitrani Art, a multidisciplinary center dedicated to photography, video art, and film.

Founded by sisters Dina and Rhonda Mitrani and designed by R&R Studios, the project transforms a former mechanic shop into galleries and artist studios, with a screening room and outdoor courtyard. The opening marks a new chapter for the sisters, who previously operated the Mitrani Warehouse in Wynwood.

The inaugural exhibition, ReExposures: The Magic Within, brings together artists who have contributed to their programs over the years. Works range from large-scale photographic experiments to video installations, including a monumental photogram by Roberto Huarcaya and Antonia Wright’s video Suddenly We Jumped, in which the artist breaks through panes of glass.

what: Mitrani Art, ReExposures: The Magic Within when: Opening November 30th, 2026 (6-9 p.m. reception) where: Mitrani Art, 7325 North Miami Avenue, Miami, FL 33150

The Bass in Miami Beach presents a season of exhibitions that bring visitors into direct contact with the works on view, from interactive environments to sculptural installations.

In the museum’s Rotunda, Argentine artist Amalia Pica presents Lecturas Posibles, an almost two-dimensional library where surfaces become chalkboards. Visitors are invited to write and draw across the installation, creating an evolving collection of marks and messages.

Elsewhere, Japanese artist Yuko Mohri’s Unviolated Imperfection explores everyday objects through unpredictable mechanical systems, while architecture and design duo Pedro y Juana transform an interior with patterned surfaces and custom furniture for Aquí. Their richly colored environment draws on Miami’s subtropical setting, inviting visitors to spend time within the installation.

what: The Bass 2026-2027 exhibition season, featuring Amalia Pica, Yuko Mohri, and Pedro y Juana when: exhibitions on view throughout Miami Art Week 2026 where: Bass Museum of Art, 2100 Collins Avenue, Miami Beach, FL 33139

LVMH The Studio Miami returns for its sixth edition, bringing a five-day program of contemporary art and creative workshops to the Miami Design District.

The pop-up gallery presents works by ten artists working across painting, sculpture, textiles, photography, and digital media. Participating artists include Cavier Coleman, Glenneisha Darkins, Jay Chung, and Kristen White, alongside others exploring identity and cultural history through different materials.

Alongside the exhibition, visitors can attend public conversations and craftsmanship demonstrations organized with artisans and creative partners from Louis Vuitton, Tiffany & Co., and TAG Heuer.

what: LVMH The Studio Miami 2026 when: December 2nd — 6th, 2026, 11 a.m.-4 p.m. where: Palm Court Event Space, 140 NE 39th Street, 3rd Floor, Miami, FL 33137

The Craig Robins Collection unveils its 2026-2027 rehang, Real Shadows, featuring more than 300 works of modern and contemporary art and design.

Presented at Dacra’s headquarters in the Miami Design District, the exhibition brings together pieces by John Baldessari, Charles Ray, and Marlene Dumas, alongside recent acquisitions by artists including Alex Da Corte and Lauren Halsey.

Taking its title from Baldessari’s Tetrad Series (1999), the exhibition explores the persistence of images through painting and sculpture. The presentation also incorporates significant design objects by Zaha Hadid, Jean Prouvé, and Ron Arad, reflecting the collection’s long-standing relationship between art and furniture.

what: Craig Robins Collection: Real Shadows when: 2026-2027; Wednesdays-Fridays, noon-4 p.m., by appointment where: Dacra Headquarters, 3841 NE 2nd Avenue, Miami, FL 33137

El Espacio 23 in Miami’s Allapattah neighborhood presents Nothing Is Linear: Relations Across Latin America and Its Diasporas, a new exhibition drawn from the Jorge M. Pérez Collection. Bringing together more than 130 works by 120 artists, the exhibition examines the region’s shifting cultural identities through migration and shared histories. Curated through a collaboration between Anelys Alvarez, Patricia Hanna, Taiyana Pimentel, and Patrick Charpenel, the presentation moves between figurative art, political expression, and abstraction.

Among the works is José Carlos Martinat’s kinetic sculpture Manifestos, whose moving pendulums and speakers broadcast historical art manifestos in their original languages. Elsewhere, Hernan Bas revisits Miami’s brief snowfall in 1977, while works by Julio Le Parc and Adrián Villar Rojas explore different approaches to visual language. Across four interconnected sections, the exhibition considers Latin America as a network of relationships that extends far beyond geographical borders.

what: Nothing Is Linear: Relations Across Latin America and Its Diasporas when: November 19th, 2026 — August 14th, 2027 where: El Espacio 23, 2270 NW 23rd Street, Miami, FL 33142

Jeffrey Deitch presents Ensalada Mixta, an exhibition of work by Swiss artist Urs Fischer in the Miami Design District.

The presentation explores Fischer’s approach to transforming familiar objects through unexpected materials and changes in scale. Working across sculpture and design, the artist draws on industrial processes and common domestic forms, using materials including steel, epoxy, and electrical components.

The exhibition continues Fischer’s long-running interest in the relationship between art objects and the everyday things that surround them, bringing his playful visual language into a temporary gallery setting during Miami Art Week.

what: Urs Fischer: Ensalada Mixta, presented by Jeffrey Deitch when: November 30th — December 23rd, 2026 where: 45 NE 39th Street, Miami, FL 33137

At the Institute of Contemporary Art, Miami, Basquiat. A Hidden World offers a new perspective on Jean-Michel Basquiat’s paintings through his little-known use of ultraviolet-reactive materials.

Curated by Dieter Buchhart and Anna Karina Hofbauer, the exhibition brings together four works, including Untitled (Orange Sports Figure) (1982), Emblem (1984), Cash Crop (1984), and O.M.R.A.V.S. (1984).

Under ultraviolet light, words and markings hidden within the surfaces become visible. In Emblem, the repeated phrase ‘invisible men’ appears beneath the familiar composition, revealing an additional layer of Basquiat’s visual language.

what: Basquiat: A Hidden World when: November 29th, 2026 — February 12th, 2027 where: ICA Miami Expansion Space, 23 NE 41st Street, Miami, FL 33137

Indian artist Subodh Kerkar brings The Ocean Odyssey to Miami Beach as a free public performance presented by REEFLINE in collaboration with Andaz Miami Beach Resort & Spa.

The site-specific project brings together more than 50 community participants and dancers from Miami City Ballet, with choreography developed in collaboration with Durante Verzola.

Taking place directly on the beachfront, the performance uses movement to explore the relationship between people and marine environments. The ocean becomes the setting for a temporary gathering that connects contemporary art with Miami’s coastal landscape.

what: Subodh Kerkar: The Ocean Odyssey, presented by REEFLINE when: December 1st, 2026, 4:30-6 p.m. where: Andaz Miami Beach Resort & Spa beachfront, 4041 Collins Avenue, Miami Beach, FL 33140
