Nirvana Fans Raise $94K for Photographer Who Shot ‘Bleach’ Album Cover as She Battles Cancer

Nirvana fans have raised over $94,000 for the photographer who shot the cover of the band’s debut album Bleach, to cover her medical expenses as she enters hospice care.

Tracy Marander, the photographer behind the cover of Bleach and Kurt Cobain’s former girlfriend, has been battling cancer for four years. Last week, Marander’s family announced that the photographer was suspending her cancer treatment and entering hospice care.

Marander’s family launched a GoFundMe campaign to help cover her medical expenses, which has raised $94,101 toward its $100,000 goal at the time of writing. Hundreds of Nirvana fans have accompanied their donations with tributes to the photographer and her contribution to the band’s legacy.

Cobain’s widow Courtney Love also urged her followers to donate to the GoFundMe campaign in support of Marander in an Instagram post, calling the photographer “the unsung hero of Nirvana” who believed in the band’s lead singer “before he believed in himself.” In her post, Love describes the American medical system as “broken” and called on people to give what they could, adding “P.S. Kurt owed Tracy a lot of rent.”

A post shared by Courtney Love Cobain (@courtneylove)

Released in June 1989, Bleach was the debut studio album by Nirvana. Marander shot the iconic photograph that ended up becoming the album cover art during a Nirvana concert at Reko/Muse art gallery in Olympia, Washington, on April 1, 1989. Marander’s image shows the band performing live, with Cobain’s hair falling across his face, alongside guitarist Jason Everman, who did not play on the album despite appearing on its cover.

Lisa Orth, a graphic designer at the local music newspaper The Rocket, inverted the light and dark tones of Marander’s photograph to give it the appearance of a film negative. According to Nirvana: Taking Punk to the Masses by Jacob McMurray, Orth designed the album cover in keeping with the signature aesthetic of the band’s record label Sub Pop, which favored plain backgrounds, bold lettering, and striking photography. The cover also gave rise to Nirvana’s now-famous logo almost by accident, when Orth asked The Rocket’s typesetter Grant Alden to set the band’s name in whatever typeface was already installed on the typesetting machine.

Although Bleach received positive reviews from critics, it failed to enter the charts when it was first released. However, the album became popular when it was internationally reissued in 1992 following the commercial success of Nirvana’s second album Nevermind, released in 1991.

According to Nirvana biographer Michael Azerrad, Marander was Cobain’s first serious romantic partner and inspired him to write the song About a Girl. Their relationship lasted around three years before Cobain ended it in 1990. Marander recalled how Cobain encouraged her to develop her photographic talents, but the demands of working full-time and financially supporting him left her with little time to pursue her own creative interests.

“Near the end, we started fighting more and more,” Marander tells Azerrad in his 1993 book Come as You Are, according to reports. “He wanted me to be artistic, and I didn’t have time to be artistic. I was driving an hour to work and an hour back from work. I was supporting him.”
