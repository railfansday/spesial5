How to Correctly Add Forward and Reverse Lunges into Your Legday Workouts

When it comes to strengthening your legs, building muscles in your lower body, and improving all-round balance, lunges are a great move to lean into, but what is the difference between the forward and backward lunge? In a recent Instagram post, IFBB Pro, endurance athlete, and transformation coach Petar Klancir demonstrated the details.

Take a step in the right direction with these unique lunges.

“What is the difference?” asked The Croatian Colossus, who has built an Instagram base of more than one million followers thanks to his informative training videos. In essence, both of these variations are compound movements, so they tax multiple muscle groups at the same time. Whether you lunge forward or backward, you’ll be working the quads, hamstrings, calves and core. You will also flex the hips either way. But what does change is the emphasis placed on each muscle, and how they build your legs in different ways, as Klancir showed.

“Front step lunges target the quads more, due to the mechanics of the exercise,” explained Klancir. “The emphasis is on stepping forward, which requires the quads to control the impact of the foot’s landing. And there is a high degree of flexion.”

The quads take the majority of the load with forward lunges, because the mechanics that Klancir mentioned, require the quads to negotiate the lowering, and then lift your center of mass and return you to the starting position.

Tip: To avoid knee stress, be sure not to step out too far forward on the lunge. At the bottom of the lunge, your knee should only just be ahead of your chin.

“Back step lunges target the glutes and hamstrings,” said Klancir. “Due to emphasized hip-hinge movement.”

Reverse lunges are often considered easier to execute, because the front foot remains flat on the floor, providing a greater foundation and less requirement for core balance. They are also less stressful on the knee, because the load is shifted by the hamstrings and glutes to a greater extent than the quads.

Tip: Keep the front foot stable by keeping knee over ankle at all times.

For a complete leg blaster, your can try both movements by focusing on form. Then, once you are comfortable with your foot placings, you can build in some additional hypertrophy by adding a pair of dumbbells to the overall load.
