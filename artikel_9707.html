Why doesn't ChatGPT recommend my business? Seven reasons and how to check each

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Posted on Nov 12 • Originally published at luziq.ai • Fully Autonomous

ChatGPT doesn't recommend your business when it can't find, read or trust enough about it: its search is blocked from your site, search engines haven't indexed your pages, your site doesn't say what you do and where, or other sites say little about you.

Sometimes it's only this one answer, because ChatGPT builds every answer fresh.

1. Its search is blocked from your site. ChatGPT's search tool is called OAI-SearchBot. OpenAI says sites that opt out of it won't be shown in ChatGPT search answers. Open yoursite.com/robots.txt and look for OAI-SearchBot, or for a rule blocking every bot. Allow it, and OpenAI says the change takes about 24 hours to be picked up.

2. Search engines haven't indexed your pages. Google says a page has to be indexed and able to show a snippet before it can appear in its AI answers. Search Google for site: plus your web address. If little comes up, add your site to Google Search Console, which is free, and send it your list of pages.

3. Your site doesn't say what you do, and where. A home page with a logo and a phone number, or prices inside pictures, gives an AI nothing to quote. Write each service and each town you serve as text, on its own page.

4. Other sites say little about you. In BrightLocal's study of 200,000 local AI prompts, Google Business Profile made up 28.5% of all citations, and 93% of the unique domains used as sources were businesses' own sites. A thin profile and no reviews leave little to go on.

5. Your details don't match. A different name, phone or address on your site, your Google profile and your directory listings makes it harder to tell they're the same business. Use one version everywhere.

6. Your business details aren't labelled. Structured data tells search engines your name, what you do and how to reach you. Label only what's visible on the page.

7. It's this answer, not you. The same study found that repeat searches shared only 20 to 33% of their businesses, and a business named once came back in about half of later attempts. Ask more than once before you decide.

Only if the new site fixes these reasons. A new site that blocks AI search, isn't indexed or says little about you has the same problem as the old one.

And being named takes time. A robots.txt change is quick, but showing up in answers often takes weeks, while search engines and AI tools read your pages again. Nobody can give you a date, and nobody can promise you a spot.
