Dirty Spaghetti

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Recipe by Susan Bronson, Rhinelander, Wisconsin

Dirty rice has met its noodle match with our Louisiana-style dirty spaghetti.

Let the good times roll with this Louisiana-inspired dirty spaghetti dish! It’s a riff on Cajun-Creole dirty rice that transforms the original into an easy one-pot pasta.

The key it its flavor is Cajun seasoning, a blend cayenne pepper, garlic powder, black pepper, paprika and thyme. And herein lies an important cooking tip: When using store-bought seasoning blends like this one, give them a little taste first before adding anything to the pot. Different brands rely on different ratios of ingredients, and a tablespoon of one product could be much milder (or pack way more heat!) than a tablespoon of another. Your personal preferences matter, too. So whether you are sensitive to spice or like to pack on the heat, make sure you’re adding the proper amount of spice for your taste.

Dirty spaghetti is a riff on dirty rice, a classic Cajun-Creole side dish. Traditional dirty rice is made with chopped organ meats such as chicken livers or gizzards, which, along with the herbs and seasonings, color the white rice and earn it its “dirty” moniker. This dish, however, has a meaty base that’s made with ground beef and diced andouille sausage. Like the original, it also features the “holy trinity” of Cajun and Creole cooking: chopped onion, bell pepper and celery. A healthy dose of Cajun seasoning ensures each bite is packed with bold flavor.

Heat the oil in a large Dutch oven or large high-sided skillet over medium-high heat. Add the ground beef and cook it until it’s browned, four to six minutes, using a wooden spoon or spatula to break it into small crumbles.

Editor’s Tip: If you use a skillet, make sure it has a lid.

Add the chopped andouille sausage, onion, celery, red pepper and green pepper. Cook them until the vegetables are soft, about three to four minutes. Add the garlic and cook it until it’s fragrant, 30 to 60 seconds. Stir in the tomato paste and Cajun seasoning, and cook for one to two more minutes. Add 3 cups of broth and bring the mixture to a simmer.

Add the broken spaghetti to the pot, ensuring that the noodles are submerged in the liquid and separated as much as possible. Add more liquid if needed. Bring the mixture to a boil, then reduce the heat to medium-low. Cover the pot and simmer the pasta for six minutes.

Remove the lid and continue simmering until the spaghetti is al dente, stirring every two to three minutes. Add more broth as needed. Remove the pot from the heat, and season the pasta to taste with salt and pepper.

Leftover dirty spaghetti can be stored in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to four days.

The best way to reheat a pasta dish like this one is either in the oven or on the stovetop. To reheat leftover dirty spaghetti in the oven, transfer it to an oven-safe baking dish, cover it tightly with foil, and cook it in an oven preheated to 350 degrees for about 20 minutes or until it’s heated through. To reheat it on the stovetop, heat a saucepan over medium heat and add a drizzle of olive oil. Add the leftover pasta and sauce and saute them for about two to three minutes or until they’re hot.

Yes, you can freeze dirty spaghetti. After allowing it to cool completely, transfer leftovers to a freezer-safe container and store them in the freezer for up to three months. Allow the dirty spaghetti to thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating it.

The best pan for making this one-pot pasta dish is a Dutch oven or a large, high-walled skillet or pot with a tight-fitting lid.

First, be sure to give your Cajun seasoning a little taste first because seasoning blends like this one can vary widely in spiciness. Depending on how spicy your Cajun seasoning is, add more or less to taste. You could also make a homemade Cajun seasoning blend and customize the amount of cayenne pepper and chili powder to your personal taste. The andouille sausage also adds quite a bit of spice; for less heat, skip it entirely, or swap in sweet Italian sausage, breakfast sausage, chicken sausage or a milder smoked sausage like kielbasa.

You’ll definitely want to pair this dirty spaghetti with crusty French bread, garlic bread or cornbread to sop up any extra sauce. Southern-inspired side dishes to consider include southern green beans, collard greens or black-eyed pea tomato salad. Or, complement the pasta dish’s spicy flavor with a fresh and crisp green salad.
