This Three-Day Wedding Brought Persian Traditions, Dramatic Design, and an Ibiza-Style Rave to Oaxaca

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Brian Downs and Sarvenaz Shahbazi had a classic college library meet-cute. One day, while they were both at Columbia Business School, Brian was tutoring their mutual friend—and future wedding officiant—Alex in a study room. Sarvenaz, who goes by Sarvi, walked by and jumped right in, taking the lead in guiding everyone through accounting problems. “I remember immediately thinking how pretty she was, but also how smart and confident she was,” says Brian. The two became friends, and by the time they were back from winter break, Brian asked Sarvi out for drinks at The Commerce Inn on January 7, 2022. Says Sarvi, “I had mentioned to him in passing that I had been wanting to go a month or two earlier since it had just opened, so I was very impressed with his thoughtfulness and planning!” The two were inseparable from that point on.

The couple got engaged two-and-a-half years later, over July 4th weekend on a trip to Puglia. Sarvi’s parents had planned a week-long vacation in the region, and Sarvi and her brother decided to join last minute with their partners. Brian, who was already on an engagement ring search, expedited the process to have it ready for the trip. “At the time, I genuinely did not think an engagement was on the table for at least a few more months,” says Sarvi. “We had only recently started talking about it, and Brian had very strategically staged our first-ever ring shopping trip the week before we left, which I then learned was just a decoy.”

Sarvi’s family, who were in on the plan, told everyone to meet for sunset drinks at the beach club of the masseria where they were staying. When the couple arrived, one of Sarvi’s favorite songs was playing, and a single table was set with flowers and Champagne with views of the water. “I looked at the setup and immediately said to Brian, ‘Oh my God, I think someone is going to get engaged here. We’re going to see it,’” Sarvi says. When they walked towards the deck, Brian pulled out the ring and got down on one knee. “She was in utter shock,” recalls Brian. “I had thought a lot about what I was going to say, but the only thing I actually remember saying is ‘eshgham,’ which means ‘my love’ in Farsi.”

When choosing a destination to tie the knot, Brian and Sarvi found themselves constantly drifting back to Mexico, ultimately deciding to exchange vows in Oaxaca City on November 15, 2025. The couple went on their first trip together to the country and had each visited countless times before and after they started dating.

The bride took charge to bring their dream wedding to life. “I work in hospitality as the director of strategy and development at Catch Hospitality Group, so what I truly find most fulfilling in life is creating experiences for others centered around exceptional food and drink and a carefully curated ambiance,” she says. Alongside planners Gutierrez F Studio, Sarvi helped orchestrate three days of events that blended this ethos with traditions from her Persian culture. The weekend would kick off with a rehearsal dinner full of delicious food and speeches at Criollo, followed by a Persian wedding ceremony and party at the historic Proyecto Murguía, and finally, the big day at Loma Noble. “We had so much fun creating a completely different world, each with a distinct personality, for every part of the wedding day—leading our guests on a journey from a ceremony in a bucolic agave field, to an energetic and dramatic dinnerscape, all the way to an after-party rave in a cactus garden,” says the bride.

While Sarvi had clear ideas of how to bring together all the events, it took some work to come up with her wedding wardrobe. “I wasn’t a bride with a pre-constructed Pinterest board, so when I first started thinking about what I would wear, I was completely overwhelmed by the sheer number of options,” she says. “I never wanted to wear anything that felt stiff, stuffy, or overly traditional.” This drive to find something unique led her to a pale butter-yellow dress with a sheer cape from Harithand for her rehearsal dinner, which she paired with diamond and ruby flower earrings from her aunt. “I felt immediately comfortable in it—it was soft, ethereal and subtly sexy—and felt perfect for our dinner under the stars at Criollo,” she says.

Her ceremony dress search ultimately led her to two iconic bridal designers: Danielle Frankel and Vera Wang. When Sarvi first saw Wang’s Ariel dress, it reminded her of Van Gogh’s Starry Night. “It ultimately felt perfect for our Persian ceremony,” she says. “There is so much artistry, symbolism, and craftsmanship that goes into an ‘aghd’ and the ‘sofreh’—the traditional ceremonial table—with countless individual symbolic items and details coming together to create something incredibly beautiful. There was a similar sense of artistry and romance in the Vera Wang gown that felt especially fitting for that night.” She paired the look with pink sapphire and diamond flower drop earrings from her maternal grandmother, who recently passed.

For the western wedding day, Sarvi wore a strapless gown by Frankel. While she wasn’t planning to try it on before she entered the showroom, the style called to her. “I loved the contrasting shades of white, the different textures, and the subtle lace throughout the skirt, which gave the dress so much dimension and movement,” she says. Once again, the bride wore a jewelry piece from her grandmother: a pearl choker with a gold flower and a tiny ruby. “They were playful, distinctive, and deeply personal, with those unexpected touches of color that I always love,” she says.

After dinner, the bride changed into a custom look she made with Brooklyn-based designer Delfina Balda. The two-piece look featured a mix of textures and fabrics with varying degrees of sheerness. “It felt like the perfect party look—artful and completely unique, but something I could actually dance in,” she says. For the after-party, which was modeled after DC10 in Ibiza with a completely red lighting scheme, Sarvi decided to change out of bridal white and donned a red dress by The Attico. “It has this intricate soutache detailing and a fringe skirt that gives the dress incredible texture and movement, especially on the dance floor,” she says.

The groom wore a mix of vintage and bespoke looks throughout the wedding weekend. For Friday night, Brian donned a relaxed suit found at Ebreo Vintage in Williamsburg that fit him perfectly. “Even the owner basically said, ‘Honestly, you should just buy that.’ I got pretty lucky,” he says. His next two looks were made alongside Tyler Sequeira at P. Johnson. The groom wanted to wear a double-breasted chocolate brown suit for the Persian ceremony to complement the natural tones throughout the celebration. “On my second visit, when I was picking fabrics and locking everything in, Sarvi’s uncle Mohammad, or Momo, insisted on joining me,” says Brian. “It’s a tradition in their family to buy a new suit for the groom joining the family, so he ended up helping me design the suit for the aghd. I loved that the suit became something more meaningful than just something I had picked out for myself.” For the black-tie wedding day, Brian wore a bespoke tuxedo made in midnight blue.

The wedding weekend began at Criollo, for an open-air dinner filled with mezcalitas, tacos, and chickens roaming freely through the space. “It immediately gave everyone a sense of the place we had fallen in love with,” says Sarvi. The night full of speeches ended with a night of dancing at a local vinyl bar. “It was one of the first moments where I looked around and really processed that all of these people we loved had traveled to Oaxaca to be there for us,” says Brian.

On the Persian wedding day, the couple paraded with their guests through the streets to their venue, where the space was filled with florals, Persian food, music, and a sofreh table ready for the ceremony. The bride’s uncle officiated the aghd and her mother and other family members participated throughout. “By the time the ceremony ended, the night very quickly became a full-on Persian dance party,” says Sarvi. “We had hoped Friday would have just as much energy and dancing as Saturday, but even we underestimated it.” Adds Brian, “Seeing my parents and friends dancing to Persian music alongside Sarvi’s family is one of the funniest and most joyous memories I’ll have from the weekend.”

The ceremony the next day was held at sunset among the agave fields of Loma Noble, an agronomic project outside the city of Oaxaca. “We wanted the ceremony to feel as though it belonged within the landscape rather than creating something that completely competed with it,” says the bride. Sarvi walked down the aisle to a string rendition of “Creep” by Radiohead. “Certainly not the most traditional wedding choice, but one of my favorite songs of all time and, to me, one of the most beautiful songs ever written,” she says. The couple’s business school friend Alex officiated, weaving in sentimental passages from Brian’s late grandfather Jack, and the groom’s mother shared a reading. The couple wrote their own vows, including an entire section Brian prepared in Farsi. “I learned afterward that my younger cousin had secretly been tutoring him so he could learn and practice that portion,” says Sarvi. “It was one of the most deeply sentimental gestures I could have imagined.”

The couple share they were both emotional, yet calm and present for the ceremony. The two recessed to “California Dreamin’,” which they sang with joy down the aisle. “It was this perfect release of all the emotion we had been feeling, and immediately shifted the energy from the ceremony into pure excitement for the night ahead,” says the bride. The two spent a moment alone together in the bridal suite before joining guests at cocktail hour along a tree-lined pathway.

When guests moved into the reception tent, they found the space decorated with a nod to an eclipse, complete with a glowing orb above a circular central stage and serpentine tables along the room. Dinner was punctuated with speeches, assigned “table captains” to keep the energy alive, and dances with their parents. The newlyweds shared their own first dance at the end of dinner to “Sunny” by Bobby Hebb. “As soon as it ended, Brian and I grabbed the microphones and essentially announced, ‘Now, it’s time to party.’ At that exact moment, trays of carajillos came out for everyone, giving the room one more jolt of energy before the curtains separating the dinner space from the dance floor opened,” says the bride. Their 300 guests moved to the disco-ball-filled space complete with a martini bar and music by DJ César.

“At one point, Brian and I ended up dancing on top of the bar and apparently were dancing hard enough that the staff became concerned it might come crashing down—so the bartenders were literally holding it up beneath us while we were dancing,” says Sarvi. “That probably sums up the energy of the night better than anything else.” The party didn’t stop as guests next moved through a glowing red tunnel. “Once you emerged into the cactus garden, everything was washed in red light, with an enormous triangular LED structure framing the dance floor,” says the bride. “It felt almost otherworldly and created exactly the kind of late-night atmosphere we wanted.” The couple danced until 6 a.m.

Looking back, the couple describe it as the most magical weekend of their lives. “I constantly find myself wondering what I did to end up married to someone so cool and so loved by her friends and family,” says Brian. “She draws people into her orbit, and getting to stand next to her while all of those people showed up to celebrate us is something I’ll never forget.”
