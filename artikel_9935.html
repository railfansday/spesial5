As such, we made the difficult decision to begin the process of transitioning all of our repositories to the GNU General Public License version 2 or later. Could somebody please explain in what ways that harms the project? Sufficiently severely to justify changing the licence?

The linked report says: We do all of this because we firmly believe in Free and Open Source Software, not just as a means to an end but as the end in and of itself.

It is exactly the problem of free-riding: if the number of people that do not pay the ticket is sufficiently small then they do not harm you and in general all the people that pay their ticket since the loss is already compensated by earnings. However, without any punishment or limitation it would bring more and more people to stop contributing and instead free-riding the project. At the end less and less people are willing to contribute to the project, and instead monetize on someone's else work. Like training a commercial LLM.

I realize this is 2026 and the era of clickbait "stories" on antisocial media, of the death of numbers and of any sort of evidence, but I naively assumed LWN was one of the last exceptions... There are many projects with BSD/MIT licences that are successful and alive and some of them (like LLVM) have even significantly eroded their GPL competition. So as usual, I suspect one size does not fit all - unless you're after antisocial media clicks of course. Are LLMs affecting that? Maybe but it seems early to tell. So again: a little bit of evidence would make a licence rant look less empty - keeping in mind every project is different and lessons learned by Asahi do not automatically apply everywhere.
