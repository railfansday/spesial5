Bitcoin Falls Below $76,000 as Chip Stocks Crash and Liquidations Hit $1 Billion: Why Is Crypto Down Today?

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Why is crypto down today? A chip-stock crash sent Bitcoin below $76K, wiped out $1B in longs and pulled $430M from Bitcoin ETFs.

Short answer to “why is crypto down today?” Bitcoin fell 6% to below $76,000 after a Wall Street chip-stock crash on Thursday, November 5, wiped out $1.03 billion in leveraged bets and pulled $430 million out of Bitcoin ETFs. The selling hasn’t stopped overnight.

Bitcoin traded at about $75,700 on Binance on Friday morning, down from $80,547 at the start of Thursday. Ethereum lost 8.2% to $2,218, XRP fell 9.7% to $1.18 and Solana dropped 6.9% to $89.22. The Crypto Fear & Greed Index fell to 28, in “Fear”, from a neutral 50 a day earlier.

The trigger came from stocks. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index closed down 12.2%, its members Nvidia and AMD lost 9.3% and 17.3%, and the Nasdaq fell 5.5%. Crypto stocks went with them: Strategy dropped 9.3% and Coinbase 9.7%.

So what turned a tech sell-off into a crypto crash, and where can Bitcoin find a floor?

Today was a 4σ day for equities. The S&P 500 fell 4.4% and the Nasdaq fell 5.5%, a decline seen roughly once every 19 months. Bitcoin fell about 5%. $STRC closed at $99.56, just 44 cents below par.

Wall Street opened at 14:30 UTC, and Bitcoin broke within the hour. The 15:00 UTC candle on Binance fell from $78,288 to $75,568, the day’s busiest hour with $513 million traded.

Yahoo Finance’s market coverage linked the stock rout to the Democrats’ clear midterm win and what it could mean for AI infrastructure spending and its regulation. Chipmakers, the biggest winners of the AI trade, took the hardest hit.

Leverage did the rest. CoinGlass counted $1.03 billion in liquidations over 24 hours, positions closed by force when margin ran out. Longs made up $924.94 million of that, or 89.4%, across 199,365 traders.

Bitcoin longs lost $255.88 million and Ether longs $163.83 million. The biggest single wipeout was a $10.94 million ETHUSDT position on Binance.

US spot Bitcoin ETFs saw $430.2 million in net outflows on Thursday, and not one of the 12 funds took in money. SoSoValue’s ETF tracker shows Fidelity’s FBTC led with $199.87 million out, ahead of BlackRock’s IBIT at $104.97 million.

Grayscale’s GBTC lost $49.90 million, Franklin Templeton’s EZBC $32.92 million, VanEck’s HODL $23.64 million and ARKB $18.90 million.

It undid Wednesday in one session. Funds had added $74.6 million on November 4, the only inflow day so far this month. November’s first four trading days now add up to $869.8 million in outflows, on top of $515.9 million that left in October.

Fidelity’s turn is the detail to watch. FBTC took in $236.7 million on Wednesday while IBIT bled, then gave back most of it a day later.

Bitcoin closed Thursday at $76,088, just under its 200-day EMA at $76,536. That average had held as support through the October pullback.

The 50-day EMA at $79,822 now sits above the price, and the $81,971 high from before last week’s Binance hack is the next resistance beyond it.

On the downside, the $74,968 swing low is the first support. Below it, the 50% Fibonacci retracement of the July-to-September rally sits at $72,598.

One caveat: funding rates on Bitcoin perpetuals are still slightly positive on OKX, so longs haven’t fully cleared out even after a billion-dollar flush.

For anyone still asking “why is crypto down today?”, the answer runs through Nasdaq. If chip stocks steady when New York opens on Friday, Bitcoin has a shot at reclaiming its 200-day average. If they don’t, $74,968 is the line between a bad day and a deeper correction.
