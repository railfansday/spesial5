Bitcoin rebounds toward $57,500 after a massive $2.55 billion crypto liquidation wave.

A break below the $54,522 support could put Bitcoin’s $49,011 August 2024 low at risk.

Bitcoin open interest fell to $19.73 billion as leveraged positions were rapidly cleared.

Bitcoin has bounced back toward $57,500 after crashing below $55,500, but the sell-off is far from settled. More than $2.55 billion in crypto positions were wiped out in 24 hours, with Bitcoin accounting for $1.15 billion, leaving BTC trapped between heavy liquidation zones.Now, traders are wondering: how low can the Bitcoin price go if liquidation continues to grow?

According to the latest CoinGlass data, 361,207 traders were liquidated in 24 hours, with long positions accounting for nearly 89.4% of the $2.55 billion total. Bitcoin alone recorded about $1.15 billion in liquidations, while Ethereum followed with $582.38 million and Solana with nearly $67 million.

The damage is also visible in the derivatives market.

Bitcoin open interest has fallen to around $19.73 billion, while the long/short ratio has dropped to 0.8276. As price fell, leveraged positions were cleared rapidly, leaving fewer traders willing to keep betting on an immediate recovery.

That concern is now spreading beyond derivatives traders. Robert Kiyosaki, author of Rich Dad Poor Dad, added to the warnings with a blunt post on X,

He said he would wait for the Bitcoin crash to find a bottom before buying again.

The most important zone is sitting just below Bitcoin’s current price. The liquidation map also shows a short-position cluster between about $59,326 and $59,863. This creates a clear battle zone.

If Bitcoin breaks back above $59,300, short positions could start getting squeezed, potentially helping BTC recover toward higher levels.

But failure to reclaim that area would leave the market exposed to another move lower.

Below the current price, the next important support sits around $54,522.

A clean break below that level could bring the $49,011 August 2024 low back into focus.
