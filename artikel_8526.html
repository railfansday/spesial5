Balance your checkbook by taking the statement’s ending balance, adding deposits it doesn’t show yet, and subtracting checks and payments that haven’t cleared. The result should match your checkbook. Unless you write checks regularly, keep a near-zero balance, or have a joint account, a weekly scan of every transaction plus a list of uncashed checks does the same job.

Say you mail a $900 rent check on the 1st, and your landlord doesn’t deposit it for a week. All week, your banking app still shows the $900 as money you can spend.

The balance your bank shows includes only what has reached the bank. Balancing a checkbook makes that figure agree with your own record once you count what hasn’t cleared, and it catches bank errors and charges you didn’t make. It takes six steps.

Stick with the full register if you write checks regularly, keep a balance near zero, or share a joint account. A check can go uncashed for weeks, one forgotten charge can overdraw an account kept near zero, and a second person spending from the account can change the balance without telling you.

Everyone else can swap the register for three habits. Turn on your bank’s alerts for a low balance and for any charge over an amount you pick. Scan every transaction once a week, on its own or as part of a broader weekly money check-in. Keep a list of every check you write until it clears.

Before you spend, subtract every check on that list from the balance your app shows. What’s left is the money you haven’t already spent.
