I had my 2000 GMC truck for 19 years and usually replace the cars we drive when they become unreliable. We like Toyotas so that should tell you something. Our latest new car was a top of the line 2019 Hyundai Santa Fe that we leased then ended up buying. I would do that again.

However, I will be much more diligent in the future when buying a used car from Carmax. I'll admit to not paying close enough attention during my cursory inspection before we bought it, and we're now paying the price after several defects I've discovered since buying it in January. The lastest are the new Michelin tires I'm having to buy this week to replace the old ones that need it after only 5 thousand freeway miles driven. It's rarely used, which is the only plus I can conjure.

The moral: Don't trust the Carmax disclosures of vehicle condition and pre-existing damage. Rookie mistake made by an old man who placed too much trust in their claims of perfection, acting like an excited kid when driving off the lot. You're not in Kansas anymore, Dorothy.

Low miles aren't necessarily equated with longevity when a prior owner didn't care about maintenance.
