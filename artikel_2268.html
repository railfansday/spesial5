Tottenham's hidden West Ham, Manor Solomon clause takes new twist

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

West Ham's decision to acquire Manor Solomon on a permanent transfer from Tottenham Hotspur over the summer raised initial eyebrows and is now coming under intensifying scrutiny, leaving questions to be asked over both his long-term future and a certain clause within the deal.

The Hammers' reintroduction to life in the Championship following 14 largely-successful years at Premier League level has not quite followed the script, having headed into the campaign as overwhelming title favourites.

West Ham find themselves positioned in eighth place after 14 games, however, and recently relieved Nuno Espirito Santo of his duties in the wake of successive defeats against two promotion rivals in Southampton and West Bromwich Albion before appointing Scott Parker.

The ex-Hammers and England international midfielder's tenure got off to a shaky start on Tuesday evening when the East Londoners could only muster a 3-3 draw away to Cardiff City, albeit with a remarkable recovery from a shock 3-0 deficit at half time, and it will be interesting to see what direction of travel they enjoy under the stewardship of a three-time second-tier promotion-winner with serious skin in the game within the club.

And exactly what lies ahead for Solomon, who has predominantly fallen short of delivering the desired impact upon his switch across London, only remains to be seen, putting the microscope on the deal that took the winger to the Championship in the first place.

West Ham shelled out an initial £5 million figure to secure Solomon's services on a permanent basis and agreed a three-year contract with the former Leeds United loan star, with the deal capable of rising to £7 million subject to various add-on criteria.

It's only likely, of course, that some criteria pertains to the Hammers' ongoing promotion charge, and though they are only just inside the play-offs at this moment in time, it's hard to look past Scott Parker's men making up relative ground as the campaign toils on and achieving a top-two finish.

Another clause, however, is far more interesting, and provides an intriguing discussion point amid increasing criticism of Solomon's acquisition in East London.

It was widely reported that Spurs, where the 27-year-old wide-man made just six competitive appearances across an injury-plagued spell of three years and just as many loans away from the club, included a 10% sell-on clause for any future fee that West Ham collect further down the line.

Solomon's resale value, mind you, is open to debate, and his stock has declined after previously lighting up the Championship on loan at Leeds to inspire their 100-point title triumph with 10 goals and 12 assists during the 2024/25 campaign.

With the West Ham attacker struggling to find his rhythm in the opening months, he's only earning the ever-growing ire of the London Stadium faithful while posing questions about his price-tag, the club's decision to complete the signing and exactly what Spurs could end up getting out of their sell-on clause.

Solomon has previous pedigree in European competition with Shakhtar Dontesk and tangible top-flight experience from spells at Fulham, Villarreal and Fiorentina alongside a strong second-tier reputation from his aforementioned exploits in West Yorkshire, but none of that has been translated to claret colours since making the move to West Ham.

He started off in style by finding the back of the net on debut during West Ham's 2-2 opening-weekend draw at Burnley before adding a goal and an assist to his name when the Hammers ran riot over Wrexham in a 6-0 rout on September 11.

Solomon would then set up another in West Ham's next league fixture away to fierce rivals Millwall a week later, though it's now been a month-and-a-half - or six matches - since Solomon last provided a direct goal involvement.

For a player of the ex-Shakhtar and Spurs winger's pedigree, the lack of production has left Hammers supporters frustrated as he continues to flatter to deceive.

Notably off the pace in his side's most recent draw at Cardiff, in which he lasted 70 minutes before being hauled off for £18 million summer signing Edvin Austbo as the visitors chased an all-important equaliser, the signing is now at real risk of not working out for all parties - leaving many to wonder why Spurs even added a sell-on clause in the first place.

Unfortunately for Solomon, it would appear as though West Ham fans have already made their minds up and the winger now faces a losing battle to regain the support of the London Stadium crowd.

His anonymous display against the Bluebirds has seemingly provided the final nail in the coffin for some, with many supporters taking to social media in recent times to make their thoughts clear.

This one fan, who was far from the only punter to share such a sentiment, doesn't wish to see the ex-Spurs wide-man in a Hammers shirt again.

The pacey Norweigan was much livelier against Cardiff after coming on for the closing stages and has the same number of goal contributions - two goals and two assists apiece - in 667 fewer minutes of league action.

Indeed, Austbo has started on just two occasions as Solomon continues to fall short of providing the goods as a consistent starter, and it only remains to be seen whether Parker will take to the tricky 21-year-old more than his predecessor.

West Ham fans will hope that's the case, though, and if Austbo seizes any forthcoming opportunities for a regular run in the side then it's hard to see how Solomon will make good of his transfer and become any sort of successful signing under Parker.
