OpenAI reportedly hires from the CIA as foreign spies target AI labs

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

A Russian charged with stalking Palantir staff, a bug in an OpenAI meeting room and the Nvidia affair have put state espionage on Silicon Valley’s agenda. Anthropic’s IPO filing warns of fake job applicants.

Sam Altman speaking at Semafor’s Silicon Valley conference, November 2026

OpenAI has hired from the CIA and walls off parts of its offices with “tented spaces” and turnstiles, the Financial Times reported on Tuesday. Anthropic has recruited from the US intelligence community for “internal risk” roles that watch its own staff. Both are defences against state espionage, which the FT says now targets the biggest tech companies.

In a Big Read for the Financial Times, Patrick McGee and Rafe Rosner-Uddin describe how Big Tech is catching up with spying by hostile governments. The paper cites a person familiar with each company’s plans. At Meta, the Superintelligence Lab sits in a Menlo Park building that other staff cannot enter. Even senior vice-presidents need prior approval to visit, the FT reported.

OpenAI hired Aristedes Mahairas, an FBI veteran of nearly 24 years, as its global head of corporate security in August 2025. Its board includes Paul Nakasone, a retired general who once ran the National Security Agency. At OpenAI’s offices, private and public meeting rooms sit in separate areas with different access rules, according to the FT.

“We take protecting our technology, data and people seriously and as a matter of national importance,” OpenAI said in a statement to the FT.

Last year, Anthropic set up a national security advisory council that includes former senator Roy Blunt and David Cohen, twice a CIA deputy director. The company told the FT it runs strict personnel vetting, access controls, and insider-risk monitoring.

Its filing with the SEC before last week’s listing names the threat directly. It also warns that state-backed actors and competitors could pressure staff after they join.

“We have been and expect that we will continue to be targeted by individuals seeking employment under false identities,” Anthropic wrote in the filing.

In October, OpenAI’s security team found a recording device behind a television in a meeting room used by executives. It came wrapped in toilet paper, according to the FT, and the company called the FBI. Federal agents arrested Abdulrahman Alfozan, a former OpenAI engineer and Saudi national, on wiretapping charges. He has pleaded not guilty, and his trial starts in January. His lawyers call him an “overly curious tinkerer” with no harmful intent. Sam Altman also spoke about the bug when he told a Semafor audience that OpenAI’s security had not been perfect.

Last week, the US Justice Department indicted Igor D’iu, a Russian national, on four counts. Prosecutors say Russian handlers sent him to watch staff at a Silicon Valley technology and defence company because it supplied weapons used against Russia in Ukraine. The department did not name the company. The FT identifies it as Palantir.

According to the Justice Department, D’iu photographed employees’ laptop screens in a coffee shop near the company’s Palo Alto office. He also followed one employee for nearly 30 miles. FBI agents later found a notebook with profiles of at least three staff, including their family members’ names and assessments of their strengths and weaknesses.

Nvidia is the third case. On Monday, its board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive, 11 days after he resigned. He had stepped down after reports of an affair with Alice Huang, whom US authorities consider a Chinese intelligence asset. China’s foreign ministry calls the allegations a smear. Nvidia’s board says it has no reason to believe anyone compromised national security or Nvidia’s secrets.

Greg Levesque runs Strider Technologies, a firm that tracks state-backed threats to companies. He told the FT that the Huang affair should wake up Silicon Valley.

“The biggest tech firms now operate on the scale of countries, and foreign intelligence services target them in the same way,” Levesque told the FT.

D’iu remains in federal custody. The Justice Department listed his next court appearance for 24 November.
