Southland Holdings (SLND) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Legacy paving losses are fading as the company pivots to higher-margin bids.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

Southland Holdings, Inc. (SLND +49.22%) reported a return to profitability in the third quarter of 2026, supported by improved execution in the Civil segment and the formalization of a financial assistance agreement with its sureties. Management stated that the company is transitioning away from underperforming legacy paving work, having recently completed its final project in the West Texas region. The company is focused on bidding for higher-margin opportunities in its core water, bridge, marine, and tunnel markets while utilizing its stabilized bonding capacity to grow its $1.71 billion backlog. The company submitted a plan to the NYSE American to regain compliance with listing standards by March 23, 2028, which relies on debt-to-equity conversions and continued operational improvement.

In the third quarter, we executed the financial assistance agreement and the second amendment to our term loan facility, which formalized the support our sureties have been providing. As we discussed on our last call, a portion of the nonbonding financing provided under the financial assistance agreement is expected to convert into senior nonvoting preferred shares. The preferred shares will have perpetual maturity and are not convertible into any other securities of the company. We have extended the date for issuing the preferred shares to December 18, and we continue to work closely with the sureties on finalizing the conversion.

Third quarter revenue was $234 million, and gross profit was $21 million or 9% of revenue. The quarter's performance was driven by our Civil segment, which produced a 13% gross margin led by strong results on a data center project and a wastewater treatment plant project in the Southwest. In the third quarter, new awards were approximately $271 million. At the federal level, the Surface Transportation authorization under the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act expired at the end of September, and Congress extended certain programs through December 11, while it works on a longer-term reauthorization. The timing of a new bill remains uncertain, but a significant portion of IIJA funding has yet to be spent and should continue to support state and local programs over the next several years. We are encouraged by the bipartisan support for reauthorization, and we believe the funding environment for our core markets remains healthy.

We also continue to see strong demand for data center projects, which give us additional strong margin work for our crews, which complements our core water, bridge, marine and tunnel markets. Over the longer term, we believe that, that diversity of end markets positions us well to adapt as market demand shifts. The final contract value on the data center awards will depend on the amount of work performed as directed by the customer.

We are also completing a preconstruction contract with a major cruise facility client in the Caribbean with the intent to negotiate a progressive design build contract in the coming months. We also have several outstanding bids, including 2 data center opportunities, a water resource opportunity in the Southwest, 2 bridge projects in the Southeast, several marine opportunities in the Caribbean, the MoDOT Liberty Bend Bridge design build in Missouri and the Claiborne Pell Bridge rehab in Rhode Island. Additional opportunities in our pipeline include the Outerbridge crossing repairs for the Port Authority of New York and New Jersey, the Veterans Drive Contract 2 project in St. Thomas, the GE Booth Outfall Tunnel for the Region of Peel, a transmission pipeline in North Dakota, the Marine Creek Sanitary Sewer Interceptor for the City of Fort Worth, Brook Hollow Interceptor for Dallas Water Utilities and Duckabush restoration project for the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers. We're also tracking multiple bridge dehumidification rehab projects, including the Bronx-Whitestone Bridge, Throgs Neck Bridge, San Francisco-Oakland Bay Bridge and the Claiborne Pell and multiple other water tunnel marine and bridge opportunities across our core markets.

The pipeline remains strong, and we expect to win our fair share of upcoming opportunities. To summarize, we are starting to see positive momentum in the business. We delivered a profitable third quarter and booked strong new awards, growing our backlog to $1.71 billion. Demand in our core markets with both public and private clients remain strong. We're awaiting decisions on multiple outstanding bids across our end markets that could convert into new awards in the coming months. We are encouraged by this progress, but there is more to do.

Our near-term priorities remain completing the preferred share issuance, finishing our remaining legacy projects, collecting on money we are owed on outstanding disputes and continuing to convert our pipeline into profitable new awards. Keith Bassano: Thanks, Frank, and good morning, everyone. I'll discuss an overview of our financial performance during the third quarter of 2026. You can find additional details and information in the financial statements, footnotes and management's discussion and analysis that were filed on Form 10-Q last night. Revenue in the third quarter was $233.8 million compared to $213.3 million in the same period in 2025, an increase of $20.4 million or 9.6%. Gross profit in the quarter was $20.6 million or 8.8% of revenue compared to gross profit of $1.9 million or 0.9% of revenue in the third quarter of 2025.

As Frank described, the improvement was primarily driven by our new core work, including a data center project and a wastewater treatment plant in the Southwest, partially offset by negative impacts from a materials and paving project and a water project in the Midwest. Selling, general and administrative expenses in the third quarter were $13.6 million, a decrease of $1 million or 6.6% compared to the same period in 2025. The decrease was primarily driven by a $1.9 million reduction in compensation expense, partially offset by an approximately $1 million increase in business transformation expense. Selling, general and administrative expenses were 5.8% of revenue in the quarter compared to 6.8% in the third quarter of 2025.

Operating income was $7 million compared to an operating loss of $12.6 million in the third quarter of 2025. Other income, net was $32.4 million compared to $2 million in the prior year period. This included a $28.7 million gain on the extinguishment of a surety payable from our settlement with one of our surety partners related to the Washington State Convention Center judgment. Interest expense in the quarter totaled $4.1 million, a decrease of $5.1 million or 55.2% compared to the same period in 2025. The decrease was primarily driven by a $2.8 million adjustment to previously recorded interest expense because the lower interest rate under the second amendment applies retroactively to March 17 of this year.

On a go forward, we expect interest expense to be approximately $6 million per quarter. We recorded tax expense of $0.5 million for the quarter compared to $57.2 million in the same period last year, which reflected the valuation allowance we recorded against our domestic deferred tax assets in the third quarter of 2025. Net income attributable to Southland stockholders in the third quarter was $33.7 million or $0.62 per diluted share compared to a net loss of $75.3 million or a loss of $1.39 per diluted share in the third quarter of 2025. EBITDA in the third quarter was $43.2 million compared to a negative $3.5 million in the third quarter of 2025.

This quarter's EBITDA includes the $28.7 million gain on the Washington State Convention Center settlement we discussed earlier. Now to touch on segment performance. Our Civil segment had revenue of $107.3 million compared to $99.5 million in the same period in 2025. Civil had a gross profit of $14.3 million or a 13.3% margin compared to a gross profit of $10.4 million or a 10.5% margin in the prior year period. The increase was driven primarily by a data center project and a wastewater treatment plant in the Southwest, partially offset by lower contributions from a water project in the Midwest that is approaching completion.

Our Transportation segment had revenue of $126.4 million compared to $113.9 million in the same period in 2025. Transportation had gross profit of $6.3 million or a 5% margin compared to a gross loss of $8.5 million or a negative 7.4% margin in the same period in the prior year. The improvement was driven primarily by bridge projects in the Southeast and the Midwest and 2 dam projects in the South, partially offset by a decrease from a materials and paving project.

The Materials & Paving business line contributed $17.4 million in revenue and a gross loss of $10.2 million in the quarter compared to revenue of $22.9 million and a gross loss of $3 million in the same period in 2025. We are down to $21 million of Materials & Paving backlog from $46 million last quarter and $27.4 million of non-M&P legacy backlog from $35 million. We finished the quarter with approximately $1.71 billion of backlog compared to $1.68 billion at June 30. We expect to recognize approximately 40% of our backlog as revenue over the next 12 months. Turning to liquidity.

We ended the quarter with cash and cash equivalents of $35.9 million and total cash, including restricted cash of $46.8 million compared to $45.7 million at the end of the second quarter. During the quarter, our surety partners advanced approximately $25 million to support ongoing project performance. At quarter end, total surety financing under the financial assistance agreement was $233.9 million, made up of $65.3 million of bonding surety financing and $168.6 million of nonbonding financing. With the financial assistance agreement executed, we reclassified $209.8 million from surety payables to debt. We expect at least $151 million of the nonbonding financing to convert into preferred shares, reducing debt.

Under an amendment to the financial assistance agreement, the preferred shares are now required to be issued by December 18 of this year. We continue to monetize noncore equipment and real estate, generating approximately $13.1 million of proceeds through the first 9 months of the year and expect to further reduce debt in the coming quarters with these initiatives. Separately, a surety payable balance related to the Washington State Convention Center judgment was $55.4 million at quarter end compared to $89.1 million at June 30. This reflects the $28.7 million gain on extinguishment of surety payable and the $5 million payment we made under the settlement previously announced.

We remain in negotiation with a surety partner that funded the remaining amount, and they have agreed not to seek repayment until at least November 13, 2027. On September 23, we received notice from the NYSE American that we were not in compliance with its continued listing standards for stockholders' equity. On October 22, we submitted our plan to regain compliance by the March 23, 2028 deadline, and the plan is under review by the exchange. Converting a portion of our surety financing into preferred shares, resolving our remaining legacy surety obligations and continued profitable execution are the foundation of that plan.

Consistent with what we communicated on our last call, our focus remains on closing out our legacy work, improving the balance sheet through asset monetization and our surety partnership and executing our core business in the markets and geographies where our margins are the strongest.
