People and Vertical AI: The new operating model for energy and utilities

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

For generations, the energy and utility industry has depended on the judgment of its people.

Across the industry, people are responsible for keeping energy and water running and serving the communities that depend on them. A field crew is there when a community needs power restored. A grid operator carries the responsibility of keeping the network stable and reliable. A customer service representative is often the human connection between a customer and the utility service. An engineer makes decisions that can shape the safety, resilience and reliability of infrastructure for decades.

That expertise remains essential. What has changed is the complexity surrounding it.

Electrification, distributed energy resources, renewable generation, electric vehicles, extreme weather, aging infrastructure, affordability pressures, rising customer expectations and data centers becoming new energy consumers are all connecting decisions that were once managed independently.

The industry has spent decades digitizing those decisions, generating more data along the way. The challenge now is different: how do you make that intelligence work together and make the expertise of the people using it scalable?

This is where AI has to evolve beyond generic intelligence to a Vertical AI approach built around the specific context, complexity and interconnected realities of the energy and utility industry.

The opportunity is not to automate the people who run the industry. It is to give them better context, better intelligence and the ability to extend their judgment across more decisions, more customers and more of the ecosystem than was previously possible.

That is the premise behind People + Vertical AI.

People + Vertical AI is not a technology layer placed on top of existing operations. It is an operating model in which Vertical AI works alongside people, connecting information across the enterprise while people provide judgment, accountability and context.

The distinction matters because utilities do not operate as isolated functions.

Customer intelligence sits alongside workforce intelligence. Business decisions depend on grid conditions. Field execution affects customer experience. Distributed energy resources influence both grid operations and customer participation. Yet much of that intelligence still lives in separate systems, workflows and teams.

AI makes this fragmentation more consequential, not less. A model can analyze enormous amounts of information, but it cannot provide meaningful intelligence when the context it needs remains disconnected.

Recent research from SEW.AI, based on its work with 470+ energy and utility organizations across 47+ countries, points to the scale of this opportunity: the industry is moving from isolated digital capabilities toward a more connected model of intelligence, where Vertical AI can work alongside people across the ecosystem.

Connected Intelligence brings that context together across four dimensions: Customer, Workforce, Business and Grid. The objective is not simply to connect systems or move data between applications. It is to give people and AI a shared understanding of what is happening across the ecosystem, so that one decision can inform the next.

Energy and utilities are not generic enterprises. Reliability, affordability, safety, regulation, customer trust, asset performance and operational continuity are inseparable from the decisions utilities make every day. AI needs to understand those relationships.

Vertical AI is therefore not simply a general-purpose model trained on more utility data. It is intelligence shaped around the industry's operating reality: its data structures, business processes, regulatory requirements, physical infrastructure, customer relationships and the interdependencies between them.

The human remains responsible. Vertical AI expands what that human can see, understand and act on, for this industry.

The real significance of this model is not the scale of the technology. It is the scale of its potential impact.

Energy and water are among the few industries whose services touch virtually every person, every community and every economy. Every household depends on them. Every business depends on them. Every field worker, operator, engineer and business leader contributes to keeping them reliable, accessible and affordable.

That means better intelligence can reach far beyond productivity metrics. It can shape billions of everyday interactions and decisions.

The objective is not to scale AI for its own sake. It is to scale better decisions across an industry whose services are foundational to everyday life.

Scale, however, cannot come at the expense of trust.

The next generation of energy and utility AI must therefore be built around the people who use it and the customers who experience its decisions. It must be responsible enough to trust, connected enough to understand context and specialized enough to operate within the realities of the industry.

A Leading Canadian Utility Spearheading Customer Experience & eMobility Leadership

One of Canada’s largest electric utilities is redefining the customer relationship as electrification reshapes the energy landscape. Its transformation brings customer experience, eMobility and energy management together, positioning the utility to play a more active role in how customers adopt and manage new forms of energy consumption.

Smart EV charging is central to that evolution, creating a new customer engagement model while giving the utility greater visibility into changing energy behavior. As connected devices and flexible loads grow, automated demand response extends that relationship from the customer experience into the grid, turning participation into a source of flexibility.

The transformation reflects People + Vertical AI in action: technology connects customer choices, energy intelligence and grid needs, while people remain at the center of how that intelligence is applied. It points to a new model for industry leadership, where the utility becomes not simply an energy provider, but an energy partner.

A major U.S. public utility is demonstrating that the energy transition is as much about how people experience energy as how the grid evolves. Serving more than a million customers, its transformation has focused on making digital engagement more personal, accessible and useful while helping customers take a more active role in their energy choices.

By bringing billing, payments, energy insights, programs, communications and self-service into a connected experience, the utility has made it easier for customers to understand and manage their energy. Personalized insights, flexible payment options, multilingual engagement and omnichannel continuity turn information into action, from improving efficiency to exploring EV and clean-energy programs, while strengthening trust across the customer relationship.

The transformation also creates a foundation for what comes next. The utility is extending its connected energy ecosystem into eMobility and public charging, expanding access to charging infrastructure while creating new opportunities to connect transportation, customer engagement and energy management. This is People + Vertical AI in action: making energy participation simpler today while building a more connected and intelligent energy ecosystem for tomorrow.

The industry's next AI advantage will not come from deploying the greatest number of models.

It will come from connecting intelligence to the people, processes and decisions that matter most.

Utilities have spent decades digitizing their operations. The next step is making that intelligence work together.

People + Vertical AI is ultimately about that shift: from intelligence that sits inside systems to intelligence that moves across the ecosystem, strengthening human judgment and extending its reach.
