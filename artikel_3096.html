Adyen co-founder Pieter van der Does retires as Ingo Uytdehaage takes over

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Pieter van der Does stays on as an adviser. Adyen says its financial targets are unchanged, and the handover was planned for more than three years.

The Dutch payments company Adyen will have a single chief executive from January. Co-founder Pieter van der Does leaves the co-CEO role on 31 December, and Ingo Uytdehaage, co-CEO since 2023, will run Adyen alone, Adyen said on Monday, the company’s 20th anniversary.

Van der Does will stay on as an adviser. Adyen said it has not changed its financial objectives.

“After 20 years, this is the right moment for me to step down on a positive note and with a very good feeling. Adyen has been my life’s work,” van der Does said in the statement.

The two have worked side by side for more than 15 years, Uytdehaage said. In a letter to staff, van der Does wrote that Adyen had planned the handover for more than three years, EU-Startups reported. The co-CEO model was part of that plan.

“Pieter built more than a company. He built a culture that shapes how we make decisions, how we treat each other and how we put our merchants first, and it will outlast any one of us,” Uytdehaage said.

Van der Does founded Adyen with Arnout Schuijff in 2006, after the two had worked together at the payments company Bibit. The name means “doing it again” in Surinamese, EU-Startups reported. Adyen listed in Amsterdam in 2018 and has grown by double digits every year since 2007, the company said. It counts Meta, Uber, H&M, eBay and Microsoft among its customers.

This year Adyen made its first acquisitions, moving beyond payments. It agreed to buy Talon.One, which lets merchants adjust promotions and prices, for €750mn. It also bought the billing platform Orb for $335mn, Sarah Jacob reported for Bloomberg.

Adyen expects net revenue to grow by 21% to 23% this year at constant currency. It recently named Niclas Neglén, the departing finance chief of rival Klarna, as its next CFO, Bloomberg reported.

Adyen competes with PayPal and Stripe, which Nuvei is chasing with its Payoneer deal. It is also among the firms in the ECB’s digital euro pilot.
