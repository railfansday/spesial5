This Mindset Shift Helped Me Stop Dreading Market Sell-Offs. Now I Look Forward to Them.

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Keeping a long-term perspective can help investors stay calm and confident during stock market crashes.

It seems like all year in 2026, investors have been worrying and debating about the idea of an artificial intelligence (AI) bubble in the stock market. Well, wonder no more: the bubble seems to be bursting. In the past month, the tech-heavy Nasdaq-100 index has declined by 16%. Between Oct. 6 and Nov. 20, the index nearly reached "bear market" status, with a decline of 19.84%.

The S&P 500 index (^GSPC +1.17%)is not immune to this sell-off. That benchmark index has lost nearly 11% in the past month.

Big stock market sell-offs like the one we're going through right now aren't fun or easy. It's natural to feel worried or afraid for the future. But in a strange way, I'm in a pretty good mood this week. That's because, over my years of investing through bear markets and economic crises, I've learned a new mindset for stock market sell-offs.

I want to share my one-sentence mindset shift that helps me stay calm and confident during stock market crashes. Maybe it will work for you, too.

This mindset sounds simple, but I have repeated it to myself many times over the years when the stock market is tanking: "You don't need the money today."

Here's what I mean by this. I am a long-term investor. The money that I've put into the stock market is not money that I need today. It's not my emergency fund. It's not my vacation fund. It's not money that I need to pay bills this month or the next. It's long-term money. "Leave it alone for 20 years" money.

Because of this long-term perspective, I can (most of the time) stay calm when the stock market is crashing. I'm not trying to get rich quick by flipping stocks for a fast profit. I'm trying to buy and hold a diversified portfolio of stocks, including low-cost index funds, that will let me build wealth gradually over the next few decades.

I don't need the stock market to make me rich today. I hope that the stock market will make me richer over the next 20 years.

But just saying "I'm a long-term investor" isn't enough to build wealth. The stock market must deliver significant gains. Fortunately, that tends to happen. As part of that long-term perspective, I'm a student of stock market history.

The S&P 500 index has delivered 9.98% annualized returns since 1928. Think of all the stressful times, economic crises, and global catastrophes that have happened during those 98 years. That 9.98% average annual return happened despite the Great Depression. It includes World War II, and the 1970s stagflation, and the 2000 dot-com bust, and the 2008 global financial crisis.

Even with all the booms and busts along the way, the S&P 500 has been a strong investment for long-term shareholders. In the past 16 years since it was launched in Sept. 2010, the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO +1.17%) has delivered average annual returns of about 14.8%. VOO has delivered about 12% annualized returns in the past five years (as of Oct. 31).

Even if the next few years have lower stock market returns, buying S&P 500 ETFs tends to be a great move -- if "you don't need the money today."
