Aston Villa and Manchester United were held to a 4-4 draw at Old Trafford.

This fixture was filled with action, with four goals in the first half, including a rapid-fire start from the away side, followed by four more in the second.

Villa had gone 2-0 up within just four minutes, but Man United battled their way back into the game.

The away side, though, would respond better when the second half kicked off, restoring their two-goal lead but ultimately the home side brought it level in the end.

However, while the goals and penalty incidents will get the headlines, there were some moments that likely went amiss for supporters.

As Football Insider looks at the things you may have missed amid all the chaotic action at Old Trafford.

Protests against the current structure of Manchester United's board continued against Aston Villa.

In the Stretford End, a sign stating: "We don't want a new stadium, we want new owners" could be seen hung up.

Green and yellow balloons, detailing the historic colours of the club, were plentiful in the stand as well before the game had even kicked off.

It was far from what Michael Carrick wanted from his players when Man United went 2-0 down after just four minutes.

The home crowd were also far from amused, and chants telling the players they were "not fit to wear the shirt" went around the ground.

However, when the half-time whistle went, the cheers were incredibly loud, with the players on the pitch turning the toxicity around.

It didn't take long for Youri Tielemans to hear how Aston Villa fans are feeling months on from his departure.

In the ninth minute, there were audible boos from the away end towards the Belgian midfielder.

Tielemans would go on to score the equalising goal at 2-2, but it seems he was not willing to hit back at his former club as he refused to celebrate.

While Villa started well against Manchester United, manager Unai Emery was left fuming with a couple of his players.

In the first half, the Villans were losing the ball in avoidable situations.

As per reporter Jacob Tanswell, Emery was left frustrated with his players' decision-making early in the first half, with Johan Manzambi and Ian Maatsen in particular.

Instead of looking to keep possession, the pair opted to try to bring the ball forward, ultimately giving it away.

During the broadcast of the match, TV cameras panned to talk about Robin van Persie and his volley that won United their 20th league title against Aston Villa in 2013.

However, another Old Trafford familiar face was beside him, but did not even get a mention.

That was former striker Javier Hernandez, who had played 157 times for the Red Devils over his career.
