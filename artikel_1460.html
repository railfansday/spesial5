Max Dowman-mania could make League Cup hottest ticket in town

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

There is an obligation on all of us, from supporters to pundits and journalists, to remain calm and measured about footballers as young as Max Dowman. But, my word, he does make it difficult. Arsenal’s 16-year-old sensation continues to sparkle whenever he steps on to the field, and he did so again with this superb display against Ipswich Town.

"The hype will only continue to grow!"Max Dowman that is delightful! 👏 pic.twitter.com/UfFY1VSyXn

At this rate, Arsenal supporters will be looking forward to Carabao Cup matches as eagerly as any Premier League or Champions League game, simply because it will be a chance to watch Dowman again. The forward’s age and inexperience means he is still used sparingly in those high-priority competitions. The League Cup, by contrast, may prove to be his domain over the coming months.

Dowman’s double at Portman Road made him just the second 16-year-old to score two goals in a game for a Premier League side. The first was Wayne Rooney, for Everton against Wrexham in 2002.

The two goals underlined why there is so much excitement around Dowman. The first was a demonstration of his prodigious dribbling ability, as he weaved through Ipswich’s defence and buried his shot with the weaker right foot. The second showed his composure: a slotted finish, rolled in with ease.

Max Dowman nets his second of the game! 😍 pic.twitter.com/XRZ9GqZx3x

As Mikel Arteta approaches the point of signing a new contract in north London, one wonders how much the idea of developing Dowman will excite him as he looks to the future. Which manager would not want to coach a player of such natural ability? Which manager would not want to see how good he could be? The same is true of Marli Salmon, the 17-year-old defender who also impressed on Tuesday night.

“A joy to have him,” said Arteta of Dowman. “It’s remarkable. It’s something very natural for him. He can deliver those actions effortlessly, I would say. What we have to do is look after him. He is 16. You don’t see that in this league. In other leagues in Europe, that’s a totally different context.

“Having two teenagers playing for Arsenal at this level is so fulfilling. Because I’ve been there and just to be able to contribute to somebody’s career and dream, to be able to experience a game like tonight, is phenomenal. And yeah, medium and long term, we know that if they continue to develop in the way that they are, we are going to have two tremendous footballers.”

"He was brilliant, he made the difference - we need to look after him" 🌟Mikel Arteta says Arsenal need to "be really cautious" with 16-year-old Max Dowman after he scored twice in Arsenal's 4-2 win over Ipswich 🔴 pic.twitter.com/1msJAEkK8z

Dowman’s two goals, along with strikes by Noni Madueke and Mikel Merino, secured a comfortable victory for Arsenal against Premier League opponents. Ipswich scored a goal of their own, through Anis Mehmeti, but never seemed likely to cause the visitors too much bother. A late strike from former Arsenal player Chuba Akpom was only a consolation. “We found a way to fight back into it,” said Gary O’Neil. “Losing by two goals to them isn’t a dreadful effort.”

Dowman is already developing a trademark goal, of sorts. His first strike for the club, against Everton last season, came after he left an opponent face-down on the turf at the Emirates Stadium. He inflicted the same pain here upon poor Cédric Kipré, who tried to tackle the teenager in the seventh minute but ended up with a mouthful of grass.

It was an image that encapsulated Arsenal’s dominance in the early exchanges, as was the sight of Madueke firing in the second. The chance came after Bruno Guimarães and Merino, majestic in midfield, had monstered Ipswich’s attempts to play out from the back. Merino was dominant throughout, winning tackles with steel and then spinning away opponents with style. “He’s unbelievable,” said Arteta.

Arsenal’s long-term relationship with the League Cup is not a particularly healthy one. For a club of their stature, it is a source of mild embarrassment that they have not won this competition since 1993. They came close last season, reaching the final at Wembley, but were defeated by Manchester City.

Given the depth of Arteta’s squad, Arsenal really should reach the final again. If this is a tournament for the B-team, at least in these early stages, then Arsenal probably have the best B-team in the country. They started this game without any of Bukayo Saka, Declan Rice, Martin Odegaard, and Kai Havertz but were still extraordinarily strong in midfield and attack.

The only pre-match concern was their defence, which has been weakened by a couple of early-season injuries. Arsenal started with Salmon at centre-back and the teenager was in direct combat with the physical, sharp-elbowed threat of Ipswich striker Zian Flemming. He stood up to the challenge with determination and class.

Ipswich had made 10 changes following their victory at Crystal Palace at the weekend, which was an indication of where the Carabao Cup sits in their priorities. They were able to get into Arsenal’s penalty area but never gave the impression they could really hurt the Premier League champions, apart from when Mehmeti converted Jack Clarke’s low cross.

Akpom struck in the 91st minute to encourage O’Neil, but by then the game had been lost. Arsenal’s work, and indeed Dowman’s work, had been done.

In other leagues in Europe, that’s a totally different context, but in this league, with the demands... We need to make sure that every time we throw him in it’s in the right context with the right preparation.”

Asked about Dowman’s two-goal performance, Arteta said: “A joy to have him, Marli [Salmon] as well. Seventeen years old, to play at that level with the physicality they have to be able to handle that level, it’s remarkable. Max, yeah, it’s inevitable to talk about him when he scores and he creates the moments he has created tonight. It’s normal.”

He added: “He is coping better and better and better, he did the action where he regains the ball and wins it in the first goal, he loses his man... He’s an unbelievable player because of the character that he has and how much he loves to play on the biggest stage and still handles the situation with such naturality.”

Speaking in his post-match press conference, Mikel Arteta said: “Really pleased with a lot of things we did tonight, the way we attacked the game, how dominant, fluid and aggressive we were, right from the beginning to try to influence the result. We scored two very early goals that gave us obviously a great platform for the game.

“Individually some big performances as well. Some debuts tonight which is great to see as well, so we take a lot of positives from tonight.”

Arsenal have won the League Cup twice – in 1987 and 1993 – meaning eight clubs have better records, including Aston Villa (five titles), Tottenham Hotspur (four), Nottingham Forest (four) and Leicester City (three).

Their rather modest haul is a far cry from their record-setting 14 FA Cup titles. In the 33-years since Arsenal’s most recent League Cup triumph, the competition’s title sponsor has changed hands four times (kudos to anyone who can name all five!).

No team has scored more goals through substitutes in the Premier League than Ipswich, which is a nod to the promoted club’s depth and work in the summer transfer window.

It has meant that Gary O’Neil has had to leave established players out of match-day squads. “It was really tough leaving out Darnell [Furlong] and Anis [Mehmeti] from the squad [against Crystal Palace],” he said. “Doing that to good people is one of the toughest parts of the job. But I have faith in them, it’s just that everyone’s fit at the moment and we’ve had to recruit a strong squad to get us through an unbelievably challenging season.

“On Tuesday, whoever I pick, I have 100 per cent faith that they’re ready and that they’re going to represent what we do and how we go about it.”

Alan Davies, the actor and comedian who is a famously passionate supporter of Arsenal, recently told a story about a charity event he once attended with former captain Tony Adams. Speaking on The Tuesday Club podcast, Davies said Adams revealed his football philosophy to be: “You need seven.”

The “seven” in question, Adams explained, are seven members of the team who are ready and willing to fight the opposition in the tunnel after the game. Once a team have those seven individuals, Adams said, everything else flows from there.

For much of the past two decades, Arsenal have not had seven. In some years in the latter stages of Arsène Wenger’s tenure, they probably did not even have three or four. But that has all changed in recent seasons under Mikel Arteta, to such an extent that the Premier League champions would now undoubtedly bring considerably more than seven combatants to any touchline scrap or tunnel bust-up.

Saturday’s gritty 2-0 victory away to Sunderland provided evidence that Arsenal have added two more of these characters to their squad this summer.

Good evening and welcome to our live coverage of the Carabao Cup third-round tie between Ipswich Town and Arsenal. Mikel Arteta’s side have won all six of their matches so far this season, but travel to Portman Road fresh off an incident-filled win over Sunderland which thrust officiating back into the spotlight after Ezri Konsa conceded a penalty having been – in Arteta’s words – “judo-rolled” by Dan Ballard.

After the match, the Arsenal manager said: “I watched [the incident] 20 times and I cannot find a way to understand how to even think to give a penalty in this situation or the way the VAR has to intervene. So I don’t know what to think, really. But I’m sad, that we have to discuss, after such a beautiful game, something at this level that has the gravity of that because that changes the course of the season and it can cost you the championship… This cannot happen.”

Arteta may be pleased that VAR is not in use at this stage of the Carabao Cup, and all eyes will be on Peter Bankes who takes charge of tonight’s match against Ipswich.

This season Arsenal hope to arrest a startling League Cup record. It has been 33 years since they last lifted the trophy, during which time they have been runners-up seven times. Losing last year’s final will have also added fuel to Arsenal’s fire, and Arteta may resolve he must field a strong starting XI. In fact, he may not have much choice in his backline as Ben White has emerged as a serious injury doubt after picking up an injury at the Stadium of Light and may join Cristhian Mosquera and William Saliba on the treatment table.
