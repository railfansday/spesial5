Wolverine Loader II Review: No Break-In, No Blisters

New work boots can be miserable until they’re broken in, especially if you spend long days on your feet. My husband, a contractor who also works on our farm, tested the Wolverine Loader II HyperRest CarbonMAX boots for a full week, wearing them up to 10 hours a day. We had previously tested two of Wolverine’s Infinity System work boots, so we were curious to compare them with this pair’s comfort design. Here’s what we found. (And for more options, see our team-tested best work boots guide.)

The break-in was the biggest surprise of our test. My husband put the Wolverine Loader II boots on and went straight to work. There was no period of stiff leather rubbing against his ankles, no uncomfortable pressure around his toes, and no need to alternate with an older pair while the new boots softened up.

He wore them for a week while doing his regular contracting work and handling chores on our farm, including clearing trees and operating equipment. Some days stretched to 10 hours, but he never complained about sore feet afterward.

Wolverine credits much of that comfort to its (removable) HyperRest footbed, which cushions and supports the foot, and a polyurethane midsole that helps absorb the impact of walking on hard surfaces. The padded collar also helps prevent rubbing around the ankle. Whatever the combination, it worked well for him from the first day.

In our testing, yes. My husband is accustomed to wearing work boots, so he knows the difference between a pair that feels good in the store and one that remains comfortable through a full shift.

The Loader II passed that test. He especially liked how light they felt for boots with protective toes. Wolverine lists the weight at 2.04 pounds per boot, and the CarbonMAX composite toe provides impact and compression protection without the bulk of a traditional steel toe.

The rubber wedge outsole is another feature of the comfort design. Its broad contact area helps distribute weight on hard, flat surfaces, which can be particularly helpful for contractors who spend hours on concrete or other unforgiving flooring.

The Wolverine Loader II is more than just a comfortable boot. Its CarbonMAX toe meets ASTM F2413-24 impact and compression requirements, and the boot also carries electrical hazard and slip-resistance ratings.

The waterproof full-grain leather upper includes reinforced toe and heel bumpers, which help protect areas that often take a beating around tools, equipment, and construction materials. A breathable waterproof membrane and moisture-wicking lining provide additional protection in wet conditions.

Wolverine also uses Goodyear welt construction, which allows the boots to be resoled rather than discarded when the outsole wears out. That’s a welcome feature in a work boot intended for regular use.

One consideration is the wedge outsole. While it’s designed for dependable traction, its relatively flat profile didn’t dig into deep mud as aggressively as some heavily lugged soles. For workers who spend most of their time in muddy fields or on steep, loose terrain, outsole design deserves extra attention.

At $169.95, the Loader II is cheaper than the premium Wolverine Infinity System boots we previously tested, yet it delivered the comfort my husband needs for long workdays.

I think these boots are particularly well suited to contractors, carpenters, equipment operators, and other tradespeople who want safety-toe protection without stiff, heavy footwear. The 6-inch lace-up design also provides a more adjustable fit than a pull-on boot.

After a week of demanding use, my husband had no complaints about comfort, fit, or foot fatigue. That’s an excellent start, although we’ll need more time to judge how the leather, stitching, and outsole hold up over months of daily wear.

For workers who dread breaking in a new pair of boots, the Loader II might just be the ticket.
