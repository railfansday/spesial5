Tired of GTA 6? Here's 23 lesser-known gems that might beat it at its own game

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

You can do a lot of things in GTA 6: fishing, golfing, running, jumping, dancing, racing, roaming, shopping. It goes on! There's romancing, birdwatching, DIY, and even more besides. It really is an everything game. But GTA 6 isn't for everyone.

So what if you were in the mood for some of this stuff but away from Leonida? Where else might you go? Consider this the slightly tenuous set-up, as we continue a week of Not-GTA, for us to highlight some of our favourite smaller games; brilliant lesser-known titles from the last 12 months or so that, in their own special way, beat GTA 6 at its own game.

Available on: Android, iOS, PC (Steam), PlayStation 5, Switch, Switch 2.

It's very sweet that the central, driving force of GTA 6 is the strained romance between Jason and Lucia. You are, essentially, playing a virtual matchmaker, helping these star-crossed lovers reconnect and rediscover each other after fate conspires to drive a wedge between them. But you know what's better than that? Being an actual matchmaker, matching icons on a grid and enjoying the microdose dopamine rush when you clear a line on a board.

So here's Titanium Court, which goes even further: with every line cleared, you come closer to escaping a weird, 80s-core fairy castle in a game that's partly telling a story about introspective self-doubt, and partly about warring fairies. If you're getting tired of texting your virtual lover every 10 minutes, perhaps try lighting a virtual cigarette in Titanium Court, instead - I've heard it does wonders for your health.

Available on: PC (Steam), PlayStation 5, Switch 2.

How do you find out if you can really depend upon someone? That's a question at the heart of GTA 6. And the answer is: play Big Walk. There's nothing like gesturing through the window of a sound-proofed room hoping your friend or loved one will understand what two arms waggling around means. It means 'the high-pitched warbling sound plaque', obviously. Big Walk's wonderfully stripped-back world is also an antidote to GTA 6's maximal world. The silence and space in the game is absolutely the point. What will you fill it with?

It's not all roof repairs and late night paintjobs. Sometimes home improvement requires more drastic measures. And in Virtue and a Sledgehammer, that's taken to violent extremes. This is a game, in developers Selkie Harbour and Deconstructeam's words, about family, tradition, and being a misfit. Where an entire town - the protagonist's childhood home - faces a total spiritual teardown. Here, a brutal sledgehammer-swinging reckoning awaits as you unearth memories - some happy, some decidedly not - through violent destruction, cutting through mechanical human facsimiles with the faces of the ones you love. It's haunting, devastating stuff; a very different kind of do-it-yourself.

You work, you sleep, and then you die. That's how it's got to be. So if you're going to toil mercilessly for the man, you can at least hope for an interesting employee. And what's more interesting than the weirdo threesome at the centre of Servant of the Lake?

Developer Rusty Lake's eponymous series returned this year with another macabre blend of point-and-click puzzling and room-escape-style adventure. And it's a corker. Perhaps not as dazzlingly experimental as some other recent entries, but it's still a delightfully grim time as you work through your three days of servitude, butlering for the oddball Vanderboom family. Beware the blood moon.

Available on: PC (Steam), PlayStation 5, Switch 2, Xbox Series X/S.

You work, you sleep, and then you die. But! What if you dream of breaking free? Escaping the chains of drudgery? That's the starting point of Denshattack!, a Jet Set Radio-inspired adventure all about sticking it to the man. Or, more specifically, a shady megacorp trying to suppress human ingenuity, creativity, and spirit. It is, as Dom put it in their five-star Denshattack! review, a game of "anti-capitalist, pop-punk optimism", which sees you careening across a colourful Japan - as a speeding, trick-flipping train, of all things - to bring the Miraidō corporation to its knees. Stylish and satisfying.

Available on: PC (Steam), PlayStation 5, Switch 2, Xbox Series X/S.

Umph-umph-umph-umph umph-umph-umph-umph. Oh yeah, that's the good stuff. If you want to shoot things and see them explode, and do that with immense satisfaction and a hypnotic, pulsating electronic soundtrack, this is the one. Sektori takes the feedback of a satisfying trigger pull and boils that down to its purest essence, often in spiralling, kaleidoscopic form. Hitting a full set of letters - M-I-R-A-G-E, S-E-K-T-O-R-I, etc. - summons a rainbow of action that feels like genuine ecstasy. Pure blasting and survival.

Available on: Android, iOS, PC (Steam), Switch, Switch 2, Xbox Series X/S.

Umph-umph-umph-umph umph-umph-umph-umph. But this time imagine it's a disco beat. Let's breathe in the night, bask in those pulsing lights, and pretend we'll live this moment forever. Or, you know, we'll just shuffle our way through the throng of the dancefloor like a massive game of sokoban. That latter's the premise of developer Miracle Tea's Club Soko; a game about traversing an underground nightclub - one shunt at a time - to discover the secrets it holds. And it's good 'un, bringing a thinkier rhythm to its pounding beat.

Most people think of fishing as a calm escape from the hustle and bustle of daily life - that's certainly the case of GTA 6. But relaxation, I say, is for posers, which brings us to How to Fish. In developer Dazed Games' silly, chaotic physics-based fishing sim, you're given an arsenal of guns to unload upon fish and foe alike. Beware the seagulls, who have learned to love the taste of human flesh over the bounty of humble chip shops. It's great fun, especially co-operatively, so grab some pals and have a blast.

Available on: PC (Steam), PlayStation 5, Switch 2, Xbox Series X/S.

Neasied, the island where Queen's Domain unfolds, might not be as dizzyingly expansive as some open-worlds, but the stuff it manages to pack into a relatively compact space is astonishing. This is a game crammed with secrets, rich with mystery; a world so dense with enigmas it's a joy to explore. There's more than a touch of FromSoftware to its broader retro-inspired combat rhythms, but there are hints of Zelda and Metroidvania too. It's one of the best games this year - full of surprise and wonder as its absurdly elaborate puzzle threads unfurl - and an extraordinary adventure.

Money too tight to mention? How about ditching the life of crime for a spot of restaurant entrepreneurialism? That's the idea behind Nivalis Night - and, actually, the life of crime is still an option. First seen in 2020 courier adventure Cloudpunk, Nivalis is a striking, sprawling cyberpunk dystopia of flying cars, neon lights, and suffocating smog that sets itself apart with its lo-fi style. And in this year's follow-up, you can finally get out of your car and live in the world. But it's not just a nifty first-person tycoon sim, it's a game of stories, and developer Ion Lands imbues its city with real warmth and humanity; a perfect accompaniment, it turns out, to all the Vangelis-like synths and sushi.

Available on: PC (Steam), PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S.

There's street racing and then there's developer Milestone's Screamer. It's a narrative-focused racing game which leans into a far arcadier sort of drive compared to the more realistic Grand Theft Auto. It's also got a great anime aesthetic, so you can live out any MF Ghost fantasies you have tucked away. Or maybe Initial D for the drifting aficionados among you.

Okay, fine, so it's more of a slap than a punch, but developer Double Fine's Thank You Bus Driver still packs in more violence than you'd expect from a driving sim. This game jam experiment turned full release feels a bit like a first-person Crazy Taxi as you try and get passengers to their destination before time runs out. Happy passengers means more 'thank yous', which means more time for more pick-ups, and it's a brilliantly chaotic loop.

It's got bags of personality - its scrappy black-and-white doodle town and oddball passengers are a treat - and a bunch of neat hooks. Most notably, HQ intermittently imposes new rules - no talking! No toenail clipping! - which is where slaps come in. Rulebreakers need discipline if you don't want to lose your 'thank yous', so you spin around and wallop them about the chops. Just try not to drive off the road.

Sure, Leonida's landscape holds its own kind of sunkissed beauty, but The Game Bakers has created something really special with Cairn. It might be a game focused on mountain climbing, but it's hard not to stop and admire the view whenever you reach the next plateau. As you make your way up Mount Kami, you're greeted with almost constant lovely views, some of the most gorgeous vistas released this year. Cairn is the sort of game that warrants putting the controller down and just taking the sights in for a moment or two.

As a doctor of dubious repute once said, where we're going, we don't need roads! So wave goodbye to metropolitan squalor, farewell to suburban suffocation, and hello the great outdoors. Over the Hill, the follow-up to Funselektor's brilliant Art of Rally, is all about off-road roaming through the vast, untamed wilderness. It's a game of purposeful meandering and patient delights and you explore the secrets of, among other places, its starlit deserts and rolling forest hills. You'll bounce your 4x4 over rocks, crawl sluggishly up hills, and winch through squelching mud, and it's all so slow. That's deliberate though. And as the result, amid the gentle strum of soothing guitar, is a wonderfully meditative game.

There are few other games released this year that nail the ruthlessness of vehicle-to-vehicle combat like Airframe Ultra. An absolutely gorgeous battle-racer in which you take control of a bulky airframe. You can smack the daylights out of other riders with chains, bats, and other makeshift melee weapons. Failing that, ramming into people at full speed works wonders too. A gritty game that pleases the eye and nourishes the violent soul.

Life, eh? One minute it's all Leonida sunshine and motorboating, the next you're on the run. But it's how you deal with each roll of the dice that matters. And that's very much the premise of Cosmo D's wonderfully weird Moves of the Diamond Hand. It's back to the jazz-drenched, oddball world of Off-Peak City we go, this time as election day looms. The legendary Circus X has returned to town and you want a place in its show. What follows is a first-person narrative adventure that takes the tabletop RPG-inspired dice-throwing of Cosmo D's acclaimed 2022 game Betrayal at Club Low and turns it into something richer, deeper, wilder, and weirder. And yes, it's got a killer soundtrack.

Available on: PC (Steam, Epic), PlayStation 5, Switch 2, Xbox Series X/S.

I see you smirking, pal - cut it out. Early on in GTA 6 you're tasked with hosing off a boat in the marina. And if that scratches an unknown itch within you, PowerWash Simulator 2 has all the nozzle blasting you could hope for. Yes, it's a slightly older one, but all 2026's DLC means it still feels new. Even more grimy buildings and filthy objects are waiting for a hero with a soap dispenser, just begging for a deep clean. Is that you? Then live your dream!

Browsing the tourist traps and fashion emporiums for the next big look is all well and good, but have you ever considered taking matters into your own hands? Dressmaking might not seem like the most appealing way to spend your time if you're not particularly into wearing dresses or seamstressing. (It's me, I'm that person.) However, Dressmaker and the sheer amount of clothing combinations it makes possible are a very pleasant surprise.

Weaved throughout it all is a story that sees you designing dresses for different characters and the events they're attending, then stitching your ideas to life piece by piece with tactile minigames, And you can design your own dresses too! I can only imagine GTA 6's Jason might even enjoy designing a dress or two for Lucia if he had Dressmaker on his JoyStation...

Creator, writer, director, composer, and 'perceptual architect' Johannes Zuch's Tenebrae Lux might be wowing the critics in GTA 6, but for my money this year's best game-within-a-game is Forbidden Solitaire. It's 2019 and you've stumbled upon an old copy of notorious CD-ROM adventure Forbidden Solitaire. It was pulled from the shelves amid some mysterious deaths, but that won't stop you from playing. What follows is a wonderfully creepy haunted media yarn - told across police records, press clippings, 90s local news reports, grisly photos, even a recreation of a cheesy true-crime-style mystery show - which brilliantly evokes the 90s multimedia boom with its lurid, awkward pre-rendered game-in-a-game visuals. It also, in case you're wondering, plays a damn good game of solitaire.

I like birds. I like them so much that it's at the top of my bucket list to find a Wonga Pigeon one day. So, you can imagine how I felt finding out that GTA 6 has over 60 birds to spot, alongside binoculars and a phone camera to fulfil my bird-watching dreams.

Before GTA 6 however, there came a lovely little multiplayer game called Flock Around developed by Friend Shaped Games that lets you explore multiple biomes while trying to snap photographs of birds from around the world and fill out your guide book. I was enamoured by its simple loop of wandering around and waiting for birds to strike a perfect pose, take selfies with them, and maybe even watching them empty their insides all over your friends. I don't think it's possible to be pooped on by a bird in GTA 6, so by default, Flock Around takes the crown as one of the best games for snapping pics of birds this year.

GTA 6 might use the gorgeous relationship between Lucia and Jason to demonstrate what basic human empathy looks like in a culture that promotes instant gratification and selfishness, but Penguin Colony uses a, erm, penguin colony to highlight the existential dangers of fascism and the pervasive threat of racism and colonialism. I know which piece of social commentary I prefer.

It's not hard to have a better cleaning regimen than GTA 6's Jason: the man apparently isn't aware his nethers need a wash too. But designer Robert Yang's Rinse and Repeat's has a solution for those struggling at showertime: get someone else to help you. Rinse and Repeat is a "steamy first-person showering game ... about consent and safety" and got a remaster earlier this year as part of Yang's Radiator Forever compilation. Yang's award-winning work frequently explores queer subculture, and the NSFW compilation also contains three other games - Hurt Me Plenty, Succulent, and Stick Shift - with more to follow. Better yet, it's completely free.

Forget cracking safes in GTA 6, what about cracking the codes in a machine that seems to be, well, more than it seems? This is the hook in Inkle's TR-49, a game about a machine that once lurked in the depths of Bletchley Park's legendary codebreaking vaults, alongside the Enigma Machine. This machine works differently. It's effectively an archive you search around using codes in the format of letter-letter, number-number, hence TR-49. But the codes you use and what you find, and how you find them: that's up to you. And sure, there's probably not gold bullion on the other side of this code, but perhaps there's something even more rewarding.
