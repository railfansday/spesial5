Apple’s Music Hall signals a bigger strategic plan

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Why would Apple launch a concert venue?

This is the question I’ve been asking myself since it inaugurated its Apple Music Hall, nestled underneath its U.K. headquarters at London’s Battersea Power Station, in late September. Not just any performance space, this intimate 600-person, 48-speaker hall features Apple’s Spatial Audio technology, which uses Dolby Atmos and dynamic head tracking to turn flat stereo sound into a three-dimensional, 360-degree experience.

To my eye, the Music Hall is not just another listening room. It’s the start of an elaborate world-building exercise for Apple Music, designed to engage a community of hardcore fans. In doing so, Apple is looking to increase streaming subscriptions, convert artists to recording on its technology, and eventually challenge everyone from Google Home to Sonos for ownership of our in-home sound delivery systems.

An uphill battle

In just eight years since entering the TV market, Apple has shifted from startup streamer to a television mecca, delivering hit after hit including Severance, Slow Horses, Silo, Pluribus, Ted Lasso, and now Widow’s Bay. Astounding.

Meanwhile, in music it’s played second fiddle to Spotify, which not only boasts three times as many subscribers, but is first with innovative ideas like its Year End Review and the exclusive content serious listeners crave most. The Music Hall is Apple turning up the volume.

The risks here are real. While global spend on IRL brand experiences grew 8.3% to $138.9 billion in 2025 and is projected to rise a further 10.3% in 2026, the highways to hell have far outstripped the stairways to heaven. Samsung 837, the company’s former experiential space in New York City’s Meatpacking District, succeeded in attracting mothers with young children looking for rainy day distractions but few potential customers. Many car companies—Lexus, Genesis, Cadillac—have tried their hand at luxury lounges, only to be reminded that consumers are looking to them for, well, cars.


Those that are true to their roots, however, are hitting all the right notes. A24, the film and TV production company, experienced a huge brand win with its acquisition last year of the historic Cherry Lane Theater in NYC’s West Village. This has been a launchpad for new and experimental content that in turn has led to greater visibility for the studio, deeper fan engagement, and a two-year brand partnership with Dior. Porsche’s Experience Centers delight driving enthusiasts and attract new customers with authentic racing outings.

In seemingly sparing no expense to create a world-class performance space, Apple is signaling to audiophiles everywhere that it is serious about building the world of Apple Music.

Building a successful brand world

Three essential elements go into building a successful brand world. The first is the Functional Offer, quite simply what the brand makes. For example, Volvo builds cars. The second is the Emotional Promise, which is how a brand is experienced emotionally. Through good Scandinavian design Volvo makes people feel great driving them. The third is the Cultural Quest. This is the way the brand engages with the world or a larger topic it constantly explores. For Volvo it is to have no one die in its vehicles. Ever.

All brands achieve the first; many achieve the second; very few achieve the third. The Music Hall gives Apple a route to achieving all three: not only producing and distributing music (offer), or making people feel good while listening (promise), but in our increasingly isolated, AI-fueled world, bringing music fans together around the highest quality, most authentic audio experiences, be they physical or digital (quest).

The key is cultivating a community engaging in meaningful participation around a brand world that is authentic to music.

The key question is, therefore, “How might Apple cultivate this passionate community of music fans?”

Lean into the community

Apple Music will need to do more than deliver music. It will need to become synonymous with the production and presentation of the world’s finest content. Opening with Elton John was a signal of intent.

Baby Keem, Skye Newman, Troye Sivan, Arlo Parks, Burna Boy, Chase & Status, Charli XCX—a real lineup to be sure. Now imagine that with its war chest, Apple starts attracting more prominent acts, and that music (sounding better recorded on Spatial Audio) is available only through a subscription on Apple Music.

The athletic wear brands like On and Bandit have been very successful with their run clubs. Might Apple establish its version? In partnership with Apple Pay and a major credit card company, early access to events is not hard to imagine. Nor are listening parties at the coolest venues for those who can’t attend events in person.

What’s at stake?

In its services division, Apple has shown with Pay and Health that it likes opportunities with huge addressable markets that plug in nicely to its hardware ecosystem. The global home audio hardware market, whose current players have set a fairly low bar, is valued at approximately $43.2 billion in 2026. If Apple can capture a good share of smart home audio and combine that with a greater share of the $54.54 billion global audio streaming market, the dollars become very real.

By striking the right chord between premium audio hardware and subscription streaming, Apple is perfectly positioned to turn this massive market overlap into music to its investors’ ears.
