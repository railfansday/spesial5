Find out what medical care and health checks the shelter provides as standard. Some initial questions could be what veterinary care your cat has received (if any), including any parasite treatment, which vaccinations they have had, if they are microchipped or neutered, and whether they have any known health concerns or ongoing medications.

Although it can be tempting to let your new feline friend explore everywhere straight away, for many cats, a smaller and more predictable environment can feel much safer.

Consider keeping your new cat in one room before slowly giving them access to the rest of the home. During the first few days, keep windows secured and doors closed, and make sure they have everything they need close by.

Many pets need time to adjust to being alone in a new home. Start by gradually introducing short periods of separation and pairing alone time with positive experiences, such as treats, toys, or enrichment activities. Keeping departures and arrivals calm and predictable can also help build confidence and reduce stress.
