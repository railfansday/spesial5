The Lakers’ wing depth, adaptability are becoming strengths this season

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Entering 2026-27, perhaps the most consequential unknown resided on the wings for the Los Angeles Lakers. They knew they could count on Luka Dončić and Austin Reaves to provide All-Star-caliber (and better) production in the backcourt. They were banking on Walker Kessler, at worst, providing high-level rim protection and interior play-finishing, based on his tenure with the Utah Jazz.

Filling in the gaps at small forward and power forward, as well as minutes off the bench at those spots, was a more dubious proposition. Jake LaRavia was coming off a very poor second half in 2025-26. Matisse Thybulle had battled injuries in recent years while Ziaire Williams was yet to establish firm footing through five NBA seasons. Quentin Grimes' role and performance had been a little sporadic the past few seasons.

Yet five weeks into 2026-27, all of them are donning different caps and playing darn good basketball for the 15-3 Lakers — the product of a team-first, resilient mindset.

"I think they're all really grateful to be here, to be Lakers. They're all really grateful to be on this team and be part of the group. I know [Matisse] has expressed that to me on a couple of occasions. For [Ziaire], Matisse, [Quentin], they've experienced adversity in their careers in different ways," head coach JJ Redick said after Monday's win over the Utah Jazz." I think that gratitude piece is like…they're just all in a situation where they're putting the team first and they're putting their egos aside. That stuff matters. I think it's one of the reasons our group has had early success because we said on day one, we wanted this season to be about the team and not any one individual one of us and, so far, it's been that."

Averaging 2.3 steals and 1.0 blocks per game while shooting 40.5% from deep, Thybulle has been a defensive menace and knocked down open 3s when the ball swings his way. Williams has assumed a lot of lofty point-of-attack duties and excelled as a spot-up shooter (42.1% beyond the arc) and cutter (73.5% on 2-pointers).

Grimes scaled up his offensive role while Reaves was sidelined for two weeks and recorded three 20-point outings over that span (including a career-high 47 against the Charlotte Hornets). He's also shooting 40.3% from deep. LaRavia has rebounded from a bumpy end to last season to shoot 43.2% beyond the arc, relentlessly attack the offensive glass and be a reliable help defender.

Dončić, Reaves and Kessler may be leading the way but Los Angeles wouldn't have charged to the top of the league without their versatile quartet on the wings.

"I think just everyone is willing to sacrifice for the same goal. We just have a lot of adaptable players, I think," Jake LaRavia told reporters postgame on Monday. "A lot of it's lineup-based, matchup-based, so whoever the coaches think should start that night is who's going to start. I think, as players, we're all willing to be kind of thrown in and out of the lineup and it doesn't really change much for us as players. You just keep the same mindset and keep the same goal and just go out there and play hard."
