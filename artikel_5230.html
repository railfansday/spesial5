XRP Ledger Breaks Vote Stall on Bank-Style Lending: What It Unlocks for XRP Holders

Three XRP Ledger amendments linked to native lending have each gained two validator votes since Nov. 17, according to XRPScan data. As of Nov. 20, 2026, Single Asset Vault (XLS-65) had 25 votes, and Lending Protocol (XLS-66) and the LendingProtocolV1_1 patch had 24 votes each.

XRPScan data shows that LendingProtocolV1_1 has been up for a vote since Sept. 29, 2026, when it came with the v3.4.0 release. According to the patch's voting history, it had about 19 votes for most of October. It started to gain votes again in late October. XRP was trading at $0.96 on Friday.

Lending is built directly into the XRP Ledger's code, with no third-party smart contracts. According to Ripple, lenders deposit into a Single Asset Vault, and the Lending Protocol lets loan brokers use that capital "to issue loans and track repayments onchain."

The model is close to how banks lend. Loans have fixed terms and a repayment schedule, and credit brokers vet borrowers before the loans are issued. On Nov. 19, Clearpool showed the full lending flow on the XRPL Devnet with Ripple's RLUSD stablecoin. In the demo, "An approved lender funds a private vault, and a borrower accepts and repays a loan underwritten offchain by a broker," Clearpool wrote.

Through Single Asset Vault (XLS-65), XRP holders will be able to deposit their coins into shared liquidity pools, and institutional borrowers will take loans from those pools at a fixed rate. After the loan is repaid, "the lender redeems at a higher share price," according to the Clearpool demo.

The amendments still need more votes before they can activate. "An amendment needs more than 80% support, held for 14 days, to activate," XRPScan says on its amendment pages. For the current set of 35 validators, the site puts the threshold at "more than 80% of 35 votes, so 29 needed."
