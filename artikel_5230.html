TL;DR: Fold reported $4.2 million in third-quarter revenue and a $2.6 million net loss. The company says Bitcoin gift-card sales helped lift custody and trading revenue, while newer products aim to encourage customers to keep balances on its platform.

Selling someone their first Bitcoin product is one commercial challenge. Giving them enough reasons to keep using the same financial app is another. Fold’s latest results show a company trying to connect those two activities.

That approach could create a more durable customer relationship, but its economics depend on retention and the cost of rewards. New accounts, product launches and transaction volume are useful indicators; they are not interchangeable with profit.

In its November 10 quarterly announcement, Fold said TikTok Shop had become its largest gift-card and customer-acquisition channel. It attributed an approximate 270% increase in custody and trading revenue to gift-card sales.

This is the company’s description of the channel’s performance, rather than evidence that every gift-card recipient becomes a recurring customer. The distinction is commercially important: a promotional purchase can generate revenue once, while a lasting relationship may support repeat activity.

Fold’s explanation of its rewards model says eligible spending earns actual Bitcoin that can be retained or withdrawn. That makes the product easier to understand than a reward that first requires conversion from a proprietary points system. It also leaves the dollar value of the reward exposed to Bitcoin’s price after it is received.

Fold also reported more than 88,500 verified accounts and approximately 2,500 early-access credit-card holders. Management is emphasizing customer balances alongside transactions following the launch of Fold Reserve and Lead Bank spending accounts.

For a financial platform, retained balances can create opportunities for recurring revenue and broader product use. The relevant follow-up is whether that opportunity exceeds the cost of acquiring customers, servicing accounts and funding incentives. A larger account base alone does not answer it.

NewsBTC’s report on BitPay adding familiar mobile checkout options examines another attempt to reduce friction around crypto purchases. Easier entry and lasting engagement are related goals, with different measures of success.

Fold’s Reserve product page describes Reserve as stored value rather than an FDIC-insured deposit account. The balance determines access to reward tiers; it does not itself earn interest. The page also explains that advertised rewards depend on conditions such as qualifying balances and eligible spending.

That distinction matters when a company discusses gathering customer assets. A spending account at a partner bank, a stored-value balance and Bitcoin custody may sit inside one app while carrying different terms and protections. Product presentation should not blur them into a single type of savings account.

The company’s Bitcoin help page identifies BitGo as its Bitcoin custodian and says Bitcoin purchases and rewards do not receive FDIC or SIPC protection. NewsBTC’s recent look at BitGo’s results provides background on that infrastructure provider.

Fold’s adjusted EBITDA was a $4.5 million loss, compared with a $5.5 million loss in the preceding quarter. That improvement is relevant, but it remains a negative adjusted result and should be considered alongside the reported net loss.

The strongest future evidence would connect customer acquisition to retained balances, sustained activity and better unit economics. Investors should also watch whether incentives remain commercially affordable as the products grow.

The latest update identifies promising distribution and engagement experiments. It leaves the central question open: can Fold turn easier access to Bitcoin rewards into a consistently profitable financial-services relationship?
