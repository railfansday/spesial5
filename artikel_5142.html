Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Binance Wins $6.5B Freezing Order Over Hack That Can Be Served via the Blockchain

Binance has secured a $6.5 billion worldwide freezing order against the hackers behind last month's attack on the exchange, which the FBI has blamed on North Korea.

The injunction was issued by the courts of the Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM) against the unknown perpetrators, Binance's lawyers at Gibson Dunn confirmed, according to City AM.

The order breaks new legal ground. It can be served directly via the blockchain, and market participants will be notified of a "living list" of 26,000 wallet addresses identified as holding stolen assets, Gibson Dunn partner Kendall Day said. That will enable "those assets to be frozen rapidly even as they are laundered at pace," Day said.

Binance has also secured proprietary injunctions and delivery-up orders, including against parties holding frozen assets who are not accused of wrongdoing, according to City AM. Decrypt has reached out to Gibson Dunn for comment.

"We will continue using every lawful tool available to trace, freeze and recover stolen assets, support our partners in returning funds where possible, and keep those assets out of the hands of the attackers," Dugan Bliss, Binance's deputy general counsel, said in a statement reported by Bloomberg.

The FBI has attributed the October 29 hack to TraderTraitor, a group linked to North Korea, which has denied involvement. The attackers gained access after an engineer was phished through a fake job offer, according to Bloomberg. The Secret Service has joined the FBI's investigation.

About $2.85 billion in funds connected to the hack has been frozen, including by Ripple and World Liberty Financial, according to Bloomberg. Stablecoin issuers Tether and Circle froze $2.7 billion shortly after the attack, and the Justice Department said last week it had obtained court authorization to seize or restrain $1.2 billion of that.

The hackers have used thousands of wallets and multiple blockchains to launder the rest, and wallets linked to the hack began moving Ethereum again this week after two weeks of inactivity.

Binance has said it fully covered the $6.3 billion in customer assets affected and absorbed the losses itself.
