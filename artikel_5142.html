Nasdaq-listed BTCS has sold 27,300 ETH for $43.8 million, using the full amount to repay its Aave loans as crypto treasury firms face pressure from falling prices. The sale cut the companyâs Ether holdings by more than half and reduced its debt by about 81%.

However, the move comes as Ethereum has fallen 25% in November, bringing its price close to $1,600.

According to its Form 8-K filing with the SEC, BTCS sold about 27,300 ETH on November 20 for $43.8 million. The company used all the money to repay part of its loan on Aave, a DeFi lending platform.

The sale accounted for about 53% of BTCSâs Ether holdings before the transaction. After using the proceeds to repay its loan, the company reduced its Aave debt from $54.3 million to $10.5 million, cutting its borrowings by around 81%.

This also lowered its loan-to-value (LTV) ratio to about 32%. In simple terms, BTCS now has less debt compared with the value of its collateral, reducing the risk of forced selling if ETH prices fall further.

As of November 22, BTCS still held about 24,245 ETH and reported around $61 million in combined cash and digital assets. The company also disclosed $17.9 million in convertible notes and temporarily withdrew its funds from liquidity pools managed by its Imperium DeFi business.

BTCS is not the only corporate holder reducing its Ether exposure this year. CoinShares sold 6,562 ETH worth $16.6 million in October and fully exited its ETH treasury to support a $40 million share buyback program.

GameSquare Holdings also sold 1,209 ETH in July to fund share repurchases while its stock traded below its net asset value.

This approach is very different from companies like BitMine Immersion Technologies and SharpLink, which are still buying and holding ETH. As of November 22, 2026, BitMine held 6,085,564 ETH worth about $10.4 billion, while SharpLink held 896,003 ETH worth around $1.53 billion as of November 16.

Even after a 25% drop in ETH price this month, companies still hold more than eight million ETH in total.

As of now, Ethereum was trading around $ 1,692, down roughly 66% from its August 2025 record high near $4,954. The sharp decline has increased pressure on companies that borrowed against ETH or hold the asset as a major treasury reserve.

BTCSâs decision shows one way firms are responding: sell part of the reserve, reduce leverage, and leave a smaller debt burden.

With more than 24,000 ETH still on its balance sheet, BTCS has not abandoned its Ethereum strategy.
