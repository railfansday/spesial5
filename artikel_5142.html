Weekend stock tokens predict Monday open, but guessing up scores 55%

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Bitquery’s pre-futures weekend test found no clear directional edge, while Nasdaq’s proposed weekday expansion remains conditional.

Stock tokens kept trading through weekends, but their prices did not reliably guide the next stock-market open in a new study of 121 stocks.

Blockchain data provider Bitquery's Nov. 3 analysis found that Sunday token moves measured before equity-index futures reopened correctly called 54% of qualifying openings, so simply predicting that every stock would open higher scored 55%.

More hours can give investors another opportunity to transact, but they do not show that the resulting prices predict the next opening better.

Bitquery followed 17 weekends beginning July 10 through Oct. 30, with the final stock opening on Nov. 2. It recorded 791 stock-opening observations across those stocks, and its direction test kept the 533 openings that moved at least 0.5% from Friday's close.

Token moves pointed the right way on 286 of those observations, compared with 291 correct calls for always predicting a rise. Because more openings rose than fell, a 50% coin-toss benchmark alone would flatter the tokens' result.

The study also used balanced accuracy, which averages success on rises and falls. The tokens scored 53%, with a reported 95% interval of 44% to 62%.

Across all 791 observations, including smaller moves, the median error was 1.2% using token moves versus 0.9% assuming the stock opened at Friday's close. Bitquery put the excess error at 0.3%, with a 95% range of 0.2% to 0.5%.

A separate check using prices just before 8 p.m. returned 59% balanced accuracy, with a 95% range of 53% to 65%, across 220 openings. But it also raised the weekend-volume threshold to $1 million and changed the movement filter.

Listings needed at least $10,000 of weekend trading and a trade in the hour before each cutoff. The study excluded openings affected by stock splits or ex-dividend dates and crypto-sensitive stocks such as Strategy and Coinbase, then combined issuers using weekend-volume weights.

Earlier perpetual-market research reported improved forecasts from final weekend prices in a different sample. Different instruments, liquidity filters and observation times prevent that result from serving as a direct rebuttal to this test.

Nasdaq's Oct. 30 update described Dec. 6 as the expected transition to 23-hour, five-day trading, subject to clearing and market-data infrastructure being ready. Its related market-data proposals also remain subject to regulatory approval.

Its member implementation notice describes an added 9 p.m. to 4 a.m. ET session requiring new member ports.

If the rollout proceeds, wider conventional weekday hours would narrow one stock-token advantage for investors whose brokers and jurisdictions permit both routes. Weekend trading and the ability to hold tokens in a blockchain wallet remain separate benefits to assess.

For example, Kraken's xStocks disclosure excludes the US, Canada, UK and Australia, permits compatible-wallet withdrawals and says holders lack ownership and voting rights in the underlying shares.

The next useful evidence would hold timing and eligibility constant, compare against available market benchmarks, and measure executable results after costs. Until then, an available weekend price should not be mistaken for a demonstrated forecasting advantage.
