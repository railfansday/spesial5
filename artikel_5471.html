A Fed governor warned AI agents could speed up bank runs that unfold in minutes

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Michael Barr said AI agents could move deposits at high speed during stress periods, overwhelming liquidity management

Federal Reserve Governor Michael Barr warned in a speech for a conference in Milan on Monday that AI agents could accelerate bank runs that unfold in minutes by moving customer deposits at high speed.

"Where customers once needed to execute transfers, AI agents could instantaneously detect yield differentials or stability signals and autonomously move funds at high speed, potentially accelerating runs that unfold in minutes, overwhelming traditional liquidity management and regulatory intervention timelines," Barr said, according to Bloomberg.

Barr said major banks report finding AI agents that attempt to take actions in retail customer accounts, with the bank unable to verify authenticity, consent or alignment with customer instructions. He called for safeguards such as tight authentication, verification of explicit consent for particular actions, technical limits on what agents are able to do, and restrictions grounded in both rules and values.

Barr described himself as an optimist about the long-term promise of AI. He said AI can strengthen the financial system only if it also supports financial stability.

The speech also covered the AI investment boom. Barr cited the Fed's latest Financial Stability Report, issued Nov. 13, which estimates that the potential maximum nominal value of off-balance-sheet liabilities tied to the AI buildout now exceeds $3 trillion. He said on-balance-sheet debt of the seven largest AI firms is expected to exceed $800 billion before year's end.

Barr also pointed to more than $100 billion in cumulative equity investments by large cloud-computing firms and chipmakers in AI labs and cloud start-ups. He described these as a form of “circular financing,” in which the smaller firms use the money to rent or buy chips and computing power from the investor.

On trading, Barr said traders who rely on a small set of similar AI models face the risk of herding behavior, in which strategies become similar and react to news in sync. He said a market with many similar models that share the same flaw risks failures with systemic consequences.

The International Monetary Fund warned in October that a crash in AI-related stocks could threaten global financial stability. The IMF cited rising debt levels, concentrated market exposures and circular financing arrangements across the sector.
