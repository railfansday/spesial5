The U.S. will tighten AI chip restrictions after Nvidia's Jensen Huang saga put the spotlight on a loophole

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Free daily briefing on global business news.

A new rule follows weeks of scrutiny of chip exports, and as Trump prepares to meet with Chinese President Xi Jinping

The Commerce Department’s Bureau of Industry and Security on Monday issued an interim final rule that will require licenses to export advanced AI chips to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil. The rule also restricts remote access to U.S. chips through data centers overseas.

“The rule is designed to ensure that existing restrictions on access to sensitive American technology cannot be circumvented through remote computing arrangements involving data centers in third countries,” the bureau said in a statement. It said the Treasury Department and the National Security Council wrote the rule and directed its release.

Under the rule text, a license will be required to send chips to data centers that supply significant volumes of computing power to companies headquartered in Macau or Country Group D:5. Data centers, foreign foundries and chip exporters must perform due diligence on customers' direct or indirect ownership or control. Data centers already providing such computing power must obtain a license within 90 days of the effective date or face future export restrictions. Commercial recommender systems, such as those used by online retailers and social media companies, are exempt.

The five-country requirement applies in addition to existing license requirements, the rule says. It does not apply to chips destined for companies headquartered in the United States, or with a U.S.-headquartered ultimate parent, provided reporting requirements are met. Commerce will review applications case by case. The rule describes the five countries as presenting a higher-than-normal risk of diversion.

The rule also formally rescinds the regulations implementing the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule, which it says was never enforced.

The rule takes effect on its publication in the Federal Register, with comments due 60 days after. It had not been posted as of 9 a.m. ET Monday.

Nvidia $NVDA stock slid by as much as 2.9% on Monday, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index lost up to 5.5%.

The rule is meant to limit Chinese companies' access to advanced AI chips. It comes as President Donald Trump is set to meet Chinese President Xi Jinping on Thursday in Shenzhen, and as pressure grows on Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to tighten export controls.

The rule follows weeks of scrutiny of chip exports. Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang stepped down last week and acknowledged a relationship with Alice Huang, a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed who is suspected of spying for China. Megaspeed is accused of illegally shipping export-controlled chips into China and has denied wrongdoing. Senate Banking Committee Chair Tim Scott, a South Carolina Republican, has asked Jensen Huang to testify on Dec. 3 and Lutnick on Dec. 10. Lutnick has agreed to appear.

Before the rule, U.S. export controls covered the physical transfer of chips but not remote access to them, Cassia King, a senior researcher at the Institute for AI Policy and Strategy, said last week.
