Op-ed: Plug-in solar expands access to renewables, but needs a strong safety framework

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

By Michael Becker, Global Business Director, Energy Storage/Energy & Power, CSA | October 29, 2026

Access to plug-in solar is becoming increasingly available across North America. As more consumers explore technologies such as balcony solar and plug-in photovoltaic (PIPV) systems, these emerging solutions have the potential to make renewable energy more accessible and support broader participation in the energy transition. Solar remains one of the most accessible and promising renewable energy tools being added to the grid, and plug-in solar products may help expand that access to consumers who have traditionally faced economic barriers to participation.

As adoption grows, however, products designed to be convenient and accessible must still be treated as electrical products that interact with a building’s infrastructure. Product design, connectors, wiring, system integration and installation practices all play an important role in safety and performance. Expanding access and maintaining confidence in emerging technologies are complementary goals, and achieving both will require continued attention to the frameworks that support responsible deployment.

Clear safety standards, code-compliant installation practices and appropriate regulatory requirements will be essential to helping ensure that innovation can reach more consumers safely and responsibly. Solar-related issues are often associated not with the panels themselves, but with connectors, wiring and improper installation practices. A major misconception is that consumers can simply purchase a solar panel with a standard plug and connect it to a household outlet. Responsible deployment of PIPV systems depends on several complementary elements working together: products evaluated to appropriate safety standards, compliance with the National Electrical Code (NEC), qualified installation practices, and applicable state and local requirements.

No single element alone can provide the framework needed to support broad and responsible adoption.

An important emerging component of that framework is UL 3700, which is helping establish a product-safety benchmark specifically for plug-in photovoltaic systems. UL 3700 can provide manufacturers with a clearer benchmark for product development and evaluation, while giving regulators greater confidence as they consider how plug-in PV should be addressed within electrical codes and regulatory frameworks. Continued industry involvement in the development of UL 3700 will also be important as the standard evolves toward broader consensus and adoption. At the same time, UL 3700 is only one part of the overall approach. It does not replace NEC compliance, qualified installation practices or applicable state and local requirements that govern the deployment of electrical systems.

Emerging safety benchmarks are already moving from concept to practice. CSA has certified a microinverter to UL 3700, providing an early example of how the standard can be applied to products entering the market. The certification demonstrates how a consistent benchmark can be applied as new technologies enter the market, while helping support confidence among regulators, manufacturers and consumers as plug-in solar continues to develop.

The regulatory landscape for PIPV continues to evolve, with proposed changes to the NEC, state laws and local codes and ordinances aimed at facilitating the safe installation of these systems. As the industry works to establish appropriate requirements, keeping standards, codes and regulatory frameworks aligned with technological advancement will be critical to supporting the growth of PIPV products. Well-aligned requirements can help create a pathway for innovation while maintaining consistency in how emerging technologies are evaluated and deployed.

Technology itself continues to advance rapidly. Improvements in inverter technology are helping make solar more affordable while enabling greater integration of renewable energy into the grid. As inverters become more energy-dense and cost-effective, they can help lower the overall cost of PV systems and support broader adoption. New inverter capabilities can also provide valuable grid-support functions that help integrate more renewable resources and contribute to a more flexible and resilient electric grid. As these technologies evolve, standards, codes and regulatory requirements must continue evolving alongside them so that innovation and deployment can advance together.

Ultimately, greater accessibility brings greater responsibility. As plug-in solar technologies become easier for consumers to adopt, the safety frameworks that support them must keep pace with innovation. By developing appropriate product standards, maintaining NEC-compliant installation practices, utilizing qualified installers and advancing state and local requirements alongside technological progress, the industry can help expand access to renewable energy while maintaining the confidence of consumers, manufacturers, regulators installers and other stakeholders.

The statement “A major misconception is that consumers can simply purchase a solar panel with a standard plug and connect it to a household outlet.” is, in and of itself a misconception. If the plugin systems are safely designed, that is exactly what they are meant to prov ide the consumer–a solar panel with a plug they can connect to a household outlet. The fact that several millions of such systems have already been plugged in around the world without incident amply demonstrates their safety. All the hand-wringing about safety seems to be originating with existing vested interests that stand to loose significant revenues built on the existing over-complicated and over-regulated systems.

Helpful safety framing. One practical reader check is to separate product evaluation from deployment permission: UL 3700 does not replace NEC compliance, installation instructions, or state/local and utility rules, and effective dates vary by state. PluginForSolar.com maintains a U.S. state-by-state guide plus exact-system readiness checks so readers can verify those layers before buying.
