Mamdani's top 4 socialist promises met with reality checks as his first year in office nears its end

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Government-run supermarkets face intense scrutiny, and Joanne Trattoria owner Joe Germanotta criticized New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani’s plan to open city-owned grocery stores.

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani, a socialist, has begun delivering on several of his signature campaign promises in his first year leading the largest city in the U.S.

But the limited scope of those efforts raises questions about how much of his far-left agenda he can realistically implement.

Fox News Digital breaks down some of Mamdani's most controversial campaign promises and those he's made a priority in the first of his four years in office.

On the campaign trail, Mamdani pitched freezing monthly rent costs as a key strategy to lower the cost of housing for New Yorkers. Roughly 67.7% of those who live in the five boroughs of the city are renters, according to the most recent government data released in 2024.

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani listens to reporters' questions during a news conference at Athens Square Park Nov. 17, 2026, in New York City. (Michael M. Santiago/Getty Images)

"As mayor, I will freeze the rent every year that I’m in office. That’s a guarantee," Mamdani said in a campaign video posted near the beginning of his campaign in 2024.

True to his word, the New York City Rent Guidelines Board announced Mamdani’s promised freeze in June, detailing a 0% increase in one-year leases beginning between October 2026 and October 2027 and a 0% increase for two-year leases beginning in the same window.

Mamdani hailed the announcement as a significant victory for the city, highlighting the longer-lease lock.

"This is a historic victory for New York City tenants. After reviewing the data and hearing from New Yorkers across the city, the independent RGB has delivered a freeze on one-year leases, and the first-ever freeze on two-year leases in our city’s history," Mamdani’s office said in a press release in June.

"This is the relief that working people across our city deserve."

Notably, the freeze does not apply to all rental apartments in New York City, instead covering about 1 million rent-stabilized units. According to the Community Service Society, a nonprofit poverty advocacy group, rent-stabilized apartments make up 41% of the city’s rental units.

A residential apartment building near the Manhattan Bridge in the Brooklyn borough of New York July 3, 2025. (Michael Nagle/Bloomberg/Getty Images)

Although Mamdani’s city-run grocery stores haven’t yet launched in earnest, a pilot test of what they could look like has raised questions about whether the idea will meaningfully address the food scarcity issues Mamdani highlighted on the campaign trail.

"This month we got polling that showed that two-thirds of New Yorkers support city-owned grocery stores, a policy we’ve championed for months and would guarantee cheaper groceries," Mamdani said last year.

Even so, the first version of the city-run grocery store was met with complaints of long lines, shortages and disorganization when it launched in February.

Groceries sit behind Zohran Mamdani, mayor of New York, during an announcement on municipal grocery stores at a Campaign for Hunger community food distribution center in Brooklyn, N.Y., July 27, 2026. (Bloomberg via Getty Images)

In a recent interview, Mamdani said he still has plans to build on the idea.

"What we’re going to do is — in a city of more than a thousand supermarkets — we’re going to create five. … They’re cheaper supermarkets, supermarkets with a discount," the mayor said during a recent interview with The New York Times.

Mamdani has said he hopes to sell products at 30% below the market price.

The proposal has sparked skepticism from economists and commentators over how the city could deliver such steep discounts in an industry known for razor-thin profit margins. With grocery prices up about 33% nationwide since 2019, critics say the plan must explain how prices could undercut private retailers by 30% without significant public subsidies.

"This is completely insane. Also, not possible unless taxpayers are subsidizing all the groceries. Grocery store profits are tiny, a few percentage points a year, at best," Outkick founder Clay Travis wrote.

Like his grocery store initiative, Mamdani’s plan to implement free universal childcare has struggled to keep pace with demand, falling short of the full promise.

"Since the beginning of this campaign, I’ve championed universal childcare, making a commitment to deliver it to New Yorkers as young as six weeks to five years of age," Mamdani said in a campaign video in 2025.

New York City Mayor-elect Zohran Mamdani interacts with pre-K students at Friends of Crown Heights Education Center Nov. 13, 2025, in the Flatbush neighborhood of the Brooklyn borough in New York City. (Michael M. Santiago/Getty Images)

Mamdani argued that, without expanded childcare, the city would experience billions in lost productivity from workers who couldn’t enter the workforce.

"My administration will address this crisis by providing no-cost childcare to families with children as young as six weeks up until they’re five years of age."

To facilitate the plan, Mamdani said he believed using government spaces, co-locating with the Department of Education, subsidizing commercial rent and relaxing regulatory requirements would help bring down costs.

Mamdani launched his plans back in January, announcing a preliminary version of the plan. The rollout was limited to select areas in Manhattan, the Bronx, Brooklyn and Queens and only applied to 2-year-old children.

In an August press release, Mamdani said the program had a 2,000-seat capacity and over 5,700 applicants, leaving roughly 3,700 children on waitlists.

In the same release, Mamdani said he expected the city’s capacity to grow sixfold, going from 2,000 to 12,000.

The New York City Independent Budget Office, a government watchdog, confirmed the program’s $73 million cost and estimated that the expanded version would cost roughly $425 million in 2028.

According to the United States Census, there are approximately 489,000 children in New York City under the age of 5.

As of early 2026, New York Gov. Kathy Hochul has pushed back on Mayor Zohran Mamdani's proposal for citywide free buses, arguing the Metropolitan Transportation Authority cannot afford the estimated annual loss in fare revenue. (Nikolas Lanum/Fox News Digital)

Mamdani’s free bus plans have put him at odds with New York Gov. Kathy Hochul over whether the state should shoulder more of the burden for the mayor’s designs, leaving his campaign promises only partially fulfilled amid costs that would exceed his initial estimations.

Early on in his administration, Mamdani was asked when New Yorkers could expect the promise of free fares. Mamdani left the answer open-ended but pledged to fulfill his commitment by the end of his term.

"I have said when we ran this campaign … I’m making buses fast and free. I have said that by the time I am done being mayor, those will have been delivered," Mamdani said in January.

So far, Mamdani has taken steps toward increasing busing efficiency in NYC by developing 50 priority corridors for speed improvements. But discussions about making the buses free have stalled at the state level.

New York’s bus system is run through the Metropolitan Transportation Authority (MTA), a state-created public authority. The body is controlled by the governor, who appoints the MTA chair and its board.

The group makes controlling decisions about its fares.

Although she expressed sympathy toward Mamdani’s design, Hochul said she could not remove the sources of revenue from New York City’s public transportation.

New York Democratic Gov. Kathy Hochul is taking criticism over a bill she signed last week that empowers the state attorney general to investigate and fine groups deemed to be engaged in deceptive practices against labor union members. (Adam Gray/Pool/Getty Images)

"I cannot set forth a plan right now that takes money out of a system that relies on the fares of the buses and the subways. But can we find a path to make it more affordable for people who need help? Of course we can," Hochul said at a political retreat earlier this year.

Mamdani’s campaign originally estimated the plan would come with a $700 million price tag. However, the New York City Independent Budget Office released an estimate in June, finding that fully eliminating fares would cost New York State roughly $1.1 billion.

Even so, Mamdani said he believes the designs will ultimately be fulfilled.

"I continue to be excited at the work of making the slowest buses in America fast and free. And I appreciate the governor’s continued partnership in delivering on that agenda of affordability," Mamdani said at a news conference earlier this month.

Mamdani is approaching the end of his first year as mayor. He was elected in November 2025 and was sworn in on Jan. 1, 2026. His term runs through Jan. 1, 2030.
