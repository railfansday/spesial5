John Yoo on "Leaving a [University of] California that Rejects the West"

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Mostly law professors | Sometimes contrarian | Often libertarian | Always independent

Professor John Yoo explains his decision to leave the University of California at Berkeley

Professor John Yoo is leaving the law school at the University of California at Berkeley to join the School of Civic Leadership at the University of Texas at Austin. As I noted here, his departure means there will not be a single right-leaning professor teaching public law subjects at Berkeley--and no more than a handful (if that) at all of the UC schools combined.

Given the attention his move has received, Yoo has written an op-ed for the New York Post explaining his decision to choose Texas, and disputing any suggestion that he was driven out of Berkeley.

I'm leaving not because I feel driven out by Berkeley — anything but.

In my three decades there I've enjoyed working, on an individual level, with spirited, ambitious students and challenging, innovative scholars.

I'm moving because Berkeley, like its peers, has declined from a daring, risk-taking center of higher learning to one that wallows contentedly in a monoculture of liberalism.

Yoo says the school has been obsessed with ethnic diversity while disregarding viewpoint diversity, and that this has eroded the institution's commitment to free speech and created an environment in which anti-semitism has taken root.

I've watched students and the usual agitators disrupt debates on affirmative action, spark pitched fights on Sproul Plaza and try to shout down distinguished scholars and thinkers (home of the Free Speech Movement, indeed).

Tolerance of such shenanigans was matched by intolerance of conservative or libertarian views on the faculty.

Berkeley has consistently rejected conservative scholars, no matter how well-qualified, well-published or widely cited. . . .

One famous member of a revered Berkeley humanities department (always ranked among the nation's top three) once told me directly that he would never hire a conservative. . . .

Most student groups at Berkeley Law now boycott any event with a speaker who believes in Israel's right to exist.

For the first time in my 33 years at Berkeley I witnessed a heckler's veto, as protesters prevented a member of the Israeli Knesset from speaking — and top university officials stood idly by.

Students posted flyers depicting the dean of the law school — a liberal scholar with whom I rarely agree — feasting on human beings, then physically invaded his home while he hosted members of the graduating class, simply because he is Jewish.

Yoo argues that great universities should refocus their teaching on Western Civilization, so that the nation does not lose sight of the ideals upon which it was founded and the principles that provide the foundation for western prosperity.

The emergence of human reason in Greece and religious revelation in Jerusalem produced the free markets, natural rights, rule of law and democratic self-government that have made our astounding prosperity and power possible.

These ideals run directly counter to the poison running rampant on our campuses. . . .

Texas wants to restore Western civilization and the great books to the center of its curriculum — not to blindly agree with the past, but to understand and debate it.

I'm eager to join the Texans who want to defend free speech and thought, and to leave behind a California that rejects the West.
