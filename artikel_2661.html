6 plays that cost the Cowboys vs. 49ers

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

46 points. The Dallas Cowboys defense, that we know isn't very good, allowed 46 points on 9.4 yards per play. How many times can you be disappointed before you're no longer surprised? At least just once more as we saw the defense surrender multiple big plays to San Francisco.

The offense made their mistakes as well, but it's hard not to focus on giving up nearly a 50-burger at home. You can pick so many plays to choose from as to why the Cowboys lost but perhaps it's easier to choose the ones that most irritated you in watching the Cowboys lose to the San Francisco 49ers yet again. Here are the plays that should get under your skin the most and why they matter in the grander scheme.

The Cowboys looked like they had wrestled the early momentum back from San Francisco after a George Pickens score. It's not too many times the Cowboys have led by multiple scores this season and the defense has needed any possible large lead it could get.

Facing 3rd-and-8 with the 49ers on their own 26-yard line, the Dallas defense proved why. The Cowboys did a great job of putting initial pressure on Brock Purdy but he managed to scramble out of the pocket and found Christian McCaffrey as got behind the defense for a short pass from Purdy. From there he went untouched for a 74-yard score.

For a moment you felt that the Cowboys could put the pressure on the 49ers for a change but boy would that be an ominous sign of things to come.

The theme of today was one explosive play after another. If there's one thing you expect from a Kyle Shanahan offense is misdirection and out-leveraging the defense for an advantage. Deebo Samuel historically has been a thorn in the side of the Cowboys, and once again he stuck the Cowboys right where it hurts.

Samuel took a reverse pitch from Kaelon Black and raced 70 yards down the sideline for a touchdown. When you watch the replay, notice how long it takes for the defense to process what is happening. Samuel has a convoy of blockers out in front while the majority of the Cowboys defense is on the other side of the field.

What are some of the basic principles of defense at at any level let alone the NFL? Defensive pursuit and perimeter containment. That's where the Cowboys tend to struggle mightily an Samuel made them pay. One missed assignment or poor angle against a player like Samuel can turn an ordinary running play into an instant touchdown.

The Cowboys avoided letting the 49ers score points at the end of the first half before they received the second half kickoff. Down by a one point, letting the 49ers score a touchdown would be detrimental. Unfortunately, Dallas could not prevent that from happening and rookie running back Kaelon Black ran all over the defense to make a point.

The play puts another red-hot spotlight on Caleb Downs. The rookie safety was run over by Black on a touchdown that helped San Francisco seize control of the second half. Downs also had trouble finishing plays in space, including a missed opportunity to contain Purdy before the quarterback found McCaffrey for a long touchdown.

For a player with his expectations, the Cowboys need more consistency, particularly against offenses that force defenders to make decisions in space. We need to see better from Downs down the stretch.

This is where the wheels started to fall off. Sure Brandon Aubrey made two kicks beyond 50 yards. However, Aubrey hasn't been as automatic anymore and now you sometimes have to hold you breath if he can make the routine kicks.

With Cowboys looking to take seven-point lead, Aubrey missed the extra point. This left the Cowboys chasing points for the remainder of the game. With the game going the way that it did, any points at all were needed. Dallas had an unsuccessful two-point try in an effort to make up the difference after Aubrey's miss.

Aubrey has also had kickoff penalties this year and onside kick attempt at the end of the game wasn't nearly good enough. There's something off with Aubrey, and whatever it is, it is hurting the Cowboys.

For all intents and purposes, this was the last straw. The Cowboys fought tirelessly to get back in the game but fell short on defense. After Prescott connected with Ryan Flournoy for an 11-yard touchdown on fourth down, Dallas had reduced San Francisco's lead to 38-32 with 3:42 remaining. The Cowboys needed one defensive stop to give Prescott a chance to win.

Instead, just 10 seconds of game clock later, Purdy found McCaffrey for a 66-yard touchdown on second-and-10. San Francisco added a two-point conversion, extending its lead to 46-32. This was arguably the most decisive moment of the game. Dallas had finally pulled within one possession, only for the defense to surrender another enormous touchdown immediately.

After the Cowboys drew the score to 40-46, it was longshot that the Cowboys could get the ball back with enough time to take the late lead. Quinnen Williams who has been one of the few bright spots for the Cowboys defense almost came up huge.

Looking to drain the remaining time off the clock, the 49ers handed the ball of to Kaelon Black. Williams darted through the 49ers offensive line to meet Black in the hole. Williams was able to dislodge the ball from Black's arms. The Cowboys had a chance to recover the ball before Black quickly regained possession for the 49ers.

If the defense can't be average, the least it can be is opportunistic and Williams almost gave that much when the team needed it badly. The Cowboys scored 40 points and still found a way to lose. That's the frustrating reality of this team. Dak Prescott threw five touchdowns, George Pickens delivered three scores, and Quinnen Williams nearly gave Dallas one final opportunity to steal a victory.

Yet none of it mattered because the defense couldn't stop San Francisco from producing one explosive play after another. At some point, these breakdowns stop being isolated mistakes and become the identity of your football team. For Dallas, that point may have already arrived.
