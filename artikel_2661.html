Milwaukee Bucks vs. Toronto Raptors Preview & Game Thread: Process over results

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The Milwaukee Bucks nearly beat a Sixers team contending for a championship on Saturday, helped by a marquee 50-point performance from Tyler Herro and a career-high day for Brayden Burries. After the misery of a 13-game losing streak, Milwaukee fans got a glimmer of hope in their three-point win amid an ugly rebuild. The Bucks now travel to Canada with renewed optimism and momentum, facing a Toronto Raptors squad that recently snapped a six-game losing streak and has been underwhelming at the start of the season. The Raptors beat the Bucks 121-107 earlier this month and have won four straight overall against Milwaukee.

The Raptors are struggling to start the season. Analysts expected Toronto to hover around the playoff field and potentially challenge Philadelphia, Boston, and New York for supremacy in the Eastern Conference—but the Raptors have come nowhere close. Despite winning their first two games, they sit tenth in the East after 16 games. That includes a loss to the Celtics, a loss to the Sixers, and two back-to-back losses to the Knicks. Toronto's star acquisition, Kawhi Leonard, has missed the last 14 games with recurring knee issues and seems weeks away from returning.

Without Leonard, Toronto has looked stuck on offense. The Raptors have struggled to assert themselves on the perimeter, allowing opposing defenses to sag off unimpressive shooters like Scottie Barnes and RJ Barrett and pack the paint. Despite a tough, gritty defense that allows just 111.4 PPG, Toronto has looked uninspired in half-court offense, contributing to a mediocre start to the season. They've also struggled with injuries; Barrett will miss some weeks with a hamstring strain. Toronto recently snapped their losing streak with a 105-102 win over Detroit, led by a go-ahead layup from Scottie Barnes. However, they'll need more than that to re-enter the contention conversation.

Ousmane Dieng: Questionable (left finger fracture)

Bogoljub Markovic: Out (G league assignment)

Kawhi Leonard: Out (knee injury management)

Collin Murray-Boyles: Probable (concussion protocol)

Allen Graves has eased into a role for Toronto in Barrett's absence. The 2026 first-rounder has been elite defensively, posting 0.6 steals and 0.4 blocks in 16.1 minutes per game, pestering some of the league's best ballhandlers like Detroit's Cade Cunningham. Graves is also a driven rebounder—he's hauled in 1.9 offensive rebounds per game, again, on limited minutes, as an undersized big. He's also had some big scoring nights, like Friday's win over the Pistons, where he scored 20 points on 6/9 shooting. Graves has emerged as the Raptors' Swiss Army knife and a high-energy, high-impact stretch forward.
