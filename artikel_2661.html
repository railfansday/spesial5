Three Stars: Ohio State bombards Prairie View A&M in the second half, wins 86-59

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

After getting thumped by UConn over the weekend, the Ohio State men's basketball team (3-2) returned home Monday night for the start of a four-game homestand. The Buckeyes won't go on the road again until they travel to Lincoln for the Big Ten opener on December 2.

On Monday night, Ohio State faced off against a fellow Land-Grant university — Prairie View A&M. The Panthers came into Monday night's game with a 2-3 record, having beaten two non-Division-I schools to start the season and have since lost games to UT Rio Grande Valley, SMU, and Vanderbilt.

Rolling with the same starting lineup that he's used all season long (Justin Pippen, John NMobley Jr., Anthony Thompson, Amare Bynum, and Andrija Jelavic), Jake Diebler's Buckeyes didn't come out scorching the nets by any means, but did manage to double up Prairie View 16-8 by the under-12 media timeout with 11:51 remaining in the first half. Jimmie Williams carried over his impressive play from over the weekend (17 points against UConn) with two early three-pointers on Monday night.

Diebler and Ohio State made a pretty obvious effort to get Thompson going early, with the freshman taking and missing four shots in the first 10 minutes of Monday. Despite his struggles so far this year, (5-for-22 shooting entering this game) Ohio State got him the ball as often as they could early in this game.

Ohio State was never able to open up a double-digit lead on Prairie View in the first half, with the two teams trading baskets for much of the opening 20 minutes. Ohio State went to the halftime locker room with a 34-29 lead, with Jimmie Williams leading the pack with 11 points on 3-for-6 shooting. As a unit, Ohio State shot 32.1% in the first half, and was 5-for-17 (29.4%) from three-point range. Prairie View A&M's leading scorer Michael Eley came into Monday night's game averaging 15.4 points per game, and had 13 points on 6-for-10 shooting in the first half.

Pippen opened the second half with a three-pointer and it took Ohio State just over three minutes in the second half to push the lead to double-digits, 42-32. The Buckeye defense locked in and forced shot clock violations on back-to-back possessions heading into the under-16 media timeout. For the first time in the entire game, the Buckeyes looked fully engaged and effective on that end of the floor.

Despite the Panthers' best effort to delay the inevitable, the dam eventually did break. The Pippen three-pointer to open the second half was the beginning of what would become a 14-3 run for Ohio State, stretching the lead out to 63-44 with just under nine minutes remaining in the game. Even Thompson got in on the action with a nifty up-and-under layup in transition, which was his first basket of the game.

A pair of free throws (initiated by a technical foul on the Prairie View coaching staff), followed up by a huge dunk by Josh Ojianwuna put Ohio State up 73-50. A Pippen three — his third of the game — put Ohio State up 81-55 and Diebler began subbing the starters out with two minutes left.

It was a real life embodiment of the "They had us in the first half" meme, but Ohio State cleaned up their act in the back 20 minutes and blew out Prairie View, 86-59. With the win, Ohio State improved to 12-0 all-time against the Southwestern Athletic Conference (SWAC).

In honor of the Ohio State men's hockey team upsetting No. 1 Wisconsin over the weekend, here's a hockey-style "Three Stars of the Game" for Ohio State, although "stars" might be stretching it just a bit for this particular game:

For the second consecutive game, Williams was the one pressing the issue on offense for Ohio State. The senior from Solon had 11 points in the first half for the Buckeyes, including a pair of three-pointers. He was 3-for-6 in the opening frame, while the rest of the Buckeyes shot a combined 6-for-22 (27.2%).

Pippen had a solid offensive game on Monday night, and it seemed like his three-pointer less than 40 seconds into the second half set the tone for Ohio State for the final 20 minutes. However, his defense against Prairie View's leading scorer, Michael Eley was more impactful. After scoring 13 in the first half, Eley struggled to get going in the second half, hitting two of his first eight shots and going 1-for-3 at the free throw line.

On a night when Mobley was within 1,000 career points and could have forced things to get there, Ohio State's junior guard instead moved the ball when nothing was there for him, seeking out the best possible shot on offense. He was able to connect with Bynum on several lobs to the basket in the second half as the Buckeyes began to push the pace a bit more and get Prairie View backpedaling on defense.

Mobley entered the game needing 24 points to reach 1,000 in his Ohio State career. He finished with 10 points on 3-for-6 shooting, which means he'll need 14 against UMBC on Thursday night to reach 1,000.
