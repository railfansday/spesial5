Suno adds AI video to its music app, with filters for artists’ faces

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Visuals makes lyric videos and album art for users' songs, but blocks prompts that copy real people.

AI music company Suno has launched Visuals, which makes images and video clips of up to 15 seconds to match users’ songs, including lyric videos that lip-sync. It is on the web now, uses separate credits, and comes with filters meant to block likenesses of artists, public figures and copyrighted work.

AI music company Suno has launched Visuals, a tool that makes images and short videos to go with users’ songs, it announced on Tuesday. Users can create lyric videos, album art and clips of up to 15 seconds. It is on the web now, with iOS and Android apps due in the next few weeks.

Suno says its video model is built around music. Clips follow a song’s structure, pick up on its lyrics and lip-sync to them. Users can start from Suno’s own characters or upload an image, then extend and edit clips with text prompts.

Visuals runs on its own credits, bought separately from song credits. It follows Suno’s Speech tool, launched in October for spoken audio such as poems and meditations.

New filters will block prompts and uploads that try to show specific artists, public figures or copyrighted characters, Suno says. Videos will also carry hidden watermarks before they can be shared outside Suno. ByteDance added similar guardrails to its Seedance video tool in March.

“This means that prompts that work on other video platforms may not work on Suno and that’s by design,” the company wrote.

Those guardrails come as Suno fights fresh legal battles. Last week, Elvis Presley’s music owners and Eminem’s publisher sued Suno, and the Presley suit claims it misused his voice.

Suno has been trying to look more legitimate. It launched music models trained on licensed songs from Warner and BMG in September, and plans audio watermarks for its tracks. Investors valued the company at $5.4B in June.
