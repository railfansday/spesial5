Russia's crude exports hit highest since July as Ukraine targets refineries

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Overseas flows averaged 3.9 million barrels a day in the four weeks through Nov. 15, up 280,000 barrels a day from the prior period

Russia exported crude at an average pace of 3.9 million barrels daily over the 28 days ending Nov. 15, its biggest volume since late July, according to Bloomberg. That was 280,000 barrels a day more than in the four weeks through Nov. 8, according to Bloomberg's tanker data.

Ukraine has attacked 13 Russian refineries since the start of November, according to a Bloomberg tally, among them TAIF-NK's Nizhnekamsk plant and Ilsky, a refinery in the Krasnodar region. Strikes strand Russian oil that damaged plants can no longer handle, and those barrels end up being shipped abroad. Lulls in attacks give crews time to fix damaged units, which lets domestic plants take in more oil and reduces the volume heading overseas, according to Bloomberg.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said long-range strikes have disabled more than 55% of Russia’s oil refining capacity. He called the campaign a justified response to Russia’s refusal to de-escalate and its attacks on Ukrainian energy infrastructure. Bloomberg said it could not verify the claim.

Over the week ended Nov. 15, 39 tankers picked up Russian crude totaling 30.27 million barrels, versus 31 ships carrying 23.6 million barrels the prior week, according to Bloomberg. The weekly average of 4.32 million barrels a day was up 950,000 barrels a day and the highest since June. Weekly figures can swing sharply depending on when vessels depart, whether maintenance or bad weather interferes, and how sanctions and military action play out.

On a four-week basis, the gross value of Russia's oil shipments fell to $2.45 billion a week, the lowest in seven weeks, according to Bloomberg. Urals crude prices fell for a fifth straight week, which more than offset the higher volumes. Looking at the single week alone, export revenue climbed to roughly $2.29 billion.

China and India are still Russia's top crude customers, according to Bloomberg. Over the 28 days through Nov. 15, volumes bound for Asian buyers, counting cargoes that have no final destination listed, climbed to 3.72 million barrels a day, up from a revised 3.42 million barrels a day in the period ending Nov. 8.

Ukraine paused refinery strikes for two weeks in October before resuming attacks on major plants, including Kirishi, which is geared toward exports, and Omsk, Russia's biggest. Drone strikes in the first half of October had cut throughput at Russian refineries.
