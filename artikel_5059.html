Bitcoin’s failed Nvidia earnings rally puts $62,000 support back in the spotlight

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Bitcoin's post-Nvidia earnings rally faded within 35 minutes as ETF outflows and weak demand threaten a sustained recovery.

Bitcoin climbed to about $64,600 after Nvidia beat Wall Street’s revenue expectations Tuesday, then gave back most of the gain within 35 minutes.

In its first earnings report since Jensen Huang’s departure, Nvidia reported $115.8 billion in fiscal third-quarter revenue, up 103% from a year earlier, and topping the $109.1 billion Bloomberg analyst consensus.

Nvidia projected $130 billion in fourth-quarter revenue, plus or minus 3%, above the $125.2 billion analyst estimate Yahoo Finance reported. The forecast covers a quarter with an extra week and assumes zero data center compute revenue from China.

Nvidia’s board accepted Huang’s resignation effective Nov. 11 and disclosed it Nov. 12, according to the company’s SEC filing. Colette Kress took over as interim CEO while retaining her job as chief financial officer.

Nvidia released the figures at 4:20 p.m. ET, and Bitcoin traded close to $64,000 at the end of the one-minute interval before the report. It touched $64,602.98 during the 4:33 p.m. minute and was back to $64,246.47 by 4:55 p.m., still about $244 above its pre-release level.

Separately, Nvidia traded at $157.83 at 4:56:47 p.m. ET, up 1.52% from its $155.46 regular-session close.

In research published before Nvidia’s earnings, Glassnode found that losses from coins sold below their purchase price had reached levels seen around previous market lows, relative to profits over the preceding year.

Its model estimated that the Nov. 13-14 liquidations had cleared most leveraged long positions stacked above $62,000, leaving fewer of those previously mapped positions vulnerable to forced closure.

Glassnode estimated that about 20% of short-term holders’ coins had been acquired between $62,000 and $66,000. Within 5% of Bitcoin’s market price, the Binance and Coinbase order books showed about 2.3 times as much buy-side as sell-side liquidity.

Traders can withdraw those orders, so order book size only indicates how much buying might materialize. If the lower edge of the range gives way, Glassnode pointed to the June low near $58,500 and the aggregate realized price near $53,700, based on the prices at which coins last moved.

Bitcoin traded near $64,246 on Coinbase 35 minutes after Nvidia’s release, inside Glassnode’s $62,000–$66,000 cost-basis band for recent buyers.

At 4:55 p.m. ET, Bitcoin still sat well below the $74,100 level where short-term holders as a group would break even.

Glassnode also reported that the Coinbase premium, the difference between Bitcoin’s price on the exchange and its global price, turned positive around the weekend low before slipping back to a small discount.

Stablecoin supply fell 4.2% over 90 days while three-month Treasury bill yields rose about 45 basis points over that same period, making bills a more attractive place to hold cash.

US-traded spot Bitcoin ETFs also saw substantial withdrawals in the run-up to the report, as Glassnode reported $2.6 billion in net outflows over ten sessions.

Farside Investors’ daily totals added about $2.35 billion in withdrawals over that period, followed by a $7.9 million net inflow on Nov. 16. A longer run of ETF inflows and buyers consistently paying a premium on Coinbase would give the bounce more support.

At $74,100, Glassnode estimates that short-term holders as a group would break even, and Bitcoin’s initial move after Nvidia’s report left it well below that level.
