Hackers Redirected Bitcoin Miner Rewards via AI Attack, Luxor CEO Says

Hackers briefly hijacked computing power from Bitcoin miners using Luxor’s fleet management software, in what the company’s CEO called an AI-enabled attack.

Luxor Technology said Sunday that its internal monitoring flagged an unauthorized redirect of hashrate through Commander, its tool for managing mining machines.

Hashrate is the computing power miners devote to securing Bitcoin and earning rewards. Just over a dozen client accounts were affected, with their machines pointed to a different mining pool for a limited window, effectively sending the value of their work elsewhere.

Luxor said it patched Commander within two hours and began restoring affected machines to their original settings. No data was leaked or taken from its systems, and the issue was isolated to Commander, according to the company.

Luxor Pool and its LuxOS software weren’t affected, the company said, adding that every affected client will be paid in full for the redirected hashrate, as if their machines had mined normally.

Luxor CEO Nick Hansen said the compromise began around 6 p.m. Pacific time Saturday through “a sophisticated AI-enabled vector.” He didn’t detail how the attack worked. “You trust us with your fleet, and we need to be better,” he wrote on X.

Around 6:00p PDT yesterday, a subset of Commander customers were compromised, through a sophisticated AI-enabled vector. We were able to identify and patch quickly so that it didn't continue, then the hard work of reverting started.…

In a follow-up post, Hansen said crypto businesses are now “inundated with AI-enabled attacks,” with abliterated models, or AI models stripped of their safety guardrails, probing systems around the clock for weaknesses.

Luxor has responded by buying its own bare-metal GPUs to run such models defensively. It has also put most new feature work on hold to harden its infrastructure by rotating keys, updating kernels and pinning dependencies. Hansen urged other companies running similar services to do the same.

Bitcoin developers are grappling with the same threat. Bitcoin Core updated its security policy to address AI-driven threats, and an AI “red team” effort has turned up critical vulnerabilities throughout the year.

The incident also follows other attacks on Bitcoin infrastructure. Blockstream’s Liquid Network restored full Bitcoin backing earlier this month, two months after a $320 million exploit.

Luxor said it would soon publish a full post-mortem of the hack.
