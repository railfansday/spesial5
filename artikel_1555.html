Fat margins

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Thanks to Lewis for taking care of the blog yesterday as I was unable to watch the Borussia Dortmund match live. I saw that we’d scored early but only saw the final score well after the full-time whistle, so I guess it was a little disappointing not to have built on that fantastic hit from Viktor Gyokeres.

I think while I have some frustrations overall with our inability to turn dominance into goals, I’m more comfortable with it when we’re actually making chances. I think the manager would have a far bigger issue on his hands if we weren’t, and on Tuesday night the fact we had 30 attempts on goal tells you the problem isn’t chance creation, it’s about being clinical.

It wasn’t that long ago that it was the other way around. We found it hard to make goalscoring opportunities and for me that’s much more of a worry. It doesn’t mean we can’t improve, it doesn’t mean January becomes any less important the closer it gets, it’s just that the solution lies with the players we have who should be capable of better. There were a couple of other opportunities for Gyokeres that could easily have made this a better night for him and for Arsenal, Declan Rice hit the post, fine margins that shouldn’t be impossible to squeeze in our favour. Make ’em wider, thicker.

In the end though, I think the bottom line is quality of personnel. Over the last few seasons the only front three player we’ve had who looked like he could become top, top quality was Bukayo Saka. He was certainly on that trajectory until injuries became a real hindrance, and we saw that this season there were modifications made to his game to make him more effective. I know the discourse around him was a little polarised, but I saw a player more involved, who looked sharper and capable of producing the kind of volume that made him so impactful.

Saka isn’t one of those who finishes every chance he gets, he’s a guy who just keeps going until he makes something happen. The hamstring injury, and then the ongoing Achilles issue made it harder for him to contribute, but I really was encouraged by his season until the injury he sustained against Bayern. And it’s interesting to me that while it might be the kind of player he is anyway, we’ve basically asked Noni Madueke to replicate ‘Old Saka’ in terms of that kind of volume of shots and crosses. He’s had 11 shots across the last two games, and perhaps a single goal from those appear to be a scant return, I think it’s how we need him to play and how Arsenal can maximise what we’ve got up front.

There was an interesting comment on the Arses yesterday wondering how it is that Mikel Arteta can coach and improve defenders and midfielders so well, but not do the same with his attacking players. Some will say it’s because he’s a coach who is defensively minded and this is his wheelhouse. This is what he’s good at. Make a great defence, build a solid midfield in front of them. Perhaps there’s an element of truth in that, but for me it’s down to the players he has to work with.

If the raw ingredients are good, the recipe has a much better chance of being good, regardless of the talent of the chef. If you get to work with players like Ben White, William Saliba, Gabriel, Riccardo Calafiori, Declan Rice, Martin Odegaard etc, all of whom arrived in at a relatively young age (Rice perhaps the exception because he had a lot of experience, but Arteta has still made him better), you can develop that potential, build relationships, and see that come to fruition on the pitch.

Up front though, I don’t think we’ve had that level of talent. What can you do with a Willian or a Marquinhos? What can you do with a Gabriel Jesus whose knee explodes (perhaps as we saw in those opening few months before injury hit, you could have done a lot more)? Was Gabriel Martinelli’s plateau down to Arteta’s coaching or the player’s ceiling? I think the fact he’s playing now in Saudi Arabia says a bit about that. I think Arteta squeezed a lot out of someone like Leandro Trossard, who arrived at 28 and became a better player than he was at Brighton, but ultimately there are limits. The same goes for our two strikers, Gyokeres and Kai Havertz. On their days, they can get you goals, but there are too many days when they won’t, and I don’t really think it’s a coaching issue.

We also need to remember that there was a season when Mikel Arteta’s Arsenal scored more goals than the Invincibles did when they won the league in 2004. It’s not as if his coaching, per se, is an impediment to scoring goals – but like anything it’s layered and multi-factored. We had to become more defensive when all of our attackers were basically banjaxed, we still managed to finish second that season when all we had on the bench was a end-stage Raheem Sterling. If that has tipped the balance a little too far the other way, then you can understand it, and Arsenal are the masters of the single-goal margin victory these days.

I think we can do more to score more, I think some of the players we have are capable of delivering more goals and assists, and over the next few months – being realistic about what we might do in January – that’s what we need to lean into. But my gut feeling is that until such time as we have better attackers to work with, this will remain an issue – and it’s worth saying that said issue is one we still find a way to achieve success with more often than that.

I’d just like some fat margins once in a while.
