"The Series Was In Like Dire Straits" - Kit & Krysta Recall Zelda's Pre-BOTW Dark Times

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

While many of us can now look back on the Skyward Swords of the Zelda series with fondness, those who were in the trenches at the time might recall that things were starting to feel stagnant.

The later games of the 'traditional' Zelda formula were well received critically, but the tried-and-tested gameplay wasn't working wonders for their sales numbers, and they were struggling to bring in new fans in the process.

Cue the open-world series reinvention in Breath of the Wild and Tears of the Kingdom.

With Ocarina of Time back in the public eye (perhaps the most typical 'Zelda' a Zelda game can get), there are some who are wondering why it has been so long since we've seen the traditional format. So, naturally, former Nintendo employees Kit & Krysta are on hand to remind us all just how dark things were getting before the invigorating shot of BOTW.

"There has been some revisionist history out there lately about how the Zelda team 'ripped these [traditional format games] away from us at the peak of their popularity'," Kit Ellis said on the latest episode of the duo's podcast before setting the record straight.

"The series was in like dire straits," Krysta Yang added, "it was selling like nothing". Kit recalls going to focus groups and receiving feedback like, "the fanbase is dwindling; this is not bringing in new players; the interest is getting smaller".

With the huge success of Zelda today, it's easy to forget that such a time ever existed — particularly for those who weren't all that deep on the series at the time.

"It was depressing," Krysta adds, "it was like, we are losing this".

Of course, Breath of the Wild was so immensely popular and so very unlike anything we had seen from the series before that it gave Zelda the interest boost it so desperately needed. It's interesting to see how well-received the return of the classic format has been with OoT, so perhaps we'll see something slightly more traditional make an appearance in the next mainline game.

The dream scenario would be Nintendo leaving room for both formats to develop, but it's far too early to know what the team is cooking up for the next mainline entry. Until then, we have the pinnacle of the open-world structure and the very best of the traditional at our fingertips on Switch 2, so we're more than happy to wait and see what comes next.
