Poll: Are You Still Happy To Pay For Online Multiplayer On Xbox Consoles?

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

I'm not sure paid multiplayer makes sense anymore

Paying for access to online multiplayer has been the norm on Xbox consoles for decades now. It's something we've come to expect as part of playing online with friends and other folk around the world. The general understanding is, if you don't have Xbox Live, you're not playing online.

Eight years ago, I bought a gaming laptop to play Battlefield V on the go, and ever since paying for access to online multiplayer hasn't made much sense to me. It's just not something you do on PC. Sure, games like World of Warcraft and Final Fantasy XIV require additional subscriptions in order to play, but the majority of online games, like BFV, only require you to upfront the retail cost and crack on.

Xbox Play Anywhere is probably where console paywalls really start to look silly. Just a few months ago, I bought Subnautica 2 to play with a friend through the Xbox PC app. I could've opted for Steam, but I wanted to work towards those Xbox cheevos (when they eventually arrive). For a couple of weeks, I'd hop on the PC and dive into alien waters with my mate.

One day, I fancied playing on the big screen in my living room, so I installed Subnautica 2 on my Xbox Series X, booted it up and was prompted to buy a Game Pass subscription just to play the same save file I'd been enjoying for weeks through the Xbox PC app. Like I said, a bit silly.

Time went on, and I'd more or less let this go from my head until I read a report from Windows Central a couple days ago, claiming that Microsoft's upcoming Xbox Helix next-gen console could have plans to kill paywalls for paid multiplayer.

"Sources close to Microsoft's plans tell me Xbox Helix was originally conceived as a true console-PC hybrid with access to Windows PC games and stores such as Steam, alongside another major change: no paywall for online multiplayer."

According to Jez Corden, the original plan for Xbox Helix was to create a console-PC hybrid with access to Steam, potentially spelling the end of Xbox's staple multiplayer charges. It kind of makes sense when you think about it. If you can play your Xbox games across both console and PC, whether through disc-to-digital or Xbox Play Anywhere, it seems like pointless friction to lock multiplayer behind a paywall depending on which device you're playing on.

Back in 2021, changes to Xbox's paid multiplayer requirements allowed players without an active Xbox Live Gold subscription to access multiplayer free-to-play titles such as Fortnite, Call of Duty: Warzone and Apex Legends. And today, Xbox Live Gold isn't even a thing, as it's been absorbed into Xbox Game Pass.

So, even if Xbox Helix's original vision has changed somewhat from what Corden initially reported, it's still not out of the question to suggest that paid multiplayer could eventually become redundant.
