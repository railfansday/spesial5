New-Home Sales Drop 11.7% as Market Slips Into Underperformance

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

New-home market conditions weakened further in October as declining buyer traffic, softer sales, and persistent affordability pressures weighed on builders.

Zonda’s National Market Ranking fell to 87.4, moving the new-home market into “slightly underperforming” territory. Sales declined 3.1% from September and 11.7% from a year earlier, a significantly steeper annual decline than September’s 4.6% drop.

Zonda’s October builder sentiment survey also pointed to softer conditions. Eighty-six percent of builders reported slower-than-expected demand, up from 84% in September. Builders cited declining traffic, limited buyer urgency, and growing frustration with the effectiveness of incentives.

“One of the biggest challenges for builders right now is generating enough buyer interest in the first place,” says Sarah Bonnarens, director of economic research for Zonda and NewHomeSource. “Builders still have an advantage when it comes to pricing and financing flexibility, but those tools can only do so much when buyer traffic is down. The challenge is keeping sales moving without putting even more pressure on margins.”

Zonda reported a seasonally adjusted annualized sales rate of 638,568 new homes in October, down 3.1% month over month and 11.7% year over year. On a non-seasonally adjusted basis, 50,770 homes were sold, 11.6% fewer than a year earlier and 2.8% below October 2019.

Incentives remained a prominent feature of the market. Sixty-four percent of new-home communities advertised incentives on to-be-built homes, while 83% offered incentives on quick move-in inventory. Zonda notes the figures include only publicly available incentives and therefore underrepresent overall usage.

The New Home Pending Sales Index (PSI) registered 122.8 in October, down 11.4% from the same month last year and 29.5% below its cycle high. On a monthly basis, seasonally adjusted new-home sales measured by the index decreased 1.9%.

All of Zonda’s select markets posted year-over-year PSI declines. Minneapolis held up best, declining 0.3%, followed by Charlotte at 1.1%, and Tampa at 3.5%. The steepest declines occurred in Los Angeles/Orange County at 31.4%, Riverside/San Bernardino at 28.4%, and Denver at 26.3%.

Price reductions also became more common as builders worked to maintain sales pace. Forty-three percent of builders surveyed by Zonda lowered prices in October, up from 38% in September. Another 54% held prices flat, while just 3% increased them. In September, 10% of builders reported raising prices.

Entry-level home prices declined 2.6% year over year to $316,019, while move-up prices slipped 0.5% to $515,075. High-end prices increased 2.7% to $951,036. Zonda says the high-end increase partly reflects new communities opening at higher price points with larger homesites, bigger floor plans, and more desirable locations.

Zonda tracked 18,159 actively selling communities in October, up 6.7% from a year earlier and 0.6% from September. Community count remained 5.9% below the same month in 2019.

Quick move-in inventory (QMI) totaled 38,602 homes nationwide, up 8.6% year over year but down 3.3% month over month. QMI supply remained 72.3% above 2019 levels. There were 2.3 quick move-in homes per community nationally, about even with the same period last year.
