Five FAQs homeowners have about private flood insurance

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Private flood insurance is gaining attention as flooding shifts beyond mapped zones. Here are five common questions on coverage, NFIP differences, how private and NFIP policies can work together, eligibility and what homeowners policies typically exclude.   AI Summary

Extreme weather events caused by El Niño are dominating today’s headlines, driving an increased interest in flood insurance. However, flood patterns across the U.S. have been changing for years, even before El Niño. For example, catastrophic flooding is no longer dominated by events located in traditional flood zones or near coastal waters, but instead is increasingly defined by heavy rainfall.

As flood risks evolve, more homeowners are turning to private flood insurance – an alternative to coverage through the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP). For real estate agents guiding their clients through the home buying process, here is some basic information that clients need to know about this option.

Private flood insurance is a risk management tool backed by global capital markets that provides financial protection for buildings and personal property damaged by floods, helping families, communities and businesses to recover from these devastating events.

The primary difference between an NFIP and a private policy is the source of capital.

With an NFIP policy, FEMA holds all program risk, with United States taxpayers serving as the ultimate backstop. With private flood insurance, independent insurance companies bear the risk of loss. As a result, these companies leverage global capital structures to support risk selection and risk management in order to deliver a sustainable program capable of responding to customer needs.

While private insurers cannot be all things to all people, this disciplined and thoughtful approach means these insurers can deliver a sustainable and valuable product to their customers.

A private policy can act as either an alternative or a complement to an NFIP policy. Private policies equip agents with customizable options that can cater to individual homeowners’ needs, allowing insurance agents to craft solutions that ultimately help protect more people from floods.

Private policies can also complement an NFIP policy. If you have an NFIP policy, you can purchase private insurance to increase coverage beyond the limits offered by the federal program. Private insurance is also available to expand coverage, such as adding protection for contents in a basement or the expense to clean out a swimming pool.

The NFIP is essentially a one-size-fits-all policy available to almost every person in all geographies. However, the reality is that all homeowners aren’t the same. That’s the beauty of private insurance programs; they’re flexible programs that can respond to the individual needs of customers, like high-net-worth homeowners or renters.

Generally speaking, most homeowners’ policies exclude flood losses. A homeowners’ policy may cover water damage from broken pipes or sump pumps, but most exclude loss from inundation, whether resulting from swollen rivers, storm surge or intense rainfall.

The flood insurance industry is rapidly changing, and there are new opportunities coming online every day. To make the best decisions for clients, every real estate agent should take time to understand these new options.

In most situations, the home is the family’s most valuable investment. Wherever it rains, it can flood, so take time to consider solutions that can protect these treasured investments from catastrophe.
