Lufthansa is cutting 1,000 jobs at its flagship airline amid fuel cost pain

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Lufthansa Airlines plans the cuts at its Munich and Frankfurt hubs along with less short- and medium-haul capacity and a smaller A320 fleet

Lufthansa Group will eliminate 1,000 full-time positions at its flagship Lufthansa Airlines unit, where expensive fuel is adding pressure to the loss-making mainline carrier, according to Bloomberg and France 24.

The positions are at hubs in Munich and Frankfurt, according to Bloomberg. The cuts equal about 2.5% of the airline's roughly 40,000 employees, according to France 24.

Lufthansa Airlines intends to fly fewer short- and medium-haul services, downsize its Airbus A320 fleet and shift toward the smaller A220, according to Bloomberg. A management letter to employees outlining the plan was signed by the airline's top executives, including CEO Jens Ritter, Bloomberg said.

"After two years of the turnaround, we have to acknowledge that the original measures are no longer sufficient to secure our future," management wrote in the letter, according to Bloomberg. The letter cited the war in the Middle East, high fuel costs, nine days of strikes and the airline's cost base as factors that worsened the situation in 2026.

Over the first nine months, Lufthansa Airlines lost €180 million ($202 million) at the operating level, according to Bloomberg. Management does not expect significant earnings contributions from the airline in the fourth quarter, Bloomberg said.

The cuts land while Lufthansa is working to end a standoff with its pilots. The company submitted an offer to the Vereinigung Cockpit pilots' union on Tuesday that includes as many as 1,000 new pilot hires by the end of 2033, pay raises beginning in 2029 and one-off payments.

The union said it has not reached an agreement and set Nov. 23 as the deadline for Lufthansa to reconsider. Pilot strikes have cost Lufthansa more than €200 million ($224 million), according to Bloomberg.

The wider group saw profit climb 30% in the July-September period, helped by passengers who kept buying premium seats on long-haul flights. Its annual fuel bill is projected to run €2 billion ($2.24 billion) above the 2025 level, France 24 said. Lufthansa has twice cut its full-year profit outlook and anticipates heavier fuel costs again in 2027, according to Bloomberg.

The group announced plans in September 2025 to eliminate 4,000 administrative positions by 2030, according to Bloomberg. It was unclear whether the job reductions announced Friday fall under that plan or come on top of it, according to France 24. A Lufthansa spokesman would not elaborate, France 24 said.

Lufthansa stock slid by up to 1.7% during the opening hours of Frankfurt trading on Friday. It was up 5.3% for the year as of Thursday's close.
