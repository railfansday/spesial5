All 34 of the infamously fired Rockstar staff appear in GTA 6 game credits, though some aren't listed as "former employees", curiously

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

UPDATE: The final few names have been confirmed.

UPDATE 19TH NOVEMBER: All of the 34 fired Rockstar employees appear in the credits for GTA 6. We were able to confirm the final few names' existence there after Rockstar posted the full credits online - in searchable form. Before this, the process of cross-checking was intensely laborious: it required watching a scrolling list of names for an hour-and-a-half to see if they appeared.

Also, we couldn't identify six names in the "former employees" section of the credits before because they don't appear there, we now know. These names appear listed under the departments the people worked for, as if they still worked at Rockstar. Why this is, we don't know - perhaps it's something to do with being involved in the employment tribunal or not. We're asking Rockstar for clarification.

ORIGINAL STORY 18TH NOVEMBER: Eurogamer can confirm that at least 28 of the 34 employees fired by Rockstar roughly a year ago - in an action that directly led to the employment tribunal Rockstar is currently facing in Scotland - are named in the credits of Grand Theft Auto 6.

A number of those fired employees are seeking compensation for unfair dismissal amid allegations of "union busting", in an employment tribunal that comes to an end today. The delayed climax of the case couldn't be more fateful, aligned as it is with tomorrow's release of GTA 6, and the outcome will be historic either way.

Rockstar recently revealed that more than 4,711 people worked at the company making GTA 6, employed across 12 offices around the world - a staff-count that has more than doubled since Red Dead Redemption 2. It's a game that's wowed critics - Eurogamer awarded GTA 6 five stars in our review this afternoon - and which observers expect to generate $2bn in revenue in its opening weekend alone.

But perhaps Christian Donlan put it best when digesting the opulence on display in the Netflix 'extended look' GTA 6 trailer. "To spend all this money making something so beautifully rich and detailed, to pay billions for this game and sweat every little animation, to float it out to us on Netflix, amongst genuine discussion around whether Netflix can handle it? That's commitment," he wrote. "And if you're willing to do that, and willing to do that to tell an underdog story, you should let your workers who made everything form a union."
