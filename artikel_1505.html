Ranking the 10 left-backs in world football right now: Mendes, Cucurella, Calafiori…

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Left-backs at Arsenal, PSG, Real Madrid, Inter, Manchester City and Bayern Munich can all be considered to be among the standouts in their position across world football right now.

Whether it’s locking down elite wingers, inverting into midfield or providing width in attack, these are the players doing it better than anyone else.

Here’s our ranking of the 10 best left-backs in world football at this moment in time.

Probably not even the best left/centre-back hybrid at his club, which is more of a statement of the scary defensive depth that Mikel Arteta’s Arsenal are blessed with.

The rugged Ecuadorian is unlikely to ever challenge the assists charts, but he’s remarkably sturdy and reliable.

Few full-backs have ever been as exhilarating as Alphonso Davies in full flight.

We will never forget that electric performance against Barcelona, and desperately hope that isn’t as good as it gets. It’d be a minor footballing tragedy if Davies peaked as a teenager.

Unfortunately, we’re starting to fear that might be the reality. Injuries have certainly disrupted Davies’ progress, and he hasn’t quite looked the same electric presence we’ve come to expect.

There have, however, been enough encouraging signs this season to suggest there’s still plenty of life in him.

Regular watchers of the Bundesliga will tell you how solid Brown is. For everyone else, they discovered him at the World Cup – one of very few individuals to escape from Germany’s desperately disappointing campaign with any credit in the bank.

You can see why Bayern moved quickly to secure his signature. Given Davies’ aforementioned struggles, it was a no-brainer of a signing – the Bavarians foodchaining their domestic competition yet again.

He’s not exactly been a revelation since arriving at the Allianz Arena, but has slotted into Vincent Kompany’s side comfortably enough, contributing a goal and two assists in his first eight Bundesliga appearances. There’s plenty more to come, and if he continues developing under the Belgian, there’s no reason why he can’t establish himself among the very best in his position.

The Manchester City academy graduate has the talent and potential to be challenging for the top end of this list, but things haven’t quite clicked under Enzo Maresca just yet.

He’s been in and out of the team during an underwhelming start to life under the new manager, making it difficult to build any real momentum. We’ve still favoured O’Reilly over Arsenal’s Myles Lewis-Skelly, having seen considerably more of him at left-back over the past year.

As with Lewis-Skelly, we suspect O’Reilly’s long-term future might lie in midfield. He certainly possesses the technical skill set to make it happen. But there’s still plenty more work to do before he fulfils his considerable potential.

Manchester United were desperate to sign Hall last summer, but Newcastle stood firm. Given the bin fire they’ve subsequently continued to serve up at Old Trafford, he might consider himself fortunate.

That’s not to say Hall isn’t an excellent young footballer. He’s just no magician, and even his considerable talent hasn’t been enough to elevate Matthias Jaissle’s struggling Newcastle side.

One of those players who could look absolutely sensational in a properly functioning team. We’re still backing him to get there.

Grimaldo played a grand total of zero minutes in Spain’s World Cup triumph.

The former Bayer Leverkusen wingback spoke of his eagerness to play his part, but he’s a bit unfortunate that one of the few better players in his position across world football shares his nationality.

Grimaldo has made an outstanding start to life at Atletico Madrid, quickly establishing himself as one of Diego Simeone’s key players. He’s been a standout performer as Atleti look to challenge Barcelona and Real Madrid for the La Liga title.

Not quite to the same extent as some of the prior names in this list, but we’re still left with a sense that Calafiori’s lack of durability costs him a little.

The Italian heartthrob played a decent amount of football last season – 36 appearances and over 2000 minutes in all competitions – but he still missed vast swathes of Arsenal’s quadruple chase, including much of the Premier League run-in and latter stages of the Champions League knockouts.

Calafiori undoubtedly possesses the ability – particularly on the defensive side of his game – to challenge at the top end of this ranking, and he’s showing it more consistently this season. He’s also offering plenty going forward, an aspect of his game that’s becoming increasingly prominent.

Dimarco suffered such a colossal mare in Inter’s 2025 Champions League final shellacking at the hands of PSG that it’s difficult to imagine his reputation ever fully recovering.

But every footballer has their off days, and if you take that out of the equation and Di Marco’s track record is outstanding. Two Scudetti, three Coppa Italia, runs to two Champions League finals, twice being included in the Serie A Team of the Season.

And it’s not just pedigree. Last season was probably his best yet, having notched seven goals and 17 – yes, seventeen – assists as Inter waltzed to the title. He was deservedly named Serie A’s MVP – how often has that ever happened to a full-back in a top European league?

Inter have had an up-and-down start to this campaign, but he remains one of their best and most important players. Badly missed in the recent Milan derby defeat.

Positionally astute, bit of a sh*thouse, decent enough going forward but never at the expense of his defensive duties. Real Madrid have basically signed Jose Mourinho’s idea of the perfect left-back.

Best of all, he’s a big game player. Three summers in a row he’s shone on the big stage. The European Championship. The Club World Cup. The World Cup. Across all three finals, he didn’t put a foot wrong. He was far too good to be playing in a Chelsea side so dysfunctional.

Cucurella looked exposed and out of sorts during the unconvincing early weeks of Jose Mourinho’s reign, struggling badly in defeat to Atletico Madrid. But he’s grown into the campaign as the new manager has tweaked his way towards a more functional set-up.

He was outstanding in the recent El Clasico win, effectively shutting down Spain team-mate Lamine Yamal. Yan Diomande helping out makes a big difference, and Cucurella will probably be hoping Vini Jr doesn’t reclaim his place any time soon.

The case isn’t quite as cut and dried as with Achraf Hakimi on the opposite flank, but it was nevertheless a pretty easy decision.

Mendes and Hakimi have been integral to Luis Enrique’s PSG winning back-to-back Champions Leagues.

Their defensive ability is the starting point, with both excelling in one-on-one duels, but they are far from limited when it comes to supporting the attack. That all-round skill set forms one of the pillars of a remarkably effective set-up.
