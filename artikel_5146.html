Stellar (XLM) is an open-source, enterprise-focused Layer-1 blockchain designed to support fast, low-cost global payments. Launched in 2014 by Jed McCaleb, Stellar aims to connect banks, payment providers and digital wallets, making it easier to move, tokenize and settle money across borders.

Unlike Bitcoin, which was created as an alternative to traditional financial systems, Stellar is designed to work alongside existing financial infrastructure.

Its network focuses on cross-border payments, digital assets, and real-world asset (RWA) tokenization, giving it a growing role as a bridge between traditional finance and blockchain technology.

With XLM trading near $0.12, let’s explore the CoinPedia XLM Price Prediction for 2026, 2027–2030.

Stellar is facing renewed selling pressure after retreating from its recent recovery and moving back toward the lower support region. The chart suggests that buyers are struggling to regain control, while the broader price structure remains bearish. A sustained hold above the current demand zone could trigger a short-term relief bounce, with the previous breakdown area acting as the first major resistance. However, a decisive move below support could extend the decline and delay any meaningful recovery through December.

Stellar enters the final months of 2026 with growing attention from banks, payment companies and institutional asset markets. The network is increasingly being positioned as infrastructure for tokenized assets and cross-border payments, giving XLM a potential growth path beyond retail speculation.

A major catalyst is Stellar’s expanding real-world asset (RWA) ecosystem, which has grown to more than $3.3 billion. Institutional projects involving tokenized securities and Treasuries could further increase network activity and demand.

Stellar is also expanding its role in global payments. The reported BVNK integration could allow stablecoin payments, payouts, and treasury transfers across more than 130 markets, potentially bringing more real-world transactions onto the network.

Meanwhile, Protocol 28 is designed to improve Soroban smart-contract performance and transaction processing, supporting larger institutional workloads.

Stellar’s upcoming Protocol 30 (Bell) upgrade is an important November catalyst. Validators are scheduled to vote on the mainnet upgrade on November 23, with proposed improvements to smart-contract functionality and business-oriented account infrastructure. The development could strengthen Stellar’s appeal for institutional and financial applications.

Meanwhile, the latest market weakness has weighed on XLM despite its ecosystem developments. On November 11, XLM traded near $0.144, while broader cryptocurrency selling and Bitcoin weakness contributed to the decline. Stellar’s Q3 report webinar, scheduled for November 13, is another event investors may monitor for updates on network activity and adoption.

The long-term projection assumes Stellar sustains relevance in enterprise blockchain use cases, with growth moderating over time as the asset matures.
