ICP crypto outperformed Bitcoin last week – But it still faces rejection at $2.20

The Internet Computer [ICP] token has rallied 1.51% in the past 24 hours, a better return compared to Bitcoin’s [BTC] 0.28% gain. However, over the past week, ICP was down only 5.2%, compared to BTC’s 13.2% drawdown at the time of writing.

The liquidation data on the 13th and 14th of November showed a kind of liquidation cascade not seen since August. Bitcoin’s rapid slide from $70,200 to $62,000 on these two days resulted in $2.93 billion in just long liquidations, CoinGlass data showed.

The daily trading volume of ICP, after such an intense period of leverage shakedown, was down nearly 45%.

Yet, the past week’s losses have swept a key liquidity zone. This presented a reasonable possibility of a price rebound, provided Bitcoin can stabilize above $64k-$65k.

The weekly ICP crypto chart showed a range formation between $2.02 and $3.90, with the mid-range level at $2.96. The price has traded within this range since December 2025.

The rally in September reached $3.60, just beyond the 75% range level at $3.45. The sellers took control of the market then, as bearish sentiment also took root across the crypto sphere.

Despite the price drawdown in the past two months, the weekly OBV maintained a respectable uptrend that stretched back to March.

Zooming in to the lower timeframes, we can see that the liquidity pocket at $2.20 has been taken out. The ICP crypto price has bounced from its dip to $2.10 following the liquidation cascade in recent days.

The 4-hour chart has a bearish structure. The bounce over the past couple of days has retested the $2.20-$2.25 former demand zone as supply. It is possible the altcoin faces rejection here and falls lower.

In case of a sustained bounce, swing traders would want to wait for a reaction from the $2.44 and $2.50 levels. They had a confluence with Fibonacci retracement levels and imbalances left during the recent sell-off.

Such a bounce would likely be followed by a move toward $2.10 or lower, unless demand expands noticeably to overpower selling pressure.
