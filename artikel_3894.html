Welcome to a brand new week of GTA 6 still blowing everyone's minds. We will probably be covering this beat for months if not years as players and game developers continue unearthing new discoveries and technical marvels, with one prominent dev comparing GTA 6 to the Great Pyramids in Egypt.

Mark Richard Davies, game director at Naughty Dog and game designer on The Last of Us 2, is the latest game developer to appear awe-stricken in reaction to Rockstar's opus. A colleague of Davies, Intergalactic: The Heretic Prophet world design director Michael Barclay, recently said GTA 6 appears to him "as a Bugatti Veyron would to a nervous Victorian child," underscoring the unprecedented depth and scale of the game.

In a new tweet, Davies says we might never find every piece of the GTA 6 puzzle and jokingly predicts we'll one day discover extraterrestrial intelligence hiding in its DNA. "GTA VI is the Great Pyramids of Game Development," he says. "As in, people will be asking for centuries how it was possible and we will eventually conclude that aliens made it."

In response to a commenter who rightfully points out Rockstar's "8 to 13" year development cycle and estimated $1 billion budget, Davis simply says, "I'm sure the pyramids were expensive too," seemingly acknowledging that one part of Rockstar's magic is unfathomable financial resources. For instance, the thing that amazes Davies the most about GTA 6, in his words, is "Every NPC having voice lines and reactions. The sheer size of the script must be mind boggling." The level of interactivity and variety in GTA 6's NPCs is hugely immersive, but that wouldn't have been possible if Rockstar and parent company Take-Two Interactive didn't have the money to pay that amount of voice and performance capture artists.

All that said, in addition to lots and lots of money, there's an element to Rockstar's sterling track record for genre-defining releases that has very little to do with money and a lot to do with extremely talented individuals firing on all cylinders and a unified vision from legacy leadership. But, you know, having $1 billion dollars to spend certainly doesn't hurt.
