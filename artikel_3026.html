In Response to the Turmoil at Nvidia, Bitcoin Drops Below $70,000. Is This a Buy-the-Dip Opportunity?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Bitcoin's mediocre response to Jensen Huang returning to Nvidia is a bit concerning for the coin's potential buyers.

By Alex Carchidi – Nov 24, 2026 at 5:41PM EST

With Bitcoin (BTC -3.49%) collapsing below $60,000 on Nov. 20 to hit $55,081 the next day as the Nvidia (NVDA -5.86%) drama continued, it's worth pondering whether this is a good opportunity to buy the dip, or if it's a trap for those who refuse to recognize that the market is turning against it.

On Nov. 23, Jensen Huang's return as CEO lifted Nvidia's shares by 27%, but Bitcoin by only 0.6%, and the coin fell back beneath the $60,000 level within 24 hours anyway. In other words, it lost more during volatility that was mostly unrelated to it than it was able to regain once its trigger reversed.

This isn't the first time that dynamic has reared its head, and it tends to make the idea of buying future dips a bit less attractive. Let's dive in and analyze the situation.

The lopsided response that disfavors Bitcoin is somewhat attributable to who was selling it and the duration of their holding.

Most of November's sellers had held their coins for under six months. Their losses, measured against past-year profits, reached levels seen only near the 2018, 2020, 2022, and early 2023 lows. Such capitulation, which is to say panicked bulk selling at a loss, often sends prices well below what the news actually justifies -- and in this case, Nvidia's problems are mostly distinct from Bitcoin's, but the justified fears of a potential incoming rate hike by the Federal Reserve are not.

Nonetheless, dip buyers picked up about 66,000 BTC when it was priced between $62,000 and $66,000, only to see that price floor break within a week. Their losses may well be erased if they hold for the long term, but for now, they're underwater, which may make it stressful or unappealing for some investors to buy the slightly deeper dip this time around.

Now let's hear the other side of the argument. The Nov. 21 low comes four years to the day after the coin's 2022 bear market bottom. If the four-year cyclical rhythm of Bitcoin continues, that timing favors buyers.

The next halving, which halves new coin output, is due in April 2028. Bitcoin has historically bottomed out 12 to 18 months before a halving. That puts the window for the bottom between about October 2026 and April 2027, so those who buy the dip now could be well-positioned for that bull run, if it occurs.

The market is obviously disfavoring the coin compared to other investments, and it has been doing so for a while. It only grew by 6% over the last five years. Its core investment thesis is still valid, but there could still be downside from here.

Thus, I'm interested in buying some Bitcoin here, but only with a small amount of capital, and only because I know I'm willing to hold it for many years to come.
