Nokia vs. AT&T: Which Telecom Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

By Robert Izquierdo – Updated Nov 22, 2026 at 8:03PM EST

Connectivity serves as the backbone of the modern economy, but choosing between infrastructure and services requires careful analysis. Investing in Nokia Oyj (NOK -8.27%) or AT&T (T +2.10%) involves balancing network equipment growth against steady subscription revenue.

Nokia focuses on the hardware and software that make networks run, while AT&T is a retail giant providing wireless and fiber service to millions. This comparison examines their business models, balance sheets, and valuations to determine which stock is better for your portfolio.

Nokia is a global connectivity infrastructure company that builds networks across fixed, mobile, IP, optical, and data center systems. The company serves communications service providers, governments, and large enterprises while operating in approximately 130 countries with roughly 78,000 employees. No single customer accounts for more than 10% of revenue, which helps diversify its revenue streams across various regions and sectors.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached $22.6 billion, representing revenue growth of 4.1%. The company reported net income of $858.1 million during this period. This growth reflects ongoing investments in network technology as service providers around the world continue to upgrade their existing infrastructure.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was 0.2x, indicating a low level of total debt relative to shareholder equity. The current ratio of 1.6x shows the company has more than enough assets to cover its short-term liabilities. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was $1.6 billion, which is the cash remaining after operating costs and equipment spending.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached $128.9 billion, supported by revenue growth of 3.5%. The company generated net income of $13.1 billion, resulting in a net margin of 10.2%. This net margin represents the percentage of revenue that remains as profit after all expenses are paid.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was 1.6x, measuring its total debt against shareholder equity. The current ratio was 0.9x. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow reached $19.4 billion, providing significant capital for dividends and network upgrades.

Nokia must contend with intense competition from other global equipment providers in an industry with high research and development requirements. Rapid technological shifts in the 5G and upcoming 6G markets mean the company must constantly innovate to maintain its market share. Geopolitical tensions also carry risks of influencing where the company can deploy its critical national infrastructure projects.

AT&T faces significant risks from increasing competition in the wireless and broadband sectors, particularly from cable providers and businesses like EchoStar (ECHO -8.72%). The company is vulnerable to supply chain disruptions and inflationary pressures on network equipment costs. It also faces potential legal liabilities from lead-clad cables and risks associated with labor union contract negotiations for 43% of its workforce.

AT&T appears more attractively valued based on its lower earnings multiple, while Nokia offers a significantly more conservative balance sheet profile.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

Weighing whether to invest in Nokia or AT&T comes down to a trade-off between the former's shift toward artificial intelligence infrastructure and balance sheet cleanliness versus the latter's predictable cash flow and higher dividend income. AT&T is the better fit for conservative investors prioritizing passive income and a more attractive valuation.

However, I would buy Nokia for 2027. While AT&T sports a robust dividend yield of about 5%, it has not raised its dividend payment since cutting the amount in 2022. It also holds a lot of debt, as evidenced by its debt-to-equity ratio of 1.6x.

Nokia has the stronger balance sheet, and its focus on AI infrastructure is paying off. In the third quarter, AI and cloud sales soared 123% and customer orders rose 118% year over year.

Nokia's AI radio access network (AI-RAN) technology uses artificial intelligence to intelligently manage traffic on 5G networks for better speed, coverage, and capacity. AI-RAN is still being tested but is approaching commercial deployment. Thanks to its artificial intelligence business, Nokia stock has a higher potential for share price appreciation than AT&T.
