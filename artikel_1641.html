Mind the gap

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Over the last few weeks, I’ve grown accustomed to waking up and experiencing low-level fear first thing in the morning. Today, it was a nice change not to be focused on my own health, and instead for my anxiety to centre around someone else – David Raya.

With any team or any squad there are players whose absence will be more keenly felt than others, but one of the things Arsenal have generally done really well in the last few years is close the gaps between ‘first choice’ and those behind them. It doesn’t mean there isn’t still a kind of hierarchy, just that you’re better able to cope if injury or suspension hits. Mikel Arteta referencing ‘finishers’ rather than substitutes might be a psychological ploy to keep his squad happy, but it’s more easy to apply when you have the group of players he has at his disposal.

If Jurrien Timber is out, we have Ben White. If Riccardo Calafiori can’t play, Piero Hincapie can. We all worried about William Saliba needing a long lay-off after the World Cup, but the arrival of Ezri Konsa offset that in exemplary fashion. If we miss Declan Rice, we have two World Cup winners, a Brazilian international, and one of the most highly regarded young English midfielders around to fill that gap. Further forward, Noni Madueke is performing at a level that makes Bukayo Saka’s absence feel less impactful than it would have previously, and while I think forward line quality remains an issue we need to solve as urgently as January, you can flip a coin between most of our options there without feeling you’ve weakened yourself considerably.

Unfortunately, I don’t think the same applies between the sticks. Raya has been the stand-out goalkeeper of the last few years, establishing himself as Arsenal’s number 1 after an initial settling in period. One, by the way, which I don’t think the manager handled particularly well. Typically he’s been near flawless in terms of his man-management, and while he was fundamentally right about wanting the Spaniard to come in and take the position to a new level, the way he fudged things around Aaron Ramsdale, talking about how he’d sub a goalkeeper in a game and all that nonsense really wasn’t his best moment. It left both of his keepers in a weird place for too long, until Raya’s confidence grew and his quality came to the fore.

Since then he’s won multiple golden glove awards, his shot-stopping has been incredible at times, and although every player and every keeper will make mistakes, his have been few and far between. For me though, despite the eye-catching acrobatics – which are more easily clipped up for the social media generation – his key asset is the assurance he brings to the team. Not just when he has the ball at his feet (even if there are times I would like him to move it more quickly), but how good he is from opposition set-pieces and crosses. He comes and claims and eases pressure time and time again, and I think that’s the thing we’re going to miss most if he’s out for any significant period of time.

Kepa is obviously a very experienced second choice, but is second choice for a reason. Realistically though, I don’t think there’s much any club can do to have a back-up option that’s nearly as good as their number 1. If you’re being brutally honest, many clubs in the Premier League don’t have a first choice that comes anywhere close to be as good as Raya, despite their best efforts and spending millions to try and find that player. So, as anxious as I am about him being out, it is one of those things we can’t really complain about. We just have to get on with it.

As I said in yesterday’s blog, we are probably going to be less secure at the back, that feels kind of inevitable, so the onus on the players further forward increases. Score more goals, turn that domination of possession and territory into more chances. If we have the ball in their half, our goalkeeper becomes something of a non-issue for as long as that’s the case. There are ways to cope, but we might need to gird our loins for some iffy moments at the back that we haven’t had to experience too often of late.

As Arsenal fans though, we’ve come through worse than this with goalkeepers in the past. I don’t want to give anyone PTSD first thing on a Monday morning, but when you’ve lived what we’ve lived in seasons gone by, hopefully a little Kepa period won’t be too traumatic. And maybe, after the scan, there’ll be better news about Raya than we expect. Fingers crossed.
