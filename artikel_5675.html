FDA Moves to Boost Drug Quality Testing

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

— It's important to assess product quality, not just focus on regulatory compliance, expert says

The FDA announced that it's expanding its approach to drug quality testing, combining traditional laboratory methods with newer, faster screening technologies to identify potential problems with medications on the U.S. market. Its Enhanced Approach for Sampling and Testing (EAST) initiative builds on existing surveillance efforts and aims to use testing data to better inform regulatory decisions and inspections, the agency said.

In this exclusive MedPage Today video, David Light, co-founder and CEO of independent drug-testing company Valisure, discusses the potential benefits of expanded testing, emerging technologies being explored through the Advanced Research Projects Agency for Health (ARPA-H), and why evaluating finished drug products should complement traditional manufacturing inspections and regulatory oversight.

The following is a transcript of this video:

We need to trust but verify. We need to test more.

Is it new? I mean ... it's a new announcement. This is a program that builds upon the DQST [Drug Quality Sampling and Testing] program within FDA, which has been going on since at least 2013. So it sounds like this is an enhanced version, which also sounds exciting, leveraging some new, more modern techniques.

ARPA-H launched a program called FASTPASS just a few months ago -- that stands for Fast Assessment of Solutions, Therapeutics, Pharmaceuticals, and Supplies -- effectively trying to scan or test the quality, not just compliance, but the quality of about 70 million shipments of pharmaceutical products into the United States in all U.S. ports every year. That certainly, as they say, is an ARPA-Hard task.

The idea of using modern testing techniques is interesting. I mean, their DQST program is having conventional techniques as the foundation. Looking at a product from many different lenses, many different techniques and methods, I think, is actually really important. I think that's part of what independent testing has shown over the years and what perhaps some of the previous testing programs have missed.

I think a big part of regulatory testing is essentially seeing if something's legal to be on the road, right? Is it legal to be marketed and sold in the U.S.? And that's regulatory compliance. But what we focus on in independent testing is more of a market driver, what procurement actually looks for, which can be not just, is it legal to be on the road or legal to be sold, what are the best quality products or the lowest risk, but both have an influence on the other.

But driving the market to a race to the top, to be as good as possible, I think is certainly a key driver of independent testing and quality testing. The two can absolutely work hand-in-hand. And I think we're seeing more and more evidence of it and more growth in that area.
