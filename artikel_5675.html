13 Drugs Get Double-Digit Price Cuts in Medicare

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

— Savings of up to 84% will take effect in 2028

Medicare beneficiaries can look forward to saving up to 84% on drugs used to treat cancer, arthritis, and diabetes, among other conditions, thanks to the latest round of Medicare drug price negotiations.

"Every year, millions of seniors leave the pharmacy empty-handed because they can't afford their medication," CMS administrator Mehmet Oz, MD, MBA, said Monday in a press release. "The Trump administration is committed to use every tool at our disposal to negotiate fairer, more affordable prices to ease the burden on Medicare beneficiaries and help them get the care they need."

The negotiated prices will result in an estimated net savings for the Medicare program of 45%, or $9.2 billion, on 13 Medicare Part B and D drugs used to treat serious chronic conditions. CMS noted that when the negotiated prices go into effect in 2028, people enrolled in Medicare prescription drug coverage (i.e., Medicare Part D) would save under the defined standard benefit design an estimated $69 million in out-of-pocket costs. People with Medicare Part B coverage would save an estimated $395 million in out-of-pocket costs.

This is the third round of the Medicare Drug Price Negotiation Program, which was established in 2022 under the Biden administration and allows the federal government to negotiate directly with manufacturers to lower prices for some of Medicare's most popular and expensive drugs.

The first round of negotiated prices went into effect in January 2026, covering drugs used by 8.8 million people on Medicare, with list price reductions ranging from 38% to 79%. The second round of negotiated prices will begin in January 2027, covering drugs used by 5.3 million people on Medicare, with list price reductions ranging from 38% to 85%.

This third round covers drugs used by 1.5 million people on Medicare, with list price reductions ranging from 34% to 84%, according to a CMS fact sheet. The listed drugs accounted for $25.6 billion in Medicare drug costs in 2025.

Although tofacitinib (Xeljanz, Xeljanz XR) and omalizumab (Xolair) were originally selected for negotiation this cycle, they were not included in final negotiations because "before the end of or during the negotiation period" those drugs had generics or biosimilars approved for them, the agency said. Such drugs are not eligible for negotiation under the Medicare price negotiation program.

Healthcare groups were generally happy with the negotiation outcome. "Voters made clear in this month's midterm elections that families are fed up with the high cost of healthcare, and prescription drug prices are a big part of the problem," Anthony Wright, CEO of Families USA, said in a statement. "Medicare negotiation is showing itself to be a successful solution on prescription drug prices that should be expanded to more discounts for more drugs for more people."

In another statement, Merith Basey, CEO of Patients for Affordable Drugs, noted that "Medicare's third round of negotiated prices demonstrates what patients already know: negotiation works. Across two administrations, Medicare has secured lower prices and billions in savings for patients and taxpayers."

Finally, Nancy LeaMond, AARP executive vice president, said, "For decades, AARP has fought to lower prescription drug prices, helping secure the historic 2022 law that gave Medicare the power to negotiate lower prices. Today's announcement is another victory in that fight."
