Guimaraes looks a £75m bargain as ‘super sub’ makes difference

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Arsenal have played six Premier League games this season and Bruno Guimarães has been a match-winning force in two of them. At this rate, he might justify his enormous £75m transfer fee by Christmas.

The Brazilian was not signed to be Arsenal’s super sub but that is the role he appears to be playing in these early days in north London. Against Leeds United here, as it was against Sunderland last month, he came off the bench when the champions were struggling. And as it was at Sunderland, he produced a thumping finish to put Arsenal on their way to three points.

This is the value of signing a 28-year-old with significant Premier League experience. As Arsenal laboured against an impressively organised and courageous Leeds side, Mikel Arteta knew that Guimarães was capable of coming into the game and adjusting to its demands. There has been no adaptation period required for Guimarães, who is fast becoming one of the most popular players in north London.

The finish, which put Arsenal 2-1 ahead at the Emirates Stadium, marked an emphatic conclusion to a fabulous passing sequence. Bukayo Saka, Martin Odegaard and Declan Rice all combined at speed before Guimarães charged on to Rice’s flick. “It was a beauty,” said Arteta of the goal. The move had actually begun at the feet of Guimarães a few moments earlier, when he nutmegged an opponent in style.

The winning goal came just eight minutes after Riccardo Calafiori, Arsenal’s thrilling left-back/centre-forward hybrid, had pulled them level in the game. James Justin had struck for Leeds shortly after half-time, creating a horrible anxiety among the home fans here, and for much of the afternoon Daniel Farke’s side were the better team.

Leeds, though, could not compete with Arsenal for depth. The arrival of Guimarães as a 63rd-minute substitute represented a flexing of Arteta’s managerial muscle, in his first match since signing a new long-term contract, and the Brazilian offered a moment of attacking quality that his opponents were unable to replicate.

It was actually the first competitive home game that Guimarães had played for Arsenal since his transfer from Newcastle United, as a result of a few niggles and the club’s unusual run of four consecutive away matches before the international break. The most recent of those was the 3-0 thrashing by Brighton, which was one of their worst defeats of the last few seasons.

This gritty victory represented a necessary response to that defeat, even if the performance was far from dominant. “We had a point to prove,” said Arteta. “Something was touched in our identity against Brighton.”

Guimarães, too, clearly felt that some wrongs needed to be made right. For all his brilliance in this game and against Sunderland, he had produced a horrible showing at the Amex Stadium a few weeks ago. “He was not at that level against Brighton,” said Arteta. “This is the level for Arsenal. Every week, every game, every minute, every action you have to be at that level. At Brighton, he wasn’t. Today, he was much better.”

Leeds had not lost in their opening five Premier League matches of this season and it was easy to see why. They were willing to press Arsenal high up the pitch, taking risks and engaging in physical duels, and the strategy proved hugely effective for an hour.

If they had been more precise in the final third, they could have been a few goals ahead by the time Justin converted Gabriel Gudmundsson’s left-wing cross after 55 minutes. Anton Stach, who was accidentally blocked by team-mate Jayden Bogle, and Gudmundsson both went close in the first half.

The early kick-off, the long international break, the slow start and the memories of the Brighton defeat made it a strange atmosphere at the Emirates. You would not have known they were the champions, based on the reaction of the fans to the first-half performance, but the achievements of last season clearly do not guarantee fervent support at all times.

Calafiori’s equaliser, which marked his fifth goal involvement in 10 games for club and country this season, changed the momentum of the game. The Italian is such an unusual player but this was a simple goal, converted from close range after Ben White had headed the ball back across the penalty area. “The energy changed immediately,” said Arteta.

Right, where were we? Three weeks on from the fifth round of Premier League fixtures in which the champions Arsenal were beaten 3-0 by Brighton and todays’ visitors, Leeds United, who remain unbeaten, drew in a tepid goalless fixture with Crystal Palace, we finally get to the sixth. As worrying as the result was at the Amex, Arsenal still created a shedload of chances and last season they were the one club that battered Leeds in both games – first early in the season when they were playing four at the back and before Dominic Calvert-Lewin, a late signing, was fully fit, and then later after they had successfully switched to a “base formation” of three centre-backs and the tactical flexibility that allowed.

It was a 5-0 tanking in August and 4-0 in January, so to say Leeds travel to a ground at which they have never won more in hope than expectation would be as true as much as it is hackneyed. Adding to a sense of foreboding and thanks to Opta’s excellent Jonny Cooper, we also have to take into account the fact that Leeds, floodlight kings, have not won a Premier League match kicking off before 3pm since August 2022 and have not won a match in London since beating Brentford in May that year to save themselves from relegation. However, they do boast the division’s best defensive record – three goals conceded – and the No1 ranked goalkeeper in James Trafford who has a save percentage of 87. For all the sense of doom, though, Leeds have become a very good side and their spine – Trafford–Muharemovic–Ampadu–Calvert-Lewin marries steel and class.

Arsenall need no fanfare. Until the set-back in dear old Sussex by the sea, they had looked formidable most of the time and will have been champing at the bit to get back out there and prove it was an aberration. Mikel Arteta mentioned he has doubts about Declan Rice, Kai Havertz and Ezri Konsa but Ben White, Max Dowman and Chrsthian Mosquera are all available. For Leeds Joe Rodon is still out and Dan James was injured on international duty but Harry Wilson and Jean-Matteo Bahoya returned to training after knocks this week and Daniel Farke was relatively optimistic about their prospects.
