Joe Mazzulla downplays disastrous third straight loss: ‘We played well enough’

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

For the third straight time, the Boston Celtics went out like a dud, collapsing against the two-win Sacramento Kings on Saturday night without Jayson Tatum, Derrick White and Mitchell Robinson. But head coach Joe Mazzulla was rather dismissive afterward.

"I think we played well enough to win the game," Mazzulla told reporters, per CLNS Media, after Boston's 97-96, "and they made more shots than we did, and made a couple of more plays down the stretch. You take a look at the process of the game, we played well and lost. So that's what you take from [it] and move on to tomorrow."

Mazzulla turned to Paul George and Payton Pritchard to lead the pack. They shared the floor with Sam Hauser, Hugo González and Neemias Queta in Boston's starting lineup. Yet the team's strongest and most efficient contribution didn't come from any of them. It came from 39-year-old guard Mike Conley, who had made just four appearances for Boston entering the night, averaging eight minutes per game before receiving 18 minutes off the bench in Sacramento.

Conley scored 19 points, converting his first six consecutive shot attempts, while adding four rebounds, three assists and two steals. From the looks of it, Conley had himself a heroic, game-saving performance in the works, good enough to get the Celtics back in the column. He was active, up to speed and turned back the clock to his vintage Memphis Grizzlies days, even though he sat on the bench for the entire first and third quarters.

Nov 21, 2026; Sacramento, California, USA; Boston Celtics forward Paul George (13) reacts during the third quarter against the Sacramento Kings at Golden 1 Center. Mandatory Credit: Justine Willard-Imagn Images | IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect

Had it not been for Conley's out-of-the-blue scoring outburst, the Kings might've run the Celtics out of the building.

"He kept us in it," Mazzulla said. "Tried to finish the game with him as much as we could. Thought he was great on both ends of the floor and tonight's the reason why you have a guy like that on your roster, and he gave us a chance to win tonight."

Conley stood tall as Boston's lone bright spot, tying George for the team's lead in scoring while finishing as Boston's plus-minus leader (+17).

The Celtics performed well below their standard, underscoring the impact Tatum's absence had against a Kings team that leads the Western Conference in turnovers (17.5), boasts the NBA's second-lowest-scoring offense (106.8 points) and had won just one of its first 15 games entering Saturday night.

Without their superstar, the wheels fell off the wagon.

In the third quarter, Mazzulla's group got outscored 35-13 and went 0-for-13 from three.

With back-to-back rest days, the underperformance was all the more inexcusable.

Boston finished the night shooting 33.7 percent from the field, going 34-for-101. They shot 22.4 percent from 3-point range, going 13-for-58 and setting a franchise record for most misses (45) in a single game. At the free-throw line, they went 15-for-25, adding further fuel to the fire.

George went 6-for-21, Pritchard went 4-for-17 and Baylor Scheierman shot 2-for-11.

Coming off consecutive losses to the Orlando Magic and Denver Nuggets, this was their ugliest dud by far.

In the fourth quarter, the Celtics traded baskets with Sacramento as the clock ticked down. Queta hit a go-ahead 3-pointer from the corner with 3:47 remaining, only for Kings rookie Darius Acuff Jr. to drain the equalizer, also from beyond the arc. George grabbed an offensive rebound and finished the putback layup with 49 seconds left. Acuff answered by drawing a foul and sinking both free throws at the other end. Pritchard sank a clutch fadeaway jumper to give Boston a 96-95 lead with 12 seconds remaining. Then the Celtics failed to protect the glass on the ensuing possession.

Acuff challenged González off the inbound, drove past him and missed his right-handed layup. But Sacramento's Keegan Murray leaped over Devin Carter to secure the offensive rebound and finish the putback, giving the Kings a 97-96 lead and leaving Boston with just five seconds for redemption.

For the game, Pritchard was handed the ball against Murray but failed to create enough space for a clean look, capping an already miserable performance with a wasted opportunity.

Pritchard finished with nine points and missed all eight of his 3-point attempts, which he owned up to.

"It's frustrating," Pritchard told reporters. "I just got to play better. I gotta do more, so a lot of that is on me in the third quarter and stuff like that. So it's my fault for that one."

Pritchard on the final possession: "Obviously didn't get to a good enough spot, and [Murray] played good defense. But probably need to try to find a better look, though."

The Celtics have lost three consecutive regular-season games for the first time since opening last season with three straight defeats. They suffered their most humbling loss yet, deservedly getting served their lunch by arguably the worst team in the NBA.

"I thought we competed at a high level through just a drought offensively where we couldn't make shots," Mazzulla said. "And again, you have to stick to the process of it. I thought we played well enough to win, and we just didn't. They made a few more plays than we did."

Winning and playing "well enough" to win aren't mutually exclusive. However, the Celtics didn't meet the threshold necessary to leave Sacramento victorious. Their result was entirely warranted.
