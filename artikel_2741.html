Mitch Albom: All eyes on matchup with Bears, but let's not forget how Lions got here

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

A reporter to Dan Campbell after Sunday's Detroit Lions win over the Bucs: "I don't know if you'll get to even enjoy this for a couple hours--"

Campbell: "No, we won't. We're on to the next one! Let's go!"

OK, OK. I know everyone in Lions Land – including Campbell – is already itching for this week's showdown with the hated Chicago Bears on Thursday (1 p.m., CBS) – a matchup of two 8-2 teams atop the NFC North, and, let's face it, a measure of Midwest macho, the teacher (Campbell) versus the student (Ben Johnson) and America's previous "new" favorite (Detroit) versus America's current "new" favorite (Chicago.)

But may I suggest – if only for the length of this column – we stop and appreciate what the Lions have done the last six games to get to this point, and the mini-miracles they have pulled off to do so?

Understand that NFL teams don't often win six games in a row. Last season, only seven teams did it all year. The year before that, only six. The year before that, only four.

This is isn't the NBA or MLB, where hot streaks can carry you for several weeks. The NFL is set up every Sunday to be a challenge, a parity play, an injury or turnover-plagued game that can easily give the win to the underdog.

The Lions, after falling to 2-2 in October and hearing more soul-searching howls than a stage full of Hamlets, did nothing less than this:

Went to the desert and hung 45 points on the Cardinals.

Came home and beat the Packers on a last second field goal.

Handed Minnesota (then 6-0) its first defeat of the season.

Travelled to Miami and held the Dolphins to three field goals.

Flew a stupid number of hours to Munich, then wiped the German field with the Patriots, 35-10.

Flew all the way home and – despite having lost two defensive captains and their two best defenders, Jack Campbell and Aidan Hutchinson, to season ending injuries – clamped down on Tampa Bay, sacked Baker Mayfield four times, and held the Bucs to 274 yards in winning 31-21.

Detroit has gone from a defensive paint stain to a jelling defensive machine, from an offense that had to score 30 to win to a multi-spoked attack that can beat you any number of ways, including low-scoring affairs.

The Lions are finding ways to win, even if they doesn't always make sense statistically. They have more turnovers (eight) than takeaways (seven) in their last six wins, but when they have to, they get the key first down, they make the winning field goal, they hold the opponent out of the end zone.

Their game at Ford Field on Sunday, Nov. 22, was no different. The Bucs, despite their lousy record, have one of the best defenses in the league and are always a tough out. Detroit wrestled with them pretty evenly through three quarters, then found an extra gear in the running attack in the fourth quarter, ramming in one touchdown to push the lead to 10 and another to push it to 17.

"We're finding our strength," Campbell said in his press conference. "We're gaining strength. We're leaning on each other, and there's a lot of confidence right now in what we're able to do…just continuing to climb. And it's the right time."

Remember, this was not the conclusion after the first month of the season. And it's hard to redirect a ship once it's been out of the harbor for four weeks. The fact that the Lions have been able to do this without firing anyone, and without trading any big names or signing any big names, is a testament to the solid nature of the organization under Campbell and general manager Brad Holmes.

Nor should players like running back Jahmyr Gibbs, who was bottled up for most of the game Sunday and still finished with 98 rushing yards and two fourth-quarter touchdowns. Or quarterback Jared Goff, who is so ridiculously reliable, he is too easily overlooked. His long throws over the middle to various tight ends Sunday and his 57-yard bomb to Jameson Williams were things of beauty.

Yet as essential as he is, Goff threw just three passes among the 15 plays comprising the Lions' final two touchdown drives. Gibbs pretty much did the rest.

"Offensively, we can kind of play any way we need to, you know, depending on the opponent," Campbell confirmed. "The way we can move the ball or score … we can kind of become a chameleon."

Of course they have. You don't go from 2-2 and Campbell saying "we're playing very average football right now" and "our discipline is not good enough" and "we just kind of implode at times" to what we have right now – a churning, highly efficient, intensely focused football team that seems ready for anything.

They've been a chameleon, and it has served them well. Their changes have put them atop the division and nearly atop the conference.

But here is the best news. If you win the games you are supposed to win in the NFL, you are always going to have a shot. And because the Lions have really only lost to one team they probably should have beaten (Carolina), they go into the Chicago game with a bit of a cushion. Campbell and the team won't want to hear it, but even if they were to drop the Thanksgiving game and one or two of the remaining division games against the Vikings and Packers, if they continue their pattern of winning the expected ones, they should finish with no less than 11 wins, and 11 wins will certainly achieve their first priority when this season started:

Of course, that is shooting low. And the November Lions don't shoot low. They expect to be in every game, no matter who the opponent, and they expect to have a chance to win it.

Thursday will be a major test. The Bears have looked unstoppable at times on nationally televised wins. Their offense under former Lions offensive coordinator Ben Johnson is an eye-blinking Rube Goldberg contraption, and their defense, with the trade-deadline addition of Jeffery Simmons, is now smoking.

"This is the good stuff," Campbell said of the matchup, his voice rising. "This is this is stuff that gives you the tinglies. Big-time football. Big-time opponents, teeing off. Thanksgiving. We're the first show. It's exciting, man!"

Ah, well, call it what you will. Thursday will indeed be everything you want football to be. But we would be doing Campbell, defensive coordinator Kelvin Sheppard and especially the Lions players who have endured criticism, injuries and the Jack Campbell tragedy a big injustice if we didn't take a hat off to six straight wins in today's NFL.

And no matter what happens against the Bears, the Lions are now in it for the NFL winter run, and that's not necessarily something we would have predicted a month and a half ago. On a week that features Thanksgiving, a bit of gratitude seems only fitting.
