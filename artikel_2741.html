T.J. Watt, Steelers defense make statement in first game without Aaron Rodgers

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

PITTSBURGH – The Steelers punctuated a wild week that included the furious departure of future Hall of Fame quarterback Aaron Rodgers with an exclamation point on Black Friday.

Embattled coach Mike McCarthy's team secured an impressive 27-5 win against the Denver Broncos on Nov. 27 at Acrisure Stadium.

The defense generated a pair of touchdowns while Mason Rudolph, Rodgers' backup the past couple of seasons, led the offense to enough points to cruise amid struggles from the Broncos' own signal caller, Bo Nix.

Here's USA TODAY's report card on the action from a Pittsburgh perspective.

McCarthy didn't ask Rudolph to do too much within the game plan, and he didn't really have to as the defense led the scoring. Rudolph completed nine of 15 passes for 142 yards and a touchdown. Most of the throws were on the shorter and safer side, though he did seem more comfortable and willing to stand in the pocket to look downfield than Rodgers has been the past couple of weeks. If that continues, it will be a positive development for this offense. At the same time, it's way too soon to declare whether he'll be a better long-term option than Rodgers.

It was a third tepid week in a row for Jaylen Warren and Rico Dowdle, despite McCarthy leaning heavily into the running game by close to a 2-to-1 margin. Dowdle's 27-yard run in the third quarter helped goose the stats to over 100 yards for the day, but he gave the momentum right back by fumbling on the next play. Remove that big gainer from the equation and the rushing average was well below four yards per touch for the duo. Blocking is undeniably a problem, but neither guy is forcing many misses to bail the blockers out either. They've got to be better.

Rookie Germie Bernard generated the explosive play this offense has sorely lacked with a 58-yard catch and run in the first quarter. DK Metcalf later cashed that possession in with a 22-yard touchdown catch from Rudolph that represented his first meaningful first-half production in several weeks. It was an encouraging step forward after last week's multiple altercations with the Philadelphia Eagles that were largely forgotten once the drama with Rodgers began to unfold. Michael Pittman Jr. was rather quiet in his return from injury, but his presence seemed to help get Metcalf into single coverage. Roman Wilson added a 37-yard catch in the fourth quarter that helped salt the victory away.

Pittman's return and the heavy dose of runs marginalized Pat Freiermuth and Darnell Washington in the passing game. Both were among Rodgers' favorite targets, but it remains to be seen how much Rudolph does or does not want to utilize them, especially with his receiving group now mostly healthy for the first time since early in the season.

The protection of Rudolph was pretty good. He was sacked just once and had the luxury of several extended drops. His mobility relative to Rodgers' no doubt helped these guys look better. The run blocking still simply has not found its groove in the three games that left guard Mason McCormick has largely missed. Position coach James Campen has his work cut out for him in getting this group ready for December and January football.

Denver managed just 22 yards on the ground in the first half as the Pittsburgh front flew to the ball and staked itself to a comfortable lead. There were several tackles for a loss mixed in, and that forced Nix to throw more often than Denver probably wanted him to. Tough rush defense was a hallmark of Pittsburgh's four-game winning streak earlier in the season. If the guys up front can maintain this form, it will help as the weather conditions deteriorate down the stretch.

T.J. Watt recovered a fumble in the end zone for a touchdown in the first quarter to loudly announce his presence after a couple of quiet weeks in which he generated just one pressure in the two losses to Cincinnati and Baltimore. He added a fourth-quarter sack. Nick Herbig returned from injury with a key second-quarter sack that pushed the Broncos out of field goal range and thwarted a scoring threat. Payton Wilson and Patrick Queen were active again, each notching tackles for loss while helping the defensive front lock down on the Denver running game.

Cornerback/safety Jalen Ramsey intercepted Nix and returned it 36 yards for a touchdown shortly before halftime. It was one of the biggest plays he's made in Pittsburgh since general manager Omar Khan traded for him before the 2025 season. Asante Samuel Jr. added a fourth-quarter pick that solidified a comfortable lead. Nix generally struggled to make things happen against the Steelers' soft zone coverage scheme. Ramsey and Co. mostly kept the play in front of them and forced Denver to take small chunks at a time without making mistakes. Nix failed in that regard, so it was a nice day for this group that has really only struggled against Cincinnati's Joe Burrow over the past couple of months.

Patrick Graham's defense came ready to play at home. The disruptiveness and splash plays were badly needed and gave this offense room to breathe in front of a home crowd that probably would not have been friendly had Pittsburgh fallen behind. It was hard to see the "spark" McCarthy sought from the offense, though. The passing game still lacks verticality and McCarthy seems to want to force the run when it's not there in the absence of Rodgers to push back on him. For that reason, he's got to own it more than ever when the plan doesn't work particularly well. The offense did score just 13 points, after all.
