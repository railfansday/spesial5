What Chelsea Did Wrong vs Leeds Inc. Estevao, Henderson & More

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Humiliation is one way of putting it. Chelsea were decimated 4-1 by Leeds United at Stamford Bridge in an early low point of the Xabi Alonso era. Bad defeats have become commonplace under Clearlake but some, like this, are more difficult to stomach than others. Having had time to step back and analyse the game, we'll be going through the main things the Blues did wrong that caused the landslide defeat.

Moises Caicedo was withdrawn early on which is never a positive for the side. Before that injury, the team looked up for the match and were showing some control over proceedings. The manager made the wrong decision to replace the Ecuadorian as he brought on Jordan Henderson. The veteran had an absolute ghost session, barely impacting proceedings on either side of the ball.

His introduction forced the team to change everything and pushed Cole Palmer deeper to help the team progress the ball. With hindsight, bringing Valentin Barco on would have provided a better passer in the middle of the park and allowed the Blues to continue holding some sort of control over the match. Instead, the Argentine was left to play 0 minutes as he watched his teammates fail to create anything, finishing the match with a 0.64 xG.

The average position map for the majority of the match shows the Englishman receiving the ball very deep, very central, and completely isolated. At times his only options were to go back or pop bad passes over the back line for someone to try and run on to. It is never a good match when one of your key attackers gets just two touches in the opposition box.

With Morgan Rogers and Emmanuel Emegha focusing on the other channel and the midfield pivot being much deeper, the team was left with big gaps that prevented any sustainable build-up. The best outings we've seen from Chelsea this campaign were ones with smaller lines between players and more overloads providing options.

Comparatively, the Everton match saw Palmer supported consistently by Caicedo, wide runs from Pedro Neto, and Josh Acheampong providing a back-pass escape route with the left side overloading with a number of bodies.

Leeds are the kryptonite for Estevao, we know this, the manager knows this, he started the Brazilian against this very team in the Carabao Cup a few months ago and had to withdraw the attacker early. Last term was the same: in one of his final appearances under Enzo Maresca, Estevao was frustrated, stifled, and withdrawn early against Daniel Farke's back line. A staunch, physical back line is currently too much for the youngster to handle.

He was similarly ineffective this time around. Losing the majority of his duels, failing to hit on most of his crosses, and generally failing to impact the attacking line. A seasoned veteran like Pedro Neto could have provided a better performance, or at least more defensive help on transitions.
