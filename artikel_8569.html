Homemade gifts save the most when you make one or two ideas in a batch. Buy the supplies in bulk and make a dozen at once, so each gift costs a few dollars. Pick something people will eat or use up rather than a decoration that ends up in a closet.

Made one at a time, a homemade gift can cost as much as one from a store, because supplies come in packages sized for several gifts. A single jar of freezer jam still takes a whole box of pectin, the powder that makes it set.

A batch of a dozen spreads each package’s cost across several gifts. That brings each gift down to a few dollars, and you set up and clean up once instead of 12 times.

Say you have 12 people to buy for this holiday season and would otherwise spend $25 on each. Twelve jars of homemade soup mix at about $3 apiece come to $36 and about two hours of work, against $300 in store-bought gifts, so the batch saves $264.

Each of these 10 costs $4 or less a gift in a batch of a dozen, and all but one get eaten or put to use.

For a bridal shower, make one gift for the couple’s new home instead of a batch.

Make one test gift before you buy for the whole list, so a mix that won’t fit its jar or a paint that bleeds through a towel costs you one gift instead of twelve.
