Bears’ Caleb Williams Focused on Bouncing Back After Four-Interception Game

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Chicago Bears quarterback Caleb Williams' four-interception performance against the Tampa Bay Buccaneers in Week 9 was uncharacteristic of the ball security he had displayed throughout his NFL career.

The bigger question now is how Williams responds to that performance and bounces back over the remainder of the season. That will be especially important as the Bears enter the second half of the season with a challenging stretch of games remaining on their schedule.

Williams described two of the interceptions as "funky" and acknowledged that one was a bad decision, but the four-interception performance was still frustrating for the Bears quarterback, with his biggest disappointment stemming from feeling like he had let his teammates down.

"The biggest thing is, the frustration comes from the feeling of, for me, personally, I always don't want to let my teammates down," Williams said. "That's always big for me, and in those situations, I feel like did. That's the frustrating part that lingers."

Even after throwing four interceptions, Williams still led the Bears on a late-game drive that put them in position to win, setting up the field goal that sealed the game.

That ability to respond is something that Bears offensive coordinator Press Taylor has seen Williams demonstrate time and again, particularly in high-pressure situations. His history of delivering in critical moments has given the coaching staff confidence in how he handles adversity and moves forward from difficult performances.

"That's something that's been his thing, you know, late in last year, you think of the late game plays he's able to make," Taylor said. "He's demonstrated that over and over, so as you see it over and over and continue to reveal itself, then you're going to trust him more and more with that type of situations. And every game is really a learning opportunity for all of us to learn how everybody responds to certain situations."

Even with the four interceptions against Tampa Bay, Williams' career interception rate remains unmatched in NFL history among quarterbacks with 1,000 or more pass attempts. It's a testament to the level of ball security he has maintained throughout his young NFL career, and why one rough performance doesn't change the bigger picture.

With the bye week giving him time to step away from the field, Williams has shifted his focus from the frustration of the performance toward what he can learn from it and apply moving forward.

"I've moved on from it," Williams said. "It's more of a, how can I get better? What can I be better at? Just finding ways to be more consistent."

That starts with turning the page and shifting the focus to the New Orleans Saints. The Bears have remained committed to taking the season one game at a time, with the goal of going 1-0 each week rather than looking too far ahead.

For Williams, that means applying what he learned against Tampa Bay while keeping his attention on the opportunity in front of him.
