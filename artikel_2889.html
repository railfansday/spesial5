5 momentous plays in Week 10: Aidan Hutchinson's fateful celebration, Kirk Cousins' timely sack shift power in NFC North

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

This week's momentous plays included an injury on a celebration and a sack that actually helped the offense. That these two plays from separate games were connected via the tight NFC North race made them all the more weighty.

There's often a quirky element to game-deciding plays, but Week 10 seemed to take it to a very strange level, including a broken play that turned a potential punt into a 71-yard touchdown.

We also had another notable injury make the list, but for good measure, we finished this top-five off with a good, old-fashioned highlight-reel catch.

Here are this week's most momentous plays from the league's early games in Week 10.

One of the biggest losses happened in one of the largest victories of Week 10.

The Lions cruised to a 35-10 victory over the Patriots in Munich, and Aidan Hutchinson had a sack on the Patriots' opening drive, but it ended horribly. Hutchinson celebrated the sack of Drake Maye and came down awkwardly on his leg, falling to the ground.

The early reports are grim, suggesting Hutchinson might be done for the season. What a massive blow for a Lions defense that had made some real, tangible progress the past five games, holding each of their opponents to 21 or fewer points.

The Lions' victory kept them nominally in the tight NFC North race, sitting at 7-2 overall, but Hutchinson's injury threatens to undermine their chances of making the playoffs. It was a tough game for the Patriots, getting blown out and losing Drake Maye and A.J. Brown to injuries, but the Lions might have had more at stake.

There were several big momentum turns in the Minnesota-Green Bay game, mostly in the Vikings' favor, including a fumble recovery on the Packers' second offensive play on Sunday. But one massive sequence in the second quarter seemed to tilt the game dramatically in Minnesota's corner.

The Vikings were driving, trying to build on their 10-0 lead with the their defense dominating to that point. On a second-and-10 play from the Green Bay 20-yard line, Kirk Cousins appeared to throw an ill-timed interception in his first game back with the Vikings.

Cousins was more than happy to take the sack in that spot. His knee clearly was down as he was awkwardly trying to get rid of the ball, so the Vikings got a reprieve.

On third-and-15, Cousins made arguably his prettiest pass of the day, dropping in a beautiful ball for 24 yards to Tai Felton, setting Minnesota up at the 1-yard line. Aaron Jones would punch it in one play after that for a 17-0 Vikings lead and a stunned Lambeau Field.

The Packers lost a fumble on a wildly off-target shotgun snap, leading to another Vikings field goal, and they'd cough up a chance to make it a one-score game in the second half with a fumble on a kickoff return. It was that kind of day for the Packers, but they dug too big of a first-half hole to climb back and lost 30-10.

Drew Lock did his job replacing an injured Sam Darnold in the Seahawks' 21-13 win over the Raiders, and he did it his way. Lock's first touchdown pass of the game was a wild, swashbuckling play, hitting Cooper Kupp for a highlight score.

But Lock's second TD pass might have tilted the game in Seattle's favor — and he was lucky to have put the ball in a perfect spot.

The Seahawks were facing a third-and-3 from their own 29-yard line, nursing a 14-10 lead in the third quarter. Lock dropped back and appeared to have a wide-open Rashid Shaheed for a first-down chain mover. But Lock was dialed in on Jaxon Smith-Njigba, who stumbled a bit at the top of his route. That forced Lock to double-clutch and roll out to his left.

When Lock bought time, JSN regained his footing and turned on the jets. Lock threw a perfectly placed deep ball, and Smith-Njigba did the rest.

The Seahawks took back momentum in the game before that, but it was on a knife's edge. Had they failed to get the first down, they're punting to the Raiders in a one-score game. This touchdown provided the cushion the Seahawks needed.

The Jets' chances of beating the Bills got dented when Geno Smith took a big hit just before halftime.

Smith was high-lowed by Buffalo's Terrel Bernard and TJ Parker on the Jets' final offensive play of the first half. He went to the medical tent to be evaluated for a concussion.

The Jets kicked a field goal with 12 seconds left in the second quarter, cutting the Bills' lead to 10-6. Smith missed the start of the second half, and rookie backup Cade Klubnik led the Jets on a field-goal drive in his NFL debut, making it a 10-9 game.

Smith later cleared protocol, returning briefly,  but he left again with an ankle injury. The Jets' offense never found a rhythm, kicking one more field goal while Buffalo extended its lead to blowout territory in its 31-12 victory. Smith's injuries let Buffalo pull away after a tighter first half.

The Texans were doing whatever they wanted early on in Cleveland, storming out to a 17-0 lead as C.J. Stroud was on fire to start the game. The Browns started shifting the tide in the second quarter.

After a much-needed field goal and (finally!) a stop on defense, Deshaun Watson took a deep shot late in the second quarter. Watson had great time and found Jerry Jeudy for a 65-yard bomb down to the Houston 6-yard line.

Jeudy stumbled but hauled it in, setting up Watson's 6-yard keeper for a massive touchdown. That made it a 17-10 game with just over four minutes left in the first half and turned a potential blowout into a one-score game. The Browns finished off the first half strong with an interception of Stroud, and they dominated the third quarter, eventually taking a 20-17 lead and holding on for a 27-24 victory.

Jeudy is sometimes forgotten, but he's made a few clutch catches downfield this season, including a 33-yard grab against the Ravens in Week 6 on the late comeback drive and a 30-yarder against the Saints in Week 9 that helped set up a game-tying field goal. Sunday's catch was as big or bigger than either one.
