The Bitcoin Dominance Metric Just Fell Below 60%. Could Altcoin Season Be Right Around the Corner?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The current market environment is driven by fear rather than greed.

By Neil Patel – Nov 25, 2026 at 11:33AM EST

November hasn't been kind to Bitcoin (BTC -1.43%). The most valuable cryptocurrency has tanked 21% this month (as of Nov. 24). It's currently trading below $60,000, near its lowest level in the past 12 months.

Bearish sentiment is clearly taking over. It's impossible to pinpoint an exact reason for the recent decline. A higher-for-longer interest rate environment amid ongoing above-average inflation might be nudging investors to take some risk off the table.

As a result, Bitcoin dominance, which measures the market capitalization of the top digital asset relative to the overall cryptocurrency industry, is 58.6%. Does this reading suggest that altcoins are ready to explode?

Bitcoin dominance is calculated by dividing its market cap by the total market cap of all cryptocurrencies. As the number rises, investors are sticking with the most established digital asset. When it falls, they're usually more interested in smaller and riskier coins.

That makes it a useful sentiment gauge for the entire crypto industry.

History explains the excitement around a sub-60% reading. Bitcoin dominance started 2021 over 70% and sank to 40% by May. Altcoins like Dogecoin (SHIB -2.71%) and Shiba Inu (DOGE -2.03%) went wild that year. The same dynamic occurred to altcoins in 2017, when dominance plunged to an all-time low of about 37% in January 2018.

But those booms ended badly, and the most recent one is a warning. Dominance slipped below 60% in August 2025. It took two weeks for Ethereum (ETH -1.91%) to set a record of $4,953.73. Today, it trades under $1,700. That's a 66% plunge, worse than Bitcoin's 52% drop from its peak.

This time, the setup looks different. Past declines in Bitcoin dominance were driven by greed, especially in 2021. Today's decline is driven by fear.

Stablecoins, which are digital tokens pegged to the U.S. dollar, provide insight. They're now worth about $281 billion. The market share of Tether (USDT +0.00%), the largest stablecoin, expanded by one percentage point this month to 62.1%. However, total stablecoin supply shrank by $13.6 billion in November, so they aren't attracting fresh cash.

And investors aren't rotating into altcoins. The Altcoin Season Index sits at 57, far below the 75 that signals a true altcoin season.

I don't think Bitcoin dominance offers a clear takeaway. Investors are better off focusing on the bigger picture.

The rest of 2026 could stay rough. U.S. spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds (ETFs) saw $4.7 billion in net outflows this month. Nobody can predict when the selling will stop.

But zoom out. Bitcoin's supply is capped at 21 million coins, and it operates with a predictable inflation rate. The next halving, which halves new supply from miners, is expected in April 2028. Scarcity is central to the thesis, and it's built into the code.

Wall Street has also entered the space. The three largest U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs held 1.1 million coins. According to River, a Bitcoin financial services firm, 16 leading U.S. banks have launched or are working on Bitcoin solutions for their customers. Bank of America (BAC -1.62%) lets its more than 15,000 wealth advisors recommend a 1% to 4% crypto allocation. Vanguard reversed course and opened its platform to crypto ETFs.

Bitcoin is the safest bet in the industry. It has the longest track record, the highest value, and the most institutional support. But safest doesn't mean it's free of risk or volatility. Despite being the oldest cryptocurrency with brand recognition, tremendous liquidity, and a global network effect, Bitcoin still experiences significant drawdowns.

Investors should size any position accordingly. And stay focused on the next decade, not the dominance chart.
