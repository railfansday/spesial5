Texans' new 'HOUSTON/US' uniforms a love letter to city's culture, street tiles

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

When it comes to NFL uniform diversity, the Houston Texans deserve a nod for their willingness to break with tradition − while also embracing it civically.

While they're the league's youngest franchise, currently celebrating its 25th season, the Texans have developed a heritage aside from being on the verge of continuing their streak of being the only NFL franchise to never appear in a conference championship game. Such unwanted history aside, they're the only NFL club that's never altered its logo. And through the 2023 campaign, their uniform closet hardly changed − even their alternate helmets and jerseys were essentially "Battle Red" versions of their primaries.

But the Texans reimagined their look in 2024. That year, they slightly modified their home and road uniforms, the most notable departure a shoulder stripe on the white roadies that evoked a horn which married well with the team's "bullhead" logo. But the introduction of two new alternate uniforms represented a broad creative departure for the team, which incorporated quite a bit of feedback from Houstonians.

Now the Texans are at it again, set to debut their "HOUSTON/US" uniforms, part of Nike's "Rivalries" endeavor, in front of a prime-time audience on "Thursday Night Football" for their Nov. 19 game against the Indianapolis Colts.

As they do, the Texans have incorporated quite a bit of symbolism into their new threads.

While predominantly "Liberty White," the uniforms are trimmed in the team's familiar "Battle Red." However "H-Town Blue," a cousin of the "Columbia Blue" made famous by the Oilers, Houston's former team, replaces the "Deep Steel Blue" the Texans usually sport. Thematically, the design is inspired the city's street culture.

The Battle Red stripes − and they have fancy "candy paint" finishes − on the helmet's centerline, shoulders and pants are bordered by H-Town Blue stripes in a tiled pattern that honors the city's ubiquitous street tiles, which date to the early 20th century. The tiling is also used to spell out players' names on the back of the jerseys as well as "H-Town" and "Texans" on the front and back helmet bumpers, respectively.

The Liberty White helmet, which sparkles with an icy finish, is an homage to the version the team almost employed prior to its inaugural season in 2002 before opting for the Deep Steel Blue one that's been in use since the club's inception. The H-Town Blue facemask has a chrome finish that very much pops amid its intended callback to the city's car culture. The "Blackletter 'H' logo" the team introduced for one of its alternate helmets in 2024 appears on both sides of the headgear, redone in mostly white with silver and blue shading. A white version of the bullhead logo resides on the back of the helmet.

The blocky H-Town Blue jersey numbers have a font meant to resemble Houston's iconic "Be Someone" Bridge and also have a chrome-like sheen complementing the facemask. The same font is used to spell "HOUSTON" inside the jersey collar, mostly in Battle Red, though the "US" part is in H-Town Blue.

"If you look back at the rich history of the city and the rich history of football, there are special nods that we have on there," said team president Mike Tomon. "And to have a uniform that allows us to speak to our great city makes it so unique."

Think of them as the football version of the sports apparel company's NBA "City Edition" uniforms or Major League Baseball's "City Connect" jerseys. Nike has strived to create something that further strengthens NFL teams' bonds to their unique civic environments. And, as "rivalries" would suggest, all of them will be worn in intra-divisional matchups.

"Rivalries uniforms will celebrate storied local traditions and unite fan communities with designs unique to select cities and teams," Nike announced during the program's launch in 2025.

"The designs are rooted extensively in the legacies and inspirations true to each team, serving as authentic, competitive expressions of community pride while giving athletes and fans an opportunity to connect like never before."

Eventually all of them will. This year, AFC South and NFC North squads are joining the AFC East and NFC West teams that kicked off the initiative last year. Every team that has a "Rivalries" uni is scheduled to wear it once this season. The other four divisions will be added to the rotation over the next two seasons, and the "Rivalries" option then becomes part of a team's closet for the following three years.
