Jim Cramer says Nvidia’s leadership shake-up won’t derail the AI trade

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

CNBC's Jim Cramer said Monday that Nvidia's leadership shake-up won't derail the artificial intelligence boom.

Cramer's comments came after another rough day for AI-related stocks in the wake of Jensen Huang's resignation from Nvidia last week. Investors have been worried that Huang's departure as CEO could jeopardize the chipmaker's financial support for the broader AI ecosystem, especially computing providers known as neoclouds. Nvidia is set to report earnings Tuesday night.

Cramer argued that demand for computing power extends far beyond Nvidia and its former CEO.

"The most important thing you need to know is that Nvidia is not going to bring down the western world," the "Mad Money" host said. "It is not going to bring down the data center world."

Under Huang, Nvidia built an extensive investment portfolio, including a $5 billion stake in Intel last year and an additional investment in AI cloud provider CoreWeave earlier this year. Nvidia had helped anchor CoreWeave's IPO in 2025 and participated in a 2023 Series B round for the company. In addition to taking equity stakes in other companies, Nvidia has provided other financial guarantees to customers, such as a $105 billion backstop for a sprawling data center project intended for OpenAI.

JPMorgan strategist Michael Cembalest, who publishes the closely followed Eye on the Market commentary, estimated that Nvidia's broader financial commitments may have reached $530 billion, based on the company's latest quarterly securities filing. Still, Cramer rejected the notion that the AI industry depends entirely on the chipmaker's backing.

"You can't overstate the importance of Jensen Huang to all of the AI investment that we've seen and expect to see," Cramer said. "But the idea that he was somehow all of the AI world is ludicrous."

Cramer expects interim CEO Colette Kress to take a more conservative approach, potentially scaling back some of Nvidia's financial commitments. While that could pressure heavily indebted companies, he said strong demand for computing power will sustain the broader AI build-out.

"Will there be a rein in of guarantees?" Cramer asked. "I hope so. Will Nvidia be more conservative? I am sure of it. Will artificial intelligence be stopped in its tracks? Are you kidding me?"

More generally, Cramer expressed confidence in Kress, praising her financial discipline and expecting a measured outlook when Nvidia reports earnings Tuesday. "I think interim CEO Kress will present a balanced non-hyperbolic view of the situations at Nvidia tomorrow night," he said. "I do not think it will cause a tsunami of selling."

A successful Anthropic IPO later this week could further restore confidence in the AI trade, Cramer argued.

"If the Anthropic deal works later this week, look out – we might see investors scrambling to get back into the same stocks that were sold today," Cramer said.
