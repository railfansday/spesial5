Southwest Plans To End The Wait For Extending Its Schedules—11 Month Rolling Calendar Is Coming, Report Says

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Southwest Airlines plans to stop making customers wait for batches of new flight dates and move to a rolling booking calendar roughly 11 months into the future, more like other airlines operate, according to a new report by Enilria. That would make it easier to lock in a family vacation or flights to a cruise—and give its international partners more opportunities to sell trips that include Southwest connections. I checked Southwest’s own booking calendar. They’re accepting reservations through September 29, 2027. Their next extension is planned for December 17, when they’ll open flights through November 6, 2027. That’s 324 days into the future. That December release is still a batch. The plan to replace these releases with a rolling window isn’t something I’ve seen anywhere other than from Enilria. Southwest Has Already Sold Flights More Than 300 Days Ahead There’s some history here that helps explain what’s changing. On April 26, 2024, Southwest announced its first booking window exceeding 300 days. It was selling travel through March 5, 2025—313 days ahead. At the time, Southwest said its historical range had been 180 to 250 days, citing vacation planning, meetings and groups, Hawaii, and its international destinations as reasons to sell further out. With batch releases, though, the window shrinks every day until the next extension. You might be able to book ten months ahead on release day, then considerably less several weeks later. A rolling calendar keeps adding dates. The reported change would give customers roughly the same planning horizon whenever they shop, without needing to track Southwest’s next release announcement. Shorter Schedules Meant Fewer Reservations To Fix I wrote about Southwest’s tradeoff back in 2020: waiting to publish let the airline offer a firmer schedule, but cost them customers who wanted to commit earlier. An airline can decide where to put its planes before selling the seats, or sell a provisional schedule and fix customers’ reservations when its plans change. Southwest preferred to finish more of that work first. That gave them room to adjust for seasonal demand and aircraft availability without disrupting as many purchased trips. Their technology made changing flights expensive. In a 2010 investor presentation, then-CEO Gary Kelly explained that when Southwest canceled a flight: we have to manually rebook every single customer on that flight Southwest completed its move to Amadeus Altéa in May 2017. This allowed for automated rebooking during disruptions, better connection planning, and more sophisticated inventory management. So the technology that makes a longer selling window practical has been there for years. The commercial case for using it has grown. International Partners Have A Reason To Want More Dates Southwest increasingly needs to appear wherever customers are shopping. I explained how the Expedia deal would change their incentives when it was announced in 2025. If a customer searches dates Southwest isn’t selling, competitors get the business before Southwest even enters the comparison. The airline had already expanded booking capabilities through agency reservation systems in 2020. More recently, SAP Concur shut down its old Southwest direct connection in July 2025 as Southwest’s new fare structure required a different booking path. And Southwest now lists 12 international airline partners, including Icelandair, EVA Air, Singapore Airlines, Turkish Airlines, and newly added French bee. These combined itineraries are sold through the partners’ websites and distribution channels. A foreign airline can have its flight available for sale while Southwest hasn’t opened the domestic connection yet. The partner can’t issue the complete itinerary using that Southwest flight. It may sell a connection on another U.S. airline instead and Southwest loses the passenger. That makes partner sales a strong reason to extend the window. The underlying partnership work also predates Elliott Management’s intervention. I covered Southwest recruiting to build airline partnerships in June 2022. And the longer schedule announcement came in April 2024. Even a roughly 330 day window won’t match every overseas partner. EVA Air sells flights up to 360 days ahead. This would narrow the gap, while still leaving some early international bookings out of Southwest’s reach. Earlier Booking Comes With More Time For Plans To Change Selling earlier gives Southwest more chances to win customers. Direct bookings bring cash in sooner. The recurring benefit comes from business Southwest otherwise would’ve lost, or from selling its existing seats more profitably. Against that are distribution costs, servicing reservations, and the work of repairing itineraries when schedules change. Customers should expect more exposure to schedule revisions when booking further ahead. A departure can move, a nonstop can become a connection, and a changed domestic flight can break the connection to an international departure. With assigned seating, moving passengers also means dealing with their seat selections. And the first day a flight goes on sale isn’t necessarily its cheapest day. Opening dates and opening inexpensive inventory are separate decisions. In fact, airlines may hold back cheap fares waiting to see how flights sell, and also figuring that eager buyers may be less price-sensitive. And there’s a real a problem buying Southwest’s cheapest cash tickets this far ahead. As I’ve covered before, Basic fare credits expire six months after the original purchase. Southwest says a voluntary cancellation after that deadline produces no flight credit. If you buy a Basic ticket 11 months ahead, then cancel two months before departure, you’ve already passed that deadline by three months. The original reservation is valid, but you get nothing if you cancel. I’d be much more comfortable using Rapid Rewards points for a speculative booking that far out. Cancel at least 10 minutes before the original departure and the points go back to the account, without the six month expiration attached to the cash fare.
