Hilton spent years buying and licensing non-traditional hotel brands. Its new ad campaign is the bill coming due: It has to teach travelers about the new places to stay.

Hilton’s fastest-growing hotel categories are the ones travelers least associate with Hilton: luxury and lifestyle. Its “New Ways to Stay” marketing campaign is an attempt to close that gap.

“Our luxury and lifestyle portfolios continue to grow the most quickly across categories, with trailing 12-month net unit growth rates of 11% and 20%, respectively,” CEO Chris Nassetta said on an earnings call last month. ("Lifestyle" brands  are design-forward hotels with distinctive decor and amenities.)

Since 2024, Hilton bought Graduate Hotels and NoMad, partnered with outdoor lodging operator AutoCamp, and signed a loyalty licensing deal with Small Luxury Hotels of the World that put hundreds of independent hotels into its channels.

Customer awareness about the new properties was limited, and Hilton's research found that many travelers didn’t know they could earn loyalty points by booking them through its site and app.

Hilton's luxury and lifestyle portfolios are growing faster than any other category — trailing 12-month net unit growth of 11% and 20%, respectively, CEO Chris Nassetta said on an earnings call last month — but the company's research found many travelers didn't know they could earn loyalty points by booking those properties through Hilton's site and app. The "Is This Hilton?" campaign, launched September 14 around the Emmys, is the fix. Hilton spent $2.5 million on national linear TV across 579 airings, about two-thirds of its linear TV spending across all brands in that period, according to iSpot.tv estimates, and paired it with real-time social posting, a creator roster that runs from niche influencers to Formula 1 driver Lando Norris, Paris Hilton, and a Times Square pop-up. Much of the new inventory isn't Hilton-run: by Skift's count, Hilton neither owns nor operates roughly 585 of the luxury and lifestyle properties it has added in recent years, most through the Small Luxury Hotels loyalty licensing deal, alongside Graduate Hotels, NoMad, AutoCamp, Yotel, and an Explora Journeys cruise partnership. SVP of global marketing Daniel Reynolds said the real test is whether Hilton's brand tracker shows a shift in perception among target audiences, "and that can take a long time."
