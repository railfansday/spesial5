If you’re not booking through hotel loyalty programs, you’re leaving money on the table. These programs offer cash discounts, free upgrades, complimentary breakfast, access to exclusive lounges and even free nights once you accumulate enough points. Now the question is, which hotel program should you invest in?

In this guide, we'll cover the top hotel rewards programs and help you decide which one(s) suit your travel needs.

There are many factors to consider when choosing a hotel rewards program, including:

Let's dive into the pros and cons of each program.

Marriott's impressive portfolio features more than 10,000 properties across 30-plus brands and 140-plus countries and territories, ranging from basic business hotels to safari camps and cabins. No matter where you’re going, there’s bound to be a Marriott nearby.

A key perk of the Marriott Bonvoy program is its fifth-night-free benefit: When redeeming points for five or more consecutive nights, you’ll receive the lowest-priced night for free. This can save you a ton of points, especially if you book an award stay in a high-end destination like the Maldives.

And with several Marriott credit cards and three major 1:1 transfer partners — American Express Membership Rewards, Bilt Points and Chase Ultimate Rewards — it's easy to earn the points you need for your next redemption.

You can get even more from the Bonvoy program if you reach one of its five elite status tiers. Benefits include room upgrades, lounge access and Nightly Upgrade Awards (formerly Suite Night Awards).

Another notable benefit is that members can transfer Bonvoy points to dozens of airline partners, including some with hard-to-earn currencies.

With over 9,400 properties and 28 brands in 144 countries and territories, Hilton can give Marriott a run for its money. Couple its size with the relative effortlessness of earning Hilton Honors points, and the Hilton Honors program may be the most all-around convenient hotel rewards option.

Like Marriott, Hilton offers the fifth consecutive award night free — but you must have at least Hilton Honors Silver elite status to unlock this benefit. Thankfully, you can achieve this simply by holding any Hilton credit card. Some cards also offer valuable free night certificates or statement credits.

Hilton is an Amex Membership Rewards transfer partner with a handy 1:2 ratio, meaning you'll get 2 Hilton Honors points for every Amex point you transfer (or more if you find a transfer bonus). You can also transfer Bilt Points to Hilton, though at a lower 1:1 ratio.

World of Hyatt has long been a favorite at TPG, largely because it still uses an award chart and has relatively cheap award rates compared to other hotel programs, even after recent devaluations. For example, standard rooms at Hyatt’s most upscale hotels (excluding all-inclusive resorts, Miraval resorts and Mr & Mrs Smith properties) top out at 75,000 points per night, while a luxury Hilton property could cost up to a whopping 250,000 points.

The downside is that Hyatt offers a relatively small footprint. Hyatt operates 1,500-some hotels (and a few glamping sites) across more than 80 countries, but most are in popular destinations and cities. If you often visit rural areas, World of Hyatt points may not serve you as well.

Plus, since World of Hyatt currently offers only two cobranded credit cards, points aren’t quite as easy to accrue as in some competitor programs. (Though Hyatt has teased that additional cobranded cards may come as part of its expanding partnership with Chase.)

Members of Bilt and Chase Ultimate Rewards can transfer points to World of Hyatt, with a 1:1 ratio for Bilt and either 1:1 or 4:3 for Chase, depending on which credit card you have.

TPG staff members and readers have repeatedly stated that earning Hyatt Globalist status is the most valuable hotel elite status. With free breakfast, waived resort and parking fees on award nights, consistent suite upgrades and more, this status is about as close to a VIP experience as you can get in the hospitality world.

There’s arguably no better hotel program for stays in small-town America than Wyndham Rewards. The program encompasses over 8,000 hotels across 25 brands, many of which are budget brands — such as Super 8, Days Inn and Microtel — ubiquitous in areas where you won’t find a Hyatt or Marriott. If your travel goals involve getting off the beaten path or road-tripping through towns with populations under 10,000, the Wyndham Rewards program is a great one to focus on.

Unfortunately, Wyndham's loyalty program took a hit when members lost the ability to redeem Wyndham points for Vacasa vacation rentals, a benefit that had previously offered great value. It also suffered a devaluation in 2026 when it introduced a fourth award tier, increasing the maximum redemption from 30,000 points to 45,000 points per night.

Wyndham offers three cobranded credit cards, all issued by Barclays, to help you earn free nights. Additionally, you can transfer Bilt points, Capital One miles, Citi ThankYou Rewards and Chase Ultimate Rewards to Wyndham Rewards at a 1:1 ratio.

IHG One Rewards points are easy to earn, and IHG has properties in almost as many locations as Hilton, including some sought-after Six Senses resorts that are bookable with IHG points.

IHG offers three cobranded credit cards, which were revamped in 2026 with new benefits, more ways to earn elite status and, in some cases, higher annual fees. Unfortunately, starting in 2027, cardholders will need to spend $5,000 in a calendar year to unlock the card's fourth-night-free perk, which used to come free with any IHG card. Still, if you meet the spend threshold, this perk is unique among loyalty programs and a better value than Marriott and Hilton’s fifth-night-free policies.

You can transfer Chase Ultimate Rewards points and Bilt points to IHG One Rewards at a 1:1 ratio, though you can often buy IHG points at a steep discount, which could be a better option.

IHG One Rewards offers a solid elite status program, and elite members can earn Milestone Rewards — like an annual lounge membership, bonus points and confirmed suite upgrades — by staying a certain number of nights in a calendar year.

Points in the ALL Accor program are the most valuable — and straightforward — of all the major hotel rewards currencies. For every 1,000 points you redeem, you’ll get 20 euros (roughly $25 at the time of writing) off your hotel bill. That comes out to a value of over 2 cents per point, higher than any other hotel currency.

Although the simplicity of this program may appeal to many travelers, there are two areas in which ALL underdelivers: The vast majority of its nearly 6,000 properties are concentrated in Europe and Asia, and it does not offer a cobranded credit card to help you earn points quickly.

Additionally, while ALL Accor has several major transfer partners, all offer a less-than-ideal transfer ratio: 3:2 for Bilt, 2:1 for Capital One and Citi, and 1.5:1 for Rove Miles.

Still, Accor can be a great place to earn hotel points if you primarily travel in Asia or Europe.

The program has four levels of elite status, with top-tier Diamond offering free breakfast on weekends (or every day in the Asia-Pacific region), Suite Night Upgrades and executive lounge access. You’ll also find brand-specific benefits at each status tier, such as complimentary bike rentals at Fairmont properties.

With nearly 7,500 properties in over 50 countries, including Radisson Rewards Americas hotels, Choice’s footprint rivals that of better-known loyalty programs like Hilton and Marriott. However, the Choice Privileges program can serve you similarly to Wyndham Rewards in that its participating hotels are more prolific in smaller towns.

Choice Privileges is famously valuable for anyone staying in Scandinavia, as its award rates remain extremely reasonable despite the region’s often costly hotels. And in 2025, the program expanded its award booking window from 100 days to 50 weeks — bringing it more in line with the other programs on this list.

In terms of earning points, Choice offers two cobranded credit cards. You can transfer points at a 1:1 ratio from Amex Membership Rewards and Capital One miles, at a 1:1.5 rate from Citi ThankYou Rewards and at a 1:2 rate from Wells Fargo Rewards.

Historically, Choice Privileges elite status has offered few perks. There are limited room upgrades and only a handful of brands offer complimentary breakfast to top-tier elite members.

The Best Western Rewards program doesn’t get much attention in the points and miles world, partly because it offers only two credit cards and has no transferable rewards currency partners. However, it’s worth exploring if you often stay at Best Western properties — or if you're loyal to WorldHotels Rewards, which will merge with Best Western Rewards in 2027.

With around 4,300 hotels in over 100 countries and territories, ranging from budget to boutique, Best Western’s portfolio is in the middle of the pack. Most of its properties offer complimentary breakfast to all guests, making this a great way for hungry travelers to save money without having to earn elite status.

Another highlight is Best Western’s status match program, which makes it easy to earn top-tier elite status if you have even mid-tier status with a competing hotel chain. However, as with Choice Privileges, Best Western’s elite status tiers offer relatively small benefits.

Radisson Rewards used to be an incredible loyalty program, but many changes, including a move to dynamic pricing, have stripped much of its value over the years.

The good news is that since Choice Hotels acquired Radisson’s Americas operation in 2022, you can earn and redeem Choice points at these hotels. The rest of Radisson’s portfolio still uses the Radisson Rewards program.

This international bent means Radisson Rewards no longer offers a cobranded credit card in the U.S. and has no transfer partners. You can only earn these points by staying at Radisson hotels outside of the Americas. This has made it difficult for many U.S.-based travelers to get much value from Radisson Rewards.

Though it does not offer as big a portfolio as major hotel brands like Hilton and Marriott, I Prefer Hotel Rewards is great for travelers who want to stay at a luxury boutique property. If a historically preserved hotel, premium boutique property or castle-turned-resort is how you want to vacation, I Prefer Hotel Rewards should be on your radar.

Unfortunately, not every property is available to book with I Prefer points.

While it is harder to earn I Prefer points compared to other hotel rewards, Capital One miles, Bilt points and Citi ThankYou Rewards points all transfer to this program at an appealing rate of 1:2. Therefore, travelers who collect these currencies can score some great deals on award stays.

A hotel can be far more than a place to rest your head. Hotel loyalty programs can dramatically enhance your vacation — and even make it practically free.

We encourage TPG readers to sign up for all hotel loyalty programs, as there’s generally no cost to join, and register for hotel promotions. Even if you don’t think you’ll stay at a certain hotel chain, circumstances may dictate otherwise, and you’ll be glad you enrolled beforehand. You don't have to limit yourself to a single program, either; there are benefits to using multiple hotel programs.

Whichever program(s) you end up using, be sure to understand when your hotel points expire and how to keep them active.
