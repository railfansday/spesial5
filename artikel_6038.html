Expedia's partner business has carried the company's growth for years. A slowdown caused by a tough comparison to last year's fourth quarter would be a blip. One driven by deteriorating economics and potentially a stronger rival in Booking Holdings would be a much larger issue.

Expedia Group’s B2B business, the engine behind most of its recent growth, is set to slow. Expedia’s chief financial officer blames a tough comparison with last year. A Bernstein analyst sees pressures that could last longer.

B2B gross bookings grew 19% year over year in the third quarter, and B2B revenue rose 23%. But when Expedia reported results on November 4, CFO Derek Andersen told analysts the segment’s growth would moderate in the fourth quarter. A year earlier, heavy marketing by some of Expedia’s largest partners helped push B2B gross bookings up 24%, to $8.7 billion, a high bar to top.

Andersen also noted softer demand tied to the U.S. election cycle, geopolitical disruptions in the Middle East and Europe hitting long-haul travel, and a fall sale that pulled bookings forward into Q3.

Expedia Group's B2B segment, the main driver of its recent growth, is set to slow after a quarter in which B2B gross bookings rose 19% and revenue rose 23%. On the November 4 earnings call, CFO Derek Andersen said growth would moderate in the fourth quarter, citing last year's heavy partner marketing that pushed Q4 2024 B2B gross bookings up 24% to $8.7 billion, softer demand tied to the U.S. election cycle, geopolitical disruptions in the Middle East and Europe affecting long-haul travel, and a fall sale that pulled bookings into Q3. Bernstein senior analyst Richard Clarke called B2B the weak spot in an otherwise strong quarter and told Skift four factors are at work: larger partners negotiating bigger revenue shares, lower-margin products such as flights and expanded services in the Travel Agent Affiliate Program, new competition from Booking Holdings' newly combined Booking Partner Division, and the tough year-over-year comparison. Margins are already moving: segment adjusted EBITDA rose 17% to $470 million while margin fell 134 basis points to 27.5%, which Expedia attributed mainly to partner mix and to investments including the Tiqets and CarTrawler acquisitions. B2B generated $1.71 billion of Expedia's $4.94 billion in Q3 revenue, while the consumer business grew gross bookings only 7%. Expedia declined to comment for this story.
