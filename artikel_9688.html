Apple Crisp

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

My one-and-only apple crisp recipe! Tender jammy honeycrisp apples under a blanket of buttery brown sugar oat topping. The perfect fall treat!

Published by Lindsay Ostrom on September 29, 2026

I jotted this apple crisp recipe into the notes app in my phone several years ago, and I return to it every fall.

Jammy, cinnamon-scented apples baked under a heavy (and I do mean heavy) blanket of buttery crumble.

I remember first making this in our old kitchen for a late-October dinner party – I made wild rice soup and apple cider old fashioneds (unreal), and someone pulled out the guitar just as the smell of apple crisp was floating from the oven. Absolutely charmed. I obviously bonded with this specific apple crisp that night and it has been with me ever since.

Why it’s good, if you ask me: the caramely apple layer underneath gets properly jammy, the topping gets properly crisp – just enough to provide contrast, AND, and, and, where the two layers melt together in the middle is just pure fall magic. Soft, buttery, and delicious.

Apple crisp in general is very simple, and this one feels rustic, almost, especially if you make it in a cast iron pan, which I usually do. Is she making apple crisp or is she living out her dreams from Little House on the Prairie on a chilly fall night? We’ll never know.

Toss apples with sugar, cinnamon, and flour. This photo shows apples that have been thinly sliced on a mandoline. You can also dice them! Both delicious.

I stir it up with a spoon or fork, and then bring it together into crumbles with my hands.

It’ll turn into a beautiful golden crumble with a layer of jammy apples underneath.

This can work with or without peeling the apples. Personally, I don’t peel the apples – I am lazy, and I just can’t be bothered to mess with what is already a perfectly good fruit. The peels cook down the same way the apples do, so they become soft and unobtrusive in the apple crisp.
