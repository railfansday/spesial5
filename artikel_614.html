With ‘Guarding Stars,’ Jared Padalecki Finally Takes A Break From All That Action

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The 'Walker' and 'Supernatural' star enjoys a change of pace in Netflix's latest romantic comedy, based on Katherine Center's 'The Bodyguard.'

Jared Padalecki has been steadily employed on television since his days on aughts classic Gilmore Girls, so it’d be easy to assume that playing an actor in the new Netflix holiday offering Guarding Stars would be second nature. But the 44-year-old, who also produces the movie, found the role to be more difficult than he’d expected.

“Jack Stapleton in this world is super famous,” he tells The Hollywood Reporter about this character. “He’s Brad Pitt, he’s George Clooney, he’s Chris Hemsworth — people who can’t walk outside. It’s not as though Padalecki is claiming to fly completely under-the-radar. He admits that he’ll often encounter people in public who stop him to share their love for Supernatural or Gilmore Girls, but he’s still able to go to his son’s games or his daughter’s field hockey practices. It helps, certainly, that he and his wife, actress and fellow Guarding Stars producer Genevieve Padalecki, live in a ranch outside Austin, Texas, rather than in Los Angeles.

Guarding Stars, released on the streamer on Nov. 18, is based on Katherine Center’s romance novel The Bodyguard and features a delightful role reversal. Rather than Padalecki, who’s spent much of his adult life starring in action-driven projects, as the dutiful protector, his character Jack finds himself under the care of bodyguard Hannah Brooks, played by Leighton Meester.

The book, while not set at Christmas, has the same spirit as its onscreen adaptation. The Padaleckis were responsible for getting the project off the ground after Gen Padalecki featured Center’s book in her online book club and interviewed the author four years ago.

When the interview ended, Gen Padalecki told Center that she thought the book would make a great movie. The author agreed, and the couple soon had acquired the rights and began shopping the project around. Eventually, it landed at Netflix, underwent a name change and found its badass leading lady in Meester and formable leading man in Padalecki. Center, throughout it all, took any changes to her material in stride, Padalecki says, mimicking her response when changes would arise, “It’s not a documentary.”

Starring in a romance film is a lane change for Padalecki, who spent 15 seasons fighting demons, vampires and all other creatures that go bump in the night on the long-running CW series Supernatural. “Truth be told, I enjoy all those genres, and it’s more that’s what was hiring,” he says when it’s suggested he’s spent almost his entire career in the action space.

“Frankly, I don’t like doing action. I don’t hate it, but I really love the character exploration part of storytelling,” Padalecki continues. “I’d be happy to do a series or movie where I just sat in a chair and talked with somebody.”

There’s plenty of action in Guarding Stars, it’s just most of it falls on Meester, rather than Padalecki. She was “game for all of it,” he says. The actor recalls one of the film’s central action sequences, that forces Meester onto the roof of a car. The scene was shot practically, with Meester in a harness atop the actually moving car.

“It was -10 degrees Fahrenheit outside,” he says. “The camera equipment was freezing. It was that cold. Even the Calgarians had never seen it happen before. “But she was like, ‘Yeah, I can do it again.'”

Meester was on the top of Padakecki’s radar from the beginning. He refers to the actress as the film’s “Moby Dick” cheekily. “She already had a huge body of work. She has incredible comic timing. She has a fantastic work ethic,” the actor says about his co-star. The team called around to people that knew her, and they all came back with the same answer. They loved her.

Meester and Padalecki had plenty of freedom to have fun in the takes. “[There’s a scene where] we’re having a snowball fight, and she just smashes snow into my face. Not scripted,” he says, laughing. “Maybe she was getting out some frustration or something, but it was in good fun.”

Padalecki and his wife met on Supernatural. The pair starred alongside one another in the show’s fourth season, and according to the actor, they’ve always had spirited debates about film and television. Naturally, working together through their production company Living in the Asterisk came easy.

Padalecki enjoys the lack of hierarchy between the two. It’s just like how the pair work together at home. “If I’m free, I’m taking the kids to school. If she’s free and I’m not, then she’s taking the kids to school. It’s just really legitimately teamwork,” he says.

“I love the way her brain works, and I love to see it in action. She has an incredible work ethic. It’s intimidating, but she inspires me and motivates me,” he adds. “I hope I can offer some bit of the same to her.”

Another draw to the film for Padalecki was the understanding that this might be something the entire family can watch, which can’t be said for all his past work. When the couple optioned the book, their oldest son was 10, their younger song was nine and their daughter was five. “I was probably, whether I realized or not, hoping to find something they could watch,” he admits.

Padalecki wasn’t, and frankly isn’t much still, interested in showing the children Supernatural. It’s not that he isn’t proud of his time on the show, but more about his desire to not traumatize his children given he’s “killing everybody” in the show, including their mom, and he himself dying at different points. “Dad and Uncle Jen[sen Ackles] killed Mom,” he says, mimicking his children.

Even his last series, Walker, which ran for four seasons on the CW, featured Gen Padalecki as his late wife. Before the show premiered, the family had some friends over to watch the episode, which featured the death of her character Emily. When the show was over, no one could find their middle son Shep, who was about seven or eight at the time. He ended up being found in the backyard, crying under a tree.

“Mom, if dad’s the boss, why does he make you die and everything?” Padalecki says, repeating what his son had asked his mother after seeing Walker. As they continued to watch the show in the years down the line, Padalecki and his wife would snuggle up with their son and remind him it was fictional. But as soon as he’d see his mom unwell onscreen, he’d burst into tears.

“It breaks my heart,” adds Padalecki. “He’s such a little empath.”
