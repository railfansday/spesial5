FEMA is testing the emergency alert system: Dont panic

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

If you hear an emergency broadcast this afternoon, don't panic!

The Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) and the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) are conducting a scheduled test of the national emergency alert system at 2:20 p.m. ET on Tuesday, Nov. 17.

The FCC released a public notice about the scheduled test on Sept. 16, but it comes at a moment of high national anxiety, driven by recent tech outages — among other troubling news stories.

First, Microsoft suffered a widespread global outage on Thursday, Nov. 12, followed by a rare FAA nationwide ground stoppage the following day. And on Monday, a JPMorgan Chase outage sparked reports of a major bank outage (the problem was limited to Chase Bank and not a systemic issue or the result of a cyberattack), followed quickly by a United Airlines outage.

None of these outages were directly related to or caused by cyberattackers, but the timing has sparked conspiracy theories and generalized anxiety.

However, the emergency alert was scheduled long before these outages; the FCC also announced a backup date of Dec. 3, in the event today's test does not occur. So, if your TV or radio suddenly goes into emergency mode today, don't freak out.

FEMA will use the Integrated Public Alert and Warning System (IPAWS) to disseminate a message in both English and Spanish. The message will read, "This is a nationwide test of the Emergency Alert System, issued by the Federal Emergency Management Agency, covering the United States from 2:20 to 2:50 hours ET. This is only a test. No action is required by the public."

The test will appear on the following types of media:

Wired and wireless cable television systems

Notably, the emergency alert will not appear on your phone. So, if you do receive an "emergency alert" by text or phone this afternoon, ignore it; scammers may be trying to take advantage of the test.

FEMA tests emergency text alerts, or Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA), separately.

Outside of testing, WEA notifications are used for Imminent Threat alerts, Amber Alerts, Public Safety Messages (PSAs), and National Alerts, which are issued by the president or the FEMA administrator.
