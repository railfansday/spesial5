How This Year’s Black Friday Deals Explain the Tumultuous State of Streaming

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

There's a method to the discount madness, and the deals quietly signal where corporate priorities lie.

The streaming business is in flux, and Black Friday deals can help explain it.

It’s not hyperbole: Netflix stock has been cut in half from its all-time high. Bundles are proliferating. And Skydance CEO David Ellison has made it clear that a merger of HBO Max and Paramount+ is a question of when, not if.

Black Friday streaming deals are an annual tradition at this point, but given the state of the business, they can also shed light on how the entertainment giants behind them are thinking about the future of their products.

HBO Max and Paramount+ are offering very different types of deals, even as their parent company Skydance, is actively preparing for a discount bundle set for early next year, and with a full merger of the services following later on

HBO Max is offering an aggressive 50 percent discount on its ad-supported tier, charging $5.49 per month for a full year. Paramount+, meanwhile, is offering both its premium and standard plans for $2.99 per month, albeit only for the first two months.

With the bundle imminent and a merger coming (expect Paramount+’s tech stack to win out over HBO Max’s), the Black Friday deals are a last gasp before what is expected to be an aggressive discount campaign early next year.

The biggest new streamer to launch in the last year, Fox One, is offering a steep 75 percent discount, $5 for the first month, before it reverts back to $20 in month two.

Peacock, meanwhile, is offering generous discounts on both its select and premium plans, selling them for $2.99 and $8.99 per month for the first three months.

Disney, which has been leaning into bundles, is offering a year of Disney+ and Hulu for $5 per month, hoping to get viewers hooked on its two entertainment platforms (ESPN is the other) ahead of a full sunset of the Hulu standalone app (it is expected to continue as a standalone service within Disney+).

Apple TV, which is still trying to gain the scale that many of the other services already have, is offering an aggressive discount of over 50 percent, selling six months of the services for $6.99 per month, down from its typical price of $14.99 per month.

And niche streamers are being more aggressive: Look at Starz, which is offering an astonishing deal: $11.99 for 12 months, putting the price at under $1 per month when buying the annual plan (a more modest discount is available on monthly plans), underscoring how it needs to gain scale, and fast. Crunchyroll, Sony’s anime-focused streamer, is also offering discounts of over 50 percent for six months, ranging from $3.99 for its base tier to $8.99 for its ultimate tier.

And it’s a world where Netflix, the king of the crop, still feels as though it has no need to offer discounts of any sort. After all, when you have won the subscription streaming war, it’s better to keep those margins intact.
