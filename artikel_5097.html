ASEAN+3 announces new initiative to discuss digital asset regulations — Details

Given the rise in the acceptance of digital assets, the ASEAN+3 member countries have agreed to establish a dedicated working group to advance discussions on cross-border digital payments and regulatory approaches to digital assets.

The decision was announced in the Co-chairs’ Statement following the ASEAN+3 Finance and Central Bank Deputies’ Meeting held in Kyoto, Japan recently.

Here, the initiative aims to strengthen regional financial cooperation by improving payment connectivity and encouraging discussions on how crypto should be regulated across participating economies.

In fact, it also marks a step towards greater coordination among Asian financial authorities as digital assets and blockchain-based financial services continue to expand.

To make this successful, the proposed working group aims to operate under the newly established AMRO Cross-Border Digital Payments Program. Under the same, they will provide a dedicated platform for ASEAN+3 economies to discuss retail and wholesale payment connectivity, alongside regulatory approaches to digital assets.

This is particularly important because cross-border payments remain fragmented across different financial systems. Needless to say, differences in payment infrastructure, regulations, and compliance requirements make international transactions slower and more expensive.

However, with greater coordination, participating economies could explore inter-operable payment systems and more consistent regulatory approaches.

Interestingly, the development also comes amid broader efforts by governments and financial regulators to address the growing influence of cryptocurrencies, stablecoins, and tokenized assets.

One prominent example is the European Union’s Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) framework. It became fully applicable on 30th December 2024, following the introduction of stablecoin-specific provisions in June 2024.

For those unaware, MiCA establishes common rules for crypto-asset issuers and service providers, covering authorization, transparency, investor protection, and market conduct. Its implementation has provided a regulatory benchmark for other jurisdictions considering comprehensive crypto frameworks.

South Africa is another example of this shift. In August 2026, the South African Reserve Bank (SARB) and National Treasury proposed an activity-based reporting framework for cross-border crypto transactions.

The proposal focuses on identifying and monitoring transactions that could facilitate capital flight, illegal financial flows, or regulatory circumvention. Rather than classifying crypto assets as official currency, it seeks to bring their cross-border movement under closer financial safeguards.

On the other hand, the United States has faced setbacks in establishing similar regulatory clarity. The proposed Digital Asset Market CLARITY Act failed to advance in the Senate on 15th September, receiving 49 votes in favor and 50 against. it was short of the 60 votes required to proceed.

Its failure has left the U.S without the proposed comprehensive federal market structure framework, potentially putting it behind jurisdictions such as the E.U in providing regulatory certainty. However, the SEC and CFTC continue to pursue regulatory initiatives under their existing authority.

Against this backdrop, ASEAN+3’s latest decision is the latest in the world’s global regulatory discussion/efforts.
