The Lakers’ struggle to maintain big leads is becoming impossible to ignore

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Despite riding an eight-game winning streak and being tied with the San Antonio Spurs for the West's No. 1 seed, the 12-2 Los Angeles Lakers have continually squandered sizable leads. Rarely has it actually resulted in a loss — only once, against the Golden State Warriors on Oct. 30, when they blew a 38-15 first-quarter lead — but they're routinely allowing opponents back into games after building a comfortable advantage.

Most recently, they did so Sunday night in their 131-123 win over the Phoenix Suns. With 33.9 seconds remaining in the third quarter, the Lakers led 106-78. With 4:40 to go in the fourth, Phoenix had trimmed the deficit to six at 117-111. What should have been a final period allowing Luka Dončić to steal a bit more rest than normal became a crunch-time contest because Los Angeles couldn't manage its big lead.

"We're working too hard and we're playing too hard to build these leads. It's a disservice to each other to take your foot off. … You have to play for 48 minutes and we're going to need our defense to win us games," head coach JJ Redick said postgame. "These guys want to be a top-10 defense. We think we can be a good defensive team. I believe we can be a good defensive team. This has been consistent now. This is a trend. [We've] got to be better in the fourth quarter, but we did a lot of really good things to win the game."

Sunday was hardly the first instance this season in which the Lakers have spun an easy win into a pressurized late game scenario. Even ignoring towering first-quarter leads — which are often fool's gold in today's NBA with the breakneck pace and 3-point proliferation — like the ones they established against Golden State and the Detroit Pistons, they're still making a nasty habit of adding unnecessary stress to these wins.

They were up 16 with 2:28 left against the Toronto Raptors on Nov. 1 and brought in their end-of-bench players, who allowed Toronto to make it a five-point game with 43 seconds remaining, which prompted Redick to sub back in his starters. Five days later, a 13-point edge early with 4:21 to go against the Portland Trail Blazers was whittled down to five with 1:59 left.

According to NBA.com, the Lakers are second in first-quarter rating (plus-18.7), sixth in second-quarter net rating (plus-12.5), first in third-quarter net rating (plus-24.5) and 29th in fourth-quarter net rating (minus-24.4).

"In the fourth quarter, this whole season it's been kind of like a trend where it's just, we're kind of letting it get away from us. Maybe it's because we do have that lead and so we can get complacent, but it's something that we're obviously going to have to work on because we can't keep blowing double-digit leads in the fourth quarter," Jake LaRavia said Sunday. "We've had plenty of games where guys shouldn't be finishing the games and they're having to come back in and finish the games. We've got to do a better job collectively at finishing the game."

Dončić has been incredibly dominant this season. The Lakers have largely been tremendous as well. He's leading the charge but plenty of others are playing excellent basketball. They should be able to hold the fort for a few minutes when he exits with a big lead and give their superstar point guard a little more rest during these blowouts. Remedying that is critical — and they're fully aware.

"We know that's one of our weaknesses for that fourth quarter for sure when Luka goes out," Quentin Grimes said Sunday. "We got to just be better as a unit when he does go [off] the floor, still play our game but still play Laker basketball. Still be physical, still be tenacious on defense so we don't keep dwindling leads like that because it's a bad habit we've been doing."

Dončić knows it, too, and that concerning pattern was at front of mind during his postgame interview Sunday night, when he dropped 43 points and nine assists.

With their four-game road trip now in the rearview mirror, the Lakers return home to face the Charlotte Hornets Tuesday, when they'll hope to win their ninth consecutive contest and do so a little more easily than some of these recent victories.
