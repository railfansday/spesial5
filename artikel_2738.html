Grades: Lions offer hope despite Hutchinson injury

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

There was plenty to love about the Detroit Lions' dominant win over the New England Patriots. And while Aidan Hutchinson's injury may be the biggest headline from the Munich game, there was plenty from Allianz Arena that should keep Lions fans optimistic about the team moving forward.

Here is my Lions Week 10 report card and positional grades for Detroit's 35-10 victory.

Jared Goff threw two interceptions; both were his fault, and the second was an inexcusable decision that took points directly off the board. Because of those two plays alone, it feels like I can't give him a grade above C+ in good faith.

But the truth is the rest of his day was pretty dang good. I thought he navigated the pocket well, especially on his 50-yard pass to Jahmyr Gibbs. He didn't make a ton of downfield throws or display any jaw-dropping plays, but he was very efficient, finishing with an electric 9.8 yards per attempt and a 101.9 passer rating.

Jahmyr Gibbs was incredible all freakin' day. He finished with 252 scrimmage yards and two touchdowns. The Patriots couldn't handle his speed, his change-of-direction, or his strength. What was even better about his performance than normal is that the negative plays were almost completely non-existent. He had just one touch that went for negative yards, and only a couple more were stopped for zero yards.

Sione Vaki was very efficient, too, earning 28 yards on just six carries (4.7 YPC).

Overall, the Lions produced an impressive 55% success rate and +0.21 EPA/play on running plays vs. the Patriots. For comparison's sake, the Patriots were allowing just a 36.9 success rate going into this game and had produced a negative EPA/run.

I was a little disappointed with Sam LaPorta's play as a receiver. He didn't create much separation against the Patriots' defenders, and he had a touchdown grab knocked out of his hands. He finished with just a single catch for 16 yards on four targets.

However, the Lions used a ton of tight ends against the Patriots in the running game, and that played a significant role in Detroit controlling the line of scrimmage. LaPorta (26), Brock Wright (29), and Tyler Conklin (17) all logged over 15 run-blocking snaps on the day.

Amon-Ra St. Brown was seemingly unstoppable on Sunday. It was going to take the entire country of Germany to stop him on both of his long catch-and-run touchdowns, and they certainly weren't going to do that against their most beloved NFL player.

Jameson Williams' day shouldn't be overlooked, either. He caught four passes for 50 yards, including an impressive toe-tapping catch on the sidelines. Even Isaac TeSlaa got in on the fun with a highlight-reel touchdown grab.

This unit lived up to its full potential for the first time in several weeks.

Outside of some early pass protection issues, the Lions' offensive line was nails in Munich. They were getting significant push in the run game, and after settling down, they helped Jared Goff breathe comfortably in the pocket.

When the offensive line plays this well, the Lions' entire offense is as close to unstoppable as we've seen.

While the Lions' pressure was inconsistent at times without Aidan Hutchinson in the lineup, they still gave the Patriots hell for most of the game. In the run game, the Patriots were unable to do anything. Their running backs combined for 17 carries, 31 yards, and just 1.8 yards per carry.

The pass rush generated five total sacks, six quarterback hits, and over a 50 percent pressure rate:

It will be tough to maintain that without Hutchinson and against a better offensive line, but Derrick Moore, Alim McNeill, DJ Wonnum, and Skyler Gill-Howard have all played some pretty sound football, and all were disruptive on Sunday.

The linebackers deserve a lot of praise for Detroit's stout run defense, but they were a bit of a liability in the pass game again. It didn't end up costing the Lions too much in this game, because once Drake Maye went down, it looked like Tommy DeVito just didn't see the field very well. But coverage against tight ends and running backs continues to be a bit of an issue.

Still, this defense continues to play good football without its field general at MIKE linebacker, and that means something.

I thought the safety play was fantastic on Sunday. Brian Branch was all over the place in the run game and the pass game. Chuck Clark flipped a couple Patriots and made the receiving corps pay for every DeVito murderball.

I can't give them an A, though, because of a couple of stupid and costly pass interference flags. It wasn't a great game for Ennis Rakestraw—who also missed a key tackle—nor Nick Whiteside. But the good news is the top corners in this game—Rock Ya-Sin and Roger McCreary were very, very good. Or, as Dan Campbell said:

"I thought the back end played outstanding," Campbell said. "We played some sticky coverage today, bought us a little bit of time."

Nothing too notable from this group on Sunday. The return team didn't look particularly good, but coverage was good on both punt and kickoff. Jake Bates is still perfect when Jack Fox isn't blocking his kicks. He did put a kickoff out of bounds and a touchback in the end zone.

This was a tough week for both coaches. Flying across the globe, adjusting to a time zone six hours away, and dealing with all of the crazy logistics of getting ready for Sunday is not easy. It's clear one team was way more prepared than the other.

On top of that, Lions offensive coordinator Drew Petzing is really starting to come into his own, building plays off of previous plays, and seemingly having the right call in the right moment more often than not. As he hits his stride, the Lions offense can carry them a long way.

But don't sleep on the defensive coaching, either. Kelvin Sheppard has had to deal with two emotionally draining injuries to two of their best players. Yet through a heavy rotation at every level of the defense, things have never looked more steady and controlled. Say what you will, facing a beat-up Patriots offense with a backup quarterback leading most of Sunday afternoon, but Detroit used to get beat up by backups all the time. The defense finally feels like it's coming together.
