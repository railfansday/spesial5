Detroit Lions stock report: 18 risers, 8 fallers against the Buccaneers

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

On Sunday, the Detroit Lions continued their supremacy over their non-divisional rival, the Buccaneers, as Dan Campbell's teams improved to 4-1 against Todd Bowles' seafaring squad since 2023. Campbell's crew looked exceptionally well-prepared and executed at a high level against a physical team that was coming off a bye week, despite the Lions themselves forgoing a bye week immediately following their trip to Germany.

The Lions had plenty of solid individual performances on both sides of the ball, with a number of players starting to stack days, en route to a six-game winning streak. Naturally, with so many positive performances, we've got another stock-up-heavy edition of the stock report in Week 11.

It might not have been his most mind-blowing box score for MVP voters, but it might have been his best performance of the season, which is saying something. Goff endured the relentless, well-coached Tampa Bay Buccaneers defense and came out on top. Even accounting for the absence of Antoine Winfield Jr. and the in-game injury to cornerback Jacob Parrish, it was an impressive performance against one of the best defensive units in football. Goff registered his second-best passer rating of the season (126.9) and a season-high adjusted net yards per attempt (10.9), the ninth-best mark of his Lions career.

Aside from the 47-yard seam ball to Sam LaPorta on the opening drive, the Lions' offense started slowly, but Goff helped get things going and get the defense reeling. From the second quarter on, Goff was 17-of-21 passing (81.0%) for 205 passing yards (9.8 YPA) and two passing touchdowns. He helped open up the run game by chunking the Buccaneers downfield, picking on coverage defenders down the spine of the defense and putting them in a bind. On passes of 10-plus air yards, Goff went 7-of-11 (63.6%) for 196 passing yards (17.8 YPA) and two passing touchdowns, with his +1.099 EPA/dropback being the second-best mark against the Buccaneers' defense on such passes this season.

Goff also played notably well under pressure, going 7-of-11 passing (63.6%) for 121 passing yards (11.0 YPA) and two passing touchdowns, with his +0.492 EPA/dropback being the best mark of any quarterback under pressure against the Buccaneers' defense this season.

Goff was supremely comfortable navigating the pocket against the Buccaneers' fierce defensive front, avoiding mistakes, taking only one sack, and dispersing the ball to all of his playmakers. He even bolted for a big 10-yard rush on third-and-long scramble and executed a handful of successful quarterback sneaks, contributing to the Lions' improved short-yardage performance. The Lions are an overwhelmingly dangerous offensive attack when Goff is this dialed in.

Now half a season into being entrenched at left tackle, Sewell has been so dominant that it's reached the point where some of the loftiest draft projections and comparisons to Jonathan Ogden, Orlando Pace, and Trent Williams have become reality.

Sewell continually displays powerful fluidity as a run blocker, with exceptional balance and secondary reaction skills. Against the Buccaneers, he was single-blocking both defensive ends and defensive tackles, kicking out defenders and even erasing defenders on the backside to create cutback lanes. On the day, Jahmyr Gibbs recorded nine rushes for 63 rushing yards (7.0 yards per carry) when running to the left side through either the C-gap or D-gap (5- or 7-hole), running off Sewell's blocks.

In pass protection, Goff didn't have to worry about edge rushers winning around the outside on his blind side. Across 16 combined pass protection reps against Yaya Diaby, Calijah Kancey, and Al-Quadin Muhammad, Sewell allowed zero pressures. Sewell also moved Muhammad around with relative ease in the run game.

Sewell's well-rounded, elite attributes are worth highlighting at every opportunity because of how much he elevates the ceiling of the entire offense, and Week 11 was another unbelievable performance from him.

At no point after the Lions signed Chuck Clark, following his stints in New York and Pittsburgh, and especially after his first month of the season, did I expect to see the version of Clark that made him a dependable difference-maker for the Baltimore Ravens, albeit a step slower these days.

Even with Kerby Joseph settling back into the rotation for 46 snaps (66.7%), the Lions' newly named defensive captain and recent recipient of the green dot—the only non-linebacker in the NFL wearing one—played all 69 defensive snaps against the Buccaneers in a run-first matchup that meshed well with his skill set. Clark's backend versatility, high-level football intelligence and instincts, effective communication, and, most importantly, physicality when taking on blockers and sound, reliable tackling, have made him a dependable player amidst the Lions' recent defensive rejuvenation.

Against the Buccaneers, Clark was particularly effective firing downhill from the third level on early downs to fill the hole and stop Bucky Irving and Kenneth Gainwell in their tracks. He also recorded an emphatic unblocked sack up the middle to shut down a drive on third down and registered a disruptive pass breakup on a slant to rookie Ted Hurst.

Clark keeps checking boxes as he gets more comfortable in the Lions' defense and is now the only defensive back in the NFL this season with at least two interceptions, two forced fumbles, and two sacks.

Overall, the work-in-progress linebacker corps is still settling into its roles, but a number of Barnes' counterparts stepped up to make clutch plays against the Buccaneers' offense.

For much of the afternoon, Barnes resembled AOL Instant Messenger struggling to establish a connection. Barnes played 55 of the 69 defensive snaps, tied with Malcolm Rodriguez for the most among Lions linebackers, but he's clearly no longer in quite the same every-down role he's occupied at times. The team is still figuring out his role without his best friend Alex Anzalone on the Lions' sideline and with Jack Campbell dealing with his serious neck injury.

Barnes was either slow to process or out of position on some run plays, and when he did click and close rapidly, he struggled to wrap up ball carriers (three missed tackles). In coverage, he was beaten by tight end Cade Otton on a slant in one-on-one coverage for a third-down conversion that kept a drive alive, while also allowing catches to both Bucky Irving and Kenneth Gainwell.

He did, however, have a nice takedown of Mayfield on a scramble while operating as a QB spy to get the defense off the field on third down, as well as an athletic and instinctive chase-down of Gainwell for a minimal gain on an outside run. The hope is that he and Kelvin Sheppard can continue working to find a role in this defense in which Barnes is comfortable and can maximize his skill set.

Jameson Williams, WR: A big, playmaking day for the playmaker that NFL Network analyst Brian Baldinger aptly dubbed the Motown Comet. Williams hauled in a 57-yard touchdown bomb, totaling 93 receiving yards on four catches, including a well-rounded in-route over the middle early on and an outstanding toe-tap grab along the sideline on a deep comeback to start the second quarter. He also continued to selflessly work his butt off as a run blocker.

Jahmyr Gibbs, RB: His vision and toughness propelled him to the best performance by any running back against the Buccaneers' constricting defense this season, to the tune of 27 touches for 118 yards from scrimmage and two rushing touchdowns. Finding daylight on his clutch 26-yard fourth-quarter dash defied the laws of physics.

Sam LaPorta, Brock Wright, Tyler Conklin, TE: Arguably the most productive day of the season for the tight end room, as they combined for an explosive six catches for 122 receiving yards, while the offense relied on personnel groupings featuring extra tight ends on 69% of plays in Week 11. LaPorta's extra effort in driving his defender into the end zone on Gibbs' second rushing touchdown was worth a hefty attaboy and a pat on the back.

DJ Wonnum, DE: One of his best performances as a Lion, as he was a valuable member of the wrecking crew in run defense, registering three run stops while also generating three quarterback pressures.

Tyler Lacy, DL: He's a sledgehammer in the run game. I thought he was a big part of the team's first-half run defense efforts, while also generating a pressure on the first dropback of the game.

Tyleik Williams, NT: Like Lacy, Williams was instrumental in clogging up the Buccaneers' rushing attack early in the game.

K.J. Britt, LB: Got the start in his revenge game against his former team and showed on numerous plays why he's a human cinder block, including the colossal fourth-down run stuff in the red zone early in the game.

Trevor Nowaske, LB: One of the finest plays of the game was Nowaske swarming as the QB spy and drowning Baker Mayfield out at sea as he tried to extend a passing play in the fourth quarter. Nowaske continues to exemplify quiet competence at Sam linebacker.

Roger McCreary, CB: Up until the final 30 meaningless seconds of the game, McCreary had been targeted only once, and it was a pass intended for Ted Hurst that he broke up. He was largely lockdown while playing almost exclusively at outside cornerback (a season-high 52 snaps). He also closed well from the backside to stuff a Bucky Irving run.

Brian Branch, DB: The return of Kerby Joseph led to a number of three-safety looks, with Branch shifting more into a nickelback role. He played 41 snaps in the slot, more than he had all season combined entering the game and his most since the Divisional Round playoff game against the Commanders in 2024. Branch was still making plays all over the field, registering multiple run stops against Bucky Irving, shutting down a screen to Chris Godwin, and recording a pass breakup in man coverage against Emeka Egbuka.

Drew Petzing and Kelvin Sheppard, Coordinators: Both coordinators have consistently put together excellent game plans over the last month and a half, with their respective units continuing to find their identities and build momentum.

Detroit Lions Front Office, Pro Player Personnel: A lot of Twitter warriors and radio caller renegades will take unnecessary jabs at Brad Holmes for the one-year rentals and bargain-bin scrap signings, but the Lions won this game in large part because of the contributions of several cost-effective, well-scouted acquisitions in guys like Wonnum, Britt, McCreary, and Clark.

Fans booing Alex Anzalone: I almost feel nuts calling this out, but what are we doing here? This is a five-time captain and a Day 1 Dan Campbell guy who helped personify the resilient identity of teams that brought Lions fans more success than they'd seen in decades and, by all accounts, wanted to return to Detroit. This is the same guy who, just a few weeks ago, following a dominant two-interception performance, was more concerned with getting ahold of Derrick Barnes after the game to find out about Jack Campbell. If the consternation stems from a few questionable social media posts, get over it. He meant so much more to this fan base and city than that. I get that he's now on the other sideline, but he's not the enemy. Alex Anzalone will always be a Detroit Lion.

Isaac TeSlaa, WR: For a guy battling to carve out a role in this passing attack and build trust with Goff, he needs to come down with one or two of those targets.

Juice Scruggs, C: This was always going to be a daunting test for Scruggs, and while he anchored pretty well when matched up against Vita Vea in pass protection, the physicality of Vea, A'Shawn Robinson, and Josiah Trotter got to him in the run game, as he was pushed around a bit.

Alim McNeill, DT: It was McNeill's first game without Hutchinson and his first forgettable performance of the season. On a day when the Lions' pass rush was largely lacking, McNeill found himself matched up more frequently against the center as a pass rusher and was routinely stalemated by Graham Barton.

Skyler Gill-Howard, DT: The Lions need him to fight through that rookie wall and provide some juice as an interior pass rusher.

Malcolm Rodriguez, LB: He had some nice moments in coverage that will fly under the radar, but Rodriguez can't be committing multiple special teams penalties.

Christian Izien, S: Played only two defensive snaps in his revenge game and was beaten in coverage on one of them by Bucky Irving for a gain of 15-plus yards.
