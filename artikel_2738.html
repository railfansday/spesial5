5 questions with a Chargers expert: What to expect from D.J. Uiagalelei?

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

On paper, the New England Patriots and Los Angeles Chargers are quite familiar with one another: they last met in January, in the wild card round of the NFL playoffs. However, there have been quite a few changes since that game, especially at the most important position on the field.

Tommy DeVito will start at quarterback for New England in place of Drake Maye, while Justin Herbert's absence as Chargers QB will allow D.J. Uiagalelei to get his first career start. To learn more about him, and the rest of the Chargers in 2026, we spoke with Michael Peterson of Pats Pulpit's sister site Bolts From the Blue, the SB Nation community for all things Chargers.

1. With Justin Herbert missing this game due to a concussion, what can you tell us about backup D.J. Uiagalelei's strengths and weaknesses?

So D.J. Uiagalelei has made massive strides since coming out of college. The fact he has been able to stay with a NFL team this long is truly a testament to that because he was — to put it bluntly — a horrendous passer to end his collegiate career. With a full year and more behind Herbert, mainly as the third-stringer, he's progressed enough to be a serviceable player for an emergency situation like this.

As a passer, he is fairly composed in the pocket and has enough athleticism to escape and extend plays. He also plays with some notable confidence and will not think twice about attempting a tough throw. However, he's not reckless with the football. He knows when to pull it down and get yards on the ground when possible. In his first NFL action in garbage time against the Texans, he helped bring the offense down the field and even threw his first professional touchdown on a great rip over the middle.

His biggest weakness will be that he's still a wildcard and an unknown in this league. He could be serviceable like I mentioned, but he could also flail in the moment like most young passers in their first career starts.

I think Mike Vrabel would be wise to turn up the heat early and often and hope the second-year UDFA crumbles under the pressure.

2. Why has it taken so long for Omarion Hampton to start breaking out, and what challenges does he present for a defense?

Hampton unfortunately did not help himself this year by beginning the campaign with back-to-back games where he lost a fumble. It also did not help that he lost those fumbles in pivotal moments that directly led to the Chargers losing said games. It unfortunately took a bit for him to get back into the god graces of Mike McDaniel and now he seems to be off and running with a renewed energy. He's been phenomenal and looks every bit the player the Chargers wanted to take in the first round.

As a runner, Hampton's skillset is not designed to make someone miss by being shifty. He's going to make them miss by running full speed through the weak point of your tackle attempt to consistently gain extra yards and fall forward at the end of runs. He's the type of runner that will get the most out of the minor creases given to him and when the defense is worn down by the fourth quarter, he'll look to keep pounding it even more. Patriots should come prepared to try and get him stopped before his momentum builds, not unlike how the Chargers kept Derrick Henry in check during their lone win of the season.

3. LT Joe Alt is a Pro Bowler, but the rest of the Chargers' offensive line consists of rookies and backups. Where are the non-Alt bright spots up front, and who do you expect the Patriots to try to exploit?

One guy who went from roster afterthought to solid starter the past few weeks has been rookie left guard Logan Taylor. He was a sixth-round pick out of Boston College this year and mainly was used at tackle during the preseason despite guard be the obvious place for him in the NFL. Once he settled in after being forced into the starting lineup, he's been one of the lone bright spots outside of Alt. Another recent bright spot has also been the return of Kayode Awosika who, after being the original starter at left guard before his fibula injury, is now starting at right guard over Cole Strange (who should have been benched a long time ago).

Both of the guard spots being reinforced has led to back-to-back strong rushing games for Hampton, as well as two consecutive intereption-less games by Herbert.

As far as which player up front the Patriots should exploit, it has to be rookie right tackle Travis Burke. As good as Burke has been in the run game, he is quite a bit behind as far as pass protection fundamentals. He is athletic enough to stay outside, but he lacks proper set points and punch mechanics. He gets beat by twitchier rushers and overall speed that can threaten his corner more than he likes. He is coming off a horrible game against David Bailey and the Jets and the Patriots should do their best to exploit that side of the line this week.

4. What has contributed to the Chargers' struggles stopping opposing passing games? Are there any defenders the Patriots should try to avoid?

Against the Jets, the absence of cornerback Tarheeb Still was felt, especially when injuries have continued to rear their head in a position group that is terribly shallow. Undrafted rookie Rodney Shelley has played a lot this season and his production has been abysmal. Even veteran Donte Jackson, who has battled multiple injuries this year, is still very feast-or-famine.

The absence of Derwin James has also been apparent. He's a back-to-back second-team All-Pro slot defender and anyone who knows ball understands how good an elite slot player can be for a defense. The Chargers have been getting by — for the most part — with a group of guys no one has probably heard of at the second and third levels. The Jets game was a bit of a fluke, but they'll have to right the ship fast to avoid making that result less of "who they are" and more of that fluke I just mentioned.

5. It seems there is some disconnect between Jim Harbaugh, Mike McDaniel, and Justin Herbert. What is happening with the Chargers' current leadership structure, and who do you see remaining in L.A. when the dust settles?

What some may not understand about Harbaugh's role as the head coach is that he is a true CEO. He does not have a hand in helping scheme either side of the ball. He makes his hires and then he allows how coaches to coach. That's a major reason why no coordinator has been fired or changed during the season under his leadership and it's the main reason why McDaniel was never going to be fired. There's no one to pick up the slack and Harbaugh sure wasn't going to step in and start calling plays for the first time in decades.

When it's all said and done, Harbaugh will most likely remain, even if he truly should not. The team will owe him a ton of money over the next two years and the Spanos family will not want to fork out money to a coach that's no longer working for him. Harbaugh's two 11-win seasons prior to this year still gives him some grace, but with how bad this year is, there will be no more grace for him should this disaster continue into the 2027 season.
