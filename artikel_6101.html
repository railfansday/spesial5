The war means Mantis’ Gulf properties are serving only a domestic market for now. Its long-term bet on the region, as it builds out its "circuit" tourism offer, depends on international travelers coming back.

Mantis Collection, half owned by Accor since 2018, is pressing ahead with its Gulf expansion plan despite the U.S.-Iran conflict, which has reshaped demand across its  young portfolio.

Its Bahrain resort shut completely at times during the war’s peak, then recovered quickly, while a new Saudi mountain resort is trading in line with expectations on domestic demand. Two upcoming openings remain on track despite procurement setbacks.

“It would be naive to say the U.S.-Iran war hasn’t had an impact,” said Craig Erasmus, CEO of Mantis Collection. “Although the trends are positive, you need stability to return for a more diverse guest mix [to come back]. The region is very focused on the domestic market now.”

Mantis Collection, the experiential resort brand 50% owned by Accor since 2018, is sticking to its Gulf expansion plan even as the U.S.-Iran conflict narrows its guest mix. CEO Craig Erasmus said the brand's 104-villa Hawar Resort in Bahrain shut completely at the peak of the conflict and now draws mainly Bahraini guests plus some Saudi business, with regional visitors yet to return. Mantis has chosen not to discount to fill rooms, arguing that a new brand shouldn't chase volume on price. Its new Gamra Mountain Resort in Saudi Arabia's Al Baha is trading at around 30% occupancy three months in, all of it domestic, and the conflict has made it harder to build the winter low season. The war disrupted procurement of furniture, fixtures, and equipment, but Erasmus said construction timelines hold: Saij Mountain Lodge in Ras Al Khaimah is set for March 2027 and the 220-key Basiqat near Riyadh for mid-2027. The strategy copies what Mantis built across 17 African properties — multi-property circuits plus an in-house tour operator — with three more committed Saudi properties, a Saudi tour operator launch next year, and a new UAE emirate project to be announced. Mantis is avoiding Dubai and Abu Dhabi entirely.
