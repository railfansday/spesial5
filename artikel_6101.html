Trump banks on personal relationships moving Putin, Xi toward U.S. interests

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Russian President Vladimir Putin, Chinese President Xi Jinping and U.S. President Donald Trump arrive for a trilateral meeting in Shenzhen, China, on Thursday. (Win McNamee/AFP via Getty Images)

That was the greeting, complete with a lengthy handshake, President Donald Trump gave Vladimir Putin before two high-level meetings Thursday, including the first “trilateral” session with sitting U.S., Russian and Chinese heads of state.

“It’s nice to see you,” Trump told Putin before a private one-on-one meeting in China’s Shenzhen economic zone, before pulling the Russian strongman close and saying something into his ear too low for journalists’ microphones to pick up.

Trump and Putin then posed for the cameras with wide grins, again underscoring the U.S. president’s closeness with a world leader who has been partially shunned by the West since he launched a war with Ukraine in February 2022. Trump on Thursday also appeared several times alongside Chinese President Xi Jinping, who like Putin has been scorned by Republican and Democratic lawmakers for years.

“We’ve had a great relationship for many years. We've known each other for a long time. And I think a lot of wonderful things can come out of this — or, at least, start,” Trump told reporters as Putin beamed from a few feet away.

They later were joined by Xi for a group photo before sitting down at a round wooden table with a large arrangement of pink flowers in the center.

His interactions with and comments about Putin and Xi reflect Trump’s belief that his personal relationships can bend them toward U.S. interests. But that gambit clashed Thursday with his second term’s expiration date — the White House did not disclose any agreements reached during his various meetings with the two leaders, nor did Trump hold a press conference following the three-way session.

Both Putin and Xi are adept at playing the geopolitical long game. The 26 months left in Trump’s term is a relatively short amount of time compared to the duo that has been in power for a combined 56 years and will remain in place for the foreseeable future.

Indeed, even Trump acknowledged the first-ever meeting might not immediately yield much progress on its ostensible goal: paring each powers’ nuclear weapons arsenals.

“I must say, it's, quite frankly, a very hard thing to do, because once you have the nuclear weapons, it's very hard to not have them. It's very hard to give them up as the leader of a country,” Trump said as Putin and Xi looked on. “Something that's very difficult to say, because … it's the way life works.

“You know, you have thousands of weapons and you're supposed to give them up, and yet you have countries in addition to us, in much smaller numbers, but they have nuclear weapons, also,” he added. “So it's not an easy thing to do and give them up.”

Trump, who was convicted by a New York jury in a business records case and who saw a federal election interference case against him dismissed only because of his reelection, also appeared to join Putin’s resistance to international criminal justice entities that have alleged Putin has committed war crimes in Ukraine.

“In another area of potential cooperation between us, I'll be suggesting ways to protect our nations’ sovereignty from rogue globalist organizations such as the International Criminal Court, which has gotten out of control, which I believe we all agree has really strayed far from beyond its jurisdiction,” Trump said, a statement that would be almost unthinkable to hear from any other sitting U.S. president.

Other world leaders, including democratically elected ones, at two Pacific region economic summits in which Trump participated this week did not receive the kind of cordial and personal treatment from the U.S. side that Putin and Xi enjoyed.

And other world leaders and congressional Democrats this week expressed concern with Trump's closeness to the Russian and Chinese strongmen.

Senate Minority Leader Charles E. Schumer, D-N.Y., on Thursday called the unprecedented summit a “historic embarrassment.” He criticized Trump for leaving the meeting without any apparent concrete commitments from Moscow or Beijing on a number of issues, including curbing Chinese espionage inside U.S. companies and ending the Ukraine conflict.

“This summit was nothing but a glorified photo op and a gift to Putin and Xi,” Schumer said.

Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk wrote this Thursday on X, shortly after Trump’s warm greeting for the former KGB officer: “God, protect us from such friends.”

Nicolas Burns, a former senior U.S. diplomatic official under GOP and Democratic presidents, was critical of Trump’s meetings with Xi. He said it was “important” for U.S. and Chinese leaders to hold talks, but added: “We need stability in the relationship with China — but there’s a but.”

“The president seems to be following a detente policy with China, with very little substance. There’s a lot of pomp and circumstance, red carpets, a lot of nice words,” the former ambassador to China, NATO and Greece told “Morning Joe” on MS NOW. “Too friendly with Xi Jinping, who’s not going to be swayed by rosy words from President Trump.”

Even before Trump met with the Russian and Chinese leaders behind closed doors, Republican and Democratic lawmakers said they doubted very much would come from it.

“I want nuclear weapons to be reduced. As you know, China has been unwilling to even have a conversation with the United States about nuclear arms reduction until they reach a certain plateau in the number of weapons that they have,” House Armed Services Committee Vice Chair Rob Wittman, R-Va., told reporters Tuesday.

“I'm glad that they're willing to have the conversation. We do need a limit, as you know,” added Wittman, who lost his reelection bid earlier this month. “Right now, neither Russia nor China has a nuclear arms reduction treaty with the United States. It would be nice for us to actually have one.”

Sen. Richard Blumenthal, D-Conn., an Armed Services Committee member, wanted Trump to press Putin over Ukraine.

“Ukraine is defending itself from Putin's aggression, which this president should be opposing as well,” Blumenthal said Tuesday. “We are spending no money on Ukraine. Let's be very clear: Arms to Ukraine are purchased by Ukraine with financial support from Europe, not America. … Let's support Ukraine.”

On spending, Trump and administration officials have been pressing Senate Republicans to quickly pass another budget reconciliation measure with $350 billion more for the Defense Department. However, Senate Majority Leader John Thune, R-S.D., and other GOP senators have signaled in the last 24 hours that they had shelved the notion of another fast-track spending measure.

“You have to have a path forward. You've got to get on it, and you have to protect it, and that takes 50 in the Senate,” Thune told reporters Thursday. “And then you've got to get it through the House. As you probably saw yesterday, a lot of folks in the House were lobbing grenades at the Senate reconciliation bill and complaints about it not being offset, et cetera. So, you know, in my view at least, you want to have a path forward to success.”

John M. Donnelly and Jackie Wang contributed to this report.
