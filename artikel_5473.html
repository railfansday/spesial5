Fed Officials Expect Further Interest Rate Hikes After Pausing Last Month

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Leaders at the Federal Reserve held firm on interest rates in October, but also felt a future rate hike "would likely be appropriate" as some mulled a larger hike in December, according to meeting minutes released Wednesday.

At the Oct. 27-28 meeting of the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC), policymakers voted 10-2 to keep the overnight rate at its previous 3.75%-4% range. The decision came days before the 2026 midterms, even as FOMC members agreed inflation was a persistent concern,

Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh joined the majority, while Fed Gov. Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack, both voted in favor of a quarter-point increase. One month before, the FOMC voted to raise rates for the first time in three years in an effort to clamp down on inflation.

The minutes—which don't name FOMC members specifically while discussing their deliberations—show that some of the FOMC members considered that, while the impact of tariffs on the economy was waning, high energy costs continue to pressure Americans.

Indeed, some participants even noted that, if the inflation numbers got too unfavorable, they would consider a rate hike of more than a quarter-percent in December. Bond markets still view that as unlikely, with the probability of a larger half-point hike next month at just 5%, according to CME FedWatch.

Still, Federal Reserve Gov. Christopher Waller told CNBC Wednesday it may be necessary to "shock people" with a larger rate hike. He said in a speech this month that a half-percent point hike might be necessary next month.

"Inflation's been above our target for over five years, so if you really want to get it down you have to start taking more aggressive actions," Waller told CNBC.

FOMC members also said the rapid artificial intelligence buildout was contributing to inflation. But more scrutiny of the industry could slow spending and reduce inflation pressures.

Those members later determined holding rates steady at the October meeting would allow for time to "assess the durability of AI-related investment demand, the evolution of financing conditions, the transmission of the September rate increase to the economy, or geopolitical developments."

"A couple of participants noted that, at the current juncture, a gradual approach to policy firming wouldgive businesses and households time to adjust to higher interest rates," the minutes state.

Inflation remained hot in October. Overall prices rose 0.4% from September, according to the Consumer Price Index (CPI) data released by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) last week.

Some numbers look better. After a disappointing September, U.S. payrolls came roaring back in October as employers added 165,000 new jobs, pushing the unemployment rate down to 4.1%.

CME FedWatch currently prices a roughly 95% chance of the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) raising the federal funds to a 4%-4.25% range in December, followed by a 69% probability of 4.25%-4.5% in January.

The meeting minutes show that Fed leaders are aware that public sentiment broadly expects the December rate hike. "Some participants," the minutes state, judge those estimates to be "broadly consistent with their assessments of the appropriate policy rate path."

Several of the meeting members also said it was a "close call." In the meantime, Cook and Hammack said that inflation remained too high and they didn't see enough signs that it was letting up. They worried that delaying the rate hike any further could risk higher inflation becoming entrenched. That, in turn, could mean more drastic rate hikes in the future.

Waller isn't the only Fed leader saying that the market needs more than one quarter-point rate hike.

"The economy will likely require several interest rate increases before the spring of 2027 to achieve 2% inflation in a timely manner," St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem said this week at a conference in Argentina.

Realtor.com Chief Economist Danielle Hale said the market ultimately anticipates two or three hikes over the next 6 to 8 months. The Market has reacted. Pending home sales in September dropped to their lowest level on record.

"While the market and the Fed both seem to agree that some monetary policy restriction is warranted, most investors currently expect more restriction than the Fed has projected," Hale said last month.

But there is still an opportunity for homebuyers in this environment. Hale recommended homebuyers set themselves up for a better mortgage rate by boosting credit, making a higher down payment, and shopping around for a mortgage.
