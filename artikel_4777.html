Can you remember the last time you hugged someone?

Most people hug quite a lot over their lifetime (Ocklenburg, 2024). Many hugs are mundane, like a quick one when greeting or goodbye. But occasionally, we may experience a hug that is emotionally more significant than a quick hi. The first time someone hugs the love of their life, or the last time someone hugs their mum before she dies, may be hugs some people remember for the rest of their lives. It is, however, completely unclear in psychological research what makes a hug memorable compared to other hugs.

A study published in the scientific journal PLoS ONE investigated which factors shape a hug's memory (Dueren and co-workers, 2026). In the study, “Why we hug: Individual differences predict emotional touch memories,” the research team, led by scientist Anna Lena Dueren from the University of Bristol in the UK, analyzed data from a large-scale hugging survey with more than 15,000 participants. For the study, the scientists recruited male and female volunteers through BBC public broadcasting programs and other media.

The volunteers, ages 18 to 92, answered an online questionnaire about hugging and touch in general, as well as about several personality and other psychological factors. The volunteers were asked whether they remembered a specific hug for its positive effects on them. In addition, the scientists used advanced statistical models to find out how individual differences and experiences shape how people remember hugs. The analyses showed that people who disliked hugs, had difficulty hugging, or avoided attachment were less likely to remember a hug for its positive emotional impact. Volunteers who frequently hugged as a greeting and those who were very empathetic were more likely to remember a hug for its positive emotional impact; so were people with high attachment anxiety and high scores in the body perception questionnaire. The other factors showed no effects.

Taken together, the research shows that nearly seven in 10 people recall a specific hug because of how it made them feel. However, whether someone remembers a special hug depends on several individual factors, such as the empathy level and attachment style. These findings highlight how emotionally significant hugs can be, as we may carry the memory of some special hugs for the rest of our lives!
