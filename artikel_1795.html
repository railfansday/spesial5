Chelsea’s Problems Run Deeper Than Joao Pedro’s Injury

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Chelsea’s reliance on Joao Pedro has become impossible to ignore. The Brazilian’s absence has coincided with a sharp decline in attacking output, and there is little doubt that Xabi Alonso’s side function better when they have a true focal point leading the line.

Yet reducing Chelsea’s recent struggles to the loss of one player risks oversimplifying a far more complicated situation. The deeper concern is that ten league games into the season, it remains difficult to identify exactly what kind of team Alonso wants Chelsea to become.

Joao Pedro’s value extends far beyond goals. He provides a reference point for the attack, gives Chelsea an outlet when they play directly and creates space for players such as Cole Palmer to operate between the lines.

Without him, Chelsea have looked considerably less threatening. Attempts to compensate have felt improvised rather than planned. Morgan Rogers and Pedro Neto have both been used through the middle despite neither being a natural centre-forward, while more conventional options have remained largely on the fringes.

That has left the attack looking disconnected. Chelsea’s early-season directness worked because Joao Pedro could receive long passes, hold off defenders and bring others into play. Once that element disappeared, much of the team’s attacking structure disappeared with it.

The uncertainty surrounding the striker position reflects a broader issue.

At various points this season Chelsea have looked like a transition-heavy side willing to attack quickly and accept defensive risks. At other times, Alonso has appeared to move towards a more controlled and possession-based approach.

Neither vision is inherently wrong. What is striking is how often Chelsea seem to move between them.

Formation changes have become frequent, midfield combinations have varied considerably and even the general tempo of the team’s play has fluctuated from week to week. Injuries undoubtedly explain part of that instability, but they do not explain all of it.

The result is a team that has shown promising flashes without yet establishing a clear identity. Supporters can recognise individual ideas, but the overall picture remains difficult to define.

Chelsea’s injury list has been substantial and deserves acknowledgement. Losing key players across the pitch would challenge any squad, particularly when several defensive starters are unavailable simultaneously.

Even so, the underlying numbers suggest there are concerns beyond availability.

The goal tally looks respectable on the surface, but Chelsea’s chance creation numbers are less impressive – something Alonso himself admitted after the defeat to Sunderland, and they continue to concede high-quality opportunities too frequently. This appears to be a team whose performances have not always matched the raw results. Sometimes one way, sometimes the other.

There is also a sense that Chelsea remain caught between phases. Alonso has introduced greater calm around the club and reduced some of the volatility that characterised previous seasons, but stability off the pitch does not automatically produce clarity on it.

Chelsea will almost certainly improve when Joao Pedro returns. His qualities are too important not to make a difference.

The bigger question is whether his return will solve problems or merely disguise them.

At the moment, Chelsea’s season feels less like a story about one injured striker and more like a story about a talented team searching for its identity. Alonso has had encouraging moments and difficult moments in equal measure, and the international break offers an opportunity to establish a clearer direction.

Until that happens, Chelsea will continue to look like a side capable of exciting football one week and frustrating football the next.
