A grand new start of it: Exploring Mayor Zohran Mamdani’s effect on business—in New York City and beyond

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

A democratic socialist running the financial capital of the universe? For a magazine obsessed with how business can change—and change the world—it’s hard to imagine a more intriguing experiment.

As Zohran Mamdani wraps up his first year as New York City’s mayor, we decided to explore a possibility that got lost in the reductive shouting that surrounded his campaign: What if his democratic socialism-fueled agenda is actually good for business? And what will happen as other cities and states elect leaders with similar ideas?

Making New York more affordable is a goal that most of us can get behind. So is making city government faster, more responsive, and more innovative. The question—a thorny one—is whether Mamdani can deliver a more equitable city without throwing a wet blanket over the investment and entrepreneurship that help it thrive.

That tension animates this issue’s cover story, by Morgan Clendaniel, our executive editor, digital. Clendaniel, who has long covered the intersection of business and progressive politics, spoke to Tony Shorris, president and CEO of the NYC Economic Development Corporation, and Julie Su, deputy mayor for economic justice, about the sweeping role that they believe government can play in the economy—speeding up procurement, cutting red tape, making city services function.

The revolution may include city-owned grocery stores. It will definitely include high-tech public bathrooms.

Capitalism + democratic socialism

There’s something invigorating about that combination of grand ambition and attention to the daily details. At Fast Company, we spend a lot of time talking to visionaries who see a broken system and decide to build something better. It’s exciting to see that startup energy coursing through City Hall.

Clendaniel interviewed chief executives, too, such as Warby Parker’s Neil Blumenthal, and he spoke with key opposition figures, including Steven Fulop, president and CEO of the pro-business Partnership for New York City, and the venture capitalist Bradley Tusk. (Bill Ackman, the hedge fund manager, had his own words for the mayor at our Innovation Festival in September.)

This unfettered-capitalism crowd worries that the S-word alone is enough to discourage investment in New York and prompt companies to relocate or move jobs elsewhere. There’s some fearmongering here, no doubt, but also some merit. Business hates uncertainty.

These are complex questions worth exploring with an open mind and a willingness to scrutinize the trade-offs. Clendaniel does just that, with a big assist from his editor, Jill Bernstein.

A visual caffeine jolt

Meanwhile, our creative director, Mike Schnaidt, came up with the idea for the coffee cup on the cover, brought to life by illustrator Justin Metz. Along with Clendaniel and Bernstein’s journalism, this graphic effort reminds me why I love making this magazine. Even in the fully digitized, AI-ified year of 2026, a great magazine cover can turn a complicated argument into something immediate and surprising.

Here, an everyday New York object carries an extraordinary question about the future of the city—and the ever-evolving relationship between the private sector and the public good.
