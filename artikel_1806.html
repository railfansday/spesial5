GdS: Possession vs. directness – key tactical differences between Milan and Frosinone

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Ruben Amorim will be up against Massimiliano Alvini tomorrow evening, and as the stats show, they are two very different managers.

As highlighted by Gazzetta dello Sport, there is one interesting stat to highlight to introduce the game. In terms of shots per game, it will be the second-ranked team and against the 17th-ranked team. That in itself won’t raise any eyebrows, but the fact that Milan are the ones in 17th place will.

The Rossoneri have mustered a mere 126 shots so far, while Frosinone are quite far ahead at 221. On the flip side, the newly-promoted side have only scored one more goal than Milan (24 vs. 23), which is what makes this game so interesting.

Amorim has made it clear from the start that he wants to dominate each game. That shows in the possession, as Milan are third in Serie A with an average of 59% so far, and they also rank high in terms of passes per game.

The shots are where they fall short, even if they average over two goals per game. There are several reasons for this, but most of it can be attributed to Amorim’s recent arrival and the fact that he has experimented rather extensively.

During today’s presser, he even said that they haven’t done anywhere near enough to enter the penalty box more often, which tends to mean more shots.

In the build-up, for example, the Rossoneri move the ball forward at an average speed of 1.30 meters per second, which is the slowest in Serie A. Alvini’s Frosinone, on the other hand, are great at vertical football: the ball moves toward the opponent’s penalty box at 2.51 meters per second, the best rate in the league.

The Ciociari are masters of rapid counterattacks, so much so that on only 40 occasions have they built plays with ten or more passes. Milan, meanwhile, have 203.

Everything starts in the defence phase for Frosinone. Indeed, they always attempt to win the ball back inside the opponent’s half, which is proven by the index that measures the intensity of pressing in the attacking zone (they are first in Serie A)

Milan? They are eighth, despite being – along with Como – the team to have scored the most goals (4) from a ball recovered while pressing.

The difference between the two is well explained by the so-called “pressed sequences”, that is, the number of actions in open play in which a team manages to interrupt the opponent’s build-up within three passes or less, before the ball goes beyond the halfway line.

On that front, Frosinone are also first (161), while Milan are only 15th (102). Having said all of this, as the pink newspaper concludes, we should expect a spectacular match tomorrow. Both teams will stick to their respective game, which bodes well.
