Drones, data and Bruno - Pochettino on his early Man Utd challenges

Mauricio Pochettino has questioned what could have been gained from the drone footage of a Manchester United training session ahead of his first game in charge against Liverpool.

A 12-minute video taken over United's Carrington training ground showed the Argentine overseeing various tactical drills with his new team as they prepared for a trip to Anfield on Sunday, live on Sky Sports.

The operator has since apologised and taken down the video, but footage has been viewed over eight million times on reposts from other X accounts and has since been reuploaded by several other YouTube channels, collectively attracting hundreds of thousands of views.

Sky Sports News understands the club do not plan on taking legal action, but will not rule out doing so in the future if further incidents occur, and Pochettino questioned the motivation behind the filming.

"What are you going to find?" Pochettino said in an exclusive interview with Sky Sports. "We have young guys training with us. It’s not nice. It’s not good.

"What do they want to show? Yes, we were working on shape. We were very organised. What is the point of doing something that can be very dangerous?

"I don't want to be upset, but I think it's a process the club is caring a lot about. For sure, something will happen."

Liverpool head coach Andoni Iraola admitted that he was sent the footage after it was released, but said that the act should've been illegal.

"They sent me it yesterday, the footage. But the important part of this is it shouldn't be legal," Iraola said in his pre-match press conference.

"Yesterday you had access to our training, the cameras recorded 15-20 minutes of our training. You know who trained with us, you know who didn't train but it's not a matter of the advantage. It's the fact someone can do this.

"There is a reason we train behind closed doors because there are some things you have to respect. I don't think it's nice for any club."

Away from the training footage, Pochettino was quizzed on how much influence he wants to have over the club as he looks to become the man to finally restore glory at Old Trafford.

The Argentine has officially been appointed as head coach, the same as predecessors Ruben Amorim and Michael Carrick. That was a point of contention from the former during the final press conference before his sacking, as he defiantly labelled himself the manager.

However, the term doesn't bother Pochettino as he still hopes to be involved in all the key decisions as time progresses.

"I know that I'm the head coach. I am the guy who is going to decide the squad for Sunday, and I'm going to pick the starting XI," he said.

"I'm going to make the changes, and of course, in the future I would like to be involved in all the main decisions that will happen in the squad.

"Sometimes I worked much better when I was head coach or when I was coach than manager.

"It's not necessarily the case that if you are manager, you make all the decisions. Maybe you are a coach and you have more power in the decisions. It depends on the structure, depends on the communication, the relationship, with all the areas there."

Another point of contention at United remains the future of club captain Bruno Fernandes.

The PFA Player of the Year has struggled with his form this season and his contract at Old Trafford is due to run out at the end of the season.

The club do hold an optional one-year extension, and CEO Omar Berrada has confirmed that the club remain in discussions over a new deal for the Portuguese international.

Pochettino confirmed his intentions to keep Fernandes as captain and stressed his importance.

"Bruno is our captain, and I think he's a very important player, but all the players need to feel that they are important, from the top to the bottom or from the bottom to the top," he said.

"But Bruno is our captain and he is a very important player. I think we will have time afterwards to talk about the things that you ask me."

In terms of what is on his immediate agenda, Pochettino is focusing on improving the data metrics across the club.

"When we arrived, I think we found a group of players and we proposed the football that we feel [is the best]. That's being brave, being Man Utd. I think it is about proposing that idea, and they revise it; it is to be brave and try to bring that to the field.

"I need to be honest, sometimes data shows something that you can then see on the field. We are going to be very focused and try to improve our data, our numbers, in every single area of the field.

"We are here not to denounce things. We are here to build something with our players and with the club, and try to perform and win games."

United's running and intensity off the ball has been a major criticism of them throughout the season, with former boss Carrick flippantly saying of his players after their 4-1 defeat to Como in October "whatever tactics you play, if you don't run back, you ain't going to win."

However, Pochettino does believe that context has to be taken into account when it comes to the underlying data about his United side.

"I see that is a debate that I don't like to go in because I think we cannot talk about things without context, and data is about context," he added.

"If you don't put context on the data, I think it's very dangerous to talk, and I think that it's easy to manipulate the data."
