Strategy Slows Its Bitcoin Buying as MSTR Stock Slides: Why Has Saylor Eased Off?

Strategy said on Monday, November 16, that it bought 485 Bitcoin last week for $33.4 million, less than half of the 1,036 BTC it bought the week before. MSTR stock fell 6% at Monday’s open to about $105 as Michael Saylor’s company, which now holds 850,849 BTC, slowed its buying.

The same filing shows where most of the money went. Strategy spent $228.1 million buying back its own preferred stock, STRC, nearly seven times what it spent on Bitcoin.

We added up Strategy’s weekly filings since mid-October. Over five weeks, it spent about $1 billion on preferred buybacks and about $200 million on Bitcoin. MSTR stock, Strategy’s common shares, fell 6% at Monday’s open to about $105. Bitcoin itself trades near $65,000, down 21% since early October.

So why are Strategy’s Bitcoin buys slowing, and what is MSTR stock doing?

Strategy reports its purchases every Monday in a filing with the SEC called an 8-K. Its latest filing covers November 9 to 15. We lined it up with the four before it.

STRC, nicknamed “Stretch,” is a preferred share that pays a monthly dividend and is meant to trade near $100. It fell to about $85 in July and is back at $99.60, close to that target. Buying it back cuts Strategy’s dividend bill, which runs at about $1.43 billion a year across all its preferred shares.

The second reason is the price of MSTR stock itself. Strategy pays for most purchases by selling new MSTR shares. That works best when the stock trades well above the value of the Bitcoin behind each share.

Strategy’s own dashboard puts that ratio, called mNAV, at 1.11. At that level, selling shares adds little Bitcoin per share, so each new share does less for existing holders. Last week, Strategy sold $244.7 million of MSTR, but only $33.4 million of it went into Bitcoin.

There is also a limit on the buybacks. Strategy has $332.2 million left under its preferred repurchase program, less than two weeks at the current pace. Once it runs out, Strategy has to choose between raising the limit and moving that money back to Bitcoin.

On the daily chart of Hyperliquid’s 24/7 MSTR contract, the stock trades at $104.75. It sits below its 50-day exponential moving average (EMA), an average that gives recent prices more weight, at $131.98, and its 200-day EMA at $133.37.

Strategy has not stopped buying Bitcoin, but it has changed its priorities. For MSTR stock, next Monday’s filing is the one to watch. A bigger Bitcoin buy would show the cash is moving back to Bitcoin. Another week dominated by STRC would point the other way.
