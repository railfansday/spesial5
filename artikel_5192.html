Nvidia (NVDA) could be poised for a dramatic rally if Jensen Huang returns as CEO and several major catalysts follow. In this hypothetical scenario, Elon Musk’s Grok AI predicts that investigations close, U.S. officials clear Huang of national-security concerns, and Nvidia secures multi-year sovereign AI deals with allied governments.

If those contracts make Nvidia a preferred supplier for national AI computing through the decade, political clearance and long-term demand could drive a sharp re-rating. By the end of 2026, investors might focus less on quarterly guidance and more on years of potential government-backed demand.

Following Huang’s reinstatement as CEO of Nvidia, the NVDA share price has surged more than 30% since Yesterday’s close, and further positive news could boost Grok’s prediction even more.

From a higher-timeframe perspective, the chart is coiled for exactly this kind of vertical resolution. Nvidia’s multi-month correction from the October 2026 peak near $243 carved out a deep but constructive base.

The sharp capitulation low around $125 in late November formed a classic selling climax, followed by the violent upside gap on the reinstatement news. That gap has already reclaimed multiple prior support-turned-resistance zones.

On the weekly chart, the stock is pressing against the declining 20-week moving average while the monthly RSI, after washing out into deeply oversold territory, is beginning to turn higher with room to run into the extreme overbought readings typical of Nvidia’s historical parabolic phases.

A sustained weekly close above the $180–$190 cluster would complete a powerful higher-low structure and open measured-move targets that project well beyond the previous all-time high.

Volume on the reinstatement day was among the heaviest in the stock’s history, confirming institutional participation rather than short-covering alone.

In a fully catalyst-driven environment, these technical conditions have historically resolved with multi-week vertical advances rather than orderly grind-ups.

The broader AI infrastructure cycle remains intact. Data-center demand continues to outstrip supply, and competitors have yet to close the software and systems gap that Huang spent three decades building.

With the leadership cloud removed and new sovereign commitments on the table, the narrative shifts from “can Nvidia grow” to “how fast can it scale into a multi-trillion-dollar annual revenue run-rate.”

That psychological flip is what turns a strong stock into a generational one in a short window. Markets have priced leadership risk. They have not yet priced the full force of Huang operating with a clean mandate and government-backed demand already locked in. When that recognition hits, the move can be violent.

By January 1, 2027, under this over-the-top but internally consistent scenario, Elon Musk’s Grok AI predicts that Nvidia’s stock could reach $475.
