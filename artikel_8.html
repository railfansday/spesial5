Yemeni forces' retreat leaves Houthis in control of key Red Sea waterway

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Pro-government forces said troops were being moved to defend the interim capital, Aden (file photo)

Yemeni pro-government forces backed by Saudi Arabia have said they are withdrawing from an area overlooking the strategically important Bab al-Mandab Strait, leaving it under the control of the Iran-backed Houthi movement.

Yemen's military said it was redeploying troops from the key shipping gateway at the southern end of the Red Sea to defend its interim capital, Aden.

The shift ends a weeks-long offensive to try to secure the area near the waterway after the Houthis seized it in a surprise offensive in September, squeezing global oil exports and prompting thousands to flee.

Houthi-run media reported later on Sunday the group had taken control of the territory. Saudi Arabia has not commented.

Pro-government forces said they had "secured" the strait in early October as they sought to retake crucial ground from the Houthis, who said they had captured areas including Perim Island at the mouth of the strait and the strategic port city of Mokha a month prior.

But the Saudi-backed forces fighting on behalf of Yemen's internationally recognised government said on Sunday they were withdrawing to reinforce troops elsewhere, with the southern city of Aden a "priority".

The Reuters news agency reported a Southern Armed Forces statement which said troops were being withdrawn "from Dhubab Airport and the Bab al-Mandab area".

It said the decision had been made with the aim of "strengthening the front lines and addressing existing threats".

The Houthis said they posed no threat to international shipping passing through the waterway when they captured it, but that they would continue to target vessels from Saudi Arabia.

However, the seizure contributed to a surge in global oil prices because Saudi Arabia had been relying on the Red Sea for oil exports since the US and Israel's war with Iran effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz.

Saudi Arabia responded with hundreds of air strikes, while the Houthis launched hundreds of drones and missiles at its cities and infrastructure.

The UN has estimated that nearly 330,000 people have been displaced in Yemen as the fighting has since escalated. Hundreds of people are reported to have been killed.

Yemen has been devastated by a civil war that began in 2014, when the Houthis ousted the government from its capital Sanaa.

The conflict escalated in 2015, after a Saudi-led coalition of Arab states intervened in an attempt to restore the government's rule.

A four-year informal ceasefire began to unravel in July, when the Houthis announced a "maritime embargo" against Saudi Arabia and began missile and drone attacks on Saudi airports, oil facilities and tankers in the Red Sea.

The Houthis said they were retaliating for a Saudi blockade of ports and airports in their territory, as well as an air strike on Sanaa airport that they blamed on the kingdom.
