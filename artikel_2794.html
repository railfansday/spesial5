Sixers, Brown have a ‘humbling' night in loss to Celtics

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Sixers, Brown have a 'humbling' night in loss to Celtics originally appeared on NBC Sports Philadelphia

Whatever Jaylen Brown and the Sixers dreamed up for their first matchup against the Celtics soon flew out the window on Tuesday night.

Brown was whistled for two early fouls, the Sixers fell behind and their early-season flaws were on full display in a 123-101 loss at Xfinity Mobile Arena.

"We've just got to get better," Brown said. "Tonight was humbling. We got outplayed, out-executed. So we go back to the drawing board and get ready for the next one."

Over 26 minutes, Brown had 13 points on 4-for-9 shooting and five rebounds against the team that traded him away this summer.

As for the endless dissection of his relationship with former co-star Jayson Tatum, Brown didn't have much to add Tuesday. There was no public reconciliation, no obvious on-court animosity and generally nothing to satisfy fans hoping for some kind of fireworks on that front.

"I'm just focused on doing what's best for my team," Brown said. "Everything else … to be honest, I wasn't paying attention. But credit to Jayson; he played well tonight. Credit to the Celtics. They executed, they shot the hell out of the ball and they were the better team tonight."

Tatum scored 21 points, two of which came on a third-quarter slam over LeBron James. Paul George continued his fantastic start as a Celtic by dropping 36 points and draining six three-pointers.

The Celtics dominated from beyond the arc, shooting 22 for 49 (44.9 percent). The Sixers went just 11 for 37 (29.7 percent) on another night of underwhelming offense. The team sits 22nd in the NBA in offensive rating, 24th in three-point makes per game and 21st in three-point percentage.

"I think that we've got some guys that are obviously shooting much lower percentages than their last season or their career average — almost a handful of guys," Sixers head coach Nick Nurse said. "And I don't think it's because they're shooting fallaway, totally contested ones. I think they're shots we want them to take. So I think they'll continue to take 'em and I'm pretty sure those will even out into their career averages."

Joel Embiid is at 37 percent from the floor and 4 for 33 from three-point range (12.1 percent).

The Sixers' seven-time All-Star center got through an injury scare when he rolled his ankle late in the second quarter and hit a three early in the third. It was his first long-range make since the Sixers' home opener back on Oct. 22.

"It's a work in progress," Embiid said. "I think the biggest thing is just not thinking. I'm probably overthinking it. The rhythm of the game is different. I'm so used to having to create for myself, so it's a little bit different.

"But I think I'm slowly getting better. You could see as soon as I made a three, the next shot in the short corner … I didn't even think about it. I just shot it and let it fly, and I made it. … I think it's just about taking what's in front of you. Make or miss, keep working on your game. It's going to get there at some point."

Even with the Sixers second in the Eastern Conference standings at 9-3, Embiid believes the team's defense has "slacked off a little bit" in recent games.

"I don't really look at the record," he said. "I just look at the process of, 'Are we getting better every day?' We can always do a better job at all phases of the game."

"They're one of the top teams in the league and they've been together for a while," Brown said. "We've got a lot to work on over here, so we've got to get to work."

The Sixers will face the Raptors on Thursday night in Philadelphia.

They'll have to wait until Jan. 21 in Boston to see the Celtics again.

"I was looking forward to this," Brown said. "I was looking forward to playing well, but s— happens. Just get ready for the next one."
