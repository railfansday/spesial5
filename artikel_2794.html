Warriors always were a Steph Curry injury away, and now must avoid catastrophe

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Warriors always were a Steph Curry injury away, and now must avoid catastrophe originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

In the nicest, least degrading way possible, Warriors coach Steve Kerr gave his version of "it is what it is" to reporters Wednesday night in Dallas after his team's fourth straight blowout loss.

The Warriors extended their losing streak to four without Steph Curry on this five-game road trip, falling 116-94 against the Dallas Mavericks in a game where they trailed by as many as 29 points. They've lost by an average of 27 points the last three games and were down by 31 and 43 points the previous two.

That's the difference Curry still makes. It's also the difference of being down over $150 million in salaries to injuries within the first month of the 2026-27 NBA season.

What was obvious going into the campaign is as evident as ever: The Warriors always were a Curry injury away.

"This is just where we are right now," Kerr said to reporters at American Airlines Center. "We're wiped out with injuries. The guys are really, really competing and playing hard and staying together, and that's all we can do.

"Things are going to turn, but we've got to give ourselves the best chance to help them turn."

Sometimes, it really is as simple as that.

Golden State entered the season with $89.3 million on its shelf between Jimmy Butler ($56.8 million), Kristaps Porziņģis ($20 million) and Moses Moody ($12.5 million). Add Curry's $62.6 million, and you're now over $150 million.

And that doesn't even count Brandon Williams, whose missing firepower has been a big part of the Warriors' drowning offense.

Since Wednesday was the first night of a back-to-back, that meant 40-year-old center Al Horford also was subjected to street clothes.

The same reason fans felt such hope during the Warriors' eight-game win streak is the same reason there's such angst in this four-game losing streak, which stems from the same frustrations in the offseason.

Surround Curry with smart, competent players and your team has a chance. Take away Curry from a group of mainly role players and your chances are bare to none.

Yaxel Lendeborg's skyrocket ascension took away so much stress in the non-Curry minutes, and even in the few games the Warriors' aging star needed to rest at the start of the season.

He scored 26 points in his first game without Curry, which was just the third game of Lendeborg's NBA career, and led a valiant effort in a road loss to the Phoenix Suns on the second night of a back-to-back. Lendeborg then went on a 37-point rampage in front of his home fans to beat the LA Clippers in Curry's second rest game.

Like the entire Warriors, Lendeborg was shellshocked when Curry left against the Houston Rockets one week ago, but responded admirably in the second quarter and finished with 25 points.

Brandin Podziemski scored 22 in that loss to the Rockets. In the three games since, Lendeborg and Podziemski mostly have been letdowns as lead options offensively.

They scored a combined 23 points – 13 for Lendeborg and 10 for Podziemski – in their latest blowout loss. They've averaged a combined 26 points in the last three games while shooting 30.6 percent from the field and 22.6 from 3-point range.

"I think a lot of guys are really trying to do some things on their own," Kerr said. "Yaxel had probably four or five off the dribble fadeaway shots in the first half, just trying to carry us. He knows that we need him, and he's trying really hard. BP as well.

"We're attempting some difficult shots. We've got to get the ball moving and create easier shots for one another."

Warriors players are trying to temper expectations on Lendeborg. The ultra-skilled forward thrived playing off Curry's gravity, and already was relied upon to do more than most rookies are expected from the opening tip.

He's an older rookie, yes. He also hasn't played a full month of an NBA regular season yet.

Draft evaluators didn't see Lendeborg as a No. 1 scoring option after excelling as the ultimate offensive complement at Michigan, and he's being thrust into the fire without perhaps the most skilled offensive player in basketball history.

Podziemski has to play closer to who he was the final six weeks of last season when the Warriors didn't have Curry and Butler. Fans feel they have more reason to chastise him since signing a $110 million contract extension, but these past few games also have been a reminder that first and second options sign for more than what Podziemski did.

A Curry-less Warriors team often is a Draymond Green-less Warriors team, too. Green, who returned on a one-year, $27.7 million contract, is giving them nothing on offense. Only one of the two can be a solo act.

The shining light in Dallas was Will Richard. Playing through a torn left UCL in his thumb, Richard scored 20 points in 27 minutes off the bench. He bounced all around the court, swiped four steals and got up to throw down some emotionally-charged dunks.

Richard played with an appropriate amount of desperation that his Warriors teammates need to match.

"The Steph injury is unfortunate," Georges Niang said. "We're missing bodies. But at the end of the day, I think we have good spirit. … It's just adapting to the players that we have. Now that Steph is not out there, it's different lineups that we weren't used to playing. I think we're getting closer than what it looks like.

"I feel corny saying that, but it's going to turn."

The first few weeks of the Warriors' season have been a resurrection from an 0-3 start, riding the highs of an eight-game win streak and now crashing back through bumpy waters in a current four-game losing streak.

Butler's injury ended the Warriors last season in January. These Warriors always were a Curry injury away from catastrophe, and they'll have to right the ship quickly to not spin out of control waiting for his return in the coming weeks.
