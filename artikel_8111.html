The Best Camping Gear That Doubles as Travel Gear

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

As a frequent flier who jet-sets at least once a month and camps three seasons out of the year, I’ve come to rely on “crossover” gear that bridges both worlds. And with how expensive everything has gotten, anything I buy must earn its keep on more than one kind of trip.

I’ve honed a short list of my favorite gear that comes on most trips I take, from hopping on an international flight to camping with my family for the weekend. These dual-purpose items pack just as easily into a carry-on as they do into a trunk, are versatile enough for city streets and dirt trails, and make sense to use on a long layover and also around the campfire.

I bring these eight items along no matter whether I’m sleeping under the stars or in seat 23B.

Granite Gear is best known for its backpacking and paddling packs, but its most underrated product is the humble Zippditty Stuffsack. I use these zippered pouches for keeping all the little things in order while traveling and adventuring—toiletries, charging cables, snacks, pens, first aid items, tent stakes, you name it. A set of four comes with 2.4L, 1.7L, 1L, and 0.6L sizes and in a variety of fun colors. There’s rarely a trip where at least a couple of them don’t make it into my bag.

Dirty laundry stinks. Literally. That’s why I always pack a lightweight dry bag when I’m traveling and camping. Dry bags are the MVPs of kayaking and river rafting trips, keeping clothes and gear from getting wet. But they’re also my secret hack for stashing stinky clothes until I get home and can wash them. Sea to Summit makes some of my favorite durable bags, but honestly, any roll-top or drawstring stuffsack or dry bag will do the trick when it comes to sealing in the funk.

If you’re like me and refuse to pay a premium for caffeine on the go (hard pass on that mediocre-at-best $7 airport latte), let me introduce you to my go-to backpacking coffee. I tuck a few of these Alpine Start instant coffee packets into my bag on every trip. I’ve made it a habit to pack a leak-proof travel mug (my favorite is Ocean Bottle) and ask for some hot water from an airport café or flight attendant. Flavors include the classic, dirty chai, matcha, and select seasonals. Plus, they taste much better than anything on the beverage cart. Trust me. I’m a self-proclaimed coffee snob.

Hotel pillows are so hit or miss. Sometimes I get lucky with a fluffy, comfy place to rest my head. Other times I’m stuck with a flat or lumpy excuse for a pillow. You know the pain. I’ve started bringing my HEST Camp Pillow along so I can rest assured I’ll get a good night’s sleep, whether I’m in a tent, a hotel room, or 30,000 feet in the air. It comes in small, medium, and large sizes; the small is best for quick trips, the medium is worth the extra squeeze if you’re staying somewhere multiple nights in a row, and the large is my go-to for car camping. If you prefer a neck pillow on flights, the Hest Pro Travel Pillow will ruin all other neck pillows for you.

Most bags are built for one kind of trip. The Thule Aion Duffel is built for all of them. It has been my go-to bag for more than four years on everything from car camping weekends at state parks to international flights to Japan, Copenhagen, and Mexico. Its features make sense for both time outdoors and work trips in the air: The 35L capacity easily fits a weekend’s worth of clothes and then some, and an internal divider splits the duffel into two main compartments, with a padded laptop sleeve sandwiched in between. There’s also a TPU-lined, zippered compartment that locks in odors, moisture, and dirt from hiking boots and swimsuits.

I try to stay off my phone when I’m camping, so I rarely bring a heavy-duty power bank with me. And when I travel, I don’t often need one either since I’m never far from an outlet on a seatback or airport lounge chair. Rather than lugging around a bulky battery pack, I bring the lightweight NESTOUT 5,000mAh power bank in my bag. This little stick of energy gives my electronics (phone and headphones mainly) all the juice I need on the trail and in transit. Its 5,000mAh capacity gives my phone roughly one full charge, and its 15W output tops it off quickly if I need extra power. It’s also fully waterproof, dustproof, and shockproof.

I’ve lost count of how many times a cheap travel-sized shampoo bottle exploded in my luggage on flights and camping trips. Last year I finally invested in some reusable liquid bottles and now I no longer have to worry about a messy surprise when I unpack. Humangear’s GoToob Squeeze Bottles are made of soft, flexible silicone with a no-drip valve and a locking cap. I fill them with shampoo, conditioner, body wash, sunscreen, and even toothpaste. The wide mouth makes them easy to fill and clean, and the TSA-friendly sizes (1.7 ounces for short trips, 2.5 ounces for longer ones) always clear security.

My favorite shoe for camping, traveling, and just about everything in between is the Arc’teryx Kragg. They’re super comfortable, low-key stylish, and versatile enough to wear on the trail, around town, or on the plane. Originally designed as an approach shoe for climbers with a grippy outsole, the Kragg’s slip-on upper works wonders for a midnight bathroom run at the campsite or breezing through airport security. I love that you can collapse the heel and wear them like slippers on a long-haul flight, which has saved my feet on more than one red-eye. On work trips, they even look sleek enough to wear to dinner (as long as I’m not wearing the pair that’s scuffed up from season after season of camping). Pricey? Yes. Worth the investment? 100 percent.
