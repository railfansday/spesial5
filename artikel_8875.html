DFA awards 2026 turn regional heritage into global design

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

grounded in cultural lineage yet operating globally, this year's laureates at the DFA awards 2026 span architecture, material innovation, spatial hospitality, and electric mobility.

mariana rodrigues November 17, 2026 6 min

From sculptural jewelry and spatial architecture to material science and electric mobility, the DFA Awards 2026 place Asian design perspectives at the center of the global conversation. Looking outward while remaining anchored in local sensibilities, this year’s honorees prove that cross-disciplinary craftsmanship and territorial memory are quietly reshaping how the world creates.

Spanning diverse creative practices, the recognized designers merge rigorous technical research with deep cultural intuition, offering tangible solutions and distinct visual languages for an interconnected creative landscape.

Founded in 2003 by the Hong Kong Design Centre, the DFA Awards take root in a city shaped by cross-cultural exchange. Supported by the Cultural and Creative Industries Development Agency, the platform connects regional craft with global markets. From the international stage of Business of Design Week to the DX design hub in Sham Shui Po, Hong Kong operates as an active testbed amplifying Asian perspectives across the wider creative landscape.

The program divides its focus across key pillars of contemporary practice, tracing both lifetime legacies and emerging directions. Sustained contributions to spatial discourse, society, and education in Asia receive the DFA Lifetime Achievement Award (LAA), while the DFA Design Leadership Award (DLA) spotlights leaders who deploy design as a catalyst for viable enterprise. International influence rooted in heritage takes center stage with the DFA World’s Outstanding Chinese Designer (WOCD), honoring creators of Chinese lineage commanding global attention.

Regional collaboration and community stewardship define the DFA Designer of the Year (DOY), complemented by the DFA Asian Design Vanguard (ADV), which champions forward-looking practitioners shaping modern industry. Anchoring the framework, the DFA Design for Asia Awards (DFAA) survey built projects, objects, and visual communication that draw directly from regional cultures to elevate daily life and inform future creative output. Looking toward the next generation, the DFA Hong Kong Young Design Talent Award (YDTA) recognizes emerging practitioners in 2026 focused on environmental sustainability and social responsibility, supporting 18 of them to pursue work or study opportunities abroad and bring global insights back to the local creative scene.

The DFA Lifetime Achievement Award (LAA) honors Yung Ho Chang, founding partner of Atelier Feichang Jianzhu and Dean and Chair Professor of Architecture at the University of Hong Kong. Returning to China after graduating from UC Berkeley, Chang cofounded his studio with Lijia Lu in 1993, merging private spatial experimentation with extensive academic leadership at MIT, Peking University, and the Pritzker Architecture Prize jury. Key works like the Liangzhu Campus for the China Academy of Art, ChunYangTai Arts and Cultural Centre, and Maison de la Chine demonstrate his commitment to joining regional heritage with international architectural logic.

Awarded the DFA Design Leadership Award (DLA), Sonia Cheng proves how mathematical rigor can shape intuitive, atmosphere-driven spaces. Leading both Rosewood Hotel Group and Chow Tai Fook Jewellery Group, the Harvard-educated executive approaches built environments with analytical precision. Since 2011, she has expanded Rosewood to more than 50 locations worldwide, anchoring each property in site-specific craft and local art, while steering Chow Tai Fook through a fresh era of international retail.

The DFA World’s Outstanding Chinese Designer (WOCD) title goes to Hong Kong artist Wallace Chan, recognizing five decades of radical innovation across sculpture and jewelry art. Completely self-taught, Chan merged Chinese philosophy with scientific experimentation, pioneering the three-dimensional Wallace Cut in 1987, specialized clawless settings, and structural titanium sculpture. He expanded his repertoire with Wallace Chan Porcelain, a specialized compound significantly stronger than structural steel. With major acquisitions across the Victoria and Albert Museum and the British Museum, his practice turns esoteric concepts into palpable, light-bending structures.

Named DFA Designer of the Year (DOY), Vector Architects founder Dong Gong has established a compelling spatial language shaped by natural light, local climate, and tectonic clarity. A foreign member of the French Academy of Architecture, Gong transforms everyday landscapes into contemplative civic encounters. His defining works, including the coastal Seashore Library, the Seashore Chapel, the Yangshuo Sugar House hotel conversion, and the Jingyang Camphor Court, showcase an agile balance of raw materiality and site history. With major presentations at MoMA in New York and the Venice Biennale, his practice stands among the most resonant voices in contemporary Asian architecture.

Closing the roster of honorees, the DFA Asian Design Vanguard (ADV) award spotlights Tianyuan Li for recalibrating the contours of electric mobility. Currently heading design at Xiaomi Auto, Li arrived on the global stage as the first Chinese car designer based at BMW headquarters in Munich, steering exterior form on forward-looking projects like the BMW iX and the circular economy concept BMW i Vision Circular. At Xiaomi Auto, he translates digital operating systems into tactile aerodynamic silhouettes, establishing a fluid aesthetic across the Xiaomi SU7 electric sedan, the Xiaomi YU7, and concepts such as the Xiaomi SkyNomad and Xiaomi Vision Gran Turismo. His work demonstrates how vehicle architecture can bridge consumer electronics with high-speed engineering.

Spatial Design takes the lead as the Hong Kong Design Centre announces the 209 winning projects of the DFA Design for Asia Awards 2026. Chosen from entries across 15 economies by a 26-member jury of local and international design figures, the selection spans six design disciplines, with Spatial Design recording the highest proportion of winning entries. The 2026 edition also sees Grand Award winners from Thailand and India return to the top leaderboard for the first time since 2020, pointing to the growing influence of ASEAN design studios.
