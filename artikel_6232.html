Across thousands of years, Neanderthals and modern humans (Homo sapiens) interbred, leaving traces of Neanderthal DNA in people alive today. But how these encounters happened is a huge unanswered question. Answering it may shed light on what made us different from our close cousins — and why we thrived while Neanderthals died out.

Live Science interviewed researchers deeply invested in human evolution, and in this exclusive video we explore what scientists are uncovering about these relationships from ancient DNA and archaeological evidence unearthed in Eurasia.
