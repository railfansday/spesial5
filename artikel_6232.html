American Airlines Turns Back 10-Hour Flight To Buenos Aires After Lavatory Overflows

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

An American Airlines Boeing 777 flying from Dallas/Fort Worth to Buenos Aires returned to its departure airport on Friday night after a lavatory overflowed on board. Flight AA997 — operated by a 26-year-old 777-200ER — turned back over the Gulf of Mexico and landed at Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport (DFW) just over two hours after taking off, with most of its scheduled 10-and-a-half-hour journey still ahead.

While airlines are allowed to operate aircraft with some lavatories out of service, these rules cover known faults before departure, and inoperative lavatories must be locked and marked as out of service. Problems that develop mid-flight are a different issue — for example, last month, another American 777-200ER was forced to divert after six of its eight lavatories stopped working.

Flight AA997 was scheduled to depart DFW at 7:30 PM local time on November 20 and arrive at Ezeiza International Airport (EZE) at 9:00 AM the following morning. Flight tracking data shows the aircraft departed 42 minutes late at 8:12 PM, before heading south over the Gulf of Mexico. However, it turned back toward Dallas around an hour into the flight, eventually landing back at DFW at 10:20 PM.

In a post on X, Flightradar24 reported that the aircraft returned to DFW because of a "lavatory overflow." The aircraft involved, registered N782AN, is a Boeing 777-200ER built in 2000. American has not disclosed how many passengers and crew were on board or what caused the overflow. Simple Flying has contacted the airline for comment and will update this story accordingly.

Returned to DFW after a lavatory overflow and landed safely

American operates a daily flight between Dallas/Fort Worth and Buenos Aires, scheduling the Boeing 777-200ER on the route this winter. DFW is one of three American gateways to Buenos Aires alongside New York JFK Airport (JFK), which has one daily Boeing 777-300ER service, and Miami International Airport (MIA), which has two daily flights.

Minimum equipment lists (MELs) allow some lavatories on board a commercial plane to be out of service. However, the plumbing must be isolated to prevent leaks, and the door must be locked and signed as out of service. An overflow incident represents a biohazard risk, possibly spreading wastewater outside the lavatory and onto the cabin floor.

There are also concerns that a leak could affect onboard electrical systems, potentially creating additional safety issues. American's 777-200ERs are equipped with 273 seats – 37 in business class, 24 in premium economy and 212 in economy – and typically have eight lavatories. On a full flight, that works out to around 34 passengers per lavatory.

Returning to Dallas brought the aircraft back to American's largest hub, where the airline has maintenance teams and spare widebodies to help get passengers moving again. With fuel on board for a flight lasting more than 10 hours, it remains unclear whether the crew dumped fuel before landing or carried out an overweight landing, which would require a maintenance inspection.

Friday evening's return to DFW comes around six weeks after another American 777-200ER diverted because of lavatory problems. On October 6, flight AA37 from Madrid Airport (MAD) to Dallas diverted to New York JFK after all but two of its eight lavatories stopped working. The FAA reported at the time that the pilots cited "an inadequate number of operational lavatories" as the reason for the diversion. The aircraft had 287 people on board and spent almost three and a half hours on the ground at JFK before continuing to Dallas.

The turnback also comes just over a week after a lavatory problem forced a Frontier Airlines flight to declare an emergency. On November 11, flight F93566 from Denver to Detroit diverted to Chicago O’Hare after a leaking faucet in a forward lavatory flooded the cabin floor. In an air traffic control recording, the crew declared a Mayday, adding that they were "worried about electronics."
