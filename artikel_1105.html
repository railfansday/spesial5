1966 World Cup hero Sir Geoff Hurst backs Mail's dementia carers appeal after six of his England team-mates developed the disease

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Published: 17:00 EST, 16 November 2026 | Updated: 17:59 EST, 16 November 2026

World Cup hero Sir Geoff Hurst has thrown his support behind the Daily Mail’s dementia carers appeal.

Sir Geoff, who saw six of his England team-mates suffer from dementia before they died, is now the sole survivor of Sir Alf Ramsey’s 1966 trophy-winning team.

Manchester United legend Sir Bobby Charlton and his elder brother Jack Charlton of Leeds United lived with the condition in their later years.

So did Martin Peters, who scored a goal alongside Sir Geoff’s famous hat-trick to beat West Germany 4-2 in the 1966 World Cup final at Wembley Stadium.

Dementia also hit Ray Wilson, of Everton, Sir Geoff’s strike partner Roger Hunt, of Liverpool, and Manchester United midfielder Nobby Stiles.

Six out of the 11 England players who started in the World Cup final 60 years ago developing the disease is a startling ratio, and has driven home research linking heading the ball to degenerative brain conditions in later life.

World Cup hero Sir Geoff Hurst has thrown his support behind the Daily Mail’s dementia carers appeal

Sir Geoff has become a staunch advocate of Alzheimer’s Society and vowed to back the Mail’s appeal, delivered in partnership with Alzheimer’s Society and the Mail Force charity, to raise millions towards a nationwide support network for millions of people across Britain caring for friends and family members with dementia.

He said: ‘As always, I am supporting Alzheimer’s Society in this endeavour. Several of my colleagues suffered and are still suffering in some cases this disease and the more we can do to research it, for better things will be in the long run hopefully.

‘So, let’s get behind this Alzheimer’s campaign to help the families of those are suffering and do as much good as possible.’

The Mail’s new Dementia Carers Together appeal passed its first target of raising £100,000 in just a week, enough to pay for nearly 3,000 places on new classes to give carers vital support and information about their rights and the services available to them.

The six-week courses will allow attendees to meet others in a similar situation and help to address the loneliness, exhaustion and overwhelming responsibilities many face following a loved one’s diagnosis.

The appeal has already won the backing of every major political party leader, with Prime Minister Andy Burnham saying: ‘Just a little bit of help can make something feel bearable or doable that without it, would all of a sudden just overwhelm somebody.’

The family of Ray Wilson, England’s left back in 1966, are also supporting the Mail’s campaign.

Wilson died in 2018 at the age of 83 after years with Alzheimer’s disease, during most of which time he was cared for by his devoted wife Pat and their family.

His daughter-in-law, Linda Wilson, said: ‘It’s really good campaign. It would be nice to have a six-week programme available to everybody regardless of where they are in the country and not reliant upon some sort of postcode lottery.

‘Pat was always lucky because Ray would go to a Help the Aged day centre in Slaithwaite twice a week and it gave her some respite.

Dementia Carers Together is raising money to fund new courses across the UK to give tens of thousands of struggling unpaid dementia carers vital emotional and practical support.

‘Having support and knowing where to go and look for it is important. You feel shellshocked when you get the diagnosis. You feel quite alone and wondering what’s going to happen and how fast.

‘So much of the care is patient centred but the people supporting the patient need support as well.’

Wilson’s widow Pat died last year, and the family are on a fundraising mission of their own, selling the drawings done by Ray after his diagnosis with proceeds set to be donated to the Alzheimer’s Society.

He had never shown any inclination to draw but one day at the breakfast table picked up a biro and started to sketch on the inside of a cereal box, and hardly ever stopped, producing hundreds of drawings which Pat kept after he died.

A small number bear Wilson’s signature, some refer to his footballing past at Huddersfield and Everton.

Sky Sports presenter Hayley McQueen’s father Gordon McQueen, the former Leeds, Manchester United and Scotland centre half, died in 2023 at the age of 70 after a long struggle with vascular dementia.

She is also throwing her support behind the Dementia Carers Together appeal to make a difference for the families of those suffering from the disease.

She said: ‘I’m backing this campaign with the Alzheimer’s Society who’ve been incredibly helpful with my family throughout the battle that we had with my dad and also going through unfortunately another Alzheimer’s situation close to home.

‘I lost both my grandparents so I understand the burden of trying to care for loved ones and family members not knowing where you’re going to get help. So, please get behind this campaign and please note that you’re not going through this alone.’
