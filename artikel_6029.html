Hyatt is drawing sharper distinctions among brands such as Andaz and Thompson. Getting hotels to deliver on those promises is the harder task.

Hyatt is betting that AI travel agents will recommend its hotels more often if its brands are easier to tell apart, including from one another. Since September, it has been redrawing the lines among The Standard, Andaz, and Thompson Hotels, three brands that had all been described as “lifestyle.”

Chairman and CEO Mark Hoplamazian outlined the strategy on Hyatt’s third-quarter earnings call. Asked how AI agents would change booking, he said the company is “hyper focused” on making its brands “sharper, more defined, more findable, and more precise.”

“A well-defined brand gives both travelers and technology clearer signals about why Thompson, Hyatt Studios, or another Hyatt brand is the right fit for a particular trip and human need,” Hoplamazian told Skift in a follow-up with executives leading the effort.

Hyatt is redrawing the lines between The Standard, Andaz, and Thompson Hotels, three brands it had all described as "lifestyle," on the premise that sharper brand definitions will make them easier for both travelers and AI systems to tell apart. CEO Mark Hoplamazian said on the third-quarter earnings call that Hyatt is "hyper focused" on making its brands "sharper, more defined, more findable, and more precise," and he named Amar Lalvani — the former Standard International chief who joined through Hyatt's 2024 acquisition — as the company's first chief creative officer on September 30 to lead the work. Lalvani defines The Standard as "boldness," Andaz as "grounded discovery," and Thompson as "the home base," and said the definitions now shape hiring, training, design, sales and marketing, food and beverage, and how Hyatt evaluates markets, sites, and owners. He is also rethinking brand standards, separating standards that ensure consistent execution from "codes" whose expression varies by property, and questioning audited behaviors such as greeting every guest by name. Hoplamazian said bookings completed inside chatbots are "still very low," and the mechanics of AI discovery remain largely opaque: a Milestone survey of AI-generated answers for about 1,128 U.S. hotels and resorts found hotel websites accounted for only 8% of sources cited, with OTAs, Tripadvisor, Google, Reddit, and travel media supplying the rest. Cornell professor Chekitan Dev said the strategy faces "landmines," because hotel staff are trained to think operations, not brand, and incentives would need to change.
