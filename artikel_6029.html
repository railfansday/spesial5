The Courtin family behind the Clarins skincare empire is combining what the hotel industry usually splits apart: the buildings, the brands, and the operations of a luxury hotel group, under one owner.

The family behind skincare company Clarins is taking full ownership of Evok Collection, a French owner-operator of five luxury hotels, putting its capital into hotel real estate, brands, and operations.

Famille C Participations, the Courtin family's investment vehicle known as FCP, said Tuesday it's acquiring 100% of Evok's share capital from Zaka Investments, which had been the majority investor. FCP had invested €130 million two years ago for a minority stake.

Price and terms weren't disclosed. Family-owned Clarins produces about $2 billion in annual sales in the skincare industry.

The Courtin family behind skincare company Clarins is taking full ownership of Evok Collection, a French owner-operator of five luxury hotels, buying out majority investor Zaka Investments through its investment vehicle Famille C Participations. FCP had put €130 million into a minority stake two years ago; price and terms of the full buyout weren't disclosed. Evok runs 216 rooms and suites across the Nolinski, Brach, and Cour des Vosges brands — three hotels in Paris and one each in Venice and Madrid — and says it owns the real estate for all of them. That makes the deal a move against the asset-light model the largest hotel groups have pursued for years by selling real estate and living on management and franchise fees. FCP is targeting a €1 billion ($1.1 billion) valuation for Evok by 2032, nearly double its 2024 valuation, with three signed hotels in the pipeline: Rome in the first quarter of 2027, the Gulf of Saint-Tropez in the second quarter, and Val d'Isère by 2030. Prisca Courtin, who runs FCP and chairs Clarins' supervisory board, said the group is not thinking about an exit and called hospitality a lasting pillar. Luxury hospitality strategist Makarand Mohite said hitting the valuation target will depend on whether Evok can turn Clarins' wellness equity into serious non-room spend per stay rather than relying on room rates alone.
