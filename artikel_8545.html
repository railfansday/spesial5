Personal Investments • Re: Another roth conversion question

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

There are plenty of videos out there talking about what the expected tax will be based on the size of the pre-tax account and how much you convert and when. With a pension AND social security, that says that when you are taking those payments, your income is already going to be high. If you have a large pre-tax account, once RMDs start, you should look at not only the tax rate but IRMAA and potentially NIIT.

Having grown up in times where the employer 401k provider came in and gave a presentation every year and ALWAYS told us that OF COURSE in retirement, your tax rate would be lower. What a bunch of bunk that was. DW and I dutifully made large 401k contributions and for most of our careers, there were no Roth 401k options. Some times, we made too much money to make any Roth contributions.

If I were to go back in time, I would only do 401k up to the company match if it had to be pre-tax. I'd do the rest as Roth 401k, if that were possible. Beyond that, I'd rather have taxable in BRK and just let it sit as it spills no unwanted dividend income.

A very common strategy when one retires is to Roth convert enough to have MAGI stay just under IRMAA. This avoids the cliff and added cost for both spouses if both are on Medicare. If the pre-tax account is really excessive, some will suggest going up into IRMAA and convert massive amounts.

And don't forget that when one spouse passes, the other goes into single rates. I've done the analysis for DW and while we're just at the top of the 22% bracket now, without conversions, when I pass, DW, taking RMDs and social security will be in the 32% bracket and $730 in IRMAA payments.
