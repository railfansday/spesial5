Take a Deposit Before Custom Work Starts – Enough to Cover the Materials

Before a custom order or big project starts, take a deposit of 25% to 50% of the price, enough to buy the materials and cover the first stage of work. Put the amount and what’s refundable in the quote, and collect it before you order anything. On home improvement jobs, check whether your state limits deposits first.

On a custom job, you buy the lumber, fabric, or food and put in the first hours of work before the customer pays you anything. If they back out, you’re left with materials bought for their order and the bill that came with them.

A deposit turns that around. Collected before you buy anything, it pays for the materials, so you work with the customer’s money instead of your own. It also commits the customer, who now has money on the line too.

Say a furniture maker quotes $4,000 for a walnut dining table whose lumber costs $1,800. He used to order the wood on a handshake. Now he asks for half, $2,000, before he orders it, so a canceled order no longer leaves him holding $1,800 of walnut he paid for himself.

Ask for 25% to 50% of the price, enough to cover the materials and the first stage of work. Check whether your state caps it before you quote. On home improvement work, California caps a deposit at $1,000 or 10% of the contract price, whichever is less. The exception is a contractor whose bond on file with the Contractors State License Board covers all the work it has under way. Massachusetts caps a home improvement deposit at a third of the price, or the cost of special-order materials if that’s more.

On a project that runs for months, the deposit is only the first payment, and the rest comes due in stages as the work gets done.

Write the deposit into the quote or contract, with its due date and how much you keep if the customer cancels after you’ve ordered materials. Under the Federal Trade Commission’s Cooling-Off Rule, a customer who signs at home for a personal or household purchase can cancel for a full refund until midnight of the third business day.

Send the deposit as an invoice the customer can pay online the day they sign, and order materials only after it’s paid and any cancellation period has passed.
