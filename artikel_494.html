This sleek, design-oriented semiautomatic (“a steampunk gasworks of a thing,” according to former WIRED reviewer Matthew Korfhage) is quite dramatic in person, and it has a dual boiler—meaning you can froth milk at the same time you’re pulling a shot—and it comes with a nice set of accessories, including a tamper and milk pitcher. Note that this sale is on the 2nd Gen machine, which features expanded grinder settings and a new portafilter.

Formerly the Linea Classic, De’Longhi’s Classic is the best bare-bones espresso machine we’ve tried, with a pressure gauge, 15-bar pump, and updated thermoblock system. I used one for a full year and, while it’s not as foolproof as a semiautomatic, it was enough for morning espresso shots and cappuccinos, and I even pulled off some rudimentary latte art with its steam wand.

The Vertuo Pop+ is our favorite pod maker for espresso, and the Deluxe model adds a larger, side-mount water tank (37 ounces to the original’s 25 ounces) and new finishes, including this white one that’s on sale. (There’s also matte black-and-gold and a sort of gunmetal gray, but those are not as heavily discounted.)

We appreciated this semiautomatic’s slim profile, consistent shots, and built-in burr grinder. The cold-brew setting is more of a gimmick than a way to make actual cold brew, and some design choices are a little quirky, but it’s a beautiful, industrial-looking machine that takes up only 12 inches of counter space.

Ninja sometimes has a reputation for doing too much, but this semiautomatic we liked is a beginner-friendly option that pulls some solid shots. It’s got a built-in grinder and milk frother, the latter of which can make cold foam and even has a setting for plant-based milk. In true Ninja fashion, it also has settings for drip coffee and cold brew, but neither impressed our tester, so I wouldn’t buy it for that alone. (Note the sale is on the “Gunmetal” color only.)
