Enzo Maresca tinkers his way to nervy Man City win

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Few in elite sport have the luxury of managing their own endings as Pep Guardiola did at Manchester City, yet no one can ever fully control a beginning. Enzo Maresca’s City started their Premier League season with a win, in the end, but three points masked the scale of the job he faces.

The narrative focus has been the impossibility of filling the personality void Guardiola leaves at a club sculpted in his image. That macro reading overlooks the number of distinct issues with City that Maresca must fix. A new midfield has to be recruited then blooded, a jittery defence needs settling down, and Phil Foden clearly requires help to recover the form which made him Premier League player of the year as recently as 2024.

All of that can wait after this victory against Bournemouth, which quietened some of the alarms from last weekend’s 3-0 defeat by Arsenal in Cardiff. Josko Gvardiol scored an injury-time winner after City had trailed for most of the match, much of it a sloppy slog for the home side, who went behind in the 26th minute. The introduction of Rayan Cherki for Nico O’Reilly revived some familiar attacking effusiveness.

A lesser-spotted VAR-aided double celebration capped a tense but ultimately joyful day for Maresca. Marc Guéhi equalised from Cherki’s well-taken corner in the final minutes, then the expanded Etihad Stadium rapturously celebrated Gvardiol’s apparent winner, only to see it flagged offside. Video assistant Paul Howard intervened to show Gvardiol was onside from Cherki’s pass, before City’s fans, players and staff shouted twice as loud for twice as long to salute a morale-boosting victory.

“It’s the perfect day to be honest,” Maresca said, although he may wish to set his expectations slightly higher. The East Stand which faces the dugouts at the Etihad unfurled a banner before the game welcoming him back to Manchester after his spell as Guardiola’s assistant. “I was very pleased when the game started, to watch the banner at the other side, and I was pleased at the end because we gave them the joy of the three points.”

His team made a fluid and furious start to Premier League football, suffused with a desire to prove the Community Shield is just a jumped-up friendly. A newly streamlined short-haired Erling Haaland ran with impatience, Elliot Anderson was a sinewy presence in midfield, Foden pulled off a gorgeous flick which led to a shot by Rico Lewis, well saved by Djordje Petrovic.

Maresca’s selection was surprising, with six defenders in his XI, some deployed unexpectedly. Guéhi partnered Anderson in defensive midfield, while their England team-mate O’Reilly was often just behind Haaland in a No 10 role. There was an air of destiny about one O’Reilly run down the left flank in the first half, cruising to the byline and crossing presentably. Haaland was well positioned in the middle, but utterly fluffed his lines. Samson similes on standby, although perhaps he too was short of full match fitness during his early-season visit from the Philistines?

Momentum shifted after Haaland’s miss, and Bournemouth took the lead when an innocuous high ball flummoxed Abdukodir Khusanov and Rúben Dias. They both pursued it, neither could make decisive contact and it left Evanilson on the ball with space to manufacture an unmissable cross for Marcus Tavernier.

Marcus Tavernier fires Bournemouth ahead against Manchester City! 💥 pic.twitter.com/lSyNRfrTlc

Marco Rose, their new manager, is facing a similarly daunting task to Maresca in living up to a predecessor who led Bournemouth to their highest-ever league finish and European football. “How the game went, it was quite unlucky to lose,” he said. “We had two situations, one corner kick and one attacking when we defended too passive, and that’s why we lost.”

His team showed an appealing coiled-spring quality after weathering the early City onslaught. They have players of class with the ever lively Tavernier, new signing António Silva seamlessly replacing Marcos Senesi at centre-back and silky Alex Scott, the Guernsey Grealish, whose current standing has now overtaken the man who gave him his nickname.

They could not hang on here though, with Guéhi atoning for a free header planted wide with his day-saving equaliser. Cherki’s corner was nodded in from around the six-yard line then, with the game in the balance, the Frenchman provided another, thanks in part to the semi-automated gods smiling on Gvardiol and his new manager.

City will need more, especially in midfield. Anderson at his best can cover the ground and roles of two men, which is just as well as City await reinforcements. The incoming Ayyoub Bouaddi will help, but it is a big ask for an 18-year-old to fill a spot vacated by a Ballon d’Or winner. Maresca was asked after the match about finding a “new Rodri” and deadpanned “Marc Guéhi”, who played acceptably, but is probably not a long-term solution.

There was a jadedness at times from City, which is not a great way to start the season, although they will surely improve with a bolstered squad and fitness levels. Maresca said “four or five” of his players who returned late from the World Cup are still effectively in pre-season training, including Haaland and Anderson.

Of course City are not the finished article, what team are one game into a new season? While there is a vast gap from this performance to their best, it was still impressive to smuggle three points from zero with six minutes left to play. Guardiola’s influence will be hard to shake, but that need not be a negative.

Pep Guardiola’s successor assessed the breadth of the challenge before him and offered a candid appraisal of how to replace the irreplaceable. “It would be silly to try and change it,” he said of Guardiola’s grand design.

Tito Vilanova was addressing the world’s media in Camp Nou. For Barcelona in 2012, read Manchester in 2026. Enzo Maresca would be wise to study the manual for standing on the shoulder of a giant.

Charged with maintaining the standards of a two-time Champions League-winning team many still consider the best ever, Vilanova’s big idea was effectively to leave the existing one alone, mastering the art of the subtle tweak.

Describing the transition as seamless barely does justice to the changeover; Vilanova’s Barcelona side won 23 of their first 25 La Liga matches, snatching back the title lost to Real Madrid in Guardiola’s farewell campaign. Tragically, the promise of further honours to build on sturdy foundations was realised by others as Vilanova died from cancer in 2014.

After a brief stint by Gerardo Martino, Luis Enrique gave the dream team of Lionel Messi, Andrés Iniesta and Sergio Busquets further La Liga titles and another Champions League win.

The so-called “impossible job” of being bound by the weight of the preceding genius, therefore, was no such thing.

Welcome to live coverage of Sunday afternoon’s 2pm Premier League kick-offs. Enzo Maresca faces the unenviable task of following Pep Guardiola’s decade of dominance at Manchester City and his reign begins in earnest with a visit from Bournemouth, who themselves are ushering in a new era after Marco Rose succeeded Andoni Iraola. A bruising 3-0 defeat in the Community Shield last weekend does not bode well for City’s hopes of closing the gap to Arsenal, but Maresca will be telling his charges that this afternoon is a fresh start: a new week and a new competition, plus Erling Haaland has a new haircut…

The Italian’s squad has been shorn of World Cup winner Rodri, who departed for Barcelona this week, and the spotlight will turn to £116m club-record signing Elliot Anderson who is expected to make his Premier League debut with City. Jérémy Doku is out for two to three weeks after suffering a calf injury, which could open the door for Jack Grealish, with City bereft of wide options. City will have their work cut out as Bournemouth, fresh off a historic sixth-place finish last season, look to extend their 18-match unbeaten run from the end of last season. Fabian Hurzeler’s side face the defending Europa League champions without Carlos Baleba, who is on the brink of joining Manchester United, as well as injured wingers Kaoru Mitoma and Yankuba Minteh. While Unai Emery has lost key players in Morgan Rogers, Youri Tielemans, Ezri Konsa and Lucas Digne, the funds raised has gone towards seven signings, with Zion Suzuki, the Japan goalkeeper, in line to make his first start for Villa.
