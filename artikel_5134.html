Nvidia stock tokens show price spreads, but slippage crushes the arbitrage

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Two $1,000 comparisons leave about 1 basis point and negative 15 before fees, while access and share adjustments complicate the trade.

Tokens tracking Nvidia shares can trade at different prices across issuers and exchanges, but buying the cheaper version and selling a dearer one leaves little room for profit in two modeled comparisons published by data provider Coin Metrics on Nov. 3.

The data provider's execution study shows about one basis point remaining in one $1,000 comparison after entry and exit slippage, and a negative 15 basis points in another.

The results show how a visible price difference can shrink when an order reaches the market, while preserving a small positive remainder in the first example.

A holder must compare the same share exposure, have something to sell on the expensive side, and qualify for the route used to settle the trade.

Coin Metrics' spread and slippage chart compares buying Hyperliquid NVDAx with selling Binance NVDAb, printing a gross gap of 10 basis points, entry and exit slippage of 10 basis points, and a one-basis-point remainder before trading fees.

Tokenized-equity framework xStocks announced NVDAx/USDC spot trading on HyperCore on Aug. 10, separately from equity perpetuals. Moving those spot tokens to or from HyperEVM involves wrapping and bridging.

The chart's venue labels alone do not establish that transfer route for every observation in its June-November sample.

A second dataset shows why even a clean-looking price comparison needs adjustment. In a Nov. 1 correction, Bitquery normalized its Nvidia tokens per underlying share and adjusted dividend treatment.

Across its Sept. 15-Oct. 13 public-pool sample, pairwise median daily gaps among the four busiest versions rounded to no more than 0.12%. The widest pair was 0.1225%, rounded to 0.12%.

Kraken's multiplier explanation shows that dividend reinvestment can increase the share exposure displayed in an xStock balance while the number of on-chain tokens stays unchanged. For wrapped xStocks, the issuer separately says token prices must be adjusted using its multiplier.

Bitquery says their Nvidia tokens have separate contracts without a direct bridge or issuer swap between them, so buying the cheaper product does not supply the different token needed for the sale.

Kraken's Oct. 30 FAQ lists a 10-basis-point taker fee and a two-basis-point maker rebate for its Pro spot order book. App purchases using US dollars or USDG have no trading fee but may include a spread, while xStock conversions carry a 1% fee.

The issuer's primary-market documentation requires onboarding, identity and anti-money-laundering checks, and whitelisted wallets. Its named cash Market Flow lists a $5,000 minimum, 24/5 availability, and fees currently set to zero.

Kraken also excludes US persons and customers in the US, UK, Canada, and Australia from xStocks access. Compatible-wallet withdrawals are supported, but transfers into a traditional brokerage account are not. The issuer classifies xStocks as tracker certificates providing economic exposure, without direct share ownership or voting rights.

The usable margin depends on executable prices for matching exposure, both legs' costs, and the ability to deliver or sell the relevant instrument.
