Hyatt Adds Stackable Awards, But Globalists Lose Free Parking Benefit

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

World of Hyatt is adding new Milestone Rewards and allowing members to combine awards starting in 2027. There is plenty to like, particularly the ability to apply a suite upgrade to a free night certificate. But Globalists will lose automatic free parking on award stays, and a uniform one-year expiration will make some awards harder to use.

Hyatt has announced changes beginning January 1, 2027, that will give members more ways to earn and redeem rewards. But there are concessions here too, especially for frequent guests who already make good use of the existing benefits.

Globalists currently receive complimentary parking on eligible award nights at participating hotels where parking can be charged to the room. That can be a substantial benefit, particularly at city hotels and resorts with expensive parking.

Hyatt is replacing that status benefit with a selectable Free Parking Award, first available at 60 qualifying nights or 100,000 Base Points. Each award will cover one qualifying stay of up to seven nights and can be gifted. The improvement is that the award will work on eligible paid stays too. The drawback is that members must select it instead of another reward.

“This decision was made as 70% of Globalists did not use the free parking benefit in 2025; 95% used it 4 times or less, and moving this to a choice allowed the program to retain it for members who value it and introduce other exciting new awards like the $200 Thompson Hotels Credit and the $500 Premium Suite Credit.”

After that, Globalist status alone will not provide free parking.

I appreciate the advance notice, but this is a devaluation for members who regularly drive to Hyatt properties and redeem points, a benefit I used regularly when I was still Globalist and still use on Guest of Honor awards today.

The ability to stack eligible awards is the most appealing part of this announcement and is a very positive change. Starting in 2027, members can combine a Suite Upgrade Award with a free night certificate, including Category 1–4, Category 1–7, Ultimate Free Night, and other eligible promotional certificates.

That addresses a frustrating restriction and having both a free night and a suite upgrade in your account should make it easier to plan a special stay. Other eligible combinations will include Suite Upgrade Awards, Guest of Honor Awards, and Free Parking Awards on the same reservation.

For someone without Globalist status, combining a confirmed suite with Guest of Honor benefits could make for a very nice stay. For a Globalist, being able to confirm a suite while using a free night certificate is also very useful.

There are still restrictions. For example, the new Thompson Hotels and Premium Suite credits cannot be combined with a Free Night Award (I address the new milestone awards below). Hyatt has published the details on its 2027 updates page.

Awards issued beginning January 1, 2027, will expire 365 days after issuance.

That makes the rules easier to remember, but whether it helps depends on the award.

Some milestone free night certificates currently expire after 180 days. Doubling that window is welcome…six months can pass very quickly when you are trying to find the right opportunity to use a certificate.

But Suite Upgrade Awards, Club Access Awards, and Guest of Honor Awards currently can remain valid considerably longer, through the end of February of the second calendar year after they are earned. Reducing those to one year is a loss. Even today as I look to book stays in January and February of next year, I’ll be using those “older” Guest of Honor Awards.

That said, I would not call this change unreasonable and for most people it will be a net positive.

Milestone Rewards will begin at 10 qualifying nights or 20,000 Base Points, with additional milestones every 10 nights through 150.

Here are some notable new choices (which are in addition to the existing choices):

The $500 Premium Suite Credit awarded at 100 nights requires an eligible paid stay of at least two nights. I would evaluate that against what I would otherwise spend…a discount on an expensive suite is only useful if you would not otherwise get the upgrade anyway as a Globalist.

At 50 nights, the suite upgrade choice drops from two awards to one.

At 60 nights, My Hyatt Concierge becomes a selection rather than an automatic benefit (a worthless benefit, in my opinion). Members will choose two rewards from a menu that also includes suite upgrades, parking, points, and a skimpy Delta eCertificate.

If you value your concierge and want to retain the suite upgrades, that leaves no selection for parking…

The new Delta partnership adds eCertificates at milestones from 20–60 nights, two single-visit Sky Club passes at 70, 80, and 90 nights, and Medallion status options at higher thresholds. Of course the Delta Air Lines awards replace the American Airlines awards.

Members will need to link their Hyatt and Delta accounts. Hyatt is also enhancing “Pay My Way” so members can choose awards, points, cash, or Points + Cash for individual nights within a stay.

Hyatt’s technology has long lagged, but this is a nice new update.

The ability to combine free night certificates with suite upgrades is an excellent change. Earlier milestones and additional reward choices should also give members more opportunities to find something useful.

But the parking change is a loss for Globalists who regularly use that benefit. Shorter validity for some awards, fewer suite upgrades at 50 nights, and making concierge access a choice deserve attention too, as much I always loathed my own concierge agents.

I appreciate that Hyatt is providing notice and protecting existing parking benefits during the transition. All in all, I’d say these changes are positive, but will hurt some travelers a lot more than others.

Going to respectfully disagree here on the parking. And no, I am not some defensive fan boy.

As a many year Lifetime Globalist I have taken advantage of the free parking perhaps twice total on redemptions. I would say most are like me and that Hyatt is correct on those numbers they cited. The reality is that aspirational properties, the ones a Globalist would likely be wanting to use points at (I hope!) are rarely going to result in the need for parking. Maldives, Paris, London, New York, Milan, Tokyo, Any SE Asia property are all examples of Park Hyatts (which comprise the most percentage of aspirational hotels) and for which I imagine Hyatt’s number is conservative – betting parking comprises less than 5% of total check-ins on points. In fact, the only Park Hyatt I have ever redeemed points and used parking was in Sydney. And I have stayed at most all of them across the world. Properties like in Chicago are usually priced such as to not justify using points anyway.

I am actually much happier with the 7 day free parking milestone which I can use somewhere like in Chicago where I never redeem points.

Hyatt’s concierge service for globalists is generally useless.

So while having to choose concierge service over other benefits is a devaluation of globalist, it’s, practically speaking, not a huge loss for most members.

The loss of free parking on award-redemption stays is somewhat similar. On paper, it’s a significant devaluation. However, how many guests actually use it? If I’m going to a resort, I’m not arriving with a car. Same for if I’m redeeming points to stay at the Park Hyatt in Paris or wherever. So, practically speaking, it may not be a huge loss for many elites.

What I don’t understand is why wouldn’t you come up with better offerings for globalists who do 100, 120 or 150+ nights?

I wish they would have created a benefit to compensate for the growing number of Hyatt Regency and Grand Hyatt properties WITHOUT an open or operational club lounge.
