Airbnb’s LA election spending is framed around the 2028 Olympics. A bigger bet could be on the rules after the Games.

Few companies have spent more on Los Angeles’ elections this year than Airbnb, which has put millions behind Mayor Karen Bass and other local candidates.

Airbnb’s spending touches several fights that matter to its business in the city, including a proposed temporary easing of short-term rental restrictions during the 2028 Olympics, which are expected to draw in roughly 2 million visitors to the greater Los Angeles area. As much as $4.3 billion in direct visitor spending is expected to benefit hotels, restaurants, and retailers, according to a study from the Los Angeles County Economic Development Corporation commissioned by LA28.

Airbnb is looking for a bigger slice of that spending.

Since 2018, only primary residences have been allowed to list on platforms like Airbnb. Proposals for a vacation rental expansion would ease restrictions through the end of 2028.

Airbnb has spent more than $12 million on Los Angeles politics since 2025, backing Mayor Karen Bass, city attorney candidate John McKinney, and two City Council candidates, while pushing a Vacation Rental Ordinance that would temporarily let second homes be listed through the end of 2028. Los Angeles has limited short-term rentals to primary residences since 2018, and the 2028 Olympics — expected to draw roughly 2 million visitors and $4.3 billion in direct visitor spending, according to a study commissioned by LA28 — is the opening Airbnb is using to argue the city needs more lodging. The City Council struck the vacation rental program from the budget it adopted in May, and the planning department's August framework would sunset eased rules in December 2028, limit permits to individual owners and trusts, and cap them at about 15,000 units — a cap planning says the city has too few second homes to reach. City finance staff estimated the program might generate $25 million to $30 million in tourist tax, well below the up to $110 million cited by Airbnb's coalition. Los Angeles hoteliers, including the general manager of the Hoxton Downtown LA, told the Council the city's 113,750 county hotel rooms are already enough for the Olympics, World Cup, and Super Bowl.
