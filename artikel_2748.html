The Good, Bad & Ugly from the Miami Dolphins Week 10 loss to the Indianapolis Colts

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The Miami Dolphins were extremely shorthanded heading into their Week 10 road-trip against the Indianapolis Colts. Starting quarterback Malik Willis was placed on injured reserve earlier in the week with a lingering groin issue. Stud center Aaron Brewer missed Sunday's tilt due a concussion he suffered in last week's loss to the Detroit Lions. Defensive leader Zach Sieler tore his biceps in that same loss — forcing him to not only miss Miami's game against Indianapolis, but the rest of the 2026 season as well.

Well, as we all know, adversity is opportunity.

The Dolphins — while starting rookie quarterback Kyle McCord — nearly overcame all of that adversity to upset their foes on Sunday. Instead, despite a spirited effort from Jeff Hafley's squad, Miami lost by two-points against Indianapolis, dropping their record to 1-8 on the year after falling by a score of 24-22.

In a season where finding bright spots has sometimes felt like searching for a needle in a haystack, seeing two young players make game-changing defensive plays for Miami on Sunday was encouraging.

Rookie safety Michael Taaffe and rookie cornerback Chris Johnson each came away with an interception of Colts' quarterback Daniel Jones.

Taaffe got things started with an interception with 10:30 left in the second quarter — giving Miami's defense a much-needed takeaway while the Colts were driving for points. It was Taaffe's third interception on the season. Not bad for a 5th round pick!

But it was Johnson who provided the highlight of the afternoon.

Miami's 1st round rookie cornerback intercepted Jones with 8:52 left in the 3rd-quarter and returned the ball 59 yards for a dazzling touchdown — giving Miami a big lift and injecting some life into a game that was quickly getting away from the South Florida squad.

Obviously, one game doesn't make a career, but Taaffe and Johnson have been playing solid football for the Dolphins all season long. There hasn't been a whole lot to celebrate in Miami this year, but seeing two rookies stepping up when their team needs them is part of the fuel that keeps the fires of hope burning as Dolphins fans look towards a brighter future.

Now, if only the rest of the defense could figure out how to stop somebody…

On Sunday, Indianapolis ran the football 34 times for 172 yards, averaging a whopping 5.1 yards per carry.

The Colts didn't need to get particularly creative. They didn't need to rely on some elaborate offensive scheme or trick plays. They lined up, handed the football to their running backs Jonathan Taylor and Seth McGowan, and repeatedly found themselves moving the chains.

The Indianapolis rushing attack consistently found room to operate, putting Miami's defense on their heels and making life difficult for the Dolphins throughout the afternoon, despite the fact that Miami's defense only gave up 104 yards passing.

When an opposing offense can consistently pick up five yards every time it hands the ball off, you're eventually going to run out of opportunities to get off the field.

The Dolphins have plenty of things to figure out defensively, but stopping the run needs to be near the top of that list — especially when a date with Josh Allen, James Cook and the Buffalo Bills is circled on the calendar for next Sunday.

Zane Gonzalez misses two field goals and an extra-point try in a two-point loss

When the Dolphins gave kicker Riley Patterson his walking papers on October 20th so they could sign Zane Gonzalez to take over kicking duties for the team, fans were encouraged by the move.

Fast forward four games and that warm and fuzzy feeling has vanished in a hurry.

On Sunday, Gonzalez missed two field goals and an extra point try. That's 7 points Gonzalez was unable to put on the board for a Dolphins team desperate for them.

Reminder: Miami lost Sunday's game by two points.

Gonzalez's first missed opportunity came on a 47-yard attempt in the first quarter. Later, after Miami had clawed their way back into the game, he missed the extra point try following Chris Johnson's spectacular interception return for a touchdown.

However, Miami's kicker saved his most heart-breaking moment for the fourth quarter when he missed a long try that would have given the Dolphins the lead in a tight, back-and-forth contest.

As I gaze into my crystal ball, I predict the Dolphins to look elsewhere for a place kicker in very short order.

The Dolphins, with their backs against the wall, played a close game against the Colts on Sunday. Alas, they fell short — dropping the team's record to 1-8 on the year.
