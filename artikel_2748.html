Scouting Report: Georgia Tech Yellow Jackets

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Losses: Colorado, Tennessee, Stanford, VT, BC, Pitt, Louisville

Recruiting Rank: 36 (4 4-stars, 20 3-stars)

Transfer Portal Rank: 35 (3 4-stars, 16 3-stars)

The Deacs are on the road for the final time in the regular season to take on Georgia Tech, a team Wake has not beaten since the 2006 ACC Championship game. The Yellow Jackets currently hold a 5 game win streak against the Deacs and are 1-6 against Wake Forest at home in the last 30 years. The stakes in this one are pretty big for Wake, as the Deacs will likely make their 3rd ACC Championship Game appearance with a win (there is 1 convoluted scenario with a 3-way tie that requires Miami to lose twice to VT and BC and Wake to lose 1 of their last 2 where they could potentially lose a tiebreak, so they don't technically clinch with a win).

Georgia Tech's offense has taken a pretty significant step backwards from last season, producing just 23.5 points and 365 yards per game. That is not completely unexpected, considering the Yellow Jackets lost the 2025 ACC Player of the Year Haynes King in the offseason. King's replacement, Indiana transfer Alberto Mendoza, just hasn't been able to replicate how much a player like King carried the offense over the past couple of seasons.

While Mendoza is completing 68% of his passes this season, he has thrown just 11 touchdowns to 7 interceptions so far. Where GT is really missing a guy like Haynes King is in the rushing attack. Last season King led GT with 953 rushing yards and 15 rushing touchdowns—in 10 games this season, Mendoza has run the 48 times for a net of -52 yards and not a single rushing touchdown. His inability to generate anything with his legs led Brent Key to start splitting reps between Mendoza and Grady Adamson, who is a much more mobile QB, a few games ago.

Despite the two QB system almost never working out, it was good enough to beat Clemson 20-6 last weekend. Adamson ran the ball 19 times for 87 yards and a touchdown—he only attempted 1 pass. Mendoza was 8-14 for 136 yards and an interception—he ran the ball 2 times for 1 yard. It seems pretty clear that the Jackets have a designated running QB and designated passing QB, almost as if they are trying to recreate Haynes King in the aggregate Moneyball style. It might seem impressive that they were able to beat Clemson this way, but keep in mind that the Tigers are just 2-9 in their last 11 games at home against p4 opponents.

The running game may have benefitted slightly from Adamson getting more playing time, but it has come at the expense of the passing game. In their last 3 games, GT is completing just 53% of their passes for 173 yards per game—their only 2 passing touchdowns over that span came in a 44-17 blowout loss against Louisville—after the Jackets had already fallen behind 27-3.

The biggest threat on the Georgia Tech offense is running back Justice Haynes (no relation to last year's Jamal Haynes). Haynes played 2 seasons at Alabama and led the Big10 with 7.2 yards per carry at Michigan last season before transferring to Georgia Tech. In 2025, King ran for over 100 yards in 6 of the 7 games he played in and had 4 runs of over 50 yards. Haynes currently leads the Jackets with 137 carries for 751 yards (5.5 ypc) and 5 rushing touchdowns. He accelerates quickly and has break away speed, so if he ever gets out into the open field, he is a big time playmaker. Explosive plays in the run game is one of the few things that the Georgia Tech offense has done well this season (image from Game On Paper).

Last week, Haynes ran for 116 of Georgia Tech's 216 rushing yards against the Tigers. Slowing down that rushing attack should be a priority for the Deacs, because when the Jackets rush for over 150 yards, they are 3-1 this season—when they are held under 150 rushing yards, they are 0-6.

This should be a very favorable matchup for the Wake Forest defense—the Yellow Jackets haven't been able to win without running the ball, and the Deacs haven't given up more than 75 rushing yards in an FBS game since September. John Madden used to say "if you have 2 quarterbacks, you actually have none," and I just don't think putting in 2 very different quarterbacks over the course of the game is something that is going to work on this Wake Forest defense.

The Georgia Tech defense really hasn't been that good this season either—the Yellow Jackets have given up over 400 yards of offense in 7 of their 10 games and 35 or more points in 6 of their 10 games. While they did hold Clemson to just 6 points on Saturday, the Tigers have one of the worst offenses in the country (115th) and haven't scored a touchdown in 2 weeks.

The biggest problem with the GT defense is that they have not stopped the run at all this season—in their 10 games so far, the Yellow Jackets have only held 2 teams below 150 yards rushing (Clemson, Syracuse). Even FCS Mercer ran for 188 yards and over 4 yards per carry on the Jackets. Even after holding Clemson to just 86 yards on the ground last week, GT is still allowing 254 rushing yards and nearly 3 touchdowns on the ground per game over their last 4 games. In a 27-49 loss to Boston College a few weeks ago, the Yellow Jackets allowed the Eagles to run for 399 yards and 5 touchdowns—BC entered the game averaging 149 rushing yards and 20 points per game. After the Jackets gave up 44 points and 251 yards on the ground to Louisville 2 weeks ago, Brent Key fired his defensive coordinator and OLB coach.

The secondary has been better, allowing 190 yards per game through the air—of course, that could just be a case of teams not throwing the ball because they can just run the ball on every play and still score. Still, Georgia Tech has the 7th best defensive success rate against the pass in the country so far this season—it's more or less the only thing the Yellow Jackets are doing well on defense (image from Game On Paper).

Some guys to watch out for in the secondary are safety Tae Harris, who leads the team with 63 tackles and is among the top graded underclassmen in the country this season at safety, Jonas Duclona, who leads the team with 5 pass breakups, and Daiquan White, who has 4 pass break ups.

There is a reason that Wake Forest is favored in this game by over 20 points—the Georgia Tech defense really doesn't keep teams out of the endzone, they can't stop the run, the don't get a lot of pressure, they don't force turnovers, and they aren't particularly good on 3rd down. As long as the Deacs continue to play the way they have played for the past couple of months, this game should not be close at all. Wake Forest is just better in every facet of the game.
