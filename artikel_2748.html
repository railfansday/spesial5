Did Commanders lose the Rams game when they released starting center Tyler Biadasz in February? - Daily Slop

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Prior to the start of the season, not many people would have expected the Commanders to beat a Rams team consisting of Matthew Stafford, Puka Nacua, Aaron Donald and Myles Garrett. But the Rams were 4-4 entering the game and it was a game very much there for the taking. The Commanders defense held up relatively well, but the offense struggled to get much going after a strong start.

One of the biggest issues the Commanders had on offense was the protection plan. The Rams had star pass rusher Myles Garrett back healthy after he missed most of the season injured. It was the first time the Rams had Garrett and star defensive tackle Aaron Donald on the field together. It's a formidable pair. Donald may well go down in NFL history as the best interior defensive lineman ever and Garrett might well become the best edge rusher in NFL history. Both are freak athletes and are incredibly hard for even the best offensive lineman to block one-on-one.

With such a strong pair of defensive lineman, it would make sense to build an entire game plan around preventing those two players from blowing up the entire offense. The last time the Commanders faced Garrett was in 2024, Jayden Daniels' rookie season. Kliff Kingsbury was the offensive coordinator then and he put together a strong game plan to try and negate Garrett's effectiveness. They used run plays, read-option plays, run-pass option plays (RPOs), multiple forms of misdirection, screens. Kingsbury also made sure to play with lots of tempo to tire out Garrett and used no-huddle to attack when Garrett wasn't on the field and stop him from subbing back in.

Most importantly, Kingsbury made sure to get a second pair of hands on him on almost every play. They would slide the line his way when they could, but they would always chip him. Everyone had a go at chipping him, receivers, tight ends and running backs. He never knew exactly who was going to chip him on any given play because of how often they switched it up. It was a terrific plan and Garrett failed to register a single stat in the game, which is a large part of why the Commanders were able to win that game back in 2024.

Now, this game in 2026 is a different animal because the Rams also have Aaron Donald to worry about. But offensive coordinator David Blough was on Kingsbury's staff back in 2024 and I had hoped he might have learned some lessons from Kingsbury's plan to help deal with Garrett. Unfortunately, the protection plan was poor and nothing like that 2024 plan.

The two most basic weapons you have in pass protection are chips and line slides. Chips are where you have a tight end, running back or occasionally a wide receiver align tight to the formation and bump the edge rusher before releasing into their routes. Line slides are when either all of the line, or at least part of it, slides to one side, providing extra help to that side. Far too often in this game, the Commanders didn't use either of these weapons.

Lucas Patrick's concussion sent rookie Matt Gulbin in against Myles Garrett while Nick Allegretti stayed on the sideline

[Washington's] latest issue on the offensive line stems from the center position: this past weekend, when their starter Lucas Patrick went down with a concussion, they turned to rookie Matt Gulbin. It's ironic because Adam Peters and Dan Quinn spent the offseason trying to convince everyone that Nick Allegretti was that guy and would be the center for 2026. Allegretti has played two snaps since his last start, which came at right guard against the Seattle Seahawks in Week 3. In other words, he's still the same journeyman offensive lineman who came here years ago looking for a chance to start, but better suited as a backup. They tried to sell the fanbase a pumpkin, hoping Cinderella logic would kick in and he'd turn into something more than a dried-up gourd that rots on a warm fall night.

When asked about the situation Sunday after the game, Quinn said the staff has been working Allegretti more at guard now: "In that moment, I would have felt comfortable with Nick as well, but I thought, let's give the guy who's got the most reps." Quinn also confirmed Allegretti was healthy. It makes sense that the coach would turn to the player who has practiced the most in the role; the problem is he said this in the preseason: "Nick is one of our smartest players, so that's a position that you rely on." Relied on so much that when the team looks for a replacement weeks later in crunch time with the season on the line, they turn to the sixth-round prospect with very little experience. Gulbin gave up a 12-yard sack to Myles Garrett on third-and-5 at the Rams' 38, leaving Washington facing fourth-and-17, but who could be mad at a rookie who should have been given more help on that play? The Commanders' protection plan failed when it mattered most Sunday.

Look, the Commanders didn't lose to the Rams 20-13 solely because Patrick got dinged up and Gulbin had to replace him, tasked with trying to help block Myles Garrett and Aaron Donald when he hadn't played in more than a month and had essentially just one game of experience in his NFL career.

"It's an excuse," Gulbin said afterward. "I got to go out and do the expectations for the center position. I got to go out there and block them. And I don't think I did a great job."

Credit for self-awareness. The situation is not of Gulbin's making, and he can only perform the job in adverse circumstances to the best of his ability.

But we're focusing on Gulbin's participation and Patrick's injury and Allegretti's absence because — well, in some way, the center position drove the talk around Washington's entire offseason. And here it is, November now, with the Commanders' season on the brink at 2-5-1, and it's still dogging them as they head into a short week with a season's-over-if-you-lose game at the New York Giants on Thursday night.

This all sounds familiar, and some of it is definitely a rehash of old material. But it matters. It matters because, with the Commanders trailing by a touchdown and facing third-and-5 late in the fourth quarter, Garrett — playing for the first time in nearly two months — came from his position on the edge to rush up the middle.

The issue isn't that a rookie taken in the sixth round came in as an emergency replacement and couldn't block Myles Garrett. The issue is how we got here. This will be playin' the hits, to a certain extent. But it's worth going over for the umpteenth time.

The pared-down version: The Commanders cut Tyler Biadasz. They pursued Tyler Linderbaum. They lost out to the Las Vegas Raiders — understandably — because who wants to spend $81 million on a center?

Then they leaned in — hard — on the idea Allegretti could be their center. The week before the Commanders opened their season in Philadelphia, general manager Adam Peters sat for a Q&A session with radio hosts Grant Paulsen and Danny Rouhier of 106.7 The Fan in front of a live audience. They joked about how much of the offseason had been spent discussing the center position, particularly after Allegretti missed most of training camp with a calf strain.

Allegretti started in the opener at the Philadelphia Eagles. Two days later, the Commanders signed Patrick — cut Aug. 30 by the Giants — as a free agent. In Week 3, Patrick started at guard, alongside Allegretti at center. By the Week 4 game in London, Patrick took over at center.

The Commanders have been trying to put together a complete game all season. Nine weeks later, they are still searching for such a game.

Washington got out to a good enough start in the first half. It was not perfect — they still had pre-snap penalties and a turnover — but it was enough to outperform a talented Rams team that was sluggish to start the afternoon. Jayden Daniels was 6-of-7 during the offense's first two drives and threw a seven-yard touchdown to Van Jefferson.

The Rams found little success outside of the field goal on their opening drive with back-to-back three-and-outs in the second quarter. The Rams ended the half with another field goal, but it remained a four-point lead in Washington's favor.

The second half, however, was a different story. The Rams didn't do much more, but the two touchdowns they scored at the top of the third and fourth quarters were enough to give them a lead that Washington could not overcome. The Commanders' offense did little in the third quarter with just 20 yards on 10 plays. There was more production in the final 15 minutes, but the results were much the same: a punt, a turnover on downs and a fumble.

Washington finished the day with 313 yards, 157 of which came on three of their 11 possessions. The Rams were only marginally better, but they generated six more first downs and converted all three of their fourth-down attempts. As head coach Dan Quinn alluded after the game, the Commanders must keep searching for an answer until they find one.

There was an eminently winnable game at Northwest Stadium Sunday, against a good but hardly great Los Angeles Rams team, and a chance to stay, sort of, in the NFC East race. The Commanders scored on their first two possessions, with a lot of game left to add more, especially after the defense took the ball away from Los Angeles twice. Instead, almost 45 minutes of futility, plus Puka Nacua, followed, as did a 20-13 loss. It's not the end of the season, but the end is a short Uber ride from 2-5-1. (Hey, there's still a 6 percent chance. Stop whining.)

"Once again, we have not nailed our complementary football, and I will keep saying that until we do," coach Dan Quinn said. "We're halfway through the season, and that all has to click."

Quinn has said some version of this after just about every game so far this season. Yet, other than center Nick Allegretti (more on him below) and corner Mike Sainristil, almost no one's been replaced in the rotation after subpar play.

Daniels and the offense were shorthanded Sunday, playing without three of their top receivers. Terry McLaurin's lost for the season, of course, but Washington lost Treylon Burks and Dyami Brown to injuries this week, too. There wasn't a lot of YAC left in the receiver room.

With left tackle Laremy Tunsil out since training camp, Washington turned to third-year lineman Brandon Coleman to replace him. Coleman played well there at times as a rookie in 2024. But he's has struggled badly this season with false starts. He had two more Sunday. The Commanders keep rolling him out there.

The penalties aren't all on Coleman. A new offense has new cadences pre-snap. Sometimes it's hard to hear exactly what the quarterback is saying out there. Washington used its bye week, Quinn said, to try cutting some of those cadences down and make it easier for the O-linemen to communicate with one another. But Sunday, the line was again jumbled.

Remember: The Commanders cut Tyler Biadasz, their starting center last season, last winter. They never really have said why, but it doesn't matter now.

The line struggled, mightily, in the second half against the soon-to-be Hall of Fame duo of Aaron Donald and Myles Garrett, the latter returning to action Sunday after being on injured reserve for most of the season. In the second quarter, Washington's defense turned the game's momentum when K'Lavon Chaisson stripped Nacua after a catch, and safety Nick Cross recovered the fumble with 2:34 left. The Commanders had first-and-10 at the Rams' 40; up 10-3, Washington was set up to increase its lead to two scores just before the half.

The definition of "must-win" changes throughout the season. Some would argue—probably most rightfully—that a "must-win" game is only ever a game where your season is legitimately on the line. I have sliced that deli meat a little finer, instead arguing that through the middle of the season, teams have different possible season outcomes available to them.

For example, for the Commanders to have meaningfully expected to be part of the wild-card chase, that must-win game has almost certainly passed us by (and we lost it). The weak NFC East, however, has kept the Commanders in the playoff picture, offering the more traditional route to a playoff spot as division winners.

With the New York Giants and Dallas Cowboys winning yesterday, I think it's safe to say that Thursday Night Football is a must-win game for the Commanders. A loss to the Giants would give us six losses, with at least a few opponents still on the schedule against whom we will be significant underdogs.

Coaches and players understandably resist labeling games as must-win affairs, especially when elimination isn't technically on the line. But Washington has reached the point where such distinctions are becoming meaningless.

"Yeah, I think next week is a must-win," quarterback Jayden Daniels said following Sunday's 20-13 loss.

"Damn right," edge rusher K'Lavon Chaisson said postgame when asked if there was concern over the season slipping away. "Obviously, you want to stay competitive, stay in that race, to play football throughout the year. We never want to neglect that or seem like that's not the goal. So, it's something that we want to clean up with urgency.

"That's our word throughout the year. We have to clean up the self-inflicted wounds."

Having lost consecutive games after a 6-0 start, will they use the Tuesday afternoon NFL trade deadline to add a quarterback? Or will they try to make it work for the rest of this season with a combination of Kyler Murray, Carson Wentz and Max Brosmer?

Coach Kevin O'Connell deferred the question to general manager Nolan Teasley after Monday night's 23-6 loss to the Buffalo Bills. It was the third game this season that the Vikings failed to score a touchdown, a level of production that has threatened to waste high-level play from their defense and special teams.

Wentz completed only 8 of 25 passes for 81 yards while throwing two interceptions and having a third dropped. His 2.3 QBR was the lowest for any quarterback this season and the lowest by a Vikings quarterback since Tarvaris Jackson recorded a 1.8 QBR during a game in 2006. It was also the lowest in a "Monday Night Football" game since Sam Darnold posted a 1.1 QBR in 2019 while playing for the New York Jets against the New England Patriots, a game where he was recorded saying he was "seeing ghosts."

"I haven't had many starts over the last couple years in general," Wentz said, "but this one felt pretty terrible. I'm not going to lie. Yeah, this one wasn't my favorite, that's for sure."

Wentz' disaster class wastes Flores' masterclass. Kyler Murray's hamstring injury opened up the possibility that Carson Wentz might step up in prime time and prove himself a more effective quarterback for Minnesota's offense, which had struggled to find much traction under the two-time Pro Bowler. He did the exact opposite of that, ridding Vikings fans of any notion that there was a QB on the bench who represented a better option.

Facing the 30th-ranked defense against the pass, Wentz delivered a miserable performance. He missed pass catchers short, high and wide. He threw one interception deep to T.J. Hockenson where only Bills linebacker Dorian Williams could catch it, and another one directly to LB Terrel Bernard while trying to hit Justin Jefferson. He went 3 of 8 on short throws (0-9 yards air yards) and 3 of 13 on anything deeper than that, finishing 8 of 25 overall for 81 yards and an 8.9 passer rating — the lowest by any QB to attempt at least 25 passes in a game since Sam Darnold's 3.6 passer rating during his 2019 "seeing ghosts" outing, per NFL Research.

Despite all that, Minnesota's tremendous defense kept it to a one-possession game until 6:17 remained. A two-play first-quarter sequence perhaps most accurately represents how brutally Wentz offset his team's defensive efforts. Byron Murphy made a stellar interception, playing center field on a Josh Allen end zone prayer, to kill Buffalo's second drive. He even returned it to midfield to put Wentz and Co. on the doorstep of field goal range. Instead, on the very next snap, Wentz carelessly whipped a wide receiver screen to Jefferson that went both backward and too high. Jefferson couldn't corral it and the Bills recovered the fumble to get the ball right back.

That set the tone for a most disappointing day. The QB position has been a massive problem for the Vikings in 2026. They overcame it in their first six games, but an inept offense has now cost them two straight. They must fix it, either in house or before the trade deadline, before it's too late.
