Court Bars BYD Ti7 From Sale In Europe For Looking Way Too Much Like A Land Rover Defender

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

China's BYD is now officially barred from selling its Ti7 SUV in Europe because a Dutch district court in The Hague ruled that it looked far too much like the Land Rover Defender. While it's certainly not a one-to-one copy, I can certainly see why the folks over at Jaguar-Land Rover's legal department would be a bit miffed.

Although it has been on sale in China since last year, JLR launched the case earlier this year after BYD announced on its UK website that the seven-seater would be available to order, with customer deliveries starting in January of 2027, according to Reuters. Now, there's an injunction against its sale in the EU. The one caveat of all of this is that this court in the Netherlands has no jurisdiction over BYD's affiliate in the UK.

The injunction against BYD will take effect a week after the ruling since the automaker "currently has no concrete plans to place the Ti7 on the market in the EU," according to Reuters. Until the whole intellectual property case is resolved, the court is banning BYD from producing the — admittedly good-looking — vehicle at its new factory in Hungary. It's due to spin up mass production of other vehicles next year, but the Ti7 will not be one of them unless something drastic changes very quickly.

"In these summary proceedings, the preliminary relief judge rules that ​the Ti7 infringes the EU design of Jaguar Land Rover," Reuters reports the court ruling said. "The characteristic ⁠and distinctive elements of the design are reflected in the BYD Ti7."

Some of the more notable visual similarities are the boxy, flat-roof silhouette, its upright proportions, its side-hinged tailgate and squared-off wheel arches. The real kicker has to be the solid body-colored panel behind the rear passenger door, which is controversial on the Defender, yet BYD designers decided to include it anyway.

It's actually fairly rare in Europe for a large automaker to go to court over design rights infringement. It's also uncommon for a ruling to come down so early in proceedings for a case that is ongoing and likely to last some time. JLR, of course, is pleased with the outcome, telling Reuters, "We protect our intellectual property and take legal action when necessary."

Still, this isn't exactly the first  time something like this has happened, as Reuters explains:

Legacy automakers ​have often complained that ⁠Chinese automakers have copied designs, though little has been proven in court. In 2005, for instance, General Motors and Chinese rival Chery settled a case over the Chery QQ ​that the No. 1 US automaker said was a copy of its Chevrolet Spark.

BYD, for its part, "strongly disagrees" with The Hauge's ruling and is planning to appeal, telling the Wall Street Journal that the Ti7 is "the result of BYD's own independent design work and infringes none of JLR's rights." It added that it will "pursue every legal avenue to demonstrate that this decision ⁠is incorrect."
