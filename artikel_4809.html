7 Best Moroccan Rugs (2026)

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

The Moroccan rug is one of the easiest ways to add texture and intrigue to any room. Originally made to protect floors against the harsh cold of the Atlas Mountains, we see these lush, woolly carpets as a staple in the modern design world because of the range of color and style options available. The rug type has become synonymous with traditional undyed cream colors, vibrant jewel tones, and geometric patterns. It’s no wonder they pop up across tons of our home tours—like this artful Brooklyn studio and floral Victorian house—and have become a favorite among artists like Claudio Bravo and celebs like Patrick Dempsey.

Boucherouite, the hand-weaving technique employed to make these rugs, was created by semi-nomadic Amazigh tribes, and the style can be found in abundance in the busy souks of Marrakech. Now, with many direct-to-consumer and vintage Beni Ourain-inspired styles on the market, the perfect Moroccan rug is just one click away. With all the options at your disposal, it may be intimidating to know where to start. Fear not, we’ve rounded up a few top picks below along with advice on how we’d style them.

Give your guests the coziest welcome with this Azilal rug from Revival Rugs. Its long, narrow design makes it ideal for tight entryways. And while the oblong diamond pattern speaks to geometric patterns you’ll see across traditional Moroccan rugs, the pink tassel is a niche, colorful detail that won’t overpower surrounding decor. This is an authentic hand-knotted Moroccan rug, with wool locally sourced from the Atlas Mountains. Because of its small size, you may also consider hanging it up to add a textural element to your wall decor.

Break up the monotony of home office decor by placing an eclectic rug under your desk setup. While the soft grey and subtle design still create a distraction-free workspace, the cheeky oxblood hues add a touch of personality to make you smile between emails and Zoom meetings.

You don’t have to get a custom carpet straight from Marrakesh to have a high-end rug. The Saira rug from Quince is a handwoven option at an affordable price. The geometric motif is heavily inspired by Moroccan design and the medium-pile, hand-spun wool is designed to feel plush under your feet.

Calling all maximalists and color drenchers. The rich, vibrant colors from this rug come from a family of third-generation dyers, and the irregular shapes of the boxes infuse a quirky texture to whatever room they are placed in. Either have this rug as the standout piece in a room that is otherwise neutral, or pair it with other green pieces like this couch from Ellison Studios to engulf yourself in a verdant indoor forest of your own making.

Even though this muted rug may look understated at first glance, it brings visual interest thanks to the grid design along the border. The motif takes inspiration from Mediterranean tile patterns, in case you were wondering. We can see this as a soft, elegant layer underneath your dining table. Water-resistant and machine washable, this rug can survive stains and spills without making you bat an eye.

Why should the adults have all the fun? Bring whimsical design to your child’s bedroom or playroom with this Moroccan area rug from Benisouk. We have a feeling that the high pile will feel comfy for the kids to run around on and durable enough to withstand a little wear and tear from playtime. The dreamy creamsicle hues of this rug can match youthful decor, but the abstract minimalist design is elevated enough to be a piece that grows with them through the years.

If you find yourself longing for sleek, beige interiors—or are in the midst of remodeling your home to mirror an Aman resort—look no further. This handmade sheep’s wool rug is a minimalist dream. The neutral cream brings a layer of calm, while the organic shapes offer a wabi-sabi element that may be necessary in a pristine white room.
