Modelling EUDR Lot-to-Plot Traceability for Coffee: Plots, Lot Edges and GeoJSON Exports

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Posted on Nov 16 • Originally published at factoraerp.com

By Isaac Muthui, CEO, Tamalaki Business Network Ltd. Originally published at factoraerp.com.

If you build or integrate systems for coffee processors in Kenya, the EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR) is a lineage problem. From 30 December 2026, the EU operator that first places coffee on the EU market (usually the importer) must file a due diligence statement that includes the geolocation of every plot the coffee came from, plus the production period and supplier details. Micro and small operators follow on 30 June 2027.

Any row with plot_id IS NULL is a traceability gap you must resolve before the lot goes into an EU contract. Turn the distinct plots into a GeoJSON FeatureCollection for the buyer, with MIN(delivered_on) and MAX(delivered_on) as the production period.

Kenya is classified as low risk under the EU country benchmarking, which allows the operator simplified due diligence (no risk assessment or mitigation unless there are signals of risk). It does not remove the need for plot geolocation. In July 2026, the Kenya Coffee Platform told the EUDR Learning Community for Africa that about 90 percent of active coffee farmers had been mapped, and that the main remaining gap is traceability from cooperative level down to individual plots. That gap is a data model.
