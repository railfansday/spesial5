There's still a place in the modern game for managers like Kevin Blackwell, says former Hearts and Rotherham United striker

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Kevin Blackwell has been in charge of Crawley Town on an interim basis since the end of October following the sacking on Colin Kazim-Richards after a terrible start to the season for the Reds.

The 67-year-old has a vast amount of managerial experience including jobs at Leeds United and Sheffield United and, despite an embarrassing exit in the FA Cup to seventh tier Wimborne Town, has had a good start to his Reds stint with four points in two league games.

And Washington, in his latest exclusive EFL column for BestBettingBonuses.co.uk, is happy to see Blackwell involved.

“I think it's great to see genuine football people like Kevin still involved in the game,” he said. “Football feels very modern and young at the minute, and sometimes it seems like coaches are inventing new vocabulary every other week to describe things we've been doing for decades!

“The reality is that so much of what we're seeing tactically has been done before in some way, shape or form. Of course the game evolves and you have to adapt with it, but the fundamental aim of football hasn't changed.”

And he added that experience can be seen as detrimental sometimes when it comes to older managers. He said: “So much of management is still about understanding people, building relationships and helping players through difficult situations. I think we're sometimes too quick to dismiss experienced managers or label them as dinosaurs because they might talk about defending deep rather than playing in a low block.

" Ultimately, they're describing the same thing, and just because someone doesn't use the latest terminology doesn't mean they don't understand the game.”

But Washington said experience is what Reds need right now. “In Crawley's situation, I think having someone who's experienced difficult periods before and isn't afraid to be demanding could be exactly what the players need,” he said.

"That doesn't necessarily mean being abrasive for the sake of it, but sometimes players need clarity, honesty and someone who's prepared to make difficult decisions.”

But should Blackwell be considered for the job permanently? Washington said: “I think that should come down to the impact he has on the players and the results he gets, rather than any preconceived ideas about his style or personality.

"There's absolutely still a place in the modern game for managers like Kevin, and I think it would be a shame if we lost sight of that.”
