Dave Challinor on why he thought Sheffield Wednesday's early lead backfired against Barnsley FC

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Two goals inside the opening quarter of an hour from Jamal Lowe ought to have seen the Owls to a comfortable victory over opponents who have now not won in ten League One games.

But they did not have another shot on target all night, and were made to sweat it out on a freezing cold night to claim a 2-1 victory.

"You've always got to work hard," said Challinor, who was taking charge of his first League One away game as Owls manager.

"The players probably felt we didn't work hard enough in the first half. After going after going in front, sometimes it's the worst thing can happen because players think the game's done and we can now sit back and not have to have to defend with the personnel we've got on the pitch.

"Us defending in a block with looking to break with Slatts (Callum Slattery) and Davis (Keillor-Dunn) and Barry Bannan doesn't really work for us, so we need to be more aggressive, high up the pitch, and expose ourselves. We didn't do that, it'll take time.

"I'll own that in terms of what it looks like but that's where we need to get to.

"We've got to at that point really be on the neck of people and go and get the third goal, not be comfortable thinking two is an easy night for us because you allow a young team to get some momentum and come back into the game."

But as critical as he was of areas of the first-half performance, Challinor was delighted with the spirt he saw in his new players in the second half.

"We've got we've got a group that want to defend right across the board," he said. "We've done some work this week around emergency defending.

"You're not going to get much better than Coops (Liam Cooper), Sil (Swinkels), Dish (Di'Shon Bernard), Yan (Valery) and Rico (Ricardo Santos).

"They want to block, they want to get themselves in the way.

"We were a little bit better second half.

"We had a really good chat at half time. I told them what I felt, we'd just got to make better decisions.

"We've got we've got an experienced group, but some of our decision-making in that last half an hour caused us more problems than it should have.

"If it's a derby, you need to be able to be 100 per cent because Barnsley, at that point, are going for it, are open, and two or three passes, we could have we could have been in a lot more often, and we made the wrong decision at the wrong time.

"Winning's a brilliant habit, but I'm very realistic about the work that needs to be done.

"I never have been a great winner, as daft as that sounds. My job's to be analytical and understand where we get better.

"I want them to enjoy winning. I've said that to them. I want it to be absolutely bouncing because winning's a brilliant feeling and a brilliant habit to get into.

"They found a way when maybe not performed the best. There's loads more to come from this group. My job is to make sure that happens.

"You're always going to be try and be a perfectionist as a football coach. You'll never get to that but that doesn't mean you stop trying."
