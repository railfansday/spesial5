HP sees premium-priced AI PCs as its meal ticket in a shrinking market

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

PC shipments set to fall further thanks to the memory shortage, but high-margin models for AI at the edge keep profits up

HP Inc expects the PC market to shrink by "high single to low double digits" in calendar 2027, squeezed by the memory chip crisis, but execs reckon the corporation can still gain share by focusing on premium categories and AI PCs.

Interim CEO Bruce Broussard said HP would do this "by capitalizing on the growing shift of AI workloads to the edge and leveraging our industry-leading portfolio designed to meet the needs of users with more demanding workloads."

The pitch echoes the previous quarter's earnings call. "Our focus is on helping customers implement and manage AI across their environments. We believe that the future of AI is hybrid with workloads running both in the cloud and closer to where people work and data is created," he added.

Broussard cited IDC estimates that half of enterprise AI inference workloads will run on endpoints or edge nodes by 2030, and claimed that with half a billion devices shipped, HP has "deep expertise in how devices perform, how they're secured and how they're managed at scale."

HP fourth quarter revenue - for the three months ended October 31 - was $16 billion, up 9.3 percent year-on-year. Full-year fiscal 2026 revenue was up 9.5 percent to $60 billion. Personal Systems drove the growth, as its printer business slipped slightly.

Personal Systems revenue grew 14 percent on commercial and advanced compute sales, and AI PCs represented nearly 60 percent of HP’s shipments in the quarter.

"That said, higher component costs and challenging pricing environment, particularly in consumer, pressured profitability," said Broussard.

Like its rivals, HP is leaning on higher-margin premium models and signing long-term deals with the memory chip makers to secure supplies. Nikkei Asia named HP among the firms buying a limited amount of memory from China’s ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT).

Asked whether it planned to procure more Chinese chippery, HP’s CFO Karen Parkhill sidestepped the question: "We don't expect any material changes in our suppliers right now. We're confident in the suppliers that we have, and we are confident in the supply that we have what we need to fulfil the demand this year."

She conceded that "We are using suppliers from all over the world."

Parkhill said HP expects memory and storage costs to continue rising, but at a slower rate than during this last fiscal year. "We also expect CPU costs to increase, but not as much as what we've seen with memory and storage. And all of that is baked into our outlook."

Broussard expects Q4 to prove the low point for Personal Systems margins, with improvement coming from further optimization of its product design and component configurations.

In Ferbruary, HP said memory and storage costs had grown from 15 to 18 percent of the PC bill of materials at the start of the year to a figure closer to 40 percent. Parkhill declined to update the figure, saying the moderating cost curves and fast-changing production mix make the BOM percentage "less useful."

HP’s newly hired CEO, former GSK boss Emma Walmsley, will start work at the company on January 4, leading it through what continues to be uncertain times for the PC industry. ®
