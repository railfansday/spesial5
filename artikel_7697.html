Court Blocks FCC from Early Review of ABC Broadcast Licenses

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Mostly law professors | Sometimes contrarian | Often libertarian | Always independent

From today's decision by Judge Loren Alikhan (D.D.C.) in ABC, Inc. v. FCC:

Plaintiffs assert that Defendants violated their First Amendment rights by designating their broadcast licenses for early renewal as retaliation for Plaintiffs' broadcasting unfavorable speech about President Donald J. Trump and the Republican Party….

Because broadcast media requires access to radio spectrum to operate, and because radio spectrum is a finite public resource, the FCC is responsible for granting and renewing broadcast licenses…. The FCC may grant an initial license or a license renewal for a term of no more than eight years, and it "ordinarily" grants licenses for eight-year terms, with term dates staggered by the geographic location of each station. "However, if the FCC finds that the public interest, convenience and necessity will be served thereby, it may issue either an initial license or a renewal thereof for a lesser term." ….

The FCC argued that the ABC licenses were being designated for early review because the FCC was investigating the possibility that Disney and ABC were discriminating in employment for "DEI" reasons. But Judge Alikhan concluded that this was likely a pretext, and the likely real reason for the FCC's action was unconstitutional Administration retaliation against ABC's speech:

"Courts have recognized that [] circumstantial evidence, including proximity in time between the protected speech and government's adverse actions, the defendant's expression of hostility to the protected speech, and the absence of a proffered legitimate alternative explanation for the action[,] can support a finding that protected speech caused the agency's response."

Here, robust circumstantial evidence strongly supports Plaintiffs' theory of causation. Although Defendants proffer a legitimate alternative explanation, the court does not find that explanation to be credible on the record before the court. Starting with expressions of hostility, President Trump has set his sights on Plaintiff ABC for several years now. What started in 2020 as questions about how ABC "can keep getting a license" if it "report[s] fake news" has crescendoed into repeated calls for the Commission to strip ABC's broadcast licenses for their editorial decisions.

The court counts eleven such calls made by President Trump over the past two years—the most recent being in July 2026. See ECF No. 1 ("When you have a network and you have evening shows, and all they do is hit Trump[,] … I think maybe their license should be taken away. It will be up to [Chairman] Carr."); ECF No. 2-3, at 109-10 ("[T]hey ought to take away [ABC's] license."); id. at 157 ("ABC's license should be TERMINATED."); id. at 166 ("CBS should lose its license, and it should be bid out to the Highest Bidder, as should all other Broadcast Licenses, because they are just as corrupt as CBS—and maybe even WORSE!"); id. at 252 ("I think ABC should lose its license."); id. at 303 ("ABC & NBC FAKE NEWS … SHOULD, ACCORDING TO MANY, HAVE THEIR LICENSES REVOKED BY THE FCC."); id. at 306 ("ABC and NBC FAKE NEWS … should lose their Licenses."); id. at 321 ("[T]he Federal Communications Commission should look into the license of … ABC Fake News."); id. at 340 ("I think the license should be taken away from ABC because your news is so fake and it's so wrong. And we have a great commissioner, the chairman, who should look at that."); ECF No. 2-4, at 13 ("If Network NEWSCASTS, and their Late Night Shows, are almost 100% Negative to President Donald J. Trump, MAGA, and the Republican Party, shouldn't their very valuable Broadcast Licenses be terminated? I say, YES!"); id. at 99 ("ABC fake news ha[s] [] said that [it] would not cover this speech…. Fraud like this should mean a revocation of their licenses.").

To be sure, President Trump is within his rights "to persuade Americans, including by speaking forcefully or critically, in ways that [he] believes would advance the public interest." Here, however, the President's persistent "rhetoric demonstrates that [he] harbored animosity" toward Plaintiffs because of their protected speech, specifically—to quote the President—their "negative" and "unfair coverage" of him and the Republican Party.

What is more, Chairman Carr has taken President Trump's rhetoric as his marching orders. The same week he was tapped to be President Trump's FCC chair, following months of President Trump's calling for "ABC [to] lose its license" for how it covered the presidential campaign, then-Commissioner Carr lauded "license revocation" as a "remedy" to "deter this kind of conduct." Then, early in his tenure as chairman and after committing himself to President Trump's agenda, Chairman Carr ordered the Enforcement Bureau to investigate Disney's employment practices, and soon ordered another investigation into The View.

When President Trump first called for Mr. Kimmel to be fired—as well as for ABC's license to be "taken away"—Chairman Carr vowed to intervene if Plaintiffs did not "take action on Kimmel." Months later, when President Trump decried Mr. Kimmel's skit about the First Lady, the Commission, at Chairman Carr's behest, initiated early license renewal proceedings the next day. Chairman Carr said of the move, "If you didn't take us seriously, now you should."

Most recently, when President Trump touted that Plaintiffs' not broadcasting his address justifies "revocation of their licenses," Chairman Carr announced that the Commission was "looking into" Plaintiffs' decision, and that he expected it to come up during the early license renewal proceedings. This conduct and rhetoric are "powerful evidence of [Defendants'] retaliatory motive."

In response, Defendants point to Chairman Carr's assurances that the Commission "has not singled out any one company for special treatment," and will remain "open-minded" throughout the early license renewal proceedings. But these assurances fall flat when Chairman Carr is simultaneously making comments that the Commission is "looking into" Plaintiffs' "decision [not] to air" a presidential address as part of the proceedings, especially given that Chairman Carr has said that this specific presidential address is "something that broadcasters should be carrying."

Turning to temporal proximity, against the backdrop of President Trump and Chairman Carr's call-and-response, it is no coincidence that President Trump criticized Mr. Kimmel's skit on social media and the very next day Defendants initiated the early license renewal proceedings. Indeed, in the wake of Mr. Kimmel's September 2025 comments about President Trump and his supporters, Chairman Carr stated, "We can do this the easy way or the hard way. These companies can find ways to take action on Kimmel, or there is going to be additional work for the FCC ahead."

Chairman Carr made this promise at the same time that President Trump said of the situation: "When you have a network and you have evening shows, and all they do is hit Trump[,] … I think maybe their license should be taken away. It will be up to [Chairman] Carr." Chairman Carr's "fast-moving" inquest into Plaintiffs' business operations and editorial decisions from the moment he was appointed as chairman and seemingly at the President's direction further "indicates [Defendants'] retaliatory animus." …

The court therefore enjoined the FCC from "from taking any continuing or further action against Plaintiffs in relation to the early license renewal proceedings before the Federal Communications Commission, including issuing a Hearing Designation Order;"
