Bitcoin’s $60,000 bear market is testing miners harder than the 2022 crypto winter

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Bitcoin’s hashrate rebounds as mining revenue per unit hits $29.36 and AI competes for power and infrastructure.

Bitcoin’s hashrate rebounded above 1,000 exahashes per second in the week to Nov. 23, while the expected dollar revenue per unit of that computing power fell over the same week.

Luxor’s weekly mining update put the seven-day average hashrate at 1,033 EH/s, up 7.8% from 959 EH/s a week earlier.

Over the same week, dollar hashprice fell 3%, from $30.27 to $29.36 per petahash per second per day. Hashprice is the gross daily revenue a miner can expect from a fixed amount of computing power, before electricity and other costs.

The rebound came with Bitcoin trading near $60,000 on Nov. 24, about 52.5% below its October 2025 price record. The 30-day average hashrate was about 1,013 EH/s on Nov. 23, or 91.2% of the roughly 1,110 EH/s high recorded on Nov. 11, 2025, in the period analyzed.

The average had fallen to 896 EH/s on July 28, about 19.3% below that high.

The same point in the two previous Bitcoin bear markets gives that 91% some context.

The comparison uses the 30-day average 413 days after each cycle’s peak daily price close, measured against the highest 30-day hashrate recorded between the price peak and day 413.

It also uses Blockchain.com’s daily estimates, with Coin Metrics readings filling three missing dates in November 2025. A 30-day average smooths swings when blocks arrive more quickly or slowly by chance.

In the 2017–18 cycle, mining power kept rising after the December 2017 price peak. It reached 52.4 EH/s in October 2018, then fell to 37.6 EH/s on Dec. 25. By Feb. 2, 2019, the matched date, it had recovered to 41.9 EH/s, just under 80% of its high.

In the 2021–22 cycle, hashrate also set a high well after the price peak, reaching 265.2 EH/s in November 2022. It stood at 246.8 EH/s on Dec. 26, 2022, or 93.1% of its high. Today’s network is slightly farther below its peak than it was at that point in 2022 and closer than it was after the 2018 slump.

Hashrate Index recorded a 2022 hashprice low of $55.94 per petahash per second per day, almost twice its Nov. 23 reading. The April 2024 halving cut Bitcoin’s fixed subsidy from 6.25 to 3.125 BTC per block. Transaction fees add to that subsidy, but they made up just 0.64% of block rewards in Luxor’s latest weekly snapshot.

Bitcoin’s mining difficulty fell 9.13% on Nov. 14. Using power prices and machine economics, Luxor’s West Texas analysis estimated that miners cutting electricity use there could account for roughly three-quarters of the 91 EH/s drop in the network’s 14-day average around that adjustment.

Riot Platforms previously earned credits for reducing power use.

Core Scientific’s third-quarter results show $215.4 million in quarterly colocation revenue, and the company was billing for 485 megawatts of capacity as of Sept. 30.

IREN said its Horizon 1 facility entered service for Microsoft during the September quarter. The company’s mining revenue fell to $28 million from $67 million in the previous quarter.

Riot’s November results show the company mined 1,637 BTC in the third quarter, up from 1,406 a year earlier, and separately disclosed a 910-megawatt powered-land lease for AI at Corsicana with the first delivery scheduled for September 2027.

A sustained loss of mining power would lengthen the expected time between blocks at the prevailing difficulty.

Bitcoin adjusts the difficulty every 2,016 blocks to bring the pace back toward one block every 10 minutes.
