What the junk bond market can tell Bitcoin traders after Monday’s rebound

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Risk premiums on the weakest U.S. corporate bonds widened last week, while U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded $570 million in net outflows on Monday.

U.S. junk-bond yields rose to 8.46% at the Nov. 20 close as investment-grade yields fell, according to ICE BofA data published Nov. 23 by the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis. Bond investors demanded a bigger premium to own the weakest corporate debt. That gives Bitcoin buyers a separate gauge of risk appetite after Monday's rebound lost momentum.

The yield on the U.S. high-yield index rose from 8.25% on Nov. 16 to 8.46% on Nov. 20. The biggest yield move among the three corporate indexes was in bonds rated CCC or lower. Its effective yield rose from 17.88% to 18.61%.

Over the same dates, the investment-grade corporate yield fell from 5.95% to 5.84%. The two-year Treasury yield fell from 4.83% to 4.63%.

The gap over the Treasury curve widened across all three ICE BofA corporate bond indexes between Nov. 16 and Nov. 20, 2026.

The option-adjusted spread for bonds rated CCC and lower widened 92 basis points, compared with 42 for high yield overall and 8 for investment grade.

CCC and lower is a subset of the high-yield index. Option-adjusted spreads measure the yield premium over the Treasury curve after accounting for embedded options.

The high-yield spread widened from 3.34 to 3.76 percentage points between Nov. 16 and Nov. 20. The CCC-and-lower spread rose from 13.10 to 14.02 points. The investment-grade spread widened from 0.90 to 0.98 points. Even as investment-grade yields fell, investors demanded a bigger premium to hold those bonds over Treasuries.

U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded $570 million in net outflows on Monday after $669 million on Friday, according to Farside Investors. On Monday, Bitcoin approached $64,000 after trading near $55,100 early Saturday. The Monday rally gave way to a pullback toward $62,500.

The authors of The Crypto Cycle and US Monetary Policy, a 2023 IMF working paper, found that U.S. monetary tightening weighed on a common factor in crypto prices through investors' willingness to take risk, a channel they also identified in equities. The latest bond and ETF readings are consistent with shared caution, though they cannot explain Monday's Bitcoin price moves on their own.

Further widening in high-yield spreads alongside continued spot Bitcoin ETF outflows would point to caution persisting in both markets. Narrower spreads alongside renewed ETF inflows would support the case for a broader recovery in risk appetite. Sustained ETF inflows while spreads stay wide would suggest demand through those funds can recover even as bond investors remain wary.
