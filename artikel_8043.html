These cats may live outdoors and have limited contact with people, but they are still an important part of many communities and deserve care and compassion.

Feral cats are unsocialized domestic cats who typically avoid close human interaction. Some were born outdoors, while others may have become feral after living without regular human contact. Community cats can also include friendly strays and other free-roaming felines who rely on outdoor resources.

One of the most widely used approaches for managing feral cat populations is trap-neuter-return (TNR). Through TNR programs, cats are humanely trapped, spayed or neutered, vaccinated, and returned to their outdoor homes. This can help prevent litters while allowing cats who arenât comfortable living indoors to remain in familiar surroundings. Through surgical sterilization, TNR reduces feral cat numbers without culling or disrupting cat colonies.

National Feral Cat Awareness Month is also an opportunity to learn how to safely interact with community cats, support local TNR efforts, and recognize when a cat may need additional help.
