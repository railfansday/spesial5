I wanted a perfect mobile PageSpeed score for my side project, a free game backlog tracker. It started at 86. Within an hour, it was 77. By the end of the afternoon, it was 99. Here is the part of that story that taught me something.

My report listed “render-blocking requests” with an estimated saving of 840 ms. The biggest blocker was the Google Fonts stylesheet. Every performance article gives the same answer: load it asynchronously.

I applied it, rebuilt, ran the test again. The score fell from 86 to 77. First Contentful Paint did not improve. Cumulative Layout Shift went from 0.001 to 0.131.

The fonts now arrived after the first paint, text rendered in a fallback font, then jumped when the real font loaded. I had not removed the cost; I had moved it from “wait” to “shift”, and the metric that punishes shifting is worth more points than I expected.

Instead of trying a third trick, I read what the tool listed under diagnostics, line by line, and checked each with curl.

1. Nothing was compressed. The report said text compression was missing. I did not believe it, because my reverse proxy config has encode gzip zstd. So I tested:

No Content-Encoding header. Not on the HTML, not on the 76 KB stylesheet.

2. A hidden @import. A second font request appeared in the waterfall that I had never added to the page. It came from a scoped style block inside a “Buy me a coffee” button component, where someone (me, months ago) had written @import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/...'). The bundler had merged that into the main stylesheet, so my entire CSS was waiting on a third-party server. I replaced that font with a system stack.

3. The headline was waiting for its animation. My hero used a fade-in with opacity: 0 at the start, 0.8 seconds long, with a stagger delay of up to 0.85 s. The browser cannot count an invisible element as the largest paint, so the largest contentful paint was literally behind an animation. I shortened it to 0.35 s with no stagger and a nearly-visible starting state.

The score is a proxy. What I actually care about is that the page now appears in about a second and a half on a mid-range phone on a slow connection, and that I can explain why.
