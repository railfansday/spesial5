Pharming: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

A rare-disease player with a stabilized core product is now targeting a market 26 times larger, fueled by cash generation that needs no external financing.

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Oct. 27, 2026, Pharming (PHAR -3.64%) announced total revenue grew 21% to $117.6 million. Operating profit increased 89% to $29.9 million as the business expanded sales of its primary rare disease treatments. Strong cash generation gives the business funding to expand its clinical drug pipeline without needing extra financing.

RUCONEST, a treatment for hereditary angioedema (HAE, a rare genetic condition causing severe swelling), returned to revenue growth after earlier quarterly declines. Management maintained over 90% of active patients despite new oral competition, stabilizing demand among high-volume users.

Joenja, a therapy for activated phosphoinositide 3-kinase delta syndrome (APDS, a rare primary immunodeficiency causing immune system dysfunction), expanded through new international launches and U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) pediatric approvals. International sales now generate roughly one-third of total Joenja revenue.

Management's advancing leniolisib into common variable immune deficiency (CVID, a primary immunodeficiency disorder affecting immune function), a market 26 times larger than APDS in patient prevalence. Positive phase 2 data in related primary immunodeficiencies (PIDs) provides proof of concept for the broader indication.

Operating profit is surging as commercial sales expand, and management isn't hiding its satisfaction. High patient retention and specialized manufacturing create durable competitive moats. Management is strategically watching channel inventory timing and pediatric adoption.

Disciplined execution and clinical expansion set up long-term growth across broader immunology markets.
