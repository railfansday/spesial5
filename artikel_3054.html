e.l.f. Beauty vs. lululemon athletica: Which Consumer Goods Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Two very different valuations and a growth-versus-margin trade-off could tip the scales for your portfolio. Here is how the numbers stack up.

Choosing between a high-growth cosmetics disruptor and an established athletic apparel leader requires balancing expansion potential against valuation. e.l.f. Beauty (ELF -4.16%) and lululemon athletica (LULU -2.78%) represent two distinct paths for retail investors.

e.l.f. Beauty dominates the masstige beauty market by offering vegan, prestige-quality products at accessible price points. lululemon continues to lead the premium activewear segment through technical innovation and a massive global store footprint. Both companies face evolving consumer trends and competitive pressures as they eye growth through 2027.

e.l.f. Beauty operates in the retail stocks landscape by selling clean, vegan, and cruelty-free cosmetics through mass-market and specialty channels. The company relies on key retail partners like Target (TGT -1.11%), Walmart (WMT +0.41%), Amazon (AMZN +0.48%), and Sephora to reach its core audience. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business because a small number of retailers account for a large portion of net sales.

In the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026, revenue reached nearly $1.6 billion. This represented a revenue growth rate of approximately 24.6% compared with the prior fiscal year. The company reported net income of roughly $26.3 million, which resulted in a net margin of about 1.6% for the period.

As of its March 2026 balance sheet, e.l.f. Beauty carried a debt-to-equity ratio of approximately 0.8x, which measures total debt against shareholder equity. The current ratio stood at nearly 2.3x, while free cash flow reached about $190.1 million. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 40.9% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since it is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

lululemon athletica operates an omni-channel retail model that combines more than 800 company-operated stores with a powerful e-commerce platform. The company designs technical athletic apparel and footwear, selling directly to consumers and through wholesale partners like fitness studios and athletic organizations. This multi-pronged approach allows the brand to maintain its premium status while expanding into new international markets.

In the fiscal year ended Feb. 1, 2026, revenue reached close to $11.1 billion. This figure reflected revenue growth of approximately 4.9% compared with the prior fiscal year. The company reported net income of nearly $1.6 billion, supporting a healthy net margin of roughly 14.2%.

As of its February 2026 balance sheet, lululemon athletica reported a debt-to-equity ratio of approximately 0.4x. Its current ratio was nearly 2.3x, indicating a strong ability to cover near-term liabilities using current assets. Free cash flow for the most recent fiscal year was close to $921.7 million, providing ample capital for store investments and brand expansion.

e.l.f. Beauty faces intense competition from large multinational consumer products companies like Procter & Gamble (NYSE:PG) and Unilever (UL +0.38%), as well as numerous emerging influencer-backed brands. The company relies heavily on third-party manufacturers primarily in China, which exposes the business to geopolitical instability and tariff volatility. Furthermore, any failure to accurately forecast demand could lead to significant inventory write-downs or lost sales.

lululemon athletica competes in a crowded athletic apparel market against both global giants and nimble new entrants. The company depends on international suppliers mostly in Asia, creating risks related to geopolitical tensions and supply chain disruptions. Additionally, it faces legal and reputational risks from pending securities class action lawsuits alleging misrepresentation of brand health and financial guidance.

lululemon athletica currently offers a more conservative entry point for investors based on its significantly lower earnings and sales multiples.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

The Forward P/E ratio compares the current stock price to future earnings estimates, providing a look at how the market values anticipated growth. The P/S ratio measures the market value of the company relative to its sales over the past twelve months.

I'd go with e.l.f. Beauty. It's a growth machine with a remarkably long track record, and it just delivered another quarter that blew past expectations. Sales have now risen for 31 straight quarters, and last quarter's adjusted profit per share came in at roughly double what analysts expected. The company just raised its outlook again.

e.l.f. easily beat Wall Street's forecasts for its fiscal Q2 2027, with sales up 51%. rhode, the high-profile brand it bought last year, has been a big part of that growth, so the company needs it to stay popular. Still, its namesake brand gained market share last quarter too, a good sign heading into 2027.

To be fair to lululemon, it is still very profitable and growing internationally, and a new CEO with a long Nike track record just took over. But the core business is under pressure and a rebound is far from guaranteed.

For anyone investing for the long haul, the choice comes down to waiting on a turnaround or owning a business that is already growing. e.l.f. has grown for nearly eight years and is still expanding into new brands and new countries, and that momentum is why it is my pick.
