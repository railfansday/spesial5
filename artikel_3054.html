Lamar: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Advertisers are already renewing contracts for 2027, but management warns that frothy asset prices will test its discipline on acquisitions.

By The Motley Fool Team – Nov 21, 2026 at 5:13PM EST

On its third-quarter of 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 5, 2026, Lamar (LAMR +0.10%) raised its full-year guidance for adjusted funds from operations (AFFO), a metric that measures cash available for distribution in real estate investment trusts. The outdoor advertising operator is benefiting from accelerating demand across its digital billboard platform. It's also purchasing real estate beneath its display sites to secure long-term locations.

Political candidates and advocacy groups are shifting more budget toward digital displays because they can deploy campaign messaging quickly. Lamar expects its political ad revenue to exceed $40 million this year.

Lamar is taking advantage of acquisition opportunities to expand its footprint and secure core assets. Management spent $147 million on billboard acquisitions and another $55 million buying easements, which give the company permanent property rights beneath its displays.

Local businesses provide a predictable revenue base that protects the company during broader economic swings. That local advertising book has expanded continuously for more than five years.

Advertising demand is accelerating into the fourth quarter as businesses renew display contracts for 2027.

Lamar's nationwide footprint and low debt leverage provide room to pursue strategic deals that increase per-share cash flow, even as asset prices rise. Management is watching pricing discipline and real estate expansion to sustain long-term returns.
