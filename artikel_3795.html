Best Hunter Pets in WoW Forever

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

A Hunter isn't whole without their Pet, especially in WoW: Forever where Azeroth is a brave new frontier.

The Hunter’s Pet forms a core part of the Hunter experience in World of Warcraft: Forever, with much of their toolkit heavily relying on these partners in combat. In fact, the Hunter has become quite the iconic WoW Class for being able to tame a huge part of the MMO’s bestiary, giving players literally hundreds of options for Pets.

But for those still leveling their Hunters or curious for what other kinds of Pets are awaiting them all over Azeroth, there are indeed some creatures you should tame and fight with in specific situations.

When Forever launched on Nov. 4, Blizzard was quick to roll out a patch two days later (Nov. 6) to strengthen the ability of Hunter’s Pets to become semi-tanks, at least for the Hunters and smaller groups. We’re keeping track of how these changes can affect the performance of Pets so far, and we’ll update this list as necessary.

Hunters are DPS characters by nature, and a lot of that damage is usually facilitated by their Pets. And there are just some Pets that stand out among the rest as better damage dealers than their other counterparts. Cats are a standout here, as they’re among the most common Pet Families you can find as you level up.

Some iconic Cats you could aim for would be Broken Tooth (Badlands) and The Rake (Mulgore), but even the Mountain Lions in Hillsbrad can perform well if you’re not gunning for a rare find. Raptors are a close second among recommended damage Pets as they share the same damage mod as the Cat, whereas the Gorilla has a decent AOE and the Scorpid has a stacking Poison.

Hunter’s Pets don’t make good tanks, unlike traditional tanking Classes. With the right Pet Family, a Pet could still provide decent protection to your Hunter while you’re adventuring. Granted, they’re not as strong as we would want a “tanking” Pet to be, but these can take a beating and give a beating of their own.

Boars remain the best first Pets as they stun and give an attack boost to their next hit. If you level up Beast Mastery with the Boar, it might just be a long-term Pet. Meanwhile, Turtles and Crabs have the best defense stats among all Pets.

Say you want to tag along with a party but want to offer something other than having an extra member (your Pet) in tow. Then try to bring any one of these Pet Families in your dungeoning or leveling session. The idea here is that they’re not solely focused on dealing damage, but rather supporting the team in some capacity.

In this case, bring a Wolf to bolster everyone’s melee attack power. But if you feel you already have enough buffs, then a Carrion Bird to lower the enemy’s attack or a Tallstrider to lower the enemy’s armor could be more practical options.

Contrary to popular belief, the Hunter’s Pet should not focus on tanking in PvP, but rather on some form of control. The ideal PvP Pet should usually fit your current target at the moment, or if you’re unequipped to deal with a particular threat that you feel your Pet could help with.

Spiders usually win, as the 4-second rooting can be debilitating for most rotations and setups. If you prefer chasing down targets, then Hyenas and Crabs can slow them enough to leave them vulnerable.

There might be situations when you prefer to not really play with a Hunter’s Pet. Maybe you feel they’re just bringing you down, or you prefer the damage numbers when you’re performing solo.

You can perfectly go solo as a Marksmanship Hunter, courtesy of the Lone Wolf Talent. Getting this Talent gives you a 20% boost to all attacks as long as you don’t have an active Pet.
