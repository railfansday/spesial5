SteamDB will add "affiliate links to authorised game retailers" and a new tab dedicated to mods, first update since Nexus Mods takeover confirms

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

"We're still in the early stages of what we want to achieve"

In a first substantial update since acquiring SteamDB from its founders back in September, the owners of Nexus Mods have outlined some new additions coming to , the two sides in the deal have announced some additions coming to the community-run backend database. Affiliate links aimed at providing options to help folks find the cheapest price for a game and a new tab dedicated to the mods Nexus host for a game are incoming, as work on behind-the-scenes improvements continues.

"We're now a couple of months in since [the takeover] announcement, so I thought it was about time we shared a quick update on what we've been working on behind the scenes, along with a few changes you'll start to see rolling out over the coming weeks," Victor Folmann, CEO of Nexus Mods owners Chosen wrote in a blog post on SteamDB.

There are two main new features he pointed to that you'll notice popping up on SteamDB soon. The first is one that anyone could have seen coming, a new tab on each moddable game's page that shows off some mods hosted for it on Nexus Mods and a host of modding-related data. "The new tab will help you discover mods for games you're browsing on SteamDB, making it easier to see whether a game has an active modding community and explore what it has to offer," Fomann wrote. "For now, we'll only be showing mods from Nexus Mods, as we already have access to that wealth of modding data, making it a straightforward starting point for this new feature.

"We appreciate that some of you might be wondering why we're not including Steam Workshop, too. It's certainly something we'd be open to exploring as we develop the feature further, along with other modding sources. With that in mind, if you run a modding website and would be interested in having your community's mods included, we'd love to hear from you."

That seems perfectly fine, but the other incoming feature's a bit more controversial given SteamDB's historically not been all about the money. "One thing we've seen people ask for is an easier way to find the cheapest place to buy a game, and we're now working on making that possible," Folmann wrote. "We'll be introducing affiliate links to authorised game retailers, giving you more options when looking for the best available price on a game.

"We recognise that introducing affiliate links may raise some concerns, so we want to be clear about how they'll work. First and foremost, as I mentioned, we'll only feature authorised retailers; we will not be including grey-market resellers. We've also made a deliberate decision about how prices are displayed: retailers will be ordered by the cheapest available price in your currency, not by whichever retailer offers us the highest affiliate commission. If a retailer doesn't offer pricing in your currency, we'll still show deals in other currencies provided they are available in your region."

Folmann was clear in the initial announcement of the takeover that Nexus Mods' bosses would be actively looking into ways SteamDB could make more money to sustain itself, so the addition of these links isn't out of the blue either. Whether SteamDB regulars agree that they're a "lighter-touch" way of earning the site some cash without detracting from the traditional experience of using it is another matter. I'll need to see how I feel once I've actually navigated a few pages with the links in as part of looking up stuff about games, but my first instinct is that as long as the pages don't end up revolving around the links and it's still easy to get to the info you want without interacting with them, I probably won't be too put out.

Beyond those tweaks, Folmann mentioned "improvements to how updates are tested before they go live" and how quickly the team can react to issues have been among the less obvious work going on behind the scenes. "We're still in the early stages of what we want to achieve, and there's plenty more work we need to do and would like to work on," he wrote in closing.

Folmann and co only took over Nexus Mods in June last year, making them still relatively new custodians of both it and SteamDB.
