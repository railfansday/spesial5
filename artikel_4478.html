BioWare Documentary Slated for Release in 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Daniel is a lifelong gamer with a love for various genres and gameplay styles who enjoys exploring games and playing from various angles.

It’s been a few weeks now since the sudden announcement of EA’s closure of BioWare, longtime developer of the fan-favorite Mass Effect series. The major fervor surrounding this landmark closure, as well as the cancellation of the next Mass Effect, has gradually started to die down, and all that’s left is a sad little hole in our hearts where hope for a new spacefaring adventure once lived.

In spite of its unfortunate denouement, it doesn’t change the fact that BioWare was, is, and shall always remain a beloved studio, whose stories and worlds touched many a heart. In fact, BioWare and its staff were something of hometown heroes to its origin in the city of Edmonton, Canada. Because of this, a production effort has been spurred forth by the Canadian entertainment industry to properly document the studio’s 30-plus years of history.

For an in-depth look at how we lost one of the gaming greats, this is how the fall of BioWare was years in the making.

According to a Deadline exclusive published today, a documentary outlining the history of BioWare, from its founding in 1995 to its closure this year, is in production now, with hopes to have it up on screens sometime next year, including a run through all of the major film festivals. This will be the definitive BioWare story, told not just by its staff, but the many fans whose lives were changed by its productions.

The documentary, titled “Massive Effect: The BioWare Story,” is being written by Canadian writer and director Akash Sherman, best known for his 2018 sci-fi film, Clara. Interestingly, the actual filming process for this documentary began about two years ago, which means everything was already well underway the moment the decision came down from EA to shutter the studio. Sherman and his crew have captured the moment the BioWare story came to an end, as well as the rippling effects it had on the gaming community.

The headliners of the documentary are BioWare’s three original founders, Ray Muzyka, Greg Zeschuk, and Augustine Yip, though it’s also slated to feature appearances by a variety of other game industry alumni, including Lucasfilm Games Vice President Douglas Reilly, Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic director Casey Hudson, Game Awards creator Geoff Keighley, and even EA’s infamous former CEO, John Riccitiello.

A ranking list of all the games BioWare has ever developed in the history of the studio.

“When the production team asked for our blessing to tell the BioWare story, we were honored and deeply moved,” Muzyka and Zeschuk said in a statement obtained by Deadline. “BioWare was never just about the logo or the building. It was about the stories brought to life by the amazing people on our dev teams, and the fans who defended those worlds, championed them and carried them forward.”

The film’s creation and production is something of a love letter from BioWare’s home turf of Canada. In fact, its creation was commissioned by Canada’s Super Channel, with the Canada Media Fund offering support throughout the production.

“We are thrilled to bring Massive Effect: The BioWare Story to Canadian audiences,” Kim Ball, EVP of Content & Communications for Super Channel told Deadline. “As an Edmonton-based company, we’re especially proud to celebrate BioWare’s extraordinary hometown story and the people, creativity and groundbreaking storytelling behind some of the most beloved games of a generation.”

The film’s producers are inviting BioWare’s fans to contribute photos, videos, and stories of how BioWare’s games have affected their lives, with a select few slated to be featured in the film. “BioWare’s fans have been part of its journey since the very first game,” said one of the film’s producers, Adam Scorgie. “It’s only right that they help us tell the rest of the story.”

Sherman himself has similar reverence for BioWare, saying that their games are what helped ignite his initial creative spark, and that it was his “privilege and responsibility to share the journey of underdogs who came together to create deeply beloved storytelling.”

There's still a chance that Mass Effect can survive.
