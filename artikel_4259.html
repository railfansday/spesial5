Grand Theft Auto 6 is one of the most hotly anticipated games of all time, and few things can slow down its momentum, thanks to the sheer hype and quality on display. The quickest thing to put a damper on excitement, sadly, is bugs, hiccups that are both unintended and hard to fix in any quick manner.

GTA 6 is on track to be one of the biggest games of all time, but developers at Rockstar Games still have worries about job security.

The streets of Leonida have been plagued by a number of minor graphical and physics bugs that have led to laughs, but one repeat offender is causing issues for PS5 players. Internally titled error code CE-108255-1, this bug causes a no-frills, hard crash back to the PlayStation home screen after a brief play window, causing players to lose progress and time as they’re forced to reset.

For a massive open-world story game like Grand Theft Auto 6, forced short playtimes can be a killer, and the constant crashes forced by error code CE-108255-1 are causing just that. Complaints across the internet say the error has no discernible cause, but it only appears on PlayStation 5 systems, leaving Xbox players in the clear.

A recent set of firmware updates could be causing the error afflicting PS5 players, but neither Sony nor Rockstar has released any statement on it. Hopefully, as more players stumble into it, a swift hotfix patches the issue before too many lose potentially hours of playtime to a simple bug. GTA6’s robust simulation elements would make the loss even harder, taking tons of hard work with it.

No usual methods seem to help; reinstalling the game, going offline, and keeping the system cool all seem to be fruitless. Without a proper fix, players are stuck just hoping it doesn’t happen to them, a frustrating possibility that could easily tarnish their experience playing such an anticipated release.

Across the board, Grand Theft Auto 6 seems very well optimized, despite being trapped at 30 FPS across all platforms. A frame unlock is likely to come with the PC version, either officially or thanks to mods once the title jumps ship from consoles. Until then, fans are forced to pray and keep playing, crossing their fingers that their hard-earned cash isn’t lost due to the whims of an error they can’t avoid.

Over the last decade or so, a number of massive AAA titles have been released frankly unfinished. With Cyberpunk 2077 as the biggest offender, players have gotten sick and tired of being forced to shuffle through mountains of bugs and errors in order to play the games they pay for.

That makes Grand Theft Auto 6’s error code CE-108255-1 all the more frustrating; with how well polished the vast majority of the experience is, a hiccup like this sticks out like a sore thumb. The fine folks at Rockstar have clearly spent over a decade of blood, sweat, and tears to make Grand Theft Auto 6 the powerhouse it is, and losing face because of a bug like this is just sad.

With no acknowledgement from the studio yet, fans are left to their own devices, and some have gotten more than a little spiteful.

No physical copy, a $79.99 base price tag, and multiple on-disc locations barred behind a special edition give GTA6 an outer coat that has nothing to do with its criminal focus. It feels like the industry is yet again at a tipping point set by a big player, with the future in the hands of the rest of the AAA developers surrounding them.
