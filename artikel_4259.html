New York Bans Post-Purchase Subscriptions on Vehicles

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Monthly payments for heated seats? Not in the state of New York.

Are you paying a subscription fee for hardware already installed on your car? Not for much longer, you’re not. At least, not if you live in New York. Governor Kathy Hochul just signed a bill prohibiting automakers, dealers, and other third parties from charging subscription fees for vehicle features that use pre-installed components or hardware. Those heated seats you’re paying for? Get ready to cancel that subscription. That remote start you buy each winter? Treat yourself to an expensive latte from that cafe you like instead.

The new law (S.9348A/A.10411A) doesn’t stop automakers from charging subscriptions entirely, but it does stop them from being able to charge recurring payments for hardware that doesn’t cause them any recurring costs.

Hochul’s office specifically pointed to heated seats, enhanced navigation, hands-free driving, and entertainment options. Heated seats are pretty cut and dry, but hands-free driving poses an interesting question. Systems such as Tesla’s Full Self-Driving (FSD), GM’s Super Cruise, and Ford’s Blue Cruise all require customers to pay for a subscription to operate. All three systems work with the hardware installed on their respective vehicles, but the automakers could—and likely will—argue that they have recurring costs to keep the systems updated and safe.

Car and Driver reached out to Governor Hochul’s office for clarification on how the law will affect hands-free driving systems. We will update this story if we hear back. Meanwhile, the new law will go into effect on February 27, 2027, giving automakers and third-party companies time to enter into compliance.
