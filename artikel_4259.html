Countdown: Zelda: Ocarina Of Time Dungeon Crawl #9 - The Grand Finale

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Now yes, I know I said this series was going to work on a weekly basis, but as we hit launch week, I suddenly realised that this wouldn't give me time to touch on the big finale. And that wouldn't do! I've recapped everything from the Great Deku Tree to the Spirit Temple - did you really think I'd be able to stop without a nod to the last challenge of them all?

And so, welcome to the final edition of my Ocarina of Time Dungeon Crawl. I've awakened all the Sages, finished all the dungeons and now it's finally time to put the big bad to bed. Just in time to do it all again.

Review The Legend Of Zelda: Ocarina Of Time (Switch 2) - A Stunning Remake, But Not Quite Definitive

As with every other entry in my replay series, I'm going into endgame spoilers here, so turn back now if you've been saving yourself for the remake!

I didn't think of Ganondorf's Castle as a 'proper' Ocarina of Time dungeon because of its size, but returning to it on this playthrough may have changed my mind. Sure, it's on the shorter side, but it has a boss key, unique puzzle rooms, a big fight at the end, and even a unique item found within. This is definitely a dungeon.

And what a great way to wrap things up. I love the unique temple rooms, each based on mechanics that you have learnt from the game up to this point. Each one feels like a fitting send-off to the dungeons that inspired them without retracing steps that we've taken before. Even though I did the Spirit Temple last, its respective room in the Castle always makes me smile with its Bombchu-shaped hole in the top of the cage.

None of the rooms outstay their welcome, either. They serve as a nice little reminder of how far you have come and then get out of there before things start feeling repetitive. Finishing the 'Light' room only to find the final orb not where you expect it to be and a liminal extra tunnel in its place (only seen with the Lens of Truth) freaked me out back in the day, so that was a nice bit of resurfaced trauma for me when I came to it this time.

Powering down the beams is an easy enough process, and it's only amped up on the ensuing staircase ascent. Splitting the grand finale into puzzle and combat gauntlets is a really great idea, and it works wonders here. I don't like facing off against two sword-wielding enemies simultaneously any more than the next man, but it suitably ramps up the stakes as I get ever closer to the top floor and the ominous piano music.

And what a treat it is when you finally get to the top. This is high camp. You can't tell me that Ganondorf perfectly whacking out an overture on the keys while Princess Zelda floats over him is anything but. It's a foundational memory, and it rules.

For all of its grandeur, I still find the initial 'tennis' fight a little on the easy side (after restarting when I realised I had foolishly got greedy on the penultimate floor and needlessly gobbled up all the Green Potions, leaving me none for a mid-battle refill). It's epic, and a fitting throwback to the Phantom fight all those hours ago, but a little more attack deviation wouldn't have gone amiss.

With Link winning game-set-match, it was time for things to get cinematic. There's something about the N64 Rumble Pak going ham as the castle crumbles that never fails to tickle me. The falling effect looks naff in 2026, but I don't care. The final escape is still as tense as anything, and I always cheer when the top floor finally hits the ground.

But, of course, I wasn't done. All of my issues with the simplicity of the Ganondorf fight go out the window in the final 'Boar' battle.

On paper, it's just as straightforward as the previous phase — roll between the legs, hit the tail, repeat — but it just feels so damn cool doing it! I didn't have time for side quests in this playthrough, so I polished off the majority of the fight with the Megaton Hammer instead (the first time I've ever done so, I think). So yes, running over to re-collect the Master Sword and deliver the final blow at the end felt all the better for it.

And just like that, the game was complete. I lose track of how many times I have watched the finale by this point. It always makes me weep.

When I first played, I was rooting for Link to go back to his old time and catch up on the childhood that he'd missed. Now that I'm older (much older than 'Adult' Link is supposed to be), there was a faint pang in my heart when I realised that he'd never see the world he'd saved or the lives he'd pushed back on track.

I had forgotten how quickly things cut to the credits as Zelda plays her final lullaby, but let it be known, I was a wreck as the devs' names rolled by.

As a victory lap of everything that has come before it, Inside Ganon's Castle doesn't really put a foot wrong. It's a brief one, and the final head-to-head isn't quite as intense as I remembered it, but man, it doesn't get much more epic than this.

I can't wait to cry my eyes out all over again in the coming days weeks on Switch 2.

That's yer lot! To those who have followed this Dungeon Crawl series from the beginning, thank you for joining me on this replay. To those who only just dropped in for the finale, there's eight weeks' worth of catching up to do!
