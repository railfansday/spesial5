James Talarico’s ‘Incarnational Politics’

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The new senator from Texas has bad ideas about Christianity and democracy, but the press seems okay with that.

The day after the Democrats trounced the Republicans in the midterm elections, Ezra Klein of the New York Times tweeted, “One of the more exciting parts of this election — among many — is the return of a religious left.” Accompanying the enthusiastic remark was a clip from his interview earlier this year with James Talarico, the Democrat who won the Texas senatorial race. In the clip, Talarico — a Presbyterian seminarian — advocates a political attitude in which Americans recognize each other as being made in God’s image and treat each other accordingly. He calls this “an incarnational politics.”

Incarnational politics — hoo boy. The term, a nearly paradoxical union of the sacred and the profane, sounds just enough like intersectional politics to catch the attention of progressives. It also sounds like an edgy indie band that never quite made it. (“I saw Incarnational Politics open for Bad Religion in ’91.”)
