The Quest comes with a higher $350 annual fee, compared to the Explorer's $150 annual fee ($0 introductory annual fee for the first year, then $150). But it also offers additional perks, including two free checked bags, $200 in annual United TravelBank cash and a head start toward United elite status.

As a Quest cardholder myself, I think the card can be well worth its annual fee; however, there are a few things I'd consider before upgrading.

Here are five reasons you might want to make the switch — and a few things to consider before you do.

Before deciding whether to upgrade, it helps to see how the two cards stack up. Here's a side-by-side comparison of their key benefits.

If you regularly check bags when flying United, you likely already appreciate the 's complimentary first checked bag for you and a companion on the same reservation.

The takes that benefit a step further, offering both your first and second checked bags free for you and a companion on eligible United-operated flights.

It might not sound like much, but it can lead to substantial savings if you regularly travel with multiple bags.

For example, United's second checked bag can cost up to $60 each way on domestic flights. If you and a companion each check two bags on a round-trip flight, the Quest could save you up to $240 in second-bag fees compared to the Explorer.

That's already more than the $200 difference between the cards' ongoing annual fees.

As a Quest cardholder, I've gotten substantial value from this benefit, since I frequently travel with checked bags. Of course, if you rarely check more than one bag, the Explorer's baggage benefit may be all you need.

Keep in mind that to receive the complimentary checked bag benefits, you generally must include your MileagePlus number in the reservation and purchase your tickets with your eligible United card.

Sure, the might cost $200 more per year than the after the first cardholder year, but there's one perk that can help make up that difference in one fell swoop: the $200 annual United TravelBank benefit.

Quest cardholders automatically receive $200 in United TravelBank cash after account opening and each year on their account anniversary. These funds can be used toward future eligible flights operated by United or United Express.

Meanwhile, Explorer cardholders can earn $100 in United TravelBank cash after spending $10,000 on purchases in a calendar year.

If you regularly book United flights, the Quest's $200 TravelBank benefit should be relatively easy to use. And if you value the full $200, it effectively brings the card's $350 annual fee down to $150 before factoring in any other benefits.

I've personally found this to be one of the most straightforward Quest benefits to maximize. Since I fly United regularly, I typically put my TravelBank cash toward flights when fares are on the higher side, such as cross-country trips from my home base in San Francisco to Washington, D.C., where I have family.

If you know you'll spend at least $200 on eligible United flights each year, this benefit alone could make a strong case for upgrading to the Quest, especially if you aren't currently earning the Explorer's TravelBank benefit.

If you regularly purchase United flights, upgrading to the Quest could help you earn more MileagePlus miles.

The United Quest earns 10 total miles per dollar spent on eligible United flights, compared to 9 total miles per dollar spent with the Explorer. These totals include the 6 miles per dollar spent that general MileagePlus members earn on eligible fares, plus 4 miles per dollar spent from the Quest or 3 miles per dollar from the Explorer.

The Quest also earns 4 miles per dollar spent on other eligible United purchases, compared to 3 miles per dollar spent with the Explorer (mileage earning rates differ for Basic Economy or base-option tickets).

That means you'd earn an additional mile per dollar on eligible United spending by upgrading.

For example, if you spend $3,000 on eligible United purchases in a year, you'd earn 3,000 more miles with the Quest than with the Explorer.

While earning more miles is always nice, I wouldn't recommend upgrading solely for this difference. Unless you're spending a substantial amount with United, the additional miles probably won't justify the $200 difference in ongoing annual fees.

But they're a nice bonus if you're already considering the Quest for its other benefits.

If you're trying to earn United Premier status, the Quest offers a couple of advantages over the Explorer.

Both cards earn 1 Premier qualifying point (PQP) for every $20 spent on purchases. However, the Explorer caps these earnings at 1,000 PQP per calendar year, while the Quest allows you to earn up to 18,000 PQP annually through card spending.

The Quest also provides an annual bonus of 1,000 PQP, awarded within six to eight weeks after Feb. 1 each year. To qualify, your account must be open as of Dec. 31 of the previous year and remain open when the points are awarded.

The annual bonus provides a head start toward Premier status without a spending requirement.

As a United Premier Silver member, I've found the Quest's PQP benefits helpful in working toward maintaining my status.

That said, I wouldn't necessarily recommend putting large amounts of everyday spending on the Quest solely to earn PQP. Depending on your goals, you may get more value from using a card that earns transferable rewards.

Both the Explorer and Quest offer statement credits for purchases like ride-hailing services (activation required), Instacart orders and hotel stays. But the Quest offers higher potential savings in several categories.

For example, the Quest provides up to $100 annually in ride-hailing credits and up to $180 in Instacart credits, compared to $60 and $120, respectively, with the Explorer. Both cards' Instacart benefits are currently scheduled to end Dec. 31, 2027.

The Quest also offers up to $150 in annual credits for eligible Renowned Hotels and Resorts stays, up to $80 in United TravelBank cash for qualifying Avis or Budget rentals and up to $150 in annual JSX statement credits.

The Explorer offers similar credits for hotel stays, rental cars and JSX flights, but with lower annual allowances.

I've received some value from Quest's Instacart credit, and while it's nice to save a little on an online grocery order each month, it's not a perk that significantly affects how I value the card.

These credits could help justify the Quest's additional $200 in annual fees if you'll actually use them. Otherwise, I wouldn't give them too much weight in your decision, especially since some have specific booking requirements.

While the offers several advantages over the , upgrading won't make sense for everyone.

If you rarely check a second bag, don't fly United frequently enough to use the $200 annual TravelBank cash or aren't working toward Premier status, the Explorer may offer everything you need at a lower annual fee.

Both cards also share several benefits, including priority boarding, no foreign transaction fees and a credit of up to $120 for Global Entry, TSA PreCheck or Nexus every four years.

Before upgrading, I'd also consider whether applying for the Quest as a new card makes more sense, since you generally won't receive a welcome offer through a product change.

New cardholders can currently earn up to 90,000 bonus miles and 500 PQP: 500 PQP after their first purchase, 80,000 bonus miles after spending $4,000 on purchases in the first three months from account opening and an additional 10,000 bonus miles after adding an authorized user in the first three months from account opening.

However, if you're over Chase's 5/24 limit, you may not be eligible to open a new Chase card. In that case, upgrading could be worth considering if the Quest's benefits are a good fit for you.

Depending on your travel habits, it might also make sense to hold both cards. TPG credit cards editor Olivia Mittak is considering this strategy. She has the United Explorer and appreciates it for its lounge access, but believes she can also get substantial value from the Quest's various United perks.

If you're ready to upgrade from the to the , you'll need to call the number on the back of your credit card. Explain to the representative that you want to upgrade your card to the Quest Card.

Be clear with the agent on the phone that you want to change to the new card to ensure that Chase doesn't process a new card application or close your current card by mistake.

You should be able to continue using your current card until the new one arrives in the mail.

If you regularly check bags, can use the annual TravelBank cash and value the Quest's other perks, upgrading could make sense. Otherwise, you may be better off sticking with the Explorer.
