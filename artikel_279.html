On Dakar’s Packed Beaches, Africa Welcomes Its First Youth Olympics

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

The 2026 Youth Olympic Games are taking place in Senegal, where the beaches and streets are teeming with sports fans and fitness fanatics.

Ruth Maclean and Ricci Shryock reported from the beaches of Dakar, Senegal, where it seemed as though they were the only ones not exercising.

Teenage athletes from all over the world have descended on Senegal this month to compete in Africa’s first ever Olympic events, the 2026 Youth Olympic Games.

And in Dakar, Senegal’s sports-crazy capital on Africa’s westernmost tip, the locals are ready.

Fitness and athletic prowess have long been all-important here in Dakar. On any given day, ordinary young people put some of the world’s most toned muscles to work, wrestling, skateboarding, surfing and bike riding.

Women toting goggles and foam noodles make their way down alleyways to the sea to take part in mass early-morning water aerobics classes with energetic coaches.

Groups of men do synchronized calisthenics on the city’s public beaches, hopping backward in low frog jumps and coating themselves in fine sand after sets of push-ups.

Weight lifters lift homemade barbells formed by pouring concrete into buckets and sticking a piece of metal into it to dry.

And every evening, crowds gather up and down the city’s main coastal road — the Corniche — to run, lunge, jump and use the many outdoor gyms that have opened in the city in the past decade.

On a recent evening on Koussoum Beach, hulking men tied traditional loincloths around their waists and between their legs. Then they circled each other, pawing at each other’s hands like cats and pulling each other to the ground.

Beach wrestling is making its Olympics debut at the Games, delighting many in Senegal, where traditional wrestling is wildly popular. Traditional Senegalese wrestling has been practiced for centuries here, and in recent years has become extremely popular and lucrative for top-tier competitors.
