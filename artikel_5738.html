RISE St. James, Sierra Club Sue LDEQ to Vacate Hyundai Steel Air Permit

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

LDEQ issued air permit to the facility despite serious flaws in permit.

Donaldsonville, Louisiana — Today, RISE St. James Louisiana and Sierra Club filed a lawsuit challenging Louisiana Department of Environmental Quality’s decision to issue an air permit to Hyundai-POSCO for its proposed steelmaking facility. The groups claim that the air permit must be vacated because LDEQ did not conduct an environmental justice analysis, nor did the agency give full and careful consideration to mitigation measures like electrified components and green hydrogen, or to the environmental costs of Hyundai’s added pollution in an already overburdened area, as is required by the Louisiana state constitution and the Clean Air Act.

Throughout 2026, Sierra Club, working with other experts and local groups, submitted a series of comments to LDEQ in an effort to bring to light the errors, miscalculations, and omissions in the permit application. Despite some improvements to the permit application, made by Hyundai Steel following public pressure, the final air permit issued by LDEQ authorizes the facility to emit substantial quantities of greenhouse gases, nitrogen oxides, particulate matter, carbon monoxide, lead, mercury, ammonia, and other pollutants. Many of these pollutants cause significant and irreversible damage to human health even at low levels.

The area around the planned facility already bears a toxic air pollution burden greater than 96% of the rest of the country, and ranks high across a range of environmental and socioeconomic indicators. The Hyundai Steel Plant is one of multiple polluting facilities planned as part of the 17,000-acre Riverplex MegaPark in an area known as Cancer Alley in Ascension Parish, Louisiana.

In response, the petitioners issued the following statements:

Andrea Issod, Sierra Club Senior Attorney, said: “LDEQ must ensure the Hyundai Steel Plant permit complies with the legal requirements to reduce harmful pollution impacts as much as possible on the surrounding community, which already bears a 96% percentile burden of toxic releases as compared to the national average. By refusing to conduct an environmental justice analysis, consider cleaner technologies like electrifying components and replacing dirty gas with green hydrogen, or consider any environmental costs of Hyundai’s pollution, LDEQ has failed to perform the most basic function of its role and deprived the community of a transparent, thorough, and accurate assessment of the facility’s potential negative impacts. For that reason, we are asking the Court to reverse LDEQ’s final decision and vacate the permit for Hyundai Steel’s proposed facility.”

Mekaylyn Lavigne, Modeste Coordinator for RISE St. James, said: “I was born and raised in the small and peaceful community of Modeste. Although this region is now known as Cancer Alley because many chemical plants have polluted our air and water, my family and neighbors still enjoy sitting on our porches, watching birds and wildlife, and fishing. Now, LDEQ is allowing a steel mill to move in next door to my home, and tells us the air quality is fine and this massive iron and steel making facility will not have any negative impacts. Modeste residents deserve the truth. The court should make LDEQ go back to the drawing board on this permit to disclose all the impacts and use the cleanest technologies to reduce pollution.”
