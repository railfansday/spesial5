Welcome to Dungeon Master, PC Gamer's regular RPG column, where Online Editor Fraser Brown delves into PC gaming's most beloved and enduring genre. Grab a seat in our badly-lit tavern and please ignore the goblin puke.

I will probably be in the ground when Bethesda finally releases The Elder Scrolls 6: Sentinel—which is kind of liberating. I have hype immunity. But what's even more liberating is the fact that there's already an RPG that does everything I want from an Elder Scrolls game—2025's Tainted Grail: The Fall of Avelon.

Tainted Grail is unabashedly created in the mould of The Elder Scrolls, in that it is a primarily first-person fantasy RPG where flexibility is its superpower. You chart your own course and effectively construct your class, enhancing skills by using them as much as possible. But it is not merely the Elder Scrolls we have at home—it captures the very best that this kind of RPG can offer, and in some ways manages to outdo Bethesda.

This is apparent right from the get-go. In classic Elder Scrolls form, Tainted Grail begins with you banged up in a facility that incarcerates and studies unfortunates afflicted with a magical plague, of which you are one. Follow the critical path and you will meet a new friend, get in some tough fights and then escape.

By the time I finally escaped the island and began my adventure proper, I already knew this RPG was special.

But this island asylum is big. Really big. It's full of locked doors, hidden rooms, large spaces ripe for some big brawls, a subterranean area waiting to be delved into, oodles of lore—there are hours of rooting around to be done. So I ignored the critical path, had a big old wander, and by the time I finally escaped the island and began my adventure proper, I already knew this RPG was special.

While Tainted Grail is full of bespoke quests sending you off into the open world on countless adventures, these are really just designed to get you out the door so you can stumble upon something cool—a new enemy that will utterly destroy you, a powerful weapon hidden in a crypt, an eccentric immortal being who wants to make a deal. The vibe is very TTRPG, which isn't surprising, given that it's based on one.

And these adventures are actually dangerous. Deadly, even. The villains of Tainted Grail hit hard, and early on you will feel horrifyingly vulnerable. Basic bandits and skeletons can easily spell your doom. And if you stay out at night, you're screwed. Avelon is a miserable, cruel place, cursed and plagued, and you will struggle.

Developer Questline didn't simply up the lethality and call it a day, though. Tainted Grail, unlike The Elder Scrolls, actually has a quality combat system that falls firmly into the skill-based category. It's no Dark Souls, but it's more Dark Souls than Skyrim. So that increased level of challenge feels earned because of the tools you have available to you, and the range of combat styles you can develop.

With all the options you have at your disposal—which has been further enhanced by DLC additions, letting you, for example, go through this Arthurian fantasy RPG as a kung-fu master—it can be hard to commit to a build. I've been spellblading it up, with my alchemical skills helping to shore up my weaknesses, but every time I encounter a new piece of gear I wonder what else I could create. But you do need to commit eventually. This isn't a game for a jack of all trades.

While Bethesda is more deft at crafting believable, reactive lands, Tainted Grail's world design also deserves its flowers. It's a smaller space, divided into three main areas, and while you can absolutely muck around for hundreds of hours, you can experience most of what it has to offer in 50. But what you'll experience in those 50 hours is so much more meticulously designed, with Questline getting so much out of each location.

It's a game full of aesthetic juxtapositions.

It's a looker, too. What it lacks in fidelity it more than makes up for in the art design department, and it's another place where Dark Souls rears its gorgeously hideous head. Tainted Grail finds beauty in the grim misery of its fantastical post-apocalyptic realm. It's a game full of aesthetic juxtapositions—the arboreal and the horrific, the grounded and alien. Avelon is delightfully strange, and that makes exploration all the more thrilling.

As games grow ever larger and more bloated, my appreciation for richer, better-scoped worlds like Avalon only grows. And this does not indicate a dearth of ambition on Questline's part. Tainted Grail is a complex, elaborate game that will happily devour your days and keep showing you new things.

Clearly people want this—Tainted Grail has done very well for itself, garnering more than 20,000 user reviews on Steam, where its rating sits at Very Positive. It's a reassuring reminder that you do not need a gargantuan studio and big publisher money to make a quality RPG in the Elder Scrolls style.

We don't know how many people are working on Sentinel right now, but we can expect the number to be large. Skyrim had around 100 people working on it, and with Starfield that jumped to more than 400. Tainted Grail was made by 50 people. That's quite the achievement.

So if you're craving a side-quest-heavy sandbox RPG with plenty of sword fights and spellflinging, Tainted Grail should absolutely be your next port of call. And the good news is that there's a demo. No excuses. Go play it.
