Where to Stay in Big Sky, Montana, When You’re Dreaming of the Wild West

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

I’ve visited Big Sky, Montana, a handful of times over the past six years, and I’m still completely smitten. This corner of the Rockies, roughly 45 miles south of Bozeman, is a choose-your-own-adventure kind of city. Winter ski days beneath the hulking silhouette of Lone Peak give way to summer rafting trips and fly-fishing on the Gallatin River, while fall brings horseback rides that sprawl across open ranchlands, and spring offers hikes through wildflower-strewn meadows. The iconic Yellowstone National Park is about an hour south too, putting geysers, steaming hot springs, and roaming bison within day-tripping distance.

The city itself is a quaint yet buzzy collection of restaurants, shops, and watering holes, all adding a welcome dose of small-town charm to all that wilderness. And lately, the destination’s hospitality scene has been booming, with a new wave of stylish hotels and mountain retreats bringing some fresh takes to the city’s rugged Western roots. From luxe resorts with front-row views of the towering peaks to authentic dude ranches and design-forward Airbnbs with cozy fireplaces, there’s an accommodation for every style of Big Sky getaway on the list below. Read on for the best places to stay in Montana’s sweet mountain hub.

Famed for its sumptuous yet tasteful resorts in far-flung destinations like Mauritius, Rwanda, and Malaysia, One&Only’s 2025 Moonlight Basin opening was the brand's American debut, as well as its first foray into the outdoor adventure space. A luxury alpine retreat like this requires a specialized type of hospitality, and the team here—a crew who can do everything from book your activities to whisk you between buildings on a side-by-side ATV—knows exactly what they’re doing. Guests get a direct line to the slopes via the One&Only gondola, as well as access to plush locker rooms, tuning equipment, and gear rentals that will impress even members of Yellowstone Club (the famously tiny private ski community next door).

Moonlight Basin is worth visiting outside of ski season too. Situated on 240 secluded acres within the Greater Yellowstone Ecosystem, the resort links two sections of protected wilderness in southwestern Montana’s Rocky Mountains, providing critical migration routes for species like moose and bobcats, which means you're in for some exceptional wildlife sightings. Add fly-fishing, stargazing at the on-site observatory, and unwinding at the 17,000-square-foot spa, and even the most discerning ski bums will find themselves spoiled rotten. —Hannah Towey

Lone Mountain Ranch, one of Montana’s longest-standing dude ranches, has plenty going for it: a wonderfully attentive staff, character-filled cabins, and a calendar of special events that draw in both longtime regulars and first-time horseback riders. The on-site wranglers are happy to work with riders of all levels one-on-one, even if that means helping you get past a few preexisting fears before climbing into the saddle. The ranch’s roots reach back to the early days of Yellowstone, with its owners involved in conversations around Big Sky’s formation and the region’s ranching, logging, and wilderness preservation. Yet unlike many of Montana’s more remote ranches, it’s conveniently less than a 10-minute drive from town.

You may still find little reason to leave: Set among towering aspens, the 150-acre guest ranch has 25 restored historic cabins, each with its own Wild West–inspired name and one to six bedrooms. And then there are the signature experiences, including Cowgirl Up, a weeklong retreat that brings women from around the world together to experience ranch life and release their inner cowgirl.
