X-Men Officially Releases Design For New 'Imago' Mutant Type

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

There's a new type of mutant in X-Men lore, as Marvel introduces terrifying 'Imago' mutants as part of the DNX event. These horrifying additions to canon aren't just nightmare fuel, they're the dark endpoint of a founding hero's journey.

In recent comics, Hank McCoy - the hero formerly known as Beast - has entered a new era of villainy, rechristening himself as 'The Chairman.' As the Chairman, McCoy has embarked on a campaign of mutant supremacy that makes Magneto look restrained.

That campaign just resulted in the creation of a new type of mutant, prompting Cyclops and the former Beast to enter into a battle that Marvel has warned fans one founding X-Man won't survive.

In the recent DNX #4 - from Jed MacKay, Federico Vicentini, David Curiel and Clayton Cowles - the Chairman unleashes his deadly X-Virus on Juniper, Pennsylvania. The virus is designed to transform humans into mutants, but when the Chairman goes to inspect his work, he finds that he's created an army of monstrosities who can't survive in their new forms.

Previously, the Chairman explained that he's trying to achieve 'imago' - a real-world term referring to both the final stage of biological metamorphosis (e.g. a caterpillar becoming a butterfly) and an unrealistically idealized image of another person. Tragically, Beast has failed, calling the mutants he has created "wretched prototypes."

While Cyclops begs his former friend to let the X-Men and Fantastic Four tend to the Imago mutants, Beast refuses, claiming that he intends to clean up his mistake - he's set up a fusion bomb contained by a forcefield, intending to wipe Juniper and its Imago mutant residents off the map. The Chairman allows Cyclops to enter the forcefield in an attempt to change his mind, setting up the final issue of the event story.

In X-Men lore, mutant powers trend towards giving their wielder an increased chance of survival, even in highly unusual or counterintuitive ways. A mutant physiology which immediately kills the mutant is essentially unheard of (though they sometimes kill others around them), making the Imago mutants not just a failed experiment, but a huge step backwards.

Not every X-Men reader is a fan of Hank McCoy's villain arc, but no-one can deny that creating a species of monstrous mutants isn't the perfect ending to his fall from grace.

Hank's natural mutant powers gave him enlarged hands and feet as well as superhuman agility. His sharp teeth, claws, fur and animalistic physiology came from him experimenting on his own genetics, rendering his DNA unstable as a result.

There's a long thematic history of Beast's hubristic attempts to control the X-Gene having terrible consequences, most often for Hank himself. Now, that pattern has been repeated again, writ large,

The mutants created by the Chairman are already dying due to their powers, but that doesn't mean they're all done for. The Fantastic Four have been a big part of DNX, scouring Magneto's DNA for a cure for the X-Virus sent back in time from the future.

With the survival of Juniper's Imago mutants set up as the stakes for Cyclops and the Chairman's final battle, it makes sense that if Scott Summers wins, the Fantastic Four may be able to stabilize the Imago mutants, allowing those still alive to live on with their X-Gene activation intact.

If that happens, then DNX will have just given X-Men lore a permanent mutant subspecies - decidedly nonhuman mutants whose powers have yet to be fully explored. It's possible that Imago mutants could take the role of the classic, underground Morlocks, as mutants who can't pass as human.

Of course, for that to happen, they'll need to survive, with the epic confrontation between two founding X-Men primed to decide their fate.

DNX #4 is available now from Marvel Comics.
