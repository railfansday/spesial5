Choose a warehouse club by which one is near you and stocks what your household buys most, since Costco, Sam’s Club, and BJ’s all charge $60 or $65 a year for a basic membership. Join only if a year’s savings on your own shopping list beat that fee.

With fees that close, compare what each club sells, which coupons and credit cards it takes, and where it has stores.

Costco’s U.S. warehouses take only Visa credit cards, so pay there with one of the best credit cards for Costco. Sam’s Club and BJ’s take all major credit cards, and the BJ’s One Mastercard earns 3% in BJ’s rewards on most purchases there.

BJ’s has about 270 clubs, all in 22 states. Each club’s upgrade, $130 a year at Costco and $120 at the other two, pays up to 2% back on eligible purchases, which covers its extra cost only once you spend $3,000 to $3,250 a year there.

Before you join, compare unit prices at each nearby club and your usual store on 10 to 15 things you buy monthly. Multiply the monthly difference by 12, add any gas savings, and weigh the total against the fee. At a club that charges 20% less, a family of four that spends $150 a month on diapers, coffee, and paper towels saves $360 a year, more than five times a $65 fee. A single shopper buying mostly fresh food saves far less than that.

Join the club whose total beats its fee by the most. A household with no club nearby, or savings below the fee, does better at a discount grocer.

Shop from a list, split big packs with another household, and skip the bulk buys that go bad.

All three refund the year’s fee to an unsatisfied member, so treat your first year as a trial. Keep your receipts, price the same items at your old store, and cancel for a refund if the savings fall short of the fee.
