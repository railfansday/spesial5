When to Tuck Your Trousers Into Boots This Fall and Winter

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Consider this your cheat sheet for every pants-and-boots combo.

Every fall, the same fashion question finds its way back into our closets: Should you tuck your pants into your boots, or let them fall over the top? As a Southerner, who loves a good cowboy boot, I’ve been debating this topic for years. It’s a deceptively tricky styling decision. One minute, you’re channeling an effortlessly chic equestrian look; the next, you're wrestling with a pair of jeans that won’t stay put inside your knee-high boots. And sometimes, if you’re like me, your pants just aren’t able to squeeze into your boots, so you have to let it all hang out.

Whether you're reaching for riding boots, chunky combat boots, ankle booties, or a slouchier silhouettes, the secret is knowing when a tuck works—and when it's better to let your pants do their thing. Below, please see my definitive guide on when to and when to not tuck those pants into your boots. And there’s no better time than today to figure it out, since we’re deep into fall and heading toward winter.

I know there’s a debate every day on the internet about skinny jeans being dead, but they are alive and well during the winter months! You need a good skinny jean for all your tall boot days. Yes, boyfriend jeans and ’90s baggy are popular, but those cuts are not sliding into your leather knee-highs.

Maybe because I love a good lazy girl fall, there’s nothing better than tucking your baggy sweats into your Ugg boots. It works great for the overall vibe you’re curating and keeps your pants from dragging on the dirty ground.

A straight-leg jean is sublime with this style of pointed boot. Whether it’s actually a bootie or rises to your calf, keep these untucked for a classic day-to-night moment.

Similar to straight leg, there are very few occasions where I personally believe you need to tuck some flare pants into a boot. They are the star of the look, creating a silhouette that you’d lose if tucking them in. This is also the case for flare jeans with cowboy boots. Let the boots poke out a little while the jeans do all the work.

Similar to sweats and a shearling boot—but opposite of the straight leg—if you’re going to rock one of my favorite styles, the slouchy boot, you’ve gotta tuck. It’s so fab and gives that cool girl, 2016 era, full-beat party girl energy. So, if you plan to go slouchy in the next few weeks, make sure your pants are baggy enough to handle the tuck.
