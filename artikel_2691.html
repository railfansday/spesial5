Under new chief marketing officer James Ruth, the Dallas Mavericks are telling a fresh story

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

A blue rotary phone sits on a stand. On its dial, a badge with the number 11. One by one, fans from across the metroplex — fathers and sons, aunts and uncles, young and old — step up and dial 1-1.

"I have a son that adores you," one fan says. "And we love your game, and we hope you have a phenomenal year, and you tear it up this year for the Mavericks."

They welcome Kyrie Irving back home. They thank him, congratulate him and motivate him as he returns to the American Airlines Center after more than 600 days away. After a long road of blood, sweat, and recovery.

"Kyrie Irving, you're an inspiration. Setbacks happen, but you can't stay down. You gotta keep getting back up, and that's what you're showing the world. And for that, we'll always be grateful for your leadership. You'll always have a special place in my heart."

When they speak into the phone, they speak to him like family. Their uncle, son, brother, friend. It's a simple video produced by the Mavericks content team, but it speaks to the connection between the franchise and its fans.

There is a new story being told in Dallas, and if you haven't taken notice, it's time you did.

"What makes sports an amazing thing is the idea that it's a community connector," James Ruth, Dallas Mavericks Chief Marketing Officer, said in a recent exclusive interview with Mavs Moneyball. "We have these opportunities to create sort of collective moments, and what an awesome thing to be able to do that in a city like Dallas, Texas. To serve as a sort of connected device. And oh, by the way, we get to watch players like Cooper and Kai and PJ and all these others. It's an amazing thing."

For Ruth, who was hired in July after serving as CMO for the Tampa Bay Buccaneers for the last three years, this is a return to Texas. He led the launch of MLS club Austin FC during the pandemic and did his undergraduate work at SMU. Now in Dallas, Ruth leads a talented group of producers and creators to tell the story of the Mavericks and the fans that support them. The rotary phone video, "From Dallas with Love", is one of an already long list of pieces that have captured the attention of the fan base. Spend just a short time on the Mavericks social media comment sections, and you'll see this revitalized content team isn't going unnoticed.

"We have these opportunities to create sort of collective moments, and what an awesome thing to be able to do that in a city like Dallas, Texas. To serve as a sort of connected device."

Team media content isn't new, of course. But in seeing what Ruth and his staff have put out in this young season, it is immediately apparent that the Mavericks' story is being framed with new eyes and new energy for a new era. Take, for instance, a series of clips that have popped up after the team's first two NBA Cup games. The experience is visceral. It gives you the highlights from a 116-111 win over the Houston Rockets, but it also puts you in the stands. In the above clip a static camera from a fan's view hyper-cuts the climax of the stadium roar with the calm before the storm. We already know the outcome, but reliving the moments, the push and pull of the theater of sport, is what draws you in and makes you want to be at the American Airlines Center the next time the magic happens. And you'll notice it isn't just telling the story of the game, but also the story of those working behind the scenes, and the sensory connection and contribution fans bring to a win.

"We want to get back to the fans understanding the role with this team, which is: y'all are part of it. You are all creating this experience at AAC. And that's a thing where players pick up on it."

This inclusiveness is an intentional and impactful choice. It is no secret that the last 20 months have often felt tense between Mavericks fans and the organization. We're barely one year removed from the arena echoing with chants to fire the team's general manager. That road created distance between the fan base and leadership. For the last six months, team governor Patrick Dumont took steps to mend some of that – to start, by bringing in Masai Ujiri to run basketball operations. In his opening remarks, he made clear his objective was bringing joy back to the fans. He's now revamped the organization by hiring Mike Schmitz to help lead basketball decision-making, and Dusty May as head coach, who, to start the season, has been something of a breath of fresh air in strength and leadership.

In bringing Ruth into the fold to tell the team's story, the team has someone with experience in franchise transitions: in introducing Austin FC to the city for the very first time and in taking over the Tampa Bay Buccaneers brand just after Tom Brady retired. Whether that's coincidence or not, Ruth and his team have a wealth of experience in helping fans and franchises navigate new eras. And his latest opportunity in Dallas invigorates him.

"I think the opportunity that we have with the Mavericks is arguably the best opportunity in North American sports, period, full stop," he said about what drew him to the role this summer. He quickly understood what the team needed to do in "making sure that the Mavericks are always creating a world where players, fans, front office, they all feel like they're part of the same ecosystem."

"It's all getting to this North Star idea of how are we blurring the lines between players and fans. Because when we blur that line, everybody feels more ownership and engagement."

Some of that connection also comes through seeing players in new and different ways — not just who they are on the court, but who they are as people. The team has done plenty of this in the past and has highlighted their work in the community. In this season's Home & Away series, fans get to see what made the player who they are today. Giving a voice to the player beyond their reach on the floor.

The season is still developing. With so many moving parts and new pieces, on the floor and off, expectations on where the team could go were relatively quiet. Their surprising record in a very tough early schedule injected even greater energy into the fan base and team. Winning does wonders for the outlook of a franchise. Inevitably, just like the team's most recent road trip, there will be valleys ahead. Cooper Flagg and many of the key pieces on the team are still learning and evolving as players, individually and together. And that's why, for Ruth and his team, it is important to fortify the relationship and continue following their North Star in the way they capture everything that happens ahead.

"It's the power of fandom. When you make sure that you are spending as much time on what's happening on the court as you are on what's happening in the stands…if you walk too far away from what's happening in the stands, you start to walk away from what I believe is the sort of core value proposition to sports, which is that connective device."

It remains to be seen where this season and beyond will go. But maybe now this team and its fans are ready to write a new chapter.
