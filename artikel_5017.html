Bitcoin’s smallest miners face a decade-old payout problem that may require middlemen

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

P2Poolv2 wants buyers to aggregate small miners' reward claims, but the proposed marketplace could introduce new dependencies into decentralized mining.

Bitcoin's push to decentralize mining is confronting a decade-old problem that makes it harder for smaller operators to receive rewards.

A reconstruction of 2,545 Bitcoin blocks mined by P2Pool, an early decentralized mining pool, found that none of the 15,865 payouts recorded in July 2014 fell below 0.001 BTC.

The findings, published by a former P2Pool node operator and highlighted by Bitcoin Optech on Nov. 20, expose a constraint that developers are still struggling to overcome.

Unlike traditional mining pools, which typically distribute earnings through their own payment systems, P2Pool paid miners directly from Bitcoin's block reward transaction, known as the coinbase.

However, each recipient requires a separate transaction output, increasing the transaction's size. Mining firmware restrictions and competition for block space limit how many miners can receive direct payments.

To manage that constraint, the original P2Pool increased the difficulty of qualifying mining shares when a miner's expected payout fell below 0.001 BTC.

As a result, smaller miners earned qualifying shares less often, making their payouts less predictable.

P2Poolv2 is developing a different approach that would allow smaller operators to sell their mining shares rather than depend on direct block rewards.

Under the proposed system, larger miners would continue receiving Bitcoin through coinbase transactions, while smaller participants could sell their shares to buyers willing to aggregate them.

Those buyers would collect the eventual Bitcoin rewards if their combined shares qualified for direct payouts.

The arrangement would give smaller miners access to earlier payments while transferring the uncertainty of future block rewards to buyers, who would seek discounted prices for assuming that risk.

However, the model introduces another challenge: smaller miners would depend on buyers offering acceptable prices before their shares expire.

Developers have also warned that large miners could divide their computing power across multiple identities to secure limited direct payout slots, potentially crowding out smaller participants.

P2Poolv2 currently operates its sharechain, accounting and direct payout systems on Bitcoin's testnet5, while its proposed marketplace remains under development.

Its success will depend on whether that marketplace can attract enough buyers to pay smaller miners without concentrating reward distribution among larger operators.
