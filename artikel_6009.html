American Airlines has the best overall U.S. program for making credit card spending count toward status, and you don’t need one of its expensive premium cards to take advantage of it.

The Citi® / AAdvantage® Platinum Select® World Elite Mastercard® [See rates and fees] earns one Loyalty Point per eligible dollar, without a cap. You can combine those points with what you earn flying, shopping and through many of American’s other partners, all the way up to Executive Platinum. Meanwhile, the card’s checked bag benefit can cover its annual fee on a single family trip.

I recently covered the card’s refresh, which adds Walmart+, an inflight credit and travel protections while raising the annual fee. Those changes take effect January 24, 2027. They improve the card for some customers, but the reason to put spending on it remains earning status.

American’s current offer is 50,000 bonus miles after $2,500 in purchases within the first three months. There’s no annual fee for the first 12 months then $99, increasing to $150 with renewals starting January 24, 2027. That means for new applications, after the introductory year, the card’s annual fee is $150. Citi’s initial bonus restriction looks back 48 months. You aren’t eligible if you’ve received a Platinum Select new account bonus during that period, or converted another Citi card on which you earned a new account bonus during that period into a Platinum Select. A bonus on a different American Airlines card doesn’t, by itself, disqualify you. The miles are worth having. I’ve written about American’s partner award sweet spots, like 70,000 miles for business class between North America and the Middle East or Indian Subcontinent. And I find the miles useful for domestic travel too, often uncovering good mileage deals when cash prices are high (my sense that American doesn’t want to discount the fare, but doesn’t expect to fill the plane without doing so). Why American Is Best Overall For Spending Toward Status The card earns one Loyalty Point per dollar of eligible purchases. There’s no annual cap on that earning and no minimum flight requirement to qualify for American’s published status levels. Here are the thresholds, and the spending that would reach them if you earned nothing from flights, partners or separate Loyalty Point bonuses: AAdvantage Status   Loyalty Points   Card Spending Alone Gold   40,000   $40,000 Platinum   75,000   $75,000 Platinum Pro   125,000   $125,000 Executive Platinum   200,000   $200,000 American’s qualification year runs March 1 through the last day of February.If flights and other activity get you to 110,000 Loyalty Points, another $15,000 in eligible card purchases gets you to Platinum Pro. You can fill the gap with expenses you were going to incur anyway. The same Loyalty Points also move you toward Loyalty Point Rewards, including opportunities to select systemwide upgrades. Compare this with the UnitedSM Explorer Card (See rates and fees), which earns one Premier qualifying point per $20 and caps that earning at 1,000 points annually. That’s only part of the 6,000 points needed for United’s points-based Silver qualification path, which also requires at least four United or United Express flights. The similarly priced Platinum Select can take you all the way to American’s top published tier. Even Delta’s premium Reserve card takes $255,000 in purchases to earn Diamond through spending plus that one card’s annual head start. That’s 28,000 qualifying dollars, less the card’s 2,500-dollar head start, earned at one qualifying dollar per $10 spent. American’s $200,000 Executive Platinum threshold is available on this much less expensive card. Alaska’s Atmos™ Rewards Summit Visa Infinite® credit card ($395 annual fee) earns one status point per $2, without a cap, plus 10,000 status points annually beginning with the first anniversary. It can require less spending for particular tiers. American is my overall pick because of the combination of inexpensive cards, uncapped earning, and the ability to combine spending with so many other sources of status credit. The Citi Strata Premier® Card (See rates and fees.) earns three points per dollar on airfare, hotels, restaurants, supermarkets, gas and electric vehicle charging. Its points transfer to American, and its annual fee is $95. If I just wanted redeemable American miles, I’d generally prefer Strata Premier for those categories. The value in spending on this card is progress towards AAdvantage status. Free Checked Bags Can Pay For The Card The primary cardmember and up to four companions on the same reservation get their first checked bag free on eligible domestic American Airlines itineraries. American currently charges $45 online or $50 at the airport for the first checked bag on a standard domestic ticket. Basic economy costs $5 more. For two people each checking one bag on a domestic roundtrip, the savings are $180 compared with paying the standard fee online. That’s already more than the coming $150 annual fee. A family of four doing the same saves $360. You don’t have to buy the tickets with this card to receive the bag benefit. Your AAdvantage number needs to be on the reservation. The whole itinerary must qualify as domestic American Airlines or American Eagle travel. The primary cardmember must travel. An authorized user doesn’t get an independent bag allowance when traveling alone. If your status or cabin already includes a free bag, holding the card doesn’t add another one. You also get Group 5 preferred boarding, including up to four companions on the same reservation. That puts you ahead of later economy groups and improves your chances of finding overhead bin space. There are no foreign transaction fees, and authorized users can be added without an additional annual fee. Their spending earns miles and Loyalty Points for the primary cardmember. Holding an eligible AAdvantage card also keeps the miles in the primary cardmember’s account from expiring. What The January Refresh Adds The annual fee rises $51. Key incremental benefits are: Walmart+ annual membership. The primary cardmember gets the $98 annual plan, including delivery benefits, fuel savings and a streaming choice. Someone already paying for it can come out ahead from this benefit alone. Up to $100 in annual inflight credits. Eligible food, drinks and Wi-Fi purchases qualify; Admirals Club purchases don’t. This replaces the existing 25% food and beverage rebate. Half off an annual FOX One subscription. I’d assign this value only if it’s something you already want to buy. The inflight credit is better than the old rebate for someone making a modest amount of purchases. Spend $100 on eligible food and drinks and you’d receive $100 back instead of $25. The added travel protections may be more useful than the subscription benefits. Trip delay: Up to $500 per covered traveler for eligible expenses after a covered delay of at least six hours. Paying just the taxes on an AAdvantage award doesn’t limit reimbursement to that small cash payment. Trip cancellation and interruption: Coverage is limited to the lesser of $5,000 per trip or the covered amount paid with the card and/or AAdvantage miles. Lost baggage: Up to $3,000 per covered traveler, with a $10,000 total per trip; a lower per-bag limit applies to New York residents. Rental cars: Up to $75,000 for covered damage, for eligible rentals up to 31 days. Coverage is secondary in the U.S. and primary outside it. These protections begin with the refresh. Required payment methods and covered reasons differ by benefit. The card also retains up to $180 in Turo credits per calendar year, limited to $30 per eligible completed trip. You need six rentals to use the full amount, must link the card to your Turo account, and need to pay directly with it rather than through a digital wallet. There’s also 10% off eligible American Airlines Vacations packages. The $20,000 Spending Bonus Is Useful, But Do The Math Currently, spending $20,000 during your cardmembership year and renewing the card earns a $125 American Airlines Flight Discount. The refresh increases that to $150. The new discount adds 0.75% of the $20,000 in spending, assuming you use its full value. The increase itself is only another 0.125%. This isn’t an automatic annual flight credit just for holding the card. Under the published terms, the certificate expires one year after issue and can’t pay award-ticket taxes. Your cardmembership year also isn’t necessarily the same as the March-to-February status year. I’d count this as an extra return if I were already putting $20,000 on the card for Loyalty Points. I’d also consider moving a little spending to finish the threshold if I were close. I wouldn’t move $20,000 away from much better earning elsewhere simply because the flight discount happens to match the annual fee. Which American Airlines Card Should You Have? Platinum Select doesn’t include Admirals Club access or a general discount on award redemptions. The higher fee and extra benefits don’t change that. For an occasional American Airlines flyer without status, I’d choose it when checked bag savings and benefits I’d actually use cover the renewal fee. The first year’s $0 annual fee and initial bonus make trying it particularly easy. For someone who already has status, or another American Airlines card, I’d compare these options: Citi® / AAdvantage® Globe™ Mastercard® [See rates and fees] The $350 annual fee buys four Admirals Club Globe passes each calendar year, among other benefits. More interesting to me, the Flight Streak Bonus earns 5,000 additional Loyalty Points after every four qualifying American Airlines flight segments, up to 15,000 per qualification year. You don’t need to put spending on Globe to earn that flight bonus. Citi® / AAdvantage® Executive World Legend Mastercard® [See rates and fees] At a $695 annual fee, this is the card for an Admirals Club membership. It also awards 10,000 bonus Loyalty Points when you reach each of 50,000, 90,000, 165,000 and 240,000 Loyalty Points in a qualification year. Those thresholds can be reached through flights, partners or spending on other American Airlines cards. Citi® / AAdvantage BusinessTM World Elite Mastercard® [See rates and fees] Business owners should compare its separate initial bonus and spending benefits. Through December 31, 2028, eligible employee-card spending earns a Loyalty Point per dollar for the employee and an additional Loyalty Point for the primary cardmember. That’s status credit for two people from the same spending, rather than two points for one person. For instance, someone holding Executive can put dining and gas purchases on Platinum Select for two miles per dollar, earn the same basic Loyalty Point per dollar, and still trigger Executive’s account-wide Loyalty Point bonuses. They’d need enough value from Platinum Select’s extra miles, benefits and flight discount to justify its separate annual fee.
