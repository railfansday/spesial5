Startup Spotlight: An engineer’s search for better, safer baby products led him to launch Birch

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Startup Spotlight profiles tech startups in the Pacific Northwest.

by Lisa Stiffler on Nov 20, 2026 at 9:41 amNovember 20, 2026 at 9:41 am

A new baby can be the ultimate anxiety trigger: When will she sleep through the night? What if nursing is a challenge. What is that rash?! Given the countless unknowns, the last thing a parent needs are worries about the quality of the baby’s crib or the chemicals in a pacifier.

But when Karthik Gangiredla was preparing for the birth of his first daughter, he and his wife spent hours trying to figure out which baby products were trustworthy.

“We struggled a lot,” Gangiredla said. “We did a lot of research on what was safe, what’s not.”

An engineer with roughly 20 years of experience in supply chains and logistics, including roles at Amazon, Flexport and TikTok, Gangiredla found himself on the consumer side of the retail equation, scrutinizing strollers and bottles.

To spare other parents the same headache, he turned that research into Birch, an online marketplace that offers baby product buying guides and assigns each item a TrustScore. Its scoring system draws on certifications, lab reports and recall databases to help parents assess goods ranging from baby wipes to teething toys.

The site provides information on more than 10,000 items across 110 brands, including manufacturing locations, suppliers, certificate numbers and test reports.

Birch has contracts with brands it has checked out, and serves as their merchant of record, meaning if a customer has issues with a product or wants to return it, Birch handles those steps. It operates fulfillment centers in Los Angeles and New Jersey.

Gangiredla said the startup flips the e-commerce approach of retail giants, focusing on the shoppers’ needs, not those of the merchant. It does not accept ads or sponsored rankings.

If you took away the ad revenue for Amazon or TikTok, “the business wouldn’t exist,” Gangiredla said. “So it’s very difficult for them to approach the problem in a different way.”

The platform also offers a gift registry and tools for tracking fetal development during pregnancy, an infant’s nursing and sleep schedules, and other developmental steps. The tools are free for users.

We caught up with Gangiredla, Birch’s CEO and founder, for this edition of GeekWire’s Startup Spotlight.

In 50 words or less, give us your startup’s elevator pitch

Birch is the trust-first platform for baby and kids products and parenting resources. We research and score products across 50+ safety and quality signals before listing — and stock only what clears the bar — so parents skip the endless research and buy with confidence, from pregnancy through the toddler years.

What problem are you obsessed with solving?

The impossible research burden on new parents. Every purchase feels like a safety decision, the information is scattered and often unverified, and the stakes feel enormous. We’re obsessed with moving the work of figuring out what’s trustworthy off the parent and onto a system that does it rigorously — and then showing our work.

What surprised you after talking to customers?

How universal the trust problem is. We assumed it would track with income or with first-time parents, but our customers span all 50 states and every kind of household — big metros to small towns. And what they consistently want isn’t more options or a lower price; it’s someone to tell them, credibly, what’s actually worth buying.

How has AI changed the way you build your company?

It’s core, not a feature. Our TrustScore engine evaluates thousands of products across dozens of safety and quality signals in ways a human team our size never could, and the same intelligence powers discovery and personalization. It also lets a very lean team operate like a much larger one — and we now license that engine to other baby and kids brands as a product called Juno.

What’s one thing people misunderstand about your startup?

That “curated” means limited or expensive. Curation isn’t about carrying less for its own sake, or about selling premium — it’s about removing the untrustworthy. In our own data, price and trustworthiness barely correlate; some of the highest-scoring products are among the most affordable. Fewer and better, not fewer and pricier.

What’s the toughest decision you’ve made in the past year?

Turning away brands and revenue. When you’re early, every dollar is tempting — but our entire promise is that everything on Birch has earned its place. Saying no to products and partners that didn’t clear the bar, even when the revenue was real, was hard. It’s also the decision that defines what we are.

What’s the one piece of advice you give to other entrepreneurs?

Earn trust before you try to scale it. It’s slower and less glamorous than chasing growth, but trust compounds and shortcuts don’t. And talk to your customers far earlier, and far more often, than feels comfortable.

…a parent’s first instinct, the moment they find out they’re expecting, is to come to Birch — and “did you check the TrustScore?” is just how families talk about buying for their kids.
