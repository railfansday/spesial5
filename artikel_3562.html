The 65-inch LG Evo C6 OLED TV has dropped to $1,399.99 from $1,599.99, a $200 price cut that brings it to its lowest price ever, according to price trackers—and the savings aren't limited to this particular size either, with discounts extending across the C6 lineup, from the compact 42-inch model to the massive 83-inch version, although the 65-inch screen is particularly appealing if you want a larger OLED without spending more than $1,500. You also get a 90-day free trial of Amazon Music with the purchase and complimentary room-of-choice delivery (which would otherwise cost you an extra $19.99).

Beyond the discount, there's plenty to like about the C6 itself, with PCMag calling it an excellent midrange OLED that is slightly brighter than last year’s Evo C5 while still retaining its color accuracy and sleek design, giving you much of what makes LG’s flagship OLEDs appealing at a considerably lower price. The nearly bezel-free design also keeps the focus on the picture, while its 4K OLED panel delivers bright HDR highlights in Dolby Vision, HDR10, and HLG content. That should work well in most living rooms, although a particularly sunny space may still benefit from a brighter Mini-LED TV.

For streaming, LG’s webOS platform covers most major services, and AirPlay and Google Cast make it easy to share videos or photos directly from your phone. You can also use hands-free voice controls through LG's assistant, Google Gemini, or Microsoft Copilot to search for content or ask questions without reaching for the remote. Gamers will also appreciate the C6’s native 120Hz panel, which supports variable refresh rates up to 165Hz, along with AMD FreeSync Premium and Nvidia G-Sync compatibility to help keep fast-moving games smooth and tear-free. It’s also highly responsive, with an input lag of just 4.6 milliseconds at 1080p120 and 12.8 milliseconds at 4K60.

The C6’s main drawback is its Wi-Fi 5 connection, which feels dated for a TV at this price, although it should still handle most 4K streaming without trouble. It also lacks an ATSC 3.0 tuner, meaning you won't get support for newer over-the-air broadcast standards.
