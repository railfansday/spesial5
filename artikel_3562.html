Xbox Is Looking For Smarter Ways To Stop Its Next Console From Costing A Fortune

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

No one knows yet exactly what the next-gen Xbox console, Project Helix, will be, but most people are convinced it won't be cheap. The ongoing memory shortage has driven current-gen console prices skyward since 2024, with the Xbox Series X rising from $500 in 2020 to $800 in 2026. Project Helix is projected to cost as much as $1,800 at launch, but Microsoft aims to keep the price down when it eventually releases the device.

According to VP of next-gen Jason Ronald, techniques like neural rendering could help manage costs. "It's definitely a challenging time, but as a system and a platform designer, it's also one of the most exciting times, because we have to invent entirely new technology to continue to advance the state-of-the-art while also continuing to make things accessible and affordable," Ronald said in the Xbox 25th Anniversary Magazine. "This is a big reason why we're really focused on advancements like neural rendering, because it's an entirely new way to generate a frame without requiring more computational horsepower, additional memory, and things like that. I think you're going to see more innovation over the next three to five years than you've seen in the last 10 years."

In a nutshell, neural rendering uses AI to predict and generate high-quality video game frames while using only a portion of hardware power, similar to upscaling technoligies like PSSR, DLSS, and FSR. This could enable next-generation visual leaps while keeping the console accessible and reasonably priced for consumers. The other benefit is that the AI software models can improve over time through updates, as console hardware remains fixed over its lifespan, hence Ronald's quote about improvements reaching Project Helix over a few years into its run.

It remains to be seen how these breakthroughs will be spread across Project Helix, which Ronald teased as a family of devices that can address home, mobile, and cloud gaming needs. Xbox suggesting that it's working smarter to deliver an affordable gaming experience is an idea that Shawn Layden touched on recently, as the former PlayStation boss spoke about how Sony should be less concerned with chasing improved graphics and rather focus on making a cheaper PS5-level console instead.

Meanwhile, Sony is squeezing more life out of the PS5 generation with AI-guided upscaling technology, and the base model PS5 got its own version of this in the form of Quick Spectral Super Resolution. Originally available for only two games, QSSR is coming to more titles in the future, like Control Resonant, Arc Raiders, and No Man's Sky.
