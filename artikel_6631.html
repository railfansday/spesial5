3 Easy Hacks To Make Instant Mashed Potatoes WAY Better

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

You might prefer homemade mashed potatoes, but sometimes you just need a shortcut—and on days like that, grabbing the box is WAY easier. But while the back-of-the-box directions will yield just-fine results, a few easy hacks will give you much better results.

As a recipe developer and professional cook, I don’t often make instant mashed potatoes, but I was excited by the challenge of leveling them up—without adding a bunch of extra ingredients or steps that take the convenience out of this convenience food. So I tried making instant mashed potatoes every way I could think of (including the way the box says!). In the end, I found three tricks that, while super simple, massively improve that final bowl of mashed potatoes.

Here are three ways to instantly upgrade instant mashed potatoes.

Most boxes tell you to rehydrate instant mashed potato flakes in hot water enriched with a little butter and a few tablespoons of milk—but I’m going all in on the milk here. The extra dairy really fills out the flavor of the mashed potato and makes it creamier—especially if you’re using a higher-fat milk, like whole or 2%.

When swapping milk for water, it’s best to increase the liquid amount just slightly. For some reason (totally scientific, I’m sure!), the potato flakes require more milk than water to hydrate properly. I recommend 2 extra tablespoons for every half cup of instant mashed potatoes.

Also, add the extra milk the box typically calls for. If you leave it out, the potatoes will be dry. When I was testing, I found it didn’t really matter when that second amount of milk went into the pan (at the beginning versus just before the potatoes), but some boxes emphasize putting it in cold just before adding the potatoes—so if you want to do that to make you feel safer, be my guest!

All the instant mashed potatoes I made lacked seasoning—until I doubled the salt. Then they were perfect. Now here’s the thing: I was testing with Diamond Crystal kosher salt, which is less salty by volume than table salt or Morton’s kosher. The back of the instant mashed potato box didn’t specify what type of salt they used, so if they tested with a fine salt or Morton’s kosher, maybe ¼ teaspoon was the perfect amount. But in my kitchen with Diamond Crystal, it was woefully underseasoned.

Maybe you’re nervous about oversalting and don’t want to double or even slightly increase the salt right away. But regardless, I’m begging you: Taste those potatoes before you serve them and don’t feel bad if you have to add another hefty pinch.

You might assume that mashed potatoes should be made in a pot—I definitely did. But the fluffiest, creamiest instant potatoes I made in this whole testing process came from a wide, high-sided sauté pan. The reason for this? Absolute science.

When adding the instant potato flakes to the hot liquid, the goal is to distribute them as evenly as possible without working them too much. Yes, the back of the box says to “stir” the potatoes in, but in testing, I quickly discovered that even a few strokes can quickly lead to gummy potatoes.

Here’s where the pan size comes in. Using a wide pan increases the liquid's surface area, so you can sprinkle the potatoes evenly over the top and pat them down into the liquid instead of stirring. From there, let them stand until they’ve absorbed the liquid, then give them a few stirs. Trust me: They’ll be way fluffier because you messed with them less!
