The Best Air Fryers For Faster, Crispier Cooking (2026)

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Buzzy as they’ve become over the past several years, air fryers are really just small ovens with fans built into them, i.e., convection ovens that circulate hot air around your food. Our years of testing air fryers (and we’ve used over two dozen in that time) have taught us that there are plenty out there that can give you crispy-enough results—but the very best air fryers churn out not only golden brown French fries and crispy-skinned chicken wings, but tender fillets of salmon and even whole roast chickens at the touch of a button.

Air fryers can take a few forms; the robot-esque basket-style models are probably what come to mind when you think of an air fryer, but there are versatile countertop convection ovens that add bake, broil, toast, and slow-cook options to the air fryer functionality. Some even dehydrate and steam too. And most recently, more bowl-style air fryers have started to hit shelves, which swap a slide-in basket for a glass container and top-down, air-frying lid.

The best for all varieties of air fryer needs are below.

Most air fryers fit into one of three categories: oven, basket, or bowl.

A countertop convection oven, or air fryer–toaster oven, combines most of the crisping power of the basket-style air fryers you know and love with the multifunctionality you get from a toaster oven or countertop oven. Generally, the “air frying” function is achieved with a perforated slide-in rack, which usually means lots of cooking surface, but the racks are usually shallow and can require careful tossing or flipping of whatever you’re cooking, depending on the dish. But air fryer ovens, like the Breville Smart Oven Pro with its 13 different cooking modes, can do a lot, making them bona fide do-it-all kitchen appliances.

Basket-style air fryers aren’t as versatile as air fryer ovens, but they’re also less expensive and take up less space. They also tend to deliver better final results on traditional air fryer stuff like crisping up small foods that require shaking (tater tots, French fries, or Brussels sprouts), for which a handled basket is far easier to maneuver. Minuses are that they can be hard to clean and are limited in square inches inside the basket. They’re also sometimes made of plastic or nonstick-coated materials, which may not appeal to some consumers.

Bowl air fryers offer some of the best (and worst) of both worlds and also some unique benefits. Most of these include a heat-resistant glass bowl acting as the basket. Some models operate much like basket air fryers, with the glass container sliding into the base unit, while others opt for a “lid” that sits on top and houses the air frying mechanism. The glass baskets make these very easy to clean (included crisping trays are another story), however their functionality for shaking and pausing to flip foods can be cumbersome. But many come with lids for the glass containers, letting them double as prep and storage containers too.

What is an air fryer? Is it just a convection oven?

An air fryer is a countertop oven that uses hot air, circulated by a fan, to give foods crisper textures than you can achieve in a regular oven or toaster oven. What makes an air fryer a “fryer” is a built-in convection fan that blows the hot air around the food to crisp it up. It’s the combination of heat and moving air that produces the kind of crispy exterior you’d otherwise get from deep-frying or pan-frying. But unlike a deep fryer, an air fryer requires far less oil to achieve the desired effect—and it doesn’t leave your house smelling like a grease trap for days or weeks. So, technically speaking, yes, air fryers are just small convection ovens, but the most classic form of air fryer isn’t usually oven-shaped, rather more like egg or block with a slide-out basket on the front.

Whether you’re a purist and only consider products that contain baskets to be true air fryers, or you find the term air fryer to be stupid marketing jargon for a convection oven is up to you to decide. For the purposes of this review, we’ve included both toaster ovens that contain convection or “air fryer” settings and basket or bowl air fryers.
