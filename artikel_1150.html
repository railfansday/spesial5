Hapless and hopeless Man Utd bring curtain down on out-of-depth Carrick

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Hapless, hopeless and utterly humiliated. And that has to be that for Michael Carrick. It is surely inconceivable that he will continue any longer as Manchester United’s head coach. A proud club on its knees and a favoured son on his way out.

The former midfielder will carry the can. As managers always do. Even if the fault lines run so deep at United that it feels like they are almost irreparable. Owners – the Glazers and Sir Jim Ratcliffe – executives, players. A collective failing of monumental proportions. Although, again, it will be the manager who goes. Carrick knows it and it looked like it at the end. He knows the score, especially after this score.

More than four years ago Ralf Rangnick, the then interim manager, declared that United needed “open heart surgery”. Now there is barely a pulse.

An announcement must be imminent, with United turning to someone else in the fight against Premier League relegation. A joke? No, this abject performance against Bournemouth leaves United in 16th place, just a point above the bottom three, so the priority for the new man will be clear. Save them from the drop. They are 14 points off the top already, but forget that. It is irrelevant given their plight.

That is how far United have, once again, fallen. Fans in the Stretford End unfurled their “Standards” banner, bearing the faces of Eric Cantona, Roy Keane and Bryan Robson.

It was first revealed in December 2021 in Carrick’s final match as interim following Ole Gunnar Solskjaer’s sacking. This should be his final game again, under different, more traumatic, circumstances.

If it is, he is going out having made a stand. He dropped Bruno Fernandes for his outspoken comments, aimed directly at Carrick and his tactics, following the dismal 4-1 Champions League defeat away to Como on Wednesday.

At 2-0 down here it was telling that Carrick turned to Matheus Cunha when he substituted Youri Tielemans, who had been so terribly slow and ineffective, at half-time. Carrick did not bring on Fernandes. And he did not turn to the captain after, as he made four substitutions, including bringing on 19-year-old Shea Lacey. That said it all.

The television cameras catching Omar Berrada, the United chief executive, shaking his head in the stands as Carrick’s team gave away goals also told its own story. They have two wins from 12 in all competitions and none in eight. They have only beaten newly promoted Ipswich Town, in August, and Azerbaijan minnows Sabah this season. United were so careless it looked like they could not care less.

There was no guarantee, after Como, that Carrick would remain in charge after this game. So, losing and losing so embarrassingly – it was three, it should have been five – has to have obvious consequences. However cherished Carrick is. However much the fans excuse him. Yes, he has not been helped enough, but he has also not been good enough.

Finishing third and qualifying for the Champions League last season has proved to be the kind of bounce a new manager can achieve, after the pressure was relieved by the sacking of Ruben Amorim, rather than something more sustainable. There was always the fear that Carrick, despite his playing pedigree, might be out of his depth in this job. And so it is proving to be. Especially as he has been hung out to dry. Carrick certainly appeared resigned to his fate.

But what is he coaching? What is he trying to do with United? How does he want his team to play? Yes, there are limitations, but they are badly set up, tactically poor, and struggle to build attacks. The players are not responding, but it does not mean Carrick is blameless.

Bournemouth were not just collectively and individually better – and despite how good they are, that is some indictment of United – but better organised. They knew what they were doing, believed in it and relished it as they collected a fourth victory, and a fourth clean sheet in a row, after a slow start under Marco Rose, the new head coach. It was no shock.

Welcome to Telegraph Sport’s live coverage of Manchester United vs Bouremouth.

United enter Sunday’s clash against Bournemouth in the grip of a deepening crisis. Wednesday night’s dismal 4-1 Champions League defeat to debutants Como extended United’s’ winless run to seven matches across all competitions - their worst sequence since 2015.

With just one victory from their opening seven Premier League fixtures, United are enduring their worst start to a top-flight season since 1986-87. Head coach Michael Carrick, a multiple champion at Old Trafford during his playing days, finds his position under severe threat, with reports suggesting another defeat could seal his departure. While both of United’s wins this season have come at home, they remain winless in their last three at Old Trafford. On an individual level, Bryan Mbeumo has offered a rare bright spark, scoring four goals in seven league outings while Bruno Fernandes continues to search for form, and the captain’s recent outburst only makes the atmosphere more febrile.

In stark contrast, Bournemouth arrive in Manchester brimming with confidence. Marco Rose’s side have enjoyed a flawless start to their inaugural European campaign, sitting joint-top of the Europa League standings after a dominant 3-0 victory over Ruben Amorim’s ten-man AC Milan on Thursday. That result marked a third consecutive home win and clean sheet, with Bournemouth outscoring opponents 8-0 in that stretch. Midfielder Marcus Tavernier has been instrumental, leading the club’s scoring charts with five goals across 11 appearances.

Bournemouth will feel no trepidation visiting Old Trafford, having gone unbeaten in their last six Premier League meetings with United (two wins, four draws). With Carrick desperate for a stabilising result and Rose’s side riding high on European momentum, today’s fixture carries significant stakes for both clubs.
