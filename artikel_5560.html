Roofing Expert Michael Majeski Identifies Snow and Ice Load Risks That Can Damage Winter Roofs

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Michael Majeski, owner of Over The Top Roofing, explains how snow accumulation, ice dams, and preventive maintenance affect winter roof safety.

FULTON, N.Y., Nov. 9, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- How can homeowners protect their roofs from heavy snow and ice during Central New York winters? Michael Majeski, a roofing expert in Fulton, New York, explains how snow and ice loads affect roof safety in a HelloNation article. Michael Majeski owns Over The Top Roofing.

How Do Heavy Snow and Ice Loads Affect Roof Safety?

The HelloNation article explains that roofs are designed to support specific weight limits. When accumulated snow and ice exceed those limits, structural damage becomes a concern.

Wet, compacted snow weighs considerably more than light, powdery snow. The article emphasizes that homeowners should monitor accumulation rather than judge roof safety by snow depth alone.

Warning signs include sagging roof sections, cracked ceilings, and new leaks following winter storms. These conditions may indicate excessive structural stress requiring professional evaluation.

Why Do Ice Dams Cause Winter Roof Damage?

Ice dams develop when melted snow refreezes near roof edges. This frozen barrier can prevent additional melting snow from draining properly.

The HelloNation article explains that trapped water may move beneath shingles and enter attics or walls. Repeated moisture exposure can damage insulation, wood framing, and interior surfaces.

Michael Majeski, a roofing expert in Fulton, New York, highlights the importance of proper attic insulation and ventilation. Maintaining more consistent roof temperatures helps reduce the melting and refreezing that contributes to ice dam formation.

What Maintenance Helps Prevent Winter Roof Problems?

The article recommends inspecting roofs before winter to identify vulnerable areas around flashing, vents, and valleys. Addressing damaged shingles, deteriorating sealants, and existing leaks can reduce potential problems during storms.

Clean gutters also support drainage as snow melts. However, gutter cleaning alone cannot prevent ice dams caused by temperature differences between the roof and the air.

Homeowners may use roof rakes from the ground when conditions allow. The article cautions against climbing onto icy roofs because slippery surfaces create serious fall hazards.

When Should Homeowners Consider Professional Roof Evaluation?

Older roofs and structures exposed to repeated heavy snowfall may benefit from professional assessment.

The HelloNation article notes that some roofs may require structural reinforcement. Appropriate improvements depend on the roof's existing condition, construction, and expected snow loads.

Regular inspections and early repairs can help homeowners catch developing problems before winter weather makes them harder to address.

Michael Majeski emphasizes attic insulation and ventilation as important preventive measures. More consistent roof temperatures can help limit the melt-and-refreeze cycle.
