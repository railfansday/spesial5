West Capital Lending hit with 1099 independent contractor suit

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

A new lawsuit against West Capital Lending (WCL) alleges the company misused contractor and 1099 classifications, renewing scrutiny of loan officer pay models.  AI Summary

A former branch manager has filed a proposed class-action lawsuit against West Capital Lending (WCL), renewing questions about how mortgage companies use independent contractors and 1099 compensation structures for their staff in California.

In a complaint filed Nov. 12 in the Superior Court of California for the County of Orange, Jason Heaps alleges that Irvine-based WCL misclassified him and other California workers as independent contractors, depriving them of wages, breaks and other protections owed to employees under state labor law.

WCL and Heaps did not respond to HousingWire’s requests for comment.

According to the complaint, Heaps began working for WCL in November 2023 as a branch manager. In January 2024, he also took on internal technology responsibilities, including customer relationship management (CRM) administration, software development and systems integration. His employment ended in September 2025.

Heaps claims WCL classified him as a 1099 independent contractor while paying him a fixed recurring amount regardless of hours worked and still exercising control over his work. He alleges he reported to a manager, worked from an assigned office in Irvine, did not submit invoices, regularly worked more than 40 hours per week without overtime pay and did not receive his final paycheck after his employment ended.

“This treatment was not merely occasional, but reflected Defendants’ pattern and practice of willfully misclassifying Defendants’ employees as independent contractors,” the lawsuit states.In a statement to HousingWire, Morgan & Morgan founder John Morgan and attorney Michael Sigall, who represent the plaintiff, said they had filed “a class action lawsuit against West Capital Lending, Inc. alleging the company misclassified workers as independent contractors when they should have been treated as employees.”“As a result, workers were allegedly denied basic wage and hour protections California law guarantees. Hardworking people deserve to be paid every dollar they’ve earned, and we are committed to holding West Capital accountable,” they wrote.

WCL, which has about 1,900 sponsored LOs and originated about $10.8 billion year-to-date, has been the target of several lawsuits over its business and employment practices in recent years. Competitors, including loanDepot and Griffin Funding, along with former employees, have filed actions challenging the company’s model. WCL has denied wrongdoing in those cases.

The 1099 independent contractor structure is also part of loanDepot’s ongoing lawsuit against WCL. In that case, loanDepot alleged that WCL misclassified about 600 loan officers as independent contractors, paying them on a 1099 basis and avoiding employment-related costs.

“loanDepot’s investigation has revealed that all of its former California loan originators poached by WCL who still hold California licenses to originate loans switched from operating under the standard MLO license issued by the Department of Financial Protection and Innovation (DFPI), which requires mortgage loan originators to be employees of their sponsoring lender, to a ‘real estate salesperson with MLO endorsement’ license issued by the DRE [Department of Real Estate] after joining WCL,” the loanDepot complaint states.

The loanDepot complaint alleges that WCL’s California LOs perform exclusively mortgage loan origination duties, not real estate sales activities. WCL allegedly classifies its California originators as independent contractors while treating those in other states as employees. (WCL also sued loanDepot, alleging Truth in Lending Act violations).

California Labor Code Section 2775 codifies the so-called ABC test for determining whether a worker is an employee or an independent contractor. However, Section 2778(c)(1) excludes qualifying real estate licensees from the ABC test and directs their classification to California Business and Professions Code Section 10032(b).

That provision allows real estate brokers and salespersons to establish their relationship as either employer-employee or independent contractor. For other purposes under the Labor Code, the Borello multifactor test, which is a more flexible, fact-specific analysis, generally applies.Under the ABC test, which applies to most workers, an individual is presumed to be an employee unless the hiring entity establishes all three conditions: The worker is free from the company’s control and direction, both under the contract and in practice; performs work outside the usual course of the company’s business; and is customarily engaged in an independently established trade, occupation or business of the same nature as the work performed.

In the Heaps case, the complaint asserts that WCL’s treatment of the plaintiff “cannot meet any part of the ABC Test and Defendants knew it.”

The DFPI referred questions about this licensing and employment model to the DRE, which did not respond to requests for comment. The Department of Industrial Relations, which enforces labor law, also did not respond to questions.

The use of 1099 models for loan originators and branch personnel has become a flashpoint in California, where some lenders argue that compliance-focused firms are at a competitive disadvantage.

Alan Pezeshkian, president of California-based House America Financial, said he is not opposed to 1099 compensation structures in principle but is concerned about inconsistent application of labor and tax standards.

“When companies gain a competitive advantage by circumventing employment and tax laws, it creates an uneven playing field and penalizes those of us who make compliance a priority,” Pezeshkian said. “Let’s call it what it is: when a company improperly classifies employees as independent contractors to avoid payroll taxes and other employment obligations, that’s not innovation, that’s just gaming the system. And when that becomes a recruiting strategy, companies that follow the rules are effectively being punished for doing the right thing.”

Troy Garris, co-managing partner at Garris Horn LLP, said the inquiry under many laws focuses on control versus independence.

“Under many laws, a worker is an employee if the business can dictate not just what is done, but how, when and where it is done, and by whom. Independent contractors, by contrast, run their own businesses: they set their own methods, use their own tools, take on the risk of profit or loss, and are free to work for other companies,” Garris said. “Some laws make proving contractor status especially difficult, such as under California’s ABC test.”

Colgate Selden, a founding member of the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau and a shareholder at Baker Donelson, said hiring 1099 independent contractors, from a regulatory perspective, is a “dangerous game” because the definition of “employee” touches multiple regimes.

“Regulation Z does not define employees,” Selden said. “If a term is not defined within the regulation, you look to the contract and state law. So, then that’s gray.”Update: This story has been updated to include a statement from the plaintiff’s attorneys.
