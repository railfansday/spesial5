The Slowest Contracts Spend over 40% of their Time Waiting On Signers, ContractSafe Data Shows

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

ContractSafe's 2026 Contract Lifecycle Report is built on timestamp analysis from more than 55,000 contracts. For contracts that take more than three weeks from start to finish, the longest period is waiting for signatures â more than during approval and negotiation combined.

MALIBU, Calif., Nov. 12, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Among companies that use contract lifecycle management (CLM) software, half of contracts are signed within 2.8 days of being added, but the slowest 10% take 24 days or more. For the slowest contracts, 40% of that time passes after they've been sent to be signed, according to ContractSafe's 2026 Contract Lifecycle Report.

ContractSafe's 2026 Contract Lifecycle Report is built on timestamp analysis from more than 55,000 contracts.

For those slowest contracts, the biggest wait comes at the last step. Of the time they spent in the four steps the report tracks (waiting to start, in progress, approval, and signature), 44% went to waiting for signatures, more than twice the 19% that went to approval. That means most of the delay comes after the contract has already gone out. It's sitting with someone who only has to sign it.

"Everyone has a theory about why contracts are slow, and most of those theories come from surveys," said Randy Bishop, CEO and co-founder of ContractSafe. "We looked at what contracts actually did. Most are signed in days. The ones that get stuck are often waiting on one person, an approver who hasn't answered or a signer who hasn't signed. And these are teams that have visibility into their contracts. Teams working out of email and shared drives usually can't tell which contracts are waiting, or who they're waiting on."

The report, "Where Contracts Get Stuck (...and How to Fix It)," is ContractSafe's 2026 proprietary data white paper. It isn't survey results. It's built on real-world anonymized timestamps showing how contracts actually moved at companies that use ContractSafe, from August 2025 to September 2026, including more than 15,000 approval requests and 48,000 e-signature requests.

The report advises seven moves a team can make this quarter, including naming an owner the day a contract is added, reminding the one approver who hasn't answered, and using an intake form so new requests arrive with the key facts.

"Small and midsize teams don't need a six-month project to speed up contracts," said Ken Button, co-founder of ContractSafe. "They need to see which contracts are stuck and whose turn it is. Once you can see that, the fix is usually one reminder or one setting."

The full report is available free at https://www.contractsafe.com/2026-contract-data-white-paper. All data is anonymized and aggregated, and no company, contract, or person can be identified.
