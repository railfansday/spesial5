Susie Wiles Is Out. What Will Trump Do When He Runs Out of Women to Fire?

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

You know how after millions of people make clear that they despise not just your political project but also you personally, the logical response is to triple down on the specific things they hate?

That’s Donald Trump’s signature move. Three days after Democrats flipped seven Senate seats—nine if you feel bullish on Mississippi and Alaska—and secured a House majority, winning 253 seats and counting, the president announced that his chief of staff, Susie Wiles, would be replaced by Stephen Miller.

“I am pleased to announce that Mr. Stephen Miller, the current Deputy Chief of Staff of the Trump Administration, and one of my most trusted advisors, will become the Chief of Staff starting Monday, November 9, 2026,” he wrote on Truth Social. “Susie Wiles, who has done an outstanding job, will be moving over to the Trump Political Operation. I want to thank her for her service as Chief of Staff — She has done a great job!”

Even though polls indicating a blue wave proliferated before the election, this particular outcome still comes as a surprise. After all, Wiles had apparently proven her ability to ride out storms before—including everything that rocked Trump’s 2024 campaign, which Wiles managed, and her choice to sit for 10 interviews with Vanity Fair’s Chris Whipple in 2025, during which she discussed Elon Musk’s drug use and JD Vance’s true motives for embracing Trump. “Despite his travails, Trump needs Wiles and almost everyone around him knows it,” Whipple recently wrote in a follow-up story tracing rumors of Wiles’s imminent axing.

But now Wiles’s luck has finally run out. Why? Perhaps because she is, in the end, a woman.

Trump’s first term was marked by all sorts of chaos, including frequent arrivals to and departures from the White House. (Remember Omarosa?) But it doesn’t take Jessica Fletcher to detect that this time around, there’s a distinct pattern when it comes to whom Trump is willing to toss aside when the going gets rough. The scalps of the no-scalps era all have barrel curls. As Texas representative Jasmine Crockett observed in April, “He will throw the incompetent women under the bus a lot faster than the incompetent men.”

On March 5, following an uprising against the administration’s mass-deportation effort that culminated in two protesters being killed by ICE agents in Minnesota, Secretary of Homeland Security Kristi Noem was axed. After wrath over the administration’s botched handling of the Epstein files, Attorney General Pam Bondi was fired in April.

Lori Chavez-DeRemer, Trump’s labor secretary, resigned later that month amid a misconduct investigation. Tulsi Gabbard, the director of national intelligence, who couldn’t sell Trump’s disastrous war with Iran, submitted her resignation in May, after her husband was diagnosed with cancer. Press secretary Karoline Leavitt stepped down—of her own accord—in August. Following an embarrassing Wall Street Journal investigation, Kimberly Guilfoyle, the ambassador to Greece—and former fiancée of Donald Trump Jr.—was forced to resign last month.

Though you may have already forgotten all about her, Katie Zacharia had perhaps the least distinguished tenure of all: She gave her first briefing as Trump’s new press secretary on a Tuesday and was forced out by Friday of the same week. Shortly before Wiles’s ouster this week, Harmeet Dhillon, who helped kill DEI after joining the civil rights division of Trump’s Justice Department, announced she was stepping down.

All this, and Pete Hegseth still has a job.

What are we to make of the fact that Trump’s departed all happen to harbor two X chromosomes? Could it be, perhaps, that President “Grab ’Em by the Pussy” has a problem with women—even the loyalists who are largely responsible for helping him claw his way back into the White House in the first place? (Does Trump win, or even run, in 2024 without Wiles? It’s a possibility I’d like to see Rodham author Curtis Sittenfeld explore in an upcoming novel.)

The one thing we can be sure about is that, as of right now, there are only three women left in Trump’s 21-person Cabinet. The members of that trio—Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins, Education Secretary Linda McMahon, and Kelly Loeffler, administrator of the Small Business Administration—might want to watch their blowout. (So should Natalie Harp.) Perhaps after they go, Trump will start employing the principles of diversity, equity, and inclusion while stabbing his allies in the back.
