10 Questions The Body Season 2 Still Needs To Answer

The Body ended with a bang, but the hit coming-of-age drama left plenty of loose ends to tie up. Set in 2004 at a co-ed Catholic school in the Northeast United States, The Body follows the St. Mark's Lions, a high-octane dance team on the road to regionals. When a Halloween night hazing goes awry for the squad's newest member, however, the status quo is disrupted by a transfer student with potentially deadly ambition.

Between immense streaming success and a quickly-secured cult following, the smash-hit series is the latest addition to a new era of Netflix, characterized by thrilling stories with supernatural twists. Unsurprisingly, many viewers are already clamoring for The Body season 2, but the first eight episodes are filled to the brim with unresolved mysteries and burning questions that Netflix needs to answer.

From the very beginning, Lisbeth Anderson (Geena Meszaros) is desperate to join the Lions, though her dancing ability is nowhere near the level expected from the team. As such, when co-captain Anya Butler (Kristina Bogic) invites Lisbeth to the fateful Halloween party, the pair easily become partners in crime after Grace Franklin (Sofia Wylie) seemingly dies. For the bulk of the season, both girls keep their involvement a secret, only for everything to unravel in the final episode.

Whereas The Body season 1's ending sees Anya remorsefully confess to their cover-up, Lisbeth takes a different approach by telling her teammates she is the reason Grace came back from the dead. As her mother revealed earlier in the season, the cult Lisbeth grew up in hailed her as a healer, giving her the perfect backstory to manipulate the fairly gullible Lions. Yet, there's no telling whether Lisbeth's luck has run out or whether the team will fall under her spell once again due to their religious paranoia.

When Father Franklin (Nnamdi Asomugha) reveals that Grace has fallen into a minimally conscious state, the Lions immediately fear that it was somehow their fault. Slowly but surely, Lisbeth feeds into the panic by centering the narrative on the prank they played Halloween night: jokingly sacrificing Grace to Satan. While not every dancer was convinced they had actually communed with the Devil, the more impressionable students bought in quickly. The loudest contrarian, Elise Zakaria (Sara Boustany), decides to poke fun at them by staging a "vision" of Grace in the middle of mass.

Unexpectedly, her plan backfires as other Lions gradually pretend to see Grace themselves, save for Maddie Delaney (Shirley Chen). While The Body is forthcoming in stating the majority of the team faked their visions, Maddie— the only girl to honestly hold out— claimed she saw Grace during their performance at regionals. Similarly, Anya's visions of Grace were one of The Body's key thriller elements. As Elise posited, they could have been sleep-deprived hallucinations, but there's still a chance that Grace wasn't simply a manifestation of Anya's guilt.

Although the running assumption throughout The Body is that Grace either had a bad reaction to an ecstasy pill or was possessed by the Devil, the series never outright confirms what happened to her on Halloween night. The last time the viewer sees her as her normal self is when she wanders into the nearby woods, followed closely by Leo Anderson (Jackson Kelly) while Steven Maxwell (Orlando Norman) watches.

Before the twist ending of episode 1, it seemed like The Body was veering into murder mystery territory. Of course, the story changed once religious hysteria began to trickle in. Nevertheless, it's possible something happened to Grace before Anya and Lisbeth found her body. Granted, the show explicitly clears Leo, as he's the one who finds her unconscious later that night, but Steven makes a point throughout the season to mention that he blacked out on Halloween and has no recollection of his time spent with Grace.

Though the Andersons may not be the most dysfunctional family in film history, what we know about Lisbeth and Leo's upbringing is tragic. The children grew up in a religious fundamentalist group that their father led until his untimely demise. With the help of the Catholic Church, the two transfer to St. Mark's with their mother, Catherine (Louisa Krause).

Throughout The Body season 1, Catherine is an awful parent, with Lisbeth often having to act as both sister and mother to Leo. Via home videos, the viewer gets snippets of their life in West Virginia, but the true extent of Lisbeth and Leo's religious trauma remains to be seen. Likewise, it's suggested that Lisbeth may have been involved in her father's death, which would be a fascinating point to explore in season 2.

As the tongue-in-cheek title suggests, much of The Body is rooted in biblical horror. The motif is carried through nearly every aspect of the show, from the soundtrack to the set design. Yet, the most intriguing symbol is that of the snake, both literally and figuratively. When Lisbeth finds Grace's body in the woods, a snake appears to slither out of her hood. Similarly, on a snowy weekend retreat, Lisbeth wakes up to a snake under her blanket in the middle of the night.

Given her family's secret history of snake handling, Lisbeth accuses someone of messing with her, causing the group to suspect Elise, who is ostracized as the metaphorical snake of the team. Later, Anya and Elise discuss the possibility that Lisbeth planted the snake herself. While that's not unfathomable by any stretch of the imagination, The Body never shies away from showing the viewer every gritty detail of Lisbeth's schemes. Consequently, it doesn't make sense for the snake to be the one thing kept in the dark— unless, of course, it holds a deeper significance that season 2 can elaborate on.

Despite Grace and Leo initially seeming close, whatever friendship they had erodes when Grace looks through Leo's sketchbook and finds nude depictions of herself. The repressed teen boy explains that the drawings were meant to be an outlet for his "impure" thoughts, which makes it all the more interesting that each picture is modeled after an iconic religious artwork.

The Netflix thriller only shows a few sketches in full, but they seem to match the broad framework of the narrative, with Grace going from sacrificial lamb to a venerable saintlike figure over the course of the season. Maddie goes so far as to call him Grace's first apostle, so if there is a supernatural religious plot coming, Leo's sketchbook may reveal "Saint" Grace's true fate.

By the end of The Body season 1, Catherine has lost control of both her children. While she earnestly believes a holy reckoning is coming, Leo accepts Grace as his new deity, and Lisbeth seems completely disillusioned with spirituality as a whole.

After the teenagers diverge into their two distinct paths, Catherine stays home watching videos from their old church, specifically focusing on a sermon that claims they're immune to poison as long as they have faith. Sadly, it's implied that Catherine's religious delusion claims her life. With Lisbeth and Leo now orphaned, it's unclear what role—if any— they can play in season 2, unless a member of the community graciously takes them in.

In one of the most emotional scenes of the series, The Body's finale blows up Anya's relationships with Elise and Steven, which proves to be the final straw for the queen bee. As Lisbeth attempts to mastermind a new ruse at the debutante ball, Anya tearfully confesses to Father Franklin that she is a liar, cheater, and possible murderer.

Yet, Father Franklin may not have any reason to report her to the authorities. Despite Anya admitting to leaving a presumably dead body at the Franklin house on Halloween night, Grace wakes up before anyone can even call the police. With the young girl apparently back— and claiming to be God in the flesh— Anya's confession may have been in vain.

The Body set up numerous possibilities for Grace's death in episode 1, ranging from a snake bite to an overdose on ecstasy, but there was no explanation given for what happened after Lisbeth and Anya dropped off her corpse. Furthermore, everything that Grace experienced in the hospital— inexplicable seizures, miraculously breathing on her own, etc.— adds up to something science can't explain.

Netflix's smash-hit psychological drama The Body is full of complex characters, but some of the biggest players are more sympathetic than others.

Considering the camera took special care to show her ears during pivotal conversations, Grace could have heard everything that was said to and about her during her coma, inspiring the young girl to feed into her burgeoning sainthood. Some viewers even believe Grace faked the entire ordeal, which would both explain why the hospital's tests all came back normal and be a major twist that recontextualizes The Body's psychological drama.

To that end, The Body enjoys making the viewer question everything— including the genres it is categorized under. Despite the series being labeled as supernatural horror, there's an underlying skepticism baked into the narrative, as nearly every strange occurrence the main characters experience could be logically explained.

Regardless, The Body leaves just enough room for doubt to linger in the audience's minds. Be it Anya's so-called hallucinations, Grace surviving after her heart stops, or the countless little details meant to unnerve you, the drama delights in challenging you to separate the benign from the divine. Without question, The Body is designed to be enigmatic, but season 2 will have to decide whether the mass hysteria is truly groundless or if paranormal activity is haunting St. Mark's.
