EJAE Talks 'KPop Demon Hunters' Fame, New Holiday Song and GapStudio Campaign

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

It’s been quite the “Golden” year for EJAE, but the singer-songwriter is learning that staying on top can be just as challenging as getting there.

“I’m not gonna lie, it’s hard,” Kim Eun-jae, better known by her stage name, EJAE, exclusively told Us while reflecting on her career-altering year and latest venture as the face of GapStudio’s Winter 2026 campaign. “You find success in it and you get this award [and] it’s really cool, but weirdly it stresses you out more because it’s like you have to live up to that,” she told Us.

After years of working behind the scenes writing songs for some of KPop’s biggest artists, the Korean-American hitmakerr, 34, finally found her chance to shine thanks to the record-breaking success of Netflix’s KPop Demon Hunters.

“Because of Demon Hunters, it kind of allowed me to have a platform, a dream that I gave up as a kid that I didn’t think I deserved or didn’t think I could have,” she shared, reflecting on the opportunity to revisit her longtime ambitions as a performer. “Now that I have the platform, just to see if I can do it again, [I’m] kind of reopening a dream that I shelved, just to see what can happen and work hard on it.”

Before lending her vocals to Rumi in the animated hit, EJAE started training to become a KPop idol at just 11 years old, under SM Entertainment before the agency dropped her in 2015. Little did they know the star would later make a name for herself as a songwriter and go on to win an Oscar, Grammy and Golden Globe for cowriting and performing the chart-topping anthem “Golden” alongside artists Audrey Nuna and REI AMI.

“I think the biggest thing I learned with this whole journey is you’ll have years where things just don’t work out, and then you’ll have years where things do work out, like this year for me,” she told Us.

As she continues to find her voice as a solo artist, EJAE is already putting her own spin on another musical genre — just in time for the holidays. On November 13, she released her first holiday single “Christmas in New York”, which she says pays homage to the music she grew up listening to and the city she calls home. “I’ve always wanted to write a Christmas song,” she said. “It’s always been a dream of mine.”

Having spent part of her childhood split between New York, New Jersey and South Korea, EJAE said she wanted to capture what makes Christmas in the Big Apple so special. “I just miss the classic New York Jazz,” she said, explaining her love for the sound of artists like Frank Sinatra and Nat King Cole. “So I wanted to kind of hint at that, and just have fun with my lower register rather than hitting all those high notes.”

Calling the track a “love letter to New York,” she shared how the song even became a family affair, with her husband, Samuel Kim, stepping in as her recording engineer. The couple, who tied the knot October 17 after 9 years together, are also settling into married life — though EJAE admitted that not much has changed in their day-to-day routine.

“It’s not just us getting married, it’s like the families are getting married. We’re a family now and It’s a different mindset,” she shared. “ I think it’s just fun to be experiencing life with a partner.”

With plenty to celebrate this year, the musician is also moving into the fashion realm as the face of GapStudio’s Winter 2026 campaign, modeling a 12-piece capsule collection designed by Zac Posen.

Launching November 20, the collection gives Gap’s iconic staples an after-dark makeover, featuring figure-hugging knit dresses, structured denim, and tailored pieces available in rich shades of black, royal blue, and red. Prices range from $54 to $268, with the collection available online and at select Gap stores.

For EJAE, being featured in the campaign she says is a “huge milestone” and comes with a dose of nostalgia. “Growing up, I wore a lot of Gap,” she recalled. “We used to live in New Jersey growing up, and literally my comfort clothes were Gap sweaters, Gap hoodies and the Gap zip-ups, I used to wear it all the time.”

As it turns out, the singer was already a fan of the man behind the collection long before getting the chance to work with him. “He’s just an artist himself,” she said of Posen, 46, and his flair for fashion. “He has so much charm, and he’s just a very charming person, so talented … And such a great hype man.”

She even revealed that Project Runway, where Posen previously served as a judge, was her favorite reality show growing up, saying the designer’s passion and creative vision drew her to the campaign, though his personality certainly didn’t hurt. “The clothes are incredible. Like, it’s so classic and timeless, but still has a modern twist to it, which I love,” she told Us.

When it comes to her own wardrobe, the singer says she finds plenty of inspiration just outside her front door — with New York City serving as her inspiration. “New York has truly—it’s a place where I found my fashion sense. And also, as a songwriter, it’s where I grew as a songwriter,” she explained.

While the star also names Rihanna and Zendaya among the fashion icons she admires, she says her Korean heritage is one of her biggest influences—and that she even works with creative directors Jeffrey Jin and Theo Song to incorporate elements of her culture into her looks.

As for what’s next for the singer-songwriter, she says her next big goal is releasing an album, but admits she’s still figuring out her sound as a solo artist.

“I’m currently just continuously trying to figure out what my sound is, EJAE’s sound,” she told Us. “You know the music that people are familiar with, like ‘Golden’, is again for HUNTR/X. It’s for an animated film, so it’s not necessarily me, I would say. But I’m very excited for people to kind of follow me with my journey in finding that.”
