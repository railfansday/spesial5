"Does changing where games run cross a legal line?" Microsoft-owned Github ask in now-deleted post celebrating GTA 6's effect on PC port-focused PS5 emulator

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

"GTA 6 is finally here, sparking one of the biggest weeks in gaming and open source on GitHub"

'Yay, GTA 6 is out on consoles and lots of people are playing it' is a fairly harmless thing to say. Unless you're, I dunno, a development platform mentioning that in a post celebrating the recent success of a PS5 emulator used for making unofficial PC ports of console games. You know, the sorts of unofficial PC ports vibecoders and proper coders having been scrambling to make of GTA 6 since its Xbox executable was allegedly leaked. The sorts of unofficial PC ports publishers like Rockstar tend to quickly move to have taken down.

"GTA 6 is finally here, sparking one of the biggest weeks in gaming and open source on Github," read a Github tweet screengabbed by Kotaku before it was deep sixed. "The repo AnyPS5 has passed 58K stars since its August launch, rewriting PS5 executables to run on Windows and Linux. Does changing where games run cross a legal line? Share your take. Happy GTA VImas!"

A video alongside the post seems to have dug the hole even deeper. "The project rewrites a game's executable so Windows and Linux can run it directly," a presenter cheerily declared. "Performance seems to be the main issue for users wanting to run GTA 6, with one contributor telling the project's community to lock in and get their agents to help if they need to." From there, the "big question" of whether it's actually legal to change where and how your games run was posed by Github.

And now their tweet is deleted. Here was the text. https://t.co/kNbmmPCGdf

One can only imagine how raised the eyebrows of Rockstar affiliated lawyers may have become if they read the post before Github yanked it.

Another big question posed a lot recently is whether the threat of bodged PC recreations based on the alleged leaked executable may lead Rockstar to narrow the gap between GTA 6's console release and its eventual Windows launch, which reports have previously claimed is planned for the opening months of next year. You know, because the company and publishers Take-Two presumably don't want to risk the sales of an official port being undercut by an unofficial one, no matter the degree of impact that might actually have (my presumption would be the official port would still rake in a fortune).

Given the reception their baffling AnyPS5 post received, I doubt Github'll be providing any more commentary on the rush to unofficially port GTA 6 and the debate as to when it might hit PC officially.
