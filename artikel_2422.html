Alyssa Naeher announces retirement from professional soccer after 17 years

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

The prolific and longtime National Women’s Soccer League goalkeeper has announced her retirement from professional soccer. Naeher has been part of the NWSL since its inception in 2013, and has spent the last 11 seasons with the Chicago Stars / Red Stars.

“The decision to retire is never an easy one, but after a lot of prayer and reflection, I feel like it is the right time,” Naeher said, via a press release. “When I got traded from Boston to Chicago at the end of 2015, I felt like my world got turned upside down, but Chicago very quickly became my home away from home. I feel incredibly blessed to have shared the field with so many amazing teammates over the last 11 years, and have created a lifetime of memories on and off the field that I will always cherish. I want to thank the coaches and the organization for their support and belief in me. Captaining this team has been a great honor of my career, and shoutout to the fans for their continued support of me and the team.”

Naeher’s career began long before her time in Chicago, though. The goalkeeper began her professional career in 2010 with the Boston Breakers in the former Women’s Professional Soccer league (WPS). She spent two seasons with Boston before heading overseas to join Turbine Potsdam from 2011-13.

Naeher returned to Boston in 2013 — the first year of the NWSL. She remained with the Breakers through the 2015 season, where she won Goalkeeper of the Year in 2014. That season, Naeher recorded 106 saves in 24 matches, and was also named to the NWSL Best XI.

The veteran goalkeeper joined the Chicago Red Stars in 2016. She was nominated for Goalkeeper of the Year in 2016 and 2017. She was named to the NWSL’s Second XI in 2016. Naeher and the Stars went to the NWSL Championship in 2019 and 2021.

“There are no words to truly encapsulate what Alyssa has meant to the Chicago Stars,” Stars President Karen Leetzow said, via a press release. “She has lived through the greatest achievements and most trying moments in our history, and she has never wavered as a leader. Chicago has been fortunate to enjoy the greatest goalkeeper of all time in its backyard, while also sharing the joys of her journey on the game’s biggest stages. She is any teammate’s greatest advocate, on and off the pitch, and her partnership has been a gift to me personally in my role at the club. We wish Alyssa all the best, and we’re eternally grateful for all she’s done for the women’s game.”

Internationally, Naeher’s story is well known. A veteran backup behind Hope Solo, Naeher rose to the starter position for the U.S. women’s national team in 2016. She helped the U.S. win the 2019 FIFA Women’s World Cup, and the 2024 gold medal at the Paris Olympics. She was also the starter in the USWNT’s bronze medal tournament in 2020 in Tokyo.

Naeher was on the U.S. roster for the 2015 World Cup, where the USWNT captured a third title. She was named the U.S. Soccer Player of the Year in 2024.

Naeher retired from international play at the end of 2024, playing her final match on December 3, against the Netherlands.
