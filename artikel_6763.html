Are You Ready for the ’80s Fashion Revival?

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Rejoice: shoulder pads and opulence are back.

It happened suddenly: golden shades, huge shoulders, stacks of oversize jewelry. At Anthony Vaccarello’s swan song for Saint Laurent, it was all golden with big jackets, safari suits, and joyfully ruffled blouses fit for a Joan Collins-era Dynasty, but built for 2026. For spring/summer 2027, the ’80s have been a big focus. Valentino showcased jewel-toned gowns with piled-on maximalist pearls, and Giorgio Armani’s strong shoulders were straight out of a fashion history book. Mugler and Calvin Klein did strong silhouettes that alluded to the era. And even Louis Vuitton couldn’t help but look back to New York in the 1980s for its recent cruise collection, presenting models in stiff jeans and Keith Haring tops.

And why not? The ’80s were a time of experimentalism and personality, making it ripe to be mined for fashion inspiration and also less referenced than the forever hall of famer ’90s and Y2K style that fashion can’t stop loving.

The obsession has already started (or, rather, it never really ended) in the vintage world. Haile Lidow, founder of Lidow Archive, has seen a demand recently for her vintage drop waists and ruffle-skirt dresses in wild patterns. “I have so many in my collection that have gone neglected for years and are finally being noticed,” she tells ELLE. “Also, the ’80s power suits! They are back! Sometimes I update the skirt length if it feels too dated. We call it ‘minify’ but regardless, the requests for these are pouring in.”

For some, it’s about nostalgia, but for others, it’s totally new. Violet Sky, a 26-year-old Gen Z singer first became inspired to dress totally ’80s after watching the film Girls Just Want to Have Fun while she was in high school. “It was all the bright colors and just how fun everything was and the big hair,” she tells ELLE. “I just felt really drawn to it. I didn’t really have my own personal style, and so at that moment, I was like, well, why don’t I just explore this?” She started by pulling pieces from her mother and grandmother’s old wardrobe in her attic, and now exclusively wears acid-wash denim, tops bedazzled with rhinestones, and Reebok Classics every day.

“I think fashion, and interiors, and so many other aesthetic-based industries has been stuck in a really boring rut that’s been disguised as minimalism,” adds Lidow. “It started in the late ’90s as ‘chic’ and developed into an excuse for society to try less. The ’80s were the opposite: one bangle was never enough, you had to have four. Eyelids were splashed in bright colors and patterns were clashing all over the place. I think we’ve finally grown bored of the boring. We want to have fun again.”

Even fashion historians can’t help but think of the ’80s as a sort of golden age of fashion, when Vivienne Westwood and Christian Lacroix were sending highly creative pieces down the runway. Valerie Steele, director and chief curator of The Museum at the Fashion Institute of Technology, even associates the era with a shoe renaissance, which we’ve also been experiencing in 2026 with the rise of It shoes. “I think what we’re seeing in part may be a hope that we can come back to a period when fashion really was fashionable, because now we’re seeing so much dissatisfaction with fashion, and with the costs of fashion, not only personally, but also to the environment, and to workers and so on,” she tells ELLE.

The familiar feeling of power dressing in the air (and on the runways) with these ’80s silhouettes is deeply tied to the female gaze. “What you see in the ’80s is that it’s coming out of a feminist movement that was growing in the ’70s, and then in the ’80s became a somewhat different feminist movement, so it was more kind of, girly, economic feminism rather than a sort of leftist, ideological feminism of the ’70s,” adds Steele. “So instead of working-class androgyny, you had suits like men had. You had powerful shoulders, you had a look of Amazonian femininity going down the runway, and that resonated with a kind of booming market, and a period when maybe, maybe feminism was becoming more commodified, or that was certainly a period when people realized that they could market specifically to women by having things that look both powerful and sexy.” Who can argue with that?

“I think that bright colors and loud fashion of the ’80s is something that’s so opposite than what’s kind of like going on today at least in everyone’s everyday life versus the runway,” adds Sky. “People are kind of intrigued about that. And they want to maybe try something different.” The ’80s revival may be nostalgic, but its appeal feels entirely of the moment: it’s instant personality in an era of sameness.
