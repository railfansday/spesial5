Turkey Pot Pie

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Chicken pot pie is a perfect cozy dinner for chilly fall days, but like most classics, it could use an upgrade. This turkey pot pie is a perfect way to switch up your meal rotation and make sure those Thanksgiving turkey leftovers don’t go to waste. That being said, this dinner is so comforting, it deserves to be made all season long.

Pop your butter in the freezer for about 15 minutes before you start making the crust so it’s adequately cold. Grab a large food processor and pulse together the flour, sugar, and salt until they’re mixed. Toss in the butter and pulse again until you see pea-sized pieces and larger chunks.

With the motor running, add vinegar and four tablespoons of ice water, one tablespoon at a time, until the dough just comes together and becomes moist. Make sure the dough isn’t wet and sticky or too crumbly (you can test this by squeezing some with your fingers). If the dough is too crumbly and not coming together, add more ice water, 1 teaspoon at a time.

Turn the dough out onto a lightly floured surface and gently squeeze it together. Knead it just a couple of times so it comes together, then split it into two balls. Flatten each one into a smooth disk, wrap them up in plastic, and pop them in the fridge for at least an hour or even overnight if you want to get ahead.

Move your oven rack to the center, then preheat to 375°. Grab a big, deep skillet and melt the butter over medium heat. Toss in the onions and carrots, and stir until they’re soft. Give it about 7 to 8 minutes. Next, add the garlic and cook, stirring, just until it smells amazing (about a minute). Now, grab a spatula or wooden spoon and stir in the flour. Cook, scraping up any tasty bits from the bottom, until the mixture turns golden and starts to bubble—just 2 or 3 minutes.

Pour the wine in slowly, stirring as you go. Let it simmer for about 3 minutes, just until that alcohol smell is gone. Then, slowly add 1 1/3 cups of cream, still scraping up all the good stuff from the bottom. Keep cooking until the mixture starts to boil and thickens up a bit. This should take around 5 minutes.

Fold in the turkey, peas, thyme, and parsley. Give it all a good season with kosher salt and pepper. Take the skillet off the heat and let the filling cool until you’re ready to use it.

Butter up a 9" pie dish or a deep skillet for your crust. On a lightly floured surface, roll out one disk of dough into a circle about 16" across and 1/4" thick. Carefully lay the dough into the pie dish, letting the edges slump down naturally. Trim the dough, leaving roughly a 1" overhang. Finally, pour in the cooled filling.

Roll out the second disk of dough into a 16"-diameter round about 1/4" thick and arrange it on top of the filling. Fold the top overhang into the bottom, pressing firmly to seal. Crimp the edges if you’d like. Using a paring knife, cut slits on top, and brush with the remaining 2 tablespoons of cream and sprinkle with sea salt.

Bake the pie (cover the edges with foil if they start getting too brown) until the crust is golden and the filling is bubbling. This takes about 1 hour 15 minutes. Once it’s done, move the pie to a wire rack and let it cool for about 15 minutes before digging in.

Cover your pot pie tightly with plastic wrap or store in an airtight container in the fridge for up to five days after baking. If you’d like to make your pot pie ahead of time and freeze it, wrap the whole (baked or unbaked) turkey pot pie tightly in plastic wrap and store in the freezer for up to 2 months.
