Which WoW Forever ruleset to pick (and can you change later?)

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Instead of realms, you just choose a ruleset to play. It's a pretty way of saying "playstyle" - and World of Warcraft: Forever likes things pretty.

World of Warcraft: Forever opens your latest journey to Azeroth with a big first step, as logging in for the first time gives you a choice of rulesets. No sign of the ages-old realmlist, just one choice of four (soon five). In theory, this choice should be simple – but somehow it’s still a headscratcher. It’s like the stakes got bigger, somehow.

So there’s Normal, PvP, Roleplay, and Roleplay-PvP. The first two you’re already familiar with. And if you’re not used to seeing roleplay servers, this can be quite new (but it’s what the “RP” in MMORPG stands for). Is there a difference in the choices, though?

As it turns out, not a lot. But they’re significant.

As the name implies, the Normal ruleset is for anyone who wants to log into WoW: Forever after a busy workday and do whatever. Maybe you want to grind to a certain level, do a couple of raids, or just walk around Dalamar (the lighting is amazing now, thanks to visual upgrades).

If you’re playing WoW for the first time, or if you’re not really into PvP, you can choose Normal. For MMO vets, you’d recognize Normal as the standard-fare “PvE” default mode. This is where we think a huge portion of players will be during launch, and maybe even afterward.

PvP is available but at your discretion. It’s a separate setting you have to enable, and it only applies in Contested or Enemy Zones.

Adventuring around Azeroth is fun, but what if you want to test your build against other players? What if you’re a Horde player and you meet some punk from Stormwind? There’s no need to resist going berserk in PvP, because duels are actively encouraged here.

The main ethos behind the transition from Warcraft 3 into World of Warcraft was always living inside Azeroth. What if you lived as a character who just started a life of adventure after the Third War?

With the Roleplay ruleset, you do exactly that. If you’ve always imagined acting out your character and immersing yourself in Azeroth, this is the ruleset for you.

Roleplay gives a massive “space” for players who love the idea of playing their own characters (r/WoWRolePlay has been up since 2012, for instance). Its naming conventions are stricter (no puns, please) and players are expected to behave a certain way (mostly roleplay).

If you want to kick things up a notch, there’s Roleplay PvP. It’s similar to Roleplay, except PvP is actively encouraged. Expect a lot of drama and grief (both the emotion and being maliciously killed in the game) from the other faction.

There’s also a fifth “wildcard” ruleset that has yet to arrive in WoW: Forever, and that’s Hardcore.

As the name implies, Hardcore is for players who want a no-handholding experience. Naturally, Blizzard ups the ante for this challenge mode. It’s one strike, and you’re out.

Unlike realms where each choice reveals a population density that preferred a “general type” of gameplay, rulesets in WoW: Forever have put a big emphasis on playstyle.

Remember, players in one ruleset likely enjoy and want to facilitate a specific playstyle. If you’ve gotten used to other players being in character in Roleplay, then Normal or PvP will be a jarring transition.

To avoid any conflict there, Blizzard put its foot down: no transfers between rulesets. If you want to play in another ruleset, you make a totally new character.

Likewise, you can’t play across rulesets. You’ll have to hop onto an alt in a different ruleset, say PvP, to play with your other friends in PvP. Same goes for the others.

Blizzard named one exception, and that’s Battlegrounds, which might include characters from different rulesets. No Hardcore, though.

There’s another catch, if “catch” was spelled “your name.”

When Blizzard introduced WoW: Forever, it also came with a big proposal: forging your legacy. Characters now come with first and second names, and no one else can have the exact two-name combination you picked for your character.

The idea is that, if you do something historic, everyone will know for sure that’s your character. Lore-wise, it also establishes a family name your future characters could use.

Naturally, this “exclusivity” component came with an early name reservation event between October 27 and November 3, 2026.

The big deal is that your choice of name is hard-set across all rulesets. That one name you picked in Normal can’t be used in PvP, Roleplay, and Roleplay-PvP.

If you’re not into roleplay, you’re good. But if you want a casual WoW experience and really sink your teeth into being an adventurer in Azeroth, well, that’s a tough choice.

Rulesets finally offer cleaner, much simpler choices for how to go about the World of Warcraft experience. At the end of the day, realms were only really important for two things:

If you don’t like fighting other players, stay away from PVP. Then high-population realms mean more chances of securing raid members all the time.

Now Forever makes this more intentional. Your playstyle takes precedence, and Blizzard handles everything else. So pick that ruleset and start grinding. Azeroth awaits.

Blizzard released World of Warcraft: Forever on November 4, 2026, for PC and macOS.
