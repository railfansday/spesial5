Rocket Lab Stock Just Fell to Its Lowest Price in About a Year. Is It a Buy Now?

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Shares are back where they were a year ago, but the business behind them is much bigger.

By Daniel Sparks – Nov 26, 2026 at 10:23AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Rocket Lab ›

Rocket Lab (RKLB +2.26%) stock fell about 7% on Wednesday, Nov. 25, to roughly $43 as I write. The drop left shares at their lowest price since early December 2025 and over 70% below their May 27 record close of $150.23.

The growth stock had closed above $66 just two weeks before, soon after Rocket Lab's third-quarter results, and about a third of that value has vanished since.

But the business behind the stock is far bigger than it was the last time shares traded this low. What hasn't changed is that Neutron, the reusable rocket behind Rocket Lab's long-term growth plans, hasn't yet flown.

The last time Rocket Lab stock traded this low, in early December 2025, the company's revenue for the previous four quarters came to around $555 million. For the last four quarters, it's about $882 million, almost 60% more. Growth has sped up, too. Revenue for the third quarter of 2026 climbed 73% from a year earlier to a record $268 million, after growing 36% in the fourth quarter of 2025. And management expects fourth-quarter revenue of $270 million to $290 million, which at the midpoint would be 56% above the year-ago quarter -- still fast, though slower than the third quarter's rate.

The share count grew, too, but far more slowly. Rocket Lab sold around $3.9 billion in new stock through September, partly to pay for its pending purchase of satellite operator Iridium Communications. Counting preferred stock that converts to common shares, it had about 15% more shares at the end of September than at the end of 2025.

That leaves much more revenue behind each share. At roughly $43, Rocket Lab is valued at about $29 billion, which comes to 33 times the revenue it took in over the last year. At the same share price in early December 2025, the stock traded at more than 40 times sales.

Still, 33 times sales is a high valuation for a business that isn't profitable yet, and I think much of that price depends on Neutron.

Rocket Lab now has 16 Neutron missions booked before the rocket's first flight, and half of them were signed in the third quarter alone, for over $515 million.

But at least six of those eight new missions aren't scheduled until late 2028 or later. Radar-satellite company ICEYE's three launches start in late 2028, Muon Space's two are set for 2029 and 2030, and Starcloud's is booked for 2029.

Rocket Lab expects only around a third of its $3.6 billion total backlog (signed work it hasn't yet counted as revenue) to become revenue within 12 months. Neutron's far-off missions likely make up a big part of the rest.

Neutron's first launch, meanwhile, is expected in 2027. Before Rocket Lab picks a specific date, it still needs to put together the rocket's first-stage tank and finish pressure and cryogenic (super-cold) testing, according to its last quarterly report.

Neutron has to fly first, and even then its early missions might not make money. In the third quarter, Rocket Lab recorded a forward loss reserve (an expected loss booked ahead of time) on the vehicle for Neutron's second flight, because the rocket is still so early in development.

Until Neutron flies regularly, the losses will likely keep coming. Rocket Lab lost $18 million in the third quarter on a non-GAAP (adjusted) basis, measured by earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA). That was smaller than its $26 million loss a year earlier, and management expects another loss of $14 million to $20 million in the fourth quarter.

On the third-quarter earnings call, CFO Adam Spice said capital spending should stay high as Neutron heads toward production, but he expects adjusted EBITDA to go positive within six months of the rocket's first successful test flight.

I think it can be, for investors willing to wait for Neutron. Each share now comes with much more revenue than it did at this price a year ago, and customers keep booking Neutron launches. But Neutron still has to show it can fly, then fly often. And a stock that shed a third of its value over two weeks might swing just as hard again. I'd consider buying in stages instead of all at once.
