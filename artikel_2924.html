Brock Purdy is ticking two historically significant boxes for MVP

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Brock Purdy faces arguably the biggest test of his MVP credentials this Sunday in Mexico City when the 49ers face the Minnesota Vikings.

The Vikings have been a thorn in Purdy's side both times he has faced them in his career. Indeed, Brian Flores' defense has been the puzzle Purdy has yet to figure out, with San Francisco's signal-caller completing over 74 percent of his passes but throwing three interceptions and also losing a fumble across two defeats to Minnesota.

And the challenge that lies ahead of Purdy at Estadio Azteca is a formidable one, as the Vikings' ultra-aggressive defense leads the NFL by EPA per play and success rate, Minnesota allowing a successful offensive play just 37.8 percent of the time.

Should he overcome it and record his first win over the Vikings to solidify the 49ers' stranglehold on the NFC by improving to 9-1, Purdy's MVP case will be significantly strengthened.

However, the numbers suggest he is already ticking two important statistical boxes with his performances through the first nine games of the season, albeit three other quarterbacks are doing the same.

Per Sumer Sports, nine of the last 10 players to win MVP had a EPA per play average of 0.159 or more, and a win rate of at least 68.8 percent.

With the 49ers at 8-1 and Purdy comfortably leading the NFL with an EPA per play of 0.313, the San Francisco signal-caller is meeting both criteria with little difficulty.

The problem is that three AFC quarterbacks operating at the top of their game — Josh Allen, Joe Burrow and Trevor Lawrence — are also checking both boxes through 10 weeks of the season.

As such, despite his extraordinary level of play so far this campaign, Purdy will need to do plenty more to separate himself from a group of players who might have been viewed as more obvious MVP candidates going into the season.

The best way to do that is by delivering more signature performances in high-profile games. Purdy's standout display to this point is his effort in leading the 49ers on a game-winning drive on the road against the Seattle Seahawks in Week 6, while his superb show of playmaking ability in last week's shootout win over the Dallas Cowboys will also have helped his cause.

But it will be the stretch run that decides whether he becomes the 49ers' first MVP since Steve Young in 1994. Strong performances will be required against Vikings, in the return meetings with the Seahawks and Los Angeles Rams, and in a mouthwatering road game with the Kansas City Chiefs in Week 16.

Deliver in that set of fixtures, and it will be difficult to dispute Purdy's case, and confidence in him as an MVP candidate and in the 49ers as the class of the league should increase significantly if he can pass the Flores test on Sunday.
