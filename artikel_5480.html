SK Hynix will spend $1 billion to build up its South Korean chip supplier base

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The chipmaker plans an 874.6 billion won shared testing facility at its Yongin complex, scheduled to open in May 2027

SK Hynix announced Monday that it plans to commit 1.4 trillion won ($1.05 billion) across the coming five years to back South Korean suppliers of semiconductor materials, parts and equipment, according to Reuters. The spending is meant to shore up the chipmaker's supply chain amid sharpening rivalry in the market for AI chips.

The support program spans four phases, starting with research and development and moving through testing and mass production to sales. It is meant to ease the financial and technological obstacles that smaller firms face.

Through its research support program, SK Hynix will pay suppliers in advance for as much as half of their early-stage development expenses. It will also pay suppliers for effort already spent if a project falls short of its goals. Together with financial institutions, SK Hynix will supply 2 trillion won of financing, with backing from the Korea Trade Insurance Corporation. Other programs extend technical consulting and loans at low interest rates, and smaller subcontractors are among those eligible.

SK Hynix also plans an 874.6 billion won testing facility named Trinity Fab, funded by the company, the central government and local governments. SK Hynix will put up 443.2 billion won, while the central government will provide 373.6 billion won and local governments will add 57.7 billion won. The facility is due to begin operating in May 2027.

Trinity Fab will be built within the first plant of SK Hynix’s Yongin chip complex, roughly 40 km (25 miles) south of Seoul. It will house a 3,305-square-meter (35,600-square-foot) cleanroom plus tools for lithography and etching as well as deposition and inspection. Suppliers can assess their materials and equipment on 12-inch wafers before the items go onto production lines.

An independent non-profit foundation will operate the facility, a setup meant to safeguard intellectual property and confidential business data. SK Hynix said the shared site would let small suppliers spend less on testing and get their products to market faster. The company intends to put 600 trillion won into four chip fabrication plants at the Yongin cluster.

SK Hynix reported record third-quarter operating profit of 83.33 trillion won last month and said it expects demand for memory chips to exceed supply through 2030. The company said it expects investment to increase significantly next year as the first phase of Yongin enters full-scale operation.

In August, the company's board approved 54 trillion won in investments to build two new fabs, one for DRAM in Yongin and one for NAND flash in Cheongju.

SK Hynix stock fell 4.4% in Seoul on Monday.
