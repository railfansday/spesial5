Double guns: ultra-class haul truck packs dual-gun MCS charging, 4 MWh battery

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

NHL may not be a household name, but its all-new, all-electric NTE240E ultra-class haul track could change that with a 240 ton payload, over 4.1 MWh of battery, advanced regenerative braking, and a world’s first double-gun MCS charging setup.

For the uninitiated, Inner Mongolia North Hauler Joint Stock Co., Ltd. (NHL) is a Chinese heavy equipment manufacturer specializing in the enormous rigid-frame haul trucks used in open-pit mining operations. Established in 1988, NHL has decades of experience building mining trucks for customers around the world, including some of the industry’s largest diesel-electric hybrids. The brand’s new NTE240E is different, representing a company’s real push into battery-electric mining equipment, and it’s powering that push with some seriously ambitious technology.

Let’s start with the battery. The NTE240E packs an absolutely MASSIVE battery pack, with the highest-capacity versions offering up to 4,100 kWh (4.1 MWh) of onboard energy storage. Keeping that much battery topped off is no small task, which is why NHL developed an equally ambitious energy replenishment strategy supporting dual-gun MCS charging, allowing its town-sized battery to go from 20% to 90% in under an hour.

That means operators can take advantage of shift changes and scheduled rest breaks to top off the battery without sacrificing an entire work shift to charging – but even more impressive than the fact that all that juice can be crammed into so big a battery in so little time is the fact that, according to NHL, it can be done at just 25°C (about 77°F).

Plugging in isn’t the only way the NTE240E drinks its juice. NHL’s new dynamic power reception system uses overhead current collectors to supply electricity directly to the truck while it’s climbing loaded mine roads, allowing it to then “ride the brakes” for more energy all the way downhill.

The combined technologies allow NHL’s operators to keep the massive trucks moving without relying entirely on their onboard batteries. The result is a claimed 15% improvement in energy efficiency over the ultra-class industry average.

That efficiency turns into savings, which means the economics of running an NTE240E could be just as compelling as the emissions savings – and those are substantial. NHL says each NTE240E can reduce harmful carbon emissions by more than 2,000 tons annually, even as it cuts operating costs by more than 60% compared to a comparably capable diesel.

What’s more, because the big BEV’s electrical system eliminates the need for engine-related hardware like generators and the maintenance and overhaul costs associated with those components.
