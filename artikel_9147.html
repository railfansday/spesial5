Haunted Ride on the 3 Train

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Hyperallergic’s Halloween Party, Cici Wu’s memory-laden lanterns, and an Epstein survivor’s urgent message for MoMA.

I thought this Halloween would be one for the books because it lined up on a Saturday, but that evening took an absurd, grotesque turn that truly illustrated the complexity of the human condition. I was on my way to a haunted housewarming party when the 3 train abruptly stopped just after leaving Bergen Street station. After 10 minutes, the conductor announced that the MTA was investigating an unauthorized person on the tracks, and everyone, including me, sighed, groaned, rolled their eyes, and swore under their breath.

With the onslaught of subway surfing incidents and other underdeveloped frontal lobe nonsense that goes on, we all assumed it was some mindless teenagers trying to make the most of fright night at the expense of us costumed strangers. Until the following announcement mentioned a “possible injury,” and then the next one confirmed that a “person jumped off the platform” and that this train was now out of service.

The utter ridiculousness set in when I took stock of the characters in my car — aside from myself as Snoopy, there was Kayako’s ghost from The Grudge, Andy Warhol, Oscar the Grouch and his trash can, a witch, a luchador, and some children dressed as superheroes. All of us, stuck on a powered-off train in a dark tunnel some 50 feet underground on Halloween night. It wasn’t funny in the slightest, but the city’s collective jadedness with the inconvenience of fatality culminated in a veneer of forced levity to hide an ugly, unsympathetic frustration.

As we filed through the dark, empty cars of the rescue trains that led us back to the platform 90 minutes later, the atmosphere shifted when an elderly passenger began suffering from an asthma attack. Within two minutes, the same people who weren’t hiding their irritation with the body under the train ended up sourcing and passing along someone’s spare inhaler to the MTA worker tending to the passenger. I called it a night once I saw the conductor with tears in her eyes.

Last week, I reported on an artist who went viral for secretly installing his own painting at The Met. Depicting police in riot gear beating and arresting protestors in the foreground of the celebrity spectacle at the Met Gala, the artist told me the work captured the absurdity of “A-list celebrities prancing, while the world outside the museum gates is collapsing.” While totally different situations, there are parallels between what the artist wanted to say and what I experienced on Saturday night. We’re obviously not rich celebrities being photographed in haute couture at a Bezos-sponsored soiree, but we were costumed for other arrangements and shielded from the pain, suffering, and loss that occurred in our vicinity.

But some people are resisting these wicked dynamics. Today’s Election Day, and we have candidates like NYC Assemblymember, Ridgewood-based artist, and Democratic socialist Claire Valdez making tangible progress in taking on the affordability crisis. We have fighters like Danielle Bensky, an Epstein survivor holding the Museum of Modern Art accountable for its allegiance to Epstein affiliate and billionaire museum trustee Leon Black.

I won’t end this with some corny message to get out and vote, but I will stress that we have to show up for each other and ourselves, no matter how futile and scary it might feel.

For more than two decades, one question has followed me since I was abused by Jeffrey Epstein: “Why me?” In trying to understand what happened, I have come to recognize that the answer lies not only in the manipulation I endured, but also in the institutions and social structures that helped make that manipulation possible.
