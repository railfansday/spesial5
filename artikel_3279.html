U.S. Army Blows Up Nearly 5 Tons of Explosives in Iowa

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The U.S. Army blew up nearly 5 tons of explosives in Iowa on Thanksgiving Eve ... and for all that firepower, the two plumes of smoke cleared pretty quickly.

Soldiers blew up barrels of deteriorating lead azide stored in two concrete bunkers at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown. A reporter for CNN affiliate KWQC saw the smoke from more than a mile away, and the station reported feeling a shock wave.

As we reported ... the Army was rushing to blow up the deteriorating material out of fear that dropping temperatures could lead to an uncontrolled explosion. Officials said it was too unstable to dispose of safely any other way.

Local residents and environmental groups have voiced concerns about the toxic lead dust that was expected to be released into the air after the explosion. The Army acknowledged the blast would release some lead dust that could travel across multiple counties in Iowa and Missouri.

A federal appeals court put the operation's environmental permit on hold temporarily ... however, the Justice Department argued the Army could proceed under an emergency exemption.

The Army has promised to carry out the detonation safely. All 363 Middletown residents were asked to evacuate the city. They will be housed at an alternative space until Friday, November 27.

Before the boom, FEMA sent out an emergency alert to everyone within 20 miles of the blast area, telling folks in Middletown, Augusta, and Burlington to shelter in place and close their windows to block lead dust.

The alert, which was sent to people's phones, warned that "EVACUATION IS NO LONGER SAFE OR ADVISED!"
