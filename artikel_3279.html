With MSRPs of $549, $449, and $459, respectively, the Apple AirPods Max 2, Bose QuietComfort Ultra 2nd Gen, and the Sony WH-1000XM6 aren't exactly cheap. That's why Black Friday deals are so great, as they're all on sale right now.

I've tested all three of these headphones — and a boatload more of their closest competitors — so I can help you choose the correct pair. Do you value sound quality? ANC? Comfort? Extra features?

I'll go through everything the AirPods Max 2, Bose QC Ultra, and Sony XM6 offer right now — let's figure this out together. Which headphones should you buy this Black Friday?

If you're an Apple user, there are no better headphones than the AirPods Max 2 (at this price, of course). With gorgeously detailed sound and an expansive soundscape, I'm happy to overlook the slightly heavy head-feel.

The Bose QuietComfort Ultra are ideal headphones if you value comfort and ANC over anything else — or, of course, if you have a predilection for bass. These headphones don't sound as detailed as Apple or Sony's versions, but they pack a major punch.

Sony's WH-1000XM6 are great headphones for most people, with clear call quality, proficient ANC, and detailed sound. They're the best all-rounder for both iPhone and Android users.

We've reached the point with Bluetooth headphones where you're probably not going to be disappointed by any pair. I've tested super-budget options worthy of making a "best of" list (Soundcore Space 2, CMF by Nothing Headphone Pro, both $99), just as I've tested these three behemoths.

There's no one "best": it all depends on your personal preferences. For the purpose of this article, I re-tested all three headphones with three of my go-to testing tracks.

The AirPods Max 2 have the most dynamic and exciting sound quality. "space cadet" by sim0ne is an energetic hard house track with euphoric '90s pianos and expansive hi-hats layered under a pitched-up female vocal. The tick-tock synths tinkled in the background with energy and zeal, while the kick drum pummelled the low-end with control.

Rock also sounds excellent on the AirPods Max 2; "Be Quiet and Drive (Far Away)" by Deftones starts with a grating guitar, which came through dynamically on the Max 2. The crashing cymbals at the top of the soundstage didn't wash out the grunge instrumentation, and although I can see the percussion growing tiresome after a long listening session, there's vibrancy in the soundscape.

"Vogue (Q-Sound Mix" by Madonna is excellent for testing spatial imaging. On the AirPods Max 2, Madonna's vocals came through in each earcup independently, with no warping or confusion in the stereo. The subtle synth bassline rounded out the track delicately, without overwhelming the mids or vocals.

The Bose QuietComfort Ultra 2nd Gen are more for bassheads. If you really like a punchy, propulsive low-end, I'd recommend the Bose.

I tested the Bose QC Ultra with the same three songs as the AirPods Max 2. "space cadet" by sim0ne wasn't as expansive or detailed as the AirPods, but the kick drum hit so much harder. As soon as the chorus broke, the synth bassline and drum machine took control of the track. The bass pulsed in my ears. sim0ne's pitched-up vocals were still clear, but the bass was the true star of the show.

"Be Quiet and Drive (Far Away)" benefited from the same elevated bass — the crunchy, almost too-sharp opening guitar gave way to a truly raucous verse. The bass carried the entire track, somewhat pushing the percussion to the soundstage's edge, but didn't overpower the vocals. Anyone into rock or grunge would seriously love these headphones.

Lastly, I played "Vogue (Q-Symphony Mix)" to test stereo imaging. Bose's "Immersive" feature really came into play here: I could hear the track coming from behind me, beside me, in front of me. I'd say this immersion was even better than the AirPods Max 2's — my brain was well and truly tricked. And when that bassline came in? I'm gone. It's over. I'm on the dancefloor, bye!

Last but certainly not least, let's chat about the Sony WH-1000XM6. These are famous the best "all-rounder" headphones: they're detailed and expansive, bassy yet clear, and are the best of all worlds.

Once again, I played "space cadet" by sim0ne. This track was bright and fun, with a whole load of clarity across the treble and mid-range. Every snare and hi-hat coruscated across the soundstage without overpowering those lovely tick-tock synth notes. The euphoric pianos were lovely and detailed, and when the bassline kicked in, it kicked in. Obviously, it wasn't as hard-hitting as the Bose, but the XM6s prioritize clarity and detail over booming bass.

Heavier genres fared well, too. "Be Quiet and Drive (Far Away)" by Deftones pounded through the headphones with the ferocity of a runaway freight train. Bass and guitars snarled in my ears without overpowering the vocals. Although the bass, again, didn't have quite as much boom as the Bose headphones, these are detail-focused instead.

Finally, I played "Vogue (Q-Symphony Mix)" to test spatial imaging. Madonna's voice danced around my head, making me feel like the music was coming from everywhere. Fingers snapped in my left ear, and bongos drummed in my right ear. I will say the Sonys weren't as truly immersive as the Bose and AirPods, but this was still a phenomenal performance.

The AirPods Max 2 are 13 ounces; Bose QC Ultra 9.3 ounces; Sony WX6 8.96 ounces. If you're going to be wearing your headphones all day, I'd recommend the Bose or Sony.

Although I can wear the AirPods Max 2 for at least a few hours without needing to take them off, and the mesh headband alleviates a lot of weight, if you need as little head fatigue as possible, I'd go for the Bose or Sony.

Especially if you're a frequent flyer — the Bose QC Ultra are unbeatable for flights. You won't be able to hear the engine, other passengers, or even your own thoughts with these. It's a win all round.

You won't be disappointed by ANC on any of these headphones. I've worn all three while working, running errands across the city, and in noisy environments like bars.

If you're wearing these headphones while working in an office, all three will minimize ambient sounds like keyboards clacking and mice clicking. I found the best to be the Bose QuietComfort Ultra, but the AirPods and Sony also cocooned me in a vacuum of quiet.

For really loud environments, or if you have noisy neighbors or construction outside your house, I'd recommend the Bose QuietComfort Ultra. While the AirPods Max 2 and Sony WH-1000XM6 are excellent, Dr. Bose literally invented ANC — and it shows.
