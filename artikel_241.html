Takeoffs from airports throughout the US paused on Friday after the FAA issued a national ground stop “due to extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety,” while planes in the air will be allowed to land. US Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy tweeted:

The FAA issued a nationwide ground stop for all U.S. airports following a communication issue impacting all Air Route Traffic Control Centers. FAA technicians are investigating the issue. We will provide immediate updates as we have them.

Speaking to reporters at the White House, Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin told reporters that there is currently no reason to suspect a cyberattack or other national security threat, but that so far no cause has been ruled out for the “telecommunications outage.”

Jon Ostrower of The Air Current reports that the FAA has said to airlines that the stop is “due to several impacts to ATC Frequencies and/or Radar capabilities.” The flight tracking site FlightAware lists 9,086 US flights as delayed as of 5:19PM ET.

According to CNN, the last nationwide ground stop in 2023 lasted for about 90 minutes after the NOTAM (Notice to Air Missions) system for pilots went down, and the last one prior to that was after the attacks on September 11th, 2001.
