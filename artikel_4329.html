Volvo A40 Electric articulated haul trucks get to work in the UK

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

After becoming the world’s first all-electric 40 ton class articulated haul truck into regular series production earlier this year, the Volvo A40 has finally reached its first customers in the UK – and they wasted no time putting the big BEV to work on one of the nation’s largest, most important infrastructure projects.

More than sixty years after Volvo’s “Gravel Charlie” invented the articulated hauler, Volvo CE has delivered four customer A40 Electric articulated haul trucks to BMJV (a joint venture between Bouygues Travaux Publics and J Murphy & Sons) for use on the Lower Thames Crossing, further demonstrating that zero-emission machines are ready to move material at scale at major job sites right now.

“Going electric today is no longer about compromise, it’s about the benefits,” explains Shaun Pidcock, Lower Thames Crossing Programme Director at BMJV. “These remarkable machines show that even the heaviest jobs can be done cleanly, and in a way that is better for those working on or living near our sites.”

The Lower Thames Crossing project is approximately 14.3 miles long, and includes more than 2.5 miles of tunnels criss-crossing beneath the River Thames. Once finished, the project will connect the Kent and Essex meters, and promises to nearly double road capacity across the east side of London while becoming the first major infrastructure program in Britain completed with carbon-neutral construction.

By using the next generation of construction machinery to make the Lower Thames Crossing Britain’s greenest road today, we are helping the industry set a new standard for tomorrow and make diesel-powered sites a thing of the past.

Since grid power isn’t available on site, BMJV is using fuel cell generators fueled by British-manufactured green hydrogen to charge the A40s. The generators produce no exhaust emissions and are noticeably quieter than combustion machines, further improving living and working conditions for the humans around the site.

The “40” in the A40 Electric’s badge refers to the truck’s whopping 40 ton (39 tonne) payload capacity. Once loaded up, the hauler’s 350 kW (about 470 hp) electric motor sends power through a torque-amplifying gear reduction gearbox that, depending on how you do the math, delivers some 200,000 lb-ft of “at the wheel” torque. More than enough twisting force, in other words, to match the performance of diesel and possibly enough to have a measurable impact on the rotational speed of a planet.

That motor draws power from a 540 kWh li-ion battery good for up to six hours of continuous operation, but the hauler’s advanced regenerative braking system usually collects enough energy during operation to make it good for a full day’s shift. And, if there’s a Circle K nearby, operators can plug their A40 Electric into a 350 kW DC fast charger and get their battery from 10% to 90% in about an hour.
