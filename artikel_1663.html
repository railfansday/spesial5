I can understand why fans are frustrated about lack of progress under Pocognoli, admits Andy Robertson

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

By JOHN MCGARRY, SCOTTISH CHIEF SPORTS FEATURE WRITER

Published: 17:30 EST, 17 November 2026 | Updated: 17:38 EST, 17 November 2026

Andy Robertson says he fully understands why the Tartan Army are increasingly concerned about where the team are going under Sebastien Pocognoli.

Monday’s heavy defeat to Switzerland marked the conclusion of a dismal Nations League campaign which saw the Belgian’s men beat only North Macedonia across six matches to finish a distant third.

After the high of qualifying for the World Cup a year ago, supporters have been brought crashing back to earth since the summer with signs of progress proving hard to come by under the new manager.

When the full-time whistle blew in St Gallen to confirm a 4-0 loss, there were audible jeers from the travelling fans.

Skipper Robertson has no issue with what he heard and knows the players have a job on their hands to turn the situation round.

‘I can understand why they’re concerned. I can understand why they’re frustrated,’ he said. ‘I can understand why the away fans didn’t really want us to go over and applaud them.

Robertson can't hide anguish after 4-0 loss on what was his record breaking 103rd cap

‘I can completely understand that. I’ve been around a long time now and unfortunately I’ve had these nights a couple of times where the fans do react like that.

‘You have to let them do that and you also understand why they’re doing it. But I suppose if we’re trying to flip a positive on it, I think maybe the first five or six games under Steve Clarke were maybe similar where we had a lot of change in the squad, we were trying to build something, we were trying to integrate new people into a new squad and things like that.

‘We’re trying to do that and we have to be positive about that. We need the young lads to feel comfortable around us. We need them to step up.

‘We need the older players to really help them in that and really, really step up. And that’s the only way we’re going to be successful.

‘We’ll still be positive because that’s the only way we know, and we know we can build something special. We’ve done it. We’ve been questioned a few times and bounced back and now we’re going to need to do it again with a newer squad.’

Robertson admits that the adverse results have fed into a lack of harmony behind the scenes — a sharp contrast to the positivity which was palpable as the side qualified for the World Cup a year ago today.

Robertson understands why fans booed another dismal performance by Pocognoli's team

‘I still think we need to work on it,’ he said. ‘We still need to get closer as a group again because — I don’t like looking at the past any more — but under Steve we had a really close-knit group where what we created was pretty special.

‘And that’s rare but we also have to try and do that again and we’re trying to make the new players and the young lads feel as comfortable as possible. That’s the role of the older players and we have to keep doing that.

‘We have to keep encouraging them because they’re the ones that want to take us forward in years to come and they’re also the ones that are going to help us now.

‘Us older lads, we need to put a lot of responsibility on (ourselves) to try and help them do that, to try and keep pushing them, and we have to keep stepping up as well.

‘As a collective, we all need to step up more and if we do that then hopefully we can be successful again.’

While the spotlight has largely fallen on the manager for persisting with a 3-4-2-1 formation, Robertson is adamant that it’s only fair the blame is shared around.

‘We all have to take responsibility,’ he said. ‘Tactics and the way the manager sets us up and everything like that is one thing but then you’ve got 11  players, and we had 16 players, who have to go out and perform.

‘I don’t think we performed as a collective good enough. When you get beat 4-0, there’s not going to be any positives. There’s no fan in this stadium that’s going to go home and go: “We played well in the first half”.

‘That’s not how football works and we’re very aware of that. I’m very aware of that in terms of I don’t take any positives from a 4-0 defeat. I’m sorry but that’s the way I am and unfortunately that’s the way it is.’

The Tottenham man became Scotland’s most capped player on Monday when he pulled on the jersey for the 103rd time, but that achievement was scant consolation at the time.

‘I’ve not given myself the chance to reflect,’ insisted Robertson. ‘In a couple of days’ time, probably when I unlock my phone and I get a couple of messages, I’ll realise what I’ve achieved and I don’t take it for granted.

‘I don’t take the magnitude of it for granted either, being able to overtake the legend of Kenny Dalglish. But it’s very hard to focus on myself after a result like that. I always focus on the team first and that’s always been my way.’
