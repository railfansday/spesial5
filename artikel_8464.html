A broken lamp, toaster, jacket, or bike may need only a new part, a few stitches, or a reconnected wire. At a repair café or fix-it clinic, volunteer fixers work on it with you at no charge, so you learn how to fix it. Search Repair Café International’s map and your library’s events calendar for one nearby.

Say your toaster’s lever won’t stay down and your jacket’s zipper has split. Replacing both could cost about $70, and paying a shop to fix them could cost nearly as much.

A library repair café could have both working again in an afternoon without charging for the work. Repair cafés also run at community centers and shared workshops called makerspaces, where volunteers with repair skills take on small appliances, lamps, clothes, bikes, and toys. You do as much of the work as you can, with a volunteer beside you.

There’s usually a donation jar, and you pay for any new part, like a plug or a fuse.

Start by searching Repair Café International’s map for your town. It lists more than 4,000 groups worldwide, but only those that have joined its network. Also check the event calendars of your library, nearby makerspaces, and your local recycling program, since many run the same kind of event under names like fix-it clinic or repair event.

Each group posts its own limits, so check the list before you go. Many turn away gas-powered equipment, microwaves, and anything too big to carry in. For a washer or fridge, work out whether a paid repair is worth it instead.

Bring one item at a time, since many groups look at one per person and send a second to the back of the line. Include everything needed to show the problem, such as its cord or charger, plus any part you know it needs, because most groups stock only small parts like plugs, wire, and thread.

With no group nearby, order a replacement part from the manufacturer and fix it at home, or ask your library to host one. Maine’s Department of Environmental Protection publishes a free guide to organizing a repair clinic, from recruiting fixers to the liability waiver each visitor signs.
