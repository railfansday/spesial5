Gary Neville slaps himself in the FACE in bizarre on-air moment after Man United's humiliation at Anfield - weeks after claiming his old club are 'in a better place' than Liverpool

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Published: 04:43 EST, 23 November 2026 | Updated: 10:28 EST, 24 November 2026

Gary Neville showcased the dramatic decline of Manchester United this season as he bizarrely slapped himself in the face on Sky Sports after his former side's 4-0, 10-man, defeat to Liverpool at Anfield.

Neville's dramatic example of Man United's lack of hunger and sharpness came in stark contrast to his pre-season proclamations for the side, when he informed his co-pundit Jamie Carragher that he believed that then-manager Michael Carrick's starting XI was stronger than Liverpool's after a summer recruitment drive.

Instead, Man United saw their numbers reduced inside the opening stages of the game after a reckless challenge by Leny Yoro, and 2-0 down after 13 minutes, with the club legend resorting to drastic methods to convey the side's weaknesses.

Of his old club's defenders, he said: 'I never see any of them on their toes, alert, looking like they've got something fixed in here', before pointing to his head.

'I never see any of them look like that,' he added. 'They look like that -'

Neville then stared into the middle distance for a few moments to share the blankness he sees on the faces of the back line, before spitting: 'Wake up!'

Gary Neville took to slapping himself to convey the lack of focus he sees in Man United's stars

The club legend had previously tipped his old side to have a stronger XI than Liverpool's

But when the two teams met at Anfield, Man United were left woefully exposed after going down to 10 men

The Overlap broadcaster then slapped his own face a number of times repeating 'wake up!' before adding: 'You play for Man United'.

The gesture left Carragher in stitches, with the Anfield icon collapsing onto the sofa in hysterics - although less impressed with Roy Keane, who displayed his usual sangfroid.

So tickled by the moment was the former Liverpool star that he later changed his profile picture on social media site X to a still image of Neville's hand catching his face.

Neville had more sharp criticism for Man United's defensive selection assessing the inclusion of Matthijs de Ligt in the starting line-up.

The Dutch defender has been out of Premier League action for 51 weeks after suffering a back injury, but was named by Mauricio Pochettino in his first-ever XI.

'I have a lot of sympathy for him,' Neville admitted. 'It's like playing football after having six pints. It's a strange feeling.

'I have a lot of sympathy for him, it's alarming to watch.'

Less sympathy for Man United's players came from Keane, who attacked the team - and Neville and Carragher - after the heavy defeat at Anfield confirmed the club's worst start in over 53 years.

The firebrand midfielder was on his usual caustic form as he assessed the damage, hitting out at those who might have accepted the score line, having feared a greater deficit.

'If I hear that one more time from the lads in the commentary that 4-0 is not too bad when you're down to 10 men - has a team ever got a result with 10 men before?' Keane said immediately after the final whistle. 'It can be done.

'I thought United were desperate. I thought they were so bad and everyone and Nev, who played for Man United saying 4-0 ain't bad.'

'(The red card) doesn't help but you're telling me they couldn't have done better? They lost 4-0 with 10 men, that's not too bad? Has a team ever got a result with 10 men? It can be done. At least show a bit more fight, a bit more quality - I can't believe what I'm hearing from people.

'You played for the club, saying 4-0 isn't too bad. Wow. Fans are applauding. Applauding what? Performing rubbish. Liverpool were excellent, but how easy did United make it for them? I can't believe what I'm watching sometimes.

'This team two years ago finished 15th. The last year or two, we're talking. We're blaming the board, blaming ownership, blaming managers.

'When do the players take responsibility for some decision-making? Liverpool probably should have scored eight, probably eight or nine, unbelievable chances they had. And everyone's coming off the pitch saying 4 is not too bad? I feel like swearing.'

The difference between the two teams felt a far cry from Neville's late-summer prediction, when he told Carragher that he had greater hopes for Carrick and Man United over incoming manager Andoni Iraola at Liverpool.

'I think Manchester United are currently in a better place than Liverpool,' Neville said in August. 'Would you agree?'

Carragher said that he did not, and will have been confident in his assessment on Sunday night.
