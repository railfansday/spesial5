Should You Buy Tesla or SpaceX Stock Before Their Potential Merger?

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The potential combination has a clear winner.

By Adam Levy – Nov 22, 2026 at 5:01PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Space Exploration Technologies ›

While it's long been rumored that Elon Musk could move to combine Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -7.69%) and Tesla (TSLA -7.64%), the megamerger is closer to a reality than it's ever been. The two companies are in advanced discussions, and a merger announcement could come as soon as next month, according to Bloomberg.

The new company would combine SpaceX's artificial intelligence capabilities and infrastructure with Tesla's physical AI (self-driving cars and robots), potentially benefiting from vertical integration and scale. Investors considering an investment in either Tesla or SpaceX ahead of any merger announcement need to consider what they stand to gain from a deal and what they're giving up.

A merger of Tesla would be an all-stock deal with SpaceX paying Tesla shareholders in stock to absorb the company. That dilutes existing SpaceX shareholders' stock, but they end up owning a piece of a larger company.

Tesla shareholders could benefit in the short term. Acquisitions usually require the acquiring company to pay a premium for the acquired company’s shareholders to agree to sell their shares. But that premium can quickly disappear in an all-stock deal. If the market thinks the acquiring company overpaid, it can send the share price lower well ahead of the merger closing, decreasing the actual value received.

Furthermore, Tesla's share price will become pegged to SpaceX's performance until the deal closes. Anyone considering owning Tesla stock needs to be comfortable with the idea that SpaceX’s financials will impact its share price.

That's a key question, given that SpaceX stock is currently priced for significant revenue growth over the next few years. It holds major contracts with multiple AI companies to sell infrastructure at a premium price and plans to add a whopping 10 gigawatts of capacity in 2027, building on the 1.7 gigawatts it ended the third quarter with. Achieving that expansion and selling its full capacity while maintaining its high pricing would result in phenomenal growth, but that two-step process is far from guaranteed.

At the same time, investors need to be comfortable with the fact that even if SpaceX and Tesla agree to a merger, it might not receive approval. SpaceX is a contractor with the U.S. government. Tesla's ties to China -- its Shanghai plant built more than half its vehicles in 2025 -- could deter regulators from approving the deal.

That said, other U.S. defense contractors have been able to maintain operations in China, maintaining a firewall between different segments. However, that could limit the potential synergies between SpaceX and Tesla.

Finally, SpaceX buyers have to be comfortable with acquiring Tesla. The majority of the auto company's value is based on the potential of its fledgling robotaxi business and its humanoid robotics. Neither generates any meaningful revenue at this point, and Musk has been prone to overpromising and underdelivering on Tesla's real-world AI implementations.

While a merger has potential benefits and drawbacks for regular Tesla and SpaceX shareholders, Musk would be the clear winner. Any merger agreement will likely ensure Musk maintains majority control over the vote at the combined company. That means Tesla shareholders have to be comfortable with Musk having the freedom to practically do whatever he wants with the business.

To be sure, both Tesla and SpaceX investors are already likely big fans of Musk. He has full control over SpaceX, and Tesla shareholders eventually approved a $1 trillion pay package for Musk last year, as he threatened to leave the company if voters didn't pass it. Musk previously posted on X that he wants at least 25% voting control at Tesla, "enough to be influential, but not so much that I can't be overturned." If the merger goes through, however, he will have enough control to avoid being overturned.

This might not be a big issue to most shareholders, but it's certainly something to consider. There are many public companies where the CEO controls the vote. But Tesla shareholders will be giving up any checks they previously had on Musk. When you combine that with the premium price both stocks currently trade at, it's hard to recommend buying either stock ahead of the potential merger.
