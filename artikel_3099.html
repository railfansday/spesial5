Where Will Micron Stock Be in 3 Years?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Micron's stock has plunged in recent months, but the next three years could still be lucrative.

By Keith Speights – Nov 22, 2026 at 4:26AM EST

Micron Technology (MU -13.89%) has lost nearly half its market cap in five months. That's the bad news. The good news is that the semiconductor stock is still up nearly 120% year-to-date.

Wall Street thinks that Micron has a lot of room to run. The consensus 12-month price target reflects a potential upside of around 160%. But where will Micron stock be in three years? There are three potential scenarios.

The bull case for Micron is that its stock soars to between $1,800 and $2,200 by late 2029, a gain of 3.2x at the midpoint of the range. To make this scenario possible, the demand for the company's memory chips would need to remain strong over the next several years. In addition, the humanoid robot market would need to gain momentum. Micron CEO Sanjay Mehrotra said in the company's fiscal 2026 third-quarter earnings call that he expects a "sustained, substantial multi-decade memory demand cycle" to begin later this decade.

The base case is that the stock jumps to $1,100 to $1,400. Micron's share price would roughly double at the midpoint of this range. The high-bandwidth memory (HBM) market would need to continue growing robustly in this scenario. It also assumes that Chinese memory chip manufacturer ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) gains only modest market share with its HBM chips. However, Micron probably wouldn't require the humanoid robot market to pick up significant steam for the base case to be achieved.

What's the bear case? Micron's stock declines to a range of $400 to $600 over the next three years. The most likely way this scenario could unfold is if CXMT advances its HBM technology faster than expected and changes the market dynamics. An influx of HBM supply could trigger a price war among major HBM suppliers. As Micron's margins fall, so would its share price. A slowdown in AI infrastructure spending could also weigh on Micron's stock. With many investors already nervous about hyperscalers' massive AI-related capital expenditures and a growing public backlash against AI data centers in the U.S., such a slowdown isn't out of the question.

Micron is still a cyclical stock. Any of the three scenarios is possible, depending on the duration and intensity of the current memory chip boom. That said, if I had to predict where Micron stock will actually be three years from now, it would be around the lower end of the base-case range.

I fully expect the demand for HBM to remain strong. Whether the humanoid robot market becomes a significant tailwind for Micron between now and 2029 remains to be seen, though. As for CXMT, I think the company will be a key player in HBM. However, I don't anticipate that Micron's growth will be derailed because of increased competition. A cumulative gain of 80% or more for Micron over the next three years seems attainable, in my view.
