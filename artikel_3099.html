HeartFlow: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Operating losses are narrowing as revenue outpaces expenses, and the company's $253 million cash pile is meant to carry it until it turns cash-flow positive.

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 12, 2026, HeartFlow (HTFL -19.13%) raised its full-year revenue outlook to between $262 million and $264 million. The company is accelerating commercial adoption of its artificial intelligence diagnostic platform across cardiovascular care pathways.

Expanding adoption and clinical trial milestones offer long-term investors a clear view of sustainable top-line growth. Three things management said on the call stand out.

Revenue grew 57% year over year in the third quarter of 2026, driven by higher utilization of FFRct, an artificial intelligence tool measuring coronary artery blockage, and Plaque Analysis, a software program analyzing arterial vessel walls. Expanding sales of high-margin software helped lift non-GAAP gross margin, an adjusted measure excluding non-cash compensation, to 83.8%.

A one-year study of 6,000 patients revealed that clinicians using Plaque Analysis reduced major cardiovascular disease (CVD) events by 34% compared to standard coronary computed tomography angiography, or CCTA, scans alone. Demonstrating improved patient outcomes encourages health systems to adopt the software across a broader portion of scan volumes.

Management is initiating three clinical trials in late 2026 and early 2027 to evaluate high-risk patients without symptoms, aiming to unlock an additional $6 billion market. Winning regulatory and reimbursement coverage in this population would expand the company's total addressable market, or TAM, to $11 billion.

Operational leverage continues to strengthen as revenue growth outpaces expenses, narrowing operating losses significantly. The company holds $253 million in cash and short-term investments, which management expects will fund operations until achieving positive cash flow.

Structural advantages in proprietary imaging software, growing clinical trial evidence, and expanding health plan coverage protect its competitive position as management focuses on account activations and pricing discipline.

New products like PCI Navigator, a software tool for planning catheter-based heart stent procedures, extend the platform into treatment planning. Clinical data supporting these tools helps embed the software into hospital workflows and drives long-term customer retention.
