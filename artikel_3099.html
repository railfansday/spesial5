Coca-Cola at $95 or PepsiCo at $140: Which Dividend King Could Turn $1,000 Into More Money by 2036?

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Coca-Cola and PepsiCo have rewarded investors with decades of dividend growth. But which Dividend King offers the better opportunity to turn $1,000 into substantially more money over the next decade?

By Matt DiLallo – Nov 24, 2026 at 9:09AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Coca-Cola ›

Coca-Cola (KO +2.32%) and PepsiCo (PEP +1.97%) are two of the most dependable dividend stocks investors can own. Both are Dividend Kings, having increased their payouts annually for more than 50 consecutive years.

However, these consumer staples giants offer distinctly different investment opportunities right now. Coca-Cola is delivering stronger earnings growth, but its stock commands a substantial valuation premium. PepsiCo has struggled with slower growth, especially in North America, but offers a higher dividend yield and a lower earnings multiple.

With Coca-Cola stock recently at $95 and PepsiCo trading at $140 a share, here's which one could turn a $1,000 investment into more money by 2036.

Coca-Cola is thriving these days. The global beverage giant reported 7% net revenue growth during the third quarter (all organic), driven in part by 5% global unit case volume growth. Meanwhile, its comparable earnings per share rose 11%. That strong showing gave the company the confidence to boost its full-year outlook. Coke now expects to deliver at least 11% comparable earnings-per-share growth this year, or about $3.33 per share.

With its share recently trading around $95 apiece, Coke sells for almost 29 times earnings. That's a premium price for a premium business. For comparison, the S&P 500 trades at about 20 times earnings. As a result of its high valuation, Coke's dividend yield is currently down to around 2.2%.

The challenge is that Coke's long-term ambition is to deliver 7%-9% comparable earnings-per-share growth. Add in its roughly 2% yield, and we're looking at 9% to 11% annualized returns, assuming the company maintains its premium valuation multiple. That's a demanding outlook.

PepsiCo isn't thriving like Coke. It's facing headwinds in both its North American beverage and snack businesses. While its revenue rose 5.6% during the third quarter, organic revenue growth was only 3.1%. Meanwhile, it delivered only 1.5% growth in core earnings per share.

As a result of headwinds, Pepsi cut its guidance and now expects to deliver 2.5% to 3.5% core earnings-per-share growth this year (down from 5% to 7%). That implies it will earn around $8.40 per share at the midpoint. With Pepsi stock at $140, it trades at less than 17x earnings.

That's a much less demanding valuation multiple. It's also why the beverage and snacking giant offers a much higher current yield (4.2%). If Pepsi can turn things around and get back to its long-term growth ambition of delivering high single-digit core earnings-per-share growth, it could deliver total returns in the 11%-13% range, and that's before any valuation multiple expansion.

I considered a rather simple scenario for both Coke and Pepsi over the next decade. I assumed both companies would deliver 6% annual earnings-per-share growth, while Coke's valuation multiple contracts slightly to 23x, and Pepsi's expands to 19x. As that chart shows, PepsiCo generates the larger projected payoff. Its lower initial earnings multiple, higher starting dividend yield, and potential valuation recovery would combine to deliver a much higher total return compared to Coke. However, that assumes Pepsi eventually turns things around and starts delivering earnings growth approaching its long-term target range.

Both Coca-Cola and PepsiCo should continue rewarding shareholders with growing dividends over the coming decade. However, the price investors pay to open a position will play an important role in their total returns.

While Pepsi currently has a higher stock price than Coca-Cola ($140 vs. $95), it has a much lower valuation and higher dividend yield. As a result, it could grow a $1,000 investment into around $2,850 over the next 10 years, $1,000 more than the same investment in Coca-Cola. That's why I'd favor PepsiCo at these prices.
