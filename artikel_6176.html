The Capital One VentureOne Rewards Credit Card is a solid option for travelers who want to earn transferable miles without paying an annual fee.

While it offers access to Capital One's 15-plus airline and hotel transfer partners, its earning rate of 1.25 miles per dollar on most purchases and limited travel perks may leave some travelers wanting more.

Still, I think the VentureOne is worth considering if you're new to transferable rewards and aren't ready to commit to a more expensive travel card. Card rating*: ⭐⭐⭐

*Card rating is based on the opinion of TPG’s editors and is not influenced by the card issuer.

The Capital One VentureOne Rewards is a no-annual-fee travel rewards card that earns Capital One miles.

The biggest draw here is the ability to earn transferable miles without paying an annual fee. You can get more value from your rewards by transferring miles to airline and hotel partners, or redeem them at a fixed rate toward travel purchases if you'd rather keep things simple.

The card doesn't charge foreign transaction fees and comes with a few travel perks, including complimentary Hertz Gold+ Rewards Five Star status.*

That said, the VentureOne's everyday earning rate is on the lower side. I'd recommend it primarily to travelers who want access to Capital One's transfer partners without paying an annual fee, rather than those looking to earn the most rewards on everyday spending.

The VentureOne doesn't offer the extensive travel benefits of its higher-tier counterparts, but it still has a handful of perks worth knowing about.

One of the VentureOne's most useful benefits is that it doesn't charge foreign transaction fees.

Some credit cards charge around 3% on purchases made abroad, which can add up over the course of an international trip.

With the VentureOne, you can make international purchases without worrying about additional card fees.

Related: Best credit cards with no foreign transaction fees

VentureOne cardholders can enroll in complimentary Hertz Gold+ Rewards Five Star status.

Normally, earning this status requires completing three rentals or spending $1,000 on eligible rentals in a calendar year.

This status can provide benefits such as vehicle upgrades when available and additional rewards on eligible rentals.

While I wouldn't recommend choosing the VentureOne solely for this perk, it's a nice addition for travelers who occasionally rent cars.

The VentureOne also includes several protections that may come in handy when traveling or making purchases.

These include auto rental insurance, extended warranty protection and travel and emergency assistance.

For coverage details, including terms and exclusions, refer to your card's guide to benefits. These aren't necessarily benefits you'll use regularly, but they can provide some reassurance.

One of the VentureOne's biggest advantages is that it earns flexible Capital One miles rather than rewards tied to a single airline or hotel program.

That gives you several ways to use your rewards, depending on how much effort you want to put into maximizing them.

The VentureOne earns 5 miles per dollar spent on hotels, vacation rentals and rental cars booked through Capital One Travel and 1.25 miles per dollar spent on everyday purchases.

To put that into perspective, spending $1,000 on everyday purchases would earn you 1,250 miles. Based on TPG's November 2026 valuation of 1.85 cents per Capital One mile, those miles would be worth approximately $23 when maximized through transfer partners.

That's not a particularly strong return, especially when other no-annual-fee cards offer higher earning rates.

Still, the VentureOne keeps things simple. You don't have to keep track of rotating bonus categories, and you'll earn miles on every purchase.

If you're more concerned with earning transferable miles without paying an annual fee than maximizing every dollar you spend, that might be enough for you.

Related: How to earn, redeem and transfer Capital One miles

One of the easiest ways to use your Capital One miles is to use them to pay for eligible purchases as a statement credit.

You'll get a fixed value of 1 cent per mile, meaning 20,000 miles would cover a $200 travel purchase. This can be especially useful for expenses that aren't always easy to book with traditional airline or hotel points, such as Airbnb stays or Uber rides.

I like this option for beginners because there's no need to search for award availability or figure out how different loyalty programs work. You can just make an eligible travel purchase with your card and use your miles to cover it afterward.

You can also redeem your miles for Amazon purchases, Capital One entertainment purchases, cash back and gift cards. However, these options generally offer less value than travel redemptions, so I'd stick with using miles for travel whenever possible.

Related: How to get the maximum value when redeeming Capital One miles

If you're willing to put in a little more effort, transferring your Capital One miles to airline and hotel partners can be a great way to get more value from your rewards.

Capital One has more than 15 transfer partners, including programs like Avianca LifeMiles, British Airways Club and Choice Privileges. Most transfers are at a 1:1 ratio, although some partners have different ratios.

Personally, I love transferring Capital One miles to airline partners like Virgin Atlantic Flying Club and Air France-KLM Flying Blue.

For example, I recently transferred 6,400 Capital One miles to Flying Blue to book a Scandinavian Airlines flight from Stockholm Arlanda Airport (ARN) to Amsterdam Airport Schiphol (AMS). I paid just $10.70 in taxes and fees for a flight that would have otherwise cost $163.95.

That worked out to about 2.39 cents per mile, well above TPG's valuation of Capital One miles. And while I booked through Flying Blue, I actually flew on Scandinavian Airlines (SAS) — a good example of how you can use one airline's miles to book flights on its partners.

Of course, not every transfer will give you that kind of value. I always recommend comparing the cash price of a flight to the number of miles you'd need to transfer.

I'd also avoid transferring miles before you have a specific redemption in mind, since transfers generally can't be reversed. Instead, find the flight you want, confirm award availability and then transfer your miles.

If you're new to points and miles, redeeming miles to cover travel purchases might be an easier place to start. But once you're comfortable with the basics, learning how to use transfer partners can really help you stretch your rewards further.

The VentureOne's biggest drawback is its relatively low everyday earning rate.

Earning 1.25 miles per dollar may be simple, but several competing cards offer higher returns without charging an annual fee — more on that below.

The VentureOne also lacks many of the travel benefits that make premium rewards cards appealing, including airport lounge access and annual travel credits.

If you travel frequently or put a significant amount of spending on your credit cards, you may find that paying an annual fee for a more rewarding card makes sense.

If you're considering the VentureOne, I'd also take a look at the Capital One Venture Rewards Credit Card.

Both cards earn transferable Capital One miles, but the Venture Rewards earns at least 2 miles per dollar spent on purchases, compared to the VentureOne's 1.25 miles per dollar spent. It also comes with a few additional travel perks, including up to a $120 statement credit every four years for the application fee for either Global Entry or TSA PreCheck

The trade-off is that the Venture Rewards has a $95 annual fee, while the VentureOne doesn't charge one.

Still, depending on how much you spend, that $95 could be worth paying. Based on TPG's valuation of 1.85 cents per Capital One mile, you'd need to spend about $6,850 a year on purchases earning the base rate for the extra miles to offset the Venture Rewards card's annual fee.

That's before factoring in welcome offers or other benefits. And if you typically redeem miles for travel statement credits at 1 cent apiece, you'd need to spend more to make up the difference.

Personally, I'd lean toward the Venture Rewards if you plan to use your card regularly and want to earn miles faster. But if avoiding an annual fee is your priority, the VentureOne still gives you access to the same transfer partners.

I think the VentureOne is worth considering if your priority is earning transferable travel rewards without paying an annual fee.

However, if you spend heavily on your card or travel frequently, the Venture Rewards or another higher-earning card could provide better long-term value.

There are currently two welcome offers available on the VentureOne, each with a different spending requirement:

Based on TPG's valuations, which peg Capital One miles at 1.85 cents apiece, these bonuses are worth $370 and $740, respectively.

The biggest difference between the two offers, aside from the bonus miles and spending requirements, is that the standard VentureOne offers a 0% introductory APR for 15 months on purchases and balance transfers, followed by a variable APR of 18.74%-28.74% (a balance transfer fee applies). The Miles Boost version doesn't offer this benefit.

Compared to the card's offer history, the standard 20,000-mile bonus has been available for most of the past few years, although Capital One briefly added a $100 travel credit to the offer in 2025.

The Miles Boost offer, on the other hand, matches the highest bonus we've seen on the VentureOne. So if you've been considering this card, now could be a particularly good time to apply for that version.

Personally, I'd lean toward the Miles Boost offer if you can comfortably meet the higher spending requirement and don't need the introductory APR benefit. You'll earn twice as many bonus miles for an additional $500 in spending.

Keep in mind that Capital One has a 48-month welcome bonus restriction across its Venture card family. If you've received a new cardmember bonus on the VentureOne, Capital One Venture Rewards Credit Card or Capital One Venture X Rewards Credit Card within the past 48 months, you won't be eligible for another VentureOne bonus.

However, earning a VentureOne bonus doesn't, on its own, prevent you from qualifying for a future Venture Rewards or Venture X welcome bonus.

The VentureOne isn't the only option for travelers who want to earn rewards without paying a high annual fee. Depending on your spending habits and travel goals, one of these cards could be a better fit.

For additional options, check out our full list of the best no-annual-fee cards.

The Capital One VentureOne offers something you won't find on every no-annual-fee card: the ability to earn miles that transfer directly to airline and hotel partners.

That makes it a reasonable starting point for travelers who want to explore transferable rewards without committing to an annual fee.

However, its modest earning rate makes it harder to recommend to anyone looking to maximize rewards on everyday spending.

If you're comfortable paying a modest annual fee, I'd consider the Venture Rewards instead. But if avoiding an annual fee is your top priority and you want access to Capital One's transfer partners, the VentureOne is worth a look.
