Lufthansa Abruptly Cancels This 13-Hour Boeing 747 Route: See Map Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The Boeing 747 has become a rare sight these days, as only a handful of airlines still operate the Queen of the Skies. Lufthansa is one of them and remains the largest operator of scheduled passenger flights using the type. According to data from aviation analytics company Cirium, 15,061 Boeing 747 flights are scheduled worldwide between December 2026 and October next year.

Of these, Lufthansa accounts for more than 60% of the flights, around 9,673. The German flag carrier continues to deploy the aircraft on several routes from Frankfurt Airport (FRA) to destinations across North America and Asia, including Singapore Changi Airport (SIN). But in its latest schedule update, the carrier has removed the 747 from its Frankfurt-Singapore route next summer.

Lufthansa currently operates one daily flight between Frankfurt and Singapore using the Boeing 747-400, and the aircraft will remain on the route until March 27, 2027, when the northern winter scheduling season ends. The airline had originally planned to continue using the jumbo jet during the summer 2027 season, but that is no longer the case.

Instead, the German airline will deploy its 293-seat Airbus A350-900 on the route. The aircraft features the airline's new Allegris cabin, comprising 48 business class seats, 21 premium economy seats and 224 economy seats. By comparison, the 747-400 currently operating the route accommodates 371 passengers, including 67 business class seats, 32 premium economy seats and 272 economy seats.

The aircraft change will therefore result in a reduction of 78 seats per flight, or approximately 21%. However, the change appears to be temporary, as the airline's current schedule indicates that the 364-seater Boeing 747-8 will take over the route next winter.

The changes come as Singapore Airlines has also decided to remove the Airbus A380 from the route next summer. The aircraft has not operated scheduled flights to Frankfurt since the northern winter season began on October 25, but was originally due to return on March 28, 2027, with daily flights from Singapore.

However, the airline now plans to use the four-class Boeing 777-300ER throughout the summer season, rather than splitting operations between the 777 and A380 as previously scheduled. Singapore Airlines’ 777-300ER accommodates 264 passengers, including four in first class, 48 in business class, 28 in premium economy and 184 in economy.

By comparison, its A380 has 471 seats, comprising six first-class suites, 78 business-class seats, 44 premium economy seats and 343 economy seats. The changes will reduce the airline's weekly seat capacity from 5,145 in summer 2026 to 3,696 next summer, a reduction of approximately 28%.

The carrier has previously operated three daily flights to Frankfurt on certain dates during the summer seasons of 2023 and 2024, but a third daily service is not scheduled for next summer. This means that the Frankfurt-Singapore route will not see double-decker passenger aircraft during summer 2027.

The handful of remaining 747-400s are set for a packed schedule as retirement looms.

While airlines have increasingly retired their 747 fleets, Lufthansa remains one of the largest operators of the type. According to ch-aviation, the German flag carrier has eight 747-400s in its fleet, although only five remain active, alongside 17 Boeing 747-8s.

These aircraft continue to help the airline maintain capacity across its long-haul network. Cirium data shows that Lufthansa has scheduled around 6,820 Boeing 747 flights for summer 2027, covering a total of 16 routes. All of these destinations will see a daily 747 flight.

Half of these serve destinations in the United States, including Newark Liberty International Airport (EWR), George Bush Intercontinental Airport (IAH) in Houston, Los Angeles International Airport (LAX), Miami International Airport (MIA), and New York (JFK).
