How Dylan Larkin blockbuster impacts Rangers, rest of Eastern Conference

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Since the New York Rangers were never in on the Dylan Larkin trade talks, it can be argued that his move to the Montreal Canadiens on Sunday doesn't directly impact the Blueshirts. It's not like they took a big swing at a lineup altering star player and missed out.

That said, the blockbuster with Larkin heading to the Canadiens and top prospect Michael Hage topping the return package to the Detroit Red Wings could have some ramifications on the Rangers this season and down the road. That's because nothing happens within a bubble in this league, much less within the same conference.

This trade immediately makes the Canadiens a more formidable team. That's saying something because Marty St. Louis' crew qualified for the Stanley Cup Playoffs two years in a row, reached the Eastern Conference Final last spring, and is considered to be a legit Cup contender here in 2026-27.

And all of that was before they acquired a veteran center who's scored 30 or more goals each of the previous five seasons. Montreal now has a 1-2 punch down the middle of Nick Suzuki and Larkin, similar 20-minute per night workhorses, who thrive offensively and are equally as solid defensively. Each is a top-six standout at even strength, force on the power play, and excels as a penalty killer.

Suzuki is Montreal's captain; Larkin wore the C in Detroit, at least until he was stripped of the letter after requesting a trade from the Red Wings, who reached the playoffs once in his 11 full seasons with them.

With Philip Danault as their 3C, the Canadiens are the envy of many teams with what they've now got down the middle. That this trade only cost them one player off the current roster — forward Kirby Dach — is an absolute win for Montreal in its quest to win the Stanley Cup in the here and now.

A really good team in the same conference as the Rangers just got much better. And if the Canadiens ever get fully healthy — forwards Ivan Demidov, Chris Kreider, and Josh Anderson are on IR — watch out.

So, yes, this impacts the Rangers, who are trying to make the playoffs for the first time in three seasons. At the time of the trade, Montreal was five points ahead of New York in the Eastern Conference standings, having played one more game. This after the Rangers shut down the Canadiens in a 3-1 win last week at Madison Square Garden. Though in different divisions, the Rangers realistically could be in a race with the Canadiens for a wild-card berth from the East. Now, it looks like Montreal is just another team that will be out of reach for New York by time the curtain closes on 2026-27.

It feels like the Rangers best path to the playoffs is by finishing in the top three of the Metropolitan Division, and not needing to worry about keeping pace with the Canadiens and other challengers from the stronger Atlantic Division to snag a wild-card berth. This trade further strengthens that belief.

From the Rangers point of view, there's another side to this, because they can't ignore the Red Wings, who also reside in the same conference. Right now, the Red Wings are 10 points out of the second wild card, and eight points behind the Rangers in the East. They're just 8-12-3 and on track to miss the postseason for the 11th straight year.

The Rangers are a better team than the Red Wings. But there are two ways that this trade helps Detroit in the present term. First, they add a decent NHL player in Kirby Dach to their middle-six forward group. They also landed veteran goalie Sam Montembeault — who's currently injured — to their mix between the pipes, perhaps making it more possible to move John Gibson ahead of the trade deadline in March.

What this trade also does is lift the dark clod hanging over the Red Wings. The Larkin drama was a daily issue for this team, especially when he decided to stop playing and was suspended Oct. 26. With this issue cleared up, the Red Wings, perhaps, will relax and simply play hockey and that could lead to better results — even though they must still resolve Simon Edvinsson's holdout.

The Rangers have bigger issues to be concerned about than the Red Wings making a post-trade run in the East, but it's something to consider.

In the bigger picture, Detroit's rebuild got a major shot in the arm with the acquisition of Hage and a haul of draft picks including conditional first-rounders in 2027 and 2029. Hage is the key here. The junior forward leads top-ranked Michigan, where he's teammates with Rangers prospect Malcolm Spence, with 26 points (five goals, 21 assists) in 13 games. The 2024 first-round pick (No. 21 overall) could be a star in the NHL, and that, of course, makes the Red Wings a more formidable foe within a packed conference for the Rangers to contend with the in the future.

Sure, the Rangers must focus on themselves, something they've been very good at this season. Front of mind should be the Rangers figuring out a way to be the best version of themselves after a 5-2 loss to the defending Stanley Cup champion Carolina Hurricanes on Sunday.

But it's not worth pretending that this Larin trade doesn't impact them, either.
