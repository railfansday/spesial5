NBA power rankings: All 30 teams ranked entering Week 5

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

We are now five weeks into the NBA season, and the landscape has been completely upended. Miami has executed a season-saving revival, catching fire and rattling off five straight wins. Just as Memphis appeared to be headed to the bottom half of the standings, the Grizzlies responded by winning three in a row, establishing themselves as the biggest surprise of the season. Meanwhile, a new team has seized the coveted No. 1 spot.

Utah has had a rough stretch after starting the season 1-1, losing 12 straight games. Injuries haven't helped, with Lauri Markkanen, Jaren Jackson Jr. and Jusuf Nurkic all sidelined. Those are three key contributors, and without them, it's hard to expect much winning from this group. Rookie Darryn Peterson and Keyonte George have looked good, though, providing a few bright spots.

Milwaukee continues to struggle, losing 10 straight games and falling toward the bottom of the standings. Jaime Jaquez Jr. and Tyler Herro have been two of the team's few bright spots, providing some much-needed scoring. The bigger concern is the development of its rookies. Nate Ament and Brayden Burries have both been inconsistent and haven't provided reliable minutes, even with plenty of opportunities on a struggling roster.

Sacramento is pretty much where most expected it to be, but its lack of offensive options has become a concern. Darius Acuff has been forced to take more than 20 shots per game, which could lead to some poor shooting habits as he continues to develop. The Kings have also lost six straight, and without more scoring help around Acuff, it's difficult to see this team turning things around anytime soon.

It's the same conversation in Brooklyn as in previous weeks. Julius Randle and Michael Porter Jr. have put up impressive individual numbers, but the rest of the roster hasn't contributed much. Mikel Brown Jr. has provided some additional scoring, although he's been inconsistent. The Nets have now lost two straight and still look like a team without enough reliable pieces to compete.

Trae Young has had a shaky start to the season, looking like an All-Star in some games while disappearing in others. That inconsistency has made it difficult for Washington to find any offensive rhythm, contributing to its eight-game losing streak. There have been some positives, though. Rookie AJ Dybantsa has looked every bit like a first overall pick, while Anthony Davis has been a reliable presence.

Chicago is finally showing some signs of life after a miserable start, going 2-1 over its last three games following a 1-9 opening stretch. Norman Powell has led the way offensively, but the biggest loss has been Caleb Wilson, who is sidelined with a fractured hand. Wilson had been the team's biggest bright spot, and getting him back will be important if Chicago wants to continue building momentum.

Charlotte hasn't been able to find much consistency, going just 2-8 over its last 10 games. Coby White hasn't come anywhere close to living up to his contract, leaving the Hornets without the production they expected from him. Rookie Kon Knueppel has also yet to play meaningful minutes this season and is now sidelined with a hamstring strain. It's been a disappointing start for a team that needs its young players to step up.

Toronto Raptors forward Kawhi Leonard (2) Credit: Brad Mills-Imagn Images

The absence of Kawhi Leonard has really hurt Toronto, which has now lost four straight games. Immanuel Quickley has struggled toward the end of games, particularly with his playmaking and decision-making when the Raptors need him most. R.J. Barrett and Scottie Barnes have been two of the team's brighter spots, but Toronto needs more consistent production from its backcourt to get back on track.

Indiana is still waiting for Tyrese Haliburton to return, as he is expected to remain sidelined through Nov. 21. Pascal Siakam has done a good job leading the offense, while Andrew Nembhard has stepped up and taken on a larger role in Haliburton's absence. The Pacers are 3-7 over their last 10 games, with one of those wins coming against the Knicks in the NBA Cup. They've managed to stay competitive at times, but getting their All-Star point guard back should make a major difference.

New Orleans has looked downright terrible at times, despite having a roster with plenty of talent. Dejounte Murray has also requested a trade, adding more drama to an already disappointing season. The Pelicans have actually been worse with him on the floor, making his situation an interesting one to monitor. There are some positives from the younger players, though. Second-year player Jeremiah Fears recently recorded his first triple-double, while he and Derik Queen have looked like great draft picks. Zion Williamson has also been good.

The Clippers are starting to show some signs of life after taking down Oklahoma City in an NBA Cup game and winning two straight. Darius Garland, Brook Lopez and Rui Hachimura have led the way, providing reliable and efficient scoring. Garland has been particularly impressive, showcasing his playmaking ability in addition to his scoring. Los Angeles still has plenty of ground to make up, but its recent performances have been encouraging.

Cooper Flagg and Kyrie Irving have continued to look good individually, but figuring out how to play together remains important for Dallas. The two are still developing their chemistry, and the Mavericks have dropped three straight games. There's plenty of talent on this roster, but it needs to translate into more consistent results.

Phoenix has really turned its season around after a poor start, climbing back to 6-7. Devin Booker, Dillon Brooks and Jalen Green have all stepped up, giving the Suns a much more reliable offensive attack. Miles Bridges has also been a key contributor and is looking like a great offseason addition. This team is starting to find its identity and could become a much bigger threat as the season progresses.

Franz Wagner has looked like the star of Orlando's roster, helping lead the team through a difficult start. After opening the season 2-7, the Magic have won four straight games and are finally starting to find their rhythm. Paolo Banchero remains sidelined with an injury, making this turnaround even more impressive. Once Banchero returns, Orlando should have an even better chance to climb the standings.

Stephen Curry's absence has exposed just how much Golden State relies on its superstar. Curry is expected to miss at least a month with an oblique injury, and the Warriors have lost three straight games since he's been out. Yaxel Lendeborg has been carrying much of the load, but replacing Curry's production is nearly impossible. Golden State just needs to stay afloat until he returns.

Donovan Mitchell has been vital to Cleveland's success, playing excellent basketball throughout the season. James Harden has also gotten better as the season has progressed, gradually shaking off the offseason rust. Evan Mobley has continued to look like the All-Star center he's proven himself to be, while second-round pick Meleek Thomas has looked like the steal many expected him to be. Cleveland has the talent to compete, but it needs to put together more consistent performances.

Nikola Jokic and Jamal Murray have been forced to do just about everything for Denver, combining for 50 points per game. Jokic also leads the team in both rebounds and assists, showing just how much of the offense runs through him. The problem is that nobody else has emerged as a notable scoring option. The Nuggets didn't add much during the offseason and that lack of reinforcements is starting to show.

Jalen Johnson has arguably been Atlanta's best player, leading the team in rebounds and assists while averaging close to 20 points per game. Nickeil Alexander-Walker has also been impressive, leading the Hawks in scoring with 21.4 points per game. Despite their individual performances, I'm not completely sold on this team. Their most notable win has been a three-point victory against Cleveland at home, while most of their other wins have come against bottom-feeding teams. Atlanta could be a potential fraud if it can't start beating better competition.

The Knicks haven't looked like the championship team many expected them to be entering the season. Losing an NBA Cup game to an Indiana team without Tyrese Haliburton certainly doesn't help their case. Mikal Bridges has looked like a shell of himself, while Jalen Brunson, Karl-Anthony Towns, OG Anunoby and Josh Hart have all played well. There's plenty of talent in New York, but the team hasn't put everything together yet.

Deni Avdija and Damian Lillard have Portland looking like one of the top teams in the league, giving the Trail Blazers a strong offensive foundation. Ja Morant has also looked like a great pickup, especially considering how little Portland gave up to acquire him. He's been a key contributor and has added another dimension to the offense. The biggest problem for Portland is that it's competing in a loaded Western Conference.

Mar 1, 2026; Dallas, Texas, USA; Oklahoma City Thunder guard Shai Gilgeous-Alexander (2) looks to move the ball past Dallas Mavericks guard AJ Johnson (8) during the game between the Mavericks and the Thunder at American Airlines Center. Mandatory Credit: Jerome Miron-Imagn Images

The injuries to Jalen Williams and Chet Holmgren are really starting to hurt Oklahoma City. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander looks drained, carrying most of the scoring and playmaking responsibilities. Jared McCain and Ajay Mitchell have both been great, but their contributions haven't been enough to make up for the missing production. I still expect the Thunder to bounce back, but they need reinforcements in a bad way.

Miami has been one of the hottest teams in the league, winning eight of its last 10 games and putting together a five-game winning streak. Davion Mitchell has provided reliable playmaking for Bam Adebayo and Giannis Antetokounmpo, while the team's chemistry is finally starting to click. After a disappointing start, the Heat are beginning to look like the team everyone expected them to be.

Cade Cunningham has continued to look like one of the best point guards in the NBA, leading Detroit's offense and creating opportunities for his teammates. The Pistons have looked like one of the top teams in the league at times, although they've also had some shaky performances. Jalen Duren is getting closer to returning after resuming on-court activities, and his presence should give Detroit a significant boost. I expect this team to climb once he's back.

Just when you think Memphis is coming back down to earth, the Grizzlies respond with another winning streak. They've won three straight and continue to look like a legitimate playoff team. Cameron Boozer has been making a strong case for Rookie of the Year, while Ty Jerome has arguably been the biggest surprise of the NBA season. He's looked like a legitimate top scorer in the league, giving Memphis an offensive weapon few expected. I'm still not sure how sustainable this is, but the Grizzlies have certainly earned their spot.

With Anthony Edwards sidelined, there were plenty of questions about whether Minnesota could continue winning games. LaMelo Ball has answered those questions, leading the way in scoring and setting up his teammates. He's been excellent, doing a lot to quiet the narrative that he isn't a winning player. Minnesota's role players have also stepped up, giving this team a chance to remain competitive until Edwards returns. The sky's the limit for this group.

Houston Rockets forward Kevin Durant Mandatory Credit: Jerome Miron-Imagn Images

The return of Fred VanVleet has made a massive difference for Houston, providing the team with a reliable ball handler and changing the way its offense operates. Jabari Smith Jr., Alperen Sengun and Amen Thompson have all taken huge leaps, while Kevin Durant continues to be the efficient scorer he's always been, simply taking fewer shots. Houston looks like a completely different team and has established itself as a legitimate contender.

Boston has looked impressive despite still having a significant hole at center. Jayson Tatum has returned to his superstar form, while Paul George has experienced something of a career revival. Payton Pritchard and Derrick White also remain two of the best supporting pieces in the NBA, providing reliable production and depth. The Celtics have the talent to compete with anyone, but their lack of a reliable center could become an issue.

Philadelphia has looked like the best team in the Eastern Conference, winning three straight games and climbing to 12-3. LeBron James has embraced whatever role the team needs, focusing on playmaking and rebounding while still contributing offensively. Tyrese Maxey and Jaylen Brown have carried much of the scoring load and have been impressive all season. This looks like the most complete team in the East.

Luka Doncic has been absolutely unreal, putting together an MVP-caliber start and looking like the early favorite for the award. The Lakers have won eight straight games and have established themselves as one of the league's top teams. Austin Reaves and Walker Kessler have also been two of the team's biggest contributors outside of Doncic, making their recent injuries something to monitor. Both missed the last game, and their availability will be important to Los Angeles' continued success.

San Antonio has been nearly unstoppable, winning 12 straight games after dropping its first two contests. Victor Wembanyama has catapulted himself into the early MVP conversation, while Dylan Harper, Stephon Castle, Devin Vassell, and De'Aaron Fox have all been reliable scorers. What's most impressive is how comfortable the younger players look. Despite being early in their NBA careers, they play with the confidence and composure of players who've been in the league for a decade. The Spurs have earned the top spot.
