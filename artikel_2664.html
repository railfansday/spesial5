Paige Spiranac Reveals Panic Attack After Internet Invitational Trauma

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Paige Spiranac has broken her silence regarding the intense psychological toll of competitive golf, revealing she suffered a full-blown panic attack during a recent Internet Invitational. The former professional golfer described the experience as her most nerve-racking moment since retiring from the tour, driven by an overwhelming fear of letting her teammates down in a high-stakes environment.

The admission comes after Spiranac stepped away from the event temporarily to regain her composure. According to EssentiallySports, the pressure mounted when she realized she potentially had to step up and make a critical play for her team. The situation escalated quickly, forcing her to leave the course for a moment to manage the severity of her anxiety.

Spiranac explained that the anxiety stemmed from a combination of factors, including the heat, overstimulation, and the massive financial stakes involved. She noted that the environment felt incredibly strange, with so much happening at all times that it became impossible to focus. Beyond the physical discomfort, the mental burden of knowing that a mistake would be amplified by the public weighed heavily on her.

According to EssentiallySports, Spiranac admitted that the fear of public failure was a primary driver of her distress. She stated, "Like, you know, if you mess up, everyone is going to see it and comment on it, and it becomes like a whole big storyline in and of itself." This heightened visibility turned a simple golf shot into a potential viral moment, adding layers of pressure that do not exist in traditional tournament play.

The incident was not an isolated event for the golfer, who returned to the Internet Invitational carrying the baggage of a controversial 2025 final. In the prior match, Spiranac partnered with Frankie Borrelli and Malosi Togisala to face Brad Dalke, Cody "Beef" Franke, and Francis Ellis. Cameras captured Spiranac pressing down the grass around Togisala's ball in the rough, leading to accusations that she had improved his lie.

Even though Spiranac eventually confessed she was unaware of breaking a rule when it happened, the event sparked severe backlash that haunted her for months. Telling Dan Rapaport, she disclosed that the controversy brought tens of thousands of death threats, which compelled her to quit social media completely. This history of abuse made the return to competitive play a daunting challenge, as the fear of another public mistake loomed large.

The situation highlights a growing issue in the modern golf landscape where online engagement and real-life consequences collide. Spiranac described the specific moment of her breakdown, "Internet Invitational. I was blacked out. I was so nervous. And then at this last one, there was this moment when I potentially had to step up and do something for my team. I had to step away for a second. I had a full-blown panic attack."

Spiranac emphasized that the pressure to not let her teammates down was compounded by the sheer volume of money on the line and the lack of a supportive buffer against public criticism. The combination of these elements created a perfect storm for a mental health crisis on the course.
