Flying Blue Delays 50% SAF XP Price Hike After Admitting “We Didn’t Handle The Rollout Well”

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Flying Blue has postponed an abrupt devaluation to the elite status credit members earn through sustainable aviation fuel and charitable contributions. I remain skeptical of the underlying scheme, but program chief Tiffany Funk deserves credit for acknowledging the botched rollout, listening to members, and giving them time to adjust.

Air France-KLM Flying Blue initially changed its earning rates on November 2, 2026, without advance notice. For cash contributions toward sustainable aviation fuel (SAF), the cost increased from €10 per XP to €15 per XP, a 50% increase.

XP, or Experience Points, determine Flying Blue elite status. Members who had incorporated these contributions into their qualification plans suddenly faced a more expensive path to their goal.

Now, as One Mile At A Time also reports, Flying Blue has postponed the changes until December 1, 2026. The previous earning rates return effective November 4, and contributions made since November 2 will also receive credit at the prior rates.

Tiffany Funk, Head of Flying Blue, addressed the reprieve:

On Monday, we updated how many XP you earn on Sustainable Aviation Fuel and charitable contributions. We didn’t handle the rollout well, and the new rate went live with unclear messaging and without notice. While this affects a small share of our members, we recognize for those members it matters a great deal.

The update was part of a continuous review of our elite program. We want to ensure we can keep delivering the benefits and service you expect.

For now, we’re pressing pause. From tomorrow (4 November), SAF and charitable contributions will earn XP at the previous rate, and any contribution made since 2 November will be credited at the prior rate as well. The new rate of €15 per XP begins on 1 December 2026; even at the new rate, no program in the market will recognize SAF contributions more generously than Flying Blue, including on award tickets.

We want Flying Blue to be a program you build your life around. Many of you put a lot of care into planning your flights and status qualification, and we understand your need to have time to make adjustments.

And to everyone who told us why this matters to you: thank you for caring this much about Flying Blue.

I appreciate the candor. Funk acknowledges that Flying Blue handled the rollout poorly and offers a practical correction. Members now have a transition period, including protection for contributions made during the initial rollout.

To be candid, I consider this arrangement a greenwashing revenue-generating measure. Awarding elite status credit for an environmental contribution is a convenient way to monetize the pursuit of status while wrapping the transaction in a sustainability message, a win-win for both Flying Blue and frequent travelers but probably not for the environment.

We don’t have to litigate that here and I readily acknowledge SAF can play a role in reducing aviation’s lifecycle emissions, but purchasing a contribution does not make a passenger’s flight emissions disappear. And I suspect the additional XP is the deciding factor for many members who participate, not altruism for Mother Earth.

Flying Blue certainly understands the appeal. Someone approaching a qualification deadline may find paying for a contribution far more convenient than taking another flight. But my skepticism about the arrangement does not diminish the importance of treating participating members fairly. Flying Blue offered an opportunity, and members planned around it. Their reliance was entirely foreseeable.

Flying Blue owes no one a permanent opportunity to acquire additional XP at a particular rate. It can decide that the existing formula is too generous or that it wants a different balance between flying and other qualification methods. Members can then decide whether the revised offer is worthwhile.

My larger objection, as with most devaluations, concerns candid communication and advance notice. Tell us what is changing. Give us time to adjust plans made under the existing rules. Please spare us the suggestion that a reduction in benefits is an “enhancement” inspired by member feedback.

Funk avoids that familiar gaslighting here. She does not claim members asked to pay more for their XP. She acknowledges that feedback exposed a problem with the rollout and explains what Flying Blue will do about it.

That is a much more credible use of member feedback.

Consider someone who booked travel expecting to earn a certain number of XP from flights and make up the remainder through SAF contributions. An overnight change can upset that calculation after the flights have already been purchased.

That member is not necessarily demanding that the rules remain unchanged forever. The objection is that a plan made in good faith has become more expensive without warning.

The additional time gives members an opportunity to complete contributions at the previous rate or reconsider their qualification strategy. Crediting contributions made since November 2 at the old rate also avoids penalizing those who happened to transact during the botched rollout.

Neither measure eliminates the devaluation. Both make its introduction fairer.

I commend Funk for listening and adjusting. Management can respond to criticism without abandoning the underlying business decision. Here, a relatively straightforward concession makes loyal customers  feel heard rather than taken for granted.

I remain skeptical of Flying Blue’s SAF contribution scheme, and the higher cost per XP will make it less attractive. Heck, I remain skeptical of status in general as a proud free agent for two years running. But the program has corrected the avoidable failure of introducing that change without clear communication or notice.

Funk acknowledged what went wrong and gave members time to prepare. Flying Blue is entitled to revise its earning rates. Members who planned around those rates deserve a reasonable transition, and they now have one. That is a welcome response…I never thought Funk would make the program more generous as other loyalty programs cut and devalue, but measured and clear changes with advance notice to members are all anyone can ask for.
