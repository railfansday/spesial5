India’s government ponders cradle to grave AI oversight

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Machines of loving grace to turn social media grumps into services, finger painting into career advice

Indian politicians have proposed that AI could take a major role in citizens’ lives, partly through pervasive surveillance.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi last week spelled out his vision for an AI-based app that would collect data about children as they grow and analyze it to determine their aptitude for different types of study or careers.

Modi suggested that parents of three-year-old children should enter data about their offspring’s activities and accomplishments into a government-run system that is also accessible to teachers and even a child’s friends. Those records, the PM suggested, will “help identify children's interests and aptitude from an early age and redirect them in the study of their choice in future.”

That’s not an entirely creepy idea, because gaining access to India’s top tertiary institutions requires students to pay a hefty fee to sit for entrance exams. Modi pitched recommendations from the AI-powered app as something parents will appreciate as they contemplate whether to fund their offspring’s studies.

India’s most populous state, Uttar Pradesh (UP), has proposed to build an “AI Stack” to serve its 240 million-plus residents.

A 178-page tender seeks bidders to build a system that uses the state’s Family ID – a unique identifier for each household – to link data held by multiple agencies so it can be put to work in a dozen AI use cases.

One of those use cases is a tool for “AI-Enabled Social Media Monitoring and Urban Grievance Detection” that “analyses publicly available social media content including online news channels and connected grievance channels to detect emerging urban issues, citizen complaints, service interruptions, and recurring service gaps.”

The government wants that system to autonomously route complaints to relevant agencies, to automate actions like sending crews to patch up damaged roads. Critics have pointed to vague wording in the tender as potentially allowing the UP government to link this system to other agencies – a matter of concern given Indian state and national governments have implemented internet shutdowns to stop protestors from communicating on social media services and messaging apps.

Another use case is a knowledge assistant for state police that looks up case histories and other data but can’t use it to assess evidence or provide investigative conclusions. An app for teachers will advise them on how to create custom content to meet students’ needs. Healthcare workers would also get an app to help them make recommendations during patient visits.

Citizens get natural language interfaces to a system that offers advice on how to identify and access government services and schemes.

UP wants to drive these apps with self-hosted open-weight models, and to use only anonymized datasets.

India’s Internet Freedom Foundation worries that the systems described in the tender aren’t explicitly authorized by any legislation, and that the document doesn’t mention important oversight measures for the AI Stack. ®
