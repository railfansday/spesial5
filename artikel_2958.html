Gold Falls Below $4,000: Is Now the Time to Invest in This Gold-Backed ETF?

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Investors have been bearish on gold of late, but that could change in the not-too-distant future.

The price of gold has been falling sharply over the past several months. The days of it being well over $5,000 per ounce are long gone. On Tuesday, it was trading below even the $4,000 mark, as a rise in interest rates has led to investors seeking assets with high yields.

As a result of the decline, SPDR Gold Shares (GLD +0.16%), which is backed by physical gold, is down 11% this year and has declined more than 30% from its 52-week high. In the past five years, however, it has doubled in value. Could now be an opportune time to invest in this fund while its valuation is lower?

While gold is not typically known for being a great investment, the SPDR Gold Shares fund has surprisingly outperformed the broader market over the past five years. It's up around 104% during that time frame, while the S&P 500, which tracks the top stocks on U.S. markets, has generated total returns (which include dividend payments) of around 70%.

Many investors see gold as a way to diversify their portfolios and even hedge against market uncertainty. Late last year, it rose significantly in value as investors grew concerned about high valuations in the market. While valuations remain high today, interest rates have also been climbing, making gold less attractive to hold. The concern is also that in the near term, rates may rise even higher given that inflation isn't at the Fed's target level of 2%.

Right now, the outlook doesn't look all that enticing for buying the SPDR Gold Shares fund. Interest rates are on the rise and could continue climbing next year.

However, I'm optimistic that this won't be a repeat of 2022, when inflation was wildly out of control due to high post-pandemic spending. This time around, it's more to do with higher commodity prices and the war in Iran. With Democrats having control of Congress next year, there could be more of a push to end the war and to help alleviate inflation. Should that happen, that may lessen the need for higher interest rates.

The economic picture can change quickly. While the expectation right now is that interest rates will continue to rise next year, I believe those concerns may be a bit overblown. If the war in Iran ends, that could drastically change that outlook. Meanwhile, with many stocks still looking overpriced, gold could remain in high demand as investors seek safe-haven assets to hold, which is why I believe investing in the SPDR Gold Shares fund may prove to be a good move right now.
