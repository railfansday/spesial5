Player Grades: Lakers vs. Kings

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Not all wins need to be pretty, but it would be nice if the Lakers had one soon.

Their current homestand has been defined by some really ugly basketball. Quentin Grimes saved them from a loss against the Hornets and it was a team effort to outlast the Kings on Friday.

Fatigue wasn't an excuse the Lakers wanted to use and it stops holding weight when you're stringing together home games. At this point, it's just bad basketball.

Every team goes through a rut. The Lakers are mostly winning during theirs. However, let Friday be the last of the ugly games, especially with a match-up with the Spurs looming next week.

So, let's dive into the win. As always, grades are based on expectations for each player. A "B" grade represents the average performance for that player.

27 minutes, 14 points, 1 rebound, 1 assist, 1 steal, 1 block, 1 foul, 5-8 FG, 4-5 3PT, +5

As he said in the walk-off interview on Spectrum SportsNet, this was some regression to the mean. Thybulle was due for a good night from beyond the arc.

27 minutes, 5 points, 1 assist, 2-8 FG, 1-6 3PT, -5

If Redick was looking for a larger sample size of Williams with the starters, I think it's pretty clear at this point that it isn't working. While he didn't get into foul trouble on Friday, it was another mostly unimpactful game from Williams, save for the big three he hit in the fourth.

30 minutes, 15 points, 7 rebounds, 2 assists, 1 steal, 3 blocks, 5 fouls, 2 turnovers, 7-11 FG, 1-1 3PT, 0-2 FT, +4

This stat line would have looked a lot better with an extra point or two after those late free throws. It was a much better second half after a first half that was defined by the cliché of "million dollar move, 10 cent finish."

34 minutes, 13 points, 5 rebounds, 7 assists, 1 foul, 3 turnovers, 2-7 FG, 1-5 3PT, 8-9 FT, +3

After back-to-back solid performances, the pendulum swung back for Grimes. Still, he was responsible for the Lakers' final five points, including the biggest shot of the night for his only three.

36 minutes, 25 points, 4 rebounds, 8 assists, 2 steals, 4 fouls, 6 turnovers, 9-18 FG, 2-9 3PT, 5-6 FT, -5

Luka's funk continued on Friday. His overall shooting is a bit of an illusion as he continues to struggle from three. More concerning was the overall poor decision-making, whether it was on numerous bad passes or some really bad shots.

20 minutes, 9 points, 9 rebounds, 2 assists, 1 steal, 1 block, 1 foul, 2 turnovers, 4-8 FG, 0-2 3PT, 1-1 FT, +1

Like Thybulle, Mamu was due for some regression to the mean. He still can't make a 3-pointer if his life depended on it, but was a huge spark in the team's second-half run.

23 minutes, 6 points, 3 rebounds, 1 foul, 2-4 FG, 1-3 3PT, 1-2 FT, +1

By Jake's standards, a pretty quiet night that didn't include his normal stat-stuffing.

18 minutes, 10 points, 1 rebound, 4 assists, 2 steals, 1 foul, 4-7 FG, 0-1 3PT, 2-2 FT, +6

Again, some important baskets and production from Sexton, especially in the second half. He had a couple nice takes to the basket and showed his chemistry with Kessler during that stretch.

10 minutes, 4 points, 4 rebounds, 2 assists, 1 foul, 2-2 FG, +4

As deceiving as a stat line as there is. Vando was all over the place and was at the center of the team making a run in the third.

11 minutes, 5 points, 1 rebound, 1 steal, 1 foul, 2-5 FG, 1-4 3PT, -4

For the first time this season, Carr got some regular rotation minutes and looked mostly good. He certainly wasn't afraid to shoot, which was mostly a good thing until his final few attempts. Still, this was an encouraging game for him.

I'll continue to dock him for having him Ziaire in the starting lineup, but I did like adding Carr to the rotation. He also admitted to ripping into the team at halftime, which was deserved. They came out much better in the second half, which is probably related.

Friday's inactives: Carson Cooper, Arthur Kaluma, Chris Mañon, Austin Reaves
