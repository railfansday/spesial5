ServiceNow vs. Synopsys: Which Technology Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Two cloud giants with very different engines, one automating corporate workflows and the other designing the chips behind them.

By Sara Appino – Updated Nov 27, 2026 at 10:36AM EST

10 stocks we like better than ServiceNow ›

As businesses race to integrate artificial intelligence, choosing between enterprise workflow automation and semiconductor design tools involves weighing two different growth engines. Should you favor ServiceNow (NOW +0.47%) or Synopsys (SNPS +2.79%)?

ServiceNow provides a cloud platform that streamlines corporate operations, while Synopsys builds the software essential for designing the next generation of chips. Both companies benefit from the digital transformation trend, but their business models target different layers of the technology stack, making them intriguing options for investors seeking growth.

ServiceNow provides an enterprise software platform that connects data and workflows to automate functions like HR and customer service. The company serves roughly 8,700 enterprise customers across various industries, utilizing its platform to streamline complex corporate processes. Its growth strategy involves deep integration with artificial intelligence and strategic technology alliances with partners like Microsoft (MSFT +0.37%), NVIDIA (NVDA -0.86%), and OpenAI.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $15.4 billion, growing roughly 21.8%. Net income for the period was close to $1.4 billion. These results reflect a consistent expansion in the adoption of cloud-based automation tools as organizations prioritize digital efficiency.

As of its September 2026 balance sheet, the current ratio is approximately 0.7x (0.72x), which indicates the ability to pay short-term obligations using current assets. The debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 0.6x (0.64x), measuring total debt against shareholder equity. In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, free cash flow was nearly $4.8 billion ($4,750 million), representing cash flow from operations minus capital expenditures. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 43.6% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Synopsys offers engineering solutions that span from silicon design to system analysis, supporting industries like aerospace, automotive, and healthcare. The company is a key player among semiconductor stocks by providing essential tools for creating advanced chips and autonomous machines. Its growth is bolstered by its merger with Ansys and a partnership with Microsoft to deliver cloud-based design solutions.

In the 12 months ended July 31, 2026, revenue reached approximately $9.4 billion, which represents growth of roughly 46.3%. Net income during this period was nearly $1.1 billion. This significant increase in revenue highlights the expanding demand for engineering solutions as more industries transition toward connected technology and advanced silicon design.

As of its July 2026 balance sheet, the current ratio is close to 1.3x (1.34x), measuring the ability to cover short-term liabilities with current assets. The debt-to-equity ratio, which measures financial leverage by dividing total debt by shareholder equity, is approximately 0.3x (0.35x). In the 12 months ended July 31, 2026, free cash flow was roughly $2.7 billion ($2,748 million). Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 32.3% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Synopsys carries significant indebtedness following its merger with Ansys, which limits its financial flexibility and requires substantial cash flow for debt service. The company is also exposed to geopolitical and trade regulation risks, particularly involving U.S. and Chinese trade policies that impact the semiconductor supply chain. Furthermore, Synopsys faces securities litigation and class action lawsuits related to its Design IP segment. Competition remains intense in the electronic design automation market from rivals like Cadence Design Systems (CDNS +0.98%), and the company must also manage the integration risks associated with its large-scale merger.

I'd go with ServiceNow. Big companies lean on its software every day, from IT help desks to HR requests, and now it is weaving AI into the mix. Here's the best part: With a 98% renewal rate, almost every customer sticks around.

AI is already adding up. Management expects its AI products to reach $1.7 billion in yearly contracts by year-end, and overall sales are still growing at a fast clip, though the revenue it has already lined up for the next year is expected to grow a bit more slowly.

Synopsys is well placed, too. It sells the software engineers use to design computer chips, and AI is making those chips harder to design. But much of its recent growth came from buying Ansys, which loaded it with debt, and export limits on China weigh on parts of its business.

ServiceNow, meanwhile, has fewer moving parts. For long-term investors, software that customers almost never leave, with AI adding a new growth leg, makes ServiceNow the better buy for 2027.
