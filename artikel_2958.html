Kohl's Stock Rocks on Tuesday. Here's Why.

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Kohl's astounded investors who were expecting no profit this morning -- by earning $2.15 per share instead.

By Rich Smith – Nov 24, 2026 at 10:59AM EST

It's Tuesday morning, investors, and one of the hottest stocks on the market today is... Kohl's Corporation (KSS +16.51%).

Why? Shares of the department store chain surged 13.4% higher through 10:35 a.m. ET this morning after Kohl's beat analyst forecasts for (checks notes) zero profit, reporting pro forma earnings of $1.76 per share instead. Kohl's also beat on sales, reporting $3.5 billion for Q3 2026 versus forecasts of $3.4 billion.

The story is not quite as simple as that, of course. The zero-profit quarter that Wall Street analysts were forecasting for Kohl's was based on pro forma, non-GAAP profit. Kohl's crushed that estimate, but it also reported $2.15 per share in earnings calculated under generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP).

Even with more than half that money coming from a tax benefit, Kohl's quarterly earnings would have been nearly $1 per share.

And judging from Kohl's guidance, investors can expect things to get even better -- for a short while at least. Kohl's grew its same-store sales just 1.2% in Q3, and total sales only 1.3%. It also improved gross and operating profit margins, helping to earn much more on the slight increase in sales.

How long can that last, though? Management sees sales growth accelerating slightly in Q4, but to no better than 2%. Full-year profits will probably be about $4.40 per share, valuing Kohl's stock at about 5.5 times current-year earnings.

This soundsvery cheap. Still, Kohl's expects to get half its profit this year from "tariff refunds" and "tax benefit," with about 20% of the remainder from selling off real estate. These are all one-time items that will be difficult for Kohl's to replicate in the future. I expect Kohl's stock to look very cheap by the end of 2026 -- but when 2027 arrives, it will look a whole lot more expensive.

Caveat investor. Kohl's looks like a value trap to me.
