Knicks 111, Raptors 102: Scenes from a mirror game

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

What a weird one. The Knicks (8-6) beat the Raptors (5-9) on Sunday and stayed in town for a rematch two days later; in some ways, it felt like watching a replay of the first game. The Knicks lost the first frame by six, outscored the Dinos by 10 in the second, lost the third quarter by seven, and finally got straight in the fourth. Winning the frame 31-19 thanks to great play from the usual suspects (Captain Jalen Brunson, Karl-Anthony Towns, Landry Shamet, and OG Anunoby), New York hit the gas in crunch time to seal the deal, 111-102.

When these teams last met, New York needed a late rally to escape a 14-point hole and win in overtime. They coughed up the rock 18 times, tying their season-worst total. It wasn't pretty until late. According to the broadcast, Scottie Barnes (who scored 27 in the loss) credited Mikal Bridges specifically for dialing up the defensive intensity in the fourth quarter. Bridges has logged just three double-digit point games this season, which is less than ideal for a starter making $33.4 M, but when he applies the defensive screws, it makes up for his sins on the offensive side. The 30-year-old wing had his number called first tonight. He repaid the privilege by fighting for a contested foul-line jumper and scored seven points in his first seven minutes.

On the whole, however, his team had their difficulties through the first quarter. After scoring the first six points of the night, they watched nine straight points go right by them. The visitors outrebounded the Raps by a factor of two (good), but failed to capitalize on many of those second chances (less good). Blame their 39% shooting from the field and 29% from the perimeter (very not good). Meanwhile, the home team hit 63% of their first-quarter attempts and continued to dominate their guests in the paint. In their last tilt, the Dinos outscored them in the paint 52-36, and they looked ready to have a similar result. Score after one: 29-23.

Shades of a game just past haunted the 'Bockers in the second quarter, as Toronto stretched its lead to 13 points by the 5:38 mark. Ja'Kobe Walter did much of the damage, scoring eight points in a little over a minute, including two three-pointers, while Jamal Shead (who impressed on Sunday) repeatedly found open teammates for five assists. OAKAAKs RJ Barrett and Immanuel Quickley combined for a three-pointer, a layup, and a floater early in the period.

New York struggled to generate consistent offense. Bridges whiffed on a pair of threes, and Jordan Clarkson contributed little beyond an offensive foul and turnover. OG Anunoby provided some excitement with a dunk, two blocks, and three free throws, but the offense was flowing like ketchup from a bottle.

The Knicks finally found their footing. Josh Hart and Landry Shamet hit longalls, fueling a 9-0 run that cut Toronto's lead to 48-44. Towns then took over inside, smashing the offensive glass, scoring on a putback, a floater, and a dunk. The Raps are familiar with scoring droughts, and they slipped and tripped while the good guys rattled off a 19-2 run to end the half, 54-50.

By intermission, both teams were shooting 29% from downtown, but the Knicks were surviving inside, now outscoring the Raptors by four in the paint. They dominated the glass, 24-13, and turned the ball over only four times—although Toronto was almost perfect, committing just one. Through the half, Towns and RJ Barrett had scored 11 apiece.

Knicks coach Mike Brown is fortunate to have a full stable of guards. Of late, he's favored Tyler Kolek over Jose Alvarado, but tonight, after Kolek missed 3-of-4 from the field and offset his one dime with a turnover, Brown paired Alvarado with Brunson in the backcourt for a productive stretch in the third frame.

Unfortunately for our guys, the quarter was on the whole a more disappointing affair. Walter continued to shoot flames from his fingers (four triples and 12 points in the period), and Barrett added four points and some dimes. Brunson scored half of the Knicks' total in the third, as they were outscored 33-26. Bridges lent a layup and a three, but was also the team's worst -19 plus/minus. Josh Hart, with five points so far, was +15. You figure it out. The Knicks trailed 83-80 going into the final frame.

As on Sunday, so on Tuesday. The Knicks, mostly sleepy for most of the night, released a lion-like yawn and rose from their slumber to start the fourth with a 10-3 run. On the floor at the start of that good stuff were Kolek, Shamet, Hart, Anunoby, and Towns, with Jordan Clarkson spelling Anunoby a few minutes in. Redeeming himself for the iffy first half, Kolek was cooking—he had an especially beautiful behind-the-head pass from the paint to Shamet, who side-stepped a defender for a practice swish. We're salivating already at the idea of having Kolek and Alvarado off the bench in the playoffs.

Ha. Nevermind. After scoring 11 straight points, they took a break and let Toronto go on a 12-4 run. Quickley started that with a trey and Barrett ended it with another. At the four-minute mark, Anunoby netted a 28-footer (his first) to knot the score at 98. Then it was tied at 100. And yet again, the Americans and Canadians headed into crunch time with a very real threat of another overtime. At 1:36—102-102, thanks to a Barrett layup.

During the fourth, Walter was driving to the cup when the most unlikely of blocks occurred:

KAT dimed Cap for a bomb with 1:25 left to make it 105-102. On the shot, Walter was given a flagrant foul for violating Jalen's landing space (for once, we disagree with the call, but shhh). Brunson's face showed pain, but he walked it off and converted the four-point play. Toronto would not score again (Boyles fouled Shamet, Barrett missed a jumper, and Quickley lost the ball to Anunoby), and successfully completed their 15th straight loss to the Knicks. Well done, lads!

New York travels to Minnesota for a tussle with the Timberwolves. Mo is applying a second coat to your recap. Safe travels, Knickerbockers.
