Virginia Tech football: 6 takeaways from Hokies’ embarrassing loss to Stanford

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The Virginia Tech Hokies had what many believed was a season-defining win over the Clemson Tigers in Death Valley three weeks ago. Since then, the Hokies have had a bye week and two ugly blowout losses.

On Saturday night, Virginia Tech suffered one of the most embarrassing, inexplicable losses in school history, falling 40-22 to Stanford. You could make the case that the ODU loss that got Brent Pry fired was worse, or that horrific Friday night loss to Duke under Justin Fuente, but considering everyone's overall optimism with the program under James Franklin, this feels worse.

Now that we've started with that, here are six takeaways from Saturday night's disaster.

Tell me I'm wrong. I don't care. That entire defense acted like it was too cold last night and they would've rather been anywhere else. Yet they were facing a team from California, which isn't used to playing in cold weather. Yet, the team that flew across the country and hadn't won a road game in two years looked much more comfortable. How is that possible? In our preview, we shared Stanford's offensive and defensive rankings. The Cardinal were bad — like really bad. You can always tell a player's effort by how they tackle. Well, no one on Virginia Tech's roster was interested in tackling on Saturday night. With no disrespect meant to Davis Warren, Micah Ford and Benji Blackburn, they aren't Andrew Luck, Christian McCaffrey, or Zach Ertz. The Hokies made them all look like that and more. Ford probably broke 77 tackles on 27 carries. A bad look for Virginia Tech's players in front of another sold-out crowd that came to support them.

When Jeffrey Overton Jr. (more on him later) put the Hokies on his back for that touchdown drive before halftime, it looked like Tech offensive coordinator Ty Howle found something that worked. After all, Stanford couldn't stop the run entering the game, so it made sense to ride Overton. I mean, if they couldn't run the ball against this defense, who could they run against?

The Hokies opened the second half with the football, trailing 24-7. There was cautious optimism that they had figured something out. So how did Howle respond?

Grunkemeyer's pass incomplete to Ayden Greene on fourth down

This was a pathetic series. After no gain on first down and one yard on second down, you chose to run again on third down? Why? Why not throw on one of the first two downs? I mean, you have an All-American tight end who has dominated everyone all season. This was a shameful series from Howle.

As he has all season, the sophomore put the running game on his back. As a team, the Hokies rushed for only 128 yards on 39 attempts, averaging 3.3 yards per carry. Grunkemeyer was sacked several times, which made those numbers look worse. Overton carried the ball 19 times for 107 yards and a touchdown. Make no mistake, when Overton got the ball, there were no holes. He created holes. He ran over defenders, made guys miss. It was another impressive showing from Overton, who is known for his big-play ability but also gets it done between the tackles. Virginia Tech's offensive line put in another poor run-blocking effort, but Overton came to play. He's a stud.

I don't like to say a player is playing scared, but you could say that about Virginia Tech quarterback Ethan Grunkemeyer on Saturday night. Grunk has been phenomenal this season. There's no doubt about that. He's given the Hokies the best QB play they've had in a long time—all fair points. But on Saturday night, he was a part of the problem. He held onto the ball too long. He made bad decisions. He was inaccurate. His fourth-down pass to Ayden Greene should've been a huge gain, but Grunkemeyer threw it late. He sailed multiple passes high toward Luke Reynolds. And how many times did Grunkemeyer bail the pocket when he shouldn't have? VT's pass protection wasn't great by any stretch, but some of those five sacks belong on Grunkemeyer.

Grunkemeyer wasn't great at SMU, but Saturday's game was truly his worst game as a Hokie. There was a point late in the second half where I thought, why not give Bryce Baker a shot to spark them? I was in no way suggesting Grunk be benched. He's the starter, and he's earned it. However, the Hokies couldn't afford to lose that game, and Grunkemeyer didn't have it. I get why Franklin didn't do that. Here's hoping this was just a bad night for Grunkemeyer and everyone else, and he plays out of his mind the next two weeks. We know he's capable.

James Franklin is only in his first year, but he could be forced to make some big changes this offseason. Let's get the big one out of the way: Brent Pry is going nowhere. Pry's game plan over the last couple of weeks made no sense. He struggles to adjust or know when to send pressure. The tackling keeps getting worse. This may be the worst secondary for the Hokies in 40 years. They can't cover or tackle. It doesn't matter who the opponent is; this secondary will allow a big play or two. So what changes could be made? It's fair to wonder if Franklin would consider moving on from cornerback coach Nick Perry and offensive line coach Matt Moore. Perry served as Arkansas's defensive backs coach last season, but he'd never specifically coached cornerbacks. He also played safety. His unit isn't getting better; it's getting worse. Why not play some of the younger cornerbacks? They can't be worse. Let them play through their mistakes. I wouldn't be surprised if Franklin does make a change; he starts with Perry.

Offensively, the same logic applies to Moore. Franklin inherited Moore, but he chose to keep him. Moore is a good recruiter, which helps his cause. But this offensive line has been a mess all season. Sure, some of that is a talent issue, but the Hokies should find ways to block teams like Stanford and Cal and run it down their throats. Moore has dealt with injuries, and he has several promising young players waiting in the wings, so Franklin may remain patient with his staff.

The Hokies aren't winning next week, which means they'll be 6-5 heading into a must-win regular-season finale against Virginia. People will demand changes if this team finishes with four straight losses to end the regular season — and they should.

There is no way that this team should look like they have over the last two weeks coming off a bye. Inexcusable — and that's on the coaching staff.

Virginia Tech fans have enjoyed an extended honeymoon with new head coach James Franklin. A 6-2 start didn't hurt either. Now, 6-4 and massive underdogs on Saturday, the Hokies are staring at a 6-5 record going into the finale. At first glance, people wouldn't be too unhappy. After all, Tech won only three games last season, so this was a big step up. However, everything requires context. The Hokies didn't play a difficult schedule, so they should have 7-8 wins going into this weekend. How you got to 6-5 matters. Virginia Tech should have never lost to Cal or Stanford. If not for turnovers, the Hokies would've beaten Pitt, too. These are some unforgivable losses. So Hokie Nation's patience with Franklin is gone. He needs to finish strong, because when Franklin looks for donations this offseason, some may be less likely to give, given how the Hokies have played in recent weeks. Sometimes it's not the losses, but how you lost and who you lost to that matters.
