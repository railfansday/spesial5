Virgin Atlantic Ends All Flights To 2 Countries: See Full list Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Virgin Atlantic is the UK's second-largest long-haul operator. The SkyTeam member's schedule submission to OAG shows that the airline plans to fly to 14 countries in the first half of 2027. Gone are Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates, although things might not be quite this simple.

Additions include South Korea and Thailand for the first time. The latter involved the launch of London Heathrow-Phuket service on October 18, 2026. The carrier will be pleased that this route will no longer face indirect competition with Norse Atlantic from Gatwick, as that route is now unlikely to begin.

Simple Flying's Live Flight Tracker shows that Virgin ceased flying from Heathrow to Riyadh on March 7, 2026, shortly after the start of the war in Iran. As this was the airline's only route to Saudi Arabia, all flights to the country ended. Virgin had its codeshare and broader partnership with fellow SkyTeam member Saudia, and had an increasingly close partnership with Riyadh Air.

The route did not last long, as the first departure only took place on March 30, 2025. With a daily service using the four-class, 262-seat Airbus A330-900, the new offering to one of aviation's fastest-growing markets was designed to make a statement. Booking data shows that Virgin revolved around the local London-Riyadh market, which has always been high-yielding from lots of premium traffic.

It is unclear if Virgin benefited from funding from Saudi Arabia's Air Connectivity Program. This has underpinned a considerable number of routes to the Kingdom. For example, Delta Air Lines relies on it for its upcoming service from Atlanta to Riyadh, if it ever materializes, while Wizz Air did so for its now-eliminated links from Gatwick to Medina and Jeddah.

When Virgin disclosed that it would end the route, reference was inevitably made to the “evolving situation in the Middle East." This meant the war in Iran and all the consequences of it. Obviously, this remains a problem today, including for aviation. Many airlines have suspended routes to Gulf nations, delayed the start of services, or removed links entirely.

For example, British Airways reintroduced flights to Dubai in November 2026. The airline usually has three daily departures to the UAE destination, including one on the A380, but now only has a daily offering until at least October 2027. The Boeing 787-10 is mainly used, although the 777-300ER will take over in the summer. The much lower frequency reflects the lower demand from reduced confidence in travel.

Virgin also mentioned demand and operating costs. This could have been a generic, broad-brush comment, similar to many press releases that have many words but say little. But it might have hinted at the considerable capacity growth from Heathrow to Riyadh recently, which probably undermined the market's economics. Virgin played a part in this.

In 2025, OAG data shows that nearly 1.2 million round-trip seats were scheduled. This was up by a considerable 38% year-on-year and was nearly three times more than in 2019. Compared to 2024, the substantial increase was influenced by the entry of Virgin and British Airways growing to two daily flights. While Riyadh Air's debut at Heathrow was in 2025, the airline did not operate on a normal commercial basis as seats could not be booked by the public. The airline simply protected its slots. Regular service began in 2026.

In a similar vein, Virgin ended flights from Heathrow to Dubai on March 6 and, therefore, pulled out of the UAE entirely. However, the airline stated that it aims to return to Dubai in winter 2027/2028. Virgin was contacted for comment about this at 4:07 AM EST on November 26.

The carrier originally served Dubai between 2006 and 2019, when the A340-300 and A340-600 were primarily used. Service resumed in 2023, with the 787-9, A330-300, A330-900, and A350-1000 all used at times. In winter 2025/2026, only the 397-seat A350-1000 was deployed, which is Virgin's highest-capacity equipment.

Like Riyadh, Virgin's flights to Dubai were massively about the local London market.
