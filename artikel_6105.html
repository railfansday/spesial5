Travel interest is slipping, but revenue is holding because prices are doing the work. That cushion only lasts as long as travelers keep paying more, which makes falling search activity worth watching closely heading into 2027.

Across global markets, fewer travelers are searching for trips and fewer say they plan to travel, but the slowdown hasn’t reached the bottom line yet. Higher airfares and accommodation rates are holding up revenue as traveler interest cools.

Travel demand signals softened in September 2026 while pricing kept revenue intact. The Skift Travel Health Index stood at 99, down 1% year-on-year, and the destinations index, which tracks travel interest through online search activity, fell 12% to 88 as inbound flight and hotel searches weakened across most tracked markets. Inbound flight search growth was negative in the majority of the 21 destinations tracked, with the United Arab Emirates down 37%, Hong Kong down 18%, Australia down 16%, and Mexico down 15%, while China (+11%), India (+7%), and Italy (+5%) gained. Intent data points the same way: the European Travel Commission found the share of global travelers planning a long-haul trip fell from 55% in autumn 2025 to 50% in autumn 2026, and interest in visiting Europe slipped from 38% to 34%. The share of travelers who had already booked flights stayed broadly unchanged, so the cooling is concentrated at the top of the funnel. Higher airfares and accommodation rates are holding up revenue as traveler interest cools.
