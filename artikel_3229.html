Justin Timberlake Visits Jimmy Fallon And Shares Thanksgiving Dish On New Apple TV Doc Series, How David Bowie Influenced ‘SexyBack’ & Performs Dance Classics

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Thursday was a dual celebration for Justin Timberlake‘s visit with Jimmy Fallon on NBC’s Tonight Show: There was Thanksgiving, of course, which you can see in the photo above was most certainly not ignored by the two old friends, and then for the former boy bander the visit was an opportunity to celebrate the 20th anniversary of his album FutureSex/LoveSounds.

And of course there’s always new material to plug, and last night it was Timberlake‘s four-part doc series streaming on Apple TV beginning December 11.

The Thanksgiving visit began with Timberlake joining Fallon in a musical number celebrating “The History of Party Songs.” With Timberlake initially putting up some mock reluctance, the two then launched into Soul II Soul’s “Back to Life (However Do You Want Me),” then proceeded through a smorgasbord of classic dance hits, including Ini Kamoze’s “Here Comes the Hotstepper,” Mark Morrison’s “Return of the Mack,” Montell Jordan’s “This Is How We Do It,” Spice Girls “Wannabe,” Bell Biv DeVoe’s “Poison,” Timberlake’s own “Rock Your Body,” Wild Cherry’s “Play That Funky Music White Boy,” “Good Times” by Chic, Cool and the Gang’s “Celebration,” and some others you might be able catch before the big finish of The Backstreet Boys’ “I Want It That Way.” Related Stories News Ariana Grande Crashes Jimmy Fallon's Monologue With 'Focker-In-Law's Ben Stiller & Robert De Niro Late Night TV 'Tonight Show': Jimmy Fallon & Jamie Lee Curtis Channel Fielder & Holmes In Cold Open

Timberlake also reflected on the making of the FutureSex/LoveSounds album, noting that at the time of its recording he’d been listening to “Bowie, David Byrne, Talking Heads, Freddie Mercury, Queen, Billy Idol, James Brown, Eurythmics, INXS…but we were kind of thinking, How we can take the sonics of dance music and flip it?”

Timberlake recalls that, as presented in the upcoming docuseries, his record label at the time was not initially impressed with the album, thinking it was “too fast,” to which Timberlake responded, “That’s why it’s going to work.” He also remembered that the label was disappointed that he wasn’t using his signature falsetto enough. “I was listening to [David Bowie’s] ‘Rebel Rebel’ a lot…and that music at that time sounded so daring.” Timberlake says he wanted to create the impression that the singer was a character in the song, as Bowie so often did, and the result was “SexyBack.”

Regarding the upcoming four-part Timberlake documentary series, the singer explained that he overcame his initial reluctance – “I’m sort of famously private” – by trusting director and friend Fisher Stevens. “I feel like I’ve gone through a lot in the last five years that made me a better human,” he said.

“But the more we sat down and talked, the more I found myself searching for something that was beneath the surface a little more,” he continued. “And I think that at the end of the day I sort of mustered up some courage to talk about things that I’ve never talked about before. Things that even those that are closest to me don’t know.”

Timberlake also paid tribute to the late singer-songwriter D’Angelo, the major influence who died October 14, 2025. “D’Angelo, for me, he changed everything,” Timberlake told Fallon. “You know, he was one of those artists. Rest in peace. What a brilliant, brilliant, brilliant artist. But I remember when he came out, it just like everything changed. [He] was a once-in-a-generation artist.”

And finally, here’s Timberlake’s performance that capped the Fallon appearance, taking place at New York’s famed Rainbow Room atop 30 Rock. He performs a medley of “FutureSex/LoveSound,” “SexyBack,” “Summer Love” and “My Love.”

The docuseries Timberlake airs on Apple TV on Friday, December 11 with the first two episodes, followed by the third episode on Friday, December 18 and the finale on Wednesday, December 23, 2026.
