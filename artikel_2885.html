Knicks eke out lucky win in Minnesota after fourth-quarter collapse

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

"For 'Dancing with the Stars,' " Karl-Anthony Towns said. "For us, at the end of the day, as long as we win, this team is just trying to stack the number on the left side."

The Knicks did stack another win in that left standings column. Perhaps they didn't deserve to.

They did just about everything they could to throw away that win amid a brutal collapse.

Karl-Anthony Towns goes up for a shot over Rudy Gobert during the Knicks' 102-99 win over the Timberwolves on Nov. 19, 2026, in Minneapolis. NBAE via Getty Images

Somehow, they escaped with a 102-99 win over the Timberwolves on Thursday night.

The Knicks led by 12 entering the fourth quarter and then quickly went up by 16 after Tyler Kolek's 4-point play to start the quarter. They had led since early in the first quarter and were in complete command.

But they proceeded to miss their next 13 shots and saw their lead dwindle to four. Target Center finally had life.

It appeared they had stabilized a bit when Towns' driving layup and Mikal Bridges' subsequent 3-pointer put the Knicks back up by nine.

But Towns, after a Timberwolves challenge, was handed a flopping technical for trying to draw a foul called on Rudy Gobert. A free throw, a Gobert alley-oop and an Ayo Dosunmu 3-pointer later, and the Knicks led by just three. Towns then was called for an offensive foul, and Bridges badly missed a jumper on the Knicks' next offensive possession.

The Timberwolves couldn't find a quality shot, though, with Dosunmu and Gobert both missing shots. Minnesota never got closer than three.

LaMelo Ball badly missed a potential game-tying 3-pointer with under 10 seconds left. Jonathan Kuminga got the rebound but threw it away to Landry Shamet, and the Timberwolves weren't able to foul as the Knicks tossed the ball up the court.

Miles McBride (left) and Jaden McDaniels battle for the ball during the Knicks' win over the Timberwolves on Nov. 19, 2026, in Minneapolis. AP Photo/Ellen Schmidt

"It was hectic," Josh Hart said. "We gotta get better at finishing a game like that. But it's a win, 3-1 on the road trip. We'll take it."

Despite the ugly finish, the Knicks closed their four-game road trip with a win over a strong opponent. The Timberwolves have not missed much of a beat this season without injured superstar Anthony Edwards and possessed the league's best defense entering the game.

"Great to finish off a road trip, getting a win in a pretty tough environment by a well-coached team," head coach Mike Brown said. "It'll make the plane ride a little bit better going home."

Nobody has shot over 50 percent from the field against them this year. The Knicks could not change that, though they shot 57.1 percent by halftime as Towns — returning to Minnesota as an opponent for the third time since the Knicks' blockbuster trade to acquire him two years ago — was dominating his old team. He scored 17 points in the first half, and Jalen Brunson then erupted for 14 points in the third quarter. The Knicks' offense as a whole was humming.

It appeared the Knicks would cruise the rest of the way before the Timberwolves defense came alive.

"Our execution the first three quarters was at a pretty high level against this team," Brown said. "And then you give them credit. Fourth quarter, they did a phenomenal job defensively, kind of bogged us down. The ball and the pace offensively in the frontcourt wasn't popping like it was the first three quarters."

Towns shot 1-for-5 in the fourth. Brunson went scoreless in the period. As a team, the Knicks went 3-for-18 from the field and scored just 12 points over the final 12 minutes.

Brunson finished with 21 points. OG Anunoby added 15.

Kuminga led the Timberwolves with 23 points. The Knicks held Dosunmu, who has broken out in Edwards' absence, to just six points. Ball had 15 points on rough 6-for-21 shooting. The Timberwolves' 99 points were their fewest this season.

For three quarters, it was vintage Knicks — getting a little bit from everyone. Until it unraveled in the fourth quarter, where the Knicks had been so good in recent games.

"If you wanna win a championship, you gotta be ready to win in any way possible," Towns said, "because you never know what the game will be."
