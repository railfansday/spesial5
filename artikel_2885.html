Anthropic Has Plunged 30% From Its Peak. Here’s the Price at Which I’d Buy.

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Anthropic's historic IPO is already losing momentum, but a deeper sell-off could present a compelling buying opportunity.

By Adam Spatacco – Nov 24, 2026 at 8:00PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Anthropic ›

Following months of anticipation, artificial intelligence (AI) developer Anthropic (ANT -6.54%) made its public market debut on Nov. 19. While the maker of Claude initially received an enthusiastic reception, the excitement has been short-lived. Less than a week after going public, Anthropic stock has already started selling off.

Indeed, Anthropic's revenue is growing at an extraordinary rate. But smart investors are not overlooking the capital-intensive nature underpinning the company's expansion. Furthermore, competition across the large language model (LLM) landscape raises important questions about Anthropic's path to profitability.

Let's explore what's driving the sell-off in Anthropic stock and assess the valuation at which the company could become a compelling investment.

Anthropic completed the largest initial public offering (IPO) in history, surpassing the $75 billion raised by Space Exploration Technologies back in June. Including the full exercise of the underwriters' option to purchase additional shares, Anthropic's IPO generated $93.5 billion in gross proceeds.

The company also completed a concurrent $26 billion private placement involving Nvidia, Microsoft, Alphabet, Broadcom, Advanced Micro Devices, and Micron Technology. Combined gross proceeds from the two transactions reached $119.5 billion.

When Anthropic began trading on the Nasdaq, shares opened at $111.50, or about 34% above the offering price of $83. Shares subsequently climbed to an intraday high of $115.10 before reversing course and closing their first day of trading at $97.17. As of this writing (Nov. 24), Anthropic stock is hovering around $80, representing a decline of 30% from its high.

Some of the selling pressure likely reflects momentum traders locking in profits, but attributing the entire decline to short-term trading overlooks several important fundamental concerns.

Anthropic's growth is difficult to dispute. According to its S-1 filing, the company generated approximately $17 billion in revenue during the third quarter, representing 1,127% growth year over year. Furthermore, Anthropic ended October with an annualized revenue run rate of $81 billion.

Revenue growth is only one part of Anthropic's financial profile. The company has $900.9 billion in infrastructure and computing commitments, including $451.6 billion expected to be incurred before Dec. 31, 2030.

While these obligations shouldn't be confused with conventional debt, they illustrate the capital requirements required to develop and operate frontier AI models. Anthropic's ability to generate sustainable profits depends heavily on whether customer demand and premium pricing can justify these investments.

Anthropic competes against OpenAI's ChatGPT, Alphabet's Gemini, Meta's Muse, and an expanding ecosystem of open-weight models. As generative models become more sophisticated and less expensive to operate, Anthropic may face pressure to lower its prices while continuing to invest aggressively in computing capacity.

Unlike Alphabet or Meta Platforms, Anthropic doesn't have a mature, highly profitable advertising or cloud computing business that generates cash to help subsidize its AI infrastructure investments. As a result, investors are being asked to place enormous amounts of faith in Anthropic's ability to translate its revenue growth into durable earnings and free cash flow at some point in the future.

Given Anthropic's lack of profitability, a traditional price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio isn't particularly useful in this analysis. Instead, I'd value the company relative to its annualized revenue run rate. At $80 per share, Anthropic's market capitalization is roughly $1.1 trillion. This equates to 13.5 times its annualized revenue run rate from October.

I am more interested in Anthropic stock at $50 per share. At that price, the company would boast an implied market cap of about $690 billion, equivalent to 8.5 times its annualized revenue run rate. Anthropic's growth could allow the company to grow into this valuation relatively quickly. For example, if its annualized revenue run rate doubles to roughly $160 billion, a $690 billion valuation would represent just 4.3 times that figure.

With that said, investors shouldn't assume Anthropic's current growth trajectory will last indefinitely. Moreover, the company will need to demonstrate that its revenue can fuel sustainable free cash flow despite its infrastructure commitments.

Although Anthropic is one of the most premier businesses leading the AI revolution, an exciting company doesn't always make an attractive stock at every price. At $50 per share, I'd be more comfortable initiating a modest position and gradually building exposure as Anthropic makes progress toward profitability. Until then, I think patience is warranted.
