’Hoos in the NBA: Early-season report card

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

The Virginia Cavaliers men's basketball program is enjoying an excellent start to the season. The current 'Hoos earned a statement win over No. 12 Arkansas in Washington, D.C., on Sunday afternoon to improve to 3-0, while a handful of former Virginia stars are enjoying strong starts to their seasons at the next level.

With most NBA teams reaching the 10-game checkpoint over the weekend, here's how the former faces of UVA basketball have been faring in The Association so far.

There's no better place to start than with Ty Jerome, who has become one of the NBA's feel-good stories early in the season. While Virginia fans have long known what Jerome is capable of, the national basketball audience is finally getting its formal introduction to the 6-foot-5 assassin from New Rochelle, New York.

After years of dealing with injury while intermittently posting stretches of highly efficient and majorly underappreciated play, Jerome has erupted in the early going for Memphis, averaging 26.6 points and 5.3 assists per game while piloting the Grizzlies to an unexpected 6-3 start.

The former Virginia star is playing the best basketball of his pro career, and Memphis is reaping the rewards. Jerome has already racked up two 40+ point performances this season plus a highlight reel's worth of clutch shots. Averaging a league-best 14 points per game on drives, Jerome's signature floater has become one of the league's deadliest weapons.

Coming off back-to-back seasons averaging north of 20 points per game, Trey Murphy III is back to knocking down threes and throwing down eye-popping dunks for the Pelicans.

Though his raw numbers are down slightly this season (15.6 points, 4.8 rebounds per game), his three-point efficiency (37.7%) remains sky-high.

Once again, Murphy's name is also swirling around the NBA rumor mill, frequently coming up in trade talks as New Orleans looks to rebuild. If given the chance to play for a contending team, Murphy's status as one of the league's most valuable 3-and-D weapons could finally be solidified over the back half of the 2026-27 campaign.

Now playing for his third different team in three seasons, the former fourth-overall pick has made nine starts for the Kings this season.

After an uncharacteristically poor season from three-point range in 2025-26, De'Andre Hunter is knocking down 35.3% of his attempts from downtown so far this season, propelling him to 13.1 points per game thus far. Unfortunately, despite four double-digit scoring efforts from Hunter — including back-to-back 24-point games on Oct. 27 and 29 — Sacramento is off to a frigid 1-9 start.

After a breakout campaign in Indiana last season in which he appeared in all 82 games, Jay Huff is back at it throwing down reverse slams for the Pacers.

The 7-footer, now in his sixth NBA season, has made five starts and nine total appearances this season, averaging 7.1 points per game on 49% shooting from the field. The ever-efficient Huff went off for a season-high 22 points (8-for-14 FG) and eight rebounds last Monday night against the Atlanta Hawks.

The sharp-shooting Sam Hauser continues to be one of the league's most valuable three-point specialists for the Boston Celtics. Playing 21 minutes per game (mostly off the bench, though he did start one game for Boston last week against the Milwaukee Bucks), Hauser is averaging 7.7 points per game on 40.4% shooting from the outside.

A 2024 first-round pick by Phoenix, Ryan Dunn's third professional season is off to an efficient start despite a somewhat limited role in the Suns' rotation.

The 6-foot-7 defensive ace is averaging 5.6 points, 3.6 rebounds, 1.4 stocks per game while playing 16 minutes per game for the 3-5 Suns and is coming off his best week of the season, posting a season-high 11 points in Thursday night's win against the Orlando Magic and 8 points in a weekend victory over the Houston Rockets.

With Dunn, the question is always going to be his three-point shooting. And so far this season, he's knocked down 7-of-13 attempts from distance.

The former ACC All-Defensive Team selection at Virginia in 2023-24 has yet to shoot better than 33.1% from deep in a season, but if he can keep his early-season stroke going, 'Hoos fans are well aware of the eye-popping athleticism that made Dunn such an intriguing pro prospect coming out of Charlottesville.

Beekman, now a third-year pro, signed a two-way contract with the Washington Wizards this past offseason after a year in the G-League in 2025-26. Unfortunately, Beekman is currently dealing with an acromioclavicular joint injury that will keep him sidelined for at least three weeks.

Meanwhile, the other former 'Hoo in the Nation's Capital, Anthony Gill, is the longest-tenured player on the Washington Wizards' roster. Gill has become the ultimate locker-room presence and player-coach for a franchise trying to transition from a rebuild to contention in the Eastern Conference. The seventh-year NBA vet has appeared in just two games this season, but his wisdom and steadying presence are immeasurable for the young Wizards.
