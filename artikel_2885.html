The Trade Desk's 2027 Outlook: Poised for a Rebound After a Rough 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

This under-the-radar advertising technology stock boasts a Superscore of 87 from our Hidden Gems Primary database, part of The Motley Fool's Moneyball Database system. Here's why.

When an advertising executive needs to reach a viewer watching a show on a connected television, they no longer buy spots directly from every TV network. Instead, they turn to The Trade Desk (TTD +0.14%). This global technology platform acts as a cloud-based digital switchboard, allowing advertisers to bid on and manage ad campaigns across video, display, and social media with precision. As of November 19, 2026, the stock trades at $14.87, having declined 63% over the past year as the market grapples with shifting ad-tech budgets and internal restructuring.

Our proprietary Hidden Gems scoring system assigns Trade Desk an overall Superscore of 87 out of 100, placing it in the Strong category. The Superscore is an AI-powered score that evaluates a company's overall strength by combining financial performance, product market position, technological capabilities, leadership quality, and relative valuation. It represents the unification of all our scores into a single score for public companies, with five rating bands: Exceptional (90-100), Strong (75-89), Above Average (60-74), Average (40-59), and Cautious (0-39).

The Superscore provides a quantitative snapshot of strength, but it is just one data point. You should always weigh this signal against your own financial goals, tolerance for volatility, and independent research into the advertising technology sector.

At this point, The Trade Desk's ability to grow in the coming years looks at risk. It enjoyed years of rapid expansion, but in 2026, that trend reversed, as year-over-year sales growth slowly declined. In the third quarter, its revenue of $736 million represented a 0.5% year-over-year drop, and Q4 is expected to be worse. Management forecasted Q4 sales of $755 million, a significant fall from 2025's $847 million.

Despite the grim tidings, I believe The Trade Desk's stock can deliver a return in the coming years. That's because its share price valuation has fallen substantially. Its trailing P/E ratio of about 21 hovers around a multi-year low. The company just needs to stop its current revenue slide, which it is positioning itself to do.

The Trade Desk has focused on pursuing joint business plans (JBPs). These are agreements with advertisers to align on shared business objectives and measurement objectives. Rather than relying on ad campaigns that come and go, JBPs provide long-term partnerships, stabilizing revenue.

The company also made a number of leadership changes. This includes bringing back co-founder Dave Pickles, who was formerly the Chief Technology Officer, to provide guidance over daily platform development and to accelerate product releases. With these changes, The Trade Desk is poised to rebound and for its stock to deliver a solid return in the coming years.
