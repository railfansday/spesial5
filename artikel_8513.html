Move Your Staples to a Discount Grocer Like Aldi – Price Your List First

A discount grocer like Aldi keeps prices below a supermarket’s by selling mostly its own brands in small stores that are cheap to run. Compare prices on your regular items at both stores before you switch. Then buy your staples there and keep a short supermarket trip for whatever it doesn’t carry.

When the nonprofit Consumers’ Checkbook priced common groceries at stores in seven metro areas, Aldi’s prices were 30% to 40% lower than the average store’s, and Lidl’s were about 20% to 25% lower. A gap that size makes the discount grocer the place for your weekly staples.

The same choices that keep prices low come with trade-offs. Aldi and Lidl sell mostly their own brands, which make up more than 90% of Aldi’s products, so the name brands you usually buy may be missing or come in just one size. Their stores are smaller and much cheaper to run than a supermarket, with a narrower selection. That’s why you’ll still make a short supermarket stop.

You also do more of the work. At Aldi, a quarter unlocks your cart and comes back when you return it, and the store asks you to bag your own groceries in bags you bring or buy at checkout.

Find the discount grocer nearest you, whether that’s Aldi, Lidl, WinCo, Grocery Outlet, or Save A Lot, and check how it works before your first trip. WinCo takes no credit cards, so pay with cash or a debit card and its PIN. Grocery Outlet sells discounted name brands, but its stock changes often.

Splitting the shopping keeps most of the savings. Say your family’s staples cost $180 a week at the supermarket and $140 in Aldi’s own brands, and the $30 of specialty items Aldi doesn’t carry stays at the supermarket. You’d save about $40 a week, more than $2,000 a year.

Start with your last supermarket receipt. Pick 15 to 20 items on it that you buy most weeks, and look for each one at the discount grocer, in the same brand or the grocer’s own. Compare the unit price, the cost per ounce or per count, since package sizes differ.
