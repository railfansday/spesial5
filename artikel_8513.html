Your old laptops, phones, tablets, and game consoles still have buyers, even if they don’t work. For anything that works, compare offers from a few places that buy used electronics and check whether each pays cash or store credit. Sell broken devices to a buyer that pays for parts, and wipe every device before it goes.

The world generated a record 62 million metric tons of electronic waste in 2022, and only 22.3% of it was documented as properly collected and recycled, according to the United Nations’ Global E-waste Monitor 2024. Selling your old devices keeps them in use longer and pays you for them.

How you’re paid depends on who buys. Some buyers pay cash, by PayPal, a bank deposit, or check. Others pay in a gift card or credit toward something new. That credit is worth its full value only if you’d spend that much at the store anyway.

Every online quote is an estimate until the buyer inspects the device, and a device in worse shape than you described gets a lower offer. Describe its condition honestly, and get quotes from two or three buyers before you ship it.

A phone has more buyers than these, so compare other places to sell your phone before you take one of these offers.

Take anything none of these buyers want to a store’s or maker’s free recycling program, not the trash. A working device that draws only a few dollars in offers can go to a group that refurbishes donated computers and electronics instead.

Before any device ships or changes hands, back it up, sign out of every account on it so the next owner can use it, and reset it to factory settings.
