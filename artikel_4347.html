Sonic Pico Park Review: A Great Party Favor

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Sonic the Hedgehog becomes friends with the cute little cats of Pico Park, inviting all of us into a co-op puzzle-platformer that's sure to make you laugh one way or another.

This year marks the big 3-5 for Sega’s patented Mario killer, Sonic the Hedgehog. It’s been an eventful time for all kinds of fans. Of course, it’s hard to celebrate this momentous occasion with the all but ignored elephant in the room that is the mainline game Sonic Team has all but officially unveiled that’s still in development. But we aren’t going without a game for the anniversary, at least!

Enter Sonic Pico Park. Like the title implies, it’s a return to the chaotic co-op world of Pico Park with a new hedgehog-shaped coat of paint. Ever since it was announced in time for the blue blur’s birthday, Sonic Pico Park has been teasing the mechanics and playable characters slowly leading up to the game’s November launch. It’s been a nice little treat between Sonic Racing: CrossWorlds DLC, monthly comics, and a stream of other goodies throughout the year. But there’s a question that I’m sure a lot of us want to ask.

Sonic the Hedgehog is turning 35 this year, and if you're looking for games to celebrate the occasion, there are plenty of options for you.

Is Sonic Pico Park a good game for this milestone? Is it as good and as cute as it’s been advertised? Is it more Pico than Sonic? Luckily for you, I’m here to answer these questions and more. A short answer to keep things quick: Yes, and no. And if that makes you happy, let’s get into deeper details so you know what you’re getting into before dragging your friends into playing with you.

I’ll be honest and say that it’s hard for me to not fall victim to Sonic Pico Park’s charms. I’ve always adored sprite art. Seeing some of my favorite characters of all time as these miniaturized, big-headed versions, dancing around and reflecting their personalities in these quick animations is just too good to resist. But there’s more to Sonic Pico Park than that.

Like the original Pico Park, Sonic Pico Park is a co-op game where teamwork is key to win. It can be played with at least two players, but you can navigate this game with eight people max if you really want these short and sweet levels to be complete ragers in two different senses of the word. I got to team up with two other writers to play this game. The fun of Sonic Pico Park definitely grows with the number of players involved.

The puzzling co-op of Pico Park fits like a glove with Sonic’s trademark speed. If you thought the original Pico Park may have been too slow, this game picks up the pace with ease without making anything too uncontrollable or without giving you and your friends time to think things through. There are fewer twists to the levels that gave Pico Park what fame it has here, though, likely to keep things less frustrating for younger players and their families. Saboteurs who purposefully ignore instructions for a laugh will still have their ways, though.

Plenty of Pico Park gimmicks, like buttons, numbered platforms and blocks, are scattered throughout the level with springs, loop-de-loops, and rings from Sonic. There are more Sonic mechanics to play with throughout the whole game than Pico staples, and there are plenty of surprises for the average Sonic fan to spot. It’s because of these features that Sonic Pico Park really sticks out as a Sonic game, and one that certainly rises to the 35th anniversary occasion. But like most parties, it will end and become a good memory in time.

One important factor Sonic fans should understand before jumping into Sonic Pico Park is that the games that came before it aren’t particularly long. Pico Park itself only takes about three hours, and I managed to play everything the game offered in about four and a half hours according to my playtime stats. Thankfully this balances out with Sonic Pico Park’s asking price, which I think is fair for a cute little romp with friends. Especially since all it takes is for one of those friends to choose violence and a level that would take five minutes has now taken fifteen because everyone’s too busy laughing and screaming to get back on track. You know. Typical co-op game stuff.

As a Sonic game, Sonic Pico Park is a great snack for the hungry, but won’t be enough for the ravenous.

Sonic fans will still have fun seeing the unexpected twists to what Pico Park is known for. If you remove the Sonic flavoring, you can still find a fun platformer underneath, but the personality of each of the playable Sonic characters really completes the experience. It gives it a sense of style that blends both games together to create something unique that fans will love. It might also serve as the perfect gateway for any Sonic-obsessed friends to finally pull you into this series. Play at your own risk.

Without going into too many details, Sonic Pico Park sticks the landing as an anniversary game. There are a lot of surprises to find in this game as you go along, but it comes at the price of the game’s identity at times that might rub a few players the wrong way. There’s an element to Pico Park that remains in these moments, bringing in a new challenge of figuring out how to tackle the old with the new. But that might not be enough to save these moments from feeling like jingling keys to a fan worn out on the last decade or so of nostalgic references.

Still, that’s only a more cynical look at things. Among the game’s levels and modes, I feel like there are far more interesting twists on Sonic mechanics with Pico Park-style platforming and puzzle-solving than anything else. As a Sonic game, Sonic Pico Park is a great snack for the hungry, but won’t be enough for the ravenous. But it might satiate them a little if their friends bring the laughs and some good nibbles to go with it.

Any newcomers to Sonic don’t have to worry much. Sonic Pico Park doesn’t overstay its welcome. There are a few modes to play on the side, and you can always drag more friends into the chaos and not backseat them, but otherwise, it’s hard to see groups of friends coming back to Sonic Pico Park after clearing everything the first time. And sometimes, it’s okay if a party doesn’t last forever. What’s important is the chaos and fun you have with the people you share it with that will stay with you long after those few hours are up.

Sonic Pico Park is a step up for the iconic puzzle-platformer. It adds a sense of style and quick-thinking that only Sonic could bring to Pico Park while giving an element of chaos not entirely new to Sonic, but elevated through the humor of Pico Park. The dedication and love to the Sonic series can be felt in everything it squeezes into its short run time, as well as how every single character’s personality is made far bigger than the sprites used to portray them. It might not be the game fans expected out of this year, but it’s certainly what they need and deserve. Sonic Pico Park, like the games that came before it, will bring plenty of laughs and good memories to players long after this anniversary is over.
