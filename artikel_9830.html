What We Missed: Knicks Phishing Test Goes Out of Bounds

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

In this video conversation, Dark Reading editors discuss some of the news they didn't get a chance to cover, from an ill-conceived phishing simulation to a hacktivist attack on a US government agency.

Phishing tests can be an important part of cybersecurity awareness training. But as the NBA champion New York Knicks recently showed, they can also go awry.

According to a recent report in The Athletic, Madison Square Garden (MSG) Sports, which owns the Knicks, deployed a poorly timed and ill-conceived phishing simulation ahead of its banner-raising ceremony on Oct. 20. MSG Sports sent out emails to employees with offers of free championship rings commemorating the team's historic NBA Finals victory in June.

Team employees often receive championship rings at the start of the following season. However, the emails in this case were part of a phishing simulation, and when employees clicked on the links to select their rings, they were informed they failed the test and had to attend mandatory cybersecurity training. And they didn't get the rings, either.

Dark Reading senior writer Alex Culafi and senior news director Rob Wright discuss the situation on this episode. Also on tap: a hacktivist group breached the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) and published personnel records for more than 40,000 employees, and it's unclear how the agency was compromised; and a New York Times report claims a NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang was targeted by a Chinese intelligence asset.
