The Holiday Shopping Season Is Here. Are Consumers Ready to Trust AI Assistants?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Joanna is a Head of Consumer Research and Marketing at PissedConsumer.com.

Businesses are investing heavily in AI shopping assistants, yet consumers remain hesitant. Why does shopper enthusiasm for AI additions to the shopping journey appear to be trailing behind that of retailers?

A recent survey of holiday shopping trends finds that only 9.9% of consumers intend to use AI tools in their 2026 holiday shopping plans, while 52.2% completely rule out using AI at all.

According to Adobe's holiday shopping research, AI-driven traffic to US retailers' websites will grow 130% in the 2026 holiday shopping season, but this increase is still modest compared with traditional advertising and other channels. So while there is an uptick in AI involvement in seasonal shopping, it’s still not quite in the realm of mainstream adoption, so expectations for AI-driven revenue should be tempered accordingly.

Unsurprisingly, as AI excels at sifting and retrieving relevant data from vast amounts of information, comparing products and prices is the standout use case here. In the holiday shopping survey among PissedConsumer’s users in four countries, respondents who say they use AI shopping tools cite comparison as their number one benefit, at 27.9%. Helping to draw up personalized shopping lists and sourcing discount codes are also ways these tools boost buyer confidence.

From the retailer's perspective, an AI assistant that shortlists 100 items into five top contenders brings time-saving value to the customer, whereas distractions and unnecessarily complicated processes will make the checkout feel more distant.

Misleading, irrelevant, or just plain wrong information causes shoppers to turn away from AI assistants. According to other research, shoppers try an average of only three prompts before giving up on AI recommendations, and 32% blame AI for poor recommendations if they are unsatisfied with the results of the search.

An AI tool can only ever be as good as the data it works with, so keep all information that it depends on, such as prices, inventory counts, return windows, etc., accurate, up to date, and synchronized with your storefront. Inaccurate details will guarantee complaints, cancellations, and lost customers.

Focus investment in areas that have proven worth to consumers. Comparisons and gift ideas are high-value to shoppers and low-risk applications. Be conscious of AI agent limitations and, when these are reached, enable an easy handover to a human customer service agent.

And finally, test and track performance. Just because clicks are going up doesn’t mean customer trust is following. The goal is to nurture consumer confidence.

The consumer data portrays a curious but cautious customer. Even though about one in four consumers might use an AI shopping assistant this holiday, it’s not a sign of significant growth or widespread adoption.

Smart implementation could be the answer. Retailers that lead with robust, proven AI tools that fix fundamental bottlenecks or resolve customer uncertainty, even in small or subtle ways, will build customer confidence, turning curiosity into repeated use of such tools.

The 2026 holiday shopping season is an opportunity to show your customers that you can save them time, money, and stress. Build trust in December, and you’ll have a strong platform for success when adoption finally does take off.
