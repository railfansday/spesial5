The Ultimate Dividend Stock to Buy With $5,000 Right Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

This REIT regularly raises dividends quarterly.

The markets may feel nervous right now. That includes high-flying tech stocks, particularly those involved in artificial intelligence (AI), that had been performing well. They have taken it on the chin lately due to concerns about AI. You can see this by examining the performance of the technology-heavy Nasdaq-100, which lost 7.8% over the last month through Nov. 12.

One way to counteract that volatility is to own dividend-paying stocks. That's easy to say, but which one should you choose?

It's a good first step to narrow down the sector. Real estate investment trusts (REITs), which own or finance real estate properties, stand out. These stocks are tailor-made for dividend investors because they must distribute at least 90% of their taxable income. Within that sector, Realty Income (O -0.74%) tops my list.

Realty Income gets more than three-quarters of its rental income from retailers, which may put off some investors. However, this venerable REIT has built a remarkable record of dividend increases. In fact, the company recently announced that it would raise the monthly dividend from $0.2715 per share to $0.274.

Investors have gotten used to quarterly dividend increases. That's because, remarkably, Realty Income has increased dividend payments for 117 straight quarters. It has also raised payments 137 times since the company went public in 1994.

The company's not merely raising dividends without being able to afford them, either. Realty Income's adjusted funds from operations (AFFO), which measures cash available for distribution, covered 73% of its third-quarter dividends. That should provide investors comfort, along with the fact that the company has been steadily growing AFFO. In the latest period, AFFO per diluted share gained 3.7% year over year to $1.12.

Turning to the dividend yield (dividends divided by the share price), investors will undoubtedly be pleased. Realty Income's shareholders receive a higher dividend yield than holders of the typical large-cap stock and the average REIT.

The stock yields 5.6%, but it's important to put that in context. That's more than five times the popular S&P 500 index's 1.1%. Additionally, Realty Income's stock has a 1.8-percentage-point yield advantage over the FTSE Nareit All Equity REITs Index, which yielded 3.8% as of Oct. 30.

Realty Income has secure and growing dividends, along with an above-average dividend yield. That's a powerful combination that should entice dividend investors.
