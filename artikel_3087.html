6-2 Jaguars Facing Difficult Brian Thomas Balancing Act

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

With the NFL trade deadline arriving Tuesday, few Jacksonville Jaguars storylines carry as much league-wide attention as the future of wide receiver Brian Thomas Jr. His diminished role since Week 4 has fueled a steady stream of speculation about what it means for his long-term place in Jacksonville, and the answer is slightly more complicated than simple usage charts suggest.

In a recent report, NFL Network's Cam Wolfe added some important context to the recent BTJ buzz. According to Wolfe, Thomas and Travis Hunter are close friends. Thomas is also well liked in the Jaguars' locker room, and his speed remains an asset even without the production to match. Wolfe also reported that the Jaguars have wondered whether last year's Tyson Campbell trade hurt their immediate locker room energy, and that the organization has "learned from it."

That last point deserves attention. After the Campbell trade to Cleveland in Week 6 of 2025, some players were reportedly unhappy with the move, and Jacksonville followed it with back-to-back tough losses to the Seattle Seahawks and Los Angeles Rams. As is often the case in the NFL, winning will always cure the discomfort, however. The Jaguars closed out 2025 by going 9-1, and the locker room appeared no worse for wear. Still, it's easy to see how that experience could shape the team's approach to a third-year receiver this time around. However, it is hard to argue that the team made the wrong decision, based on the on-field outcomes, and their defensive scheme not matching the player. That's the key piece that makes this such a fascinating conversation. Where is the line between doing whatever is necessary to win versus doing what is needed for the locker room to be together?

The numbers, on the surface, show a player whose role has clearly shrunk considerably. Thomas currently ranks fourth on the team in targets (21), fifth on the team in receptions through eight games played (10), and seventh in yards (117) behind rookie Josh Cameron who has 123. Over his last four games combined, he has just one catch, a 12-yard touchdown against the Indianapolis Colts. On Thursday, he logged only 19 snaps against the Baltimore Ravens, a game in which he was relegated to the fourth receiver spot. However, that trend didn't start in Week 8. Thomas played a career-low 15 snaps against the Houston Texans in Week 6, before the Bye Week. After that game, head coach Liam Coen described the team's confidence in Thomas as a week-to-week matter and said simply, "Guys have to step up and make plays."

Ahead of the Colts game, Coen expressed faith that Thomas would continue helping the offense make plays. He quickly supported that faith, securing the opening-drive touchdown, his lone catch of the game.

Meanwhile, around the league, interest is as real as ever. ESPN's Dan Graziano reported that opposing teams are asking about Jacksonville's plans for Thomas and are expected to continue reaching out before the November 10 deadline. ESPN's Jeremy Fowler reported that several executives believe Thomas's days in Jacksonville are numbered, and noted that a change of scenery could bring out the best in him. For a former 2024 Rookie of the Year finalist who topped 1,200 receiving yards as a rookie, that kind of interest is not surprising. What would be surprising is the team trading that caliber of player away for less than a first-round pick.

Thomas is not publicly asking to be traded. By all accounts, he's a hard worker and has embraced the team-first approach being built. But within Liam Coen's offense, his role appears to be that of the sacrificial X, stretching the defense vertically to open space underneath for the middle-of-the-field throws that drive the scheme. With Trevor Lawrence at his best working inside the hashes, that job rarely comes paired with a major statistical output. Jakobi Meyers, Parker Washington, Josh Cameron, and the Jacksonville tight end group all share that strength. Thomas doesn't, and that's okay. Thomas' role can be valuable to the offense without ever showing up in his stat line, which is an extremely difficult reality for a player of his pedigree.

Take the Baltimore matchup as an example: while Thomas was only on the field for 19 total snaps, he ran a route on 11 of Trevor Lawrence's 26 pass attempts (42.3%). Meanwhile, the team built up a 20-point third quarter lead in the rainy Baltimore night. On those 11 dropbacks, Lawrence was 6/11 (54.5%) for 109 yards, with four of the team's 11 passing first downs (36.4%), and the only passing touchdown – a 50 yarder to Parker Washington. The argument that BTJ pulls the primary coverage or opens up space could be supported.

This is a delicate situation. The Jaguars have spent two years shaping a finely tuned locker room, and they're looking to mold this roster into a legitimate Super Bowl contender. Trading away a well-liked player who is also a close friend of Hunter's carries risks that don't clearly show up on a stats sheet. For that reason, I expect Jacksonville to be buyers only at Tuesday's deadline.

Coen touched on the locker room impacts of prospective trades in his Friday media availability:

"How does it affect things moving forward, cap, future, other players, how we can resign or not, other guys, there's so much that goes into this time of the year. Also, being cognizant of the group that you have in the locker room and making sure that the guys all know in the building we're trying to do everything we can as an organization to make the best decisions for the group and for our team, because those guys are so important to what we're doing. It's a fine line. It's definitely a balancing act for sure, and everything that comes across your desk, you've got to make sure that you're looking at it through that lens; at least we are."

The one exception I could see would be a trading partner offering a corresponding move that makes sense for both sides, such as a deal that returns defensive tackle or safety talent, which effectively forces the team's hand in moving Thomas. With injuries already thinning the safety room, that kind of return is the only scenario where moving Thomas feels justified for a team with the roster Jacksonville has built.
