Warren Buffett Successor Greg Abel Piled $28 Billion Into This Stock in the First Half of 2026, But Opted Not to Buy More at a Lower Price Last Quarter

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Has something changed with Abel's biggest investment so far?

By Adam Levy – Nov 21, 2026 at 7:58AM EST

Greg Abel has already started to make his mark on Berkshire Hathaway (BRKA -0.47%) (BRKB -0.36%). The CEO took over the job from Warren Buffett at the start of the year, and he's since gone to work deploying tens of billions of dollars worth of capital for shareholders. He completed the acquisitions of OxyChem and Taylor Morrison Homes for a total of $18 billion. He reinitiated repurchases of the stock, buying back more than $16 billion worth of Berkshire through October. And he's added considerably to Berkshire marketable equity portfolio, including buying an estimated $28 billion worth of Alphabet (GOOG -2.59%) (GOOGL -2.70%) stock.

While Abel increased his share repurchases in the third quarter and continued to look for opportunities to fully acquire other companies, he stopped buying Alphabet shares. That's despite the fact that the share price moved meaningfully lower than the price he paid in Alphabet's $10 billion private placement with his company. Investors may be wondering if anything's changed for Alphabet since that purchase and whether it's worth buying right now.

Greg Abel. Image source: The Motley Fool.

Buffett built Berkshire Hathaway on the ethos of long-term buy-and-hold investing, and Abel has promised to maintain that philosophy in every investment decision he makes for the company. That said, a long-term outlook doesn't mean Buffett hasn't been known to quickly change his mind on a company, especially if things change. Only a handful of marketable equity investments have stuck around for Buffett's preferred holding period of "forever."

Buffett laid out the investment thesis for Alphabet, a position he initiated for Berkshire last year, in an interview this summer. It boils down to the fact that Alphabet has an opportunity to deploy a lot of capital and earn a high return on its invested capital doing so. In other words, Buffett believes Alphabet's massive AI build-out will produce exceptional cash flows over the long run.

While there are growing concerns that Alphabet won't achieve high returns on capital, the financial results so far suggest it's seeing excellent results. Alphabet's Google Cloud segment grew revenue 103% year over year last quarter while expanding its operating margin. That acceleration is tied to a ramp-up in investments starting in 2023, right in line with the timeline of how long it takes before breaking ground on new data center construction translates into placing servers and networking equipment available for rent on its platform.

Alphabet's reported payback period is well above that reported by other hyperscalers. Google Cloud CEO Thomas Kurian said it takes about two years for it to generate revenue equal to the cost of the average server for its cloud platform. However, its custom TPU systems have a payback period of half of that. Over time, a greater portion of Alphabet's capital expenditures will go toward revenue-generating servers and a greater share of those servers will be its own custom hardware with faster payback periods.

As such, it makes sense for Alphabet to raise capital and burn cash to build new data centers. The return on invested capital is extremely high and the opportunity is absolutely massive. It's playing out exactly as Buffett expects.

If Abel liked Alphabet stock at a price of $355 per share, which is what Berkshire paid for the stock in its private placement, he should like it even more at $280 per share, where it trades today.

The stock traded around $340 per share throughout the second half of the third quarter, so there was an opportunity for Abel to add to the position before the quarter ended at a price he was previously comfortable paying. However, Alphabet was already Berkshire's third-largest position as of the end of the third quarter, without any additional shares. That could push the bar higher for Abel to add to the position and increase Berkshire's portfolio concentration. (It's notably why I personally haven't added to my own position in Alphabet amid the pullback, as it already accounts for a significant portion of my portfolio.)

For investors without a significant position in Alphabet, though, right now looks like an incredible buying opportunity for the stock. Shares trade at just 21 times forward earnings expectations, and the long-term growth potential of its cloud computing segment (which includes its TPU system sales) looks better than ever.

Considering the drop in share price pushed Alphabet to become a much smaller portion of Berkshire's portfolio, Abel may be inclined to buy more shares again this quarter after last quarter's pause.
