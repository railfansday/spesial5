Antonelli fastest but Verstappen on pole after dramatic Las Vegas qualifying

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Kimi Antonelli was fastest in qualifying for the Las Vegas Grand Prix but a five-place grid penalty means Max Verstappen will start on pole position from George Russell.

New Formula 1 world champion Antonelli will start sixth due to a grid penalty for exceeding his internal combustion engine allocation and surprisingly went fastest in a hectic end to Q3.

The Mercedes driver only just crossed the line in time to start a final flying lap and was helped by a huge slipstream from the Racing Bulls throughout the lap to beat Verstappen by 0.258s.

Russell was 0.331s behind Antonelli but the Verstappen-Russell front row cannot be missed when the lights go out at 4am UK time on Sunday (build-up from 2.30am), live on Sky Sports F1 and Main Event, after the pair made contact in final practice.

Neither driver was penalised as Verstappen was given a warning for driving erratically when trying to stop Russell from gaining track position in final practice earlier on Saturday.

McLaren's Lando Norris will start third with Red Bull's Isack Hadjar in fourth and Racing Bulls' Arvid Lindblad will start in fifth, his highest career grid position.

Oscar Piastri qualified seventh but the McLaren driver has a 10-place grid penalty for exceeding his engine allocation, so will start 17th, so Racing Bulls' Liam Lawson will start seventh instead.

Alpine's Pierre Gasly and Franco Colapinto had great pace throughout qualifying but were caught up in the traffic chaos in Q3 on the out laps, so only qualified ninth and 10th.

Ferrari had a disaster as they struggled in the cold conditions and on the long straights, so both Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton were knocked out in Q2.

It is the first time no Ferrari driver has reached Q3 since the 2024 Canadian Grand Prix and Hamilton was shocked both he and Leclerc did not get through.

Due to the grid penalty for Piastri, Leclerc will start 10th and Hamilton in 11th for Sunday's race.

After the opening day of track action was limited due to rain, final practice was very busy as the teams were keen to get as many dry laps in as possible.

Russell ended up fastest by nearly one second from Antonelli in a Mercedes one-two, so the Silver Arrows were big favourites going into qualifying.

But, as has happened several times this season, things closed up come qualifying and pole position was anyone's guess heading into Q3 as Verstappen was fastest from Gasly in Q2.

The first runs in Q3 saw Verstappen outpace Norris by 0.173s with Russell in third and Antonelli only sixth.

Mercedes sent their drivers out early for the second Q3 runs with Russell ahead of Antonelli, despite the Brit having requested after the first runs whether he could follow the Italian "given the situation for tomorrow".

Russell braked hard at the start of the second warm-up lap to try to get a benefit from being behind other cars who were leaving the pit lane for their own out laps.

It led to the two Mercedes, two Alpines and two Racing Bulls all packed together on the out lap and Antonelli ended up at the back of the queue, so was in danger of missing out on a final Q3 flying lap.

The Italian just made it by a few seconds but was around 1.5 seconds behind the Racing Bulls of Lindblad, so was expected to be badly affected by the dirty air.

However, the multiple long straights on the Las Vegas circuit cancelled out the disadvantage of being behind another car in the corners and Antonelli remarkably took pole by a quarter of a second from Verstappen.

"It felt like going back to 60 mini cars where you do the qualifying all together, all super close!" said Antonelli.

"It was a very weird qualifying for me. At first I couldn't get the tyres working, just zero feeling with the car, and also the brake pedal was very long so struggling on the braking.

"But, all in all, it was so unexpected and that last lap I thought I was screwed because I started one second from the car in front, but the tow is so big here that I was able to just gain with that and put it on pole."

Russell improved to beat Norris to third, while the two Racing Bulls drivers moved up to sixth and eighth, but nobody else went quicker on their second Q3 runs.

"I wanted to be on pole but I am probably more pleased with the performance today than I was, let's say, in Mexico a couple of weeks ago when I was on the front row," said Russell.

"But just timing that out lap, getting the slipstream, was just huge. It was pretty crazy, very similar to what we saw in Monza."

Ferrari did not expect to be competitive in Las Vegas but they did not anticipate both drivers being knocked out in Q2.

Hamilton went off track several times in final practice as he struggled under braking and reported he has "no grip" in qualifying and revealed he suffered some damage.

"I'm devastated. We work hard to do a good job," he told Sky Sports F1.

"I didn't really know where we were going to be today. We knew we would struggle a little bit with these long straights, but I was optimistic going in, I thought the car would be OK.

"But on the first or second lap, we took a hit from something on the floor, some debris or something damaged the floor and we lost a bunch of downforce with it, a few tenths."

Leclerc made a mistake in the middle sector on his final run in Q2 which proved very costly as he missed out on Q3 by one tenth.

"For sure (Q3 was on the cards) but we didn't do a good job. I did a mistake the only lap I was given, basically," Leclerc told Sky Sports F1.

"And at the same time, the execution has been very poor. I don't know. We'll review. I'm very angry now, so I would rather not speak too much. But I think there were plenty of things we could have done much better.
