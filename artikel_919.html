LaLiga hit by cyber attack with fans’ personal data exposed

A cyber attack has compromised personal data stored on a LaLiga digital platform, Spain's top football league confirmed in an official statement on Monday.

The precise scale of the breach, which occurred between 14 and 16 November, remains unknown. However, LaLiga stated that personal data from both a fan platform and individual football clubs were affected during the incident.

The matter has been escalated to the Spanish Agency for the Protection of Data, as well as reported to the National Police's Central Unit of Cyber-Delinquency.

LaLiga discovered the cyber incident on 16 November and immediately implemented measures to contain it.

The organisation's IT security personnel are currently evaluating platform logs to determine which specific data was compromised, while monitoring across all online systems has been stepped up to prevent further security breaches.

A number of cyber attacks have taken place across the globe in recent weeks, with the European Union (EU) confirming it had been targeted last week.

An official said cyber security specialists had been responding to an artificial intelligence-generated attack on a key EU institution for almost a month, leaving a number of the bloc's digital systems offline.

The official offered no further details on the nature of the breach or who might be responsible. It represents the first instance of the 27-member union disclosing a cyberattack carried out using an AI tool.

"AI-assisted tools can be used and especially misused to detect vulnerabilities," said Thomas Regnier, spokesperson for the European Commission, the executive branch of the 27-nation EU.

"Indeed we can confirm that this was done with AI tools," he added. "We have seen many global cyber attacks recently, and of course no one is immune to this."

Regnier noted that the EU initially detected the security incident on 22 October. Officials immediately opened an investigation while implementing "preventative measures."

The incident was first reported by news website Politico, which stated the breach lasted for at least 10 days and impacted systems used for translation, reimbursement, salaries and timesheets. Regnier declined to confirm that report.

"We temporarily took down several applications, and most of them have since then been restored," Regnier said. So far, he added, "we have not detected any evidence of a data breach affecting commission staff as a whole."
