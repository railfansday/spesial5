Sheffield Wednesday & Sunderland won’t be surprised by Ashley Fletcher, Huddersfield Town situation

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Ashley Fletcher's two years at Blackpool indicated that his best years may still be ahead of him, but so far at Huddersfield Town, he's showing inconsistencies that the likes of Sunderland and Sheffield Wednesday know all too well.

The 31-year-old has been one of the more frustrating frontmen in the EFL over the past decade or so.

Fletcher had a massively bright future coming out of the Manchester United academy, but couldn't realise his Premier League potential at West Ham United during his sole campaign at the London Stadium. In 2017, he moved to Middlesbrough and, barring an 11-goal, seven-assist campaign in 2019/20, struggled to make an impact in the Championship.

However, dropping down to League One with Blackpool a couple of years ago seemed to be the catalyst for some productive campaigns, registering 19 goal involvements in his first season in Tangerine and following that up with 21 goals in all competitions last season.

Unfortunately, he hasn't been able to carry that momentum in West Yorkshire.

With Fletcher's Blackpool contract running out at the end of last season, all eyes were on where the 31-year-old would go.

He'd shown himself to be at least a top-end League One striker, and despite the Seasiders' ambitions, it was his goals which stopped what would have been a disastrous relegation to the fourth tier. If anything, his decision to leave was accepted at Bloomfield Road.

Huddersfield were the side to snap him up, announcing a deal at the start of June, before his contract with Blackpool even expired. The haste in the deal indicated that the 31-year-old was going to be a big player for Martin Drury this season, but that hasn't been the case so far.

Fletcher has continued to be a good link-up striker, someone who holds the ball up as well as anyone in his position, but his finishing ability hasn't followed him from Blackpool.

He has just two league goals in 720 minutes of action, from an expected goals tally of 3.25. One of his efforts this season saw him tackle Burton Albion goalkeeper Corey Addai into the net in their 3-1 win last month, too — not exactly a clinical finish.

With Bojan Radulovic boasting better scoring stats in around 250 fewer minutes, it may only be a matter of time before the Serbian replaces Fletcher once he's back to full fitness, which would be a big disappointment given the excitement surrounding the 31-year-old's arrival.

Fletcher's inconsistencies in front of goal will make Sunderland fans remember his loan spell at the Stadium of Light back during the second half of the 2017/18 campaign, their first back in the Championship, and what would end up being their second of back-to-back relegations.

The then-22-year-old was brought in on loan from fellow second-tier outfit Middlesbrough to help drag an endangered Sunderland side out of the bottom three, but he massively underwhelmed.

The Black Cats didn't win any of his first eight games at the club, dropping to the foot of the second-tier table, before his first goal on March 30 led to a rare win against play-off-chasing Derby County.

That didn't end up being a catalyst for a great escape, though, as Sunderland would win just one more game that year, with Fletcher scoring just once more, too. Coincidentally, they both occurred in the same game, against Champions Wolves on the final day, with his side already relegated.

A difficult loan spell in troubling circumstances; Fletcher could have become a Sunderland hero, but instead struggled to make an impact.

Fast forward five years from that Sunderland relegation, Fletcher was under contract at Watford, but the Hornets, despite being a Championship club, weren't interested in having him lead their line.

He was loaned out to three different sides during his time at Vicarage Road, the third of which came with Sheffield Wednesday in the 2023/24 campaign — their first back in the second tier following their miraculous play-off win under Darren Moore.

What followed was, at the time, the worst Championship start ever, with Xisco Munoz sacked after 10 games with no wins, and it wouldn't be until their 14th league outing that Wednesday picked up their first three points of the campaign.

Fletcher was around the squad, but never really started enough games to make an impact, and the same could be said during the early stages of Danny Rohl's time at Hillsborough.

Come the end of the January transfer window, the frontman was starting to be left out of matchday squads, until a mid-February match against Millwall saw Fletcher subbed on with 11 minutes to go, before picking up two stoppage-time yellow cards and being sent off.

That proved to be his final appearance for Wednesday. He failed to score in his 23 Championship appearances, and it was from there that he dropped into League One with Blackpool.

Ultimately, we are only 14 games into the new season, and Fletcher is part of a Huddersfield squad flying high with plenty of capable goalscorers, so he's not the only person relied upon to find the net at his new club.

But with a few wasted chances already, it's hard not to hear alarm bells, given his past before that switch to Blackpool.

The Terriers will be hoping that the 31-year-old can find his feet in front of goal soon in West Yorkshire, as he'd become quite the impactful all-rounder if he could. As prefaced, his overall hold-up play and aerial threat are still among the best in his position.

Martin Drury's side are in a great position in the League One title race during this international break, and if Fletcher can find his form again, they could be nearly unstoppable.
