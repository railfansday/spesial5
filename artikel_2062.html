Crawley Town winger misses out on League Two Goal of the Month gong

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The 24-year-old had been shortlisted for his goal in the 3-2 home defeat to Shrewsbury Town on October 20.

Bobby Kamwa’s sensational 70-yard strike for Bristol Rovers against Crewe Alexandra on October 3 has won the Sky Bet League Two Goal of the Month for October.

The Pirates’ wing collected possession well inside his own half at Mornflake Stadium and launched a perfectly weighted, right-footed chip over Tom Booth and into the back of the net.

Kamwa said: “It feels amazing to win the award. These types of goals are the goals every footballer dreams of scoring, and I'm buzzing that I managed to do it.

“As soon as it left my foot, I connected with it right. As soon as it left my foot, the pace of it and everything, I knew it was going in.

“I'm an instinct player. I try to imagine these goals before I even do them. That’s why sometimes it might look good, and sometimes it might look bad, but that’s just me. I always try to do the extraordinary.

“I have tried it in training a few times, but in a game, I've never really tried it. I would say it’s because that day I kept looking at the goalkeeper, and he was always quite far off his line. So, I thought, you know what, at that minute of the game, I thought, why not? Then, yeah, I'm just buzzing that it went in. I’ve scored some good goals before, but that's got to be up there.”

Lead Sky Bet EFL Goal of the Month judge and Sky Sports pundit Don Goodman said: “It always takes an incredible strike to score from inside your own half, but you won’t see many better than Bobby Kamwa’s effort.

“He had his back to goal when he received the ball, and in a split second he had taken one touch out of his feet and launched it 70 yards over the helpless keeper.”

Kamwa beat off competition from Morgan, Barnet’s Zac Williams and Tranmere Rovers’ Will Vaulks.

The winners of the Sky Bet EFL Goals of the Month were selected by a panel of experts and a public vote.

The panel comprises Sky Sports’ EFL experts Goodman and David Prutton.
