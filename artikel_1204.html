Suzuki howler costs Aston Villa crucial points

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Aston Villa goalkeeper Zion Suzuki went from being serenaded with a chorus of “world’s No 1” to making a bizarre blunder that denied Unai Emery’s side a third successive Premier League victory.

The Villa manager, however, preferred to focus on a string of important saves from Suzuki rather than the uncharacteristic mistake that allowed Fulham to collect a deserved point.

It all went wrong for the hosts just after the hour mark in a match where Fulham provided more than a few scares before finding the net in unexpected fashion.

Alex Iwobi, who had been denied superbly by a Suzuki save just moments earlier, swung in a corner. It was right on top of the Villa goalkeeper, who tried to keep it out but only succeeded in fumbling it over the line. A frantic attempt to gather the ball was in vain and goal-line technology confirmed the goal stood.

Suzuki appeared to have been put off by the presence of Fulham’s Shea Charles, but Emery refused to single out the player for criticism.

“I prefer to speak about the saves that he made,” the Villa manager said. “This is part of football. I think he played fantastically and he is doing a lot of saves so he can make one mistake. It is part of football. We can be mature about this.

“I still want to see consistency. Over the last five Premier League matches, we are winning three and drawing two. It’s not bad but with our poor start it’s not enough.

“We have to change our mind quickly for Tuesday against Barcelona. We want to test ourselves at this level.”

Villa go to the Nou Camp with a 100 per cent Champions League record to their name this season. But they will need to improve on Saturday evening’s performance to get a result in Spain.

As well as Suzuki’s error, a 14th-minute penalty miss from John McGinn also proved costly.

It was awarded after Leon Goretzka, making his full league debut for Villa, saw his header hit Sander Berge’s raised hand. Nobody appealed at the time but referee Sam Barrott was sent to the touchline monitor by VAR to review the incident and gave the spot-kick.

McGinn’s attempt, however, hit the crossbar and the chance to take an early lead went begging. The Villa captain seemed a strange choice to take the spot-kick as he has only ever scored one career penalty, but Emery defended the choice, saying: “We analysed the penalty takers in the squad and it was McGinn here.”

Nicolas Jackson was on hand to ease Villa’s nerves before the break, though. the Villa manager had said before the game that the striker’s goalscoring capability meant he was capable of matching the likes of Harry Kane. His assessment was backed up by Jackson scoring the opening goal, after 40 minutes, which summed up the striker in one moment of pure power combined with agility.

Collecting Johan Manzambi’s superbly weighted pass, Jackson forced his way past two defenders before sliding in the goal that put Villa ahead.

Emery’s touchline celebrations said it all as the Senegal forward continues to flourish under the man who knew how to get the best out of him at Villarreal.

That allowed Fulham’s recent revival under manager, Alvaro Arbeloa, to continue – they now have seven points from their past three games.

“We spoke at half-time about what we had to do,” Arbeloa said. “If you want to win a game you have to fight and I think we have to believe in our way, we’ve shown we can beat anyone in the way that we play but we have to be aggressive.

“We created so many chances and at the end it’s one point in a difficult stadium. I’m so happy with the second half because of the way we played, this is the team that we want to be.”
