Priscilla Presley Scores Partial Win Over Fraud Suit, But Claims About Lisa Marie’s Death Can Stand

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Priscilla Presley scored a partial victory in her bitter $50 million legal war with her former business advisers Monday, persuading a judge to dismiss four of the 21 claims filed against her in a fraud and breach of contract lawsuit.

But Presley failed to persuade the judge to spike lengthy paragraphs from the lawsuit concerning her divorce from Elvis Presley and the shocking 2023 death of their daughter, Lisa Marie Presley. The judge blasted some of the “extremely long” complaint as “gratuitous and mean-spirited” but said striking the disputed language would make little difference because the claims were already public.

The new ruling issued Monday dismissed claims that Presley interfered with a cosmetics contract and improperly used her own name, image, and likeness in violation of an alleged agreement with business advisers Brigitte Kruse and Kevin Fialko. Ruling from the bench, Los Angeles County Judge Mark Epstein said the allegations were not sufficiently supported by facts in the complaint. Though she won on four causes of action, Presley lost her bid to toss out 10 other claims related to fraud, breach of contract, and the alleged misappropriation of funds allegedly belonging to companies set up with Kruse and Fialko.

Among the allegations Presley sought to remove from the lawsuit were claims that her “thirst for power and money was insatiable” during her divorce from Elvis Presley and that she prematurely “pulled the plug” on their daughter, Lisa Marie Presley, for financial gain. Lisa Marie died in 2023 after being hospitalized with a small bowel obstruction linked to a prior bariatric surgery. (In her memoir, Softly, As I Leave You: Life After Elvis, Presley wrote that Lisa Marie went into cardiac arrest at the hospital and was resuscitated, but then doctors told her she had “little brain activity” and would have “no quality of life” if kept on life support.)

Monday’s hearing came three years after Presley’s legal battle with Kruse and Fialko first reached the courts. Kruse, a prominent memorabilia dealer, filed the initial legal action in Florida in October 2023, accusing Presley of abandoning the series of companies they formed to capitalize on her name, image, and likeness. Presley filed a separate lawsuit in Los Angeles in July 2024, accusing Kruse and Fialko of elder abuse. She described Kruse as a “con artist and pathological liar” who had misappropriated more than $1 million and fraudulently induced her to sign agreements giving the pair an 80 percent stake in some of her business ventures. Kruse and Fialko subsequently moved their claims from Florida to California, filing a $50 million cross-complaint last year.

In video exclusively obtained by Rolling Stone, Presley is seen signing the contracts behind her deals with Kruse and Fialko. In the ensuing legal battle, Kruse has claimed Presley was facing “impending financial ruin” when Kruse and Fialko stepped in to help rescue the cultural icon. Kruse alleges Presley then cut her out because her financial circumstances changed dramtaically in the wake of Lisa Marie Presley’s death. As Rolling Stone previously reported, two weeks after Lisa Marie died on Jan. 12, 2023, Presley challenged a 2016 amendment to her daughter’s trust that removed her as a co-trustee. Presley later reached a settlement with granddaughter Riley Keough that granted her a $1 million lump-sum payment, a $100,000 annual salary, and burial rights near Elvis at Graceland.

On Monday, Judge Epstein gave Kruse and Fialko an opportunity to amend their complaint and revive some of the dismissed claims under different legal theories, potentially including breach of contract. But he cautioned against expanding on the background paragraphs Presley challenged as “inflammatory.”

The judge also expressed frustration with the pace of the litigation. When the lawyer for Kruse and Fialko suggested they might seek to add Authentic Brands Group as a defendant, Epstein urged him to act promptly. Authentic Brands acquired certain commercial rights to the Presley name in 2013 through its purchase of Elvis Presley’s licensing business. Priscilla Presley had sold those rights to a predecessor company in 2005, a transaction Kruse and Fialko allege she concealed from them.

“My goal is to get this done. If there’s a third party to sue, sue them,” Judge Epstein said. “This case is getting really long in the tooth.”

Presley’s lawyer, T. Wayne Harman, tells Rolling Stone that his client “continues to seek justice for the manipulation, elder abuse, and financial exploitation that she suffered at the hands of Brigitte Kruse and her co-conspirators.”

“In an effort to deflect from their significant liability to Mr. Presley, Kruse and her co-conspirators filed a set of salacious-sounding counterclaims, in which they did not simply attack Ms. Presley, but in which they also shamefully filed claims against Ms. Presley’s son, her personal assistant, and her cousin. In today’s ruling, Judge Epstein appropriately found that certain of the Kruse parties’ counterclaims, including that Ms. Presley somehow misappropriated her own name, image, and likeness, and that Ms. Presley is obligated to indemnify the Kruse parties for their own wrongful conduct, should be dismissed,” he says. “While the Kruse parties have another opportunity to amend their claims, Ms. Presley is thankful that her case is moving closer to trial, and she looks forward to obtaining justice, for her and her family.”
