CBS News Staffers Take Buyout Offers As Skydance Looks For Cost Savings

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

This week was a Skydance buyout deadline for employees to decide whether to accept a buyout offer, including at CBS News.

The offer came following the closing of Skydance’s purchase of Warner Bros. Discovery, as the company seeks $6 billion in savings. The merger puts CBS News and CNN under the same corporate umbrella.

At CBS News, the buyout was accepted by employees across the news division, with the London bureau among the most impacted, sources familiar with the plans said. Some staffers on 60 Minutes also took the buyout, although none were in key lead editorial roles, according to a source. In the Washington bureau, Mary Hager, senior executive producer of Face the Nation, announced her departure earlier this month after 35 years at the network, although CBS News did not say whether her exit was tied to the buyout offer. All told, less than 10% of staffers offered buyouts accepted them, according to a source. In its report on the buyouts, Status newsletter reported that dozens took the offer, including producers and members of the operational staff in London. The UK’s labor laws also require robust offers compared to the U.S., while there also has been some anxiety over the future with two major news brands under the same ownership.

While CNN and CBS News remain as separate news entities, media companies looking to cut costs often look to foreign bureaus for savings. And this week, Skydance CEO David Ellison said that while he’s “incredibly happy” operating CNN and CBS News as they are, there “might be some news sharing” in the future.

CNN’s buyout deadline was earlier this month.

CBS News went through a round of cuts last October and another round in March. The latest round represented about 6% of the workforce, or around 60 to 70 employees out of about 1,100.
