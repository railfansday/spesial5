Bayer is licensing an experimental cancer drug from China's Hansoh for up to $3.25 billion

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Hansoh will get $150 million upfront; the license takes effect after antitrust clearances are received

Bayer signed a license agreement with China's Hansoh Pharmaceutical Group for HS-20122, an experimental cancer drug, in a deal worth up to $3.25 billion, according to Bloomberg.

Hansoh said it will collect $150 million at signing and stands to earn as much as $3.1 billion in additional payments tied to development, regulatory approval and commercialization milestones for the drug. It is also eligible for tiered royalties on potential future net sales.

Bayer receives an exclusive license to develop, manufacture and commercialize HS-20122 in all markets except mainland China, Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan, Hansoh said. The agreement becomes effective after the necessary antitrust clearances.

HS-20122 is a bispecific antibody-drug conjugate that targets both EGFR and c-Met and delivers a cytotoxic payload to tumors. Hansoh said results from a Phase 1 study in patients with advanced solid tumors, such as non-small cell lung cancer, were presented at the European Society for Medical Oncology 2026 Congress.

"We are delighted to partner with Bayer on HS-20122, which has demonstrated promising clinical activity across multiple tumor types," Eliza Sun, executive director of the board at Hansoh, said in a statement.

Juergen Eckhardt, head of business development and licensing at Bayer's Pharmaceuticals Division, said in a statement that people with lung cancer need new options with greater efficacy and improved tolerability. He said the deal supports Bayer's precision oncology strategy of advancing antibody-drug conjugates that more selectively target tumor cells.

Bayer CEO Bill Anderson said in an interview earlier this week that he hopes to do more early-stage pharmaceutical deals as the fallout from U.S. litigation over its Roundup weedkiller recedes, according to Bloomberg. Bayer won final court approval last week for a $7.25 billion Roundup settlement proposal.

Bayer is up against generic rivals to its Xarelto blood thinner and its Eylea treatment for eye conditions, according to Bloomberg. Earlier this year, the company reached an agreement valued at up to $2.45 billion with Perfuse Therapeutics, a developer of eye medicines.

The agreement adds to Hansoh's deals with European drugmakers, including Roche Holding and GSK, according to Bloomberg.
