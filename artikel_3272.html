Duran Duran Guitarist Andy Taylor Dead at 65

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Andy Taylor, the founding guitarist in Duran Duran who co-wrote several of the group’s most beloved songs, including “Hungry Like the Wolf,” “Rio,” and “Girls on Film,” died Monday after a long battle with stage 4 metastatic prostate cancer. He was 65.

Taylor’s death was confirmed Wednesday with a statement from Duran Duran and Taylor’s family.

“Andy was there at the very beginning of our story,” Duran Duran said. “From the moment he arrived in Birmingham from the North East with a guitar and an unshakeable belief in what we could become, he brought an indelible fire and swagger to our music. His playing drove the songs that took us around the world, and with a dry wit that was matched only by his wonderfully unsentimental, no-nonsense outlook on life, he kept us grounded and gave the band its edge through the extraordinary years that followed.”

The band said Taylor faced his cancer battle with “remarkable courage, honesty, and grace,” adding, “It was great to make music with him again when the original five reunited in the early 2000s, and that time together, both onstage and in the studio, meant a tremendous amount to all of us. When we were inducted into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame in 2022, Andy was too unwell to join us, but his words that night reminded everyone of his strength and the bond we shared.”

Taylor’s wife, Tracey, said, “To the world, Andy was a brilliant guitarist, but to us, he was the most wonderful husband, a loving dad, and the proudest grandfather. He was the rock of our family. For all the incredible experiences music brought him, family always came first. Nothing made him happier than having us all together, and no matter how far music took him, his heart was always at home.

“Andy faced the last few years with so much courage and determination. He never gave up, fighting until the very end, never losing his love of life. Taylor’s guitar work gave Duran Duran a critical hard-rock edge in their early days on the British New Romantic scene. “It really worked because it was going completely against the disco grooves and the electronic pulses [popular in England],” Duran Duran keyboardist Nick Rhodes said in 2021. “It was what the Americans liked as well. Because they were still playing FM rock in America; we were the only New Wave band that had heavy guitar, so it crossed over.”

“Literally from the day I met Nick, John, Simon, and Roger, they truly valued the contribution of a rather noisy, versatile Northern brat,” Taylor said in 2022. “We all grew up on the same vinyl records and live gigs, from David Bowie to Roxy Music, the Sex Pistols, and, of course, Chic. I could go to all those places as a player and developed a hybrid guitar style that fit this amazing concept for a band.”

In 1984, Taylor formed Power Station with Robert Palmer, Chic drummer Tony Thompson, and Duran Duran bassist John Taylor. The short-lived supergroup scored worldwide hits with “Some Like It Hot” and a cover of the T-Rex classic “Bang a Gong (Get It On).” He also recorded with Rod Stewart, Paul Rodgers, Belinda Carlisle, and many other big-name acts, but his legacy will forever be defined by his work with Duran Duran.

“Nobody else sounded like us,” Taylor told The Irish Times in 2022. “We were ripe to absorb what was the art of analogue recording, but with some different kit, Nick’s artful obsession with synth technology was something I’d never seen before, and I was introduced to layers. Because we were instinctively the right fit, we evolved very quickly, writing Rio as our second album with the confidence our very early success [that] ‘Girls on Film’ and ‘Planet Earth’ inspired. Taylor grew up in the Northeast England town of Cullercoats. His mother abandoned the family when he was 11, leaving him with deep psychological scars, but he found refuge in the music of AC/DC, Van Halen, and other hard-rock bands of the time. He learned guitar as a teenager and played around England in a series of unsuccessful groups until 1980, when he came across an ad in Melody Maker by a new band in Birmingham seeking a “live-wire guitarist.” When he arrived for the audition, he met Nick Rhodes along with bassist John Taylor and drummer Roger Taylor. “The three of us had a good laugh about the fact that by coincidence we all shared the same surname,” Taylor wrote in his 2008 memoir Wild Boy. “They had long, floppy hair and were into David Bowie and Bryan Ferry and the whole new fashion trend that had started to gain currency ever since punk died away.”

This was the start of the New Romantic scene, and Duran Duran would become its most famous export once they teamed up with frontman Simon Le Bon. The band’s 1981 debut single, “Planet Earth,” became an immediate hit in England and set the tone for much of the music that followed by mixing New Wave synthesizers with Taylor’s searing electric guitar.

By the time they released their second LP, 1982’s Rio, MTV had launched in America. The fledgling cable channel, desperate for content, put the videos for singles “Hungry Like the Wolf” and “Rio” into heavy rotation and turned the band into superstars. The group became so popular that the press labeled them the leaders of a “Second British Invasion,” alongside groups like Spandau Ballet, Flock of Seagulls, Human League, Dexy’s Midnight Runners, and Eurythmics. The hits continued in 1983 when the band released Seven and the Ragged Tiger, featuring “Union of the Snake” and “The Reflex,” but the group was melting down behind the scenes due to personality clashes, disagreements over the band’s musical direction, and heavy cocaine use.

In 1984, during a break in the Duran Duran schedule, Andy and John formed Power Station with Palmer. “The music that John and I made together in Power Station was born partly out of frustration at not being able to play the way we wanted in Duran Duran,” Taylor wrote in Wild Boy. “The memory of how painstaking it had been to record Seven and the Ragged Tiger was still fresh in our minds, so when the opportunity came along to do something different, we seized it with both hands.”

The warring factions of Duran Duran were forced back together in 1985 to perform at Live Aid in Philadelphia. “We’d reached rock bottom, and we had absolutely nothing to say to each other,” Taylor wrote in Wild Boy. “We drove to the venue through streets packed with excited rock fans, but inside the bus, the atmosphere was as if we were on a way to a funeral. Little did we realize that we were on the way to a funeral — it was ours.”

Taylor quit Duran Duran the following year to start a solo career that stalled after two unsuccessful albums: 1987’s Thunder and 1990’s Dangerous. He spent much of the Nineties spending time with his wife, Tracy, and their children, Andy, Georgie, Bethy, and Izzy.

Duran Duran scored a massive comeback in 1993 thanks to the hits “Ordinary World” and “Come Undone,” but were on a downward spiral at the turn of the millennium with only Le Bon and Rhodes remaining from the original band. They reunited with the classic lineup — Taylor included — for a reunion tour in 2001 that led to the 2004 LP Astronaut. Ticket sales were strong, but old problems quickly resurfaced. Taylor left the band during the making of an album in 2006, following months of tension with the group and their management.

“Perhaps things might have been different if we all sat down together and discussed the tensions that tore us apart the first time around, but that never happened,” he wrote in Wild Boy. “In fact, I don’t think I ever once went to dinner with Nick during the first six years that we were back together. There was no cocaine the second time around, but in the end, it was the similar tensions caused by internal disagreements that came bubbling back to the surface. Taylor kept a low profile in the decade following his departure from Duran Duran. In 2019, he reemerged with the new single “Love or Liberation.” The news arrived three years later that Duran Duran were going to enter the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame. While plans for an onstage reunion with Taylor were made public, he was nowhere to be seen when the group entered the theater. When they took the stage, Le Bon read a letter revealing Taylor’s battle with stage 4 metastatic prostate cancer.

“I’m truly sorry and massively disappointed I couldn’t make it,” Taylor wrote in an open letter that night. “Let there be no doubt I was stoked about the whole thing, even bought a new guitar with the essential whammy! I’m so very proud of these four brothers; I’m amazed at their durability, and I’m overjoyed at accepting this award. I often doubted the day would come. I’m sure as hell glad I’m around to see the day.”
