Maybe Dark Energy Isn't so Inexplicable After All

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The Universe as we know it is comprised of three "things": baryonic matter (planets, stars, galaxies, us), dark matter (still unexplained) and dark energy (really, really still unexplained). Of the three, dark energy remains the most challenging. It's "something" that's driving the acceleration of the expanding Universe but that's all we know about it. We can't see it, or even put a description to it, other than "it's mysterious".

This cosmic component accounts for about 70 percent of the known Universe's energy budget. Physicists have spent a lot of time trying to explain it, often invoking some hitherto-unknown fields, or exotic physical particles, or some kind of "cosmological constant" that is the same everywhere in space and time. Despite these and other attempts at explaining dark energy, physicists still don't know exactly what it is. However, there's hope that an explanation will come in our lifetimes. A team of scientists at Canada's University of British Columbia and the University of Sheffield (in England) have come up with a possible model that provides an explanation. They think dark matter may be a product of the same physics that governs the strength of the atomic nucleus. If that's true, then they could use the standard model of particle physics to understand this mysterious thing that is pushing the Universe apart.
