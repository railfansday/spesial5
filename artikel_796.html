Fifa to confirm UK as host for 2035 Women's World Cup

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Spain beat England 1-0 to win the 2023 World Cup

Fifa is set to confirm on Monday that a first football World Cup will be hosted in the United Kingdom since 1966.

England, Northern Ireland, Scotland and Wales are the only bidders to jointly stage the 2035 Women's World Cup.

A delegation will be present at Fifa's Zurich headquarters with national associations holding a specially arranged virtual meeting, and voting to ratify the co-hosts.

The tournament would involve 104 matches contested by 48 teams over 39 days.

Nineteen venues are proposed by the 'All Together' bid, with 14 in England, one in Northern Ireland, two in Scotland and two in Wales.

Fifa's evaluation of the bid, published earlier this month, rated it 4.3 out of five. However, its contractual framework was rated 'medium risk', and the document claimed that "certain government guarantees contain material qualifications, particularly regarding tax and safety and security".

This is thought to refer to a lack of agreement over a request by Fifa for tax-free status for all football-related activities.

Fifa's bid evaluation said: "The bidder has submitted an extensive set of government support documents and contractual hosting agreements, demonstrating the commitment of the relevant authorities and stakeholders.

"However, certain government guarantees, particularly those relating to safety and security and tax, contain certain qualifications which have been duly noted in the legal evaluation.

"While certain contractual matters remain subject to further discussion, the bidder's proactive engagement with Fifa and the substantial lead-in time provide a sound basis for resolving these outstanding issues."

Everton's Hill Dickinson Stadium is one of 19 stadiums included in the 2035 bid

England hosted the women's European Championship in 2022, which the Lionesses won.

England, Scotland, Wales, and the Republic of Ireland are also joint hosts of the men's European Championship in 2028.

Mark Bullingham, chief executive of the Football Association, said: "Hosting Euro 2022 allowed us to change the game in our country, by creating a generation of aspirational role models and growing demand from women and girls to play.

"Hosting a World Cup would give us the chance to build on that across all four nations."

The English stadiums included in the 2035 bid are: Villa Park, The Amex, Ashton Gate, Elland Road, Hill Dickinson Stadium, Emirates Stadium, Selhurst Park, Tottenham Hotspur Stadium, Wembley, Etihad Stadium, St James' Park, the City Ground, the Stadium of Light and Old Trafford.

If Manchester United's proposed new Old Trafford stadium does not go ahead, it will still be considered in its current form.

A further three stadiums could be added - Birmingham's new Powerhouse Stadium, Wrexham's redeveloped Racecourse Ground and a potential new Chelsea ground - which would take the tournament to 16 cities and 22 venues.

The Principality Stadium in Cardiff is one of two Welsh venues, along with the Cardiff City Stadium

Cardiff City Stadium and Principality Stadium the two Welsh venues.

Noel Mooney, the chief executive of the Football Association of Wales, said: "Wales has been a football nation for 150 years and we have never hosted anything on this scale. World Cup football played here, in front of Welsh children, would change what this game looks like in Wales: in the clubs, in the schools, and in the number of girls who grow up seeing a pathway into it."

Northern Ireland missed out on co-hosting the men's Euros in 2028 after the UK government said in 2024 it would not provide funding to redevelop Casement Park in time for the tournament - but a redeveloped Windsor Park is set to stage matches in 2035.

The Irish Football Association's chief executive Patrick Nelson said the event "would be felt in every club and every age group, in every part of the country. Our young players would grow up having watched the best in the world play at home, and there is no substitute for that."

Easter Road in Edinburgh and Hampden Park in Glasgow are the Scottish venues proposed, with Scottish Football Association chief executive Ian Maxwell saying hosting the tournament would be "the most significant thing to happen to the women's and girls' game in Scotland".

He added: "The impact would go far beyond just the matches themselves. The level of investment into coaching, facilities and player pathways in the years beforehand will shape the future of the game for the generations to come."

The special meeting of Fifa's Congress is also set to approve the joint bid by Mexico, the United States, Costa Rica and Jamaica to host the 2031 finals, which will be the first to feature 48 teams.
