Appeals court rules Arizona media outlets can publish statements made by daughters of accused murderer

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

A judge had issued a "gag order," barring the media from reporting on what was said in open court by the daughters of farming tycoon Michael Abatti.

(CN) â The Arizona Court of Appeals ruled Tuesday that news organizations can publish statements made, in open court, by the daughters of farming tycoon Michael Abatti, who is accused of shooting his estranged wife to death.

“We are troubled that no one told the daughters the consequences of reading their statements in open court,” wrote Judge Jennifer Perkins in the unanimous opinion. “But once the daughters spoke in open court, the First Amendment protected the pressâs right to publish what they said, even if the failure to warn the daughters was inconsistent with the Victimsâ Bill of Rights.”

Abatti, a 63-year-old prominent farmer, is charged with first-degree murder in the death of his wife, 59-year-old Kerri Ann Abatti. The Imperial Valley couple had been embroiled in a bitter, three-year-long divorce, at the heart of which lay a $200 million trust. Arrested four weeks after the shooting, Abatti pleaded not guilty and is being held in custody, apparently unable to post the $5.5 million in bail.

At Abatti’s arraignment in December last year, his two daughters, who attended the hearing remotely, were allowed to read victim impact statements. A reporter from KPNX-TV Channel 12 was also listening to the proceedings remotely, with the court’s approval. The daughters’ statements were admitted into the record under seal, but they were also allowed to read them aloud during the hearing, which was open to the public. After the first statement was read, both the prosecutor and the defense attorney asked the court to issue a “gag order,” barring members of the media from reporting what the daughters said.

KPNX objected to the gag order, arguing it was unconstitutional prior restraint. But both Abatti and his daughters urged the judge to stand by the order, Abatti arguing that publishing the statements would taint the small-town jury pool, and the daughters arguing they didn’t know the hearing was a public forum. The judge kept the gag order in place, and KPNX, joined by a handful of other local media outlets, filed a petition to overrule the order.

The three-judge panel found the gag order was unconstitutional under both the Arizona and U.S. Constitutions.

“A court may not bar the press from publishing what happens in open court unless the ‘gravity of the evil’ resulting from publication, ‘discounted by its improbability, justifies such invasion of free speech as is necessary to avoid the danger,’” Perkins wrote, citing a 1976 Supreme Court ruling.

As to the argument that the daughters’ statements would taint the jury pool, Perkins wrote, “Even if the small, rural community here increases the risk of jury prejudice, that risk remains too speculative … Abatti has not shown, for example, that ordinary tools â a larger jury pool, questioning prospective jurors about media exposure, and instructing jurors to consider only the trial evidence â would fail to protect him from prejudice caused by reporting on his daughtersâ statements.”

The panel found that while the daughters might be harmed by the publicizing of their comments, “the gravity of the potential emotional harm to the adult daughters does not justify the prior restraint.”

Judges Jon Saline and Samuel Clark rounded out the panel.
