Which Hotel Credit Cards Offer Free Night Awards, And Are They Worth It?

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Hotel credit cards are among the most underrated out there. Understandably a lot of people get cards like the Chase Sapphire Preferred® Card (learn more) and Chase Sapphire Reserve® Card (learn more) to maximize the rewards they’re earning for their spending, and that’s a great idea. These cards offer valuable bonus categories, no foreign transaction fees, great travel coverage, and more.

But I think supplementing one of those cards with hotel credit cards can make a lot of sense. That’s because hotel credit cards largely offer some incredible perks that more than justify the annual fee, including free hotel elite status, and even some free night certificates. So even if you’re not loyal to a hotel group, it’s easy to get outsized value.

In this post, I’d like to specifically look at which credit cards offer free nights as part of their rewards structure, how you can earn them, and if they’re worth it.

There are hotel credit cards issued by Hilton Honors, IHG One Rewards, Marriott Bonvoy, and World of Hyatt, which offer the opportunity to earn free night awards. To be clear, I’m not talking about the ability to earn points, but instead, the ability to earn free night awards that can be redeemed at a set of properties.

These free nights come in different forms — some free night awards are offered for spending a certain amount on a card, and some free night awards are offered as an annual benefit just for having a card, with no spending required.

So let’s take a look at the details of how these free night awards work, and then we’ll talk about whether they’re worth it. Note that I’m focusing specifically on cards that offer free night awards on an ongoing basis (rather than as part of a welcome offer), and am also limiting this to cards that are open to new applicants.

Hilton Honors has two credit cards that give cardmembers the ability to earn free night awards:

Hilton Honors free night awards are my single favorite hotel free night awards out there, given that they have no category caps, and they’re valid for 12 months from when they’re issued. I value Hilton points at 0.5 cents each.

Hilton free night awards can be redeemed for standard rooms at properties costing up to 250,000 points per night, and there’s only a small list of excluded properties. They’re even valid at Small Luxury Hotels of the World (SLH) properties that are part of the Hilton Honors partnership. It’s easy to redeem these at properties costing $1,000+ per night.

IHG One Rewards has three credit cards that give cardmembers the ability to earn free night awards:

IHG One Rewards free night awards are also potentially quite valuable, and they expire 12 months from when they’re issued. I value IHG points at 0.5 cents each.

While IHG free night awards are capped at properties costing a certain number of points, what I really like is how you can top them off with an unlimited number of points. So if a hotel requires 100,000 points, you could use a 60,000 point free night certificate, and then top it off with 40,000 points.

Marriott Bonvoy has four credit cards that give cardmembers the ability to earn free night awards:

Marriott Bonvoy free night awards are also extremely lucrative, and they expire 12 months from when they’re issued. I value Marriott points at 0.7 cents each.

While Marriott free night awards are capped at properties costing a certain number of points, you can top them each off with up to 25,000 points, to redeem at more expensive properties. So you could book a hotel costing 60,000 points per night by redeeming a 35,000 point free night award, and then redeeming 25,000 additional points.

World of Hyatt Honors has one credit card that give cardmembers the ability to earn free night awards:

World of Hyatt free night awards are valuable, but definitely the most restrictive of the bunch. Free night awards issued with the card expire 12 months after they’re issued. I value World of Hyatt points at 1.4 cents each.

Category 1-4 covers a lot of Hyatt properties (these can cost up to 25,000 points per night), but the catch is that you can’t use these certificates to redeem at more expensive properties by topping them off. Still, it should be very easy to get outsized value.

I’ll let my credit card portfolio speak for itself — I have quite the selection of hotel credit cards. They’re not necessarily cards that I spend a ton on, but instead, they’re cards I largely have because of the very strong perks that they offer, which to me, more than justify the annual fee. Let me just give a few direct examples…

I have the Hilton Honors Aspire Card, and it’s my single favorite hotel credit card. The card offers a massive number of perks, but in particular, the free night alone more than justifies the annual fee for me. I try to use this at properties that would retail for $1,000+ per night, so it’s pretty awesome to get that, along with all kinds of other perks, just for having the card.

I also think there’s merit to spending $60,000 per year on the card, if you can swing it. That spending would earn you a minimum of 180,000 points (since you earn at least 3x points per dollar spent with the card), plus two additional free night awards, potentially worth up to 500,000 points. So this is a great card to consider if you’re making a big purchase.

In the case of Marriott Bonvoy, I have both the Marriott Bonvoy Brilliant Card and Marriott Bonvoy Business Card, which get me free night awards worth 85,000 and 35,000 points, respectively. These also in and of themselves more than justify the annual fees on the cards. I generally redeem the larger free night award for a luxury hotel stay, while I redeem the lesser free night award for a stay at a mid-range property.

Lastly, I also have the World of Hyatt Credit Card, and that free night award also more than justifies the annual fee in and of itself. Admittedly I wish the free night award cap would be increased to reflect inflation, but even so, I get a ton of value out of this. At an absolute minimum, I use it when I have an overnight near JFK to stay at the Hyatt Regency JFK, which typically costs $300+ if paying cash.

There are potentially lots of great benefits to hotel credit cards, including big welcome bonuses, easy opportunities to earn hotel status, generous rewards structures for spending, and more.

The best part is that most issuers let you have multiple credit cards, meaning you can potentially earn more than one free night certificate per year. In the case of just about all of the above cards, I’d say the free night certificates more than justify the annual fees.
