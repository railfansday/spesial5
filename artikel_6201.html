American’s Longtime Chief Operating Officer to Retire

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

American Airlines is making another change to its senior leadership team, with Chief Operating Officer David Seymour set to retire at the end of the year.

Seymour will step down on Dec. 31 and remain with the Fort Worth, Texas-based carrier in an advisory capacity through the first quarter of 2027, CEO Robert Isom announced Friday.

Seymour has served as American’s chief operating officer since 2020. He joined the airline in 1999 and held various leadership positions overseeing technical operations, crew scheduling, and flight operations.

“He’s the guy you want in the room when the stakes are high,” Isom wrote in a letter to employees.

JonCarlo “JC” Gulbranson, American’s current senior vice president of operations, will succeed Seymour on Jan. 1. He will report directly to Isom and assume oversight of the carrier’s technical operations, safety, and regulatory compliance teams.

Gulbranson began his airline career at Northwest Airlines in 2000 before joining US Airways in 2008. He helped oversee the integration of flight operations systems following the merger with American.

American will also move its corporate security team under its government affairs division. A new reporting structure for the airline’s regional operations will be announced in December.

“For nearly three decades, jet fuel hasn’t just been in my veins. It’s been part of my DNA. To this day, I wake up excited to get to work — which is just about all you can ask for in a job,” Seymour wrote in a LinkedIn post.

The changes follow a broader management reshuffle announced in August as the carrier works to narrow its profitability gap with competitors Delta and United.
