'George had perfect position' - Wolff defends Mercedes' Vegas Q3 strategy

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Kimi Antonelli was fastest in Las Vegas GP Qualifying but will start from sixth because of a grid penalty; George Russell will start from second after finishing third; watch Sunday's race at 4am (build-up from 2.30am), live on Sky Sports F1 and Main Event

Mercedes chief Toto Wolff dismissed suggestions the team should have done more to aid George Russell's bid for Las Vegas Grand Prix pole as he insisted the British driver had "perfect" track position for his final run in qualifying.

Russell had gone into the final part of qualifying as the favourite for pole but ultimately finished third after his team-mate Kimi Antonelli topped Red Bull's Max Verstappen.

Many had assumed Mercedes would use Antonelli, who has already sealed the Drivers' Championship and is serving a five-place grid penalty in Las Vegas, to give Russell a tow up the main straight to help him in his bid to chase down Verstappen for second in the drivers' standings.

Instead, a frantic finish to qualifying saw the Mercedes duo and several other cars in a scrap for track position, which left Antonelli at the back of the pack scrambling to start his lap before the session ended, but then benefitting from a huge multi-car tow to go fastest.

Speaking to Sky Sports F1 pundit and former Aston Martin head of strategy Bernie Collins after the session, Wolff admitted he was shocked Antonelli had taken pole given the Italian had to closely follow Racing Bulls' Arvid Lindblad around the lap.

"We have absolutely no clue," Wolff said. "And you (Collins) as a strategist, you know we thought Kimi was either going to miss the lap or he's going to be way too close to Lindblad.

"He crossed the line 1.4 seconds behind, and that was basically it. So, I don't understand how being that close you can even do this time.

"If you're 1.4 seconds behind the car in front, you would think this is too close, especially with a car that went eight tenths slower, but for whatever reason, that works."

Asked why Mercedes hadn't prioritised giving Russell a tow, Wolff suggested that a driver error, rather than the team's strategy, had cost the Brit pole.

"Actually, George, for me, had the perfect position," Wolff said. "He was four seconds behind the car in front.

"We need to find out because I think there was some kind of… turn seven-eight-nine, whether that was a mistake or something else happened.

"We need to… I don't know, to be honest, but it looked like the ideal position for a pole lap, because he was quickest all the time."

Former world champion Jacques Villeneuve suggested that Antonelli "went out of his way" to avoid giving Russell a tow.

"The team didn't do anything to help Russell," the Sky Sports F1 pundit said.

"When Toto says Russell was four seconds behind and he was in a good position, that was from his (Russell's) doing. The team did not help him.

"He kind of skated the answer there. Because Kimi went out of his way to make sure he wasn't ahead of Russell, to make sure he wouldn't help his team-mate, and that was obvious.

"He played his own card, he played his own game even though he knew that he couldn't get pole in the end.

"That was a little bit nasty, and George will be really, really upset. Mostly when, Antonelli has got the world championship, the team has got the championship, they had every interest in helping George."

Sky Sports F1's Jamie Chadwick was also surprised that Russell wasn't given more support.

"I think the reality is that, regardless of where those two Mercedes found themselves, for me it just felt like the order should have been Kimi in front of George," Chadwick said.

"If they were at the front of the pack like Red Bull were, that's one thing, and then George would have benefitted from Kimi. If they were at the back of the pack, then he could have benefitted like Kimi did at the end."

Following the first Q3 runs, after which Russell was third behind Verstappen and McLaren's Lando Norris, he had asked over team radio whether the team could get Antonelli to help him.

Despite that not materialising, Russell was not critical of his team or team-mate when asked about the situation after the session.

He told Sky Sports F1: "It's so difficult to orchestrate and getting the perfect tow is high risk.

"Kimi, if he started his lap a second later wouldn't have got the lap in and, in the end, he gained a second in the straight - maybe more - and maybe lost six tenths in the corners. So, it's unbelievable the impact.

Asked during the post-session press conference for the top three whether he was hoping for more assistance from Antonelli, Russell added: "No, I mean, obviously, it's still important, if you've got a grid penalty, to start as high up the order as possible.

"But we'll take it offline and we can talk about it."

Antonelli, who will start the race from sixth on the grid, suggested he was unhappy that Mercedes had left himself and Russell leading other cars in Q3.

"Well, first thing, as George said, we will talk about it because we've been sent out at the front in qualifying always," Antonelli said.

The 20-year-old explained the range of emotions he went through as he dropped to the back of the pack, barely made the line in time, and then contended with closely following Lindblad through the corners.

He added: "About Turn 5, it was fully on me. Like I said, we were three-wide, first of all, and I just said, 'you know what? I'm just going to back off.'

"It was tricky because, first of all, I almost missed the flag. Yes, I did get a big tow, but I was also so close that getting around the corner was actually difficult with the dirty air and so on. We're already on a lower-downforce package, so you do feel even more the loss when following.

"But overall, it was just one of those times where you think you're screwed, but actually it works out.

"On Turn 5, I just decided to back off. I didn't really know how tight I was with the timing. I probably would have done it differently, but in hindsight, it still worked out."
