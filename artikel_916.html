Sloppy Socceroos given a reality check as Jordan dominate in friendly win

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Jordan 2-0 Australia (Al-Taamari 34, Ali Olwan 90+3)

Socceroos had just one effort on target in defeat

In their final game of 2026, the Socceroos were given a reminder that they remain a work in progress, beaten 2-0 by Jordan in an international friendly where the scoreline failed to reflect the first-half siege the victors laid on their opponent’s goal, nor the unforced errors and brain fades that helped doom the Australians’ hopes of firing a shot in response.

Optimism was high for those in green and gold after their comfortable win against the reigning Asian champions Qatar on Friday but very quickly on Tuesday it was made apparent that the Jordanians were to provide far more spirited opposition. This time around it would be the Australians that were to be the subject of a first-half onslaught, as a Jordan team inspired by Musa al-Taamari launched forward with wicked pace and ruthless intent whenever the opportunity, or space left by the Socceroo defence, presented itself.

Australia’s men have often found themselves under the pump in recent years, only to find a way to ride out the pressure without being too uncomfortable. Across a furious first half, however, they were repeatedly left scrambling to keep Jordan in front of them, as the pace of al-Taamari and Mahmoud al-Mardi turned them around and prevented them from assuming their defensive shape. This was bolstered by a highly vocal contingent of supporters whose volume levels delivered a level of inferred threat even as attacks broke down, even with the game having been moved from Amman to Doha.

“Poor, very poor,” the coach Tony Popovic said of the opening 45 minutes. “We played into their hands; our ball speed, our aggression, we didn’t win any duels, and we constantly got caught on the counterattack.

“We had what we deserved in the first half. They showed the energy, intensity and aggression that we showed against Qatar. It just shows that we’ve got a long way to go. It’s great lessons for all of us, coaching staff and players, on where we’re at.”

Within minutes of the opening whistle, al-Mardi sent a shot on target, albeit one contested by Alessandro Circati and Harry Souttar. Guided wide by Souttar, al-Taamari then forced another save from Patrick Beach soon after, before blasting over the bar minutes later after bursting into space left vacant by Lucas Herrington getting forward. Three shots in six minutes, the same number Popovic’s side had faced across 90 against Qatar.

With the pressure continuing, al-Taamari wove across the park and into the penalty area before inducing Connor Metcalfe to stick out a lazy leg and give away a 31st-minute penalty. And while Beach was able to deflect away his resulting spot-kick with a trailing leg, few would have begrudged his opener buried into the bottom corner three minutes later. Indeed, most would have considered him and his Jordan teammates unlucky to only have the one goal heading into the half-time break.

For the Australians, it was a goal that served to punish the sloppy touches and misplaced passes that had infected their collective football throughout the opening 45 minutes. This time, Herrington was the unlucky one punished, his wayward touch ricocheting to Amer Jamous, who whipped the ball into the space left vacant by the Hull defender and allowed al-Taamari to go to work.

“Not good enough from us; not good enough,” said Beach. “We know that. I think we had a good discussion at half-time about that. I think we went into the game with the handbrake up a bit.”

Souttar was replaced by Diesel Herrington at half-time – the Leicester defender watching the second half from the stands in street clothes – and Australia were able to cut down on the volume of Jordan forays forward – the Chivalrous Ones’ first-half intensity not a sustainable level – but did little to suggest they were on the verge of turning the tide.

They also continued to be let down by their touch even as they were able to work the ball into more threatening areas of the park – Popovic emptying his bench once more as the second half progressed – and they would end with just a single tame effort on goal, from Riley McGree in the 49th minute. Topping it all off, the substitute Daniel Arzani picked up a second yellow card 15 minutes after his introduction to reduce the Socceroos to 10 men, and Ali Olwan put a bow on things in the 93rd minute.

With the postponement of the Asian Cup, Popovic is now out of contract. The side have made significant progress under his watch but Tuesday served as a signal that progress in football is very rarely linear. Heading into 2027, the question is who will be bouncing back. Popovic? Or someone else? It certainly wouldn’t have been the way he would have wanted to go out if it was the latter.

“That’s not for me to comment on,” Beach said of Popovic’s future. “The boss, he’s been great with me, and he’s been great with the team. We’ve had a lot of success with him, and hopefully that long continues. But whatever happens with him and his future here, that’s not for me to decide.”
