The tightest survival scrap for years? Why it's not too early to worry

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Is it too early for the Premier League bottom three to worry?

Despite being only 10 games into the season, sitting in the relegation zone even at this early stage is more worrying than you might think.

But with how tight the Premier League table is, this could also be one of the fiercest relegation battles we have seen in a long time.

Two of the current bottom three, Crystal Palace and Manchester United, will resume their campaigns at the weekend with new managers after appointing Thomas Frank and Mauricio Pochettino respectively.

Sunderland, wedged between the pair in 19th, have not pressed the panic button and have kept faith with Regis le Bris.

But is it too early to worry? We look at what the stats tell us about relegation battles in the past and what we can learn for the months ahead.

Looking at every Premier League season since it changed to a 20-team league in 1995-96, the current bottom three have cause to be concerned.

In 19 of the previous 31 seasons (61%), only one of the bottom three sides after 10 games managed to avoid relegation.

For fairness and accuracy, the 'bottom three' are the three sides with the worst record after 10 games, not simply those in the relegation zone after matchweek 10, since in some seasons a team had played nine games by that point.

Only in 10 seasons (32%) have two or more of the bottom three stayed up - and that has not happened in any of the past eight campaigns. The last time more than one team avoided the drop was in 2017-18, when all three survived.

That has only happened twice, although only twice have all three sides been relegated.

Overall, of the 93 previous teams in the bottom three after 10 games, 41 survived and 52 were relegated - a survival rate of 44%.

This is the third time that Palace have been bottom after 10 games. On both previous occasions, they did not just survive but thrived, finishing 11th.

They did so under Tony Pulis in 2013-14 and under Roy Hodgson in 2017-18. Both managers, like Frank, were appointed early in the season following poor starts under Ian Holloway and Frank de Boer respectively.

The 2013-14 season is the only previous time that Sunderland and Palace were the bottom two at this stage. The Black Cats also survived thanks to a great, late escape under Gus Poyet.

This is the first time United have been in the bottom three after 10 games of a Premier League season.

Given the size and resources of the club, it is unlikely they will go down, although the team in 18th place at this stage have been relegated in each of the past six seasons.

Fans of all three clubs will hope for a repeat of 2017-18, when not only Palace but the bottom three improved significantly to finish comfortably mid-table.

While history suggests only one of the bottom three will survive, there is also evidence to suggest that this might not be a standard Premier League season.

Palace have seven points, which is the most the bottom side after 10 games have had for 24 years. The bottom three all had eight points in 2002-03.

Sunderland's eight points is also the most that a side in 19th place have had for 16 years. Wolves had nine points in 2010-11 and survived in 17th.

In fact, only five points separate the bottom half of the Premier League, with Tottenham in 11th on 12 points and Palace bottom with seven â the smallest gap at this stage for 24 years.

The highest that a team in the bottom three after 10 games have finished the season is eighth - Bolton in 2003-04, Tottenham in 2008-09 and Everton in 2017-18 - which suggests it will be Conference League at best for Manchester United next season.

Then again, the table is so tight that only nine points separate Chelsea in fifth and Palace - the smallest gap at this stage for 16 years.

Given that fifth place has been enough to qualify for the Champions League in recent seasons, optimistic United fans may not be ruling it out just yet.

That means even sides who have started well could be involved in the relegation battle.

The most points a team have had after 10 games of a 38-game season and been relegated is 14 - Joe Royle's Manchester City in 2000-01.

The most recent team to start well but be relegated were Eddie Howe's Bournemouth in 2019-20, who were sitting pretty in ninth with 13 points at this stage but went down on the final day.

As mentioned, this season has both the 'best' bottom side in terms of points and the most compact bottom half of the table that we have seen at this stage since 2002-03.

In that season, West Ham were relegated on the final day with 42 points - the most a side have been relegated with in Premier League history.

Given the way this season is shaping up, 40 points might also not be enough to survive.
