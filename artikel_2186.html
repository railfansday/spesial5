Pocognoli reveals plan to get best from returning McTominay, and from Scotland

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

As Scott McTominay returns to the Scotland squad, a nation expects. The current Tartan Army talisman was back among the list of names selected by Sebastien Pocognoli yesterday after recovering from a heart issue, and then immediately recovering his goalscoring touch with Napoli.

A disappointing World Cup in the summer, both collectively for Scotland and on a personal level for McTominay, is largely being put down in hindsight to the mild benign arrhythmia that left the midfielder struggling in the sweltering summer heat in the States.

“It was much harder,” McTominay explained after the tournament.

“Two days before the first match I felt unwell and didn’t know whether I’d play or not.

“It was tough to prepare myself mentally. Then, the high temperatures made the situation even more difficult. Looking back, it’s a huge regret of my career.”

Pocognoli, interestingly, has another theory about why McTominay never shone in the way that Scotland had hoped in the World Cup, citing a lack of structure in the team. Regardless, with a corrective ablation procedure safely behind him, McTominay is now back and raring to go, having bagged four goals in seven matches since his return to club football.

But his new manager is hoping that his masterplan can eventually lead to Scotland being a team that consistently carries a threat, rather than one that constantly looks to individuals like McTominay to produce moments of magic.

“It was a big reflection for me over the last weeks, to try and reduce the expectation here on two or three players,” Pocognoli said.

“The first thing is, why? When you have players who have made the difference in most of the games through individual actions or a situation from nowhere, it’s normal that people then expect them to do it in every game.

“But for me, I have to find a way of playing to give Robbo [Andy Robertson] an opportunity to score two or three goals like he nearly did in the last camp. Or to give John McGinn an opportunity to score. The strikers, Billy Gilmour - it’s about giving everyone the chance to shine as a collective.

“That will maybe decrease the expectation of one or two players. That’s something I’d like to try to do.

“It’s normal that people expect Scott to always be the best, if that’s what he’s had to deal with in the past. In the World Cup, it was less because the structure around him was less. But expectation was still big on him, it didn’t matter.

“It’s up to me to have a good structure around him so he can shine, but not only him - all of the players.”

All that being said, Pocognoli is as excited as any member of the Tartan Army to see McTominay back in the dark blue, and to see how he will fit into the system that he has implemented since taking the reins.

“I just want to know him a little bit more, from the personal aspect,” he said.

“We all know his quality. I think he can fit into the way we want to play. So, I’m just excited to see him on the pitch, to see if he can bring something extra in the way we want to play.

“Also, to see how he fits into the group with my own eyes. I want to make a real contact with him because before it has only been on the phone or by video call. I’m looking forward to meeting him.

“He’s a big player. He’s a very complete player on the pitch. Scott is good on the ball, he can infiltrate without the ball. He can press high on the pitch or he can be box-to-box.

“It’s a massive boost for us. My job is to combine his quality with other players around him and I think we will.

“I’ve watched most of his games. His first game back was difficult in the last 20 or 30 minutes which was normal. After that, he started to find his way again. He’s now ready. He’s scored goals playing in a similar shape as us at Scotland.

“It was good to see him higher and lower in that shape on the pitch. Is he hungry? Yeah. He was really disappointed to miss the first camp and was already talking about the next camp.

“At that point we weren’t sure because we had to see his rehabilitation. But he was excited to try his best to reach the November camp and he’s still excited now, which is good.

“He will bring experience, quality, an expectation of goals, a presence in the box - and he’ll bring leadership as part of the captain’s group.

“He has a strong voice. He’s a different character to the others but it’s up to me to produce the game plan for him and the players that allows him to shine, and not him to make the team shine, if you know what I mean? That’s the challenge.”
