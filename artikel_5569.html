Thieves stole $11.4 million in Nvidia hardware from Oakland shipment

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Six pallets bound for Fort Worth, Texas, disappeared after leaving Oakland on Aug. 29, and police have announced no arrests

Oakland police are investigating the theft of $11.4 million in Nvidia $NVDA hardware that disappeared after leaving Oakland for Fort Worth, Texas, on Aug. 29, according to ABC News.

The shipment consisted of six pallets. A truck linked to a food-hauling company collected the cargo on schedule, and investigators later lost its trail in San Bernardino County, California, according to ABC News.

The truck stopped repeatedly for hours at a stretch once the hardware was aboard, according to Ars Technica. The buyers realized in early September that the shipment had failed to arrive.

Oakland police said in a statement, according to ABC News: "The Oakland Police Department is investigating a grand theft that occurred in East Oakland. The victim reported the crime to OPD on Sept. 13, 2026." Authorities have announced no arrests, and the investigation is continuing.

Authorities have not disclosed the type of Nvidia hardware that was taken or the method the thieves used, according to Ars Technica.

Olaf Groth, a professor at UC Berkeley's Haas School of Business who studies artificial intelligence and emerging technology, told ABC News that Nvidia chips draw strong demand. "There's lots and lots of incentives to hijack those chips anywhere where security is weak," Groth said.

Groth said the chips sell for about double their U.S. price outside the country. "So, if a chip costs about $550,000, then they go for about $1 million or more abroad," he said, according to ABC News.

The Oakland case follows other attempts to steal Nvidia cargo in the Bay Area, according to ABC News. Police in Fremont and Union City looked into break-ins last year that targeted Nvidia cargo worth over $7.5 million. In a separate attempted theft in September, suspects who thought the trucks they were after held Nvidia technology instead found 40,000 pounds of sand, according to police cited by ABC News.

Thieves have taken upward of $100 million worth of cargo bound for data centers off the highways since June. Targeted goods include bitcoin mining rigs, servers, memory chips and fiber-optic cabling. In one of the largest heists, the thieves falsified tracking coordinates so electronic surveillance would not catch them.

The Oakland theft adds to a surge of hardware heists, according to Ars Technica. Ars Technica said thieves can dismantle the stolen components for resale on gray markets, or send them to countries where U.S. technology hardware may be off-limits.
