Leaks have become their own subculture, drawing both fans and detractors of any given property to catch fresh news weeks before its intended release date. This can create not only an incredibly toxic hype cycle, but also spawn small cults of personality around the leakers themselves. Those following the leaks will often entertain wild demands to keep them coming, as long as the request isn't ridiculous.

One such leak saga began in 2022, when Arion Kurtaj, a hacker from Oxford, England, cracked Grand Theft Auto 6 far before its intended advertising date. Kurtaj is back in the news now, days before GTA6’s release, pleading guilty to all charges brought by Rockstar. Facing an estimated $5 million in damages, Kurtaj might just get the book tossed at him.

Kurtaj was the very first to break all kinds of news surrounding Grand Theft Auto 6, releasing info not only on the source code of the game but also on major narrative focuses. This leak was actually some of the first news we got about Grand Theft Auto 6’s female protagonist, Lucia, a major advertising angle Rockstar was banking on to surprise fans.

From an anonymous account, Arion demanded a ransom from Rockstar, who entirely stonewalled him. This led to him releasing more and more clips, amassing around an hour of gameplay, alongside chunks of the early source code. Luckily, not enough for any kind of major reverse engineering effort, but just enough to cause Rockstar an even bigger headache.

Rockstar was far from the only target of Kurtaj’s efforts, with previous successful hacks on Uber and various individual scam projects giving him a layer of infamy long before he tried to expose Rockstar’s pet project. In fact, Kurtaj’s hack on Rockstar reportedly happened while he was in a hotel under protective custody.

While under police protection from a number of rival hackers aiming to get back at him, Kurtaj claims to have hacked Rockstar using an Amazon Fire Stick active on his hotel TV. He then entered Rockstar’s internal Slack message boards to demand repayment, only to be met with silence.

Kurtaj’s guilty plea also saw him accept responsibility for numerous other cyber attacks undertaken by both himself and members of a hacker group he worked with, leaving his individual sentencing for a later date. As it stands, Rockstar seems to want to make a bit of an example of him in order to dissuade any further hacks, both from new faces and those still at large.

These hacks, while centralized on big projects and gathering info to share with the public, are far from victimless crimes. Even ignoring the chaos hacks like Kurtaj’s cause on a corporate scale, various previous hacks throughout the gaming industry have managed to target both players’ and developers’ personal information.

This easily crosses a line from inconvenient to straight-up malicious, forcing teams to take strides to protect themselves and players while throwing production pipelines wholly out of whack. The gaming industry’s hard reliance on tech makes it uniquely susceptible to this kind of cyber attack. Cybersecurity has become a major talking point as such, making any attempt to slow down these leaks.

Even with launch right around the corner, hackers are surely eager to pick apart both the game and Rockstar itself for any kind of post-launch content on the horizon. Whether it's in the form of expansions or the upcoming multiplayer segment of Grand Theft Auto 6, Rockstar can’t rest on their laurels quite yet; as much as they may have earned the break, launch just means a different kind of headache.
