German Museum Transforms Permanent Collection Galleries into Gym to Attract Youth

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

We’ve seen dance and musical performances in museums, as well as yoga classes, but a full array of gym equipment and gear? Beginning earlier this month and running through February 21, 2027, the Kunstpalast in Düsseldorf, Germany, has set up weight machines, mats, treadmills, ellipticals, and all manner of gym equipment in its permanent exhibition galleries, so that visitors can work out beside the historic and contemporary artworks, reported the Times of London. Whether or not participants are able to take in the art hanging in front of them while bench pressing, is anyone’s guess, but the idea has its fans, some of whom show up to the museum appropriately dressed in workout clothes. What artworks do these health-conscious visitors get to enjoy while sweating away? The program titled “Gym. Fit with Art” unfolds across 20 stations beside the museum’s collection of over 800 works, which span pieces by Peter Paul Rubens to Gerhard Richter. It was conceived as a tribute marking the centenary of the GeSoLei, which was the largest trade fair held during the Weimar Republic, which was also dedicated to health.

“We want to target people who might prefer going to the gym rather than a museum on weekends,” and this particularly includes younger audiences, said Alina Fuchte, who has led the project. To wit, “the museum is transforming its collection into an extraordinary fitness circuit,” states the museum website. “Instead of just strolling from painting to painting and admiring the art, visitors will soon be able to get active,” it adds. The endeavor is sponsored by TARGOBANK in cooperation with Technogym, which makes fitness equipment, according to the website.

“Anyone who would rather simply take in the art is equally welcome,” continues the website. This, however, begs the question of whether that simple act may be tough to do while standing beside someone jogging on a treadmill in front of large busts of Johann Wilhelm, Elector Palatine, and his wife, Anna Maria Luisa de’Medici, per just a few examples of the works on view.

One visitor, Kai Heimes, 25, tells the Times of London, “It’s cool to work out in the collection,” and if it feels “awkward” for some, “I don’t think it’s a big deal,” he added.

However, another, Moritz Rossmanith, 40, admitted to being “a bit confused by it all.” Rossmanith was visiting from Rome, and thought it was “very strange to see kids holding thick barbells while there’s old Italian art on the wall.” Children are reportedly the biggest fans of the gym gear.

As for bringing water bottles to the museum-gym, given that many other museums sternly scold visitors for taking covert sips of water inside art galleries, that rule doesn’t seem to apply here.

“Coming here and realizing this is also a space for me, I can feel comfortable here, I can be free here, I can try new things—that, for me, is the first step,” said Fuchte.
