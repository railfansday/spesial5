India: Umar Khalid, Sharjeel Imam go back to Supreme Court for bail

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Umar Khalid and Sharjeel Imam have asked the Supreme Court for bail once more, having spent over six years in jail. Elsewhere, the top court has clarified when the 24-hour clock for producing an arrested person starts.

Here is a roundup of the biggest headlines from across India on Friday, November 6.

Delhi's air edged toward the "severe" category on Friday, two days before Diwali. The overall Air Quality Index (AQI) stood at 391, and 30 of the city's 59 monitoring stations were already in the severe band, news agency PTI reported. India's pollution board classifies readings above 400 as severe.

A government-backed early-warning system expects severe air quality from Friday through the major festival of Diwali on Sunday. Mahesh Palawat of Skymet told PTI that weakening winds could trap pollutants from Monday.

The Supreme Court took up the crisis Friday in a case on pollution in the capital region. Chief Justice Surya Kant opened by saying: "I hope you all are enjoying the weather in Delhi," local media reported.

The bench pointed to vehicles as a major cause. "Look at the roads in Delhi. Look at the roads connecting Delhi to Noida… the kind of bumper-to-bumper traffic… That's the major cause," the court said.

The chief justice added that "AQI is already touching 400."

Amicus curiae Aparajita Singh told the court that breathing the region's air is like smoking 30 cigarettes a day and that crop burning in nearby states accounts for 14% of Delhi's pollution.

India's Supreme Court has ruled that police must bring a person before a magistrate within 24 hours of first detaining them, not 24 hours from when an arrest is formally recorded, local media reported.

The constitution requires anyone held by police to be presented to a magistrate within a day. Reports suggest that police have often kept people in informal "detention" for hours or even days before logging an arrest, which delays that clock.

Justices Ujjal Bhuyan and Atul S. Chandurkar criticized the practice as unconstitutional.

"An artificial distinction between 'arrest' and 'detention' would be highly problematic," the court said, as reported by Live Law.

The case had been brought to the court after four people were detained for some 18 hours before formally being arrested. The Supreme Court ordered their immediate release.

Going forward, anyone held beyond 24 hours without being brought before a magistrate must be freed, and only travel time to court may be excluded, the court ruled.

Magistrates must record when a person was actually detained and may refuse to extend custody if the detention has been unreasonably long. The court ordered the judgment be sent to state police chiefs.

National Human Rights Commission data show 11,656 deaths in police or jail custody between 2016 and 2022, the Telegraph reported.

Activists Umar Khalid and Sharjeel Imam approached the Supreme Court on Thursday for bail once again in the 2020 Delhi riots "larger conspiracy" case.

They are challenging a September 30 Delhi High Court order that rejected their pleas, Bar and Bench reported. The pleas had not yet been listed for hearing.

The High Court held that judicial discipline bound it to the Supreme Court's January 5 order and that it could not alter the terms set there. In that order, the top court refused bail to the two while granting relief to five co-accused, saying they "stand on a qualitatively different footing as compared to other accused," local media reported.

The ruling allowed them to reapply once the protected witnesses had been examined or a year had passed, whichever came first.

Imam says he has spent six years and nine months in jail as an undertrial without the prosecution examining any of its 47 protected witnesses, Live Law reported. He argues the witness condition is therefore unlikely to be met in time.

The pleas also say over 900 witnesses are yet to be examined and that continued imprisonment violates the right to life and liberty under Article 21.

On May 18, another Supreme Court bench criticized the January order, observing that "bail is the rule and jail is an exception" even under the anti-terror law UAPA.

Delhi Police have described the two as "masterminds" of the violence that took place in February 2020. However, nearly seven years later, the trial has not yet begun.
