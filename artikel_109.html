Fred Nile, former NSW politician and figurehead of the rightwing Christian Democratic party, dies aged 92

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Controversial figure frequently took positions against drug use, abortion and homosexuality, but in later years supported legislation expunging homosexual convictions

The former New South Wales MP and longstanding figurehead of the rightwing Christian Democratic party, the Rev Fred Nile, has died aged 92.

Nile was a member of the NSW legislative council for more than 40 years. He first entered the state’s upper house in 1981 and retired after unsuccessfully contesting the 2023 election.

Nile was a controversial figure; a standard-bearer for Christian and social conservative politics since long before the formation of the Australian Christian Lobby and other faith-based political movements.

“It is with deep sadness the Nile family announces the passing of Rev. the Hon. Frederick John Nile who passed away peacefully on the 21st of November at the age of 92,” his family said in a statement.

“Rev. Nile is survived by his wife, children, grandchildren and great grandchildren.

“Details about a service or memorial will be shared soon.”

Nile, an ordained Congregational Union minister, and the political party he led for decades frequently took positions against drug use, abortion and homosexuality.

Alex Greenwich, the member for Sydney, said that despite disagreeing on “a lot” with Nile, the pair had a good working relationship, particularly in relation to protecting First Nations cultural sites.

“Rev. Nile also played a key role in passing legislation for the expungement of prior homosexual convictions, landmark laws that cleared the names of hundreds of gay men,” Greenwich said.

“Rev. Nile played the ball, not the person. His opposition to many of the reforms I delivered came from his faith, and he didn’t engage in the personalised vitriol that comes from many of today’s right-wing politicians.”

Greenwich said he had asked the premier, Chris Minns, for a state funeral for Nile.

Lyle Shelton, who had initially been chosen by Nile as his successor to lead the Christian Democratic party in 2021 before the pair had a falling out, said Nile “stood in the public square for Christian ethics and moral virtues at time when few would”.

“Cruelly caricatured by his opponents, anyone who met him knew the caricature of the mockers did not fit,” Shelton said.
