This 377-Square-Foot Island Getaway in Denmark Is a Picture-Perfect Pint-Sized Retreat

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

A mini vacation home in Zealand, Denmark, is a particularly appealing retreat, no matter the season. With its rugged coastlines, forests, and small villages, the western side of Zealand offers an ideal setting for a break from everyday life. Nestled among trees is a tiny house that was designed to be as unobtrusive as possible. Its compact shape, natural color palette, and large windows allow it to blend almost seamlessly into the forest. Here, nature isn’t just right outside the door—it’s always present inside as well.

Surrounded by trees, this small wooden house in Denmark blends seamlessly with nature.

Named the EcoNeo House, the home was designed by the Danish architectural firm SAGA Space Architects, both as a family home and as a prototype for tiny house-style vacation homes. Although originally conceived as a summer house, the cottage has been insulated and heated, allowing it to be used even during colder weather. At only 377 square feet, it combines the essential features of a vacation home, sleeping up to six people. The design prioritizes a thoughtful use of the available space and forward-thinking architecture that blends into the landscape.

The spacious terrace is perfect for sunbathing and dining outdoors, especially in the summer.

Inside, the living and dining areas flow seamlessly into one another. Thanks to the large windows that offer views of the surrounding trees, there’s a connection between indoors and outdoors. The kitchen is set slightly apart, but opens up into the dining area, blending smoothly into the floor plan. Designed with functionality in mind, it’s still fully equipped with all the necessary appliances.

The dining area flows seamlessly into the kitchen. Additional storage space was created below the stairs on the left, which lead to the sleeping lofts.

Instead of breaking up the floor plan with additional walls, the architects made use of the home’s height: Stairs next to the kitchen lead to two sleeping lofts that offer a private retreat for up to four people. Storage was also a priority in the design. Items not needed on a daily basis can be stowed under the stairs, a clever solution that again makes the most of the small space.

Large windows let in plenty of natural light and create a connection between indoors and outdoors. Despite its compact size, the kitchen has all the necessary appliances.

There’s even a sauna, which can be converted into a sleeping space for two additional guests if needed. The generous window front offers a view of the surrounding landscape, while a cold plunge pool on the terrace is waiting for a refreshing dip afterward.

Through the sauna’s large window, you can enjoy the surrounding nature even while relaxing. It’s an appealing retreat, especially on chilly days. There’s also a cold plunge pool on the terrace.

The choice of materials complements the understated architecture. The floor’s wide Douglas fir planks introduce a warm color and vibrant grain. The use of wood is consistently carried through to other areas of the house, creating a visual connection between the living space and the sauna. Natural materials are also used in the bathroom, including Kolmården marble, a Swedish stone distinguished by its characteristic green hue.

Wood defines the interior and lends the rooms a natural warmth. Large windows bring the surrounding nature into the home, while the dining area is spacious even within the compact layout.

Together with the stones and the greenery outside, the result is a design that deliberately avoids contrasts and excess. “When designing the summer house, every detail was intended to reflect the harmony between sustainable innovation and timeless craftsmanship,” explains Niklas Munk-Andersen, architect and partner at SAGA Space Architects.

Beanbag chairs provide extra seating and blend harmoniously into the interior with their colors.

The Douglas fir floorboards also give the clean design a natural, tactile quality. The material is more than a surface design choice, instead it shapes the way that the rooms feel.

The bathroom features natural materials and Swedish marble finishes.

The sauna can also be converted into a sleeping area for two additional guests.

The compact design also has a positive impact on the home’s carbon footprint. According to calculations by SAGA Space Architects, the EcoNeo House produces approximately 386 pounds of CO₂ annually, whereas an average vacation home for six people is estimated to emit about 2,282 pounds. This means emissions are approximately 83% lower. In addition to the small living area, bio-based materials are the primary factor contributing to this difference.

The boundary between indoors and outdoors blurs, bringing nature into focus.

The architects also designed the construction process to be as efficient as possible by digitally planning the house, precisely prefabricating its individual components, and then assembling them on-site in a modular fashion. This approach not only simplified assembly but also minimized the impact on the site.

Thanks to its small footprint, this vacation home’s emissions are 83% lower than those of the average vacation home that can accommodate six people. In addition, the modular construction minimizes the impact on the natural environment.

The EcoNeo House demonstrates that sustainable construction isn’t just a matter of the materials used. The size of a building and how the available space is utilized also play a crucial role. Though it may sound small, 377 square feet can be perfectly sufficient for a getaway home—provided the floor plan, storage space, and amenities are all carefully designed.

Although primarily intended as a summer home, this small vacation home can also be enjoyed in colder months.
