Uber is testing rides for kids as young as 10

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Getting kids where they need to go is a job in itself. Between school, extracurriculars, and appointments, families often piece together rides with help from relatives and other parents.

Uber is testing whether it can take on some of that driving.

The company's new pilot, Uber Kids, lets parents and guardians book rides for children ages 10 to 12 to take on their own. The service is available to select families in Austin, Texas, Nashville, Tennessee, and Madison, Wisconsin, starting Nov. 19. This comes after some rumors: Bloomberg first reported in August that Uber was developing the option.

Here's what parents should know before booking, from how to set up a ride to how to follow it in real time.

To book a ride, a parent or guardian first adds their child to the Family hub in the Uber app. Then they select the child as the rider. If the pilot is available to their family, Uber Kids will appear as a ride option, and the app will guide them through adding pickup and drop-off details.

Kids don't need their own Uber account or app. They can get updates about the trip by text at the number listed on their profile. Parents can request rides from 5 a.m. to 9 p.m. daily, as long as a driver is available.

Each ride goes straight to one destination. A parent or the adult handling pickup can ride along, and kids ages 10 to 17 can join with their guardian’s permission, as long as the child the ride was booked for is in the car. And, if no Uber Kids driver is available, a child ages 10 to 12 needs an adult to ride with them in a regular Uber.

A parent or another adult selected in the app must be at pickup to give the driver the PIN that starts the ride. If someone else needs to handle pickup or drop-off after booking, the guardian contacts the driver and sends the new adult the trip details and tracking link. Only the guardian can change the destination in the app.

Once the car is moving, the guardian can track it on a map, contact the driver, or tap "Watch" to see live video from the driver's phone. The driver gets a notification when the feed starts, and only the guardian and safety agents can watch it. Uber can flag an unexpected stop or route change so a safety agent can check in. Kids can also call their guardian or the support number in their trip texts. At drop-off, the driver confirms the child reached the entrance or met the adult assigned to greet them. If that person hasn't arrived, the driver tries to reach them or the guardian, then calls Uber's safety line if nobody answers.

The app also makes a separate audio and video recording. Neither the guardian nor the driver can access it. Uber says it receives the encrypted recording only if someone reports a safety incident. Otherwise, it’s deleted after 14 days.

Uber says it invites experienced, highly rated drivers who have passed a background check. Drivers have to complete a training module on how Uber Kids trips work and turn on audio and video recording in the Driver app. Uber's driver guide explains how drivers should handle pickup and drop-off.

It's interesting that Uber is expanding to younger riders as it faces thousands of lawsuits from passengers who say they were sexually assaulted by drivers.

As of Nov. 2, 4,548 cases were pending in the coordinated federal litigation, according to the U.S. Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation. In February, a federal jury in Arizona found Uber liable and awarded $8.5 million to a woman who said a driver raped her when she was 19. And in April, a North Carolina jury awarded $5,000 in a related case after finding that a driver committed battery. (Uber settled another federal case in October before it went to trial.)

Uber has been giving teens a way to get around on their own since 2023. Kids ages 13 to 17 can request rides through an account linked to a parent's account, and Lyft offers a similar service in select areas called "Lyft Teen." When a child in the Uber Kids pilot turns 13, they can set up a teen account and request their own ride (or their parents can still do it for them).

Families with younger kids already have options like HopSkipDrive, which takes riders as young as 6. The company says its drivers need at least five years of caregiving experience and must pass a fingerprint-based background check. There's no saying whether Uber utilizes similar investigations.

For plenty of families, getting to practice or a lesson still means a parent does the driving. In a 2025 survey by AP-NORC and HopSkipDrive, 75 percent of parents whose kids participated in extracurriculars said they drove their kids themselves. When asked about the school commute, 28 percent of K–12 parents said they considered a hired ride, like a taxi, Uber, or Lyft, at least somewhat safe for their child.

This pilot will ultimately test whether features like a PIN at pickup and live video during the ride make parents comfortable enough to book one.
