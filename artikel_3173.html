Intelligent Protection (IPM) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Procurement fueled a 75.5% revenue jump, but margins still lagged.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

Intelligent Protection Management Corp. (IPM -2.96%) reported a 75.5% increase in third quarter revenue, primarily driven by a rise in procurement activity related to infrastructure investments. Management indicated that the company is executing a strategy built around six complementary service lines including managed IT security, private cloud hosting, and disaster recovery. The company achieved positive adjusted EBITDA for the quarter and generated $5.6 million in operating cash flow. Management stated that the business is focused on converting one-time procurement and professional services engagements into long-term recurring revenue through its managed services platform.

Jason Katz: Thank you, and good afternoon, everyone. Third quarter was a strong one for IPM as we continue to build momentum across our core business while advancing key strategic initiatives that position us for long-term growth. Exceptional customer demand and continued execution of our growth strategy drove total revenue up more than 75% to approximately $11 million compared with the third quarter of 2025. For the first 9 months of the year, revenue grew 36% to $23.8 million compared to the prior year. The primary driver was procurement. Procurement revenue rose approximately 271% during the quarter compared to the prior year period as customers accelerated infrastructure investments in cybersecurity, cloud modernization, data center expansion and artificial intelligence.

Additionally, I'm pleased to report that we successfully fulfilled a significant customer order during the third quarter, further demonstrating our ability to execute on large-scale opportunities and meet customer demand. Our strategy is to build IPM around 6 complementary service lines: managed IT security services, secure private cloud hosting, managed backup and disaster recovery, professional services, procurement and web hosting. Together, these offerings connect the infrastructure customers invest in today with the protection, resilience and ongoing support they need over time. Our focus is not simply on supplying technology, but on becoming a long-term partner in how customers secure, operate and recover their IT environments. Procurement generally carries lower gross margins than our managed services, cybersecurity and cloud infrastructure offerings.

So profitability did not rise at the same rate as revenue. At the same time, procurement can provide an entry point for broader customer relationships. Our professional services help customers plan their technology investments. Our procurement services source the infrastructure. And our managed IT security services, private cloud and managed backup and disaster recovery services create opportunities to support those environments over time. Our objective is to convert these opportunities into recurring engagements, recognizing that conversion is not automatic and depends on customer needs and execution. We are making progress in expanding our recurring business. Core managed services revenue from nonaffiliated customers increased 33% over the prior year period.

That result supports our focus on deepening customer relationships and diversifying our revenue base. We remain focused on expanding higher-margin recurring engagements across our service portfolio, while continuing to execute on professional services and procurement opportunities. Year-to-date, we have made significant operational progress as we continue to execute against our strategic priorities. We launched IPM GO, your always-available AI virtual assistant that is hosted within IPM's private cloud infrastructure, enabling clients to deploy, manage and scale web-based engagement solutions related to customer sales and support interactions. We added a dedicated sales territory covering the Phoenix metropolitan area and broader West Coast market, bringing an established customer base and industry expertise.

We extended our Phoenix data center colocation agreement through August of 2032. We entered into a strategic collaboration with an AI platform designed to reduce the cost, complexity and time of developing small and medium AI models. We achieved SOC 2 Type 1 compliance, a key milestone in safeguarding customer data. We repurchased 50,000 shares of common stock during the first quarter of 2026 under our stock repurchase plan for an aggregate of $83,491. As of September 30, 2026, all shares of common stock available for repurchase under the plan had been repurchased, and the plan has expired.

Subsequent to quarter end, in October, we entered into a unit purchase agreement to acquire AdTech IT LLC, a subsidiary of Advanced Technology Distributors through a 2-step closing process. AdTech has built a managed services business with long-standing customer relationships and recurring revenue model that aligns with our strategy. Once completed, we believe the transaction can expand our customer reach and create opportunities to introduce IPM's managed IT security services, private cloud, managed backup and disaster recovery, professional services and procurement offerings across a larger combined customer base. These service lines address different but connected customer priorities. Managed IT security services combines monitoring and remediation to help customers manage cyber risk. Private cloud provides infrastructure hosted and managed by IPM.

Managed backup and disaster recovery supports data protection and recovery planning. And professional services help align technology decisions with business priorities, while procurement enables customers to source the equipment needed to implement those plans. By combining these capabilities with AdTech managed services expertise, we believe we can offer customers a broader, more coordinated approach to their technology needs. The strategic rationale for the acquisition is straightforward: expand our capabilities, deepen customer relationships and build more opportunities for recurring services. Our focus will be on disciplined execution and on translating those opportunities into customer value rather than assuming that a larger platform alone will deliver growth.

In October, IPM's managed security services provider, or MSSP, practice moved quickly to help protect managed clients from the critical PHP2Shell vulnerabilities, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, which CISA added to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog following evidence of active exploitation and warned it could enable unauthenticated remote code execution in PHP environments. Our teams prioritized identification and remediation of affected systems, including patching and backported security fixes where appropriate, while using additional protective controls such as web application firewall protection for certain legacy assets. This response reflects IPM's MSSP strategy of combining rapid vulnerability remediation, layered security controls and continuous monitoring to reduce cyber risk for the organizations we protect.

Our balance sheet remains strong with $12.5 million in cash and cash equivalents and no long-term debt at quarter end, giving us flexibility to pursue both organic growth and strategic opportunities with disciplined capital allocation. With that, I'll turn the call over to our CFO, Kara Jenny.

Kara Jenny: Thank you, Jason, and good afternoon. For the third quarter ended September 30, 2026, total revenue was $10.95 million, up 75.5% from $6.2 million a year ago. By component, procurement revenue was $6.3 million, up 271%. Managed IT technology revenue was $3.9 million, up 1.8%. Core managed IT revenue, which consists of revenue from our managed IT security services and managed backup and disaster recovery solutions from nonaffiliated customers, grew 33%. Professional services revenue were $545,000, up 14.5%. And subscription revenue was $244,000, down 10.4%. Third quarter revenue growth was driven by both new customer wins and the expansion of services provided to existing customers as well as increased procurement revenue from the sale of AI-related equipment.

This revenue included the fulfillment of a significant customer order that was previously discussed in our second quarter update. Loss from operations for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026, improved 38% to $886,000, which included approximately $195,000 of acquisition-related expenses incurred in connection with the AdTech acquisition, from a loss of $1.4 million. Net loss narrowed to $779,000 from a loss of $1.1 million. Adjusted EBITDA was positive $159,000 compared with negative $260,000 in the prior year quarter. Cash provided by operations in the third quarter ended September 30, 2026, was $5.6 million, an increase of $5.5 million from $133,000, primarily reflecting product sourcing tied to procurement activity during the quarter.

Deferred revenue stood at $4.9 million as of September 30, 2026, which we expect to recognize in future quarters as product and services are installed. For the 9 months ended September 30, 2026, total revenue rose 36% to $23.8 million. Managed IT revenue grew 6.7% to $11.6 million and procurement grew 158% to $10 million. Loss from operations improved 19% to $3.2 million. Net loss was $2.8 million compared with a loss of $1.3 million. Adjusted EBITDA improved to negative $536,000 from negative $1.1 million. And operating cash flow was $4.9 million, an increase of $3.9 million versus $1.0 million in the comparable 9-month period.

We ended the third quarter with $12.5 million in cash, representing an increase of $5 million or 67% compared to $7.5 million at June 30, 2026. The company finished the third quarter with no long-term debt. That concludes my review. I will turn the call back to Jason.

Jason Katz: Thank you, Kara. Our priorities remain clear: grow recurring revenue and customer lifetime value through managed IT security services, private cloud and managed backup and disaster recovery; use professional services and procurement to address customer needs and create opportunities for broader engagements; pursue strategic acquisitions that enhance our capabilities and market reach; and maintain a strong balance sheet. This was a quarter of meaningful progress. We grew revenue by more than 75%, narrowed our net loss to $779,000, generated $5.6 million in operating cash flow and reported positive adjusted EBITDA of $159,000. Core managed services revenue from nonaffiliated customers grew 33%.

Our year-to-date operational milestones, including the launch of IPM GO and subsequent to quarter end, we signed an agreement to acquire AdTech. These developments support a consistent strategy. We use professional services and procurement to help customers build their technology environments and expand the managed IT security services, private cloud and managed backup and disaster recovery relationships to support those environments over time. Looking ahead, our goal remains to achieve positive adjusted EBITDA for the fourth quarter. Our $4.9 million in deferred revenue is expected to be recognized as products and services are installed. Our new West Coast territory creates opportunities to reach additional customers. And once completed, the AdTech acquisition should broaden our capabilities and cross-selling opportunities.

Across these initiatives, our focus is on converting infrastructure projects into long-term, higher-margin recurring relationships. We believe our balance sheet and service capabilities position us to pursue that objective while recognizing that the timing and results will depend on customer demand and execution. To our customers, thank you for trusting us with your most critical technology. Your confidence drives everything we do.

Joe Diaz: Thank you for those comments, Jason. Operator, we will now turn to the questions submitted in advance of today's call. I'll direct the first question to Jason. Procurement drove significant growth this quarter. Beyond the immediate revenue, how can a large procurement win translate into recurring managed services, cybersecurity, cloud and backup revenue for IPM?

Jason Katz: We view procurement services as much more than a transaction. It can be an entry point into a much broader customer relationship. When we help a customer design and source a meaningful technology deployment, we gain a detailed understanding of their environment, their security requirements and where they are going strategically. That naturally creates opportunities for professional services, managed IT security services, secure private cloud hosting, managed backup and disaster recovery and ongoing support. It can also work in the opposite direction. When we win a managed IT services customer, that relationship often generates professional services and procurement services opportunities as we build out the customers' technology road map.

So the real opportunity is the life cycle of the customer, not any single transaction. We don't assume that every procurement engagement will convert into recurring revenue, but having that trusted relationship gives us an opportunity to earn a larger share of the customers' technology spend over time. That is the model we are focused on.

Joe Diaz: We'll next go to Jared. Core managed services revenue from nonaffiliated customers grew 33% year-over-year. What's driving that growth? And what are you seeing in the pipeline, Jared?

Jared Mills: The 33% growth is really coming from 2 places: adding new managed IT services customers and expanding the number of services we provide to existing customers. We have been deliberately targeting highly regulated markets where our security, compliance and infrastructure experience is particularly relevant, while also going back into our existing customer base with additional managed services, cybersecurity, cloud and backup solutions. We are encouraged by the pipeline. Earlier in the year, we were seeing specific traction in legal and finance and a strong pipeline across health care, energy, private equity, manufacturing and retail customers. So while procurement services revenue can move around quarter-to-quarter, what we are most focused on is building a recurring revenue base.

We believe the 33% increase is a good indication that the strategy is gaining traction, and we believe there is a meaningful runway ahead of us.

Joe Diaz: In that regard, Jared, what differentiates IPM when competing against traditional managed service providers, cybersecurity firms and large cloud providers?

Jared Mills: I think the biggest difference is that we don't approach the customer through a single product or service. We can sit with a customer as a technology adviser, help them design the architecture, procure the infrastructure, secure it, host it in a private or hybrid cloud, manage it and protect it with backup and disaster recovery. That gives the customer one partner across a much larger portion of the technology life cycle. Second, we have a real depth in highly regulated industries. Our experience includes banking and financial services, health care, legal, merchant processing and other industries where reliability, security and compliance are fundamental requirements rather than add-ons.

We also operate our secure private cloud from private suites leased within U.S.-based Tier 3 data centers alongside our managed IT security services. And finally, we differentiate on service. We're not trying to replace personalized support with automation. The model is highly consultative and white glove. We believe we combine large provider capabilities with the responsiveness and customer intimacy you generally associate with a smaller specialist. And that combination is difficult to replicate.

Joe Diaz: Jared, so what are customers telling you today about cybersecurity, private cloud and AI that is different from what they were asking for a year ago? What's changed?

Jared Mills: The conversation has become much more integrated. A year ago, customers often approached cybersecurity, cloud infrastructure and AI as separate initiatives. Today, those conversations are increasingly converging around one question: how do I modernize the business without losing control of my data, increasing risk or creating unpredictable costs? On cybersecurity, customers increasingly expect continuous protection rather than periodic projects. As we discussed in our prepared remarks, a good recent example is our response to the critical PHP vulnerabilities. Our managed IT security services team moved quickly to help protect managed clients by prioritizing identification and remediation of potentially affected systems and applying additional security controls where appropriate.

That is the type of proactive capability customers increasingly expect from their technology partner. In cloud infrastructure, we're seeing greater interest in understanding where private, dedicated private, hybrid and public cloud each make economic and operational sense rather than simply taking a move-everything-to-the-public-cloud approach. Our secure private cloud hosting strategy is particularly relevant because it gives customers another option around data control, security, performance and cost. AI has probably changed the conversation the most. Customers are moving from asking what can AI do to how do I deploy AI securely against real company data. We are receiving requests from managed clients about bringing tools such as Microsoft 365 Copilot and other AI capabilities into their business.

And the conversation quickly becomes one of governance, data loss prevention, compliance and control. That fits very naturally with our managed IT security services and secure private cloud hosting capabilities. It is also why we launched IPM GO, our always-available AI virtual assistant hosted within IPM's private cloud infrastructure, which enables clients to deploy, manage and scale web-based engagement solutions related to customer sales and support interactions.

Joe Diaz: And Jason, what specifically attracted IPM to AdTech? And where do you see the greatest opportunity to create value from this acquisition?

Jason Katz: What attracted us to AdTech was very straightforward. It is a long-standing managed services business with recurring revenue, established customer relationships and a strong presence in California. That aligns extremely well with the business we are building at IPM. We believe the biggest value creation opportunity from the pending AdTech acquisition will be cross-selling. AdTech brings relationships with small and medium-sized businesses that already rely on them as a trusted technology partner. Following the final closing, we believe we can bring IPM's managed IT security services, secure private cloud hosting, managed backup and disaster recovery, professional services and procurement services capabilities to those relationships.

At the same time, we expect the acquisition to expand our geographic reach and give the combined organization a larger customer base across which to sell complementary solutions. So we don't view AdTech simply as buying revenue and EBITDA. We view it as an opportunity to add a recurring customer platform where we believe the combined company can provide more services per customer, deepen those relationships and increase customer lifetime value over time. That is exactly the type of acquisition that fits our strategy of building a scaled managed technology services platform.
