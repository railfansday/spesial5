Two Bitcoin miners reported nearly $2 billion in profit. The biggest boost wasn’t cash

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Core Scientific and TeraWulf’s reported profits include large non-cash gains. Their operating results and construction cash flows tell different stories.

Core Scientific and TeraWulf reported nearly $1.9 billion of combined third-quarter profit as they shifted from Bitcoin mining toward data-center hosting for AI customers. Their earnings included $1.76 billion of pretax non-cash gains from revaluing financial instruments.

The November 5 Core Scientific disclosure and November 6 TeraWulf disclosure show different operating outcomes for the quarter ended September 30, 2026. Core earned $72 million in operating income under U.S. accounting rules; TeraWulf recorded an $88 million operating loss. Core's operating result itself included a $19 million digital-asset fair-value gain. Hosting now supplies most of both companies' revenue, following their shift from Bitcoin mining toward computing leases. Assessing whether the conversions fund themselves requires the separate cash-flow and financing disclosures.

Core’s $1,178 million consolidated net income and TeraWulf’s $710 million add up to approximately $1.89 billion.

In Core's filing, the $1,034 million revaluation gain comprised $964 million on legacy Tranche 1 and Tranche 2 warrants, $69 million on the liability portion of an AMD warrant and $1 million on contingent value rights. Legacy instruments supplied the largest component of Core's gain.

Warrants give holders rights to buy shares. Core's AMD warrant accompanied July customer leases, with its issuance value recorded as an incentive reducing future lease revenue. Its liability portion is remeasured through earnings. Core attributed changes in its broader warrant and contingent-value line primarily to stock-price changes.

TeraWulf's $728 million gain came from revaluing Google warrants issued in exchange for credit support covering certain Fluidstack lease obligations. The credit support can help finance projects. Remeasurement raises reported earnings through a non-cash accounting entry.

The revaluation gains enter earnings before tax, while the net-income figures also reflect tax effects and other gains. Core also recorded a $106.5 million tax benefit, primarily from releasing part of a valuation allowance associated with its Polaris acquisition. TeraWulf recorded a $95 million gain on selling its Abernathy joint-venture stake.

Core Scientific and TeraWulf reported $1.89bn in consolidated Q3 net income alongside $1.76bn of pretax non-cash revaluation gains.

Legacy warrants supplied most of Core's $1.034bn pretax revaluation gain. Its AMD warrant is a separate lease-linked instrument.

Google warrants supporting Fluidstack leases produced TeraWulf's $728.4m non-cash gain. Credit support and cash proceeds are distinct.

The difference also reaches per-share results: Core reported $3.42 basic earnings versus $0.47 diluted, while TeraWulf reported $1.42 basic earnings and a $0.03 diluted loss. The diluted calculations include warrant-gain reversals in the earnings used per share, alongside share-count adjustments.

Hosting growth is visible in both companies' sales. Core's colocation revenue reached $215 million out of $233 million in total sales, up from $137 million in the preceding quarter. Colocation gross profit was $128 million at a reported 60% margin.

TeraWulf generated $63 million of high-performance computing (HPC) lease revenue, approximately 83% of its $76 million total. Its $11 million HPC segment profit excludes expenses including corporate overhead and depreciation that enter consolidated operating results, giving it a different expense allocation from Core's colocation gross profit.

For the nine months ended September 30, Core generated $296 million of operating cash while spending $1,495 million on property, plant and equipment and another $677 million on asset acquisitions. Net financing cash inflow was $3,200 million.

Over that same nine-month period, TeraWulf used $179 million of operating cash and spent $2,231 million on plant and equipment purchases and deposits. It received $1,010 million of net financing cash, including approximately $1,200 million from common-stock issuance.

Core says conversions for hosting customer CoreWeave are largely funded by that customer. Development under its AMD and other cloud-customer arrangements will require additional external financing. It plans project-level bond financing. Management says existing liquidity plus anticipated funding sources support planned development and operating needs.

TeraWulf's Abernathy sale supplied $250 million of cash during Q3, distinct from the accounting gain; further installments remained expected. After quarter-end, it increased revolving-credit commitments to about $400 million, with an additional conditional, uncommitted $150 million expansion option. Its post-quarter November 4 Google credit support for leases with customer Anthropic concerns anticipated project financing. The Fluidstack warrants behind Q3's gain relate to a separate arrangement.

For the next phase, the operating split provides a more useful starting point than the combined profit: Core has reached positive operating income, while TeraWulf still reports an operating loss. Delivered hosting capacity, cash collections and financing commitments will determine how much of the construction bill the growing businesses can carry.
