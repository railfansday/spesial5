One Vote Left: XRP's Bank-Style Lending Nears Activation Countdown

Two XRP Ledger lending amendments, LendingProtocol and LendingProtocolV1_1, each have 28 votes out of 35 validators, or 80%, according to XRPScan data on Thursday. Each of them needs one more vote to start a 14-day countdown to activation, according to XRPScan.

On Wednesday, the SingleAssetVault amendment reached 29 votes. According to XRPScan, its earliest activation date is Dec. 9, and its support has to stay above 80% until then.

XRP was trading at around $0.95 on Thursday, up 4.1% over 24 hours, according to CoinMarketCap.

On Nov. 20, XRPScan showed 25 votes for SingleAssetVault and 24 votes for each of the two lending amendments. Validators run by Interledger, onXRP, GateHub and Flare were among the last to vote for them.

RippleX opened LendingProtocolV1_1 for voting with a post on X on Sept. 30, when the amendment arrived with rippled version 3.4.0. "LendingProtocolV1_1 is open for validator voting. It introduces closed-ended vaults and cash-basis accounting for XRPL lending, and it must activate before LendingProtocol and SingleAssetVault can proceed," the RippleX developer account wrote.

J. Ayo Akinyele, head of engineering at RippleX, explained the role of the update in a post on June 25. "v1.1 is an enhancement to the Lending Protocol, not a replacement for v1.0," he wrote, adding that "Lending on XRPL will evolve dynamically."

Ripple described the mechanics of the protocol on Nov. 19. "The upcoming XRP Ledger Lending Protocol is designed to bring loan issuance and repayment directly onto the ledger. Lenders deposit into a Single Asset Vault, and the Lending Protocol lets loan brokers use that capital to issue loans and track repayments onchain," the company wrote on X.

Ripple engineer Ed Hennis presented the protocol in a thread on X in December 2025. He called it a protocol-native system for "fixed-term, fixed-rate, underwritten credit." "XRP's $124B market cap can finally be lent into institutional credit facilities to generate real yield instead of sitting idle," Hennis wrote at the time.

Jazzi Cooper, head of product at RippleX, wrote on Sept. 11 that "XRP as collateral for institutional credit is a killer use case (and supported by xls65/66 lending protocol!)"

On the same day, the XRP Ledger Foundation shared an interview with its chief technology officer, Denis Angell. "We are writing TradFi primitives into the XRP Ledger," Angell said. In the interview, he compared the lending protocol to a closed-end fund, where investors put in XRP or RLUSD and receive interest.
