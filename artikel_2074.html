Mikkel Ladefoged is Lorent Tolaj 2.0 for Lincoln City

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Lincoln City star-power recruit Mikkel Ladefoged has the potential to be the difference maker for the Imps this season, and has a blueprint to copy.

The side jointly-managed by Tom Shaw and Chris Cohen might have lost their most recent three games, but have four wins in the Championship next to their name, including an impressive 4-1 victory over Burnley.

Lincoln sit five points above the drop zone after almost a third of the season. That is a good start for a team promoted last term, heavily tipped to return straight back to League One, and under somewhat new management.

Michael Skubala is under-fire at Bristol City, having jumped ship from the Imps after completing a historic and impressive title win. That left Shaw and Cohen to lead Lincoln, having been elevated from their backroom staff positions.

Lincoln City’s return to the Championship has been 65 years in the making, and they have prepared for it in a risky way.

And, they have done that admirably to date. Avoiding relegation has to be the aim for the Lincolnshire outfit, and they have started well enough to sit outside of the dreaded drop zone. A key part of that early success has been the strikers who made the difference last season.

Ben House (against Bolton Wanderers) and Freddie Draper (against both Preston North End and Bristol City) scored the only goals of 1-0 wins. But, there is a more headline-grabbing attacker who has begun to make a difference too.

Signed by the Imps in the summer for a club record fee, Ladefoged left Swedish outfit Vasteras SK after netting 19 goals in 29 games for his previous club. The fee is believed to be beyond that of the £2.5million paid for commanding Romanian international centre-back Andrei Coubis.

With the hefty price tag, relative to the club’s previous deals, Ladefoged came with high hopes and a large weight rested on his shoulders for the relegation fight. He has shown enough already to prove himself as the key to potential Lincoln success.

The 6’8” Dane took a while to get his first league start, but marked it with a brace against Burnley. He followed that up with an assist in defeat to Birmingham City, and has looked a real threat at the top end of the pitch.

Across nine appearances, he has three goals, netting a late equaliser against Southampton off the bench in the weeks after his signing. The 23-year-old’s height, physicality, pace, and decision-making make him the perfect striker for Lincoln and their quest for continued Championship status.

He has the potential to score plenty more as well, having shown his willingness to create chances for his teammates as well as scoring his own.

In 2010-11 Adel Taarabt ran Championship defences ragged as QPR won the title. Now it’s your turn: weave him past cones, lunging defenders and flying slide tackles, and get the ball in the net at the end of each level. There are 15 levels and only three lives. How far can you take him?

He fits the mould of Lorent Tolaj. The 25-year-old Swiss striker has found goals hard to come by since being elevated to the Championship this summer. He signed for Skubala’s Bristol City after a spell-binding campaign at Plymouth Argyle.

In League One, Tolaj found the net 18 times across 33 games, being only three others in the top scorer charts. Considering he missed a portion of the latter stages of the season due to injury, it is an impressive return.

It has been a fast rise for Tolaj, scoring 25 in a season in the National League, then recording 24 goal involvements the following term in League Two before his move to Plymouth.

He does not match Ladefoged for height, as very few do, but his pace and power are a real comparison. Tolaj finds space in tight pockets in and around the box, shifts through seemingly too narrow alleys to create dangerous shooting opportunities.

Ladefoged is a similar archetype, and could propel well beyond Tolaj given his additional qualities and early signs in a Lincoln shirt.

Simply put, if the Imps can keep Ladefoged firing and craft a unit that suits his strengths, then relegation will be very little of a concern come the end of the Championship campaign.
