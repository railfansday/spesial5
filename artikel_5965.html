Is There Still Hope for Postliberalism?

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The rejections of Viktor Orbán and Donald Trump hold sobering lessons for the nationalist right.

Former Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán (left) and President Donald Trump.

NR Illustration; Marton Monus, Evelyn Hockstein/Reuters

On Election Night this past May in Budapest, I was in the ballroom when a defining political era ended faster than it takes to place a drink order at a party-district ruin bar on a Saturday night. Viktor Orbán, the four-term prime minister who for 16 years was the undisputed sovereign of Hungarian politics, and the monster that haunted the nightmares of globalists from Brussels to Washington, took the stage not long after election results began rolling in — and gracefully accepted defeat. It was as if magician David Copperfield had made the Matterhorn disappear before the audience settled into its chairs on the chalet lawn.

Just like that, Orbán was gone. When I first arrived in Budapest for a fellowship in 2021, a Hungarian conservative friend told me that everybody knows Orbán’s Fidesz Party is riddled with corruption, but people tolerate it because life has been getting better and better for them since the reformist prime minister took power in 2010. “But if the economy ever turns,” he said, “then corruption is going to matter. A lot.”
