Floyd Samba’s Man City debut double has father Chris dancing

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

There was very much a Samba beat to this Manchester City victory – and it almost had a famous father in the crowd up dancing.

This was a night when Brazilian Allan Elias scored his first goal on an enterprising first appearance for City.

But it was another debutant who stole the show as the Carabao Cup holders sailed past Norwich City to set up a fourth-round meeting with Brighton.

Floyd Samba scored two fine goals and had a hand in another on a dream debut that had his beaming dad up off his seat and thrusting his arms in the air as if ready to party.

If the Samba surname feels familiar, that is because Floyd’s father Christopher played more than 200 games for Blackburn Rovers, Queens Park Rangers and Aston Villa.

In fact, it was very much a family affair as Floyd’s brothers Tyrone and Emerson, who are also in City’s academy and hoping one day to tread a similar path, joined in the jubilant celebrations alongside their mother, a visibly emotional Theresa.

This particular evening belonged to Floyd, though. Deployed as a lone striker by Enzo Maresca, you would not have known he ordinarily plays as a midfielder such was the confidence and composure the 17-year-old oozed.

Norwich could not live with him or City for that matter as Maresca’s impressive start as manager since replacing Pep Guardiola continued apace.

This must have been an incredibly satisfying moment for Samba Sr. He came through incredible hardship – at one stage as a teenager he was rough-sleeping – to forge an impressive career totalling more than 450 games for club and country.

So to be in a position to now have three sons in City’s academy and one produce a debut like this will fill him with enormous pride. Floyd was later seen enjoying a warm embrace with his dad in the crowd, moments after being congratulated by Tyrone, a year his senior, and 12-year-old Emerson, who plays for City’s Under-13s.

“I knew there would be an opportunity to get some minutes for the first time with the first team – to get a debut like that is mind blowing,” Samba said. “I couldn’t believe it myself, but that’s just the beginning, I’ve got a big career ahead of me. It was a great debut.”

Floyd caught the eye with a fine free-kick in City’s 2-1 FA Youth Cup final victory over Manchester United last season and now he has goals for the senior side to his name. Pretty stunning they were, too.

The first was a superbly steered header past two Norwich bodies on the line from Allan’s cross and the second a lovely, nonchalantly curled finish low into the far corner.

Chris Samba has deliberately kept a low profile, keen to let his sons get on with their own careers free of distractions, but there was no chance he was going to be able to suppress his emotions in that instant. And nor should he. It was some moment for the boy and his family.

As impressed as Maresca was by the goals, it will have been other elements of Samba’s game that also caught the eye. He did extremely well to hold off the attentions of Norwich’s centre-backs when receiving a pass from Ayyoub Bouaddi and then returning the ball to the Moroccan who released Rayan Cherki through to score City’s second. His movement was also sharp, at one point taking a difficult pass sweetly in an adjusted strike, only for Norwich’s Bruno Alves to come across to make a timely intervention before Samba could get his shot off.

“It was satisfying not just for the two goals but for the entire performance,” Maresca said. “He didn’t play many games as a nine but he was good. He’s young, full of energy, strong, he can finish.”

Allan, a £34m summer signing from Palmeiras, will have been just as pleased with his contribution. Maresca’s fluid formation initially resembled a 3-1-5-1 with Allan effectively operating as a right wing-back but he stuck doggedly to the task before eventually getting the moment to showcase his quality, first with his cross for Samba and later with a neat solo run and finish.

Driving inside Sam Field and then cutting back inside the Norwich man before curling an inch perfect finish into the bottom corner – all of it done with his left foot – it was quite the introduction.

Hugo Viana, City’s director of football, has said supporters will need to be patient with Allan and that it may take the 22-year-old time to adjust to English football but this first outing will do his confidence plenty of good.

“It was important for him to play 90 minutes and to see a little bit the English competition that is a bit different from South America so overall was good,” Maresca said.

Cherki was the only survivor from the team that started Sunday’s victory over Manchester United but it all felt pretty seamless from City. Bouaddi, the £85m signing from Lille, was poised and graceful in midfield alongside the impressive Mateo Kovacic and Geronimo Rulli could not have asked for a much quieter debut in goal.

Nico O’Reilly looked like he had barely broken a sweat despite Norwich’s players routinely being unable to keep up with him. Norwich themselves made nine changes but the gulf in class was enormous and testament to City’s strength in depth.

The game also gave Maresca the opportunity to give run outs to two more academy graduates who played in that Youth Cup final triumph, Kaden Braithwaite and Ryan McAidoo and the latter got his own name on the scoresheet to make it 5-0.

All told there were seven City academy graduates on the pitch, two of them playing for Norwich. This was certainly a night to remember for the club’s youth production line.

With the dominant performance, Man City have earned themselves the privilege of hosting Brighton in the next round, who will no doubt prove a much stiffer challenge than Norwich. Good evening and welcome to another night of Carabao Cup coverage! It is the final fixture of round three (I promise) as Manchester City host Norwich.

These two sides have not met since 2022, when Norwich were still in the top flight and were beaten 4-0 by City. Looking at their previous meetings, the odds are definitely stacked in favour of Enzo Maresca’s side, who have scored 14 unanswered goals in their last three meetings with Norwich, while the Championship side have not scored any.

City will be looking to make it six straight wins across all competitions tonight, having started perfectly in the Premier League with four wins from four, and comfortably seeing off Porto in their Champions League opener.

They were the talk of the weekend with their deeply controversial 1-0 win over rivals Manchester United as – having gone down to 10 men after Phil Foden was sent off – their winning goal was allowed to stand despite Enzo Fernández being clearly in an offside position that impacted United’s keeper and backline.

City are defending their title in this competition – beating Arsenal last year in the final – but like all Premier League teams playing in European competitions, have only entered the tournament this year in the third round.

Philippe Clement’s side beat MK Dons 4-1 in the first round, and followed that up with a 2-1 win over Cardiff City in the second to reach the Etihad tonight. The fourth-round draw has already been done, and it has been decided that the winner of this match will face Brighton.
