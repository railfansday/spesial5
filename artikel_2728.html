Diving deeper into Cavs 121-102 loss to Knicks: Even when the stars are at their best, this team isn’t

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The Cleveland Cavaliers looked like they could compete with the New York Knicks until they couldn't. Cleveland went into the final quarter with a narrow 85-82 advantage, but then lost the fourth 39-17 to drop this game 121-102.

19 points on 9-of-15 shooting, 8 rebounds, 2 assists, 1 block

Bridges covered up for pretty pedestrian performances from Jalen Brunson and Karl-Anthony Towns. Bridges provided efficient scoring, was an impactful rebounder, and was good defensively. This all added up to the Knicks winning his minutes by 33.

30 points on 12-of-24 shooting, 4 rebounds, 4 assists, 4 steals, 2 blocks

Mitchell was unstoppable in the first half. His 14 points in the opening quarter and six more in the second put the Cavs in a good position to steal this game. Mitchell seemingly couldn't miss before the break, going 8-for-11 from the field with most of his damage coming at the rim.

The magic went away in the second half. Mitchell scored just 10 points on 4-of-13 shooting in the final two quarters. Cleveland then proceeded to lose those minutes by 15.

The Cavs needed a special performance from Mitchell to defeat the defending champions. They had that in the first half, but not the second.

15 points on 6-of-14 shooting, 12 assists, 7 rebounds, 3 steals, 2 blocks

Hart is a winning player. He does all the little things that championship-level teams need from their supporting cast. Rebounding, additional ball handling, defensive versatility, and ancillary playmaking are attributes you hope to accumulate in your entire supporting cast. Hart provides that as just one player.

Those skills were all on display against the Cavs. Hart rebounded well, forced turnovers, and was a useful facilitator. The Cavs have often decided to cheat off Hart offensively. He didn't make them pay as a shooter, but he used the extra space to find openings for his teammates.

On a night when both teams got similar contributions from their stars, it was the play of the supporting players, like Hart and Jose Alverado (21 points on 8-of-10 shooting), that made all the difference.

5 points on 2-of-8 shooting, 1 rebound, 0 assists

Merrill has had a tough time finding his shot this season. There's many things Merrill does well, but those are mostly muted if the outside shot isn't falling, as was once again the case here.

Head coach Kenny Atkinson has asked a lot out of Merrill this season. He's been consistently forced to guard up a spot or two when he's on the court — particularly when he's been inserted into the starting lineup. That isn't an ideal place for him considering he's undersized for that role, and has been sharing the floor with guards that don't pull their weight defensively.

I don't envy Merrill. He's been continually put into positions to fail this season.

10 points on 5-of-7 shooting, 5 rebounds, 1 assist

Allen is supposed to be a key member of this team, but you wouldn't necessarily know that from watching games like this. The Cavs need Allen to be a force inside as a roller and rebounder with Evan Mobley out of the lineup. He wasn't either of those things, and played with little to no energy, which is why the Cavs lost his minutes by 32.

The Cavs are built around their guards. If that backcourt is going to work on both ends, they need their interior players to consistently show up. They haven't, which is one of the many reasons for the underwhelming start to the season.

The Cavs benefited greatly from Bryant's energy and willingness to be physical. Both of those things stood out on a team that is in desperate need of interior size and presence. It's no coincidence the Cavs played their best basketball of the evening when he was on the court.

28 points on 7-of-19 shooting, 5 assists, 1 rebound

This was a solid night from Harden. He kept the offense moving, took smart shots, and continually forced his way to the line (13-for-14). A poor shooting night from three (1-for-8) kept this from being a great offensive game.

Defensively, the Cavs had some success with hiding him on Towns. Harden held his own in that matchup, resulting in the defense not completely falling apart with him on the floor.

Harden did a good job of forcing his way inside. The offense is in a much better place when he can consistently do that.

Jose Alverado scored as many points off the bench (21) as all five of Cleveland's reserves combined.

This was a bad night from Cleveland's supporting cast, particularly their bench. Tyrese Proctor had just two points on 1-of-6 shooting, Jaylon Tyson went 2-for-8 from the field with four turnovers, and Mario Hezonja registered no points in 10 minutes.

Conversely, New York got key contributions from their role players, including Alverado. He scored 16 of his 21 points in the fourth to help put the Cavs away.

This team makes much more sense on paper than it does on the court. The Cavs have more well-rounded role players than they have had in recent years. This has shown through when they get opportunities to shine. Yet, that may not be what works with this particular core.

Typically, the Cavs are at their best when Mitchell and Harden are playing well, as they were on Wednesday. This formula has been the recipe for their top performances, but the Cavs didn't come close to pulling off the upset because they got next to no help from their supporting cast. No player besides the starting backcourt scored more than 10 points. It's nearly impossible to win games against an elite opponent when your role players add nothing.

Somehow, the Cavs have constructed a team that relies heavily on their guards to be at their best to win alongside a supporting cast that doesn't really fit with how the guards play. That is a problem, and how you get nights like this where Peyton Watson and Mario Hezonja combine to take just five shots from the field.

The Cavs are pot committed to this backcourt both in terms of their recent contract extensions and how they play. Atkinson hasn't indicated that he wants to move away from this isolation- and high pick-and-roll-heavy offense that caters specifically to the two guards. But that style isn't conducive to a team that has struggled to knock down catch-and-shoot threes (36.3%, 16th in the league), doesn't have a versatile defense, and features two bigs that need to be force-fed to be effective offensively.

Koby Altman has built an interesting supporting cast. On paper, it would fit nicely with a team that played like the 2024-25 Cavs. However, this isn't that team. Any hopes of playing that way went out the window when they traded for Harden last winter.

Cleveland has been one of the most inconsistent groups this season. Few teams have as impressive highs and as disappointing lows. Many factors contribute to that, but one of them is that the puzzle pieces don't really fit together. Or at the very least, they don't fit with how they're being used now.

There's no easy fix for this. Getting a team to drastically change how they play 19 games into the season is nearly impossible. At the same time, there isn't an easy way to pivot off either the star or supporting players due to their contracts. It's difficult to see a trade drastically changing the outlook of this team. They're more or less committed to this current group and playstyle. And I'm just not sure what the pathway is to being a legit title contender, which is what you would hope they'd be coming off a run to the Eastern Conference Finals.

Even when the stars are at or near their best, this team isn't.
