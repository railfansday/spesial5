Nvidia closes lower ahead of earnings as company reels from Huang resignation

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Nvidia shares closed marginally lower Tuesday as former CEO Jensen Huang released his first public statement since his resignation and the company prepares to report earnings after the bell.

The stock move Tuesday marked Nvidia's fourth straight daily decline.

In the statement, Huang said he is open to returning as Nvidia's CEO and that he is committed to cooperating with the board's investigation into his behavior.

"I created problems for the Board that I deeply regret," Huang said in a statement obtained by CNBC's David Faber. "I own my mistakes completely, and I am committed to cooperating with the Board's independent investigation."

"If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO," Huang continued.

Huang resigned from Nvidia, the company's board announced on Thursday, following reports that he had a relationship with a woman U.S. intelligence reportedly believes to be a Chinese spy. Huang told employees he had an affair in an email on Nov. 8. Outlets also reported that Huang also had an affair with a vice president at the company and an "intimate relationship" with an unnamed second employee.

Nvidia's stock slid 4% on Monday, bringing shares to their lowest close since July 1, 2025.

Lawmakers also announced Tuesday that Huang will appear before the House Foreign Affairs, Intelligence, and China Select Committees on Dec. 1. Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter will appear before the same committees on Dec. 2.

Nvidia's stock slid 4% on Monday, bringing shares to their lowest close since July 1, 2025.

An online petition calling for Jensen Huang's reinstatement as Nvidia CEO drew more than 190,000 signatures, including Elon Musk. But Nvidia's board of directors is not considering bringing back Jensen Huang as CEO, a person familiar with the matter told CNBC on Monday.

The chip giant is also facing increasing scrutiny from U.S. lawmakers over Chinese companies' access to its advanced chips, which U.S. export controls restrict from going to China.

The U.S. Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security, or BIS, announced on Monday an interim final rule designed to prevent Chinese companies' remote access to AI chips.

Nvidia announced on Tuesday morning that its board had established a committee responsible for oversight of the company's national security policies, export control compliance and global trade risk management.

The National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee will be chaired by director Suzanne Nora Johnson and will include directors Stephen C. Neal and John O. Dabiri as members, the company said in a statement.
