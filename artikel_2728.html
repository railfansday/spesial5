UK to jointly host 2035 Women&#8217;s World Cup

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

England, Northern Ireland, Scotland and Wales will jointly host the 2035 Women's World Cup.

It will be the first football World Cup staged in the United Kingdom since the men's tournament in 1966.

The four countries were the only bidders for the competition and their joint proposal was ratified at a specially arranged virtual meeting of FIFA Congress from Zurich.

The expanded tournament will feature 48 teams playing 104 matches across 39 days.

The 'All Together' bid proposed 19 venues, including 14 in England, two in Scotland, two in Wales and one in Northern Ireland.

Wembley, Emirates Stadium, Tottenham Hotspur Stadium, Old Trafford, the Etihad Stadium and St James' Park are among the English venues included.

Cardiff City Stadium and Principality Stadium are the two proposed Welsh grounds, while Hampden Park and Easter Road are set to represent Scotland.

A redeveloped Windsor Park is due to stage matches in Northern Ireland.

Three further stadiums could yet be added to the plans, including Birmingham's proposed Powerhouse Stadium, a redeveloped Racecourse Ground in Wrexham and a potential new Chelsea stadium, which would take the total to 22 venues across 16 cities.

FA chief executive Mark Bullingham believes hosting the tournament can build on the impact of Euro 2022, when England hosted and won the Women's European Championship.

"Hosting Euro 2022 allowed us to change the game in our country, by creating a generation of aspirational role models and growing demand from women and girls to play," Bullingham said.

"Hosting a World Cup would give us the chance to build on that across all four nations."

FIFA has not yet confirmed whether England, Northern Ireland, Scotland and Wales will all qualify automatically as co-hosts.

The 2031 Women's World Cup was also awarded at the meeting, with Mexico, the United States, Costa Rica and Jamaica confirmed as joint hosts of the first 48-team edition of the tournament.
