Unfinished business: Shenendehowa girls' volleyball chases program's 2nd state title

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

CLIFTON PARK - The Shenendehowa girls' volleyball team approaches each season expecting to win a sectional title, and this season those expectations were met again as the Mustangs claimed the program's sixth consecutive Section 2 Class AAA championship.

After coming up short of an appearance in last season's championship match, Shenendehowa, one of four teams set to compete in Class AAA pool play Friday afternoon at Harding Mazzotti Arena in Glens Falls, is determined to win the program's first state title since 2022.

"We didn't really play the way we wanted to," Shenendehowa junior opposite hitter/setter Hadley Ennist said of last postseason. "I feel like that just gave us so much motivation for this year to do what we can. Yes, we are a young team, but we have so much potential and we are a great team as well."

Shenendehowa, ranked No. 1 in the state and a program with 15 sectional titles since 2010, defeated Section 3 champion Liverpool to advance to the final four. The Mustangs will face Corning (Section 4), Mercy (Section 5) and Bay Shore (Section 11).

Mustangs' coach Lori Kessler has been pleased with the development of a roster comprised of four seniors, six juniors, six sophomores and two freshmen.

"They are very special to me. Some of them I have coached since 12-(and-under)," Kessler said. "To do it with this group would absolutely be fantastic. We learned a lot last year after not getting the job done. It left us with a yearning to get back there again and finish it out this time. We've had this motto all year: 'Leave no doubt.'… We do not want to have any regrets this time around."

Ennist, the 2025 Times Union Large School Player of the Year, believes this season's squad is deeper, more dangerous and poised to achieve the desired objective this weekend.

An Old Dominion commit who has produced another superlative season, Ennist enters play Friday after registering 543 assists, 273 kills, 239 service points (64 aces) and 227 digs.

"I think she has emotionally matured a lot," Kessler said of Ennist. "Saturday night (against Liverpool), we had some tough moments we needed to work through. She did that. She is capable of doing it. She finished some big points.

"She is doing it all out there offensively and defensively."

Shenendehowa uses a 6-2 rotation, meaning two setters are on the court. Ennist starts on the right side, joined by freshman Kyle Kessler, the coach's daughter.

"Being able to have both of them is such an advantage because they are both really good setters," middle blocker Claire Siska said. "We have developed chemistry and team connections with both of them."

Senior middle blocker Ava Los, with 194 kills and 185 service points, joins Siska (236 kills, 78 blocks) as vital components of the offense and defense.

"We have a lot of variation [in the] front row and that balances out our offense. I think it is one of our [best] benefits compared to other teams," Los said. "It is so much fun. We have so many good hitters and we have been able to balance things out."

In addition to the exploits from Los and Siska, Kessler has relied on solid production all season from Tubbs (176 kills), Nadia Nikiforov (201 kills), and Ella Nikiforov (196 kills). Defensive specialist Avery Young, a sophomore, has delivered 476 digs and 242 service points and Kyli Kessler has produced 549 assists, 219 service points (94.9 percent) and 157 digs.

"She is doing really well. You can't tell she is a freshman out there," Ennist said of Kyli Kessler. "She keeps her composure very well and plays at a high level. She is a great piece to have out there for the team."

Ennist, Los and Siska all said they had a feeling something special was in order for this season's squad before practice even began.

"I do think this is a team that can do it," Ennist said. "So it comes down to playing at the level we can and being disciplined."

"I felt last year in states, we overplayed," Siska said. "We're ready this time."
