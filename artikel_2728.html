7 individual performances that could define 2026-27 women’s college hoops season

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

A few key players will really shape the 2026-27 women's college basketball season and the race for the championship. Some are coming back after great years and want to keep getting better, while others are ready to step up and lead their teams further than ever. With tough games ahead in the ACC, SEC, Big Ten, Big 12 and Big East, how each player performs will matter a lot.

Returning stars will face focused defenses now that everyone knows how good they are. Top scorers will have to work hard to keep putting up big numbers, and taller players will need to control the area near the basket on both offense and defense.

New talent will also make things exciting. Freshmen who played great overseas will get their first shot against older, proven college players.

A few names already stand out as the new season gets underway. How well they score, rebound, defend and set up their teammates will likely decide how far their teams go. Here are seven players to keep an eye on in 2026-27.

UConn Huskies center Olivia Vukosa (25) defends against Cornell Big Red forward Rachel Kaus (5) in the first half at PeoplesBank Arena. Credit: IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect (Reuters)

Olivia Vukosa could give UConn a major boost in the paint during her freshman season. The 6-foot-4 center arrives in college basketball after an outstanding summer with Croatia at the 2026 FIBA U18 EuroBasket. She led the tournament with 24.9 points and 14.3 rebounds per game. Those numbers showed her ability to score consistently and dominate the boards against international competition.

The next challenge will be carrying that production into the Big East. Vukosa will have to adjust to the physical nature and pace of college basketball. However, her presence alongside Sarah Strong could give UConn a powerful frontcourt combination. The Huskies have not produced an All-American traditional center since Stefanie Dolson in 2014. Vukosa has the potential to bring that level of interior impact back to the program. If her international form carries over, UConn could become much stronger in offensive rebounding and interior defense.

Texas Longhorns forward Madison Booker (35) against the UCLA Bruins during a semifinal of the Final Four of the 2026 Women's NCAA Tournament at Mortgage Matchup Center. Credit: IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect (Reuters)

Madison Booker remains one of the most important players in Texas' championship plans. Her value comes from her ability to create offense from different positions. She has the vision to find open teammates and the scoring ability to attack defenses at all three levels.

Booker's point-forward role allows Texas to use bigger lineups without losing ball-handling or playmaking. That flexibility makes her difficult to defend. The Longhorns also have added more perimeter spacing, including former five-star recruit Jordan Lee, who continues to develop into a dangerous outside threat. As a result, opponents will have fewer options when deciding how to defend Booker. Packing the paint could leave shooters open, while switching defenders could give her favorable matchups. Her ability to control the half-court offense will be important throughout the season.

Iowa center Ava Heiden (5) reacts Oct. 29, 2026 during an exhibition game against the Maryville Saints at Carver-Hawkeye Arena in Iowa City, Iowa. Credit: USA TODAY Network via Reuters Connect (Reuters)

Ava Heiden gives Iowa a reliable scoring option in the paint. The 6-foot-4 center averaged 18 points per game as a sophomore while shooting an impressive 64 percent from the field. Her performances earned her first-team All-Big Ten honors.

That efficiency makes Heiden an important part of Iowa's offense. She can score close to the basket and force opposing defenses to pay attention to the interior. Her presence should also help create better opportunities for the team's perimeter players.

Heiden's experience will be valuable as Iowa brings in highly rated freshmen, including 6-foot-2 forward McKenna Woliczko. With Heiden providing a dependable post-up option, the newcomers will not have to carry too much offensive responsibility immediately. Her ability to control the paint could determine how quickly Iowa's young players settle into their roles.

Michigan Wolverines guard Olivia Olson (1) warms up before the game against the Texas Longhorns at Dickies Arena. Credit: IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect (Reuters)

Olivia Olson enters the 2026-27 season after establishing herself as one of the Big Ten's leading scorers. She averaged 18.9 points per game last season and earned All-American and First-Team All-Big Ten honors.

Michigan will depend heavily on her scoring and shot creation. Olson's size at the guard position gives her an advantage against smaller defenders. She can also create her own shot when the offense struggles to find an opening. That ability becomes especially important late in the shot clock, when teams need someone who can produce without relying on a designed play.

Michigan has yet to win a regular-season or conference tournament title in program history. Olson could play a major role in changing that record. The Wolverines have added incoming transfers and a top-25 recruiting class. If those additions settle in quickly, Olson will have more support around her. Her performances could help Michigan compete for its first conference title.

South Carolina Gamecocks forward Joyce Edwards (8) against the UCLA Bruins during the National Championship game of the women's 2026 NCAA Tournament at Mortgage Matchup Center. Credit: IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect (Reuters)

Joyce Edwards has already shown that she can make a difference on both ends of the floor. She recorded 19.2 points and 6.8 rebounds per game last season. Her impact on the glass and her defensive numbers were just as impressive, pulling down 115 offensive rebounds to create second-chance opportunities and recording 72 blocks to anchor the paint.

Those numbers explain why Edwards is so important to South Carolina. The Gamecocks have plenty of depth on the perimeter, but Edwards gives them a strong interior presence. Her shot-blocking helps cover defensive mistakes and makes it harder for opponents to finish around the basket.

Playing for Dawn Staley's No. 1-ranked team comes with high expectations. South Carolina will be looking to maintain its position among the country's top programs. Edwards' ability to produce consistently on both ends could be a major reason the Gamecocks remain difficult to beat.

UConn Huskies forward Sarah Strong (21) returns the ball against the Cornell Big Red in the first half at PeoplesBank Arena. Credit: IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect (Reuters)

Sarah Strong enters the new season as the reigning AP Player of the Year. She averaged 18.4 points and 7.7 rebounds per game last season while shooting 58;2 percent from the field. Her efficiency and all-around game make her one of the most important players in the country.

Strong offers much more than scoring. She can pass effectively from the post, defend multiple areas and collect rebounds. Her ability to score in transition also allows UConn to keep its offense moving without slowing down to run set plays. That makes it difficult for opponents to focus their defensive attention on one area.

As UConn looks to make another deep NCAA Tournament run, Strong will remain at the center of its plans. Her presence should also create better opportunities for the Huskies' perimeter shooters. Defenses that focus too much on stopping her inside could leave openings around the 3-point line. Strong's all-around production could once again determine how far UConn goes.

Vanderbilt Commodores guard Mikayla Blakes (1) dribbles the ball up court against the Notre Dame Fighting Irish during the second half at Dickies Arena. Credit: IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect (Reuters)

Mikayla Blakes tops this list after leading the nation in scoring during the 2025-26 season. She averaged an outstanding 27 points and 4.5 assists per game. That production made her one of the most difficult offensive players to contain in college basketball.

However, maintaining those numbers could become harder this season. SEC opponents will have a full season of film to study and will likely send double-teams and aggressive traps to limit her scoring opportunities. Blakes will need to find ways to handle that defensive attention without losing her offensive rhythm.

Her ability to involve Vanderbilt's other creators, including Aubrey Galvan, will be just as important as her scoring. When defenses focus on Blakes, she must make the right passes and create opportunities for her teammates. That could help the Commodores avoid becoming too dependent on one player.

Vanderbilt will need her scoring ability throughout the season, especially in difficult SEC matchups. If Blakes maintains her efficiency and becomes even more effective as a playmaker, she could help the Commodores move beyond Sweet 16 contention and challenge for a Final Four spot.
