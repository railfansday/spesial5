Landry Shamet has arrived. Why can’t he be Sixth Man of the Year?

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Chicken Littles can relax. Our world champion Knicks are 11–6, sit fourth in the Eastern Conference, and have won eight of their last 10 games, including five straight. Karl-Anthony Towns is having an MVP-caliber season, and Captain Jalen Brunson is warming up after a short, celebration-heavy offseason. Another player who's getting some buzz isn't one of the starters, but the team's sharpest weapon off the bench.

When asked about Landry Shamet's play this season, head coach Mike Brown said the 6'5" guard "should be Sixth Man of the Year. It's not close." Mike should know. He has a front-row seat to the Shamet show every night and at every practice.

One stat I take note of is Game Score. John Hollinger designed it to measure a player's box-score production in a single game. Game Score uses a scale that roughly resembles points scored, with 10 considered an average performance and 40 an outstanding one. It rewards scoring, made field goals, rebounds, assists, steals, and blocks. Shot attempts, missed free throws, turnovers, and personal fouls subtract from the total, and offensive rebounds count more than defensive rebounds.

Through 17 games, Shamet has averaged a Game Score of 8.24. That puts him fourth on the team behind Towns (19.83), Brunson (18.66), and OG Anunoby (11.57). Two starters, Josh Hart and Mikal Bridges, rank fifth and sixth at 6.35 and 5.94, respectively.

This season has been a coming out party for Landry. Although primarily a linchpin of the second unit, he has filled in for starters five times this campaign and is averaging 26.9 minutes per game, the second-most of his NBA career. Consider these statistics, all career-best:

11.4 points per game, fourth-best on the team

The only Knick shooting better from deep is Jordan Clarkson (50%), coincidentally the 2020–21 Sixth Man of the Year. And Jordan's attempted only 12 longballs.

When Hart and Bridges have struggled to find the net, plenty of people have beaten the drum for Shamet to replace one of them in the first five. That sounds fine in theory, but consider Mike Brown's perspective. First, there's the harmony of keeping the Villanova brethren together, and who knows what effect a "demotion" for Josh or Kal might have on the locker room? Second, Shamet is Mike's security blanket. If the starters are spinning their wheels, nothing injects energy into the festivities like a Landry pull-up three on the move. The MSG crowd explodes, and you can see his teammates quicken with excitement. Furthermore, Shamet is doing much of his damage against the opponent's backups. That's hardly a fair fight. AND when the score is close and the team needs a bucket, Brown can turn to Captain Clutch, Towns, and his sparkplug Shamet. Even if Landry doesn't start, he sure can finish.

One downer about Landry's success is the ripple effect on the roster. Overshadowed by Shamet's sizzling shooting is the fact that Miles McBride is also shooting a career high from downtown (42%). Deuce was once a first-off-the-bench candidate. Now, with Shamet hitting the three-ball and Tyler Kolek coming into his own as a playmaker (averaging 4.1 assists to 0.6 turnovers in 13.3 minutes per game), McBride is down to 17.2 minutes per night, the fourth-highest average of his six-season career. Meanwhile, the Knicks find themselves dreadfully thin at center, with Andre Drummond losing the plot around the 10-minute mark most nights and James Wiseman yet to prove he can hang. Shamet + Kolek + Drummond + Wiseman = an expendable Deuce. No one here will be surprised if McBride, the longest-tenured Knick, is traded before the deadline for a serviceable big man. No, we won't like it, but we'll understand it.

Consider this: only three Knicks have won Sixth Man of the Year.

Why can't ShamWow be the fourth? He'll have some stiff competition in the race. Boston's Payton Pritchard is the current betting favorite for Sixth Man of the Year (+160 at BetMGM). That vanilla Milk Dud averages 13.7 points per game. Other top contenders include Ajay Mitchell (Thunder), Dylan Harper (Spurs), Jrue Holiday (Blazers), and Ayo Dosunmu (Timberwolves). Jared McCain and Daniss Jenkins are dark horses.

The stats make Landry a strong candidate, though. Through Nov. 22, Shamet ranked fifth in the NBA in on/off net rating among 242 qualifying players on HoopsNightly's leaderboard. The Knicks outscored opponents by 17.2 points per 100 possessions with him on the floor and were outscored by 8.3 with him off it. That's a swing of +25.5. (KnickStats gives him an even better number, +28.)

There used to be two knocks on Shamet, now in his ninth season. The first was that his defense stunk. Well, whattayaknow: according to KnickStats, the Knicks have allowed 101.3 points per 100 possessions with him on the floor and 117.5 with him off it. The other concern was the injury bug. He's missed significant time with lower-body issues, including multiple ankle sprains, a foot injury that kept him out for an extended stretch in 2023, and a calf strain that ended his 2023–24 season. Then came the right-shoulder setbacks, a dislocation in the 2024 preseason and a sprain in 2025 that sidelined him for roughly a month. For now (no jinxes), he's been the picture of health, playing every game of the campaign (we all wince whenever he smashes to the floor).

Everything's good until it isn't again. So it goes. For now, the Knicks are surging and tonight they'll face a familiar rival in the Cleveland Cavaliers. If you saw the injury report, it's a little dicey. Anunoby and Clarkson are out, while Hart, McBride, and Towns are all game-time decisions. That's a concerning list at first blush, but with sixth man Shamet in their back pocket, the Knicks still have a good chance to win their sixth straight. What a time to be a fan.
