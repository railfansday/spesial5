Would Wayne Rooney have worked in Pep Guardiola’s Barcelona?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

“I would have loved to play for Barcelona,” Wayne Rooney said recently.

“Barça beat us in the Champions League final and they didn’t have a reference No. 9. I think it would have been easy to play in that team, I would’ve fitted in perfectly.”

Well, Wayne, let us be the judge of that as we picture a world where Rooney does indeed join Pep Guardiola’s Barcelona team.

First, let’s discuss the kind of player he was.

Terrifyingly for the rest of us, there will be some football watchers who now know Rooney as a manager or a pundit more than they did as a player, so let us fill you in on what he was like on the pitch.

Simply put, Rooney was one of the best young talents ever seen in football. There were non-ridiculous comparisons between him, Cristiano Ronaldo and Lionel Messi.

Rooney never finished in the top three of the Ballon d’Or for his entire career, which seems mental to even think about, but if you would have asked anyone from 2005 to 2010, who the best player in the world was, he would have got a mention.

What made Rooney excellent was that he in a lot of ways a typical English striker. He is only 5ft 10’ so shorter than your average centre forward but what he lacked in going upwards, he made up for in going through.

Rooney weighed around 80kg during his United days. As a comparison, Eli Junior Kroupi is the same height and weighs 70kg. Yoane Wissa, also of a similar height, weighs 74kg.

But it was not fat that pushed Rooney up to the higher weight, it was muscle. Rio Ferdinand said Rooney had a “caveman” strength from a very young age. There are reports he was able to bench press 110 kg as a 15-year-old.

Rooney’s physicality was a nightmare for defenders to play against. We can all remember him for the reckless challenges and flying slide tackles but for every one of those, there are 20 examples of him muscling free of a defender.

A number nine who is strong isn’t groundbreaking but what separated Rooney from the pack was that he had the physicality but combined it with a technical ability rarely seen in an English centre forward.

It was his most continental ability and one that was there from very early on. Look at this goal for Everton against Arsenal, the one that had commentator Clive Tyldesley telling everyone to remember the name.

The experienced Sol Campbell gives Rooney plenty of space on the edge, for the logical reasons that this is a 16-year-old who does probably not have the confidence to be trying those kinds of shots and you would have to be a pretty good player to beat David Seaman from that range.

Given that space, Rooney looks up then picks his spot. He sidefoots it, giving it the curl needed to go up and down in the short space of time. Seaman is at full stretch and doesn’t get close to it. It kisses the bar on the way in one of the most perfectly hit shots in Premier League history.

There are countless examples of Rooney displaying the kind of technical superiority ahead of players in the league.

It was not just shooting too, Rooney was one of the best passers in the league too. His chip to Robin Van Persie in 2013 was hit hard enough to travel 40 yards in no time at all but with a delicacy that allowed it to slow and drop perfect for Van Persie, meaning the Dutchman did not have to break his stride before volleying it in.

Rooney’s ability was not lost on other players either. In 2015, he received this compliment from Xavi: “Wayne Rooney is a player I have admired for a long time. He is an extraordinary player with extraordinary capabilities.”

As well as the strength and technical ability Rooney had, he combined that with a relentlessness. Rooney grew up boxing in Merseyside, he is the footballing version of Rocky Balboa. And what that gave him was an incredible work rate. What he lacked in diet discipline over the summer, he made up in running hard yards during games, something he now laments the current United squad for failing to do.

That desire comes with a lot of positives, not least it gets the crowd on your side, but there are negatives too, such as being dragged out of position in search of the ball but also Rooney had a rawness and rashness when it came to challenges. Four club career red cards feels on the low side.

Put it all together and you have a physical, relentless player who also has the technique to play at the highest of levels. Now what about Barcelona?

The forgotten story of… Wayne Rooney’s Man Utd transfer request in 2010

The tactical philosophy of Pep Guardiola’s Barcelona has been well studied and adopted all over the world to varying degrees of success.

It was most effective at Barcelona due to being relatively in its infancy and also Guardiola was blessed with infinite talents who had been developed to play this exact style of football.

It resulted in two Champions Leagues, three league titles and the first ever sextuple. But what was it exactly?

The best example of it came in that Wembley final, when Barcelona picked apart Sir Alex Ferguson’s Manchester United.

Tiki-taka, as it came to be known, was an evolution on the foundations laid by Johan Cruyff. It can rather falsely be labelled as pass, pass, pass but every pass in a perfect Guardiola system is with a purpose. The idea is to pull your opponent into areas they don’t want to be so that their team becomes streched, opening gaps. Being a Barcelona player is as much about running off the ball to create space for your teammate as it is what you do on it.

For players to succeed in Pep’s system, they need the combination of great passing ability, the technical quality to quickly control the ball and release and the spatial awareness to create space for their teammates via their positioning. They also need to run an awful lot.

As for what Guardiola wanted from his number nine, he very rarely played with a traditional number nine. He tried it with Zlatan Ibrahimovic and it famously did not work, although they may just as much to do with his Swedish attitude as it is with his ability.

Typically though, Guardiola wanted a number nine who could drop deep and overload the midfield. Guardiola loves his teams to exploit the half spaces between defensive lines and by having a false nine, he could operate in the area too far forward for the centre backs and too deep for the midfielders.

So the profile of player we are looking for is a technically excellent, intelligent player who can operate just as well in a 10 as he can as a nine. And that sounds a lot like Wayne Rooney.

As Rooney showed in his late career once his speed gave way, he is able to drop deep and essentially play as an advanced midfielder. At Barcelona, his move to that position may have been expedited by Guardiola’s desire to have him play as a false nine.

Rooney’s physicality would also add something new to a largely unphysical Barcelona front line. At the time they had Messi, Pedro and David Villa, three players you would not describe as overly physical, even if Messi in particular is far stronger than he often gets credited for.

There is every reason to think that Rooney would actually work very well in a Barcelona system, the only issue is one that Ibrahimovic also ran into – Messi.

For that Wembley final, Guardiola deployed Messi in the number nine spot which is where he played for the majority of the season. It was a new tactic by Guardiola as in the previous season, Messi played 39 matches on the right side compared to 12 in the centre forward position.

In 2010/11, those numbers flipped with Messi playing 43 times through the middle compared to 11 on the wing. In those 43 games, Messi scored 46 goals.

In their ideal worlds, Messi and Rooney would likely have wanted to operate in similar areas of the pitch and there would only be one winner in a potential battle for supremacy.

So while Rooney would have worked in Guardiola’s Barcelona, he would have arguably been even more impactful in Luis Enrique’s Barcelona. By the time Enrique took over, Barcelona had become much more direct and played Luis Suarez as a typical nine who could bring both Messi and Neymar into play.

Put Rooney into that system and it would have been scary.

Overall, it is fair to say that a player as technically gifted as Rooney would have definitely worked at Barcelona but he would have found himself in the shadow of Messi as so many other great players did. Still, he probably would have had a lot of trophies to help him get over that fact.
