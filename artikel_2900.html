Bucks Reacts Survey: If you could solve one Bucks problem, what would it be?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Welcome to SB Nation Reacts, a survey of fans across the NBA. Throughout the year we ask questions of the most plugged-in Bucks fans and fans across the country.

Last week's "Who won the Giannis trade?" debate will continue long into the future, but right now it's clear the Milwaukee Bucks are struggling. Big time. After losing to the Atlanta Hawks on Sunday, the Bucks are on a 10-game losing streak, and, at 1-13, sit last in the Eastern Conference standings. Making things worse, they now have sole ownership of the worst record to start a season in franchise history. So, if you could concoct a magic elixir to rid the Bucks of one of their woes, what would it be?

The Bucks are letting it fly from downtown, taking 44.0 threes per game on the season, the second most in the league. Yet, they're hitting them at just a 34.1% clip, the 20th-best mark in the league. And it's not as if the Bucks have a few volume shooters who have gone cold and are dragging the team down. No, outside of Jaime Jaquez Jr. and AJ Green, the slump is across the board. Kel'el Ware (23.7%) and Myles Turner (29.5%) are both shooting career-lows from downtown—Turner's rookie season where he seldom took threes notwithstanding—Tyler Herro is also essentially at a career-low mark (36.1%), and Ryan Rollins is down six percentage points on his career mark (34.4%). In turn, Kasparas Jakučionis, who hit 42.3% of his threes last year, is converting on just 27.5% of his attempts—and that number's buoyed by his 4/10 display against the Atlanta Hawks.

While Milwaukee's raw rebounding numbers look promising—they currently average the eighth-most rebounds per game—that total is deceptive. In fact, advanced metrics paint a very different picture. The Bucks rank 28th in the league in both total rebounding percentage (47.4%) and defensive rebounding percentage (64.6%). Furthermore, despite averaging the sixth-most offensive rebounds per game (13.6), they place a mediocre-at-best 18th in offensive rebounding percentage (30.5%). The discrepancy, then, is further testament to the Bucks' poor shooting: they grab more offensive rebounds because they frequently miss and therefore can, not because of any actual excellence in that area.

However, none of this should really come as a surprise. After all, the Bucks' best rebounder, Ware, is struggling to stay on the court, playing a career-low 18.2 minutes per game, and ranks 43rd in the league in total rebounding percentage (15.7%). Their second best is a rookie point guard. That's not hyperbole, either—Burries ranks second on the team in rebounding percentage at 10.8%. Rollins, another point guard, ranks fourth (9.2%). Outside of a magic elixir, there's no easy fix; these Bucks simply lack strong rebounders—especially in the forward positions—and I will continue to preach it until it's addressed.

Completing the unholy trinity of team flaws, Milwaukee ranks 25th in the league in both turnovers per game (16.9) and turnover percentage (16.7%). Per Cleaning the Glass, Jakučionis ranks in the seventh percentile in the league, relative to position, in turnover percentage. But he's not alone. Kuzma's in the 10th percentile, Turner's in the 12th, Rollins is sinning too (in the 27th percentile), and Jaquez is in the 31st. In fact, the only Buck to rank in the upper echelon for their position is the seldom used—and now injured—Ousmane Dieng. Considering this, there's no wonder why the Bucks are struggling to score; not only do they lack a legitimate go-to scorer (sorry, Tyler), they also lack top-level playmaking (yes, ball security is an essential component to that).

So, long-range shooting, controlling the glass, or taking care of the ball, which of these is your elixir targeting? Alternatively, is there something else atop your priority list?

Let us know your thoughts by voting below—and don't forget to explain your reasons in the comments.
