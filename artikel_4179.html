OG Xbox Game 'Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee' Gets Remastered, But What Do The Critics Think?

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Was it worth digging the original Xbox title back up?

Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee launched on the original Xbox in 2003, a few months after its GameCube debut, and sadly, only in North America. The kaiju arena-brawler, which depicted the iconic King of Monsters, Godzilla, body-slamming Ghidorah into various cityscapes, has apparently made a big enough impact to warrant Atari funding a remaster in 2026.

Sadly, the whole North American release made it quite difficult for us Brits to get our mucky mitts on the game, and DAMM wasn't a big enough deal for Xbox to share the game with the rest of the world. DAMM did get the backward compatibility treatment, however, only on Xbox 360, and again restricted to North America.

I guess then, we're quite pleased Atari decided to take a punt on a DAMM remaster. Even though it seems to share many of the original's downfalls, Godzilla's WWE antics are going to be available on November 3rd on Xbox Series X|S, and best of all, I can play it from my lovely little home in Cornwall.

So, here's what the critics are saying about Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered:

"Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered brings the classic kaiju brawler roaring back with impressive rebuilt visuals, satisfying arena destruction, and chaotic multiplayer battles. While its limited roster and tedious character unlocks feel dated, the accessible combat and faithful Godzilla presentation make this a thoroughly entertaining return for the King of the Monsters."

"Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered feels like a new game with its graphical upgrade, held back slightly by a lack of any other meaningful new additions and a somewhat grindy gameplay loop."

"Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered is a satisfyingly faithful update to a beloved Kaiju brawler. It’s simple and fun, with local and online multiplayer keeping you around for the long haul despite a wafer-thin single-player experience. Now if we could just get a remaster of Save The Earth and Unleashed."

Review Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered (Switch 2) - The King Returns With A Fun But Thin Kaiju Brawler

"Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered is at its best when it simulates the idea of smashing kaiju toys together as a kid in short sessions with friends, and at its worst when it attempts to prolong a game that never had much depth to begin with. The result is a remaster I’m glad exists considering the drought of Godzilla brawlers, but with a few caveats–not to mention the sting of realizing that the original GameCube and Xbox game were absent of arbitrary grinding."

"Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered remains goofy fun, and Atari’s remaster looks and runs well. It’s still a rather simple game and probably not the most comprehensive when it comes to the characters and lore. But it’s a good time and offers quite a few different modes to get acclimated and beat up famous faces."

"Right now, Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered is a great version of a good retro game. Because the game was remade from the ground up, I wish the developers had made a super version of the Godzilla trilogy with more content. While Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered’s combat is really fun, there’s not much depth. There are lots of modes, and a bit of RPG unlocking, but this isn’t a deep fighting game that’s going to be played at tournaments. It’s a great budget buy for Godzilla fans. But more content would have made Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered a more obvious recommendation. If you’re at all curious, or have any fondness for the original game, it remains a fun time."

"In the media shadow of sacred monsters like Grand Theft Auto VI and The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Remake, only an equally fearless monstrosity could face them: Godzilla. Nearly a quarter century after its release on GameCube, Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee returns with a remaster that discreetly refreshes the audiovisual experience and improves the progression without betraying its structure. The core is that of a 3D fighting game in which kaiju clashes are accompanied by the destruction of entire cities, in a blaze of explosions and particle effects. Behind this chaos lies a combat system that's immediate, but not always precise and balanced, suspended somewhere between classic fighting games and the festive jumble of a party game. The mechanics are simple, despite a certain unwieldiness in the movements, and the best of the action is found especially in local multiplayer."

Well, there you have it, folks! Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered is an almost perfect example of a good '7' rated game. At the time of writing, the remaster is holding down a 72 score on Metacritic.

What's interesting is that the original Xbox release scored 73 on Metacritic - a single point higher than the remaster. It's funny how 23 years can pass and a game can be reviewed almost identically.
