Tirico caps fourth-quarter comeback as Seneca earned state title berth

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

TABERNACLE — One more time, Seneca's field leader connected with the team's vocal leader.

Just 27 seconds remained in Friday night's state semifinal with Burlington Township when quarterback Anthony Tirico lofted a perfect strike to Chase Horner in the right corner of the end zone. Horner made the grab in tight coverage to catapult the Golden Eagles into the lead.

An interception by Shane Richardson as time expired wrapped up a heart-pounding 30-27 Seneca victory, as the Golden Eagles head to their first ever state final.

"It's just trust," Tirico said. "(Chase and I) are brothers on and off the field. So, in a situation like that, with the game on the line, there's no one else I want making that catch, and that's why I gave him a chance."

Seneca will take on Old Tappan next Saturday at 4 p.m. in the NJSIAA Group 3 State Final at MetLife Stadium.

A tremendous season for Burlington Township (7-6), a season that included the team's first sectional title since 1977, came to a close.

The game saw four lead changes in the first half alone and six total. Seneca trailed 27-17 going into the fourth quarter.

"That was the best win in my eight years," coach Anthony Diorio said. "That was unbelievable. The boys just kept fighting."

Trailing 27-24, with a 1st-and-10 on the Falcons' 18, Tirico went to Horner. Diorio was not surprised.

"He's the vocal leader of our team. He just goes until there's nothing left in the tank," Diorio said of Horner. "He just went up and made a play, and it was awesome."

Tirico (9-of-21, 142 yds) threw three touchdown passes in the contest, including two to Horner (6-for-56 yards receiving; 10-for-51 rushing).

"I looked at the play call and I knew I was the only option — I had to go get it," Horner said. "I just had full trust in my quarterback to throw me a good ball and give me a fighting chance. When adversity strikes, we always prevail."

The senior also gathered in a 20-yard swing pass down the left sideline for a score early in the second quarter, which provided a 7-3 Seneca lead. The two touchdowns bookended one heck of a contest.

"Chase is a baller," Tirico said. "(On the winning score), I knew the line would give me the time I needed and Chase would make a play, and that's a play I knew he was gonna make."

The versatile Tirico also rushed for 138 yards including a four-yard score and hit Nate Sauerwald for a 67-yard touchdown that provided a 17-14 advantage in the second quarter. Even three uncharacteristic interceptions did not sink Seneca's poised senior quarterback.

"He had a couple tough plays in the third and fourth quarters," Diorio said. "So for him to bounce back and keep fighting and make that kind of a throw (on the final TD), that's impressive."

What set the winning drive in motion was a big play by the Seneca defense. Hit as he threw, Falcons quarterback Bryce Carter was intercepted by Peyton Preston, who came up with the game's most pivotal takeaway. Ten plays later, Seneca was in the end zone.

Nate Sauerwald made a huge impact on his three receptions. They went for a total of 86 yards, including a touchdown.

Aiden Binns pounded away for 152 yards on his 22 carries for Burlington Township. His four-yard touchdown run in the second quarter gave the Falcons a 14-10 lead before Seneca pushed back in front before the half.

Seneca got a shot in the arm when Brayden Van Horn pounced on a muffed punt by Burlington Township just as the fourth quarter got underway. Tirico dashed in from five yards out moments later to slice the deficit to 27-24.

Along with Binns' output, Burlington Township got a nice performance from Carter, who threw for 151 yards and a pair of scores. He found a wide open Mekhi Curry for a 33-yard TD in the second quarter and later hooked up with Gabriel Atz for a 63-yard touchdown that gave the visitors their 27-17 lead before Seneca made its final push.

Gerald Sackie had Burlington Township's other touchdown, a four-yard rush.

Hunter Donovan booted a 23-yard field goal for Seneca to open the scoring.

"You come out here, and it's a small community, you know?," Tirico said. "And yet these stands are full of people who love and care for this program. I couldn't ask for a better feeling."
