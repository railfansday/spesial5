Knicks 121, Cavs 102: Scenes from a second-half bench bash

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The Knicks' hopes of repeating as NBA Cup champions hinged on beating Cleveland (9-10) tonight and Miami on Friday, while Philadelphia had to beat Miami (which they did) and Indiana has to topple Philadelphia. Even then, advancement would come down to point differential, meaning New York (12-6) would likely need to win both games by double digits.

Consider the mission half completed. As you could deduce from the blazing blue court at Madison Square Garden, tonight's tilt was a Cup game. Three Knicks were sidelined by injury: OG Anunoby, Miles McBride, and Jordan Clarkson. Anunoby's absence was felt as the Cavaliers scored 61 first-half points (and a lopsided free-throw disparity stung, too). The adjustments Mike Brown made in the locker room paid dividends on the court. His squad applied stingier defense, got their turnovers under control, won the fourth quarter 39-17, and flipped the game to win, 121-102.

Of the six Knicks with double-digit points tonight, Jose Alvarado's barrage of fourth-quarter triples and his season-high / team-high 21 were arguably the most impactful. On a short-handed night, and with multiple starters playing through injury, Jose was once again prepared to step into the spotlight and contribute meaningful minutes. What a professional! What depth!

Three consecutive turnovers didn't help (two by Karl-Anthony Towns, one by Josh Hart) as the Knicks fell behind early in the first quarter. Midway through the period, Jalen Brunson scored seven in under two minutes to regain the lead. In this streaky contest, though, it made sense that he missed his next five attempts. Towns (12 PTS, 10 RBS, 7 AST, +21) was a game-time decision due to knee soreness, and his scoring showed the limp, producing a single point in the frame. Filling in for Anunoby in the first five, Landry Shamet made his sixth start of the season. He finished with a tidy 11 points on three made three-pointers.

The quarter was competitive until the final three minutes. After Pacôme Dadiet (yes, him!) drilled a triple to pull the Knicks within one, Cleveland rattled off a 10-0 run, powered by five consecutive points from Meleek Thomas, two Thomas Bryant free throws, and a Donovan Mitchell and-one. The latter easily had the biggest impact on the quarter, tallying 14 points, three dimes, and a steal. As for New York's bench support, Dadiet (7 PTS, 14 MIN) provided positive defensive efforts with a block and a steal, but neither Tyler Kolek nor Andre Drummond moved the needle. New York managed just six points over the final five-ish minutes, and Cleveland closed the quarter ahead, 34-23.

To start the second, the good guys outscored the villains by ten, chiseling an 11-point deficit to one. Credit Towns and his four points, two dimes, and two blocks for the comeback. Mohamed Diawara lent a triple, too, and Mikal Bridges found his rhythm, blasting out seven fast points. Naturally, Cleveland responded with a 9-2 stretch when James Harden (28 PTS, 1-8 3PT) finally got on the board. Dadiet drizzled water on our good feelings by missing three straight shots, and Brunson still couldn't regain his touch, going scoreless from the field in the period.

Playing from 55-44 down with four minutes left in the half, the hosts posted 11 unanswered points, eight of which belonged to Bridges. He'd finish the half with 17 points. This, my friends, is what's known as a circus shot:

Despite dealing with a sore shoulder, Hart picked Harden's pocket twice, converting one steal into a layup. Unfortunately, after Bridges tied the game at 55, Donovan Mitchell (30 PTS, 1-7 3PT) and Harden combined for six unanswered points, aided by a Bridges turnover. The home team won the quarter 33-27 but entered intermission trailing 61-56.

Through the half, Harden and Mitchell combined for 34 points in the half, while their bench contributed 13. New York had shot better from the field (54% to 49%) and significantly better from deep (50% to 31%), they were on the wrong end of the score due to a glaring free-throw disparity. New York had attempted just eight freebies, while Cleveland shot more than twice that. The Cavs outscored won the paint, 28-16, and the boards. Another glaring stat: Cleveland converted six Knicks miscues into 13 points, while New York squeezed just two points out of five giveaways.

Cleveland opened the second half on an 11-5 run to restore its double-digit lead. Down 74-65 midway through the period, the Knicks got a boost from an unexpected source: Tyler Nickel. The rookie sank a triple, followed by another from Landry Shamet, to cut the deficit to three. Nickel contributed five points but also committed four fouls in under six minutes—that will get straightened out with experience. Harden extended Cleveland's advantage to 85-77 with under three minutes remaining. But then another unexpected player pitched in: James Wiseman provided some valuable minutes off the bench, finishing the game with six points and seven boards in six minutes. Shamet helped the Knicks win the quarter 26-24 and enter the fourth trailing 85-82.

The Knicks opened the fourth quarter on a 12-0 run, turning a three-point deficit into a nine-point advantage in less than three minutes. Jose Alvarado (a team-high 21 points, 8-10 FG, 19 MIN) was the catalyst, drilling consecutive triples and picking Donovan Mitchell's pocket to set up a Dadiet layup. Hart took a turn orchestrating the offense, collecting four assists during the opening surge. The Ohio Players missed their first five shots, four of them by Mitchell, and committed two turnovers as the Knicks piled on the points.

Wiseman (!) helped goose New York's lead to 13 with four points and three offensive rebounds in a brief fourth-quarter stint. When Mitchell and Harden scored five unanswered points to trim the deficit to eight, Alvarado responded with a jumper, and Jalen Brunson buried a triple, his only basket of the quarter. Alvarado was the unquestionable star of the fourth, scoring 16 of his 21 points, including four three-pointers. Hart was nearly as impactful, contributing eight points, six assists, three rebounds, a steal, and a block in the frame. The Knicks closed on a 7-0 run, their defense holding Cleveland scoreless over the final two minutes and change.

Welcome back to associate head coach Chris Jent, who returned to the sidelines after a month away.

The Knicks hang around town to face the Heat on Friday in the last of their first-stage Cup games. Mo is folding and pressing your recap. Happy Knicksgiving, turkey fans.
