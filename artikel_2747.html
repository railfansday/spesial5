Celtics’ historic start against Mavericks proved plenty: ‘We got a really, really good team’

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

BOSTON — The Dallas Mavericks stood no chance coming into TD Garden on Sunday night. It only took the Boston Celtics 12 minutes to make that abundantly clear.

The Celtics outscored the Mavericks 42-9 in the first quarter. No, that's not a typo. It was the largest first-quarter point differential in franchise history and the biggest margin in a single quarter since the Los Angeles Lakers outscored the Sacramento Kings 40-4 in 1987.

"We got a really, really good team," Jayson Tatum said after Boston's 131-94 win. "And I think it really just starts with our depth. Especially in that fourth, fifth spot of whoever we want to come in and start. Whether it's Hugo [González]. Whether it's Baylor [Scheierman]. Whether it's J-Walsh. Ron [Harper Jr.], all those guys are interchangeable, more than capable."

Dallas entered on the second night of back-to-back, without Kyrie Irving, and coming off brutal consecutive road losses to the Minnesota Timberwolves and Cleveland Cavaliers — in which they allowed an average of 130 points, the most they've allowed back-to-back this season.

Boston entered riding a five-game winning streak with the best record in the Eastern Conference, fresh off three days' rest and with a squeaky-clean injury report.

From the jump, the difference between the two teams that crossed paths in the NBA Finals just two years ago became glaringly apparent.

The Celtics made 16 of their first 27 shots, including seven of their first 13 threes. The Mavericks, ice-cold as soon as they came out of the players' tunnel, opened the game 2-for-18, missing all eight of their 3-point attempts. From there, nothing changed.

At least nothing significant enough to turn the tide.

Dallas found somewhat of a rhythm from long range, knocking down five 3-pointers in the second quarter, then again in the third to cut Boston's lead down to 25 points, but that was as close as the Mavericks ever got to sniffing a competitive ballgame.

For Boston, concerns about maintaining double-digit leads were a non-factor. Celtics head coach Joe Mazzulla believes focusing on protecting a lead misses the bigger picture of what his team is truly honing in on.

"It's not about hanging onto leads," Mazzulla said. "That's a passive mindset. It's not about building on them. It's understanding the ebbs and flows of the game and constantly answering the questions: Are we playing well? Having a good process? Or are we not playing well? And that's something you learn over the course of the season."

Tatum was the only Celtic to play over 25 minutes, logging 30:08, giving Mazzulla plenty of leeway to unleash Boston's depth. González, Scheierman, Harper, Luka Garza and Devin Carter each played at least 12 minutes, combining to score 35 points off the bench.

Their ability to easily insert themselves into action remains a part of the team Mazzulla will never look past.

"They don't care about who starts, who finishes, 10 minutes, 24 minutes," Mazzulla said. "They just impact winning. They're not distracted by any nonsense, and they understand that any night they can do something different that gives us a chance to win — whether it's guarding the other team's best player, whether it's rebounding, whether it's crashing on the offensive glass. Those guys take pride in having a role on our team that's necessary to get to where we wanna get to."

In the closing seconds of the fourth quarter, with Boston's lead hovering around 40 points, Scheierman dove headfirst toward a loose ball that eventually went out of bounds. Mazzulla and the rest of the Celtics' bench immediately rushed over to help him up.

That best reflected the standard Mazzulla and his coaching staff expect from this group, playing until the final buzzer regardless of the score rather than easing up when the outcome appears decided.

Even Maine native Cooper Flagg was impressed.

"Just the level that they were competing at," Flagg said. "You know, something that we need to look at and look in the mirror and decide if we're competing at that same level. And if we want it as bad as they did, and I don't think we did."

Flagg continued his praise of Boston: "I feel like we weren't competing at a high enough level, and that's kind of been — over the last three games on this road trip — what it's been. So taking that from them. Just we gotta compete at a high enough level."

The Celtics checked just about every box on Sunday night:

Shoot 41.8 percent from 3-point range on 55 attempts: Check.

Build a double-digit lead and never relinquish it: Check.

Hold Flagg to 17 points on 33.3 percent shooting and a minus-30: Check.

Run Dallas into the ground and give the end of the bench some extended minutes: Check.

Limit every starter (besides Tatum) to fewer than 25 minutes: Check.

It was the most dominant version of this Celtics team we've seen all season.

Tatum's smiles in the locker room suggested he and his teammates know it: They've got something special brewing in Boston. That doesn't mean they'll score 130 points, hold an opponent to nine in a quarter and run teams into the ground every night.
