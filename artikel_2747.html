Wojo: Wolverines, Whittingham fight back. Can they do it again in Columbus?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

ANN ARBOR — Kyle Whittingham and the Wolverines earned respect and applause, and for what it's worth, likely a bowl bid. Those all count for something, and they'll count for even more if they follow up with a victory in Columbus next Saturday.

It's the question that will linger after another pulsating, final-play outcome, almost as improbable as all the others. The Wolverines handed No. 4 UCLA its first loss, piling drama on top of drama in an 18-16 triple-overtime victory Saturday, Nov. 21, in Michigan Stadium. The home season ended as it began, with a walk-off score and a spontaneous field-storming by the crowd.

Whittingham hasn't had many signature moments here, and I'm not sure one is enough. It's enough to suddenly make The Game — downgraded this year to "the game" — a hotter stew of debate and discord. Whittingham's job security rightly is under scrutiny, especially with a new athletic director, Trev Alberts, on the way. Michigan is 6-5 with seven of its games decided on the final possession. Fighting past the Bruins, who were 10-0 and one of two unbeatens left in the country, was gutty and laudable.

Meanwhile, the Buckeyes are 7-4 after losing to Nebraska, and Ryan Day is getting heat, just two years removed from a national title. What once appeared to be a rivalry blowout looks much more intriguing now. The Buckeyes have collapsed since quarterback Julian Sayin went down with an injury. And no matter how many times UM's offense collapses, its defense keeps rising.

Whittingham indeed may have bought time, and not just because UM should be headed to a bowl game. Many of us had him fired before Alberts even showed up, and the outcry is understandable. But it's also why, in today's crazy college football, you have to let it play out.

There's also a chance his quarterback, Bryce Underwood, bought more time, even as the passing game continues to falter. Underwood, reinstated as the starter, made the biggest throws of the game, including a 3-yarder to little-used senior tight end Jalen Hoffman on the game-winning two-point conversion.

Under no circumstances, not even after a scandal and abrupt coaching change, is 6-5 acceptable at UM. That said, there have been no circumstances in program history quite like what we've witnessed this season.

"The (possible coaching) change is above my pay grade," Whittingham said. "I just know we're waiting to see what the change actually turns out to be. I can say it's been a pleasure working for (former AD) Warde Manuel during these first 10 months here at Michigan. I actually gave him the game ball. But going forward with this win, I don't know exactly how to compare it to the new guy (Alberts), but it was big for our program."

Like many UM games this year, it appeared to be won and lost multiple times. UCLA tied it 10-10 on a 1-yard jump pass from running back Anthony Woods to tight end Brayden Loftin with 10 seconds left in regulation.

UCLA came in with a playoff spot virtually clinched, and with the hottest name in college coaching, Bob Chesney, who keeps popping up in UM fans' dreams. But when the Bruins lost quarterback Ty Dieffenbach to injury on the first play of overtime, Chesney's team looked doomed. And UM's defense made sure of it, stuffing backup Madden Iamaleava.

For much of the season, the Wolverines have been maligned and mis-aligned. Whittingham didn't stop searching, toggling between Underwood and Tommy Carr, shaking up the offensive line, firing coordinator Jason Beck. And the Wolverines never stopped battling, especially on defense.

None of it makes much sense. How does a team that took the Big Ten's supposed best to overtime and beyond, barely beat Western Michigan on a controversial Hail Mary and suffer a humiliating home loss to battered Michigan State? Against the top three teams in the conference — UCLA, Indiana, Iowa — the Wolverines won one in overtime, lost one in overtime and lost to Iowa by one.

What they did Saturday, missing their top two receivers, was both a testament and an indictment. They again showed their defense is elite and their offense needs a complete overhaul.

Whittingham thought it showed more than that.

"It shows that our guys have a lot of toughness, and that's our culture that we're trying to build and instill," Whittingham said. "And not to say they weren't tough in years past, but that's something that has always been a linchpin of programs that I've been involved with. Physicality, toughness, warrior mentality. That's something that's woven into the fabric of our culture."

They needed every bit of it, and will need it against OSU, which looks as vulnerable as UCLA was. The Wolverines said they were playing for the seniors, but they also were playing for Whittingham, who turned 67 Saturday. They sang "Happy Birthday" in the locker room, and their coach admitted it was one of the best presents he ever received.

"I've had some other really gratifying birthdays," Whittingham said of his 21 seasons at Utah. "But I don't think there's any that have been better than this. … I'm so proud of those guys. Love this football team."

Whittingham may appear out of touch at times, adjusting to a new culture and learning old traditions at UM. But he's never out of control, and through all the missteps, his players kept battling.

"He always believes in us, no matter what the situation is," Underwood said. "I feel happy to go out there and dominate and be the best we could possibly be for our team and for each other."

The defense under coordinator Jay Hill is so good, it should be spared from a new regime's overhaul. The Wolverines' secondary, led by Jyaire Hill, has been outstanding. Safety Chris Bracy, linebacker Troy Bowles and former Utah end John Henry Daley, are everywhere. UM sacked Dieffenbach four times and held UCLA — the Big Ten's top running team at 202.7 yards per game — to 107, and only 30 for standout Wayne Knight.

There's decent talent in spots on offense from Savion Hiter, who had his second straight 100-yard rushing effort, to receiver J.J. Buchanan, who missed the game with an injury. So did Andrew Marsh, recently charged with a misdemeanor charge of indecent exposure. Whittingham indicated it would be a one-game suspension, barring further developments.

Underwood remains the riddle, and he narrowly avoided the crushing mistakes in this one. He lost one fumble and nearly threw an interception that would've ended the game in the first overtime.

His accuracy issues aren't going away, and he was 11-for-24 for 127 yards. He ran for 31 yards and was sacked four times. He also lofted a perfect throw to freshman tight end Mason Bonner for a 6-yard touchdown early in the fourth quarter that put UM on top 10-3. Playing without his top two receivers, Underwood targeted the tight ends more often and effectively.

Afterward, he sounded like someone humbled by the trials.

"The way I took this season was more of a blessing," Underwood said. "I took this as lessons being learned and everything. I feel like the way you can show how to be a man is by facing adversity and how you take it home."

Obviously, UM's offense needs to be revamped no matter who's the head coach. If Whittingham is willing to change his staff, and maybe find another quarterback in the portal, perhaps he could make an argument for more time. I don't get the impression Alberts is the patient type, but if the Wolverines go to Columbus and beat the Buckeyes, anything is possible.

They earned another chance to make something of their season and Whittingham earned another chance to make his case. With one more win, it might be a case worth hearing.
