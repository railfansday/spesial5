Tobacco for Cancer Therapy? Panel Backs HPV Test Over Pap; Too Few Cancer Surgeons

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

— News, features, and commentary about cancer-related issues

Could the tobacco plant ironically provide a platform for developing paclitaxel, a drug commonly used to treat lung cancer? (University of Copenhagen via EurekAlert!)

Arcus Biosciences announced the termination of a phase III trial of its investigational CD73 inhibitor quemliclustat plus chemotherapy for advanced pancreatic cancer after an interim analysis showed no improvement in survival over chemotherapy and placebo.

Over the past few decades, a longstanding Black/white mortality disparity has decreased, in large part because of progress in treating cancer, heart disease, and HIV, according to a study by the American Cancer Society.

The U.S. Preventive Services Task Force has taken a preliminary step toward finalizing recommending human papillomavirus testing over Pap tests as the preferred screening method for cervical cancer in women 30 and older. (NBC News)

In an action designed to reduce treatment burden for patients, prescribers, and pharmacies, the FDA has changed the risk evaluation and mitigation strategies requirements for three drugs widely used to treat multiple myeloma -- thalidomide, lenalidomide (Revlimid), and pomalidomide (Pomalyst).

Women whose Hodgkin lymphoma was treated with newer therapies had a 25% reduction in the risk of second malignancies. (Journal of Clinical Oncology)

A shorter radiation therapy (RT) protocol for patients with breast cancer undergoing reconstruction proved to be just as effective as standard-duration RT, according to published findings of the previously reported RT CHARM trial. (University of Utah Huntsman Cancer Institute)

Treatment with an investigational drug blocking access to glutamine led to objective responses in two thirds of patients with platinum-resistant ovarian cancer in a preliminary clinical trial. (University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center)

Giredestrant (Wilserdly), the newly approved oral selective estrogen receptor degrader for breast cancer, will cost $22,500 a month. (Yahoo! via Reuters)

A recent sketch on "Saturday Night Live" got some laughs but also struck a chord that resonated with many caregivers. (CNN)

Almost half of U.S. counties do not have a cancer surgeon, which has been linked to increased mortality in colorectal and upper-digestive-tract cancers. (American College of Surgeons)

ProPublica examined concerns surrounding prior authorization practices for cancer care in Affordable Care Act plans.
