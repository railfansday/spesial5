The concessions field problem that breaks pay-for-listing data models

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Contribution schemes that credit MLS data by listing totals overlook whether key valuation inputs are complete. A single MLS can require a numeric concessions amount whenever concessions are yes, improving downstream appraisal and model reliability.  AI Summary

Metering who uses listing data is an engineering problem with engineering answers. Deciding what a contributed listing is worth is not, and the difficulty sits in the field definitions rather than in anybody’s policy.

The proposal itself is sound, and it is gaining ground. Meter extraction, credit the organizations that supply the data, and reward the brokerages that send more of their eligible inventory into the system rather than less. Paper agreements stop working the moment data leaves the system, and an incentive attached to the record travels wherever the record goes.A contribution model based primarily on listing counts risks rewarding volume rather than the completeness and usefulness of the information. That is the obvious unit, and it is the wrong one, because two listings entered on the same morning by the same brokerage can differ by the only thing a valuation needs.

The RESO Data Dictionary permits three answers to whether a closed sale carried concessions: yes, no and call listing agent. The ‘Concessions Amount’ sits beside it as a number field, ‘Concessions Comments’ as free text. All three belong to the closing group, which RESO describes as intended to describe facts that occurred at the closing of a transaction rather than offers made before it. RESO’s own guidance says recording this data consistently is important, and notes that adopting the newer concessions fields is optional for any organization.A concession is consideration. Fannie Mae specifically says concessions adjustments should reflect their impact on the transaction price, rather than automatically deducting the full dollar amount of concessions. In some cases, the market-based adjustment may be smaller than the concession or unnecessary. Also, RESO’s concessions fields for closed transactions shouldn’t be confused with fields advertising potential seller concessions before closing.

RESO explicitly distinguishes the two uses in its April 2024 guidance. It moves the figure a buyer paid without moving the figure the deed records, and that gap is the entire reason an appraiser adjusts for it. Fannie Mae requires adjustments to reflect the market’s reaction to the difference between properties, naming paired sales as one method among statistical analysis, modeling and other commonly accepted approaches. Every one of them needs the magnitude.

It is not one field. View carries 38 category words, among them ‘Bay’, ‘City Lights’, ‘Ocean’ and ‘Panoramic’, with no amount and no rating attached. ‘Entry Level’ carries the floor as a number and nothing joins the two, while Fannie Mae’s condo section requires special attention to a unit’s location within its project and says nothing about view at all.So, a contribution model priced by record count pays for the half of the work that costs nothing. An address, bedrooms, bathrooms and a price make a record. The concession amount, the extent of the view and the condition detail make it evidence. Paying the same for both, or partial credit for the thinner one, is a reliable way to end up with more records rather than better ones.

I think requiring a number whenever the answer to the concessions question is ‘yes’ is a rules change inside a single MLS’s authority, enforceable at the point of entry, and it costs an agent the half minute it takes to read a closing statement already in front of them. Apply it to the other fields that carry magnitude rather than category and a contribution model has something worth metering.The alternative is a market that pays for volume. Records multiply, credits clear and the fields that decide what a house is worth carry whatever they happen to carry, because nothing in the scheme ever asked for them.The governance conversation is about who may read the record. What the record contains is a separate question, and it is the one that can be answered this quarter by any organization willing to make a single field mandatory.Nikola Gojkovic is the founder of CMAflow and writes on residential valuation methodology.
