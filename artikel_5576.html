Ex-Alibaba AI chief's startup Pragmatik Labs closed funding at $5B value

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The Shanghai-based AI agent developer was valued at $2 billion in August, when it raised several hundred million dollars

Pragmatik Labs, launched by Lin Junyang after he served as Alibaba Group Holding's top AI executive, has secured a funding round that values it at $5 billion, according to Bloomberg.

The valuation is up from $2 billion in August, when the company completed its first financing, according to Bloomberg. That first round raised several hundred million dollars. The new round brought in tens of millions of dollars.

New investors include HyT Capital, CDH Investments and automaker Geely Automobile Holdings, according to Bloomberg. Representatives for Pragmatik, HyT, CDH and Geely did not respond to requests for comment from Bloomberg.

Pragmatik, a Shanghai company nicknamed p7k, develops AI agents that operate in virtual settings as well as real-world ones. Investors that took part in the first round were Gaorong Ventures, HSG, Tencent Holdings and Shanghai Engine Fund, an investment arm supported by the city government, according to Bloomberg.

Lin set up Pragmatik over the summer after leaving Alibaba, according to Bloomberg. He gets credit for overseeing the creation of Alibaba's Qwen family of large language models, which went on to underpin the company's main AI apps and services.

Lin is one of several Chinese artificial intelligence researchers who have launched research-oriented companies, called neolabs, aiming to take on established players such as DeepSeek and Moonshot, according to Bloomberg. The group is following a path laid by American researchers including Ilya Sutskever and Yann LeCun, both of whom quit large technology companies to found ventures of their own.

The neolabs want to emulate what Anthropic and OpenAI have achieved, but within narrower specialties such as equipping AI with an understanding of the physical world or creating coding systems that can improve on their own, according to Bloomberg.

Lin has said China probably will not overtake Anthropic and OpenAI within the coming three to five years, since computing resources are limited, according to Bloomberg.
