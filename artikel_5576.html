Antibiotic Paste Approved to Prevent Surgical Site Infections

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

— Bacfenssi becomes the first approved product for local application following colorectal surgery

The FDA approved doxycycline hyclate (Bacfenssi) to reduce the risk of surgical site infections (SSIs) in adults and kids undergoing colorectal surgery, the agency announced on Tuesday.

Sold as a powder to be reconstituted into an extended-release paste, the tetracycline product is indicated specifically for surgeries involving incisions larger than 20 cm, as efficacy for smaller incisions was not established in trials. Pediatric patients must weigh at least 45 kg, according to the agency.

It marks the first approved option for preventing SSIs that is applied locally, the FDA said. Before closing the incision, the paste is applied directly to the fascia and tissues of the abdominal wall along the incision.

Support for the approval came primarily from SHIELD II, a multinational double-blind phase III trial, which showed that adding the doxycycline hyclate paste to standard of care reduced the risk for SSIs, mortality, or surgical reintervention by 40% over the month after surgery. In the investigational arm, that composite outcome occurred in 10.9% of patients compared with 18.1% in the control arm (P=0.004).

When looking at SSIs specifically, a key secondary endpoint, the product reduced the risk by 60%, with infections recorded in 3.8% of the doxycycline hyclate arm and 9.5% of controls (P=0.001).

Commonly occurring following colorectal surgeries, SSIs "can lead to prolonged hospital stays, reoperation, and significant patient morbidity, including wound breakdown and systemic infection," the FDA noted. "Despite the administration of systemic antibacterial drugs prior to colorectal surgery to reduce SSIs, the infection rate remains substantial, underscoring the need for additional preventive strategies."

Adverse events observed in trials of doxycycline hyclate were mostly mild and included incision site reactions, procedural pain, anemia, and wound dehiscence.

Labeling for the antibiotic paste includes potential risks for tooth discoloration and inhibition of bone growth if used in pregnancy and in children, Clostridioides difficile infection, potential for microbial overgrowth, along with known class effects of tetracyclines -- photosensitivity, fixed drug eruption, pseudotumor cerebri, and anti-anabolic actions. Drug interactions are possible with concomitant use of anticoagulants.

Doxycycline hyclate can cause fetal harm, the FDA noted, and "breastfeeding is not recommended for 40 days after administration."

Launch of the product is expected in early 2027.
