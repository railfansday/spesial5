Tonali explains his ‘two stories’ with Milan after turning from fan into player

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Sandro Tonali has once again revealed his love for AC Milan, and spoke of the emotion of playing for his boyhood team.

Tonali played for Milan from 2020 to 2023, making 130 appearances and scoring seven goals, winning the Scudetto in 2021-22. He moved to Newcastle for €64m in what was a painful exit, given that he was being talked about as a potential future captain.

With the Magpies, the Lodi-born midfielder played 110 games, scoring 10 goals, before moving to Tottenham Hotspur in the last summer window for €108m. There, he has linked up with Roberto De Zerbi, someone who also has Milan in his heart.

There continues to be sporadic rumours about a return to the Rossoneri one day, but given his value it remains a pipe dream for now. Tonali was interviewed by Fútbol Emotion Italia and he recalled some significant moments that he had at Milan, with his comments relayed by Calciomercato.com.

“I have two stories with AC Milan. The first is up until I was 20, when I had never fully embraced the AC Milan world. The second is from 20 onwards for the following three years,” he began.

“I was a fan for a long time. Then when the matches allowed it, maybe there was a game at San Siro when in Serie B we almost always played on Saturdays, we often went to watch. It was something passed down in the family from my father, so I’ve always had it even since I was a child.

“Then past 20 years of age it changed, because as a player you can’t maintain the same relationship with the team that you have as a fan. I did that the first year too, but I felt it too much, so after that I experienced it differently.

“But maybe it was even more beautiful because you put the fan aside and you started to realise that I suddenly found myself there, with the team of my heart and in a stadium like San Siro.

“The first year wasn’t easy, I found myself struggling a bit. But it went well for me also from the point of view of playing without fans. It wasn’t nice, but for me personally as a player at that moment it was good because there was less pressure.

“But all in all it was bad because that year without fans it all seemed like friendlies, it didn’t feel like football. Milan means a lot to me.”
