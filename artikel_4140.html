8 Best Anime Where Friendship is Built Through Small, Everyday Moments

Oh, the sweet art of life known as friendship; it’s beautiful. It makes us realize just how deep human connections are forged, and that this extends to fictional media, whether it be your favorite bromances in gaming or just some of the best presentations of this bond showcased in movies, shows, or even anime.And you see, that’s exactly where some of my expertise lies, both professionally and personally. Being the only child of my parents, the only kind of sibling-like relationship I’ve had growing up, and even now, are my countless friends I’ve met online or IRL. Call it a “My friends are my Power” situation.

This list goes into extensive detail on the best romance anime where falling in love is just the beginning, but these shows prove that happily ever after is never that simple.

So, thinking about them and then my rather intermediate experience with anime, I woke up today and figured, let’s discuss some of my hand-picked anime where friendship is built through small, everyday moments. Where, no matter the ongoing circumstances that may ensue, the main cast manages to grow an everlasting bond with each other until the end of the show’s runtime.

I bet only some hardcore Kyoto Animation fans have seen this show, but trust me, it’s such a warm ray of sunshine. The series follows Tamako, a cheerful schoolgirl who spends most of her time helping at her family's mochi shop. Between school and her responsibilities at the shop, she shares plenty of moments with her best friends, Midori and Kanna.

But that balance is exactly what creates this sweet blend of friendship with the everyday life of a neighbourhood shop, pretty much exactly what you want from a slice-of-life anime. Oh, and if you want a romantic subplot payoff for Tamako, be sure to check out the sequel movie, Tamako Love Story, which is just as heartwarming as the OG show.

I’m switching tracks to a recent anime, one whose manga I read a bit of before the anime came into production. Skip and Loafer is mainly centered on Mitsumi Iwakura, a country girl who moves to Tokyo to attend a prestigious high school. While she's academically gifted, navigating the social dynamics of a city school proves more challenging than her studies. Fortunately, she meets Shima and gradually gets to know the other wildcard cast of classmates.

And while there is a romantic slow-burn development between Mitsumi and Shima, it’s not the main focus, since the main draw is Mitsumi slowly forming friendships with everyone through awkward conversations, shared school experiences, or small acts of consideration. It is genuinely a great slice-of-life seasonal show to watch from recent years of anime.

If you loved Frieren: Beyond Journey's End, this list goes into detail on the best anime to watch if you loved its delicate yet naturally paced vibe.

This one’s familiar to many readers, I hope. While Barakamon initially seems like it’s centered on a struggling calligrapher trying to reignite his creative spark, the story’s real soul comes from the surprising connections he builds after relocating to the Goto Islands.

Seishuu Handa starts out as a gifted but pretty snobbish artist who gets banished to a sleepy countryside village after an embarrassing incident, and he's so used to his quiet city life that he’s totally not ready for the lively locals who pop by his house for all sorts of reasons.

But you see, that’s the beauty of this show. Handa’s companionship with these locals slowly changes his outlook on life, especially through his interactions with the mischievous little girl named Naru. He develops a family-like bond here, and that's one reason this show is a perfect pick here.

This one’s actually one of my best friends’ favorite shows that he’s been begging me to watch for years, and I can honestly understand why. It's not only because of my fondness for Yuru Camp, but also because Non Non Biyori is one of THE defining Iyashikei (healing) shows on the block. It takes place in the sleepy countryside village of Asahigaoka, where a tiny school has only five students across different grade levels.

The anime lets you find plenty of warmth in these girls’ everyday interactions, whether it's Renge's amusing childlike observations or Natsumi and Komari's sibling antics. There is no kind of elaborate storyline either; it’s simply one of the coziest shows in the medium.

If you wanted peak relatability, then look no further than Hitoribocchi no Marumaru Seikatsu. That name is a mouthful for anyone preferring English titles, I know, but bear with me if it's your first time hearing about this cute little show because it’s fit for anyone who’s ever tried to get out of their comfort zone.

It’s about a girl named Hitori Bocchi (no, not the pink-haired anxiety rockstar) who is so anxious that talking to classmates feels like a final exam, but her goal of making friends is so adorable yet a little painful to watch. What I love is the show’s patience because her victories are tiny, like a successful greeting or a shared lunch, and the show treats each moment as a milestone.

As much as I wanted it at the top for personal reasons, it’s also fine to include Yuru Camp here, because I could gush about it endlessly.

The series follows Rin Shima, a girl who enjoys camping alone, and Nadeshiko Kagamihara, an outgoing newcomer who shares her enthusiasm for exploring the outdoors, even if her initial impression here is sleeping on a bus stop bench at night. Their initial encounter eventually brings them into contact with a wider circle of friends at their school who also enjoy spending their free time camping as well.

The show is one of the best modern healing anime out there, especially since it teaches you so much about camping itself with Rin and the others’ trips. Even better is how it takes place at actual campsites and in key locations across Japan’s rural prefectures and towns.

It would’ve felt wrong not to mention K-On! on this list, especially given the whole emphasis on anime friendships with this list. What starts as an attempt to save her school's Light Music Club eventually becomes one of anime's most endearing friendships, as Yui Hirasawa joins Ritsu, Mio, and Tsumugi despite having practically no musical experience.

Of course, the girls spend plenty of time practising, but that's hardly what makes their relationship so enjoyable, since their afternoons are just as likely to involve drinking tea, chatting about random, inconsequential things, or finding excuses to avoid doing any actual work. Even after Azusa joins the club, watching this group become increasingly comfortable with one another is a genuine delight.

To me, if there’s one anime that defines a goofy, whimsical yet endearing friendship, it's Azumanga Daioh. Originally released in 2002, this beloved slice-of-life comedy follows six high school girls with wildly different personalities: the exceptionally gifted Chiyo Mihama, the absent-minded Osaka, the energetic Tomo, the short-tempered Yomi, the quiet, reserved Sakaki, and the competitive, eccentric Kagura.

Much of the show revolves around everyday moments, whether it's studying together, going on summer vacations, or simply finding new ways to annoy one another with all kinds of tomfoolery; these girls will always find a way to make you grin at the slightest bit of their lighthearted banter or actions.

And you see, watching this group become increasingly comfortable with one another throughout their high school journey makes their inevitable graduation feel incredibly bittersweet. It’s genuinely one of the shows my friends and I have come to adore, not because of its niche memes, but because there’s an actual heartwarming friendship here that grows with each episode.
