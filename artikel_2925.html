Predictions: Lions vs. Tampa Bay Buccaneers

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Nolan Bianchi, Richard Silva, John Niyo and Bob Wojnowski of The Detroit News offer predictions for Sunday's game between the Lions and Tampa Bay Buccaneers at Ford Field in Detroit (1 p.m., Fox/97.1 FM).

▶ Nolan Bianchi: The Lions' last three wins have been emotionally charged, but they return home for some normalcy against the Tampa Bay Buccaneers. Tampa will be well-rested coming off a bye, while the Lions will be fighting the drag of international travel, further adding to the "trap game" sentiment surrounding a contest against the 3-6 Bucs. But with one injury wake-up call after another on the defensive side of the ball, I expect the Lions' defense to be well-prepared and playing with a sense of urgency. Offensively, the Lions know exactly what they're up against when facing one of the league's best units. I can't imagine Dan Campbell will have his team ill-prepared for what's sure to be a rock fight. Pick: Lions, 23-17

▶ John Niyo: The NFL schedule-maker didn't do the Lions any favors here. They're coming off a game in Germany last Sunday and they're facing a Tampa Bay team coming off a bye. But Cincinnati proved last week that doesn't have to be a losing proposition. And Dan Campbell seems hell-bent on his team doing the same. It won't be easy facing a top-five defense that also leads the league in takeaways this season. Still, Tampa is 0-3 against the NFC North this season, while the NFC South is 8-19 outside of its own division. So I'll take the Lions to keep rolling with that Thanksgiving clash looming. Pick: Lions, 24-17

▶ Richard Silva: On paper, the Bucs are much better than their 3-6 record indicates. They run the ball with efficiency and volume, and they stop the run with consistency and haste. Plus, they lead the NFL in takeaways and head coach Todd Bowles has dialed up a blitz on 43.9% of opponents' dropbacks this season, the second-highest rate in the NFL, behind the Minnesota Vikings (72.3%). This'll be a wonderful test for Detroit's offensive line, which has helped the Lions rush for more than 100 yards in five straight contests. The Bucs, meanwhile, haven't allowed more than 100 rushing yards since Week 1, when the Cincinnati Bengals reached 106. If Jahmyr Gibbs wants to win Offensive Player of the Year, an impressive performance against this stout defense would be quite the feather in his cap. Pick: Lions, 23-20

▶ Bob Wojnowski: The Bucs might be the most-dangerous 3-6 team in recent history. They're No. 1 in the NFL in rushing and No. 1 against the run. Their defense under Todd Bowles also has more takeaways than anybody and allows the fewest explosive plays. So what does this require from the 7-2 Lions? Patience. Jared Goff has to pick his spots and Jahmyr Gibbs has to gain more tough yards than pretty yards. Mainly, the Lions' defense has to wait for the Bucs quarterback – whether it's Baker Mayfield or Jalon Daniels – to make a mistake. It's a fine time for Kerby Joseph's return, and a finer time for him to resume what he does and pick off a pass. The Bucs' offensive line grinds well and Bucky Irving is an extremely underrated back. But Tampa Bay is last in the league in passing, and if this turns out to be a ball-control slugfest, I'll take Gibbs and the Lions' steadily developing offensive line. Pick: Lions, 24-13
