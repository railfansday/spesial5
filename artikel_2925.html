Rocket Lab Sold Out Neutron Through the 2030s Before It Ever Flew. Here’s Why the Stock Fell After Earnings Anyway.

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Rocket Lab's Neutron rocket has yet to take off, but it has customers booked through 2030. So why is the stock down?

By Courtney Carlsen – Nov 19, 2026 at 9:45AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Rocket Lab ›

Rocket Lab's (RKLB +1.86%) next big opportunity hasn't left the launchpad, but customers are already lining up. The Space Exploration Technologies challenger has carved out a niche for itself in launching small payloads into orbit. Its next move is Neutron, a medium-lift rocket that could open a new high-margin revenue stream, with 16 flights planned through 2030.

If Neutron's flight schedule is filling up, why did Rocket Lab shares fall after earnings? The answer starts with the gap between booking a launch and delivering one.

Rocket Lab has emerged as the top space launch company after SpaceX thanks to its Electron launch vehicle. This small-lift rocket has a payload capacity of roughly 300 kilograms (kg) to low Earth orbit, which pales in comparison to SpaceX's Falcon 9, which has a payload capacity of 22,800 kg. This massive difference gives Rocket Lab an advantage: It can provide dedicated launches catered to customers' specific needs.

Electron earns roughly $8 million to $10 million per launch. However, to better compete for larger contracts and improve profit margins, Rocket Lab has been developing its medium-lift Neutron launch vehicle, which can carry payloads over 40 times larger than Electron.

At 13,000 kg, Neutron can enable human spaceflight, including cargo and crew resupply to space stations, and help it compete more effectively for contracts to deliver government or commercial satellite constellations into space. According to management, recent flights are commanding up to $70 million per launch and margins of 50% or more.

Getting Neutron off the ground hasn't been smooth. On Jan. 21, the company experienced a testing failure during a hydrostatic pressure test when one of its tanks ruptured, requiring management to refine its tank manufacturing process. Then in October, the company discovered tiny air pockets in the carbon-fiber composite of Neutron's forward dome.

As a result of these setbacks, the company has pushed back Neutron's launch timeline to 2027. Given the setbacks, CEO Peter Beck hasn't committed to a specific time period. Instead, the company wants to see its Stage 1 tank succeed in testing before committing to a given launch window.

These setbacks have weighed on Rocket Lab's stock, which has fallen 64% from its 52-week high price of $151 per share.

On top of this, Rocket Lab has a growing space systems business that helps complement its launch services. Here, the company provides a wide range of services for engineering and manufacturing space components, including space solar cells, satellite trackers, ground command-and-control systems, on-orbit operations management, and satellites.

As part of this growing business, on June 29, Rocket Lab announced it would acquire Iridium Communications, valuing the company at roughly $8 billion. To fund this acquisition, Rocket Lab issued 29 million new shares in September, adding further selling pressure on the stock.

The acquisition gives Rocket Lab Iridium's global satellite communications network spectrum, including 66 active satellites, further expanding its end-to-end space stack.

In the third quarter, Rocket Lab booked eight Neutron missions totaling $515 million, and the company now has committed to 16 missions over the next several years. Because roughly half of its launches will be for its own satellite constellations, available commercial slots for Neutron are booked into the 2030s.

According to CFO Adam Spice, the company expects adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) to turn positive about six months after Neutron's first successful flight, with positive free cash flow coming later.

Rocket Lab continues to build on its end-to-end space business, and Neutron will be a big part of its future. For investors bullish on the long-term outlook for the space economy in the next decade and beyond, Rocket Lab is a top pure-play space stock to buy now.
