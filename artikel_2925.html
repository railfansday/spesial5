A customer walks into a CAVA Group (CAVA +6.74%) location, grabs a salad or bowl, and chooses their toppings as they move down the assembly line. It is a simple, repeatable process, but behind the scenes, it represents a massive logistical operation that has scaled to 500 locations while maintaining consistent food quality. CAVA presents a classic high-growth, high-valuation trade-off. The company is rapidly scaling a popular Mediterranean fast-casual concept with strong unit economics, though its premium stock price leaves little room for operational missteps or slowing foot traffic.

The latter, combined with the Cyclospora outbreak, contributed to shares plunging from a 52-week high of $98.79 earlier this year, falling nearly 50% to its current level. Same-restaurant sales rose 9% year over year in its fiscal second quarter ended July 12, 2026, but fell sharply to only a 3% increase in Q3, as customers were scared off by the Cyclospora onset. Yet these short-term headwinds provide an opportunity for investors to jump in.

While its valuation remains elevated, CAVA's forward price-to-earnings ratio of 70 is at a low point for the past year. Moreover, the company is at around 500 locations, halfway toward its goal of 1,000 restaurants by 2032. This gives it plenty of room for further growth in the years ahead.

CAVA enjoys a dominant early-mover advantage in the Mediterranean fast-casual niche. It's resonating with younger, health-conscious diners while extending brand reach through its consumer packaged goods (sauces and dips) in grocery stores. These factors point to CAVA's stock rebounding into 2027 and beyond once worries over the Cyclospora scare subside.
