Chelsea are great entertainers but possess clear weakness

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Chelsea are promising to be this season’s Premier League entertainers and yet manager Xabi Alonso seemingly believes less is more when it comes to possession.

Alonso has started with two wins from two league games in charge of Chelsea, with his team scoring seven goals and conceding five. So far, it has been a lot of fun – if not entirely convincing.

But perhaps the most significant numbers are around how much – or how little – Chelsea are having the ball. Having finished their 3-2 victory over Fulham with 38.11 per cent of possession, Chelsea had just 25.6 per cent in the 4-3 success over Brighton and Hove Albion and successfully completed 146 passes. Both were their lowest figures on record in the Premier League.

“The data is not important,” Alonso said. “It’s how you play and what we can do better. I don’t say ‘today we need to have 60 per cent or 20 per cent’. I never talk about those things. It’s about how to prepare, how to be more competitive.”

Chelsea’s third goal against Brighton, scored in the 32nd minute, best summed up the London club under Alonso to date. It took the home side three passes to go from the edge of their own penalty area to João Pedro scoring against his former club.

Jorrel Hato passed the ball to Morgan Rogers, he then released Hato down the left and the wing-back slid the ball across for João Pedro to finish. It was a devastating counter-attack and should have put Chelsea on course for a much easier win.

Joao Pedro finishes off a deadly Chelsea counter-attack and gives the Blues a 3-0 lead 🔥 pic.twitter.com/wAr0ckIgXJ

“After 30 minutes, we are 3-0 and probably, in that moment, we have played too much the result,” Alonso said. “It’s quite normal, it happens in many teams. I am sure in the future we can manage it better.”

It is one thing outscoring Fulham and a depleted Brighton team without having the ball, but can Chelsea do that against the bigger and better teams? We will get an early indicator in the next game against Arsenal at the Emirates.

Alonso’s approach has some similarities to the way in which Antonio Conte set up his Chelsea title-winning team. Just as Conte converted Victor Moses into an effective right wing-back, Alonso started Pedro Neto there on Sunday.

After Roméo Lavia had opened the scoring, it was Neto who grabbed Chelsea’s second goal against Brighton by drifting into the penalty area to finish from Rogers’s low cross.

But whereas Conte’s team gave up little at the back, Alonso’s Chelsea are yet to balance their effective transitional play with shutting out their opponents.

Neto was partly to blame for Brighton’s first goal, as he missed a challenge in the build-up to Malick Yalcouyé’s brilliant volley. Making his Chelsea debut, goalkeeper Emiliano Martínez could do nothing to stop it and can expect to be kept busy this season if things do not change.

“I have come here to challenge and to try and win the Premier League,” Martínez said. “I know the talent we have up front. I try to bring that solidity at the back.”

Conte’s Chelsea had N’Golo Kanté to mop up in midfield and that is an area of the pitch that is causing Alonso some difficulties. Manchester City target Enzo Fernández was once again left out of the squad and Moisés Caicedo was not deemed fit enough to start.

Caicedo was eventually thrown on when Chelsea were trying to stop Brighton completing a remarkable comeback, but he was quickly forced off again with an injury. The club have been exploring a move for Monaco midfielder Lamine Camara and that need looks even more pressing now.

“We want the best for the team and that’s why we took a decision [on Fernández] against Luton and today,” Alonso said. “What’s going to develop? We’re not sure. We can prepare. We are preparing for all the scenarios. But I assure you that whatever happens, from Wednesday [when the transfer window is shut] we’ll have a good enough squad to compete in all the Premier League games.”

Chelsea’s new set-piece coach Austin MacPhee had celebrated Lavia’s goal, which came partly as a result of a corner but owed more to a mistake from Brighton goalkeeper Bart Verbruggen.

MacPhee, however, will not have liked Brighton’s second-half goals. It was from a short corner that the visitors managed to work the ball to Pascal Gross and his cross was diverted into his own net by João Pedro.

Fabian Hürzeler, the Brighton manager, said his team “dominated” the game, but acknowledged: “I think if you spend so much money on these kinds of players, like Rogers, Palmer or João [Pedro], then, of course, a team like Chelsea can rely on this individual quality. And they showed it in certain moments of the game.”

Palmer, João Pedro and Rogers are clearly going to be a huge problem for Chelsea’s opponents. But Alonso’s team cannot rely on having to score three or four goals to win games. And they might need to have a bit more of the ball.
