7 Greatest Romantasy Book Series of All Time, Ranked

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Romance and fantasy have been closely bound for literally centuries, but there’s no denying that the relatively recent trend of "romantasy" books has pushed this genre-blend to greater heights than ever before. In the past decade or so, romantasy books have sold millions upon millions of copies around the world and developed massive international fanbases. However, as with any genre, not all romantasy books are equally great.

Many, if not most, romantasy books tend to fall short of the mark with critics and readers, finding limited audiences and fading quickly from the limelight. However, there are a handful of great romantasy books that have proven successful enough to spawn major series, with multiple hit novels and further expansions. These series draw the attention of millions of readers around the world with each new release, and many of them have also landed lucrative film and television deals that are sure to raise them to even greater heights once they arrive on our screens. With all that said, here’s our ranked selection of some of the greatest romantasy book series of all time.

Written by Jennifer L. Armentrout, the Blood and Ash series is a high-fantasy romance series that has six books so far, with a seventh planned for 2027. The story takes place across the vampire kingdoms of Solis and Atlantia, following Penellaphe "Poppy" Balfour, a young woman chosen at birth to be the Maiden, living an isolated and controlled life while she waits for her Ascension. The first book, From Blood and Ash, sees Poppy’s life take an unexpected turn when she meets Hawke, a royal guard with a dark secret.

The Blood and Ash series takes the vampire romance genre in a new direction, delivering an addictive, high-stakes story anchored by the strong chemistry of its leads. The series has also been expanded to include a prequel series, Flesh and Fire, and multiple companion works, which haven’t all been that well-appreciated, but the primary novels are still quite engaging. The series has proven a bestseller, and a TV adaptation is currently in early development.

Written by Eliza Phillips and Annie Paige under the shared pen name Sable Sorensen, The Wolves of Ruin series is set in a world where humans and direwolves form unbreakable bonds to fight for survival. The series follows Meryn Cooper, who trains to become one of the Bonded in order to save her sister. But to do that, she’ll have to survive the grueling competition known as the Bonding Trials.

Frequently compared to Fourth Wing and The Hunger Games, The Wolves of Ruin combines direwolves, a deadly training system, and a classic enemies-to-lovers romance. The series started with Dire Bound, which was initially self-published before being picked up by Hachette, and the planned trilogy has since become a New York Times bestseller. The second book, Fury Bound, was released in 2026, and a third and final novel, Ruin Bound, is expected in 2027.

Written by Callie Hart, Fae & Alchemy is a romantasy series centered on Saeris Fane, a young woman with alchemical powers. When she finds herself transported to a Fae realm, Saeris becomes entangled in an ancient conflict. Things get even worse when she mistakenly binds herself to the Fae warrior Kingfisher, who will not hesitate to destroy her for the sake of his people.

Fae & Alchemy began with 2024’s Quicksilver, a self-published novel that proved very popular on BookTok and landed Hart a publishing deal with Hodderscape. With plenty of steaminess, action, and banter, the series presents a darker take on fae romance that’s resonated with many people, selling more than three million copies across the books. Quicksilver was followed by the 2025 book Brimstone, with a third and final novel scheduled for 2027. The series has also been picked up for a film adaptation by Netflix.

Four worlds and four completely different ideas of what a good life looks like. This is where you belong, what it would ask of you, and what you would get back in return.

You belong somewhere old enough that the mountains have names and somebody can still tell you who gave them. Middle-earth is for people who think the right thing is worth doing even when it will not be finished in their lifetime.

You want the sea, a crew, and absolutely nobody telling you where you are allowed to go. The Grand Line is the world for people whose main ambition in life is finding out what is over there.

You would rather be feared and useful than liked and useless. The Continent is a world where the monsters are the straightforward part of the job and the people are where it all gets complicated.

You want the myths to be real and loose in the actual world, and you want to find out at about thirteen that you were never anywhere near as ordinary as everybody kept telling you.

A gothic romantasy duology written by Rachel Gillig, The Stonewater Kingdom kicked off with the 2025 novel The Knight and the Moth. The series is centered on Sybil Delling, a Diviner who can see the future in dreams. Confined to a cathedral alongside five others like her, Sybil’s life takes an unexpected turn when she meets the rude, heretical, and handsome knight Sir Rodric. When her fellow Diviners start disappearing, Sybil is forced to seek the knight’s help to find them.

The Stonewater Kingdom series is a lot moodier than most of the titles on this list, delivering an atmospheric story with an ominous cathedral, fog-bound landscapes, prophecy, and court intrigue. The first book was a big success, receiving a lot of praise from readers, and the second book, The Knave and the Moon, was released in September 2026. Though the duology is now complete, a companion book, The Sprite and the Gargoyle: A Bartholomew Tale, is expected to be released in 2027.

Written by Stephanie Garber and set in the same world as her Caraval series, Once Upon a Broken Heart is a separate series of three books and a companion novella without direct ties to the earlier books. The series follows Evangeline Fox, who seeks the help of the mercurial and powerful Jacks after the person she loved marries another. What starts as a simple bargain soon transforms into a dangerous romance.

The Once Upon a Broken Heart series takes the idea of fairy-tale romance and pushes it to darker territory, and the books have earned praise for their richly imagined world-building. A major bestseller and TikTok hit, the original trilogy consists of the books Once Upon a Broken Heart, The Ballad of Never After, and A Curse for True Love. A companion novella, The Mirror of Infinite Endings, was released in 2026.

Written by Rebecca Yarros, the Empyrean series is an ongoing collection of new adult fantasy romance novels centered on Violet Sorrengail. Forced by her mother to become a dragon rider, Violet endures deadly competitions that push her to the limit, while developing an unexpected romance with rival rider Xaden Riorson. The series includes the 2023 smash hit Fourth Wing and its sequels, Iron Flame and Onyx Storm, with two more books planned. Additionally, a short story collection, Threshing Day, was released in September 2026.

Fourth Wing was one of the biggest BookTok hits in the romantasy genre, and that momentum has continued into the sequels. It’s an engaging romance saga that features fast-paced action, high-tension romance, and, of course, dragons. A series adaptation of the Empyrean series is currently in the works, with Yarros serving as an executive producer on the project alongside Michael B. Jordan, Lisa Joy, Jonathan Nolan, Liz Pelletier, Athena Wickham, and showrunner Meredith Averill.

Sarah J. Maas’s A Court of Thorns and Roses is by far the most popular romantasy book series of our time. The series consists of six books so far, with a seventh, A Court of Forgotten Melody, due in January 2027, and an eighth and final installment in the works. The series is centered on Feyre Archeron, following her on adventures across the faerie lands of Prythian.

Widely regarded as the gold standard of modern romantasy series, A Court of Thorns and Roses is a New York Times bestseller, with over 13 million copies sold worldwide. The books have also earned critical acclaim and won several accolades. A TV adaptation was in the works at Hulu until it was scrapped in 2025, but the fanbase is still holding out hope for the project to find a second chance at a new home.
