HBO Max is going all in on its end-of-the-year and New Year’s strategy. Firstly, the streaming platform has launched its Black Friday sale as of November 23 (it will last till November 30), with its Basic (with Ads) subscription available at 50% off for the whole year. Then the first-ever Harry Potter series arrives on December 25. But that’s not all. There are several more movies and TV shows arriving on the platform in the upcoming month or so that will help the platform retain all those new subscriptions.

There are three franchise plays happening simultaneously. Harry Potter, the entire Rocky saga, plus all three Creed films arriving together on December 1 for Rocky’s 50th anniversary. And the drip-drop franchise-play is just the tip of the iceberg. For DC fans, there’s a fresh new animated film coming in that will be the first of three and is helmed by Jeff Wamester. The back half will then add Luther, Alien: Romulus, and A24’s Onslaught. Not to mention dozens of other titles that beef up the releases so much more.

The DC release is Batman: Knightfall Part I, which is the first part of the R-rated trilogy and will be released on December 7, 2026. Then there’ll be Part 2: Knightquest, which is set for release on December 15, 2026. The third part will arrive sometime in 2027.

The animated Batman: Knightfall trilogy adapts DC’s enormous 1993–1994 comic-book saga, which is really three connected storylines: Knightfall, Knightquest, and KnightsEnd. The story begins with Bane deliberately exhausting Batman by freeing Gotham’s criminals from Arkham Asylum. This forces Bruce Wayne into a relentless gauntlet before Bane finally breaks his back. This is essentially what Christopher Nolan’s The Dark Knight Rises followed, albeit a different version of it.

But the original comics from that point onward follow a different path. Bruce is replaced by Jean-Paul Valley/Azrael, whose increasingly brutal version of Batman becomes a problem of its own, before the saga ultimately becomes about Bruce recovering both physically and psychologically and reclaiming the mantle. DC is splitting that material across three feature-length animated films because the original crossover was simply too large for one movie. The adaptation is also trying to preserve the 1990s comic identity rather than modernize everything.
