‘Superman’ Star’s New Heist Thriller Is Officially 2026's Latest Box Office Bomb

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Nicholas Hoult has enjoyed a pretty remarkable run over the past few years, appearing in everything from The Menu and Nosferatu to James Gunn's Superman, where he stepped into the role of Lex Luthor. With a growing collection of major franchises and acclaimed performances under his belt, the British star has become one of Hollywood's most recognizable leading men. Unfortunately, that hasn't translated to box office success for his newest movie.

How to Rob a Bank has officially opened to just $5.175 million domestically across 2,810 theaters, making it one of Hoult's weakest wide-release debuts. The Amazon MGM Studios thriller also earned approximately $5.1 million internationally, bringing its worldwide total to $10.275 million following its first weekend in US theaters. The movie reportedly cost around $40 million to produce, meaning its opening represents a difficult start for a film that will need considerably more revenue to cover production and marketing expenses.

In How to Rob a Bank, Hoult is The Wolf, an anonymous YouTuber whose merry band of bank robbers — played by Pete Davidson, Anna Sawai, and Rhenzy Feliz — go viral broadcasting their heists on their channel. As they continue to garner more views, an FBI agent (John C. Reilly) enlists the help of a hacker under house arrest (Zoë Kravitz) to help track them down. Director David Leitch spoke to Collider's Steve Weintraub earlier this month about the inspiration behind the film, and how there's more to it than a simple cat-and-mouse thriller.

"It was very, very contemporary. I think these characters, although you don't really find out their motivations until the second act, which I've held off that reveal by choice, they're very relatable. People are dealing with health care debt, people are dealing with homes they can't afford, people are dealing with medical issues, and the system is not necessarily working for them."

Leitch added that he knew he still had to make the movie entertaining as well as informative, however, adding: "That's what art is supposed to do, and that's what this film is supposed to do, outside of it being a big, fun commercial heist movie. I think I set that up in the first act, but then I'm taking you on a journey."
