Will Robert Pattinson’s Bond Casting Help Land Him His First Oscar Nomination?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

How will Robert Pattinson‘s casting as James Bond affect his Oscar chances? The hard-working star, who was established by the “Twilight” franchise, has ricocheted from Christopher Nolan projects (“Tenet” and “The Odyssey”) and the DC franchise’s “The Batman” (“Part Two” is shooting in London) to riskier indies like the Safdies’ “Good Time” and Robert Eggers’ “The Lighthouse.”

This award season, Pattinson was juggling three long-shot potential Oscar nods: Best Actor for A24’s marital deconstruction “The Drama,” and Supporting Actor for two Snidely Whiplash villains, lead suitor Antinous in Universal’s blockbuster “The Odyssey” and shape-shifter Scytale in Warner Bros.’ “Dune: Part Three,” respectively. His best chance at an Oscar nomination, though, was always for his creepy lead turn as TV journalist Chris Hansen in “Primetime,” another A24 release. When Daniel Craig was cast as James Bond during the awards tour for Steven Spielberg’s “Munich” (2005), the immediate result was a marked increase in attendance at all screenings, recalls one veteran publicist. “There was a different before-and-after energy,” he said. That did not land Craig a nomination, however. The only Bond to score an Oscar nod and win after a long and storied career was Sean Connery (“The Untouchables”). Playing Bond does not lend an actor cred with the Academy. But it does extend a career.

The impact for Pattinson, who is ubiquitous in online media, is the same. At this point, most awards voters will have seen “The Odyssey,” but those seeing the Christopher Nolan blockbuster for the first time will view the actor differently. “He will have heightened scrutiny,” said one studio Oscar expert. “Clearly he is being tapped to anchor a franchise. For the actors [branch], he will be someone to reckon with.”

Clearly, fewer people have screened “Primetime.” Pattinson’s Bond ascension will push the movie up in the must-see rankings. “Bond probably gets more people to watch the movie,” said one Oscar strategist. “That’s half the battle. The goal is to get people to watch the movie. But the movie has to do the work.”

At the box office, “Primetime” has fared better domestically ($47 million) than abroad ($12 million), which is significant given an Oscar voting pool of more than 10,000 voters, one-quarter of whom are international. How will “Primetime” do with the upcoming critic votes? Or BAFTAs, which will reveal the international tale? And SAG’s Actor Awards, where, at this stage, Gold Derby has “Digger” star Tom Cruise edging out Pattinson? The race between those two actors is “squishy,” said Debra Birnbaum, Gold Derby editor-in-chief, who distinguishes between early season “hopedictions” and “real predictions.”

It helps to remember that the Academy is just starting to catch up with the contenders. The experts on Gold Derby (I’m one), at this stage, have seen way more movies than the site’s users or the Academy. Later in the season, Gold Derby users and experts are more in sync. That’s why we should take with a grain of salt the recent ascension among the Gold Derby users to number five for Best Actor contender Pattinson. They’re responding to publicity. The experts have him at seven. The same is true for the betting site Kalshi’s odds. Their accuracy improves the closer you get to the Oscars. At this stage, their bettors haven’t seen everything. And the key Oscar season markers, from critics’ groups to Guild awards, haven’t played out.

The American Cinematheque came out way ahead by honoring Pattinson with its annual award fete, set to take place on February 9, 2027. Tables are selling out and Bond studio Amazon/MGM — and Hollywood — will be there in force.

While Pattinson has never been an actors’ actor, he’s a hugely bankable global movie star. He’s going to keep us interested for a long time.
