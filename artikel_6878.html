What the Surprisingly Tender Ending of <i><em>The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping</em></i> Really Means

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

This story contains spoilers for The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping.

When Suzanne Collins announced that Haymitch Abernathy’s story would be brought to life on-screen, fans of the Hunger Games franchise knew that it was going to be an emotional undertaking that would dive into one of the series’s most resilient—and at times, deeply misunderstood—characters.

Haymitch’s story is filled with loss, and that doesn’t only include the death toll that comes with winning the Second Quarter Quell. After becoming a victor, the toll only continues to rise as his loved ones are whittled down one by one. Through the seeds he planted in the 50th Hunger Games and in his mentorship of Katniss 24 years later, Haymitch finally fulfills his mission to not let another sunrise fall on the reaping—but exactly how did he get there? Let’s break down the ending of The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping.

Haymitch wins the 50th Hunger Games by using the arena to his advantage. Before going into the Games, he hatched a plan with Beetee and Beetee’s son, District 3 tribute Ampert, to break the arena. The plan consisted of Haymitch breaking into and blowing up Sub-A, the arena’s underground control panel, by using the explosives concealed within District 9’s sunflower necklace tokens in combination with the flint striker gifted to him by his girlfriend, Lenore Dove.

The plan works, but only briefly—the arena shuts down for a few minutes, but at the expense of all his allies left in the game. In the movie, Plutarch urges President Snow to keep Haymitch alive and make him suffer by seeing his remaining allies perish. Ampert dies, but it isn’t until Maysilee Donner’s (his fellow District 12 tribute) death that he comes up with another plan to send the Capitol a message.

In the final moments of the Games, Haymitch faces off against Silka, who uses her axes in an attempt to take him out. Earlier in the Games, he discovers the force field at the end of the arena and uses it to win—when Silka throws her ax at Haymitch, the ax rebounds off of the force field and strikes her in the chest, declaring him the victor (the ax is lodged in Silka’s head in the book). After Silka dies, Haymitch lights the sunflower necklace with the flint striker and sends it into the force field, blowing up what lies outside the confines of the arena.

President Snow crowns Haymitch victor and tells him to “Enjoy your homecoming,” hinting at what awaits him when he returns to District 12. When he gets back, he finds his home on fire with his mother and his brother, Sid, locked inside. The pair die fiery deaths without any chance of being saved from the inferno.

A funeral is held for Haymitch’s mother and brother, as well as the other District 12 tributes, Louella McCoy, Wyatt Callow, and Maysilee Donner. He finally reconciles with Lenore Dove in the meadow after the funeral and feeds her gumdrops, thinking they’re the same gumdrops that his brother Sid gave Lenore after he departed for the Games—but he quickly realizes the gumdrops are poisoned by Snow to kill her. Lenore dies in Haymitch’s arms, using her last words to beg him not to let another sunrise fall on the reaping.

In the final moments of Sunrise on the Reaping, Haymitch meets Katniss and Peeta near a memorial fountain in District 12 that honors the 1,802 lives lost in the Hunger Games.

Katniss wishes Haymitch a happy birthday, which he shares with the annual reaping date. “Nice place to spend it as any,” Haymitch responds. “No reapings, no tributes, no Hunger Games,” Peeta adds. “Nevermore,” Haymitch says, a reference to Edgar Allan Poe’s poem, “The Raven,” which is featured heavily throughout the book.

To celebrate, Haymitch says, “I might meander out into the meadow, see some geese. Thank you, both of you.” Katniss thanks him in return. “Thank you, Haymitch,” she says. The movie concludes with Haymitch sitting peacefully with geese where Lenore Dove used to sit and sing to them.

There is no after-credits scene in Sunrise on the Reaping, though the final moments of the film borrow heavily from the epilogue in the book. Katniss and Peeta notably appear in the epilogue, but instead of wishing Haymitch a happy birthday, Katniss gifts Haymitch goose eggs and Peeta creates an incubator for them to hatch.
