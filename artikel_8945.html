Specifying a window involves more than simply choosing an aluminum profile that meets an aesthetic intent. It requires understanding how technical variables interact and determining which combination of properties best suits the building's location, orientation, use, and environmental conditions. A system designed for a residential opening in a relatively protected environment, for example, may not be the most appropriate solution for a large glazed facade exposed to strong winds, heavy rain, or urban noise. Yet, beyond the availability of systems, common errors continue to impact the specification process. One of the most frequent is prioritizing thermal insulation while overlooking acoustic performance, water tightness, air permeability, wind resistance, solar control, safety, and durability. Furthermore, glass is often treated as a secondary component, even though its composition can significantly influence the overall performance of the enclosure. Choosing glazing without considering the frame's capacity, solar orientation, outdoor noise levels, or safety requirements can compromise the effectiveness of an otherwise adequate system.

Moreover, a window's performance is intrinsically linked to its size, proportions, number of sashes, and operating mechanism. Larger openings carry different structural demands, influence hardware selection, and may require specific reinforcements or alternative configurations. Similarly, sliding and hinged systems offer different sealing solutions and operational characteristics that must be considered during the design phase. Installation must not be underestimated either. Even a high-performance window can lose much of its effectiveness if the joint between the frame and the surrounding wall is poorly resolved. Thermal bridges, inadequate sealing, a lack of insulation continuity, or poorly executed drainage details can compromise the performance levels verified in laboratory tests.

Comparisons, Relationships, and Performance

When comparing window systems, the overall performance of the assembly is influenced by frame depth, sash dimensions, the configuration of the thermal break, and the capacity to accommodate different glazing compositions. In aluminum windows, the thermal break typically consists of polyamide strips that separate the inner and outer sections of the frame, thereby reducing heat transfer through the metal. Its dimensions, geometry, and integration with other insulating materials determine the profile's thermal performance.

The STRUGAL S64RP GALA, S74RP OPTIMA, and S82RP AURA systems illustrate different approaches to meeting these requirements. With frame depths of 64, 74, and 82 mm, respectively, these systems incorporate various profile configurations and insulation strategies. While GALA offers a relatively compact solution with reinforced insulation options using polyamide and optional polyolefin foams, OPTIMA incorporates tubular polyamides in the sashes, providing high thermal performance even in demanding climate zones. AURA takes thermal insulation to the next level through the use of 42 mm polyamide strips, a cellular EPDM center gasket, and passive insulating foams, offering optimal performance even in the harshest weather conditions.

Their differences are also reflected in their glazing capacities. STRUGAL S64RP GALA accommodates glass thicknesses of up to 51 mm in the visible sash, STRUGAL S74RP OPTIMA reaches up to 61 mm, and STRUGAL S82RP AURA supports glazing up to 69 mm. These variations allow for a wider range of double- or triple-glazing configurations, depending on the project's requirements for thermal and acoustic insulation, solar control, and security.

A different approach is found in the STRUGAL S88RP ARTIS sliding system, designed with a focus on a minimalist aesthetic—featuring slim profiles—and large glazed surfaces. Its perimeter frames are available in depths of 74, 85, 94, and 112 mm, integrating polyamide strips of up to 34 mm. Additionally, it accommodates glazing up to 32 mm and offers various configurations: sliding, lift-and-slide, single- or triple-track, and even pocket versions (concealed within the wall).

While thermal transmittance is typically one of the first values considered during specification, air permeability, water tightness, wind resistance, and acoustic insulation are also vital aspects of a window's performance. For example, water tightness, evaluated under the UNE-EN 12208 standard, measures resistance to water penetration. The STRUGAL S74RP OPTIMA system achieves class E2400, whereas STRUGAL S82RP AURA and STRUGAL S64RP GALA reach E2100, and STRUGAL S88RP ARTIS features a 7A rating. In terms of acoustic performance, the certified values range from Rw ≤ 42 dB for STRUGAL S88RP ARTIS to Rw ≤ 47 dB for STRUGAL S82RP AURA.

How Project Requirements Dictate System Selection

In real-world projects, the selection process becomes clearer when observing the convergence of technical requirements, architectural intent, and environmental conditions. In the renovation of 27 residential units in Salou, designed by Nua Arquitectures, the STRUGAL S64RP GALA system was chosen as part of the intervention on an existing residential building. Its 64 mm frame, relatively compact profile, and reinforced insulation options illustrate how window replacement can adapt to the constraints of a pre-existing facade. Similarly, at the multi-family residential complex designed by Chamizo Arquitectos on San Juan beach in Alicante, the STRUGAL S64RP GALA and STRUGAL S88RP ARTIS systems were used, demonstrating how different opening configurations can coexist within the same project.
