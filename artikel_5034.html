Bitcoin Policy Institute Renames to Finney Foundation for Open Technology

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The Bitcoin Policy Institute announced Tuesday that it is now the Finney Foundation for Open Technology, a new parent organization whose brief spans open-source artificial intelligence, digital cash and encrypted messaging. The institute’s bitcoin work continues inside the foundation, and bitcoin remains a primary focus.

The name honors Hal Finney (1956-2014), a cryptographer and software developer who helped build the email encryption program PGP, ran one of the first anonymous remailers and received the first bitcoin transaction from Satoshi Nakamoto in January 2009. In a November 1992 essay, Finney wrote that the computer “can be used as a tool to liberate and protect people, rather than to control them.” He was diagnosed with ALS the same year he received that first transaction and died in 2014.

Washington is now writing the rules for artificial intelligence, the foundation said, and fear drives much of that debate. As the Bitcoin Policy Institute did for bitcoin, the Finney Foundation said it will argue that open systems outcompete closed ones, that individuals should control the tools they rely on, and that American strength comes from building in the open.

“We set out to make Washington think about Bitcoin differently — to educate them on the importance of open monetary networks,” said David Zell, president of the Finney Foundation. “Our fellows now serve in congressional offices for both parties and in the executive branch. Our ideas have shaped discourse and policy. Our expansion will help us stay at the forefront of today’s most consequential policy debates, especially those around open source AI.”

That argument was already on stage in September at Freedom Tech DC, the institute’s Washington summit, formerly the Bitcoin Policy Summit. The invite-only gathering put open-source and open-weight AI, data centers and energy infrastructure on the agenda alongside bitcoin and dollar-backed stablecoins.

Block co-founder Jack Dorsey said there that “the next and probably largest element [of freedom technology] is open intelligence and making sure that it’s not locked up in the hands of any one government or any one company… that’s where we’re going to get the best outcomes.”

Alongside the new name, the foundation published an Open Technology Declaration, titled “For Us, Not Over Us,” which argues for machines that serve people rather than rule over them. The text takes up Finney’s line about computers as tools of liberation and says the foundation will offer research, policy ideas and educational programs aimed at that goal. It asks who the new machines are for and who is in control of them, and it calls for people to build the tools they need without waiting for permission.

It is also launching an inaugural class of Finney Fellows, researchers with expertise spanning machine intelligence, free speech, national security, biotechnology and space. The foundation says that cohort will be named Wednesday at 10 a.m. ET. A separate roster of Bitcoin Policy Institute fellows remains listed on the same page.

The shift does not retire the institute’s bitcoin brief. The Bitcoin Policy Institute, founded in 2022, has run a congressional fellowship program to place bitcoin specialists in congressional offices, joined the State Department’s Freedom Tech Excellence Program as a founding partner, and urged Congress to include bitcoin in proposed de minimis tax relief. In August, it named Conner Brown executive director as it expanded its leadership team.

The Finney Foundation describes itself as a nonpartisan, nonprofit 501(c)(3) think tank in Washington, D.C. Zell is president. Brown remains executive director on the new staff list. For now, the bitcoin think tank still exists, just under a wider roof, with a new class of fellows due to be named in the morning.
