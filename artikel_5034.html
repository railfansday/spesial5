Analyst: Bitcoin Below $70,000 is a ‘Gift From the Market’

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

K33 Research said bitcoin is trading in “deep value territory” after a 14% weekly drop, arguing that “any entry below $70K is a gift from the market” for medium- to long-term investors.

The call comes in the firm’s weekly report, “No gain, plenty of pain,” which published Nov. 16. Head of research Vetle Lunde wrote that bitcoin’s slide has erased its August and September gains and left the market deeply oversold. Bitcoin has posted six straight red weekly candles and is on track for a record 13 consecutive days of lower daily highs, according to K33.

Lunde said a streak of six weekly losses has happened only twice before, from Jan. 26 to March 1, 2026, and from April 3 to May 29, 2022. Daily RSI, a momentum gauge, hit 19.95 on Saturday and has stayed below 25 since Friday. Bitcoin also fell below its 200-week moving average, near $68,000, after its worst weekly return since June 7.

At the bottom on Nov. 15, bitcoin traded about 8.5% under the 200-week average, a wider gap than the 7.3% recorded at June’s low of $58,500. K33 said 51.5% of the bitcoin supply last moved on-chain at prices above the current level, meaning the average holder could have sold for more at some point in the past four years.

Lunde said those readings, taken together, have typically lined up with market bottoms. K33 also noted that crypto has lagged traditional markets, with the Nasdaq and S&P 500 recovering modestly last week while bitcoin sank.

Bitcoin was trading near $62,300 on Wednesday, according to Coinbase spot prices, below the roughly $65,000 level cited in the K33 report, which showed bitcoin down 14% on the week and about 20% over 30 days. Ether also fell 14% over the week, to around $1,827.

Capital continues to leave bitcoin funds. Net outflows from bitcoin exchange-traded products reached 44,900 BTC over the 14 trading days since Oct. 29, K33 said, short of the record of about 71,000 BTC set in June. Total ETF holdings fell to about 1.46 million BTC, the lowest since Aug. 19, and leveraged ETF exposure dropped to 21,900 BTC, the lowest since March 2024.

Forced selling hit derivatives, too. About $1.3 billion in long positions were liquidated on Nov. 13 and 14 as bitcoin broke below $70,000, K33 said, and funding rates on perpetual futures repeatedly turned negative. Open interest fell by about 30,000 BTC but stayed above 330,000 BTC, leaving leverage elevated against historical norms.

Institutional futures players largely stayed on the sidelines. Open interest in bitcoin futures on CME stayed below 100,000 BTC all week, K33 said, a sign that regulated-market participants have not rushed in to buy the dip.

Demand for downside protection is also surging. The one-month 25-delta skew, which compares the price of puts to calls, rose above 20 for the first time since July, a level Lunde said has historically coincided with “selling exhaustion near local bottoms.”

Stablecoins offer another sign of stress. K33 said net redemptions totaled $16.1 billion over the past month, the largest outflow since the collapse of Terra.

Lunde stopped short of calling the decline over. He said earlier recoveries from similar conditions involved “prolonged consolidation,” pointing to a choppy recovery rather than an immediate rebound.

There were some constructive signs in trading activity. Spot volume ran near $5 billion a day from Friday through Sunday, the busiest weekend since February, which K33 said could give the market firmer footing after weeks of one-way selling.

Even so, leverage remains high, and ETF money is still heading out the door. Bitcoin was near $62,300 at the time of writing, below the roughly $68,000 200-week average K33 flagged as a key level.
