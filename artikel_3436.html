GTA 6 Hits 99 on Metacritic, Crowned Best Game Ever in a Wave of Rave Reviews

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

It has beaten the original Zelda: Ocarina of Time in the best game of all time rankings

Grand Theft Auto 6 has displaced the original Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time as the best video game of all time according to review aggregation site Metacritic. Its Metascore of 99 matches Nintendo's 1998 masterpiece, but is presumably a few decimal points higher behind the scenes.

It is rated even more highly than Rockstar's last two monoliths, Grand Theft Auto 5 and Red Dead Redemption 2, which both earned Metascores of 97. It's a similar story on OpenCritic, where GTA 6 has a rating of 98.

Reviewers are universally bowled over by the scale, ambition, and artistry of Rockstar Games' magnum opus, which they have hailed in a near-unbroken string of five-star, 10/10 reviews.

IGN's Luke Reilly called GTA 6 "one of the most impressive games ever made" in his 10/10 review, and called it "Unprecedented. Unbelievable. Unmissable." Like many reviewers, Reilly noted how the latest GTA had inherited the sophistication and maturity of Red Dead Redemption 2 as well as the anarchic spirit of its predecessors. "As optimistic as I was about a new GTA built in the wake of everything Rockstar learnt from the majestic Red Dead Redemption 2, I was simply not ready for just how much richer, deeper, and more satisfying Grand Theft Auto 6 has proven to be," Reilly wrote.

Reviewers are awed by the game's unprecedented realism. "I cleaned a boat with a pressure washer and watched the suds build up realistically in the concrete below my character’s flip-flops. I ran through a tram station and spotted over 100 different NPCs milling about, all unique and all moving," wrote Kotaku's Zack Zwiezen. "It’s a massive experience that is filled with more content and more ideas than some entire game franchises."

No, GTA 6 is not the Last Video Game, but things might be different from here on

The Guardian's Keza MacDonald explained that the experience is so varied and overwhelming it can be hard to pin down. "Is it an interactive crime movie? A city simulator? A driving game? A sort of absurdly expansive version of The Sims in which the people you’re dressing up and looking after just happen to be professional criminals?" she asked. MacDonald noted that she got easily tangled in GTA 6's complex controls and systems. But, like many reviewers, she focused on the touchingly portrayed love story between GTA 6's two central characters, Jason and Lucia, and noted Lucia's characterization as a landmark step for a once-misogyistic series.

MacDonald said this was reflective of a broader shift in tone: "I was astonished to discover that the game offers you small incentives for acts of kindness, such as texting your partner a selfie, doing your neighbour a favour or throwing your rubbish in the bin rather than dropping it on the ground. Jason and Lucia are not extravagant caricatures, but two people trying to make it in a world totally stacked against them."

The closest I could find to a dissenting opinion was a review from ScreenHub's Alice Clarke, who gave GTA 6 four stars out of five and said it didn't immediately live up to the hype. Along with some other reviewers, Clarke notes that it's slow to get going. "I spent the first dozen hours of the game completely bored," Clarke wrote. "I went into the game expecting the usual chaotic fun. [...] Instead, I faced an on-the-rails ‘Good Boyfriend Simulator’ with limited opportunities for mischief."

But for Polygon's Giovanni Colantonio, this opening section, which establishes Jason and Lucia's relationship as he waits for her to be released from jail, is central to the game's narrative and emotional strength and its clear political message. "Justice is big business in the Florida-inspired state of Leonida, and people like Lucia are products to be cycled in and out of a flawed system," Colantonio wrote. "All the while, the real criminal masterminds of Leonida trash their penthouses into the wee morning hours."

For Colantonio, GTA 6's story is uneven and padded with repetitive chases, but is more than fully redeemed by its heart. "Through Jason and Lucia’s genuine chemistry, GTA 6 gives you a better reason to work through Leonida’s daily grind," he wrote. "Yes, there’s always another bank to rob or car to steal, but that’s not the reason you wake up in the morning. It’s to prove that love can survive in a cruel culture that tries so hard to beat it out of people." He concluded: "People will hem and haw about Grand Theft Auto’s purported glamorization of lawlessness. They always have; they always will. But it will be different this time. GTA 6’s empathy is bound to be the most controversial thing about it."

Grand Theft Auto 6 will be released on Nov. 18 on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X. If you're in New Zealand, it's out now!
