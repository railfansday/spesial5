Everything You Missed at Vanity Fair's Screening Room Los Angeles

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Not even a nationwide FAA ground stop could prevent movie stars from descending upon Los Angeles for the Governor Awards and Vanity Fair's first annual Screening Room this past weekend. Hosted at LACMA's brand-new Steve Tisch theater, actors including Matt Damon, Penélope Cruz, Pedro Pascal, Andrew Garfield, John Leguizamo, and Andrew Scott joined Q&As to discuss their films and pressing topics in the entertainment industry. AI was a major theme throughout the weekend, and not just at the screening for Artificial, as actors and directors spoke about the desire to continue making things that feel “real” and “made by humans.” Following each screening, talent mingled with guests to continue the conversations.

The weekend began with a screening of Wild Horse Nine, director Martin McDonagh's dark comedy starring John Malkovich and Sam Rockwell as CIA agents behind the plan to overthrow the Chilean government in 1973. Following the showing, VF's Senior Hollywood Correspondent Rebecca Ford led a Q&A with McDonagh, Malkovich and Mariana di Girolamo, who also stars in the film.

Next up was Elsinore, which tells the story of Scottish actor Ian Charleson (played by Andrew Scott) who attempts to perform Hamlet at the National Theatre while secretly living with AIDS. In conversation with VF's Rebecca Ford, Scott, his costar Billie Piper, and writer Stephen Beresford, Scott discussed what it was like to tell Charleson's story. “I think … why the film is so moving is because [Charleson’s] not wallowing at any point. He was insanely courageous,” he said.

Come Friday night, audiences gathered for a screening of the acclaimed Spanish language drama La Bola Negra, which follows several gay men whose lives are bound together by their identities, love and loss, and the looming presence of the Spanish Civil War. It stars Miguel Bernardeau, Álvaro Lafuente Calvo (the singer known as Guitarricadelafuente) in his first major acting role, Carlos Gonzalez, Milo Quifes and Penélope Cruz.

After the film, Ricky Martin moderated a panel conversation featuring directors Javier Ambrossi and Javier Calvo, and stars Cruz, Guitarricadelafuente and Bernardeau. Calvo talked about how they are all riding high on the film's positive reception. “Creating this sense of community and hearing people saying, ‘This movie changed me. It really made me understand things about me.’ The power that art has, we forget it, but then this happens and … the rest is a dream. The rest we're living is a dream.”

Saturday's slate of programming began with a live recording of Vanity Fair's “Notes on a Scene” with Matt Damon, John Leguizamo and Himesh Patel, who recounted what it was like to film The Odyssey with director Christopher Nolan. Next, Seth Rogen joined Screening Room for “Scene Selection,” taking the audience through what it was like shooting Freaks and Geeks, Knocked Up, The Studio and more.

Later that afternoon, Pedro Pascal and Tony Gilroy joined the festivities following a screening of their new film Behemoth!, which tells the story of a prodigal cellist who returns to Los Angeles and to score films after decades performing symphonic work around the country. In conversation with VF's West Coast Editor John Ross for Behemoth!, Pascal spoke about how making the film gave him “a tremendous amount of hope.” “We weren't making money on this movie. Nobody was. And you had the most expensive people involved in this film because of their talent and their experience. So there was just this purity of love in the work from every single department, and that was incredibly contagious," he said.

Come Saturday night, guests were eager to see Luca Guadagnino's Artificial, which follows the rise of OpenAI and a few of its pioneers: Sam Altman, Elon Musk, Ilya Sutskever and Mira Murati. Guadagnino was joined by cast members Andrew Garfield (who plays Altman), Yura Borisov (who plays Sutskever) and Monica Barbaro (who plays Murati) for a post-screening conversation with VF's Global Editorial Director Mark Guiducci. “It's important to try to understand because as Luca said, these are the kids that have the reins of the future and we've somehow allowed them to just hold onto them. And we've kind of given our autonomy over or it's been taken from behind our backs,” Garfield said.

On Sunday, Cruz joined director Florian Zeller for a screening of the film Bunker, a timely film about a famous architect, played by Javier Bardem, and his wife Sofia, played by Cruz, who is asked to build a bunker for a tech giant. This sparks a rift in their marriage as the couple examines how success and monetary gain have affected their lives. Cruz, who is having a huge year with The Invite and La Bola Negra alongside this film, expressed her excitement that younger audiences are coming back to theaters and are looking for films that don't “over-explain."

The inaugural edition of Vanity Fair’s Screening Room paired this year’s most compelling films with a slate of programming that included post-screening panels and conversations with actors and filmmakers. Following the program in Los Angeles, Vanity Fair’s Screening Room will arrive in New York, where screenings and events will take place at Cherry Lane Theatre from December 4–6.
