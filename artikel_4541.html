Mercedes-Benz Asks: What If We Put Screens in a Car’s Windows, Too?

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

New window display technology could unlock the opportunity to include even more screens in Mercedes automobiles.

If there’s a space to insert a screen, the folks at Mercedes-Benz will find it. And turns out, the carmaker’s engineers have found what they consider the next frontier to be conquered: your car’s windows. On Tuesday, Mercedes released previews of two new types of window displays, one called Intelligent Display in Glass and the other named Holographic Display.

The “Intelligent Display in Glass” essentially puts LED display elements directly into the vehicle windows. Why? Mercedes says warning symbols or traffic-related information could be displayed on the rear window to warn those behind you. One might presume brake lights do the trick of letting folks know you’re slowing down, but the examples Mercedes shows are much more specific as to the reason you’re slowing down; the glass can project the symbol of a car accident, pedestrian crossing, or all sorts of other things.

Our favorite is the text that simply reads “KEEP DISTANCE.” Could that potentially just enrage a tailgater even more? Sure, but it’s also objectively funny to have your car tell people behind you to back off.

Mercedes says it’s investigating two different LED technologies for this application. Mini LEDs are best-suited to larger displays in the rear window; meanwhile, Micro LEDs could be used on smaller areas to show animations, thanks to their higher resolution capabilities.

Examples of Micro LEDs in action are shown in the side window screens, where the car can display messages to its driver like battery charge status or a simple “goodbye” as you walk away. In both cases, when no content is displayed, Mercedes says the display elements laminated into the glass composite “remain almost invisible.” The “almost” there gives us some pause, so we’ll have to see it in person in order to make a judgement.

The other tech Mercedes previewed is the Holographic Display, which looks a bit like a HUD on steroids. A projector in the instrument panel would project content onto a new windshield that uses a special transparent film laminated into the glass. Mercedes says the display will be viewable only at specific viewing angles—the driver wouldn’t be able to see what’s projected in front of the passenger, and vice versa. The carmaker says this means the passenger could be watching a film projected onto the windshield, while the driver is shown navigation information.

In pictures, at least it’s better than a HUD in that the display looks like a projection you might see in a home theater setup. You get full color and a sharp image, though the examples that the carmaker showcases aren’t exactly too enticing, being images of Mercedes vehicles and the weather.

Just like the other displays shown in this preview, Mercedes says all of these ideas simply demonstrate potential future approaches. None of them are likely to end up in a production car any time soon, as they’re all currently under development. That said, now would be the time to sound off and let Mercedes know if any of the above is something you’d want in a car one day ... or not.
