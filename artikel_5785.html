Democrats, Republicans find rare common ground when asked what they're thankful for

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

House lawmakers on both sides of the aisle spoke to Fox News Digital about what they are most thankful for as they get ready to celebrate Thanksgiving with their families.

An ocean of ideological differences separates Democrats and Republicans on Capitol Hill, but they're largely in agreement over one thing this Thanksgiving, and it's usually miles away from Washington, D.C.

"I'm thankful for family," Rep. Eric Burlison, R-Mo., told Fox News Digital. "It's what drives me to keep going, to keep doing this, keep fighting for the country."

Families across America are gathering for Thanksgiving, kicking off a holiday season that's meant for reflection and refocusing on things that matter most. And members of Congress who spoke with Fox News Digital about that special day are no different.

The U.S. Capitol in Washington, D.C., on Feb. 10, 2026. (Al Drago/Bloomberg via Getty Images)

"You know, elections can be a bumpy road in many ways, and it's easy to take a shot at somebody who's running — we put ourselves out there. But it's the family, really, and I'm grateful that my wife and my three kids who are in their 20s are so supportive," Rep. John Mannion, D-N.Y., said. "So that's what I'm thankful for."

Reps. Randy Fine, R-Fla., and Tim Burchett, R-Tenn., singled out their children.

"I'm very grateful to how great my boys are doing despite the challenges of this job, and just my whole family for putting up with all of this and being there for me," Fine said.

Burchett told Fox News Digital he was "more than blessed."

Rep. Eric Burlison, R-Mo., leaves the Capitol Hill Club after a meeting of the House Republican Conference on March 25, 2025. (Tom Williams/CQ-Roll Call, Inc. via Getty Images)

"I've got a beautiful wife and a daughter, and I never thought I was gonna have a family. And I prayed, asked Jesus that I'd have a family, and I got one, dadgum it, and it is wonderful," Burchett said.

Rep. Mike Quigley, D-Ill., said he was grateful for the U.S.'s ability to still come together despite people's many differences.

"I'm most thankful that this country still reminds itself that there's more that unites us than divides us," Quigley said.

Two more Democrats said they were thankful for their party’s success in the midterm elections.

Rep. Shri Thanedar, D-Mich., arrives for the vote on the budget resolution in the U.S. Capitol on April 10, 2025. (Bill Clark/CQ-Roll Call, Inc. via Getty Images)

"I'm thankful for the excitement of next year, to get things done," said Rep. Brad Schneider, D-Ill.

Rep. Shri Thanedar, D-Mich., said he was thankful for "an opportunity to make a difference in people's lives."

"Democrats got a huge majority, and that allows us to help people," Thanedar, who lost his own re-election bid, said.
