Barcola starts to justify £123m price tag with hat-trick as Liverpool hit LASK for six

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

There was no grand gesture as Bradley Barcola walked back to the bench, just a high-five from Andoni Iraola and some of Liverpool’s coaching staff.

It was a similar situation as he left the stadium. Hood up, jacket zipped, baseball cap pulled on tightly, he politely turned down all requests to talk in English. “Not good enough,” he said with a smile, but that was the only thing that had not been up to standard.

The giveaway here was what he held in his right hand – the match ball. On it were the signatures of Liverpool’s squad, congratulating him on a momentous performance that had arrived right on cue.

If you are going end a barren spell, you do not want to just turn on the taps but burst the dam, and that is precisely what Barcola did on a wild night in Austria. He needed one goal to get his Liverpool career up and running, he ended up scoring three.

Iraola had said he was not concerned about Barcola’s numbers when Liverpool arrived in Linz, even if no goals and no assists since his £123m move from Paris St-Germain had caused debate, but those numbers look infinitely better now after his first senior hat-trick.

“He was always one-on-one, certainly in behind and he helped us a lot to control the game, finding the pockets. I am happy for both of them, yes. For their confidence for sure, it’s very good. I think they feel now that we trust them a lot. It’s going to be very good for them.”

Barcola should have had the stage to himself on an evening when Liverpool were so obviously better than LASK in Linz but it will have frustrated Iraola immensely that his team scoring six times was tarnished by three daft goals being given up in the final 25 minutes.

We can look at the late switch off in due course but the only place to begin is with Barcola, who looked ready for business as early as the eighth minute when his first touch was a cushioned back heel, taking possession by crossing one leg behind the other, and how he seized his moment.

Liverpool had already taken the lead when Ronald Araújo rose powerfully to head in a Kostas Tsimikas corner but, two minutes after, the Barcola show began, a confident right-footed shot being swept past Lukas Jungwirth from 10 yards after some good build-up play from Rio Ngumoha and Lewis Koumas.

It is early days for Bradley Barcola but is has been an underwhelming start to his Liverpool career. The winger was a rotational player at Paris St-Germain, as he is for France, so Liverpool spending £120m is a gamble on his raw athletic attributes and the hope that he pieces everything together.

As Dominic King writes in this piece: “Barcola had no control over the price that Richard Hughes, Liverpool’s former sporting director, brokered with Paris St-Germain for his release. But something he can control is public perception and, with no goals or assists so far, opinions are starting to form.”

Of the five Premier League teams in the Champions League this season, Liverpool have the friendliest league-phase fixtures and they can secure back-to-back wins against Austrian side LASK in this evening’s early kick off.

Having already vanquished one of their pot one opponents by beating Atletico Madrid at Anfield, Andoni Iraola’s team really should be targeting one of the top spots.

They travel to Linz without some key players, though. Alexander Isak, Cody Gakpo and Dominik Szoboszlai missed the defeat by Manchester City and remain sidelined, while Hugo Ekitike is a long-term absentee.

There were positive aspects to Liverpool’s performance against City, but they collapsed like a deck of cards after Ryan Gravenberch’s harsh second yellow card.

Tonight’s game is one they should dominate, and it is a presentable opportunity for Florian Wirtz and Bradley Barcola to kick-start their seasons and respond to the recent criticism of their performances. Iraola hopes to have a stronger squad to choose from at the weekend, when Liverpool face a stringent test at Brentford. This is the second of nine matches in 28 days for Liverpool.

“You don’t have a lot of time to train,” said Iraola. “You try to prepare the games now a lot of times just with the videos and the tactics boards, just a little bit outside. You have to prioritise the freshness of the players.

“Especially now we are playing four games in 10 days, it’s keeping all the legs, all the energy in the games.

LASK are currently third in the Austrian league, but booked their place in this season’s Champions League proper with a remarkable comeback from 4-0 down on aggregate against Celtic.

They have injury problems of their own to contend with George Bello and Moses Usor limping off in the weekend’s defeat by league leaders Rapid Wien and Ramiz Harakate tearing his anterior cruciate ligament in July.
