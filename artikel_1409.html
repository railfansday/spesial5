Freddie Potts on the pain of leaving West Ham, why he had to move on this summer, upsetting the odds in the Champions League with Club Brugge - and why they don't fear Liverpool!

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Published: 08:00 EST, 24 November 2026 | Updated: 14:15 EST, 24 November 2026

There are few transfers that get West Ham supporters talking quite like the sale of an academy graduate.

Who can forget the uproar after Grady Diangana joined West Brom in 2020, when club captain Mark Noble released a public statement voicing his anger and disappointment at the move.

Noble’s thoughts were echoed by team-mates at the time, while supporters were similarly up in arms at the sale of the winger.

So, when Freddie Potts joined Club Brugge for £10million on the eve of the season to mark the controversial departure of another product of the proclaimed Academy of Football, you can imagine the reaction.

Potts represented more than a graduate. A boyhood West Ham fan whose dad Steve is a club legend and still serves as a first-team coach, he joined the academy aged six. His brother Dan also came through the ranks.

The midfielder had just enjoyed a breakthrough campaign despite West Ham’s relegation, and many felt he was the perfect player to build their Championship promotion push around.

Freddie Potts is shining in Belgium with Club Brugge after joining from West Ham in the summer

Potts joined the Hammers academy aged six and enjoyed a breakthrough year last season

Hammers favourites Kevin Nolan and Tony Cottee weighed in with their disapproval at his sale, while celebrity fan Ray Winstone expressed his shock and anger live on talkSPORT.

‘Of course you see it (the division about the move) on social media,’ Potts tells Daily Mail Sport. ‘Every player does. But even though last season was tough I always felt I had a connection with the West Ham fans.

‘No matter if I played well or bad I always felt I understood what it meant to play for West Ham. It's a great club with great fans and people. In the end, I had to do what was best for me. I wanted to play this season, and I felt like this was the best place to do that.’

Potts, 22 at the time of his exit, admits it was extremely tough and emotional for him and his family to leave a club he had been at for 16 years.

But, during pre-season he became aware he wasn’t likely to play as much as he wanted to under Nuno Espirito Santo.

‘In the end it’s the manager’s decision,’ Potts says. ‘I wasn’t getting the minutes I wanted. The feeling was that I wasn’t guaranteed to be in the plans as much as Club Brugge were promising me, so I thought it was best for me to move.

‘It was very hard. West Ham have been my club my whole life and I have a lot of love for West Ham. But I had to take into account what was best for my career and I’m loving it at Brugge. It’s a really exciting place full of great people and a great manager.’

Potts had multiple options in the summer, including Premier League interest, yet the chance to play in the Champions League and compete for titles with Brugge attracted him.

Their record of developing young players and a talented squad that includes the likes of Carlos Forbs and Nicolo Tresoldi was equally compelling, and the midfielder hasn't looked back.

Potts started Brugge’s first 10 league games, missing their final one before the international break due to suspension. He returned at the weekend as they thrashed Mechelen 5-1. Ivan Leko’s side are one point adrift of Union Saint-Gilloise at the top of the Belgian Pro League, and they have also impressed in the Champions League.

In Europe, Brugge have beaten Inter Milan, PSV and Lens on their way to nine points from 12, only narrowly losing 3-2 to Aston Villa. Potts has started each match, including their memorable 4-3 win against Inter at the San Siro, and they host Liverpool on Wednesday.

‘It's a really good group of lads,’ he says. ‘Everyone speaks English as well which helps me. It's a good team and we're happy playing with each other.’

He is clearly enjoying life and is just the fifth English player in history to play for the club. Potts did take time to settle though, and admits he initially suffered from homesickness.

‘I found it difficult because East London and Essex have been my home for my whole life,’ he says. ‘West Ham was my life. It was tough in terms of moving into a new language and everything but you get used to it and I’m really enjoying it here. It’ll be strange going back home to England.'

Potts has been a regular and has had a key role in Brugge's impressive early exploits in Europe

Brugge have beaten PSV, Lens and Inter Milan, and they take on Liverpool this Wednesday

Potts’ family have popped over to see him and he has enjoyed exploring his new surroundings. He is yet to learn the local language and scolds himself for being lazy for relying on English, although he has picked up little bits of Flemish.

He has immersed himself in the culture too, and reveals he has tried the world-famous Belgian fries and a few local beers, albeit only when he has enough time to recover between games.

Despite his new life in Europe, Potts remains a West Ham fan and tries to watch them on TV every week.

He has been surprised by their struggles on their return to the Championship for the first time in 14 years, with Nuno paying the price by losing his job. Former Hammers midfielder Scott Parker has since taken the reins.

‘I still think they're playing well,’ Potts says. ‘They've got a great team and Scott Parker is a really good manager. You get ups and downs but I still 100 per cent think West Ham will get promoted this year.'

Potts’ stay in the Hammers first team may have been brief, yet there were still plenty of highs, not least a storming performance on his first Premier League start. That came in a win over Newcastle last November in the Hammers’ first home victory of the season. He was also coached by his dad, an experience he jokes had positives and negatives.

He left West Ham after realising he was unlikely to be first choice under Nuno Espirito Santo

Potts is a boyhood West Ham fan and is fully expecting them to get promoted this season

Attention now turns to Brugge’s clash against Liverpool. It promises to be a tricky challenge for Andoni Iraola’s side against a team who are determined to prove themselves on the European stage.

The two teams are level on points in the league phase, and Potts is keen for his side to take another scalp.

‘I don't think there's much difference in the way we've been performing in the Champions League,’ Potts concludes. ‘It'll be difficult for them to come to our place as well.

‘They're a great team, but we're going to approach it like we have with every Champions League game. We've gone away and beaten Milan, we've beaten PSV away. These are top teams.

‘Results like that give you a boost of how far you can go. You don't want to look too far ahead but every game in this competition is a chance to show everyone what you can do.

‘Liverpool will be a tough game but we've proved it against top teams before and we're going in thinking that we can do it again.’
