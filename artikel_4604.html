General Motors Is Reportedly Preparing to Replace CEO Mary Barra

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The goliath automaker is said to be working with an outside executive search firm to prepare for the eventual exit of its longest-serving CEO in modern times.

General Motors is looking for a new captain to steer the company, according to a story published on November 17 in the Wall Street Journal. The company is reportedly preparing for a future without CEO Mary Barra, who has led the automotive giant through a myriad of tumultuous times since taking the helm in 2014.

According to the WSJ report, GM is working with an executive search firm in an effort to identify potential outside candidates to succeed Barra. That said, the company has not publicly commented on when the executive could step aside.

Having led the company for 12 years, Barra is GM’s second-longest-serving CEO (the longest was Alfred P. Sloan, who lasted in the role for 23 years starting in 1923). She is also the first woman to lead a global automaker, and her time at the top of GM is about twice as long as the average tenure of modern S&P 500 CEOs, according to the WSJ.

Barra has reportedly been laying the groundwork for her exit for several months. Despite looking at outside contenders, GM has a long history of selecting its chief executives from within the company, a move it could repeat when Barra exits. According to the WSJ report, top candidates for the role include the company’s finance chief, Paul Jacobson; the company’s top product officer, Sterling Anderson; and Mark Reuss, who has spent 40 years at GM, during several of which he served as Barra’s No. 2.

Barra took over the company as it was still regaining its footing following its 2009 bankruptcy and has created a leaner but more profitable General Motors. The automaker sells millions fewer vehicles each year than it did a decade ago, although, thanks to a focus on higher-margin vehicles (specifically trucks), the automaker is generating record levels of profits. While the search is on to find an eventual successor, Barra told Fortune magazine in September that “I still feel like I can contribute and continue to build the foundation.”
