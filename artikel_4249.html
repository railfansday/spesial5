9 PS5 RPGs Where You’re Actually the Villain

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Eric Warner is a Journalist and Multimedia Producer based in New England with over 7 years of experience producing stories for multiple publications

Most video game narratives typically have players embody heroic roles, such as Link in The Legend of Zelda series, but several games do allow people to adopt more villainous roles.

This is especially seen in role-playing games, as they offer players the chance to go down heroic paths, neutral paths, or even downright diabolical ones, with some of the best RPGs featuring karma systems that track good and back actions and have characters and factions respond to players’ choices.

While some RPGs still push players towards acting heroically, here are nine RPGs available on the PlayStation 5 that allow people to play completely villainous roles and wreak havoc. Naturally, there are big spoilers ahead for these titles, so beware if you haven't played them yet!

Warhorse Studios’ Kingdom Come: Deliverance surprised many players in 2018 by depicting an engaging, realistic medieval RPG centered around the Bohemian commoner turned hero Henry, as he sets out to avenge his family and join Lord Radzig Kobyla in fighting invading forces.

Henry’s story continues in the recently released Kingdom Come: Deliverance II, but Warhorse Studios granted players more freedom in how to control Henry’s life and how he interacts with NPCs. Players are able to keep Henry on the path of a hero or have him become a complete jerk.

For instance, Henry can tattle on people to authorities resulting in their imprisonment or death, sabotage businesses, and commit various crimes, with these diabolical actions ending up with the spirits of Henry’s parents disavowing him from the afterlife.

Some of the best RPGs to play as a villain in are those created by Bethesda, and luckily Virtuos remastered one Bethesda’s best RPGs for modern platforms with The Elder Scrolls IV: Oblivion Remastered.

Like many Elder Scrolls games, The Elder Scrolls IV: Oblivion Remastered starts with players embodying an unnamed prisoner destined for greatness, but they can quickly ignore that grand destiny and go about slaying almost everyone in Cyrodiil.

Players can even go so far as to join the Dark Brotherhood, a merciless cult of shadowy assassins, and take part in several contracted killings, such as staging a fatal accident to kill the innocent Wood Elf Baenlin and wiping out every living member in a family.

Obsidian Entertainment is widely regarded to be one of the best RPG developers in modern gaming, having created Fallout: New Vegas, South Park: The Stick of Truth, and The Outer Worlds, with one of their better RPGs available on PS5 so far being Avowed.

A first and third-person fantasy RPG, Avowed has players embody a character simply known as the Envoy as they journey to the Living Lands region of Eora, to investigate a mysterious plague known as the Dream Scourge and confront several factions vying for control over the region.

Easily the most evil faction in the region is the Steel Garrote, a corrupt order of paladins, and, if players chose to kill the assassin Ygwulf, allow the Garrote to burn the Fior mes Ivèrno settlement, and help destroy the Godless Ruins, they’ll be able to slay the god Sapadal, betray their companions, and formally join the Garrote.

While Fallout 4 did originally launch on the PS4, Bethesda did recently rerelease the game, complete with all DLCs, for all modern platforms, in the form of Fallout 4: Anniversary Edition.

Here, players embody a long-frozen survivor of both the Sino-American War and the Great War, who reawakens in the Commonwealth wasteland in search of their lost son who was kidnapped by a mysterious organization.

Throughout this journey, players can aid and join many factions such as the Minutemen and the Brotherhood of Steel, but easily the most villainous factions to align with in Fallout 4 are the Institute and the Nuka-World raiders. Both will continue to capture and experiment on humans and sentient synths alike.

FromSoftware’s Soulsborne titles are notoriously abstract with their storytelling, with even their endings often confusing players. There are still several ways to play more destructive, villainous roles in their games, leading to concrete chaotic endings, such as one seen in Elden Ring.

Similar to Dark Souls, Elden Ring has players embody an unnamed, reanimated being known as a Tarnished, as they set out to explore the Lands Between and strive to find a way to repair the titular Elden Ring, fighting multiple gigantic, monstrous bosses along the way.

While players can restore order to the Lands Between, they can also choose to destroy the world by defeating Mohg, the Omen and becoming imbued with the Flame of Frenzy by the Three Fingers, ultimately resulting in the Erdtree and the greater world’s destruction.

One of the best RPGs to debut in the ninth console generation was 2023’s record-breaking Game of the Year, the exploratory turn-based RPG Baldur's Gate 3.

Set roughly 100 years after the events of Baldur's Gate II: Shadows of Amn, Baldur's Gate 3 stars a new player-character attempting to stop mind flayers, cure themselves of parasitic tadpoles, and stop the Absolute, otherwise known as the Netherbrain, from restoring the Illithid Empire.

However, players can ultimately take the Netherbrain’s power for themselves in the end, resulting in the citizens of Baldur’s Gate becoming mind flayers, dutiful warriors, or mindless fighters, among other dark outcomes such as the player simply killing everyone.

When it comes to finding a series full of brutal, often villainous protagonists, players would be hard pressed to find one darker than Warhammer 40,000. It’s filled with ruthless Space Marines that perceive any life that is not human as heretical and ripe for slaying.

While there are several great 40K games available on the PS5, there’s only one RPG from the series that debuted on the system, with that game being Warhammer 40,000: Rogue Trader. It stars the titular Rogue Trader, as they venture into the Koronus Expanse beyond the borders of the Imperium of Man.

Here, players can conquer alien worlds in the name of the God-Emperor or go rogue by aligning with the forces of Chaos, but no matter what path players go down, the people of the Koronus Expanse will surely suffer.

One of the most recent RPGs to allow players to dive headfirst into a truly despicable playthrough is Rebel Wolves’ debut title, The Blood of Dawnwalker.

Set in the fictional Eastern European kingdom of Vale Sangora after it was taken over by the Ancient Roman vampire Brencis, The Blood of Dawnwalker introduces the young half-human, half-vampire Coen of Laslea, as he sets out to save his family from the clutches of Brencis.

While Coen can be played as a heroic young warrior, players can instantly go about drinking the blood of everyone in the valley and even go so far as to make a deal with Brencis in the end. Even if Brencis is defeated, it might not have been a good choice in the long run, as the Black Death is said to return to the valley following Brencis’ death and the loss of his healing blood.

Without a doubt, the most villainous RPG PS5 owners can play as of the time of this article’s writing is Stunlock Studios’ V Rising.

Unlike the more altruistic half-vampire protagonist of Dawnwalker, V-Rising has people play as a monstrous, customizable, ancient vampire lord right from the get-go. They awaken from a 800-year-long slumber to reconquer the medieval steampunk land of Vardoran.

As a vampire lord, players can set out to say humans and monsters alike with a wide variety of weapons, spells, and vampiric abilities, but they can also build a living, six-story-tall castle that can be used to farm humans and animals alike for future thirst-quenching activities.
