6 JRPGs That Got Snubbed at The Game Awards 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Murillo Zerbinatto is a JRPG enthusiast, writer, and editor. He has played over 300 RPGs during his gaming career, with 90% of them being JRPGs.

As has already been publicly revealed, DualShockers is part of The Game Awards jury, so while I have some say in which games get nominated, I am just one voice among hundreds of journalists and many, many outlets. And just because I sit on the jury doesn't mean I agree with every outcome. Speaking from personal experience, none of the games I nominated for Game of the Year made the cut, and fair enough, my ballot was mostly JRPGs.

Each year, JRPGs continue to earn greater recognition, and that shift is clearly reflected at The Game Awards.

With that in mind, I put together a list of JRPGs that got snubbed at The Game Awards 2026. You will find titles overlooked entirely, with no nominations, alongside JRPGs that earned a spot in one category but deserved recognition in others. That isn't to say the nominees didn't earn their accolades, but as usual, JRPGs keep getting neglected at these events in favor of mainstream juggernauts that sometimes fall short of the sheer excellence found in B-side JRPGs.

It was a massive joy to see Trails in the Sky 2nd Chapter earn a nomination for Best Role Playing Game. I recognize that landing a Game of the Year nod would have been a tall order, and that is completely okay. However, if there is one thing the entire Trails series excels at, it is total dedication to its story, character development, and overarching narrative. With that in mind, leaving it out of Best Narrative feels borderline criminal.

The entire heart of Trails beats through its storytelling. 2nd Chapter picks up right where Trails in the Sky 1st Chapter left off, expanding across Liberl and the growing threats of Ouroboros while diving intimately into each party member's backstory. It's gripping and enthralling, and even though it can occasionally be a slow burn and a bit bloated, the narrative payoff is sensational. Across all the Trails games I have played (seven so far), the series has never once missed the mark on its climaxes and final chapters, a feat worthy of serious praise.

One of the strangest choices at this year's TGAs was seeing Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave land in Best Role Playing Game, especially considering Fire Emblem Engage previously competed in Best Sim/Strategy. I heard compelling arguments from a friend suggesting that because the RPG elements in Fortune's Weave were pushed further into the spotlight, the jury may have deemed it a better fit for the RPG category. Still, I suspect it was simply an oversight.

To me, it would have made far more sense for Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave to compete in Best Sim/Strategy, since being a tactical strategy RPG is the franchise's entire identity. There are two major reasons for this: first, if it hadn't taken a slot in Best RPG, another deserving JRPG could have slipped in, such as the very next entry on this list. Second, looking at the current Best Sim/Strategy lineup might leave you scratching your head, since the nominees barely seem connected to one another. What do Pokémon Pokopia, Mewgenics, and Star Wars Zero Company actually have in common? Nothing, aside from sharing a TGA category.

Following my train of thought about Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave ideally competing in Best Sim/Strategy instead of Best RPG, I could easily picture The Adventures of Elliot: The Millennium Tales sliding into that open RPG slot. I realize the title, The Adventures, might make it sound like a pure action-adventure game. Yet, Square Enix officially classifies it as an RPG, and gaming outlets generally follow suit.

Square Enix crafted an action RPG utilizing the HD-2D visual style, and the result turned out wonderfully. While its review averages hovered around 78, which explains why it missed out on nominations, I firmly believe the game deserved far more love. It is colorful and whimsical, brimming with a nostalgic sense of adventure and an intriguing narrative spanning multiple timelines (even if it completely glosses over the classic paradoxes of time travel). Then again, maybe this is just me wanting to shine a brighter spotlight on HD-2D projects so Square Enix keeps investing in smaller original IPs.

Do we need a dedicated Strategy/Tactical category separated from Simulation games? Hard to say, though several tactical JRPGs could definitely benefit from the spotlight.

Brigandine Abyss is nothing short of a miracle, truly. The original Brigandine debuted on the PS1 and remained completely obscure, even among hardcore enthusiasts. Against all odds, Happinet revived the dormant IP with The Legend of Runersia, which apparently performed well enough to greenlight another mainline follow-up: Brigandine Abyss. If The Game Awards ever introduces a category like Second Wind or Back From the Dead, Brigandine would easily earn a spot on the ballot.

I know seeing two Trails games nominated in the same year would sound wild, especially since the franchise only recently began gaining wider global recognition thanks to its remakes, but Trails in the Sky 1st Chapter could run only because the modern mainline entries came first. Do you enjoy the seamless blend of real-time field action and command-based turn combat? That hybrid system was born in Trails through Daybreak and carried forward into every subsequent release.

Trails beyond the Horizon continues the overarching saga that began in Trails in the Sky, which makes it a baffling place to jump in if you haven't been following the story. That would be like starting The Lord of the Rings with The Return of the King. Nihon Falcom makes minimal effort to recap past events, yet largely assumes enthusiasts have played the earlier chapters (which is precisely why they are working so hard to remake older entries). Hopefully, in the coming years, we will see non-remake Trails entries breaking into major RPG categories as well.

Honestly, it is well past time The Game Awards introduced a dedicated Best Remake/Remaster category, as in my experience, these projects are often judged unfairly by the jury when stacked directly against brand-new releases. Without diving too deep into that debate, if Dragon Quest VII Reimagined had been a brand-new original title rather than a remake, I am almost certain it would have landed a TGA nomination, particularly in Best RPG.

Dragon Quest VII Reimagined was, well, thoughtfully reimagined for modern audiences. The original release was notoriously bloated, taking hours just to reach your very first combat encounter. While the remake trimmed away a few islands from the classic version, those cuts worked wonders for the pacing. Both combat and character progression were cleanly streamlined, and random encounters were ditched in favor of visible monsters roaming the map. Dragon Quest VII Reimagined is a phenomenal achievement that delights newcomers and longtime veterans alike.
