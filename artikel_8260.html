Day 31: The Concert From the Castle

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

With the moon still plump and full, I set out through the mountains from the restful town I’d slept in.

It was daylight, and as I carefully descended the path strewn with loose rocks, I listened to the book that taught me about the Road to Santiago.

In the town before my destination, I planned to stop and eat breakfast. The cafe cats caught my attention as I approached. As the whole street came into view, I saw my Italian brother smiling at me. I ran to him and gave him a hug.

We always managed to stumble upon each other without planning, and I loved it.

When we made it to the city of Ponferrada, we went directly to the donativo albergue—San Nicolás de Flüe Pilgrims Hostel—to wait for it to open. The courtyard was large, with many trees, a large wooden totem, and a vine-covered sitting area. I went into the church nearby looking for a restroom. What I found, though, made me take a seat in the pew.

Within those tiny walls, an extraordinary mural was painted on the ceiling, depicting scenes from the Camino de Santiago, the four cardinal directions, the elements, and the stars. It stirred both the artist and the spiritual side within me.

When I went back to where we were relaxing, our Argentine brother had arrived. The three of us called ourselves “Familia Real”, the name emphasizing the bond we had created.

Once again, without planning, we were all together.

As a religious donativo albergue, I was placed on the woman’s side of the building. When I saw the name of the room I’d been assigned to, I couldn’t help but smile. “Cruz del Ferro.”

That place would forever hold a special place in my heart.

The brothers and I went on a side quest to a bodega overlooking the Castle of the Knights Templar.

After the siesta hours ended, I went to the Castle of the Knights Templar to embark on the tourist part of the day. I’d made it a few feet into the castle walkway when I saw my pilgrim friend below on the street. He was a musician who carried a flute with him everywhere, taking full advantage to play and sing and jam at every opportunity. I yelled down, asking him to play, but that was already his plan.

A man he met in the city joined him with a violin and I was entranced.

Standing on a castle wall, listening to music from artists I knew, with the city and mountains stretching out behind them, felt surreal.

That moment will forever be stamped into my memory.

After each song ended, I clapped from my perch on the castle wall, and the pilgrim smiled, skipping around with pride. His passion for music poured out of him with such a burning fire, and I felt lucky to witness it.

I knew I had a lot more to see, though, so I went on my way.

Exploring the exterior, I went level after level, corridor after corridor. I don’t like big houses, but now I want to live in a castle.

The secret passageways and rooms reminded me of the forts I used to build as a child.

And being on top of that tower overlooking the castle, the city, and the mountains?

I learned more about medieval culture than I ever had in my life. I explored an exhibit of a collection of books. The texts on display were all shapes and sizes, some as thick as my head, some smaller than a deck of cards.

Had it not been for exhaustion hitting me, I could’ve stayed for several more hours.

After 2.5 hours, though, I knew it was time to move on.

A grocery run on my way back to the albergue, a simple dinner in the chaotic kitchen, then quality time with my brothers.

The day had mirrored the life I’d grown accustomed to on the Camino.

In the kitchen at the end of the night, I met a fellow American for the first time. He’d seen me before in a different town and started the conversation with,

“You were working on your laptop and all I could think was ‘damn, she must be writing a book.’”
