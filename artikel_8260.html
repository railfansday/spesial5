Snow Capped Mountain Views in Arizona

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

This morning I got up nice and early at our tent site before the sun came up. The wind was pretty bad last night and was whipping our tents around. I managed to sleep just fine though. Once I got up this morning I made coffee and then began breaking down camp. Then we got moving just as the sun was coming up.

The goal for today is basically just to hike as far as we possibly can. I was looking through my food last night to access how much I had left. There are a couple of different options in terms of which road we hitch into to get back to Flagstaff. If we push really hard we could get in tomorrow night. And if we go a little slower or go further we could get in Sunday night. I think even being out for 2 more full days is going to be cutting it close in terms of how much food I have left. I definitely did grab enough from Gorf’s car. Or maybe I’ve just been eating more than I expected to. Resupplying is always one of the hardest things to do on a thru hike in my opinion. I’ve thru hiked nearly 23,000 miles and I still struggle with it regularly. It can be hard to properly predict the kind of appetite you’ll have and also what kinds of foods will be appealing to you.

Our day started with some double track dirt road walking, with Mount Humphreys peaking out in the distance.

Once we got going the trail continued through rolling fields of shrubs and small trees. It reminds me of a Montana prairie at times to be honest. I’m surprised how the Arizona Trail can remind me of so many different places all at once! Some sections remind me of Montana, Idaho, Wyoming, and even Colorado and New Mexico. Our route brought us through the Babbitt ranch passage this morning. I believe we’ll continue on this passage through the ranch property for the next full day at least.

Unfortunately the wind that persisted for the entire day yesterday was still raging on today. It was still only around 20-30 MPH with gusts up to 40 MPH. There are certainly worse environmental and weather conditions that you can deal with on the trail other than wind, but it can still be its own problem. There have been a handful of days on the trail over the years where I encountered wind around 50-60 MPH consistently. That speed of wind can literally push you over at times. I always try to remember those moments when I get bothered by some light, manageable wind.

The miles were nice and easy this morning through this prairie.

As we continued on this morning the trail weaved through wide grasslands with views of Mount Humphreys off in the distance. We’re going to get closer and closer to the mountain over the course of the day until we eventually go right by the base of it. There is a side trail to bag the peak which is around 13,500 feet high. It was absolutely stunning watching the mountain get closer and closer as the morning progressed. The peak got snow in the recent rainstorm, and it looks lovely up there now.

Early on in the morning I had already begun to feel a pretty ravenous hunger coursing through me. I don’t know if it’s because I’m aware of how little food I have, or if it’s just true hiker hunger setting in. Either way, I was starving. I ate a bunch of my snacks and the remaining rations for the whole day were already running low. Every now and then Slide would snack on something and offer me some of it, knowing I really didn’t have much. I’ll have plenty to eat to get to the next town safely. But when you’re hungry out here sometimes it is really hard to satisfy with just small snacks and things. I call it a hunger that only town food can solve.

As the morning went on we got closer and closer to the massive mountain in the distance.

Around 10:30AM we went past signs for a wildlife watering station, which is of course our next water source. We’re the wildlife out here now! It was just off the trail and a necessary stop. The next sources all look pretty nasty, and the most appealing next source is a long way ahead. We won’t reach that until sometime this evening around the time it gets dark. The wind was still whipping by the time we arrived so we hid behind some shrubs for a brief reprieve. I appreciate the brief “quiet” as well.

After we left the water source we continued along similar terrain for the next few hours. The trail got a bit more “forested” at times, especially in terms of a desert environment. We even watched a large group of elk which were grazing on a hill above the trail. There was one big bull in the group and they all scattered when they heard us coming from a distance. Elk hunting season just started here and the elk definitely know it. We’ve seen a lot of hunters around.

This puffy plant looks like some sort of milkweed. It looked wonderful dancing in the wind.

For most of the afternoon we were continuing through pastures and grasslands, with Mount Humphreys getting progressively closer. The wind made it comfortable to keep all of my layers on for most of the day. I quite enjoy hiking a bit more bundled up when it’s comfortable to do so. It feels cozy! I can’t believe my Sambob Alpha 90 layers are still going out here on the AZT after all of the miles in them last year. But they sure are. And I’ve been loving the ibex wool air puffy that I’m using out on a longer thru hike for the first time. That thing is really cozy but doesn’t get me too sweaty because of the wool. I’m surprised how much I’ve hiked in it on the AZT. I think I’ve done more hiking in a puffy on this trail than any other!

Later on in the afternoon the trail transitioned a bit from the never-ending grasslands to a taller wheatgrass. Which might not seem like anything too special, but is incredibly beautiful while it blows in the wind. All of the tall grass and wildflowers were dancing around in the heavy wind. That was the one bright side of the wind I suppose. By this time Mount Humphreys and its snow-covered peak were right in front of us as well. Boy, is that mountain massive and stunning. It’s hard to imagine such a large peak in Arizona.

Mount Humphreys much closer in the distance now!

Slide was up ahead of me at one point and stopped off to look at something alongside the trail. It was a horny toed lizard that was absolutely adorable. It stuck around for a while and we got to get really close to it. That was one of the bigger horny toed lizards that I’ve seen.

In the late afternoon the sun actually peaked out from the clouds for the first time all day. I was still wearing my Alpha and my puffy and all of a sudden it was scorching out! And just as quickly the clouds rolled back in and I was cold again with fewer layers on. The desert is a wild place.

The trail continued to bless us with great views, large juniper trees, and flowers. As well as those lovely little bridges across the cattle guards. There were some large white plants along the trail which looked like a sort of fluffy milkweed, and those looked fantastic in the blowing wind.

By around 4:30PM the trail had brought us out onto dirt roads. We passed by a bike packer who stopped to chat with us for a minute. He said the upcoming water source was fantastic, which is always nice to hear. But to get to the water source we would have to climb up a particularly steep hill. There was no choice but to climb it. Up top there was a massive trough overflowing with cold, clear water. The recent rain had that thing filled to the brim! As we sat there and the wind blew, water splashed up over the edge of the trough. Slide and I sat there and filled up everything that we would need for the rest of the day, overnight, and tomorrow morning.

Our delicious water source for the evening. It was ice cold and crystal clear thanks to lots of rain.

When we left the water source we got to enjoy going back down that large hill. Then continued along a dirt road for the remainder of the night. By 5PM we had already made it 28 miles, but still had a ways to go for the day. I’m always pleased though when the mileage remaining for the day is less than 10 miles. Somehow 9 miles or less feels very manageable. It’s easy to tell myself that I only have a few more hours of hiking to go.

Before the sun set a jackrabbit ran by us near the trail and we got to watch it scamper off. That was delightful. Those jackrabbits are so fast and hear you coming from a mile away! Then we enjoyed glorious views of the sunset over a field, with Mount Humphreys off in the distance. And a lone cow was out there in the field too in front of the mountain. I thought that made the moment even more beautiful.

This cow looked like it was modeling for calendar or something in front of Mount Humphreys.

My favorite time of day always seems to be when the sun is going down. Watching the sunset is a reward for a long day of hiking. Hiking into the dark is a fair price to pay to get to bear witness to the totality of a glorious sunset. By the time the sun went down I was quite ready to be done for the day. I’d been tired for a while but managed to keep it moving regardless.

Sometime after 7PM I checked my watch and saw we had gone about 36 miles for the day. So we continued along and started looking for a nice flat spot to get set up for the night. We wound up going 36.8 miles before finding a spot and calling it a day. I was tired but felt good! My calves were still sore today, but not any worse than yesterday. They were maybe ever so slightly less sore today. What more could you ask for? After I got my tent set up I made dinner quickly and did my stretching. Then I hardly had any energy left and just called it a night.
