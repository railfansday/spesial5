Dow futures shed 90 points as traders await Nvidia earnings and Anthropic’s IPO

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Nasdaq-100 futures declined 1.4% as Nvidia stock fell close to 3% before its Tuesday earnings report and Anthropic's Thursday debut

Nvidia $NVDA stock fell close to 3% in premarket trading on Monday, weighing on U.S. stock futures ahead of the chipmaker’s fiscal third-quarter earnings report and Anthropic’s initial public offering this week.

Dow Jones Industrial Average futures were down 90 points, a drop of 0.2%. S&P 500 futures fell 0.6%, and Nasdaq $NDAQ-100 futures declined 1.4%.

Nvidia reports results after the close on Tuesday, Nov. 17. Interim CEO Colette Kress will field questions, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Jensen Huang stepped down as Nvidia CEO amid reports that he had two extramarital affairs, one of them with a woman suspected of spying for China, according to CNBC. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has called for a “dramatic shakeup” and pressed the board to find its next chief executive from beyond Nvidia’s existing ranks. Huang’s allies are campaigning for his return, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Other stocks tied to artificial intelligence also declined in premarket trading. Shares of Micron $MU Technology, Intel $INTC and Advanced Micro Devices all slid by more than 5%. Dell $DELL Technologies stock dropped more than 5%, and Oracle $ORCL stock fell close to 4%.

Anthropic is scheduled to begin trading Thursday, aiming for a valuation of at least $1.3 trillion, according to The Wall Street Journal. CNBC said the target is roughly $1.4 trillion, with the offering price projected to land in a range of $70 to $78 a share.

The Commerce Department unveiled fresh curbs on how advanced American AI chips can be accessed from afar, according to The Wall Street Journal. The possibility of steeper interest rate increases by the Federal Reserve is also putting pressure on technology shares.

Tech stocks sank in Asia. Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. closed nearly 6% lower, Japan’s Nikkei 225 fell 2.4% after the country’s third-quarter GDP shrank, and South Korea’s Kospi slumped 3.6%. Hong Kong’s Hang Seng Index gained 0.5%. In Europe, the Stoxx 600 was up 0.1%.

The Dow, S&P 500 and Nasdaq each climbed over 2% last week, their best weekly showing since August, according to CNBC.
