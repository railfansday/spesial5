Nissan posted a net loss and cut its sales forecast as China slump deepens

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The automaker lowered its fiscal-year volume target to 2.97 million-3 million vehicles from 3.15 million, its second cut since August

Nissan Motor posted a net loss of ¥43.5 billion ($275 million) for the quarter ended Sept. 30 and cut its full-year global sales forecast as demand in China weakened.

Over the 12 months through March 2027, Nissan expects to sell 2.97 million to 3 million vehicles worldwide, a reduction from its earlier projection of 3.15 million. The company said China sales are under more strain than it had foreseen, pointing to soft demand, a quicker move toward new energy vehicles than predicted, and tougher competition.

The quarterly net loss narrowed from ¥106.2 billion a year earlier, a change the company tied to lower restructuring-related items. Net revenue rose 8.2% to ¥3.11 trillion. Operating profit fell to ¥2.1 billion from ¥51.5 billion, which Nissan attributed to the absence of last year's benefit from lower U.S. emissions-related expenses.

Analysts had expected operating profit of ¥54.9 billion, according to CNBC. They had also expected a net profit of ¥15.57 billion, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Nissan kept its full-year operating profit forecast of ¥200 billion and its target of automotive net cash above ¥1 trillion at year-end. It expects ¥120 billion in operating profit in the second half. The company raised its sales outlook for the U.S. and Japan.

"China remains our most significant challenge, and we need to remain agile," CEO Ivan Espinosa said in a statement.

Espinosa told CNBC that Nissan intends to widen its lineup of China-developed vehicles for export, raising the count to five models from two and aiming at 12 regional market clusters in the "very near term." "We can defend ourselves from these very aggressive players by using exactly the same elements that they have: speed, cost, and good technology," he said.

Espinosa characterized China's market as a tough one in which volumes are falling steeply, according to The Wall Street Journal. Chief Financial Officer George Leondis said ending the year with a net profit is Nissan's top priority.

In August, Nissan trimmed its annual global sales target to 3.15 million vehicles from 3.3 million, citing persistent weakness in China. At the time, the company reported a net profit of ¥3.8 billion for the April-June quarter, its first quarterly profit in two years.

The company said its Re:Nissan restructuring plan generated ¥110 billion in additional cost savings in the first half. Nissan will announce a new business plan in February 2027.
