A BMW supplier warned that chip prices are about to get 'mind-boggling'

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Aumovio's interim head of finance said the company expects a gross earnings hit of about €1 billion in 2027 from higher material prices

Aumovio, the German auto supplier spun off from Continental, cautioned on Thursday that chip costs will climb sharply in 2027 because AI data center demand is strengthening chipmakers’ leverage over prices, according to Bloomberg.

Claus Bauer, Aumovio's interim head of finance, told analysts during a conference call that the supply crunch's impact on the auto industry will "explode" in 2027, according to Bloomberg. He said Aumovio anticipates a hit to gross earnings of about €1 billion ($1.1 billion) in 2027, driven by rising costs for materials, chiefly chip inflation.

Data centers are buying up chips in such quantity and at such high prices that automotive customers can't keep up, Bauer said. He called the cost dynamic "mind-boggling."

Bauer estimated that data centers could see the capacity chipmakers allocate to them double, and said that segment has "no price sensitivity whatsoever." Aumovio does not expect much respite from data center demand in 2027, he said, though limits on power generation could eventually slow the buildout.

Aumovio negotiates supplier contracts annually, so pricing can shift substantially whenever a new year begins, according to Bloomberg. The company plans to recoup part of the added costs by raising prices for customers, and to cover the remainder by redesigning products and trimming spending more widely. Aumovio counts BMW, Volkswagen, Ford $F and BYD as clients, and relies on chips in a range of products, including brake systems and vehicle computers.

Aumovio reported third-quarter results on Thursday. Adjusted sales were €4.0 billion, down 12.0% from €4.5 billion a year earlier. The adjusted EBIT margin was 3.5%, compared with 3.4% in the year-earlier quarter.

The company confirmed its 2026 guidance of adjusted sales of €17.0 billion to €17.5 billion and an adjusted EBIT margin of 3.0% to 4.0%. It said it expects higher raw materials prices and increased costs for memory components to weigh on 2026.

Memory makers have issued similar warnings about tight supply. SK Hynix CEO Kwak Noh-jung forecast in July that 2027 will be the worst year for memory supply in the industry's history. Kwak said customer demand would stay above the company's capacity beyond 2030.

Logitech CEO Hanneke Faber said in September that AI demand had made chips scarce. She said she expects little relief over the next year to year and a half.
