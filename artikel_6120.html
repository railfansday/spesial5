The (see rates and fees) has a strong reputation — and for good reason. With high earning rates, benefits that easily outweigh its $95 annual fee and access to Chase's transfer partners, it's easy to see why this card is a great fit for both beginners and die-hard points and miles enthusiasts alike.

This card received a refresh in June. While one change might've disappointed Hyatt fans, many cardholders came out ahead thanks to the added perks and bonus categories.

That said, no card is a perfect fit for everyone. If you're looking for lounge access, elite status or high earning rates on general travel purchases, another card may be a better option. The 's hotel credit can cover the card's $95 annual fee on its own if you use the full $100 on a stay you would have booked anyway.

Compare the Chase Travel rate with the price of booking directly before you pay. (The first $100 in eligible Chase Travel hotel purchases each account anniversary year does not earn points.)

Maximizing this credit will, on its own, put you $5 in the green against the Sapphire Preferred's annual fee.

When former TPG credit cards writer Augusta Stone used this credit, she effectively paid just $35 for a night away at the Atlanta Marriott Alpharetta.

The card also reimburses one Global Entry, TSA PreCheck or Nexus application fee, up to $120 (every four years). Charge the eligible fee to the card to receive the statement credit. If you already have a membership, you can use the credit to pay an eligible application fee for someone else. I recommend Global Entry if you travel internationally because it includes TSA PreCheck benefits.

It's worth noting that the Sapphire Preferred is one of the few cards with a sub-$100 annual fee to offer this benefit.

Primary cardholders can also get at least 12 months of complimentary Apple TV (by activating the offer through Chase by Dec. 31). Apple TV costs either $14.99 per month or $119 for the annual plan, so either way, you're saving a lot of money with this perk. Be sure to activate it soon, since you only have until the end of 2026.

TPG credit cards writer Stephanie Stevens calls out the vacation homes category as a big plus for her. She's booked over 60 Airbnbs, so there's plenty of opportunity for her to earn lots of bonus points with this category.

I live in Portland, Oregon, a city with a strong mass transit system (by U.S. standards). That means I often swipe my Sapphire Preferred for transit tickets, so I'm glad that this card's general travel rewards category is broadly defined.

My main criticism of the Sapphire Preferred's earning categories is that its grocery category is restricted to online orders only. Walmart, Target and wholesale stores are also excluded from this category, which is a major downside for me as a frequent Walmart shopper. Still, this is a relatively minor critique given that the card's overall earning structure is very strong for a $95 annual fee.

You can redeem Ultimate Rewards points for a variety of options, including statement credits, gift cards, Amazon.com purchases and travel booked through Chase Travel.

The includes access to Chase's Points Boost feature, which means you can redeem your points for up to 1.75 cents per point (depending on the redemption). While Points Boost can offer solid value in the right circumstances, I generally prefer transferring my points to Chase's airline and hotel partners, where I can often get significantly more value.

Speaking of transferring points, Chase offers 14 transfer partners, including World of Hyatt, United MileagePlus, Air Canada Aeroplan and Air France-KLM Flying Blue.

Personally, I transfer most of my Ultimate Rewards points to World of Hyatt. Last year, I transferred 87,500 Ultimate Rewards points to Hyatt to book 10 nights at different hotels across Germany, easily saving me hundreds of dollars. (Note that the number of points you'd need to replicate this strategy would be higher by default with the updated 4:3 transfer ratio.)

Despite the unfavorable transfer ratio, Hyatt is still a compelling transfer partner thanks to its fixed award chart pricing. And with a deep roster of airline and hotel partners, Chase still offers plenty of opportunities for valuable redemptions with other partners.

For example, TPG's managing editor for credit cards, Giselle Gomez, transferred 22,000 Ultimate Rewards points to Air France-KLM Flying Blue for a one-way flight between Miami International Airport (MIA) and Charles de Gaulle Airport (CDG). Thanks to a 20% transfer bonus, she was able to save 3,000 points. This ticket would've cost $1,504 without points, so after accounting for $139 in taxes and fees, she received a phenomenal over 6 cents per point in value.

The is one of the best travel rewards cards for its annual fee, but it isn't the right fit for everyone.

Getting the most from the Sapphire Preferred means tracking bonus categories, statement credits and partner perks. If you prefer a card that earns the same rate on every purchase, this setup may feel like more work than it's worth.

This card provides the most value to people who travel regularly and redeem their rewards for travel. While Ultimate Rewards points can be redeemed for cash back, higher value comes from booking travel through the Chase Travel portal or transferring points to airline and hotel partners. If you don't expect to use either strategy, the Sapphire Preferred may be harder to justify.

The Sapphire Preferred is undoubtedly one of the strongest mid-tier cards, but as with any other card, it's important to make sure it fits your lifestyle and travel needs.
