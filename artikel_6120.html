Hotel Chains’ Middle East Recovery Stalls as Peak Season Arrives

The Middle East is a small slice of most hotel chains’ revenue but a bigger part of their pipelines. So far, they’re holding to their development plans, even as a second weak peak season looms.

The world’s largest hotel groups saw their Middle East business recover in the third quarter from the collapse that followed the outbreak of the U.S.-Iran war. But most now expect the region to weaken again in the fourth quarter, its most important season of the year, as Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia prolong the uncertainty — and event cancellations there weigh on demand.

Accor, the France-headquartered hotels giant, has the most exposure to the region compared with its peers. The Middle East accounts for about 11% of its global room revenue, with Saudi Arabia at about 5% and the UAE at roughly 4%.

Its Middle East RevPAR fell 3% in the third quarter. “Saudi and Egypt continued to perform well, and the UAE RevPAR improved materially from the second quarter,” said Martine Gerow, group CFO, on an October 22 earnings call. UAE RevPAR was down by just over 20% and occupancy was back in the mid-60s percent, she said.

The world's largest hotel groups reported a Middle East recovery in the third quarter from the collapse that followed the outbreak of the U.S.-Iran war, but most now guide to renewed weakness in the fourth quarter — the region's peak season. Accor, with about 11% of global room revenue in the region, saw Middle East RevPAR fall 3% in Q3 and assumes a roughly 12% decline in Q4, with CFO Martine Gerow saying the company has taken a tougher view on Saudi Arabia as Houthi attacks intensify on Riyadh. Marriott's Middle East RevPAR fell 12% in Q3, an improvement from a drop of more than 40% in Q2, and the company expects a steeper fourth-quarter decline against tough comparisons. Hilton's Middle East and Africa RevPAR fell 5.6% and the company now expects a low double-digit decline for the region for the full year, with CEO Chris Nassetta saying owned hotels there will cut more than $20 million from earnings this year. IHG was broadly flat across the wider region but down 12% across the six Gulf countries, and Hyatt held RevPAR down only about 3% as domestic travel offset a 40% drop in international inbound. Development pipelines, meanwhile, are holding: IHG said it is aware of no project cancellations, and Wyndham recently signed five new contracts across Saudi Arabia and the UAE.
