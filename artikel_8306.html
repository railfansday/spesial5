Arctic Town Kills Dozens of Grizzlies Feeding on Dump

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

For years, the grizzlies near Inuvik in Canada’s western Arctic relied on a stable source of food — the town dump. Generations of bears grew up feeding there. In 2025, wildlife officials found that over 40 grizzlies regularly visited the dump.

In 2025, before the fence was built, trail cameras captured multiple grizzlies hanging around the dump, looking for food. Photo: Government of the Northwest Territories

It was too much. This year, the town built an electric fence around the site, hoping the bears would move on. They didn’t. Some started coming into town looking for food. Others just hung around the fence, searching for a way in.

“They would just do these laps around the dump, and they would just be emaciated, sickly,” said Toby Halle, a Renewable Resources officer based in Inuvik, told the CBC. “They don’t know what to do with themselves. And quite frankly, we don’t know what to do with them.”

Inuvik, population about 3,500, was the first community in that part of the Arctic to try to wean the bears off their dependence on dump food with such a fence, which cost about $700,000. The problem was, the bears had not learned to depend on any other source of food.

“We’ve probably had three to four generations of bears that have been using that landfill,” said local mayor Peter Clarkson. In the end, the town took a drastic solution — but one that was probably unavoidable in the circumstances: They shot 28 bears.

Tragic though this is, officials hope that within the next couple of years, the remaining bears in the area will move on and learn to eat more natural fare.

Many other Arctic towns still have open dumps where bears come to feed. Photo: Government of the Northwest Territories
