XRP Scams Soar Across Social Media, Here’s What To Watch Out For

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Scam attempts aimed at XRP holders have reached intense levels, according to recent alerts from a well-known figure in the XRP Ledger community. Vet, a pseudonymous X account who also works as a dUNL validator while contributing to the XRP Ledger

Foundation has used his platform several times to highlight the growing threat.

In a recent check using platform data tools, he found that around 11.5 million posts directed at the XRP community appeared within a single 30-day stretch.

The vast majority dangled fake airdrops or reward schemes designed to trick people into handing over funds or linking their wallets. He described the sheer number as eye-opening and pushed users to stay highly alert.

Vet has also pointed out how quickly fraudsters react to genuine positive news. When real companies announce partnerships or integrations involving the XRP Ledger, scammers often rush to set up lookalike addresses and launch tokens that borrow those brand names.

His advice is straightforward: treat every unexpected offer with suspicion, and trust only details that come directly from the company’s official channels. In many cases, the real institutions never publish public issuer addresses.

He has spoken about the constant stream of scam tags that can make notifications almost impossible to manage. Seeing community members engage with these posts frustrates him, and he keeps returning to the same clear message. No one is giving away free XRP. The safest move is usually to ignore the approach completely.

Even specific token launches have drawn his scrutiny. After a large Japanese firm introduced a yen-backed stablecoin, Vet warned that any version appearing on the XRP Ledger under a similar ticker was almost certainly fake without an official issuer confirmation.

His ongoing posts underline a simple reality. Rising interest in XRP attracts more bad actors who try to cash in on the attention. For everyday holders, staying skeptical and double-checking everything remains the best protection.
