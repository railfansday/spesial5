Hong Kong has just put Hyperliquid [HYPE] on its watchlist. And yet, traders don’t seem to be bothered.

In fact, on Hyperliquid itself, the action is only heating up now.

On Wednesday, Hong Kong’s Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) added Hyperliquid to its Alert List. The problem cited was that they don’t hold a licence to operate in the city.

The regulator’s entry covers two names; Hyperliquid and Hyperliquid Hong Kong. It also shared the exchange’s trading website. It is believed that the platform may be serving Hong Kong users, presenting itself as a local operator, or pitching its services to people in the city.

With this addition, the SFC’s list of suspicious crypto trading platforms has grown to 69 names now. At the time of writing, Hyperliquid hadn’t commented on the development.

Back in June, Singapore’s central bank placed Hyperliquid on its Investor Alert List which flags firms people might wrongly assume are regulated there. At the time, Hyperliquid’s response was simple — it never said it had a Singapore licence.

Now, there are two Asian financial hubs keeping an eye on the exchange.

While Hong Kong was flagging Hyperliquid, traders on the platform were busy betting on Anthropic.

On 18th November, the number of traders holding long positions on Anthropic perps hit a record 5,567.

A week ago, that figure was 2,926; nearly doubling in seven days. There were 1,996 short holders, with the same hinting at 2.8 longs for every short.

Most of that action was on Trade[XYZ]’s ANT market. According to Token Terminal, this alone accounted for 4,190 long holders,.

One caveat, though. These numbers count traders, not the size of their bets. And since the data adds up three markets, anyone trading on two of them gets counted twice.

The altcoin was trading at $67.66, at press time, following a hike of just 1% in the last 24 hours. Its price was still a long way from its late-October levels when it briefly climbed above $95. Since then, it’s been mostly downhill for the crypto.

There seemed to be some sign of a breather though. The RSI was below 30 during that drop. It has since come back to just nudging above its average.

The CMF at -0.06 suggested that sellers still had the upper hand, but the outflows were clearly weaker than earlier this month.
