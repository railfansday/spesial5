Why wasn't Szoboszlai's free-kick ruled out vs Man Utd?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Dominik Szoboszlai sparked Liverpool's rout of Man Utd with a free-kick goal after Leny Yoro was sent off, but should it have stood? United head coach Mauricio Pochettino believes Bradley Barcola was interfering with their defensive wall, although Bruno Fernandes appeared to push him

Mauricio Pochettino fumed over Leny Yoro's red card for Manchester United in their defeat to Liverpool, but also felt Dominik Szoboszlai's opener should not have stood – is he right?

Yoro's one-match suspension for the red card he received in the 4-0 defeat at Anfield has been overturned, despite referees' chief Howard Webb supporting the decision to send him off.

But Szoboszlai's goal from the free-kick Yoro conceded in the same incident was allowed to stand, with Pochettino believing it should have been disallowed because Liverpool winger Bradley Barcola was interfering with the Man Utd wall.

The IFAB Laws of the Game state: "If, when a free-kick is taken, an attacking team player is less than 1m (1yd) from a 'wall' formed by three or more defending team players, an indirect free-kick is awarded."

Pochettino referenced a similar decision last season when Eberechi Eze's goal from a free-kick for Crystal Palace at Chelsea was disallowed after ex-Palace captain Marc Guehi pushed Moises Caicedo to create a gap for the ball to fly through.

Pochettino said: "No one talks about that the goal wasn't legal, it should be disallowed because (Bradley) Barcola was touching our wall.

"Maybe in the last two years, they've changed the rule because I've been in America, but last season when (Eberechi) Eze scored for Palace (at Chelsea) you can see [it was disallowed].

"But VAR didn't check [Dominik Szoboszlai's goal]; that must be disallowed."

When Szoboszlai strikes the free-kick, Barcola is clearly within 1m of the United wall and, under the Laws of the Game, the goal should be ruled out.

However, a key difference between Eze's disallowed goal and Szoboszlai's free-kick is that Guehi shoves Caicedo and moves within 1m of the Chelsea wall, whereas Bruno Fernandes appears to push Barcola towards the Man Utd defenders.

In this case, it could be argued that Fernandes created the issue and Barcola ended up next to the Man Utd wall only because of his opponent's actions. The Laws of the Game do not provide an explanation for an attacker being pushed towards the defending team's wall.

VAR checks every goal and would have to decide whether it was a 'clear and obvious' error. Eze's goal was disallowed after the VAR had recommended a review to the referee over Guehi's involvement.

Another consideration is that Fernandes' push occurred before Szoboszlai struck the free-kick, meaning the ball was not in play, so it would not have been a foul by the United captain.

Had the referee spotted the push in the seconds before the free-kick was taken, he could have delayed the set-piece, disciplined Fernandes and then restarted from the original position.

Webb did not analyse the incident on the latest episode of Match Officials Mic'd Up.
