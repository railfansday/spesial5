The Chelsea problem Xabi Alonso cannot ignore - and who is really to blame

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Your weekly briefing on Chelsea, featuring team news and expert analysis from Dom Smith

There is no defending Chelsea, because again there was simply no defending by Chelsea.

One goal up and with home advantage against a Leeds team they had already beaten at Stamford Bridge this season in the Carabao Cup, Xabi Alonso and his players took that encouraging platform for success and ripped it up, deservedly losing 4-1.

At least Mauricio Pochettino and Manchester United could play the “we only had 10 men” card.

Whatever is failing to function right now at Chelsea is a complex web spun by poor decision-making and performances from a whole host of implicated parties.

The Chelsea defence showcases the Blues’ endemic struggles for consistency in a perfect microcosmic form, not least because there are so many stakeholders who must each accept their share of the blame.

An injury record peculiarly problematic for a team largely only playing once a week this season in the absence of any European football has left them without captain Reece James, summer signing Maxence Lacroix and the improved Jorrel Hato - players whose availability might not cure all their defensive ills, but would certainly help.

Their absences mean more minutes for players not showing any signs that they possess the composure or robustness needed to provide support for a potent attack and secure a return to the Champions League.

Malo Gusto was beaten twice for Brenden Aaronson’s run to goal for Leeds’ fourth - something that happens all too often for a player valued by Chelsea at £75million in the summer transfer market.

Wesley Fofana remains a liability, and his £75m transfer fee back in 2022 - £89.2m adjusted for inflation - surely makes him the worst pound-for-pound signing Chelsea have made in the BlueCo era.

And while Josh Acheampong, 20, has been the one Chelsea defender to draw consistent praise in the first three months of the campaign, he was suspect against Leeds.

So too was Levi Colwill, a player with enormous potential but who has so far failed to rediscover his physicality and form since the torn anterior cruciate ligament that kept him sidelined for almost all of last season.

A question it is worth Alonso and his coaching staff asking themselves: is 34-year-old Emi Martinez clearly a better goalkeeper than Mike Penders, the inevitable long-term No1?

There is a case to be made to fully blood Penders now, whatever promises might have been made to Martinez when he joined. The Argentine currently looks a shadow of the commanding player he was at Aston Villa.

Wesley Fofana must surely be Chelsea’s worst pound-for-pound signing in the BlueCo era

Individual errors from Fofana and Gusto and the softness of Colwill and Martinez mean plenty of the blame is rightly being portioned in their direction, but other parties are just as culpable.

Alonso, for example, has flattered to deceive as manager so far, switching between a back three and a back four with the result that his team look capable of playing neither, not both. His Chelsea side have scored a laudable 20 league goals but conceded a lamentable 20 at the other end.

Responsibility also lies at the boardroom door, where questionable recruitment has taken place. Benoit Badiashile and Axel Disasi were not at the standard required and have taken too long to move on (still neither player has permanently left the club).

And while he is far from the complete product, Trevoh Chalobah had been one of the more consistent centre-backs of the Clearlake era yet was moved on to high-flying Como this summer in favour of handing a greater amount of game time to Fofana.

If Chelsea are to get back to the pinnacle of English football, which is where the club sees itself, then recruitment, coaching and player performances must all improve.

Accepting shared responsibility is the first step towards revamping and properly rebuilding a defence that simply isn’t going to deliver Champions League football on current form.
