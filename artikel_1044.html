Mykhailo Mudryk has blamed the Ukrainian national football federation for the failed drugs test which saw him banned from football for 20 months.

Mudryk tested positive for the banned substance meldonium in 2024, and was initially banned for four years by the Football Association. He successfully appealed against the length of his ban to the Court of Arbitration for Sport, and returned to football in July 2026.

The Tottenham winger, who is on loan from Chelsea, spoke to Ukrainian YouTube channel Guest of Gordon, and said he blamed “the environment in the Ukrainian national team” for his suspension.

“I had never heard of a drug like meldonium. It is a drug you can only get in Eastern Europe. It could only have got into my body in Ukraine. There is no such drug in England.”

He said he had only been in Ukraine with the national team, which “points to the fact it was in a national team camp”.

Mudryk said that he received little support from association president Andriy Shevchenko or the rest of the organisation.

He said he cried and turned to alcohol in the immediate aftermath of the suspension.

“It was a bolt from the blue. I couldn't believe it [the positive test] was true,” he said. “It was like falling off a skyscraper. In a dream you would hit the ground. In life, I just kept on falling. It hit me very hard, mentally.”

Mudryk added he had spent millions of pounds on legal fees as well as personal training and physiotherapy in order to continue his career while not having access to facilities at Chelsea.

The Ukrainian football federation has been contacted for comment.
