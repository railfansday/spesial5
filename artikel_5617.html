COP31 Plants A Big Wet Kiss On Failed Carbon Capture Technology

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

COP31, the annual UN climate conference that took place this month, ended on a sour note after negotiators were unable to reach an agreement to include language calling for a transition away from fossil fuels in the final communiqué  — language that was included two years ago at the conclusion of COP 29 in Dubai.

How is it possible to explain such backsliding at a time when a Kelvin wave — a wall of unusually hot water — is impacting the coast of every country along the eastern edge of the Pacific Ocean? Authorities say unusually high temperatures all across Europe led to the premature death of more than 50,000 people last summer. In addition, huge swaths of the Earth’s land mass are experiencing either severe drought or punishing floods. How can anybody ignore the events happening before their very eyes?

You know the answer — MONEY! According to Kathryn McCallum, the director of strategy at the Climate Action Network Australia, there were 624 delegates from the 14 Pacificc Small Island Developing States at the conference in Turkey this month and 700 paid lobbyists for the carbon capture industry. In an email to CleanTechnica, she said,

“There is no public momentum from parties in the COP31 process asking for carbon capture, utilization and storage, yet it is in the final draft text. As a result, in the coming hours, parties at COP31 will decide whether to lock in a [methane] fired future with a rapid scale-up of carbon dumping. The potential inclusion of a huge increase in global carbon capture, utilization and storage (CCUS) would be one of the biggest loopholes for the fossil fuel industry ever to be included in a COP text. Climate advocates are highly alarmed and are urging parties to come to their senses and make decisions that advance global climate action rather than the interests of the gas industry.”

As it turns out, the knife in the back delivered by fossil fuel interests at COP31 was too much for negotiators from the EU and the UK to swallow. According to reNEWS, the European Union expressed its “profound disappointment” with the draft cover decision following the close of the COP31 conference.

“At a point where the world urgently needs action to avert disastrous level of global warming, the draft cover decision falls short,” a joint EU statement read. “We will not endorse a cover decision that does not respond to the science, that disregards the implementation gap, and risks deepening the chasm between global commitments and the action required to correct the overshoot and bring 1.5°C within reach. We will not endorse a cover decision that does not even mention fossil fuels, the driver of climate change.”

The statement listed a number on other concerns with the draft cover decision, including that the draft extols carbon capture, usage, and storage technologies “disproportionally” as a method to deal with the climate crisis. This is magical thinking, the statement by the EU said.

The UK also refused to be sucked in by the absurd claims that carbon capture was a plausible alternative to reducing the world’s reliance on fossil fuels. The Independent reports that UK energy secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh expressed regret that the talks had failed to produce the “bold ambition” needed, as the negotiations became mired in disagreement over a draft agreement that contained no mention of fossil fuels.

The UK formally objected to a proposed deal that “moves us backwards on the science” of tackling climate change. In a statement, Fahnbulleh said, “This COP sought to confront hard truths — that global warming is set to overshoot 1.5C and that we must act with ever more urgency to address it. The moment required bold ambition and we have been working tirelessly to try to build consensus. It is immensely disappointing we have been unable to do so.”

There are a number of ways to remediate the emissions from burning fossil fuels, which are nothing more than stored sunshine from millions of years ago. We could irradiate them with unicorn farts, incinerate them in nuclear fusion facilities, are store them in the basement at Mar-A-Loco, where large quantities of toxic fumes are already present. There is only one problem — none of those solutions work. In fact, the only thing that does work is burning less fossil fuels, until we are able to phase them out entirely.

In  2025, Mark Jacobson, a professor of civil and environmental engineering at Stanford University, and his colleagues, published a research paper in the journal Environmental Science and Technology.  In it he wrote that if the nations of the world transitioned to renewable energy — solar, wind, and hydroelectric power — they would eliminate energy-related air pollution deaths and CO2-equivalent emissions while reducing their energy needs by 54.4 percent, annual energy costs by 59.6 percent, and annual social costs — energy, plus health, plus climate — by 91.8 percent. Who wouldn’t want that?

On the other hand, Jacobson and his team wrote that every dollar spent on carbon capture instead of wind, water, and solar, “increases carbon dioxide pollution, energy requirements, energy costs, pipelines, and total social costs.” This holds true even if zero-emission energy systems power the technology deployed to extract carbon dioxide. “It’s always an opportunity cost to use clean, renewable energy for direct air capture instead of replacing a fossil fuel CO2 source, just like it’s an opportunity cost to use it for AI or bitcoin mining. You’re preventing renewables from replacing fossil fuel sources because you’re creating more demand for those renewables,” he said.

“Opportunity costs” is the operative concept here. If you are in Peoria, you cannot be in Philadelphia. If you spend $10.00 on pretzels, you cannot spend it on vegetables. If you buy a new car, you cannot use that money to go to Cabo San Lucas. The money the world pisses away on carbon capture cannot be used to build solar, wind, and hydro resources. It’s as simple as that.

The take away here is that fossil fuels will always win the policy debate. So what will drive the transition to renewable energy? Lower prices for renewables and eliminating concerns about energy insecurity from geopolitical disruptions like the closure of the Strait of Hormuz. Low prices always win and people are pretty sick and tired of paying six bucks for diesel while watching the cost of electricity soar higher and higher.

“The first one now shall later be last,” Bob Dylan taught us. That’s a message of hope inside the carbon dioxide cloud the fossil fuel industry has gifted us. The only question is whether the transition will happen soon enough to avoid a climate catastrophe. It’s hard to imagine destroying a planet and decimating your customer base is a good long term business strategy, but that’s what the executives in charge of the world’s oil, methane, and coal industries seem to think makes for good business. There are none so blind as those who will not see.
