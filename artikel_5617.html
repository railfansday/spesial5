FERC approves MISO plan to speed legacy interconnection study process

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Without the rule changes, the Midcontinent Independent System Operator’s queue cycles — totaling about 125 GW of generation and storage — would have been delayed, according to the grid operator.

The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission on Friday approved the Midcontinent Independent System Operator’s plan for clearing out its legacy generation and storage interconnection queue, in part by giving project developers a chance to withdraw nonviable projects without paying a penalty.

The plan is designed to speed up MISO’s review of its “Definitive Planning Phase–2022” interconnection queue, which contains about 35 GW of active projects. Without the approved changes, that queue cycle would have been delayed by roughly 18 months, affecting other pending study cycles, according to MISO.

In total, MISO has almost 600 projects representing nearly 125 GW in its interconnection queue, which consists of four study cycles. The queue contains 44 GW of solar, 25 GW of batteries, about 20 GW of wind, 19 GW of gas and 10 GW of hybrid projects.

“MISO’s proposed tariff revisions accomplish the purposes of the commission’s final rules on generator interconnection … by helping to ensure that interconnection customers are able to interconnect to the transmission system in a reliable, efficient, transparent, and timely manner,” FERC said.

MISO’s rules for its 2022 study cycle — which was not affected by more recent interconnection process reforms — created incentives for nonviable projects to remain in the interconnection queue because by doing so they avoid potential withdrawal penalties, according to the grid operator. This can lead to withdrawals later in the process, which trigger restudies that delay the process for everyone in the queue, MISO said in its Sept. 21 proposal.

To eliminate that problem, the proposal for the 2022 queue cycle would allow projects to exit the interconnection queue penalty-free before “Decision Point II,” which occurred on Oct. 20. MISO’s plan also limits the number of restudies performed in Definitive Planning Phase Phase III to a single restudy for the DPP-2022 cycle and all future DPP cycles.

FERC approved MISO’s plan with an Oct. 19 effective date. Some details remain outstanding pending FERC decisions in two other pending dockets at the agency.

In its decision, FERC approved MISO’s plan to raise the bar for allowing a penalty-free exit later in the review process. Currently, a project can drop out of the queue without penalties if MISO finds it faces a 35% network upgrade cost increase. The proposal increased that threshold to 50%.

FERC rejected arguments from Ranger Power, a renewable energy developer, that MISO’s penalty-free withdrawal proposal rewards unwanted behavior and is discriminatory.

“MISO has demonstrated a need to expeditiously process its queue and reduce the risk of cascading withdrawals and restudies that have delayed previous queue cycles,” FERC said.

FERC also dismissed an assertion from Pathway Power, a clean energy developer, that MISO failed to justify the increased 50% threshold for penalty-free withdrawal. The threshold is consistent with FERC’s approval of a 50% threshold for MISO’s queue process starting with the 2023 study cycle and it doesn’t unfairly burden small project developers, the agency said.
