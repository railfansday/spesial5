SpaceX sets a date to catch both Starship stages for the first time

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Launch is pending regulatory approval, with a 75-minute window opening at 4:45 p.m. CT; SpaceX said the schedule is likely to change

SpaceX is preparing to launch Starship Flight 15 on Friday, Nov. 13, at the earliest, pending regulatory approval. The company plans to attempt the first catch of both the Super Heavy booster and the Starship upper stage at its launch site.

The 75-minute launch window opens at 5:45 p.m. ET, the company said. SpaceX said the schedule is dynamic and likely to change. A live webcast will begin about 30 minutes before liftoff and is planned to continue through the ship catch.

The booster’s catch is scheduled about 6 minutes and 35 seconds after liftoff, according to the company’s flight timeline. The ship is expected to orbit at an altitude of about 275 kilometers (171 miles) and complete roughly 14 orbits over a flight of more than 22 hours. Its catch is scheduled about 22 hours and 32 minutes after liftoff.

This will be the first return and catch attempt for a Super Heavy V3 booster. Three V1 boosters were caught on earlier flights, and two of them flew again. The ship's return to the launch site depends on the flight control team confirming that the systems needed for reentry maneuvers, the landing burn and the tower approach are healthy.

If both catches succeed, SpaceX said, it would be the first full recovery of an orbital launch system.

The ship will deploy 26 Starlink V3 satellites. Each will add 1 terabit of capacity to the constellation, and the satellites should begin serving customers a few weeks after launch at the earliest. Three of them carry cameras that will scan the ship's heatshield and send imagery to operators.

The heatshield includes the improvements made on Flight 14. They include additional retention mechanisms on tiles in areas at highest risk of falling off during ascent. A large portion of the heatshield also uses a curved tile design that has reduced heating in the gaps between tiles.

CEO Elon Musk outlined the plan to catch both stages on SpaceX's third-quarter earnings call last month. "I'd say it's more likely than not that this succeeds next flight," Musk said. SpaceX had not yet attempted a ship catch, which is harder than a booster catch because the ship flies a longer route at greater altitude and velocity.

Flight 14, on Sept. 28, was the first to send the ship to orbit, where it released 26 Starlink V3 satellites. On that flight, the booster made a soft splashdown in the Gulf of America instead of returning to the tower, as SpaceX tested changes to improve engine relight reliability.

SpaceX is aiming for Starship to be up and running at full capability around the end of 2027, according to Barron's.
