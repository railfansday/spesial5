Who’s Buying Homes with Cash Right Now?

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Ryan Serhant, CEO of SERHANT, says there isn’t just 1 housing market right now. There are really 4: cash buyers, buyers who need a mortgage, homeowners with low mortgage rates, and builders.

Today, let’s zoom in on the 1st group – cash buyers.

According to the National Association of Realtors (NAR), 27% of homes sold in September were bought with all cash. That’s more than 1 in 4 buyers. And that’s a pretty big number, especially when so many people are struggling with affordability.

So where is all that cash coming from? When you hear a number like that, it’s easy to picture wealthy investors buying up houses left and right. But that’s not necessarily the case. Some of those cash buyers are actually homeowners who’ve built up enough equity to buy their next home outright.

Here’s what you should know about who’s paying cash and what that really means for the market.

Cash buyers tend to fall into a few groups, according to NAR:

Homeowners who sold a house and are using that money to buy their next one

So, it’s not all those big investors social media talks about. That 1st group may include people a lot more like you. Here’s how. According to Cotality, the average homeowner with a mortgage has about $310,000 in equity.

That’s a big deal right now. Many homeowners feel stuck because they don’t want to give up the low mortgage rate they have today. But once they learn they may have enough equity to move and pay cash for their next home, today’s rates don’t matter nearly as much. Because buying in all cash means…

No need to borrow at today’s mortgage rates

No loan approval process, so you may be able to close faster

And even if the equity won’t cover the whole purchase, putting more of it toward the next home means borrowing less. That can mean a smaller monthly payment, too.

But cash buyers like this are more common in some places than others. And how big of a segment of your local market this is, is going to depend on where you live.

For example, Realtor.com data shows cash buyers made up almost 47% of sales in Mississippi and 46% in Montana earlier this year (see map below).

According to Realtor.com, the states with the most cash buyers reflect “a mix of affordability, wealth and buyer demographics.” Think places with lower home prices, or lots of retirees and 2nd-home buyers.

So, depending on where you live, you may see a lot more (or a lot fewer) cash buyers than the national number suggests. An agent who knows your area can tell you who’s actually buying near you.

And if all of this has you thinking everyone’s paying cash, they’re not. If you’re planning to buy and need a mortgage, you’re actually in the majority.

Based on NAR’s numbers, nearly 3 out of 4 home purchases still involve financing. And for homes priced between $200,000 and $750,000, only about 1 in 5 sold for cash, according to Realtor.com (see graph below):

That means most people buying in that price range are financing their purchase, too. So, there’s no need to assume you’re at a disadvantage just because you’re using a loan.

So, what does all this mean for you? Maybe more than you think.

If you already own a house, you may have enough equity to make your next move more affordable than you realize. And if you’re buying with a mortgage, remember, you’re in good company. Most buyers are financing their purchases, too.

The takeaway? Don’t make assumptions about what’s possible based on how other people are buying homes. What really matters is your equity, your budget, and what’s happening in your local market.
