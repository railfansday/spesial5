Delta SkyMiles Gold Card Review: Solid Perks For Occasional Delta Flyers

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

The Delta SkyMiles® Gold American Express Card is the mid-range personal credit card in the American Express and Delta co-brand card portfolio, which includes seven cards, covering a variety of annual fee levels, across both personal and business products.

This post is my in-depth review of the Delta SkyMiles Gold Amex Card. The card has a reasonable annual fee and is ideal for someone looking for basic benefits that come in handy when flying Delta. However, if you’re a Medallion elite flyer on Delta, another card might be a better option (more on that below).

Link: Learn more about the Delta SkyMiles® Gold American Express Card

Here are my thoughts on the pros and cons of the Delta Gold SkyMiles Credit Card:

To start, let’s take a look at the Delta Gold Amex welcome bonus and ongoing fees, since these are some of the most important features to consider when opening a new card.

The Delta Gold SkyMiles Amex is currently offering new cardholders the following bonus: You may be eligible for as high as 80,000 Bonus Miles after you spend $2,000 in eligible purchases on your new Card in your first 6 months of Card Membership. Welcome offers vary and you may not be eligible for an offer.

Based on my latest miles & points valuations, SkyMiles are worth 1.1 cents each. That means to me, the welcome bonus is worth up to $880.

This is a solid welcome bonus, though the card recently offered a higher one that included a statement credit along with bonus miles. Everyone has to decide for themselves whether they’d rather wait for a better offer, or apply with this offer so they can take advantage of the perks ASAP.

The welcome offer on the Delta Gold Amex card is “once in a lifetime,” so it’s not available to you if you currently have the card, or if you’ve had the card in the past.

You’re also not eligible for the welcome offer on this card if you have or have had the personal versions of the Delta SkyMiles® Platinum American Express Card (learn more) or the Delta SkyMiles® Reserve American Express Card (learn more). However, you are eligible for this card if you’ve had any version of the business Delta Amex cards before.

One thing worth emphasizing is that if you’re going to apply for multiple personal Delta Amex cards, you’ll want to pick up the Delta Gold Amex first. That’s because if you do, you’d potentially still be eligible for the Delta Platinum Amex and Delta Reserve Amex. Meanwhile if you apply for one of those cards first, you’d no longer be eligible for the “lesser” card.

When it comes to credit scores, there’s not a consistent score you need to get approved for the Delta Gold Amex. I’d hope to have a score in the “good” to “excellent” range, ideally 700+ points. However, people with lower scores have been approved, and people with higher scores have been denied, as it’s only one of many factors that determines card eligibility.

You can read more about Amex’s general application restrictions here.

The Delta Gold SkyMiles American Express Card has a $0 introductory annual fee for the first year, then $150. (Rates & Fees). This is a great opportunity to try the card before you have to “buy” it. The card also has a reasonable annual fee on an ongoing basis, so it’s easy to recoup that fee by taking advantage of the card perks.

You can also add authorized users to the Delta Gold Amex at no extra cost.

If you are going to put spending on the card, the good news is that there are no foreign transaction fees (Rates & Fees) with the Delta Gold Amex, so you can use the card abroad without paying those high fees.

When applying for a credit card, the rewards structure is obviously a major consideration for people. With airline credit cards, the question typically comes down to both the miles offered on spending, as well as the ability to qualify for elite status with spending. Unfortunately the Delta Gold Amex isn’t much to get excited about on that front, but let’s still go over the details.

When it comes to the return on spending, the Delta Gold Card offers:

While it’s nice that the card has some bonus categories, I don’t find the bonuses to be particularly compelling. There are still better cards out there for airfare purchases, restaurant purchases, and grocery store purchases.

Nowadays it’s common to see airlines offer elite status qualification through credit card spending. Delta SkyMiles elite status is based on the concept of Medallion Qualifying Dollars (MQDs), and it’s possible to earn elite status with credit card spending.

The catch is that the Delta Gold Card doesn’t earn any MQDs, so if you want to qualify for status, you’ll have to pick up the Delta Platinum Card or Delta Reserve Card. More on that later.

While Delta SkyMiles is far from my favorite mileage currency, it does have a high floor value, so there are good ways to redeem Delta SkyMiles. You can redeem SkyMiles for flights on Delta or on Delta’s SkyTeam partners, like Air France, China Airlines, and Korean Air.

Partner award flights can be an especially good option if you focus on flights that don’t touch the United States, since flights that do are more expensive and get you less value for your miles. I’ve also seen some good award prices for Delta One (business class) awards, specifically for booking roundtrip rather than one way.

As I’ll cover below, having the Delta Gold Amex also gives you access to the TakeOff 15 discount on award tickets, and that can also help you stretch your SkyMiles for travel on Delta.

The real reason you’ll want to keep the Delta Gold Card long term is because of the perks and benefits that it offers, which are great for the casual Delta flyer. Truthfully, this is the value proposition of many airline credit cards — it’s largely about the welcome bonus and the perks, so let’s take a closer look at those details for the Delta Gold Amex.

Perhaps one of the biggest incentives to actually spend money on the Delta Gold SkyMiles Credit Card is the ability to earn a flight credit. Specifically, if you spend $10,000 on the card in a calendar year, you can receive a $200 Delta eCredit to use toward future travel, which will be deposited right in your SkyMiles account. The credit expires one year after it’s issued.

Getting a $200 Delta eCredit in addition to any miles could be a good incentive to spend $10,000 on the card per year. The credit alone is the equivalent of a 2% return on spending, assuming you consider it to be worth face value.

Just for having the Delta Gold Card you receive up to $100 in Delta Stays credit annually. The way this works, you receive up to $100 in statement credits per calendar year when you use your card to pay for a new Delta Stays prepaid hotel or vacation rental booking on the Delta Stays platform.

While I’m not usually a fan of using airline portals to book hotel stays and vacation rentals, this could prove worthwhile, as you can use it for a hotel stay costing right around $100, if you wanted to. If used right, the value here is considerable.

Just for having the Delta Gold SkyMiles Amex, you get a first checked bag free. You can also receive a second checked bag free on eligible domestic Delta flights.

This benefit is limited to the primary card member and up to eight companions traveling on the same reservation. You don’t have to pay for your flight with your Delta Amex, though you do need to have your SkyMiles number in the reservation.

With the ever-increasing cost of a checked bag with Delta, this can really add up. A first checked bag on domestic flights costs $45, and a second costs $55, so even just checking one bag on two roundtrip flights could effectively cancel out the card’s annual fee.

You receive priority boarding just for having the Delta Gold Amex. This helps maximize your odds of finding space for your carry-on and getting to your seat sooner. To receive this benefit, you must ensure the primary card member’s SkyMiles number is linked to the reservation. This benefit is valid for the primary card member and up to eight companions traveling on the same reservation.

If you have the Delta Gold Amex then you can save 20% in the form of a statement credit for eligible inflight purchases of food and beverages. The statement credit will post within 8-12 weeks of an eligible purchase.

Delta has a Pay With Miles feature, where if you have a Delta co-branded credit card, you can redeem miles for 1.0 cent each toward a Delta flight purchase (in increments of 5,000 miles per $50). That’s not the best deal in the world, though this is a feature that’s exclusively available to those with an eligible card.

Some people find this to be useful since they can redeem their miles toward flights, and then they still earn miles and MQDs on the portion of the fare they pay in cash.

With the Delta TakeOff 15 perk (exclusively for those with select Delta Amex cards), you can save 15% on the cost of a SkyMiles award ticket when traveling exclusively on Delta flights booked through delta.com or the Fly Delta app. Delta SkyMiles award pricing is dynamic, and generally you’ll find that each mile gets you a bit over a cent toward the cost of a ticket.

Saving 15% on these awards is valuable, and means that you’ll generally get somewhere around 1.2-1.3 cents of value per mile (or sometimes even more). In many cases this will end up being a better value than using the Pay With Miles option, so it’s a real perk of the card.

The Delta Gold SkyMiles Credit Card also comes with a rideshare credit. The way it works is that you get up to $10 back as a statement credits each month when you use the card on U.S. rideshare purchases with select providers, including Uber and Lyft.

You need to enroll in this benefit in order to get the money back, and it doesn’t kick in until after your first card renewal. So while it’s a useful perk if you already pay for rideshares at least once a month, it doesn’t really make or break the card.

One of the great features of Amex cards is access to Amex Offers, which offers savings or bonus points on purchases with all kinds of retailers. There’s huge value to be had in having as many Amex cards as possible, so that you can get these offers on multiple cards.

Amex Offers could save you hundreds of dollars per year. You can access these offers by logging into your account and scrolling down to the bottom of your account summary page. Note that enrollment is required to take advantage of individual offers.

It also has the most benefits, so it could be worth it if you frequently fly Delta and want the best perks like lounge access:

It also lets you earn MQDs on spending and gets you upgrade priority over other Delta SkyMiles members with your same Medallion tier, product, and fare experience purchased. The annual fee is high, but it’s absolutely worth a look if you want as many Delta perks as possible.
