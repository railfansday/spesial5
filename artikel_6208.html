Southwest Airlines Passenger Was Kicked Off When Her Husband Didn’t Board The Plane—Even Though She Offered To Pay

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

A Southwest Airlines passenger says she was settled into an exit row window seat in Las Vegas, pleased about the empty middle seat beside her, when the crew began looking for her husband. He wasn’t coming—and she was traveling on his Companion Pass. Angie Batis says she offered to buy her own ticket, using a credit card or points, but was told it was too late. Instead, she was escorted off the roughly 9 p.m. flight to Boise and quoted about $400 for a departure the next morning. Southwest was right about the Companion Pass rule. Surely though it shouldn’t take materially longer to sell a ticket than to remove a passenger. She’d spent four days in Las Vegas, checked two 50 pound bags, and was ready to get home. Sitting in row 14, she’d already given the flight attendant her verbal agreement to help with the emergency exit. Her headphones were on. She was listening to an audiobook and playing a game on her phone. Then came the announcement asking Mr. Batis to identify himself. She told the crew he wasn’t on board and wouldn’t be joining them. That’s when she realized why the middle seat was empty. An employee came back to her row and explained that her free ticket depended on her husband actually taking the flight.
