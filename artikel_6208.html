Hilton Is Growing Faster Than Marriott, Hyatt And IHG

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Hilton opened 182 hotels and 27,000 rooms in the Q3 2026, which works out to 23,000 net additional rooms and net unit growth of 6% from a year prior, according to its earnings release. Net income was $536MM, up from $421MM, and adjusted EBITDA was $1.058bn. Management and franchise fee revenue rose 9.5% which is the company’s core business model.

Comparing that to the other three big loyalty hotel companies, Marriott added roughly 20,000 net rooms and grew 4.6%. IHG reported net system growth of 4.9% after opening 12,900 rooms across 92 hotels. Hyatt posted net rooms growth of 3.8% over the trailing 12 months. The companies define growth a little differently, but Hilton leads on every version I can find, and it did so while adding more net rooms in a quarter than Marriott, the larger of the two companies.

More rooms does not necessarily mean higher earnings. Hyatt’s comparable system-wide RevPAR rose 7.2%, Marriott’s rose 6.5% worldwide and 7.4% in the US and Canada, while Hilton’s rose 5.8%, and IHG’s rose just 5.3%. Hilton ranks third of four on that list which starts to eat into its net rooms added lead. Hyatt’s caveat is its all-inclusive business, where Net Package RevPAR fell 2.5%, which matters because every big hotel brand wants your all-inclusive money right now. This, for me, probably deserves its own post if the drops continue into the fourth quarter compared with YoY.

Net profit confirms the rate growth relative to both peers and net rooms is more tied to RevPAR than absolute growth. Marriott earned $794MM in the quarter and $1.52bn in adjusted EBITDA, well ahead of Hilton. Hyatt’s net income was $58MM, or $122MM adjusted, which is considerably less but HYatt is far smaller (and underperforms.) I wrote last week that Royal Caribbean out-earned Hilton’s whole 2025 in a quarter, and the hotel sector is a reminder that growth and earnings are different races.

Marriott’s development pipeline hit a record of more than 634,000 rooms across over 4,200 properties, with 44% of those rooms under construction. Hilton’s pipeline is 543,100 rooms across 3,869 hotels in 135 countries, with nearly half under construction and more than half outside the United States. IHG’s pipeline is 353,000 rooms, up 3.2% and equal to 33% of its current system. Hyatt’s is about 158,000 rooms, up 12.1%.

Marriott has the biggest pipeline in absolute terms, Hyatt’s is growing fastest of the three that disclosed growth, and Hilton grew 5%. Conversions matter here too. Marriott said conversions (existing independent hotels or those being reflagged) made up over a third of signings and over 40% of openings in the first nine months, and IHG says development splits roughly evenly between new builds and conversions. Hilton opened its first Select by Hilton hotels this quarter, one of several newer brands it is leaning on to fill that pipeline.

As someone whose agency sells hotel rooms, both of these numbers are important but for different reasons. My clients and I prefer to see the rate creep slow or stop (rate regression might be a dream) with more stays. But more properties to choose from is good news too and increases competition.

For travelers, the bigger question is what all this growth does to loyalty programs especially when comparing Hilton vs Marriott. Marriott Bonvoy and Hilton Honors each span well over a million rooms, and their luxury brands include Waldorf Astoria, Conrad, St. Regis, Ritz-Carlton and JW Marriott. But with so many of the new rooms being conversions and limited-service hotels where elite status buys less, that can reduce the shine of the programs. Lounge access and complimentary breakfast (a daily food and beverage credit at most U.S. Hilton hotels) are what business travelers chase but lounges are almost gone completely, and those perks are thinner at a select-service property. Hilton offers elites a fifth night free on standard reward stays and waives resort fees on free nights, and Hilton points pile up quickly through its brand credit cards with American Express. Long term, more hotels should mean more places to redeem, not just more places to earn. But the question also becomes how important those advantages are, and with both Bonvoy’s accelerated status earning, and Hilton’s Diamond via credit card option, the true value of those benefits continue to diminish.

Hilton returned $879MM to shareholders in the quarter, $2.9bn so far this year, and projects about $3.5 billion for 2026. It also moved its stock listing to Nasdaq on November 5, 2026 under the same HLT ticker. IHG has completed $707MM of a $950MM buyback and expects to return over $1.2bn in 2026. Hyatt projects $375MM to $425MM in capital returns. Hotels are on a tear over the last couple of years and Hilton’s growth in room count demonstrates confidence in the market for the future.

Hilton is winning the room race and not the rate race, and Marriott is winning the profit race. I expect Hilton to hold the unit growth lead through 2027, but Marriott’s larger pipeline will make that lead uncomfortable. Hyatt will continue winning on rate while sitting out the growth sprint barring another acquisition which it has been drawn to as of late, which is exactly how a luxury-leaning brand should behave. Still, it’s impressive to see Hilton with such strong numbers for growing the brand.
