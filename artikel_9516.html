8 Fish With More Protein Than Salmon

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Unless you've been living under a rock, you'll know that protein-maxxing is all the rage right now. The conversation largely revolves around go-to options like chicken breast, but we'll let you in on a little-known secret: Several meats out there actually outrank poultry when it comes to the macronutrient. Among them are certain types of fish, a protein source that often gets sidelined in the conversation, but leaving it out would be a serious mistake. Aside from being delicious, versatile, and (much of the time) budget-friendly, fish is also seriously easy to cook.

Salmon may be the most popular high-protein fish, and 100 grams of the farmed Atlantic variety contains around 20.3 grams of protein. That's already impressive, since it beats the 18.2 grams found in the same amount of 90% lean ground beef and comes up just under 2 grams shy of chicken breast. Compared to other fish, however, salmon actually gets outdone.

Tuna sits at or near the very top of the seafood protein hierarchy thanks to its lean, muscular build, but beyond whole fish, another serious source of protein is the canned variety. At face value, a tiny, oily anchovy isn't much to look at. But once these fish are packed tightly into cans, you're left with a surprisingly concentrated source of protein, along with a healthy dose of other beneficial nutrients. If anchovies pack too much of a punch for your taste, canned sardines also make for an excellent high-protein swap — and that's only the beginning.

Salty, briny, and often budget-friendly, canned anchovies contain a whopping 25.4 grams per 100-gram serving. You can enjoy them straight from the can since they're already cured, or if you want to take a more elevated approach, take a cue from the Portuguese and use them as an elegant garnish on toast alongside capers, good-quality olive oil, pickled red onions, and a sprinkling of parsley for good measure.

Sardines might be tiny, but they're loaded with protein (around 24 grams per 100 grams, to be precise). Like anchovies, they're an oily, nutrient-rich fish, but they set themselves apart with a milder, less salty flavor. Often sold whole, they make for a visually appealing garnish if you're looking to add a little drama to your dishes, and since canned sardines have already been cooked, they're good to eat straight out of the tin.

Yellowfin tuna, also known as ahi, is one of the more premium tuna species out there. Alongside its firm, beef-like texture and clean flavor, it's loaded with protein, with a 100-gram serving containing around 24.7 grams. It's also exceptionally versatile, since suppliers cut it into loins, saku blocks, and steaks for a hearty meal, while thinly sliced or cubed pieces work wonders in a tropical poke bowl or as delicious nigiri if you're craving something lighter.

Bluefin is the undisputed king of tuna; it's prized for sushi and sashimi, and its price can reach heights that can only be described as absurd — a 612-pound bluefin once sold for $3 million at a 2019 Tokyo auction. Like its price, its protein content sits on the higher end of the spectrum, too, reaching roughly 23 grams per 100-gram serving. But bluefin is better known for its impressive fat content, with the fatty otoro belly especially prized for its smooth, buttery texture.

Tilapia is one of the most popular fish in America, and despite its bargain price tag, it delivers a serious amount of protein, with a 100-gram serving containing 26.2 grams. It's also a solid pick if you've got sustainability in mind, since responsibly farmed tilapia can be relatively easy on the environment. This lean whitefish has a firm, flaky texture and mild flavor that stands up beautifully to grilling or pan-searing, making for a quick and satisfying weeknight dinner.

Halibut is a fan favorite that just edges out salmon's protein content, with approximately 20.8 grams per 100 grams. Despite those similar protein profiles, halibut is a lower-fat alternative with a considerably less fishy taste, making it a popular pick for those who have yet to come around on salmon. There are plenty of ways to prepare it, and grilling, pan-frying, or baking will all make for a delicious meal.

Notable for its vibrant red skin, firm white flesh, and uniquely sweet, nutty flavor, red snapper is a hugely popular seafood choice that packs 20.51 grams of protein per 100-gram serving. Compared to your everyday whitefish, its price tag ranges from moderate to expensive, but it more than makes up for it with a wonderfully tender texture. To make use of its dramatic red hue, pair your snapper with seasonings and aromatics and bake it whole.

If you've grown tired of salmon's ever-rising price tag, look no further than rainbow trout. Also available as steelhead trout, this seriously underrated fish has a milder, more delicate flavor than salmon and leaner, finer-flaked flesh. Rainbow trout contains 20.5 grams of protein per 100-gram serving, though its leaner profile makes it less forgiving than salmon at very high heat, so it's best suited to gentler methods like pan-frying or whole roasting.
