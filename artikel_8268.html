The Road To Royalty

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

I, sir See You Tomorrow, knight of the Appalachian Trail, am called to guide the King and Queen through a section of the trail in rocky northern Pennsylvania.

I woke up just once during the night, a much better showing that most of the recent nights in my tent. Even better, it was around 6:00AM and I’d have no trouble getting a decent start to the day. What a difference moving the clocks back an hour makes. I fed spoonfuls of oatmeal to myself and took advantage of the warm temperatures and packed up quickly. The temperature may have been decent, but it had become windy, making it feel cooler. Putting on my gloves and buff warmed up my most temperature sensitive areas and made hiking plenty comfortable. I now had a good idea of the shortcuts along the trail – or maybe it would be more accurate to say the parallel pathways that were less rocky than the AT.

Once I crossed Blue Mountain Drive I could follow a service road for the next 3 miles and make seriously good time. The service road ended at a junction with a connector trail to the winter route down to Lehigh Gap. The trail had no rock scramble, and was a well built, gentle slope all the way down to the road. As usual, I was planning to tear a path back to my van and get a jump start on my zero day tomorrow. If I could wrap up my blog posts and have a second consecutive free Sunday, all the better.

Unbeknownst to me, I was probably hiking a few dozen feet from the hikers I’d encountered yesterday while up on the ridge. I turned to cross the river when I heard a commotion behind me.

No sign of a clear view yet, still pretty though.

A spotted salamander still lurking under its blanket of leaves.

Ca-caw! Ca-CAW! I was sure someone, or perhaps something, was trying to get my attention. I turned around and saw the unmistakable cotton candy hoodie of Rebar. Come to think of it, Pebbles’ hoodie is also cotton candy colored. I am still getting used to the call they use to get the attention of friendly hikers. They looked different with slack-packs on their backs and paper Hello Kitty crowns on their heads. They were slightly confused by the road crossing, but I of course knew the way having made my way through yesterday. We were all heading south, so I knew I had to join up with them and hike with some company for the first time in who knows how long. After a short snack break we made our way up to the spring at the George W. Outerbridge Shelter to stock up on water for the long miles ahead. I may have slightly over-promised on the flow rate of the spring, though it was at least where I’d left it right on the trail.

Greasetrap and Soaked soon joined us, having been treated to a breakfast by some visiting family. We all left the shelter together but it took mere minutes for Greasetrap and Soaked to speed off up the trail leaving Rebar, Pebbles, and I in their literal and figurative dust. Over the next few hours we hiked as a group, getting better acquainted over discussions of TV shows from our past and our plans for after the trail. Rebar is a cool human and fellow Trek blogger and instagramer. He was given his trail name after persevering through countless difficult life events. Though difficult, they have also forged a strong and likable character.

The most noticeable part of hiking with my companions was how much faster the miles seemed to go by. I wasn’t bothered by the rocks, we were pushing through them together. Pebbles kept Rebar and I going by sharing her protein gummies and guava hard candies with us. She had procured a modest surplus of the latter with the help of about half a dozen friends from the Chinese buffet in Gorham. I have been leaving my teeth brushing supplies behind and am careful not to bring more squares of toilet paper than I could reasonably need over the course of 2 days. Meanwhile Pebbles has been hiking with enough candy to give out like…well, candy. The women out here on the trail are strong as they come folks. To maintain diplomatic relations, I agreed to offer each of Pebbles and Rebar a Powerade upon reaching my van.

Pebbles and Rebar navigate through the rocky trail.

Pebbles and Rebar navigate through the rocky trail.

Before I knew it, we were making our way back up the rocks to Bake Oven Knob. Rebar wanted to scramble up rocks on the approach to the viewpoint but I told him the best view was just a little further, it would be worth the wait. We made our way through the last rocks and joined a few day hikers taking in the sight. It was much clearer this time around, revealing a spectacular landscape. We took our position on the rocks and had our picture taken, Pebbles and Rebar still adorned in their resplendent crowns.

Back at my van we said goodbye, for now. Their day required a few more miles to reach the next hostel. Rebar offered me his crown, which he said fit me better anyway. On the Appalachian Trail, I’ve worked my way to knighthood, and now to outright royalty. Not bad for a lowly trail peasant who had never backpacked before and wasn’t even sure he wanted to commit to it.
