YouTube adds co-view counts to CTV metrics

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Let Social Media Today's free newsletter keep you informed, straight from your inbox.

The platform will now CTV view counts based on television industry standards, and apply those new view counts across formats moving forward.

YouTube will now report higher viewer counts on CTV, with the addition of co-views to its CTV stats. YouTube said that the expanded numbers will provide a more accurate reflection of total viewer numbers.

As per YouTube, CTV viewing has become a major part of the YouTube experience, with over a billion hours of YouTube content now being watched on TV sets every day. But videos watched via CTV are often viewed by several people sitting in front of a TV set together, and as such, YouTube’s current CTV view count metric approach is too limited, because it only shows a single viewer per CTV session. That’s why the company is updating how it counts CTV views.

The solution, however, is based on estimates, because YouTube can’t track actual CTV viewer counts.

YouTube announced the addition of co-views in May, when Rene Ritchie, the platform’s head of editorial, gave an overview of the logic behind how YouTube will calculate co-views.

At the time, Ritchie said that YouTube would add additional viewers to CTV views “based on factors like demographics, video genre, even time of day.”

In its most recent announcement, Ritchie provided more clarity, saying that co-views would provide a more complete picture, “aligning with TV industry standards, including how traditional broadcast and streaming networks estimate the total number of people exposed to content.”

So how does the TV industry estimate TV viewers?

Audience analytics company Nielsen uses a people meter with families who take part in its research panel, which sees different family members manually reporting their viewing behaviors via response buttons when they're watching. The rise of VOD services with individual log-ins has expanded the pool of information on this front, and based on these sources, YouTube appears to be using an industry-standard estimation measure to suggest total CTV viewers of each video, which will then be reflected in its view counts.

Which won’t be exact, of course, but it could be more accurate than the current system.

Ritchie added that historical view counts won't be changed, and that co-views will only be applied to new TV views, including new views of old videos.

Ritchie also said that co-views won't impact ad revenue or YPP thresholds, while, creator earnings, AdSense payouts and YouTube partner program eligibility will still be based on qualified views and watch hours.

Though the change will impact watch time metrics.

As per Ritchie “While co-views also don't impact metrics like thumbnail impressions, click-through rates and average view duration, watch time will be consistent with the updated public view counts to reflect the co-view audience watching together.”

In other words, watch time calculations will rise based on the assumption that more people are watching at certain times. So if YouTube estimated that three people were watching a video, for example, the watch time count for that video would then be tripled for that session.

The change will see many YouTube videos get higher view counts and watch time results, so if there’s a sudden spike in these metrics, this is likely why.

YouTube’s co-view update is being rolled out to all channels.
