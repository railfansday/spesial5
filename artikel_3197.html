Bristow Group (VTOL) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Supply chain delays pressured margins even as guidance held steady.

Bristow Group Inc. (VTOL -4.13%) reported sequential revenue and earnings growth for the third quarter, supported by the integration of its recent special missions acquisition and favorable offshore energy market fundamentals. Management reaffirmed its full-year Adjusted EBITDA guidance midpoint despite persistent global supply chain challenges that have hampered aircraft modification timelines for government contracts. The company reported that its offshore energy services segment is benefiting from a multiyear contract reset cycle and a tight supply of heavy and medium helicopters globally. The company stated that it is executing a capital allocation strategy focused on fleet modernization, opportunistic share repurchases, and the maintenance of a quarterly dividend program.

Chris Bradshaw: Thank you, Red. I will begin with updates on five key topics. First, safety, which remains Bristow's number one core value and highest operational priority. The company delivered on our goal of zero air accidents through the first 10 months of 2026. In occupational safety, we remain on track to achieve our third consecutive year of fewer lost workdays. I would like to thank everyone on the Bristow team for their continued focus on Target Zero, which is our commitment to zero accidents and zero harm. Second, Bristow announced strong financial results for the third quarter, and we are reaffirming the midpoint for the company's full year 2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance, which reflects year-over-year growth of approximately 25%.

The ability to confirm this outlook, despite macro uncertainties and continued supply chain challenges, is a testament to the complementary nature of Bristow's business segments and the benefits provided by the significant geographic and customer diversity in our business model. Third, Bristow Special Missions, formerly known as Berry Aviation, has contributed strong financial results since the acquisition closed in July, and we expect this growth to accelerate in the fourth quarter and full year 2027. This acquisition has strengthened Bristow's business by adding specialized capabilities across a range of mission-critical operations and strong relationships with key military and defense customers, better positioning the company to benefit from increased defense spending trends.

Fourth, persistent industry supply chain challenges have hampered our performance. The fact that this is a familiar theme common across the aviation sector does not make it any less frustrating. Since 2020, on-time deliveries from key original equipment manufacturers have fallen by approximately 20%. Moreover, when delivery dates are missed, they have been missed by extended intervals. Slide number nine of this quarter's earning presentation summarizes extended maintenance downtime periods experienced across the helicopter industry. We have been compelled to increase inventory levels and implement other mitigations that come at a cost to the company. But unfortunately, key components continue to have increased turn times and arrive late from the suppliers.

While we are experiencing these challenges across Bristow's business, the most acute impacts can be seen in delayed aircraft modification programs for the company's new search and rescue contracts. These delays and their associated knock-on effects are depressing current margins in our government services segment. While the most readily visible effects may be contractual performance penalties, these penalties dramatically underestimate the total impact. The full knock-on effects of these delayed contract transitions include, but are not limited to, lost revenue opportunities, manpower and time spent chasing parts, cannibalization of the company's internal fleet to keep aircraft flying, and delayed aircraft lease returns, not to mention retaining additional headcount, overtime expense, and costs for outsourced third-party maintenance support.

Looking forward, based on detailed discussions and work with our key vendors, we expect these supply chain issues to stabilize during 2027, facilitating a return to historically strong margins in our government services business. Fifth and finally, Bristow's overall business is continuing to perform well despite the supply chain headwinds. Our offshore energy services business has continued to demonstrate robust profitability, and we believe positive offshore energy fundamentals and the tight supply of relevant offshore helicopters combined for more favorable industry conditions than we've seen in more than a dozen years.

I'll take some time to discuss more of the drivers poised to benefit the company later in this call, but for now, I'll hand it over to our CFO for a detailed discussion of Q3 results. Jennifer?

Revenues in our offshore energy services, or OES segment, were $5.6 million lower in Q3, primarily due to lower utilization in Europe and higher penalties in Africa, partially offset by higher utilization and rates in the Americas. Adjusted operating income in OES was $2 million higher this quarter due to lower total operating expenses of $8.3 million and general and administrative expenses of $1.1 million, offsetting the lower revenues. The decrease in costs was primarily driven by lower utilization levels in the North Sea and lower global fuel prices.

Given the North Sea is one of our higher-cost markets in which we operate, these decreased costs, coupled with successful contract resets and growth over the past few years in key markets such as the Americas and Africa, have led to margin expansion. Our OES segment adjusted operating income margin was 27% this quarter, compared to margins in the low teens when we first began to reset our contracts towards the end of 2023. Our OES contracts are now effectively fully reset, with full-year impact of the more recent rate adjustments to be seen in 2027.

Given the continued performance of our OES business, we are tightening our 2026 guidance and increasing the adjusted operating income guidance range to $240 million-$250 million for this segment. Revenues on the U.K. search and rescue contract were $1.6 million higher as a result of lower penalties and increased fuel revenues. Revenues on the Irish Coast Guard contract were $0.7 million lower due to higher penalties related to aircraft availability.

Adjusted operating income was $2.5 million higher, primarily due to the revenues from BSM, partially offset by higher operating expenses of $13 million and general and administrative expenses of $2.4 million, both of which were driven by the inclusion of BSM and ongoing contract transitions. While government contract transitions have continued to progress and penalties have decreased in the U.K. this quarter, overall penalties related to aircraft availability, which has been adversely impacted by continued supply chain challenges, have remained elevated throughout this year. As Chris noted, the knock-on effects of these supply chain issues have been more significant than the penalties themselves.

At this time, we are updating our 2026 revenues guidance range to $470 million-$490 million and the adjusted operating income guidance range to $35 million-$45 million for this segment. As noted on our previous call, some of these transition costs will extend into early 2027, and margins on these government SAR contracts are expected to recover over time. As a reminder, these are typically 10-year base contract periods plus option years, and we still expect to generate attractive long-term cash flow yields on these important government services mandates.

2027 and beyond will also benefit from the full-year impact of the BSM acquisition and the new opportunities that are expected to arise from Bristow's new multi-mission government services offering. In our other services segment, revenues were $14 million higher this quarter, primarily due to the inclusion of BSM revenues of $9.2 million and higher activity in Australia, resulting in higher revenues of $4.8 million. Adjusted operating income for this segment was $7.4 million higher in the current quarter, mostly resulting from the increased revenues, partially offset by higher operating expenses of $4.7 million and general and administrative expenses of $1.9 million, both of which were largely related to the inclusion of BSM.

We are affirming our 2026 other services revenues and adjusted operating income guidance ranges of $155 million-$175 million and $25 million-$30 million, respectively. Lastly, the $4.5 million increase in corporate expenses this quarter is attributable to higher professional service fees related to the acquisition of BSM. Otherwise, our corporate costs remain consistent. During the quarter, we launched a new initiative aimed at retiring our legacy enterprise resource planning system and implementing a new ERP system over the course of the next year. Once live, the new ERP system is expected to save the company a meaningful amount of annual costs while offering more robust capabilities than our existing system. The new ERP is expected to go live in 2028.
