The Denzel Washington Action Thriller That Made Quentin Tarantino Cry Officially Hits Streaming

In the run-up to the release of The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth, Quentin Tarantino admitted that while the world sees him primarily as a director, he is an equally respected screenwriter. Before he broke out with films such as Reservoir Dogs and Pulp Fiction, Tarantino made a name for himself as the writer of True Romance, directed by the late Tony Scott.

Tarantino always admired Scott deeply, and was a massive fan of several of the late director's hit action movies, which include the Kieran Knightley-led Domino, Tom Cruise's Days of Thunder, and Brad Pitt's Spy Game. Another of Tony Scott's action movies was added to a free streaming platform's library this month.

In a tribute to Scott following his death in 2012, Tarantino screened the film in Los Angeles. He said that he wept throughout the screening because of what Scott meant to him. "He’s like Douglas Sirk, he never got respect, was too commercial, people put him down. Now they teach classes about him,” Tarantino said, adding that the filmmaker's creative partnership with Denzel Washington — including The Taking of Pelham 1 2 3, Crimson Tide, and Unstoppable — was “one of the best actor-director combinations of our time.”

We're talking about Scott's 2004 action-thriller Man on Fire, which was based on a 1980 novel by A.J. Quinnell. Starring Washington, Dakota Fanning, Christopher Walken, and Marc Anthony, the movie grossed $130 million worldwide against a reported budget of $70 million. It now holds a 38% critics' score on the aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes, where the consensus reads, "Man on Fire's solid action and top-shelf cast are undone by a relentlessly grim story that gets harder to take the longer it goes on." The film's 89% audience score, on the other hand, more accurately represents its cult status.

Tarantino has another indirect connection to Man on Fire; when he worked at a video rental store in Los Angeles, he recommended the 1987 adaptation of Quinnell's novel to writer Brian Helgeland, who eventually wrote the Scott movie. Another adaptation, an eight-episode series on Netflix starring Yahya Abdul-Mateen II, was released earlier this year to mostly positive reviews.
