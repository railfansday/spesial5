Final Fantasy 7 Remake Director Confirms He Doesn’t Let Fans’ Reactions Shape His Games

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The final chapter in the Final Fantasy 7 remake trilogy, Final Fantasy 7: Revelation, is just a few months away. Speculation is growing about how Square Enix and the development team will bring the game to a close, with fans sharing their own opinions and theories on what to expect when it debuts this April.

One thing remains clear, however: Naoki Hamaguchi, the game director behind the series' reimagining, has always known the direction of the trilogy — and fan feedback does not factor into it.

The changes surrounding Remake and Rebirth — such as the introduction of the Whispers and Zack Fair’s role — have always been a hot topic for fans to debate. The Whispers, for example, were not involved at all in the original game, so their appearance fundamentally changed how fans perceived the events of Remake and Rebirth, and we can assume Revelation.

While some fans welcome the new themes, characters, and features, others worry that Final Fantasy 7’s new direction could reimagine the original so extensively that its story becomes unrecognizable.

However, in a social media post on X, Hamaguchi set the record straight about the direction the developers are taking with Final Fantasy 7.

“We believe it’s our responsibility as creators to decide the major direction of the story, rather than having it shaped by fan reactions. Together with a new generation of developers, we’re reimagining FINAL FANTASY VII for today.”

Furthermore, in an interview with Xbox Dynasty, Hamaguchi explained that the original creators of Final Fantasy 7 ultimately have the final say on any changes to the story, with younger developers on the team reimagining “familiar content from the original for modern players.”

As fans, we naturally have opinions about the things we love — that’s what makes us fans. Still, it’s reassuring to hear Hamaguchi make clear that the development team isn’t swayed by shifting fan opinions. Focus groups and fan screenings can help creators feel confident that people will enjoy their work, but that approach can also feel clinical, even dishonest. Creatives want their work to succeed both financially and critically, but catering only to what fans want risks costing them their creative vision.

At least, that doesn’t seem to be a risk with Revelation. Whether it pays off for Square Enix and the team remains to be seen when Final Fantasy 7: Revelation releases on April 8, 2027.
