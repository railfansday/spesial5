Game Awards 2026: All Game of the Year Front-Runners and Predictions

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

What’s in pole position for gaming’s top prize? Let’s make some predictions

With only a month to go until the 13th Game Awards, set for Dec. 10, every key competitor but one (the big one) has been released. The likelihood of anything emerging to stop Grand Theft Auto 6 from sweeping the night looks increasingly slim in what is turning out to be a much weaker year than it looked on paper.

At this point, we have a clear idea of what games will be favored by The Game Awards' voting body: A high Metacritic rating (preferably over 90) is a must; role-playing games and action-adventures are strongly favored, as are games with strong narrative elements; indie and multiplayer games struggle harder for recognition than AAA single-player adventures. We used these metrics to correctly predict last year's winner, Clair Obscur: Expedition 33, and the 2024 winner, Astro Bot. In 2025, we also predicted all six GOTY nominees and correctly called 20 other categories.

Below, you'll find our predictions for the most likely nominees, of which there are six each year, for Game of the Year 2026 — taken only from games that have already been released (or made available for review). We'll update these rankings throughout the year as new games are released. We'll also list some likely upcoming contenders and some of the 2026 GOTY race's dark horses, too.

Update (Nov. 9): The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake has emerged as the highest-rated game of the year to date, and critical acclaim has been followed by a huge wave of social love as players share their rediscovery of the classic adventure. Ocarina's status as a dead-cert for a GOTY nomination is only matched by the seeming impossibility of a remake of a 28-year-old game winning Game of the Year. That said, it looks stronger than much of the competition, most of which is struggling to maintain momentum in one way or another; the most recent entry, Phantom Blade Zero, seems to have failed to capture the conversation.

Why it could win: Resident Evil is a reliable contender at The Game Awards, with three GOTY nominations since 2019, and many more in other categories. Requiem combines the fan appeal of the remakes with the freshness of a new entry, and blends their gameplay successfully, too. It's slickly produced, digestible, thrilling, with both global appeal and a serious side: a perfect Game Awards GOTY contender.

Weaknesses: Requiem's critical reception was very good overall, but perhaps a tiny bit soft to be a true GOTY contender — it sits in the high 80s on Metacritic and OpenCritic. How well it fares in the long run will depend on the strength of the competition, but for now, it's in the lead.

Momentum (holding steady): Potential challengers to Requiem's status as pack leader just keep falling by the wayside. Nothing else released this year combines critical acclaim with a perfect Game Awards genre profile so effectively, and nothing else will until GTA 6 comes out. It's a lock for a nomination.

Read Giovanni Colantonio's full review of Resident Evil Requiem.

Why it could win: A beloved classic, with a reputation as the best game ever made, lavishly remade by a team at the top of their game, and showered in critical plaudits like no other game this year. It may be just a remake, but Nintendo has mustered a huge sense of event around Ocarina, from its dramatic visual reinvention to its bold positioning against GTA 6 in the calendar.

Weaknesses: After all, though, it's still a remake of an old game, and a very faithful one. The jury will want to recognize Nintendo's achievement here (as it has done for the likes of Resident Evil 4 and Final Fantasy 7 Remake in the past), but a Game of the Year win seems inconceivable for such a well known quantity.

Momentum (new entry): Shortly after launch, Ocarina fever is at an all-time high and shows little sign of abating. And while the art direction initially seemed divisive, players and press alike seem united in their praise.

Read Giovanni Colantonio's full review of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time.

Why it could win: House House's co-op puzzle adventure has a fantastic critical reputation — it currently sits at 91 on Metacritic, higher than any other game on this list. The developer has pedigree: Untitled Goose Game was a dark horse GOTY contender in 2019, and even won the overall award at DICE. It's a refined design in a currently voguish genre. Co-op games go down well at The Game Awards (It Takes Two won in 2021). And there's a good chance jury members have had a good time playing it together.

Weaknesses: As an online multiplayer game, best experienced with a group of friends, Big Walk requires time and social organization to experience properly, which may be tricky for many jury members.

Momentum (📈 from No. 4): Big Walk is now fully confirmed as the top indie title of 2026, and while it doesn't look like a traditional GOTY contender, that could be to its advantage amid a field of underachieving AAA games.

Read Giovanni Colantonio, Julia Lee, and Johnny Yu's full review of Big Walk.

Why it could win: Reviews are very strong, at 89 on Metacritic. Critics love that Fortune's Weave has united strong storytelling with refined combat in a Fire Emblem game for the first time in a long time, and it helps that it's done so on an epic canvas. There's growing recognition at the TGAs for deep and authentic Japanese-developed RPGs like Metaphor: ReFantazio, and there's big fan support too: Fire Emblem: Three Houses was an unlikely winner of the Player's Voice award.

Weaknesses: Fortune's Weave is a tactics RPG, but the Game Awards jury always nominates Fire Emblem games in the Sim/Strategy category, which never overlaps with Game of the Year. If it is nominated, it will be the first strategy game ever to break into GOTY consideration. And as a Nintendo release, it may be eclipsed by the Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake.

Momentum (📉 from No. 3): Fortune's Weave still looks like the best-positioned RPG (or RPG-adjaccent game) of 2026. But it hasn't quite managed to break containment and reach beyond series and genre fans, while some aspects of the game are proving more divisive than they seemed at first.

Read Josh Broadwell's full review of Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave.

Why it could win: Phantom Blade Zero is this year's Black Myth: Wukong, a visually spectacular action game from an exciting new Chinese studio that muscled its way into the GOTY race. Unlike Black Myth, and unlike this year's Korean competitor Crimson Desert, it's got strong reviews (88 on Metacritic, 90 on OpenCritic).

Weaknesses: While it has reviewed well, Phantom Blade hasn't quite pulled away from a crowded action-adventure field that will be competing for the last couple of nominations, including Control Resonant and 007 First Light.

Momentum (📉 from No. 2): Phantom Blade Zero had a colossal launch on Steam (presumably powered by its Chinese fans) but has notably failed to inspire any conversation at all in critical circles, which doesn't bode well for its status with the jury.

Read Paulo Kawanishi's full review of Phantom Blade Zero.

Why it could win: IO Interactive's debut James Bond game has made a confident splash with critics, earning a decent 87 Metascore. This kind of slick, cinematic action-adventure usually goes down well with the jury, while the inclusion of some of Hitman developer IO's trademark "social stealth" gameplay lends it additional gamer cred.

Weaknesses: Licensed games very rarely get nominated for Game of the Year, and First Light's critical reception is a little soft, with some reviewers unimpressed by IO's shift in a more commercial direction.

Momentum (holding steady): Like Crimson Desert before it (only better), 007 refuses to be dislodged from this list. Until GTA comes along, it stands as the best-executed classical AAA adventure of the year, which stands it in good stead for GOTY.

Read Ford James' full review of 007 First Light.

The Blood of Dawnwalker: This vampire RPG from key talent behind The Witcher 3 underwhelmed a little on release, and lives deep in its big brother's shadow, but it's going to be a Best Narrative contender and is a perfect Game Awards game in many ways.

Star Wars Zero Company: It would be unprecedented for one strategy game to breach the GOTY field, never mind two, but jury members who have stuck with Bit Reactor's tactics game have universally fallen in love with it, and will be strong advocates for it.

Pragmata: Capcom's endearingly old-school sci-fi action game has some strong sentiment behind it, and gameplay that's innovative enough to register.

Pokémon Pokopia: On review sentiment alone, Pokopia would be a lock for a nomination, but as a life sim and a Pokémon game, it isn't what we usually think of as GOTY material. Nevertheless, it remains in the conversation.

Crimson Desert: Pearl Abyss' much-hyped everything game never gathered the critical support necessary to compete, and yet it remains on the fringes of the competition through sheer tenacity.

Control Resonant: Never rule out Remedy Entertainment at The Game Awards, although Resonant's reception is perhaps not what the developer was hoping for.

Grand Theft Auto 6: The presumptive Game of the Year looks like a dead cert… as long as it makes its release date, which is only days before The Game Awards' eligibility deadline.
