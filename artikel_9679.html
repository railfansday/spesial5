Oracle Insists It Has The Cash To Build OpenAI's Giant Data Centers

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Posted on Nov 22 • Originally published at fathom.news

Oracle says it now has every dollar it needs to build the giant data centers it promised OpenAI, and it called any reports saying otherwise "incorrect and wrong" before they had even come out.

The short statement, issued from Austin on Nov. 20, said the financing Oracle laid out at its investor day, including vendor financing, is in place. Any further private talks with suppliers are "just additional options," it said, and its ability to deliver for OpenAI does not depend on them.

The unusual part is the warning. Oracle said "any media reports that will come out" saying the opposite are wrong, a rare move that shows how closely investors are watching whether AI's biggest builders can pay for what they have promised.

Oracle set out its funding plan in a Feb. 1 release. It said it was raising money to build capacity for its largest cloud customers, naming AMD, Meta, Nvidia, OpenAI, TikTok and xAI.

Selling new shares lets Oracle raise cash without piling on debt, but it spreads existing owners' stakes thinner. Oracle said the balance of shares and bonds was meant to protect its investment-grade credit rating.

The buildout is moving too. Oracle said its Abilene campus in Texas, where the Astra-6 AI model was trained, is nearly complete. Its next gigawatt-scale campus, Shackelford, is fully permitted, and the handover of Nvidia's newest Vera Rubin server racks is on track for the coming weeks.

The AI boom runs on borrowed and raised money. Companies like Oracle are spending tens of billions on data centers now for contracts that pay off over years. If a builder cannot fund its share, the AI capacity that OpenAI and others are counting on arrives late or not at all.

Oracle's own February filing named the risks: changes in a customer's "ability to fund its commitments" and construction delays at data centers. Saying it is fully funded answers the first half of that worry for Oracle itself. It does not answer whether OpenAI can keep paying for everything it has ordered across the industry.

Watch whether Oracle keeps its promise of no new bond sales and how much more stock it sells under its program. Each new share sale is a sign of how much cash the buildout still needs.

The bigger test is cash flow. Oracle expects positive free cash flow, the cash left after paying for its buildout, in the second half of fiscal 2028, then more than $30 billion in fiscal 2029, the year it says growth becomes self-funded. Oracle noted these targets carry material risks and uncertainties.
