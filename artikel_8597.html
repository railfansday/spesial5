You’ll save more on your wedding reception’s food and drink by changing when and how the meal is served than by trimming the menu one dish at a time. Ask your caterer to price a brunch or afternoon buffet with beer and wine against a sit-down evening dinner with a full bar.

Those two quotes cover the second-biggest share of the average wedding budget. In The Knot’s wedding budget breakdown, food and drink take 24%, behind only the venue and rentals at 29%.

A brunch or lunch reception usually costs less than dinner, because the menu is lighter and guests drink less earlier in the day. A buffet usually costs less than a plated meal brought to each seat, since it needs fewer servers, though it takes more food. Family-style platters, passed around each table, cost somewhere between the two. Food stations can cost more than a plated meal, because each station needs staff to run it.

In The Knot’s 2026 Real Weddings Study, catering averaged $80 a guest at 2025 weddings, and alcohol averaged $2,800 a wedding. Say you’re feeding 120 guests, and your caterer quotes $110 a guest in food and drink for a plated evening dinner with a full open bar, and $70 for an afternoon buffet with beer and wine. The afternoon buffet costs $4,800 less than the evening dinner. Every guest you take off the list saves the full per-guest price as well.

Get every quote as a per-guest price with the service charge and tax included, so you compare the totals you’ll actually pay.
