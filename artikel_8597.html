Heat Yourself, Not the House – Layers, Rugs & Curtains Cut the Heating Bill

Turn the thermostat down a few degrees and keep yourself warm instead of heating the whole house. Wear a sweater and slippers, lay rugs over bare floors, close the curtains at night, and sleep on a heated mattress pad. Each degree you lower it for eight hours trims about 1% from your heating bill.

Central heat warms every room to the thermostat’s setting, including rooms nobody is using. What you feel is the room you’re in and what you’re wearing.

Keep yourself and that room warm, and the thermostat can come down a few degrees. New York State’s energy agency, NYSERDA, estimates that each degree you lower the thermostat for eight hours cuts about 1% from your heating bill. The Energy Department notes that the percentage saved is bigger in mild climates than in cold ones.

Take a couple who keep the house at 70 degrees for the 16 hours they’re awake. Setting the thermostat to 66 instead takes 4 degrees off for two eight-hour stretches. By our math, that’s about 8% off their heating bill. A programmed setback while they sleep would save more on top of that.

Dress for the lower setting with a sweater, warm socks or slippers, and a throw on the couch. At night, an electric blanket or heated mattress pad warms the bed instead of the bedroom. A double electric blanket draws about 100 watts, Virginia Tech’s extension service says. The Energy Information Administration puts the average U.S. home electricity price for August 2026 at 18.16 cents per kilowatt-hour, the unit your electric bill charges by. At that price, running the blanket for a full eight-hour night costs less than 15 cents, by our math.

Then warm the room you use. Lay rugs over bare wood or tile floors, and close the gap under any drafty outside door with a door sweep. Set a ceiling fan with a reverse switch to spin clockwise on low. ENERGY STAR says that moves the warm air gathered at the ceiling down to where you are, and it recommends switching the fan off when you leave.

Open the curtains on south-facing windows on sunny mornings so the sun can warm the room, then close them at night. The Energy Department says most ordinary drapes cut heat loss by up to 10%.

Lower the setting you keep while you’re awake by 2 degrees this week and 2 more next week.
