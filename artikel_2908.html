LIV Golf Collapse Complete: The $5 Billion Bet that Failed

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

LIV Golf is ending its operations after years of controversy and intrigue. The rival tour announced on Thursday in a New Jersey bankruptcy court that it will wind down rather than launch a second version. This move caps five years of trying to split professional golf with huge signing bonuses. The project ran out of money and lost the players it needed to survive.

The league spent more than $5 billion on the sport before Saudi Arabia's funding stopped. It filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on September 8. A proposed $300 million rescue deal from BC Partners Credit fell apart because top golfers refused to sign. The court hearing on Thursday confirmed that the new plan is dead.

The main reason for the collapse was a lack of commitment from the biggest names in the game. LIV needed stars to make the new version attractive to investors, but they walked away. Sports Business Journal's Josh Carpenter noted the absence of key players in the proposed structure.

"None of the big names signed. DJ, Cam Smith, Niemann, Bryson," Carpenter wrote. Even players like Jon Rahm and Tyrrell Hatton were not interested in the new terms. Without these players, the league had no chance to restart. The investment group BC Partners Credit withdrew its support after this failure.

LIV attorney Matthew Williams explained the situation clearly in court. He said the group could not reach the needed level of support in time. "We just couldn't get to the level of combined financial and player support necessary for a viable LIV 2.0 in the required timeline," he said.

The financial cost of this failure is staggering. Before filing for bankruptcy, seven executives retained by the league received $15.6 million. CEO Scott O'Neil received $6.375 million of that total. These payments were made as the company tried to find a way to emerge from its debt.

Now, questions remain about whether those payments can be returned since the business is closing. The league launched in June 2022 with a promise to change the sport. It offered nine-figure bonuses to lure golfers from the PGA Tour. That strategy burned through cash faster than anyone expected.

Scott O'Neil, who took over as CEO at the start of 2025, reflected on the effort. He called the experience a "round of a lifetime" for everyone involved. He stated that LIV Golf was "fun, demanding, inspiring, at times challenging — but always rewarding." Yet the business reality was too harsh to ignore.

The wind-down changes the path for the golfers who signed up. Jon Rahm has already agreed to forfeit about $100 million in claims against LIV to get out of his contract. He has resumed playing on the European Tour and aims to return to the PGA Tour. However, the path back involves restrictions tied to his last LIV appearances.

BC Partners Credit issued a statement saying they were "disappointed that mutually acceptable terms could not be reached among the various stakeholders." The group had supported a transaction that aligned interests, but the deal never closed. The league enters a liquidation phase with no return to play.
