SpaceX Is Buying Turbines, Building Pipelines, and Hiring Gas Traders. Here's What $5,000 in SpaceX Could Be Worth by 2030 If Its Energy Bets Pay Off.

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Space Exploration Technologies' energy investments could fuel growth, but shareholder returns remain uncertain.

Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +5.81%), also known as SpaceX, is making a strategic move into the natural gas sector. The company is buying gas turbines, planning new pipelines, and hiring natural gas traders to secure fuel for Starship launches and electricity for its artificial intelligence (AI) data centers.

These investments could make Starship launches more reliable and bring AI computing capacity online sooner.

But could they also make SpaceX a more valuable company? Here's what a $5,000 investment in SpaceX could be worth by 2030 if its energy plans succeed.

SpaceX subsidiary Coastal Connect Services has proposed a 32.4-mile natural gas pipeline in Florida to supply its launch operations near Cape Canaveral. On Oct. 6, Florida regulators approved the pipeline's proposed charges for transporting natural gas, although construction still requires other approvals. SpaceX is also planning an eight-mile pipeline called Starpipe in Texas, expected to begin operations in January 2027.

Starship uses liquid methane and liquid oxygen. The pipelines could reduce the need to transport methane by truck, although SpaceX would still need to purify, liquefy, and store the gas before using it as rocket fuel. SpaceX is also hiring natural-gas traders to secure fuel supplies, manage price fluctuations, and reduce delivery risks.

More reliable gas supplies could help SpaceX increase Starship launches and deploy additional Starlink satellites. However, increasing launch frequency also depends on regulatory approvals and Starship's operational readiness.

Since Coastal Connect is a SpaceX subsidiary, payments between the two companies would not generate additional revenue for SpaceX as a whole. The financial benefits would instead come from lower fuel delivery costs and more efficient launch operations. SpaceX has not disclosed enough information to estimate these benefits.

SpaceX is also trying to secure electricity for its AI data centers. In April, the company signed an agreement to buy roughly $2 billion of mobile gas turbines and related equipment. The company is also making turbine blades and vanes internally to reduce delays from suppliers.

With electricity available sooner, SpaceX could start earning revenue from AI computing equipment that might otherwise remain unused. However, generating electricity does not automatically translate into higher profits. SpaceX's Super Intelligence (SI) segment, previously called the AI segment, reported $7.6 billion in revenue but only $431 million in operating profit in the third quarter. This translates into an operating margin of just 5.7% for the SI business in the third quarter.

Regulatory approvals also remain a challenge. On Oct. 30, a federal judge rejected an argument that gas turbines powering SpaceX's AI data center operations in Mississippi were exempt from air permits. The lawsuit remains ongoing. While securing electricity faster could support SI's growth, the financial returns will depend on how profitably SpaceX uses the additional computing capacity.

SpaceX generated $13.1 billion in operating cash flow during the first nine months of 2026 but spent $48.1 billion on capital investments. Hence, the company reported $35 billion in negative free cash flow in the first nine months of 2026. SpaceX's SI business accounted for about $40 billion of the spending.

SpaceX's Connectivity business, which includes its Starlink satellite network business, reported nearly $2.4 billion in third-quarter operating profit. The company also held nearly $90 billion in cash and short-term investments at the end of the third quarter, providing substantial funds for expansion.

However, the spending commitments continue to grow. In October 2026, SpaceX announced plans to lease approximately $40 billion worth of AI chips and related equipment, with payments spread over roughly five years. However, SpaceX's cloud service contracts generally allow either party to terminate the agreement with 90 days' notice , even though the contracts typically run for three years. This creates a risk that SpaceX could remain responsible for expensive equipment even if customer demand weakens.

SpaceX closed at $134.27 on Nov. 6. Hence, with $5,000, investors would buy approximately 37.24 shares. SpaceX had a market capitalization of roughly $1.82 trillion and was trading at nearly 57 times its trailing-12-month revenue.

Wall Street analysts expected SpaceX's annual revenue to reach approximately $417 billion by 2030. However, SpaceX's future valuation could significantly affect shareholder returns.

Assuming SpaceX trades at 4 to 10 times annual sales by 2030, this range could provide a reasonable benchmark for bear, base, and bull cases. This range is broadly consistent with INDmoney's August 2026 valuation model, which implies price-to-sales multiples of approximately 4.3 in bear case, 7.7 in base case, and 9.8 in the bull case for SpaceX in 2030.

Applying this 4- to 10-times sales range to SpaceX's projected 2030 revenue of $417 billion would value the company at approximately $1.7 trillion to $4.2 trillion. A $5,000 investment at its Nov. 6 closing price of $134.27 could be worth between $4,500 and $11,200 by 2030.

These calculations account for the approximately 262 million shares SpaceX expects to issue under its pending EchoStar spectrum acquisition, increasing its outstanding share count by nearly 2%. However, these calculations exclude any additional dilution through 2030.

However, strong revenue growth may not translate into equally strong cash generation. Morgan Stanley analyst Adam Jonas expects SpaceX to turn free cash flow positive only in 2031, reflecting its substantial capital spending requirements.

SpaceX's pipelines and turbines could help it expand faster, but this alone may not justify its current valuation. The real test is whether its AI operations can generate enough cash to cover its substantial investment costs. Until then, I would wait before investing $5,000.
