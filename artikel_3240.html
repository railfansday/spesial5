Plural raises €1bn for European start-ups in defence, energy and AI

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The London firm’s third fund doubles its last one. Two-thirds goes into new early-stage bets and the rest into its breakout companies, from Helsing to Proxima Fusion.

Plural’s six partners: Ian Hogarth, Taavet Hinrikus, Khaled Helioui, Carina Namih, Sten Tamkivi and Pierre-Dimitri Gore-Coty

Plural, the London-based venture firm run by former founders, has raised €1bn ($1.1bn). It will back European start-ups in energy, AI infrastructure, defence and health. It is the firm’s largest raise yet, almost four times its €260m first fund in 2022. Its second fund, in 2024, was €500m.

Plural announced the money on Monday on its website and in a press release. About two-thirds goes into its third early-stage fund. The rest goes into a new “opportunities” fund for its existing companies, partners Khaled Helioui and Carina Namih told TechCrunch.

“We’re convinced these frontier founders are capable of building Europe’s first trillion-dollar company, and we want to be backing them from day one,” Helioui said in the release.

Plural says each partner will lead a small number of new investments a year. Partner Sten Tamkivi told Tech.eu that Fund III should make 25 to 35 investments with six partners. Fund I made 31 with five.

The extra money goes deeper into each company, Tamkivi said. A software start-up can often prove its idea with a €1.5m seed round, he said. A fusion, hypersonics or chip company may need well over $20m to reach its first milestone.

Co-founder Taavet Hinrikus told Reuters the partners will look at AI infrastructure, including chip plants, plus robotics and defence.

“We do not pretend to know in which area the next great company will be built,” Hinrikus told Reuters.

Plural launched in June 2022, a few months after Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine. Its portfolio includes the defence company Helsing, which raised $1.8bn in July. Proxima Fusion closed a €411m round the same month. Plural also backs the chip start-up OLIX, The Exploration Company and the missile maker Hypersonica.

Its companies have raised more than €3bn in follow-on money in the past 12 months, Plural says. More than 100 European companies have raised rounds of $100m or more this year. About 10% of Plural’s portfolio is among them.

All six partners have built or run companies. Hinrikus co-founded Wise, Tamkivi co-founded Teleport and Ian Hogarth co-founded Songkick. Helioui ran Bigpoint, Namih co-founded HelixNano and Pierre-Dimitri Gore-Coty is a former Uber executive. The firm has offices in London, Tallinn and Paris.

Backers of the second fund put in more than 125% of their earlier commitments, Helioui told Ingrid Lunden at Resilience Media. Endowments and foundations are the largest group of investors, at over 30% of the capital. Plural does not disclose its returns, Lunden reported.

The raise comes days after Sequoia closed its London office.

“It’s one firm’s decision about its office, not about where great companies get built,” Helioui told Resilience Media.
