Wireless networking chip startup Altair nabs $100M investment

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Altair Semiconductor Ltd., a supplier of wireless networking chips for connected devices, today announced that it has raised $100 million in funding.

Maverick Silicon led the Series A round. It was reportedly joined by Pitango, which invested $50 million in Altair when it spun out of Sony Corp. earlier this year. The Japanese electronics giant is said to have retained a stake.

Connected devices such as wearables and industrial sensors transmit their data traffic via the same carrier infrastructure as smartphones. However, they access that infrastructure in different ways. Connected devices using specialized, low-power network protocols to exchange data with each one and to upload readings to cloud-based backends.

Altair’s flagship modem chip, the ALT1550, supports several such protocols. The most advanced technology in the lineup is the 5G NR eRedCap protocol, which uses 5G networks to transmit data. It consumes significantly less power than a standard smartphone connection and can transmit up to 233 megabits per second.

The ALT1550 also supports two alternative protocols called LTE-M and Cat 1bis. Both use 4G network infrastructure, which is more widely available than the latest 5G hardware. LTE-M can process up to 1 megabits per second, while Cat 1bis is ten times faster and uses more power.

The chip combines a transceiver that picks up and generates radio signals with a baseband processor. That’s a chip specifically optimized for wireless networking tasks. It’s supported by an onboard memory pool, power management circuits and a module that can connect to a satellite when there’s no cell tower nearby.

The ALT1550 is one of several chips offered by Altair. An earlier module, the ALT1255, supports a network protocol called NB-IoT that uses even less power than LTE-M and Cat 1bis. It’s designed for devices that only transmit data every few weeks or months. The ALT1255 provides additional power savings by deactivating many of its features when it’s not in use.

Altair says that its silicon can be found in millions of devices. Utilities use it to power smart meters, which automatically transmit usage data to the cloud and thereby remove the need for manual readings. Delivery companies can use Altair chips to track the location of their trucks. The company’s modules also power medical devices, street lights and a wide range of other systems.

Altair will use its new funding round to kick off the mass production of its flagship ALT1550 chip. Additionally, the company plans to develop new networking products and grow its international presence.
