Ayo Edebiri Got the Forehead Curl Memo

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Ayo Edebiri is one of the best-dressed people in Hollywood. In 2024, GQ named her a rising star in menswear. At our 2026 Men of the Year party, she made an even stronger case for herself as a style icon for the ages. Walking the red carpet at the Chateau Marmont in West Hollywood, the Emmy-winning actor continued her winning streak of power suiting in a navy chalk-stripe ensemble, a bright emerald-turquoise dress shirt, and a burgundy geometric tie. Her bob was styled in a side part, with a pronounced curl sweeping across her forehead. It’s a look that’s become a staple among Hollywood’s best-dressed men this year, a callback to Superman’s signature swoop, minus the Spandex tights and cape.

In the 1920s, the playful wisp of hair was known as the kiss curl: a deliberately placed lock of hair that sat on the forehead, even amid an otherwise carefully sculpted ’do. (As Seth Rogen helpfully pointed out on Instagram after we noted the kiss curl’s 2026 resurgence: “It’s called a Forelock.”) Its prominence in men’s grooming can be traced back in part to the French-American entertainer Josephine Baker, whose iconic curl ushered in an era—and now a century—of rogue forehead curls.

The kiss curl was so emblematic of the era that it made a triumphant return at the 2025 Met Gala, themed “Superfine: Tailoring Black Style.” A host of women paired the look with impeccable tailoring, including Simone Biles, Taraji P. Henson, Teyana Taylor, and, yes, Ayo Edebiri.

The modern appeal of the forehead curl is easy to understand. As hairstyles have grown longer, looser, and less structured, the curl lends an air of studied nonchalance—as if you didn’t spend too much time getting your hair just right. It’s sexy in a just-got-out-of-bed kind of way, especially when everything else—from the rest of the hair to the outfit—remains crisp, clean, and pristine.

As Edebiri once told Vogue of dressing for the red carpet: “One of the fun parts of getting to do this sort of thing is that you get to transform and be these different permutations of yourself.” At GQ’s Men of the Year party, she once again understood the assignment.
