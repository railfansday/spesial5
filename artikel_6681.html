Ditch Your Fleet of Coffeemakers—The Chefman Coffee Station Pro Handles Everything

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Can't decide between a drip coffee maker, Nespresso, Keurig or an espresso machine? The Chefman Coffee Station Pro does all four—and takes up very little room.

As Kevin Malone famously said in Season 8, Episode 2 of The Office, “Why waste time say lot word when few word do trick?” In my opinion, the same concept should apply to countertop appliances. After ditching my microwave, countertop oven, air fryer and toaster for the all-encompassing Panasonic HomeChef, I began to wonder what else I could condense into a singular appliance.

After musing over my fleet of five(!!!) coffeemakers consisting of Keurig and Nespresso machines, an automatic espresso maker, a manual espresso maker and a classic drip coffeemaker, I realized that it was time to address the barista elephant in the room. I had to reclaim my coffee bar counter space—but how? The answer, it turns out, is the Chefman Coffee Station Pro.

Here’s how I tested it over several months and why it’s become my go-to coffee machine.

Can't decide on a brew method? The Chefman Coffee Station Pro tackles them all. Yes, really.

In the age of the all-in-one appliance, maximizing countertop space just makes sense. In small kitchens especially, cramming half a dozen different-yet-similar appliances into limited space feels like such a waste. And sure, you don’t need both a blender and a food processor, but it’s nice to have the choice between the two. That’s why combo machines like the Ninja CrushBoss came out and started changing the game. That’s also the idea behind the Chefman Coffee Station Pro.

Unlike other x-in-one machines that incorporate a couple of different brewing methods (looking at you, Keurig K-Duo), the Chefman Coffee Station Pro combines 10 different functionalities into one machine. Craving a quick single cup? You can use it with both K-cups and Nespresso pods. Need to fill up a full carafe for a party? It does that, too. But what if you want to leisurely enjoy an iced latte or a shot of espresso on the weekend? Well, that’s also an option with the built-in manual espresso machine and automatic milk frother.

In terms of functionality, the Chefman Coffee Station Pro offers espresso, cappuccino, latte, single-serve coffee, 12-cup carafe, cold brew, cold-pressed espresso, coffee over ice, hot milk and cold foam. To try it out, I packed up all my other coffee makers and made it my primary coffee machine for two months.

You’d probably expect a machine with so many capabilities to be enormous, heavy, complicated, unwieldily. Not so with the Coffee Station Pro. It’s so simple to set up and use, I didn’t even need to read through the provided booklet before I was whipping up drinks. It also takes up barely more space than a dedicated espresso machine.

Right off the bat, I brewed a pot of drip coffee. First, I’m super impressed with the way the machine can read what I’m brewing based on the basket insert I choose. Pop in the drip basket, and it’s prompting me to choose the number of cups. Swap it out with the K-Cup pod holder, and it’s now asking how many ounces I’d like. It’s truly like magic!

Then, there’s the customization. First, pick between a single cup or a whole carafe. Then, choose to enjoy your coffee either hot or over ice. You also get the option of brewing a cup or a whole pot of cold brew for summertime get-togethers. There’s even a delay brew function, which makes it easy to prep your poison of choice the night before.

As I mentioned, the machine changes its touchscreen prompts based on which brew basket you choose. When you pop in a K-cup, you’re given the option of having it hot or over ice. There’s also a choice of 4-, 6-, 8-, 10- and 12-ounce cups. Then, you can adjust the strength and temperature, with three different settings for each. Finally, there’s a small flip-down platform to rest your cup on so that it doesn’t splash everywhere as it brews.

Want to use a Nespresso capsule instead? A pod insert pops into the K-cup adapter. Again, the machine will intuit this automatically and adjust accordingly. Overall, this aspect of the machine is incredibly intuitive. Plus, you can save specific settings as one of three “favorites,” if you want to speed things up with one-touch operation.

As for espressos, this thing does work. It boasts a 20-bar pressure system that brews espressos topped in a beautiful, silky crema. Pick from a single or double shot, or opt for a cappuccino or latte, both with a choice of single or double. If you choose a milk-based drink, ensure the milk reservoir is filled and angle the metal wand into your cup—the machine does the rest.

If you’re familiar with a standard manual espresso machine, you already know how to operate the portafilter. If not, no worries—there are helpful videos from Chefman to guide you through the process. You can also opt for hot milk, or warm or cold foam on its own by choosing the “froth” setting if you want to add any of those to your cup of regular coffee.

On the top of the machine next to the brew basket is an area with accessory storage for the portafilter baskets, Nespresso adapter and combination scoop/tamper. On the side of the coffee station is a place to stow the portafilter. While there’s no built-in storage for the brew basket, K-cup basket and carafe when they’re not in use, I do love that the smaller accessories have a dedicated space so they won’t be misplaced.

In terms of cleaning, this might be one of the easier machines I’ve ever used. All of the inserts simply rinse clean. But the part I really love is the milk frother. Instead of nooks and crannies that collect smells and residue, the entire thing comes apart into several pieces, so you can thoroughly rinse and wash every single part that touches milk. Win!

Chefman isn’t the only brand to claim that they’ve created an “all-in-one” solution for flexible baristas. In our research, we also found three other similar machines: The De’Longhi All-in-One, the Ninja Prestige Dual Brew Coffee Maker and, of course, the classic  Keurig K-Duo.

The only issue? We couldn’t find a single other appliance that offered K-Cup compatibility and Nespresso pod capability and a carafe and a manual espresso machine and automated milk frothing/latte settings. All of the options above offer at least two functions. Maybe four. But not all five. That crown belongs to the Chefman Coffee Station Pro alone.

Hopefully, we see more options pop up in the near future. But for now, the Chefman Coffee Station Pro is in its own category of coffee maker.

In my home, I used to have five different coffeemakers, including a Keurig, a Nespresso, an automatic espresso machine, a manual espresso maker and a classic drip coffeemaker. In fact, there were so many appliances that they began spilling into my dining room after I ran out of space on my dedicated coffee bar. After converting to the Chefman Coffee Station Pro, I’ve pared that number down to just one. One!

The only changes I’d make after using this machine since August 2026 are minor gripes. I do wish that there was a built-in coffee grinder, since having a separate bean grinder means having yet another appliance. And while I appreciate that there is a small storage cavity for tinier accessories, I don’t love having to find a place in my cupboard for the carafe and basket inserts when I’m not using them.

But honestly? Even with these small annoyances, I wouldn’t even knock off a full star on my final rating. The size, ease of use and cleanliness of the machine make it more than worth it—especially for flexible home baristas like me.

Condense five coffeemakers into just one with this ingenious appliance.

Want to clear up your counter space and join me in the future? You can snag this new appliance on the Chefman website or on Amazon. At $400, the Coffee Station Pro is absolutely an investment. And for some folks who won’t use all the different settings, it may not be worth the cost. But for coffee lovers who want to brew based on vibes (or who already have a long lineup of machines like I used to), the price point feels totally fair. After all, you’re getting five different machines in one!

The easier question is what can’t it make? It’s capable of espresso, single-serve coffee, a 12-cup carafe, cold brew and coffee over ice. While it’s not programmable to make specific drinks such as a cafe crema or Americano like some automatic espresso machines, the manual espresso component and automatic milk frother still offers extreme flexibility for budding baristas.
