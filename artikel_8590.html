Pick the money-making app that fits what you can put in: Survey Junkie for spare minutes, Rakuten for online shopping, DoorDash or Uber for a car, and Neighbor for an empty garage. Favor apps that pay cash to your bank or PayPal, and expect real pay only for hours of work, a car, or things you own.

Most apps that pay you hand over small sums for spare minutes or spending you’d do anyway. By Survey Junkie’s own figures, three surveys a day earn as much as $40 a month, which works out to under $2 an hour by our math, since its surveys average 15 minutes.

Pay grows with what you put in. Uber drivers in Chicago earned a median of $27.97 an hour in fares, tips, and certain promotions from Oct. 5 to Nov. 2, 2026, before gas and other car costs. A private garage on Neighbor in Seattle lists for $333 a month on average. The picks below are the best apps for each way to earn. We judged each on what it pays for the time, spending, or space it takes, whether it pays cash rather than points or gift cards, its minimum to cash out, and who can join.

Survey Junkie lets you cash out from $5 by PayPal or bank transfer, and PayPal usually pays within a few hours, so you’ll know fast whether surveys suit you.

One long survey can pay up to $50, with cash-outs from $10 by PayPal, Cash App, check, or gift card. With slightly less frequent surveys, the panel works best beside a daily survey site.

Snakzy pays coins for playing the Android games it recommends, and once patient players collect 35,000 coins, about $35, they can cash out to PayPal or Venmo or take gift cards. There’s no iPhone app.

Field Agent’s jobs at stores near you generally pay $3 to $20 each, and $3 is enough to cash out, so it pays best at stores you already pass on errands.

Shopping online through Rakuten earns back a share of what you spend at its partner stores, and the cash goes out by PayPal or check four times a year once $5 has cleared.

Grocery shoppers get cash back on specific items with Ibotta, which pays out from $20 by PayPal or, where your bank allows it, to a bank account.

For more information, read our full Ibotta Review.

Upside’s gas offers average 11 cents a gallon by its own count, paid on top of your card’s rewards, and a bank transfer of $10 or more costs nothing.

For more information, read our full Upside Review.

Dashers get $2 to $10 or more in base pay per delivery offer plus all of their tips, paid by weekly direct deposit or, for eligible Dashers, daily for $1.99 through Fast Pay.

For more information, read our full DoorDash Dasher Review.

In most cities Uber shows what a trip pays before you accept it and passes on every tip, and you can cash out up to six times a day through Instant Pay for a fee.

For more information, read our full Uber Driver Review.

Taskers on Taskrabbit set their own hourly rate in most categories, from furniture assembly to help moving, and clients pay Taskrabbit’s service fee on top of that rate.

Rover sitters set their own rates and schedule, Rover keeps 20% of each booking in most of the U.S., and the rest reaches their Rover account two days after each completed service.

Mercari is for small things you can mail. It takes 10% of the price plus any shipping the buyer pays, and direct deposit to your bank is free.
