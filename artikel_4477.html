Jeep Is Auctioning Off the Only Copy of the Wrangler 392 Commando Available to Non-Military Personnel

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Of the 250 units of the Hemi-powered special edition, 249 went to veterans or active duty U.S. service members. This is the exception.

The Jeep brand has an indelible connection to the U.S. military, even if it has been nearly 60 years since the armed forces actually used a Jeep-branded vehicle. So it wasn’t a huge surprise last year when Jeep announced the wildest factory Wrangler it had yet produced would be called the Wrangler Commando 392.

More surprising was that Jeep had no plans to sell the Wrangler Commando 392 to the general public. Jeep built a limited run of 250 vehicles, and earmarked 249 of them for active-duty service members and veterans. That left only Wrangler Commando 392 available to civilians—and it’s now up for auction on Bring a Trailer. (Usual disclosure: Bring a Trailer, like Road & Track, is part of Hearst Autos.)

The Wrangler Commando 392 is not your typical Jeep Wrangler. As the name suggests, the Commando 392 packs the 6.4-liter Hemi V8 from the still-very-much-alive Wrangler Rubicon 392 and adds a Whipple supercharger for a total of 705 horsepower. (That’s nearly six times the output of the four-pot Wrangler I drove in high school.)

The Commando 392 rides on 37-inch Nitto Ridge Grappler tires, with an internally mounted spare, and 17-inch beadlock-capable wheels. Additional off-road modifications for this Wrangler Commando 392 include a 3.5-inch JKS suspension lift kit, Fox 3.0 internal bypass shocks with remote reservoirs, and a Fox ATS steering stabilizers. It also has a Warn winch and a Magnaflow exhaust system.

Visually, the Wrangler Commando 392 is a bit more sedate. It features a Willys trim-like Army Green paint job, Commando graphics inside and out, and a unique interior trim. The new owner will have to equip the Commando 392 for all-season capability themselves; this Wrangler only comes with the bimini top and tube doors as depicted, not with a hard roof or full-size doors.

The Jeep Wrangler Commando 392 auction will benefit United Service Organizations (USO). If you’re interested, prepare to have your financial ducks in a row as well as your novelty rubber ones; bidding for the only civilian Commando 392 is already up to $100,000 as of this writing, with nine days remaining.
