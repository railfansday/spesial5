5 iPhone security settings you should change to protect your data

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

It can be a little scary to think about all the personal data on your phone and what could happen if that fell into the wrong hands. Thankfully, for the iPhone, Apple offers many layers of protection in iOS to prevent sensitive information from falling into the wrong hands.

Your iPhone's Lock Screen is what anyone sees immediately when they turn on the screen. Even without your passcode, it exposes more than you might think because by default, anyone can open Control Center or use Siri. That might not seem like much, but someone could use this to play pranks or even put your lost iPhone into airplane mode, making it harder to find. The good news is that you can limit what appears on your iPhone's Lock Screen.

Start by making sure your iPhone only shows notifications when it's unlocked. Open the Settings app and go to Notifications, then Show Previews. Select the When Unlocked option so they remain hidden until you authenticate with Face ID or Touch ID. Next, go back to the main Settings screen and tap Face ID & Passcode (or Touch ID, depending on your iPhone). Enter your passcode and scroll down to Allow Access When Locked.

Here, you can limit what can be done from the Lock Screen without authenticating, including accessing Control Center, using Siri, searching, replying to messages and controlling your home. Turn off the features you want to restrict (Control Center is one you should turn off). The features you disable will become available again as soon as you unlock your iPhone.

Sometimes we hand our iPhone to a friend to show them a photo or video, which is fine. But with your unlocked iPhone in their hands, someone can easily look through your messages and notes. That's why it's a good idea to lock some of your apps with Face ID or Touch ID, or even hide them in a secret folder (a feature that's been available since iOS 18).

To lock an app, press and hold its icon on the Home Screen, then tap Require Face ID (or Require Touch ID) and confirm. From now on, the app will require authentication every time it's opened, even if the iPhone is already unlocked. This protects against schemes where someone asks to use your phone to make a call in an "emergency," then quickly use payment apps like Venmo to take your money.

If you don't want anyone to know you have a specific app, you can also hide apps on iOS. Follow the same steps of pressing and holding an app icon, but then choose the Hide option from the confirmation menu. The app will disappear from your Home Screen and move to a Hidden folder in the App Library; accessing this folder requires authentication every time. Keep in mind that hidden apps won't show notifications or calls, so this option works best for apps you don't need alerts from.

Apps and websites can collect a lot of info about you, and that data often ends up being sold to advertisers. Since iOS 14.5, Apple has required apps that collect your data to explicitly ask for permission to track your activity, with this pop-up showing the first time you open an app.

Criminals have figured out a trick to unlock a stolen iPhone: they watch someone enter their passcode from a distance, steal the phone and use that passcode to access apps that require authentication or even change your Apple Account password. But you can prevent this with Stolen Device Protection, and you should definitely have this feature on.

Once enabled, your iPhone detects when it's away from familiar places like your home or work. Then even if someone has your passcode, it blocks sensitive actions such as accessing your passwords or changing Apple Account settings. You can still do all this in unfamiliar locations after a one-hour delay, but you'll have extra time to mark your phone as lost and contact your bank to block your cards in case something bad happens.

Newer iPhones come with this feature enabled by default, but it's worth checking to be sure. Open the Settings app and go to Face ID & Passcode (or Touch ID). Look for Stolen Device Protection and make sure it's enabled — you'll need two-factor authentication set up on your Apple Account, and Find My must also be turned on. Don't forget to turn off Stolen Device Protection before selling your iPhone or giving it to someone else.
