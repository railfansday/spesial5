AI factories push liquid cooling beyond CPUs and GPUs to nearly every rack component

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The liquid cooling supply chain is under mounting pressure as AI factories pack more heat into every rack.

To meet demand, operators have invested heavily in denser racks and liquid cooling, as well as the hoses and couplings that move the fluid must arrive faster and in larger volumes than ever. But for component makers, keeping up takes more than extra shifts. With liquid cooling reaching more of the rack, equipment makers need connections suited to a wider range of components, according to Tyler Glenn (pictured, right), engineer, product applications and data centers, at Danfoss Power Solutions, a division of Danfoss A/S.

“We went from air cooling to hybrid cooling and we’re moving a lot now towards full liquid cooling,” Glenn said. “Traditionally, it was just the CPUs and GPUs that were being liquid cooled. But as more components are starting to have to get integrated, there’s a lot more opportunity for us to have different types of connections to solve specific challenges that this industry faces.”

Glenn and Lauren Witter (left), vice president of sales at Omni Services Inc., spoke with theCUBE Research’s John Furrier and Rebecca Knight at SC26, during an exclusive broadcast on theCUBE, SiliconANGLE Media’s livestreaming studio. They discussed the liquid cooling supply chain, the universal quick disconnect and designing systems for rising heat density. (* Disclosure below.)

When a rush order comes in for a large data center build, suppliers need parts on hand to assemble hoses quickly, Glenn explained. Danfoss works with distributors that carry its hoses and couplings to keep a minimum stock available as part of a strategy to stay ahead of projections

“A lot of times our customers will provide us forecasts, but we like to shoot above that and look ahead and make sure that in case that the demand increases beyond that, that we’re prepared for that,” Glenn said. “When it comes to our component suppliers, we’re making sure to order additional materials, but also keeping track of making sure we have enough stock.”

But when a data center builder needs parts for denser AI racks on short notice, having stock on hand gives distributors a head start. Omni’s CoolFlow division works directly with Danfoss’s data center team to handle those requests, according to Witter. Both teams focus on liquid cooling, so Omni can take a rush order straight to the engineers it already works with.

“No one gets frustrated when we’re calling Tyler saying we need these parts yesterday,” Witter said. “They get it. They’re playing the same game we are.”
