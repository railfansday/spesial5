Homebuilder confidence sinks to a 14-year low as buyers hibernate

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

NAHB’s HMI fell 4 points to 25 in November, the lowest since 2012. Builders reported heavier concessions, with 46% cutting prices and 68% offering incentives as rates above 7% weighed on demand.  AI Summary

Homebuilder confidence fell in November to its lowest point since the latter days of the Global Financial Crisis, as elevated mortgage rates, persistent inflation and buyer hesitancy continue to weigh on demand.

The National Association of Home Builders (NAHB)/Wells Fargo Housing Market Index (HMI) fell four points to 25 in November, representing the weakest homebuilder confidence reading since April 2012.

Builders have relied ever more heavily on discounts and mortgage buydown tactics to move inventory, with NAHB reporting that 46% of builders cut prices during the month, the highest share since the onset of the Covid-19 pandemic. Typical discounts ran flat at 6% for the eighth straight month, and 68% of builders used sales incentives, another post-Covid high. It was the 20th consecutive month that at least 60% of builders had featured margin-eroding price-cut promotions.

Further, in November, all three of the key HMI components sank. The index of current sales conditions declined six points to 28, while the measure of sales expectations over the next six months slipped two points to 30, signaling a prolonged pessimistic outlook. Meanwhile, the gauge of prospective buyer traffic dropped two points to 16, the lowest level in 15 years outside of the early months of the pandemic in 2020.

“Builders are confronting multiple challenges in today’s economy,” NAHB Chairman Bill Owens, a home builder and remodeler from Worthington, Ohio, said in a statement. “Stepped-up immigration enforcement is keeping legal workers off job sites, deepening labor shortages and driving up costs. Higher fuel prices are prompting subcontractors to raise their rates sharply, and with fewer buyers in the market, more builders are scaling back construction.”

NAHB Chief Economist Robert Dietz said in a statement that a combination of policy and macroeconomic pressures is straining both supply and demand.

“The housing market faces a range of supply-side challenges, including building material costs pushed higher by tariffs, an ongoing shortage of construction workers and excessive regulations that undermine housing affordability,” Dietz said. “Mortgage rates above 7%, softer consumer confidence and an uncertain economic outlook are holding buyers back.”

Public homebuilders echoed that picture on recent earnings calls, citing hesitant buyers, heavier discounting and thinner margins.

Century Communities Executive Chairman Dale Francescon, during an earnings call on October 28, noted that high mortgage rates, continued inflation and macroeconomic uncertainty weighed heavily on consumer confidence, particularly among first-time buyers.

“As we moved through the quarter, our buyers became more cautious, did not feel an urgency to transact and felt more comfortable moving to the sidelines,” Francescon said during the call.

It’s not that buyers have abandoned the market. Rather, they are waiting out volatility and elongating decision-making timelines. At the same time, the number of households that can qualify for a mortgage at today’s borrowing costs and sales prices continues to shrink.

LGI Homes chairman and CEO Eric Lipar noted the increasingly challenging market conditions during an earnings call on November 3.

“As expected, the market became more challenging as the quarter progressed,” Lipar said. “Affordability remained the defining story of the quarter.”

The homebuilder, which primarily serves rate- and payment-sensitive entry-level buyers, reported a cancellation rate of 53.8%, compared with 33.6% a year earlier and 26.2% two years ago. While this trend was an outlier among most of its public homebuilding peers, it underscores how strained entry-level buyers are right now.

During PulteGroup’s earnings call on October 22, the builder’s president and CEO Ryan Marshall agreed that market conditions have become more challenging in recent months, leading many buyers to put their homebuying process on the back burner.

“What changed in the quarter…is that the big move higher in interest rates, along with inflation, global tensions, and waning confidence, reached the point where more consumers simply put their search on pause,” he said during the call.

Traffic to PulteGroup communities fell 14% year over year and 11% sequentially in the third quarter, with first-time buyers making up most of the missing traffic.

Marshall also noted that, with 30-year mortgage rates still sitting at around 7.5%, move-up buyers are increasingly having a difficult time selling their current home, further dragging on homebuying demand.

“One of the biggest impediments that we’re seeing right now is that our move-up and active-adult buyers need to sell their existing home, and that is tough,” Marshall noted.

With homeownership increasingly out of reach for the average working American, renting has become the more affordable option. Zillow research found that renting a home costs more than $1,000 less per month than buying one, a gap that draws many would-be buyers away from for-sale homebuilders and toward the rental market.

For many homebuilders, winning over cautious buyers now means resorting to more and more incentives, in effect paying for sales. Concessions are climbing, and total sales prices are coming down with them. The median new-home sales price in September was about $400,000, down nearly 4.0% from a year ago.

Builders continue to use mortgage rate buydowns as a sales tool, and the numbers show it. A recent Realtor.com report found that the average new-home buyer had a starting mortgage rate of 5.01% during the third quarter, compared with 6.15% for existing-home buyers.

Century Communities is among the builders leaning hard on incentives to facilitate sales. The company reported that, during Q3, incentives averaged about 13.0% of the total sales price, up from 12% in Q2 and 11.0%. The builder’s CFO Scott Dixon noted on an October earnings call that incentives will likely rise in the fourth quarter as Century pushes to meet its year-end goals.

“We expect incentives on closed homes in the fourth quarter of 2026 to be 200-250 basis points higher than third quarter levels as we navigate a higher rate environment, muted consumer confidence and compete with other builders for year-end closings,” he said during the call.

Meritage Homes Executive Vice President and CFO Hilla Sferruzza concurred that the incentive environment is likely to stay elevated for some time, as she addressed analysts on an October 29 earnings call. The builder continues to use incentives and price discounts, with its average closing price down 4% year over year to $365,000.

“Absent a sustained market-driven catalyst that provides meaningful mortgage rate relief, we expect incentives to remain at this elevated level for the near term,” Sferruzza said.

On the other hand, during a session at the HousingWire Homebuilder Summit last month, Taylor Morrison CEO Sheryl Palmer warned that homebuilders have trained today’s buyers to expect more and more concessions.

“We, as builders, created this environment,” Palmer said during the session. “We created this place where the longer the house has been sitting there, the more we’re going to give you.”

For builders, the question is how much profit they are willing to trade for sales. Those who protect margins risk losing volume, while those who chase volume are watching margins thin.

Beyond the immediate sales and margin challenges, many homebuilders are struggling to determine how much inventory to carry and what their 2027 production plans should look like.

Meritage Homes, for example, declined to give formal Q3 guidance, arguing that volatile market conditions limit their visibility.

PulteGroup’s President and CEO Ryan Marshall agreed that “the current environment has made it a more difficult planning time.”

And D.R. Horton Executive Vice President Jessica Hansen echoed this sentiment during an October 29 earnings call.

“Given the uncertainty in these conditions and the broader economy, our initial fiscal 2027 guidance reflects wider ranges than usual,” Hansen explained.

Builders have navigated this challenging market by cutting cycle times, extracting costs, improving floor plans and leveraging AI to improve operational efficiency. What’s out of their control is whether buyers feel ready to commit, or when that will change.
