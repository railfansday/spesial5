Man Utd fan criticism has gone too far at times - Berrada

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Manchester United chief executive Omar Berrada (left) with director of football Jason Wilcox

Manchester United chief executive Omar Berrada says some of the criticism aimed at the club's hierarchy has "crossed a line".

In a rare interview to Sky Sports as head coach Mauricio Pochettino prepares for his first game in charge, at Liverpool on Sunday, Berrada said he "understood" the frustration of supporters at United being 18th in the Premier League. He also said some of the criticism was "fair".

However, he feels some of it has gone too far.

"Being in this position comes naturally with the criticism and scrutiny we're going to get," he said. "We accept it. Some of it is fair, some of it does cross the line.

"We do live in this world of social media, where people like to generate negative stories about the club because it drives more engagement, and it drives more general noise and activity around the club. But we park that."

United's director of football Jason Wilcox has been singled out by supporters at recent games. There have also been social media calls for both Wilcox and Berrada to lose their jobs.

In addition, songs are now sung regularly calling for minority owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe to leave, while opposition to the Glazer family has remained intense since their leveraged buyout in 2005 saddled debt on to the club that now exceeds Â£1bn.

Wilcox, in particular, has been blamed for what is perceived to have been poor summer recruitment. After the 2-0 defeat by Atletico Madrid in September, defender Lisandro Martinez said he "expected more from the technical director".

Ousted boss Michael Carrick felt he was let down by a transfer strategy that targeted midfield, while not addressing issues at left-back and in attack - which has been compounded by injuries to Luke Shaw and Benjamin Sesko.

Carrick wanted to target Elliot Anderson and Mateus Fernandes, but United backed off when Nottingham Forest and West Ham's demands went higher than they wanted to pay. Anderson eventually joined Manchester City for Â£116m, with Fernandes moving to Tottenham for Â£85m.

Instead, United paid a combined Â£118m for Carlos Baleba and Andrey Santos, who have started seven Premier League games between then, and triggered Youri Tielemans' Â£35m release clause at Aston Villa. Tielemans scored his first United goal in the 4-4 draw with Aston Villa on 8 November and has one assist.

However, Berrada has defended United's transfer business, saying: "When we look at the summer transfer window of 2026, I know we're receiving a lot of criticism for it, but we're actually happy with the players we bought.

"We think that Carlos Baleba, Youri Tielemans, Andrey Santos, and Karl Darlow - and let's not forget that we've also welcomed back Marcus Rashford, who had a very good season on loan at Barcelona - these are five additions that we think have made the squad fundamentally better.

"We went in knowing what we needed to do. Of course, there have been different dynamics at play and we've said consistently in the last few years we need to stay disciplined. We need to take the right decisions. We need to buy the right players, for the right value at the right time - in terms of what the squad needs.

"When we find the right opportunities, we will spend. But what we don't want to be doing is spending just for the sake of it. So, could we have signed an additional defender to straighten the depth and the quality? Yes, but we have to find the right players at the right value.

"There's no point in us spending Â£40m, Â£50m for players we're not convinced are going to add value to the team."

When he spoke to the wider media for the first time, 30 minutes before Berrada's interview was aired, Pochettino said he hoped to have a say in all areas of the playing side, even though his official title is head coach.

"I know that, being a head coach, I am the guy that is going to decide the squad for Sunday," he said. "I'm going to pick the starting 11 and I'm going to make the changes.

"Of course, in the future, I would like to be involved in all the main decisions that will happen on the squad."

Berrada said there had been little option other than to dismiss Carrick given United's plight and the team had "dropped off in many areas".

Pochettino has spoken individually to virtually every player to get an understanding of what the issues are.

He said he was wary of reading too much into data that shows United's players have not been running enough, arguing "if you don't put context on the data, it's very dangerous to talk".

The 54-year-old also said that, despite talking extensively about culture in his first in-house interview on Thursday, it could take 10 years to build one.

"The culture of the club and the identity of the club is very clear," he said. "It's a historic club, one of the best in the world. It's difficult to find another club the size of Manchester United.

"The culture of the club and the identity of the club is very clear. It needs to be in a different place than we are now."

Pochettino did confirm Bruno Fernandes will remain as skipper for the trip to Liverpool.

Fernandes has struggled for form and, with the 32-year-old's contract due to expire in the summer, there has been no breakthrough in negotiations over an extension.

United do have the option to trigger an additional year on the Portugal playmaker's present deal but, given the massive dip from the levels that earned him all last season's major player of the year awards, there are some who wonder whether a clean break might be best.

Pochettino preferred to deflect the wider aspect of Fernandes' future and concentrate on the immediate.

"Bruno is our captain and he's a very important player," he said.

"I think we will have time after to talk about the [other] things that you are asking me."
