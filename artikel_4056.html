Exclusive – Behind The Romance: Rockstar Talks GTAVI’s Jason And Lucia

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

After dozens of hours exploring Leonida, I have a lot of positive things to say about Grand Theft Auto VI, and shared as much in our review of the game. But for my part, the feature that elevated the game most prominently above Rockstar Games’ other (excellent) games wasn’t its graphics, missions, or action – it was the storytelling and interplay between the two lead protagonists. Jason and Lucia bring heart, emotional depth, and a sense of genuine threat to the crime fantasy, as you grow attached to them and start cheering for them against all the odds stacked against them.

We reached out to senior members of the Rockstar Games team to ask about their thoughts on Jason and Lucia, and this is what they had to share.

During my playthrough, the Jason and Lucia dynamic really elevated the experience, adding heart and emotional weight to the expected mix of action, driving, and activities around Leonida. Was a couple or romantic relationship the plan or goal for the game from very early on in development, or did that come later? Do you see the focus on Jason and Lucia’s relationship as one of the features that separates Grand Theft Auto VI from your prior work on storytelling in the game series?

Stephen Donkin, Vice President of Design: “The dual protagonists and the relationship between the two of them was there from very early in development, but exactly how that was expressed in the game did change over time. It was an enormous challenge for us to get them working together in the open world together and finding exactly how that would work. As we started to layer in more mechanics, dialogue, and incremental features across all teams, it just clicked at some point. The ability to be together in the open world also allowed us for new ways to tell the story of the game and to bring some of the exposition and narrative into the open world in a way we hadn’t been able to do before. Hearing these little bits of dialogue as you are just tooling around the world that have specific context to what you as a player have just been doing in the story really knits it all together.”

The player has a choice for how to develop the relationship between the two leads, but I imagine most players are drawn toward helping it thrive. In early playtests, is that what you observed?

Katie Pica, Design Director: Player Systems & Progression:“Overall, we’ve seen that players are invested in supporting Jason and Lucia’s relationship but we don’t favor a specific path through the game, it is completely down to player choice.”

It feels like a lot of the game’s driving narrative beats factor for a close connection between Jason and Lucia. Was it challenging to craft variations that included a more tense or distant connection between the two? Is that even possible?

Luke Howard, Senior Design Director: Missions & Visualization:“One of the core goals of this game was to tell the story of Jason and Lucia with a base line relationship where they are amicable, if just platonic, as they still share the same ultimate goals and need each other to achieve them. So, this gave us a good platform on the main flow to expand particularly intense or emotional moments they experience with variation that can expand on their romantic relationship, while not losing anything if that doesn’t exist. In short, we did develop variation in scenes and interactions that range from subtle to more significant, but done in a way that should feel authentic to the player’s choices, relationship-wise and also criminal profile.

One example is after Lucia helps Kordell’s gang pull off an intense armored truck robbery she shares the information with Raul’s crew. Afterwards Jason pulls Lucia aside to see how she's coping. This moment carries several variations where the performance direction was captured multiple ways to cover a range emotional energy between them. The actors deliveries are brilliant and nuanced – these small tender moments are where we tried to craft pieces that when added up together over the course of the story, author an authentic version of the player’s Jason and Lucia.”

Were there any fictional couples or real-life couples that the team looked at for inspiration in shaping the Jason and Lucia dynamic, whether those couples came from big crime stories or not?

Rupert Humphries, Senior Vice President of Narrative:“We definitely started by looking at all the “couple on the run” movies. There are a lot of them but the ones that we kept coming back to were Warren Beatty and Faye Dunaway in Bonnie and Clyde, McQueen and MacGraw in The Getaway (the Jim Thomson book is also great), True Romance, Thelma and Louise. Clooney and JLo’s dynamic in Out of Sight, as well. And then a lot of buddy movies actually – Riggs and Murtaugh, Butch and Sundance, Mike Lowry and Marcus Burnett, etc. for their back and forth together. In terms of having a game where the characters can change based on how you play and whether you really lean into and enjoy the crime spree we thought about how both Pulp Fiction and Natural Born Killers start with a couple in a diner pulling out guns, but then how different those couples are and how different those scenarios play out. Jason and Lucia are a lot tougher than Tim Roth and Amanda Plummer but we liked the idea of telling a story where Jason and Lucia could end up being closer to either end of that spectrum based on what the player was doing.”

Sex and intimacy are notoriously complicated and challenging to present in games. How did you aim to strike a balance with their private moments together that felt authentic? Any thoughts about how or why it’s so challenging to present adult relationships in video games?

Rupert Humphries, Senior Vice President of Narrative:“This is definitely something we thought a lot about and spent a long time trying to get right. The actors who play Jason and Lucia were so good, and they had a natural chemistry and part of it was just trying to capture some of that.

There’s this trade off in making games between player control and then scripted scenarios. We try and give the player as much control as we can because we are working in an interactive medium and actually doing something rather than watching it is going to feel more powerful. But then you have the ability to really mess around and break the illusion of a tender or romantic moment or whatever it is. So the relationship aspect of this story is there if a player wants to engage in it, but if they don’t the story can play out differently.”

In its best moments of genuine support, regular connection, open communication, and flirty or engagement with one another, Jason and Lucia can come across as a pretty ideal model of good relationships – even while they’re simultaneously committing major crimes together. Was that juxtaposition of a healthy romantic relationship against an external world of illicit activity an explicit goal for how the narrative came together?

Rupert Humphries, Senior Vice President of Narrative:“That was the goal, yes. You are really living with these characters – they’re eating, sleeping, showering, throwing away their trash, or not. The relationship needed to feel real to sit alongside all of that. Then even just as a development team, we live with these characters for years and years. We wanted them to be people we could like, care about, and root for. And they needed to like each other. But then the world itself is so crazy and chaotic and seemingly on the brink of collapse.

When we set out to make this game, and go into that multiyear development marathon, we said we wanted to spend our time working on something with heart, and with some characters you could care about and root for. It’s barely worth even stating, but we are living in a time when people are divided along almost every line, and feelings about the future are pretty bleak. We wanted to create a world that held up a mirror to that, showed that fever pitch of craziness that we are all maybe a couple of bad decisions away from, but we wanted the story we were going to tell to have something positive at its core and the message that human connection is better than division.”

The game automatically moves you to play one character or another at certain points, but for many sequences, you can choose your playable lead. In your experience playing and watching others play, do most players gravitate toward one character or another as “their character?”

Stephen Donkin, Vice President of Design:“It really varied in our playthroughs. They are both such great characters and performances that people seemed to gravitate to both of them in different ways. But overall, we have found the split of time between characters to be relatively even on a per-playthrough and also at a macro level.”
