It may be hard to notice what with all the Grand Theft Auto 6-ness happening right now, but the games industry has had a very rough last few years. This isn't to say no great games have come out this year. I've actually had a great time, what with Resident Evil Requiem, Onimusha: Way of the Sword, and Big Walk—oh and I've heard that Queen's Domain is rather good also.

But the issue of how big studios are able to balance their budget still stands. “Around content, AAA is under the most amount of threat," former Take-Two CEO, Ben Feder says in an interview with The Game Business. "They’re not big enough to really be platforms on the one hand, and they’re not small enough to really innovate on the other. If you talk to the major publishers, they cannot figure out a way to make a game for less than $300 million, and that to me is just not sustainable."

We now know for sure that GTA 6 is a winner: just look at this review score round up. But there are some worried that replicating this success is nigh impossible for any other studio. As Feder puts it, not everyone can take their "entire development team, putting them in a time capsule, burying them in the ground, and five years later they pop out and say, 'Voila, here it is.'" Most studios simply do not have the capacity or funds to have a team solely working on a project for around a decade.

"Three to five years ago, that was reasonable because we know what they’re building, it’s good IP, they have a niche, and we know what the world’s going to look like when they pop out of the ground," Feder adds. "Today, while they’re underground, on the surface of planet Earth all of this change is happening, not just technologically but also with respect to consumer tastes and expectations. They may come out five years from now, and the world may not care."

Feder goes on to explain that GTA 6 is the exception to this rule as it's able to deliver "escape velocity". I take this to mean the ridiculous amounts of hype and money that surround this game is why it's able to retain this old way of working. "Scale matters there, and you need an enormous amount of capital to do that,” Feder says. "The amount of capital required starts with a 'B' not with an 'M', for sure."
