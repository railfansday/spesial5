Trafford shows he has credentials to dislodge Pickford as England No 1

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Has there been a better goalkeeper in the Premier League this season than Leeds United’s James Trafford? It is a question worth asking after his brilliance secured his side a point against Sunderland.

Opinions will vary, of course, but the body of evidence to back Trafford’s argument to permanently dislodge Jordan Pickford as England No 1 looked even stronger at the Stadium of Light.

After leaving Manchester City in the summer to play regularly following a frustrating season as backup, Trafford is proving to be a brilliant buy for Leeds. He is the most expensive British goalkeeper. More importantly, he is playing like it too.

This was a tough examination for Leeds, who were not at their best, against a typically robust and physically challenging Sunderland who are getting back to doing the things that made them so difficult to play against last season. Regis Le Bris’ side created the better chances but were repeatedly denied by Trafford.

With the windy weather playing havoc, ruining the game as a spectacle, as both sides struggled for control of the ball, the two moments of quality came from Trafford in the first half.

Goalkeeping is defined by athleticism, physical strength and reflexes, but it is also about decision-making and mental fortitude. The two saves Trafford made to deny Sunderland’s Brian Brobbey in the first half were very different, but equally impressive.

The first was deceptively hard. Although the connection on Brobbey’s shot – back in the Sunderland side and causing chaos as usual – from the edge of the area was not the cleanest and that almost made it harder, as the flight of the ball was difficult to pick up. The bounce just in front of the goalkeeper meant he had to watch it closely, judge the trajectory in the air and spin off the grass and still keep a strong hand, at full stretch, to push it away from danger.

The second was, arguably, even better. Trafford had come off his line as Brobbey ran into the box. He was in a really vulnerable position, well outside his six-yard box in a one-on-one with the Netherlands international. The way he kept calm, stood up and timed when to move for the ball was superb, especially as an error could have led to a penalty.

Trafford may have saved with his feet, but he had cramped Brobbey for room, waited for the centre forward to make a move and timed the moment to dive at his feet perfectly. It was elite-level goalkeeping in a really tricky situation that favoured the striker.

Trafford made two more fine stops in the second half too, both from Nilson Angulo. The first was getting down low to tip another bouncing ball wide, which had flicked off a defender too. The second punching another deflected effort away after a block had taken some of the venom out of the shot.

Sunderland will feel like they shaded the battle, but Robin Roefs also made two decent stops from long-range shots in the second half while substitute Sean Longstaff saw an injury-time effort cleared off the line.

Sunderland were warned about “second season syndrome” and shrugged them off in the summer as they looked forward to their first European campaign for more than 50 years.

They had dismissed the gloomy pre-season predictions the previous year and had gone on to enjoy their most successful season in the top flight since the heady days of the Peter Reid era at the start of the century.

So when the “experts” told them it would be much harder this season—that they would no longer have the element of surprise and the squad would be badly stretched by the extra games in the Europa League—it was easy to ignore.

You cannot blame them. It has been a magical couple of years on Wearside where everything has gone in their favour, but reality has hit hard this season.
