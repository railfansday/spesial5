Anthropic cuts its bug disclosure window to 30 days as AI finds flaws

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The new disclosure window applies to serious flaws reported from 1 January 2027. Google’s Project Zero is shortening its own deadline in steps, to 30 days by 2028.

Anthropic is giving software makers less time to fix the security flaws it reports. AI models now find bugs much faster, it says. Its Frontier Red Team said on Monday it will publish serious flaws 30 days after reporting them, down from 90 days. That covers high- and critical-severity flaws. All other flaws get 45 days.

The new deadlines apply to flaws reported on or after 1 January 2027. Reports already in progress keep their old deadlines.

Anthropic is making two other changes. If a maintainer does not answer its first report, it will go to an outside coordinator after 15 days instead of 30. And once a patch is out, it will wait 30 days instead of 45 before sharing full technical details.

Some serious flaws Anthropic reported over 90 days ago are past its current deadline and still not public. Anthropic says it is telling those maintainers they have 30 more days. After that, it will publish the reports on its dashboard under the new policy from 1 January 2027.

“A 90-day window made sense when a vulnerability could be expected to stay private for months. We no longer believe it can,” Anthropic wrote.

The old rules assumed fixes moved at human speed and that two people rarely found the same bug, the company said. Now a bug one model finds is more and more likely to be found by another, it said.

Some flaws take longer than 30 or 45 days to fix, such as those in critical infrastructure software or in a technical standard. In those cases, Anthropic says it will agree a timeline with the vendor. Its seven-day window for flaws under active attack stays the same. The policy does not cover AI findings that no person has reviewed, such as those from its OSS Scanner service.

Anthropic says shorter deadlines may put more pressure on maintainers. It says it will keep sending fix guidance, and a patch where it can, with each report.

Google’s Project Zero is shortening its disclosure window too, SecurityWeek reported on Monday. From 2 February 2027, it will publish flaws 60 days after reporting them. From 3 January 2028, the window drops to 30 days.
