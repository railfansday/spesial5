Ross Stores: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Management is betting on shopper volume over price hikes, a choice that tests whether the value gap can keep customers loyal.

By The Motley Fool Team – Nov 20, 2026 at 3:27PM EST

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 19, 2026, Ross Stores (ROST -2.92%) announced a 12% total sales increase to $6.3 billion, powered by an 8% gain in same-store sales. That top-line momentum shows how off-price retail continues to take market share from department stores through disciplined cost execution and store expansion. Three key insights from management stand out for long-term investors.

Same-store sales rose 8% primarily because more shoppers walked through the doors, not because the company charged higher prices. That surge in customer transactions spanned every age bracket and income level, proving the brand's broad market appeal.

Ross Stores finished its 2026 store rollout by adding 115 total locations across its flagship and dd's DISCOUNTS concepts. Expansion in high-density markets like the Northeast delivered strong store productivity that beat management's pro forma estimates, or projected financial targets.

Rather than pushing up average unit retail (AUR) prices, or average price per item, management focuses on selling more items per visit. Keeping prices low preserves the company's price gap over traditional retailers and keeps bargain hunters loyal.

Shopper momentum accelerates into the holiday season, backed by faster checkouts and lean inventory. Management uses self-checkout tech and strategic buying to maintain pricing discipline.

Steady unit expansion and off-price flexibility give the business a clear pathway to market share gains.
