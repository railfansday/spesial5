Federal Student Loans Can Be Discharged – Check Closed School, Disability & Fraud Rules

Your federal student loans may be canceled if your school closed while you were enrolled, misled you, or signed you up for a loan you weren’t eligible for, or if you have a total and permanent disability. Check which of these discharges fits you and apply free through StudentAid.gov before your next payment.

Years of payments aren’t the only way out of federal student loans. The law also cancels them through discharges, and most happen only after someone asks.

The Education Department checks VA and Social Security records for borrowers who qualify by disability, writes to each, and discharges their loans unless they opt out. If no letter has come, apply.

Every application is free, through StudentAid.gov or your loan servicer, so never pay a company to apply. A closed school, false certification, or forgery discharge also refunds your past payments, and an approved borrower defense claim can.

Say your for-profit college closed two months before graduation and you never enrolled elsewhere. A closed school discharge would cancel your remaining $14,000 and refund what you’ve paid.

A 2025 law made death and disability discharges permanently free of federal income tax, if the tax return lists a Social Security number. Your state may still tax a disability discharge.

None of these needs a court, unlike discharging student loans in bankruptcy. Before your next payment, log in at StudentAid.gov, find each loan’s servicer, and ask which discharge application fits.
