Newer Credit Scores Ignore Paid Collections – Get the Deal in Writing First

Paying off a non-medical collection helps only newer credit scores. FICO Score 9 and 10 and VantageScore 3.0 and 4.0 stop counting a collection once it’s paid, while paying does nothing for FICO Score 8 and older versions. Before you pay anything, get the collector’s terms in writing, including whether it will delete the account from your credit reports.

On its own, paying won’t take a collection off your reports. It stays seven years from the missed payment that started it, paid or not, unless it’s a medical collection, which the credit bureaus drop once it’s paid.

FICO says its Score 9 and Score 10 ignore a collection agency’s account once it’s reported paid in full or settled to a zero balance. VantageScore’s models have ignored paid collections since version 3.0 in 2013. Paying does nothing for FICO Score 8 or older versions.

That difference matters for a mortgage. The Federal Housing Finance Agency lets lenders selling loans to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac choose Classic FICO, an older model that still counts a paid collection, or VantageScore 4.0, which ignores it. Ask your lender which one it uses, and whether the loan requires collections paid off by closing.

Say a collector agrees in writing to settle your $900 cable bill for $500. FICO Score 9 then treats the zero-balance account as paid and stops counting it, but the report lists it as settled, not paid in full. The $400 forgiven counts as taxable income unless an exception such as insolvency applies, with or without a Form 1099-C reporting it.

Pull your free weekly reports at AnnualCreditReport.com after you pay, and dispute the account with any bureau whose report doesn’t match the signed letter, sending a copy as proof.
