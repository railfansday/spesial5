Ambidestro clads coastal home in Brazil with rough local stone

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Contrasting volumes of smooth plaster and rough local Morelo stone define this coastal home in Brazil, designed by local architecture practice Ambidestro.

Named Casa Los Cobos, the home is located in Xangri-Lá on Brazil’s southern coast, and was designed to make the most of panoramic views out over the sea from the rear of the site.

Set back from the road, a series of routes both through and around the home “reveal the landscape gradually,” including a path beneath a timber canopy and via a paved veranda screened by lush plants.

“The main opportunity was the merging of two adjoining lots into a single site that extends to the rear, where the landscape opens onto the water,” Ambidestro co-founder João Pedro Crescente told Dezeen.

The ground floor of Casa Los Cobos has been given over almost entirely to a single large living, dining and kitchen space, wrapped by service spaces and bathrooms.

Full-height sliding glass doors allow this space to be opened out onto a large sheltered terrace alongside a swimming pool, with a lawn that slopes down to a nearby waterway.

“The ground floor is free of partitions: living, dining and the entertaining kitchen are integrated and turned toward the greenery, accommodating everything from a weeknight dinner to a Saturday party,” Crescente told Dezeen.

“Between the living room and the water lie, in sequence, the veranda, the garden and the pool, which wraps around the house. The eye passes through these layers before reaching the backdrop,” he added.

Both the exterior and interior have been given contrasting finishes of rough Morelo stone and white plaster and paint, with the rear terrace framed by white-painted masonry.

This is complemented internally with bespoke carpentry in pale oak, including a floating timber bar supported by a concrete column that defines one edge of the living space.

“The intention was to use natural, local materials in light, warm tones repeated inside and out, making the transition between spaces almost imperceptible,” said Crescente.

“Moledo stone clads the facades and changes in appearance from morning to late afternoon. Other natural stones continue onto the floors and wet areas,” he added.

A skylit staircase leads up to the more compartmentalised first floor of the home, which contains six en-suite bedrooms, two of which open onto a balcony overlooking the sea.

Other homes in Brazil recently featured on Dezeen include a home in São Paolo by Studio Arthur Casas featuring dramatic overhangs and cantilevers, and a low-lying dwelling by Sabella Arquitetura that is topped by a gridded timber roof.
