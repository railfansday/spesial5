Notre Dame football makes it look easy, then not in win over Miami

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

SOUTH BEND — This one was seemingly headed for Easy Street for Notre Dame football.

Flex some muscle long enough to build a three-touchdown lead at home over the nation's top-ranked team easy. Continue to play determined defense easy. Throw together some long, time- chewing drives easy. Easy.

Irish head coach Marcus Freeman is often quick to insist that nothing in this game is easy. Even when it looks like it. When it demands it.

Instead of putting this one on cruise control, the Irish coughed and wheezed their way across the finish line. Drama, anyone? You got it.

No. 11 Notre Dame 31, No. 1 Miami (Fla.) 24. It came down to the final snap of the game.

Don't drop those December and beyond College Football Playoff plans, Irish fans. Not after this one. The Irish and this 2026 football season still have a pulse, one that's beating rather well, thank you.

Losing just couldn't be an option for Notre Dame football, so it did the opposite Saturday night against top-ranked and undefeated Miami (Fla.) at Notre Dame Stadium.

What helped seal this one? How about a wheel route pass from CJ Carr to Nolan James down the far sideline that picked up 38 yards on fourth and 3 from the Miami 44 early in the fourth quarter. Four plays later, Notre Dame was in the end zone with a James scoring dive to make it 31-17. With it, a collective exhale of relief. Maybe even exhaustion.

Where was that Notre Dame football team three weeks earlier at BYU? Where was that Notre Dame football team one week earlier against Navy? Don't care where it was hiding or why that dominance went dormant, only that the team we watched steamroll through the season's first six weeks finally resurfaced.

Notre Dame again looked like Notre Dame. It looked like a College Football Playoff team. It looked like it could carry national championship dreams. It also looked like it was holding on for its postseason life.

Someone find Michael Irvin and offer a wellness check.

Four quarters away from a season lost, Notre Dame is three regular season wins away from a big CFP payoff. Top four finish anyone? Better beat Boston College, SMU and Syracuse.

He may not have delivered the kind of game where legacies are defined, but CJ Carr had one of those drives late in the first half that swing games. This one swung. Gave the Irish the green light to play even more loose and free and confident than they were. Gave the Hurricanes the idea that they were heading for their charter and long flight home with their first loss.

With Carr at the controls, where he pushed all the right buttons and made all the right plays when he needed to do both, Notre Dame marched 81 yards in eight plays in 1:59. When Carr zipped a pass to Micah Gilbert through the middle of the field and into the end zone, it went to 17-3 Irish.

One half still to play. Plenty of football stuff still to do. Taking that two-score lead back up to the locker room, you could sense that this was going to be Notre Dame's night. When it got to 24-3 early in the third quarter, it was already late for the Hurricanes. Too late.

Where was the gameday hype for this one? Certainly not off the chain as the kids like to say. More like cautious optimism (skepticism?) in and around the stadium parking lots, which were jammed four, five hours before kickoff.

Halftime power naps likely were necessary for a crowd that had been on campus for the better part of 12 hours by the time this one hit intermission.

Notre Dame stretched its lead to 10-0 on the third play of the second quarter when Carr put a 27-yard pass to Jordan Faison in the only place he could put it – in the near left corner of the end zone.

Fiason did his acrobatic best to do the rest – snare the pass, get his foot down, secure the catch – to put the place up for grabs for the first time. One veteran scribe on the ninth floor of the east side of the stadium mentioned that a touchdown on that drive, and Notre Dame would win, a field goal, and it would lose.

The Irish threw the night's first two punches. The Hurricanes rarely punched back. A jab here and a cross there, but nowhere near the knockout many feared.

As this one unfolded, Miami looked more like a team that knew it had more important matters down the road – like winning the Atlantic Coast Conference championship and securing an automatic bid back to the College Football Playoff. Notre Dame looked like a team with everything to play for – and everything to lose.

No way could Notre Dame let this one get away on a night that turned into a three-hour infomercial for everything Notre Dame – and not just football. Hey, let's cheer for four NFL Hall of Famers (Jerome Bettis, Tim Brown, Joe Montana and Bryant Young) during one stoppage. Oh, here comes World Series winning manager (and former Irish head coach) Pat Murphy in another one.

For good measure, let's bring on Jon Bon Jovi to stump for his recently announced 2027 show at Notre Dame Stadium. Never too early to get those tickets for October, people.
