What is ‘texture-maxxing’? The food trend putting crunch on the menu

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Texture-maxxing is the latest food trend taking social media by storm as consumers, driven by aesthetics and taste, look for a sensory experience.

British supermarket Waitrose's latest food and drink report highlighted "texture-maxxing" as one of the trends of the year. It describes the phenomenon as a multi-sensory indulgence that is "fuelled by texture-maxxing crunch and intense flavour hits," pushing shoppers to fill their trolleys with crunchy peanut butter, dumplings, and trifles.

"This year, the sound of food went mainstream," Waitrose's Senior Development Chef Will Torrent said in the Wednesday report. "Driven by social media's obsession with audible, multi-sensory cooking, we're creating high-contrast meals that combine shatter-crisp crunch with pillowy chew."

"The emphasis in social media content has flipped from narrative voiceovers to ASMR-inspired audio, where the evocative sounds of food are just as important as the visuals," he added.

Food trends on social media have ranged from the obvious to the absurd. Fibermaxxing and proteinmaxxing have taken off in the past few years. Meanwhile, food and beverages like Dubai chocolate, protein coffee, and ube were some of the big social media hits.

Nestlé's USA 2026 trends found that 75% of Gen Z and 80% of millennials sought out texture in snack cravings with the hashtag "crunchtok," amassing 1.5 billion views.

Waitrose noted that shoppers looking to maximize on texture bought its viral No.1 Alphonso Mango Dessert, which involves "cracking through the crisp shell" to access a "smooth mango mousse and passion fruit curd on a crunchy white chocolate base."

Waitrose's TikTok and Instagram videos of eating the dessert were watched by more than two million people, with a whopping 6,173 hours and 36 minutes of play time on TikTok. Its videos featuring crunchy toast also went viral.

The U.K. grocer's sales of jarred chili crunches, which include crispy rayu oils such as itsu Crispy Chilli Oil, saw a 13-fold jump. Meanwhile, crunchy peanut butter outsold smooth.

Dumplings also had a moment, with sales of chicken gyoza dumplings up 116% this year and searches on waitrose.com for gochujang and peanut butter udon noodles jumped 60%.

"We've seen an explosion in demand as shoppers treat dumplings not just as a quick weeknight bite, but as a total sensory experience," Waitrose's Executive Chef Stephen Parkins-Knight said.

"Driven by the rise of texture-maxxing, people want that ultimate contrast: delicate skins, juicy fillings and crispy skirts, topped with very chilli crunch. With innovation ranging from fusion fillings to rich dipping broths, dumplings have taken a delicious centre stage in 2026," he added.

Texture-maxxing also extended to desserts, as shoppers bought twice as many chocolate trifles compared to 2025, and sales of strawberry trifles increased 160%.

Tiramisu was also popular, with searches for waitrose.com's tiramisu recipe page increasing 155% year-on-year.
