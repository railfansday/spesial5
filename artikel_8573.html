Personal Investments • Re: Help with Parents

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

as POA, you have a fiduciary duty to manage your parents investments in their own best interests. I became my Mom’s Financial manager when she started to show signs of Cognitive impairment. She asked me to help her and volunteered to give me her DPOA. I managed everything for 12 years, and knew her investment philosophy, which was to follow John Bogle. She, also, was frugal. Nearly all her investments were taxable, her index funds were held at Vanguard, but she had a muni-bond ladder and a tiny IRA at another brokerage. I moved the Muni bonds to Vanguard.

Her SS and widow’s retirement covered much more than her living expenses when she lived independently, and the majority of her long term care expenses, which spanned 8 years. My focus was on making sure she had the best care possible, with any leftovers going to her heirs.

I had a rather contentious sibling. I kept a monthly spreadsheet of all income, expenses, receipts and provided him with a copy. If he had questions or concerns, it was easy to provide answers. Keeping siblings in the loop keeps everything transparent, and minimizes conflict.

I also used the powers of the DPOA to create a revocable living trust following the provisions of her will exactly, and moved everything except her tiny IRA into the trust. This simplified everything when I had to settle her estate.
