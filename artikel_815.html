Arsenal’s Lisa Baum: ‘I believe in us. I think it’s all about the small margins’

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Germany forward on her new life in London and drawing strength from the memory of her late brother, Dennis

Lisa Baum is 19, for the next few days at least, but you wouldn’t know it. The Germany forward plays with a maturity that explains why she was handed her Arsenal debut in their opening game of the season, less than two months after joining.

Baum had been told that the 2025 European champions had a vision for her but that coming in as a young player she would have to be patient. Ten minutes into her debut against Brighton, in Crawley, the young winger scored what would be the winning goal.

“It was unbelievable,” says Baum, speaking in a small meeting room at Arsenal’s London Colney training ground with a maturity that matches her play. “Everything just went so quick. I just shot, my brain was just switched off, it just came out.”

That win kickstarted Arsenal’s season but it quickly stalled. In the league, the team went five games without a win. After a 2-0 defeat by OL Lyonnes in the Champions League on Thursday night, Arsenal have five wins from their opening 15 games. It is far from the season they, or many others, envisaged.

Sunday’s final home game of the year, against West Ham, offers an opportunity to take some positivity into the international break, after which Arsenal face three away fixtures before the winter pause.

“I believe in us,” says Baum. “I think it’s all about the small margins. Sometimes it’s luck, sometimes it’s in the final decision-making, sometimes it’s how close we are to a goal that doesn’t go in. But we work really hard in every training session and on trying to keep ourselves up mentally and physically. We’re trying to push through the barrier that we maybe have right now, but we get a lot of trust from the staff and coaches. Everybody really believes in each other and I think it’s not going to take long before we’re through this part of the journey.”

Overall, Arsenal have not been playing particularly badly. They have been creating chances and dominating games but struggling to translate that into goals and points. In October, Baum earned a first senior Germany call-up and made her debut off the bench at half-time against Australia, having previously featured for teams from the under-16s to under-23s.

Baum moved to Germany when she was four. Born to a Tanzanian mother and German father, she spent her first years in Dar es Salaam in Tanzania. They swapped a city with a population of about four million for Ahrensbök, a municipality with a population of just over 8,000 near the Baltic coast. “I think my mum had the hardest time out of us in the family, with the move to Germany,” she says. Baum says she and her brother, Dennis, “learned to adapt easily”.

Baum doesn’t remember her years on the east coast of Africa but when she went back three or four years ago, some sort of memory was triggered. “It was a feeling, or feelings,” she says. “It was so good to grow up there. It was warm, it was the beautiful beaches, it was having all your family around. I was in kindergarten or I was in the water a lot – I love to swim.”

She started playing football after the move to northern Germany, desperate to copy Dennis. “My father played football when he was younger – he was a goalkeeper – but he stopped when he didn’t really enjoy it any more. My mum wasn’t a big fan of me playing; she dressed me like a girly girl, with princess dresses and that kind of stuff, but I was never really like that at that age. I always wanted to follow my older brother – I wanted to wear his clothes. He started playing football before me and I wanted to be like him.”

Baum would play on the side of the pitch at her brother’s club until a parent said she should be playing too. After playing with various local teams she joined Hamburg’s academy. She worked her way into the first team and they won promotion to the second tier in her first season. In the 2024-25 season they reached the DFB-Pokal semi-finals and the top flight. Baum moved to RB Leipzig and scored five goals in 27 games before Arsenal swooped.

“Everything was just so quick,” she says of the move to London. Training required getting used to: “Everything was so sharp, everything was technically excellent, like 10 out of 10. I was quite nervous and had some clumsy actions.”

The transfer also involved swapping an average attendance last season of 1,651 for one of 33,808. “It’s unbelievable,” says Baum of playing at the Emirates Stadium. “I had only played in a big stadium once before. You can’t even believe how much the fans give you when you step on the pitch. There’s so much support, that of course we need in this current time. I think Arsenal is a club that holds on to us, on to the team, and gives us a lot of strength.”

Look closely at Baum’s left wrist when she plays and you will usually see a strip of white tape around it. Sometimes it has something written on it. She’s done it “since that day” – the day in 2022 when Dennis died in a car accident, aged 17.

The brother that inspired her on to a pitch is with her on it at the highest level. “The next time I was on the football pitch, I just felt I wanted to do that,” she says. “I feel like it’s given me a different kind of strength and confidence. Sometimes I don’t write anything, but it’s about just having the physical feeling that he is with me.”
