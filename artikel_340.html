Canadian Authorities Kill 4 Bears Involved in Deadly Attack on Hikers

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Wildlife authorities said evidence, including animal DNA, conclusively linked a sow and her three cubs to the deaths of two women along a hiking trail in British Columbia.

Four grizzly bears were responsible for the attack that killed two women in British Columbia this month, the wildlife authorities in the Canadian province said on Tuesday.

The sow and her three cubs were captured over the weekend and forensic testing, including the examination of animal DNA, conclusively linked them to the attack, the provincial Conservation Officer Service said. The investigation found that the attack likely involved predatory behavior, and the bears were put down to protect public safety, the service added.

The remains of the two victims, who were identified by their family as Jennifer Balcarras and her daughter Selah Reed, were found along a hiking trail near Bella Coola, a small coastal community northwest of Vancouver. One of Ms. Balcarras’s brothers told the Canadian Broadcasting Corporation that she and her daughter were familiar with the trail and had been out for “a casual walk.”

The two women went hiking on Saturday, Oct. 31, and were reported missing the next day, according to the broadcaster. The authorities said the first victim’s remains were found on Monday, and the second’s on Tuesday.

The discovery of the women’s remains set off a dayslong investigation to determine what happened to them. The wildlife authorities said on Tuesday that more than a dozen officers were involved in gathering evidence and working in difficult terrain and weather to capture the bears.

The area is home to a large population of grizzly bears, but the authorities said fatal attacks by the animals involving predatory behavior are rare.

Last November, a grizzly bear severely mauled three children and an adult on a school trip. That attack occurred about two miles from the site of the deaths this month. The authorities did not capture that animal but they were able to collect its DNA. Testing confirmed that the four bears captured and killed this month were not involved in the attack last year, the wildlife authorities said.

In response to the deaths, the Nuxalk Nation, the Indigenous governing body in Bella Coola, said last week that it was recommending an 8 p.m. curfew for residents. It also said the Royal Canadian Mounted Police was conducting night patrols and escorting students from the school bus.

That curfew has been lifted following the capture of the four bears, Samuel Schooner, the elected chief councilor of the body, said in a statement. The night patrols will continue and will be reassessed in the coming days, he added. Mr. Schooner urged the community to remain vigilant and report any bear sightings to the authorities.

The Conservation Officer Service said in its statement on Tuesday that it will conduct further analysis to determine the circumstances that led to the deadly attack.
