Airbnb is making its rivals look relatively sluggish. Is it affordability that's kickstarting demand across regions?

Airbnb said Thursday that its revenue grew 19% in the third quarter to $4.9 billion, its fastest growth in three years, outpacing both Expedia Group and Booking Holdings.

The acceleration marks a turnaround from a year ago, when CEO Brian Chesky said at Skift Global Forum that he was “not happy” with Airbnb’s growth. Revenue rose 9.7% that quarter, and growth has accelerated almost every quarter since.

Expedia’s revenue grew 12% in the third quarter, and Booking’s fell 1%, a decline that reflected a $1 billion accounting hit related to a possible FTC settlement over allegedly deceptive practices.

Nights and seats booked, which include homes, hotels, experiences and services, grew 16%. Airbnb didn’t point to a single factor.

Airbnb said third-quarter revenue grew 19% to $4.9 billion, its fastest growth in more than three years and ahead of both Expedia Group, which grew 12%, and Booking Holdings, whose revenue fell 1% after a $1 billion accounting charge tied to a possible FTC settlement. Nights and seats booked — a combined measure covering homes, hotels, experiences, and services — rose 16%, against 7% room night growth at each of its two larger rivals, though the metrics are not directly comparable. Airbnb attributed the acceleration to demand across all regions and to product work including search and merchandising, pricing tools, flexible payments, and Reserve Now, Pay Later, which it said has extended booking lead times. The expansion beyond homes is showing early traction: Airbnb now lists thousands of independent and boutique hotels in 60 top destinations, up from 20 in May, and said about 35% of guests whose first booking is a hotel come back to book a home. Car rentals, sourced from Expedia's CarTrawler, have become one of its most popular services. AI customer support now runs in 50 languages, resolved roughly 40% of all support issues without a human agent, and cut support costs per booking 17%. Net income rose 35% to $1.9 billion, and Airbnb raised full-year revenue guidance to 16-18% growth from a prior "mid-teens" forecast.
