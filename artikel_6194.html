American Airlines Officially Welcomes Passengers Back To Flagship Dining At LAX After 6 Years

Travelers with American Airlines can now enjoy the carrier's Flagship Dining facilities at Los Angeles International Airport (LAX). The upgraded facility provides à la carte dining experiences within the Flagship lounge. The airline's premium lounge is available to travelers in premium cabins and those with AAdvantage elite status on long-haul flights.

According to The Points Guy, an enhanced guest experience awaits with table service and an a la carte menu, a vast upgrade from grabbing a plate and loading it up with food from the buffet. This complimentary sit-down meal service can include several courses.

The exclusive Flagship Dining experience is now open in Los Angeles, complementing the existing facilities open in Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport (DFW) and Miami International Airport (MIA). These airports provide the dining experience within the carrier's Flagship Lounge. Historically, American limited Flagship Dining to passengers in the Flagship First Cabin only on the Boeing 777-300ER and Airbus A321 transcontinental service; however, recent changes from phasing out international first class have expanded access to additional passengers and routes.

American allows business class passengers access to the exclusive dining experience to those booked in the Flagship Business Plus seat on 787-9 Dreamliner or 777-300ER flights, and on those in business class qualifying transcontinental or Hawaii flights that have been marked as Flagship. This change for California departing passengers will allow business class domestic passengers on select transcontinental flights to allow access for those with a Flagship domestic first-class ticket (standard first-class ticket holders are not eligible).

Simple Flying reached out to American for comment on Saturday; however, an airline spokesperson could not immediately be reached.

Subject to availability, eligible passengers are able to arrive at the Flagship Lounge for a sit-down meal. During peak times, there may be a waitlist, which you can request when you enter the lounge. You can even order your meal via a QR code.

American previously had the Flagship dining experience at its John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK) hub lounge. It now operates three premium lounges alongside British Airways within the airport's Terminal 8 (Greenwich, Chelsea and Soho lounges). For those departing from Philadelphia International Airport (PHL), À la carte menus are available within the regular AAdmirals Club.

With Flagship Dining now available at three major US airports, this also marks the upcoming end date for American's long-haul first class standalone product. The airline is phasing it out as it opts for Flagship Business as its premier cabin for international and Hawaii flights.

This has led to jets being retrofitted with new interiors and the addition of its highest-ever business class seat count onboard. These changes are expected to end on November 19, when the airline no longer offers long-haul first class. Transcontinental Flagship First is set to end in March 2027.

The carrier is opening its doors to more flyers as it rolls out lounge upgrades across the US.

American's Flagship Business Class offers a lie-flat long-haul and transcontinental experience with direct aisle access and upgraded amenities. Jets are configured in 1-2-1 reverse herringbone and staggered layouts on the 777 and 787, and select Airbus aircraft.

Dedicated priority check-in lanes, fast-track security screening at select airports, priority boarding, and expedited baggage handling all complement access to the airline's Flagship Lounge, where an à la carte menu provides delicious bespoke dishes, complemented by a range of hot and cold alcoholic or nonalcoholic beverages.
