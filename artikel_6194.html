Trump Blames Buttigieg For FAA Meltdown While Elon Musk Blames Sean Duffy

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

President Donald Trump blames Pete Buttigieg for the nationwide FAA meltdown. Elon Musk, meanwhile, is pointing at the tTransportation Secretary Trump appointed, claiming Sean Duffy rejected an offer of free Starlink service. The finger-pointing continues, but neither accusation tells stranded passengers exactly why a router installation brought the system down.

Speaking to reporters on November 16, Trump singled out his predecessor’s transportation secretary for the failure that halted departures across the United States:

“Buttigieg ruined the system. He totally ruined it.”

When asked what personal responsibility he accepted, Trump responded:

He also praised Duffy and attributed the disruption to aging equipment. I have already addressed the problem with blaming the previous administration. America’s air traffic control infrastructure has suffered from decades of deferred investment. That includes the Biden administration, but also Trump’s first term and the administrations before it.

Friday’s failure, however, occurred during work performed under the current administration. An explanation of what Buttigieg allegedly did wrong would be useful, but of course Buttigieg is just the whipping boy for an administration that shows no personal responsibly for any of its deleterious actions.

Musk has offered a different accusation. In a post on X, he wrote:

“Free Starlinks for FAA were offered to Sec Duffy, but he said they were not needed. As it turns out, they were.”

Free Starlinks for FAA were offered to Sec Duffy, but he said they were not needed. As it turns out, they were. https://t.co/OZwDeiXOAr

— Elon Musk (@elonmusk) November 16, 2026

That is a pointed criticism of the same official Trump says handled the situation well, but Musk does not explain when the offer was made, what equipment or service was included, where it would have been deployed, or how it would have prevented this particular failure…what a surprise.

I would like Duffy to answer the allegation directly. Was there an offer? What did it cover? Why was it declined? If the proposal was unsuitable, explain why.

Musk should also substantiate his assertion that the service would have been needed here. Being able to offer satellite internet does not establish that his proposed “gift” would have kept this particular network operating.

I love Starlink as an airline passenger. It is easily the best inflight internet service available now, and it increasingly influences which flights I book. But that does not mean it is or would have been a solution to an air traffic control network failure.

According to the FAA’s explanation, excessive connection attempts flooded its telecommunications network, destabilizing aircraft communications and radar feeds. Investigators identified sites in El Dorado, Arkansas, and Amarillo, Texas. L3Harris had been working at El Dorado, including installing a new router. Blocking those locations apparently stabilized the network. The broadcasting function on the new routers was also disabled as a precaution.

To support Musk’s claim, we would need to know whether his proposed Starlink connections would have bypassed the affected equipment and preserved the necessary communications.  Perhaps Starlink could have provided useful redundancy…but maybe the proposed installation would have had no bearing on this failure.

Buttigieg has defended his record, pointing to improvements in controller staffing, runway safety, and flight cancellations during his tenure.

He also says he launched the communications modernization project that the current administration is continuing. His response asks for an accounting of the money already authorized and an explanation of why the execution of that work triggered such a widespread failure.

As secretary, I made sure to leave air travel in better condition than when we arrived. We reversed a decade of decline in air traffic controller staffing, drove down runway safety incidents, and when I left office we were experiencing the lowest flight cancellation rates in… https://t.co/68KleIhFEc

— Pete Buttigieg (@PeteButtigieg) November 16, 2026

They do not mean Buttigieg’s tenure should escape scrutiny. The January 2023 nationwide ground stop occurred on his watch, and the system still required enormous investment when he left office while his boss prioritized student loan forgiveness and federal mask mandates instead of accelerating infrastructure programs like Air Traffic Control modernization.

But acknowledging those shortcomings does not relieve the current administration of responsibility for its own decisions. Duffy has been Transportation Secretary for nearly two years. He oversees the department responsible for explaining this failure and should be held accountable for the actions under his watch.

There is an obvious temptation to enjoy the spectacle: Trump blames Buttigieg while Musk suggests Trump’s own transportation secretary rejected help. I find the contradiction revealing, but passengers deserve something more useful than competing accusations.

The investigation must establish what changed in the network, why that change caused such a widespread disruption, and whether the safeguards surrounding the work were adequate. Congress should examine the Starlink allegation too, if Musk can provide a concrete proposal and evidence supporting his account.

Duffy may have had reasonable grounds to decline whatever Musk offered, but he should be able to articulate them. Likewise, Musk may have identified a missed opportunity, but he should be able to demonstrate his rather glaring accusation.

And Trump should stop treating an investigation as unnecessary because he has already identified a political opponent to blame. The age of the equipment was known. The difficult task was upgrading it while maintaining reliable service. That is the work his administration undertook, and that is the work it must explain to a weary public.

Trump says Buttigieg ruined the system and accepts no personal responsibility. Musk says Duffy rejected free Starlink service that was needed. Both claims deserve scrutiny and neither establishes the technical cause of Friday’s meltdown.

I would be far more receptive to an administration that acknowledged a failure during a difficult modernization project and explained how it would improve the process. Instead, we keep receiving accusations, praise for the response, and demands for more money.

The passengers who lost days getting home deserve a candid explanation of what went wrong. So do the taxpayers being asked to fund the repair. Is this really a political issue that should divide us?
