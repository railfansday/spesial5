FIFA hit by cyberattack with 'personal and business information' stolen - including immigration documents and financial records - after hackers 'impersonated staff'

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Published: 07:12 EST, 26 November 2026 | Updated: 08:10 EST, 26 November 2026

Personal information, immigration documents and financial records have been stolen from FIFA in a cyberattack.

Football's governing body confirmed that a hacking group 'impersonated staff' to gain access to their system before taking the sensitive files.

In total, more than 12 gigabytes of data has been stolen and a group called Handsome Boy Hacking Club (HBHC) shared a screenshot of FIFA documents on X last week, claiming credit for the theft and finding access through FIFA’s Microsoft app portal.

They are understood to have then published the stolen files on a forum on the dark web, writing 'more leaks soon'.

A FIFA spokesperson said: 'FIFA can confirm that it has been the victim of a criminal cyber-attack resulting in unauthorised access to a limited set of personal and business information.

'FIFA is working closely with the relevant authorities, including law enforcement.

'FIFA condemns in the strongest possible terms the unlawful acquisition and publication of information, particularly personal data.

According to the Athletic, immigration, financial and personal documents were stolen by the hackers impersonating staff but did not include the FIFA Forward Enterprise (FFE), a plan to effectively sell off the World Cup that collapsed and piled pressure on president Gianni Infantino.

The FFE plan first became public knowledge in July. Infantino sought to create a company to manage the commercial interests of FIFA’s competitions, including the World Cup, but wanted a minority stake to be made available to private investors.

The lead investor was set to be Thrive, a company founded by President Trump’s son-in-law’s brother, Josh Kushner, but the plan was abandoned on August 1 after three continental confederations rejected it.

It remains to be seen whether files relating to Infantino himself were taken by the hackers.

The 56-year-old is currently running for reelection against Kohzo Tashima of Japan and France football federation president Philippe Diallo.

He remains the strong favorite to win FIFA's election is held on March 18 and has already served three terms.

FIFA are the latest football-related body to have fallen victim to cyber crime, with LaLiga targeted earlier this month.

Spanish police are still investigating but LaLiga confirmed their security teams identified the source and stopped the hacker from stealing more data.
