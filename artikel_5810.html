MG U9 EV Ute — It’s A Monster Truck

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

We visit the MG dealership and get to know the MG U9 EV ute up close and personal.

As I have said many times to CleanTechnica readers, Australia needs more BEV utes. The PHEV utes are taking a bite out of diesel sales, but we need some monster truck battery electrics to push electrification deep into the heart of the bush-bashing Aussie tradie. I think that MG may have achieved that with the launch of the MG U9 EV. Yesterday, Majella and I went to have a look. Even Russell Coight would be impressed and the Mitsubishi Outlander PHEV should be feeling threatened. Take a break and watch the funny video. I doubt it will affect the sales of the Shark though.

So, yesterday, Majella I set out to have an adventure visiting the various car yards near our home. We were actually planning to look at some new electric vans, but ended up in the MG showroom with a very knowledgeable sales rep exploring the MG U9 EV. What a surprise! An early morning google search assured me that there were none in Brisbane available to view. This dealership not only brought one out from the pre-delivery area for us to check out, but also told us they had already sold and delivered their first one and had six more on order.

The MG U9 EV (it also comes in diesel) is joining the thin ranks of BEV utes already available in Australia — the KGM Musso and the Toyota Hilux BEV. So far this year, 269 KGM Mussos have sold in Australia. 174 MG U9 EVs are listed as delivered, possibly mainly to dealers. The cost of the MG U9 EV is approximately AU$80,000, just a few thousand more than the Toyota Hilux BEV. The diesel version of the MG sells for around AU$50,000.

The sales rep told me about his first customer for the MG ute: “He was keen. He already had home solar and a battery and was producing far more electricity than he could use. His solution: trade in the old diesel ute on a U9 EV ‘rolling battery.’ It will complement his Kia EV6. With the 6.6 kWh of the U9 vehicle-to-load [capability], he plans to power his house.” The ecosystem expands. For those with a query, it is not worth selling power back to the grid.

The MG U9 has a 102.2 kWh LFP battery, big enough to give a range of up to 430 km and send this behemoth hurtling down the road — accelerating to 100 km an hour from zero in 5.8 seconds. That’s as fast as my new Tesla! And an advertised top speed of 190 km/hour. Just as well it has disc brakes all round — with the front ones ventilated. And to recharge — 42 minutes from 20–80%.

Tare weight comes in at 2815 kg, towing capacity is 3500 kg, and it has the capacity to carry 685 kg in the tray. Unladen ground clearance is listed as 230 mm, and it has a wading depth of 550 mm. Length is 5500 mm; width 2265 mm; height 1874 mm. Very similar dimensions to the GMC Hummer with half the battery capacity. Hummer: L 5507 mm; W 2201 mm; H 2055 mm.

The MG U9 BEV includes a fold-down rear bulkhead to extend cargo-bed length inside the cabin, plus a 1,200 L tub capacity and a 236 L front trunk (frunk).

Google tells me: “The MG U9 EV Dual-Cab Ute comes equipped from the factory with a four-wheel electronic air suspension system offering over 100 mm of vertical height adjustment (including an Off-Road Lift mode, and automatic lowering by -20 mm at 80 km/h and -40 mm at 120 km/h.”

Of course there is a lot more to know, especially about tech and safety features. I am just whetting your appetite. You can find out more here. Or watch a video review on YouTube here.

At the moment, the dealership is fielding 8 enquiries a week online, but it is expecting some headwinds in mid 2027 when the Australian federal government is planning to review Fringe Benefit Tax benefits for electric vehicles. My comment was that somehow car manufacturers seem to find ways of reducing costs when government support is modified.

While I peppered the sales rep with questions, Majella did her usual photographic exploration and analysis.

She was impressed by the massive frunk, as was I — you could fit a whole body in there! The frunk can be opened from the outside by a physical button.

… and it has a power point for your fridge or coffeemaker!

The ute features doors with physical buttons and handles for ease of access.

The gear lever reminded Majella of the ones we see in the cockpit of a plane.

A variety of charging options for your tech are provided: cigarette lighter, USB charger, and more.

Perhaps MG went overboard with buttons — lots of physical buttons on the dash and on the steering wheel, plus the screen. Majella thought that this was a comfortable wheel size and shape for a long drive.

Behind the back seats in a cubby hole we have the charge cords: a trickle charger and type 2 to type 2.

The back tailgate has a discreet button to push to fold it down so you can load the tray.

An emergency glass breaker and a seat belt cutter is provided.

Although MG appears to have thought of everything, Majella commented that the only thing they don’t supply is a first aid kit.

The air suspension is easily adjusted air suspension from the centre screen. The sales rep confided that the staff were playing with it the day before.

The 360 degree camera extends to the trailer, with an aerial view for easily reversing.

Those looking for a truck to haul stuff, power their tools, and store excess energy would do well to consider the MG U9 EV. It may not be a Hummer, but it’s close. For tradies and bush bashers, the future can now be bright and electric.
