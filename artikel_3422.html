10 Netflix Series To Watch Before Kicking the Bucket (#1 Is Actually Flawless)

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Netflix has completely changed the game in terms of the future of television, as the format of binge-watching has now become the standard for which all streaming services release their content. Although HBO was the network that kicked off the era of “prestige television” through its high-quality writing and acting, Netflix is responsible for getting bigger stars, bigger budgets, and more eyeballs on its programming.

Netflix has never been consistent in terms of its output, as there is no way to guess which of its shows will end up breaking out to become a phenomenon, but one of the joys of its release strategy is that it can often bring obscure shows into the spotlight and introduce them to a much broader audience than would have ever independently checked them out based purely on word-of-mouth buzz. The standout titles have truly become pop culture events, making them essential Netflix series to watch before kicking the bucket.

Stranger Things is not only the most culturally relevant and massive show in Netflix’s history in terms of what it did to expand a brand, but also a highly influential achievement that ushered in a new wave of nostalgia for the classic films of the 1980s. While it has been debated whether Stranger Things became too reliant on its own worldbuilding and foreshadowing for its own good, it is hard to deny how exciting it was when it came out of nowhere to be a hit.

Stranger Things will likely be better remembered for its characters than its plot, as the best that can be said about the more divisive final two seasons of the show is that it continued to provide a platform to a cast of young stars who truly grew into their roles as the series progressed and got more mature.

BoJack Horseman is unlike anything that has ever been done before in terms of animation, as it is simultaneously a deeply rich satire of Hollywood, a hilarious adult animated sitcom that offers an endless array of puns, and an existential, harrowing character study about the nature of addiction, loss, sexual exploration, and trauma. That BoJack Horseman is so consistently charming in its quirky approach to worldbuilding was necessary in order to keep its audience engaged, as the series goes to some very dark places that would have otherwise been challenging to reckon with.

BoJack Horseman looked at the messiness of existence in a profound way that showed a remarkable lack of judgment for its characters, and the fact that it was able to air for six seasons and amass a significant following in the process is a minor miracle when considering the subject material.

The Crown is a highly ambitious series that took on the challenging task of exploring the entire history of the modern British Royal Family through the eyes of Queen Elizabeth II, who ascended to the throne after the death of her father, King George VI. Elizabeth’s rule of over 70 years obviously gave the showrunners a ton of material to draw from, especially when considering that she was alive for almost the entirety of its run.

The Crown was able to smartly encompass the enormity of contemporary British history by essentially diving itself into three two-season arcs, each of which swapped out the main cast in order to appropriately fit the age of the characters. The Crown is a highly informative (if not entirely accurate) look at history that offers a comprehensive perspective on the evolution of the monarchy, offering it both praise and criticism.

Squid Game is still the most-viewed show in Netflix’s history in terms of raw viewership numbers, and it is hard to imagine anything catching up to it anytime soon. The series follows hundreds of financially desperate contestants who enter a secret competition built around deadly versions of children's games, with a life-changing cash prize waiting for the last survivor.

A key array of factors helped Squid Game take advantage of a specific cultural moment and register with a substantial number of viewers; it aired towards the first wave of post-COVID reopenings when viewers were still used to binge-watching, resonated with an international audience at a time in which subtitles were becoming more accepted, and tapped into an “eat the rich” sentiment that had grown more prominent both in its production country of South Korea and its primary audience in the United States. Squid Game is a dark show that looks at the manipulation of the media from a cynical outlook, but the nature of the individual challenges is so entertaining that it also succeeds as a captivating thriller.

Black Mirror is the heir apparent to The Twilight Zone as a science fiction anthology show that offered a variety of compelling stories about the near-future, but its perspective was far darker and more psychological. If The Twilight Zone often approached high-concept premises that had either a supernatural tilt or posed some sort of outstanding ethical debate, Black Mirror was a grave warning about the rapid accumulation of technology that also showed the inherent cruelty of mankind.

Black Mirror was the perfect show for a Netflix audience because each episode was standalone (outside of a few crossover events), allowing for certain episodes to rise above the rest and peak in viewership. The best thing about Black Mirror is that there is no strict definition of what it has to be or what rules it needs to abide by, which is why it is still an ongoing series with a ripe future in front of it.

Four Netflix characters, four completely different ways of showing up for somebody. This is the one who fits how you actually do friendship, and what having them in your life would be like.

You do not do friendship at half volume, and neither does he. What you want is somebody who says yes to the plan before you have finished describing the plan.

You are the friend who makes things happen, and so is she. Together you would be mildly exhausting to everyone around you and completely delighted with yourselves.

You have no patience for pleasant lies, and she has no ability to tell them. What looks like coldness from the outside is the most reliable friendship on this list.

You look after whoever is having the worst day. So does he. It is the least strategic way to move through the world and the best possible quality in a friend.

Mindhunter is a two-season masterpiece created by David Fincher, the award-winning master filmmaker responsible for several of the greatest serial killer films ever made. Mindhunter is loosely based on the true story of the FBI Agent John Douglas, the real-life inspiration for the character of Will Graham in the Thomas Harris novels, and follows the FBI specialists Holden Ford (Jonathan Groff) and Bill Tench (Holt McCallany) as they travel the country to interview famous serial killers and help local law enforcement officers solve particularly puzzling cases.

Mindhunter is sharp and stylized, exploring the nature of psychopathy in a way that has never been seen before, and it uses history to create deeply upsetting drama. Although the show was never picked up for a third season, likely because of the high price paid for its precision and attention to detail, it will go down in history as one of the greatest things that Fincher has ever made.

Narcos was one of the first shows that proved that Netflix could produce hits that rivaled what could be seen on traditional networks, as the fact-based cartel thriller offered a riveting encapsulation of the history of the South American drug wars. Although Boyd Holbrook and Pedro Pascal made for a compelling set of protagonists who offered a perspective on what the DEA’s goals were during this period in history, Narcos was anchored by the brilliant performance of Wagner Moura as Pablo Escobar, as he was able to turn one of history’s greatest monsters into a nuanced anti-hero in the vein of Tony Soprano.

Narcos is a fascinating example of a series that was able to evolve and adapt over time, as it turned into a dense political drama in Season 2 before becoming a full-on espionage thriller in Season 3.

The Queen’s Gambit was a ministries phenomenon that became such a cultural event that it sparked a global rising interest in the art of chess, taking advantage of the game’s inherent dramatic power in a way that had never been captured before. Although anyone who is well-versed in chess knows that there is a very complex philosophy involved in strategy, The Queen’s Gambit matched that dynamic subject with a rich historical story about a complex anti-hero trapped in the midst of a historical period of siege and a personal battle with addiction.

Anya Taylor-Joy has already proven to be an exciting young actress based on her performances in Split and The Witch, but The Queen’s Gambit offered her the opportunity to give her best performance to date. It is one of the defining TV characters of the modern era, and one that it's impossible to imagine anyone else doing.

Ripley is hardly the first adaptation of the classic Patricia Highsmith novel The Talented Mr. Ripley, but showrunner Steve Zaillian reinterpreted the story in a fresh new way that stood as the most authentic to the book. Even if Ripley aged up the characters so that they would be older than they were in the novel, its use of stunning black-and-white imagery helped capture the noir spirit of a lonely, moody story about disenfranchisement among American expatriates living in Paris during the ‘60s.

Ripley is authentic to the source material in its decision to turn the titular anti-hero into a true cypher whose emotions are challenging to predict, as Andrew Scott was able to create a threatening, unsettling portrayal that was also strangely charismatic in a very unusual way. Rarely has a television show ever looked and felt so cinematic.

Adolescence is a great example of how Netflix can elevate a smaller-scale show and expose it to a much wider audience, as the series was so impactful that it might have caused change in the real world. The four-part series follows a 13-year-old boy accused of murdering a classmate, tracing the devastating impact of the case on his family while examining the influences that may have shaped his behavior.

Stephen Graham developed a highly emotional, challenging exploration of gender roles and the use of social media among children that led to global conversations about enacting change so that the type of violence that the show depicted could be avoided in the future. Adolescence walks a very tight line of being authentic in its exploration of relevant topics without being exploitative, and it was undoubtedly a positive move for Netflix to release a show that inspired such debate. It is hardly an easy watch, but one that would almost feel necessary in this day and age.
