The 10 Best Fire-Type Pokemon, Ranked

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

From being one of the three different types from a trainer's first starter Pokémon to powerful legendaries that create scorching infernos, Fire-type Pokémon have been a staple of the franchise from the very beginning. While there aren't as many Fire-types compared to the other two starter types, the 103 different Pokémon that feature the Fire type are so rich with charm and blazing power that it's no surprise that the type has been an absolute fan-favorite.

When at their best, Fire-types can feature some of the most exceptional offensive of any Pokémon type, quite literally being unmatched in terms of their firepower with attacks that eviscerate opponents. Combined with the strengths of being more powerful in the Sun and the burn status effect, there are many different ways for a Fire-type to find great success with distinguished strengths throughout Pokémon history. While everyone is going to have their own personal pick for their favorite Fire type, these 10 consistently stand out as some of the best Pokémon that the type has to offer.

One of the three mythical Pokémon from Generation V and one of Pokémon's best balances of overwhelming cute factor with destructive power, Victini has been an icon ever since its debut in Pokémon Black and White. The Victory Pokémon is said to guarantee victory for any trainer who uses it, and even if this isn't the case in terms of gameplay, its overwhelming strengths certainly make the path to victory an easy one for trainers who utilize it.

It's Psychic/Fire type gives it incredible offensive potential, made even more powerful with its powerful exclusive moves, Searing Shot and V-create. Not only does it hit incredibly hard, but with its base 600 stat total, with 100 in each stat, makes it an excellent all-rounder in terms of defense and speed, able to adapt to any type of opponent. The only thing that has held it back is that, by virtue of being a mythical Pokémon, there are very few official ways to receive this powerful and adorable Pokémon.

One of the three legendary birds that make up the very first legendary trio in the series, Moltres was the original go-to choice for a powerful Fire-type Pokémon, with its design largely paralleling and finding inspiration from the iconic phoenix. Even after 30 years of power-creep and additional Fire-types that have taken its mantle, the Pokémon continues to find strength in the modern day, largely utilizing its exceptional defensive ability Flame Body and its highly versatile move pool.

The Pokemon has frequently made appearances throughout the series as a Legendary able to be caught, even outside of its home region of Kanto. It's about as consistently strong as a Fire-type can get, although it got considerably more viable as an option after the addition of the Heavy-Duty Boots item, which stopped it from losing half its health to Stealth Rocks. Moltres even received an exciting new Galarian Form in Pokemon Sword and Shield, complete with the exclusive move Fiery Wrath, but this alternate form sadly isn't a Fire-type.

For a long time in the Pokémon series, the Bug-type was associated with weak, low-power Pokémon that were useful in the early game, but never stayed as a permanent member of the team. One of the biggest Pokémon that helped change this perception was Volcarona, the Bug/Fire-type that, while taking a long time and lots of investment to evolve, is one of the strongest non-Legendary Pokémon of all time. Its level of power is so high that some people are often mistaken and believe it to be a Legendary.

Volcarona's exceptional strength comes from its usage of good speed, high attacking power, and usage of one of the greatest setup moves of all time, Quiver Dance, which makes its already great strengths even better. It also features an incredibly striking and beautiful design, being a beautiful moth creature with fires so powerful that it provided a replacement for the sun by ancient people.

The newest Fire-type legendary that was just recently introduced in Pokémon Scarlet and Violet, Chi-Yu has completely redefined the concept of a strong Fire-Type in the eyes of trainers. This specially offensive member of the treasures of ruin is defined by its powerful ability, Beads of Ruin, which lowers the special defense of everyone else on the field, making its already powerful fire attacks even more powerful. It proved to be so powerful that it became one of the only Pokemon to be directly nerfed in a patch, with its base Special Attack stat being lowered in the Day 1 patch for the game.

Still, with overwhelming power coming from its Overheats and Dark Pulses, Chi-Yu has some of the highest and most immediate damage potential out of any modern Pokémon. It seems a little silly that so much power can come from such a tiny fish, but Chi-Yu is a Pokémon that should not be underestimated, as it will wipe away opponents in a blaze of destructive flames.

Arguably the fan-favorite member of Generation II's iconic trio of legendary beasts that were resurrected after the burning of Brass Tower, Entei has been an icon of blazing power ever since its debut. Even from the early days, Entei was given considerably more attention than the other two members of its trio, most notably getting a prominent leading role in its own movie, Pokemon 3: The Movie. This singular appearance in one of the best Pokemon movies alone has made it a fan-favorite Fire-type, yet its consistent strengths over the years further amplify its icon status.

With high-power moves like Eruption, Sacred Fire, and Extreme Speed at its disposal, Entei is a powerhouse of offense while also having some of the best HP stats of any Fire-type. Entei even managed to get a considerable buff in later generations with its newer hidden ability Inner Focus, making it immune to flinching and incredibly useful in the official VGC format. Entei also directly correlates to its paradox relative, Gouging Fire, the Fire/Dragon type that is seen as an ancient relative of Entei from the far past.

Over 25% of all Fire-Type Pokémon are starters, so it simply makes sense that some of the most memorable and impactful Fire-Types just happen to be the ones that many trainers start off their journey with. This trend continues into the modern age of Pokémon with Cinderace, the soccer player rabbit who fires off burning soccer balls to deal devastating damage to opponents. It's exclusive moves Pyro Ball and Court Change, give it an immediate presence in a battle, not just with high power, but able to completely shift the tides of battle.

A major part of Cinderace being such a powerful starter is its usage of the ability Libero, which parallels the previous Protean ability and allows Cinderace to change its type by using a move, catching the opponent off guard and giving it exceptional offensive and defensive capabilities. It also features a memorable Gigantamax form, where it grows super long ears and balances on top of a giant flaming fireball.

The Fire/Steel legendary Pokémon from Generation IV that has utilized its power over magma and volcanic surroundings to deal powerful damage to opponents, Heatran may not be as immediately iconic as the other Gen IV legendaries, but deservedly earns a reputation as one of its strongest. With its signature move Magma Storm, Heatran deals out loads of damage, not just from the initial hit itself, but from residual damage each turn that keeps the opponent stuck on the field.

Heatran's only major weakness that it has to look out for is its 4x weakness to Ground-type moves, but a well-utilized Air Balloon item and some half-decent defenses can help a skilled trainer navigate its weakness while making its strengths all the more impactful. Heatran also has all sorts of eyes on its potential with its recent new mega evolution, with Mega Heatran making its debut in Pokemon Legends: Z-A, but will finally be able to use for traditional battles in Pokemon Champions in December.

The final evolution of the very first Fire-type starter and the mascot Pokémon for Pokémon Red, Charizard is synonymous with the Fire-type as a whole, for many being the unbreakable symbol for blazing glory in the series. Being one of the fan-favorites of the series, Charizard has received a lot of love in all different corners of the franchise. From being playable in Super Smash Bros. to extremely expensive Pokémon cards to standout appearances in the anime.

However, in terms of the core main series of games, Charizard's greatest strengths arise in the frequent new forms it has received over the years, including two mega evolutions in Mega Charizard Y and Mega Charizard X, as well as a Gigantamax form used by Pokémon Sword and Shield's champion, Leon. Mega Charizard Y especially being able to both set the sun and make exceptional use of its offensive potential makes it one of the strongest Mega Pokémon to date, completely dominating the meta of Pokemon Champions.

The mascot pokemon for Pokémon Gold and its remake Pokémon HeartHold, Ho-Oh's exceptional defenses combined with great offenses and overwhelming stats has continously made it one of the strongest Pokemon of all time. The rainbow bird is a symbol of glory and pure-hearted kindness in the Pokemon world, with its bright shining lights making it a beautiful sight to behold for the trainers who witness it. Ho-Oh also famously had its first appearance in the first episode of the Pokemon anime, with Ash witnessing it flying across the sky, as it was a complete mystery until the Generation II games were released.

In battles, Ho-Oh absolutely lives up to its mythos and popularity, having one of the highest base stat totals of any Fire-type Pokémon, one of the best abilities in the game in Regenerator, and one of only 2 Pokémon with access to Sacred Fire. Ho-Oh's role as a defensive Legendary that can still deal out massive damage has made it a consistent threat ever since its introduction, as well as a continuous fan-favorite all these years later.

While Blaziken may not be as overwhelmingly iconic and recognizable as Charizard in terms of Fire-type starter status, the Fire/Fighting type has arguably reached higher highs in terms of overwhelming strength in-game and an incredibly endearing design. The blazing chicken dedicated to fiery kicks was already a massive icon in its debut game, but everything changed for the Pokemon when it recieved its hidden ability, Speed Boost.

With the ability to increase its speed every turn, Blaziken soon turned into one of the greatest setup sweepers of all time, able to snowball into an unstoppable fiery force that completely destroyed teams. Even more egregiously strong was when Blaziken's star-power and fan-favorite status gave it a super-powerful Mega evolution, one that it received before its fellow Generation III starters and still maintained the Speed Boost ability. With some of the highest power and speed of any Pokémon, fire-type or otherwise, teams unprepared for Blaziken inevitably go up in flames.
