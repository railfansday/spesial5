From many previous detonations, something like 85% of the lead remains in the crater where it was blown up. It's basically lead and nitrogen. The nitrogen and lead rapidly breaking apart will leave about 7000 lbs of elemental lead, and the nitrogen will be gas. The idea is to keep the explosion plume as small and low as possible so as not to distribute all that elemental lead over Iowa. The lead content in the area (Iowa) is about 20 mg/kilo, so there's already quite a bit of lead in the soil. If you raised that over 100 mg/kilo, you might want to start taking that surface soil and decontaminating it. If the plume doesn't reach way up into stronger wind currents, ie hundreds of feet into the air, then the containment is pretty good. The fallout area might not extend much beyond that explosion site. It's an acceptable risk given the dangers of it going off unexpectedly.
