Cboe and Citadel Sue to Stop US Stock Perps Hours After the CFTC Approved Them: Why Hyperliquid’s $5 Billion-a-Day Market Has the Most at Stake

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Cboe and Citadel want US stock perps blocked. Hyperliquid already trades $5B of them a day, and ICE wants to bring it onshore.

A group backed by Cboe and Citadel Securities sued the CFTC on Tuesday, November 3, to block stock perps hours after the regulator cleared them for US exchanges, a fight that could decide whether Hyperliquid’s $5 billion-a-day market reaches American traders. The approval named no offshore venue, but it was aimed at one.

CFTC Chairman Michael Selig said Americans should not be “forced to turn to offshore, and often unregulated, venues” to trade perpetual futures on individual stocks.

The biggest of those venues runs on Hyperliquid. Trade.xyz, its largest builder market, did $4.99 billion in volume over the past 24 hours across 142 contracts on stocks, indexes and commodities, with $4.42 billion in open interest.

Six days ago, NYSE owner ICE agreed to bring those markets to US traders through a CFTC-regulated exchange of its own. HYPE jumped as much as 21% that day, to $97.74, just short of its $97.96 record.

The lawsuit didn’t knock it over. HYPE dipped to $81.01 early Wednesday and was back at $85.93 by 08:00 UTC, up 2.8% in 24 hours.

So what is the lawsuit about, and what does it mean for perp DEXs like Hyperliquid?

Who gets to regulate single-stock perpetuals?  The Capital Markets Alliance, whose members include Cboe, Citadel Securities and the Healthy Markets Association, filed in federal court in Manhattan. It argues the contracts are security-based swaps, which belong to the SEC and are kept away from retail investors.

“This issue, however, is about investor protection for securities trading and the rule of law,” Cboe CEO Craig Donohue said.

The CFTC hit back. “Our markets should be governed by law, not litigation campaigns intended to block innovation,” a spokesperson said.

Four exchanges are waiting on the outcome. Kalshi filed first, on September 18, followed by Coinbase Derivatives, Bitnomial and Crypto.com’s North American Derivatives Exchange. CME Group is already suing the CFTC over Kalshi’s Bitcoin perpetual, approved in May.

Big enough to rival its crypto markets. Hyperliquid’s own crypto perpetuals did $7.14 billion over the same 24 hours, so trade.xyz’s markets made up about 41% of everything traded on the chain.

Indexes and oil lead, but single stocks fill most of the list:

Trade.xyz launched in October 2025 as the first market built on HIP-3, Hyperliquid’s system that lets outside teams list their own contracts. It now handles about 98% of HIP-3 volume and says it has processed $650 billion in total trading.

It is built for traders outside the US. Hyperliquid blocks US users on its own front end, and that is the gap Selig wants US exchanges to fill.

Very little for its markets today, and a lot for its path into the US. The case can’t touch trades on Hyperliquid itself. What it decides is whether American exchanges can compete for the same flow, and whether ICE can bring trade.xyz onshore.

The two outcomes cut in opposite directions:

Neither will happen soon. ICE’s exchange still needs its own CFTC approval, and neither company has given a launch date.

The first real test comes sooner. The court still has to decide whether to freeze the approval while the case runs. A freeze would stall US listings before Kalshi or Coinbase trade a single contract.

HYPE has given back most of last week’s ICE spike. It hit $97.74 on October 29, slid back over the next few days, and now trades at $85.93, just above its 20-day EMA of $84.02.

For now, the fight over stock perps is about who can sell them to Americans. Hyperliquid already sells them to everyone else, $5 billion a day of it.
