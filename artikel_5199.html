Dogecoin wallet 'MyDoge' has rolled out a quick update to address Bitcoin swap and send issues affecting users of its Classic Wallet, ahead of the planned release of version 3.1.2 this weekend.

Alex, CTO of MyDoge Wallet, announced the update, explaining that the team had pushed an interim fix to resolve the reported BTC transaction problems before the broader software release.

Pushed an update to quickly address BTC swap/send issues while using "Classic Wallet" before we release the full v3.1.2 this weekend Simply close / reopen the app and you should see: 3.1.1-652.1 or 3.1.1-652.2 in the "About MyDoge" settings

According to the announcement, users do not need to manually download a separate update. Instead, they can simply close and reopen the MyDoge app to receive the latest version.

Users can verify whether the update has been applied by navigating to the "About MyDoge" section in the app settings. The expected version numbers are 3.1.1-652.1 or 3.1.1-652.2.

In Dogecoin ecosystem news, DogeOS is nearing mainnet launch, bringing an EVM-compatible app layer to DOGE that would let developers build DeFi, games and other apps on Dogecoin for the first time.

In October, DogeOS opened a public test network for Ethereum-style trading, lending and other applications using DOGE, seeking to give Dogecoin holders more ways to use their coins beyond payments and price speculation.

In a recent post on X, Dogecoin Foundation director of product Timothy Stebbing shared a Dogecoin ecosystem 2026 update.

Stebbing predicts that the next three to four years for Dogecoin are likely to be shaped more by the development and adoption of smart-contract application layers than by large, pre-emptive changes to Dogecoin’s base protocol. He cited DogeOS as an early and visible example of that direction. If it and similar efforts succeed in attracting sustained developer activity and real usage while continuing to treat Dogecoin as the settlement and value-transfer foundation, Stebbing sees the ecosystem gaining additional utility without compromising the simplicity and reliability of the Layer 1.

However, the proposed mechanism for native proof verification on Dogecoin still requires community discussion and, ultimately, activation. Thus, adoption will depend on whether the resulting applications are useful enough that people choose to use them. None of these uncertainties change the underlying architectural advantage: the Layer 1 can remain a focused means of exchange while the surrounding ecosystem gains the capacity for richer functionality.
