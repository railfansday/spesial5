‘Long term coup’ ousted Christians, conservatives from major watchdog, insider claims

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Former Southern Poverty Law Center executive Heidi Beirich faces federal charges of wire fraud and money laundering conspiracy. The Department of Justice alleges Beirich hid over $4 million in payments from SPLC donors to fund White supremacy.

A former Southern Poverty Law Center official who led its hate-group tracking work told FBI agents he tried to make the watchdog’s controversial "Hate Map" more ideologically balanced, while saying Christians and conservatives were the first staffers pushed out in what he called a "long-term coup" at the organization after he left in 2013.

The former director said he pushed for a more evenhanded SPLC, arguing that its "Hate Map" — which lists conservative groups alongside Ku Klux Klan chapters — should also include left-wing extremists, including violent animal-rights and extremist environmental groups. However, the former director said the effort met resistance and that, as liberal leaders reshaped the organization, most of his 17-person team was replaced.

"There was some kind of coup, long-term coup going on there between some, what I would — what I heard were some pretty liberal people, extreme people that had other ideas about what we were doing," the unnamed former director told agents, as first reported by The Daily Signal's Tyler O'Neil, who has been covering the SPLC's legal troubles.

The former director’s comments appeared in an FBI interview transcript filed as an exhibit to Dr. Heidi Beirich’s Nov. 2 court filing. Beirich, a former SPLC official charged alongside the organization in a federal case alleging a scheme to mislead donors and banks about donations used to pay informants associated with violent extremist groups, pointed to multiple FBI interviews with an unnamed former SPLC director, identified in the filing as "Individual-1."

In a January interview, Individual-1 told investigators he ran the SPLC’s field-source program, set up accounts used to pay sources and directed payments, according to Beirich’s filing. During a March 23 follow-up interview with Individual-1, FBI Special Agents David McDaniel and Parker Still framed the conversation around the program having "gone off the rails" under Beirich and told the former director they were not there to discuss the bank activity, noting he had "obviously nothing to do with all that," before questioning him about the SPLC’s alleged targeting of conservative organizations and Beirich’s role, according to the transcript.

Individual-1 responded that, as his "gut impression," he believed her actions were political and accused some liberals at the center of having "a good degree of hatred" for conservatism, according to the March 23 transcript.

Individual-1 also alleged a shift in power at the SPLC. After he left in 2013, he said, people were appointed to the SPLC’s board as "figureheads" and eventually gained enough voting power to take over the center. He said only one or two data-entry members of his roughly 17-person team remained, with the others replaced by staff he believed shared the new leadership’s politics.

"That's who they ran off first were the Christians and conservatives," the unnamed former director told agents when asked why Beirich took the actions she did after his departure. "Then they — then the rest they just replaced with people they liked, you know, that agreed with them politically."

In separate comments about the SPLC's controversial "Hate Map," Individual-1 said he persuaded SPLC staff, despite internal resistance, to list Black separatist groups, which he considered comparable to White racist groups, on the map. However, he said he unsuccessfully pushed the SPLC to include violent animal-rights and environmental extremist groups and described his team’s approach during his tenure as director as "very nonpartisan" and "middle of the road."

"We were very nonpartisan, or at least when I was running things, when I was director, we stayed right in the middle of the road," he said.

In a June follow-up, the unnamed former director also criticized the SPLC's "Hate Map" for listing local chapters separately, saying it made the map's totals of the extremist groups it chose to identify look larger.

While the unnamed former director was not charged in the SPLC case, Beirich was. Her lawyers say the interviews with the unnamed director warrant discovery into whether she was singled out because of her perceived role in the SPLC’s political speech, arguing that investigators’ questions about the SPLC’s treatment of conservative groups — and her alleged role in that work — could show the prosecution was motivated by hostility to the center’s political speech.

"Individual-1’s statements confirm my longstanding criticism of the Southern Poverty Law Center, that it exaggerates ‘hate’ by inflating the chapters on its ‘hate map’ and that it systematically demonizes conservatives and Christians while turning a blind eye to extremism on the Left," O’Neil, the author of "Making Hate Pay: The Corruption of the Southern Poverty Law Center," told Fox News Digital. "Now, we have the former director of the Intelligence Project saying the SPLC appears to have pushed out Christians and conservatives, while rejecting his suggestion to include environmentalist agitators on the map."

Fox News Digital reached out to the SPLC and attorneys for Heidi Beirich for comment but did not receive a response in time for publication.
