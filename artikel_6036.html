Allegiant Air Cancels 17 Routes From 3 Busiest Airports: See Full List Now

America's ultra-low-cost carrier Allegiant Air has cut 17 routes from its three busiest airports between 2025 and 2026. This comes as the airline continues to reevaluate underperforming routes and redeploy its fleet of Airbus and Boeing narrowbody aircraft elsewhere.

Allegiant, based in Nevada, is a budget airline that operates more than 120 Airbus A319 and A320 family and Boeing 737 MAX 8-200 aircraft. As expected with a no-frills carrier, airfares are usually relatively cheap, but additional fees are incurred for services including checked baggage, seat selection, and meals onboard.

Orlando Sanford, St. Pete-Clearwater, and Punta Gorda are Allegiant's top three airports; combined, they catered for more than 2,299 total departures in November alone. Given Allegiant's business model, it attracts primarily leisure-focused travelers, which is why its domestic-only network serves a large network of destinations in Florida.

At the beginning of 2025, Allegiant decided to end a number of underperforming routes. Data from Cirium, an online aviation analytics company, showed that in March, two routes were terminated; in April, five services were shelved; and in May, a single service to Greensboro was taken offline.

These changes cut specific routes to destinations in Texas, Georgia, Minnesota, Virginia, North Dakota, and North Carolina.

In August 2025, Allegiant undertook another network restructuring, exiting a number of high-cost, underperforming routes. This saw the limited aircraft in its fleet being reassigned to other, more profitable leisure corridors. This included the airline's complete withdrawal from Norfolk.

Its decision to back out of Norfolk came as the airline cited rising per-passenger airport fees, which fundamentally affect how a budget airline operates. Allegiant's withdrawal from Norfolk was also influenced by the rapid expansion of competitors Breeze Airways and Southwest Airlines.

Allegiant removed its Huntington service because it was hyper-seasonal and, following persistent fleet constraints, the airline trimmed routes that could not meet strict profit and load factor targets.

Allegiant has dropped 20 Florida routes, but surprising passenger data reveals many were performing better than expected before cancellation.

This year, Allegiant has shelved six routes as the airline looks to expand operations elsewhere (such as Fort Lauderdale). This restructuring was intended to free up aircraft so the airline could boost its services in Fort Lauderdale by 33%.

As a result, low load factors led to a number of market exits between April and May, with Columbia being dropped just one year after it was launched. New Orleans also had shocking load factorsof around 44%, according to Aviation Shop, prompting the airline to withdraw from the route after just six months of service.
