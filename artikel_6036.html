There is a lot to love about JetBlue. The airline offers generous legroom, tasty snacks and free Wi-Fi. It also has a fairly straightforward loyalty program.

JetBlue TrueBlue points are easy to earn through flights, partner activity, cobranded credit cards and transferable rewards. They are also easy to redeem because any seat on a JetBlue-operated flight can be booked with points, and there are no blackout dates. The program has become more useful through an expanding network of airline partners, including the Blue Sky partnership with United Airlines.

Here is everything you need to know about the JetBlue TrueBlue loyalty program.

JetBlue serves more than 100 destinations throughout the U.S., Latin America, Canada, the Caribbean and Europe. The airline isn’t part of any global alliance but has a growing roster of airline partners.

With JetBlue being one of the youngest major airlines in the U.S., the TrueBlue loyalty program is a relative newcomer to the points and miles world. The program is revenue-based, so earning and redeeming points are typically tied to ticket prices. However, there are also many ways to earn JetBlue points without even getting on an airplane.

TrueBlue points do not expire. Points Pooling also lets between two and seven friends or family members combine their balances, although members must contribute all of their points to the pool while participating.

Blue Sky lets TrueBlue members earn points and Tiles on eligible United-operated flights booked through JetBlue and redeem TrueBlue points for United flights through JetBlue. Mosaic members also receive reciprocal perks on United, including priority boarding, one complimentary checked bag, preferred seating and Economy Plus seats at check-in. Base fares receive the above benefits except seat selection, and the partnership does not provide reciprocal lounge access.

Government-imposed taxes, government-imposed fees and travel insurance do not earn Tiles. Tiles are a status metric and cannot be redeemed for travel.

This means you could earn elite status entirely through credit card spending, through qualifying travel spending or with a combination of the two.

Before reaching Mosaic 1, members can choose a different Perks You Pick benefit at 10, 20, 30 and 40 Tiles. Through Jan. 31, 2027, the choices include early boarding, priority security, a complimentary first inflight alcoholic drink, double points per dollar spent on a JetBlue Vacations package or 5,000 bonus points.

You can pick each of these only once, even if you earn all four Tile amounts.

Mosaic members also choose one Perks You Pick benefit at each status level. Current choices include a pet fee waiver, IHG One Rewards Platinum Elite status, Mint Suite selection priority, FoundersCard Blue membership, a $99 statement credit for an eligible JetBlue credit card, a 20-Tile bonus for the member or another TrueBlue member and 15,000 bonus points.

Changes are coming in 2027: Beginning Feb. 1, members will receive a new tier-specific Perks You Pick menu at each Mosaic milestone, may select the same perk again at a later milestone and will have 60 days to make each choice. A pet fee waiver will become an automatic Mosaic 2 benefit, while eligible JetBlue Premier Card members will gain additional card-exclusive choices.

There are many ways to save up for a JetBlue award ticket.

When flying JetBlue, you’ll earn TrueBlue points based on the ticket cost, the fare type and whether you book directly with JetBlue.

JetBlue now sells Main, EvenMore, BlueFirst and Mint experiences, with Base and Flex variants available in most cabins. Base fares earn 1 base point per dollar spent, plus 1 point per dollar when booked on jetblue.com or through the JetBlue app. Standard and Flex fares earn 3 base points per dollar spent, plus 3 points per dollar when booked directly.

You can earn TrueBlue points on eligible flights operated by United Airlines, Condor, Etihad Airways, Icelandair, Qatar Airways, Singapore Airlines and South African Airways.

United is different from JetBlue's other partners. TrueBlue members earn 5 points per dollar spent on eligible United-operated flights booked through JetBlue; government-imposed taxes, fees and surcharges are excluded. Eligible United flights credited to TrueBlue also earn Tiles. For most other partners, earning depends on the operating airline, the booking class and the distance flown.

You can find each partner’s earning chart in JetBlue’s partner directory.

JetBlue uses a dynamic pricing scheme for its own flights, so redemptions are pretty straightforward. You can redeem points for any seat on any JetBlue-operated flight across its entire route network. The more a flight costs in cash, the more it will cost in points.

According to TPG’s November 2026 valuations, TrueBlue points are worth 1.35 cents each on average. To calculate the value of a specific award, subtract any taxes and fees from the cash price, divide the result by the number of points required and multiply by 100. Mint and Cash + Points bookings may offer less value, while some partner awards can offer more.

To check award availability, search for flights on JetBlue’s website as you normally would and check the “Use TrueBlue points” option at the top of the search box. Base fares can now be booked with points, although JetBlue charges $150 per person to change or cancel a Base award.

Cash + Points bookings start at 500 points and are available for JetBlue-operated flights and eligible JetBlue Vacations packages. This option can be useful if you are short on points, but it often delivers less value than a standard award. Cash + Points bookings are not available for partner airline awards.

TrueBlue points can also cover certain seat assignments, checked bags, pet fees and priority security. You can redeem points through TrueBlue Travel for eligible hotel stays and car rentals as well.

In addition to using your points for JetBlue flights, you can redeem TrueBlue points on JetBlue's site for eligible flights operated by United Airlines, Condor, Etihad Airways, Icelandair, Qatar Airways and China Airlines. Cape Air redemptions are temporarily paused.

Partner award availability can be limited, and each one-way award must be operated by a single carrier. Frustratingly, you cannot combine a JetBlue flight and a partner flight on the same one-way award, although you can book the outbound and return on different airlines.

A Points Pooling account can have two to seven members and must include at least one person age 21 or older. Members contribute 100% of the points they earn after joining. The pool leader and anyone granted redemption privileges can use pooled points without the other members' permission, so only pool with people you trust. A member who leaves takes their remaining points balance with them.

While you’re unlikely to get incredible value from your points, the JetBlue TrueBlue program is still useful for many travelers. TrueBlue points are among the easiest domestic airline points to earn and redeem. JetBlue-operated awards do not require an award chart and have no blackout dates, though partner award availability can be restricted.

JetBlue's expanded partner network makes TrueBlue more globally useful. New transfer partnerships with Rove and Bilt also make it easier to top up a balance. JetBlue's revenue-based pricing usually limits the opportunity for exceptional value on its own flights, but straightforward redemptions and occasional high-value partner awards make TrueBlue worth understanding.
