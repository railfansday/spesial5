Fitch affirms Finance of America’s CCC rating as key risks, leverage persist

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Fitch Ratings affirmed Finance of America Companies' long-term issuer default rating at CCC with a positive outlook, citing improved liquidity and better underlying profitability as the company addresses near-term debt maturities. The agency maintained Finance of America Funding’s senior secured rating at CCC- with an RR5 recovery rating. Despite FOA’s leading position in reverse mortgages, Fitch highlighted weak liquidity, very high leverage of 33.7x, a largely secured and short-term funding structure, and earnings pressure from interest rate volatility.   AI Summary

Finance of America Companies’ long-term credit ratings remain deep in speculative-grade territory, even as the company reduces near-term risk and improves underlying profitability, according to a review published Monday by Fitch Ratings.

Fitch this week affirmed the long-term issuer default ratings of Finance of America Companies and its subsidiaries, Finance of America Equity Capital LLC and Finance of America Funding LLC, with a CCC rating and a positive outlook. Fitch also affirmed Finance of America Funding’s senior secured debt rating at CCC- with a recovery rating of RR5.

Fitch defines CCC ratings as having a very low margin for safety, with default considered a real possibility. But the ratings agency also explained that it maintains a positive outlook for Finance of America (FOA) due to its “established market position within the reverse mortgage sector,” along with experience senior management and support from Brian Libman, who chairs its board of directors.

The action is part of Fitch’s periodic peer review of nine publicly rated nonbank mortgage companies. Like other lenders, FOA has been contending with volatile interest rates, high leverage and constrained liquidity as the broader mortgage market remains cyclical and capital intensive.

Earlier this month, FOA reported a net loss of $110 million in the third quarter of 2026 as noncash fair value adjustments offset growth in its home equity lending business. The company reported funded loan volume of $748 million from July through September, up 24% from the same period in 2025, while its year-to-date volume of $2.07 billion was up 17% year over year.

The company also said during its Q3 earnings call that it expects to complete a $60 million paydown of senior secured notes in November. In June 2024, FOA announced a restructuring of unsecured debt into secure notes while extending the original 2025 maturity date. That debt was restructured into two tranches, with $200 million in secured first-lien notes due in 2026 (along with an option to extend to 2027) and $150 million in exchangeable senior first-lien notes due in 2029.

In its report, Fitch said the positive outlook reflects FOA’s work over the past year to address material near-term obligations and its improved profitability when excluding fair value marks.

The agency indicated it could raise FOA’s issuer default rating to B- if the company builds a “substantial” liquidity cushion beyond remaining repayment obligations and continues to de-lever through retained earnings.

The ratings remain constrained by relatively weak liquidity and high leverage, a short-term and largely secured funding profile, and elevated key person risk tied to Libman. Additionally, the review factored in the highly cyclical nature of mortgage originations, as well as the volatility tied to mortgage servicing rights valuations and securitization residuals.

The announcement illustrates that while FOA has taken steps to stabilize its balance sheet, its capital structure and earnings volatility still present elevated credit risk for mortgage and capital markets participants — including warehouse lenders and secondary market investors.

Fitch noted that its positive outlook could be revised to stable, or the CCC rating could be cut, if Finance of America fails to bolster its liquidity before upcoming maturities.

Other factors that could drive a downgrade include deteriorating profitability and sustained leverage above 15s. The ratings agency said that FOA’s ratio of total debt to tangible equity — excluding obligations tied to HECM Mortgage-Backed Securities (HMBS) and nonrecourse debt — was 33.7x at the end of September, up from 14.0x at the end of 2025. Fitch said it believes leverage will stay sensitive to interest rate movements.

In December 2025, FOA issued $50 million of preferred stock to funds managed by Blue Owl Capital. Fitch treats these preferred shares as debt, since change-of-control provisions mandate redemption of the preferred shares in cash. But the agency said it does not believe the preferred shares will be a permanent part of FOA’s capital structure as scheduled increases in coupons, starting with a 300-bps increase at the seventh anniversary date, provide an incentive to redeem and create an effective maturity.

“We think it speaks a lot to the interest in the reverse segment, the fact that they’re willing to make an equity commitment to us and acquire the product that we’re generating,” FOA CEO Graham Fleming told HousingWire‘s Reverse Mortgage Daily earlier this year about the transaction.

Fitch also noted potential upsides for FOA’s ratings. These include sustained improvement in liquidity after near-term obligations are repaid, return on average assets being maintained above 1% with positive cash generation, and gross leverage staying below 10x.

The agency also cited better funding flexibility through long-term and more committed facilities, growth that strengthens the FOA franchise brand, and deeper management expertise and clear succession planning as potential reasons to bolster the company’s ratings down the road.
