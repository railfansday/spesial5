8 real estate tech updates show where AI and payments are headed

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Real estate tech firms rolled out AI, listing integrations, and digital payments updates. Highlights include Zillow Preview listings on RealScout, Earnnest Cash to Close nationwide, and Lofty citing 1.13 million tasks and 131,248 hours saved.  AI Summary

New product launches and partnerships this month highlight how real estate technology companies are using AI, payments innovation and listing integrations to streamline transactions and give agents more measurable value from their tools.

RealScout has turned on Zillow Preview listings inside its collaborative home search platform, giving agents and their buyers earlier visibility into homes before they become active on the MLS.

First announced in September, the integration is now live for RealScout’s network of more than 100,000 real estate professionals across the U.S., according to the company announcement.

Zillow Preview lets sellers market their homes on Zillow, Trulia and Realtor.com before they go active on the MLS, while allowing agents to comply with local MLS rules. Participating brokerages can also choose to syndicate those Preview listings to RealScout, increasing exposure to buyers who are searching with their own agents.

Why it matters for housing professionals: As pocket listings, coming-soon inventory and off-MLS marketing continue to draw scrutiny from regulators and MLSs, structured preview programs and controlled distribution channels give listing brokers a way to build early demand while keeping agents and buyers inside a compliant workflow.

BrokerBot secured new capital, including a strategic investment from the California Association of Realtors (CAR) and follow-on funding from Grand Ventures and other investors, and launched BrokerBot 2.0, its upgraded AI platform for brokerages and associations.

The announcement comes shortly after BrokerBot won the $15,000 grand prize at NAR’s Tech & Innovation Pitch Battle at NAR NXT.

BrokerBot 2.0 positions itself as an “AI brain” for a brokerage or association, connecting multiple large language models to the organization’s documents, systems and communication channels. The company says the platform is SOC 2 compliant and E&O insured and that no model is trained on a client’s proprietary data.

CAR President Mark Peterson said the association’s investment is intended to help ensure that AI tools for Realtors are “practical, secure and focused on delivering greater value to their clients.”

Why it matters for housing professionals: As brokerages experiment with AI across marketing, transaction coordination and compliance, questions about data security and governance are becoming central to vendor selection. Backing from a large state association and an emphasis on SOC 2 controls could influence how firms evaluate AI platforms in 2027 budget cycles.

Zillow rolled out four new tools designed to answer what it calls the four core questions of moving: how much a buyer can afford, where to live, which home to choose and which agent to hire.

Why it matters for housing professionals: More granular affordability, neighborhood and agent-matching tools on consumer platforms can shape lead quality and expectations before buyers ever talk to a lender or agent. Understanding how these features work can help professionals respond to better-informed — and sometimes more opinionated — clients.

Lofty released what it calls the industry’s first AI index for real estate, a benchmark intended to quantify how many tasks and hours its AI tools are saving agents across the country.

According to the company’s November announcement, Lofty AI completed 1.13 million tasks in October 2026, saving real estate agents an estimated 131,248 hours by handling follow-ups, social media posts, homeowner check-ins and CRM updates. The Lofty AI Index breaks down results by task type, product, team or brokerage size and geography. More detail is available at the company’s website.

The launch comes as 81% of agents cite “saving time” as the primary reason for adopting new technology, according to NAR’s 2026 Realtors Technology Report, but many still struggle to measure impact.

Why it matters for housing professionals: Brokerages and teams under margin pressure are looking for hard numbers on productivity gains from AI and automation. If widely adopted, indices like Lofty’s could influence tech stack decisions, staffing models and how vendors are held accountable for ROI.

Constellation Data Labs announced an expanded strategic partnership with Canva to support the underlying real estate data infrastructure for Canva’s enterprise real estate offering in the U.S. and Canada.

The collaboration is designed to remove the friction of licensing and normalizing MLS data so brokerages and agents can create compliant, on-brand marketing materials inside Canva using live listing feeds.

Constellation Data Labs will manage the data pipeline, MLS licensing workflow and compliance monitoring on behalf of Canva and its real estate clients. Once a brokerage is onboarded, agents can access their listing data directly within Canva to build dynamic marketing assets more quickly.

Why it matters for housing professionals: As listing marketing becomes more visual and omnichannel, agents need faster ways to turn accurate listing data into compliant campaigns. Offloading MLS data complexity to a specialist while using a familiar design platform could reduce turnaround times for print, social and digital assets.

Earnnest rolled out its Cash to Close solution nationwide, extending its digital payments platform beyond earnest money deposits to help homebuyers fund closings electronically instead of using wires or cashier’s checks.

The company began piloting Cash to Close with select settlement companies in July and says it has successfully supported transactions across five states. With the national launch, settlement companies across the country can now use the tool.

Earnnest’s expansion builds on its experience processing more than 1 million escrow payments without a single instance of fraud or a chargeback against an escrow holder, according to the announcement.

Why it matters for housing professionals: Wire fraud remains one of the biggest operational risks in residential real estate. Moving more closing funds into controlled digital workflows could change how brokerages, title companies and buyers handle payments, insurance requirements and consumer education at the end of the transaction.

Real Geeks unveiled AI Connect, a new feature that lets real estate agents use popular AI tools to redesign and optimize their IDX websites using natural language instructions.

The Dallas-based company says AI Connect is a first-of-its-kind capability in the real estate IDX website space. Agents can work with tools such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok to create or update neighborhood pages, adjust site design, generate SEO-focused content and tailor pages for emerging AI search experiences — all through conversational prompts.

The goal is to help agents compete more effectively for leads in both traditional search engines and AI-powered search interfaces without needing deep technical or design skills.

Why it matters for housing professionals: As search behavior shifts toward AI assistants and vertical search tools, IDX sites that can quickly ship structured, localized content may have an advantage in visibility and lead capture. Features that turn AI prompts into site changes could influence how teams manage their web presence and content strategy.

Inform, a deep-tech spatial AI company, launched today with technology that turns ordinary video of a property into a trusted 3D building record. Every year, millions of U.S. properties are measured, photographed and described by hand, often more than once, by appraisers, inspectors and insurance adjusters who each start from scratch. Instead, from one video, Inform rebuilds a building in 3D, works out its rooms, measurements and features, and keeps a record of exactly what the camera saw, so every fact about a property can be traced back to evidence.
