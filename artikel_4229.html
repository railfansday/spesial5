10 Game Boy Metroidvanias You Can Finish in Under 10 Hours

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Shane is a Contributor for DualShockers writing guides and other materials. He has a degree in Game Design and Criticism.

For a long time, Metroidvanias have been a bit of an oddball when compared to some other games. They’re still great games, sometimes the best titles of a console generation, but back in the day they were more niche.

Very few games ever even attempted to push the envelope of Metroidvanias aside from those that coined the term, Metroid and Castlevania. One of the biggest draws of these games is that you could complete them pretty quickly, especially when it came to the handheld consoles.

Discover 10 Game Boy Advance Metroidvanias that respect your time, from Metroid Fusion to Castlevania: Harmony of Dissonance.

The Game Boy, Game Boy Color, and Game Boy Advance could see players complete the game in one long car ride, provided it wasn’t too dark outside just yet. This list encompasses the shortest Metroidvanias of the broader Game Boy generation that you could finish in under ten hours.

Starting off with a strange one that fits a bit more into a Metroidvania-like category, Wario Land 3 did incorporate a lot of aspects of Metroidvanias. Playing as the treasure-obsessed Wario, players would navigate various levels attempting to find all the treasure chests available.

In true Metroidvania fashion, players could return to older levels of the game with new abilities to uncover other secrets they couldn’t get to before. The game did manage to make a name for itself thanks to some innovative gameplay elements and its relatively quick run-through.

Now the Castlevania series is absolutely a true Metroidvania, and one of the more difficult by many people's standards. Playing as Nathan Graves, players will enter Dracula’s Castle in an attempt to rescue their mentor.

While not part of the main continuity of Castlevania, the game gained a following thanks to the difficulty and use of the Dual Set-Up System. This system gave players the ability to create new combinations of attacks via a card-collecting system, for interesting results.

Many of the best Castlevania games incorporate some sort of gimmick to make them a bit more interesting, and Castlevania: Harmony of Dissonance is no exception. Fifty years before Simon Belmont took on Dracula, there was Juste Belmont and their journey through Dracula’s Castle.

The main feature of the game was the two versions of Dracula’s Castle that saw players moving between the two to solve mysteries and unlock paths intertwined between them. That’s probably the reason the game is on the longer side compared to most of the other Metroidvanias of the time.

In a surprising twist, this Castlevania game is actually set in the future, in 2035. It also abandoned the Belmonts for a brand new protagonist, Soma Cruz, who would discover his strange ability after being drawn to the castle during an eclipse.

In keeping with the lore of the game, the major system here was called the Tactical Soul System, and it would allow Soma to gain new abilities based on the soul he collected. This drove the main idea behind the Metroidvania aspects of the game and granted some freedom in character builds that players enjoyed.

Whether you’ve heard of the series or seen something from it, almost everyone can recognize Shantae for one reason or another. The series launched in 2002 with some great Metroidvania elements that gained a large following, making it fairly popular.

The game introduced players to the half-genie protagonist Shantae as she did battle against her arch-nemesis Risky Boots. The key feature of the game was that Shantae could dance to transform herself into animals to perform different tasks and movement challenges, accessing other areas.

The first Metroid game on the list, but not the first to pioneer the series, Metroid Fusion was the fourth game in the series. It brought a different kind of focus to the series, with the game sticking more to narrative and suspense than previous installments.

This proved to be a great idea as the game revolved around Samus Aran and the infamous SA-X, a duplicate made with an infected suit. It brought an air of suspense to the game that stuck with players despite how short the game was.

Unlike some of the other Metroid games, Metroid: Zero Mission sought to update the original game for a more modern era. Zero Mission introduced a lot of quality-of-life features to make the age of the original game a bit more manageable.

The game took pieces from previous titles that it had learned from, incorporating more story and expanded content. While still short, Metroid: Zero Mission managed to set a new path for the Metroid series as a whole, and is still seen as one of its highlights.

The second game in the series, Metroid II: Return of Samus, is one of the most iconic of all. This title introduced a few of the most memorable features that would become staples of the series, like the Spider Ball.

Not only did the game introduce some new mechanics, but it also focused on a darker world and story for the Metroidvania. The game clearly helped to build a dedicated fanbase despite its shortness, as its influences can be seen even in the newer Metroid releases today.

A Metroidvania before the genre was even established, TMNT III: Radical Rescue was arguably one of the first few Metroidvanias. It featured a story focused on rescuing the turtles from the claws of Shredder in a beat-em-up exploration-focused game.

Each of the four turtles had a special use that would unlock more of the map for you to explore, effectively making the game a great Metroidvania-style game. There’s certainly an argument to be made that it would go on to inspire features in the genre.

Regardless of any of that, the game is the shortest Metroidvania from the Game Boy era, clocking in at around two hours total. While perhaps not the most important Metroidvania over the years, it certainly has its place in history and our hearts. What a little curio.

Metroidvanias can take a long time to get through, but these ones deliver all the punch of a long game in a package that fits your life.
