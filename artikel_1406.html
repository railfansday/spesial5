Hadean mantle depletion and 3.95-Gyr subduction implied by North China rocks

Nature (2026) Cite this article

The scarcity of terrestrial rocks older than 3.9 billion years (Gyr) has hindered our understanding of Earth’s early evolution, fuelling persistent debates on how and when the first continental crust formed and plate tectonics started. At present, the only unequivocally documented >3.9-Gyr-old rocks are from the Acasta Gneiss Complex, Canada1,2,3. Here we report a 3.95-Gyr-old trondhjemitic gneiss from eastern Hebei, North China Craton, in which primary magmatic zircon records elevated δ18O values (average +6.77 ± 0.23‰) and positive εHf(t) values (weighted mean +1.45 ± 0.27). This suggests a low-temperature altered basaltic source that originated from a mantle depleted in the Hadean. In contrast to the low-pressure origin inferred for the roughly 4.0-Gyr-old Idiwhaa unit in Acasta2, the 3.95-Gyr-old trondhjemitic gneiss reported here exhibits geochemical signatures attributed to a high-pressure origin4. Thermodynamic and trace element modelling demonstrate that its parental magma formed by means of low-degree (8–16%) melting of basaltic crust at >1.6 GPa and 740–820 °C, suggesting rapid burial of (near-)surface-altered oceanic crust to mantle depths along a cool thermal gradient. We propose that this rapid burial was most likely achieved through a transient subduction event.

Comparison shows that plagioclase accumulation and hornblende fractional crystallization can explain the positive Eu and Sr anomalies in our samples (a,b) but they fail to simultaneously reproduce the high (La/Yb)N and (Gd/Yb)N ratios and the high SiO2 contents observed in the rocks (c,d). The positive εHf(t) values of magmatic zircons from the 3.95-Gyr-old trondhjemitic gneiss indicate that its basaltic precursor was young juvenile crust derived from a DMD. Even if the basaltic precursor (assuming 176Lu/177Hf = 0.022) was derived from a 4.5-Gyr-old DMD with a similar 176Lu/177Hf ratio (0.0384) to modern depleted mantle, its extraction time from the mantle could not be earlier than 4.0 Ga (solid blue line). If the 3.95-Gyr-old trondhjemitic gneiss was formed by partial melting of basalt immediately extracted from a DMD with similar 176Lu/177Hf ratio, then the timing of mantle depletion can be estimated to be about 4.35 Ga (dashed black line). Earlier depletion of the mantle is required if partial melting of a basaltic precursor occurred some time after its extraction from a DMD (dashed blue line) or the degree of depletion in the mantle is less than modern depleted mantle (dashed red line). If the basaltic precursor was derived from a possible ultra-depleted mantle proposed by some researchers100, the depletion can be slightly delayed. Nevertheless, the depletion still needs to be no later than 4.2 Ga even if the 176Lu/177Hf of the ultra-depleted mantle is as high as 0.041 (solid purple line). Therefore, a Hadean DMD is necessary to yield the Hf isotope composition of the 3.95-Gyr-old trondhjemitic gneiss.
