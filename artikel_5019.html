Ionic Digital (IOND) Sells 737 Bitcoin to Fund Data Center Buildout

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Ionic Digital (IOND) sold about 737 bitcoin after the end of September for roughly $61.6 million and plans to spend the money building out its data center sites, the miner built from Celsius’ old mining business disclosed in its latest quarterly report. The company said in its 10-Q that the coins sold at an average of $83,600 each, net of trading costs. The sales cut its bitcoin holdings by about 25% from the Sept. 30 balance.

Ionic held 2,954 bitcoin at the end of the third quarter, up from 2,719 at the end of 2025. That implies about 2,217 bitcoin left after the sales. “The Company intends to use the proceeds to fund the development of its existing data center sites,” the filing said.

The filing does not say when the coins were sold. Bitcoin traded near $64,000 on Wednesday morning, about 23% below Ionic’s average sale price.

Ionic was formed in January 2024 to take over the assets of Celsius Mining under the bankrupt lender’s Chapter 11 plan. It started out as a pure bitcoin miner, running fleets of ASIC machines at its own and hosted sites. That changed in October 2025, when it signed a lease with a single tenant for its Ward County, Texas, facility, which it previously called Cedarvale. The lease runs through January 2037.

The third quarter shows how far the shift has gone, with revenue rising to about $49.0 million from $37.1 million a year earlier. Digital infrastructure leasing brought in $43.9 million of that, while mining revenue fell to $5.1 million from $37.1 million.

Ionic swung to a net profit of $17.4 million for the quarter, compared with a $4.5 million loss a year earlier. That result included a $73.2 million gain on the fair value of its bitcoin, offset in part by a $65.9 million loss from remeasuring warrants. For the first nine months of the year, Ionic posted a net loss of $30.9 million.

In February, Ionic amended the Ward County lease. The tenant is now obligated to lease another 89 megawatts of capacity when it becomes available. Control of that added capacity had not passed to the tenant by the end of September, so Ionic has not booked any revenue on it. The company also had about $19.6 million left to pay on transformer purchases and a substation expansion at the site.

The conversion has cost Ionic mining capacity. The company said it does not have room at its owned and leased sites to relocate all of the miners it used to run, so it has decommissioned some of them. Ionic went public through a direct listing on July 28 and raised $400 million in a private placement before $16.7 million in costs. It had about $381.4 million in cash and equivalents at Sept. 30.

Ionic is one of several public miners turning bitcoin into cash for AI and data center projects this month. MARA sold 13,597 bitcoin for $1.1 billion and said more sales were coming. Core Scientific plans to exit bitcoin mining by year-end, and Hyperscale Data sold its last bitcoin to fund an AI data center.

Ionic’s sale is smaller, but the share is large. A quarter of its treasury is now going into the data center buildout. The company still mines. In July, it moved its hashrate from Foundry to Luxor’s mining pool, and mining added about $5.1 million worth of bitcoin to its holdings in the third quarter.

The test now is whether the 89 megawatts come online fast enough to justify selling coins that would fetch about $14 million less at Wednesday’s price.
