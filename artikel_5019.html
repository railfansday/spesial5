Ansem claims wallet hack after an 87 million ANSEM token dump, but on-chain data raises questions

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Ansem claims his Solana wallet was hacked after 87.49 million ANSEM tokens were sold, sending the memecoin tumbling 57%.

Crypto trader Ansem said his Pump wallet was hacked on Tuesday, roughly 51 minutes after a Solana wallet labeled “ansemconzimp” on Pump.fun finished selling about 87.49 million ANSEM, according to a Coindaro tally.

In his post, Ansem said he suspected someone had accessed his Bullpen profile, a trading app where he said he had imported the wallet’s Solana private key. In a later reply, Ansem denied making the trades and said he was conducting a security audit.

Ansem also said he believed the activity was limited to Solana. He said Bullpen was the only place he had imported his private key and that he didn't think anyone had accessed his Ethereum-compatible Pump wallets.

Solscan’s wallet page lists the Pump.fun name “ansemconzimp” and identifies the wallet as the owner of the token account for The Black Bull, the token that trades as ANSEM.

Coindaro counted 25 ANSEM sales from 08:57 to 10:08 UTC on Nov. 24, with about 21,520 SOL received. It reported that the wallet began the period with about 87.48 million ANSEM and received a few thousand more while selling was underway, accounting for the slightly higher total sold.

Solscan showed about 85 ANSEM in the token account later that afternoon.

A transaction tally and a public statement track two moments in the Nov. 24 sell-off.

The public records leave who authorized the sales unresolved.

Bitquery also tracked a wider burst of Solana memecoin sales by the wallet that morning, measuring a 57% price drop in under an hour, from $0.0367 to $0.0158.

An Arbitrum address highlighted by blockchain tracker Lookonchain held about 2.57 million USDC at 15:34 UTC, and Bitquery traced transfers through the Relay bridge, finding that some of the stablecoin reached the address before the first ANSEM sale.

Lookonchain also flagged the sales at 09:39 UTC and asked whether the wallet had been hacked. At 09:55 UTC, it said the activity looked like a hack and pointed to an address on Arbitrum as the destination for funds.

In an afternoon update, it characterized about $2.57 million as stolen.

On-chain researcher MLM questioned the hack account, saying that an older wallet he had labeled as Ansem’s sent funds to the eventual recipient before the selling began. Bitquery also traced early payments from an older Solana address and said it could not identify its owner.
