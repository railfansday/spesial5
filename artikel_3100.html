Dell Just Reported Earnings and Raised Its Forecast by $10 Billion. Is the Stock a Buy After Tuesday's Selloff?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Dell shares fell 12% on Tuesday after a rival warned about AI-server profits. Hours later, Dell reported higher margins.

By Jonathan Ponciano – Nov 24, 2026 at 4:54PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Dell Technologies ›

Dell Technologies (DELL -11.83%) shares rose about 3% to $305 in after-hours trading Tuesday after the company reported that quarterly revenue more than doubled and raised its full-year forecast. The stock had closed at $295.28 in the regular session, down 12% from Monday's $334.91, as AI hardware shares sold off.

Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE -16.31%) led that decline. Its shares fell about 16% after executives said on Monday's earnings call that memory and other components could stay scarce through at least the second half of 2028. HPE also guided for its cloud and AI business to keep about 14% of sales as operating profit in fiscal 2027, down from 18.4% in the quarter it just reported. Dell, which sells many of the same servers, fell with it.

Dell's earnings release for the quarter ended Oct. 30 showed revenue of $54.9 billion, up 103% from a year earlier. And adjusted earnings rose 265% to $9.45 per share, well above the $6.59 analysts expected.

Most of the growth came from the Infrastructure Solutions Group, which sells servers, networking, and storage. Its revenue tripled to $42.0 billion. AI-optimized server sales rose 291% to $22.0 billion, and traditional servers and networking rose 235% to $15.0 billion. "We are now a different company," Vice Chairman and Chief Operating Officer Jeff Clarke said in the release.

Dell booked $60.2 billion of AI orders in the quarter and ended it with a record AI backlog of $132.3 billion, about two-thirds of the revenue it expects for the whole year.

Gross margin was 21% of revenue, up from 20.7% a year earlier. Helping to drive that increase, the infrastructure business kept 17% of its sales as operating profit, compared with 12.4% a year ago, and its operating income quadrupled to $7.1 billion.

Lower spending also helped. Operating expenses fell to 7.5% of revenue from 12.9%, and Chief Financial Officer David Kennedy said that is driving "higher profitability, even as AI servers become a larger part of our business." AI servers tend to carry thinner margins than storage, so a rising AI share usually pulls margins down, but this quarter it clearly didn't.

On the other hand, PCs were the weak spot. The Client Solutions Group grew revenue by 3% to $12.9 billion, consumer sales fell by 15%, and the segment's operating income slipped by 6% to $704 million.

Dell raised its full-year revenue forecast by $10 billion to $202 billion, up 78% from last year, and its AI server sales forecast to $78 billion from $74 billion. It now expects adjusted earnings of $29.68 per share, up from $25.50. For the fourth quarter, it guided revenue to $56.3 billion and adjusted earnings to $8.40 per share.

Separately, the company returned a record $4.9 billion to shareholders through buybacks and dividends in the quarter, and its board approved an additional $25 billion in repurchases. Free cash flow rose to $3.5 billion from $506 million a year earlier.

Inventories rose to $29.4 billion on Oct. 30 from $10.4 billion at the end of January, and total debt rose to $37.7 billion from $31.5 billion. If component costs keep climbing, as HPE expects, Dell may need to keep raising prices, and one quarter of higher margins doesn't show it can do that indefinitely.

The stock is also volatile. It rose about 20% on Monday after Nvidia (NVDA -5.86%) reinstated CEO Jensen Huang, then gave back most of that gain on Tuesday.

Even after Tuesday's after-hours gain, the stock trades at about 10 times the adjusted earnings Dell expects for the fiscal year ending in January. That's a low multiple for a company whose revenue just doubled and whose backlog covers much of next year. And while the stock fell on worries about a rival's margins, Dell's own margins rose.

The case depends on AI spending holding up, the same bet behind most AI stocks. Investors who already own plenty of semiconductor stocks and other AI infrastructure names should know Dell adds to that exposure rather than diversifying it. Those who want to see whether component costs hit margins can wait for next quarter's report.
