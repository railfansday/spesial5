The inception, no pun intended, of Christopher Nolan's Oppenheimer is quite the legendary tale at this point. This is probably because it involves the very buzzy and prolific actor Robert Pattinson planting the idea in Nolan’s head, despite having nothing to do with the movie. The director, characteristically, had a very matter-of-fact version of the story that people love. However, the way Robert Downey Jr. tells it makes it even better.

In an interview with Tara Hitchcock, Christopher Nolan talked about how he initially had an interest in J. Robert Oppenheimer and his role in American history. He specifically noted that a speech by Oppenheimer was actually used as a catalyst for Tenet and the way they wanted to tell that story. He said:

I wrote a thing about this incredible moment that Oppenheimer and the scientists of the Manhattan Project had, where they could not completely eliminate the possibility that when they triggered that first gadget – that first atomic device – they might start a chain reaction that would destroy the world. And we use that as a metaphor for Tenet, which Rob was in.

The Batman star recognized this interest, and by the end of the production, Pattinson decided to give Nolan a book of Oppenheimer’s speeches as a wrap gift on Tenet. Clearly, this interest in the speeches and the story had legs, as it helped inspire one of the best movies in the last decade and even led to a Best Picture win. I guess you can say that Robert Pattinson is really great at gifts.

In true Robert Downey Jr. fashion, the way he tells this story has its own special kind of energy. During his Actors on Actors interview with Mark Ruffalo, Downey told the tale like a fable, showing this gesture as a kindred energy between two creative men. He told Ruffalo (via Variety):

Pattinson gives him unwrapped Oppenheimer's letters—these moments in life where you realize the inception of something occurred in the most organic way. And also, Pattinson in Tenet was an incredible departure for him. So this incredible departure from one of our younger peers turns into him making a personal gift to arguably one of the greatest directors of—of his generation, turns into him deciding that it's sticking with him.

The way Downey tells it, it was a kinship moment. Robert Pattinson was seeing his career turn with Tenet, making him a more serious, sought-out character actor. And this made way for Nolan’s own transition, as he pivoted from tales based in sci-fi and action to one entrenched in the inner world of one of our most fabled historic figures. It was a world-stopping moment for two of our great creatives that went on to start a new and important chapter in both of their careers. It’s a beautiful way to put it, and it speaks to the storyteller that Downey is.

Pattinson may not have been in Oppenheimer and opted to do both Mickey 17 and The Batman instead; however, his impact on the Oscar-winning film is obvious. Clearly there was a connection with these two creatives, as Downey puts it, that made their separate careers flourish and then come together again for The Odyssey this year. It’s a great pairing, and I hope this partnership continues for years to come.

You can revisit Oppenheimer now, as it is currently streaming with a Peacock subscription. Christopher Nolan fans can also see him reunite with Robert Pattinson in The Odyssey, which is currently playing in select theaters around the country. It is one of the best films on the 2026 movie schedule, so make sure to check it out while you still can.
