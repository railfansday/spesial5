Birmingham City have a number nine problem to solve when August Priske returns from injury: Opinion

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Birmingham City have plenty of reasons to be optimistic about their Championship promotion push, but Jay Stansfield’s recent performances could prove particularly significant. The 23-year-old scored twice against Swansea City, including an 87th-minute winner, to send BCFC top of the table.

He also passed 50 goals for the club before his 24th birthday, underlining his importance since arriving as their record signing. However, it is not just his finishing that makes Stansfield so valuable.

His recent return to the central striker position suggests Chris Davies may have found the best way to get the most out of his €18m-rated forward. However, it may cause an issue with top-scorer August Priske returning from injury.

Across 15 starts and 1,275 Championship minutes this season, Stansfield has registered four goals and one assist, averaging a 7.26 FotMob rating. His 42 shots, 20 efforts on target and 4.88 expected goals demonstrate a consistent appetite for getting into dangerous positions.

Stansfield has frequently operated in attacking midfield during his Birmingham career, but his historical numbers make a compelling argument for keeping him further forward. In 71 appearances as a striker, he has scored 29 goals. By comparison, 46 outings as an attacking midfielder have produced just 12 goals.

That difference is particularly relevant with Priske recently absent. Stansfield has stepped into the central role and responded with three goals across the Millwall and Swansea fixtures, despite accumulating just 1.09 xG in those matches. Against Swansea alone, he registered seven shots and earned a 9.1 FotMob rating.

Crucially, playing centrally does not mean sacrificing his work rate. Stansfield has recorded 39 defensive contributions, including 22 tackles, eight interceptions and 65 recoveries this season.

He is not a conventional target man, winning just 28.6% of his aerial duels, but his mobility, pressing and ability to link play make him a different kind of centre-forward. FotMob’s positional data also highlights why moving him deeper is not necessarily beneficial.

His shot volume and defensive contributions are strengths, whereas his relatively low touches suggest he is not someone who needs to dominate possession to influence matches.

Birmingham invested heavily in Stansfield because of his goalscoring ability, and his record already justifies that faith. With a contract until 2031, there is little reason to reinvent his role.

Priske’s eventual return could give Davies another attacking option, but Stansfield should remain the first-choice striker. The recent evidence suggests Birmingham have a better chance of maximising their record signing when he is leading the line rather than supporting someone else.
