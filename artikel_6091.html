3 Women Stranded By FAA Nationwide Ground Stop Bought A $48,000 Ford Bronco—Then Drove 27 Hours Home

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Three women stranded in Charlotte after Friday’s nationwide flight grounding bought a $48,000 Ford Bronco and drove 27 hours home to North Dakota. They’d gone away for work training. They came home with a new car. Tara Harding (I first read this as Tonya Harding) and two employees needed to get back to their families in Bismarck, and had patients to see Monday morning. Tara Harding (I first read this as Tonya Harding) and two employees needed to get back to their families in Bismarck, and had patients to see Monday morning.

Tara Harding and her employees were at the airport trying to get home from a work trip when a nationwide ground stop left them stranded in Charlotte, North Carolina — roughly 1,600 miles from home in Bismarck, North Dakota.

She found a $48,000 solution in the form of a new Ford Bronco. https://t.co/xpP9fmCsm8

They couldn’t find a rental car within four hours of Charlotte. They tried other ways home, including a train, bus and U-Haul. None worked. Delta Air Lines left them stranded. Harding’s mother-in-law suggested buying a car.

They bought the Bronco with no money down. Harding initially planned to sell it to a dealership once they reached home. The women took turns driving and sleeping.

While flights resumed at 8:15 p.m. Eastern on Friday, over 18,000 trips were cancelled and there simply weren’t seats to accommodate all passengers on later flights and it took several days to clear everyone out.

There’s a calculation in buying the car, deducting depreciation, taxes and fees, financing costs and operating expenses, and selling it on the other end. There’s a very close precedent. During Southwest Airlines’ Christmas 2022 meltdown, a couple bought a $500 used car to get home, and Southwest reimbursed them. This incident wasn’t any airline’s fault. In the end, Harding abandoned the plan to resell the car.
