SkyW4lker (SkyW4lker x365 Sentinel) is an active device-code phishing kit targeting Microsoft 365 users in the United States, Europe, and other regions. By abusing Microsoft’s legitimate authentication process, the kit can give attackers access to corporate accounts without stealing passwords or bypassing MFA. This puts business emails, sensitive documents, and other Microsoft 365 resources at risk of unauthorized access.

The ANY.RUN team identified 618 sandbox analyses related to SkyW4lker between June and October 21, 2026, with 46% of submissions coming from the United States. Activity increased from 20 analyses in June to 200 in September, highlighting the growing exposure of organizations relying on Microsoft 365.

You can examine the full SkyW4lker attack chain in this ANY.RUN Sandbox analysis.

The attacker sends the victim a phishing email with a password-protected attachment. Typical subjects involve commercial offers, contracts, invoices, or confidential documents. The attachment is a PDF, DOCX, or XLSX file, and the password is provided in the email body.

The document contains a link to a CAPTCHA page. Some variants include an additional lure page hosted on a legitimate service such as Miro, Google Sites, or Zoom, which redirects the victim to the next step.

Finally, the victim is directed to a phishing page that instructs them to enter a device code on Microsoft’s legitimate authentication page, potentially granting the attacker access to their Microsoft 365 account.

The phishing page relies on obfuscated JavaScript functions and separate script files. Using ANY.RUN’s In-Browser Data Inspection, analysts can examine the page’s DOM and HTTP activity to understand how these scripts operate.

The scripts keep the page in regular contact with the PHP backend and determine the location of the victim from their IP address. Within the page DOM, the main script loads additional modules, sets click handlers on buttons and builds GET requests to the PHP endpoint /session_public.php with the action parameter (start or poll).

With action=start, the script sends the geolocation and IP address of the victim and receives a session ID. After that, it sends a request with action=poll every few seconds to check whether the victim has authenticated.

SkyW4lker demonstrates why organizations need to look beyond traditional phishing prevention and MFA when evaluating identity security. Three priorities deserve attention:

Close authentication policy gaps. Review where device-code authentication is genuinely required. Restrict or block unnecessary use through Microsoft Entra Conditional Access to reduce opportunities for attackers to abuse legitimate authentication flows.

Strengthen detection across email and identity systems. Ensure the SOC can connect suspicious attachments, phishing infrastructure, and unusual authentication events. Better visibility across these stages helps teams identify potential compromises before attackers expand their access.

Reduce investigation time and escalation costs. Equip Tier 1 analysts with historical threat context and clear escalation criteria. Establish procedures for containing compromised accounts and reviewing unauthorized activity, helping Tier 2 teams focus on incidents requiring deeper investigation.
