I let AI agents build and run my T-shirt store from Slack in just 24 hours

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Most "AI agent" demos are one chatbot in one window. For the WeMakeDevs Flip the Switch hackathon I wanted to see what happens when you treat agents like a company instead: separate departments, separate permissions, people and agents in the same Slack channels, and work handed from one to another.

The result is Switch Gear, a fictional T-shirt brand from Bangalore with exactly one human: me. Everything else is an AI agent working in Slack through Switch. And the agents didn't just run the store. Most of the store was built by agents too, in a Slack channel, while I reviewed and merged.

It's a demo store: payments, printing, couriers and most orders are simulated. The agents, the Slack channels and the code are real.

Switch is what turns a pile of agents into an org. It runs the agents on my laptop (Claude Code and Codex sessions in the background), bridges them into Slack, and handles who can talk to whom. In practice:

I set most of this up by chatting with Switch's built-in assistant: "create a researcher agent in this folder", "add a second builder on Codex", "run the template".

The part I didn't expect to work as well as it did: the #engineering channel.

Using two different AI models for author and reviewer was deliberate, and it paid off. Reviewer caught real bugs that tests didn't:

In about 30 hours, 17 tasks went through that loop and were merged, from the monorepo skeleton to payments, print and shipping simulators, the sg CLI and the Gatekeeper agent.

One builder is a bottleneck. With the deadline closing in, I added a second builder on Codex in its own worktree, with its own database and ports, so two lanes could run in parallel: the Switch side on one, the store side on the other.

Tell agents exactly which task to take. Both builders' rules said "pick the lowest open task". With two of them, that's a race. Naming the task in every prompt fixed it.

Long conversations burn usage. I hit my Claude limit mid-task. What helped most:

Put decisions in files, not chat. A reset wipes chat memory. docs/decisions.md doesn't. Every judgement call (GST basis, money in integer paise, "anything waiting on a human gets a real-time minimum") lives there.

Demo clocks need a human floor. At demo speed one business day is ten minutes, so "cancel unpaid orders after 30 minutes" gave a real person about 12 seconds to pay. Now it's 30 business minutes or 5 real minutes, whichever is longer.

Make the PR mark the task done. My first rule was "the founder marks a task done after merging". I forgot every time, and the builder refused to start dependent tasks. Now each PR sets its own task to done, and merging makes it true.

Live: the store and checkout, mock payments, print and shipping simulators, approval gates, the sg CLI, all nine AI agents in their Slack channels, and the org template.

Built but not wired up yet: Gatekeeper, a small custom Switch agent (no AI) that streams every store event into #events, pings the right department, and accepts approve APR-7 only from my Slack account.

Next: the double-entry ledger, GST invoices, refunds and reports, so finance has real books to close.
