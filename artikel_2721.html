Diving deeper into Cavs 122-108 loss to Bulls: Cleveland’s defense finds a new low

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The Cleveland Cavaliers can't get out of their own way. A poor defensive effort against a bad Chicago Bulls team that was on the second end of a back-to-back led to a frustrating 122-108 loss.

It's easy to criticize coaches for their rotations and closing lineups. I try my best to keep from doing so for a simple reason: coaches know much more about their team and players than we on the outside ever will. But sometimes coaches can overthink simple things.

Atkinson did that midway through the fourth quarter. The Cavs went into the final frame trailing by just four, but a slow start put the deficit at 12 with just over six minutes left. Atkinson panicked. He opted for an ultra-small lineup featuring Mitchell, James Harden, Tyrese Proctor, Sam Merrill, and Peyton Watson to try to kickstart the offense. Yet, it didn't do so. Cleveland scored just four points in the nearly three-and-a-half minutes this group was on the floor. This allowed the deficit to go from 11 to 14, which all but ended the game.

There's numerous reasons teams don't play with groupings that small. The defensive limitations are obvious. Lineups with no real size inside often struggle to defend the paint and rebound, although that wasn't necessarily this group's issue. Their problems came on the other end.

Size is helpful offensively as well. Being able to create space with on- and off-ball screens, along with the threat of rim pressure, keeps an offense flowing. There's more to spacing than just having shooters. Spacing comes down to having players in positions where they can score, while also forcing the defense to bend with your moments. If an offense gets too one-dimensional or lacks some of the skills traditional bigs provide, then the offense can get stagnant, just like it can when there's too many non-shooters.

Here, the Cavs offense ground to a halt. Harden tried to set high on-ball screens, but because there was no rim pressure associated with it, all this caused was a simple switch. Merrill found himself caught in the dunker's spot and in the paint a few times, but wasn't a threat to score from there. In short, there's only so much room on the perimeter for four shooters to stand. This is how you end up with four points and one field goal against the 27th-ranked Bulls defense in the most critical moments of the game.

"Just effort. I think a lot of it comes down to how we start the games and the approach we take to the games. A lot of those mistakes that we made early defensively just have to do with our effort. That's the number one thing we have to address."

The second quarter was a microcosm of what has been wrong with the Cavs all season. The Bulls scored 45 points in that quarter alone, with a majority of those coming from the bench unit of one of the worst teams in the league. However, you don't need to be the 2016 Golden State Warriors to light up this defense. You just need one person who can dribble the ball inside the three-point arc, and the entire defense collapses.

It's impossible to have a good defense if you can't contain the ball. The Cavs can't do that right now. They constantly find themselves in rotation due to the help defense needing to step up. They compound this by not being quick with rotations. The guy beaten off the dribble doesn't rotate over to where the help defense just came from, leaving the backside defense at a disadvantage. If you have a team that's willing to make the extra pass like Chicago was, you'll probably end up with open shots like the Bulls had repeatedly.

Being without Evan Mobley has obviously hurt this defense. His presence alone would go a long way in making this unit more competent, but he can't fix everything.

The Cavs defense is broken. Their system doesn't work, they are weak at the point of attack, and the effort isn't there to make up for it. There aren't many reasons at this point to believe that they can be passable on that end — they're now 21st in defensive rating — let alone good enough to be actual title contenders.

Cleveland came into the season with the goal of being a top five defense. We're still early into the year, but we can safely say that won't happen.

Consistency has been an issue through their first 18 games. That will continue to be a problem unless the defense can turn things around. You can't rely on your offense to outscore your opponent every night, even against a bad team like Chicago.
