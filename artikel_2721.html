Fantasy Football: Christian McCaffrey turns back the clock in Week 10, while Jordan Love gets Flores'ed

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

I start collecting notes on the games soon after kickoff. And I'm not just jotting down what happened, but an angle on the action. For instance, while the Jacksonville "Trevor Washingtons" were playing well in the first half, the running game was stuck in neutral. However, we (I, mostly) forget how quickly things can change. Sunday wasn't just about early-game narratives flipping, but even perspectives from the previous week getting challenged.

Anyway, let's go through some of the top storylines from Week 10 and what they could mean for fantasy.

It was just seven days ago when I was heaping praise on Jordan Love and the Packers' passing attack. The whole operation, minus the occasional drop from Matthew Golden, looked easy. DC Brian Flores reminded Green Bay that defenses can be more complicated from time to time.

The Vikings defense reset Love's single-game, career-high sack total from five to eight. He now has three games in the season with double-digit hits from the opposing defense. Minnesota owns two of them. However, the problem wasn't just Love's health being at risk. And it wasn't that he fell below 250 passing yards for the first time since early October, either. Flores had Love playing a different style of football.

Passing aDOT: 8.6 (Week 10), 10.0 (Last Six Games)

Middle-of-the-Field Throw Rate: 61.5%, 46.6%

Coming into Week 10, Love had the fourth-most yards on throws to the perimeter. He tacked on just 45 against the Vikings. He was fifth in EPA per dropback on throws of 15+ air yards. One, after averaging 9.6 attempts per game of similar depths in Weeks 1-9, Love just threw four similar passes in the divisional rematch. And two, the majority of the completions didn't come until the second half. The one positive was that Love kept the same trust in his WRs as he had in weeks past (complimentary). But, as I mentioned for Golden, his play remained unchanged (derogatory).

To be clear, I'm not knocking Golden for the drop. Love came right back to him on the next drive for the Packers' first explosive play of the game. However, in a game where the margins were already thin, adding to his league-high drop total only helps the people who roster him. Because yet again, Golden (31.0% target rate, 22.0% air yard share) and Christian Watson (17.2%, 22.0%) dominated the opportunities. And not much changed for Tucker Kraft. The Packers used two or more TEs on 61.1% of their offensive plays (season-high), but Kraft's utilization (78.4% route rate, 13.8% target share) didn't dip. Playing the Vikings was just a complete 180-degree shift from their previous opponents. Luckily, they've got the bye week to recalibrate before facing the Rams in Week 12.

I'll answer my own question here. I'd say James Cook III is properly rated. Actually, we answered this question back in August. Players don't just randomly wind up with a single-digit ADP. That's especially the case when their QB turns into a vulture at the goal line. So, it shouldn't be too surprising to see Dalvin's younger brother put on a show.

Honestly, it's better seeing Cook back in this condition compared to last week. And sticking with Buffalo's bout with Minnesota, Cook is the lesser-discussed reason for the Bills coming out on top. Jahmyr Gibbs is the only other RB to face the Vikings this season and churn out more than 100 yards on the ground. And those gains didn't just help the people rostering Cook. In the second half, with a stronger lean on the run (PROE down to -10.3%), his early down runs set up Allen with more manageable passing situations (average 7.2 yards to the sticks). That's where his complementary play to Allen's becomes critical to Buffalo's success. The numbers bear out the same story.

Rushing Success Rate: 47.1%, 10th (out of 18 qualifiers – min. 50% rushing share)

Adj. Yards After Contact per Att.: 3.95, 7th

On a per-touch basis, few have done it better than Cook. Of the 18 RBs with more than half of their team's carries, Cook has seen the highest rate of eight or more defenders in the box (54.5%). And yet, he's one of the five rushers in that same category with a positive EPA per tote. Plus, with Allen leading his offense, even fewer have access to the same number of opportunities. Only Javonte Williams has played more snaps from inside the five-yard line. Of course, Cooks' problem is that less than half of those plays have turned into actual touches (15 of 32).

Cook has been what we wanted Chase Brown to be. Cook can grind out short yards or break off an explosive play like any of his peers. But being a caddy to one of the league's best QBs has its weekly drawbacks. You'll get outings where Allen will have the only rushing scores. But with someone like Cook on your roster, you'll take the highs and lows.

If you played against Christian McCaffrey today, just close your league app.

Hey, sometimes these things happen. In hindsight, with the 49ers going up against Dallas' defense, the chance for a knockout performance was always possible. But seeing it in real time was one of the best performances of 2026.

If there was any credit to give to Dallas' defense, they were at least able to keep McCaffrey bottled up as a rusher. Through two quarters, his eight attempts resulted in just 21 yards on a 12.5% success rate. The same was the case for Kaelon Black. His lone carry picked up four yards. As a rushing unit, the 49ers had just one first down from non-Brock Purdy runners.

I know this section is about McCaffrey, but Black plowing through Caleb Downs for the TD deserved its own highlight. Regardless, rushing success rates for both RBs were up to 40+% to close out the game. But McCaffrey's real damage was done as a receiver.

I realize per-route metrics need more than 17 routes to stabilize. But the video-game results are too gaudy to let slide. Purdy buying time with his legs and pulling multiple defenders forward is the type of offensive harmony you don't see too often. Every target to McCaffrey added 4.2 EPA. Half of his catches turned into explosives.

Unfortunately, the 49ers go from an easier defensive matchup to the Vikings and Seahawks in back-to-back weeks. But with McCaffrey this effective as a receiver, his floor should be worth a start every week.

I can see both sides of the "letting a QB sit behind a veteran" debate. Not everybody's ready to play at the NFL level. Besides, Las Vegas' coaching overhaul was in its infancy, and Kirk Cousins was the ideal operator for HC Klint Kubiak's under-center, play-action offense. But the Raiders planned to start Fernando Mendoza soon anyway. I would've opted for his debut to come against a different opponent, and the results were about as I expected.

Vegas' opening script went as well as Kubiak designed it. The offense marched right down the field, with Mendoza capping off the drive with the same draw play that helped him earn a title. But a few things to consider before adding him to your list of viable streamers. First, the drive started on Seattle's side of the field after Drew Lock fumbled. Second, of the 11-play series, Mendoza threw the ball three times. No pass went farther than four air yards. Most importantly, Brock Bowers was on the field for just two snaps on that drive. And without Mendoza's "WR1," much of the play-calling tendencies that kept the offense on schedule for Cousins went out the window.

The Raiders ran 11-personnel (three-receiver sets) on 65.7% of Mendoza's dropbacks. For reference, their previous high was 58.1% back in Week 2. But here's where playing against Seattle's defense in your first start amplifies the issue. Ten of Mendoza's 19 pressures came in 2.5 seconds or less. And with just one pass-catcher above the 50th percentile in creating separation, Mendoza's average time to throw continued to tick up. Seattle pressured him on 48.7% of his dropbacks, and he did complete over half of his throws under duress. But the result was just two first downs.

We don't know the severity of Bowers' injury yet. And both of the Raiders' opponents before their bye have top-five marks in pressure rate. As a result, it might be toward the end of the season before we see the best of Mendoza and the Raiders.
