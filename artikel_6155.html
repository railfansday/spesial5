The is the premium card for Alaska Airlines and Hawaiian Airlines loyalists, but look beneath the surface and you'll find a trove of rewards for Oneworld and other flyers, too.

Take it from me: I live in the southeastern U.S. (outside Alaska and Hawaiian’s core markets), but I highly value Atmos Rewards points because I can use them to book tickets on American Airlines, the carrier I fly most often, for excellent value. Of course, the Atmos Rewards Summit is a stellar option for Alaska Airlines and Hawaiian Airlines flyers and those based in the Pacific Northwest as well.

That broad appeal helped the Atmos Summit win TPG's 2026 award for Best New Personal Credit Card. Card rating*: ⭐⭐⭐⭐

*Card rating is based on the opinion of TPG's editors and is not influenced by the card issuer.

The is a cobranded airline card for Atmos Rewards, the joint loyalty program for Alaska Airlines and Hawaiian Airlines. The card earns Atmos Rewards points, which TPG's November 2026 valuations place at 1.6 cents apiece — one of the highest airline currency valuations.

Its headline perk is an annual 25,000-point Global Companion Award, but its suite of benefits also includes a free first checked bag and preferred boarding (when you purchase your Alaska or Hawaiian flight with your card).

Cardholders also have a faster path to Atmos Rewards elite status, as they earn 1 status point for every $2 spent (with no spending cap) and receive an anniversary status points boost.

One of my favorite card perks is the Atmos Rewards Summit's standout earning rates, which are especially impressive compared to other airline cobranded cards. Cardholders earn 3 points per dollar spent on all foreign transactions, an unusual (and potentially quite rewarding) bonus category.

One of the best ways to redeem Atmos Rewards points is for flights on Alaska, Hawaiian, Oneworld and other partner airlines.

Additionally, cardholders can share points with up to 10 other Atmos Rewards members at no additional charge.

The Atmos Rewards Summit provides an annual 25,000-point Global Companion Award that offers a discount on a second award ticket on Alaska, Hawaiian or a partner airline in any cabin class. This perk is worth $400 per TPG's valuations.

High-spenders who put $60,000 or more on their Atmos Rewards Summit every anniversary year can earn a second 100,000-point Global Companion Award, which TPG values at $1,600. If you already spend a lot on your credit cards, it may be worth re-routing those purchases to your Atmos Summit card.

Related: Atmos Rewards elite status: What it is and how to earn it

Cardholders receive two Alaska Lounge passes each quarter. These passes can be shared with friends and family.

You must be traveling on a same-day ticketed flight on Alaska, Hawaiian, Oneworld or a partner airline to receive access with your lounge pass. A standard day pass costs $70 per person.

While these perks certainly help offset the Atmos Rewards Summit's $395 annual fee, this card doesn't offer a full Alaska Lounge membership or the broad collection of lifestyle credits found on some competing premium cards. This could be a downside for cardholders looking for more comprehensive benefits.

However, if you redeem the full 25,000-point Global Companion Award at TPG’s valuation, its estimated $400 value slightly exceeds the card’s $395 annual fee.

Cardholders who have an eligible Bank of America account also earn a 10% rewards bonus on all points earned from card purchases.

The foreign transaction category stands out at 3 points per dollar spent, or an estimated 4.8% return on purchases abroad (5.3% with the Bank of America bonus). The earning rate for Alaska Airlines and Hawaiian Airlines purchases is standard for an airline cobranded card.

You'll generally get the most value by using your Atmos points for award flights.

TPG staffer Gabrielle Bernardini recently used her points to snag American Airlines flights from Florida to Philadelphia. With holiday fares being so expensive, redeeming 7,500 points saved her about $200 per person one-way. Plus, since she has the Atmos Summit Card, the $20 per-person partner booking fee was waived.

If an international flight in luxury is more your speed, you can fly from the West Coast to Tokyo in Japan Airlines' business class or to Taipei, Taiwan, in Starlux Airlines' ultramodern business class from 75,000 points. Atmos Rewards allows you to book stopovers and/or multicity award itineraries at no additional cost, so you can combine destinations in one trip.

If you're interested in an Atmos Rewards card but don't want to pay a $395 annual fee for premium perks, I recommend considering the instead.

The Atmos Rewards Ascent charges a $95 annual fee and offers perks similar to those of the Atmos Rewards Summit when flying on Alaska or Hawaiian, including free checked bags and preferred boarding.

Atmos Rewards Ascent cardholders can earn an annual $99 Companion Fare (plus taxes and fees from $23) upon each account anniversary after spending $6,000 or more within the prior anniversary year.

The Atmos Rewards Summit doesn't include this perk, and the two variations aren't direct substitutes. The Ascent’s benefit lets you add a companion to an eligible paid Main Cabin itinerary within North America for $99 (plus taxes and fees), while the Summit’s award discounts award travel to any destination in any cabin by up to 25,000 points.

To learn more, read our showdown of the Atmos Rewards Ascent versus the Atmos Rewards Summit.

The currently offers new cardholders the chance to earn 80,000 bonus points and a 25,000-point Global Companion Award after spending $4,000 on purchases in the first 90 days from account opening.

Per TPG's valuations, the points and the Global Companion Award included in this bonus are worth $1,680.

We've seen this identical offer come around a few times in the Atmos Rewards Summit's offer history. It's neither the highest or lowest offer the card has, so you'll need to determine if it lines up with when you can meet the spending requirements and have a use in mind for the bonus points and Companion Award.

Since Bank of America issues the Atmos Rewards Summit, you'll most likely be subject to the 2/3/4 rule. This means you can only get approved for two new Bank of America cards in a 30-day period, three cards in 12 months and four cards in two years.

In addition, datapoints suggest Bank of America is inquiry-sensitive, so it may be worth waiting several months from your last hard inquiry before applying. TPG staffers have also found that holding Bank of America checking or savings accounts may increase your odds of approval.
