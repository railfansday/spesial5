’The Mummy’s’ Best Line Is Still the Greatest Quote in Franchise History

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The Mummy movies are a celebrated blend of action, adventure, and comedy that still remain popular — and immensely quotable —decades after the first film’s 1999 release. There are many memorable lines that fans of the trilogy will quote to each other, from the swashbuckling Rick O’Connell (Brendan Fraser) declaring: “I only gamble with my life, never my money,” or Dr. Bey (Erick Avari) despairing: “When Rameses destroyed Syria, that was an accident. You are a catastrophe!” However, one quote from the original movie stands out as the greatest in the whole series. Spoken by Evelyn Carnahan (Rachel Weisz), it defines exactly why, with the fourth movie on the horizon, we hold the world of these films so close to our hearts.

For the uninitiated, 1999’s The Mummy follows Evelyn, a librarian and aspiring Egyptologist who, along with her brother Jonathan (John Hannah), free adventurer Rick O’Connell from prison so that he can help them find a mythical location, where they accidentally awaken the cursed high priest Imhotep. Along with a series of delightful action sequences, the film focuses on the clash of personalities between bookworm Evelyn and cavalier treasure hunter Rick.

It’s this difference in worldviews that leads to the film’s best line. In a drunken moment beside a campfire, Evelyn declares: “I may not be an explorer, or an adventurer, or a treasure-seeker, or a gunfighter, Mr. O’Connell, but I am proud of what I am.” Amused, Rick asks: “And what is that?”, before Evelyn looks to the middle distance, smiles, and replies: “I... am a librarian.” It’s a moment of comedy, but one that also sums up the charming relationship at the center of this film and the 2001 sequel, The Mummy Returns. Weisz was unavailable for the 2008 third movie, The Mummy: The Tomb of the Dragon Emperor, replaced by Maria Bello, but her return for the fourth film, currently in production, was celebrated by fans precisely because of the dynamic that this speech encapsulates.

The Mummy movies have broadly the same appeal as the Indiana Jones franchise, with exotic locations, ancient mysteries, and colorful characters populating a rich and entertaining world. While they may attract similar audiences, The Mummy differentiated itself from the classic Steven Spielberg franchise by making two characters the focus, rather than a single adventurer with a secondary character who serves as a potential love interest. The Mummy doesn’t work without Evelyn and Rick bantering, as well as using their very different skills in order to achieve their goals. Evelyn’s speech, while funny, highlights how crucial that dynamic is to the story.

Rick is strong, courageous, and charismatic, getting the group out of more than one dangerous moment over the course of the movies. However, it takes more than his heroism to get the job done. Evelyn’s brains, instincts, and curiosity are vital to their discovering hidden wonders, making her as important to the movie as her future husband. She is not a sidekick, or a damsel in distress, but an equal partner in an on-screen coupling that made Hollywood magic. There’s no Rick without Evelyn, giving her every right to cherish her love of knowledge.

The long-awaited sequel is set for a 2028 release.

The fourth movie in the franchise, The Mummy: Book of the Dead, is currently filming for a planned 2027 release. The first images of Weisz in costume as Evelyn recently emerged online, with fans delighted to see her stylish return to the beloved role. The popularity of the leak stems from fans who grew up with the movies acknowledging her as a vital part of the franchise, someone that audiences are keen to see as an older woman, a mother, still tearing across the world in search of adventure.

Many movies have had long-awaited sequels in recent years – Gladiator and Beetlejuice both got follow-ups in 2024, while fellow globe-trotter Indiana Jones came back for one last mission in 2023’s The Dial of Destiny. By the time the fourth Mummy film arrives, it will have been 17 years since the last time we saw an entry in the saga, and 26 since we saw Weisz play Evelyn. So why are fans so excited to see her back? Because, in all that time, her wit and fearlessness have been replayed on home video and streaming services for an entire generation. She is a character that endured because of her nerdiness, not in spite of it. We love this librarian and celebrate her return.

The Mummy has been adapted twice since this action-adventure trilogy was released: in 2017 as a Tom Cruise blockbuster, and in 2026 with Lee Cronin’s The Mummy. Both were poorly received, both critically and by theater audiences, and perhaps the reason for that is the affection with which Evelyn and Rick are held in the hearts of movie fans. Maybe you don’t need elevated scares or plane crash sequences to make a hit. Perhaps you just need a librarian.
