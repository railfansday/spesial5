Child, 12, Discovers Mother and Her Boyfriend Dead in Apparent Murder-Suicide

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

A family tragedy occurred in San Antonio, Texas, when a 12-year-old child discovered that their mother and her boyfriend had died in an apparent murder-suicide that took place while several other adults and children slept inside the home.

Bexar County Sheriff’s deputies were called to a home around 3 a.m. on Wednesday, November 25, after they received reports of a shooting. Upon their arrival, authorities found a woman in her mid-30s and a man in his 40s dead upstairs.

A preliminary investigation was launched into the incident, and Bexar County Sheriff Javier Salazar said during a press conference that officials believe the woman shot her boyfriend before she died by suicide. However, Salazar stressed that those findings are preliminary as the investigation remains ongoing.

Salazar said that the couple worked at the same business. They apparently stopped to pick up fast food after they left work, and the shooting took place shortly after they returned home.

Investigators believe the couple got into an argument shortly after they went upstairs, and the conflict quickly escalated.

“That food is still sitting there untouched,” Salazar said. “Whatever argument happened, it seems that it happened … within very quick succession and escalated very quickly.”

Authorities said that six adults and seven children, who ranged between 1 to 12 years old, were inside the home at the time of the murder-suicide. Salazar also noted that the home was cluttered, and clothing and miscellaneous items were scattered throughout the household.

Most of the people in the home were believed to be asleep when the argument broke out. Some of the people reported that they heard loud noises coming from upstairs, though they did not initially realize the sounds were gunshots.

The woman’s 12-year-old child went upstairs to see what had happened, which was when he discovered the bodies. Salazar added that two children allegedly witnessed the aftermath of the shooting, noting that they have been “traumatized” by the experience.

In addition to the children, Salazar said that some of the people staying in the home were visitors.

Salazar added that there was no known history of domestic violence between the couple, though they were known to have arguments.

Following the tragedy, Salazar urged anyone experiencing domestic violence to seek help.

“This is not a crime that goes away on its own. It doesn’t just stop,” Salazar said. “Unfortunately, even more frequently, especially toward the holidays, we will see things end up like this if something drastic doesn’t change within that relationship.”

If you or someone you know is struggling or in crisis, help is available. Call or text 988 or chat at 988lifeline.org.

If you or someone you know is experiencing domestic violence, please call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233 for confidential support.
