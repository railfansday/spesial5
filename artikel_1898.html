The Uruguayan media were impressed with Liverpool man Ronald Araujo after his display in Friday's 2-1 win over Argentina.

The 27-year-old featured throughout as Diego Forlan's side came out on top in the friendly, an own-goal from Leonardo Balerdi and Maxi Araujo's strike enough to cancel out Julian Alvarez's late effort.

The appearance was the 30th cap for the defender, who has become stalwart for both his nation, and Liverpool this season.

The Reds signed Araujo on loan from Barcelona in the summer transfer window, with many believing he had brought in to provide cover at centre-back.

But the Uruguayan was quickly moved by Andoni Iraola to right-back and has not looked back, producing consistently excellent performances in the position and fuelling clamour for him to be signed permanently.

Liverpool have the option to buy him for £47million included in the loan, but will be keen to keep their options open and watch the season progress.

Araujo was situated back in the middle of the defence for Uruguay on Friday, but played with the same intensity.

Liverpool have quickly come to learn this season that the defender wears his heart on his sleeve, and leaves everything out on the pitch.

And despite Friday's game being a friendly, Araujo was keen to win South American pride for his nation.

The 27-year-old completed two tackles, two blocks - the joint-most of anybody - and three clearances.

He was fouled once, made an interception, a recovery, and won six of the eight duels he contested, including everything in the air.

On Araujo's performance, Uruguayan outlet Montevideo wrote: "He had a mixed performance in the first half, lacking his usual composure on the ground, but winning the aerial duels.

"He deserves credit for the flick-on on the first goal, and when the team sat back to defend the lead, he responded well and capped off a very good second half."

El Pais gave him a seven out of 10 and added: "He tried to intercept Julián Álvarez in what almost resulted in the first goal of the night at La Bombonera.

"He went up a couple of times to try and capitalize on his good aerial game, but the crosses weren't good enough for him.

"In the second half he showed all his class when it came to marking and holding off the local attacks."

Few could have imagined just how big of an impact Araujo would have at Liverpool this season.

The right-back has already earned the favour of the Anfield faithful, but he is keen for more.

"My hunger and ambition is to win trophies," he said this week. "We have a great team and given the size of Liverpool as a club, it needs to be fighting for every trophy this season.

"It's been lovely but I also came here prepared for this challenge. From the first moment the possibility was there for me to come to this huge club, I prepared myself to come here in the best of conditions so I could adapt to this club, to a new league with a completely different style.

"But I worked a great deal for this. Even during my first few weeks at the club, we worked really hard to be up to speed."
