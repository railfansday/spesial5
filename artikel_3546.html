Apple TV’s 6-Episode Fantasy Series Is a Masterclass in Imaginative Worldbuilding

Over the past few years, Apple TV has slowly been building a hefty catalog of endearing dramas in addition to its beloved sci-fi and crime thrillers. From the Jason Sudeikis-led Ted Lasso to the underseen drama Trying and, of course, Jason Segel's Shrinking, the streaming platform has become a go-to for heartwarming and perhaps even tear-jerking stories. Among them is Small Prophets, an original BBC production which first landed on Apple TV on October 7, 2026.

Small Prophets, created by Mackenzie Crook, is a drama and comedy series already sporting a perfect 100% score on Rotten Tomatoes. The audience was also quickly won over by the show, as shown by the 95% score on the same site. Plus, with Season 2 of the show already confirmed, there's no better time than now to tune in.

Small Prophets follows the story of Michael Sleep (Pearce Quigley), an eccentric man who works at a DIY store and longs for the return of his darling Cleo, who suddenly disappeared seven years ago. One day, his dad, Brian (Sir Michael Palin), shares an old recipe involving rainwater, horse manure and more than a little alchemy that supposedly create homunculi, magical spirits that can predict the future. With the recipe in hand, Michael becomes determined to DIY his own homunculi in the hope they have the answer to his burning question, “Will I ever see Clea again?”

A Christmas Special is set for this December.

Along the way, Michael develops a sweet and unlikely friendship with his young coworker Kacey (Lauren Patel), partly through their dislike of store manager Gordon (Crook), while alarming their nosy neighbors who become obsessed with finding out what's going on in the garden shed. The series, which incorporates animation into the live-action, also stars Sophie Willan, Jon Pointing and Paul Kaye.

The first season of Small Prophets, which premiered on February 9, 2026, on BBC iPlayer and BBC Two in the U.K., was beloved by critics and audiences alike. Among the reviews are critics raving about the series' ambitious plot, one that brings excitement and fantasy to one man's ordinary and otherwise melancholic life. "A gentle, jewel-like, unhurried masterpiece that is funny, profound and manages to break and mend your heart in equal measures," wrote one review. "Small Prophets is richly steeped in Crook’s glorious brand of gentleness, his ability to imbue the ordinary with extraordinariness," wrote another.

'Widow's Bay' isn't the first kooky murder mystery set in a small town.

Plenty of critics also compare the series to Detectorists, a three-season series also written and starring Crook that also had Quigley in the cast. The series, which first premiered in 2014, followed the "lives of two eccentric metal detectorists, who spend their days plodding along plowed tracks and open fields, hoping to disturb the tedium." "As in Detectorists, the result has a winning blend of mild melancholy, calm acceptance of the way these people are and something approaching defiance about their right to be so. It is, in short, completely lovely," wrote one review comparing both series. "For those of us who loved Detectorists – and we are many – Small Prophets is compulsory viewing," stated another.
