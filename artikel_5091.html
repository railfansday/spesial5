Bitcoin’s $56K dip attracts Michael Saylor – Yet whales open $91M short

Is THIS dip a good time to invest? One analyst thinks so.

Bitcoin’s Fear & Greed Index stood at 11, compared to 50 in early November, reflecting a sharp shift in market sentiment alongside BTC’s 20% correction.  Long-term investors may benefit from this as many weak holders are offloading their BTC. This allows large investors to take up the BTC on sale, allowing them to buy it cheaper.

Against this backdrop, Michael Saylor’s recent post showing his famous Bitcoin buying signal suggests whales may be getting into the market.

With BTC falling toward $56k, extreme fear, and Strategy’s continued purchases, it does lend some support to the analyst’s view that this dip could be a buying opportunity.

But the bigger question remains: Does buying the dip offer enough long-term upside?

In terms of on-chain and technical indicators, BTC has seen a 40% pullback from its post-election rally in 2024. From a long-term perspective, it has also pulled back to levels seen 5 years prior.

This means Bitcoin has erased years of price gains, raising questions about whether the recent correction can be viewed as a buying opportunity or if it is indicative of a deeper market downturn.

Simply put, does Bitcoin’s long-term return profile justify Michael Saylor’s latest purchase and analysts’ bullish outlook? Or could weakening returns and a prolonged price decline discourage smart money from accumulating Bitcoin [BTC]?

Key onchain signals are already showing a divergence from the analysts’ bullish outlook.

While there is extreme fear in the market, a whale moved 2,279 BTC into Binance, while another whale took a 40x BTC short worth $91 million.

Extreme fear has historically attracted bargain hunters. However, these whale transactions suggested some large investors were preparing for further downside.

Meanwhile, Bitcoin’s supply distribution painted an equally concerning picture. Nearly 57% of BTC’s supply was sitting at a loss, leaving just 43% in profit.

However, large players are taking a more risk-averse stance, making the possibility of further capitulation harder to ignore.

Ultimately, extreme fear alone may not be enough to confirm a bottom. This puts Saylor’s latest Bitcoin buy signal in a different light.

With BTC’s long-term structure weakening, limited signs of whale accumulation, and capitulation risks rising, buying the dip may carry more downside risk than potential upside.

Extreme fear and Strategy’s continued Bitcoin purchases suggest a potential buying opportunity, but they do not confirm a market bottom.

Rising supply in loss, whale selling, and large short positions point to further downside and capitulation risks.
