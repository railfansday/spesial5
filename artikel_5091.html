Bitcoin’s oil relief has a catch: more oil at sea, less on land

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

The IEA raised its fourth-quarter supply forecast by 3.3 million barrels a day, while depleted land stocks and diesel shortages keep relief conditional.

The International Energy Agency raised its fourth-quarter 2026 oil supply forecast by 3.3 million barrels a day in its November 13 report and now expects a quarterly surplus. The improved outlook could reduce oil-related inflation pressure, which matters to Bitcoin through interest rates and investors' willingness to take risk.

The upgrade follows an October supply increase of 2.3 million barrels a day to 105.4 million, as Gulf exports recovered faster than demand. The IEA projects a larger inventory build in 2027. The 3.3 million figure is an increase in its supply forecast, not the size of the expected surplus.

Observed global inventories rose by 52 million barrels in October, the IEA reported. Oil on water, a component of the global total, increased by 86 million barrels, while on-land stocks continued falling and reached their lowest level since at least 2016. OECD total stocks declined by 47 million barrels.

Refining presents another constraint. Gulf refined-product and liquefied petroleum gas exports remained around half their pre-war levels, while attacks on Russian energy infrastructure restricted diesel supplies. At the report's time of writing, European diesel was near $180 a barrel, still 77% above pre-war levels, even as North Sea Dated crude traded around $93.

The IEA forecasts refinery runs will rebound from October's 81.3 million barrels a day to 84.7 million in December. It expects emergency diesel releases to bridge the supply gap until refinery output recovers.

Cheaper delivered fuel could help reduce inflation pressure, potentially easing one constraint on monetary policy. Broader prices still matter: the Bureau of Labor Statistics' October CPI release showed consumer prices rising 0.4%, energy 0.7% and prices excluding food and energy 0.3%, all on a seasonally adjusted monthly basis.

The Federal Reserve held its policy-rate target at 3.75% to 4% on October 28 and said inflation remained elevated. In a November 13 speech, Fed Vice Chair Philip Jefferson said energy costs were feeding into airfares and transportation and warned about persistent broader inflation. He identified inflation's trajectory as the more important policy signal, while keeping future adjustments dependent on the data.

Bitcoin's connection runs through financial conditions and willingness to take risk. A 2023 IMF working paper found that U.S. monetary tightening reduced a common component of crypto-price movements through the risk-taking channel, resembling the effect on equities.

For Bitcoin, the potential relief depends on oil reaching refineries and cheaper fuel easing broader inflation. Rebuilt land stocks and recovering fuel exports would help; renewed shipping attacks or stubborn inflation could keep the pressure on.
