Ford is taking a minority stake in satellite maker Revolution Space

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Financial terms were not disclosed, and closing depends on regulatory approvals and other customary conditions

Ford $F Motor Company said Monday that its subsidiary, Industrial Platform, signed an agreement to make a minority investment in Revolution Space, a Boston-based prime contractor for advanced space systems.

The companies did not disclose financial terms. Closing of the transaction is subject to customary conditions, including regulatory approvals. Ford will apply its manufacturing capabilities to scale production of Revolution Space's next-generation spacecraft, Ford said.

"Ford's strength is building complex products in large volumes, something today's growing satellite market requires," Marin Gjaja, Ford's chief strategy officer, said in a statement. "Becoming a partner to Revolution Space and working to industrialize and scale their advanced technology for space is an exciting opportunity for us to diversify our business in an attractive, high-growth market."

Daniel S. Goldin, Revolution Space's chairman of the board and the longest-serving NASA administrator, said in a statement that the company looks forward to working with Ford to industrialize its systems for a wide range of missions across all orbit regimes.

Ford said the investment is part of a strategy to diversify into businesses with strong growth and return potential that are less cyclical and where Ford has a competitive advantage.

Ford cited a World Economic Forum projection that the global space economy is expected to grow from about $600 billion to $1.8 trillion by 2035.

Ford has a history in the sector. From the 1960s into the 1970s, it built communications and weather satellites for NASA, the military and NATO, the company said. In 1976, its aerospace division won a $235 million contract to build seven communications satellites for INTELSAT.

The move comes as Ford pursues new revenue streams outside of autos, the segment that supplies the majority of its earnings, according to Bloomberg. Ford is launching an energy-storage operation that would put spare capacity at its battery factories to work, following the $19.5 billion of EV-related writedowns it announced last year.

Other automakers have turned to the space industry as well. Toyota $TM Motor has backed several space companies, among them Interstellar Technologies, which was pledged $44 million in a deal revealed in early 2025, according to Bloomberg.
