7 Years Later, Hollywood’s Most Notorious Fantasy Flop Deserves Another Shot

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Years after Cats taught me the phrase “uncanny valley,” I finally watched it and had a weirdly good time

I vividly remember learning the phrase “uncanny valley” in 2019. First came the trailer for Sonic the Hedgehog that April, with those alarmingly human teeth. Then came the first trailer for Tom Hooper’s Cats in July. Suddenly, there was a name for a feeling I’d had my whole life. The uncanny valley is that uneasy feeling you get when something looks or moves almost like a real person, but some small detail makes the whole thing feel wrong. That feeling is a lot more common these days, now that AI-generated videos are everywhere. But it was Cats that really brought the uncanny valley into the popular lexicon.

Even though I recently got my first dog, I’ve always been a big cat guy. I’ve had two cats for almost 16 years. I also love musicals and regularly listen to tracks from Hamilton, Rent, and Dear Evan Hansen. Heck, I also consider myself a Swiftie. By all accounts, Cats seems like something I should have become obsessed with years ago.

So for Polygon’s annual Turkey Day celebration, during which we celebrate the “turkeys” that flopped hard but we can’t help but love, I felt obligated to finally watch Cats and write about it. Was it worth it? I’m still not sure. I can see why Cats earned a measly 19% from critics and 53% from audiences on Rotten Tomatoes. The CGI is often distractingly bad, the tonal whiplash is enough to give you a headache, and I’m pretty sure that the story has always been kind of terrible. Despite that, I found the whole thing feverishly fun and, more often than not, surprisingly sexy. (You know, for something with cat people in it.)

Victoria (Francesca Hayward) is a young cat abandoned in a London alley. She’s taken in by a group of felines called the Jellicles on the night of their annual ball, during which Old Deuteronomy (Judi Dench) will choose one of them to ascend to the Heaviside Layer and be reborn. The candidates make their cases by performing songs. If you want to know why there’s a cat named Skimbleshanks and what he does on the railway, boy, does he have a number for you.

There’s a plot in there, especially once Idris Elba, who is genuinely great as Macavity, starts getting rid of the competition with his teleportation powers. (Also, why do some cats have magic powers?) Most of the movie plays like a series of vignettes in which a new cat shows up and sings about the one thing it really wants you to know about itself. That makes some sense for a stage musical adapted from a book of T.S. Eliot poems about different cats. Onscreen, it means the movie keeps asking you to get invested in a new character just as you’ve adjusted to looking at the last one. But because each has a distinct musical styling and energy, the whole thing feels rather uneven and muddled as it shifts between mania and despair on a dime.

In Cats, everything rides on how much you enjoy spending a few minutes with each cat. Some are delightful. Some are horrible disasters. Hayward, a Royal Ballet dancer when she’s not covered in fur, is exceptional throughout as the new cat on a dizzying adventure. Steven McRae as Skimbleshanks is probably the most energetic and entertaining of them all. Rebel Wilson as Jennyanydots and James Corden as Bustopher Jones are both the epitome of cringe and perhaps the best examples of why Cats struggles as a movie.

I spent an embarrassing amount of time watching Cats thinking about clothes. Corden’s Bustopher Jones looks like he’s dressed for dinner in a sort of half-tuxedo. Most of the cats just have fur, including Wilson’s Jennyanydots. There’s a moment when she sort of falls off a chair in a way that really plays up the buffoonery of fat cats. Then she rolls onto her back and exposes herself to the camera — it makes you want to shriek and cover your eyes — but then her tail appears strategically propped up between her legs. Not long after, she’s singing into a tail when her actual tail is clearly still behind her. Later in the same number, other cats peel off her outer layer of fur to reveal an outfit underneath, with yet another layer of fur. (WTF?) There’s also a point where she starts eating cockroaches that, like all of the cats, are people in animal costumes. (WTF!?)

I’ve never looked at either of my cats and thought, You are naked. But the Jellicles have human faces and mostly human bodies. There are no visible nipples or genitals. Why are some fully clothed? Why are some partially clothed? Does the existence of the clothing make the others seem even more naked than before? Why does there appear to be a correlation between a cat’s age and the amount of clothing they wear? Why didn’t the production design commit fully to one extreme or the other?

One of the funniest things I’ve ever heard was a rumor that, at one point, all of these cats had buttholes. According to an anonymous crew member whose account was reported by Vanity Fair in March 2020, the simulated skin and fur folded in ways that accidentally resembled buttholes and genitals in roughly a dozen unfinished shots. An anonymous visual-effects editor told The Daily Beast that one crew member was literally brought in to remove all of the buttholes. #ReleaseTheButtholeCut

Jason Derulo’s Rum Tum Tugger was the first cat I was genuinely excited to spend a whole song with. Derulo plays him like a guy who knows everybody in the room is looking at him and would be insulted if they stopped. There’s even a part where Victoria is dancing with her leg stretched up into the air. He leans his face in. For a fleeting moment, you can’t tell if he’s going to use her foot as a microphone or suck on her toes​​​​​​​. For a certain kind of pervert, Cats can get decidedly sexy. It wants your mind to go there on more than one occasion.

When Taylor Swift shows up rather late in the movie as Bombalurina and dumps catnip on a room full of cats, the sexual energy is dizzying. The song itself is mediocre at best, but the vibes are immaculate. There’s something refreshing about a number where the movie seems fully aware of how horny it’s being. For a few minutes, I stopped trying to figure out how anybody’s fur worked and just enjoyed the song. (Is this Taylor Swift’s sexiest music video ever? Probably.)

The biggest bit of tonal whiplash comes with Jennifer Hudson’s Grizabella. Hudson has an incredible voice (she’s an EGOT winner!​​​​​​​) and really gives it her all for “Memory,” but Grizabella spends every one of her scenes scream-cry-singing about feelings of rejection and lamenting her lost life as a really cool cat. Hudson once told Empire that she had no idea her character would have ears or a tail, and all of the snot pouring out of her face​​​​​​​ is real. It’s powerful stuff, but also dreadfully unappealing. For me, similar numbers work in something like Les Misérables, where​​​​​​​ the narrative builds up to powerful emotional beats. In Cats, it just feels like a total buzzkill. It made me grateful that Old Deuteronomy selected Grizabella as the Jellicle Choice, which itself feels like a bizarre form of ritual sacrifice and rebirth, if only so she would stop.

Seven years after learning the phrase “uncanny valley,” I finally understand why people wanted to watch Cats together and yell at the screen. My wife refused to watch it with me, so every time I wanted to turn to somebody and say, “Did you see that?” I had to sit there alone with whatever I’d just seen. More often than not, I was pleasantly uncomfortable.

I’ve seen people argue that Cats falls short of “so bad it’s good.” I think it gets there. It helps if you’re willing to embrace the silly fun and admit that people wearing digital fur can be kind of sexy​​​​​​​. Does that make me a furry?
