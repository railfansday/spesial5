X-Men Unveils Cyclops' Darkest Moment, Worse Than Anything Magneto Ever Did

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

X-Men just took Cyclops to his darkest place ever. The franchise has gone full horror as "the clock strikes midnight" in the newest offshoot of the Marvel Universe, and the genre switch has quickly proven to be about more than just gore. X-Men is now a full-blown psychological horror story, and it is Scott Summers who takes center stage, and the brunt of the suffering, in the latest chapter.

What are the first things that come to mind when fans think about Cyclops? Natural leader. Mentally tough. Stalwart hero. What would it take to break him? To bring him to his knees? That's the question Midnight X-Men #2 answers, and the shocking thing about the issue isn't necessarily what happens to Scott, but how easy it turns out to be.

As the saying goes, "love conquers all." Usually, that's supposed to be a good thing. Well, author Jonathan Hickman has managed to give the axiom a dark new meaning in Midnight X-Men, as Cyclops' undying love for his wife, Jean Grey, is weaponized against him in the most horrifying possible way.

Warning! Spoilers ahead for X-Men Midnight #2!

Midnight X-Men #1 introduced readers to a new Marvel timeline, in which vampires and mutants had combined to become a terrifying third thing: "empyres." Legions of these empyres, led by Storm, exist in an uneasy truce with the rest of mutantkind after years of conflict. The series second issue goes back in time to explain how so many mutants were turned before the "ceasefire" was established. It all goes back to one person: Scott Summers.

No shame on Cyclops for the first trap he leads his team into, which ends up with most of the original X-Men, including Jean Grey, turned by Storm. His real tragic mistake is letting himself be fooled into thinking he could rescue Jean, leading him to bring another X-squad to the vampirized mutants on a silver platter. If all that doesn't sound bad enough, that's when the story takes its truly horrific turn.

Utterly defeated, Cyclops begs to be turned, so he can join his team. And he's rejected. Instead, he becomes Jean's thrall. Her Renfield. His job? Keep assembling teams to bring to the empyres to be converted or killed. Use mutantkind's trust and faith in him in the ultimate act of betrayal. It is a hardcore twist on Cyclops' usual portrayal, and it sets him up for a gripping character arc as Midnight X-Men continues.

Cyclops narrates Midnight X-Men #2 from the vantage point of years later. At a point in the future well after Jean Grey's vampiric-psychic hold on him has been broken. He knows the truth of what he did, and he carries around the crushing guilt of it. That is going to have major implications for the story of Midnight X-Men. It could be laying the groundwork for a redemption arc, or it could be set up to turn Cyclops into a villain. Either way, it has made him a tragic figure to a degree few X-stories have ever done before.

[Midnight X-Men #2] uses lore and backstory to serve a heartbreaking character portrait, rather than using characters as "instruments of a greater plot."

For now, though, readers are left with Cyclops' haunting monologue as he reflects on his sins, the terrible price he paid for his failure as a leader and a hero. "Love is total annihilation of the self," Scott Summers concludes. "I have loved...and it destroyed me."

It is one of those moments where a story transcends IP, and lore, and canon, and becomes about something universal. It uses lore and backstory to serve a heartbreaking character portrait, rather than using characters as "instruments of a greater plot," as Chris Claremont once put it. That puts this new horror genre spin on the X-Men and Cyclops' lore off to a strong start only two issues in.

X-Men Midnight #2 is available now from Marvel Comics.
