The Wallet-Sized Xteink X4 Pro Is Lifehacker's E-Reader Gift of the Year, and It's on Sale for Black Friday

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

I've read more than 30 books in the past year, all of them on an e-reader with a screen roughly the size of a credit card. If you think that sounds untenable, I say: Don't knock it 'til you've tried it. Once you're immersed in a book, you'll find the size doesn't matter, and the convenience of being able to slip it into any pocket and forget it's there means you need never be without it.

Right now, the Xteink X4 Pro, the best tiny e-reader I've tried so far—and Lifehacker's pick for the 2026 E-Reader Gift of the Year—is on its best sale yet for Black Friday: When ordered directly from Xteink, you'll save 15% off the usual $99 price, bringing it down to $84. This is the cheapest e-reader with a light and a touchscreen that you can buy right now, and it's well worth the money.

The X4 Pro is the newest and best e-reader from Xteink, the Chinese tech startup that turned the e-reading world upside down last year with the launch of the original X4 pocket e-reader. While the X4 Pro is the same size as the basic X4 Classic, it adds two big upgrades—a temperature-adjustable front light and a touchscreen—plus a faster processor and more memory. As a result, it costs a bit more, but you can pick one at a $15 discount right now. (Even at full retail, the X4 Pro is $50 cheaper than the entry-level Kindle after its recent price increase—and even considering the Kindle's current Black Friday discount).

Meanwhile, if you're a reader who prioritizes the crispest text over conveniences like a front light, the 4.3-inch Xteink X4 Classic and the smaller 3.7-inch Xteink X3 are about $30 less during Amazon's own Black Friday sale.

As much as I love them, these minuscule e-readers aren't for everyone. Some may indeed find the screen too small (though if you've ever read a book on a phone, the experience is similar). Then there's the fact that they can't load your existing libraries of Kindle books; you'll need to source copies of e-books lacking Digital Rights Management (DRM) software. But if you can jump those hurdles, you might just discover it's the weirdest, most lovable piece of tech you've used in years.
