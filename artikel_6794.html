Inside a Layered Ski-in, Ski-out Retreat in the Mountains of Utah

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

An affectionate nickname inspired much of the design details.

Designers learn a lot of things about their clients when building or renovating their homes—it’s how they ensure the spaces actually feel like the homeowners. Their hobbies shape the purposes of distinct rooms, their tastes affect the color palette, and their experiences help to build out the decor. So, when designer McKenzie Matthews of Coven Haus (accidentally) learned that her client affectionately calls her husband The Wolf, she simply had to weave that part of their story into the design of their Park City, Utah, winter retreat.

“What started as a playful detail became a recurring theme, from a custom wolf head silhouette repeated along the secondary staircase to wolf hooks in the mudroom lockers,” Matthews says. The designer, who was part of the six-bedroom, seven-bathroom new-build from the start, wanted every aspect of the interior to feel like a luxurious and elevated boutique ski resort but with more personalized charm. “We carried the wolf motif into decorative pillows, artwork, and accessories, creating little moments of personality and meaning throughout the home.”

Outside of these smaller details, the rest of the ski-in, ski-out style home is layered with natural elements, like stone, reclaimed wood, and plaster, to complement the views outside of the Wasatch Mountain Range. The front door opens to a wood-clad foyer, topped with a vaulted ceiling accented with reclaimed wooden beams, which continues into the great room. Flanked by two double-sided stone fireplaces and looking out beyond the second floor of the deck into the woods, this gathering area invites any guest to escape the cold of the slopes and stay as long as they like.

Handmade and vintage pieces fill in any gaps, giving each room a collected feel. “One of my favorite examples is the primary bedroom bookcase, filled with unique objects and accessories we’ve gathered over the years,” Matthews says. “It’s those little details that give a home so much character.”

Spanning 11,758 square feet, it can be hard to make a house of this size truly feel homey, but Matthews’ dedication to the details ensured it was. “Every room has something special and unexpected, yet the entire home still feels cohesive,” she says. “I love that there's always something new to discover as you move through each space.”

The entry sets the tone for the rest of the home, with the wood-paneled walls, vaulted ceiling, limestone floor tile, and reclaimed beams serving as a warm, inviting escape from the cold outside. The variety of textures brings character and, along with the sculptural entry table, helps to soften the grand architectural details.

No detail is too small in this ski room. Marble knobs dot the top lockers, while a Calacatta Viola marble door jamb makes the mundane feel special. “With our clients being from Argentina, we wanted to do something a little unexpected and playful, starting with the textural floor tile,” Matthews says.

The fun continues to the ceiling, which is coated in the same red that lines the interior of the oak lockers, just in a matte finish. “We loved the idea of it feeling like a jewelry box,” the designer says of the painted locker interiors.

Fabric panels on the upper lockers soften the millwork and break up the oak, as does the painted boot wall which opens to the ski patio. “There are so many little details in this room, and I love every one of them!” Matthews adds.

Laying out the great room was a bit of a challenge. Flanked by two double-sided stone fireplaces, Matthews wanted to take advantage of the hearths without blocking or sacrificing the incredible view. “We opted for movable seating that could easily shift depending on how the space is used, while keeping the view as the star of the room,” she explains. The tumbleweed chandelier was the cherry on top, creating a bit of unexpected fun while bringing in an organic element.

To the right of the living room, beyond the fireplace, is a gaming corner, slightly blocked away from the main area in case any matches get a bit rowdy.

Downstairs in the living room, Matthews wanted plenty of seating for family and friends to gather, so they ordered a custom sectional with extra-deep seat cushions that comfortably seats at least eight people, if not more. “The reclaimed wood ceiling and exposed beams add so much warmth and character, while the soft furnishings keep the space feeling relaxed and lived in,” she adds.

If the family still has energy after a day skiing, they can always bond over a game of poker. Rich dark cocoa millwork and light-blocking drapery close off this space from the rest of the house, while the wall of books gives it character and ensures it still feels homey. If poker isn’t the game of choice, the wood top is removable so it doubles as a regular gaming or puzzle table.

“This might be my favorite room in the house,” Matthews says. “The ideas just kept flowing, and we reworked the bar layout a handful of times before landing here.” While the entire setup would make the most seasoned bartender jealous, it’s the details—like the patinated brass island, the tiled ceiling, the double bullnose countertop edge, and necklace-like sculptural chandelier—that makes the space a moment. “A little five-star resort experience without ever leaving home.”

Because Matthews’s clients wanted a dirty kitchen and a main one for entertaining, the designer could be a bit more creative here, choosing the finishes and features more for the way they look rather than how well they function. A calming blue and brown color palette makes it the most enticing spot for a quiet morning coffee or for setting out a grand buffet for party guests.

Since this is where all the cooking happens, functionality was Matthews’s biggest priority, from the layout to the actual appliances and finishes. “The kitchen layout changed several times throughout the process, and it certainly helped that our builder, Don Jones [of Craig Construction], was once a chef!” she explains. “We really prioritized flow and functionality first, then let the design follow.”

Blue cabinetry ties in with the stove while bronze hardware does the same with the custom patinated brass hood, which warms everything up and will only get better-looking with age, as will the quartz countertops. The vintage plates on the wall add personality and style to this workhorse space.

In the wine cellar and tasting room, the architectural elements were quite linear, so Matthews opted for an arched doorway to create some movement. “We loved the idea of the temperature controlled wine cellar having a cavernous feel, which is why we chose to bring natural stone into the interior,” she explains.

Another favorite room of Matthews’, she especially loves the bookcase since it gives the cozy room an almost historic, collected feel. To add height and balance the scale of the bookshelves, she hung a tapestry rug above the bed, creating a layered, timeless escape for the homeowners.

The wallpaper, paired with the ruched velvet bed frame, adds romantic appeal to this guest bedroom. Vintage artworks and furniture, like the trunk, lend a collected feel, bringing the space to life. “The vintage sketches were carefully framed and hung from brass chains, adding a nod to the past and giving the room a sense of history,” the designer explains.

With over a decade in the design industry, McKenzie Matthews firmly believes good design should feel effortless and evolve over time. Matthews is the founder and principal designer of Coven Haus, a boutique interior design studio based in Utah and serving clients nationwide. The studio’s mission is to bring its clients’ visions of home to life through thoughtful design, collaboration, and authentic representations.
