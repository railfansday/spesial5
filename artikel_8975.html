Royal Society for Arts appoints five Royal Designers for Industry

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The UK’s Royal Society for Arts has announced five Royal Designers for Industry, who include Durrell Bishop and Bridget Baines, as well as naming an Honorary Royal Designer as it celebrates its 90th anniversary.

The awards, which were established in 1936, recognise outstanding talent across landscape architecture, technology, illustration, fashion and theatre. They are given by Royal Society for Arts (RSA) and can only be held by 200 designers at any one time.

This year’s winners are product and interaction designer Bishop, multidisciplinary designer Harris Elliott, illustrator Laura Carlin, costume designer Tahra Zafar and landscape architect Bridget Baines.

Baines co-founded international practice Gross Max, and her fellow Gross Max founder Eelco Hooftman (pictured in main image with Baines) was given the Honorary Royal Designer award this year.

The winners were chosen for their “joyful” designs,

“Ninety years on, the Royal Designers are still looking forward,” Keeper of the Faculty of Royal Designers for Industry Johanna Gibbons said. “This year’s appointments show how far design now reaches, from public landscapes and picture books to exhibitions, the stage and the objects we use every day.”

“Their work is joyful, and it is serious about making life better,” she continued. “That is what unites Royal Designers across the decades: a living entanglement of disciplines that, with the RSA Fellowship, is a profound force for good.”

Last year’s winners included designer Yinka Ilori and journalist Mona Chalabi, and the award has also been given to designers including Es Devlin, Vivienne Westwood, James Dyson and Zandra Rhodes.

According to the RSA, the winners represent the “sheer breadth of design today”.

“Great design changes how we experience the world, often in ways we barely notice,” RSA programme manager Catherine Joyce.

“For 90 years, Royal Designers have shown what extraordinary creativity can achieve, and how it can make life better for everyone,” she continued.“These six appointments are a wonderful reminder of the sheer breadth of design today.”
