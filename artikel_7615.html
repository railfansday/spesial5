Will AI slop overwhelm creators? Mark Rober isn’t worried–but his team is using AI as a “thought partner” in production

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Mark Rober told Business Insider he thinks his content has a “moat” that will keep it safe from being affected by the AI slop deluge.

But, at the same time, he’s said his team is using generative AI as a “thought partner” for writing, and that it can make “the perfect meal” for students using his Class CrunchLabs modules.

With BI, Rober addressed creators’ anxiety about AI-generated videos encroaching on the watch time that should go to their genuine content.

“Do you want to see a simulation of someone dunking a basketball with a sick trick, or do you want to see someone actually do it?” he told BI. “The fact that [viewers] know it’s real is what makes it crazy.”

He said creators should be reassured that “[o]ur brains developed for hundreds of thousands of years with human connection,” and viewers want “real and authentic” content filmed by people who interact with the world around them.

He also added that people need to retain their cognitive skills instead of just ChatGPTing everything. Both creators and viewers should investigate things themselves, using their own human brains.

“Curiosity is so important in this new age of AI,” he said. “It means you just have a lifetime of learning, versus like, ‘Oh, this thing knows the answer, so who cares?'”

But, again, Rober has implemented AI across his processes. This past June, he told The Information that AI “has the power to really adapt” educational settings.

“Basically what we’re doing with Class CrunchLabs is we’re making great ingredients, really fresh ingredients, and then AI can be like, ‘Hey, I have three kids in my class who are challenged this way, and they’re not great at reading, but these ones are. We’ve already covered this stuff.’ And it could take those ingredients and make the perfect meal for your class,” he explained. “I think it’s gonna be very good at that.”

He said that on the creator side, “I think it would allow me to scale myself in certain ways. We’re certainly using it as a thought partner on writing, on some thumbnail stuff.”

Like he did with BI, Rober said that in science content specifically, viewers probably want something real instead of something LLM’d. “I think it remains to be seen what the public’s appetite is for just pure AI-generated content,” he said. “I would think in science, you actually want to see the lava melt through the thing, versus it just being completely fake. But we’ll see.”

We’ve written before about how big tech platforms like YouTube have found themselves in a tricky catch-22: They want to make money off the AI bubble (uh oh), but as they roll out more generative tools and push creators to use them, they end up combating endless amounts of slop pumped out by those very same tools.

With Rober and other creators, we see something similar: They feel their content is safe from AI content made by other people, but also use AI in their processes, in the name of “scaling” or “ideating” or streamlining and cost-lessening production.

Rober’s team is using AI in its writing process, and a script is the foundation of long-form, educational YouTube content like his. Is the vibe just that viewers won’t care, so long as no fully AI-generated video makes it into an upload? Intriguing…
