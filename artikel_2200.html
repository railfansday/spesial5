3 reasons why Bristol Rovers must sack Steve Evans

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

It was just a few short months ago that optimism was high and Bristol Rovers were widely being tipped for a hugely successful season. The Gas had ended the 2025-26 season unbeaten in nine matches and then started the new campaign by breaking the club record for consecutive wins at home. The optimism appeared well-placed.

Steve Evans was being lauded by Gasheads who were daring to dream. Iconic scenes of the gaffer in the Thatchers will be hard to replicate.

Now we’re approaching the festive period, and Gasheads are up in arms with large portions of the fanbase calling for Evans to go.

The Gas are once more on a miserable run of defeats in the league and have a manager either by accident or by nature creating division between himself and supporters, and even perhaps himself and his players.

Supporters have become increasingly frustrated by the manager's post-match comments in recent times, adding to the dismay at results on the pitch.

With so many Gasheads now calling for Evans to go, Football League World offers three reasons why now is the right time to act.

The last time Bristol Rovers were on such a deep losing streak — a run that saw the Gas fall to a club record 10 straight league defeats — the ownership decided to act and dismissed Darrell Clarke. A man widely seen as a great of the club following his promotion success years earlier. Rovers' latest defeat under Evans, going down 2-0 at home to Chesterfield, was their eighth loss in their last 10 matches in League Two.

When you compare the final 12 League matches for Darrell Clarke (one win, one draw, 10 losses) with Steve Evans' last 12 league matches to-date (two wins, one draw and nine losses), there is very little between them.

Both men, though, can have strong claims to be deserving of more time to put things right at the club.

Darrell Clarke was, and remains, a hero for Gasheads. Clarke is one of Bristol Rovers' most successful managers in history, having achieved a unique feat by gaining back-to-back promotions. And his connection with the fan base is unrivaled by any manager, perhaps since Paul Trollope.

Steve Evans has won nine promotions across his 32 years in management. And was able to turn the Pirates' fortunes around following his arrival after Clarke's dismissal last season, enjoying a run of eight straight league victories (their best run since the 1950’s) alongside breaking the club record for consecutive home wins. All of which means he may have plenty of credit in the bank with the current Rovers ownership, even if support among fans has all but extinguished.

As pressure increases on Bristol Rovers manager Steve Evans, we looked at six potential replacements the Gas should consider.

Evans caused a stir earlier this week when his post-Chesterfield comments left supporters confused and even concerned.

Following the Pirates’ defeat to Chesterfield, Evans took to the media to unleash a barrage of comments that left many Gasheads asking what on earth he was on about, and ultimately forced Evans into the most remarkable backtrack.

On Saturday, Evans told the media that he and his staff were being let down by “inconsistent and poor players”. Players, incidentally, who were predominantly recruited by Evans and his staff. Whilst perhaps unhelpful to the cause of pulling your squad together, these comments were not what got the heckles up amongst Rovers supporters. Instead, it was his remarks that many interpreted to be an allegation against a previous manager having been turning up to work at Bristol Rovers drunk when results were going against them.

Evans told local media after the match on Saturday: "We’re not doing our jobs, but we’re being let down by inconsistent players. Poor players, in my opinion."

He then went on to add: "I’m still first in. Half six, till six. I’m watching games midweek. I’m not like before, when they’re coming in at nine o’clock, leaving at two, being drunk half the week. That’s not me, yeah."

It sparked widespread condemnation of Evans and also speculation as to who he was referring to.

On Monday, Steve Evans was forced into an incredible backtrack from those comments as he spoke to Bristol Live in an attempt to quash the speculation. The under-pressure veteran manager told the local news outlet: "I get really annoyed when people speculate or make something up when I speak openly. I spoke about other managers waiting for payoffs, and yes, that happens in football because our jobs are hard to get, and you can be out of work for some time."

Adding: "Further, I spoke about some individuals having alcohol-related issues here. Some have made two plus two = 12, with social media alluding to I was referring to one particular manager, and nothing could be further from the truth. I was actually generalising about the behaviour of some managers when pressure mounts. I am indeed the opposite. Work and Bristol Rovers are what matter to me. I need a clear focus. However, that cannot be attributed to many other individuals."

Football League World has also spoken directly to players who were at Bristol Rovers during previous regimes, who strongly denied suggestions that any manager turned up drunk while they were playing at the club.

For a large swathe of supporters who were undecided whether Evans was the right man to guide the Gas out of their latest slump, this episode has tipped the scales.

In recent seasons, Bristol Rovers have acted in the weeks leading up to Christmas with four managerial changes happening in mid-December since 2018. And with the transfer market opening on January 1st, along with question marks around the strength of the squad Steve Evans has assembled, there's an argument for making that decision sooner.

Any prospective new manager will need matches and plenty of time on the training ground to assess the quality of this Bristol Rovers squad before setting in motion any recruitment plans. A new manager will need to identify the weak spots in the squad, starting perhaps with bringing in sufficient goalkeeper backup, and trying to address the lack of potency in the Gas attack.

Rovers have six matches scheduled for December, including a busy festive period. And with the Gas currently looking in the wrong direction on the League Two table, the pressure will be on any new manager from the outset.
