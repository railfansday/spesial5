Skip The Pan: Grilled Cheese Is 10x Crispier When You Cook It Like This

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Whether you like it on its own or coupled with a bowl of roasted tomato soup, a grilled cheese is one of life's simple pleasures. Making one for yourself is simple enough in a pan or skillet. However, preparing half a dozen or more can be a pretty big undertaking on the stovetop. Fortunately, there's an easier way. By using a couple of baking sheets and your oven, you can conveniently whip up a large batch of these cheesy sandwiches that come out super crispy.

This technique mimics a panini press, which functions by heating top and bottom metal plates connected by a hinge. This gets both sides of the sandwich crispy at the same time, eliminating the need for flipping. However, these presses have their downsides. The weight of the plates, for instance, can turn softer bread into a pancake. Baking sheets, on the other hand, are much lighter and won't compromise the integrity of more delicate breads.

The oven also has the advantage of providing ambient heat since it's an enclosed environment. This means that your sandwich is getting heated on all sides instead of just the top and bottom, helping to create crisp crusts and melted cheese. However, you still need to preheat your top baking sheet for even crispness, since the bottom of your sandwiches could brown before the tops get crispy.

Depending on how many sandwiches you want to prepare, you can vary the size of the baking sheet you use. To make about six to eight at once, use a half-sheet pan, which measures about 18 inches by 13 inches and is probably the size you have at home. You can also use a quarter-sheet pan if you want to make fewer.

While the fat you spread on your bread (we recommend mayo for the crispiest results) will help ensure that your sandwiches don't stick to the baking sheets, you can also take a tip from Ina Garten and use parchment paper to line the pans for extra insurance. Also, don't stick to just using your baking sheets right side up. In fact, using your bottom pan upside down will allow for more airflow since the rim won't get in the way, thus crisping your sandwiches more effectively.

To make this technique really work, though, you're going to need to make sure you choose the right kind of cheese that will melt easily by the time the crusts of your sandwiches become golden brown. After all, you don't want to have to keep putting your grilled cheeses back in the oven to finish melting. The best options have a good balance of moisture and fat, and younger cheeses tend to be the go-to varieties that meet this standard. Some recommendations include Havarti, Gruyère, and cheddar. However, you can opt for some underrated (but tasty) cheeses to give your sandwiches extra character. You can also combine several types of cheese for a more varied flavor and texture.
