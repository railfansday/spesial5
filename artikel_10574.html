UK Government cracking down on phones being used in schools from next year

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

The UK government is set to introduce “tougher” measures against phones being used in schools after warnings from teachers and school leaders.

Over the years, mobile phones have become more commonplace in schools. Students need them to contact parents when the day is over, and they’re sometimes used during interactive parts of lessons as well.

However, some schools see them more as a disruption. As a result, a number of schools across the UK have introduced systems where students place their phones into sealed bags at the start of the day and aren’t allowed to open them until they have free time, or the school day has ended.

Yet, in 2027, phones might be completely banned from schools following “tougher” measures from the government.

As per the BBC, the government is set to ban phones from being in students’ bags or pockets throughout the day.

This, they say, is a “tougher” measure than the current “no see, no hear” policy that schools operate with regard to phones. Plenty of schools are fine with phones being in students’ pockets or bags as long as they’re not seen or heard during lessons.

According to Education Secretary Lucy Powell, the new policy will help show that “school is for learning and friendship, not scrolling” and access to phones “risks pulling children away from their learning.”

These rules are separate from the impending social media ban for under 16s, which has affected a number of social platforms. Under those rules, teenagers will even have access restricted to sites like eBay and Facebook Marketplace in order to keep them safe.

UK officials have also considered preventing children from talking to strangers in games like Roblox, Fortnite, and Minecraft.

Similar stances have been taken in Australia and Canada too, with the Australian government facing lawsuits over it.

The European Union has also considered sweeping social media restrictions for under 16s as well, but they have not come into law yet.
