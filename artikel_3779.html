Now that the cat is officially out of the bag regarding Amanda Seyfried’s surprise role in the hit A24 release You Can See Everything, I’ve been waiting to hear from Christopher Abbott. The East of Eden star plays Elizabeth Holmes’ partner, Billy Evans, in the recreations that Seyfried is also a part of. Like so many spoilers in the movie, the cast and crew have been pretty mum about the production. Finally, we’ve heard from Abbott.

Abbott has been very busy over the last couple of years with projects like Wolf Man and The Testament of Ann Lee, plus his Tony-nominated performance in Death of a Salesman on Broadway this year. He's been a little overshadowed in the hype around You Can See Everything, directed by Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim. Seyfried’s appearance is such a WTF moment that, speaking as an audience member, it’s almost like we were all still processing that to even register Abbott’s cameo as Evans. It took a couple of minutes before I put it together, honestly. Abbott himself admitted to the process being rushed for him, as well, telling The Hollywood Reporter:

Nathan and Lance, not that long before I went to go work on [it], had just called me and asked me to come mess around. I got to see some of the footage that they had shot before.  I did it, so you know, it was a very kind of quick run-and-gun thing for me, at least.

Abbott also revealed that because it was so quick, he didn’t have a lot of time to prepare or study the character and relied on the footage that Fielder and Oppenheim shot while they were embedded with Holmes and Evans before the former Theranos fraudster reported to prison in 2023. Of the prep, Abbott says:

I've been a fan of both of them for a long time, so I kind of said yes before they even told me what they wanted to do. For me, it was just sort of fun to not to make it so basic. I didn't really know of him until they showed me the footage from the thing. So I just thought it was a very interesting character. I didn't dive deep. I sort of had just enough time to maybe try to do some version of mimicry or find little nuances.  He's an interesting subject.

I think part of what makes it work so well in the box office hit is that we, as an audience, like Abbott, weren’t really familiar with Evans. By the time Abbott appears, we know as much about Evans as he did when he took the role. He’s playing the same villainous character we’ve seen over the previous two hours of the movie, and it works really well.

Abbott probably won’t get any Oscar buzz like Seyfried is getting, but that’s not a slight on him in the least. He provides another bonkers moment in a movie full of them. I really can’t wait for this movie to hit streaming so I can really dive back into it and his performance.
