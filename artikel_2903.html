Wembanyama’s 50-bomb demolishes the Pelicans

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

It is far too early to talk about the NBA's Most Valuable Player award. But Victor Wembanyama is making it clear that he belongs in the conversation – whenever it's time to have it.

Wembanyama had another dominant performance in the San Antonio Spurs' 114-91 win over the New Orleans Pelicans. San Antonio's big man had 50 points, 19 rebounds, 4 steals, and 5 blocks.

Wembanyama was red-hot from three. He knocked down five of his eleven three-pointers, taking advantage of the Pelicans' relaxed defense. It wasn't just their perimeter defense that struggled; Wembanyama was able to get open for lobs all night. He shot 20-for-29 from the field, getting a lot of good looks inside the arc.

Wembanyama's low post game has been one of the most interesting developments in his game this season. He looks legitimately more comfortable with his back to the basket. His baseline fadeaway has been money, and his touch on turnaround hook shots is soft. I'm a sucker for a good up-and-under move, but Wembanyama doesn't normally utilize them. This was a sweet one, tricking Yves Missi and leading to the dunk.

Wembanyama wasn't the only high-flying star for the Spurs. Stephon Castle had 19 points and 6 assists on an efficient 7-for-12 shooting. Castle has already recorded some of the NBA's highest vertical leaps on dunks. This transition lob has to be close to the peak of his jumping ability.

Castle was on fire from deep, hitting five of his seven three-pointers. The Spurs desperately need him to hit open shots. When he does that, the offense sings. The blowout win over the Pelicans is an example of what is possible when he's hitting threes.

This is a great example of what defenses are doing to the Spurs, and why Castle is open so often. Jeremiah Fears is tagging the roller, leaving Castle wide open because he's not a knockdown threat from deep. This blows up the pick-and-roll, but leaves Castle free on the lift. He hits the shot, making New Orleans pay for leaving him open.

Carter Bryant recorded one of the most powerful dunks of the game. There have to be more ways for him to utilize his athletic finishing ability inside without driving to the basket. He's not a strong ball-handler, but could he become a top-of-the-line cutter and offensive rebounder so he can get more attempts around the basket, rather than being a spacer off the ball? Figuring that out could take Bryant's game to the next level. The sophomore had one of his better games of the season with 8 points, hitting two of his threes.
