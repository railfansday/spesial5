If Fitzgerald continues to struggle, could the Panthers look for a replacement in College Station?

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The 2027 NFL Draft feels like forever away, and we're all caught up in the NFL season. Regardless, it's never too early to look ahead to the Draft. This twice weekly series will take a closer look at some of the prospects the Carolina Panthers could select in the 2027 iteration of the Draft. In this series, we'll only be looking at prospects the Panthers could seriously consider. Today our profile will focus on Texas A&M Aggies kicker David Olano.

Olano (5'10", 160 lbs.) was originally a three star recruit out of Naperville, IL. He spent his first three collegiate seasons with the home state Illini, taking over the full time kicker role his sophomore season. During his two on-field seasons with Illinois, Olano made 37 of 43 field goal attempts while going 82/83 on extra points. That resume earned him an offer from Texas A&M, and he transferred to the bigger stage. He has been just as good so far this season, nailing 16 of 18 field goals (including 4/5 from 50+ yards) and making all 27 extra point attempts.

Olano is an ultra reliable kicker, especially in the mid range. He's shown the ability to hit longer kicks this season, including a career long 57 yarder against Arkansas. His leg strength has shown on kickoffs as well, getting a touchback on 87% of his kickoffs, up from a sub-70% mark for his career at Illinois. Olano also has a hanful of clutch kicks on his resume, including a couple walk-off winners in 2025. He has good trajectory and lift on his field goal attempts, only having two career kicks blocked.

Olano has a strong enough leg to be effective at the next level, but he doesn't have the "nuclear" leg of someone like Cam Little. There are also some concerns this year about his fatigue. He missed a field goal in two consecutive games after starting the season out 16/16, so some scouts are concerned about his ability to make it through a 17 game season without splitting kickoff duties. As with most college kickers, the change in NFL kickoff rules makes them tough to project in that area of the game. Kickoffs are not preferred in the NFL like they are in college, so Olano's ability to drop kicks inside the landing zone is untested.

Olano has the precision from every distance that NFL teams covet. He has enough leg to consistently hit from 50+, though the longer 60+ yard kicks might be out of his range. He is consistent year over year, making over 85% of his field goal attempts every season in college. His lack of a monster leg and unproven directional kickoffs might push him toward the end of Day 3 of the Draft or potentially out of the Draft altogether. The Carolina Panthers don't normally use draft capital on kickers (the only kicker drafted by the team was almost ten years ago when they took Harrison Butker). However, Ryan Fitzgerald's sophomore slump has caused some concern among fans. Dave Canales has publicly backed Fitz, but if his struggles continue the team could look to upgrade the position.
