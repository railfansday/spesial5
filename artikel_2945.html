Bayern Munich News: The aftermath of Bayern’s 1–1 draw with 1. FC Köln; Real Madrid out on Michael Olise?; and MORE!

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Bayern Munich started its sprint to the winter pause with a really poor showing in a 1–1 draw with 1. FC Köln.

To add injuries to the insult, both Michael Olise and Harry Kane were felled by injuries and had to leave the game. While Olise should be back next week, Kane could be out for months.

That is the last thing that this team needed during a period when so many other players are out.

Let's dive into the details of this debacle—here are some quick hitters on the match:

As always, let's begin with a look at the starting XI:

Vincent Kompany went conservative with his lineup and resisted the urge to start Jamal Musiala, as had been rumored earlier in the week. Instead, Lennart Karl played the No. 10 position, Nathaniel Brown started at left wing, and Josip Stanišić played left-back.

Karl never really found his way into this game. He was scorching hot before the break, but did get dealt a massive slice of humble pie as he could not get any significant minutes for Germany after Brajan Gruda overtook the Bayern Munich youngster on the national team's depth chart. You have to wonder if that didn't shake his confidence a bit.

An uncharacteristic miscommunication between Olise and Laimer resulted in an early counterattack for the Billy Goats. The long breakout resulted in a pass to Said El Mala, who deftly touched the ball to a hard-charging Mikey Moore, who scored to give 1. FC Köln a 1–0 lead just 12 minutes into the game.

As hard as it might be to imagine, things actually got worse—much worse. Bayern Munich striker Harry Kane suffered a heavy challenge inside the box around the 18th minute as he went down clutching his shin/ankle area, but later got up and managed to keep playing. This would only last a couple more minutes, however, as Kane was clearly unable to continue. Kane exited in the 20th minute with what looked to be an ankle injury and was replaced by Tom Bischof, requiring some shuffling along the front line. It would later be diagnosed as a syndesmosis tear, which could keep him out for three months.

The Kane injury definitely seemed to unsettle the Bavarians, and with Karl at striker and Bischof at the No. 10, the whole look of the attack was different. If you were seeking a positive to all of that, Bayern Munich did seem to grasp the urgency of the situation. No one was content to just cycle the ball until they figured things out.

In the 37th minute, that mentality would pay off. As Bayern Munich was starting to figure out how to work within this setup, Bischof slid a pass to Brown, who had drifted into a very central position from his spot at left wing. Brown took a touch to his left and swung his shot back to the right, confusing everyone and somehow finding a lane to knot the game at 1–1. The approach from Brown might not have been conventional, but it worked.

Bayern Munich would create chances, but was just woefully inefficient with them.

As for Bischof, he just makes things happen and is a positive influence in just about every game he plays.

It was very clear that Bayern Munich had a lot of players out with their respective countries. Everything was just a little off: the timing, the passing, the partnerships. Nothing clicked.

Olise had an injury scare in the first half, too. This was not how anyone envisioned this game going. Bayern Munich players were dropping like flies, leading to a disjointed first 45 minutes and the reality that the Champagne XI is going to need some time to get right. All that said, it did not feel as if Bayern Munich was out of the game at all.

Olise was eventually subbed out after halftime and was deemed to just have a bruise. In an odd twist, it looked like Olise's injury might be worse than Kane's to this writer's naked eye, but it turned out to be the complete opposite, and my reign as an internet doctor ended abruptly. Paging Dr. Howard, Dr. Fine, Dr. Howard! Only some true old heads will get that reference (sigh).

Urbig had a massive save in the 58th minute. For the game, Urbig was terrific. What a player he is turning out to be. The youngster has really embraced his opportunity to play more. Bayern Munich could have easily lost the game without him.

In the 76th minute, Aleksandar Pavlović had a good look, but sent his attempt wide. Over the course of the second half, a few Bayern Munich players (including Karl) should have done better with the opportunities they had.

El Mala should have given Cologne the win in the 82nd minute, but he sent what should have been an easy goal just wide.

In an act of desperation (why not?) late, Kompany brought on youth striker Noah Codjo-Evora in the 87th minute. That had to be wild for the 19-year-old.

Overall, the game itself was not great. Bayern Munich did battle, but it was not sharp, efficient, or good. It happens. Normally, a draw like this would feel very unsatisfactory and disappointing, but with the injuries to Kane (sounds bad) and Olise (sounds good), the end result and the sloppy play from the opening tap to the final whistle almost did not matter. This game was really nothing more than a kick in the shins. A hard, swift, donkey kick. Let's hope for more wins and fewer injuries moving forward. If you really want to extract a silver lining from this game, it could be this: even on a day where almost nothing went right, two key players went down with injuries, and the coach only had one functioning arm, Bayern Munich still didn't lose. At least we have that. On to Lille!

Per this report, Real Madrid is going to walk away from its interest in Michael Olise because of the rumored €300 million price tag:

According to AS journalist Jose Felix Diaz, Real Madrid have moved away from the possibility of signing Michael Olise after Bayern Munich made it clear that taking the French winger away from the Allianz Arena would require an extraordinary transfer fee.

As reported recently, the German champions have placed a €300 million price tag on the 24-year-old, effectively putting Real Madrid out of the picture.

Olise has been on the radar of Europe's leading clubs since last season, with his performance against Real Madrid at the Santiago Bernabeu in the Champions League drawing particular attention.

So far, however, Real Madrid had made no notable progress, and Bayern's current valuation effectively rules them out of the equation.

AS reports that the winger's potential starting price is around €200 million, although the Bavarian club would push that figure towards €300 million.

Eh… this writer does not see Real Madrid giving up on Olise anytime soon. If he becomes available, Real Madrid will find a way to raise funds.

Weston McKennie might be linked to Manchester United, but the USMNT star might not want to leave Juventus:

Meanwhile, despite Juventus midfielder Weston McKennie claiming he doesn't want to leave the club, Gazzetta dello Sport insists that Pochettino still wants to reunite with the 28-year-old USMNT international at Old Trafford. Juve are looking to sign Marseille midfielder Pierre-Emile Hojbjerg, but bringing in the 31-year-old could depend on what happens with McKennie and whether United make a €40 million offer to land him.

With any coaching change comes roster fallout, and it looks like former Bayern Munich center-back Matthijs de Ligt could be a potential sale candidate for Manchester United:

Besiktas are keen on Manchester United centre-back Matthijs de Ligt, with sources confirming they are exploring a January window deal for the injury-struck Netherlands international, and with the Red Devils' verdict on letting him leave also coming to light.

De Ligt, who joined United from Bayern Munich in August 2024 for an initial fee of £42.9 million (€50m, $57m), has had a difficult period at Old Trafford. A serious back injury sidelined him for nearly a year, forcing him to undergo surgery and miss the majority of the 2025/26 campaign, as well as his country's World Cup involvement.

Although De Ligt has recently returned to the squad, making two appearances in the Champions League as he feels his way back to fitness, his involvement has still been limited, and the defender looks way down the pecking order at Old Trafford.

De Ligt's long-term contract, which runs until June 2029 with the option of an extra year, does not guarantee his future at the club.

Pochettino is going to want to come in, establish himself, install his system, and work with his own type of players. It might not just be De Ligt who could be on the move soon.

And the club have also renewed their efforts to sign Brighton's Luka Vuskovic, according to TEAMtalk. Vuskovic, 19, only joined the Seagulls in the summer from Spurs for £46 million, while Bayern Munich, Real Madrid and Liverpool are also monitoring the Croatia international's situation.

Hansi Flick sure seems to want some defensive help.
