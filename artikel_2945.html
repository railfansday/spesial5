Accenture vs. Intuit: Which Technology Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Accenture serves about 75% of the Fortune Global 100, while Intuit's 93 million customers span TurboTax and QuickBooks. Valuation and growth tell very different stories.

By Sara Appino – Updated Nov 27, 2026 at 7:46PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Accenture Plc ›

As digital transformation and artificial intelligence redefine corporate operations and personal finance, choosing between service giant Accenture (ACN +0.56%) and software leader Intuit (INTU +0.08%) represents a fundamental choice for investors.

Accenture focuses on global enterprise consulting and technology implementation, while Intuit dominates the tax and accounting software market for individuals and small businesses. Both companies leverage massive data sets to improve their platforms, but they operate with different growth profiles and capital structures.

Accenture serves approximately 9,000 clients, including about 75% of the Fortune Global 100 and 500 companies. This broad reach across the tech stocks landscape is supported by 18 key technology ecosystem partners that generate around 65% of its total revenue. The company maintains deep roots with its clients, as 85% of its top 200 commercial partners have worked with the firm for 20 years or more.

In the 12 months ended Aug. 31, 2026, Accenture reported revenue of about $74.2 billion, representing 6.5% growth compared to the prior year. The company delivered GAAP net income of approximately $8.4 billion during this period. This steady growth reflects a consistent demand for digital transformation services across its five major industry groups, including communications and financial services.

Accenture maintains a debt-to-equity ratio of about 0.4x, which compares its total debt to shareholder equity. As of its August 2026 balance sheet, the current ratio is approximately 1.4x, indicating it can cover short-term liabilities with short-term assets. In the fiscal year ended Aug. 31, 2026, free cash flow was about $11.6 billion, representing the cash left after capital investments.

Intuit serves about 93 million customers through its integrated financial technology platform, which includes household names like TurboTax and QuickBooks. The company provides essential tools for tax preparation, small business accounting, and credit monitoring to a global user base. Its strategy focuses on an AI-driven ecosystem that automates business workflows and financial management for consumers and mid-market enterprises.

In the 12 months ended Oct. 31, 2026, Intuit achieved revenue of about $21.9 billion, which marked a 12.8% increase over the previous year. The company generated GAAP net income of approximately $4.8 billion during this same period. These results highlight the performance of its financial technology ecosystem, particularly as it expands into larger enterprise markets while maintaining its core consumer base.

Intuit reported a debt-to-equity ratio of about 0.4x, while free cash flow reached about $8.7 billion in the fiscal year ended July 31, 2026. As of its July 2026 balance sheet, the current ratio of approximately 1.5x suggests it can meet short-term obligations. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 23.3% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Accenture faces intense competition from global technology providers like Amazon (AMZN +3.96%), offshore IT firms, and specialized consultancies. The company also manages significant cybersecurity risks, as sophisticated attacks targeting sensitive client data could damage its reputation and financial standing. Additionally, uncertain economic conditions may lead clients to reduce or defer spending on major digital projects.

Intuit faces aggressive competition from both established entities and emerging start-ups, including potential government-funded tax software alternatives. The company also relies on third-party relationships with financial institutions and digital platform stores like those operated by Apple (AAPL +0.83%). Disruptions in these partnerships or shifts in generative AI accuracy could harm operations and customer trust.

Accenture offers a lower P/S ratio, measuring price against sales over the past twelve months, while Intuit features a lower Forward P/E using future earnings estimates.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

I'd go with Intuit. Millions of people and small businesses count on its software every day for taxes and bookkeeping, from TurboTax to QuickBooks, and Intuit has built AI into those tools to handle chores like payroll, paying bills, and sending invoices. What impresses me is the momentum: Sales rose 12% year over year in its latest quarter, and customers can already use QuickBooks right inside popular AI chatbots.

Intuit does have things to work through, though. Its growth is expected to slow to about 8% to 9% this fiscal year. Even so, its AI push gives it room to keep growing.

To be fair to Accenture, it's a giant at helping companies put AI to work, and it returns lots of cash to shareholders. But analysts keep asking whether AI will let clients pay for fewer consulting hours, and Accenture says prices fell in many areas last quarter. Only about 3% of last year's 5% growth came from its own business, and it expects just 3% to 6% growth in the year ahead.

Intuit, meanwhile, is growing faster and turning that growth into even bigger profit gains. For long-term investors, a software leader with momentum like this makes Intuit the better buy for 2027.
