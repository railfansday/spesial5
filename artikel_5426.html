Stocks rise on Black Friday as investors set to close a shortened, volatile week

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The Dow rose 507 points, or 1%, while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq gained more, with the three indexes on track for weekly gains

The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 507 points, or 1%, on Friday, while the S&P 500 gained 1.2% and the Nasdaq $NDAQ Composite added 1.5%. Investors were preparing to end a trading week shortened by the Thanksgiving holiday.

The three indexes were on track to finish the week higher. Including Friday's gains, the Dow was up 5.3% on the week, with the S&P 500 up 5.1% and the Nasdaq ahead 5.8%. The Dow was tracking toward its largest one-week gain since April 2025, according to CNBC.

U.S. markets were closed Thursday for Thanksgiving. The New York Stock Exchange and Nasdaq are scheduled to close at 1 p.m. ET on Friday. Gains extended beyond tech stocks at the open.

The week began with Nvidia $NVDA reinstating Jensen Huang as CEO on Monday, according to CNBC. Nvidia stock rose 31% in that session, and the Dow gained more than 1,400 points. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq each logged their strongest single-session showings since April 2025.

The S&P 500 and Nasdaq have since lost ground as Nvidia and other AI-linked shares surrendered part of Monday's advance. Sentiment on AI shares had already soured before Huang left the company earlier this month, according to CNBC. Investors had grown uneasy over debt financing, indications that capital spending was cooling and a slower pace of data center development after Democratic victories in the midterms, CNBC said.

Richard Clode, a portfolio manager at Janus Henderson, said on CNBC's "The Exchange" on Wednesday that the large cloud computing companies need to earn a return on their investment and rebuild free cash flow for spending on AI to be sustained. He said AI companies such as Anthropic also need to show revenue growth and profitability.

Some on Wall Street put the latest softness down largely to the possibility that the Federal Reserve will lift interest rates, according to CNBC. This week's newest personal consumption expenditures price index figures strengthened traders' bets that the Fed will hike rates several times in the months ahead, CNBC said.

Warren Pies, co-founder of 3Fourteen Research, told CNBC's "Closing Bell" on Wednesday that the Fed is the intermediate-term factor behind the moves. He said "many" of those with whom his firm had talked were worried about a "replay of 2018," a year in which the central bank lifted rates four times.
