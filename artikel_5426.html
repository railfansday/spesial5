Done right, verification gives loan officers their time back

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

When verification is fragmented, loan officers absorb delays and manual follow-up. A connected workflow pulls income, employment and asset data early in the application, syncs to the LOS and reduces rekeying and repeated document requests.   AI Summary

Verification has a way of becoming the loan officer’s problem.

A missing pay stub turns into an email. An employer that doesn’t respond turns into a phone call. A borrower who missed a verification request needs another reminder. And bank statements tend to be the biggest headache of all: pages go missing, borrowers send photos instead of PDFs or the statements don’t cover enough months, so the request goes out again.

If income, employment and asset verification are handled through separate systems or at different stages of the loan, someone has to keep track of what was completed, what was not and what still needs to happen. Even when the underlying problem sits with an employer, vendor or data source, the loan officer is often the one who has to absorb the delay and explain it to the borrower.

As more lenders move verification earlier in the workflow, often into the point of sale, it is easy to see why LOs might worry that more of that work is being pushed onto them. But moving verification earlier does not have to mean moving more work onto the LO. Done right, it can do the opposite: give loan officers reliable information sooner, reduce manual follow-up and preserve more of the time they need for borrowers, referral partners and new business.

Loan officers know how quickly administrative work can take over a day. Individual verification tasks may be small, but together they eat into the hours an LO could spend advising borrowers, structuring deals, developing referral relationships and bringing in new business. That is the “green time” loan officers need to protect.

For lenders still relying heavily on manual verification, reducing document collection is one of the clearest places to reclaim time. When borrowers can connect directly to their payroll provider, employer or financial institution, the lender can receive income, employment and asset data without asking the LO to collect every piece manually. That means fewer pay stubs to chase, fewer employer responses to track down and fewer borrower reminders to send.

A direct connection to the borrower’s financial institution also solves much of the pain with bank statements because far less time is spent on things like combing through to identify and source deposits. But automation is only part of the equation. Timing matters, too.

Traditionally, verifications happened after application, once a file reached processing or underwriting. More lenders are now moving those steps to the point of sale, while the borrower is already completing the application. From the LO’s perspective, that can look like more work moving closer to the borrower relationship they manage.

A well-designed workflow does the opposite. When verification is built into the application experience, the borrower can complete it while already engaged in the process by connecting directly to their accounts instead of manually entering income, employment and asset information in the form fields.

That gives borrowers a faster, simpler application, which makes for happier borrowers, and it gives the LO reliable information sooner without creating another request to initiate or chase later. That earlier visibility also gives the LO more time to address missing information or a financial scenario that needs a closer look before it becomes a downstream surprise.

Moving verification earlier does not help much if the workflow surrounding it is fragmented. When income, employment and assets live with different providers, the resulting data can land in disconnected systems. Loan officers and operations teams may have to toggle between platforms to confirm what’s been completed and rekey information into the loan file.

This fragmentation also creates friction for borrowers, who may not know verifications are the cause, but who will certainly notice they are waiting or being asked for another document. In the worst case, that frustration costs the lender the loan. Even when borrowers stay, verification delays stretch application-to-close timelines, negatively affecting performance metrics that may factor into LO compensation and creating additional costs for the lender.

A connected verification experience brings income, employment and assets into one borrower flow and syncs the resulting data back to the LOS in real time. That means fewer systems for lending teams to manage, less information to rekey and fewer separate requests for borrowers to complete, helping keep the file moving without adding more work for the LO. Direct-source data can also be refreshed when reverification is needed later in the loan cycle, without sending the borrower back through another round of document collection.

Done right, verification takes administrative work out of the loan officer’s day instead of simply moving it from one stage of the process to another. It gives LOs better information earlier, keeps borrowers moving and protects more of the green time loan officers need to serve customers and grow their businesses.

Loan officers notice which lenders give them verification technology that protects their selling time and which ones expect them to work around operational friction.
