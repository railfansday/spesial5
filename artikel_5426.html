Nvidia’s Jensen Huang resigns and CFO Colette tapped as interim chief

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Nvidia’s board of directors accepted Chief Executive Jensen Huang’s resignation, the company announced Thursday, an ouster that follows his admission of having an extramarital affair with a woman U.S. intelligence officials deemed to be a Chinese spy. The decision by the chipmaking giant deals a swift end to Huang’s 33-year reign as CEO and is sending shockwaves throughout the technology industry.

In his stead, the board has tapped Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress as interim chief. A steadfast presence with Huang before Wall Street, Kress will be a familiar face and voice with Wall Street as Nvidia navigates through an abrupt CEO transition that promises to be precarious. Nvidia’s board has started a search for a permanent successor to Huang, and will consider internal and external candidates.

The board for the first time has appointed a chair of the board in Harvey Jones. He’s a managing partner with Square Wave Ventures, and joined the board in 1993 when the company was founded. As a veteran who has overseen Nvidia since its earliest days, Jones will likely serve as a conduit for the institutional memory that departs with Huang.

On Wednesday, the New York Times reported that Huang, 63 years old, had also had an extramarital relationship with Kimberly Powell, the company’s vice president of health care. Powell could not immediately be reached for comment. Observers have said the board could not keep Huang in his role following that second reported breach of trust. It’s also unclear whether the U.S. government shared more information with Nvidia privately following his confession.

According to LinkedIn, Powell has been with Nvidia for nearly two decades and previously spent 10 years in AI business development. Nvidia describes her as formerly leading the company’s higher education and research business, alongside Nvidia AI labs.

Huang’s long-term future with the company is not immediately clear. On Sunday, Huang acknowledged being unfaithful to his wife, and Nvidia announced an investigation into the reports that he had been targeted by a foreign intelligence effort. At the time, the board said it had “no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised,” but that its inquiry would examine “all available information.”

Huang co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and has been integral to the company’s success, particularly its magnificent run in recent years. Growth accelerated in the first half of its fiscal 2027, with revenue of $96 billion, up 85% year-over-year. Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress said the company expected 70% revenue growth in fiscal 2028.

Huang reigns supreme over the company. Since its founding, he has created a unique, flat structure in which he sits at the center of most decisions, with about 60 direct reports. Nvidia’s board operates without a chair, and the company has not set out a clear succession plan. It also does not have a chief operating officer. Huang is known to text and call small clients himself.

Nvidia’s succession challenge would not be whether its engineers can keep building chips without Huang, analysts say. It is whether anyone, or any leadership team, can inherit his authority to decide what gets built, secure the industry’s commitment to it, and keep customers confident in the next bet.

The factors behind Huang’s potential removal “hasten a transition the company is not prepared for,” Gil Luria, an analyst with D.A. Davidson & Co., told Fortune.

Nvidia’s board decision would be a forced hand. “There are rules about that; there are laws about that,” Jay Goldberg of Seaport Research Partners told Fortune, speaking about the untenability of Huang as CEO if there were internal policy violations. Nvidia won’t “go away. They’re sold out. It’s all contracted into next year, but it loses a lot of its luster. The chances of making a misstep are higher.”

If Huang is ousted, the biggest questions are who will succeed him in the long run and what plan the company will put in place to fill the power vacuum.

“Rarely has a board faced this much pressure. This is the board’s moment to shine or fade,” Michael Useem, a professor of management at the Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania, told Fortune. “If the board had been doing its job, it would already have a slate of possible successors: people identified as likely successors.”

Business and law experts have said Nvidia’s Kress, the CFO, could be an option for interim CEO. Another option would be to have one of the company’s directors, such as lead independent director Stephen Neal, who is chairman emeritus and senior counsel at law firm Cooley LLP, take over as interim CEO if Huang is removed. Huang’s children and son-in-law also work at the company.

Bloomberg reported Wednesday that Nvidia is working with an outside firm to identify potential replacements.

Jeffrey Sonnenfeld, senior associate dean for leadership studies and Lester Crown Professor in Management Practice at Yale University’s School of Management, compared the Huang saga to what happened at McDonald’s with former CEO Steve Easterbrook, who was ousted in Nov. 2019. The McDonald’s board investigated Easterbrook for a consensual relationship with an employee that involved the exchange of videos and romantic text messages, and then found that other undisclosed, improper relationships had occurred.

Easterbrook was later charged by the Securities and Exchange Commission with making false and misleading statements to investors about the circumstances that led to his termination. Easterbrook didn’t admit or deny the findings, but agreed to bar himself as an officer or director of any public company for a period of five years, and he paid a $400,000 civil penalty.

Huang’s ouster would create a massive hole for the AI industry. Huang has been central to the industry’s rapid growth. His success in creating world-leading graphics processing units, the chips that power AI by handling model training and regular user queries, has been instrumental in ushering in the AI boom and in the U.S.’s leading position.

Few executives in Silicon Valley and beyond exert the level of power and influence over one company and industry. Huang’s institutional knowledge and deep industry connections have been among the reasons tech analysts and other experts have said it would be impossible to replace him. Another reason has been President Trump, who has become a close friend of Huang and stood by the executive since Sunday’s revelations, saying Huang is an “amazing guy” and “honorable man.”

Amanda Gerut is the west coast editor at Fortune, overseeing publicly traded businesses, executive compensation, Securities and Exchange Commission regulations, and investigations.
