Washington Senator Maria Cantwell, one of the top Democrats on the Senate commerce committee, has taken fire at the Department of Commerce (DOC) under Secretary Howard Lutnick for neglecting to enforce protections intended to keep Nvidia’s AI chips away from China. In a letter to Lutnick on Sunday, Cantwell said the DOC has “undermined America’s national security,” and that its failures “help give away America’s technology advantage in AI.”

“Despite clear authority from Congress, the Department of Commerce has allowed advanced AI chips to flow through companies with ties to the People’s Republic of China (PRC). The Department of Commerce has resisted efforts to restrict companies flagged as national security threats, weakened safeguards, and left open pathways for the PRC to train advanced AI models using sensitive American technology.”

In her letter, Cantwell specifically calls out instances when the DOC reportedly didn’t heed warnings from White House and intelligence officials to restrict Megaspeed’s access to AI chips, prior to allegations that former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang had an affair with a then-Megaspeed executive suspected of being a Chinese spy. Cantwell also criticizes the DOC for allowing Alibaba and ByteDance to access Nvidia chips through overseas data centers, and extending a loophole that let restricted companies obtain American AI technology through subsidiaries.

The DOC is also accused of “putting business interests ahead of national security,” with Cantwell noting that Cantor Fitzgerald, the financial services firm formerly led by Lutnick and now steered by his sons, reportedly held $1.2 billion in Nvidia stock at the time of Lutnick’s nomination for Commerce Secretary.

The letter comes ahead of a meeting in China this Thursday between US President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping. While Congress is looking to bolster export restrictions around AI chips, The Wall Street Journal reports that Lutnick is instead expected to unveil a new initiative that will designate Nvidia — along with companies including OpenAI, Anthropic, Amazon, and Microsoft — as an AI “national champion.” The designation reportedly aims to help these companies expand overseas and compete with Chinese technology firms by granting them expedited export licence approvals.

“America cannot maintain its leadership in AI if the agency responsible for protecting that advantage fails to enforce its own controls,” Cantwell said, closing her letter. “The Administration must account for its failure to enforce these protections and take immediate action to prevent America’s most sensitive technology from strengthening our adversaries.”
