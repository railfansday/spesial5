There is no shortage of choices for a cruise to the Caribbean — the most popular cruise destination in the world. More than 200 cruise ships spend at least part of every year in the region, and cruise-selling websites list thousands of individual Caribbean sailings.

This can be great news for would-be Caribbean cruisers, but it can also be overwhelming. With so many options, where do you even begin? Which one of these ships and sailings is the best?

We get the latter question a lot, and we always answer the same noncommittal way: It depends. The best ship and sailing in the Caribbean for whom? The best for us? The best for you? The best for your kids?

When picking the best cruise in the Caribbean, as with choosing the best cruise anywhere, a lot comes down to personal tastes and preferences. Some people love the big-resort feel of the biggest-of-big ships. Others are horrified by the very idea of them. Some people demand (and are willing to pay for) the highest levels of luxury. Others are on tight budgets. The best cruise for a family with kids isn’t necessarily the same as the best cruise for a couple looking for romance.

What kind of cruiser are you? It’s important to think that through before narrowing down your choices.

The bottom line is that the best Caribbean cruise for you depends a lot on your travel style. Below, we'll help you narrow your choices by looking at Caribbean cruises by category. The good news is that in the Caribbean, there really is something for everyone.

If your idea of a great vacation is a week at a big, bustling resort filled with every sort of amusement known to humans, you'll probably want to start your Caribbean cruise search with a look at the biggest vessels from lines like Royal Caribbean.

If "more is better" is your mantra, skip the search and look up Legend of the Seas, the newest of the Icon class ships.

At nearly 250,000 gross tons, the 20-deck-high vessel is among the world’s biggest cruise ships, alongside its sister ships Icon of the Seas and Star of the Seas.

Some of the wow factors on these Icon Class ships include massive water parks, cantilevered infinity pools, huge glass domes encompassing an indoor AquaTheater for acrobatic and diving shows, and innovative new cabin and suite layouts for families.

Legend of the Seas will depart Fort Lauderdale, Florida, on six-, seven-, and eight-night Western and Southern Caribbean itineraries.

Southern Caribbean sailings head to the ABC islands and stop at Perfect Day at CocoCay. Prices at about $1,509 per person, based on double occupancy.

Western Caribbean itineraries call at Costa Maya and Cozumel in Mexico as well as at Perfect Day at CocoCay. Prices start at $1,264 per person, based on double occupancy.

With at least two sea days per sailing, passengers will have ample opportunity to explore all the attractions the ship has to offer.

In the family cruise arena, it’s hard to top Disney Cruise Line. As you might expect, the line caters heavily to families in every way, from onboard attractions to family-friendly cabin configurations.

Disney's special brand of magic translates just as well at sea. Kids receive the royal treatment with high-quality kids’ clubs and onboard attractions such as the 765-foot-long AquaMouse water coaster. Plus, there are plenty of chances for photo ops with their favorite Disney characters.

Parents will find opportunities to unwind at adults-only sundecks and pool areas that are closed to kids; there are also bars and clubs that are off-limits to anyone younger than 18.

Additionally, many cabins on Disney ships feature extra pull-down bunks and sofa beds, allowing four or even five people to stay in a single cabin. Most cabins have two bathrooms — one with a sink and a toilet, and the other with a sink and a shower or tub. This is rare in the cruise world, and it's designed to make it easier for families sharing a room to get ready.

For family fun on the high seas, try a seven-night Eastern Caribbean cruise aboard Disney Treasure. Departing from Port Canaveral, the ship stops in Tortola, British Virgin Islands; San Juan, Puerto Rico; and Castaway Cay, Disney's private island.

Sail on a themed cruise for Halloween or Christmas, and you can enjoy special onboard activities and events.

Prices for seven-night Caribbean sailings start at $3,648 for two people sharing a cabin.

When it comes to affordability, the undisputed leader in the Caribbean cruise market is Carnival Cruise Line. Not only does Carnival offer lower fares than you'll find at most rival lines, but it also purposely deploys its Caribbean-focused vessels to a wider variety of U.S. home ports than any other line. The idea is that a large percentage of the U.S. population can reach a Caribbean-bound Carnival ship by car, saving the cost of flights.

Carnival ships are packed with fun-focused attractions, including multiple pool areas, water parks with waterslides, basketball courts, miniature golf courses and even roller coasters on select ships.

The cruise line is known for its multitude of included-in-the-fare dining spots. Every vessel has two main dining rooms and a casual buffet eatery. Most ships feature two of the best quick-serve poolside dining venues you'll find on mass-market ships at sea: BlueIguana Cantina for burritos and tacos, and Guy's Burger Joint.

Carnival ships sail to the Caribbean from all the major Florida cruise hubs but also from ports as far-flung as Baltimore; Norfolk, Virginia; Mobile, Alabama; New Orleans; and Galveston, Texas.

The line's seven-day Western Caribbean sailings from New Orleans on Carnival Liberty are a solid choice for budget-minded travelers seeking an affordable Caribbean escape from the middle part of America. Stops include the Honduran island of Roatan; Belize City, Belize; and Cozumel.

Rates for seven-day sailings out of New Orleans start at $444 per person, based on double occupancy.

Looking to dip your toes into cruising for the first time, but not sure you'll love it? A great option in the Bahamas and Caribbean region is Margaritaville at Sea.

Founded in 2022, the Florida-based brand is particularly known for short and affordable sailings that offer the perfect entry to cruising for someone who isn't yet ready to commit to something longer and more expensive.

The line's original vessel, Margaritaville at Sea Paradise, operates out of West Palm Beach, Florida, with sailings as short as two days — an unusually short length in a market where most sailings are at least three nights or longer. These short two-night sailings start at just $189 per person, based on double occupancy.

At that length and price, even if you discover cruising isn't for you, you haven't committed all that much in time and money to figuring that out.

But you may very well find that you love it — particularly the sort of cruising that Margaritaville at Sea offers.

With its ships themed around the signature laid-back, Jimmy Buffett–inspired aesthetic that Parrotheads have come to love, the brand leans into a fun, social atmosphere (as the song says, "It's five o'clock somewhere"). It's more laid-back than the traditional party-focused vibe you find on some lines.

And should you eventually want to try a longer cruise, Margaritaville at Sea now offers those, too. In addition to two-night sailings, Margaritaville at Sea Paradise also operates three-, four- and five-night voyages out of West Palm Beach. The addition of a second ship in 2024 — the Tampa-based Margaritaville at Sea Islander — brought sailings as long as 10 nights.

Rates for four-night sailings out of West Palm Beach start at $149 per person, based on double occupancy.

When it comes to solo cruisers, the king of the hill in the Caribbean is Norwegian Cruise Line — at least, among the big-ship lines. In 2010, the Miami-based cruise operator began adding entire zones for solo travelers to the center of every new ship it deployed to the region.

You’ll now find these zones on Caribbean-focused vessels like Norwegian Encore, Norwegian Breakaway, Norwegian Getaway, Norwegian Epic and Norwegian Prima. Each of these solo cruiser zones, which are unrepeated in the industry for now, includes dozens of cabins for solo travelers, all clustered together. There is also a private lounge with a bar and a TV where solos can mingle at daily happy-hour gatherings.

Known as Studio cabins, the tiny solo rooms in these complexes measure just 100 square feet, but they're superbly designed to maximize storage space. The cabins' futuristic design and the multicolored mood lighting are particularly noteworthy.

One big caveat about Norwegian's solo cabins: Most are interior rooms without an ocean view. Instead, they have windows overlooking a corridor. Another downside is that these cabins are so popular that they often sell out far in advance, sometimes at fares not much lower than fares for a cabin booked for two.

Single cruisers seeking rest and relaxation should consider the seven-night Caribbean sailings aboard Norwegian Luna, which depart from Miami.

The 3,550-passenger ship offers no shortage of restaurants, bars, late-night entertainment, comedy and slick theater shows, including the tribute show, "Elton: A Celebration of Elton John." Norwegian's freestyle dining is another boon for solo travelers. Rather than being locked into set dining times with strangers, passengers are free to dine with newfound friends at a time that suits them.

Norwegian Luna sails the eastern and western Caribbean on seven-night sailings out of Miami. Western Caribbean cruises also include a day at Great Stirrup Cay, Norwegian's private island in the Bahamas, which is home to the Caribbean's largest water park.

Rates for a Studio cabin start at $398 per person.

For those who wouldn’t be caught dead on a vessel that doesn’t have butler service and free-flowing caviar, the Caribbean can be a tough spot. Many of the world’s most luxurious cruise ships spend a lot of time in Europe and Asia or on round-the-world cruises. For some luxury lines, the Caribbean is almost an afterthought.

Luxury sailings in the Caribbean are also concentrated in the winter months, from December through March. Between April and October, options are much more limited.

However, if an upscale cruise experience is what you seek, Seabourn Cruises can deliver. The line's seven-day "Caribbean Gems" itinerary aboard Seabourn Ovation should be on your radar.

Seabourn specializes in small ships carrying fewer than 600 passengers. The line markets its vessels as luxury ships with a yacht-like atmosphere, while still offering familiar cruise-ship amenities, including indoor lounges and multiple restaurants and bars.

The 600-passenger Seabourn Ovation offers all-suite accommodations, with every suite featuring a private verandah. These range in size from 251-square-foot entry-level suites to the 1,306-square-foot owner’s suites.

Along with a lavish spa and fitness center, multiple pools, restaurants and bars, and a dedicated sushi bar, Seabourn Ovation offers butler service and the line's signature caviar service, available anytime, anywhere.

Rates start at $3,279 per person, based on double occupancy.

Virgin Voyages appeals to vacationers averse to the traditional cruising experience who crave a hip, party atmosphere. Ships are blissfully free of children — cruisers must be over 18 — making it a prime choice for an adults-only cruise.

Virgin has unique onboard offerings such as the first tattoo parlor at sea, numerous included dining options, free exercise classes and an app that lets you simply shake your phone to order Champagne (which can then be delivered just about anywhere on board). Fares also include all meals, soda and basic Wi-Fi.

Virgin's first vessel, Scarlet Lady, launched in late 2021, and the line has since added three more ships: Valiant Lady, Resilient Lady and Brilliant Lady.

Ships are filled with stylish, adult-oriented bars promising a hopping scene late into the night, as well as hipster venues like a colorful karaoke lounge. Onboard entertainment is inventive and often edgier than what you might see on traditional cruises.

Cabins sport a minimalist look with futuristic touches, and suites exude a rock 'n' roll vibe with in-room turntables and peekaboo showers.

The line's five-night "Dominican Republic and Bimini Beach Club" itinerary — which visits Bimini, Bahamas, and Puerto Plata, Dominican Republic — is available on multiple ships out of Miami and is a top option. With two sea days, the sailing offers a good mix of onboard activities and port-of-call exploration. Party by the pool or swim with stingrays during your day at Virgin Voyages' Bimini Beach Club. Chill out on a powder-white beach or go waterfall hunting in the jungle in the Dominican Republic.

Rates start at $695 per person, based on double occupancy.

For couples wanting to dial up the romance, Windstar Cruises offers a more intimate way to explore the Caribbean. Its small, yacht-like ships cruise at a leisurely pace, anchoring off small islands and calling at quiet harbors far from the big-ship crowds.

Windstar specializes in small ships carrying between 148 and 342 passengers each. The line calls them yachts, but the ships are more like small cruise ships, with indoor lounges and communal dining rooms.

The line's seven-night "Classic Caribbean" itinerary aboard Wind Surf is a good choice for couples, with stops at some of the region's most appealing islands.

The five-masted vessel carries just 342 passengers and offers four types of accommodations, ranging in size from 188-square-foot cabins to 376-square-foot suites.

None has a private balcony, but the ship's open decks offer plenty of room to take in the views.

One of Windstar's biggest draws is its watersports platform, which opens at the stern when the ship is anchored and conditions permit. Couples can go kayaking or stand-up paddleboarding, swim in the sea or simply float in the water while taking in the views of a harbor dotted with yachts.

Back on board, Wind Surf has multiple dining venues, a pool, hot tubs, a spa and a fitness center. The ship's smaller scale also makes for a less crowded experience than you'll find on the big resort-style ships.

The seven-night "Classic Caribbean" itinerary begins and ends in St. Maarten. Expect sun-kissed beaches, secret coves and gin-clear waters in idyllic ports like St. Barts, St. Kitts and Nevis, Guadeloupe, Dominica and St. Lucia.

All-inclusive rates start at $2,922 per person and cover Wi-Fi, gratuities and unlimited beer, wine and cocktails.
