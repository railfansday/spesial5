Switzerland 4-0 Scotland: Swiss stroll leaves serious question marks over Sebastien Pocognoli as Scots round off miserable campaign with defeat on captain Andrew Robertson's record-breaking night

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

By JOHN MCGARRY, SCOTTISH CHIEF SPORTS FEATURE WRITER

Published: 17:06 EST, 16 November 2026 | Updated: 18:08 EST, 16 November 2026

Despite re-establishing Scotland’s credentials as a credible football nation, by common consent, Steve Clarke’s time in charge of the side was up in the summer.

With qualification for a major tournament no longer satisfying the desires of the Tartan Army, a seven-year itch led to a parting of the ways.

In order to take that elusive step into a knock-out round, the theory went that employing a bolder coach who’d get more out of the players was all that was required. So much for that.

While few would dispute that a change at the helm was required, it was legitimate to question the wisdom in the SFA making one which was quite so radical. Those concerns have only been amplified by what’s been witnessed so far under Sebastien Pocognoli.

The Belgian is young, smart and intense. He won a title in Belgium with Union Saint-Gilloise and will have taken much from his brief, ill-fated spell at Monaco. He wants to play the kind of football supporters long to see.

But this Nations League campaign has been nothing short of dismal. Two wins from six matches, both of those coming against a poor North Macedonia side, and three nights to forget against the Swiss and Slovenia, is a lamentable record.

Andy Robertson endured a miserable night on the occasion of his record-breaking 103rd cap

Notwithstanding the fact that there were always likely to be teething problems, it’s fallen miles short of modest expectations.

Talk of it taking up to 18 months to all come up roses is all very well. But to believe that’s going to be the case, you need to see more green shoots at this point.

Having finished third in the section, Scotland are bound for pot three when the draw for Euro 2028 is made in Belfast next month.

Unless there’s a radical improvement come the qualifiers in March, it’s hard to see how Pocognoli’s side will get there by any means other than the back door.

This was another tough watch for the travelling supporters, one which will only lead to growing concerns about where all this is heading.

For half of a half, Scotland actually looked like they were getting somewhere under their new manager.

Scotland boss Sebastien Pocognoli faces some serious questions over a miserable campaign

From the minute Lewis Ferguson scored a horrible own goal, though, they became a rabble. Not nearly enough punch on the front foot. And far too easy to play through.

They shipped two goals inside five minutes early in the second half and looked like they’d have taken the final whistle there and then.

Individual mistakes from players are troubling Pocognoli in the same way they pockmarked the later days of Clarke’s reign.

Having witnessed Grant Hanley, Scott McKenna and Andy Robertson err in the World Cup, the Tartan Army had already seen Jack Hendry sent off against the Swiss at Hampden before Ferguson joined the growing list of sinners here.

Arriving midway through the first half, his unfathomable moment came when there wasn’t a red shirt in close proximity. It was a kick in the teeth for Pocognoli after a reasonable start.

While it would have been stretching matters to suggest that his team were vulnerable, Murat Yakin was without four of his big hitters in Manuel Akanji, Johan Manzambi, Dan Ndoye and Granit Xhaka.

There was a smattering of starters with five or six caps to their name. Two of the front three, Samuel Ballet and Winsley Boteli, were just 20.

Lewis Ferguson can't believe his misfortune after slicing the ball into his own net for the opener

It was a night when Scottish football history was made with Robertson winning his 103rd cap to finally rise above his great friend, Sir Kenny Dalglish.

Pocognoli stuck by the back-three – Ryan Porteous, Luke Graham and Kieran Tierney – who acquitted themselves well against North Macedonia.

There was no great shock that the more defensive-minded Aaron Hickey replaced Ben Gannon-Doak on the right. Without a goal in 21 matches before a ball was kicked, Oli McBurnie’s selection ahead of Che Adams was intriguing.

The infuriating thing from Pocognoli’s perspective was that his side began with the right attitude.

Aside from an Isaac Schmidt shot which was fielded by Angus Gunn, they looked comfortable.

John McGinn’s pass into McBurnie saw the Hull forward flick the ball into space for Hickey to chase. Just when it looked like he might get a shot away, he was caught.

The positive early approach was typified by Porteous flying into a tackle 30 yards from the Swiss goal and winning the ball.

Winsley Boteli enjoys the moment after putting the stylish Swiss two goals ahead on the night

Had it not been for Hickey hesitating to pick out Scott McTominay’s run, the Napoli man would have been in on goal.

To put it mildly, the Swiss didn’t need an invitation to go down. The appropriately named Samuel Ballet was especially fond of the theatrics.

For a brief spell, Scotland had their opponents hemmed in. Pocognoli was miffed that two quick corners didn’t amount to anything.

Billy Gilmour probed and McTominay made key interceptions and sprayed passes. McGinn fired over.

At the midway point in the first half, there was much to admire. Then disaster struck.

The Swiss did well to advance down the right. Zachary Athekame simply launched a cross into the box and hoped for the best.

Aaron Hickey endured a painful injury blow and could now face another spell on the sidelines

With Ferguson running back towards goal and Porteous waiting to boot the ball to safety, there was no obvious danger.

For reasons only he could explain, the Bologna man stretched out a leg and watched on in horror as the ball sailed past Gunn. All that hard work undone in one desperate moment.

Hickey passed up a chance to immediately level when he headed Robertson’s cross wide. It was to be the Brentford man’s last meaningful involvement before he felt his hamstring tighten. Nathan Patterson replaced him.

A gift of a goal gave the Swiss a boost. Boteli launched himself at Alvyn Sanches’ cross. Gunn was untroubled.

Patterson did well to head Robertson’s deep cross back into the danger zone. McGinn’s header was straight into the arms of Yvon Mvogo.

For all Scotland were entitled to feel a little sore at being one down at the break, it could have been worse. The defence were completely static as Adrian Bajrami launched himself at a header. It was a good thing, from Gunn’s point of view, that the defender missed the target.

Alvyn Sanches drills home Switzerland's third goal in what was an entirely one-sided contest

Ferguson truly had a night to forget here. Within two minutes of the restart, he needlessly invited pressure when he launched a ball straight out of play.

Within seconds, with Porteous advanced, the Swiss broke down the left. Ballet fed Remo Freuler who returned the favour. Ballet rolled the ball towards goal from 12 yards with Boteli claiming the final touch. The visitors’ hopes of a VAR reprieve for offside were dashed.

And with that, the roof caved in. Patterson was dispossessed and Boteli worked the ball into the feet of Sanches. You could only admire the finish, but Scotland were an absolute mess in the build-up. It was threatening to get ugly.

With Tierney, Scotland’s best player on the night, lying on the deck, Ballet was only denied by Gunn. The Tartan Army looked at the clock and wished it was over.

With a minute remaining, a bad night got worse when Zeki Amdouni’s deflected shot beat Gunn.

Mercifully, this tortuous Nations League campaign can now be consigned to history. It’s been the most chastening experience for all concerned.
