Trump says it would be 'hard' to work with Chuck Schumer: 'He will fail again'

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Political strategists Tiffany Smiley and Al Mottur join 'The Faulkner Focus' to analyze the growing progressive push for a sweeping legislative agenda on Capitol Hill. The panel debates whether Sen. Chris Van Hollen's challenge against Sen. Chuck Schumer for Senate majority leader exposes deep fractures within the Democratic Party or represents a routine internal disagreement.

President Donald Trump predicted it would be "hard" to work with Sen. Chuck Schumer, D-N.Y., if he is chosen to continue leading Senate Democrats next year.

Trump wrote on Truth Social on Sunday that Schumer is likely to "fail again" if he is elected. Schumer is running for Senate majority leader once again, but opposition to his leadership is gaining steam.

"It is going to be very hard for me to work with Chuck Schumer should he become Senate Majority Leader. He failed badly as 'Leader' once before, one of the worst in history, and I have little doubt that he will fail again," Trump wrote.

"All he did was perpetuate the Russia, Russia, Russia Hoax, and many other Hoaxes and Scams, making my victory in 2024 an easy and glorious one. Here we go again!" he added.

CHUCK SCHUMER AFTER SWEEPING DEMOCRATIC MIDTERM VICTORIES: 'I'LL BE RE-ELECTED AS MAJORITY LEADER'

Schumer's main challenger is Sen. Chris Van Hollen, D-Md., who represents a more radical wing of the party. His base of support includes Sen. Bernie Sanders, I-Vt., who caucuses with Senate Democrats; Sen. Jeff Merkley, D-Ore., and Sens.-elect Abdul El-Sayed and Peggy Flanagan, both progressives whom Schumer did not initially back in their respective races.

SCHUMER'S FORMER ALLY MOUNTS LEADERSHIP CHALLENGE AS DEMOCRATS FACE RARE INTERNAL POWER STRUGGLE

Sen. Elissa Slotkin, D-Mich., also threw her support behind Van Hollen last week.

Schumer, who has led Senate Democrats for nearly a decade as both minority and majority leader, so far has 15 senators in his camp, according to previous Fox News Digital reporting.

Sen. Chris Van Hollen, D-Md., speaks as he campaigns for Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed during a campaign rally in Ferndale, Michigan, July 25, 2026. (Rebecca Cook/Reuters)

The longtime Democratic leader has remained confident, but quiet, about his chances of retaining his job, posting shortly after Van Hollen’s announcement last week that he welcomed "discussion about the future of our caucus and the important work ahead," and that he has "the votes to continue serving as Democratic leader."

Fox News' Hanna Panreck contributed to this report.
