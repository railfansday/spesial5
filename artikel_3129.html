AMD Stock Has More Than Doubled in 2026. Here's How Its Last 8 Doubles Played Out the Next Year.

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

The chipmaker has seen eight other years like this one since 1981. Did the year after usually reward buyers?

Advanced Micro Devices (AMD +11.04%) shareholders are having a huge year. Trading around $486 as of this writing, the shares are up about 127% in 2026, on top of a 77% gain in 2025.

The company has kept up, too. AMD's third-quarter revenue grew to a record $13.4 billion, a 45% rise from the year-ago quarter, as data center sales almost doubled.

But a year like this isn't new to AMD. Going by year-end closing prices since 1981, the shares at least doubled in eight other calendar years. And the year after split evenly with four gains and four losses.

What does that record tell someone thinking about buying the shares now?

Put simply, investors learned nearly nothing about the next year's direction from a double. AMD rose in 21 of the 45 full calendar years from 1981 through 2025, or around 47% of the time. After a double, it rose in four of eight. Either way, the odds were near a coin flip.

And 2020 almost made the list itself. The shares climbed just under 100% that year, then added another 57% in 2021.

The average follow-up year was far better than the split suggests, with a gain of around 33%. But that average leans on two years, 1983 and 2020, with gains of about 168% and 100%. The other six follow-up years averaged a small loss.

I think the more useful pattern is in the size of the losses. All four of AMD's down years after a double were fairly modest, ranging from a 9% decline in 2017 to an 18% drop in 2024.

A bad year for this growth stock normally looks far worse. AMD fell in 24 of its 45 full calendar years, and its typical decline in those years was around 31%. In 14 of the 24, it lost over 20%, including a 71% drop in 2008 and a 55% slide in 2022.

So, an investor who owned AMD heading into any of the double years kept most of the gain through the next year. The worst case was 2023 to 2024, when a 128% gain was followed by an 18% drop. Someone who held through both years still had around 86% more money than at the start.

Yes, four losing years is a small sample, and the next one might be worse. But the record does argue against the idea that a stock that doubles is due to give it all back. In none of those eight cases did the next year undo the double.

The year isn't over, of course. To finish with a double, AMD has to end December above about $428, or around 12% below where it trades as I write.

Calendar returns can also hide a lot of the ride in a year. AMD's highest close ever, $649.42, came on Oct. 6, when the stock was up around 203% for the year. By its Nov. 2 close of $466.84, it had slid around 28%.

Measured from that peak, AMD has already given back more than a third of its 2026 gain.

The pullback has at least made the price easier to defend. At around $486, the stock has a forward price-to-earnings ratio of about 30, using analysts' consensus estimate for 2027 earnings per share. At the October peak, that ratio would have been about 40.

But paying 30 times the earnings analysts expect for 2027 arguably assumes that sales of AMD's data center chips, including its artificial intelligence processors, keep growing fast.

So, how much of this year's rally should a new buyer expect to keep? Going by AMD's own record, a double alone isn't a reason to steer clear. The year after one was a coin flip, and the bad results were milder than the stock's typical losing years.

But eight years is a thin record. What a buyer should pay today depends on whether AMD's earnings keep up with the valuation. If I were buying now, I'd start with a small stake and expect something closer to the tamer follow-up years than 1983 or 2020.
