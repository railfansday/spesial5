PS5s Are in Short Supply for Black Friday, but You Can Still Buy One

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

There may be a ton of Black Friday deals on electronics, toys, and gadgets out there, but don't get your hopes up if you're looking for a PlayStation 5. Not only are they not discounted, but the combination of international chip shortages, high demand caused by the release of Grand Theft Auto 6, and the general holiday shopping frenzy has also made it nearly impossible to purchase a new, unbundled PlayStation 5 at any price—nearly impossible, but not impossible.

Whether you're looking for a PlayStation 5 Digital Edition, a PS5 with a disc drive, or the flagship PlayStation 5 Pro, you won't find them at major online retailers. PlayStation Direct lists all three consoles as "currently unavailable." Best Buy suggests making an account with them to "improve your odds" of getting one. I can't find a listing for a new PS5 on Amazon either.

You still have some options for obtaining a PS5, but none of them are great. Many refurbished PS5s are selling for well above the list price of a new unit. There are some bundled, special editions available, but you'll likely be paying a ton for those. If you're willing to get your hands dirty by dealing with the wild west of Facebook Marketplace, you might be able to track down a used PlayStation 5 at a reasonable price, but approach with extreme caution.

If you're looking for a refurbished PlayStation 5, the best way to avoid a headache is to focus on who did the refurbishing. Buying refurbished electronics directly from a manufacturer is generally safer than buying from a third party, and in this case, it's cheaper too. PlayStation Direct is offering a "Certified Refurbished PlayStation 5 Console" for the relatively reasonable price of $519, while a refurbished Slim sells for $499. But if you're in the market, I'd lock it in quick: PlayStation Direct is already out of refurbished PS5 Pros.

Amazon's Renewed program has some strict requirements for third parties to stay in the program, like keeping defect rates under 0.8%, but refurbished PlayStation 5s are going for a premium on Amazon at the moment. A quick look at the site reveals that the price of a refurbished PlayStation 5 is hovering near $900; a refurbished PS5 Slim will run you somewhere around $750, and the current average price of a refurbished PS5 Pro has ballooned to over $1,700. These are well above the retail prices of $649 for the standard version, $599 for the digital version, and $899 for the flagship Pro version of the PlayStation 5.

There are some bundled versions of the console listed for sale online too. If you buy one of these, you're getting a new console and some other piece of hardware or a game, but there's a ton of upcharge at the moment. They're generally more expensive than the original price and more expensive than refurbished units as well. Here are some examples:

Best Buy lists this PS5 Pro with PlayStation Portal Remote for $2,134.99.

GameStop lists a Ghost of Yotei Gold Limited Edition PS5 bundle for $769.99 ($731.49 for GameStop Pros).

Best Buy lists a PS5 Slim with an external drive for $979.99.

By far the largest used market for anything in the U.S. is Facebook Marketplace, including PlayStation 5s. I checked out my local Marketplace, and there are a lot of listings for the console within a 40-mile radius of Los Angeles. The average price of used PS5 consoles here is hovering between $300 and $500, which seems fairly reasonable. But with Facebook Marketplace, the number of potential red flags rises with the demand for the product.

I've bought and sold a fair number of things on Marketplace with no problems beyond the occasional flaky person, and I've written a list of hacks for the service, but I wasn't covering a "shortage buying" situation like this. Scams tend to rise with demand, so when I see a listing for a $150 PS5 console (that contains the text "$250 or best offer"), I'm suspicious. It could have been posted by someone who doesn't know what they have, but I wouldn't count on it. If you're buying a PS5 on Marketplace, check the model and generation details very carefully against the actual item. Make sure the seller is offering what you want and not a PlayStation 4 with PS5 games. Verify how it was stored and the condition it's in. But it ultimately can come down to vibes: Don't let your desire to score a PlayStation 5 get in the way of your gut check about the seller or situation.

In short, yes, Black Friday still offers discounts that can be rare throughout the rest of the year. If there’s something you want to buy, or you’re shopping for gifts, it’s a good time to look for discounts on what you need, especially tech sales, home improvement supplies, and fitness tech. Of course, if you need to save money, the best way to save is not to buy anything.

Follow our Black Friday coverage for the latest and best deals our team finds. Another good tip is to only buy what you need. It sounds simple, but making a list of the things you want before jumping into a sale with millions of products helps you focus and make smarter decisions.
