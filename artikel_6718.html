5 Designer-Approved Ways to Make a Small Bathroom Feel Full of Personality

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

When decorating a small bathroom, it's tempting to stick to the tried-and-tested rules. White walls to make the space feel bigger, minimal accessories to avoid clutter, and perhaps a neutral tile or two. But where's the fun in that?

Just because your bathroom is short on square footage doesn't mean it has to be short on personality. In fact, smaller spaces can be the perfect place to experiment with colors, patterns, and decorating ideas you might hesitate to try elsewhere in your home.

Whether you're working with a compact family bathroom, a downstairs WC, or a tiny en suite, here are five ways to give your space a little more character.

White might be the traditional choice for small bathrooms, but it's certainly not the only option.

Bold colors can make a compact space feel intentional and full of personality, whether you opt for a rich, cocooning shade or something brighter and more playful.

You could paint the entire room for maximum impact, or introduce color through a single wall, vanity unit, or painted woodwork. For a more cohesive look, try picking out a shade from your tiles and repeating it elsewhere in the room.

If there's one room where you can afford to be a little adventurous with tiles, it's the bathroom.

Colorful square tiles, checkerboard patterns, and interesting layouts can all introduce character without taking up valuable floor space.

And you don't necessarily need to tile every wall either. A bold backsplash behind the basin, a tiled shower enclosure, or a contrasting border can make just as much impact.

For a more understated approach, consider using simple tiles in an unexpected color or experimenting with contrasting grout.

When space is limited, every surface counts—and your bathroom windowsill is the perfect place to introduce a little personality without cluttering your countertops. Pictured here, Annie Sloan's bathroom features a sweeping landscape painted right on the bath panel.

Rather than leaving it bare, use it to display a few carefully chosen accessories. A small vase of fresh flowers or a decorative ceramic ornament can make an otherwise functional space feel more considered.

You could even create a little display by grouping objects of different heights and textures, taking inspiration from the way you might style a shelf in your living room.

Just be careful not to overcrowd the space. A few thoughtfully chosen pieces will make much more impact than a windowsill packed with accessories, and you'll still want to leave enough room to open the window for ventilation.

Houseplants aren't just for living rooms and bedrooms. They can bring a welcome splash of color and life to a bathroom, softening hard surfaces and making even the smallest space feel more inviting.

Bathrooms can be particularly well suited to tropical plants that appreciate humidity, such as ferns, peace lilies, and orchids, provided they receive enough natural light.

If you're short on floor space, consider hanging a trailing plant from the ceiling or placing one on a wall-mounted shelf. A pothos cascading from a high shelf can look particularly striking against colorful tiles.

And don't forget about the pots themselves. Colorful ceramics and woven planters can be just as decorative as the greenery they hold.

Sometimes, it's the smallest details that make the biggest impact.

Swap plain towels for colorful or patterned designs, introduce a sculptural soap dispenser, or choose a mirror with an interesting frame.

A brightly colored bath mat can bring an otherwise neutral scheme to life, while coordinating toothbrush holders, storage jars, and trays can make everyday essentials feel more considered.

The trick is to choose a few accessories that complement one another, rather than filling every available surface.

And if you're decorating on a budget, this is one of the easiest ways to experiment with a new look without making any permanent changes.
