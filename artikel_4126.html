8 Long JRPGs That Are Best Played in Switch 2 Handheld Mode

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Murillo Zerbinatto is a JRPG enthusiast, writer, and editor. He has played over 300 RPGs during his gaming career, with 90% of them being JRPGs.

Many JRPGs are long by nature, whether because of seamless exploration, a narrative packed with colorful dialogue, or a post-game that increases our playtime considerably. The more adulthood knocks at the door, the less time we have to dedicate to our favorite hobby, and well, if that hobby involves playing JRPGs as mine does, you know you are in a bind.

As we approach the end of 2026, our lives are going to get even busier as the days pass, so why not add a hundred hour adventure or two to the mix?

One way to keep up with JRPGs is to buy a handheld console, which is becoming increasingly common. To ensure you have a packed catalog that gets new games every day, nothing beats having a Switch 2 at home. So, keeping portability, comfort, and time constraints in mind, I gathered some of the longest JRPGs best played in Switch 2 handheld mode.

When Final Fantasy XIV was first announced for Switch 2, I was like: okay, neat! More ways to play. As someone who has played the MMORPG for 13 years on PlayStation consoles, starting on PS3, I couldn't grasp the full potential of that announcement. Then, a friend of mine started the Free Trial, and it hit me after he shared his experience. MMORPGs are, almost by nature, a repetitive endeavor, with dailies, beast tribe quests, crafting, gathering, events, and whatnot. Doing all that in handheld mode? It is the Will of the Light.

FFXIV runs very well on Switch 2, so it's worth it even for veterans or those just starting out. Grinding was never this satisfying. The only caveat is that you have to buy the base game plus expansions again and pay a separate monthly subscription using a token made by Nintendo. But once you surpass these hurdles, portable Eorzea awaits.

I did a reverse process that left me a bit spoiled: I played Persona 5 before playing Persona 3, the portable version. That left me a bit tired of Tartarus, which felt too repetitive, even though I understood it was a product of its era. Persona 3 Reload improved the Tartarus dungeon crawling by removing random encounters and adding gimmicks like changing dungeon rules and even Shuffle Time.

Before each full moon in Persona 3, there is a bunch of downtime, just evolving Social Links, doing daily chores, and increasing your protagonist's stats. They are all a joy to watch, but since they are less heavy-loaded than the main story, you can easily enjoy them wherever you want to take your Switch 2 and just enjoy handheld mode.

I remember perfectly when I started playing Dragon Quest XI and assumed I liked the slime franchise more than Final Fantasy. After advancing a bit further, I realized I still like Final Fantasy more. Still, Dragon Quest XI is so spectacular and such a stunning evolution of the Dragon Quest formula that I fell in love immediately. DQXI mixes the simplicity and classic feel of its predecessors while modernizing and expanding all features and mechanics, making it the most ambitious title ever made in the series.

Like every Dragon Quest, the JRPG features many, many side objectives, exploration, collectibles, and of course, a robust post-game. To this day, I don't know if Act 3 is canonical or post-game, but either way, expect hundreds of hours simply to complete the main campaign and much more if you want to do everything. And there is nothing better than doing all of that on Switch 2.

Normally, I'd recommend Trails in the Sky 1st Chapter and 2nd Chapter, because they are flawless JRPGs remakes that will guarantee you hundreds of hours of playtime. However, considering I have already recommended them countless times, and in the hope that some of you heeded my call, the next step would be to go to the next arc, Crossbell, featured in the duology Trails from Zero and Trails to Azure.

Coincidentally, both recently received a Switch 2 version, adding more quality-of-life features and visual upgrades. I recognize that the whiplash of coming from a full-fledged remake like the Trails in the Sky versions to the graphical style of Zero and Azure can be a bit shocking, but hear me out: if you don't mind that and manage to finish both, reaching the ending of Trails to Azure, your mind will be blown away, trust me. Plus, there is never a wrong time to meet the members of the Special Support Section.

For when an open world feels like too much. Check out these JRPGs that don't feature an open world.

One of the biggest criticisms I read about Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave was its runtime. Players are loving Nintendo's tactical JRPG, but constantly complain that even after 100 hours, it still doesn't feel close to the end. Honestly, it seems to me that this protest is not because they are not having fun or are tired of the game, but because Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave came out in a period packed with other games, and well, you want to play it all.

But forget about that. There is no time limit on your fun, so just grab your weapons, choose your route, and enjoy your protagonist's story. Fortune's Weave introduces emblematic gameplay changes, freshening up the whole loop while still keeping elements Fire Emblem fans have learned to love and appreciate.

Honestly, I could put any Xenoblade Chronicles here, and it would fit. All of the games received, or will receive in the case of Xenoblade Chronicles 3, an enhanced version for the Nintendo Switch 2 that even added new content. And while all of them feature an immense open world you can explore from the get-go, if you want to play more leisurely in handheld mode, Xenoblade Chronicles X is the way to go.

Xenoblade Chronicles X also has plenty of story and characters to get to know, but director Tetsuya Takahashi said his goal in creating the game was to test the Wii U's technological capabilities with a gigantic world. And the madman did it. You will certainly spend much, much more time doing side content, installing probes, facing Nemeses, and flying around with your Skells than on the main story. And do you know the best way to do so? Yes, Switch 2 full-armed handheld mode.

Metaphor: ReFantazio, just like many Atlus games, is an effortlessly long JRPG. With its calendar system, every day brings a new activity, keeping the experience fresh and engaging us without us even noticing. Then, whenever I got tired of roaming around a new city, I could go hunt some monsters, tackle a new dungeon, or watch some impressive story beats unfold in this political race.

The entire design of Metaphor: ReFantazio is highly gripping, and I was completely consumed by the JRPG without even seeing the hours go by. Come to think of it, maybe suggesting playing Metaphor in handheld mode is a bit dangerous, because if I lost track of hours sitting in an uncomfortable chair, imagine if I could play it on my Switch 2 lying in bed with the air conditioning on. Okay, scratch that, too fun, too dangerous.

In its most recent fiscal report, Square Enix showed great profits and dedicated much of that success to the performance of Final Fantasy VII Rebirth on Switch 2. I understand that in terms of visual quality and performance the JRPG leaves a bit to be desired on Switch 2 compared to other platforms, but in overall experience, gameplay, and comfort, it certainly triumphs. The sprawling open world of Rebirth becomes much more practical when played in handheld mode, especially if you are hunting down minigame trophies.

What took me the longest for the Final Fantasy VII Rebirth platinum was getting high scores in a bunch of Chocobo minigames, Cactuar Crush with Aerith, and of course, Chadley's challenges. That was because I had to stay glued to my TV on my PS5, whereas if I had done it on the couch, all leisurely while watching my son play or my wife watch another K-Drama, the experience would certainly have been much more pleasant. Mind you, story-related moments and cutscenes should have your full attention, especially during the Gold Saucer!

Where do you even begin with JRPGs? These titles will provide a gentler welcome for newcomers.
