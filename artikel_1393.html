Where Are They Now? Atletico Madrid’s 2016 Champions League final XI

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

It’s been 10 years since Atletico Madrid last reached the Champions League final and as Diego Simeone tries to mastermind a path there once again, we are taking a look at the players from that final and seeing where they are now.

Atleti lost to their city rivals Real Madrid for the second time in as many finals, as they were denied a first Champions League trophy via penalties.

Sergio Ramos opened the scoring in the 15th minute before Yannick Carrasco levelled the match in Milan, but what about the Atletico XI that started the match? Here’s where they are now.

One of two survivors from that team is Jan Oblak who has racked up 555 appearances for Atleti.

He is still only 33, no age for a goalkeeper, and remains first choice. He looks set to stay for at least two more years as well with his contract expiring in 2028.

The right back left Atleti in 2019 to move to Brazil and Sao Paulo where he spent two seasons before retiring in 2020.

Since retiring, he has become a business owner and pundit. He appears on the Spanish version of DAZN and is CEO of Red Thunder Sports, a sports event agency.

The hardman centre-back is still playing even at the age of 35.

He left Madrid in 2024 to join Trabzonspor and is still with the Turkish side. He is still featuring reguarly too and he’s got Andre Onana behind him now, which will be a lot more stressful than Oblak, we’d imagine.

One of the best centre backs of his generation, Godin left Atletico after a nine-year stint to join Inter Milan in 2019.

But at the San Siro, he fell out with Antonio Conte and left for the relegation-threatened Cagliari, helping them to stay in the league.

Two seasons there, of which he made just 11 league appearances in the second, and he was on the move again, this time for a nine-match stint at Atletico Mineiro in Brazil.

Lacking game time, he moved to Velez Sarsfield in Argentina, where he retired in 2013.

He did though briefly come back out of retirement in February 2024 when he signed for Uruguayan amateur side Porongos. He won the Copa Nacional de Clubes and then retired a second time.

The left back has developed into one of the best young coaches in football and looks set to one day be working at an elite club if his career continues on the same trajectory.

After retiring from playing in 2023, he cut his managerial teeth at his final employer Flamengo in their youth setup.

In 2024, he was given the top job and won the Copa do Brasil, the Carioca, the Supercopa do Brasil, the Copa Libertadores and Serie A but life comes at you fast and he was sacked in March 2026 following a poor start to the season.

That did little to dent his reputation though and he picked up his next job at Monaco. His team are currently seventh in Ligue 1, five points off leaders PSG.

The fall of Saul is one nobody would have seen coming back in 2016.

A year later, he would sign a nine-year contract that was scheduled to end this past summer, but even at the time, he was suffering from a kidney issue that would soon disrupt his career.

During a match against Bayer Leverkusen in 2015, Saul collided with Kyriakos Papadopoulos and had what the Atletico president declared a “destroyed” kidney.

He ignored doctors’ advice to keep going, playing with a catheter, but it all caught up with him. He failed to nail down a position in Simeone’s side and soon fell out of favour.

He moved to Chelsea in one of the more forgettable loan deals and even after he looked to be back in the Atletico first team, he was back on loan in 2024, this time to Sevilla.

His stint with Atletico ended permanently in 2025 when he left Europe to join Flamengo in Brazil where he remains to this day at the age of 32.

Gabi began his coaching career in charge of Getafe’s youth team but took over the B team in November 2023.

In 2025, he was named Zaragoza boss and helped the club avoid relegation to the third division. After a poor start the following year, he was sacked.

In the summer, he returned to Madrid to become Simeone’s assistant, replacing former Arsenal man Nelson Vivas.

Torres was on loan at Atletico when they reached the Champions League final but he rejoined permanently in the summer, staying until 2019 when he left for Japanese side Sagan Tosu.

Griezmann was their best player during this era of Simeone’s Atletico but he had an unfortunate habit of dodging trophies.

He left to join La Liga winners Barcelona in 2019 who proceeded to lose out on the title in his first season. To make it even worse, Atletico won the league in 2020-21.

Having been unable to make a success of his big-money move to Barcelona, he returned to Atletico initially on loan before a permanent move and, you guessed it, Barcelona won the league.

Regardless of his lack of silverware for his club, Griezmann left an Atletico legend and is now tearing it up in the MLS.
