This Is the Number One Cheese You Need on Your Holiday Cheese Board (No, It’s Not Cheddar)

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

I chatted with two cheesemongers to find out exactly what I should be putting on my cheese spreads this holiday season. Surprisingly, a classic cheddar isn't at the top!

After playing Tetris in my oven an hour before a holiday party, I’m always relieved I decided to serve a cheese board as the appetizer. Cheese boards don’t require much time to put together (and take up zero oven space), but I seem to linger on the cheese selection, always asking myself the same question: Which cheeses would everyone like to eat?

So, I sat down with the experts: Molly Browne, dean of mongers at Cheese State University and education director at Dairy Farmers of Wisconsin, and Emilia D’Albero, the first American ever crowned World’s Best Cheesemonger. They gave me the scoop on putting together the perfect holiday cheese board. Molly and Emilia recommend three to five cheeses per board, representing a variety of milk types, styles and flavors.

But which cheese should I buy that everyone would like? More specifically, what feels indulgent but won’t alienate picky eaters? I expected Molly and Emilia to recommend some extremely specific, hard-to-find cheeses, but both suggested varieties I could pick up at the grocery store.

According to Molly, the best cheese for a holiday cheese board that appeals to all ages is butterkase. This semi-firm cow’s milk cheese is creamy, smooth, relatively mild and exceptionally buttery—hence its German name, which translates to “butter cheese.” (If you’re as chronically online as I am, you may have heard of butterkase from @courtneycookinsta, who famously stuffs butterkase in a warm sweet potato and eats it for lunch.)

Butterkase is typically aged for only about a month, so it doesn’t have much funk, making it approachable for the under-10 and over-60 crowd. “I’ve seen my own child pick up a piece of it and eat it like an apple on multiple occasions,” says Molly. But because of butterkase’s slight complexity, Molly explained that it won’t leave those in the middle feeling devoid of flavor or a good eating experience, so it’s a safe bet for a charcuterie board.

Butterkase is made in Wisconsin and sold nationwide, but if you’re having trouble finding it, Gouda is a good alternative, especially Emilia’s recommendation: aged Gouda.

Aged Gouda is a Dutch-style hard cheese that matures anywhere from nine months to five years. As Gouda matures, the soft paste turns firm and speckles with those irresistible crunchy crystals. It takes on an indulgent butterscotch flavor, with subtle tropical notes, so it’s on the sweeter side. The longer the Gouda ages, the more intense those characteristics become. “In my experience, nobody’s ever turned down an aged Gouda,” says Emilia.

Because it’s especially buttery and mild, butterkase pairs well with tangy, acidic foods, like olives, the foods on a relish tray and all kinds of pickles: classic pickles, pickled vegetables and pickled fruit. Their acidity cuts through the richness of the cheese. Butterkase would also be a fantastic addition to a supper club board. Its soft texture is great on crisp crackers studded with nuts and fruits, with a German mustard nearby.

For wine and cheese pairings, opt for something light and refreshing, like Prosecco or an unoaked chardonnay, to balance the richness of the cheese. Even a bottle of inexpensive champagne makes the cheese board feel a little fancier.

Slice butterkase while it’s still cold from the fridge. If it sits for a while, butterkase can become soft enough to spread, making it difficult to slice. As a general rule, Molly and Emilia recommend slicing harder cheeses in advance to get ahead of cheese board prep. Keep backup portions in the fridge to quickly replenish the board as the night goes on.

Aged Gouda pairs well with sweeter fruit jams, chocolate, candied pecans and even tropical fruits like mango. Fruit-and-nut studded crackers work just as well with aged Gouda as they do with butterkase. For dessert, try pairing the cheese with brownies or caramelly stroopwafels. Drink pairings range from red wine to spiced rum.

While there are guidelines for pairing, Emilia encourages people to step out of their comfort zone and explore out-of-the-box options. “There’s no wrong answer to pairing,” she says. “People tend to think that they have to only pair fancy things with cheese, and honestly, some of the best pairings I’ve had have just been snacks or things I love that are lying around the house.” Take a cue from Emilia’s cheese competition days: Try the cheese with whatever you have in the kitchen; you may discover an unexpected favorite. “Get weird with it,” says Emilia.
