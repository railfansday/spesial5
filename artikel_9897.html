How to Assess OT Incident Response Preparedness

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Cloud Range's free 28-question OT Cyber Readiness Assessment helps organizations identify gaps in operational technology (OT) incident response.

Cloud Range introduces a 28-question OT Cyber Readiness Assessment to help organizations identify gaps in preparedness for cyberattacks affecting operational technology.

The assessment covers seven critical areas, from OT asset visibility and IT/OT coordination to incident detection, response, recovery, and readiness validation.

Recent OT cybersecurity research reinforces the importance of preparing IT, security, OT, and engineering teams to investigate and respond to attacks that can disrupt physical operations.

NASHVILLE, Tenn., Nov. 10, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) — Cyberattacks affecting operational technology (OT) can disrupt production, equipment, and essential services, resulting in consequences that extend beyond disruptions to traditional IT systems. To help organizations evaluate their preparedness, Cloud Range today introduced the Cloud Range OT Cyber Readiness Assessment, a free, 28-question resource designed to identify potential gaps in OT incident response preparedness.

The assessment provides a practical starting point for IT, security, OT, and engineering teams to evaluate how well they understand their environments, coordinate responsibilities, and prepare to detect, investigate, respond to, and recover from cyber incidents affecting industrial operations.

“Organizations can have incident response plans, security tools, and experienced teams in place without knowing how effectively those capabilities will work together when an attack affects operations,” said Debbie Gordon, CEO of Cloud Range. “The first step is understanding where teams are prepared, where they may be relying on assumptions, and where further validation is needed. The stakes are different in OT because decisions made during a cyber incident can have direct operational consequences.”

Recent research illustrates the scale of cyber threats facing industrial organizations. The Dragos 2026 OT Cybersecurity Year in Review reports that approximately 3,300 industrial organizations were affected by ransomware in 2025.

Palo Alto Networks’ 2026 State of Critical Infrastructure Cybersecurity Report found that 59% of surveyed critical infrastructure organizations experienced a significant security breach in the past year. The research also found that 74% had not fully integrated IT and OT security operations, highlighting the importance of coordinated incident response.

The report also identifies operational consequences of cybersecurity incidents, including safety concerns, unplanned downtime, production disruptions, and financial losses.

Together, these findings underscore a central challenge for organizations operating OT and industrial control systems (ICS): preventing attacks is important, but so is understanding whether teams can recognize, investigate, and respond quickly and effectively when incidents occur.

An attack does not have to originate within an OT network to affect industrial operations. Compromises involving enterprise IT systems, third-party access, supply chains, or interconnected infrastructure can create consequences for production environments and critical services.

The Cloud Range OT Cyber Readiness Assessment examines seven areas that influence an organization's ability to prepare for and respond to cyberattacks affecting OT environments:

Know Your Environment: Understand critical OT assets, systems, dependencies, and connections between IT and OT environments.

Know Your Threats and Stakes: Recognize relevant cyber threats and their potential impact on operations, equipment, and safety.

Bridge IT and OT: Establish shared responsibilities, communication, and decision-making across IT, security, OT, and engineering teams.

Detect and Investigate: Identify suspicious activity, investigate threats, and determine their potential operational consequences.

Respond Without Making It Worse: Make coordinated incident response decisions that account for production continuity, equipment, and safety.

Recover and Maintain Resilience: Understand recovery priorities, system dependencies, and procedures for restoring critical operations.

Prove Readiness Before It Matters: Validate response capabilities through realistic cyberattack simulations and measurable performance.

Each area includes three questions, with responses of Ready, Developing, or Unsure.

Rather than assigning a numerical score, the assessment helps organizations identify gaps, uncertainties, and areas requiring further attention. IT, security, OT, and engineering teams can complete it independently and compare their responses to uncover differences in understanding, unclear responsibilities, or differing assumptions about preparedness.

Why OT Incident Response Preparedness Requires Cross-Functional Coordination

OT environments present distinct incident response challenges because cyber decisions can affect physical equipment, industrial processes, production availability, and potentially safety.

Actions that may be appropriate in an enterprise IT environment, such as isolating a system or shutting down a device, can require additional operational evaluation when industrial systems are involved.

Effective OT incident response therefore depends on coordination among teams with different responsibilities and expertise.

IT and security personnel may focus on identifying malicious activity, containing threats, and investigating compromised systems. OT and engineering personnel understand process dependencies, equipment limitations, and the operational consequences of response decisions.

Both perspectives are essential. The integration gap identified in the Palo Alto Networks research reinforces the importance of shared understanding, clear responsibilities, and coordinated decision-making across IT, security, OT, and engineering teams.

Understanding how to assess OT cyber readiness across these functions helps organizations identify where their procedures, communication, and decision-making may require further attention.

The assessment is intended to support those conversations before an actual incident demands coordinated action.

An assessment can help organizations identify preparedness gaps, but it cannot establish how effectively teams will perform during a real cyber incident.

That requires opportunities to investigate attacks, make decisions, coordinate across functions, and evaluate the results in realistic environments.
