Smash Tacos Are The Dinner Hack My Family Never Stops Requesting

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Whether I’m whipping up a quick weeknight dinner for one or feeding the whole family, smash tacos are always a good call. Featuring a layer of crispy meat pressed into a soft tortilla that’s topped with condiments of your choice, they’re a speedy, flexible dinner that readily adapts to whatever you have in your fridge.

While I have tons of experience with smash dumplings, I made my very first smash tacos while developing the recipe for chicken Caesar smash tacos and coming up with the concepts for meatball sub smash tacos and Philly cheesesteak smash tacos. As I quickly learned, smash tacos are pretty simple to make, but if you haven’t done it before, there are definitely a few things to know.

Here are a few tips to make your first experience making them just a little smoother:

Whether you’re mixing in a ton of spices or keeping it simple with just salt and pepper, it’s important to work the ground meat until it feels tacky; otherwise, the meat won’t hold together on the tortilla when you sear it, and you’ll end up with a fragmented patty instead of one smooth layer.

Even if you’re using 90% lean, beef shrinks when it cooks, so if your patty is beef, spread it to the very edge of the tortilla—maybe even a little over. That gives you the best chance of getting full coverage once it’s cooked. Chicken, on the other hand, doesn’t shrink much at all (in my experience), so in that case it’s fine to leave a little border around the edges.

Let the patties sear, undisturbed, for a solid two minutes before flipping them. This gives you the best shot at that caramelized crust, and that’s where all the flavor is!

A smash taco doesn’t always require cheese (see the gyro smash tacos below!), but if you’re going there, I highly recommend using the broiler to melt it. In my experience, Kraft singles are the only type of cheese that will melt completely before the bottom of the smash taco burns. It might be an extra step, but it’s worth it to avoid an acrid bottom.

A truly amazing smash taco needs a plethora of toppings, so don’t hold back here! Load it up. While the OG smash tacos were a mash-up of a smash burger and a taco, you don’t need to limit yourself to classic burger flavors here. Almost any sandwich can be hacked into the smash taco format, but as I’ve discovered, salads are also fair game for inspiration. Here are five of my favorite smash taco flavor profiles:

Taking inspiration from the granddaddy of all burgers, these smash burger tacos feature all the familiar fixings of a classic Big Mac, including American cheese, “shrettuce,” pickles, and Big Mac sauce. All they need is a side of fries.

When you’re craving a salad, but everyone else at the table insists they need “real” dinner, these chicken Caesar smash tacos are the perfect compromise. Ground chicken patties are topped with squiggles of Caesar dressing, a mound of romaine, crispy croutons, and tons of cheese for a dinner that manages to make even a taco feel healthy.

I love ordering a cheesesteak when I’m out, but if I’m craving one at home, these Philly cheesesteak tacos are my lazy go-to. Topped with sautéed peppers and onions and lots of melty provolone cheese, they’re the perfect quick weeknight meal—or late-night snack. And, unlike an actual cheesesteak, you can definitely eat more than one!

Gyros and tacos are iconic all on their own, but now there’s actual proof that they can become something even better when they’re combined. Loaded with shawarma-spiced chicken, cucumber, tomato, tzatziki, and (of course!) shoestring fries, these gyro smash tacos are the definition of over-the-top indulgence.

Forming and baking individual meatballs can take some time, but these smash tacos are the perfect shortcut. Topped with marinara, mozz, and plenty of Parm, they’re bringing all the saucy, cheesy comfort vibes of an actual meatball sub—just in a quicker, more convenient weeknight package. Fresh basil on top is nice if you have it. If you don’t, a little dried oregano also works!
