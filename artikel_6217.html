How to Decide Which Ski Pass Is Best For Your Winter Travel Plans

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

With snow already falling in the Rockies and a strong El Niño weather pattern expected to benefit certain US mountain regions, skiers and riders are already dusting off their gear and booking their winter trips.

Of course, skiing and snowboarding aren’t typically budget-friendly sports. Once you factor in meals, lodging, and transportation in addition to a lift ticket (which costs well over $300 per day at several US resorts), it’s almost worth taking out a loan to pay for a one-week family ski trip.

But in recent years, the industry has moved away from one-day lift tickets in favor of multi-mountain ski passes with heavily discounted rates and additional perks, like Epic’s “Pay Over Time” or Ikon’s deals on friends and family tickets.

The dreamy Big Bear Resort is included on Ikon Pass.

"For those who ski or ride more than six to eight days a season, multi-mountain passes have reshaped the [ski] resort industry,” says Olivia Rowan, publisher of trade magazine Ski Area Management. “The multi-mountain pass opened up new possibilities for snow sports travel and tourism beyond your home hill. They're a great deal for an in-the-know skier or rider.”

Before choosing a pass, determine your skiing goals for the season. If you’re looking to check out a handful of new resorts for a day or two each, the Indy Pass might be your best option. But if you’re hoping to spend a week at a more popular resort like Park City, the Epic or Ikon passes are the way to go. To figure out which pass is best for you and your travel plans, here's a breakdown of the major players.

Best for: Individuals and families who want unlimited access to some of the world's largest and most popular ski areas

When the Vail-owned Epic Pass debuted in 2008, it was the first of its kind and set the stage for the multi-mountain passes that followed in the coming years. Today, it remains one of the biggest players in the industry, offering access to over 90 resorts in North America, Europe, Japan, and Australia.

Pricing: The standard Epic Pass, which offers unlimited access to all its resorts, costs $1,169. But if you don’t plan to ski during holiday weeks and weekends, consider the Epic Local Pass, which excludes peak dates at certain resorts. Epic also offers a customizable pass that lets skiers choose up to 7 days across different resort tiers. The cheapest option, at $337, gives skiers 7 days at 22 resorts in the Midwest, Northeast, and Mid-Atlantic.

Best for: Skiers and riders who want to check out several uncrowded, under-the-radar ski resorts

The Indy Pass, which is the antithesis of larger passes like Epic and Ikon, offers two days of skiing at each of its over 300 partner resorts across four continents. The pass partners with smaller, independently owned resorts that are typically uncrowded and have deeply rooted local vibes. This season, Indy is rolling out an upgrade that lets passholders ski up to five days at 13 resorts, starting at $199. The pass sells out quickly and is already off sale for the 2026-27 ski season, but now is the time to get on the waiting list for next season (sales sometimes reopen mid-season, too).

Pricing: The Indy+ Pass, which doesn’t have blackout dates, costs $469 for adults and $299 for kids 12 and under.

Best for: Skiers who live near one of Ikon’s premier destinations, or families with children who want to take advantage of Ikon’s discounted kids passes

Like the Epic Pass, the Ikon Pass offers unlimited access to some of the world’s most popular ski resorts. The pass includes access to over 70 resorts across 13 countries, including some bigger US destinations like Jackson Hole, Deer Valley, and Aspen Snowmass.

Pricing: Like Epic, Ikon offers tiered passes, with its unlimited pass starting at $1,529 (or $1,129 for young adults ages 13-22 and $499 for children ages 5-12). The base pass, which has some blackout dates, starts at $1,099 for adults over 23. Skiers and riders who want to dip their toes into the sport or who may not have time to get out much during the season should consider a personalized pass that allows two, three, or four days at partner resorts.

Best for: East Coast skiers and riders who mostly plan on skiing in the Mid-Atlantic, Northeast, or eastern Canada, or individuals looking to supplement another multi-mountain pass; It includes two mountains in Colorado and Wyoming, but they are on the smaller side.

New for the 2026-27 ski season, the Snow Pass is the newest player in the game. Like Indy, the Snow Pass offers two days at each partner resort, but so far it has only 14 resorts to choose from, most of which are in the Northeast. You can buy additional days for 30% off the retail price. Snow Pass offers an alternate three-day pass—Snow Triple Play—that includes three days total at 23 partner resorts.

Pricing: The Snow Pass costs $379.99, and the Snow Triple Play costs $189.99.

Best for: Skiers and riders who want to check some big-name resorts off their list but aren’t looking to ski for an extended period at one ski area, or those looking to supplement another multi-mountain or season pass.

The Mountain Collective Pass offers passholders two days each at its 27 resorts in the US, Canada, Japan, Europe, South America, Australia, and New Zealand. It’s a slightly more affordable alternative to the Epic and Ikon, yet it still gives skiers access to big-name resorts like Jackson Hole and Big Sky.

Pricing: The Mountain Collective Pass goes for $729 for adults, $594 for teens, and $304 for kids 6-12. Skiers and riders who want to ski more than their allotted 2 days at a specific resort can purchase day tickets at half off.
