The Tripadvisor-Viator experiences business has long had a "Tripadvisor" brand problem. The consolidation of their teams didn't work, and both experiences revenue and profits fell in the third quarter.

Tripadvisor is cutting jobs in its legacy hotel business as part of a new global restructuring, and CEO Matt Goldberg, whose departure the company announced Thursday, acknowledged that combining its Viator and Tripadvisor experiences teams hasn’t worked as expected.

CFO Michael Noonan is also leaving, the company said.

Tripadvisor is cutting jobs in its legacy hotel metasearch business under a global restructuring, and both CEO Matt Goldberg and CFO Michael Noonan are leaving. Goldberg said the decision to combine the Viator and Tripadvisor experiences teams "hasn't proved out as we expected," a judgment backed by the numbers: third-quarter experiences revenue fell 3% and adjusted EBITDA in the segment fell 27%. Total revenue dropped 11% to $436.4 million, and the company posted a net loss of $316.9 million against net income of $48.5 million a year earlier. Goldberg said Viator and B2B2C experiences bookings grew in the low teens while the Tripadvisor channel declined by high single digits, citing SEO headwinds, weakness in the North America to Europe corridor, and execution problems. The restructuring shifts product engineering and data analytics roles into experiences and is expected to deliver $40 million in gross annualized savings. Tripadvisor said it will keep investigating strategic alternatives and will use the $685 million from the sale of TheFork to American Express to pay down debt. Goldberg leaves February 16, 2027; Noonan departs December 31, with Chief Strategy and Operations Officer Kristen Dalton stepping in as interim CFO.
