Steelers should have known Aaron Rodgers, Mike McCarthy couldn't co-exist

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

When the Steelers hired coach Mike McCarthy, we cautioned against assuming that this meant there would be a reunion between McCarthy and quarterback Aaron Rodgers. That position assumed there would be a commonsense assessment of the specific history of McCarthy and Rodgers in Green Bay by those with the power to bring them together for the present and the future.

The reporting of Tyler Dunne, who at the time wrote for Bleacher Report, made it clear that there were issues between them during the latter years of McCarthy's time with the Packers. Rodgers took it to another level on Wednesday, telling Pat McAfee and company that McCarthy believed Rodgers got him fired by the Packers — and that McCarthy had done some of the things he's been doing with the Steelers when McCarthy coached the Packers.

If McCarthy believes Rodgers got him fired by the Packers, why in the world would McCarthy want Rodgers in his Pittsburgh locker room? It's ludicrous. It's asinine. It's self-destructive.

From Rodgers's perspective, why in the world would he want to play for McCarthy again? Although Rodgers said Wednesday that he believed McCarthy would be different given it's Rodgers's last year in the NFL and McCarthy's first year in his dream job, it was naive for Rodgers to think that, at 63, McCarthy would suddenly start working harder than he ever has.

Oh, they tried to sell us on the idea that he would. "Attention to detail" became a talking point during training camp. Plenty of media members swallowed the hook.

Rodgers knew better. But he ignored his own experiences with McCarthy — in the same way McCarthy ignored his own experiences with Rodgers.

The situation cried out for someone, anyone in the organization to stand up and say, "Are we nuts? Once adversity strikes, this situation will crumble. And it will take us with it."

It didn't just crumble. It imploded into one of the most embarrassing situations any NFL franchise has endured in years, if not ever.

As the saying goes, when you dance with the devil, you don't change the devil. The devil changes you. By allowing McCarthy and Rodgers to reprise their Lambeau rumba, the Steelers thought the music would stop in the postseason. Instead, McCarthy and Rodgers have pulled a franchise with six Super Bowl trophies into the eighth circle of dysfunction.

In the end, the team in the city known for blast furnaces has found itself in football hell.
