Matisse Thybulle drops firm stance on ‘clear’ role for Lakers

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

LOS ANGELES – The Los Angeles Lakers are rolling right now, currently holding the best record in the NBA at 13-2 following the team's thrilling come-from-behind win against the Charlotte Hornets on Tuesday evening. The Lakers' roster depth, which has been questionable at times in recent years, has shined this season, and perhaps none more so than Matisse Thybulle.

While Walker Kessler is the interior anchor of the Lakers' defense, there's an argument to be made that Matisse Thybulle has a similar effect while manning the perimeter. Thybulle is the only player in the league this season that has registered at least 30 steals and 10 blocked shots. Following the win against the Hornets, he is at 34 total steals and 14 total blocked shots.

In a league in which many players sometimes struggle to accept a role, Thybulle has fully embraced the 3&D archetype throughout his career. It's what's kept him in the NBA this long, and it's a role he's played to perfection for the Lakers this season. When the team signed him in the offseason, he knew right away what he was going to be asked to do, and he accepted it. That was made clear when this roster was assembled.

"I think my whole career I've been a very similar player, and embracing that has taken me pretty far. But I think on a team like this, with a roster like this, it's very clear what we're all here to do. And I think Rob [Pelinka] and JJ [Redick] did a great job in just crafting a roster that is able to compliment Luka [Doncic] and AR [Austin Reaves] and Walker [Kessler] in a lot of ways," Thybulle said after Lakers' shootaround on Tuesday morning.

"I think there's not a lot of grey area for us as players to think, what are we supposed to do and bring. It's just doing what you're doing at a high level cause you can trust that our main guys are gonna do what they do."

As good as Thybulle has been defensively this season, there are still times when he finds himself in a pickle. He's been the biggest risk taker on the team in terms of gambling for steals, and it doesn't always work out the way he wants it to. Sometimes the gamble doesn't work and then the Lakers' defense is in a compromised state.

But the numbers suggest that Thybulle is highly effective at what he does. He is currently tied with New York Knicks guard Josh Hart for fifth in the league in deflections per game (4.1). He's second in the NBA in total deflections with 62. His teammates know he's going to knock the basketball free more often than not, and that's why he has more defensive freedom than almost any other player on the roster.

"I think it can be challenging at times to play with someone like me who's taking a lot of risks and gambling and getting out of position and sometimes throwing off the team defense. It can be really easy for them to just be like, 'Matisse keeps messing up our defense, what the hell is he doing,'" Thybulle said. "But they understand and they let me learn from my mistakes and at times, they're really just trying to protect me so I can do these things. I think ultimately, they trust that I'm gonna try and make the right play."

Thybulle has appeared in all 15 games to this point for the Lakers, at just about 25 minutes per game. He's averaging 7.4 points, 2.5 rebounds and 2.3 steals with splits of 43 percent shooting from the field, 38.8 percent shooting from the 3-point line and 84.6 percent shooting from the free-throw line. He's second in the league in steals behind only Atlanta Hawks guard Dyson Daniels (2.9).

The 29-year-old is healthy after injuries limited him the past couple of seasons. And he's provided the Lakers with the defensive spark they've been lacking on the wing. The way that Luka Doncic and Austin Reaves have freedom on the offensive end, Thybulle has that trust from the coaching staff and his teammates on the defensive end.

The majority of the time he gambles, he won't come up with a steal. But the team has encouraged him to continue to do so. Only that when the Lakers' defense is potentially at risk due to his gambling, he wants to make sure the rest of the team is in position to clean up for it if he fails to force the turnover.

"Just trying to find ways for me to be me within the foundation of our defense," Thybulle said. "They like me gambling because it generates turnovers whether or not I'm getting the steal. It's just a matter of doing it in ways where our defense can make up for the mistake if you will."
