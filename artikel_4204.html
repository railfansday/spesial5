Are You A Tired Parent? The Witcher 3's New Accessibility Mode Was Built With You In Mind

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

CD Projekt Red's director of player experience, Karolina Niewęgłowska, has taken to the company's official forum to explain the approach behind a new accessibility preset for The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt - Remastered (thanks, Push Square).

Dubbed 'Bard's Ballad', this option contains a number of individual accessibility toggles that you can tinker with to suit your own needs, or you can simply apply all of them at once as a blanket preset. So for example, this includes disabling item weight and durability along with the ability to disable herb icons on the mini-map.

Niewęgłowska explains that the option has been built for those who may have trouble concentrating, regardless of the underlying reason. It also considers players who may simply be feeling a bit tired, including parents. Here's what she had to say:

"Bard’s Ballad is for those having difficulty concentrating, including those with ADHD, or those experiencing cognitive fatigue, for example due to conditions like depression. But it’s also for folks who simply feel tired or overwhelmed in this busy world. A colleague to whom I described Bard’s Ballad said “so… a tired parent mode”. Very much so. But it is for anyone who wants to use it, to play in a way that fits their needs best."

Essentially then, Bard's Ballad is partially for those who only have limited time at the end of the day and don't want to faff about trying to find replacement weapons or managing inventory space.

"So you have an hour for yourself after a busy day managing a million topics, and you wanna play a game and focus on the story. You load your save, continue your quest, and your weapon immediately breaks. You have to figure out what to replace it with, and where you need to go to fix it. Then your inventory gets full. What to drop, what to sell, what to dismantle, what may be useful? That’s immersive to many people. But if you only have an hour at the end of your busy day, and have little energy left to manage and organise things, it may be the opposite of what you need. And Bard’s Ballad is addressing that."

Of course, as with all accessibility options, none of it is mandatory, so if you want to experience The Witcher 3 the way it was originally intended, you can do that (with stunning visuals and performance on the Switch 2, no less!). But it's really cool that developers are taking the time to think of those who might not, for whatever reason, be able to sink quite as much time into such a lengthy quest.
