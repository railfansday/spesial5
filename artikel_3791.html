"I think the version of BioWare that survives EA better is a version where we drop down to one franchise at a time, maybe just one franchise at all. Maybe we just carve SWTOR off and they become an independent studio within EA, because I don't know how what I'm about to say makes sense if you don't do that. But you ship Mass Effect, you ship Dragon Age, and then one of those wins—I hate this—one of those wins and then you just become a one-project studio. There are lots of studios within EA that did that and it didn't work—they still got murdered in their sleep, so maybe that doesn't work anyway." Via an interview with Pembridge.

Many are asking themselves how a legendary studio like BioWare, which is responsible for staples of the PC gaming genre like Neverwinter Nights, Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic, and the OG Baldur's Gate, alongside Mass Effect and Dragon Age, can be shuttered.

Former Dragon Age executive producer Mark Darrah has already explained how the studio was backed into a corner by EA. Escalating financial pressure led to the poor decisions and subpar games we saw ahead of BioWare's closure. But what if it had all gone differently? Despite everything, Darrah does question whether there was a way for BioWare to survive under EA.

"BioWare's entire existence under EA—it was essentially zero sum, if somebody was getting resources it was coming at the expense of somebody else," Darrah explains. "It's almost like BioWare has run exactly the same way for its entire existence. But the external constraints were different. So in the early days when BioWare needed more people it hired more people. That's like Baldur's Gate 1, Neverwinter Nights, in the early days when we have all these different threads and branches.

"And then in 2007/2008, when the EA constraints come in, the same desires are there, we need more people to ship our games so the way we fix that in that time period is by cutting off all those other branches. So [Jade Empire sequel] Revolver goes away and the Handheld [Development] Group goes away … and so Dragon Age and Mass Effect eats everything."

The alternative? Scaling things down to meet EA's needs. "Budgets keep going up, but we're still not getting bigger… So what's happening is that the walls are closing in and we're trying to be three things [SWTOR, Mass Effect, Dragon Age] and arguably we can only afford to be two things, or one thing." There's no telling whether this would've saved BioWare, but it has worked for other studios.

"Maxis figured out how to live inside of EA and BioWare never did," Darrah adds. Focusing on just one project does seem to be the answer, or at least Darrah believes it has been for Maxis and Battlefield Studios, which both had games that initially underperformed and weren't well received. "If [Battlefield Studios] don't work on Battlefield then you might as well shut the studio down. So they all work on Battlefield 4 and they fix Battlefield 4 and it becomes successful, like very successful after the fact. The Sims 4, same thing."

On the other hand "Mass Effect Andromeda ships and then Dragon Age 4 which has been starving for resources is like 'No, no [the devs] are supposed to come to us. Where are they?' That need to keep them in place isn't there in the same way. So when I say BioWare didn't adapt to EA, that's what I mean."
