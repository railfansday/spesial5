Brooklyn Nets respond with a fight, but not a win, in 113-111 heartbreaker vs Magic

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

There is a funny irony to sports, especially the NBA.

You can spend hours and days dissecting what's wrong with a team, questioning everyone from the top of the organization down. The roster, the direction, and everything in between. And then, two nights later, the team goes out and completely contradicts the narrative, for better or worse.

After getting embarrassed by 30 points against the Chicago Bulls on Wednesday, the Nets entered Friday night's matchup against the Orlando Magic with expectations as low as they've been this season. The squad entered with a 3-11 record, with five of their losses coming by 15+ points, the most by any team in the league this season.

Yet, somehow, 48 hours later, the Nets looked like an entirely different basketball team.

The energy was palpable, the defensive effort was there, players were diving for loose balls, and the team looked noticeably better.

Danny Wolf, getting the start over Egor Dëmin, delivered 20 points and four 3-pointers. Julius Randle added 27 points, nine rebounds, and six assists while Mikel Brown Jr. contributed 18 points off the bench, tied for his career-high.

And for a while, it looked like the Nets might actually have something to celebrate. Instead, they were reminded of another cruel reality of basketball: you could do a lot right and still lose games.

Despite overcoming an early 13-point deficit and taking a fourth-quarter lead, Brooklyn fell to Orlando, 113-111, on a Franz Wagner game-winner with under three seconds to play. Wagner finished with 35 points. It could have been a signature win for the team, but instead, it ended in heartbreaking fashion.

With 2.4 seconds left, the Nets got the ball back and had a chance to seal it, but Julius Randle missed an open game-winning 3-pointer.

"Julius had the ball in his hands… that's what I wanted," said Jordi Fernandez post-game. "He shot a step back and it was wide open, and I live with it,"

The game shouldn't have even come down to that moment, though. Unfortunately for the Brooklyn Nets, there isn't a standing for moral victories, and they now fall to 3-12.

The Nets jumped out to a 17-11 lead in the first quarter before Orlando started to catch up. By the end of the first frame, the score had flipped, with the Magic up 38-25.

Brooklyn's biggest early problem was stopping the Magic from shooting from deep. Without strong perimeter defense, which the Nets have lacked all season, it's going to stay tough for them to beat teams with more roster talent, like Orlando.

To the team's credit, though, there was a clear increase in effort and pride, which had been in question over the past few games. Perhaps the "internal discussions," as Michael Porter Jr. described them, after Wednesday's blowout sent a message.

From Julius Randle to Mikel Brown Jr., everyone looked to be giving their all early on, diving for loose balls, and everything that comes with being a high-motor team even if they were trailing.

Subsequently, the Nets started to catch up. With under eight minutes to play in the second quarter, Brooklyn cut the score to 42-38 after a strong run fueled by Michael Porter Jr. MPJ, who scored 14 points in his first 13 minutes of the night after finishing with just five points in 27 minutes against the Bulls on Wednesday night.

Fernández also made a change to his starting lineup on Friday night. Danny Wolf replaced Egor Dëmin, who had been in a rough patch entering the game. It also marked his first time coming off the bench since November of last season. That decision worked out, as Wolf thrived while Dëmin's struggles continued, scoring six points in 21 minutes on 1-of-6 shooting, including a 0-of-3 mark from 3-point range.

"We were struggling with rebounding and with our defense, and I felt like Danny was playing well," Fernandez said, adding. "Obviously, there's no guarantees or anything for anybody."

Entering halftime, the Nets trailed just 62-61, chipping away at the 13-point deficit they faced at the end of the first frame. It was a battle from beyond the arc, as Orlando continued to find success from deep, hitting a whopping 57.1% of their tries, while Brooklyn converted 41.2% of their 3-point attempts.

The Nets also did a strong job of forcing the Magic into mistakes, forcing 12 turnovers in the first half compared to their eight. The team also capitalized on the extra possessions, scoring 18 points off turnovers.

Coming out of halftime, the Nets stayed in the game but didn't do enough to take the lead entering the fourth quarter, still trailing 91-88. Nolan Traoré left the game with a hamstring injury during the third frame and was quickly ruled out, limping into the locker room.

Asked about whether Traore injury was serious, Fernandez responded: "I don't know."

Traoré has been a crucial piece in the Nets' backcourt this season, but when he went down, rookie Mikel Brown Jr. stepped up in a big way.

Brown logged 13 points in the third quarter, keeping the Nets in the game while hitting both of his shots from beyond the arc. By the end of the frame, he had logged 15 points in 18 minutes. Fernandéz sent him to the bench to begin the fourth quarter, giving Keon Ellis his first minutes of the night, before returning with just over six minutes remaining in the final frame.

Early on in the fourth quarter, the Nets grabbed their first lead since the first half. Wolf had a breakaway dunk and a crucial 3-pointer, marking his fourth deep make in moments, while Julius Randle hit his first three of the game. It brought the score to 98-93 with 8:35 left, forcing Orlando to use a timeout.

But as the Nets have learned plenty of times this season, building a lead and protecting one are two very different things. Orlando worked their way back, setting up a dramatic finish. And despite the Nets' improved play throughout the night, it was the Magic who closed out the game.

With the game tied 111 in the closing seconds, Wagner knocked down a contested fadeaway jumper from just inside the 3-point line with 2.4 seconds remaining, putting Orlando ahead by two. Brooklyn still had a chance to steal the game, but Randle's potential game-winning 3-pointer missed.

Just like that, the Nets had another loss to their record, falling 113-111.

Randle still led Brooklyn with 27 points, nine rebounds, six assists, and three steals, shooting 9-of-19 from the field. Day'Ron Sharpe also continued his strong start to the year, adding another double-double with 17 points and 11 rebounds.

Meanwhile, Wolf arguably had his best game as a pro, finishing with 20 points on 6-of-10 shooting overall, including 4-of-6 from deep. Post-game, he talked about his game.

"It's hard to come up here and keep having these moral victories. There's been a lot of those this year. I think we responded very well after that Chicago game. But with how hard guys played, we really wanted that win and we needed that win."

Meanwhile, Clowney's struggles continued, as he went scoreless on four field-goal attempts in 22 minutes and the Wagner game-winner came over him on defense.

The positive spin is that the Nets showed much more fight than they did two nights ago, but there's still no win to show for it.

Although the Nets called up three playes from the G League, Ben Saraf, Drake Powell and Joshua Jefferson didn't play, with only Saraf suiting up.

Final Score: Orlando Magic, 113, Brooklyn Nets, 111

Friday also marked the first matchup between the Wagner brothers: Franz and Mo. Safe to say Franz took home the family bragging rights.

With their parents looking on, Franz and Moe Wagner shared some salty but mostly emotional talk. The 6'10" 25-year-old Franz is four years younger than the 6'11" 29-year-old Moe.

"It's awkward, I'm not gonna lie. I don't really know how to compartmentalize it, really deal with it, and try to focus on the game plan and see what happens emotionally," Moe said pre-game. "It's a dilemma because you want him to do well. At the same time, you game plan for him, you prepare to limit him. So, it's an awkward feeling. I've never been in this position."

"My whole NBA career I played with him. The first time in my life playing against him," Franz said. "But as weird as it's going to be, it's a blessing to share the court with your family.Not a lot of people get to do that, so that's how I look at it."

In the end, of course, it was Franz's game, scoring 35 points, bringing his season averages to 24.4 points, 8.5 assists and 6.9 rebounds per game while Moe, who went into the game as the NBA's leading 3-point shooter, put up three points.

"He's playing incredible," Moe said. "New coach, new system, found a way to be a playmaker and scorer at the same time, dominating the pace, the paint. Obviously with an enormous usage rate as well, which you've got to give him credit for the physicality he plays with … He's a joy to watch."

A lot of Nets fans would like to see the Wagner brother reunited in Brooklyn but that seems unlikely. Franz has become the face of the franchise in Orlando and is under contract through 2029-30 and is owed $185 million.

The Nets will have two days off before heading to Minnesota, where they will face the 11-5 Timberwolves on Monday. Tip-off is set for 7:30 p.m. ET.

It will be another uphill battle for the 3-12 Nets, who continue to spiral.

Monday will mark Julius Randle's first matchup against Minnesota since Brooklyn acquired him over the summer. It will also be Brooklyn's first of two nationally televised games this year.
