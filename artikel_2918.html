The WTA faces criticism after Jessica Pegula and Mirra Andreeva’s incident, ‘extremely disappointing’

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Controversy has plagued the WTA Tour and its players over the past week.

Ahead of the WTA Finals, Jessica Pegula and coach Mark Knowles were caught expressing some choice words about Mirra Andreeva and her coach, Conchita Martinez.

"Conchita's always been very nice to me, but I've lost a little respect for her because I'm like, this girl – I think she's a nice girl – she's just a diva," Pegula said during her practice session, which was broadcast live on YouTube.

"She needs to be humbled. She needs to be [inaudible] putting her in her place; [inaudible] stand up to her."

2025 Qatar Open first round – Mirra Andreeva and Diana Shnaider defeated Jessica Pegula and Ashlyn Krueger, 4-6, 6-3, 10-7

2025 US Open quarter-finals – Jessica Pegula and Jack Draper defeated Mirra Andreeva and Daniil Medvedev, 4-1, 4-1

2026 Italian Open semi-finals – Mirra Andreeva and Diana Shnaider defeated Jessica Pegula and Storm Hunter, 6-1, 6-2

The WTA have since apologised for their error, admitting that they failed to properly inform WTA Finals participants of the presence of microphones around the practice courts.

WTA Chairwoman Valerie Camillo has also apologised to Pegula, stating that the live-streaming of her private comments was 'regrettable and unacceptable.'

Brad Gilbert, the former coach of Andre Agassi and Coco Gauff, has now reacted to the controversy.

Andrea Petkovic, the former world number nine, began the discussion by expressing her disappointment, arguing that Pegula and Knowles' private conversation should have never seen the light of day.

"It's obviously extremely disappointing," Gilbert, speaking on the Big T Podcast, then added. "I think as a player, you know on the Centre Court there is a microphone and you got to always be careful. But on a side practice court, you're not thinking about it.

"I think she did the right thing going up to Mirra and trying to iron things out. Obviously Coco got brought into it because she was talking about her in light, that if she did things like Coco, she would do… none of that matters.

"But, to me, it was very disappointing of the WTA to not let the players know that. Then this thing would have never happened. But obviously, it's out there now."

"We talked about it a little bit with the Jannik Sinner situation. My argument – I think I'm the only one arguing that – but I'm saying that sickness, injury, illness, those are private things," Petkovic added.

"In a normal environment, you are not supposed to disclose whether you are sick or injured. So we don't have the right to know about Jannik Sinner's injury if he doesn't want to tell us, that was my chance, and I got kind of a little bit of backlash against it and I understand it.

"But I have the same stance on private conversation. As you said, a side court somewhere in Indian Wells, it's a private conversation. And I have to say, the stress is really high a couple of days before the WTA Finals – you want to do well, it's one of the most important flagship tournaments for the WTA.

"There is a huge prize money amount on the table, and you say things sometimes you don't think about, even if you weren't aware there were microphones. If I had been microphoned during my practice sessions when I'm a little stressed. Boy, I would have been cancelled a million times!"

She later added: "I spoke to Garbine Muguruza [WTA Finals Tournament Director] just a couple hours ago, we were watching that match together. She told me the first things Jess did when she found out it was out there, was go straight to Mirra.

"I think a lot of other players would have probably thought about, 'how can I save my image? Can I put out a statement?' No, the first thing Jess did was go straight up to Mirra and talk to her."

As noted by Petkovic, Pegula deserves a lot of credit for the way she handled the situation. Many individuals would have hidden behind statements and their image, instead of taking full responsibility.

Pegula demonstrated class in this regard, which may explain why she is Chair of the WTA Tour Architecture Council.

Pegula squandered a big opportunity during her loss to Coco Gauff on Sunday evening.

At 4-4 in the first set, Pegula had three consecutive break-point opportunities on Gauff's serve. The American failed to capitalise and eventually lost the set 6-4.

Gauff went on to triumph in California, winning 6-4, 6-7, 6-2.

Pegula will play Marta Kostyuk in her next match at 12pm local time on Tuesday. Gauff will play Linda Noskova afterwards.
