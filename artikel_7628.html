Bar pass rates drop in 37 states

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Overall passage rates for the July 2026 bar exam fell in 37 states compared with a year earlier, according to the National Conference of Bar Examiners.

As of Tuesday, the NCBE’s tally shows only four of the 31 jurisdictions that used the legacy Uniform Bar Exam and have released their results reported a better pass rate than July 2025, while one state had the same rate. There are nine states classified as non-UBE that use independent designs for their exam, and seven of those states saw drops in pass rates this year; one state held steady, and another has not reported results. Of the six states offering the new NextGen Uniform Bar Exam, four saw increased bar pass rates.

In California, less than half the candidates—48.3%—passed the bar in July, a drop from 54.8% last year. That’s the lowest pass rate to date of any U.S. jurisdiction that has reported results.

The pass rates fell from coast to coast. In New York, the jurisdiction testing the most candidates, 67% passed compared with 70% last year. The biggest drops occurred in West Virginia and Wisconsin, which each fell 15 percentage points from the previous year. According to NCBE data, West Virginia went from 77% in July 2025 to 62%, and Wisconsin fell from 68% to 53%.

Nine states—Connecticut, Hawaii, Iowa, Maryland, Oregon, Utah, Vermont, Washington and Wyoming—saw increases in overall pass rates. Of those Connecticut, Maryland, Oregon and Washington administered the new NextGen UBE, which emphasizes skills over memorization.

While acknowledging that many variables are at play and that pass rates fluctuate over the years, Sean Silverman, founder of Silverman Bar Exam & LSAT Tutoring, tells the ABA Journal, “There was such a lopsided result this year with over 75% of the states that have reported results publicly reporting lower scores in 2026 than in 2025.”

Students taking the legacy UBE in July mentioned that the essays felt especially difficult, he says.

“I can only speculate, but with NCBE dropping a number of subjects from the exam this time for the first time, it might have compensated by the content it chose to test, and it’s possible that it overcompensated,” he adds.

The subjects dropped were wills, trusts, family law, conflict of laws and secured transactions, he notes.

Only South Carolina (78%) and South Dakota (73%) maintained the same overall pass rates compared with the previous year, according to the NCBE. South Carolina is a UBE state while South Dakota is designated as a non-UBE state.

The national mean score for the Multistate Bar Exam fell 1.7 points to 140.7 from 142.4 last year, marking the first decrease since 2022, according to data released in September by the NCBE.

“This July’s MBE mean is consistent with performance over the past five years,” said Bob Schwartz, the NCBE managing director of psychometrics, in a news release. “Although 10 fewer jurisdictions administered the MBE this year, our analysis indicates that their absence from the national pool did not meaningfully affect the mean, as MBE mean scores over time are nearly identical whether those 10 jurisdictions are included or excluded.”

In July, 44,472 examinees took the MBE, a drop of about 5% compared with o the 46,959 examinees a year earlier. The decline stems from the fact that only 44 jurisdictions administered this July’s MBE as some moved to introduce the NextGen UBE.

The NextGen UBE’s pass rate for the inaugural 2026 administration hit 72.7%, within 4.3 percentage points of every July national pass rate from 2021 through 2025, according to the NCBE.

NextGen UBE will be fully rolled out by July 2028, when the legacy exam will sunset, and it has been adopted by 52 jurisdictions.

At press time, results from New Hampshire, New Jersey and Mississippi were not available.
