Magnus Ditlev Sets New Iron-Distance World Best With 7:19:17 at Ironman Florida

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Magnus Ditlev of Denmark wins the 2026 IRONMAN Florida in a World Record Time of 7:19:17 on November 7, 2026 in Panama City, Florida. (Photo by Donald Miralle/Getty Images for IRONMAN) (Photo: Getty Images for IRONMAN)

One month after a heartbreaking DNF in Kona, Magnus Ditlev (DEN) rode away from the field at Ironman Florida and never looked back. His 7:19:17 took 1:47 off the previous men’s iron-distance world best that Sam Laidlow (FRA) set at Challenge Roth earlier this year.

Florida was a chance at redemption for Ditlev, who had come agonizingly close to winning the Ironman World Championship in Kona. He’d taken the lead late on the bike and looked on course for the win until he abruptly came to a stop on the run with roughly 30 minutes of racing left.

However, ahead of Kona, Ditlev said he was putting up career-best numbers across the swim, bike, and run at his training camp in Colorado. In Florida, he finally got to reap the rewards of what fitness could produce all the way to the finish line.

Ditlev came out of the water in fifth, right on the heels of Ben Kanute (USA) and Rudy von Berg (USA). He took his time in T1 and started the bike nearly a minute down, but it didn’t take him long to ride back to the front. And then, just past mile 10, he made his move. No one could go with him, and they wouldn’t be able to stay remotely close to him either.

By the time Ditlev came off the bike, he was 11 minutes clear of a chase group that included Sam Long (USA), von Berg, and Kanute. His 3:51:16 was the fastest bike split ever recorded at an Ironman, nearly two minutes quicker than the 3:53:12 Robert Kallin (SWE) rode at Ironman Kalmar in August.

Off the bike, he once again took his time in transition. But transitions were the only slow part of Ditlev’s day. Once through T2, he picked up another minute on the chasers over the opening 10 miles. He hit halfway in 1:15, on pace for a 2:30 marathon and well within reach of Laidlow’s 7:21:04. With such an unassailable lead, one can assume he must’ve known the record was within reach to keep such an aggressive pace.

Ditlev eventually closed with a 2:33:31 marathon, crossing the line in 7:19:17. Von Berg took second, 14 minutes back, securing the early Kona slot and podium he’d come for. Matt Hanson (USA), meanwhile, ran a remarkable 2:28:07 to beat Long to the final podium spot, but still finished 16 minutes behind Ditlev.
