China’s military seeks Nvidia’s advanced chips for AI capabilities, commission says

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

China's People's Liberation Army has been "actively soliciting" Nvidia's most advanced semiconductor chips to develop its artificial intelligence-enabled military capabilities, a congressional commission said in a report released Wednesday.

The report comes as Nvidia has been rocked by the resignation of its CEO, Jensen Huang, after reports that he had an affair with a suspected Chinese spy who worked for Singapore-based Megaspeed, which is being eyed by U.S. investigators for potential diversion of Nvidia's chips to China.

"China is seeking to evade U.S. export controls to gain the most advanced semiconductors that underpin its intelligentization drive," the U.S.-China Economic and Security Review Commission said in its 2026 annual report to Congress.

China is making that move as it faces a quality gap between chips produced by Chinese companies, including Huawei and Cambricon, and the more advanced ones produced by Nvidia and other U.S. companies, such as Advanced Micro Devices, the report noted.

The report's findings could fuel concerns about advanced Nvidia chips having found their way to Chinese users amid escalating investigations from Congress and state and federal regulators.

"Although China has made some progress in achieving its goals, it has not yet closed the hardest chokepoints — including the most advanced capabilities in logic fabrication, memory, and semiconductor manufacturing equipment, as well as the Nvidia Compute Unified Device Architecture (CUDA) software moat — and likely will not for some time," the report said.

"The resulting gap in compute is substantial, largely due to U.S. export controls on advanced chips," the report said.

The report highlighted the fact that in the meantime, Chinese companies have obtained remote access to Nvidia chips through data centers in third countries, including Malaysia.

On Tuesday, the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security issued a rule intended to cut off that workaround.

The move came in response to criticism from Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Congress members that the Commerce Department had not done enough to prevent Nvidia's most advanced chips from being diverted to China in violation of U.S. export controls.

The U.S.-China Economic and Security Review Commission was created by Congress in 2000 with a mandate to monitor and report to Congress the national security implications of the trade and economic relationship between the U.S. and China.
