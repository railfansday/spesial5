Mavericks vs Jazz Recap: 3 things to know from the Mavericks 116-114 win against the Jazz

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The Dallas Mavericks survived Sunday night, defeating the Utah Jazz 116-114 in Salt Lake City. Dallas has now won three in a row after getting blown out in three straight, stabilizing themselves a bit after things looked rocky after the Mavericks were destroyed by the Celtics last week.

The Mavericks didn't get off to a great start and they certainly didn't have a great finish, but at least the middle parts were fun! Cooper Flagg led Dallas with 28 points in what ended up being a well-rounded effort.

Dallas controlled the middle parts of the game with a swarming defense that got the team out in transition. Utah has a lot of talented guards, but no steady hand to lead the offense. Combine that with the Mavericks penchant for playing the passing lanes aggressively, and the Mavericks were able to turn defense into offense quickly.

It also led to a balanced scoring attack despite Flagg's showing. Dallas had five players in double-figures, all of the starters, and 32 assists on 43 made field goals.

Unfortunately most of the talk about this one will be about the final two minutes, where the Mavericks almost blew what seemed like an insurmountable 16-point lead. Santi Aldama finished a fastbreak layup with about two minutes left in the game to put Dallas up 114-98, and the Jazz's second unit, specifically Svi Mykhailiuk, went ballistic. Backup guard Isaiah Collier made a three, then Mykhailiuk scored nine straight points, making three free throws after getting fouled on a three, then back-to-back three pointers.

Dallas kept throwing the ball away, and coach Dusty May didn't call a timeout to put his starters back in until a Mykhailiuk three made it 114-109 with under 40 seconds left. Another bad turnover gave Utah the ball down two and Collier went full-court against Caleb Martin and scored to tie the game. Ace Bailey committed a bone-headed foul on the next Mavericks possession, allowing Flagg to make the game-winning free throws with 3.6 seconds left. A contested three pointer from Kyle Filipowski hit the back-iron of the rim and the Mavericks escaped with a win.

There's a lot to talk about with this one, but here's the three to know.

Dusty May has undoubtedly done a great job with the Mavericks. A team that looked dead in the water last season with an aimless roster past Flagg has been reset with a new foundation and leadership. The Mavericks are 10-7, competing for a playoff spot early in the season. That is absolutely a success for first-year coach May.

Unfortunately the fourth quarter was not his finest moment. May had several opportunities during Utah's comeback to call a timeout and maybe get some starters back in the game. After Mykhailiuk made three free throws to get the game in single digits with 1:08 left, Dallas could have subbed at least Flagg back in or called a timeout after the last Mykhailiuk free throw. Then when Mykhailiuk made another three with 37.4 left and the score 114-109, May let his backup unit continue and Walter Clayton Jr. committed a brutal turnover, and the Jazz called a timeout and then May put his starters back in.

It was a bit bizarre to watch in real time, especially since the Mavericks had timeouts to spare. It was a good reminder that May is new to this too, at least at the professional level. He is not infallible and rookie mistakes are just part of it, coaches too not just players. It was admirable of May to try and steal some rest for his guys on the second night of a back-to-back in high altitude in Salt Lake City, but it didn't work out. The Jazz bailed the Mavericks out, so in the end no harm, no foul. But those final two minutes were brutal.

Dallas has a lot of athletes on the team, and even if the fundamentals of the Mavericks defense don't always hold game-to-game or even possession-to-possession, it's clear May and the coaching staff want this Mavericks team to exploit their athletic gifts.

The Mavericks play the passing lanes very aggressively. Sometimes the gambles cost them, see some of the tape from the Mavericks recent three game losing streak. But against an overwhelmed and overmatched Jazz team, it can look great. Dallas had 16 steals on the night, which led to 19 fastbreak points. Flagg did a lot of damage in transition, as did Zaccharie Risacher, who had 15 points and multiple dunks.

Every starter for Dallas tonight scored in double-figures — Flagg led the way with 28, Caleb Martin had 13, Daniel Gafford had 10, Risacher had 15 and Kyrie Irving had 16. Dallas didn't get much from the bench, but the starting unit played really well together, and their unselfish play was really fun to watch, as the ball was really moving.

Not only did every starter have at least 10 points, but every starter had at least two assists! Martin, Gafford, and Flagg each had four assists and Irving had eight. It really helps that Flagg and Irving are tremendous off-ball players that are not only willing off-ball players, but enthusiastic off-ball players — they do the work when they don't have the ball to help their teammates get open looks or create opportunities for themselves or others.
