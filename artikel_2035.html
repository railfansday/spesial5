Stewart Downing reveals Adama Traore bad ‘habit’ at Middlesbrough

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

The former Middlesbrough star Stewart Downing has revealed one of the habits of his former teammate, Adama Traore, was always getting him in trouble.

Over a playing career which spanned more than 700 appearances over the course of two full decades, there wasn't much in the game that the former Middlesbrough winger Stewart Downing didn't get to witness. He won the League Cup twice, made over 400 Premier League appearances for four different clubs, and was capped 35 times by the England national team.

Since he called time on an illustrious playing career in August 2021, Downing moved into coaching and scouting. After a spell with the Boro youth academy, he moved to Leeds United in 2024 as an under-21 coach before returning to Liverpool as a Global Talent Scout in May 2025, but in a recent interview he was given a series of quick-fire questions about his playing career, and he recalled that one of this former teammates had a particular issue which hit the player concerned hard in the pocket.

Speaking on the No Tippy Tappy Football podcast, in a clip that they posted to the social media platform X, Downing was asked a series of quick-fire questions about his playing career, and in amongst a number of questions about his former teammates, managers and opponents, one of the players he formerly played with came in for a bit of a rinsing.

Downing was asked of the players that he'd played with before, "who was always getting fined?", and the former Boro star had an immediate reply: "Adama Traore. Daily. Great kid but he had a habit of being late."

Clubs often have systems of fines for players who misbehave or otherwise break their rules, and punctuality is considered very important. On one hand, this is done to instill greater discipline into them, but it's also a matter of impressing upon them the importance of respecting both their teammates who have turned up on time and acting as a reminder of how fortunate they are to be playing professional football in the first place.

The overall impact he made at his boyhood club after coming through the ranks cannot be understated.

Stewart Downing and Adama Traore were at opposite ends of their career trajectories when they spent time together at The Riverside Stadium.

Downing was born in Middlesbrough and graduated from their academy into the first team, making his debut for them in 2002. He left the club in 2009, and would play for Liverpool, Aston Villa and West Ham United before returning to the club in 2015.

Traore, on the other hand, was at the start of his senior professional career. Born in Spain, he'd come through the academy at Barcelona, but then transferred to Aston Villa before making his move to Teesside in 2016, following Boro's return to the Premier League.

Boro finished the 2016-17 season in 19th place in the top-flight and haven't returned since, but Traore impressed despite failing to score all season and was even linked with a possible move to Chelsea following his club's return to the Championship. Downing, meanwhile, made thirty appearances for Boro that season, scoring once and contributing three assists.

That move failed to materialise, but Traore did continue to enhance his reputation back in the EFL. He won Middlesbrough's Fans' Player of the Year, Young Player of the Year and Players' Player of the Year awards at the end of the 2017-18 season.

That summer he left The Riverside Stadium for Wolverhampton Wanderers, who'd just been promoted back to the Premier League, after they met an £18 million release clause in his contract. Downing, meanwhile, left the club for Blackburn Rovers in 2019.

This hasn't been the first time that Downing has spoken about Traore's issues with punctuality, either. In 2022, Teesside Live reported on how the England star had explained that these problems became so severe that they led to fines for being late being scrapped.

Downing explained at the time: "One time he went home for an afternoon nap and we were leaving on the coach for a game later on in the day. Garry Monk just told the driver to leave him, because he wasn’t waking up! He was a really good lad, and we all tried to help him as much as we could – we’d just be saying, 'Adama, come on… just set your alarm on your phone!'"

But none of this affected Traore's popularity at The Riverside Stadium, as was demonstrated by the awards that he won at the end of the 2017-18 season. His talent as a player clearly outweighed any issues over turning up on time. He wouldn't have lasted decade at the top of the professional game had that been the case.
