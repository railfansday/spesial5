The best Newcastle United number 10 for the Matthias Jaissle system

Across the season so far, Matthias Jaissle has consistently set up in a 4-2-3-1 formation, predominately using a distinct number 10 in that system.

The number 10 role is one of the most challenging roles in modern football – the best #10s in the modern game bring a mix of technique, creativity, off-the-ball running, pressing, goalscoring and defensive contributions.

They can find little pockets of space, receive the ball on the turn and find teammates in behind, or create goalscoring opportunities for themselves.

They know when to drop into midfield, to help the team control the tempo or progress the ball forwards, and they can also threaten the opponents goal with clever movements, off-the-ball runs and late runs into the box.

It’s fair to say that no one has made this number 10 position their own so far for Newcastle United this season.

If Jaissle is to persist with that system, then he needs to find the right player to play as the #10 in his system.

Here, I’m going to rank the number 10 options available to Matthias Jaissle, as it stands right now.

Wissa started the season brightly and showed promise but has since dropped off in the last 6-7 matches.

He has something to offer, particularly in home games in the #10 role, but certainly doesn’t possess the creative ability to link play between defence, midfield and attack like a good #10 can.

He’s decent on the turn, and can dribble effectively through midfield, but the technical and creative side of the role is not his strength. We’ve seen him spurn multiple chances through poor decision making and his vision and weight of pass means he doesn’t effectively utilise the space he can create through his dribbling and turn it into goal scoring opportunities for him or his team mates.

Another player who has shown flashes this season but never fully grasped the opportunity.

Joe Willock relishes space – he doesn’t have the technique or tactical nous to show for and receive the ball in tight spaces, turn and play forwards. He offers Newcastle a ‘running’ # 10 – he can test the opponents defence with runs in behind, and he can be effective when drifting into space on the left or on the counter attack. But he doesn’t help us play through the lines because he doesn’t often show for the ball from the centre backs or from our two holding midfielders. He doesn’t help connect defence and midfield with our three attackers and doesn’t help control games in the middle of the park.

When he’s fit, big Joe offers a better mix of attributes in the #10 role than Wissa and Willock.

He’s played similar roles before, and has obviously played as a centre-forward for both Newcastle and in Germany, so (yes, despite his record) he does have some of the attacking attributes and instincts to score goals from the #10. He has shown he can chip in and score goals from midfield by bursting into the box or arriving to meet crosses. He’s strong in the air and strong on the ball, so offers us an outlet in the middle of the pitch aerially and his strength allows him to receive the ball to feet in tight spaces.

While he’s not particularly creative, he can find a pass and is pretty good at finding pockets of space to receive the ball. He offers the ability to drop into midfield and help progress the ball, and obviously brings tactical understanding and a strong defensive contribution. Opposition defensive midfielders would hate having him breathing down their neck every time they get the ball.

He’s by no means the perfect fit but offers a better skill set than Willock and Wissa in the #10. With two of Nico, Steur and Miley behind him, big Joe could be an effective option in the #10 in the short-term.

In my opinion, Ramsey is the best fit for Jaissle’s #10 role from the current squad, and I’m bewildered as to why he hasn’t been given time there. Ramsey himself said a few weeks ago that he had been training in that role, so hopefully that’s a sign that he might get a chance there… and not blow it by getting sent off.

Jacob Ramsey has far and away the best array of skills in our squad to play the #10 role. He has a high level of technical quality, is adept at finding pockets of space and is great at receiving the ball on the half turn to play forwards. He has the best eye for a pass of our current options and has the passing range and technique to find players to feet or running in behind.

He is very two-footed and can score goals with right and left, and possesses the ability to make well-timed runs in behind and into the box to meet crosses. He’s shown in previous seasons (with Villa) that he can be a strong contributor of goals and assists from an attacking midfield position in the premier league.

His technique and tactical awareness mean he can drop into midfield to help the team progress the ball, or can sit in the pocket and try and find space to connect the midfield with our front three.

Defensively, he has a great engine, is strong in the tackle and can track back or press as well as any other option we have.

Matthias Jaissle has much more depth in the deep roles now (Nico, Steur, Miley etc) so there is no need to use Ramsey there. When he got a run of 10-15 games in the middle of last season, Ramsey was brilliant and very consistent for us. He put in a 7-8/10 performance across most of those games and helped knit things together in midfield. I’d like to see him given that same opportunity now.

#1 – Change the system or buy a #10 in January

It’s worth acknowledging that none of these options are the perfect fit.

In my opinion, Ramsey is the only player we have who could actually be a good option in the #10 for the medium term. He’s the best of a bad bunch really. Given this fact and the emergence of Steur and availability of Miley, Ramsey and (ahem) Bamba, then it makes sense for Jaissle to revert to a midfield three.

If Jaissle wants to persist with this current system, and a #10 were available in January and we can spend the required cash (both doubtful) then I think we have to invest in that position as a priority. It’s the key position to unlock the rest of our attack, and requires a technical leader who can create and score goals. That’s a rare skillset and something we’ll need to work hard to find the right fit.
