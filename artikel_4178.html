Best Classes To Play Solo In World Of Warcraft: Forever

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Discover the best classes for solo play in World of Warcraft: Forever—why Hunters and Warlocks shine.

You won't always have someone available to quest with in World of Warcraft: Forever, so it's worth picking a class that can hold its own. While every class can reach level 60 solo, some make the journey a lot easier than others.

Hunters can send their pets into battle to tank for them, while Paladins have healing spells to get themselves out of trouble. Other classes, like Rogues, can sneak past enemies and avoid unnecessary fights. It’s also worth considering when each class unlocks its more useful abilities, as some take longer than others to reach their full potential.

Here are my five recommended classes for solo players in World of Warcraft: Forever, including the best leveling specializations and key Forever-specific changes.

One of the biggest advantages of playing a Rogue is that you don't have to fight everything in your path. With Stealth, you can sneak past enemies to reach quest objectives, saving yourself time and potentially a few frustrating deaths.

Taking down a single enemy is rarely the problem for a Rogue. However, you’ll be in trouble when another enemy wanders into the fight, especially if you’ve already used your defensive abilities. I recommend picking off enemies individually and using Stealth to avoid crowded areas whenever possible. There’s no reason to fight half the zone just to collect a quest item.

Combat is recommended for solo leveling, as it provides consistent single-target damage and talents that improve your weapon attacks and survivability.

Your general strategy is to open fights from Stealth, generate Combo Points with abilities like Sinister Strike, and spend them on finishers such as Eviscerate. You can also use Gouge to control an enemy when you need breathing room.

Rogues also received some welcome changes in World of Warcraft: Forever. For starters, Sap no longer breaks Stealth, so you can temporarily disable an enemy without revealing yourself. Poisons have more charges, too, and you can use them alongside temporary weapon enhancements.

You'll also pick up a couple of useful abilities as you level. At level 18, Posed reduces the Energy cost of your next ability when you reach full Energy outside combat. At level 24, Shank gives you another attack that generates Energy, helping you keep attacking instead of waiting for your Energy to recover.

However, Rogues struggle when fighting multiple enemies at once. They lack reliable self-healing and depend on defensive cooldowns, crowd control, and careful positioning to survive.

Combat is a great option if you enjoy picking your battles, but it takes more patience than classes that can comfortably fight multiple enemies at once.

One of the best reasons to play a Druid is that you can change how you fight without switching classes. If you’re facing a tough enemy, Bear Form gives you extra protection. Need to get somewhere without fighting everything along the way? Cat Form lets you sneak past enemies instead.

Feral Combat is the recommended solo spec, which focuses on melee damage, mobility, and survivability through Cat Form and Bear Form.

However, Druids can feel relatively slow during the early levels. Bear Form becomes available at level 10, but Cat Form doesn't unlock until level 20. This is where leveling with Feral becomes considerably more enjoyable.

At level 20, you’ll use Energy to attack instead of mana when you unlock Cat Form. This leaves your mana available for healing between fights. As you gain more abilities, you can apply a bleed effect with Rake, use Claw to generate Combo Points, and finish enemies off with your stronger attacks.

Feral Swiftness improves your movement in Cat Form. You can also use Prowl to sneak past unnecessary enemies, similar to a Rogue.

World of Warcraft: Forever also introduces several shapeshifting improvements. Your weapon's damage now contributes directly to your attacks in animal forms, while weapon enhancements and many consumables work without requiring you to transform back.

Additionally, the new Shifting Power talent lets you convert mana into Energy, helping you maintain offensive momentum later in the leveling process.

If an encounter becomes overwhelming, you can switch to Bear Form for additional durability or leave your animal form to heal.

Feral Combat is recommended if you want a class that can handle a little bit of everything. You’ll have plenty of options for getting out of trouble, although learning when to switch forms takes some getting used to.

Paladins are the most forgiving classes for solo players because they combine heavy armor, healing spells, and powerful defensive abilities. You have several ways to recover without relying on another player should you find yourself in a bad situation.

Retribution is the recommended specialization for solo players. You'll focus on melee damage while maintaining the class's defensive strengths.

World of Warcraft: Forever introduces Holy Strike at level six, giving you an instant weapon attack that deals Holy damage. This helps improve early leveling, when Paladin combat can otherwise feel relatively slow.

The Seal and Judgement system has also received an important improvement in Forever. Judgement no longer consumes your active Seal, so you can maintain its effects without repeatedly spending mana to reactivate it.

Your general strategy is to maintain an offensive Seal, use Holy Strike and Judgment to damage enemies, and heal yourself when necessary.

Later in the Retribution talent tree, Twist of Light provides another offensive option. This talent lets your previous Seal temporarily carry over when you activate a different one so that you can benefit from two damage-dealing Seals on your next melee attack.

Protection is another option, particularly if you enjoy fighting multiple enemies. Consecration is now available to every Paladin at level 20, providing area damage that is especially effective against the first four targets.

Retribution is best for questing because most quests have you fighting enemies one at a time, so killing them faster is usually more efficient. You’ll still have access to healing spells and defensive abilities that can save you should you get into a sketchy situation.

Warlocks are great for solo players because they can summon demons to fight alongside them while using powerful spells to defeat enemies from a distance.

Affliction is recommended because it is the most efficient leveling specialization, thanks to its combination of damage-over-time effects, self-healing, and resource management.

At level 10, Warlocks can obtain the Voidwalker, giving you a tank that draws enemy attention. This allows you to apply Corruption and Bane of Agony while your demon tanks incoming damage.

World of Warcraft: Forever introduces several improvements that make Affliction more effective. Damage-over-time effects can now crit, while talents like Malediction increase their damage.

The game also separates damaging Banes from utility Curses, allowing you to maintain a Bane of Agony alongside a Curse that weakens your enemies.

However, Affliction's biggest advantage for solo leveling is its ability to sustain itself through consecutive encounters. You can use Drain Life to restore your health, and Life Tap when you need an emergency mana refill.

Affliction also performs well against multiple enemies because you can spread damage-over-time effects across several targets while repositioning.

At level 40, Wrack provides another powerful offensive option. This new Affliction talent channels Shadow damage while making your active Corruption, Siphon Life, and Bane of Agony effects deal periodic damage 100 percent faster.

Affliction is the best choice for general questing because its damage-over-time spells and draining abilities allow you to move between enemies with minimal recovery time. It also lets you fight multiple enemies at once when needed.

Hunters are the top recommendation for solo players in World of Warcraft: Forever, thanks to their combination of ranged damage, mobility, and animal companions.

Beast Mastery is the recommended specialization. This spec allows you to focus on improving your pets' strength, durability, and damage.

The real fun starts at level 10, once you complete the pet-taming quest chain. Instead of fighting enemies at melee range, your pets will tank for you. You can now attack from a safe range.

Let your pet generate threat before you start shooting. Otherwise, you might find yourself dealing with an angry enemy running straight toward you. Serpent Sting works well against tougher enemies that will survive long enough to take damage over time, while Arcane Shot is useful when you need some immediate damage. If you’re fighting several enemies, make sure your pet isn’t taking more damage than it can handle.

Your pets now gain additional stats from your equipment, meaning your companion gets stronger as you upgrade your gear. This helps keep your pet useful throughout the leveling process.

Beast Mastery also receives new and redesigned talents. Focused Fire improves damage dealt by you and your pet, while Endurance Training increases your pet's durability.

A new ability, Summon Hawk, becomes available after you spend 16 talent points in Beast Mastery. This ability summons a hawk that temporarily attacks your target.

Hunters get improvements to their baseline abilities. Aimed Shot is now available to every Hunter at level 20, while traps can be placed during combat, giving you additional ways to control enemies.

Later, Bestial Wrath provides a powerful offensive cooldown that increases your pet's effectiveness against difficult opponents.

However, Hunters still need to keep their distance, as ranged attacks become unavailable when targets are too close. You must also manage your pet's health, positioning, and abilities.

The Beast Mastery spec offers one of the smoothest solo-leveling experiences available. Your pet can handle much of the danger while you deal consistent damage, making Hunter an excellent choice whether you're new to WoW or jumping from Retail to Forever.

Hunter or Warlock are the best choices if you’re planning to spend most of your time playing solo. Your pet will tank for you, which makes questing a lot less frustrating. Paladin is the best pick if you prefer melee classes instead. With heavy armor and healing spells, you can survive when you pull more enemies than you intended.

It’s also worth giving your chosen class some time before deciding whether to stick with it. Druids, for example, don’t unlock Cat Form until level 20, while Hunters have to reach level 10 before taming their first pet. A class that may feel slow at the beginning might become one of your favorites once you’ve unlocked more of its abilities.
