U.N. Starts Uncertain Effort to Regulate Lethal Autonomous Weapons

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Delegates met in Geneva to discuss guardrails for such systems even as their development grows. Russia and the United States have resisted the efforts.

Global efforts to limit the use of lethal autonomous weapons reached a crossroads this week as countries gathered to decide whether to negotiate binding restrictions on the technology, despite strong resistance from the United States and Russia.

Delegates of 128 states met at the United Nations in Geneva on Monday for a weeklong conference to determine whether to draft a treaty that would ban lethal autonomous weapons and require meaningful human control of such systems.

“The longer the international community waits, the more difficult it will become to establish meaningful safeguards — and it may be too late,” Volker Türk, the U.N. human rights chief, said before the conference.

Artificial intelligence and its military uses are developing at breakneck speed, as seen on the battlefields of Ukraine and the Middle East. That has deepened international alarm and prompted calls for action by many countries, global leaders and activist groups, even as top military powers balk at attempts to curb their ability to develop new weapons.

“Decisions affecting human life, freedom and the use of force must always remain subject to effective, informed and responsible human oversight,” Pope Leo XIV said in a statement last month that urged those at the Geneva conference to take “concrete “steps” to ban lethal autonomous weapons. Artificial intelligence, he said, “must be ‘disarmed.’”

Russia and Ukraine have already deployed fully autonomous drones to carry out attacks. Disarmament experts fear that the low cost and easy availability of such technology could lead to its rapid spread to law enforcement, nonstate armed groups and organized crime.
