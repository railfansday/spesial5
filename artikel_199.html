First responders describe rescue efforts after New Orleans boat crash: ‘We have to save each other’

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

More than 100 people were pulled from the Mississippi River in mission being hailed as ‘Mississippi miracle’

Mariners and first responders who took part in the so-called “Mississippi miracle” rescue mission, which saw about 100 people pulled from the water after a tanker collided with a cruise ship, have said they are thankful “everything came out all right”.

The collision on Friday between the Atlantic Asphalt tanker and the American Heritage riverboat saw more than 30 people injured.

Speaking days after the incident, responders told how they heard screams coming from inside the rooms of the sinking vessel, before they were able to free those onboard and bring them to safety.

William Brunner, the captain-on-watch of the nearby Madison Stone tow boat across the cruise ship, told Fox8 he initially heard the tanker’s distress call on his radio.

“The only one that could get there fast enough was a tow boat,” Brunner said. When the ship began to capsize, Brunner said the Madison Stone pushed against it to keep it from moving while his five-person crew started to rescue the passengers.

“We had the only spot that [could] get close enough to get people off,” Brunner said. “We live out here on the water and we know that we have to save each other,” he added.

Captain Ryan Williams, who helped develop a rescue plan in the heat of the moment, told the outlet: “No 1 goal was to get everybody out.”

Williams told of how he and several other crew members began to hoist passengers off the cruise ship and on to the tow boat once they were within reach. “We heard screaming coming down from … a hole out of one of the rooms and the glass was closed. It was four individuals in there and we [were] able to get them out,” Williams said.

In total, the tug boat crew managed to rescue 27 people off the cruise ship.

Among the first responders who were also quick to the scene were more than 60 New Orleans firefighters. Varrick Dyer, captain of the New Orleans fire department’s hazardous materials unit, told WWLTV: “We train constantly. As a matter of fact, before we went on that water rescue, we had guys on the roof training doing rope rescue at that, like an hour before that incident happened.”

Dyer, whose crew is trained in rope skills, as well as rescues in water, elevators, high-rises and automobiles, said: “We [were] looking for the people who were injured the most, who couldn’t move, couldn’t swim or couldn’t help themselves.”

Many of the passengers were seniors using wheelchairs or crutches, who were unable to use the firefighters’ rescue ladders. Fortunately, they managed to disembark via a gangway connecting the cruise ship to the dock before the ship then capsized and began to sink.

The fire department’s captain, Grant Rodriguez, told the outlet: “We did not have a lot of time. It went quick. The gangway went. The boat then retracted very hard to the rear. It then pushed away from the wharf all at the same moment … As it rolled, there was a lot of panic. It was cold, people were shivering. The river was extremely cold.”

Melanie Montroll, chief of the the New Orleans Harbor police, who were also on the scene, detailed the moment she ordered two officers on to the vessel as it began to capsize.

“Looking two officers in the face and sending them into a no-win situation, knowing what the potential outcome would be, it’s tough,” Montroll told Nola.com, adding: “I know my officers. I know their families. I know their kids.”

After the rescues, Montroll waded into the river to pull out the two injured officers, one of whom could not stand. “My goal was, one, to make sure that we got as many people, but two that I didn’t leave my officers in the water. That was a big concern of mine. I made that decision to put them on there, but we don’t leave our own,” Montroll told Nola.com.

Amid the rescue operations, the captain of the cruise ship, Leonard Stewart, remained on the bridge as the vessel capsized, repeatedly calling for help and keeping the boat by the dock so everyone else could disembark safely.

Leonard has since received widespread praise from the local community, with Helena Moreno, the New Orleans mayor, saying last week: “We really also appreciate the captain of the American Heritage who was on site with us. Every moment of the critical time yesterday he did not want to leave his ship.

“In fact, he was the last to get off of the ship yesterday, and so we just wanted to express our gratitude to him for his bravery.”
