Tesla goes all-in on the Cybercab with upcoming €3B Berlin gigafactory expansion

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Just two months after putting its first Cybercabs on the road, Tesla is making a multibillion-dollar bet on mass production of the golden-hued, two-seat autonomous vehicles.

The company announced Monday that it will spend €3 billion ($3.3 billion) to expand its Berlin gigafactory, with plans to begin building Cybercabs there in 2029. The new building in Grünheide, just east of Berlin, will be capable of producing up to 1 million vehicles per year, André Thierig, Tesla’s senior director of manufacturing for the site, wrote on LinkedIn.

Construction is expected to begin in the first half of 2027. The expansion will make the facility the most technologically advanced of all Tesla factories, Thierig added.

Tesla has been directing more resources toward the Cybercab project, a reflection of its mission to become an AI and robotics company, rather than just an automaker.

The company has already announced plans to cease production of the Cybertruck next year, in part to make room for Cybercab manufacturing at its factory in Austin, Texas.

Musk doesn’t see much of a future for drive-it-yourself EVs even though demand for the Model 3 and Model Y has remained strong despite flagging U.S. sales. The company delivered more than 486,000 vehicles in the third quarter, about 6,000 more than the second quarter. Sales in Europe have been rising as EV adoption continues apace.

Musk has been far more interested in pushing the company toward fully automated driving systems that don’t require a human driver to pay attention — unlike the company’s current advanced driver assistance system, known as Full Self-Driving (Supervised).

The steering wheel-free Cybercab, which was first revealed in 2024 and launched publicly in September, is the current centerpiece of that plan. It’s notable for its lack of a steering wheel, pedals, and other traditional motor vehicle equipment, some of which is required under federal safety standards. (Federal regulators are currently investigating Tesla’s self-certification of the vehicle.)

Tesla’s approach to self-driving systems differs from that of competitors such as Waymo. Tesla relies solely on cameras, rather than additional other sensors such as lidar, to help provide its self-driving system the means to understand its environment. Camera data mimics human vision, but without sufficient training, AI models can struggle to interpret situations that additional sensor data might help clarify.

Tesla has so far taken a slow approach to its Cybercab rollout. The vehicles have been restricted from driving late at night, though Tesla has been working to extend their operating hours. In the more than two months that Cybercabs have been operating, they haven’t had any major incidents.

That track record appears to have given Tesla enough confidence to commit significant resources to expanding Cybercab production and availability. Tesla has about 1,307 robotaxis registered in Texas. Most are modified Model Y vehicles that are used in robotaxi services in several Texas cities. But the Cybercab numbers are growing with about 573 on roads in Austin and soon Dallas.

Now, Europe is firmly on its roadmap, too.
