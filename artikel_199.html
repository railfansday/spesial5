North Korea expanded uranium enrichment plant, says IAEA head

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

IAEA chief Rafael Grossi has said the ongoing expansion of North Korea's uranium enrichment program remains "a cause for serious concern."

United Nations (UN) nuclear watchdog chief Rafael Grossi said on Wednesday North Korea has built an extension on its original uranium-enrichment plant at the Yongbyon nuclear complex.

The International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) has been unable to access North Korea since Pyongyang expelled its inspectors in 2009. It now relies primarily on satellite imagery to monitor the country's nuclear activity.

Grossi addressed the ongoing operation of enrichment facilities at Yongbyon and Kangson during a quarterly meeting of the IAEA's Board of Governors.

Kangson lies just southwest of Pyongyang, while the much larger Yongbyon facility is located farther north of the capital.

"Since ​mid-September 2026, a further extension to the original ​Yongbyon enrichment facility has been under construction with the new extension externally complete by ​late October 2026," Grossi said, with no further details.

He said the ongoing expansion of North Korea's uranium enrichment program remains "a cause for serious concern."

Enriched uranium is used as fuel for nuclear power plants and the nuclear reactors that run naval ships and submarines. It can also be used in nuclear weapons.

US President Donald Trump floated the possibility of a meeting with North Korean leader Kim Jong Un as he went to China to meet with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin.

Meanwhile, North Korean state media — which typically operates behind the global news cycle — has only released images of Kim visiting a ski resort and Mount Paektu, making no mention of possible Shenzhen travel plans.

Kim has said he aims to arm the nation with an expansive nuclear force to counter the US and South Korea.

In March, soon after the US and Israel launched strikes on Iran, Kim accused Washington of "global terrorism and aggression."

The Iran war reinforced North Korea's long-standing view that it needs a nuclear arsenal to be able to defend itself against what it sees as its two largest enemies, South Korea and the US.

North Korea has been subject to various international sanctions over its nuclear weapons development and use of ballistic missile technology. But Pyongyang has repeatedly flouted the restrictions.

In September 2024, North Korea revealed its first covert uranium-enrichment plant. Kim delivered a speech then, including a call for "exponentially" expanding the country's nuclear arsenal.

In April, Grossi told reporters that his agency had confirmed "a rapid increase” in activities at nuclear facilities in North Korea.

In September, the IAEA passed resolutions at its annual conference regarding concerns about the North Korean nuclear program.

Kim Yo Jong, the powerful sister of Kim Jong Un, said in response that the country's nuclear-armed status was "absolute."

She called those expecting denuclearisation "idiots."
