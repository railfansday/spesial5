All the London events hit by Tube strike this weekend as fans at Premier League matches face disruption

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Get our award-winning daily news email featuring exclusive stories, opinion and expert analysis

A snap 24-hour Tube strike that has shut one line and seriously disrupted another is set to affect passengers heading to events in the capital this weekend.

Members of the Transport Salaried Staffs’ Association (TSSA) working on the Piccadilly Line walked out at noon on Friday in a dispute over the dismissal of a colleague.

As a result the Piccadilly Line was closed and on Friday afternoon there was a part closure and severe delays on the District line.

TfL said previously due to the shared infrastructure around the Ealing Common District line depot, there will be no service between Earl’s Court and either Richmond or Ealing Broadway from midday on Friday until Saturday evening, with a reduced service on the rest of the line.

TfL says services began to reduce after 10:30 on Friday and will not return to normal on the Piccadilly line until Saturday evening.

The Night Tube will not operate on the Piccadilly line through Friday night. The Night Tube service is expected to operate on Saturday night.

The Elizabeth line, DLR, London Overground, buses and trams are expected to operate as normal during the strike action.

However, these services are likely to be busier than usual as customers seek alternative routes, says TfL.

Customers travelling to and from Heathrow Airport, as well as those making journeys across central, west and north London, are advised to plan ahead and allow extra time for their journeys.

There are also a number of other planned closures on the Tube and Overground that will cause disruption.

There is a busy day of sport in the capital and disruption on public transport will affect the plans of fans heading to the games.

Chelsea’s home game against Leeds kicks off at 3pm, with the District line, a popular way of arriving at Stamford Bridge, still expected to be facing severe delays before and after the game.

While fans hoping to use the Piccadilly line as part of their journey to Tottenham Hotspur Stadium to watch the 3pm game between Spurs and against Ipswich Town at the will have to find alternatives, which is likely to make other forms of transport very busy.

District line delays could also hinder rugby fans wanting to use it as part of the journey to Twickenham to watch England vs the All Blacks at the Allianz Stadium.

Visitors to do any venues or destinations along the Piccadilly line may have to rethink how they are going to get to and from their destination.

The walkout will have affected anyone wanting to use the line to reach South Kensington to visit the Royal Albert Hall for the Royal Variety performance on Friday night.

Drake Millington plays the Eventim Apollo in Hammersmith on Friday night, normally easy to reach by Piccadilly or District line.

While others hoping to use the line for a night out in the West End, or visit for Christmas shopping or to see the Christmas lights on Saturday will have to rethink their journeys.

Visitors to Winter Wonderland in Hyde Park may also have to rethink their journeys.

On Saturday and Sunday there will be no service between Queen's Park and Harrow & Wealdstone on the Bakerloo line due to track work.

There will be no service between Euston and Watford Junction on the Lioness line on Saturday or Sunday.

No service between Euston and Watford Junction on the Mildmay line on Sunday, with also no service between Camden Road and Stratford after 10.15pm.

On the Weaver line on Sunday there will be no service oin the entire line until 10.15am.

There will be no service between Stratford International and Canning Town on the DLR on Saturday and Sunday.

On Sunday, there will be no service on the Elizabeth line between Paddington and Ealing Broadway until 07:45am due to Network Rail maintenance work. There will also be a reduced service between Paddington and Heathrow/Maidenhead.

Lee Stewart, Transport for London’s head of London Underground service control, said: “We are extremely disappointed that TSSA is proceeding with unjustified strike action in response to the dismissal of an employee working a second job while receiving full sick pay.

“We will do all we can to minimise disruption for our customers.

“I would encourage Piccadilly Line customers travelling on Friday and Saturday to check before they travel and consider alternative routes where possible.

“Our teams will be working hard to keep London moving and provide customers with the information they need to plan their journeys.”

TSSA general secretary Maryam Eslamdoust visited picket lines on Friday, and said: “Our members working in service control on the Piccadilly Line are determined to take industrial action in support of their colleague who has been unfairly treated by London Underground.

“We don’t take strike action lightly. Everyone here knows what it costs to lose a day’s pay.

“You’ve come out because you know that every London Underground worker deserves a fair hearing when a staffing matter arises.”

The walkout is not related to plans for a series of strikes by Tube driver members of the Rail, Maritime and Transport union in a separate dispute over shift patterns.

Their first strike will affect the whole network on November 26 and 27.
