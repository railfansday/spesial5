How America voted in maps and charts

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Americans are electing members of Congress, which is made up of the House of Representatives and the Senate. All 435 House seats are up for election. In the Senate, only 35 of 100 seats are being contested because senators serve six-year terms. This results tracker shows both the races being decided now and the 65 seats that are not up for election this year - 34 held by Democrats and 31 by Republicans.

Watch: Key moments from the US midterm election results

It's been an extraordinary night for the Democrats in the US midterms.

They have taken control of the Senate and the House of Representatives too.

The results in the Senate are beyond what the Democrats would have expected. North Carolina was a likely pick-up but the previously deep red state of South Carolina was a huge shock and Kansas had not returned a Democratic senator since 1932.

Texas, Iowa, Ohio and Maine also turned blue and the Democrats held Michigan. There are two seats remaining, Alaska and Mississippi, both are on a knife edge.

The shift from Republican to Democrat happened across the country. Some of the biggest swings were in Texas where the Democrats took the Senate seat and the governor.

The BBC's partner CBS News projected the Democrats would win the House just after 03:00 GMT on Wednesday (22:00 EST Tuesday).

Now it's a question of how big that win will be as counting continues.

Two significant seats are Florida 14 and Florida 25 which the Democrats have held. This is a blow to the Republicans' attempt to redraw the boundaries in their favour.

The redistricting saw both districts move from having a majority of 2024 Kamala Harris voters to having a majority of people who voted for Donald Trump.

Florida 22, which was also redrawn to favour the Republicans, saw a victory for the Democrats too.

A huge poll of voters suggests women turned out in numbers and most voted Democrat. Men on the other hand were evenly split between the two main parties.

Hispanic voters look to be one of the key groups in the elections in Florida. The poll suggests this group backed the Democratic Senate candidate Angie Nixon by 55% to 44% for the Republican Ashley Moody.

This is a turnaround from the estimated 55% of Hispanic voters who backed Donald Trump in 2024.

The poll also asked what issues voters were most concerned about. It suggests that across the country the economy was the biggest issue, followed by the state of democracy.

When voters were asked if Donald Trump's policies impacted economic conditions, the poll suggests that two-thirds believe they had made things worse.
