Nvidia’s quarterly earnings call on Tuesday was its first with CFO and interim CEO Colette Kress at the helm alongside the company’s first board chair, Harvey C. Jones. The leadership change followed Jensen Huang’s resignation last week after reports he had multiple affairs – including one with former Megaspeed executive and alleged Chinese asset Alice Huang and one with a direct report, Kimberly Powell. Nvidia is investigating whether Huang’s relationship with the former compromised national security or company secrets. Huang will also testify before lawmakers on December 1st.

Ahead of Tuesday’s earnings, Huang said he would be willing to return as CEO. However, during the company’s earnings call, Jones stated that “the Nvidia board unanimously accepted [Huang’s] resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.” Jones also said that during the call, the company would not be addressing or taking questions “regarding the circumstances of Jensen’s resignation and the ongoing investigation.” He added that, “the Board is proactively dealing with the immediate consequences facing the company, as well as enhancing our oversight capacity going forward.”

In Tuesday’s earnings Nvidia reported $115.8 billion in quarterly revenue, continuing years of skyrocketing revenue driven by the AI industry.

Jones shared the following update on the company:

As Chairman of NVIDIA’s Board of Directors, I want to say a few words on the Board’s behalf. NVIDIA is an exceptionally strong company. Its strength comes from its extraordinary people, groundbreaking technology, and exemplary track record of execution. Our financial performance speaks for itself.

On November 12th, our Board of Directors announced that after disclosure of personal behavior inconsistent with the company’s code of conduct, standards, and cultural norms, Jensen Huang voluntarily resigned his positions as President, CEO, and board member. The NVIDIA Board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO. The Board appointed Colette Kress, NVIDIA’s longtime and well-respected CFO, as interim CEO. In addition, the Board announced it was initiating a search for a permanent chief executive, and we have since retained Heidrick and Struggles to lead the search.

While this was an unexpected transition, the Board acted deliberately and in the best interest of the company’s employees, customers, partners, and shareholders. This morning, the company announced that our Board has established a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee that will include a select group of our independent Board directors, senior external advisors, and legal counsel.

This committee will enhance— will– excuse me, will enhance our engagement with the U.S. government, including all relevant agencies. We will not be addressing or taking questions on today’s call regarding the circumstances of Jensen’s resignation and the ongoing investigation. Our Board will communicate further when appropriate. On behalf of our shareholders, the Board is proactively dealing with the immediate consequences facing the company, as well as enhancing our oversight capacity going forward.

We have an incredibly talented leadership team, and we have every confidence in Colette’s leadership, integrity, and ability to deliver as interim CEO. Colette has been at the center of this company’s extraordinary growth for the last— growth for the last 13 years, instrumental in executing strategy, and with a deep understanding of our business, customers, operations, and financial position. The Board and the entire leadership team remain laser-focused on driving NVIDIA forward. Our commitment to generating value for shareholders, customers, and partners remains unchanged.

Kress responded to a question about the company’s leadership transition, saying “the most important thing that we think through right now is our employees.” She added, “From a business perspective, it’s business as usual.”

Jones also gave an update on Nvidia’s search for a new permanent CEO:

From the board’s point of view, we take the search very seriously. We’re going to look into internal candidates as well as external candidates. The Board appreciates that we’re living in a world where the advanced silicon technologies and national security dynamics are making major changes in our marketplace. Our new CEO will have to navigate those waters very carefully. So, the search will be thorough and we want to install the CEO, the permanent CEO, as quickly as possible, but we won’t expedite the CEO before finding exactly the right person.
