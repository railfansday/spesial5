Accor is paying up to own its fastest-growing business outright rather than list it now. The valuation is more than double what Ennismore was worth four years ago.

Accor has agreed to pay about €1.6 billion ($1.76 billion) to buy out minority investors, including co-founder Sharan Pasricha and Q Lifestyle Company, in Ennismore, its luxury lifestyle joint venture.

The deal, announced, Tuesday values Ennismore's equity at about €3.7 billion ($4.07 billion).

The Paris-based group plans to buy a combined 44.3% of Ennismore’s fully diluted share capital in three stages: 25.4% by the end of January 2027, another 6.3% by July 2027, and 12.6% by January 2028.

Accor has agreed to pay about €1.6 billion ($1.76 billion) to buy out minority investors in Ennismore, its luxury lifestyle joint venture, including co-founder Sharan Pasricha and Qatar's Q Lifestyle Company. The deal values Ennismore's equity at about €3.7 billion ($4.07 billion), or roughly 18 times 2027 EBITDA — the same multiple implied when Accor sold a 10.8% stake to a Qatari consortium in 2022. Accor will buy 44.3% of Ennismore's fully diluted share capital in three stages between January 2027 and January 2028, funded by accelerating at least €500 million of asset sales, a new credit facility, and possibly its own shares. Net debt stood at €3.5 billion at June 30. The business is small in rooms and large in earnings: Ennismore accounts for about 5% of Accor's rooms but roughly 20% of its recurring EBITDA, with brands including The Hoxton, Mama Shelter, SLS, Mondrian, Rixos, and Faena across nearly 200 hotels and more than 800 restaurants and bars. A U.S. listing is no longer the immediate path. Accor said it "continues to explore ways to accelerate the growth of this strategic asset, including through the entry of strategic partners or a public listing." CEO Sébastien Bazin said at Skift Global Forum in September that Accor had a "liquidity commitment" to Pasricha and the Qatari investors, and that Ennismore needs capital to expand in the U.S. In Tuesday's announcement, all €1.6 billion goes to the sellers.
