China blocks Bloomberg reporter from Trump-Putin-Xi meeting over question to Trump

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

A historic trilateral meeting takes place as President Donald Trump holds talks with Xi and Putin in Shenzhen, China, to address key global issues. Fox News' Bill Melugin delivers updates from Shenzhen, while American Strategy Group president Brian Kennedy joins to analyze the geopolitical significance of these high-stakes discussions.

Chinese officials barred a Bloomberg reporter from a high-stakes meeting between President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin after she asked a question during an earlier bilateral session.

The clash over press access unfolded on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation Summit in Shenzhen, China, where the three leaders gathered for their first-ever three-way meeting to discuss nuclear arms control, trade and the wars in Ukraine and Iran. Despite a push from the White House to get her admitted, Chinese officials refused to budge on barring Bloomberg reporter Annmarie Hordern from the room.

Hordern was blocked after she called out a question to Trump during his earlier one-on-one meeting with Xi: "President Trump, has Jensen Huang been disloyal to the United States?"

A second reporter also started to ask a question during the bilateral session but was shushed by Chinese officials. Beijing subsequently passed along a message to the U.S. travel pool warning that yelling questions is considered "extremely rude" and that journalists would be pulled from the room if they did so.

A White House official, speaking on condition of anonymity because of the sensitivity surrounding the issue, said Chinese officials insisted Hordern not be allowed into the trilateral meeting even as the White House advocated for her inclusion.

The dispute over press rules stood out alongside the friendly tone Trump sought to project toward both leaders during the summit. Trump praised his friendship with Xi, who greeted Air Force One on the tarmac Wednesday with a one-armed hug and called Putin "my friend" ahead of a separate 40-minute sit-down.

Tensions over artificial intelligence, however, still hung over the meetings.

Trump's ally Jensen Huang resigned last week as the CEO of Nvidia after confessing to an extramarital affair with a woman, Alice Huang, whose company helped give China access to Nvidia's advanced computer chips used to develop AI models. Beijing has denied that Alice Huang was a Chinese government agent.

Fox News’ Kaitlin Sprague and The Associated Press contributed to this report.
