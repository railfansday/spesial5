'I couldn't understand' - Kim Hellberg reveals the 'biggest surprise' at Middlesbrough

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Middlesbrough boss Kim Hellberg admitted that he "couldn't understand" how enormous the interest surrounding a football club in a specific town or city was before he came over to coach Boro last year.

The Swedish boss celebrated one year at the Riverside on Tuesday, and in that time, Middlesbrough have been transformed and look certain to be playing Premier League football in the near future.

Boro were inside the top two when Hellberg was hired, following the shock departure of Rob Edwards, but the brand of football he has instilled in the club has been thoroughly entertaining, both for fans of the club and neutrals alike.

Middlesbrough have a proud fanbase, and after years of Championship obscurity, they look to be on the rise again, and the energy surrounding the club from the fanbase is something that Hellberg has been surprised by during his first year on Teesside.

Kim Hellberg has seemed destined to coach in England ever since he started managing in Sweden a decade ago. But, as is often the case with foreign coaches, he was taken aback by the swell of support which surrounds football in this country.

It's tribalistic at times, and the fact that smaller cities and towns can have such a loyal, large following is always something that first-time coaches in this country take as a surprise. Hellberg is no different.

To celebrate one year in charge at the Riverside, Middlesbrough ran a Q&A with their head coach, with him answering questions from fans. One such query regarded what has surprised him the most about Boro.

"I know football was everything here, but the biggest surprise for me was the enormous interest and energy around the football club," he said.

"How everyone feels like they care about Middlesbrough and live through that, how important football is wherever you go, wherever you are, football is everything here. I think I knew some bits were like that, but I couldn't understand how big it was."

Kim Hellberg will likely be referencing how most English clubs have such a passionate fanbase that lives through the team, but in Middlesbrough especially, that's evident.

Boro are a one-club town. No other professional team is in the Middlesbrough area, with the closest professional club being Hartlepool United, 13 miles away. Even then, those in Hartlepool count themselves more in County Durham, rather than Teesside.

Either way, it's a proud following at Boro, and has been for years. Their time in the Premier League grew that, but even in the past few years, attendances at the Riverside have hovered around the 25,000 mark in the Championship.

All you have to do is look at the numbers over the past year to know that Kim Hellberg is bringing back some of the supporter base who may have become a little disassociated with the club as they stagnated in the second tier, though.

Boro's lowest Saturday attendance this season was against Cardiff City, at 28,991. Their average attendance in the entirety of last season was 27,622, and the year before, in Michael Carrick's final term, it was 25,416.

Over 3,000 more fans are coming to watch this Middlesbrough team under Hellberg than they were in the final moments of the Carrick reign, which shows how much bigger an already big support has been under Hellberg.

Hellberg went on to say that his dream as Boro boss was to bring the club back to the Premier League, which he hopes to do this season.

Middlesbrough currently sit atop the Championship table, and look good value for it, too. What's arguably more scary, though, is that they seem to still have a higher gear that they can reach, and if they can find it, they may win this league at a canter.

Then, the Swedish boss may end up being taken further aback by the extra support which will come at the top level. Boro are a club that has been starved of Premier League football, with this being the 17th of the last 18 seasons they've spent in the Championship.

You get the feeling, though, that if Hellberg can get this side into the top flight, they'll stand a greater chance of staying there than they did under Aitor Karanka a decade ago.
