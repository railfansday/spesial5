West Ham 1-1 Preston: Scott Parker Still Struggling To Unlock Jarrod Bowen

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

West Ham were nowhere near good enough against the worst team in the division. Whilst we had our chances, it would be a long stretch to suggest we were significantly better than Preston, particularly during a first half in which the visitors created opportunities of their own.

Edson Álvarez looked decent enough on his return to the team, but Scott Parker looks no closer to getting the best out of Jarrod Bowen than Nuno Espírito Santo or Graham Potter did. In essence, the Hammers lacked magic, and watching our best player pinned to the right flank was depressing.

The statistics certainly make for interesting reading. West Ham registered 30 attempts on goal, but only four were on target. We also hit the woodwork three times, with Bowen and Joël Veltman striking opposite posts during the same passage of play before Edvin Austbø rattled the crossbar shortly after half-time.

Preston took the lead through Liam Lindsay after just 18 minutes, following a corner which Joël Piroe inadvertently flicked towards his own goal.

Konstantinos Mavropanos eventually equalised on the hour mark, heading home Arne Engels’ cross after Manor Solomon had helped create the opening.

Yet even after drawing level, West Ham couldn’t find a winner against a team who arrived at the London Stadium bottom of the Championship.

There were chances, certainly. Tomáš Souček had a late header tipped over, while Mavropanos headed over in stoppage time. But for all the possession and pressure, there was precious little composure in front of goal.

And as far as I’m concerned, this was a match West Ham simply had to win.

What makes the situation particularly frustrating is that we’ve already seen what Bowen can do when he’s given more freedom.

During the remarkable comeback against Cardiff City, our captain was allowed to drift into central positions, where he caused all sorts of problems. His movement, passing and ability to find space helped transform a game which had looked completely lost at half-time.

I was so impressed that I subsequently wrote about how Scott Parker needed to restore West Ham’s most dangerous weapon.

Unfortunately, against Blackburn, Bowen was back operating as a conventional right winger. And against Preston, we saw much the same thing.

That’s not to suggest Bowen didn’t contribute. He was involved throughout and managed eight attempts on goal, including the effort which struck the post.

But there’s a difference between getting Bowen involved in the game and getting the best out of him.

West Ham have spent far too long relying on our captain to produce something special from wide positions, often with defenders able to anticipate where he’s going to receive the ball.

Against Cardiff, we briefly saw something different.

Bowen was the inspiration behind that second-half recovery. He looked far more dangerous when he was able to move into the middle and influence the game rather than remaining tied to the touchline.

I genuinely thought Parker had stumbled upon something which could change West Ham’s season.

Instead, two matches later, we seem to have gone backwards.

Of course, the manager has plenty of other issues demanding his attention.

There were some positives against Preston. Álvarez made his first Championship appearance for West Ham since May 2025 and looked reasonably comfortable in midfield, while young goalkeeper Finlay Herrick produced an excellent double save to prevent the visitors doubling their lead.

Mavropanos also deserves credit for another important goal, particularly given that he is a centre-back who continues to contribute at the other end of the pitch.

But the bigger picture remains deeply concerning.

West Ham simply had to start turning the London Stadium into somewhere opposition teams feared visiting.

Instead, the bottom club have come away with a point, and Parker has now overseen two draws and a defeat in his first three matches.

I appreciate he hasn’t been in the job long, and I’m not suggesting he’s solely responsible for problems which have been developing for some considerable time.

However, the international break gave him an opportunity to work with the players, and I had hoped to see considerably more improvement than we witnessed.

For me, the biggest concern remains our inability to get the best out of Bowen.

We have a player capable of providing the creativity, movement and unpredictability that this team so desperately needs. Yet we’re still asking him to spend most of his time playing in the same predictable areas of the pitch.

West Ham don’t lack effort. Against Preston, they certainly didn’t lack possession or attempts on goal.

And until Scott Parker finds a way of giving Jarrod Bowen the freedom to provide it, I fear we’re going to see plenty more afternoons like this one.
