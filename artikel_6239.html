Over the past year, I've redeemed 1.3 million IHG One Rewards points for 62 hotel nights. I got 0.88 cents per point on these redemptions, so while TPG's November 2026 valuations peg the value of IHG points at 0.6 cents each, it's certainly possible to get more value when redeeming IHG points.

All these redemptions mean I also have to earn a sizable amount of IHG points each year. There are many ways to earn IHG points, so you have options, whether you need to top off your account or substantially boost your balance. In this article, I'll describe how to earn IHG points.

As you might expect, you can earn IHG points when you stay at participating IHG hotels. However, the exact number of points you'll earn on stays depends on a few factors:

You'll only earn IHG points and get access to elite benefits when you book a qualifying rate through a qualifying method. Qualifying methods include IHG Customer Care, IHG websites, the IHG One Rewards app, directly at the hotel and through authorized travel agents. Authorized travel agents exclude online travel agents and are defined in the IHG terms and conditions as "travel booking platforms operating under direct distribution agreements with IHG which specifically provide for nights booked through their platforms to be qualifying stays."

IHG One Rewards frequently offers promotions that let you earn additional points on your hotel stays over the standard earning rates. However, you usually need to register for IHG bonus points promotions before completing your stay.

For IHG promotions that offer multipliers (such as double points), the multiplier typically applies only to the stay's base earnings. So, if you are an IHG elite member, your elite bonus usually won't be multiplied.

Cardholders of the Premier, Premier Select and Business cards get automatic access to the valuable IHG fourth-night-free reward perk. You can transfer Chase Ultimate Rewards points and Bilt Points to the IHG One Rewards program. Points transfer at a 1:1 ratio from both programs.

However, I don't recommend transferring points from either program to IHG. Based on TPG's November 2026 valuations, you'd need to redeem IHG points for at least 2.05 cents each to justify transferring Chase points or 2.2 cents each to justify transferring Bilt points.

As such, you'll generally only want to transfer points to IHG if you need to top off your account for a specific award. Otherwise, saving transferable points for higher-value redemptions is generally better.

The IHG One Rewards Dine & Earn program allows you to earn points when you dine at participating restaurants and bars. To use the program, you must sign up for an account online and link at least one credit or debit card to your account. Then, you'll automatically earn IHG points when you use a linked card to pay at participating restaurants and bars.

The points you'll earn vary depending on how frequently you use the program and whether you opt in for email notifications. You can earn between 1 and 8 IHG points per dollar spent on eligible purchases. These points are in addition to the rewards earned on your credit card, so consider linking and using one of the best cards for dining purchases.

You can also earn IHG points through the IHG Business Rewards program. This program allows you to earn points when you plan a meeting or event — including a wedding — at select IHG properties.

Some properties don't participate in IHG Business Rewards. And you usually can't earn more than 60,000 points on a meeting or event. Plus, qualifying spending usually excludes taxes, gratuities, service fees and the organizer's room.

Another option for earning IHG points is to purchase them. Buying IHG points can be a good deal, especially when the program offers a 100% bonus and you have a high-value redemption in mind.

The standard price to buy IHG points is 1 cent per point if you purchase at least 26,000 points. However, IHG One Rewards offers promotions several times a year during which you can purchase points with up to a 100% bonus. If you buy during a 100% bonus, you can earn IHG points for as little as 0.5 cents each.

TPG values IHG points at 0.6 cents each. Since it's easy for me to get more than 0.5 cents of value per IHG point, I usually consider purchasing IHG points whenever there's a 100% bonus sale. However, you usually can't purchase more than 200,000 IHG points per calendar year.

IHG members can transfer points to another member by paying a $5 fee per 1,000 points transferred. Each member can transfer up to 100,000 points per calendar year and receive up to 500,000 points per calendar year.

IHG members who hold Diamond Elite status — or belong to the IHG Business Rewards program and have "consumed a Business Rewards event in the current or previous calendar year" — can transfer points to other members without paying transfer fees.

So, if you have a colleague or family member who has Diamond Elite status (or is an active member of the IHG Business Rewards program) and wants to transfer IHG points to you, this could be a way to earn IHG points.

There are plenty of ways to earn IHG One Rewards points, from staying at IHG hotels and using cobranded credit cards to dining out and booking rental cars. You can also buy points when IHG offers a good promotion.

I frequently get significantly more than TPG's valuation of 0.6 cents per IHG point when redeeming for hotel stays, so I find it worthwhile to keep a decent balance. However, I'd generally avoid transferring valuable Chase Ultimate Rewards points or Bilt points to IHG unless you need to top off your account for a specific redemption.
