sony brings back its 1979 walkman headphones with retro foam ear pads

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

nearly five decades after the first walkman, sony revives its wired headphones with their blue and orange ear pads and a USB-C connection.

Sony has revived the headphones that came with its first Walkman in 1979, bringing the familiar MDR-3 design back as the new MDR-3R. The wired headphones retain the slender silver headband and brightly colored foam ear pads of the original, now updated with a USB-C connection for contemporary devices. Nearly five decades later, the design looks remarkably familiar, down to its small circular speakers and exposed metal supports.

When Sony introduced the original Walkman TPS-L2, it changed the way people listened to music, making personal audio something that could travel anywhere. Its accompanying headphones were an important part of that shift. Lightweight enough to wear on the move, they introduced a recognizable silhouette that soon became associated with portable music. The new MDR-3R returns to that early design, with its thin profile offering a striking contrast to today’s bulky wireless headphones.

The Sony MDR-3R weighs just 38 grams, which preserves the light construction that made the original headphones suitable for extended listening. Its narrow headband curves around the head, while slim metal arms connect to the small silver speaker housings. Each earcup is covered by a removable foam pad, available in the same vivid blue and orange that defined the original model. Both colors are included, allowing listeners to switch between them.

The design team at Sony has also revisited the original packaging, bringing back the blue box with its graphic grid pattern and oversized product illustration. Even the instruction manual takes cues from the earlier design. These familiar details make the headphones feel like a rediscovered piece of consumer electronics, particularly at a moment when the aesthetics of early portable technology are finding new audiences.

Inside the compact earcups, Sony has retained the original 23 mm driver size, with sound tuning intended to reproduce the character of the MDR-3. The company has made subtle adjustments for contemporary listening, while preserving the open, lightweight construction. The new model connects directly to compatible smartphones and other USB-C devices, with an in-line microphone and remote control for handling calls and playback.

The return to a wired connection is part of the appeal. As wireless headphones have become increasingly sophisticated, the familiar act of plugging in a cable offers its own convenience. There are no batteries to recharge or Bluetooth connections to manage. The MDR-3R brings the simplicity of early portable listening into an era when personal audio often comes with an accompanying app.
