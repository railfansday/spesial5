Best Laptops for Travel (2026): Which to Take on the Road

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

As a laptop reviewer, I have plenty of devices to choose from when I travel for work. In some cases, my favorite overall laptop doesn’t translate into the one I want to travel with. When I’m squeezed into a plane, I’ll choose maximum battery life and portability over features like a bigger screen or more ports.

Fortunately, there are many great travel laptops, and you’ll still appreciate them when you aren’t on the road. Of the hundreds of laptops I’ve tested in my life, here are the ones I always choose to travel with.

If there’s an obvious pick for the best travel laptop, it’s the Dell XPS 13. It’s been the best compact laptop you can buy for years, and that’s even more true in its latest incarnation. The key dimension to pay attention to on the XPS 13 is the depth, which is crucial for air travel. At 7.9 inches, it’s among the smallest laptops you can buy. Those economy-class seat-back tray tables on airplanes are only around 10 inches deep, and while that’s technically enough to accommodate even a 16-inch MacBook Pro, whether you can actually open the lid and use the laptop is another question. The XPS 13 is even smaller than the MacBook Neo, despite having a larger screen.

The XPS 13 achieves this by using a 13.4-inch display (measured diagonally), compared to many of the 14-inch laptops on this list. Second, it has very narrow bezels around that screen, minimizing the device’s overall footprint. While 14-inch laptops have become more popular in recent years, that bigger display size also increases the overall footprint—and every millimeter counts when working on a plane. The XPS 13 still has enough room for your palms when typing (unless you have huge hands), and the keyboard is among my favorites to type on.

The other two major advantages for travel are weight and battery life. This little laptop only weighs 2.2 pounds, a full half-pound lighter than the 13-inch MacBook Air. You might forget you have it in your backpack while traveling.

Now, the XPS 13 isn't the most powerful laptop in the world—at least not the cheaper models. The standard version comes with only 8 GB of RAM and an Intel chip with only two Performance cores. It’s good enough for basic web tasks, but for real work, you might run into limitations. You can address this by opting for the 16 GB model (which keeps the price under $1,000) or upgrading to the much more powerful Core Ultra 7 355 processor. The latter is much more expensive and comes with twice the storage, but it doesn’t add extra weight or size. Regardless of the chip you choose, the XPS 13 gets absolutely fantastic battery life. It depends on what you're doing with it, but you could easily work through a long international flight without worrying about plugging in.

If weight is your primary concern, the Asus Zenbook A14 is another contender. Despite being larger overall than the XPS 13 (with a bigger screen), the Zenbook A14 weighs 2.16 pounds—a hair lighter than the Dell. That’s seriously impressive, and I still remember how astounded I was when I first picked it up. Asus debuts its “ceraluminum” material in the chassis, which gives you the sturdiness of aluminum with the lightness of plastic. It’s not quite as rigid as metal, but it’s not as flimsy as ultra-light laptops in the past.

If you’re less worried about working on the plane and more about lugging a laptop around, the Zenbook A14 is a good choice. The Zenbook A14 has several advantages over the XPS 13 that can be helpful on the go, including built-in ports like a headphone jack, USB-A, and HDMI. If you’re working out of a remote office or presenting to a client, these can definitely come in handy. The XPS 13 only has two USB-C ports and nothing else.

There are a number of different versions of the Asus Zenbook A14 floating around out there. I’d stay away from the 8 GB model Asus sells and opt for the 16 GB model instead. There’s also a newer model that uses the Snapdragon X2 Elite chip, but I don’t think it’s worth the extra $450—especially not for a laptop focused on travel.

The 13-inch MacBook Air also makes a great travel laptop. It’s relatively small, thanks to its 13.6-inch screen.

In terms of portability, its main advantage over the XPS 13 and Zenbook A14 is its thinness. At 0.42 inches, it’s the thinnest laptop out there—and it doesn’t stick huge rubber feet on the bottom to compensate like other laptops do, such as the Razer Blade 14. You might not think thickness matters much in a travel laptop, but if you’re squeezing stuff into a backpack, every extra millimeter matters.

The MacBook Neo is also worth mentioning here. It’s not quite as thin as the MacBook Air, but because its screen is 0.4 inches smaller, it has a more compact overall footprint. Battery life is the bigger issue when taking the Neo on a trip, as it doesn’t last as long as the MacBook Air or Dell XPS 13. The 8 GB of RAM limitation doesn’t affect its use as a travel laptop much, but it's something to consider for a work laptop in general. That said, it has a very pretty screen for a laptop at this price.

Budget laptops aren’t typically known for being particularly lightweight and travel-worthy. Most of them struggle, especially with battery life. The HP OmniBook 5 might be an exception. You can usually find it on sale for under $700, even with 16 GB of RAM and an OLED display.

The OmniBook 5 isn’t quite as compact as the MacBook Neo because of its larger screen, but it is also half an inch thick and weighs only 2.8 pounds. The Snapdragon X Plus also delivers solid battery life, plenty to feel comfortable traveling with. It’s not a laptop without compromises (like the poor touchpad), but it comes with 16 GB of RAM, unlike some other laptops at this price. That’s a solid deal if you need something to travel with on a budget.

The Surface Pro 12 is the two-in-one laptop I’d recommend first. It’s absolutely tiny for a computer, even though it runs the full version of Windows 11. Even with the detachable keyboard connected, it weighs only 1.8 pounds. The only real problem with bringing this on a plane is the kickstand, which adds some extra depth. It’s just small enough that it should fit while open on an airplane tray table, unlike the 13.8-inch Surface Pro model.

Of course, that kickstand is also a huge advantage for the Surface Pro 12 if you’re using it without the keyboard. While that would make work difficult, it’s ideal for watching movies. Set this up on your tray table with some movies downloaded, and you’ll be the envy of your aisle. And when you need to get some work done in a coffee shop or at a smaller table, you’ll be happy to have access to the keyboard and touchpad—both of which are very comfortable to use. Just know that it's sold separately.

The iPad Air, paired with the Magic Keyboard, avoids the Surface Pro 12’s kickstand issue. And with only an 11-inch screen, it’s perfect for use in small spaces—with or without the keyboard (note that there is also a 13-inch version). If you can accomplish all your work on an iPad, more power to ya! The iPad Air should give you everything you need, so long as you get the Magic Keyboard. If your travels involve more leisurely activities, the iPad Air is a great device for reading by the pool or for playing games.

One benefit of this latest version of the iPad Air is not only its performance (thanks to the M4 chip inside) but also the front-facing camera, which has been moved to landscape for more natural-feeling video calls. Of course, you could also opt for the 11-inch iPad Pro for extra performance (and the incredible tandem OLED screen). It’s also slightly thinner and lighter, though I don't think it's worth the significant price difference.

Lastly, there’s definitely room here for laptops with 360-degree hinges—a different class of two-in-one laptops. I haven’t tested a recent Windows device with this form factor that I truly loved, but there’s no question it works well on an airplane. Just fold the screen back with the keyboard face down, and you have the perfect screen for watching movies. The Lenovo Yoga 9i 14 is a gorgeous option, though it’s incredibly expensive right now. But if you can afford it, you’ll love the 2-in-1 action combined with one of the slickest designs imaginable. The Framework Laptop 12 is also worth considering. While it’s a bit thick and heavy for a travel laptop, its smaller screen, touchscreen, and 360-degree hinge make it a good option. It’s also one of the cheaper options available, and it’s user-upgradeable.

Whether you’re packing for a trip across the world or across the state, a proper travel laptop shouldn’t be heavy or overly large. That rules out 15-inch and 16-inch laptops, which are so big you can’t use them on airplane tray tables or at coffee shops. But even when you narrow it down to 14-inch laptops or smaller, there are three portability factors to consider.

When you’re on the go, you want a laptop that doesn’t constantly make you worry about finding an outlet. Bringing a laptop power bank can help, but the biggest factor in staying sane while traveling is a device that can stay on. Many factors affect how long a laptop can last, including CPU efficiency, battery wattage, screen brightness and resolution, and more.

Long-lasting laptops, such as the Dell XPS 13, will get you through a long flight. The processor is the main thing to look out for. Qualcomm’s Snapdragon X series of chips are battery-efficient, as are Apple’s M-series chips. Intel also has some highly efficient CPUs in its most recent lineup, though you’ll need to pay close attention to the model number, as it can vary wildly depending on the specific CPU you’re using. To dig deeper, read my extensive guide on How to Buy the Right Laptop, which breaks down different CPU options.

The size of seat-back tray tables varies. There’s no exact size because of various factors, such as the airline, aircraft, cabin, and seat. Remember: Just because a laptop can physically fit on a tray table doesn’t mean you’ll have space to open and use it. With that in mind, here are the general averages to keep in mind. Airlines do not list tray table sizes, but you can expect one in economy to measure around 16 x 9 inches, while premium cabins will have larger tables depending on the seat.

I’ve included both tablets and laptops in my recommendations, but your choice will depend on why you’re traveling with a device. If it’s for a vacation and you really only need a device to read, watch, and play on (perhaps with the occasional email check), a tablet works perfectly. Lounging with a tablet, whether on a plane or by the pool, feels much more leisurely than using a laptop. More importantly, it'll help you avoid dipping back into work mode. Whether an Android tablet, iPad, or Windows tablet, just pair it with a compatible keyboard case—and you’re good to go.

However, if you’re traveling for work (or need to work while traveling), a proper laptop will be better. The one you already have may work just fine, but frequent fliers know they prefer a more compact device. And more specifically, a small laptop with a keyboard and trackpad will work better on your lap or on small desks than a tablet.
