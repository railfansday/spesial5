Price survives last-leg thriller against Springer to set up Doets semi-final

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Gerwyn Price survived match darts from Niko Springer to make it through to the semi-finals; he will take on Kevin Doets, who defeated Jermaine Wattimena; watch the Grand Slam of Darts in Wolverhampton live on Sky Sports

Gerwyn Price survived a dramatic last-leg thriller against Niko Springer for a 16-15 victory at the Grand Slam of Darts to reach the semi-finals.

It was a staggering encounter in which Springer would just not go away. That paid dividends when, for the first time in 29 legs, Springer took the lead at 15-14.

Price took out a clutch 143 finish to send the match all the way and in the final leg, Springer went low on D4 for 73 in a missed match dart.

That gave Price his chance at 78 and with T18 followed by D12, he was through to another major semi-final.

The Welshman is aiming for his fourth Grand Slam of Darts title, adding to his 2018, 2019 and 2021 victories.

He takes on Kevin Doets after an inspired display from the Dutchman which saw him hit two Big Fish and reel off 10 legs in a row in a 16-7 routing of Jermaine Wattimena.

"I'm probably quite lucky to come through that game. I missed so many chances. I should have punished him and being so far in front, but I kept him in the game," Price told Sky Sports.

"I kept missing doubles after doubles, even though he was scoring as well as I was.

"I was missing opportunities after opportunities. But I've got to add another one at the end.

"I'm pleased now but if I lost that game, you know what it's like.

"It's a game that you shouldn't lose and I felt like it's a game I shouldn't have lost anyway. I missed so many opportunities."

Early on it looked like three-time Grand Slam champion Price was playing in a way that his opponent would potentially not be able to keep up with as he broke the German in the first leg and then broke twice more and took four legs in a row for a 7-3 lead.

Springer was averaging over 104 but he still could not get within touching distance.

Despite Price's levels, Springer would not give up and in the third session he started to make big inroads. By taking out three of the five legs available, he drew the match closer to 11-9.

It looked like Price was going to run away again as he took a four-leg lead at 13-9 up but as was the story of the tale, back came Springer once again with the match turning edgy and doubling becoming troublesome.

A run of three legs on the spin brought Springer within one leg of Price and then a burst of two more finally saw him level at 14-14.

It was then in the 29th leg that Springer took the lead for the first time in the contest but in classic 'Iceman' style, he pinned a sensational 143 finish to take the quarter-final all the way.

Even in that deciding leg, Springer had a chance to take the match but a low dart on D4 allowed Price to check out 78.

Kevin Doets put in a stunning performance and stormed into his first TV semi-final with a 16-7 victory over his friend Jermaine Wattimena, taking out 10 legs in a row and reeling in two 'Big Fish' finishes on the way.

Doets averaged 102.63, had 46 per cent on the doubles, eight 180s and 15 140s in a complete performance.

Wattimena had the early advantage thanks to some incredible doubling and a break of throw that had him 3-2 up after the early exchanges.

But after a second blistering mini session, in which Doets averaged over 114, he had levelled the game at 5-5.

It was from that stunning standard that Doets made his move. Rattling off six legs in a row, Doets punished a bust score from Wattimena for a break of throw and then reeled in the Big Fish for another.

In the blink of an eye, he was 10-5 up and Wattimena was floundering.

Doets was on a magnificent roll and made it all the way to 14-5 ahead and 10 legs in a row before Wattimena finally stopped the rot, a second sensational 170 finish showing the ridiculous levels that Doets was reaching.

While Wattimena got a consolatory two legs back, Doets came back, broke the throw, and then finished on D18 to make it to the final four on his Grand Slam debut.

"I feel right now like an elite player, but you're not part of the elite if you haven't won anything," Doets told Sky Sports.

"I have won a floor tournament but it's not the elite yet.

"I'm working towards it. I'm touching them. I'm very close but not yet."
