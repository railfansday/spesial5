NFL Gameday Guide: Mahomes is looking like himself... be warned

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Watch Patrick Mahomes' Kansas City Chiefs face the Arizona Cardinals live on Sky Sports NFL from 5.30pm Sunday, followed by the Jacksonville Jaguars against the New York Giants and the Minnesota Vikings versus the San Francisco 49ers in Mexico City

The 'best player in the NFL' debate can be subjective. Do you lean towards longevity, legacy and historical success? Or do you favour the recent and the now?

Should it be the former, there are many footballing disciples who may still deem Patrick Mahomes as their No 1 player in the league. That is not to discount the otherworldly football being played by Joe Burrow, or the narrative-crushing exploits of Brock Purdy or the perennial brilliance of Josh Allen. But Mahomes was that special, that good, that undeniable throughout the peak of Kansas City's dynasty years that his already-imposed legacy keeps him in the discussion.

Last Sunday there were more shades of Mahomes at his video game best as he and a reborn Chiefs offense shredded the Atlanta Falcons.

The two-time MVP and three-time Super Bowl champion delivered his first five-touchdown passing game since 2022 as he threw for 348 yards and zero interceptions in a 35-13 victory that lifted the Chiefs to 7-2 at the top of the AFC West alongside the Denver Broncos.

Mahomes has now thrown for 2,385 yards and 19 touchdowns to just four interceptions while leading an offense ranked second in yards, sixth in passing, fifth in rushing and eighth in scoring. He is also playing in his first season back since undergoing surgery on a torn ACL and LCL suffered in December last year; it is a bubbling Comeback Player of the Year storyline.

The Mahomes-led Chiefs offense was famously the league's most dangerous decapitation threat, his aggressiveness and execution downfield frightening defenses across football into retreating into high umbrella coverages designed to cut out chunk plays.

It would fizzle out in the post-Tyreek Hill years as Andy Reid and Mahomes were forced to adapt to longer, more patience-reliant drives, while also leaning heavily on Steve Spagnuolo's defense to get their understaffed attack out of trouble time and time again.

The air yards still aren't close to what they were, or where Chiefs fans would like them to be. But perhaps the most pleasing sign of Sunday's performance was the 39-yard touchdown strike to Cyrus Allen and the 43-yard heave to Andrew Armstrong while rolling to his right. Here we was throwing with conviction again, dissecting a defense downfield. The latter, in particular, was vintage, off-platform Mahomes.

For context, Mahomes recorded seven passes of 20-or-more yards for just the second time since 2022.

Both cases were also a reminder of his reality. Allen - a fifth-round rookie. Armstrong - a second-year undrafted free agent. For so long it has been a mix-and-match Chiefs offense, but Mahomes and Reid are finding ways to win.

Sure, it came against a poor Falcons defense, but Sunday was also a welcome nod to their ability to move the ball without star running back Kenneth Walker III, who was absent through injury after his lightening start to the campaign.

Instead Mahomes turned to his newest star in fifth-round rookie running back Emmett Johnson as he produced a breakout game with 22 carries for 101 yards and six catches for 70 yards and two touchdowns.

"He's here extra early, he's here late, he's not satisfied with just having one good game," said Mahomes. "I'm excited for him to continue to get more and more involved in the offense and really utilising his skill set to go along with Kenneth's as well."

Johnson brings a new two-way dimension to Reid's offense, which also looked a different proposition with trade arrival Josh Downs in the lineup.

Downs marked his debut with four catches for 53 yards and a touchdown, his separation skills evident immediately as a potential difference-maker down the stretch. Funny what Mahomes can do with a little time and a good receiver to throw to.

"It looks like Pat [Mahomes] and he have a little connection thing going there, so that's something we can build on," said Reid.

Peformances like that of Sunday can nudge Mahomes into the MVP race, not that he cares for individual honours by now. Performances like that of Sunday, with Walker still to come back into the mix, can take the Chiefs back into Super Bowl contention.

Beyond this weekend's clash with the Arizona Cardinals, Mahomes and co hit the road to face the Buffalo Bills, the Los Angeles Rams and then the Cincinnati Bengals. We are about to learn who the 2026 Chiefs really are. We are also about to see one of the best three-week stretches of football all season.

No 1 Draft pick Fernando Mendoza will make his second start for the Las Vegas Raiders this weekend after taking the reins in Sunday's defeat to the Super Bowl champion Seattle Seahawks following Kirk Cousins' trade to the Minnesota Vikings. To make your first start against Seattle's league-best defense is just about as daunting an acid test as any young quarterback can face, and Mendoza faces a similar baptism of fire against a Denver Broncos pass rush ranked third in pressure rate this weekend.

The Heisman Trophy winner finished Sunday's defeat to the Seahawks 17 of 29 for 143 yards alongside a rushing touchdown on his opening drive, an impressive first half prompting Mike Macdonald to send more pressure his way after half-time while the Raiders offense also suffered after losing star tight end Brock Bowers to injury. His pocket management and blitz pick-up cost him in the face of free rushers resulting in sacks, and there were plays he would have wished to repeat but with more aggression to attack downfield. But again, it was Macdonald's Seahawks. The Broncos defense are currently allowing the sixth most yards per game in football; Mendoza will get his chance to move the ball.

Trevor Lawrence is playing at an MVP level for the Jacksonville Jaguars, having translated last season's long-awaited explosion into consistency among the league's leading quarterbacks in 2026. The former No 1 pick has thrown for 2,284 yards and 21 touchdowns to just five interceptions with a third-ranked passer rating of 112.8 behind only Brock Purdy and Joe Burrow. He continues to play the best football of his career with downfield aggression, tight window accuracy, off-platform playmaking and the decisiveness of a man with zero doubts over his ability to hurt every defense.

He is feasting in unison with star weapon Parker Washington, who is combining devastating route-running with airborne contested catches to become one of the most dominant receivers in football. Washington just came off a career day of seven catches for 152 yards and a touchdown against the Titans as he cruises towards a new contract and a big pay rise.

Arvell Reese is a problem, a kind of problem the New York Giants defense has been longing for in a position devoid of stardom for some years. The first-round inside linebacker has marched out as favourite to win Defensive Rookie of the Year while asserting himself as a future stalwart of this Giants team in a dominant first year. Reese leads the Giants with 81 tackles (including a league-high 53 solo), eight tackles for loss, 4.5 sacks, four pass breakups, an interception, a forced fumble and a fumble recovery.

It was wondered whether he would come into the league as an edge rusher when picked fifth overall, but it is clear Dennard Wilson is having more fun deploying him as an all-action sledgehammer from an off-ball alignment. His play diagnosis is veteran-esque, he attacks downhill with vicious speed, his ability to suffocate runners laterally is already elite and he offers the luxury to play in coverage, line up on the edge and be utilised as a blitzer. He has been a joy to watch.

It is déjà vu in the NFC South as football's worst division continues to fuel debate over a playoff format that last year allowed the Carolina Panthers to claim the fourth seed with a losing record; it could yet happen again. Between them the Panthers (4-5), Tampa Bay Buccaneers (3-6), New Orleans Saints (3-6) and Atlanta Falcons (3-7) hold a combined 4-17 record at home, the worst combined home record in a single campaign by any division, and surpassing the NFC South's own previous record of 10-22 in 2014, according to Opta.

It is the battle of bad teams, partially epitomised on Sunday when the division-leading Panthers beat the Saints despite posting 17 penalties for a loss of 152 yards. Tampa's efforts to put pressure on at the top could be dealt a blow after Baker Mayfield suffered an injury to his foot and ankle area in Week Nine against the Chicago Bears, putting his status to face the Lions in doubt. With that said, their best work has come on the ground this season as the only team averaging more rushing yards per game (163.9) than passing yards (141.9).

The NFL celebrates its ninth and final international game of the season on Sunday night when the San Francisco 49ers face the Minnesota Vikings in Mexico City, which returns to the schedule for the first time since 2022 following renovations to the Estadio Azteca. It pits two contenders against one another, Kirk Cousins' return having ignited Minnesota's limping offense and Brock Purdy soaring among the MVP contenders at the front of a San Francisco Super Bowl charge.

Minnesota arrive 7-2 after dismantling Jordan Love and the Green Bay Packers in an eight-sack performance from Brian Flores' frightening defense, while the 49ers just put up 46 points in a win over the Dallas Cowboys that saw Christian McCaffrey erupt for 223 yards and three touchdowns from scrimmage. It is the Vikings and their league-leading scoring defense versus the 49ers and an offense that ranks first in total yards and second in points per game. Enjoy, Mexico.
