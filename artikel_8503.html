Black Friday no longer fits in a single day. It now sprawls from early November through Cyber Monday, and every inbox, app, and billboard is shouting “lowest price ever” at the same time.

If you’re a physician, you don’t have the hours to sort real discounts from recycled list prices between clinic, call, charting, and a home life that also wants your attention.

So we did the sorting for you. This is not a shopping spree, and it’s not a detour from your FIRE plan. It’s a short list of deals on things busy physicians actually use: tech that saves time at work, gear for the home office and post-call recovery, kitchen tools that make weeknight meals easier, and a few upgrades that make hosting the holidays a lot less stressful.

The rule hasn’t changed since last year: if you need it, buy it well. If you don’t, keep the money and the mental bandwidth.

We cross-checked this year’s deals against Wirecutter, Wired, Forbes Vetted, and the New York Post, then checked every price on Amazon ourselves. We also brought back the picks Physician on FIRE readers actually bought from last year’s guide, wherever they’re discounted again.

Retailers count on urgency, not strategy. Plenty of “doorbusters” are engineered to look like a steal and end up as clutter (or e-waste) by February. A discount only matters if the product earns its place in your life.

Black Friday shopping doesn’t have to derail your financial plan or eat a day you don’t have. Everything on this list solves a real problem, lasts years rather than months, or buys back time. But value only counts if the purchase matches a need you already have.

If your coffee maker is dying, the Moccamaster is a great buy. If it works fine, keep the $248. If nobody in your house has vacuumed since September, the Shark will change your life. If you already have a routine that works, enjoy the savings by doing nothing at all.

The deals are real. The pressure to buy is manufactured.

Most of this year’s deals went live the week before Thanksgiving and run through Cyber Monday (November 30, 2026). Some Amazon prices change hourly, and popular items sell out, so if something on this list solves a real problem for you, don’t wait until Monday night.

Nearly every deal in this guide is available to all Amazon shoppers. Prime members sometimes get faster shipping or occasional member-only pricing. If you’re not a member, a free trial can cover holiday shipping. Just set a reminder to cancel if you don’t plan to keep it.

For electronics, small kitchen appliances, and home goods, Black Friday and Cyber Monday usually match or beat the year’s lowest prices. Inflated “list prices” do exist, though. We checked each item against current Amazon pricing and recommendations from Wirecutter, Wired, Forbes, and the New York Post. Price-history tools like CamelCamelCamel or Keepa can confirm a deal before you buy.

After Cyber Monday, the next big Amazon events are Prime Day (typically July) and Prime Big Deal Days (typically October). For most categories, though, the Thanksgiving-to-Cyber Monday window remains the year’s best chance at deep discounts.
