Tanggal: 2026-11-09

The entire interview with Dr. Dahle is worth a listen, including his personal reflection after his serious climbing accident. Regarding TIPs, he noted that a ladder can be a great option, including a good fit for a withdrawal strategy for people retiring with "just enough." He also mentioned that he discussed their existing TIPs ladder with his wife, and she felt a TIPs fund was just more convenient if something happened to him, given their overall financial standing. I think he took pains to stress that the choice of a ladder depends on each investor's situation, especially for those where sequence-of-returns risk is a more significant issue.
Indeed, there are many reasons why one may choose a TIPS ladder. For my case it was mainly for one reason: simplicity. In the absence of an inflation indexed annuity, this is as close as you can find to inflation indexed money showing up in a predictable manner with predictable characteristics without a lot of intervention or calculations required, especially if a relatively disinterested spouse might be involved. When combined with SS (which requires no intervention at all) and stock dividends in taxable space (which can also be set to auto transfer to checking), very little needs to be done. The TIPS ladder is but a fraction of our total investments, with the rest in stocks which can be tapped for anything else as needed.

Sometimes, there are reasons when even those with "more than enough" might choose to go this route.

Cheers.
