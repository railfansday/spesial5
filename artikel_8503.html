10 Countries Where $2,000 a Month Covers a Good Life – Check the Visa First

About $2,000 a month, close to the average Social Security check, covers a comfortable life in countries such as Colombia, Ecuador, Portugal, Thailand, and Albania. Before you move, check that your income meets the residence visa’s income test and price a health policy, because Medicare usually won’t pay for care outside the U.S.

The average retired worker’s Social Security check will be about $2,170 a month in January 2027, after a 3.6% raise, by the Social Security Administration’s estimate. That’s a tight budget in most of the U.S. and a comfortable one in much of the world.

Rent and groceries are the easy part. Whether $2,000 works abroad depends on the income a residence visa asks you to prove and on what health care will cost you there, since Medicare usually doesn’t cover care outside the U.S.

Each budget below adds Numbeo’s November 2026 estimate of one person’s monthly costs before rent to its average rent for a one-bedroom apartment outside the city center. Neither figure includes health coverage, so price that separately. Dollar conversions use the Treasury’s exchange rates for Sept. 30, 2026.

Living in Mérida costs about $1,200 a month, $730 for everyday costs plus $470 for a one-bedroom, by Numbeo’s count. The visa is the hard part. Mexico’s consulate in San Diego asks would-be temporary residents for $4,049 a month in income over the last six months, or a balance of at least $68,241 in each of the last 12. A $2,000 income qualifies only with savings behind it.

Residents can buy into IMSS, Mexico’s public health system, through a voluntary family plan that excludes some pre-existing conditions. It costs 20,600 pesos, about $1,140, a year at ages 60 to 69 for March 2026 through February 2027. The State Department rates Yucatán, Mérida’s state, at its lowest advisory level while telling Americans not to travel to several other states.

Medellín costs about $1,450 a month, $810 for everyday costs plus $640 for a one-bedroom, by Numbeo’s count. Colombia’s pension visa asks for a pension of at least three times the monthly minimum wage, which the government set for 2026 at 1,750,905 pesos. That puts the bar near 5.25 million pesos, about $1,580.

The State Department says adequate health care is available throughout Colombia, though rural care may fall below U.S. standards. Its travel advisory asks Americans to reconsider travel to Colombia because of crime, terrorism, civil unrest, and kidnapping.

Cuenca costs about $910 a month, $500 for everyday costs plus $410 for a one-bedroom, by Numbeo’s count, and Ecuador uses the U.S. dollar, so exchange rates don’t move your budget. Its retiree visa asks for a foreign pension of at least three times the basic monthly wage, which is $482 in 2026. That makes the bar $1,446 a month, and the visa lasts two years.

The State Department says Ecuador’s major cities offer adequate medical and dental care. It advises increased caution across Ecuador because of crime and civil unrest.

Lima costs about $980 a month, $590 for everyday costs plus $390 for a one-bedroom, by Numbeo’s count. Peru’s rentista residence asks for at least $1,000 a month in permanent net income from abroad, such as a pension.

The State Department says Lima and Peru’s other big cities have health facilities, though hospitals and doctors often want payment up front. It advises increased caution in Peru because of crime, civil unrest, and kidnapping risk.

Santo Domingo costs about $1,000 a month, $650 for everyday costs plus $350 for a one-bedroom, by Numbeo’s count. The country’s pensioner residence asks for a pension of at least $1,500 a month, plus $250 for each dependent, and salary income doesn’t count toward it.

The State Department calls private hospitals in large cities fairly adequate, with care that varies, and says most providers take only cash, often before treatment. It advises increased caution in the Dominican Republic because of violent crime.

Braga costs about $1,480 a month, $690 for everyday costs plus $790 for a one-bedroom, by Numbeo’s count, while the same math in Porto comes to about $1,750. Portugal’s D7 visa asks for steady income equal to at least the Portuguese minimum wage, €920 a month in 2026, or about $1,040.

The D7 also asks for an account at a Portuguese bank and health insurance for your first 120 days. Rent is Braga’s catch, since it takes more than half of the budget.

Tirana costs about $1,250 a month, $750 for everyday costs plus $500 for a one-bedroom, by Numbeo’s count. Americans can stay up to a year without a residency permit, the State Department says, so the first year comes with no income test.

Staying longer means applying for residence. The State Department also warns that rural care may fall below U.S. standards and that hospitals and doctors often want payment up front, so keep an international health policy.

Tbilisi costs about $1,150 a month, $690 for everyday costs plus $470 for a one-bedroom, by Numbeo’s count. Americans can live, work, or study in Georgia for up to 365 days without a visa, the State Department says.

It calls Tbilisi’s health care adequate but below U.S. standards and notes that hospitals may want payment up front. Its travel advisory tells Americans not to travel to the regions of Abkhazia and South Ossetia.

Chiang Mai costs about $850 a month, $540 for everyday costs plus $310 for a one-bedroom, by Numbeo’s count. Thailand’s retirement visa, for people 50 and older, asks for 65,000 baht a month in income, about $1,940, or 800,000 baht in the bank, about $23,800.

Its one-year O-A version also requires health insurance with at least 3 million baht, or $100,000, of coverage. The catch is the exchange rate. At September’s rate, $2,000 buys about 67,200 baht, so a rise of a little over 3% in the baht would leave a $2,000 income short of the test.

Cebu costs about $870 a month, $540 for everyday costs plus $320 for a one-bedroom, by Numbeo’s count. The Philippines’ Special Resident Retiree’s Visa, for people 50 and older with a lifetime pension of at least $800 a month, asks for a $15,000 deposit and a $1,500 processing fee.

That’s $16,500 in cash before you settle in, which makes savings, not monthly income, the catch here. The State Department says rural care may fall below U.S. standards and that hospitals and doctors often want payment up front.

Each year you wait to claim Social Security past full retirement age, up to 70, adds 8% to your benefit, the SSA says, which builds a cushion above a test like Thailand’s 65,000 baht.

Pension visas want a pension certified by whoever pays it, so Social Security and company pensions count, while a salary doesn’t, as the Dominican Republic’s rules spell out. Savings can do the rest. At a 4% yearly withdrawal, the rate behind saving 25 times your yearly spending, $600,000 produces $2,000 a month. Smaller savings can cover a deposit such as Thailand’s 800,000 baht or the Philippines’ $15,000.

Remote income works too, though visas for remote workers can ask for more. Malaysia’s nomad pass requires $36,000 a year from tech workers starting Dec. 1, 2026, and $60,000 from everyone else.

Pick the two countries whose visa your income clears today, price a health policy for each, and rent for a month in both cities before you sign a lease or sell your home.
