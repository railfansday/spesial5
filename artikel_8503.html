Investing - Theory, News &amp; General • Re: [2026 Boglehead] Conference experiences?

Tanggal: 2026-11-17


I don't like giving poor reviews, as the presenters are experts in their fields. However, Sunday's last session "Money and Memory" with Christine Moriarty got me going. There was absolutely no need to discuss the medical aspects on the relationship between medical conditions and dementia. To me, throwing up a bunch of medical conditions and claiming they all had a part in dementia was over-the-top. From personal experience, I disagreed with a number of those claims. There may be some relationship, but certainly not in the manner presented. All it did was get the audience emotionally hyped thinking that dementia is pervasive and everywhere. No. Please let the medical experts present these aspects. Medical discussions have no place in a financial conference.

Otherwise, the key point of recognizing dementia early-on is a good one. I also thought that this presentation somewhat duplicated earlier ones.
My take on this is somewhat different. There were a couple presenters that at least mentioned medical issues, my take is that these presentations discussed the practical issues that caregivers experience particularly the time, the money, and the frustrations involved. Hard to say where the line is drawn about how much sharing of personal experiences and discussing medical issues related to dementia are appropriate. The presenter did mention that there is a lot more hope for those with these symptoms, in some cases are caused by things that are relatively simple to correct. In other cases where the symptoms cannot be reversed, we are now better able to manage them than we were 40 years ago.

Many years ago, a family member was having mild strokes but swore friends to secrecy and unfortunately it caused personality changes that were disheartening to say the least, at some point this person had a big stroke and lived for about 6 more years before another one took him. It was a very difficult thing to deal with and family members didn't say much because it was emotionally painful and didn't want to burden the kids.

It is heartbreaking to see loved ones age and eventually pass on even if they age well and don't experience dementia. As the presenter said, dementia doesn't always run in families but aging always does. I don't like to see others decline particularly those that I love but there are certain realities to life. On the other hand, people really can age well and life at any stage is well worth living.

I viewed these presentations as a reality check as often there is a lot of denial over aging. This denial can be so strong that sometimes there is no planning for the inevitable and the children wind up scrambling. Even when there are excellent documents and preparation, there is a lot to do in the last stage of life and then when someone ultimately passes.

A person who sat next to me for much of the conference did raise the issue of repetition in the presentations. Some of this happens because the speakers cannot compare notes beforehand. My take is that repetition to some degree is good, lessons need to be reinforced.

I think this was a good critique but perceptions among the audience will vary and there will be differences of opinion. Again, it is hard to say where to draw the line on these issues. Another reason for these presentations is that retirees having parents or in-laws that are still living is becoming more common. I often tell my mom that her children are all older men.
