Why Jumia Technologies Plunged Today

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Jumia delivered mixed third-quarter earnings and fourth-quarter guidance that came in far below expectations.

By Billy Duberstein – Nov 18, 2026 at 4:15PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Jumia Technologies Ag ›

Shares of Jumia Technologies (JMIA -40.82%) plunged on Wednesday, falling 40.6% on the day.

Jumia reported earnings today that disappointed investors, particularly regarding fourth-quarter guidance. Needless to say, the market didn't like hearing this news from the Africa-focused e-commerce company, punishing the stock with a brutal drawdown.

In the third quarter, Jumia grew revenue 33%, or 28% in constant currency, to $60.7 million. Meanwhile, the company's operating loss narrowed by around 30% to a loss of ($30.8 million).

Those appear to be solid figures; however, gross merchandise volume was up only 16%, adjusted for the company's exit from Algeria. That GMV growth was actually at the low end of prior guidance, as Jumia pivoted during the quarter toward more profitable lines of business and away from a "growth at all costs" mentality.

More troubling, however, was the fourth-quarter guidance, which management lowered to just 4%-8% GMV growth. Management cited particular trouble in the Ivory Coast, where a late cocoa harvest at lower cocoa prices has put pressure on its consumers there. Additionally, higher memory prices are suppressing low-end smartphone purchases across the region, which is also affecting growth in Jumia's geographies.

Jumia has been growing revenue and reducing its operating losses, but growth investors are likely particularly disappointed by the rapid slowdown in the third quarter and into the fourth quarter.

Jumia raised $50 million in cash through an equity capital raise in August at a much higher stock price. So, that fortified Jumia's balance sheet as it invests in new growth opportunities. However, Jumia has still had a net loss of nearly $43 million for the first nine months of 2026. So, Jumia will either have to turn profitable in the medium term or raise capital again.

If the fourth-quarter slowdown is just a speed bump due to cocoa-crop factors, the stock could be a bargain. However, Jumia remains a loss-making business operating in a more volatile part of the world, and these inherent risks certainly came to the forefront today.
