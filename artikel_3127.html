Navios Maritime Partners L.P. (NMM -1.68%) reported increased net income for the third quarter as the company utilized cash flow for fleet expansion and debt reduction. Vincent Vandewalle: Thank you, Eri. Strait of Hormuz closure has created a major energy and shipping shock, affecting about 20% of the worldwide crude product and LNG flows. The disruption has tightened tanker availability and driven freight rates for all sizes sharply higher. Rates for VLCCs hit all-time highs, reaching $1.4 million per day and remained elevated even as all flows out of the Middle East returned to pre-war levels. Increased crude volumes from the U.S.A., Brazil, Venezuela and Guyana heading to both Europe and Asia are adding more ton miles as worldwide stocks have been drawn down and oil demand continues at high levels.

Product tanker rates have also risen with the Baltic Clean Tanker Index reaching an all-time high of 3,015 points last week, 264% higher than long-term average of 826. Higher fuel costs and security of supply concern continue to drive the purchasing and transportation of commodities and finished goods. This has raised rates for dry bulk assets for both Capes and Panamaxes and has continued to support container time charter rates. The conflict in Ukraine and effects due to El Nino will also add ton miles for most vessel types. We expect coal imports to continue at elevated levels, while LNG passage through Hormuz remains restricted.

With negotiations between the U.S. and Iran at an impasse and increasing attacks in the Strait of Hormuz and Southern Red Sea, vessel utilization will continue to run at high levels, supporting elevated rates for the near term. Medium-term trade adjustments depend on how long oil prices stay elevated. And, for example, whether decreased fertilizer availability affects crop supply later this year. Strategic and commercial crude and product reserves will need to be restocked, which should keep tanker rates elevated over the longer term. However, prolonged Hormuz closure could still trigger a global slowdown or a recessionary demand shock, which could affect all shipping markets. Please turn to Slide 18 for the review of the dry bulk industry.

Demand growth for dry bulk trade has been relatively stable over the last 25 years at about 4% compound annual ton-mile growth. The current order book stands at about 15% of the total fleet and is expected to remain low due to high newbuilding prices, uncertainty about new fuel regulations, yard availability and general market outlook. The fleet is aging quickly with 39% of the vessels 15 years old. With older vessels far exceeding those on order, supply should be constrained over the medium term. Please turn to Slide 19. The main driver of dry bulk demand will be strong Atlantic Basin iron ore growth over the next several years with new projects in Guinea, Brazil and Liberia.

The largest new project is Simandou in Guinea, which started shipments at the end of last year and is expected to ramp up to 120 million by '28. Mid-October year-to-date shipments were about 20 million long-haul tons from 0 last year. Vale in Brazil has three new projects totaling 50 million tonnes expected to start exporting by the end of '26 and Liberia will add 10 million tonnes of exports in '26. Other African iron ore exports projects have been announced, but awaiting final investment decisions. In total, these 180 million tonnes are all long-haul ton miles trading, creating demand for an additional 249 Capes.

With the current order book of only 228 Capes due by '28, a further tightening of supply and demand is expected over the next few years, benefiting rates. Overall, the dry bulk market looks positive based on steady long-term demand growth and a constrained supply of vessels. Please turn to Slide 21 for the review of the tanker industry. As to supply, we see a tanker order book of 26%, about 50% of the fleet is already over 15 years old, rising quickly in the next few years. With older vessels exceeding the order book and yards offering first deliveries in '29 or '30, supply is set to be tight for several years. Please turn to Slide 22.

VLCCs reached record spot earnings of $1.4 million a day recently as a portion of the fleet has been sanctioned, damaged, restricted in trading or dedicated to ship-to-ship shuttle service through Hormuz. In all, 28% of the VLCC fleet has been restricted from normal operations, leading to exceptional spot and long-term charter rates. Please turn to Slide 23. Middle East crude oil flows have recently returned to pre-war levels, although their composition has shifted significantly. Increasing use of ship-to-ship transfers, along with exports out of the Red Sea and via pipelines have created more inefficiencies and led VLCC rates to the current extreme elevated rates. This has also helped Suezmax and Aframax crude tanker rates to rise across the board.

The tanker market also looks positive over the medium term based on a low order book compared with an aging and reduced fleet due to sanctions. Please turn to Slide 25 for the review of the container industry.
