PayPal vs. SoFi Technologies: Which Financial Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

PayPal's global reach and SoFi's shift to profitability pull in opposite directions, and the valuation gap between them may tip the debate for 2027 investors.

By Sara Appino – Updated Nov 20, 2026 at 6:05PM EST

Deciding between PayPal (PYPL -5.46%) and SoFi Technologies (SOFI -7.76%) requires balancing established market dominance against aggressive digital growth. Both companies are fighting for a larger share of your digital wallet today.

PayPal remains a global giant in payment processing, while SoFi is a fast-growing digital bank targeting a younger, high-earning demographic. While they operate in different niches, both compete for the same pool of consumer financial activity, making them frequent candidates for a fintech-focused portfolio as investors look toward the future of money.

PayPal operates a massive digital payments platform serving nearly 439 million active accounts across 200 markets. It provides branded checkout services, person-to-person payments via Venmo, and even Bitcoin (CRYPTO:BTC) trading within its app. This scale makes it one of the most recognizable names in fintech stocks today as it adapts to a changing financial landscape.

Reliable recent quarterly figures for at least one of these companies aren't available, so this comparison uses each company's latest full fiscal year. For FY 2025, PayPal reported revenue of nearly $33.2 billion, representing growth of approximately 4.3% over the prior year. The company generated net income of close to $5.2 billion, showing a steady ability to convert its high volume of transactions into profit for its shareholders.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio reached roughly 0.5x, comparing total debt to shareholder equity. The current ratio, which compares short-term assets to short-term liabilities, was approximately 1.3x. Free cash flow, or the cash remaining after paying for operations and equipment, was nearly $5.6 billion in FY 2025.

SoFi Technologies is a mobile-first financial services provider that offers a wide range of products including student loan refinancing, home loans, and investment tools. It serves approximately 15.8 million members and has expanded into business services through a partnership with Mastercard (MA -0.14%). The company also supports Bitcoin trading and provides modern banking infrastructure to other firms through its proprietary technology segments.

For its latest annual report covering FY 2025, SoFi Technologies generated revenue of nearly $3.6 billion, which was an increase of approximately 35.1% compared to the previous year. The company reported net income of close to $481.3 million, marking a significant transition into consistent profitability. This growth was driven by a diversified mix of lending products and financial services that attracted new members to its platform.

Based on its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was roughly 0.2x, helping investors see the low level of debt relative to shareholder equity. The current ratio was approximately 0.2x, which measures a company's ability to cover immediate liabilities with assets that can be quickly converted to cash. Free cash flow for the year was a net loss of nearly $4.0 billion, which often occurs in banking as capital is deployed into new loans rather than held as cash.

PayPal faces intense competition from both traditional banks and newer technology platforms that offer digital checkout solutions. The company is also subject to extensive global regulatory oversight regarding payment processing and anti-money laundering rules. Any failure to manage cybersecurity risks or protect user data could lead to significant penalties and damage its reputation.

SoFi Technologies must manage the complex regulatory requirements that come with being a bank holding company. It relies on a concentrated number of whole loan purchasers, meaning its results could suffer if these buyers reduce their activity. Additionally, the company faces the challenge of managing rapid growth across its various lending and technology segments without increasing credit risks.

When comparing these two companies using Forward P/E, PayPal looks significantly cheaper than its peer based on future earnings estimates.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

I'd go with SoFi Technologies. Its sales and earnings are both growing far faster than PayPal's, and it has built a full digital bank with more than 17 million members, starting from student loans. Its bank charter lets it fund loans with its members' deposits, a cost advantage most fintech rivals lack.

SoFi also gave investors its 2027 outlook a full quarter early, and it came in above analysts' expectations, with revenue expected to grow about 27%. Management built it assuming interest rates keep rising, even after a rate hike this year hurt the stock. The shares are still down for the year, so long-term investors get a lower price for a profitable, growing business.

To give PayPal its due, it is a household name that produces plenty of cash, and Venmo is growing fast. But its original business, letting shoppers pay online with their PayPal account, is barely growing, and overall sales are rising slowly. By contrast, SoFi's lending and banking businesses are surging, and even its technology business, which lost a large client, is expected to return to growth in 2027. For long-term investors, that mix of growth, profits, and a lower price makes SoFi the better buy for 2027.
