Stock Market Today, Nov. 16: Amazon Falls on New AI Chip Export Rule and Leo Satellite Launch Failure

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Today, Nov. 16, 2026, Amazon shares slid as a new U.S. AI chip access rule and a $5 billion AWS data center deal kept AI spending in focus.

Amazon.com (AMZN -3.34%), the global e-commerce, logistics, and cloud computing platform, closed at $257.75, down 3.34%. Tech weakness and AI-chip concerns pressured shares, along with a setback in its bid to build a satellite network. A deal selling a majority stake in several data centers added to investor uncertainty.Trading volume reached 47.3 million shares, coming in nearly 2% above its three-month average of 39.4 million shares. Amazon.com IPO'd in 1997 and has grown 263,133% since going public.

The S&P 500 (^GSPC -1.92%) fell 1.92% to 7,416, and the Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC -2.49%) declined 2.49% to 24,510. Among broadline retail and e-commerce peers, Walmart closed at $128.41, up 0.25%, while Target ended at $160.32, down 0.70%, as investors also tracked holiday demand estimates.

Amazon shares moved on several news items today. A new U.S. rule tightening access to advanced AI chips in parts of Asia and Brazil has pressured AI infrastructure companies, including Amazon.

Company-specific news also disappointed Amazon investors today. A United Launch Alliance Vulcan rocket failed in flight minutes after launching from Florida, and the 40 Amazon internet satellites it carried never reached orbit. The malfunction during Sunday's climb is a major blow to ULA's new heavy-lift rocket and a costly disruption to both companies' launch schedules. The satellites were to be part of Amazon's Leo low-Earth-orbit satellite broadband network.

Amazon Web Services also announced a $5 billion data center deal with Blackstone Digital Infrastructure Trust. Selling a majority stake in some of its data center infrastructure may also have added to investor concerns about the AI sector.
