Why Gogo Stock Skyrocketed 68.7% On Friday

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Did Gogo stock really jump on a weak quarterly report? Here is why the rally may fade in a hurry.

Every so often a stock does something that makes me set my coffee down. On Friday, in-flight connectivity provider Gogo (GOGO +71.52%) jumped as much as 68.7% higher despite a weak earnings report. Sometimes, other news can override the usual market-moving headlines.

The early action made sense. Gogo's stock opened Friday at $2.22 per share, just below Thursday's closing price of $2.24. If anything, that seemed like a mild wrist-slap after missing Wall Street's expectations across the board.

Revenue fell 5% year-over-year to $213.2 million, well short of Wall Street's $229.6 million consensus. On the bottom line, a net loss of $0.02 per share missed the analyst community's expectations of about $0.08 in positive earnings per share.

But the story changed shortly after the opening bell as Gogo investors digested additional information in the earnings release.

The refinancing and the license renewal inspired an early-morning run, and then short-sellers felt the squeeze.

Despite the massive jump, Gogo stock still trades far below its 52-week high of $7.94, and the five-year chart gives me nightmares.

Even if the 10-year license extension turns out to be a game-changer, Gogo has a lot of work to do. The core business is shrinking fast as air-to-ground customers migrate to satellite-based services -- and perhaps not always from Gogo's Galileo line. Moreover, I'm not sure the short-squeeze portion of Friday's surge will last long. Those spikes tend to fade quickly, with or without real news behind the original catalyst.

This stock trades at bargain-bin multiples for good reason.
