Draw Names Instead of Buying for Every Adult – Propose It Before Thanksgiving

In a big family, buying a gift for every adult adds up fast. Propose a name draw before Thanksgiving instead, where each person draws one name and buys that person a gift under a price cap you all agree on. Everyone gives and gets one gift instead of a stack, and that one can be better.

In a family of 10 adults, buying a $50 gift for each of the other nine costs every person $450, and the family $4,500 in all.

With a name draw and a $75 cap, each person spends $75 instead of $450, or $375 less, and receives one $75 gift rather than nine $50 ones.

In Deloitte’s 2026 holiday retail survey, 47% of respondents said they would rather spend more on one meaningful gift than give several smaller ones. A name draw lets everyone in the family give that kind of gift.

Propose the switch before Thanksgiving, and before anyone has bought a gift, so the family can draw names at dinner that day or online that weekend. Pitch it as less time in stores and more fun at the exchange, not as a way to save money. Then choose the format that suits your family:

A free site such as Elfster or drawnames will run a Secret Santa draw for you. Both can keep spouses from drawing each other and let each person make a wish list, so the one gift is something its recipient asked for.

Settle the rules before anyone draws. Set one cap for every gift and treat it as a limit, not a target. Decide whether the kids join the draw or still get gifts from everyone. Agree, too, that no one buys extra gifts outside the draw, since each one raises the bill again.

Send the whole family one message with the cap, the format, and a draw date already set, so all anyone has to do is say yes.
