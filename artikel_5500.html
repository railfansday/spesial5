Ondo Stocks Now Power Spout Finance's Interest-Free Borrowing

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Covered calls on the underlying shares fund lender returns, bringing portfolio financing to eligible investors outside the U.S.

TORTOLA, British Virgin Islands, Nov. 12, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Ondo Stocks, which provides tokenized exposure to the economic performance of U.S. stocks and ETFs, announced today that Spout Finance will support Ondo Stocks as collateral on Solana from the first day of launch, enabling users to borrow USDC against tokenized stocks and ETFs with no interest charged by Spout.

This integration builds on Ondo's in-kind conversion between tokenized stocks and underlying shares via Alpaca's Instant Tokenization Network (ITN). Spout uses that route to convert Ondo Stocks to underlying shares, then sells covered calls against those shares in traditional options markets. Instead of interest payments from borrowers, the options premiums fund lender returns.

Traditional brokers typically charge interest to borrow against stocks. Spout lets users borrow against Ondo Stocks interest-free. In exchange, borrowers give up gains above the option's strike price, when the call is sold.

Spout Finance is part of Ondo Catalyst, a $250M initiative pursuing strategic investments to accelerate the Ondo ecosystem and transformation of capital markets. Ondo Catalyst backs projects building across the Ondo ecosystem, with a focus on next-generation technologies that drive utility, scalability, and adoption of tokenized assets.

"Borrowing against stocks has always meant paying interest; the only question was the rate," said Armand Khatri, Head of Ecosystem at Ondo Finance. "Spout changes what the borrower pays with. It sells covered calls on the shares behind the collateral and uses the premiums to pay lenders, so eligible Ondo Stocks holders outside the U.S. can borrow USDC at zero interest and give up upside above the strike instead. That only works because every Ondo Stock is backed by real shares, and approved institutions can redeem the tokens for those shares directly, which gives Spout actual stock to write options on. A covered call is an old strategy, and running it behind a token in a wallet is new."

Borrowers deposit Ondo Stocks as collateral and borrow USDC worth up to 50% of their collateral's value. For example, $10,000 of collateral supports a loan of up to $5,000. Borrowers must also maintain sufficient collateral. If the value of their tokenized stocks declines, collateral may be sold to repay the loan.

In-kind conversion adds a primary market route for Ondo Stocks alongside cash-funded minting. Approved institutions can transfer existing shares from their Alpaca account and receive corresponding Ondo Stocks tokens in return. Redemptions reverse the process, returning shares rather than cash.

"For decades, the income strategies of Wall Street's trading desks were available mostly to Wall Street's best clients," said J. Christopher Giancarlo, Former Chairman, CFTC; Strategic Advisor, Spout Finance Inc. "Spout is bringing a proven covered-call approach onchain, where its rules are transparent and its workings visible to all. Ondo's investment is a meaningful endorsement from one of the leaders building the share-level infrastructure that makes solutions like this possible."

Ondo Stocks are already deployed across DeFi as productive collateral. Spout will add options-funded borrowing to that growing range of uses.

Ondo Stocks is an issuance and redemption platform for tokenized publicly traded U.S. stocks and ETFs. It enables eligible investors outside the U.S. to gain exposure to the economic performance of these assets by minting, transferring, and redeeming securities-backed tokens. Each token is fully backed by the corresponding stock or ETF (together with cash in transit).

About Spout FinanceSpout Finance is a lending protocol for tokenized U.S. stocks and ETFs on Solana. It enables eligible users outside the U.S. to borrow USDC against their tokenized stocks with no interest charged, with lender returns funded by covered calls sold on the borrowers' underlying shares. Borrowers can borrow up to 50% of the value of their collateral.
