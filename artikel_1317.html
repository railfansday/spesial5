32 Highest-Paid English Footballers in 2026 [Ranked]

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Money makes the world go round. And that notion is particularly true for footballers, especially for those earning their corn in the Premier League, as they often take home jaw-dropping amounts of money each week. It has now become a construct that fans and pundits alike tend to converse about. Most notably, whether footballers deserve to be paid much more than their teammates is based on a string of factors.

The likes of Harry Kane, Jude Bellingham and Ivan Toney have seen their weekly salaries rise dramatically since their moves to Bayern Munich, Real Madrid, and the Saudi Pro League respectively. But which Englishmen are receiving eyebrow-raising amounts of money each week? And who, rightly or wrongly, is being paid the most money of all English footballers on the planet?

A goalscoring sensation for Bournemouth, it was no surprise to see Tottenham Hotspur cough up a £60 million fee for the sharpshooting Dominic Solanke in the summer of 2024. Since, the Basingstoke-born striker – capped three times at international level – has scored 24 times for Spurs. Like many on this list, the centre-forward has had to contend with a number of injury issues.

Such problems have limited his influence for Ange Postecoglou, Thomas Frank, Igor Tudor and now Roberto De Zerbi. Irrespective of how many matches he's played, he still earns £140,000-per-week. On the international stage, Solanke faces the challenge of competing with Kane for a starting berth – and, as a result, his caps have been restricted to just three.

Tammy Abraham made his return to English football in January 2026 after five years away. The Chelsea academy product, who scored 25 league goals for Aston Villa in the 2018/19 season as they won promotion back to the Premier League, completed a £18m transfer to the Villans.

He's scored plenty of goals for the club before, but has netted just twice in the league so far in his second spell at Villa Park. Villa need him to step up and be counted following the departure of Ollie Watkins and many other key players.

Having developed through Villa's academy, the 25-year-old exited for Tyneside after failing to get regular game time at Villa Park. A fee of around £43million was enough for the Magpies to add the talented midfielder to their ranks, but he has failed to catch fire so far.

Wholesale squad changes, exits and a new manager, plus injury niggles have combined to leave him struggling to find the kind of momentum that saw him heralded as a potential future England star. There is plenty of work to be done for Ramsey, as he looks to settle, find his feet and justify the £150,000-per-week that he's currently contracted for.

Mason Mount, now of Manchester United, was much further down this list while playing for his boyhood club, Chelsea. The playmaker was signed by the Red Devils in the summer of 2023 and now receives a cool £150,000-per-week in Manchester. Only a handful of players at Old Trafford take home more than the injury-prone Englishman.

His first few years in Manchester have left a lot to be desired, though his progression has been halted by a string of fitness problems. Still just 27 years of age, Mount has recorded eight goals and two assists since making the intra-Premier League switch to the Theatre of Dreams.

Carrick's system is far more beneficial to him than previous managers, but getting into the team is still proving to be a difficult task. Bruno Fernandes essentially has his dream role, which won't be changing anytime soon. Still, across four competitions, if he stays fit there's chances to prove himself once again. Whether he's able to repay his weekly wage remains unknown.

There’s an awful lot of unjust discourse over Jordan Pickford’s position between the sticks for England, isn’t there? For years, Everton’s shot-stopper has been the nation’s first choice – and, well, he’s rarely put a glove wrong. Some supporters, however, are not as convinced about the talents of Pickford, who has racked up 93 appearances for the Three Lions.

For the Toffees, the 32-year-old has been equally integral. Albeit not the tallest of goalkeepers, his combination of shot-stopping and leadership is perfect for the Merseysiders. At the time of writing, the glovesman earns £150,000-per-week and, on a separate note, he’s Everton’s highest-earning asset and an undroppable figure at international level.

The last few years of Kalvin Phillips' career can hardly have gone as he would have expected. The defensive midfielder – who earns £150,000 every week – spent eight years with boyhood club Leeds United, rising to prominence in Marcelo Bielsa's system and style of play. Such was his form that it caught the eye of Pep Guardiola and Manchester City, who moved to sign him in 2022.

Despite his calibre, Phillips struggled to break into the City first team given that Rodri was ahead of him in the pecking order. Minimal minutes led to unsuccessful loan spells at West Ham United, Ipswich Town and Sheffield United. Despite his struggles, the Blades decided to take him on loan once again for the 2026/27 campaign.

There were times when big doubts came up about whether Luke Shaw was the man to start at left-back in the long term for Manchester United. He was regularly criticised by Jose Mourinho, but has since silenced his loudest critics with excellent performances that have made him the starting left-back for his club once more when fit.

Arguably the best English player at Old Trafford, injuries plagued his progress under Erik ten Hag throughout the 2023/24 campaign and the same followed under Ruben Amorim. Are his days at Old Trafford numbered? Perhaps, but he has found better form under Michael Carrick in recent times.

After the Poole-born defender signed a brand-new bumper contract in March 2024, Ben White has proven his innate versatility by playing out of his natural position. A centre-back by trade, the Englishman had previously nailed down a regular starting position for the Gunners – but as a full-back. His influence, of course, has waned in recent times, partly due to injury issues, and he is no longer top of the pecking order.

The 29-year-old, who pockets £150,000-per-week, took to his new role like a duck to water, but he's since been dislodged by Jurrien Timber. He might not offer the same creativity as the likes of Trent Alexander-Arnold, but when it comes to a mix of attacking and defending, there aren't many right-backs better in the league than a fit White.

Is Noni Madueke a player waiting to explode? Some would suggest so; some would suggest that his poor form for Chelsea was exactly what the 24-year-old winger can provide a Premier League side. Alas, Mikel Arteta and Arsenal saw him as a forward able to provide something different from a forward-thinking perspective – and the Londoner has performed well for the Gunners.

So far, despite not being a regular starter for the north Londoners, Madueke has been a constant thorn for defences up and down the country, but 11 goals in 55 Arsenal appearances suggest room for improvement. From a wages' perspective, he earns £160,000 per-week with the Gunners as a backup to Bukayo Saka.
