Business as usual for Nvidia as it beats expectations in first earnings call after Huang’s resignation

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Nvidia Corp. delivered earnings and revenue that surpassed Wall Street’s expectations and offered strong guidance for the current quarter in its first earnings call since the shocking resignation of its iconic co-founder and Chief Executive Jensen Huang.

The results were so impressive that they slammed the brakes on a weeklong slide, as Nvidia’s stock rose more than 4% in late trading today.

The chipmaker reported third-quarter earnings before certain costs such as stock compensation of $2.68 per share, surpassing Wall Street’s target of $2.48 per share by a comfortable margin. Revenue for the period came to $115.84 billion, more than double a year earlier and above the $109.1 billion consensus estimate.

The vast majority of those sales stemmed from Nvidia’s core data center business, which delivered revenue of $107.6 billion, higher than the Street’s $101.87 billion forecast. For the current quarter, Nvidia said it’s expecting its revenue to grow by about 90%, to $130 billion. Though that suggests a slowdown sequentially from the prior quarter, it’s still nicely above Wall Street’s target of $125.1 billion.

Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress (pictured, with Huang in the background) has become a familiar face to analysts on Nvidia’s conference calls, but today she found herself in a very different position. For the first time, she was fielding questions as the company’s interim CEO, which means her responsibilities now go well beyond the numbers. She was joined on the call by Nvidia’s Chairman of the Board Harvey Jones, who insisted that the board has no intention of bringing Huang back as CEO.

Nvidia has been under a cloud all week following the abrupt resignation of Huang, which came days after he admitted to having an affair with a businesswoman who’s alleged to be a Chinese spy. In the days that followed that scandal, which included rumors of a second affair with an Nvidia executive and also a potential third fling with yet another company employee, the chipmaker’s stock had been in a downward spiral. But that trend was finally reversed following the company’s strong guidance today.

One of the central questions investors had going into the call was if Nvidia would scrap its previous forecast for 70% revenue growth in the fiscal year ending January 2028. However, Kress did the opposite and raised the company’s full-year guidance to 75% growth.

In raising the company’s outlook, Kress told analysts on the call that “it’s business as usual at Nvidia,” despite all that has happened. And indeed, throughout much of her commentary, she focused strictly to the numbers that revealed a business still in good health. With regard to the guidance, Kress stressed that this forecast “considers data center readiness, the strength and durability of customer demand and our customers’ access to capital.”

“Our leadership has stood up so strongly over the last week to support both what we are doing here at earnings, but also supporting all of our different employees around the world,” Kress said. “Remember, we have the best technologists on the planet with a world-class, multiyear roadmap. And I think that’s important from a business perspective.”

Kress said Nvidia expects frontier labs such as OpenAI Group PBC, Anthropic Group PBC and others to account for about 15% of its data center revenue this year, in a sign that they’re increasingly standing up their own data center infrastructure rather than renting from cloud providers.

“As frontier models become more capable, adoption and revenue are scaling rapidly, with Anthropic annualized revenue reaching $181 billion at the end of October,” Kress said.

Another big chunk of Nvidia’s revenue comes from so-called neocloud infrastructure providers. Much of the money those customers spend on its chips stems from a complex web of financing, with much of it being traceable back to Nvidia itself. Kress told analysts that the company is continuing to help neoclouds and frontier labs to secure the land, power and data center capacity they need to grow, and that it may be willing to share revenue from neoclouds together with the upfront sales of its products, if they meet certain conditions.

Kress said Nvidia currently has more than $32 billion in total commitments to neoclouds through 2032, but insisted that these take effect only if those customers are able to “secure the funding they need to purchase the data center infrastructure products they require, and if the related compute capacity is deployed and made operational.”

Another closely watched number is Nvidia’s gross margin, which has narrowed in recent quarters as its supply commitments have slowly piled up. Investors will have been pleased to see that Nvidia beat Wall Street’s expectations on that metric, with its nonstandard gross margin ending the quarter at 74.9%, above the forecast of 74%. Its margins still declined compared to the previous quarter, though, blamed on the ramping up of production of its latest generation Rubin graphics processing units.

The results prove that AI demand remains as strong as ever. But the bigger question for the market now is not how long Nvidia can sustain its growth, rather whether or not it can sustain its current level of profitability, Forrester analyst Naveen Chhabra told SiliconANGLE.

“The next battle is not against competitors. Nvidia must navigate export controls, sovereign AI initiatives, supply chain expansion and rising capital intensity, all while trying to protect its industry-leading margins,” Chhabra said. “Nvidia continues to defy gravity with triple-digit growth, software-like margins and a strong outlook despite trade restrictions. The next test is whether it can preserve its economic dominance as geopolitics and AI infrastructure costs rise.”

Despite the after-hours gain, Nvidia’s stock is still down 24% this month, and last Friday it completed its sixth successive weekly decline. That’s the company’s longest losing streak since 2012, and its shares are now trading at their lowest level since July 2025.

The decline began several weeks before news of Huang’s extramarital escapades became public knowledge. The company has also faced rising concerns about a possible slowdown in the artificial intelligence industry and a political shift in Washington D.C. as a result of the Republicans’ stunning losses during the midterm elections earlier this month.

Despite Jones’ insistence that Huang will not be returning to the CEO role, the prospect of his doing so cannot be ruled out. In his first public comments since his ousting, Huang said in a post on X that “if the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO.”

There are plenty of people who would like to see Huang return, including U.S. President Donald Trump and various luminaries from the technology world. Last Thursday, someone created an online petition calling for the reinstatement of Huang, and it has been signed by names including Elon Musk, Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan and Epic Games Inc. CEO Tim Sweeney.

It could be possible that Huang ends up returning to the company in a different capacity than CEO. Susquehanna analyst Chris Rolland told CNBC that the chipmaker could end up taking him back under a new role, such as a consultant or a strategic adviser.

“I think that helps bridge the gap here, he can give technical and strategic advisory services that I think would be incredibly important to the company,” the analyst said.
