Longbridge survey: Senior independence, aging in place are top retirement priorities

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

A Longbridge Financial survey of 2,021 homeowners ages 55 and older finds 90% prioritize maintaining independence and 88% prioritize aging in place, outpacing debt, leisure and long-term care. Women rated independence and aging in place 5 points higher than men, while helping family ranked lowest at 32%.  AI Summary

For older homeowners, retirement is defined less by leisure or legacy and more by a desire to stay independent and remain in their own homes, according to new research from Longbridge Financial released during National Family Caregivers Month.

The company’s 2026 Home Equity Confidence Index, drawn from a nationwide survey of 2,021 U.S. homeowners ages 55 and older, found that 90% rate maintaining independence as extremely or very important to their ideal retirement — the highest-rated priority in the study. Aging in place ranked a close second at 88%.

Both priorities significantly outpaced traditional financial concerns. Paying for healthcare was cited by 77% of respondents, followed by paying off debt (64%), leisure and travel (55%), and long-term care (55%). Helping family members financially ranked last, at just 32%, suggesting that older Americans place far greater value on their own self-sufficiency than on intergenerational financial support.

“Much of the retirement conversation focuses on whether Americans have saved enough, but our research adds another dimension — where people want to live and what it costs to stay there,” Chris Mayer, CEO of Longbridge Financial, said in a statement. “The economic challenge is aligning financial resources with the costs of maintaining a home, paying for health care, and getting additional support when needed, all while preserving the independence that homeowners clearly value.”

The research found that the pull of home grows stronger as people get older. While 85% of homeowners ages 55 to 64 consider aging in place extremely or very important, that figure rises to 88% among those 65 to 74 and 93% among those 75 and older.

Independence follows the same pattern, climbing from 87% among the youngest group to 96% among those 75 and older — the highest rating for any priority in the entire survey. Healthcare concerns also intensify with age, jumping from 73% among 55-to-64-year-olds to 85% among those 75 and older, reflecting the mounting weight of medical costs in later life.

The study also revealed consistent differences between men and women. Among women 55 and older, 93% rated independence as extremely or very important, compared with 88% of men — a five-point gap that repeated for aging in place (90% of women versus 85% of men) and healthcare (80% versus 75%).

The gap narrowed on purely financial concerns such as debt and long-term care, suggesting the divergence is rooted more in autonomy and stability than in money alone. The one area where men led was helping family financially, prioritized by 34% of men versus 30% of women.

The findings, part of a broader series of Longbridge reports, were based on a Morning Consult survey conducted between March 26 and April 5, 2026. It was designed to reflect a broad cross-section of older homeowners and was conducted without reference to Longbridge to ensure unbiased responses.
