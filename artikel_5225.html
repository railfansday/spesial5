Standard Chartered and OCBC test dollar payments in minutes without traditional banking cutoffs

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The bank says eligible clients can move funds in near real time, while a separate OCBC and StraitsX trial tested payments between banks.

Standard Chartered said Nov. 19 that eligible clients can now transfer tokenized deposits, bank balances recorded on a digital ledger, between Singapore and Hong Kong in near real time.

That gives corporate treasury teams a way to shift cash between entities in the same group on the bank’s own blockchain.

Meanwhile, a separate trial with OCBC and StraitsX tested payments across two banks’ networks.

The bank’s Singapore expansion covers treasury and investment transactions that use commercial bank deposits. Standard Chartered listed the Singapore dollar, Hong Kong dollar, US dollar, euro, offshore Chinese yuan and British pound among the supported currencies.

It named corporate treasurers, banks, investment platforms, and digital asset businesses as potential users, but detailed eligibility criteria and operating hours for the route remain unspecified.

The bank disclosed round-the-clock settlement when it launched its Hong Kong service Nov. 2. That schedule applies to tokenized deposit transactions settled between participating banks in central bank money through the Hong Kong Monetary Authority’s EnsembleTX infrastructure.

On Nov. 19, Standard Chartered, Singapore bank OCBC and payment infrastructure provider StraitsX said they had completed a controlled trial under the Monetary Authority of Singapore’s BLOOM initiative.

The trial completed US dollar payments across the banks’ separate deposit networks outside conventional cutoffs. Participants said the test showed how payments could move within minutes, including compliance checks, liquidity checks, and settlement.

The trial partners, StraitsX, OCBC and Standard Chartered, explain that each tokenized deposit remains a claim on the bank that issued it. Banks can use different platforms and standards for their deposits, so interbank payments need a way to coordinate settlement across those systems.

StraitsX coordinated payment instructions and transaction details and helped with screening and settlement. The shared infrastructure handled the payment process across both networks, from initiation to confirmation.

The partners reported that this setup reduced the need for bilateral prefunding, where banks set aside money in advance to cover payments.

A commercial service based on the trial would still depend on regulatory engagement, participant approval, legal documentation, and operational risk assessments, the partners said. They also flagged settlement finality, meaning when a payment becomes irrevocable.

Banks would need to work out who is responsible for each step, how to screen for financial crime and how to keep enough funds available for payments to settle.
