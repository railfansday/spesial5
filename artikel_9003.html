united artists for climate calls for art and culture to take stronger role in climate action at COP31

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

launched by art of change 21, the international movement brings together contemporary artists to advocate for more ambitious climate commitments, cultural representation in policymaking, and a socially just transition.

For its second edition, United Artists for Climate organizes a collective international campaign inviting artists to share their work, creative practices, and environmental initiatives through Instagram. The contributions highlight different ways of engaging with ecological issues, from collaborations with local communities and more responsible approaches to artistic production to new perspectives on humanity’s relationship with the natural world. The campaign brings together established and emerging creatives, including Judy Chicago, Olafur Eliasson, Fabian Knecht, Janet Laurence, John Gerrard, Cristina Iglesias, Julian Charrière, Anish Kapoor, Camille Henrot, Carolina Caycedo, Moffat Takadiwa, and Moara Tupinambá.

Beyond raising awareness, the initiative puts forward a set of demands directed at climate decision-makers. Its collective message calls for accelerated climate action, the recognition of art and culture as essential components of climate policy, and social justice at the center of environmental transformation. More specifically, the campaign calls for global emissions to be cut by 18 percent over the next four years and for peak global warming to be limited to 1.7°C. These figures are presented as the movement’s demands, rather than agreed international targets.

A second central demand is to recognize culture as an essential part of climate action and integrate the Culture Global Stocktake into climate decision-making. The first Culture Global Stocktake was launched at COP31 on November 10th. Developed through a year-long international and participatory process involving cultural practitioners, Indigenous knowledge holders, young people, researchers, institutions, and policymakers, it assesses how culture contributes to climate action, identifies gaps in current policy, and sets out recommendations for bringing cultural perspectives into climate frameworks. Its scope extends beyond contemporary art to include heritage, creative industries, cultural movements, and Indigenous and local knowledge systems.

The initiative also calls for social justice to be central to climate action, arguing that the transition to a more sustainable future must address inequalities and take into account the needs and knowledge of local communities.

The first edition of United Artists for Climate mobilized 290 contemporary artists from 58 countries during COP30 in Belém, Brazil. Alongside its international online campaign, eight Brazilian artists, including Christian Braga and Margem do Rio, carried out artistic interventions in and around the city. The movement has since developed into a wider international community, with more than 2.1 million combined followers across participating artists’ accounts.

United Artists for Climate is initiated by Art of Change 21, a nonprofit founded in 2014 by Alice Audouin to connect contemporary art with environmental issues and support the ecological transition of the cultural sector. Its work includes exhibitions, festivals, workshops, artistic projects, and advocacy at international climate conferences. The organization is also a founding partner of We Make Tomorrow and funds the United Artists for Climate initiative through its own resources, without brand partnerships.

By bringing artists into the conversation at COP31, United Artists for Climate argues that culture should not remain on the sidelines of climate negotiations. Its campaign asks policymakers to recognize that the ways societies understand the environment, share knowledge, imagine alternatives, and respond collectively to ecological change are also part of the work of addressing the climate crisis.
