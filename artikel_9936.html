Stale links on UK public sector websites direct visitors to gambling dens

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Consultancy finds 93 routes to casino promotions across 26 orgs, with defunct enterprise partnership domains repurposed

UK public sector websites are directing visitors to gambling promotions through old links to defunct local enterprise partnerships (LEPs), research by digital consultancy Digital Royalty has found.

The consultancy identified 93 links across 79 pages on websites belonging to 26 public bodies.

LEPs brought together councils and businesses to promote economic growth in England under a coalition government initiative launched in 2010. Central government funding ended in April 2024, with their core functions transferred to local and combined authorities.

Among other things, Digital Royalty builds and looks after websites, which means chasing down old links. In August, it found that a link on Surrey County Council's website led to a former LEP address now displaying casino content. It went looking for more.

Digital Royalty founder Alex De Sousa said: "Slow is usually a sign of caution and diligence, but 31 months after the LEPs wound up, it's crossed into inefficient and dangerous."

The consultancy offered The Register four examples of websites run by the Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport (DCMS), Hull City Council, Parliament, and the Local Government Association. One example is a government page about the Digital Skills Partnership, originally published under an earlier incarnation of DCMS.

The page includes a link that appears to be for the Humber Digital Skills Partnership within the Humber LEP website, but in fact takes users to a site advertising "Independent Non GamStop Casinos UK – New Casino Sites Outside GamStop." GamStop is the self-exclusion scheme designed to help people restrict their online gambling, which makes the links more concerning than ordinary website clutter.

The same link appears on the Hull City Council website under a list of Joint Committees, Partnerships and Trusts where it appears as Local Economic Partnership (Humber LEP).

An old page for the Parliament website detailing the Department for Business, Energy & Industrial Strategy committee's Industrial Strategy report includes a footnote (135) that appears to be for the Business Central funding finder service, but takes the user to the "Highest RTP Non GamStop Casinos for UK Players."

The Local Government Association's (LGA) sustainability and waste route map included a link to the former York and North Yorkshire LEP website. The destination initially appeared legitimate but contained links promoting crypto casinos. The LGA now appears to have removed the link.

All the organizations mentioned have been contacted by The Register to offer them the opportunity to comment.

Digital Royalty checked former web addresses associated with about 70 LEPs and growth hubs, then used a backlink index to identify roughly 3,000 incoming links from public sector sites. Most were simply outdated rather than leading to gambling content.

The findings are not exhaustive: the index may miss pages, and the research did not cover other types of defunct public body whose domains may have been repurposed. ®
