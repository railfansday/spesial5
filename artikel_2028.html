It was well-publicised ahead of the match that Yasir Al-Rumayyan would be at St James’ Park for the Newcastle United match against Arsenal.

A chance for the Newcastle United Chairman to see for himself at close quarters how things are now looking.

Yasir Al-Rumayyan seeing a game of two halves.

Newcastle United struggling to get out of their own half in the opening 45 minutes as Arsenal dominated and led 1-0 at the break.

Matthias Jaissle making three substitutions at the break and a totally different Newcastle United on show, by far the better team that ‘won’ the second half 2-1.

By the final whistle, Newcastle United had certainaly deserved at least a point, possibly all three.

The brilliant second half comeback was achieved due to the following 13 Newcastle United players and these 13 can be separated as follows:

Already at St James’ Park pre-Eddie Howe:

Tino Livramento, Malick Thiaw, Dan Burn, Lewis Hall, Sven Botman, Jacob Ramsey, Harvey Barnes, Yoane Wissa, Anthony Elanga, William Osula

Ahead of summer 2026, Newcastle United Chairman Yasir Al-Rumayyan took control of transfers from Eddie Howe and gave it to Ross Wilson.

Eddie Howe (and then Matthias Jaissle) still having some say but Ross Wilson the one dictating the overall transfer policy and holding the power.

This isn’t unusual, many clubs having a similar strategy of handing the Sporting Director control of transfer policy.

Ross Wilson the key man this summer in deciding to sell Anthony Gordon, Sandro Tonali and Bruno Guimaraes, as well as not renewing the contracts of Kieran Trippier and a number of other older players.

Altogether, approximately £260m/£270m was committed on new signings by Ross Wilson to rebuild the Newcastle United team and squad. Yasir Al-Rumayyan must have been wondering where they all were in that second half against Arsenal…

Amar Dedic – Subbed off after playing the first 45 minutes against Arsenal Sean Steur – Subbed off after playing the first 45 minutes against Arsenal Nico Gonzalez – An unused sub Matias Fernandez-Pardo – An unused sub Bazoumana Toure – Left out of the matchday squad Aladji Bamba – Left out of the matchday squad Ewen Jaouen – Injured Ousmane Diabate – On loan for the season at Turkish top tier club Gençlerbirliği

It is still early days and we need a lot longer to judge properly when it comes to allowing Ross Wilson control of transfers in and out.

However, there are inherent weaknesses with a system whereby the Sporting Director and not the Manager/Head Coach has control of selecting which players are signed and which are sold/let go.

As things stand, Matthias Jaissle clearly overwhelmingly feels he has to rely at this moment in time on players who came in before Ross Wilson’s summer of 2026. Players who have been at the club for some time, less star players such as Gordon, Tonali, Trippier and Bruno who the Sporting Director wanted/agreed to move on this summer.

By the end of this season we should have a better idea about his signings.

I certainly don’t think it is a total disaster, at least not longer-term. I think already I have seen enough of the likes of Hornicek, Steur and Dedic to believe that they are decent additions. I think Fernandez-Pardi will work out and I believe Nico Gonzalez will be okay. With the others they clearly need more time before we can judge them at all.

However, you don’t always get to tomorrow, if you can’t get through today…

For me it is very clear that Matthias Jaissle has walked into a situation where he has been left well short by Ross Wilson of a fully stocked and prepared squad to take on this 2026-27 season.

A case of needing to battle through this campaign and then hopefully as a group the summer 2026 signings can grow and help Newcastle United evolve. In the meantime, it is the established old guard that Matthias Jaissle will clearly need to have the main focus on.
