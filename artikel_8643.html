The major phone carriers discount their plans for employees of partner companies, union members, students, the military, first responders, teachers, nurses, and customers 55 and older. Find your group on your carrier’s discount page, verify once, and compare the discounted price with a cheaper plan before you sign up.

None of these discounts shows up on your bill on its own. The carrier charges full price until you sign up and prove you qualify.

Some take a percentage off a regular plan. AT&T’s discount programs take up to 20% off, depending on the plan, for employees of partner companies, union members, students, the military, veterans, and teachers. T-Mobile Work Perks takes 15% off for employees of participating employers, and Verizon’s discounts take 10% off for the military, first responders, nurses, teachers, and students.

Others are separate plans for one group, with their own prices and line limits. T-Mobile’s military and first responder plans cover as many as 12 lines, while its 55+ plans stop at two. AT&T’s 55+ plan allows up to 10 lines, though it saves the most on two.

To claim one, sign up on your carrier’s discount page and prove you qualify. Verizon confirms you belong to one of those groups through ID.me, an online identity service. AT&T asks for proof such as a pay stub or a DD-214 discharge form, and T-Mobile’s 55+ plans ask for a driver’s license or another government ID.

Ask what it combines with before you switch plans. Verizon won’t combine its military, first responder, nurse, teacher, and student discounts with one another, and AT&T’s 55+ plan can’t be combined with its discounts for veterans, teachers, nurses, or physicians. The discounted prices the carriers advertise also assume you pay by AutoPay.

Then check what it saves. A retired couple in their 60s paying by AutoPay for two lines on T-Mobile’s T-Mix More owes $130 a month. The plan’s 55+ version costs $120 and keeps the same four Picks, which you spend on perks such as Apple TV. That saves the couple $120 a year.

Before you sign up, price the same number of lines on a prepaid plan on the same network. The difference between that price and your discounted one is what the big carrier’s perks cost you.
