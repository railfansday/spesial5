WNBA Champion Gabby Williams: ‘I’m My Best When I Don’t Have a Choice’

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

The Golden State Valkyries star and Finals MVP on the moment when everything changed in Game 7.

At the end of the first half of Game 7 of the WNBA Finals, the Golden State Valkyries trailed the Atlanta Dream by six points. The Valkyries’ star, Gabby Williams, one of the best two-way players in the league, had scored a mere two points. But when the teams returned to Ballhalla—as Golden State’s Chase Center arena in San Francisco is lovingly nicknamed—for the second half, it was Williams’s time to shine. She took over, going on a 15-point run, including back-to-back-to-back three-point shots that flipped her team’s deficit into a narrow lead. By the time the buzzer sounded at the end of the game, Williams had 30 points and the Valkyries were WNBA champions.

Before she won the title—and was named Finals MVP—Williams, who was raised in Nevada but spent her summers in the Bay Area, played basketball in college for the UConn Huskies, winning back-to-back national championships. She was drafted by the Chicago Sky in 2018, before joining the Seattle Storm in 2022 and the Valkyries in 2026. As a dual French-American citizen, she also plays for the French national team, including at the 2020 and 2024 Olympics, where she led the team to bronze and silver medals, respectively.

Just over a week after she became a champion, Williams came to New York City to make the media rounds before taking a much-needed and well-deserved spa day. Below, she reflects on coming to the Valkyries, her performance during Game 7, and what little Gabby would think of her now.

It was the longest season I’ve ever played, but it went by so fast. I felt like it flew by; it was just such a really fun group of people—the staff, even the medical staff, the communication staff—everyone was just always great energy. I didn’t want it to end. Usually during playoffs, I am already playing overseas or on vacation, and watching the finals this year, I was like, “Dang, everyone else has gone and moved on and I’m still in season,” which was such a great feeling, obviously. But wow, it was long. And the longer it went on, the harder it was to sleep and eat with all the stress and stuff, but I still loved every moment of it.

I think it’s just, how do you define a star? I just define stars as people who win, as people who aren’t selfish, as good people, as good role models, as good teammates. So, I think we had 12 stars on the team, really. I know that’s what made us different and everyone is going to try to copy us now. It’s just about finding great people who want to win versus a superstar who wants accolades and stats. And then you win a championship.

We knew what we had from day one. I went to the Valkyries because I wanted to contend for a championship. That was my biggest thing in free agency—I didn’t want to go to a team that maybe I felt like wasn’t going to try to win a championship or at least didn’t have ambitions to. At the beginning of the season, obviously we’re figuring things out, but we figured it out very quickly, and we could tell just the culture of the group was a good one, and that we had something special. So, we always knew we had what it took to win and that’s all that we needed.

I wanted to go somewhere where I wouldn’t dread going to the gym every day. I think anyone, whether you play basketball or any sport, or any profession that you have, you just want to want to go to work every day—that’s a huge thing. I think you’re able to perform better when you’re in that mindset, and I knew that I would need that in order to win. So, that was one of the few questions I asked [Valkyries guard] Veronica Burton in my free agency. I was just like, “How did you feel showing up every day?” Because everyone’s going to say, “Oh, the team is great...” But you can’t lie about how the locker room is and the work environment. And she was like, “Every day was a blast, we have fun, and I genuinely enjoy coming in every day,” and that was all I needed to hear.

It started with Ohemaa [Nyanin], our general manager, and the selectiveness she uses in choosing not just players, but people. I remember that being a big thing when I was being recruited by UConn as well. They were like, “Yes, we’re looking for great players, but we’re looking for a certain type of kid too, because we have a culture here.” I saw a similar thing with Ohemaa and what she wanted to build. It was also the first time that I played for a staff that didn’t point fingers at each other. And as players, you follow their lead, so then players don’t point fingers at one another either. Also, when we went on road trips for away games, they would go by so fast because we all would spend time together. Normally, you just want to sit in your hotel room all day alone and then go home, but we wanted to spend time with one another.

There wasn’t even a team in the Bay when I was playing basketball when I was young. So it’s one of those things where people ask you what your biggest dream is and you say it’s to win a championship, but you can’t even imagine that it’s going to be in your hometown, or in the team’s second year, or your first year there. I couldn’t even dream that big. So that was a really cool moment. I’ve been watching the Warriors win since I was a kid, and having the female version of that now, and knowing how big of a party it was in the Bay when the Warriors won, and being like, “Now I get to do that?” That’s so crazy.

Don’t get me wrong, I understand the story that everyone wants to create, so I’m not mad at it that people go that route. But I think that was the American storyline. I noticed the American media and fans were really stuck on [France losing to the U.S.] at the Olympics and FIBA. I’m like, I’ve always been hungry for a W championship; I didn’t have to get two silvers to want that. And then all of the European media was like, “She won Euro league. She won the silver medal. Can she cap it off with the WNBA championship?” So, it was weird seeing these two very different ways of storytelling. Either way, I didn’t need any more motivation to try to win a WNBA championship.

We were all sad at first. I remember the whole group was really sad when we lost just because we loved each other so much and we had a really good group. But then shortly after that night, we were like, “Oh, we just got the first silver medal in France’s history.”

I learned this phrase from Coach [Geno] Auriemma [at UConn]. He’s like, “You see the best athletes when they don’t have a choice—they perform at their best. That’s when stars are created and big moments are created,” and that’s always stuck with me. I always have that playing in my head during those certain moments like, “Okay, I don’t have a choice. I just have to do it.” I even said it to Nat [Nakase, the Valkyries head coach] the day before Game 7, and she didn’t like it. She was like, “Okay, we’re going to switch your matchup. It’s something you haven’t done before; we’re going to ask you to do this in the first half. Can you do it?” And I was like, “Yeah, I’m my best when I don’t have a choice.” And she was like, “I don’t like that.” I was like, “Trust me, trust me. I like these moments.” I think now she gets it.

Yes and no. This game, more than any game I’ve played in my entire career, I didn’t really fully understand in the moment what I was doing until I went back and watched it. And I just watched the game for the first time two days ago. I’d seen highlights before of course, but I hadn’t watched it from start to finish. And watching it, I was like, whoa, I didn’t even realize half of the stuff I did. Because it’s such a high-pressure moment, and we’re very big on every possession matters. I didn’t even realize that my threes were back-to-back-to-back until I got home that night and saw it on Instagram. There was a point where I did feel the basket slowly start to feel bigger, but I wouldn’t say I knew exactly in the moment what I was doing.

I just couldn’t wait to celebrate with my teammates. It’s the moment you see on TV since you were a kid, where the whole team comes together and huddles and jumps and up and down on the court. I’ve had those moments before overseas, but it just was different after a seven-game series. To finally have it, I just couldn’t wait.

I just couldn’t believe it. I mean, I didn’t need MVP to feel as happy as I’m feeling today. Even now I don’t have the words for it. I was like, “I’m the one that gets to hold this trophy?” I didn’t even need it. This is enough. I’m just feeling really lost for words and lucky.

Oh man, she would be so happy. I hope little Gabby would understand the environment that she’s in is so loving. Just my village that I’ve had all year—I think she’d be really happy to know that she has that. And then also just going into an environment every day where people are smiling and dancing and laughing, and then you get to cap it off with a championship in the community. Yeah, she’d be really proud; she’d think I was cool.

It’s bittersweet because you just know you’re living in a moment that you know will never, ever happen again as far as this exact group, this exact timing, etc. So, the only thing that we really wanted to do after we won, was just spend as much time together as we could. Because we were like, “Oh, Ceci [Cecilia “Ceci” Zandalasini] is going overseas tomorrow; Ami [Aminata Gueye] has to go to the national team. So, it was just slowly saying goodbye to people. Everyone was like, “Well, did you party? Did you go out and stuff?” And I was like, “Oh, we just wanted to hang out.”

I wish I would’ve fought for myself a lot earlier than I did. I think I settled a lot. I was talking to Auriemma at UConn yesterday. I woke him up from his nap by accident. But he said something that really resonated with me. He was like, “I see people who have the same rough start to their career, and they kind of just end up blaming it on the coach or someone else and not living up to their full potential.” And he was like, “I’m so happy that you didn’t end up being that story.” He was like, “I’m so proud of you for taking control of your own story and making it what it is.”

Yeah, I think I was content being whatever they wanted me to be. They would call me the Swiss Army Knife, like, “Whatever you need.” I think I was a bit too complacent in that role in the W. I think it’s just my mindset: What does the team need? I always put the team first. But that means I didn’t fight for myself to have more responsibility or speak up more and understand that my voice does have the power it does. And that’s also why I wanted to go to Golden State because that was something like, Ohemaa talked me into free agency. She’s like, “I want to push you in being a leader. I want to push you on and off the court into being more vocal.” So, I’m glad I learned it now.

When he said that, I got really scared at first. But then at the same time, I was like, “Alright, you’re asking me to step up to the plate too.” It gave me motivation because I was like, this man really believes in me, wholeheartedly, for him to go to the media and say that and put his reputation on the line. So, it gave me confidence, it gave me joy of just like, “I can’t believe I’m in an organization that thinks so highly of me.” Because I’ve never asked for him to say things like that. So, the fact that he did means a lot. It felt great to be appreciated.

Just how much fun it was for everybody—that’s what sports are made for. We get lost in the competing for trophies and we forget the moments we create. Ballhalla [where the Valkyries play] was such an experience for everyone who lived it this year. I read there was like 20,000 people outside of the arena during Game 7 in addition to the 18,000 inside screaming their heads off. I hope that’s what people remember. Because that’s also what the Bay Area is about, and that’s what it’s like to win in the Bay. I’m saying that as a fan. So, I hope the party just doesn’t stop.

More of the same. I’m happy to have signed on for three years, knowing the ambition and the motivation from everyone there. And Nat is already talking about what needs to be done for next year.

It’s been cool being a part of a trailblazing franchise—that’s been the best part. We just want to continue to make history. That’s also what’s fun about an expansion team—everything is a first. I think we have a lot more firsts ahead of us.

Competing against each other is easy because it’s just work; it’s a job. And I know her style really well, so that helps. [Laughs.] During the finals, it was so nice because she gets it. She knows, “Okay, today is the day where Gabby’s going to need to think about something else.” And then, “Today’s a day where Gabby may want to hear my opinion on some things, or she might want to talk it out.” Or “Okay, she doesn’t want to talk it out.” And not taking anything personally, like, “Okay, if Gabby just wants to be quiet and watch a movie today, I know why.” So, it’s very helpful having someone who’s in the same environment as you. Also, the night before the biggest game of your life, and you’re playing everything over in your head a million times, having a million thoughts, just being able to go to a dinner and laugh, and think about something else for two hours of your day, it makes a huge difference. Then, you come back refreshed the next morning, your brain is clear, your mind is ready to take on the next challenge because you’ve been able to compartmentalize. Because when you’re by yourself and the thoughts are just going and going and going, and it can be miserable.

Playing each other in the final would have been cool too, though. The matchup would have benefited us too. [Laughs.]

It’s been a tough balance because I understand the importance of having queer representation, and what that means to people. But obviously we’re very, very private, so it’s pretty daunting having, I don’t know, all of the parasocialness and people thinking they know you or your relationship and hearing opinions about it. So, we try to protect our relationship as much as we can, but at the same time, I understand how important it can be for girls and women looking up to us. So there’s give and take. We try to stay as private as we can, but at the same time, we’re not going to hide who we are.

It was funny because there was a time in the season where my teammates all found out that I had a tattoo machine and they were like, “Oh, if we win, let’s do this.” And then we won and everything just happened so fast that we didn’t have time [for me to tattoo everyone]. We talked about doing it one night, and I was like, “Guys, I can’t tonight, I’m exhausted.” But if it happens eventually, I’ll definitely say yes to it.

She actually came over to help me get ready for this interview and we were just talking about our favorite looks. We loved the tie one. My stylist was a seamstress and designer before this; I’m the first person she’s ever styled. But she’s really an amazing seamstress, and she made an entire top out of ties. And we were like, that was the best one. And then she made me another one out of zippers that I’m excited to wear. It’s literally a corset made out of zippers. She’s so creative.
