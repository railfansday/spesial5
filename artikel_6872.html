Why Did Barry Keoghan Get XERF?

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Well, the answer is very simple, according to his facialist.

In the past week, everyone seems to have discovered XERF. A radiofrequency treatment originally from Korea, it can tighten, tone, and lift skin, and it’s now becoming a verb. Kim Kardashian got XERFed. So did Priyanka Chopra, Martha Stewart, Cardi B, and also Barry Keoghan. Out of all the celebrities who have gotten it, it’s Keoghan who has made the most headlines.

His longtime facialist, Nilam Holmes, posted a video of Keoghan receiving XERF while he says in a voice-over that “It’s very relaxing and therapeutic. I feel better about myself; I look better. I think it’s important to look after yourself inside and outside.” It’s so unusual to see an actor, let alone a male actor, star in a beauty video that ELLE called up Holmes, a U.K.-based facialist whose clients include Rosie Huntington-Whiteley (whom she prepped for the Victoria’s Secret Fashion Show) and Joe Alwyn, to learn more about red carpet protocols, how Keoghan got XERFed, and what she thinks of all the press attention.

XERF only just launched in the U.K. nine weeks ago, and there was a waiting list. Everyone’s been waiting so long to have it. Celebrities are sharing their experiences, which doesn’t usually happen with a lot of treatments. People have also been traveling to Korea to have it done and then going back to the U.S. to talk about it.

I invest in a lot of the top-of-the-line devices and use really reputable machines. Currently, I have things like Ultraformer III, Ultherapy, and Morpheus8. Most of them have some downtime, or they’re really painful—you’re breaking the skin and using heat. People can look like they have grid marks or are red for a bit.

XERF has no downtime and is not painful, so you don’t need numbing cream. I wouldn’t say it’s pain-free, but it’s not painful. I could do one now and somebody could go out tonight and be fine. You’re getting a big result quickly that carries on working, and it’s an alternative to those other treatments. It is probably one of the pricier things that I offer in my clinic. It’s a bit misleading when they say nonsurgical facelift. It’s more of a skin-tightening treatment.

When I did it on myself, I did half of my face, and I could see a considerable difference. One of the first XERF treatments that I did on a celebrity was on Nicole Scherzinger, and midway through, I ran out of the special gas that goes in the cooler and half of her face looked so different. It does give quite a dramatic, instant result.

People who don’t need a lot of lift get the best results. You see contours better. It gives a bit of a Cinderella effect, so the instant result looks better than it will look three days later. About 50 percent of people have come back and said it was great; they want a second session, and then others are like, “Yeah, it’s good, but I still think Morpheus was better.” You notice the most difference after one treatment. It’s quite a pricey treatment. I’ve not had anybody say they don’t notice a difference.

I do a lot of celebrities. The sort of higher up you go, the less you can mention. But what happened with Barry? Bless him. I did a lady here in the U.K. who is a big celebrity and normally every time she does something, the press picks it up, but this time, they didn’t; they picked Barry’s over hers. The press was very random. He does come in regularly. We don’t do anything crazy. He had his first XERF and was having his second one.

On the day he was coming in, I was meant to be filming my own face. He came really early and I was running really late and had said, “Barry, could you come back later? I’ve got to film myself having it.” He said, “I’ll do it for you,” and that was it. It wasn’t a setup. It wasn’t an endorsement. He just did it for me. The press was a little bit unfair—they said he was having it because trolls claimed he was rushing to have a treatment to change his look. Barry likes his looks. His commentary wasn’t, I look terrible. I don’t feel confident to go out. I don’t think that was the case at all.

XERF doesn’t have the ability to change anybody’s face, and a lot of the other treatments don’t either. Surgery or excessive fillers are the only things that change people’s faces. But he’s filming a movie and doing long hours. He does get a tired look on his skin. He likes to just do some things that give a little bit of a pick-me-up, and XERF does that. For poor Barry, people were saying, “I used to like you, and now I think you’re weak because you’re having something [done].” Some of the comments are like that. Well, it’s only a skin treatment.

Some old pictures from earlier this year were about his skin texture. We’ve done a little bit of micro-needling, and he has Hydrafacials, which have helped his texture a lot. It also helps when you’re lying down under good lighting as well. I’ve been looking after Barry for a while. We did him for Peaky Blinders. His skin looked great in that, but at the minute, he’s on a movie where they use prosthetic makeup and that doesn’t help the skin at all.

It’s not unusual for the guys to come in with bits of glue left from prosthetics and then that peels the skin a little bit as well. I’ve seen Barry since, and he’s fine. His agents were more unhappy about him doing that. He wasn’t saying that people should go and have those treatments.

But why shouldn’t men do skin treatments? At the moment, I have a higher percentage of male clients in my own clinic. And it’s not just actors, footballers, or pop stars. I have barristers, doctors, and high-flying professionals. [For actors], makeup ruins the skin, and they start looking more and more tired on camera. Most people do have some sort of facial when they’re on a movie and definitely before a red carpet. Now it’s the norm for everybody to have a facial prior to grooming.

I’m in London, but it’s got a very discreet and private entrance. Men realize when they come in that it’s actually a really nice experience. Men like the machines—the radiofrequency, the Hydrafacial—and they’re not into the fluffy lymphatic drainage massage. They want the tech. Once they see the difference, they’re very loyal. They don’t want to go and try somewhere else.

I don’t think it’s about that everyone’s trying to look younger. People are just trying to stay as they are. The longer you stay looking like that, the more parts you’re going to get. I’ve got actors in their eighties that still come.

Men are shamed into thinking it’s vanity or almost like, you’re defeated if you had something done. I don’t think that’s a nice message because we all want to look nice. We want to be comfortable with what we see in the mirror. I try to encourage that and take the shame out of it because it shouldn’t exist. What’s wrong with having a facial, a little collagen-boosting treatment, or micro-needling, which is one of the oldest, most organic ones you can have?

My attitude is that the firmer and healthier the skin, the more glowy it looks. Your skin doesn’t have to be wrinkle-free, but when you’ve got a bit of bounce and radiance, that makes you look younger. It doesn’t have to be stretched tight. It’s not preventing aging, but just slowing it down.

For two days, I would do a radiofrequency skin-tightening treatment—maybe not more than a Collagenwave or Exion treatment, because it will give you firmness and contour the face. Add a Hydrafacial to clean up the skin. Then, I would infuse some exosomes or a skin booster via a tiny micro-needling stamp. It basically gets into the surface of the skin. I put them under a red light. I just do some Dermalux light afterwards. If somebody wants to do something extra, EmFace is always good to add in because that helps to tone the muscles as well.

You could do it. Kim Kardashian did say, “This is my red carpet treatment.” Firstly, I think it’s too long a treatment to do for that. It’s also very expensive, too. But if I was getting someone ready for the Oscars, our prep would start a month before. Then, I would do something like XERF or micro-needling. I micro-needle with hyper-diluted Sculptra or exosomes. That takes about a month to get a full result. And I would also do dermaplaning, which takes all the peach fuzz away. When you put your makeup on, it reflects, rather than the hair absorbing the light.

I love a good eyebrow—we do Korean lamination. It softens the hairs of the brow or the lashes. It makes them more polished-looking, so that they fit really nicely and you can brush them up or across, making them look fuller so you can get a better shape out of them. I could do a skin peel in 10 minutes for somebody if they don’t have time, and then they can go straight into glam or grooming.
