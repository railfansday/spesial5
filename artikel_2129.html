Dave Challinor says one thing in first Sheffield Wednesday interview that fans are loving

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Sheffield Wednesday's new manager Dave Challinor has given his first interview since taking over at Hillsborough, and one thing that he said in particular has struck a chord with fans.

It's a clear sign of the ambition of the new owners of Sheffield Wednesday that Henrik Pedersen barely lasted into the autumn as their manager. Pedersen was sacked on the 25th October, following two successive 2-0 defeats against Notts County and Doncaster Rovers which left them in 8th place in League One, and it took just a week for the former Stockport County manager Dave Challinor to be appointed as his replacement.

Wednesday have drawn both of their matches since Pedersen's departure, with their last match against Blackpool finishing 1-1 on the 1st November, leaving them in 9th place in League One. Challinor's formal appointment into the position came two days later, with part of the duties of the new manager being to sit down for an interview with the club's media outlet. And there was one thing in particular that he said which delighted the club's fans and demonstrated that he could form a real bond with them.

In an interview with Sheffield Wednesday's media department and posted to the social media platform X, Dave Challinor laid out his ambitions for the club and his intentions for his new team.

He praised the new owners of the club for their "forthright vision" of what they want the club to be and spoke about the importance of the "standards" that are set for their players and the extent to which all the actions that take place behind the scenes at the club can influence what happens when the team takes to the pitch.

But there was one particular comment that he made which has prompted considerable excitement from Wednesday fans. Asked what his message was for them, he replied by saying, "We're all Wednesday, aren't we," before adding: "We are aligned on what we want the football club to look like. My job, and all of our jobs, really, across the executive leadership, the squad, and all the people off the pitch, is to not only represent the owners, but the supporters and the city of Sheffield."

If Dave Challinor wanted to build an immediate bond with Sheffield Wednesday fans, using the phrase "We're all Wednesday aren't we" couldn't have been a more potent one to use. Often abbreviated to "WAWAW", it's become a rallying cry for the club's long-suffering supporters although, as this thread from the Wednesday forum Owlstalk demonstrates, its origins aren't completely clear, though they could stretch back as far as the 1970s.

Challinor arrives at a club that is in a hurry to get back to the Championship following their relegation at the end of the 2025-26 season. Henrik Pedersen was given an opportunity to prove himself again this season, but was unable to find the consistency required to keep the club near the top of the table, and the new manager's first task will be to get the team back to winning ways.

There are reasons to be optimistic that he'll be able to do so. Although results were patchy enough to result in Pedersen's dismissal, there have already been clearly visible green shoots of recovery this season, not least their 7-2 win against Bromley in August and a 5-0 win against Wimbledon in the middle of October. But the Wimbledon win is Wednesday's only one in their last eight League matches, and their slide down to 9th place in the table is one that needs to be arrested.

The good news for Challinor is that there's plenty of time to do so, with more than two-thirds of the season left to play, while the tightness of the division this season - no-one in League One has won more than eight of their opening fourteen fixtures of the season so far - means that his team still only three points off 4th place and six points off second. Ironically, that second automatic promotion place is currently held by Stockport County, who Challinor left to go to Hillsborough in the first place.

Under new ownership who are clearly emotionally invested in the club, and with attendances already averaging over 31,000, the building blocks are in place for Sheffield Wednesday to make a quick return to the Championship. Dave Challinor's job now is to build the team to match that investment and that support. There are never any guarantees of success in football, as Wednesday fans know all too well from their own recent history, but they've put themselves in a strong position to achieve that off the pitch. Now it's time to prove it on the pitch, as well.
