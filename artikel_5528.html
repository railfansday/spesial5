Google is building its first Wyoming data center in a $17 billion deal

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The Laramie County project will draw power from a 702-megawatt natural gas fuel cell plant with carbon capture

David Paul Morris/Bloomberg via Getty Images

Alphabet $GOOGL's Google announced plans for its first data center in Wyoming on Thursday, a $17 billion investment in Laramie County. The company is working on the project with Tallgrass Energy, Black Hills Energy, American Electric Power $AEP (AEP) and Related Digital.

Google said it will cover the full cost of the electricity consumed at the facility, along with any infrastructure demands that its expansion generates. The data center will rely on air cooling rather than water, easing pressure on local supplies.

The project is contracted with AEP for a 702-megawatt fuel cell development that runs on natural gas, with fuel cells made by Bloom Energy. Google said carbon capture and storage will trap a large majority of the plant's emissions at Tallgrass's Eastern Wyoming Sequestration Hub. Google has not disclosed what percentage that represents, according to The Next Web.

Google is also drawing electricity from natural gas facilities that Tallgrass and Black Hills Energy operate. It is conducting an engineering and design study on fitting Tallgrass's combined cycle turbines with carbon capture technology.

Black Hills said in an Oct. 6 release that it had signed definitive agreements, effective Sept. 30, to serve the planned data center. The company plans to invest $1.8 billion between 2027 and 2029 to build 564 MW of natural gas generation. It will provide up to 590 MW of grid-connected service and manage 2.1 GW of third-party contracted resources through a private microgrid.

Black Hills said energy service is planned to begin in late 2027 and ramp to peak load in 2030. The company expects the project to provide an estimated $150 million of net income in 2030.

Google anticipates that the project will deliver local property tax revenue worth several hundred million dollars to Laramie County and School District 1. It met with more than 200 county residents at a community meeting in downtown Cheyenne, where attendees voiced unease over the effect of data center expansion on the city. Google, its partners and Bloom Energy are forming a Community Council to develop solutions for Cheyenne.

AEP said in October it plans to invest approximately $4 billion in the fuel cell plant under a 20-year power purchase agreement with Google. Full operations are expected by the end of 2028.

The announcement came two days after voters across the U.S. approved restrictions on data centers in 40 of the 41 local ballot measures put to them, according to The Next Web.
