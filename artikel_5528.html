Alphabet might sell its first Hong Kong and Singapore dollar bonds as soon as this month

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Alphabet is weighing raising the equivalent of $1 billion to $1.5 billion in Hong Kong dollars and about $1.5 billion in Singapore dollars

Alphabet $GOOGL, the parent of Google, has held talks about selling its first bonds denominated in Hong Kong and Singapore dollars, according to Bloomberg.

Alphabet has approached banks and investors to discuss a possible debt offering, which could come as soon as this month, according to Bloomberg. Alphabet is aiming for the Hong Kong dollar portion to be worth between $1 billion and $1.5 billion, while the Singapore dollar portion would be roughly $1.5 billion, with proceeds earmarked for general corporate purposes.

Discussions remain active, and the specifics of the offering may yet shift, according to Bloomberg. Alphabet declined to comment on market speculation.

A deal would gauge how much demand Asian investors have for such debt in the wake of a worldwide market rout driven by AI worries, according to Bloomberg. Investors are uneasy that AI-related projects could face tougher oversight now that Democrats have picked up ground in the midterm elections in the U.S. Credit spreads on the debt of certain tech issuers have widened, compounding a repricing across worldwide bond markets, where yields have climbed as AI-boom borrowing piled up.

Alphabet, Microsoft $MSFT, Amazon $AMZN and Meta $META Platforms have been issuing debt at an unprecedented rate while also earmarking sums in the tens of billions for AI capabilities, according to Bloomberg. Hyperscalers have been branching out into debt markets that were previously untouched by them in their hunt for additional funding.

Alphabet's first Australian dollar bond sale came in August, according to Bloomberg. Before that offering, the company set out to raise about A$5 billion ($3.6 billion) across four tranches, with the longest running 20 years. Alphabet has also issued notes this year in Swiss francs, British pounds, Canadian dollars and Japanese yen, according to Bloomberg.

Last week, Alphabet brought in €6 billion ($6.7 billion) by selling euro bonds in several tranches, marking its second offering denominated in euros so far this year, according to Bloomberg. The company had to accept noticeably steeper borrowing costs than it did on its debut euro deal in May, as worries mounted about how quickly AI spending is rising.

Meta Platforms also moved to market U.S. dollar bonds last week under a plan to borrow at least $20 billion across the U.S. and Europe.
