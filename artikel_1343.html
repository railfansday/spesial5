Carrick’s United are in crisis and now spotlight is on Wilcox

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Michael Carrick had urged his Manchester United players to come out and fight. But nobody could have imagined quite how Lisandro Martínez would interpret that message.

By the end of an extraordinary evening in Madrid, where United went 1-0 down inside a minute and never recovered against Atlético Madrid, Martínez had blown the lid off any pretence of all being well at the club as he launched a lacerating assault on the club’s executives.

United had come into this getting battered from all sides and when Pablo Barrios put Atlético ahead, Jamie Carragher’s pre-match assessment about how things could get “very messy, very quickly” for all involved at Old Trafford must have been ringing in the ears of the travelling supporters.

They certainly know how to make life difficult for themselves. Leny Yoro’s 74th-minute red card pretty much ended hopes of them taking something from the game.

Ademola Lookman scored Atlético’s second soon after and Carrick was staring at another defeat. And so they now move on to a big game at Leeds on Sunday. This was not the result Carrick would have wanted – and that is before what happened off the pitch.

Another defeat intensified the pressure on Carrick but Martínez left nobody under any illusions that he felt the finger should be pointed not simply at another manager or the players, but the hierarchy above him.

It was extraordinary to see a player speaking out so publicly in this fashion against his own bosses.

But Martínez pulled no punches, even going as far as singling out Jason Wilcox, the director of football, as he criticised the club’s summer transfer business and effectively accused the club of failing Carrick and the players in the window.

To what extent Martínez’s comments mirror the views of the wider dressing room is unclear, but you have to wonder where Carrick and United go from here.

On the one hand, Martínez was providing a show of support for his manager by attacking the people above him. At the same time, he has presented Carrick with a disciplinary problem to address.

Lisandro Martínez did NOT hold back after United's loss to Atletico 😬👀 https://t.co/OGo25Pzd30

And United have enough troubles as it is without a senior member of Carrick’s dressing room turning so squarely against the club’s power brokers.

Martínez was probably only giving voice to what many fans have been saying since an underwhelming transfer window closed with more questions than answers. But for a player to talk in such blunt, excoriating terms resonated more loudly than anything a supporter or United’s most caustic critic could say.

The message was simple: you can sack all the managers you want but until you pull together a squad worthy of United’s name and give the man at the helm the tools to compete, the club will struggle to escape this cycle of mediocrity.

No one can reasonably argue United’s window was a success. They went into it with the hopes of landing Elliot Anderson and other premium midfielders but ended up going for numbers over proven quality or pedigree. Youri Tielemans, Andrey Santos and Carlos Baleba were not a trio of signings anyone expected at the start of summer, not even the club itself.

There were glaring issues to address in defence, not least at left-back, and at centre-forward but a quality alternative to Luke Shaw never materialised and no back-up was signed for Benjamin Sesko, who was missing for four months with a shin injury that has since resurfaced and which could keep him out for a while yet.

Having done so well to lead United to third and take them back into the Champions League, Carrick would have expected further backing than he got in the summer. Ever the company man, he has been too polite to criticise that lack of investment. But the cat is out of the bag now Martínez has had his say.

It now places the spotlight on Wilcox. United head to Elland Road on Sunday and that was the scene of Rúben Amorim’s final meltdown as manager. Two days after he blew up in a meeting with Wilcox at United’s Carrington training base, Amorim took aim at the director of football and others at the club, demanding they “do their job” and repeatedly declaring that he had come to Old Trafford to “be the manager, not to be the coach”.

It is remarkable to think that, 10 months on, United head back to Leeds with just as many questions as there were back then.

United’s worst start to a league season for 40 years has ensured Carrick is already under pressure, but Martínez is very much of the view that it is no longer enough for the manager and the players to keep carrying the can and that the credibility and suitability of those who recruit the players and make the decisions must now be called into question.

That Martínez picked this game to speak out was interesting. United, lost, yes, and the manner of Atlético’s opening goal from Barrios after 53 seconds was shocking. But United at least went down fighting on this occasion and had fashioned enough good chances to at least draw level before Yoro’s harsh 74th-minute red card swung a delicately poised match decisively in Atlético’s favour.

Lookman made it 2-0 soon after, but Atlético, having looked like they would run all over United in those opening 10 minutes, were made to work hard for their victory.

United cannot keep shooting themselves in the foot like they did in the first minute. Carrick had gone with a three-man midfield to shore things up and made his team more comfortable and yet they were opened up in double quick time by two passes through the middle.

Still, you only needed to look across at United’s opponents to appreciate how chaotic it had become at Old Trafford.

Diego Simeone’s reign at Atlético covers a period stretching two years longer than the post-Ferguson era and in many ways his teams have represented everything United have not been in that time: well constructed and thought through, unified, stable, organised, hungry, aggressive, uncompromising and with a flash of devilment.

If United were not in crisis before this, they are now.

Good evening and welcome to live coverage of the Champions League tie between Atlético Madrid and Manchester United from the Metropolitano. The two sides had contrasting fortunes in their opening fixtures in this competition, Man Utd beating Azerbaijan’s Sabah 4-0 at Old Trafford a month ago while Atléti were defeated by Liverpool who came back from 1-0 down to win 2-1.

But domestically it is the other way round – Michael Carrick’s side have made the club’s worst start in the league for 40 years with one win, three draws and two defeats and have been knocked out of the League Cup. Those of us who watch them regularly – purely because of occupational obligations in this instance – have been surprised by how poor they have been. The results have been bad but the performances even worse.

They seem to lack players who can play on the front foot, who have the athletic dynamism required in this league and too many self-indulgent forward players who either can’t be bothered to help out their team-mates or cannot concentrate long enough to do so. Spooked by their lack of pace in defence and midfield, they are hidebound by caution, dropping ever deeper, ever more reliant on counter-attacking despite the blatant disconnect between the front four and back six. If they drift through this match and Sunday’s trip to Elland Road, Carrick may join the long list of Ineos-driven managerial mis-steps.

Atlético have won six of eight in La Liga and have been defeated only once. They are five points behind Barcelona in second and have score 18 goals and let in eight, which may seem a curious thing to include in a preview but it is done to emphasise that many fans’ perception of Diego Simeone’s team are out of date. Back in the days of Diego Godín, Gabi, Juanfran et all, six wins a draw and a defeat would have had goals for of about 10 and goals against of maybe three.

Simeone is a martinet about attitude not systems or styles and his team have evolved, with the World Cup-winner Alex Baena, who has scored four goals so far, driving them on. He has reintegrated Julián Alvarez, too, after his brazen attempt to force a move to Barcelona so incensed Atléti fans that it seemed they would drive him out of the club. Simeone refused to let Alvarez get his own way and he is now buckling down. Ten years ago Simeone would have bitten his head off.

The one surviving veteran in the squad from that first of El Cholo’s Liga winning sides and the two Champions League finals, victory in which would represent Simeone’s crowning achievement, is the midfielder Koke. Remarkably, if he features today, the 34-year-old midfielder will be making his 750th first-team appearance as an Atlético player.
