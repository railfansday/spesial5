Johnson & Johnson vs. Vertex Pharmaceuticals: Which Healthcare Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Johnson & Johnson's diversified MedTech and pharma mix faces off against Vertex's cystic fibrosis dominance, but their valuation gap tells a story worth weighing.

Healthcare investors often choose between established stability and high-growth innovation. Determining whether Johnson & Johnson (JNJ -1.36%) or Vertex Pharmaceuticals (VRTX -0.67%) is the superior choice requires examining their financial health and long-term strategies.

Johnson & Johnson is a diversified giant pivoting toward its most profitable medical technology and innovative medicine segments. Vertex Pharmaceuticals is a specialized biotechnology leader that is successfully diversifying its pipeline beyond its core treatments. Both companies provide unique advantages for investors looking toward 2027.

As a giant among healthcare stocks, the company focuses on its Innovative Medicine and MedTech segments to drive global growth. It distributes medications and devices to retailers, wholesalers, and hospitals, with a strategy centered on oncology, immunology, and neuroscience. Notable collaborations include an exclusive license for Darzalex from Genmab A/S (GMAB +0.76%) and a partnership with AbbVie (ABBV -0.59%) to co-commercialize the cancer drug Imbruvica.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 27, 2026, revenue reached about $99.3 billion, representing growth of roughly 7.8% compared to the prior period. During this same time frame, it reported net income of approximately $16.1 billion. This performance highlights the company's ability to maintain substantial revenue while navigating the complexities of the global medical marketplace.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the company maintains a debt-to-equity ratio of approximately 0.6x, which measures the balance between its total debt and shareholder equity. Its current ratio stands at nearly 1.0x, a metric that indicates whether a firm can cover its short-term obligations with its current assets. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 28, 2025, free cash flow was around $19.7 billion, providing ample capital for research and dividends.

Vertex Pharmaceuticals dominates the market for cystic fibrosis treatments and is aggressively expanding into gene-editing and acute pain therapies. It primarily sells its products to specialty pharmacies and distributors that provide access to patients and healthcare providers worldwide. The company is also collaborating with CRISPR Therapeutics (CRSP -0.55%) to develop innovative therapies for serious blood disorders.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached about $13.0 billion, which indicates a growth rate of roughly 10.5% over the previous twelve-month period. The company also reported net income of approximately $4.0 billion during this time. This steady growth reflects the strong demand for its specialized therapies and its successful efforts to broaden its clinical pipeline.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, Vertex carries a debt-to-equity ratio of approximately 0.1x, reflecting a conservative use of borrowed funds. Its current ratio is roughly 2.9x, suggesting a strong ability to meet short-term financial commitments. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was around $3.2 billion, which supports its ongoing investment in research and development.

Johnson & Johnson faces significant legal risks, particularly regarding product liability litigation for its talc-containing products. The company also deals with challenges to its intellectual property rights, as key products like Stelara face potential competition from biosimilars. Additionally, drug pricing reforms in the U.S. and the planned separation of its Orthopedics business introduce operational and strategic uncertainty.

Vertex Pharmaceuticals carries risks related to its high revenue concentration, as nearly all of its sales come from its cystic fibrosis franchise. The competitive landscape for biotechnology remains intense, and the company must navigate pricing pressures from government and commercial payors. Furthermore, the development of cell and gene therapies involves high regulatory uncertainty and significant financial investment in specialized manufacturing.

Johnson & Johnson currently trades at a more modest valuation than Vertex Pharmaceuticals based on its lower multiples for both future earnings and sales.

Valuation metrics include those sourced from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from those of other data providers.

The Forward P/E ratio compares a company's current stock price to its earnings estimates for the next twelve months. The P/S ratio measures the market value of the company relative to its sales over the past twelve months.

Johnson & Johnson is an overlooked behemoth compared with faster-growing biotech companies. The business has 28 products and platforms that generate more than $1 billion in annual revenue. Its pharmaceuticals arm, Innovative Medicine, has received two FDA approvals this year and has two more approved drug product launches in the E.U. and the U.S. The Innovative Medicine division accounts for 65% of JNJ's annual revenue and appears to have a robust pipeline beyond the pending products. MedTech, its other arm, sells a wide range of healthcare products, including wound care, surgical instruments and implants, hip and knee replacements, and spinal implants. The recent buy of Atraverse Medical bolsters that business. Revenue for Johnson & Johnson is expected to rise to $101 billion in the current fiscal year. However, Wall Street anticipates a $10 billion drop in net income, to around $16.6 billion.

Vertex Pharmaceuticals continues to benefit from its focus on cystic fibrosis (CF). It recently launched Alyftrek, a once-daily, next-in-class, triple-combination CF regimen. The treatment is seen encouragingly by investors. Alyftrek marks a notable improvement to the twice-daily dosage of Vertex's other CF products and was approved in Australia, New Zealand, Switzerland, the European Union, the U.K., and Canada over the course of 2025, after the U.S. approved it in 2024. Plenty more jurisdictions are evaluating the treatment today. In the U.S., label extensions (the expansion of FDA approval for use) mean Vertex can treat 95% of people with cystic fibrosis in the country today.

Vertex has expanded into treating other conditions. Povetacicept, an investigational recombinant fusion protein therapeutic, continues to show best-in-class potential as a treatment for three chronic autoimmune diseases: IgA nephropathy (IgAN), primary membranous nephropathy (pMN), and generalized myasthenia gravis (gMG). The FDA has granted its Breakthrough Therapy Designation for IgAN, with potential approval by Nov. 30 this year, as well as Fast Track Designation for pMN. If approved across the IgAN, pMN, and gMG indications, peak annual sales could reach $4 billion to $7 billion, according to equity analyst firm Argus.

For fiscal 2026, Vertex sales should rise about 10% to $13.3 billion, with net income coming in roughly even with 2025.

Both of these pharma giants can make a compelling argument for investors' dollars. Johnson & Johnson offers better value, based on its P/E and P/S ratios, and stands as the better buy for 2027.
