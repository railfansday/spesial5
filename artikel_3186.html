There’s a reason why KitchenAid stand mixers are iconic. Utter the name around a group of bakers, and you’ll elicit a chorus of reverent gasps. Without a shadow of a doubt, the KitchenAid Artisan Tilt-Head stand mixer is the best stand mixer in the world.

But what about the new-for-2026 KitchenAid Artisan Plus Design Series Luminaire stand mixer? Sure it’ll win the award for longest, most superfluous-adjective-d name, but is it also worthy of the best stand mixer crown?

At $699/£899, the Luminaire is a pricey, “special edition” stand mixer. Every year, KitchenAid releases a design-forward version of its iconic Artisan Plus, and this year, that’s in the form of the crystalline-glass-bowl, breige (that’s blue, gray, beige) spackled Luminaire. Oh, and it’s got a little light so you can see what you’re mixing (see what you’re missing? No? Just me?). Could this new special edition KitchenAid be the perfect one for you? Find out in this KitchenAid Artisan Plus Design Series Luminaire Stand Mixer review.

Double-edge flex beater, dough hook, whisk, flat beater

The KitchenAid Artisan Plus Design Series Luminaire Stand Mixer is $699 from Williams Sonoma and £899 from John Lewis in the U.K..

To save my fingers, I will henceforth refer to this product as “KitchenAid Luminaire”. If you’re wondering why the name is so long, I’ll help break it down for you.

“Artisan Plus” is how KitchenAid refers to its “Artisan Plus” line of stand mixers — this includes the iconic KitchenAid Artisan Plus ($599). This is not the same as the “Artisan” line, which lacks precision speed control, ½ step speed, and an illuminated bowl. “Artisan” models are your standard tilt-head mixers, usually around $499.

“Design Series” is kind of like a special edition. Each year, KitchenAid christens a “Design Series” model — 2022 saw this gorgeous sage-and-copper mixer ($699); 2025 was the “outdoor” theme with a forest-green finish and wooden bowl ($699). 2026 is all about the blue peppercorn/velvet finish (KitchenAid’s words, not mine), and the illuminated bowl.

Other Artisan Plus models rock the same illuminated bowl, but just look at how gorgeous the light is, streaming through the textured glass. It’s almost ecclesiastical.

Naturally, the Design Series machines are pricier than the standard Artisan Plus models ($599). You’re paying extra for the design aspect. These are special editions, like buying a book with sprayed edges or gold foil, or a supercar in British Racing Green. I’m not going to tell you if the elevated design is worth your extra $100 — only you can decide that.

Even so, this doesn’t explain or excuse why the Luminaire is so outrageously priced in the U.K. £899 is $1,200. Why is the mixer double the price in the U.K.? It makes no sense. I’d be interested to hear KitchenAid’s explanation.

The clue’s in the name, but the KitchenAid Luminaire is a design marvel. It’s breathtakingly beautiful. The light refracting through the glass bowl and dappling my countertops is one of the most gorgeous sights I’ve ever seen in my kitchen (the winner being the homemade kimchi I labored over for hours and promptly devoured in ten days).

Available in peppercorn blue, the Luminaire isn’t glossy like other KitchenAid stand mixers. It’s got a spackled, textured finish. I thought it would be harder to clean, but I was wrong — more on that in the “Storage & maintenance” section below, though. While it’s a little frustrating you can’t get this specific model in another shade, this is par for the course for Design Series models. That’s the whole point of them. They come in one “Design”.

There are four attachments: the double-edge flex beater, normal beater, whisk, and dough hook. Each attachment does what it says on the tin — now I’ve used the silicone-lined flex beater, I don’t think I can go back to the standard. Not having to pause mixing to scrape down the sides is a game-changer.

Like other KitchenAid stand mixers, the attachments click in with a gentle twist and remove just as easily. I washed mine by hand.

Unlike other KitchenAid stand mixers, the Luminaire comes with a glass mixing bowl only. This is obviously to show off the gorgeous light refractions, and I’m not mad about it at all. Actually, I’m in awe of it. It’s beautiful.

I have nothing bad to say about the design quality here: it’s a “Design Series” model, and boy, it doesn’t let you forget that.

I tested each attachment multiple times, so I’ll go through them one-by-one here. In short, the KitchenAid Luminaire is a premium stand mixer, and it sure as heck performs like one. The speeds range from 20 to 260 RPM, and unlike the standard “Artisan” line, it features stepless adjustment for seamless speed changes.

The double-edge flex beater is the best thing to happen to my home baking setup since the Sourhouse DoughBed. As the name — kind of — suggests, it’s a normal beater attachment with a silicone “flexible” edge. During mixing, these silicone edges scrape down the sides of the bowl without needing to stop and manually scrape.

As you can see, there’s an almost windscreen-wiper-like pattern on the bowl. This is where the beater has already scraped down cake batter. Obviously, as the beater moves, it scrapes the remaining batter, and so on and so forth.

Now that I've used this attachment, I’m not sure I can go back to the regular beater on my KitchenAid Artisan 5QT ($499). The double-edge flex beater is intended for sticky batters, like chocolate cake, banana bread, etcetera.

The beater is the standard rounded-triangle-shaped attachment without silicone edges. This can be used for cake batters, muffin mixtures, whipped dips, or even shredding meat. I made some whipped feta dip — using KitchenAid’s recipe — and shredded chicken.

I was a little frustrated when I had to stop mixing and scrape down the edges multiple times, but this is expected with the standard beater attachment. I like that this attachment can be a little gentler than the double-edge version, particularly helpful when I shredded a whole chicken. The resulting texture was a touch too mushy for my preferences, but it turned an entire chicken into small shreds in about thirty seconds.

As you can see, the egg texture is even and glossy, with an airy, thick texture. I baked this mixture in my air fryer (as my oven is garbage), which I think was the problem — but, again, this is my fault, not the Luminaire’s.

The mixer can be adjusted from slow to 260RPM seamlessly, ideal for technical bakes like meringue, where you need to change the speed while adding sugar.

I made KitchenAid’s “Everyday bread” recipe to test the dough hook. You shouldn’t mix yeasted doughs faster than speed 2, as it can overwork the yeast and cause a flat loaf.

As you can see, the dough hook took everything I threw at it. It blended the dough perfectly and kneaded it gently, saving my forearms. I have no issues with this at all — the dough hook is the ideal baker's assistant.

In the U.K., the KitchenAid Luminaire boasts a 5-year warranty and 15-year repairability; in the U.S., this decreases to just one year. Yep, I’m grimacing, too. $699 for a one-year warranty is pretty bad. KitchenAid, what’s going on across the pond?

Disappointing warranty aside, the KitchenAid Luminaire is easy to clean and maintain. It’s just as big and heavy as other KitchenAid stand mixers, which means it’s made of real metals and holds a high-quality motor, but if you’re only as strong as a sick Victorian child (like me), you’ll need help getting it out and putting it away.

I found cleaning the glass mixing bowl just as easy as cleaning any other bowl — ditto with the mixing attachments. Top marks all around here (well, U.S. warranty aside… yeesh!) Maybe that U.K. warranty is what you’re paying for? (£899 is $1,200…)

Would I buy the KitchenAid Luminaire? No. I live in the U.K., where it’s an abhorrent £899. I’m sorry, but I can barely afford to heat my house. I’m not going to drop almost a grand on a stand mixer.

However, should you buy the KitchenAid Luminaire? If you’ve got enough money, yes. Without a doubt. It performs admirably at every task you throw at it, whether that’s shredding chicken or making ten-tiered cakes. And that light? Monumentally helpful. Oh, and don’t get me started on the mesmerizing lace-like patterns stretching across my kitchen countertops… we’d be here all day.

For baking enthusiasts, this is a no-brainer. The KitchenAid Luminaire will not let you down. I’ll be watching from afar with my face pushed up against the glass, a wistful tear trickling down my cheek.
