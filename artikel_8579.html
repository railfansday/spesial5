Switch Banks Without a Missed Payment – Keep Both Accounts Open a Month

Switching banks goes wrong when you close the old account while a bill is still set to come out of it. Open the new checking account first, move your direct deposit and every automatic payment, and keep both accounts open for at least one full statement cycle before you close the old one.

A forgotten bill that hits the old account after you empty it can overdraw the account or bounce. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau warns that bounced payments can bring fees and make it harder to open another checking account later.

The way to catch every bill is a list built from your statements, not your memory. Your last three statements show each monthly deposit, bill, subscription, and transfer. Then search the past 12 months of statements for yearly charges, which may not show up in the last three.

Say you switch in March and empty the old account, but your car insurer still takes its yearly premium from that account each May. Two months after you thought you were done, the premium hits an empty account. Three months of statements wouldn’t have shown it, but a 12-month search would have.

With the list in hand, switch in this order.

When a whole cycle passes with no new charge or deposit in the old account, move the rest of the money, close it, and ask the bank for written confirmation that it’s closed.
