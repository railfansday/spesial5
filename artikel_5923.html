CodePink co-founder's $55M foundation reinstated in California amid grant questions

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

CodePink activists ignore questions from Fox News Digital's Asra Nomani on their intentions ahead of Capitol Hill foreign influence hearing.

CodePink co-founder Medea Benjamin’s $54.6 million family foundation has been reinstated as a California charity after years of missed financial filings, although questions linger over $2.7 million in grants it reported paying in 2024, the year its registration was suspended.

The foundation, Arc of Justice, has poured $24.6 million into protest groups, legal advocacy organizations and progressive nonprofits over more than two decades, a previous Fox News Digital investigation found. The 2024 payouts included $350,000 to CodePink, according to the foundation’s 2024 federal tax return.

Years of missed filings prompted regulators in California to revoke the foundation's charitable registration in January 2025 after more than a year of suspension for repeatedly failing to submit required annual reports or respond to regulatory notices.

State regulators restored it in September, placing the foundation on three years probation.

Under California regulations, organizations with suspended or revoked charitable registrations are prohibited from distributing or spending charitable assets without the attorney general’s prior written approval, according to Title 11, Section 345(b) of to the California Code of Regulations.

Fox News Digital asked the California Attorney General’s Office whether the foundation needed or received approval to distribute those grants.

"While the organization was suspended from activity in California, our office received no information regarding the organization’s out-of-state activities," the office said.

The office added that "a California registration revocation does not apply to grants outside California by a foreign nonprofit doing business in other states."

Arc of Justice is a California-incorporated nonprofit, and its charitable registration was suspended during 2024 before being revoked in January 2025.

The attorney general’s office did not directly address whether the foundation’s grants required or received written approval.

The foundation’s attorney, David Levitt, also did not answer Fox News Digital’s questions about whether the foundation obtained approval for the payments.

Regulators approved its reinstatement petition Sept. 9 after it submitted five years of overdue reports covering 2020 through 2024 and paid $1,800 in fees and penalties, according to the petition and state records.

Levitt previously said that foundation leaders mistakenly believed a planned relocation to Florida had ended its California reporting obligations.

"The Foundation's failure to comply with the Registry's registration and reporting requirements resulted from an administrative misunderstanding concerning the Foundation's state of registration," Levitt wrote in its reinstatement petition.

Fox News Digital previously reported that a new Florida nonprofit using the Arc of Justice name had been incorporated at the Miami home of Benjamin’s longtime partner, Tighe Barry, after California revoked the foundation’s charitable registration.

Levitt previously said the Florida corporation was created as a "placeholder" while the foundation considered relocating. He said the move was never completed, the California corporation was never formally dissolved and no assets were transferred.

Arc of Justice, a private grant-making foundation, was formerly known as The Benjamin Fund, Inc.

Benjamin's father, Alvin Benjamin, a prominent Long Island real estate developer who founded The Benjamin Companies in the early 1960s, provided some of the foundation's earliest documented financial backing.

The foundation’s assets grew from $39.63 million at the end of 2020 to $54.64 million at the end of 2024, fueled in part by investment gains even as it distributed millions of dollars annually in grants, federal tax returns reviewed by Fox News Digital show.

A review of its complete IRS filing history found it distributed nearly 1,500 grants totaling about $24.6 million to organizations involved in anti-war activism, progressive policy, legal advocacy and campaigns involving immigration enforcement, China, Cuba and the Israel-Hamas war.

CodePink was Arc of Justice's largest activist beneficiary. The foundation began funding CodePink in 2009 and provided grants to the organization in 15 tax years through 2024, totaling nearly $3.2 million, according to IRS filings.

Benjamin co-founded CodePink with Jodie Evans, the wife of Shanghai-based Marxist tech tycoon Neville Roy Singham. Benjamin remains one of the group's most visible members, frequently leading protests and interrupting congressional hearings.

Founded in 2002 with the aim of preventing the Iraq War, CodePink has grown into one of the country’s most visible anti-war protest groups, and its activists are easily recognizable in hot pink T-shirts and often wear keffiyehs.

In September, Benjamin was escorted from a House Foreign Affairs subcommittee hearing on alleged foreign malign influence after protesting during the proceedings.

On Nov. 10, Benjamin clashed with Sen. John Fetterman, D-Pa., near an elevator bank at the Capitol over Israel and the Iran war.

CodePink co-founder Medea Benjamin speaks with Rep. Dan Goldman, D-N.Y., as Tighe Barry, Jodie Evans and other activists gather inside the Rayburn House Office Building on Nov. 11, 2026. (Asra Q. Nomani/Fox News Digital)

The following day, Veterans Day, Benjamin and fellow CodePink activists confronted Rep. Dan Goldman, D-N.Y., inside the Rayburn House Office Building, pressing him over U.S. military cooperation with Israel. Evans also attended the group’s Taxpayer Uprising Capitol Hill lobbying event.

In October, CodePink organized a China tour promoting the country’s revolutionary history, infrastructure and socialist development. During the trip, Benjamin posted a video praising public safety, pointing to unattended bicycles and umbrellas as examples of belongings people could leave without fear of theft, Fox News Digital previously reported.

The group has also participated in nationwide No Kings protests, opposed ICE enforcement actions, organized anti-Israel rallies and campaigned against U.S. policy toward China, Cuba and Iran. Earlier this year, CodePink led a delegation to Cuba that included Twitch political streamer Hasan Piker.

Since 2009, the foundation and its predecessor, The Benjamin Fund, also directed about $2.05 million to Global Exchange, the anti-war and social justice advocacy group Benjamin co-founded with her former husband, Kevin Danaher. The group has long campaigned against U.S. military intervention and economic sanctions.

Arc of Justice also provided about $2.24 million to Grassroots International, which funds grassroots social movements around the world; $1.61 million to Social Good Fund, which helps charitable projects raise money and manage their operations; about $1.29 million to the Center for Constitutional Rights, a legal advocacy organization; and about $114,000 to Alliance for Global Justice, an activist nonprofit.

Other recipients since 2009 included World Beyond War, which received about $492,000; the Institute for Policy Studies, roughly $255,000; and Jewish Voice for Peace, about $124,000. The foundation also gave $110,000 to Grassroots Global Justice and about $90,000 to the Center for Popular Democracy.

IRS filings also show the foundation provided about $65,500 to Democracy Now!, where Benjamin has frequently appeared as a guest, since 2009.
