Biden-appointed judge probes whether ICE skirted judge's release orders in Colorado cases

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin told Fox News Digital that Biden-appointed judges are ‘almost impossible’ to work with as immigration enforcement battles intensify.

A Biden-appointed federal judge ordered ICE's Denver field office director to appear in court Monday amid concerns that immigration authorities violated judicial release orders by immediately rearresting detainees, including at least one instance outside a detention center.

Nathan Rasper has been summoned as part of an inquiry involving 14 people who were arrested after being released from immigration detention.

The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) maintains that ICE complied with lawful court orders and that a judicial release order does not permanently shield individuals from immigration enforcement.

"When an illegal alien is ordered released by a judge, that does not immunize the alien from immigration enforcement for all time," DHS said in a statement to Fox News Digital. "ICE may seek to rearrest the alien in a way that complies with the judicial decision. To suggest that ICE cannot do this is nothing short of sabotage."

The Nov. 5 order issued by U.S. District Judge Charlotte Sweeney followed an earlier ruling requiring the government to identify every case in Colorado since Oct. 27 in which ICE detained someone within 12 hours of a court-ordered release.

Federal officials subsequently identified 14 petitioners who had been arrested again after being released, but the filing did not provide the precise timing of when the rearrests occurred.

She directed officials to provide the exact dates and times of those releases and rearrests and identify any additional individuals immediately detained following court-ordered releases.

A man holds his immigration paperwork while handcuffed after being detained by Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents outside an immigration courtroom June 17, 2025, at the Jacob K. Javits federal building in New York. (AP)

The additional reporting requirements followed a Nov. 4 hearing at which Sweeney questioned whether ICE was circumventing judicial orders by briefly releasing detainees before immediately taking them back into custody. At that hearing, she ordered Rasper to appear at a follow-up hearing Monday to address the agency's practices.

The inquiry stems in part from the case of Ramon Quintero Alvarez, who was released from immigration detention Oct. 27 before ICE immediately took him back into custody, according to a government court filing.

In a Nov. 3 court filing, federal government attorneys said they would not submit a legal brief defending the lawfulness of Alvarez's continued detention.

The immigrant detention center operated by the privately-owned GEO Group for U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement in Aurora, Colo. (Jim West/UCG/Universal Images Group via Getty Images)

The filing prompted Sweeney to convene the Nov. 4 hearing at which Assistant U.S. attorneys Andrew Soler and Kevin Traskos appeared alongside ICE Assistant Field Office Director Nichole Black, who testified under oath about the circumstances surrounding Alvarez's rearrest.

At a Nov. 4 hearing, Sweeney ordered Alvarez released and barred immigration authorities from detaining him again unless the government demonstrated, by clear and convincing evidence at a bond hearing, that he posed a flight risk or danger to the community.

The government must submit the additional information requested by Sweeney by 8 a.m. Monday, ahead of Rasper's scheduled court appearance later that morning. The proceedings could shed light on ICE's compliance with judicial release orders, but Sweeney has not indicated whether she intends to pursue contempt proceedings.

An agent escorts a person in custody. (Lokman Vural Elibol/Anadolu via Getty Images)

The dispute comes as ICE faces similar legal challenges across Colorado, where other Biden-appointed federal judges, including U.S. District Judges Nina Wang and S. Kato Crews, have presided over cases involving disputes over immigration detention and court-ordered releases.

Fox News Digital reached out to the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the District of Colorado for comment.
