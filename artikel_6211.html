United Airlines Pilots Defy ATC Orders To Fly Through “The Worst Part” Of A Severe Thunderstorm

United Airlines pilots scheduled to operate a nonstop flight from San Antonio International Airport (SAT) to Washington Dulles International Airport (IAD) refused to accept the air traffic control's proposed routing to the US capital, after they identified that the route would take them directly through a severe thunderstorm.

A recording shared by Aviation MP4 shows the moment the pilot questioned ATC's clearance, which, instead of routing the service around the worst weather, put the jet on a trajectory through the heaviest rain and thunderstorms. The pilot in command ultimately has the final say on whether the flight can operate, as they hold operational responsibility and safety for the flight. For this event, the pilots' concern was also backed up by United dispatch.

UA471 operates from San Antonio to Washington Dulles. It is believed that this incident occurred on Friday, but has not been confirmed by Simple Flying. As the plane pushed back from the gate and taxied to the runway, the recording captured the moment the crew questioned the proposed route. Initially, air traffic control had rerouted the Boeing 737-700 to depart south and travel around the severe weather.

During the flight's taxi, the moments shared had the flight crew challenging the decision, with the pilot noting that he was going to speak with United dispatch, and that the crew was refusing to fly through what would have been up to four hours of challenging weather conditions for the crew, and unfavorable comfort for the passengers onboard.

Simple Flying reached out to United for comment on Saturday, and the airline confirmed the flight landed safely.

Pilots onboard the flight quickly noted they could not fly the proposed route and instead requested the initial routing, which would have taken the Washington-bound flight south of the severe weather. The air traffic control (ATC) reevaluated the plan, while the United pilots spoke to airline dispatch, which backed them up, noting that they should not be flying through the adverse weather.

In the end, the crew was permitted to fly an alternative route, highlighting pilots' authority to reject an unsafe clearance request and choose an alternative. While air traffic control provides instructions and proposed flight paths, pilots in command have final responsibility for the aircraft's safety and the safety of everyone onboard, including passengers and crew.

UA471 has a scheduled departure time of 7:00 AM and an expected arrival time of 11:13 AM. The usual flight duration is two hours and 45 minutes. For the proposed route, flying through the thunderstorm would have added almost an hour of extra flying time, according to the pilot's commentary in the video shared above.

The lasting impacts of the unthinkable on ATC.

The Pilot-in-Command (PIC) holds final authority over an aircraft's operation and safety. They can challenge or disagree with air traffic control or airline dispatch if they believe the situation could be unsafe. Pilots have specific rights and protocols they can use.

This can include rejecting clearance and requesting an alternative. They can also declare an emergency and deviate from any air traffic control clearance without prior permission if safety is compromised.

From SAT, United and United Express maintain regular scheduled flights to Chicago O'Hare International Airport (ORD), Denver (DEN), Houston George Bush Intercontinental Airport (IAH), Newark (EWR), San Francisco International Airport (SFO), and IAD.
