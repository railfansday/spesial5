To winterize your home before the first hard freeze, start by disconnecting the hoses, draining the outdoor faucets, insulating exposed pipes, and getting the chimney swept. Those jobs head off the burst pipe or chimney fire that can end in a repair bill and an insurance claim. Clean the gutters and seal drafts next.

A pipe nobody drained or insulated can split in the first hard freeze. An unswept chimney can catch fire from creosote, the residue wood smoke leaves on its inner walls.

Burst pipes and chimney fires fall under the two costliest kinds of property damage claims. From 2020 through 2024, water damage and freezing averaged $17,240 a claim, according to the Insurance Information Institute, and fire and lightning averaged $101,092. Your deductible comes out of your pocket before the policy pays anything.

A hose left on an outdoor faucet traps water in it, and that water can freeze and split the faucet’s pipe inside the house. The split won’t leak until the faucet is turned on again, so the damage can go unnoticed for a long time. Take the hose off even a frost-free faucet.
