Hire a House Cleaner for the Jobs You Dislike – Price Your Free Hours First

Before you hire a house cleaner, divide the price of a visit by the hours of your own cleaning it would replace. If that cost per hour is less than what a free hour is worth to you, hire help for just the jobs you dislike most, and start with one trial clean.

Take a couple who both work long hours and pay a cleaner $140 every other week for the bathrooms, kitchen, and floors, jobs that took them about three hours a visit. Each hour they get back costs about $47.

That $47, not the cleaner’s hourly rate, is the number to judge. Angi’s cost data puts a regular cleaning at $100 to $150 a visit for two bedrooms and $130 to $200 for three. Every-other-week service runs $200 to $500 a month, against $300 to $800 for weekly visits.

Then put a price on your own hour, whether you’d spend it working, with family, or at rest. Your take-home pay per hour is a fair gauge if you’d work those hours instead. Otherwise, ask what you’d pay to get a Saturday morning back. Hire the help when an hour is worth more to you than it costs to get one back.

List the jobs you dislike most, and get quotes for just those from each of three kinds of help.

If you hire an independent cleaner and control how they do the work, the IRS counts them as your household employee, and Social Security and Medicare taxes come due once their cash wages reach a yearly threshold.

Take the cheapest quote whose trade-off you can live with. Book one trial clean before agreeing to a schedule, then ask whether a recurring plan earns a discount. Skip any long contract, and get proof of insurance before the first visit.

For one week, track how long the jobs you dislike most take you, then divide each quote by those hours.
