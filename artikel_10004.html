Support tech fixed malware by charging client less for their services

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

‘I gave up at least $6,000 and I’d do it again’

Welcome to Friday and therefore to another edition of On Call, The Register's reader-contributed tech support column in which we share your tales of cleaning up messes made by clients and colleagues.

This week, meet a reader we'll Regomize as "Dennis" who told us he once ran a tech support business in a major US city.

"I got a call from a very well-known lawyer's office," he told On Call. "Over the two decades I spent supporting his office, this attorney and I became close friends."

The attorney was also a good client because Dennis found himself billing a three-figure sum every month for malware removal that resulted from the lawyer’s – ahem – personal browsing habits.

Dennis eventually tired of malware removal and told his client to expect a substantial bill for the iMac he was about to buy as a replacement for his PC.

"I told him this would pay for itself in 18 months," Dennis wrote, because he figured it wouldn't get malware all the time.

"He actually said, with genuine concern: 'If you do that, you will make less money'."

Dennis replied that he aimed to give his clients the best advice, and that on this occasion his counsel was to pay Apple instead of himself!

"It took him about five years to stumble across anything that could harm that iMac," Dennis wrote. "When he did, I had no idea what to do: I had never seen an infected Apple product."

So he did what all tech support people do: reinstalled the OS and crossed his fingers.

Dennis was rewarded when the machine came back to life, with all data and settings intact.

"I had given up at least $6,000 in support revenue by solving his problem and I'd do it again," he wrote, before winding up his story by letting us know "I retired five years ago. It’s still on his desk!"
