Stylist and Creative Director Akari Endo-Gaut’s Guide to Tokyo

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Perhaps no city juggles the past and the future as nimbly as Tokyo. Sleek skyscrapers meet centuries-old shrines; a rich culinary tradition blends with contemporary innovation (it’s no accident that this is the city with the most Michelin starred-restaurants in the world). The fashion, of course, exists in a similar dichotomy, expertly blending traditional techniques with the avant-garde. There’s never a wrong time to visit the Japanese capital—but in winter, when ski resorts a quick day trip away are in full swing and New Year’s celebrations are on the horizon, it feels particularly special. All you have to do is narrow down your itinerary.

In fact, there’s so much to discover that you could spend an entire trip dedicated to shopping alone—according to Akari Endo-Gaut, a Japan-born, New York-based stylist and creative director who studied in Tokyo, it may be worth bringing a second suitcase just for souvenirs. From the vintage shops to put on your radar to the best place to stop for a glass of natural wine, here’s a glimpse at Endo-Gaut’s Tokyo little black book.

For a one-of-a-kind clothing find: Since I was student in Tokyo, one of my favorite areas to hang around was Minami-Aoyama. We used to have amazing Corso Como store there; now, the Comme des Garçons store, the Issey Miyake Pleats Please shop, and the Auralee store there are still where you can go to find a special piece.

For vintage gems: There are so many good vintage shops in Tokyo, and it goes deep. BerBer Jin is always my first stop, and I also check luxury resale shops like Komehyo.

For unique souvenirs: You can find the best curated products, from ceramic bowls to German knives, at Arts and Science. And beautifully wrapped sweets at Higashiya are the best gifts.

For a worthwhile investment piece: Wako Arts and Culture is a new space conceived by artist Hiroshi Sugimoto in the basement of a prestigious department store in Ginza. It’s all specially curated, a great spot to find a special piece from fashion to Japanese craft.

For the magpie: My very talented friend Charlotte Chesnais from Paris recently opened a shop in Tokyo; it’s a must-visit. Or, if you’re looking for best-condition vintage watches, you can go to ECW Shoto.

For your next great read: Japan has a big book culture—go to Komiyama bookstore for vintage books, or spend a full weekend browsing at Tsutaya Daikanyama or SKAC.

For stylish home goods: You can find traditional but beautiful kitchen goods at Kiya and, of course, at Muji. Or, spend a fun day exploring all the kitchen goods stores in the Kappabashi area, which is a shopping street dedicated to restaurant owners.

For the quickest way to impress an out-of-towner: Hakujitsu houses a beautiful collection of ceramics in an old dentist building. My secret favorite is Yanaka Matsunoya—it has the best baskets and tote bags.

For a fabulous dinner reservation: I love Japanese-Chinese food or yakiniku, so I go to Chugoku Hanten with local friends or to Jojoen with my sister. If I have foreign guests, they usually have fun dining experience at Narukiyo.

For a bakery treat: Sawamura is our best local bakery cafe in Hiroo. After a soba lunch at Kanda, stop by Omiya Western Sweets Shop for the cutest pastry; for traditional Japanese sweets at an upscale, architecturally beautiful spot, go to Toraya Akasaka.

For the art-lover: Taka Ishii and Tomio Koyama are the best fine arts galleries in Tokyo. I like the artists from Kosaku Kanechika too, and at Licht gallery, you can see the best design pieces.

For an apéro.. or a nightcap: For the architecture lover, a drink at Imperial Bar, designed by Frank Lloyd Wright, is a must. When I’m hanging out with local friends, I’d go for a natural wine at Le Cabaret or Tabac.

For your packing list: Honestly, you don’t need anything, since you go to Tokyo to buy the best of all items! Bring flat shoes for walking, and an extra suitcase for shopping. An eye mask and melatonin for the 13-hour time difference will come in handy, as will a handkerchief; it’s part of local etiquette to carry one. And I always like to bring a running outfit for the famous Imperial Palace run.
