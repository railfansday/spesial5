Te Araroa: my favourite section(s) yet

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Back on the move, I felt a sense of relief at the fact that I didn’t have to rush to be anywhere. I had been anxious at the idea of falling behind but it turns out on a thru hike there is no real falling behind.

On day 13 I had a nearo, spending the day looking around Waitangi and doing a resupply. I bought polenta in place of mashed potatoes and prayed for the best. That night I stayed in a hostel and met Sven and Michael who were also walking the TA.

Sven quized me on my gear, food, and other items I had with me. We both agreed that a 1/8inch torso pad was an addition we’d like to make to our gear, he tried to give me all his extra food (and believe me, that guy has a lot of extra food) and he showed me the very sweet set of notes his girlfriend had sent with him to read each night.

In the morning, rain had set in and I was reluctant to get moving, but knew I only had about 17km to cover and a dorm bed for the night. I caught up with Lucy along the way and we walked together off and on. The day started with an incredible walk along the coast and a real test to my ability to resist spending hours hunting for sea glass.

I was a bit anxious about my ankle this day, but it generally felt good so I kept walking. It was rainy through most of the morning and in an effort to stay off the road we had taken a side path through the forest. This turned out to be several kilometres of pointless ups and downs in the world’s most humid forest. I drank all my water within the first 12km of the day. Luckily I noticed that there was a road we could follow back to the main trail and we made our way to a junction with a petrol station.

The sun began to peak out and Lucy and I split a bag of chips for lunch and drank fizzy drinks. It’s incredible how good a lemony fizzy drink feels when you’ve been hiking in a sweaty forest. Fueled on sugar and salt, we returned to our packs and started slathering on sunscreen, only to be offered a lift for the final 5km stretch which otherwise would have seen us walking on a dodgy road and diving into the ditch whenever a car passed. Our saviour was a former Te Araroa walker and knew our plight well.

At the dorm we met Issy and Riv, two British girls who were walking the TA. We spent the evening all having showers, drying clothes and eating some of our heavy food. The dorm was so hot and full of mosquitoes and a weird cat who yowled like some kind of cryptid at the door for us to let it in at about 3am (and, ants which we only discovered in the morning), so it wasn’t the nicest night but we stayed dry and so did our tents so I count that as a win.

Another relatively uneventful but extremely humid day of walking. We had another short day to the Farm, a sort of intentional living community where they serve a big dinner for $20 and offer camping and a shuttle over the next 18km of winding hard sealed road.

At the farm we met Eve, Cody, and spent more time hanging out with Issy and Riv. There was a yoga studio where we all did a much-needed stretch session, and I was able to fully charge my phone.

In the morning, my ankle was feeling better and better. The Farm shuttle zoomed through 18 windy kilometres (I did almost throw up) and dropped most of us off at the Helena ridge track. I wasn’t sure what to expect and everyone was feeling a bit apprehensive about another hill in the rain after our respective Raetea experiences. But we knew there was a shelter halfway through where we could re-group and get water which already gave it a leg-up on Raetea.

I loved this track. It was humid and sweaty and full of ups and downs and unnecessary staircases but it also had incredibly lush rainforest bush, and was the kind of terrain I hike in the most so felt like home. I flew through the first 7.5km to the shelter where I met Harry. Turns out he was mates with all the other people I’d been hanging out with for the last day. I cooked myself some pasta for lunch because I was feeling ahead of schedule and the others all caught up. I ended up taking an hour and a half lunch this day because it felt so good to just chill out on a big hill.

By the bottom of the forest Sven had caught up with me again and he used his long legs to jump over the stream at the end of the track and then built a bridge across it (thank you Sven) because he “loves to build a bridge”. We made it out to the road and waited for a hitch. There were absolutely no cars going the direction we were going. But have no fear! Sven had a plan! He pulled out his friendly hat and his lucky wrist brace and I kid you not within minutes a car appeared. Lucy managed to catch us and the three of us jumped in with someone who was staying at the campground that we were aiming for that night.

The Aussies and Brits also managed to get hitches and we all made it to Whananaki holiday park in the early afternoon and ate burgers and chocolate from the shop, jumped in the pool despite the thunder rolling in (sorry mum) and had showers. We drank 24 beers between about 5 of us and it was just such a fun, chill day. The group found out I have a PhD and started calling me Doc, and we thought maybe Sven should have the trail name “thumbs” because of his deep love of hitching.

The thunder continued to roll and the rain pelted the shelter so we all upgraded from tent sites to tiny cabins ($9 each!) and tucked in for the night.

Tomorrow it was a 26km walk to Nikau bay to stay at James’ place and to have a BIG HOT shower.
