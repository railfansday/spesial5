Your first step will be to determine your tax dependents. An excellent way of doing this is to use the IRS tool at https://www.irs.gov/help/ita/whom-may-i ... -dependent and run it twice - once for D1, and once for D2.

You will probably discover that you cannot claim D1 as a dependent in 2026 and that you can claim D2 as a dependent in 2026. I'll assume that for the rest of this post but you should use the IRS tool or a qualified tax preparer to be sure.

Your tax family / household size for 2026 would then be 3 (you, your spouse, and D2), so the relevant ACA 400% FPL number for 2026 assuming you live in the contiguous 48 is $106,600. It is not the larger $128,600 number unless D1 is somehow your tax dependent (which it really doesn't sound like given what you've written).

You only include on your 8962 the AGIs for your tax dependents, so if your D1 is not your tax dependent, then you would not include her income on your Form 8962. See the instructions for Line 2b for Form 8962 on about PDF page 8 at the link below. You would include the AGI for D2, but it sounds like she has zero income.

I believe you can have your daughters on your ACA plan until age 26. D1 could sign up for her own ACA plan.

Because D1 is on your ACA plan but is not part of your tax family (again, this assumes she's not a dependent), then you have an ACA plan being shared by two tax families. In this scenario, you must allocate the policy amounts by completing Part IV of Form 8962. See the instructions for Form 8962, especially Allocation situation #4 which you starts at the bottom of PDF page 20 at https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i8962.pdf. What ends up happening is that you and D1 will get to share the 1095A amounts on a pro rata basis which all of you agree to (or 3/4 and 1/4 if you can't agree).
