How the Glazers REALLY feel about Man United: IAN HERBERT reveals the family split over selling club, their regrets over Sir Jim Ratcliffe 'mistake' and Richard Arnold exit... and their missed chance to 'cash out'

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Published: 12:00 EST, 24 November 2026 | Updated: 12:03 EST, 24 November 2026

An Amazon Prime documentary series about the Glazers' Tampa Bay Buccaneers American football team paints a revealing picture of the billionaire family coalescing around a sport they actually love. It feels a very long way from Manchester United, the club they have taken down to the depths.

All six children of Malcolm Glazer, the man who built the business empire and bought United in 2005, share stories of their childhood NFL obsession. Darcie Glazer Kassewitz, sister to five Glazer brothers, relates how, on the day before they all went to a Miami Dolphins game, her brothers would be in the basement with bedsheets and paint creating huge banners.

Sometimes playing ‘knee football’ down there. Or competing in what they called the ‘BYFL’ – the ‘back-yard football league’ they set up among themselves and neighbours. ‘They all loved sports,’ says their mother, Linda.

Of course, the British form of football doesn’t feature.

This homespun scene contrasts with the family’s current dismay about the chaos created at Manchester United, which has led to renewed discussion among several of the siblings about selling off some, or all, of their shareholding.

Most of the siblings consider selling a minority share to Sir Jim Ratcliffe to have been a mistake, according to a source with a social connection to the Glazers. Though there is not a sense of how the family feel about individual Ratcliffe appointees, they are thought to rue the departure of Richard Arnold – former United commercial director and chief executive, whose part working on dozens of commercial deals saw him oversee around £4billion of revenue.

Manchester United's dismal form this season hasn't gone unnoticed by their owners in the United States, some of whom have discussed cashing in on their shareholding

American football - and their team the Tampa Bay Buccaneers - is the Glazers' true love. Joel Glazer led the celebrations when they won the Super Bowl in 2021

The Glazers have been ‘surprised’ at the loss of other influential senior staff, according to the source.

Arnold saw the writing on the wall before Ratcliffe and Ineos arrived. He personally negotiated his own exit with the Glazers. But they were then unable to prevent other departures which have been detrimental to the club, according to sources with no Glazer connection who have worked with United.

Those lost include the highly respected Victoria Timpson, United's former 'CEO of Alliances and Partnerships', who struck the Snapdragon and Teamviewer shirt sponsorship deal, but left in December 2024.

Florence Lafaye, former United commercial director, briefly took over Timpson’s responsibilities but also left to join Timpson in establishing the commercial agency Altius8, which was soon hired by Tottenham Hotspur.

Joel, Avram and Bryan Glazer are the three siblings thought to relish owning United – a calling card they have used at, among other settings, the Davos World Economic Forum, where United were the only football club to take a pavilion in the past.

Joel, whose 10.7 per cent shareholding is the biggest of the six siblings, was often seen as a man of interference before Ratcliffe’s arrival, pushing for higher fees when executive vice chairman Ed Woodward or others were trying to negotiate a transfer sale.

But the interest in United of Kevin, Ed and Darcie is far less certain, even though all six sit on the United board and Darcie holds the second largest share at 9.71 per cent.

The six collectively wanted to test the water for a sale after seeing US financier Todd Boehly pay £4.25billion for Chelsea in 2022. They formed the impression that United could sell for twice that much.

The sale of a share to Ratcliffe was held up by the six failing to agree on whether to sell up entirely or just let go of the football side of the business.

The interest in Manchester United of Kevin, Ed and Darcie Glazer (pictured) is far from certain, even though all six sit on the United board

An extensive report about United by Bloomberg last week suggested some individual family members were considering the sale of more of their shareholding and hoping to convince others among the six to join them.

The unexpected summer purchase of a stake in Liverpool by a Jeff Bezos-led consortium had caught several members of the family ‘off guard’, leaving some of the six feeling they had missed an opportunity to cash out. That deal has a potential takeover clause which could value Liverpool at £5.3bn.

The original plan was that Ratcliffe would ultimately buy the rest of United for £4bn, but since buying into the club, his chemicals business has been hit by a prolonged industrial downturn, collapsing profitability and high energy costs.

The Ineos Industries division recorded a £1.84bn pre-tax loss in 2025, his chemicals empire is carrying more than £10bn of net debt and he has been exiting from most of his sports interests.

The Glazers have the power, under the terms of their deal with Ratcliffe, to sell the club outright and force him to sell his stake. Another option would be to divest themselves of a small slice of their 48.9 per cent stake. But the current uncertainty about the future landscape of the Premier League created by the Manchester City case, with a slew of possible legal cases in its wake, is impacting on outside investment in top-flight clubs.

The family may have to sit tight and hope the recovery of the club’s fortunes, allied to plans for a new £2bn stadium, will take the club’s value closer to the £6bn that the six would be likely to agree on as an exit figure. They could tap into the bond market as Barcelona did to finance their $1.5bn upgrade of Camp Nou, or look to private equity funds, who would want a major pay-back when the stadium is built.

But for the first time through their 21 years as owners, United face the frightening predicament of relegation – bringing, for example, a £5.7million-a-year TV deal in the Championship compared with the £191.5m in Premier League income banked last season.

With new Premier League Squad Cost Ratio rules allowing the club to spend 85 per cent of last year’s £677.6m revenue on players and £160m in the club’s rotating credit facility, there is money.

Multiple sources say the Glazers stepped in to ensure Mauricio Pochettino’s appointment – something United deny

The Glazer family are thought to rue the departure of Richard Arnold (pictured), the club's former commercial director and chief executive

Multiple sources say the Glazers stepped in to ensure Mauricio Pochettino’s appointment – something United deny. But the source close to the family also believes they will now continue to present a greater challenge to what many see as Ratcliffe’s running down of the United operation.

The Bloomberg report showed how Ratcliffe’s 300 job cuts had focused heavily on commercial, media and administrative divisions, which former employees said had eroded relations with corporate partners and caused a deterioration in the level of service.

A former executive described how long-standing sponsors, accustomed to receiving complimentary autographed merchandise, were told they should expect to pay shipping fees for signed memorabilia. ‘Penny wise, pound foolish,’ the executive said. This was certainly not the Glazer way under Woodward and Arnold, who built that side of the operation.

The Glazers are 47th in the Forbes list of most wealthy US families and the Amazon Tampa Bay Bucs series reveals an innate obsession with the financial bottom line and making profit. ‘My father didn’t give a damn about what anybody else thought,’ Ed Glazer tells the film-makers. ‘He would always say, “Why can’t we do this?”. We could look at anything as long as the numbers made sense.’

It’s hard to believe that Ratcliffe, a man desperately trying to preserve his own business, is going to get in the way of the Glazers seeking ways to preserve the long-neglected asset which is now staring into the abyss.
