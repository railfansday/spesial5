England's All Blacks acid test, Erasmus circus, Boks to storm Dublin?

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Third week of Nations Championship Tests in northern hemisphere sees England host the All Blacks in an acid test; Ireland welcome South Africa in bonkers week Rassie Erasmus was handed six-Test ban; inaugural tournament concludes at Allianz Stadium Twickenham next weekend November 27-29

The third weekend of Nations Championship Tests in the northern hemisphere is upon us, with England hosting the All Blacks in an acid test and Ireland facing the Springboks in the week a six-Test suspension was handed down to the controversial Rassie Erasmus...

There is a sense of déjà vu, somewhat, for England this November when looking over their autumn performances and the general outlook emanating from them.

This time last year, England proved too strong for Australia and Fiji before welcoming New Zealand to Twickenham. In that Test, Steve Borthwick's side thrilled to a 33-19 victory over the All Blacks.

A nervy 27-23 win over Argentina followed, but England exited November buoyed by four wins, with many talking them up as a returning force in the world game, building towards Six Nations, Nations Championship and Rugby World Cup title tilts.

What followed was a disastrous Six Nations. The worst in England's history, in fact.

From five fixtures, they lost four for the first time ever, suffering defeat against Italy in a Test for the first time, as well as a record home loss to Ireland by a 42-21 margin at Twickenham.

They lost to Scotland for the fifth time in six years as the wheels well and truly came off a championship campaign which began with comfortable victory over a poor Wales side at home.

Displays in July's Nations Championship window were hardly overly positive either. A comprehensive 45-21 defeat in South Africa, a thrashing of a poor Fiji side in a Test bizarrely played in Liverpool, and holding on with 13 players to edge to victory in Argentina.

Borthwick and his players were suddenly under major pressure again, with stories of a camp rift emerging, but this month has seen 123 points scored in just two Tests - big victories over Australia and Japan.

Like last year, the All Blacks are coming to town with everyone wondering if England are back on track, or still a side short of the top table.

As such, Saturday's Test against a no-doubt angry New Zealand - following their shock defeat to Wales in Cardiff - represents something of an acid test for England.

Put on a show in victory, and talk of them as potentially one of the world's best will start to re-emerge. Suffer defeat, and criticism will filter back.

My word, it has been some week in Dublin as far as story-lines ahead of Saturday's crunch Ireland vs South Africa Test.

First, fly-half Sam Prendergast was cited for a hip-drop tackle committed against Fiji captain Peceli Yato in last week's 43-15 Ireland win - a first of its kind, with the manoeuvre outlawed at the start of this month.

Then, South Africa head coach Rassie Erasmus gave the mother and father of all press conferences. Even for him - and he has quite the history - it was downright bizarre.

Off the back of the Springboks losing to France in Paris last week - clearly the reason behind Erasmus' ticking demeanour - claims and comments from South Africa's revered rugby leader ranged from extraordinary to astonishing.

He began by predicting Prendergast would get off "on a technicality" and would play in Dublin on Saturday, claiming his double Rugby World Cup-winning squad and players were treated differently at disciplinary hearings than northern hemisphere players.

Erasmus even went so far as to state "things never seem to go our way." Make of that what you will when considering he is talking about a trophy-laden side who won the last World Cup following three successive one-point knockout victories.

Far worse was to follow when Erasmus claimed "17 knee-drop tackles" by France had been missed by the referee and TMO in South Africa's defeat last week - a figure almost universally debunked when reviewed by journalists and pundits in the aftermath.

Erasmus then came out with the claims which were likely the reasoning behind World Rugby snappily handing him a six-Test suspension: questioning whether TMO's and referees were "match-fixing" and whether there was "manipulation of the game or result" of Test matches.

When a journalist at the press conference commented to Erasmus he was making a "serious intimation", his response was: "It's not an accusation."

World Rugby didn't see it that way, and though South Africa Rugby requested a formal hearing take place, Erasmus has since taken to X to confirm he will watch Saturday's Test from his Dublin hotel room and has organised a free watchalong for anyone to sign up and view him and his reaction to the Test match as it is unfolding.

The 54-year-old has now been suspended three times in five years for these sorts of incidents, which constitute "public criticism of match officials, undermining the integrity of individuals or the sport, and fuelling online abuse."

Indeed, SA Rugby have since released a statement "condemning the online abuse of officials" following confirmation of Erasmus' ban.

During the 2021 British and Irish Lions series in South Africa, after the Springboks lost the first Test, Erasmus broadcasted an hour-long critique of Australian referee Nic Berry, and was eventually handed a two-month ban from all rugby activity and 10-month matchday ban.

Berry said afterwards Erasmus' behaviour had been an "assassination of my reputation" that caused "immeasurable damage." He no longer referees Tests involving South Africa.

World Rugby found the video had contained "multiple abusive and insulting comments and attacks on the officials."

In 2022 he received a two-Test ban after sarcastic social media posts following back-to-back South Africa defeats to Ireland and France. A video critical of referee Wayne Barnes led to the official revealing he and his family had received death threats.

Outside of those punishments, Erasmus has long overseen a South Africa side and setup which has sought to find loopholes in the sport.

Against New Zealand in September and France last week, he changed his starting front-row for his replacements shortly before kick-off in clear displays of gamesmanship. In 2021, he acted as a water carrier, running onto the pitch to relay messages in-game before World Rugby changed regulations.

He has fielded 7-1 bench splits to get more heavy forwards onto the park late in Tests - something criticised as dangerous - advised his team to intentionally infringe the kick-off in order to force scrums, and is widely believed to have run a Twitter burner account called Jaco Johan for a lengthy period to criticise refereeing performances and decisions against the Boks.

Mad genius? Maybe. But increasingly a toxic figure.

What does this all mean for the rugby on Saturday?

Well, Ireland were perhaps up against it to compete with this Springboks side in any case, but after all that has played out this week, the Springboks players are likely to come out snorting fire.

South Africa beat Ireland 24-13 this time last year, in a Test which saw Ireland shown a 20-minute red card to James Ryan and four further yellow cards, reducing them to 12 players at one point.

Two of those sin-binnings came as a result of scrum penalties, and that is likely to be an area of pain for Ireland this weekend too. The Springboks pack will be seeking utter domination in that area again.

There has also been a law tweak in terms of officiating maul defence, making it even more difficult to stop tries via the driving maul. In short, if South Africa get into Ireland's 22 with a five-metre lineout, the chances are it will result in a try.

Prendergast - whose tackle was found not to reach a red-card threshold - is back in the Ireland team at fly-half, but his form last year and earlier this year fell well below the level required, particularly in defence, but also in terms of running an attack against elite sides.

The 23-year-old has an immense task against this ultra-aggressive Springboks outfit, both in terms of leading a successful attack, and in avoiding being exposed.

If Ireland, as huge underdogs and substantially bested in terms of the power stakes, manage to win this Test, it will be one of the greatest achievements of Andy Farrell's career.
