England stun world champions Spain in one of their greatest victories

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Thomas Tuchel may not have planned it this way, but in one night he gave us all the rich history of the England team at once: some glorious attacking football, some self-sabotage defending, a red card, a late siege and a fight.

This was the beguiling story of Spain 2 England 3, an evening that began with the world champions parading the World Cup trophy and ended with 10 Englishmen defending a lead with their last breath. England beat the world champions in Madrid, and for more than 30 minutes including injury time they did so with 10 men, Dan Burn heading the last ball out as per the tradition of a defensive rearguard action.

This was the game that had it all: including a substitute, in Djed Spence, who had already picked up a yellow card on the bench before he came on. It was a coming-of-age performance in goal for James Trafford. So too for Ben White, the 2022 World Cup departee who scored the third and lunged with astonishing effect in the 84th minute to block a Nico Williams shot.

This was a performance good enough to win a World Cup final, never mind Nations League Group A3 – just a shame England neglected to get to that World Cup final in the summer. The fight in question was at half-time as the players came off and White and Fabián Ruiz first tangled and others dived in.

There was so much to admire. England began the night testing Spain’s first-phase passing to breaking point. They began the second half counter-attacking Spain for two goals from Ezri Konsa and White. Then after Elliot Anderson’s red card, this was a different England. With Trafford in inspired form, they held off the relentless Spain for long enough.

Harry Kane scored his 93rd England goal from the spot and was replaced before the end so that Myles Lewis-Skelly’s energy could give the midfield one more lease of life. By the end, the excitement meant that Kane was unable to take his seat on the bench, and he stood alongside Tuchel in the technical area, cajoling and advising.

England have beaten Spain on Spanish territory in recent times, but it was the nature of this performance, its many qualities when the moment required them, that made it so significant. This was the end of a 43-game unbeaten streak for the European and world champions, and it was ended by an England team in their best form in recent years.

Only three who started this game – Kane, Jude Bellingham and Declan Rice – started the final of Euro 2024, lost to Spain. Tuchel’s pool of talent is deeper than it was for his predecessor but there is also a greater maturity to the way they play. The England manager called it the greatest performance of almost two years in charge “given the circumstances… who we play, where we play” and as ever for England just slightly too late.

The defeat by Argentina in the semi-final in Atlanta just stings that little bit more when one considers the craft and resilience England showed on this night. They were under the hammer after Anderson threw an arm over the shoulder of Lamine Yamal and was dismissed before Mikel Oyarzabal converted the penalty that followed. “Thirty minutes against Spain in Spain feels like three hours,” Tuchel said, although Spain had a long way back after the blitzing they took at times in the first hour.

They all had their moments. Anthony Gordon had a strong first half. Morgan Rogers was central to the two goals England scored in the 10 minutes after the break. Anderson demonstrated that he is one of the best midfielders in the world in the hour before his red card. Spain were without Rodri and in his absence his Manchester City successor was in control.

Tuchel encouraged his players to mount a ferocious press against the best first-phase passing team in the world. Brave, but it worked beautifully and for an absorbing opening 10 minutes, Spain looked all at sea. Such was the pressure, the Spain goalkeeper Unai Simón abandoned the usual build-up strategy and launched his goal-kicks to the halfway line.

Even when Spain finally found their rhythm at the mid-point of the first half, England adapted. It took them a while to manage the discomfort of being denied the ball, and the familiar rapid incursions and switches of play. The counter-attacking switch from the early period pressing Spain in their own area yielded the penalty from which Kane scored the first.

Thomas Tuchel praised England’s “statement win” that ended Spain’s 43-game unbeaten run and secured top spot ahead of the world champions in the Nations League.

Tuchel and Declan Rice revealed they had both believed England would end Spain’s long unbeaten streak, and their confidence proved well-founded.

“This was of course a statement victory,” Tuchel said. “I’m happy the players felt so confident. I felt it as well. I also had the same belief [that England would win]. I think we felt it from each other.

“The result followed the performance. I think it was on the highest level in terms of belief, team spirit, togetherness and it was on the highest level football-wise, in all aspects of the game.”

Tuchel described the nature of England’s win as “the Azteca reloaded” in reference to the World Cup victory over Mexico, but felt the win in Madrid was the best of his reign as head coach.

He said: “Yeah [it was the best performance of his reign so far], given the circumstances and the pressure and the quality and where we played and playing against the reigning world champions. They were unbeaten in 43 games. So putting a performance out there in Madrid, in this environment, that was a very complete performance and the best I’ve had so far.

“The last half an hour felt like Azteca reloaded, to be honest. Again, it was a beautiful week. The commitment to get better is on the highest level.

“I said it many times, I love these players and I love the mentality .We’re building something and that was the next step. It was an amazing atmosphere. I will bottle it up for sure and then unfortunately we have to wait until the quarter-final.”

“We had a fantastic result and performance in Croatia followed up by a very good performance at home against Czechia. We then got our homework done against Croatia three days ago and that’s what we always wanted.

“We wanted to come back and compete on the highest level and see how far we can push our limits and how far we can push the opponent with the way we want to play and stay true to our identity. The team did excellent.”

Since that defeat, England have won four successive Nations League fixtures against the Czech Republic and Croatia by an aggregate score of 14-1. Last time out, they beat Croatia on Thursday, earning a 2-1 win as Kane, who became England’s most-capped men’s player with 126 appearances, dispatched a second-half penalty to seal the three points. Spearheaded by the Ballon d’Or holder’s brilliance, England must win this evening in order to overtake Spain and top the group.
