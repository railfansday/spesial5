Elon Musk’s Austin home targeted by well-funded leftist groups aboard party buses

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Fox News host Greg Gutfeld and the ‘Gutfeld!’ panel discuss Hillary Clinton saying she wants to force Elon Musk to testify to Congress if President Donald Trump pardons him.

Three far-left nonprofits with combined annual revenues of $56.1 million escalated their protests against SpaceX and Tesla CEO Elon Musk this week by targeting his Austin-area home with a seemingly innocuous post-election "party" featuring two chartered buses, music, speeches, theatrical Cybertruck piñatas and dancing to Queen’s "We Will Rock You."

The event’s organizers – VOCAL-TX, the fiscally-sponsored project of a New York 501(c)(3) nonprofit; the Center for Popular Democracy Inc., a 501(c)(3) nonprofit based in Brooklyn; and People’s Action, a 501(c)(4) political nonprofit based in Chicago – framed the Saturday event as a tame "People Over Billionaires Election After Party."

The demonstration reflected growing coordination among leftist networks worldwide, from Paris to Portland, Ore., supplying organizers, transportation, coalition partners, event platforms, messaging and other logistical support for actions presented as "grassroots." As Democratic politicians threaten investigations into Trump’s corporate backers, including Musk, the street protests help create the appearance of bottom-up support.

The leaders of the organizations behind the "After Party," held in West Lake Hills, Tex., where Musk owns a sprawling home, didn’t provide comment in response to queries from Fox News Digital, but they have insisted in the past that their local protests are the result of "organic" organizing in communities.

A now-ubiquitous, mass-produced anti-Musk "I AM STEALING FROM YOU" poster, featuring the tech billionaire with creepy eyes, has appeared at demonstrations nationwide and was also on display at the Austin protest outside his home.

This weekend’s organizers helped lead the "Tesla Takedown" protests that sometimes turned ugly last year. According to a Fox News Digital investigation, 258 organizations with combined annual revenues of an estimated $2.6 billion were behind those protests.

At Saturday’s event, participants arrived at the property in two blue Fly-Rides party buses and additional vehicles. Organizers blasted music including Queen’s "We Will Rock You," over loudspeakers.

"We'll bring the music, you bring the dance moves! This is family friendly, peaceful and FUN event!" the organizers wrote, promoting the event.

The Austin American-Statesman covered the event and cast it playfully, with the headline "Dozens party outside Elon Musk's West Lake Hills home after campaign donations fell flat." It wrote about how organizers led a "Love Train" conga line and distributed party hats and refreshments, including cider.

The organizations behind the campaign are veterans of the professional protest industry. While this event was peaceful, the rhetoric of demonization and polarization at protests causes researchers at organizations like the Network Contagion Research Institute to conclude that that they can create a "permission structure" for political violence.

‘ASTROTURF’: CRITICS SPECULATE TESLA PROTESTS ARE NOT A GRASSROOTS MOVEMENT, BUT A CAREFULLY ORGANIZED CAMPAIGN

The three nonprofits behind this weekend’s protests are part of a powerful national tax-exempt nonprofit industry that has increasingly taken to the streets over the past decade in coordinated, orchestrated campaigns in local communities, creating the image of homegrown organic demonstrations.

The three groups reported $56.1 million in combined 2024 revenue, according to their most recent tax filings.

Organizers promoted the event as a peaceful celebration of voters rejecting the influence of wealthy donors. About 60 people attended, according to estimates.

The demonstration followed in the spirit of the very coordinated "Tesla Takedown" protests launched at Texas dealerships in early 2025, when activists organized demonstrations against Tesla and Musk over his political activities and role in the new Trump administration and the Department of Government Efficiency, called DOGE. Those protests led to vandalism against Tesla vehicles and dealerships. A wider network of openly pro-communist groups, including Freedom Road Socialist Organization, carried posters with messages like "DOWN WITH BILLIONAIRES. WE NEED SOCIALISM."

Congressional lawmakers and Justice Department prosecutors are investigating an American-born, Shanghai-based tycoon, Neville Roy Singham, for funding and backing pro-communist groups. Some of those groups, including the Party for Socialism and Liberation, ANSWER Coalition, CodePink and People's Forum, have supported the regular protests that have hit America's streets, including the anti-Musk demonstrations.

Much like the networks behind "No Kings," "Hands Off," "ICE Out" and other protests over the past year, the financial filings of the organizations behind this weekend’s demonstration show the scale of the infrastructure behind these groups.

The Center for Popular Democracy reported $40.9 million in 2024 revenue. People’s Action Institute reported $2.1 million, and Voices of Community Activists and Leaders Inc., or VOCAL-NY, which fiscally sponsors VOCAL-TX, reported $13.1 million. The organizations are separate entities, and the figures don’t represent a shared operating budget.

VOCAL-TX’s donation page identifies VOCAL-NY as its 501(c)(3) fiscal sponsor and sister organization. VOCAL-NY describes the organization as a statewide grassroots membership group focused on "building the power of low-income people" impacted by "HIV/AIDS, drug policy, mass incarceration, the war on drugs and homelessness." It says its work is "accomplished through community organizing, campaigns & harm reduction services."

The Center for Popular Democracy Inc. lists VOCAL-TX among its affiliates and describes itself as a network of "grassroots" organizations operating across dozens of states, building a "representative, multiracial society and government where all thrive, no exceptions," according to its nonprofit mission statement.

The protesters’ goal, according to the organization's website, is to send a crystal clear message that they are against Tesla CEO Elon Musk, describing itself as a decentralized grassroots movement that will "protest Tesla for as long as Elon Musk continues to shred public services." Organizers plan to hold rallies at over 200 Tesla location across the U.S. (Getty Images)

SOCIALIST, COMMUNIST GROUPS COORDINATE, PROMOTE 'SPONTANEOUS' ANTI-ICE PROTESTS IN AUSTIN

The Center for Popular Democracy reported $38.2 million in contributions and $14.6 million in other salaries and compensation in 2024. Its two co-executive directors received an average total compensation of $234,026, including bonuses in 2024, according to their latest tax filings, about four times more than the $63,360 median annual earnings of full-time, year-round U.S. workers that year.

VOCAL-NY reported nearly $3.5 million in other salaries and wages and listed its Texas co-director among its highest-paid employees, earning $113,842 in salary and $21,854 in bonuses in 2024.

People’s Action describes itself as a national network of grassroots groups "organized to promote economic, racial and social equity through national, regional and local advocacy campaigns."

Saturday’s gathering transformed a political victory "party" into a theatrical public spectacle, using buses, coordinated messaging, music and symbolic props to deliver a broader message: that billionaire wealth shouldn’t determine the direction of American politics. Protesters didn't specifically address the millionaires and billionaires that funnel money to Democratic politicians such as George Soros, Reid Hoffman and Pierre Omidyar, to name just a few.

Participants carried signs reading "Texas is not for sale" and "Alright Texas! Now let’s tax Musk." Attendees also smashed star-shaped and piñatas shaped like a Tesla Cybertruck.

CHAOS INC.: COMMUNISTS, ANTIFA, AGITATORS IN THE RED-GREEN ALLIANCE

Eli Cortez, a VOCAL-TX bus captain, told attendees that the event was intended to celebrate voters rejecting the political influence of billionaires, according to coverage by the Austin American-Statesman. Cortez didn’t respond to a request for comment.

The demonstration focused on Musk’s spending during the 2026 election cycle. A New York Times analysis placed Musk’s total disclosed political spending at approximately $474 million, while other analyses based more narrowly on Federal Election Commission filings put the figure at roughly $445 million. About $212 million supported Texas Republican Ken Paxton’s unsuccessful Senate campaign through allied political committees.

May Day Strong, another far-left coalition tagged in the social media promotion of the event, describes itself as a "constellation" of more than 600 labor and community organizations, many of them socialist and communist. Its stated demands include taxing the rich, opposing ICE, expanding "democracy" and organizing workers to "protest, refuse to comply, and take the economic actions" needed to oppose the Trump administration.

At a May Day protest in Washington, D.C., on May 1, 2025, blocks from the White House, protesters from the communist Party for Socialism and Liberation carry mass-produced posters created by a Democratic nonprofit to vilify Tesla CEO Elon Musk. (Asra Q. Nomani)

Austin protesters carried a trademark poster with a creepy image of Musk and the message, "I Am Stealing From You," now a mainstay at protests, not as a spontaneous grassroots creation but a strategic asset. The Progressive Change Institute, the 501(c)(3) sister organization of the Progressive Change Campaign Committee, has taken credit for commissioning the art early last year by Washington, D.C., political street artist Absurdly Well.

The graphic became part of the propaganda of the broader "Hands Off" protest network, which distributed thousands of copies of the poster. CNN, the Associated Press, the New York Times, Axios, TIME, Reuters, The Hill and MSNBC host Rachel Maddow have splashed photos of the poster in its coverage Progressive Change Campaign Committee's online member engagement director has said the merchandise was intended to serve an organizing purpose beyond fundraising, with the goal of making Elon Musk "a real liability for the administration."

The group now gives donors "5- or 10-pack of anti-Elon Musk rally posters" in exchange for donations: $135 for a five-pack, $250 for a $10-pack and $30 for a single poster.

At least two of the iconic posters landed in the "party" protest outside Musk's home, held overhead by demonstrators.
