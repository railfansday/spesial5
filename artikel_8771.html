The Best Gravel Riding Pockets in the U.S.—Including One Unexpected Sleeper Spot

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

These gravel pockets deliver rail trails, rugged climbs, epic events, and one sleeper pick.

I’ve been invited on gravel rides that range from a mix of road and well-maintained rail trail to rides where I wished I had a hardtail mountain bike. Admittedly, I’ve been the ringleader on more than one occasion for a solidly underbiked ride that was listed as a gravel ride.

There are many people I talk to who say they have the best places to ride gravel. Some pride themselves on challenging and chunky gravel roads, while other places have gravel rides with a 10,000-foot view. And how each rider defines gravel will be different from person to person.

The definition of gravel typically exists between well-maintained packed gravel that feels like a road to singletrack that has some gravel between the more technical bits. Many areas of the country may boast one great gravel quality, but this list compiles the places that check off more than a few boxes.

At this point, it’s clear gravel riding is here to stay, but the challenge lies in deciding where to ride first. Our list has the best gravel riding pockets in the country and can serve as your checklist for 2027. The last destination on the list is a sleeper you shouldn’t miss.

The New York Times featured a run-down of the best spots in the U.S. for gravel biking, so you know it’s now officially mainstream. Their list included Northwest Arkansas, namely Bentonville—in the heart of the Ozark Mountains—which has become known as a gravel (and all things biking) hub. That’s thanks in large part to the Walton family. The Waltons own Walmart and run the Walton Family Foundation, which has always prioritized bikes and outdoor recreation.

If you’re in the area, there are endless ways to spend a day on a bike. According to the NYT, Chuck Campbell, an environmental manager for a wastewater recycling company, has designed several epic loops that together make up the 1,200-mile Arkansas High Country Route, a mix of paved and unpaved roads that launched in 2019.

Colorado might be home to the unique gravel races of any state, but if you’re looking for gravel events you can likely find one in just about any state these days. Steamboat Springs launched SBT GRVL in 2019. They’ve since grown to be one of the most inclusive events out there, with all kinds of training ride opportunities, and partnerships with inclusive cycling groups such as All Bodies on Bikes and Ride for Racial Justice.

If you’re looking for a quieter way to enjoy Colorado gravel, check out Nederland, a quaint mountain town 30 minutes outside of Boulder. There are probably more gravel roads in and around Nederland than paved, and almost all of them take you to excellent cafes and snack shops to recharge. Nederland to Gold Hill with a homemade slice of pie from the Gold Hill Store is tough to beat.

If you’re looking for a way to escape the cold in the coming months, Arizona offers tons of gravel-friendly riding. Many of the multiuse trails around the Phoenix State Parks can be connected for some hearty loops. AZ Gravel Rides has compiled an excellent list of gravel adventures in all areas of the state.

Patagonia, Arizona, has become a not-so-secret winter escape for gravel lovers lately. Located in the Patagonia Mountains, it’s got a desert mountain feel, with plenty of climbing and lots of sandy roads with incredible views. The area is expansive, so you can ride all day without seeing anyone. According to the NYT, “a new edition in the expanding “Gravel Adventure Field Guide” series focuses on routes in Patagonia and surrounding Santa Cruz County.”

And on the East Coast, Vermont can’t be beat. Most people in Vermont will probably tell you that they prefer dirt roads to paved ones. There’s something nice about a slower pace. Seven Days VT claims that Vermont has 8,579 miles of unpaved roads, compared to 7,182 miles of paved roads.

The VTXL is a 300-mile route that stretches from Canada to Massachusetts via 90 percent dirt and gravel roads. Former WorldTour rider turned adventure/gravel pro Ted King is currently bikepacking the route that he’s done before in a single push. This time he’s documenting the summer’s flooding that ravaged the state. “Heartbreaking but with short memories, we want to help bring attention to just how devastating it was and continues to be as Vermont rebuilds,” he posted to social media.

The gravel in Vermont is extensive, but make sure you tie in a climb up the road dubbed, “the steepest paved mile in the U.S..” Lincoln Gap may be known for having the steepest paved mile in the US, but the descent down from the climb is high quality gravel. Just a heads up—if you’re a fan of breweries, then plan for more stops than you would normally expect.

When I think of a great area for riding gravel there are some factors that the location needs to have. Hard gravel climbs with ripping descents, some views while you catch your breath on top of the climbs, and something for everyone to ride—regardless of skill level, are a few things that top my list. Northern Pennsylvania may not be among the top areas for gravel riding for many people, but it should be.

Rail trails like the Pine Creek Rail Trail leave an option for anyone to get out on gravel in this area, but there are plenty of options for the seasoned cyclists as well. Events like Endless Mountains, Unpaved Pennsylvania, and Lu Lacka Wyco Hundo boast routes from 50 miles to 430 miles long. For context—the 115-mile route of Endless Mountains had over 12,000 feet of climbing. A large majority of the route was on gravel with very little stretches of paved country roads.

While the views from the top of the hills are beautiful its worth noting you don’t have the vistas of other locations like Colorado or Arizona. The area makes up for it’s lack of high alpine overlooks or desert landscapes with tough, punchy gravel climbs and positive vibes from the locals. Make sure this unexpected gravel honey hole ends up on your destination list for 2027. If you think I missed a spot (or have somewhere I should add for my 2027 ride list), then drop it in the comments below.
