Clients are demanding discounts because we use AI in software development. Let’s actually calculate what this "cheap AI" costs. The IT industry has quietly entered a new era: the age of AI-native engineering. While the benefits are obvious, software agencies now face a universal headache.

Almost every client asks for an “AI discount” or pushes budgets down, claiming: “AI does all the heavy tasks now anyway, right? You’re just pressing buttons.” Hardly anyone understands what they are actually asking to cut. Everywhere you look, young “vibe-coders” promise the moon: “We’ll build you an enterprise-grade system in 3 days using AI!” Everyone rushes in. Everyone vibe-codes.

And in meeting rooms, the exact same question echoes: “If you’re using AI, why am I paying the same rate as last year? Where’s my discount?” It sounds logical on the surface. In practice? It’s one of the most expensive mistakes a business owner can make in 2026. Where does this logic come from? A client sees a 30-second demo where a chatbot generates a signup form and draws a simple conclusion: Better tools = lower bill.

In a single month, clients are already getting significantly higher output than they did a year ago. That speed boost is the free bonus of technological progress.

When clients ask for a discount on top of that, they are asking to cut the invisible work: Code verification. While AI generates code instantly, that code still needs to be. Discount logic made sense in an era where the main bottleneck was typing code. That era no longer exists.

"If output jumped by 24% for the same budget, shouldn’t we get a discount?" Where would that price cut actually come from? The engineering effort didn't vanish; it shifted. Let’s look at a realistic scenario: A team of 6 engineers spending 1,000 hours per month.

That’s an extra 72 hours of intense verification work per month, nearly two full workweeks for a senior engineer, absorbed within the exact same 1,000-hour team cap.

When a client insists on a 20–30% discount, there is only one place left to cut: verification time.

In 6 months, you have 70+ unverified features running in production, built on code nobody fully understands because "AI generated it and it compiled."

When half of those features break down the road, fixing them costs an entire month of full-team labor.

The discount vanished. It just returned later under a new name: Unplanned System Re-engineering.

A Padawan types faster and looks flashier than a Master. But speed without deep mastery is a structural hazard.

True engineering strength in the AI era isn't about code generation speed; it's about accountability. It’s about understanding architecture, managing risks, and ensuring that a growing codebase doesn't turn into toxic tech debt.
