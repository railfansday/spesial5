Fed's Waller Calls for 50-Basis-Point Rate Hike, Says Tokenized Finance Still Needs Traditional Banks

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Fed's Waller Calls for 50-Basis-Point Rate Hike, Says Tokenized Finance Still Needs Traditional Banks

Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller wants the central bank to get more aggressive with interest rate hikes, arguing that stubborn inflation may require a 50-basis-point increase in December rather than the smaller move investors expect.

Speaking during a CNBC interview Wednesday, Waller said the Fed must either deliver a half-percentage-point increase at its December 8-9 meeting or explicitly signal that another hike will follow in January.

“You might need to, like, shock people,” Waller said, referring to the central bank's efforts to prevent businesses and consumers from accepting inflation near 3% as normal.

“My view is then you have to go 50 in December to put it in place and get it done,” he said, warning that insufficiently aggressive action could weaken expectations that inflation will return to the Fed's target.

Waller's concerns follow three consecutive months in which core consumer prices rose 0.3%, a pace equivalent to roughly 3.5% inflation annually. That's considerably higher than the Fed's 2% target, which is formally measured using a separate inflation indicator known as the Personal Consumption Expenditures price index.

The governor had already established his threshold for a more aggressive move in a November 10 speech. He said another 0.3% core CPI reading would warrant considering a half-point hike, arguing that moving only 25 basis points could damage the Fed's credibility.

October inflation subsequently met that condition. Headline consumer prices rose 0.4% during the month, while core inflation climbed 0.3%, both exceeding economists' forecasts.

Waller's latest comments suggest he is increasingly concerned about the psychology of inflation, particularly the risk that companies begin treating annual price increases of 3% as acceptable and routinely pass higher costs to customers.

The proposed increase would lift the Fed's benchmark rate from its current range of 3.75%-4% to 4.25%-4.50%. Two consecutive quarter-point hikes in December and January would reach the same destination, but Waller argues that timing and communication matter when inflation expectations are at risk.

Markets remain less convinced that the Fed will move that aggressively in December. Futures pricing Wednesday morning showed roughly a 95% probability of a 25-basis-point increase and only about a 5% probability of a half-point move

The prospect of higher borrowing costs has been particularly painful for crypto investors. Higher interest rates make Treasury securities and other interest-bearing investments more competitive with speculative assets, while tighter financial conditions can reduce the money available for riskier bets.

Bitcoin has already suffered a sharp selloff amid the changing rate outlook. As Decrypt previously covered, persistent inflation fears have coincided with withdrawals from spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds and weakening technical indicators, adding pressure to an already fragile market.

Waller's increasingly hawkish monetary-policy stance comes alongside a more accommodating view of blockchain networks, although he sees established financial infrastructure as essential to its adoption.

In a separate November 17 speech at The Clearing House Annual Conference in New York, Waller discussed how tokenization—the representation of financial assets on digital networks—could reshape payments and securities markets. That speech focused on financial technology rather than interest-rate policy.

He pointed to a growing convergence between traditional markets and blockchain platforms, with banks and payment providers experimenting with tokenized deposits, stablecoins, and programmable transactions.

Waller described how tokenized money could allow investors to purchase securities outside conventional trading hours, with payments and assets changing hands simultaneously through smart contracts.

But he rejected the idea that the development of blockchain-based markets necessarily makes existing payment infrastructure obsolete.

“Central bank money need not be used in every transaction or on every platform, but its presence at the foundation of the system is essential,” Waller said.
