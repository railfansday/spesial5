The Jordan Clarkson moment that shows why Knicks’ unique bench works so well

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

It was a guy who didn't play who served as a catalyst for the Knicks' most all-around win this year.

Coach Mike Brown used 12 players in the team's 121-102 win over the Cavs on Wednesday night at Madison Square Garden. Without OG Anunoby (calf strain) and Miles McBride (adductor tightness), it meant end-of-the-bench reserves got a rare chance to see some action.

Pacôme Dadiet had seven points in 14 minutes. Rookie Tyler Nickel, making his NBA debut (he has spent this year in the G League), got nine minutes and had five points. Mohamed Diawara got four minutes and chipped in three points. James Wiseman saw six minutes of action and helped seal the win with a few crucial rebounds late — he finished with six offensive rebounds.

Under Brown's anything-goes system, nobody is too far away from getting a chance. His love of experimentation, particularly early in the year, means players seemingly out of the rotation will get their opportunity to prove themselves at some point.

Jordan Clarkson of the New York Knicks smiles before an NBA basketball game against the Indiana Pacers in Indianapolis, Friday, Nov. 13, 2026. NBAE via Getty Images

It has cultivated an ego-less culture among the bench players, where they know the minutes will look different every night. It's a system of basketball socialism of sorts. But it's only possible because one member of that group set the tone for the rest.

"As a coach, you could want to do that, but you have to have others around you that will at least support it initially to see how it turns out," Brown said. "And a huge, huge, huge catalyst for that was Jordan Clarkson. Jordan Clarkson was the one, if you're talking about giving 100 percent out there to what allows me to do that, I think Jordan Clarkson gave me at least 90 percent of the opportunity to do it, because here's an accomplished veteran that could be in the rotation for almost any team out there, let alone a championship team, and I can literally play him six straight games and then sit him 10 straight games and he's gonna be the same dude every fricken' day. And he's gonna talk the same way to his teammates. Not 'can you believe what Mike Brown did?' Nah, he's gonna be the same dude, uplift the group, and then when I call his number again, he's gonna be ready to go.

"He's a guy that, even when he gets done playing, it's one of those things where you hope that he can just be a part of this organization in some way, shape or form until he doesn't want to be part of this organization. He's that type of person, in my opinion."

Clarkson missed Wednesday's game — the fourth straight he's been sidelined due to plantar fasciitis. But it was he who helped Dadiet — who has largely underwhelmed, now in his third year after the Knicks drafted him in the first round — deliver his best game as a pro.

"JC is always big on that, telling guys to stay ready," Jose Alvarado said. "He was in [Dadiet's] ear the whole shootaround [Wednesday morning]. Just telling everybody to stay ready because you never know when your name is called."

New York Knicks forward Pacome Dadiet (4) puts up a shot against the Cleveland Cavaliers at Madison Square Garden in New York, Wednesday, Nov. 25, 2026. Jason Szenes for NY Post

Alvarado himself had his best game of the year with 21 points in 19 minutes. But it wasn't long ago that he was out of the rotation, with Tyler Kolek having usurped him as the backup point guard.

But Brown has recently been experimenting with having Alvarado on the floor at the same time as Jalen Brunson, in order to allow Brunson to play off-the-ball more. And Brown has liked the results.

That has meant Alvarado is now cutting back into Kolek's minutes, despite Kolek continuing to play well. That could rub other players on other teams the wrong way –— losing minutes despite not doing anything wrong, simply because the coach wants to try something new and see how it works out. For the bench players, nothing is set in stone, regardless of how you play.

This team embraces it, though. Brown compared the way this bench pulls for one another to the championship Spurs and Warriors teams he was an assistant on.

"We care about each other," Landry Shamet, currently the top dog of that bench unit, said. "We want to win for each other. We're willing to take on different roles and we know that we've got each other's back. If Jose's got it going and my minutes are shortened, awesome. And vice versa. All of us, Deuce [McBride] included in that, I would throw him in that as well, all of us kind of feel that way. That's really cool.

"I've been saying it, I've been a broken record about it, but you don't see that — that's very rare where no ego or ulterior motives get involved. That's something special that you want to be a part of."

Something special, sparked by a special personality in Clarkson.
