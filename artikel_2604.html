Eagles vs. Cowboys: 5 storylines to follow on Thanksgiving Day

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Thanksgiving and the Dallas Cowboys have been attached at the hip for generations. The Philadelphia Eagles are getting another opportunity to play a part in that tradition. There is something different about a divisional rivalry taking center stage on a holiday, particularly when the two teams have so much familiarity with one another and so little patience for giving the other an inch.

This year's meeting carries plenty of intrigue beyond the usual NFC East animosity. Philadelphia is coming off a much-needed victory, while Dallas has been putting together an eventful stretch of its own. With the season entering its final stretch, Thursday's game gives both teams a chance to make a statement in front of a national audience. Here are five storylines surrounding the Eagles-Cowboys Thanksgiving matchup as kickoff nears.

A couple of months ago, Joe Milton was on the Cowboys' practice squad. Now, he's preparing to make his third consecutive start for the Philadelphia Eagles, his second on the national stage. It's an unusual twist for a quarterback who spent training camp competing for a place behind Dak Prescott before Dallas ultimately went with Sam Howell.

Dallas has undergone some changes since Milton was there, including a switch from Christian Parker to veteran Mike Pettine handling the defensive play-calling duties. The Cowboys haven't exactly become an impenetrable unit, either. They surrendered 346 yards and 30 points to the Tennessee Titans on Sunday. That may suggest Milton and Philadelphia could find an opportunity to find some favorable matchups.

There's more to a team's record than the numbers in each column. Opponents, circumstances and the quality of those victories all help provide a better picture of how good a team really is. The Cowboys' recent offensive production has been impressive, but their 5-6 record leaves them with considerable work to do in the NFC playoff race. A victory over Philadelphia would move Dallas back to .500 and provide additional momentum entering December, while an Eagles victory would prevent Dallas from completing its first regular-season sweep of Philadelphia since 2021.

That's worth remembering when looking at the Cowboys' 5-6 record entering Thanksgiving. Dallas has won five games, but none of those victories came against a team with a winning record.

That doesn't mean Dallas hasn't earned its victories, nor does it guarantee Philadelphia will take advantage of the opportunity. However, it does raise a legitimate question about the Cowboys' standing among the NFC's contenders. A Thanksgiving win would also bring Dallas back to .500, but changing the narrative around the Cowboys' season may come only by stacking quality wins.

The Dallas defense has had its share of problems, but Quinnen Williams has been a significant bright spot. He has recorded 18 tackles for loss through 11 games, leading the NFL, while adding eight sacks and 13 additional quarterback hits. Philadelphia allowed three sacks against Pittsburgh, and Williams' production creates another difficult assignment for an offensive line that has struggled to consistently protect its quarterbacks. The Eagles need to minimize negative plays on early downs and prevent Williams from putting Milton in difficult passing situations.

He's been disruptive against both the run and the pass. The Eagles are well aware. In the first meeting between these two teams, he recorded four tackles, three tackles for loss, one sack, and another quarterback hit. The Eagles will have to account for him throughout Thursday's game. Williams can change a drive with one disruptive play, and Philadelphia cannot afford to let him dictate the terms of the matchup.

The Eagles have enjoyed considerable success on Thanksgiving, compiling a 6-1 record in seven appearances. Their history against Dallas on the holiday is without blemish. Philadelphia has won both previous Thanksgiving meetings. The Eagles' only Thanksgiving defeat came in 2015, when they lost 45-14 to the Detroit Lions. 1939 - Eagles 17, Pittsburgh Pirates (Steelers) 14

Sometimes, records don't matter in a rivalry game, and anything can happen. The Eagles will have an opportunity to extend their perfect mark against their division rival. The first meeting came in 1989, when the Eagles shut out Dallas 27-0 in the infamous Bounty Bowl. Thursday obviously will mark Philadelphia's first Thanksgiving appearance since that loss.

The Philadelphia Eagles haven't enjoyed much success on their trips to Arlington lately. They're 2-8 in their last 10 road games against the Cowboys, with their victories coming in 2017 and 2024. That recent history makes Thursday's Thanksgiving matchup another difficult assignment for Philadelphia, particularly with Dallas looking to protect its home field.

The Cowboys are also favored by entering the holiday matchup on the sportsbooks. Philadelphia will have to overcome more than its recent road struggles to leave AT&T Stadium with a victory. The Eagles have already lost to Dallas once this season, and the Cowboys have another opportunity to make life difficult for their division rival in front of a national audience.
