Like clockwork, the Pixel 11 is a way better buy after a few months [Video]

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Like clockwork, the first quarter of the Pixel 11 brings discounts, and this shines a light on the base model that needs to be explored.

By this point, you don’t need me to tell you that this is a familiar design. It’s turning into a comfortable pair of slippers at this point in time. Nothing is wrong, and nothing particularly stands out. A safe but samey design that is inoffensive.

I hoped that the maximum brightness bug would have been resolved by now with the screen. It creates a shadow around the edges of the display after a minute or so when in direct sunlight. Somewhat like blooming on a cheap LCD screen. It does diminish what is otherwise a great display.

The thickness has started to feel like a relic of the 2023 design that feels a little dated now. I have been using the Pixel 11 without a case most of the time, which mitigates it, but throw a case on and it’s a chunky boi. The added thickness would probably have been acceptable had the extra space been filled with more battery, but it feels like that boat has set sail a long time ago. A slimmer profile probably would have helped differentiate without deviating too much from last year’s phone.

After months of using the Pixel 11, the move of the NFC module to the top edge has been huge for me because my car uses an NFC terminal to lock and unlock when you’re without a set of car keys. With the Pixel 10, it worked but not quite as smoothly. It’s one of those inconsequential changes that makes a real-world difference.

Gemini Intelligence has been something I’ve started to rely on quite heavily. I love that I get a prompt to add an appointment to my calendar, and all of the important details have been taken care of, like the location, extra contact details, the works.

I’m not sure what the hold-up is for adding wider functionality. It’s actively hurting things, in my opinion. Google Messages is great, but some of the automation functions locked in there mean I haven’t been saving any time with Gemini’s prowess at doing the legwork of keeping my life up to date.

I’ve tried using Pause Point, but found it’s just easier to leave my phone face down rather than use an app to try and stop using my phone…maybe if we all did that, so many of the world’s ills wouldn’t be so pronounced. Just a thought.

The November Pixel Drop has also made some notable changes to the Pixel 11 experience that I can’t believe were held back since launch. If you waited and bought in the last few weeks with the substantial discounts kicking around, you objectively get a better phone today than at launch.

The improvements don’t overhaul things, just enhance or heighten the fluidity and experience above what was available in the months post-launch. More cohesive theming, little tweaks to the UI; it’s not reinventing the Pixel 11, just augmenting it in ways that would have been nice back in August.

I don’t really have much to complain about with the Pixel 11 lifespan even after months of charging. Yes, charging is still slow, but you only need to charge once per day. If we were talking about a phone with bad battery life and slow charging, I’d have more to complain about. However, it’s still solid and dependable.

The camera continues to impress without really improving drastically. I can’t help but feel that every couple of generations, the Pro camera setup should be passed on to the base Pixel, then we get a newer, improved system on that specific Pro model. It’d allow for quicker improvements and maybe, dare I say it, make the camera system cheaper as parts get reused. If anything, it would help Google’s supposed environmental pledges by preventing camera components from hitting landfill.

A lot of people will tell you the best camera is the one you have with you, and this hasn’t ever been a problem for a recent Pixel. I can count on one hand the frustrations I have felt with the Pixel 11 camera since it landed on my desk on launch day. I am happier than a year ago with the base Pixel 10 than the 11, which is to say this is a bigger leap or a return to form.

Having the ability to tune Camera Looks was something I have loved, but you might just fall into using one tuned look that fits your own personal style. There is an element of this that might change the perception of the Pixel 11 camera consistency versus its predecessors. What I mean is that while it does still excel at point-and-shoot, the fact that Looks are baked into the JPEG could be a problem. There are times when I’ve thought that an image might have been better using default settings.

That said, I have stuck to “Shadows” with Contrast set to -5, Highlights to -5, Shadows to -5.5, warmth to 0.5, and Grain to “Strong.” That is a recipe I’ll probably stick with for a long time to come. It’s still weird that we can do all this at the camera level with a Pixel. Whether “normies” even use the options or just stick to the out-of-the-box default settings is another discussion for another day.

At $699, the Pixel 11 becomes a decent buy for a lot of people. For others, the distinct lack of power is something that can’t ever be overcome. I would buy this for a relative because it’ll do everything they could ever want and more. For a friend who cares about having the cutting edge in their pocket? Probably not.

A lot of the problems over the past few years on Pixel phones have come from the price tag.

Even with the discounts in and around Black Friday, the fact that you can get a device like the Galaxy S25 Ultra that’ll have better specs and lots of valuable features for around the same price. The last few generations of the Pixel have been harder to recommend because of the drawbacks versus the cost and the competition.

I said a few months ago that there is a good phone under the price hikes, and that remains unchanged. However, there are better products from the year prior that won’t compromise and are even cheaper. In a silo, you’re getting a 7/10 product at a 3/10 price. Just how that changes depends on forthcoming updates. One saving grace is that no Pixel is ever quite the same months after it rolls off the production line. A few Pixel Drops, even more discounts, and maybe a true seal of approval might be easier.

To put it into context, this was never a $900 phone and arguably never should have been. That’s somewhat a failing on Google’s part in more ways than one. At least it’s down to a palatable price that should be the Pixel baseline.
