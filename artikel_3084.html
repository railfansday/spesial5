Curtiss-Wright: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Supply chain moves taken last year, like buying memory chips early, are what protect fourth-quarter deliveries for shareholders.

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 5, 2026, Curtiss-Wright (CW -4.04%) raised its full-year guidance across all major financial metrics after quarterly sales grew 10% year over year. Order momentum in defense and commercial nuclear markets provides long-term revenue visibility extending into the next decade. Three key takeaways from management explain how the company is positioning itself for sustained growth.

Curtiss-Wright is expanding its footprint in commercial nuclear power by supplying critical valves, pumps, and sensors for both traditional power plants and small modular reactors, which are compact nuclear reactors built in factories for faster deployment. Securing high-value design packages on platforms like the Rolls-Royce small modular reactor creates a multiyear revenue stream tied to global energy demand.

Defense order bookings rose 27% in the Defense Electronics segment, driving total order backlog, which represents funded customer orders awaiting delivery, to $4.6 billion. Higher defense spending by the U.S. Department of Defense and NATO allies on fighter jets, naval shipbuilding, and tactical communications equipment ensures sustained demand for production.

To prevent component shortages from disrupting shipments of electronic assemblies, management created dedicated teams and purchased critical memory microchips in advance. Securing inventory ahead of time protects sales delivery schedules and operating margins, which measure the profit remaining after production costs are deducted.

Operational velocity and global facility expansions are accelerating across core markets, backed by rising NATO defense spending and long-term nuclear supply contracts. Entrenched positions on key flight platforms and specialized naval propulsion equipment provide structural market advantages as management allocates capital toward global infrastructure.

Free cash flow conversion rates above 105% give management the flexibility to compound earnings at a mid-teens percentage pace while funding strategic plant expansions.
