The new cards offer perks like faster paths to status and elevated earning rates when purchasing tickets with partners.

Etihad Guest, the loyalty program of Abu Dhabi-based Etihad Airways, is expanding its U.S. presence by introducing two new credit cards. Most people won’t get much value from these cards, but they can make sense for Etihad frequent flyers who want elite status.

The two cards are the Etihad Guest Visa Signature® Card ($99 annual fee) and the Etihad Guest Premier Visa Signature® Card ($499 annual fee). Issued by the First National Bank of Omaha, both offer faster pathways to earning and maintaining elite status and the ability to earn extra miles on Etihad purchases.

They also offer extra miles on international transactions (neither has foreign transaction fees) and purchases with Etihad’s partner airlines, which include U.S. carriers like American Airlines and JetBlue Airways.

Here’s what you need to know about the cards.

The Etihad Guest Visa Signature® Card comes with a $99 annual fee, and it earns Etihad Guest miles at the following rates:

3 miles per $1 spent on Etihad purchases.

2 miles per $1 spent on Etihad partner airline purchases.

2 miles per $1 spent on non-U.S. purchases.

1 mile per $1 spent on all other U.S. purchases.

Welcome offer: Through Feb. 3, 2027, the card is offering the following welcome offer for new applicants: Earn up to 40,000 welcome Etihad Guest Miles, including 10,000 miles after the first purchase and an additional 30,000 miles after spending $3,000 within the first three billing cycles after account opening.

Elite status benefits: You’ll also get Etihad Silver status after your first Etihad purchase within 90 days of opening your account. Unlike most airlines, that entry-level status is enough to get you into the business class lounge when traveling through Etihad’s hub at Abu Dhabi’s Zayed International Airport.

You can retain that status by spending $20,000 a year on the card, or $3,000 a year on Etihad purchases.

The Etihad Guest Premier Visa Signature® Card comes with a $499 annual fee, and it earns Etihad Guest miles at the following rates:

5 miles per $1 spent on Etihad purchases.

3 miles per $1 spent on Etihad partner airline purchases.

2 miles per $1 spent on non-U.S. purchases.

1 mile per $1 spent on all other U.S. purchases.

The only differences in rewards between the premium and standard cards are increased earning rates for eligible airline purchases.

Welcome offer: Through Feb. 3, 2027, the premium card offers a higher welcome offer compared to the standard card: Earn up to 60,000 welcome Etihad Guest Miles, including 20,000 miles after the first purchase and 40,000 miles after spending $7,000 within the first three billing cycles after account opening.

Elite status benefits: The premium card offers a faster track to elite status: automatic Silver status upon card approval and Gold status after your first Etihad purchase within 90 days of opening your account.

To maintain Gold status, you’ll need to spend $40,000 a year on the card, or $8,000 on Etihad purchases. Otherwise, you’ll have Silver status.

Wi-Fi benefit: After your first Etihad purchase, you’ll get 3,000 Etihad Guest miles, enough to cover Wi-Fi costs on a long-haul Etihad flight. You’ll then get those miles again after each cardholder anniversary.

Travel benefits: The card offers a statement credit for Global Entry or TSA PreCheck and select travel protections. If you already have a premium travel card, you might already have access to these perks.

The card also includes a Priority Pass lounge access membership. However, an Etihad spokesperson told NerdWallet that it’s the entry-level membership tier, so you’ll need to pay for yourself and guests each time you visit a Priority Pass lounge. Most premium credit cards in the U.S. that offer Priority Pass allow complimentary visits for at least the cardholder.

The Etihad cards make the most sense for Etihad’s frequent flyers who are flying to or connecting through Abu Dhabi several times a year.

That’s because the standard card’s accelerated pathway to Etihad Guest’s Silver status tier can unlock the ability to choose business class lounge access in Abu Dhabi as a status perk.

With the premium card, you get a fast track to Gold. If you select lounge access as a Gold status perk, you can not only bring a guest on the same flight into the lounge, but also access Etihad’s worldwide lounge network. That includes Etihad’s own lounges in New York and Washington, D.C., as well as most partner lounges at other Etihad destinations.

Bear in mind that while Etihad has many partnerships with other airlines, it’s not part of an alliance, so you don’t get lounge benefits when traveling on partners. You’ll need to be flying on an Etihad-operated flight to get into the lounge.

If you primarily fly with other carriers, then Etihad status will have limited upside for you.

However, the Priority Pass lounge membership that comes with the premium card does not have that restriction. You can access Priority Pass lounges while flying on any airline, although you’ll need to pay an additional per-person $35 fee each visit.

The earning rates on the Etihad cards are lackluster for everyday spenders. The bonus categories are for airline purchases with Etihad and its partners, as well as international spending.

That means your day-to-day purchases at home, like groceries, dining out and gas, earn only 1 mile per $1 spent.

Plus, there are more ways to earn Etihad Guest miles than spending on an Etihad credit card. For example, you can transfer points to Etihad Guest from Bilt Rewards, Capital One and Citi ThankYou.

Those programs offer cards that typically have better benefits and bonus categories, and they offer flexibility on how you can use your points and miles. With an Etihad card, you can only earn Etihad Guest miles, which expire after 18 months without qualifying flight activity. (If you’re not traveling on Etihad or its partners at least once every 18 months, then you definitely should not get the cards.)

One of the most interesting perks of the new cards is the bonus category for spending with Etihad partner airlines — 2 miles per $1 for the standard card and 3 miles per $1 for the premium version. Typically, airline credit cards reward customers only for spending on the airline, not partners.

There are better cards available, though. Some offer as many or even more points per $1 on flights purchased directly from airlines.

If you want the card solely to earn more Etihad Guest miles, then the answer for most people is no.

While you can earn more miles for purchases with Etihad and partner airlines, the earning rates aren’t attractive enough to justify the annual fees, especially when compared with existing cards with currencies that transfer to Etihad Guest.

Status chasers could benefit from the fast track to elite status — but you would need to be a fairly frequent Etihad flyer to really take advantage of your elite benefits.
