Pochettino's USMNT swan song signals change

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

From the moment Mauricio Pochettino was announced as United States men's national team head coach in September 2024, things felt different. It wasn't his experience or his personality - although, both were certainly noteworthy. Instead, it was his name. His stature. His reputation.

Suddenly, somehow, the United States - the perpetually overlooked, stigmatized name in the halls of global soccer - was dating the prom king.

It was unexpected. It was surreal. And it was bound to be fleeting.

Now, following a 2-0 victory against Haiti at TQL Stadium, in Cincinnati, the Mauricio Pochettino fairytale is officially over. After just two years in charge of the USMNT (and a whirlwind two years at that), Pochettino departs to take up the coveted / cursed position of Manchester United manager.

It's a change that's as disappointing as it is unsurprising. Despite the misty-eyed soundbites over the last week in which he described the decision as "one of the toughest" of his career, Pochettino's return to club football has felt inevitable.

He made no bones about his wandering eye, always quick to profess his love for the Premier League while maintaining his London residence throughout his time with the USMNT. Even when his contract extension was announced this summer, the conditions of that deal made it abundantly clear that should an attractive suitor come calling, he would answer. Now, after years apart, he'll get to rekindle that romance.

As Pochettino celebrated Christian Pulisic putting the U.S. ahead in the 25th minute with one of the most acrobatic goals of the attacker's career (a high-flying bicycle kick that cut the ball inside the far post), some of his personnel were already across the Atlantic, laying the groundwork for his upcoming rendezvous. Even if his couching staff was divided, Pochettino insisted that his focus was not. And with Julian Hall doubling the USMNT's lead in the 43rd minute, perhaps he wasn't lying.

It's rare that football managers exit on their own terms. Even rarer still when they finish out their tenure having already secured their next job. Likely rarest of all, is to go straight from a CONCACAF Nations League quarter-final to the biggest derby in English football.

Then again, little about Pochettino's tenure has been normal.

To even appoint the Argentine, U.S. Soccer aimed higher, spent more, and was bolder than ever before. The mechanisms employed to secure his signature (including donations-as-salary from hedge-fund billionaire Ken Griffin) were as opportunistic as they were canny. Ultimately, they succeeded. But has Pochettino?

If this window is anything to go by then the answer is emphatically yes. Eight goals, zero conceded, across two competitive performances is beyond reproach. Exciting attacks, the promise of youth, undeniable tenacity - much of what fans hoped they would get from Pochettino's appointment was on display against Haiti. But stepping out of the moment, things become a little more complicated.

Pochettino's time as U.S. head coach saw no records broken and no trophies won. The USMNT's ranking is identical to when he took over while his win percentage is less than that of predecessor Gregg Berhalter. Meanwhile, his relationship with the players (Christian Pulisic, in particular) was at times tense, and his rapport with media and fans could even be patronizing. It was, however, also under his watch that 78 different players earned a cap, nine teenagers made debuts, and the USMNT put in one of their all-time great performances (Paraguay), World Cup or otherwise. Altogether, it's a portfolio that plays like a Rorschach test. Love him or hate him, you'll find stats to back you up.

But stats aren't really where Pochettino's legacy lies. Indeed, what Pochettino left the U.S. with - and perhaps even the wider soccer world - was a shift in perception.

The US can should think bigger. Our talent is plentiful. World Cups are winnable.

How true these notions actually are remains to be seen, but what isn't up for debate is who promoted them: Mauricio Pochettino. USMNT head coach Muaricio Pochettino. The sheer reality of that fact makes any future fantasies just a little more plausible. It's something that even Pochettino seemed to recognize. "Being head coach in USA now is prestigious," he said. Self-aggrandizing, sure, but also true - even if only symbolically.

And this, more than anything, is Pochettino's great success.

Two years ago, the thought of a top-tier, globally in-demand manager taking over the United States men's national team was unthinkable. International football simply wasn't a jewel in the managerial crown - let alone a program as disparaged as the U.S. But one Pochettino later, and the narrative has changed.

Now, some of the biggest names in soccer (Tuchel, Carlo, Klopp) find themselves helming national teams (England, Brazil, Germany, respectively) while arguably the biggest name is rumored to be intrigued by the prospect of taking over the USMNT.

The likelihood of Pep Guardiola becoming the next United States head coach is slim, at best. But what's remarkable is that it's no longer laughable.

This is a massive country, with tremendous resources, and enormous potential. The scale and scope of a USMNT "project", combined with the prospect of unleashing a sleeping giant, might actually entice a name as big as Pep (see Man City). Still, in the immediate context of Haiti and a Nation's League quarter final, it's a prospect that's hard to reconcile, and, ultimately, a reflection of the unique place that the U.S. now finds itself.

A place where B.J. Callaghan is mentioned alongside Julian Nagelsmann. Where the self-deprecating backwaters of USMNT fandom have turned a little less dark. Where the once overlooked now get second glances.

A place that was good enough for Mauricio Pochettino.
