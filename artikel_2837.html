Have the Chiefs finally fixed their pass rush?

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Sometimes we make defense more complicated than it needs to be. In the modern game, much of an effective defensive unit depends on its ability to get after the quarterback. Look at the top of the leaderboard this year for proof: The (7-2) Minnesota Vikings and the defending champion Seattle Seahawks lead the league in pressure rate.

The top two defenses in points per drive allowed? You guessed it, the Vikings and the Seahawks.

Get after the quarterback, and you're more likely to get consistent stops. Fail to do so, and it could be a long day. That's the case for just about every defense in the league.

Except the 2026 Kansas City Chiefs, apparently.

The Chiefs have been a rare exception to this rule for much of the season. Kansas City currently ranks 26th in sacks, 27th in QB hits, and 29th in pressure rate, according to Pro Football Reference. But, somehow, they miraculously rank 5th in the league in points per drive allowed.

It doesn't really make sense. For much of the season, it's felt like smoke and mirrors. Some of it can surely be explained by the schedule. Let's be honest, the Chiefs haven't exactly faced a murderer's row of opposing offenses. The Chiefs have made do with a prototypical 'bend-don't-break' defensive approach for much of the season.

But the tide seems to be turning over the past couple of weeks. The Chiefs are bending less. The pass rush is getting home more.

It's incredibly rare to win as often as the Chiefs did early this season with the kind of pass rush they were producing. In fact, only seven other teams in the past 15 years won at least 5 of their first 7 games while allowing 9 or fewer sacks in those seven games. The others were the 2025 49ers, the 2020 Titans, the 2018 Patriots, the 2016 Raiders, the 2015 Falcons, the 2014 Cardinals and the 2014 Cowboys. Those teams finished the season ranked 32nd, 30th, 30th, 32nd, 32nd, 24th and 28th in the league, respectively, for sacks in those individual seasons.

That's not exactly a great omen for the 2026 Chiefs. They've been trying their darndest the past couple of weeks to buck the trend.

Kansas City produced just nine sacks over their first seven games, but they've followed that up with an eight-sack outburst over the past two weeks. In fact, if you look at sack yardage lost by the opposition, the Chiefs have pushed their opponents back more (58 yards) over the past two weeks than they did in their first seven weeks combined (39 yards).

What's changed? Well, a combination of everything.

For one, edge rusher George Karlaftis has taken his game to an entirely different level. He's been earning pressures the entire season, but he's finally converting those pressures into sacks. Karlaftis had 18 pressures and two sacks in his first seven games of the regular season, and he's 8 pressures and 3.5 sacks over the past two.

Part of what's helped Karlaftis take that step is those around him winning one-on-one battles. Second-year defensive lineman Ashton Gillotte is probably the first name worth mentioning. He produced just two pressures and one sack in his first seven games, according to Pro Football Reference. He has doubled those numbers over the past two games with 2 sacks and 4 pressures.

Chris Jones also finally looks like himself again. The future Hall of Famer tallied just three hurries and 0.5 sacks in his first 7 games of the regular season. He's added 2.5 sacks and 3 pressures over the past couple of weeks.

The results are hard to ignore. The Chiefs produced 8 sacks in a 2-game stretch for the first time since December of 2024. It's worth noting this is a feat the Chiefs accomplished at some point in the 2019, 2022, 2023 and 2024 seasons. They reached the Super Bowl in each of those four years.

Time will tell. This pass rush has been underwhelming far more often than it's been great this season. But the past two weeks were legitimate signs of life. The Jets and Falcons aren't great teams, but their offensive lines aren't awful.

Brandon Thorn of Establish the Run is as respected as any offensive line analyst covering the game, and he currently has the Jets ranked as his 12th-best offensive line in the league. He has the Falcons at 19th. Not world beaters, but this wasn't the Chiefs taking advantage of the early-season Packers MASH unit offensive line.

This weekend should set up well for another dominant performance by the Chiefs' D-Line. The Cardinals' quarterbacks have been sacked 8 times over the past two weeks. Thorn ranks their offensive line as the third worst in the league. This could be yet another get-right game for the defensive side of the ball before things start to get real.

What lies ahead will dictate the Chiefs' postseason seeding. The Bills (4th), Rams (14th), Bengals (5th), and 49ers (3rd) all have top-half-of-the-league offensive lines, according to Thorn. The Chiefs' defensive line will have its work cut out for them. The past two weeks have been encouraging. There's still work to be done.

Pressure has been building on this defense to get after the quarterback. It's nice to see them finally applying some of that pressure to the opposition.
