Sterling given suspended jail sentence for dangerous driving

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Raheem Sterling pleaded guilty at Basingstoke Magistrates' Court in September and admitted possession of six nitrous oxide cannisters for wrongful inhalation and failing to provide a specimen

Former England player Raheem Sterling has been given a suspended 12-month jail sentence for dangerous driving and possession of nitrous oxide after a crash involving a Lamborghini on the motorway.

Sterling pleaded guilty at Basingstoke Magistrates' Court in September to driving a Lamborghini Urus dangerously on the M25, the M3, the A327 and Minley Road before he crashed on May 28. The sentence is suspended for 18 months.

He has also been handed a two-year driving ban and must undertake 100 hours of unpaid community work. Sterling will have to re-take a driving test to hold a licence.

The 31-year-old admitted possession of six nitrous oxide cannisters for wrongful inhalation, a class C drug, and failing to provide a specimen.

Mitigating factors taken into account by District Judge Kirsty Allman were Sterling's guilty plea, that he had no prior convictions and a clean driving licence prior to his arrest in May.

She also said she took into account Sterling's regret at his behaviour, attached great weight to testimonies about his character and the fact that, away from his footballing career and perhaps not in the public sphere, he is heavily involved with several charitable causes from the Raheem Sterling Foundation, the RS7 Academy and several charitable bursaries.

Further mitigation was that Sterling engaged with addiction services soon after his arrest in May and admitted himself into residential care for 12 days at an addiction centre, which the court saw as "genuine engagement".

The judge said Sterling was entirely culpable for the dangerous driving charge, saying: "You chose to drive, disregarding the safety of others".

Chief Inspector Chris Sharland from Hampshire Police said: "Nitrous oxide, commonly known as laughing gas or NOS, is a Class C drug, and driving whilst under its influence is an emerging threat to the safety of motorists on our roads.

"Driving whilst impaired with nitrous oxide can cause unconsciousness, and in the worst-case scenarios neurological damage or death, so there is a very real risk of causing serious harm to yourself or other motorists.

"We urge drivers to be aware of not only the law, but the risk this type of behaviour carries - one decision can have devastating consequences."

Sterling scored 20 goals in 82 appearances for England, helping Gareth Southgate's side reach the 2018 World Cup semi-finals and the final of Euro 2020. He came through the academy at QPR before joining Liverpool, where he made his senior debut.

Sterling, who was born in Kingston, Jamaica, moved to Manchester City in 2015 and won four Premier League titles, five League Cups and the FA Cup in 2019. He left for Chelsea in 2022 but after falling out of favour spent the 2024/25 season on loan at Arsenal.

Sterling joined Dutch club Feyenoord on a short-term deal until the end of the 2025/26 season. He is currently without a club.

In the 2021 Queen's Birthday Honours, Sterling was made an MBE for services to racial equality in sport after fronting anti-racism and discrimination campaigns and speaking out strongly against racism on and off the pitch for several years.

It came amid controversy over England fans booing players taking the knee at matches as a gesture against intolerance.

Sky Sports News' Geraint Hughes at Basingstoke Magistrates Court:

"When Raheem Sterling arrived at Basingstoke Magistrates Court for his sentencing, as you would expect he looked serious, but also quite anxious.

"He was wearing dark clothing and never once did his gaze do anything other than look forward or towards the judge inside the courtroom. You felt he was aware of the gravity of the situation and also - given that his legal team had told the court how ashamed he felt to be in this position and that he never ever wanted to be in front of a criminal court in his life again - you could sense that with his body language and his demeanour.

"When the district judge asked Raheem Sterling to stand while she delivered her sentence he did so at times looking downwards, but mainly straight at the judge, occasionally acknowledging her remarks.

"At the conclusion of her sentencing, when Sterling was told he was free to leave the court, there was no smile or any emotion. He just walked out of the courtroom with his legal team to whom he shook their hands and thanked them very quietly before being able to leave the court a free man having avoided a potential custodial sentence."
