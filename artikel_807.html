'Outstanding' Overton goes 'back to basics' to deliver big win for England

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Jamie Overton took 5-12, England's second-best figures ever in T20I, as Australia were bowled out for 108 inside 17 overs at The Gabba; Overton's impressive spell helped England to a rain-affected seven-wicket win in Brisbane and 2-1 series lead

Jamie Overton’s decision to go ‘back to basics’ paid off as England’s all-rounder delivered in emphatic fashion during their T20 victory over Australia in Brisbane.

Overton had conceded 72 runs from just erratic five overs during the first two matches of the five-match series, only to respond at The Gabba by producing his first five-wicket haul in international ‌cricket.

The 32-year-old became just the second Englishman to take a five-fer in T20 internationals, following on from Sam Curran, finishing with 5-12 from his four overs during England's rain-affected win.

"He wanted to really focus on tightening things up and keeping his game plan really simple, which he did," former England bowler Steven Finn told TNT Sports.

"He bowled at the top of the stumps a lot and used his short ball well on a pitch that suited him, with the pace and bounce there was. He channelled things in his mind to bowl his best ball as often as possible and it served him really well.

"What Overton did today is work to his strengths better than he has done throughout this series, and 5-12 is a nice return as a result."

Overton also claimed two catches off Liam Dawson (3-16) and was involved in the first seven Australian wickets, as the hosts were skittled for 108, before England chased down their target - after three rain delays and two revised totals - in 8.5 overs.

"I thought Jamie Overton was outstanding tonight," England white-ball captain Harry Brook said. "He has worked hard over last couple of weeks, put in little bit extra graft yesterday at training and reaped his rewards tonight.

"He was bowling it on a string and it felt you could set a different field and let them make the mistake."

Overton only allowed seven scoring shots during his four-over burst, which included him recovering from being struck on his left thumb to finish with a double-wicket maiden.

"I've been disappointed with the way I've bowled the last couple of games - a bit short and a bit erratic," Overton admitted at the end of Australia's innings.

He added after England completed victory: "It's nice to get a few wickets and get back to what I know I can be. It's been a frustrating tour and I feel like I've just not been at my best.

"I did some work with Timmy (bowling coach Tim Southee) in the nets and it's funny how quick things can change. I was working on really simple things, putting a mitt on the top of off stump and just focusing on hitting the mitt.
