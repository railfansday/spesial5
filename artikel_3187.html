JFrog: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Net dollar retention climbed to 124% as customers consolidated security tools into the company's platform, a sign that the moat may be widening.

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 5, 2026, JFrog (FROG +35.33%) announced revenue grew 31% year over year to $179.2 million. Enterprise software development is shifting toward automated artificial intelligence tools that generate binary computer code files faster than human teams can track them.

As code volume surges, large organizations rely on centralized systems of record to manage, store, and secure digital assets across multiyear cloud migrations. Three things management said on the call stand out.

Enterprise customers are converting flexible cloud usage into fixed long-term commitments. Cloud software revenue grew 60% year over year to $101.5 million, boosted by SaaS, or software-as-a-service, contracts that lock in predictable spending as workload volume expands.

Organizations are bundling cybersecurity features directly into their core software repositories to block risky software packages before deployment. This suite approach lifted net dollar retention, a metric tracking revenue expansion from existing customers over time, up 6 percentage points year over year to 124%.

Artificial intelligence agents produce unprecedented volumes of code artifacts, creating operational bottlenecks for legacy infrastructure. Software teams require unified platforms that maintain strict compliance and auditing standards while running automated build workflows.

Customer retention stays high as enterprises standardize on hybrid software management and automated governance tools to handle security compliance. Pipeline expansion and subscriptions growth for Enterprise+, the company's top-tier platform package, continues into the fourth quarter of 2026.

As a result, management raised baseline cloud growth guidance to 52% to 54% year over year, and full-year 2026 revenue guidance to between $680 million and $682 million, while monitoring cloud cost optimization.

The company maintains a competitive moat anchored in high customer switching costs, holding gross retention at 97% as customers host petabytes of data inside Artifactory, JFrog's flagship software repository manager. Expanding security products and developer governance workflows reinforces its core position across enterprise development teams.
