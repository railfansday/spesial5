Hackers breached the US Air Force’s Cloud One platform, Reuters reports

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The Pentagon, headquarters of the US Department of Defense, in September 2018.

Hackers have broken into Cloud One, the US Air Force’s main cloud computing platform, three people familiar with the matter told Reuters on Friday. The breach could put a wide range of military data at risk, from personnel records to logistics and tactical information.

Two of the people said the intrusion came to light in the past month or so, while the system was being upgraded. Reuters could not establish who was behind it, how far it reached or whether any data was stolen.

“A breach here is a big deal,” one of the people told Reuters.

The report adds to a run of recent breaches at US government bodies, as this week, hackers hit a system used by several spy agencies, and in October a Homeland Security leak exposed 41,000 staff.

Since the discovery, work to move the Air Force’s back-office applications onto Cloud One has been paused, according to two of the sources and a fourth person. Updates to applications already running there have also been blocked, leaving staff unable to install fixes for newly disclosed flaws. A fifth person said some users lost their administrator rights.

Another source said the hackers used a software flaw to reach the control plane, the layer that manages the whole platform, and compared that access to a universal remote. In theory, it could show the status of military aircraft or the movement of spare parts, but it is unclear whether the hackers used it that way.

The Air Force launched Cloud One in 2017 as a single home for its IT, built on cloud services from Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle and Google. Scores of applications run on it, covering pay, personnel, logistics and maintenance.

Among them is a command-and-control system that combines air defence radar feeds from across North America, Reuters reported. Another tracks bombs and missiles at more than 200 Air Force sites worldwide.

SAIC ran the platform as prime contractor until Leidos took over under a $454.9M task order awarded in December 2025. According to one source, US Cyber Command has been brought in to gauge the damage, and Cyber Command did not respond either.

“[The Air Force] does not comment on the security posture, vulnerabilities or operational status of specific networks and systems,” the service said in a statement to Reuters.
