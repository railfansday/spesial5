Barnsley make Huddersfield Town ticket announcement and it's caused backlash

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Barnsley have confirmed that adult tickets for their game against Huddersfield Town next month will cost £35.

The Tykes have had a tough season so far, with Daniel Stendel’s return to Oakwell not producing the results that many had hoped, as the team sit in the relegation zone.

However, many fans recognise that Barnsley’s problems run deeper right now, and there are calls for the owners to leave the club.

Whilst the criticism towards those at the top of the club is wide-ranging, one aspect that has left fans annoyed is the price it costs to watch games.

And, that has ignited again, with the club confirming that adult tickets for the home clash against Huddersfield on December 19 will be £35.

This was always going to be a game that was priced higher than most, as there is a rivalry between the two Yorkshire clubs - and it’s worth noting it’s the same pricing structure as the one they went with against Sheffield Wednesday.

Plus, with Huddersfield flying high, they are likely to shift a lot of tickets, so Barnsley, who will be charging the visitors £35 as well, will see this as a chance to make money.

Firstly, it should be said that problems with ticket prices are not exclusive to Barnsley, and fans of clubs up and down the country would want their own clubs to review prices.

But, in this instance, you can understand why Barnsley fans are furious, as £35 is an awful lot of money to watch a League One fixture.

Barnsley will be wincing at the latest news out of League One involving Bradford and a potential takeover development mirroring Sheffield Wednesday's.

Yes, it’s a game against a local side, but Barnsley are battling to stay in the third tier right now, so they should be doing all they can to bring supporters back, which isn’t going to happen when you’re charging £35.

Plus, this is a game that will be played a week before Christmas, at a time when money is tight for everyone. When you combine that with the fixture being televised, it really is a poor decision from Barnsley, who are prioritising money over fans.

Again, they’re by no means the only club to do that, but it’s a shame that they aren’t going the other way and trying to do affordable deals to entice supporters back.

Of course, the hierarchy will argue that they need to bring in funds to help the club, but it’s always about striking the right balance and making fans feel valued.
