Kevin De Bruyne insists potential punishment over Man City's guilty verdict will not tarnish his old club's achievements under Pep Guardiola - or diminish his 'love' for the owners

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Published: 16:01 EST, 23 November 2026 | Updated: 16:04 EST, 23 November 2026

Manchester City legend Kevin De Bruyne insisted that any potential punishment for breaching financial rules will not change what Pep Guardiola’s team achieved and their memories of success.

The Belgian was a key pillar during the spell of unprecedented domestic dominance and has a statue commissioned outside the Etihad Stadium.

De Bruyne is back in town with Napoli for Tuesday’s Champions League tie, reiterating his ‘love’ for City and its owners, while revealing recent talks with chairman Khaldoon Al Mubarak.

The 35-year-old brushed off suggestions that silverware collected during his decade at City would be tarnished if the club lost their appeal and were sanctioned after being found guilty of the vast majority of charges levelled at them.

De Bruyne’s trophy haul – six Premier League titles, one Champions League, two FA Cups and five Carabao Cups – were largely secured outside of the nine-year period in which City were scrutinised, up to 2018.

‘I’m proud of what I've done as a football player,’ De Bruyne said. ‘Honestly, this is my club, I love the owners. I've talked about a lot of stuff outside football (with them), I've even spoken to Khaldoon about how to buy a club and everything.

‘It (stripping of titles) still doesn't change (anything), my football has not changed. I don't think a lot of people will change in their mind how they view me as a player, or how we played.

‘But everybody has his own opinion and other people may be disgraced a bit. I can't change anything about that.’

De Bruyne’s final two seasons at City were plagued by injury before he joined Napoli on a free transfer in 2025 and his first return in last year’s Champions League lasted just 26 minutes as he hobbled off.

Classed as the greatest player in the club’s modern history, De Bruyne was typically forthright on the uncertainty surrounding his former employers after the guilty verdicts read by a three-person independent commission.

‘My feelings have never been different,’ he added. ‘Honestly, the way that I feel is just the way that I've been treated by this club, what I've done with this club for me personally, and how we've worked together as a group.

‘Of course, it's a lot of things that have been going on. I have nothing but affection for this club, and that will never change. Even now, the people who treated me and my family for 10 years is the most important thing.’

De Bruyne left City under duress, vehemently disagreeing with the club’s decision not to offer him a new contract following a spate of injuries before choosing Napoli – and his long-term future in Serie A is now appearing in doubt.

All eyes in Italy were on the midfielder’s public response to an interview published with his wife, Michele, in which she was reported to have criticised life in Naples and yearned for a move away. Those comments were taken out of context, according to a visibly frustrated De Bruyne, with PSV Eindhoven looking at securing a transfer in January.

But City supporters will have been more interested in how far De Bruyne went in backing their club ahead of next month’s appeal hearing.

‘In life there are rules, but everybody plays to the limit, I would say,’ he claimed, seemingly referring to how financial rules are interpreted by the Premier League’s elite clubs.

‘I played in between the white lines, and I competed with other players, and I had many experiences with my teams.

‘So for me that's the thing that I value most. The most important thing is the relationships between everybody that I had and how you manage these things, and I've given my all.’

De Bruyne smirked as he discussed how he knew exactly how City would attack Napoli given his knowledge of the club and manager Enzo Maresca, who was Guardiola’s assistant during the 2023 Treble campaign.

Maresca is facing criticism after three defeats and a draw in their last four league games. The Italian called for calm over recent performances and believes that the transition from Guardiola was always going to represent a bumpy road.

‘It's like a reset, a restart, after 10 years with the same manager, same face, same words, same method,’ Maresca said.

‘It's not easy to all of a sudden change. For me personally, it's all normal, and hopefully as soon as possible we can find the right balance in all the lines - defensive, midfield - and we can be consistent, which for me is the difference between us and other clubs.’
