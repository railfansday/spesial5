The Biggest Snubs and Surprises From the 2027 Grammy Nominations

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The 2027 Grammy nominations are here, and not everyone in the music industry is celebrating.

Recording Academy CEO Harvey Mason Jr. and board of trustees chairperson Dr. Chelsey Green woke up bright and early on Monday, November 16, to reveal the nominees for music’s biggest night.

With help from a star-studded crop of past honorees including Carly Pearce, David Guetta, Kehlani and Olivia Dean, fans learned Raye received the most nominations with 11.

Other musicians, however, were disappointed to learn they didn’t receive any love from the Recording Academy.

“Life goes on,” Noah Kahan wrote via X on Monday, seemingly referencing his one nomination for The Great Divide.

Keep reading to see the biggest snubs and surprises, and make sure to watch the 2027 Grammy Awards on ABC February 7, 2027, from Los Angeles’ Crypto.com Arena.

Styles kicked off a successful year in March when he released Kiss All the Time. Disco, Occasionally. The album featured massive hits, including “Aperture” and “American Girls.”

Ultimately, it wasn’t enough for the former One Direction member to receive a nomination. At least he still has his Album of the Year trophy for Harry’s House.

After Kahan’s latest album, The Great Divide, reached No. 1 on the Billboard 200, many fans were expecting the artist to receive multiple nominations.

Ultimately, Kahan only received a nod in the Best Contemporary Folk Album category. “Life goes on,” he wrote via X hours after the nominations were revealed.

It was a year to remember for the country singer, who released her debut album, earned a massive hit with “Boston” and was favored to earn a Best New Artist nomination.

While she may not be receiving a Grammy trophy in early 2027, she is joining Morgan Wallen as a supporting act on his massive Last Time I Checked Tour stadium run.

Despite a massive sold-out arena tour, Summit did not receive any love from the Recording Academy on nomination day.

“Never submitting to the grammys again unless s*** changes, it’s all a f***ing joke,” the DJ wrote via X on Monday. “I’m not just talking about myself either. There’s zero respect for real electronic dance music & dj’s. the whole dance category is just music industry fueled pop stars.”

In a separate post, Summit wrote, “Not saying the artists nominated aren’t good, they’re just not dance music, they’re pop. No dj is playing that music in the clubs.”

The former Best New Artist nominee failed to snag any nominations for her latest album, Daughter From Hell, which included her single “Hit the Wall.”

Abrams still has a reason to celebrate: The body of work was her first No. 1 album on the Billboard 200.

After a nearly two-year hiatus, West scored a nomination for Best Rap Album thanks to Bully. Fans can now prepare themselves for another possible must-see red carpet look with the artist and his wife, Bianca Censori.

Madonna earned her first Grammy nominations in nearly 16 years. Her categories include Best Pop Duo/Group Performance for “Bring Your Love” with Sabrina Carpenter and Best Dance/Electronic Album for Confessions II.

It’s also the most nominations the Queen of Pop has received for a single album since 1999. However, many fans were disappointed to see Confessions II was not up for Album of the Year.

After years of hard work, Tinashe woke up to the news that she scored her first Grammy nominations. Her categories include Best Dance/Electronic Recording for “Melatonin” and Best Dance Pop Recording for “Too Easy.”

“From underrated to GRAMMY NOMINATED bitchhhhhhh omgggggggggggggggg 😭😭😭😭😭😭,” she wrote via her Instagram.

The country icon is still being celebrated after her death in August at the age of 80. Parton received three posthumous nominations, including Best Country Duo/Group Performance for “Here” with Zac Brown Band and “Light of a Clear Blue Morning” with Lainey Wilson, Miley Cyrus, Queen Latifah and Reba McEntire.
