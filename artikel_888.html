Familiarity of Doha drives home just how far Socceroos have come in recent years | Joey Lynch

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

There is increasing evidence of an evolution not only in approach but also mentality, highlighted by a return to the scene of Asian Cup heartbreak

Last week’s 3-0 dismissal of Qatar at the Abdullah bin Khalifa Stadium marked the first time the Socceroos had returned to the Gulf state since their heartbreaking elimination from the Asian Cup just under three years ago. Back then, coach Graham Arnold called Doha a “home away from home” after they relocated there during the Covid pandemic, and went so far as to suggest that they would have some form of advantage at the 2022 World Cup as a result.

Wandering around the city in 2026, memories rush back of Socceroos fans becoming eager participants in the takeover of the city’s souks during the World Cup. So too the feelings of sadness to see the birds still cooped up in small cages at the nearby bird market. It evokes recollections of trips on the red Metro line out to the Al Janoub Stadium in Al Wakrah, aided by the faded posters and stickers celebrating the 2022 tournament’s arrival that to this day can still be seen in shopfronts. There’s the excitement, for some, when a stray cat reminds you just how personable the local felines can be, as well as the sense of unease that remains whenever one pauses to reflect on how all this was built and the hands that did so.

But for all the familiarity and continuity for the Socceroos – five players who were there when Son Heung-min broke Australian hearts featured in Friday’s win – being back in Doha rams home just how much has changed for this team, and how well and truly into a new era we are.

Tony Popovic occupies the dugout these days, after the defeat to Korea arguably represented the beginning of the end of Arnold’s tenure by sparking a fatigue that would culminate in a downturn in results and his resignation later that year.

Nestory Irankunda, the rapidly emerging star of Popovic’s team who now has eight international goals to his name, was about to suffer a 4-0 defeat against the Central Coast Mariners nearly three years ago. Alessandro Circati was left out of the Socceroos squad to ensure he retained his place in the Parma side seeking to secure promotion to Serie A. Paul Okon-Engstler, Patrick Beach and Lucas Herrington were all yet to play senior football.

These players, who all started against Qatar, are emerging cornerstones of a new Socceroos setup that fielded the World Cup’s youngest side in North America, before flashing signs of further growth against Brazil in September. On Friday, this talented group bullied the two-time defending Asian champions on their own turf in the first-half, putting the game to bed before Popovic swung the changes and things got disjointed after half-time.

There is increasing evidence of an evolution not only in approach but also mentality. Of players bringing to the national team the lessons they’ve learned not just in the green and gold, but also in their experiences at bigger, more demanding clubs and leagues – in part because of the platform they’ve been offered by Popovic.

All of which could make it serendipitous if Tuesday’s game against Jordan, at the symbolic home of his predecessor, proves to be the end of the Popovic era.

The players aren’t going anywhere but with his contract situation still up in the air, it could well prove to be Popovic’s last game in charge of the Socceroos if he doesn’t agree to an extension. Given the postponement of January’s Asian Cup, there may not be the send-off he might have envisioned. Instead, a 12,000-seat stadium – unlikely to be very full – will host a hastily relocated friendly that’s entire raison d’être has vanished.

Remarkably, after sensitive discussions to relocate Tuesday’s game after player resistance to travelling to Amman, in line with DFAT advice, Doha could once again be pressed into action to serve as a historic backdrop for the Socceroos, just as a pandemic already saw it become the site of many a moment of distinction.
