The Next Bond Girl: From Zendaya to Florence Pugh, 19 Actresses Who Could Join Robert Pattinson

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The name is Pattinson, Robert Pattinson, but who will be his first Bond Girl?

Amazon MGM Studios has made it official, naming the 40-year-old Brit the next James Bond in the exclusive franchise, with Denis Villeneuve helming the next film from a script by Steven Knight and Amy Pascal and David Heyman producing.

But 007 is only as good as the women standing next to him, and the franchise has included some memorable (as well as some less memorable) selections over the years. Some of those the classics include Ursula Andress’ Honey Ryder in “Dr. No” (1962), Diana Rigg’s Teresa di Vicenza in “On Her Majesty’s Secret Service” (1969) and Honor Blackman’s Pussy Galore in “Goldfinger” (1964), as well as more recent standouts such as Michelle Yeoh’s Wai Lin in “Tomorrow Never Dies” (1997), Eva Green’s Vesper Lynd in “Casino Royale” (2006) and the most recent, Léa Seydoux, who was Madeleine Swann in both “Spectre” and “No Time To Die” (2021).

The Bond girl (should it evolve to Bond Woman?) is a staple of the franchise. When James Bond was created by author Ian Fleming, many speculated that Fleming’s lover, Muriel Wright, may have been an inspiration for the female characters.

Villeneuve gives us serious reason for optimism, as he has helped shepherd powerful roles for female characters such as Amy Adams in “Arrival,” Emily Blunt in “Sicario” and Rebecca Ferguson and Zendaya in the “Dune” franchise.

Now that the tux has a new owner, the fun part can begin.

Variety lists the best potential Bond girls we’d like to see in the Pattinson iteration.

Few actors have built a stronger case for a license to kill in 2026 than Adria Arjona. The Puerto Rico-born star plays a battle-hardened Army sniper in “Onslaught,” Adam Wingard’s A24 actioner, joins James Gunn’s DC Universe as Maxima in “Man of Tomorrow” and plays an FBI agent chasing Michael B. Jordan in Amazon MGM’s “The Thomas Crown Affair.” That last one is practically an audition for the studio. After proving in “Hit Man” that she can seduce and outwit a leading man in the same scene, she’s the femme fatale this franchise has been missing.

Villeneuve has already shown what Emily Blunt can do with a gun. “Sicario” made her FBI agent Kate Macer the moral center of a brutal cartel thriller, and she’s heading back into that morally murky world for “Sicario 3.” Add “Edge of Tomorrow,” the “A Quiet Place” films and Steven Spielberg’s alien thriller “Disclosure Day” and the Oscar nominee has more genre mileage than almost anyone on this list. Monica Bellucci proved in “Spectre” that a Bond woman doesn’t need to be 25. Make Blunt the seasoned MI6 veteran who has watched too many 007s come and go.

Gemma Chan is technically already part of the Bond universe. In the video game “007 First Light,” she plays Dr. Selina Tan, a brilliant academic with a background in psychology and game theory whose role at MI6 is critical in Bond’s journey to earning 00 status. Now make it canon on the big screen. The British star of “Crazy Rich Asians,” “Eternals” and “The Creator” has the cool authority this franchise runs on, whether she’s behind a desk at Vauxhall Cross or across a baccarat table in Macau. She’d make a natural new M or the next Vesper.

Bond has a long history of casting French actors as both seductive allies and adversaries. So who better to bring a fresh French intrigue to 007’s world than Oscar-winning Marion Cotillard? The French star has already mastered seduction and deception in Robert Zemeckis’ spy thriller “Allied,” opposite Brad Pitt and proved she can hold her own in a blockbuster like Christopher Nolan’s “Inception,” starring alongside Leonardo DiCaprio. Cotillard and Robert Pattinson previously missed the chance to share the screen 15 years ago after Cotillard dropped out of “Cosmopolis.” At 51, she would also bring a welcome twist to the franchise’s tradition of casting younger love interests.

Chase Infiniti is named after Nicole Kidman’s “Batman Forever” character, Chase Meridian, so pairing her with the current Bruce Wayne practically writes itself. The case goes deeper than trivia, though. The former kickboxing trainer stood her ground against Leonardo DiCaprio in her film debut, “One Battle After Another” and earned a BAFTA nomination for leading actress along with a Golden Globe nod. She has called performing in a major action film a lifelong dream. Make her a rookie MI6 operative or, better yet, the agent sent to replace him.

A “Downton Abbey” alum, James has credits across film, TV and theater and playing a Bond Girl would be the major career breakthrough to skyrocket her career. She has shown off her singing chops in “Cinderella” and “Mamma Mia 2,” warmed hearts as love interests in “Baby Driver” and “Yesterday,” then subsequently broke those hearts in “The Iron Claw.” James Bond would be her first big-budget franchise, and James seems ready for it.

Gugu Mbatha-Raw has done British espionage before, with early guest spots on the BBC’s spy drama “Spooks.” Since then, the Oxford-born actor has shown she can play intellect and menace with equal conviction. Her credits include the title role in “Belle,” in addition to the TVA villain Ravonna Renslayer in Disney+’s Marvel series “Loki” and Apple TV’s psychological thriller “Surface,” which she also produced. Imagine that poise turned against 007.

Just like Pattinson started out with “Harry Potter” and “Twilight,” Merced had her Hollywood coming-of-age in movies like “Transformers” and “Dora the Explorer.” Then she started kicking xenomorph ass in “Alien: Romulus” and killing clickers in “The Last of Us.” Most recently, she debuted as the high-flying, mace-wielding hero Hawkgirl in “Superman” and she’ll be back in next year’s “Man of Tomorrow.” Though she’s 15 years younger than Pattinson, Merced’s star is on the rise and she has experience from several major franchises.

Every Bond era needs a discovery, and Inde Navarrette is the breakout of the year. She spent years working on The CW’s superhero drama “Superman & Lois” and Netflix’s teen drama “13 Reasons Why,” then turned the horror hit “Obsession” into a star-making showcase. Hollywood noticed. She has since landed the role of Rogue in Marvel’s “X-Men,” and she’ll star in and executive produce Apple’s sci-fi thriller “The Waffle House Index.” Cast her as a young field agent with something to prove, and she could do for this era what Léa Seydoux did for Daniel Craig’s.

Lupita Nyong’o was already in an epic with Pattinson earlier this year with “The Odyssey.” The Oscar-winning actress from “12 Years a Slave” (2013) also has spy experience. Her Nakia in “Black Panther” is a Wakandan undercover operative, and in “The 355″ she played a former MI6 computer specialist. She deserves a Bond role with that same double edge as an ally whose loyalty 007 the audience can never quite read.

Pugh has fight choreography down after playing Black Widow Yelena Belova in the Marvel Cinematic Universe across several movies, so she could go toe-to-toe with Bond. Playing a Bond girl who can hold her own in a fight with a supervillain’s henchmen seems like a perfect role for Pugh. And she’ll share the screen with Pattinson in “Dune: Part 3,” so they already have a head start on their chemistry.

Qualley put herself on the Bond map when she danced on stage at the Academy Awards for the telecast’s tribute to the franchise in 2025. Just watching her performance, clad in a bright red dress with tuxedoed men, it was impossible not to imagine her tangoing with Bond or waltzing at a black-tie gala in a 007 movie. A trained dancer, Qualley will show off her moves as Adele Astaire in the Fred Astaire biopic and she’ll get a taste of the spy life in next year’s “Possession” remake.

Margot Robbie has already played the con artist in “Focus,” the scene-stealer in “The Wolf of Wall Street” and the agent of chaos Harley Quinn. A Bond film would be the logical next heist. She’s fresh off playing Catherine Earnshaw opposite Jacob Elordi’s Heathcliff in “Wuthering Heights.” An untitled “Ocean’s Eleven” prequel is in development with her name attached, so she’s clearly in a caper state of mind. Picture her as a high-society villain with a twinkle in her eye and a blade in her clutch.

Sam Mendes directed “Skyfall” and “Spectre,” and he’s now cast Anna Sawai as Yoko Ono in his Beatles films. On FX’s historical drama “Shōgun,” she became the first Japanese actress to win the Emmy for lead drama actress but her action credentials go back to “Ninja Assassin” and “F9.” Sawai can convey loyalty and menace along with some heartbreak in a single look. Isn’t that the entire job description of a great Bond woman?

Not only would Hunter Schafer make history as the first trans Bond girl, she’d be a damn good one. Schafer has proven her acting skills over and over in “Euphoria” and is about to level up as one of the stars of the “Blade Runner 2099” series, which will undoubtedly have some intense action scenes. Yet she’s still at the phase where scoring a Bond role could completely catapult her career. We could totally see Schafer and Pattinson sharing flirty banter before going head-to-head against some baddies — and just imagine the looks on that press tour!

As a blonde bombshell, Sydney Sweeney would be a Bond Girl no-brainer — and she just might have the action chops to match. Sweeney is a natural athlete — she trained for months and put on 30 pounds to play a fierce boxer in “Christy” — but still hasn’t starred in a true action movie unless you count Marvel flop “Madame Web.” This could be the perfect chance for her to flex that muscle. It remains to be seen if she could match Pattinson’s zany personality — but with her Jeff Bezos connection (Sweeney attended his wedding last year and he invested in her lingerie brand), she’s likely to be at the top of Amazon’s list.

Like Pugh, Taylor-Joy is another “Dune: Part 3” leading lady who is sure to attract some Bond Girl fan castings after people see her starring alongside Pattinson. She’s had some stunt training after starring as Imperator Furiosa in the “Mad Max: Fury Road” prequel, and she’ll anchor a major franchise next year with “The Lord of the Rings: The Hunt for Gollum.” And who wouldn’t want a high-stakes chess showdown between her and Bond, calling back to her “Queen’s Gambit” breakout role?

Turner is already a major TV star thanks to “Game of Thrones,” but that hasn’t quite translated to the big screen. Playing a Bond Girl could change all that. She’s proven she can deliver a wordy monologue and give an icy stare after surviving Westeros politics in “Game of Thrones,” and she’s trained extensively to star in Prime Video’s “Tomb Raider” series as explorer Lara Croft. The Amazon MGM connection between the series and Bond is already there, giving Turner an easy jump to the spy world.

Nobody has spent more screen time with Robert Pattinson this year than Zendaya. They played Emma and Charlie, an engaged couple, in A24’s “The Drama” before reuniting in Christopher Nolan’s “The Odyssey,” where Zendaya plays Athena. By the end of the year, they’ll be together with the future 007 auteur Denis Villeneuve in his epic conclusion, “Dune: Part Three.” With three films of proven chemistry, plus a director who already knows how to make her the most magnetic presence on a desert-swept screen, give her a morally slippery MI6 asset who’s never fully on Bond’s side and let the two-time Emmy winner keep everyone guessing.
