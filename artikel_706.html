Iordanescu ready for Watford 'challenge'

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Edward Iordandescu is the first Romanian to manage in the top four tiers of English football

New Watford boss Edward Iordanescu says he has inherited a "challenge" but also "an opportunity" as the club seeks to pull away from the Championship relegation zone.

The 48-year-old has replaced Alessio Dionisi, who was sacked on Sunday after only five months in charge, following a run of seven defeats in 10 league games.

Iordanescu became Watford's sixth new boss in less than 18 months and is the 25th manager since the Pozzo family took over the club in 2012.

He will take charge of the Hornets for the first time on Saturday when they travel to bottom club Derby County.

The Romanian met the media for the first time on Friday, and set out his vision for his tenure.

The Watford job is Iordanescu's first since leaving Legia Warsaw in October 2025, having won the Polish Super Cup with them last season.

It is the 10th managerial post of his 15-year coaching career, which has taken in Bulgarian club CSKA Sofia as well as Steaua Bucharest and Cluj - who he guided to the Romanian title and two domestic cups after joining midway through the 2020-21 season.

He also spent two and a half years in charge of Romania, guiding them to the last 16 of Euro 2024, where they were beaten by the Netherlands.

However, in his first job in English football, he inherits a Watford side three points above the drop zone and facing a fight for their second-tier status.

"I'm proud more than anything else and I'm honoured and happy to be here, but I'm also very aware of the challenge in front of me," Iordanescu told BBC Three Counties Radio.

"I don't want to make promises. I know the fans are not happy in this moment, but also I know that my words are not enough to bring them back, so they wait for facts. We need that, but of course there is a lot of work to do, there is a process in front of us and I hope we make the next steps right away.

"For me it's a challenge but at the same time it's an opportunity and I want to transform this opportunity into something good for the club, for the fans. This is the most important [thing]."

Prior to the dismissal of Dionisi, former Watford player Tom Ince - who left the club at the end of the 2025-26 season - told the BBC's 72+ Podcast there was "so much control away from the manager" at Vicarage Road.

While Ince, now at League Two side Tranmere Rovers, added his respect of positives of chairman Gino Pozzo's practises, he is not alone in speaking out about the board's influence.

Former England boss Sam Allardyce stated he turned down the Watford job in 2019 after claiming he was told how the side should play and which players should be in the XI.

But Iordandescu revealed that is not his expectation.

"I know my borders and I know my responsibilities." he said. "I'm in this club with a lot of quality people, professionals, people that know, understand, in detail, football, but starting yesterday I'm the number one in the technical side.

"I don't believe there's somebody here in the club that can overcome the coach, taking the technical knowledge, so it will be my responsibility, but also the technical part will be my decision all the time."

Pioneer or coach-eater? The complex world of Pozzo's Watford

From a purely statistical perspective, Watford's laboured start to the season under Dionisi could be credited to one key deficiency.

The Hornets have scored the fewest goals in the Championship, with only 13 in their 17 matches so far.

However, Iordanescu said his primary objective was not to thrill, but to win.

He said: "I always prefer to win 1-0 than 4-2. One-nil means you have an organised team, a team with some responsibility to engage 100% and to give everything for the result.

"So for me the priority is not to score more goals - [it is] to start to be organised, to start to act and react together on the pitch, to start to understand that the mindset should be a little bit different than what it was until now and to start to be efficient.

"In time we will see about scoring more goals, definitely every coach wants the team to score as much as possible."

Iordanescu also appeared to confirm he was in talks about taking over while Dionisi was still in charge.

It was reported on 12 November that the Hertfordshire club were in talks with him, but Dionisi, who had only been appointed in June, remained in charge across the international break and for Saturday's 4-0 defeat at Sheffield United.

The Italian was sacked on Sunday, with Iordanescu appointed on Wednesday.

Iordanescu said he had "asked for time" from the Watford hierachy before accepting the role.

He said: "I wanted to understand very clearly the objectives, the targets, so when I gave the final response, I was aware of everything.

"I'm here, I'm enthusiastic, I'm positive, I'm confident we can do good things together, and the thing which I count most on is the potential of this very young team. One of the youngest teams in the league."

But Iordanescu also took time to pay tribute to his predecessor.

"He [Dionisi] definitely worked hard," said Iordanescu.

"He did and left good things, very good things, already I saw many of them. But in football when things don't run the way you want and when the results are not backing up your work, it's difficult.

"I know he is a coach that put a lot of heart in his work and thank him for the good things that he did, and also tried to achieve here."

The task at hand now for Iordanescu is to succeed where Dionisi, and so many other Hornets bosses since their Premier League relegation in 2022, could not.
