White Noise Machines Worthy of Your Nightstand in 2026: Snooz, Hatch, and More

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

I have chronic insomnia. I need a white noise machine, a sleep mask, a pitch-black room, and maybe some light tranquilizers just to fall asleep. (I'm joking, sort of.) If I'm traveling and forget my sound machine, I have to play white noise from my perpetually dying phone. Whether you're shopping for your kiddo or yourself, a white noise machine can help everyone sleep better at night.

These devices are simple, even as companies keep adding bells and whistles. They now come in all shapes and sizes, with options for playing different noise frequencies and soothing nature sounds—whether you need one at home or a portable model for life on the go. And they’re not just for kids or babies; if, like me, you want to quiet your inner monologue and finally drift off, this guide is for you.

We've tested everything: the best sleep trackers, sunrise alarm clocks, mattresses, and extreme alarm clocks. Check out all of the WIRED Reviews team's sleep coverage for our recommendations.

Note: If you're buying for a child or a baby, keep sound machines to no more than 50 decibels and farther than 200 centimeters (6.5 feet) from where they sleep.

“Colors” of noise are measured similarly to light. Pure white noise is a mix of all the frequencies humans can hear, so while these machines are often called “white noise" machines, white noise itself isn't always a relaxing sound (think of the static on your TV). Pink and brown noise are at the lower end of the spectrum, aren't as grating, and have been found to help people sleep a little more deeply. If you've seen pink noise, brown noise, and green noise amid all the white noise machine chatter, it's because, more often than not, you'll find those options bundled into one machine. If you think of sound waves as being loosely analogous to light waves, then the different colors of noise refer to different parts of the sound spectrum. The fun thing about using a sound machine for sleep is that you get to experiment with these noise frequencies to find what works best for you.

When I first get a sound machine, I note how clear the directions are, and the difficulty and time it takes to set up the gadget. I test each of these machines every night for at least one week of nightly testing (I often test them for longer).

During testing, I'm asking these questions and looking for these standard features:

Volume: Is it controlled with buttons or a knob? How easy is it to control the volume, especially when on my nightstand? I make sure to note how quiet and loud it is on each end of the spectrum.

Timers: Many folks like to turn off their sound machine after they've fallen asleep. Does the white noise machine offer preset timers, and if so, does it have several options to customize the time? Does it work accurately?

Sound options: This may be the biggest feature people are looking for. I tend to recommend white noise machines with various sounds, from white noise to nature noises, so that there's an option for every type of snoozer. I try to sleep with at least one sound per night during testing, and note if any have strange patterns. (Although I love the Homedics pick above, I tend to focus on the nature sounds, as its white noise option has an imperceptible beeping in the background that only I seem to hear.)

Other special features: Some of the best WIRED-tested sound machines on this list also double as a sleep tracker or sunrise alarm clock! If you have limited space or budget, it's ideal to get a machine that can do multiple things for you. Conversely, I love that the Snooz option above is analog and only uses a real fan machine to generate noise, so I note that for folks who want to pare down their number of bedside gadgets.

Can White Noise Machines Typically Run All Night?

If you plan on keeping your white noise machine playing sound on a loop all night, make sure first that it has the capacity to do so. Some machines run on 30-, 60-, or 90-minute timers that auto-shut off, while others are continuous.

Does a Fan Make a Good White Noise Machine?

In a pinch, a desk or box fan can stand in for a white noise machine. It provides steady noise to help you fall asleep and can also keep hot sleepers comfortable. But if you’d rather not cool the room or want a wider range of sounds, a sound machine is the better choice.
