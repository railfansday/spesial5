Te Araroa: several zeros in a row

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

When you’ve centred your whole next several months on walking it becomes really hard to stop. I knew I needed some real rest days to get my ankle back to a functional state, and I needed to wait for a physio appointment. But, man, do you ever start to go crazy when you just stop. I dried my tent (it was spread out for DAYS), scrubbed as much Raetea mud off my gear as I possibly could, washed my pot and cup and all my bottles, I back-flushed my filter and did laundry, made a plan for the next sections and resupplies, caught up with my day-one mates and then… nothing, there was nothing else to do.

The perch where I iced my ankle for four days

Unfortunately after one and a half rest days my ankle was still stiff and sore, but every day it improved slightly.

I spent my unexpected zeroes with the absolute kindest trail angels who let me stay in a self-contained house on their property, gave me eggs and beers and fried up some chorizo to add to my leftover pizza. It was not all bad. In fact, it would have been sort of luxurious if not for the stress of trying to heal an injury. I watched all the nostalgic DVDs (looking at you Amanda Bynes movies) in the house and ate as much fresh produce as I could. I tried to enjoy myself but five days is like 100 when all you want is to be out there walking.

The next section was going to be easier, lots more road walking but less intense terrain. I was looking forward to being around hikers again.

I made my way to Paihia via the Waitangi treaty grounds. This is where the country of New Zealand was essentially born (though it’s much more complicated than that). I had been there years ago but missed the museum portion so wanted to take this opportunity to visit it. It was great! Especially as someone whose doctorate is in museum studies.

With the all-clear from the physio and armed with a fresh roll of k tape and a jar of tiger balm I started walking again. Two short days (about 16km each) to warm me back up into walking. I’m so happy to be back on trail!
