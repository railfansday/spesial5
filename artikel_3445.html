Stephen King's Terrifying 8-Episode HBO Series Takes Less Than a Day To Binge

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

After the completion of Stephen King’s IT duology, no one was asking for a follow-up series. HBO’s IT: Welcome to Derry seemingly came out of nowhere, a show that depicts the 1950s Pennywise cycle in the cursed town of Derry, Maine. Director Andy Muschietti returned to the stage in a creative capacity, proving everyone wrong. If anything, Welcome to Derry is a more terrifying prospect when it comes to the alien clown that eats children.

In only eight episodes, Welcome to Derry goes to terrifying depths that the feature films could not. The series follows a new cast of young characters who face issues specific to the times they live in. Lily Bainbridge (Clara Stack) falls in with a close-knit group of friends after a mutual acquaintance disappears. Lily suffers from the death of her father and the threat of returning to Juniper Hill, an insane asylum prevalent in King’s stories. Though Pennywise (Bill Skarsgård) doesn’t show itself in clown form until well into the series, it still delivers some of the most horrifying sequences that may not have made it to feature film form.

It: Chapter One and It: Chapter Two stuck close to the literal plot points of Stephen King’s celebrated work, but HBO’s series exudes the terror and context missing from the movies. Using the real estate of long-form television storytelling to its benefit, IT: Welcome to Derry pulls the rug out from under the audience in the first episode. Lily and her comrades aren’t the Losers Club, but instead far more tragic.

While trying to figure out the mystery of their missing friend Matty, Lily and Ronnie (Amanda Christine) are drawn to a movie theater where the rest of their friends are eviscerated. IT: Welcome to Derry turns out not to be the show that it was first pretending to be. The girls attempt to combat racism as Ronnie’s father, the movie projectionist, is blamed for the deaths of the children.

IT: Welcome to Derry also brings in components of King’s world with the character of Dick Hallorann (Chris Chalk), made famous from his integral role in The Shining. Unlike the difficult adaptation of the Stanley Kubrick version, the HBO series pays homage to the events of King’s world. The military uses Dick’s ability to “shine” as a way to find what they call “the entity.” All of these paths converge to finally reveal the dancing clown that terrorizes the children decades later in the IT feature film.

IT: Welcome to Derry is brilliant in how it holds back the scares until the last possible moment. Fans are going into the series expecting to see Pennywise in earnest. They get it – more than they bargained for, probably – but only when the time comes. In just eight episodes, the series answers the question of whether viewers really needed another IT venture. The answer is a resounding yes. The narrative takes the elements that didn’t work for the movies and explores them for true fans of horror. Welcome to Derry is a love letter to King in a way that wasn’t quite accomplished with Hulu’s horror series, Castle Rock. Instead, it delivers a short first season that is easily digested in one day.
