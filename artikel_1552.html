Player Ratings: Olympiacos 2-4 AC Milan – Hutchinson unplayable; Pulisic class

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

AC Milan completed a crucial comeback as they scored four goals against Olympiacos in the second half. One man in particular was behind the Rossoneri’s resurgence and below are the player ratings.

49 minutes into the game, it looked like Ruben Amorim and his men were heading for another disappointing defeat in the Europa League. Then, Omari Hutchinson decided enough was enough and inspired his teammates to a massive comeback.

🥇 Hutchinson (9.5 – MOTM): Absolutely unstoppable in that second half. I mean, WOW! It’s not like a tap-in brought him to life either. He took matters into his own hands and scored an amazing solo goal, starting in Milan’s own half. Then the two assists on top of that, and the hockey assist for the fourth. Again, wow!

👎 De Winter (4.5): He has to take full responsibility for the second goal. It was truly a horrible pass and he then kept the goalscorer onside due to poor positioning. I actually don’t think he was good with his passing in the first half either, so the rating feels deserved.

👔 Amorim (7): I think he deserves a bit of credit as Milan’s first half was far from bad. They looked a lot more switched on compared to previous Europa League outings, and the goals could have been avoided through more player effort. Then, he made all the right substitutions.

Maignan (6): He made a great save in the first half to deny Martins. Some have suggested that he could have come out to parry the corner away on the first goal, but I don’t entirely agree. Anyway, he couldn’t do much about the second goal and was okay with his feet.

Gila (7): He was his usual aggressive self and that helped Milan on several occasions. Perhaps more so than his ability to win the ball back, however, it was his clearances that meant the most as he stopped some very good crosses. He also had a great overlapping run before the Rossoneri’s second goal.

De Winter (4.5): He has to take full responsibility for the second goal. It was truly a horrible pass and he then kept the goalscorer onside due to poor positioning. I actually don’t think he was good with his passing in the first half either, so the rating feels deserved.

Diawara (5): Touch and judgment. Those are the two things that let him down tonight. He put himself in too many tough positions due to the touch, and his judgment didn’t help when it came to anticipating the passes over the top. Just a really poor display, sadly.

Estupinan (5.5): He wasn’t terrible, but not good either. A rather quiet performance from him when you think about it, so it wasn’t enough for a pass here.

Jashari (6): It was a good opportunity for him and he sort of took it, I guess? It was very much a decent display overall. He had that great pass for Cisse that nearly led to a goal and also helped well in the build-up. However, he had a few misplaced passes and perhaps could have done more to stop Pirola in his tracks before the first goal.

Rabiot (6): In fairness to him, he did have a couple of important clearances defensively but in attack, he definitely didn’t add as much as we have seen in recent weeks. The goal contributions streak had to come to an end at some point. Still, overall, it was an okay performance.

Moreira (7): The goal obviously helps his rating a lot, especially since he remained composed, but he also had a couple of really nice switches to Hutchinson on the other side. He was a tad wasteful in some situations, in my opinion, but a decent display plus a goal deserves a good rating.

Hutchinson (9.5 – MOTM): Absolutely unstoppable in that second half. I mean, WOW! It’s not like a tap-in brought him to life either. He took matters into his own hands and scored an amazing solo goal, starting in Milan’s own half. Then the two assists on top of that, and the hockey assist for the fourth. Again, wow!

Cisse (7.5): Another goal for the youngster! I thought he did well at left-wing, nearly scoring that goal despite the goalkeeper’s challenge, but he continued to do well at centre-forward. The movement and header on the goal proved once again that he’s got it.

Ramos (5.5): Similarly to Estupinan, as both were taken off at half-time, it was more a quiet performance than it was a really bad one. That said, the No.9 had a couple of moments on the counter that could have been handled better.

Musah (6.5): It’s hard to put my finger on exactly what he changed, but he brought something to the game which helped Milan play better. Could absolutely be his movement as he covered a lot of ground and wasn’t afraid to drive forward either.

Pulisic (8.5): World class is probably the best way to describe him. You could immediately see the difference he was going to make, although perhaps no one predicted that goal. It was a tremendous strike to complete the turnaround for Milan. Tremendous! And then he got the assist for Moreira. We can forgive the miss just before that.

Tomori (6.5): Almost a 7 for him, but it’s possible that I might be influenced by just how poor De Winter and Diawara were. Anyway, the Englishman returned with a strong performance and showed that he’s one to rely on.

Terracciano (6): He’s the master of always opting for the easy way out, but in this case, that was probably exactly what Milan needed.

Amorim (7): I think he deserves a bit of credit as Milan’s first half was far from bad. They looked a lot more switched on compared to previous Europa League outings, and the goals could have been avoided through more player effort. Then, he made all the right substitutions.

We lost today because we failed to do our assignment on Omari Hutchinson.

Even in the second half, for some time, we managed it.

Hutchinson started taking responsibility.

And suddenly our defensive structure was changing because everybody was worried about him.

We actually removed two players from marking him because we wanted to protect the space around him.

He got the better of Onyemachi, scored the first goal and suddenly Milan believed again.

I was angry with my players because we allowed him to get into those situations.

When you are 2–0 up at home against AC Milan, you cannot allow one player to turn the entire match around.

There is very little you can do when you have a winger running at you with that much confidence.

He is small, he is close to the ground, his balance is excellent and he changes direction very quickly.

That makes him extremely difficult to defend.

If you go to ground, you risk catching his leg instead of the ball and getting a yellow card.

If you don’t go to ground, he pushes the ball past you and runs.

So the defender has to make a decision in one second.

Our defenders decided to give him the space.

And once he realised they were backing away from him, he became even more confident.

That is when you know you have a problem.

Because a winger like that doesn’t need five chances.

Give him one successful dribble and suddenly he believes he can beat everybody.

We went from controlling the game to defending like we were frightened of one player.

We should have made somebody stay close to him instead of allowing him to receive the ball and face our defence.

But football punishes you when you hesitate.

We went from a perfect win to a perfect defeat in fifteen minutes.

Because I don’t think we were the worse team.

I don’t think our football suddenly disappeared.

We simply failed to deal with one player when the game became chaotic.

I told him, ‘You have a very good player there.’

Because if Hutchinson learns how to control games like that consistently, not just moments, then Milan have something very special.
