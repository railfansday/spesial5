Team Themes: How to Divvy Up the Tasks (or Not!)

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Editor’s Note: Team Themes is a periodic news feature where we ask team leaders for detailed information about how they structure and organize their teams and how they achieve success for all members.

While you might think standard operating procedure for real estate team leaders is to operate under rigid weekly calendars, those in the field are proving there is no single blueprint for success.

In fact, a closer look at three mid-sized real estate teams reveals a sharp divide: Some leaders outright reject the traditional top-down weekly schedule entirely, opting instead for individual accountability or tech-driven workflows, while another takes a more traditional approach.

With each finding different ways to fuel growth, we asked three top team leaders with six to 26 agents to pull back the curtain on their operations and share their process for divvying up the daily tasks.

Do you and your team have a set weekly schedule for business-building tasks? Why or why not?

For example, do you have set days and/or times or team members who call clients and prospects, post to social media, work on lead-gen, marketing, etc.? How are the various responsibilities of the team divided? Are any set activities scheduled for certain days of the week?

John Turner, Team Lead, Turner Victory Team at Onward Real Estate Number of team members: 12 agents Region served: Murfreesboro, Tennessee https://turnervictory.com/about-murfreesboro-real-estate-team

The short answer is no. We do not run a rigid weekly schedule for business-building tasks across the whole team. Here’s why.

Years ago, we held weekly team training. Over time, we realized our brokerage already offers extensive training, so we were mostly repeating what they provide. Now we only schedule training when we spot a specific area that needs work. That keeps training useful and keeps everyone’s time focused on what matters.

Most of our team consists of experienced agents, and many have been with us for 10 years or more. They know how to do this job. What we found was that the skill was there, but the consistency was not. A group schedule was not going to fix that, so we moved to monthly one-on-one meetings with each agent. In those meetings we talk about their goals, where they are today and what habits will keep them moving forward. Each agent builds their own routine for calling past clients and staying in touch with their sphere, and we supportively hold them accountable.

The other key piece is access. Every agent on our team knows that Tracy (my wife and co-team leader) and I are easy to reach. They can come to us with a question, ask for advice or just brainstorm an idea. Sometimes the best training occurs when the agent is actively involved in a transaction and runs into an issue. Therefore, it’s important they know they have a trusted mentor who can help them walk through that deal.

For team leaders building a team, my advice is to match your structure to the people you have. A newer team may need a tight daily schedule. A seasoned team often needs accountability and an available mentor.

Chris Colgan, Founder and CEO, Colgan Team Number of members: 26 agents Region served: Northern Virginia and Metro D.C. https://www.colganteam.com/

Monday through Friday, we have a morning huddle from 9 a.m. to 9:30 a.m. I check in on everybody’s calls, what their plan is for the day, and what’s happening in the market in real-time. We talk about what’s positive, sometimes watch a motivational video, and work through role-playing or real situations agents are dealing with.

I’m still in the thick of it, selling just like they are. So when I talk about a buyer getting cold feet, an inspection negotiation or a listing that isn’t getting showings, it’s not just something I read about. It’s something I’m dealing with, too.

Every Tuesday, we also have a required in-office meeting from 11 a.m. to 1 p.m.

Outside of those meetings, I expect agents to block 90 minutes every weekday for calls and follow-up. Start with your hot leads and new leads. Talk to the people who are on the fence and figure out what’s keeping them from moving forward. Our expectation is 10 real conversations a day, lasting at least two minutes, tracked in Brivity or Follow Up Boss.

I tell them, “If you don’t have 90 minutes a day to call people, you need to ask yourself whether you’re making time for this business.” Saturday and Sunday, you’re out showing houses and doing open houses. You need to be having those conversations during the week.

We also use an AI assistant that emails each agent their top 10 leads to call that morning. It points them toward training through PLACE or Real based on where they need help. Maybe they’re struggling with scripts. Maybe they need help running an open house. The idea is to give them direction, not just tell them to work harder.

On the marketing side, I handle most of it because I genuinely love it. YouTube, Instagram, Google, listing marketing—that’s where I spend a lot of my time. We put out three YouTube videos a week, which is a lot of work. I’d estimate we’ve had around 40 to 50 closings from YouTube and Instagram this year.

But agents still need to build their own visibility. I tell them to post three valuable things on Facebook every day: being out with a client, showing a house, giving useful real estate advice. Your friends and family are already there. Let them see that you’re actually doing the work.

I also tell agents they should be doing three open houses a week in this market. Does every agent do that? No. I wish they did. We use QR codes for sign-ins and track the activity, but getting someone to sign in is only the beginning. Some agents do the open house and then don’t follow up properly. I do follow up, and it’s a huge lead source. You can’t put in the time to meet all those buyers and then never call them.

We have transaction coordinators, listing coordination and support staff who help agents manage their databases and stay on top of things in Slack. Our operations manager handles onboarding and offboarding. We’re constantly recruiting, but we’re also honest when someone isn’t a fit. You can be a good person and still not want to work the way our team expects.

The hardest part isn’t putting a schedule together. It’s getting people to follow it consistently. We track the activity, check in and have direct conversations when someone isn’t doing the work. Does everybody execute perfectly every week? No. That’s something we’re always working on.

I keep telling the team: “This is not a dabbler market. It’s the full immersion season.” That means making the calls, following up, showing up to meetings with your camera on, being out with clients and doing the activities even when you don’t feel like it. The schedule gives us a way to turn that expectation into something people can actually follow.

Phil Sexton, Founder, HomeSmart NEXT Team Number of team members: Six, with four agents Region served: Scottsdale, Arizona https://nextrealestate.team/ A. No. NEXT Team members assign weekly tasks to us, not the other way around. HomeSmart NEXT is not a classic “Last Name Team” that the industry is used to. We’re the nerds in the back office. We’re like a CTO and CMO for our agents, allowing them to be the most technologically advanced real estate marketers in the industry…without having to build the tech. Most agents want to leverage AI, but they don’t want to become a techno-nerd. Our dashboard is growing with the AI tools to choose from for marketing, prospecting, advertising and operations. If they learn about something new or cool in a class, they ask us for it, and we build it. It really is an amazing time to be in business.
