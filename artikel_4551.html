This Saab-Affiliated EV Program Never Got Off the Ground. Now, It Could All Be Yours

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Prototypes, engineering plans, and the other remnants of NEVS’s Emily GT electric vehicle project are all heading to auction.

So, you think you can run an electric car company? Well, here’s your chance to prove it. An entire electric vehicle development project is heading to auction this month, and it comes with almost everything you need to get started. There's even a connection to one of the automotive industry's most beloved defunct brands: Saab.

As first brought to our attention by Great Britain’s Car, Swedish auction company Klaravik is preparing to sell the entire NEVS Emily GT project. The auction includes four complete prototype vehicles, a fifth development mule, spare body panels, parts, tools, and engineering drawings. And unlike a typical automotive auction, where you might leave with a single vehicle, the winning bidder gets the entire collection.

A little backstory is helpful here. After Saab Automobile closed its doors in 2012 following an ill-fated tie-up with Spyker, a Chinese consortium called National Electric Vehicle Sweden (NEVS) acquired the majority of the remaining assets, including the Trollhättan factory, manufacturing equipment and intellectual property. However, NEVS was not interested in building a next-generation Saab 9-3 Aero. Instead, they had their sights set on building an electric car future.

Their vision of that was called the Emily GT, envisioned as a sophisticated electric grand touring sedan capable of competing with established luxury EVs. Development began in late 2019, and just 10 months later, the Emily GT rolled out into view. And much like Saabs of the past, the Emily GT had some unique features—the most obvious being its four electric motors, one mounted inside each wheel. Combined, they produced a healthy 490 horsepower.

However, while the power output was healthy, the financial condition of the company was not. NEVS never secured the funding necessary to bring the car into production. The ambitious project remained stuck in the prototype stage, where it’s stayed frozen .

So, do you think you’re the right person to bring it to market? If so—or even if you’d rather just own a very odd automotive dead-end—Klaravik will open online bidding on November 25, with the auction scheduled to conclude December 9. All four completed vehicles and the accompanying development materials are being offered together rather than individually.

Of course, buying the prototypes and engineering materials doesn't automatically give someone a functioning car company or guarantee a path to production. Manufacturing, regulatory approvals, and financing would still present enormous challenges. However, if you do happen to be an ambitious entrepreneur with deep pockets, this could be your chance to revive an electric vehicle that never had the chance to see the light of day.
