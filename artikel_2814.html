Former Bears guard, 8-year NFL veteran, dies at 73

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Revie Sorey, a former Chicago Bears offensive guard who spent eight seasons helping open running lanes for future Hall of Famer Walter Payton before opening opportunities for Chicago-area youth in retirement, died Thursday, Nov. 26. He was 73.

Sorey was born Sept. 10, 1953, in Brooklyn, and grew up in the Bedford-Stuyvesant neighborhood. He attended Brooklyn Boys High School, earning five varsity letters in football, basketball and track. He also became an Eagle Scout.

At the University of Illinois, Sorey played offensive guard from 1972-74 and was a two-time All-Big Ten selection. He served as the Illini's offensive captain in 1974 and was selected to play in the Senior Bowl, Blue-Gray Game and American Bowl.

The Bears selected Sorey in the fifth round (110th overall) of the 1975 NFL Draft. He played his entire professional career in Chicago, appearing in 109 games (77 starts) from 1975-83. He earned All-NFC recognition, including first-team honors in 1977, and was a regular on the offensive line that blocked for Payton.

After football, Sorey turned his attention to community service. He earned a master's degree in social work from Dominican University and became education director at Ada S. McKinley Community Services, helping provide scholarships and educational opportunities to disadvantaged students.

He also established the Revie Sorey Trunk Scholarship Program with Anita Efron, supplying hundreds of first-generation college students with essential items for college. For 27 years, he and Efron operated the Revie Sorey All-Pro Football Camp, mentoring young athletes on and off the field.

In 2010, Sorey receieved Illinois' Monsignor Edward J. Duncan Award for exemplary integrity and dedication through public service and citzenship.

A stroke in March 2012 left him unable to walk or speak, although he eventually regained limited speech through therapy. His wife, Suzy O'Malley-Sorey, died in 2011, and his son, Trace, died in an accidental drowning in 2016.
