Opinion: Hey, That Hellraiser: Revival Game Is Pretty Good

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

For as much as I love the horror genre, I've never seen a Hellraiser film. If they're as fun to watch as Hellraiser: Revival is to play, though, then maybe I need to catch up.

Since we've got The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time and GTA 6 right around the corner (I'm now playing the former), I haven't wanted to start a big game that requires a serious time investment. With a 10-hour playthrough for the Platinum Trophy (which I did earn), this new Hellraiser game seemed like the perfect fit.

This is a straightforward, mostly linear survival horror game that gets in and out without overstaying its welcome. It shows touches of flair and ambition at some points, with light takes on the more open-ended Resident Evil approach to exploration, providing a little area to progress through. Those moments are cool, and in between them is action, puzzle-solving, horror, sex, and the Labyrinth.

You know me by now: I love survival horror and all the typical trappings of the genre. When it wants to — a few times during the campaign — Hellraiser: Revival does them adequately enough. It's no Resident Evil or Silent Hill killer, so what I've loved most about the game are the factors you'd stereotypically associate with the IP: chains, Pinhead, telekinesis, and the Labyrinth.

Main character Aidan and his girlfriend Sunny decide to use a Genesis Configuration cube during sex, but activating the thing calls upon Pinhead and his cronies, interrupting the intimacy (of which there is a lot). Sunny is taken hostage and Aidan has to fight back through guns, melee weapons, and the Genesis Configuration.

This elaborate puzzle box grants him telekinesis powers, and later, the ability to wield fireballs.

The mechanic is a standout, and presents a welcome alternative to stabbing and blasting guys and gals in gimp masks.

You can summon saw blades, spikes, and sharp debris and throw them at zombies and cultists. If you see a brazier, you can steal its fire and lob it at enemies. With the blaze it creates, you can create more fireballs from the burnt remains. It is a neat little system that I thoroughly enjoyed, and would love to see expanded should there ever be a sequel.

My favourite sequences overall, though, took place in the Labyrinth. In this alternate realm, you face spike traps, moving saws, contorting paths and staircases, and demons like the Chatterer that chase you. Also requiring you to move blocks with your telekinesis powers under a time pressure, these sequences are genuinely intense. You can feel the Chatterer stalking you as its teeth, well, chatter.

Is Hellraiser: Revival a hidden gem that should be claiming Game of the Year nominations? No.

Viewing the Metacritic page for a gauge of its critical reception at launch, I think an overall rating of 69 to 76, depending on what platform you're playing on, is bang on. If I were reviewing Hellraiser: Revival, with my critic cap on, I don't think I could score it any higher than a 7/10.

However, I've been playing Hellraiser: Revival for fun rather than my job, and that's exactly what I've had: fun.

I want to see more from this universe; I hope Saber Interactive gives the green light to a sequel.

In the meantime, I'll go and catch up on those movies.
