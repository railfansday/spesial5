Pulborough football club fears over future of social club as council explores replacement plans

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The parish council said it has been ‘open and transparent’ throughout the process of ‘exploring options for the future of the Pulborough Sports & Social Club building.

It said the project ‘remains at an early stage’, and ‘no final decisions have been made’ regarding the detailed design or layout of any replacement facility.

The club's members say the building is about far more than somewhere to have a drink after a match – it is a place where children make friends, parents get to know one another and volunteers help keep village football going.

Matt Parry, who has been involved with Pulborough football for around 30 years as a player and now as a parent and youth coach, said the club had become an important part of family life.

He said the youth section had grown to six teams, with players competing in local leagues and cups against clubs from much larger towns.

Matt said the club tried to keep costs down so that children could take part without financial barriers, adding that friendships formed through football often extended beyond the pitch.

After matches, parents and players regularly head into the clubhouse, while events bring the different age groups together.

A special family fun day last weekend celebrated 128 years of the youth football club. Matt said parents came into the building after the matches to socialise and take part in a fundraising raffle.

“We would not have been able to do that without the building at all,” he said.

Tom Hayes, who helped establish the club's youth football provision, also stressed the importance of the social club's support.

The youth section began with just four players and has since grown to six teams. Tom said the social club had helped from the outset, providing equipment and supporting the teams with kit sponsorship and presentation events.

He believes losing the facilities would mean losing an important part of the experience for children and their families, particularly those who value having an affordable place to meet after playing.

Natalie Smiley, who has been the football club secretary for around 17 years and also chairs the social club, said the two organisations were closely connected.

She warned: “If the social club goes, the football club will go with it.”

Natalie said the club had expanded considerably during her time involved, with six youth teams now playing alongside the men's side.

She said the work was carried out by volunteers, who gave up their own time to organise matches, prepare pitches and keep the club running. The social club also provides a base for other activities, including darts, pool and snooker.

The family day on Sunday, November 22, brought teams, families and supporters together. Natalie estimated that more than 400 people attended, while a raffle raised nearly £400 for football club funds.

Oliver Last, clerk and responsible financial officer, issued a statement on behalf of the parish council.

He said the council’s aim was to modernise the facilities rather than remove them, describing the existing building as ageing and no longer meeting modern standards.

He said: “The council's objective is not to remove facilities from the village, but to improve and modernise them.”

The statement added that the building ‘no longer meets modern standards’ for accessibility, safeguarding, energy efficiency or flexible community use. The council had been exploring whether to ‘substantially renovate or replace it for a number of years’.

The current concept being considered is a new sports and community hub, which the council says would continue to support sport while offering facilities for wider community use.

It said early proposals included modern changing facilities, social and hospitality space overlooking the recreation ground, community rooms, a youth area, storage for sports clubs, and ‘flexible spaces that can support a range of activities and community projects’.

The council also sought to address concerns about the future of the bar and social facilities, stating: “The aspiration includes a café/bar and social space and is not currently being considered to be used as parish council offices.”

It said the plans had developed since earlier proposals, following more recent consultation about local needs.

“More recent community consultation indicated strong support for a modern hub that combines sports facilities with wider community services,” the statement read.

The council added that sports clubs and community areas were identified as the most important activities for the hub, while respondents also supported social areas, café facilities, youth provision, community events and support services.

The council said it recognised Pulborough Football Club's contribution to the village and wanted the new facility to improve, rather than reduce, sports provision.

“The intention of the project is to strengthen and enhance sports provision, not diminish it,” Mr Last said.

He added that the proposed hub would provide ‘improved sports facilities, modern changing accommodation and enhanced social spaces’ that can ‘continue to support clubs, players, families and spectators’.

During the transition from the existing building to a new facility, the council said it would provide temporary changing facilities.

These would include separate male and female changing rooms, toilets and showers.

The council said the football club and other long-term users had been identified as key stakeholders, and that it remained committed to ensuring future facilities met their needs.

The condition of the existing building is another point of disagreement.

Mr Parry said there had been uncertainty about the proposals for years, and questioned whether money earmarked for a new building could instead be used to improve the existing facilities.

He said the building was still being used by the community, despite problems including leaks, and questioned why it could not be repaired and given a new roof.

The council acknowledged that users and volunteers had contributed to improvements over the years, but said it remained responsible for the building as its owner.

“The council remains responsible for the building as the owner and has continued to maintain and manage the facility while exploring its long-term future,” the statement said.

It added that ‘significant investment is required’ and warned that continuing to fund repairs to an ageing structure could become increasingly difficult.

The council said this could divert resources from a longer-term solution it believes would better serve the community.

The council said it had already undertaken a public consultation, which received responses from residents, many of whom were existing users of the sports and social club.

It said: “This feedback is helping to shape the brief for the project. Community engagement will continue as designs develop and before any final decisions are taken.”

Mr Last acknowledged the strength of feeling surrounding the building, saying the council recognised it was ‘far more than simply a bar’ and was an ‘important social venue and a valuable part of village life’.

He said the council wanted any future development to build on that heritage while providing a modern, accessible and financially sustainable facility for the future.

“The ambition is to create somewhere that local clubs and residents actively want to use and are proud to call their community hub,” he said.
