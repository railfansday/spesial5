Peonies are pretty, we know, but peony-like tulips give you way more petals for far less effort, and you can plant the spring-flowering bulbs in November.

We all adore the richly-colored, ruffled petals of peonies but if you’ve only got one plant in your garden, the show’s over in little more than a week. Plant some peony-like tulip bulbs, however, and you can enjoy months of very similar-looking flowers from early April all through May and sometimes even into June. These double tulips couldn’t be easier to grow, and this month is the exact right time to plant the bulbs.

Most tulips have six petals but a natural genetic hiccup capitalized upon by plant breeders means that some cultivars can have up to 50 packed into a single bloom. They’re known as double tulips, and their petal-explosion flowers look remarkably similar to peony blooms. Typically hardy in zones 3-8, they’re some of the best tulip varieties to kickstart spring in your garden, and the most striking spring-flowering bulbs to plant in November.

The trick to enjoying months of flowers is to pick a pair of tulip varieties, one that opens early and one that blooms a little later. Early-flowering double tulips (officially known as peony-like tulips) begin blooming early in April. These tend to have shorter, sturdy 12-16 inch-high stems so stand up well to spring storms. Their tight layers of petals protect the flower core from cold weather, too, so each bloom can last for around 3 weeks – far longer than a standard tulip.

Late-flowering double tulips (known as peony-flowered tulips) bloom late April through May and are even more spectacular with huge flower heads on taller 16-24 inch-high stems. Because they're so top-heavy, these are more vulnerable to toppling in strong winds so in an exposed garden, give them a sheltered spot.

So, if you love peony flowers and you're wondering what to plant in November, look no further. Part of the Weekend Wins series, planting double tulips that look like peonies is one of those easy garden jobs that brings a big reward.

When shopping for double tulips, it's important that bulbs are big enough to fuel all those petals so early in the year, so always buy from a quality supplier. All our recommendations are for 10 bulbs.

‘Lorenzo’ gets spring off to a cheery start with long-lasting blooms in a luminous apricot-orange softly edged in creamy yellow, all through April.

With enormous blooms opening in late April and peaking through May, ‘Gudoshnik Double' has painterly petals streaked in yellow, orange and red.

A few double tulips are fragranced, and 'Avant Garde’ opens its buds in early April to release a sweet, floral perfume with beautifully fresh undertones.

In late April, ‘Pistache’ brings more creamy-white petals, this time splashed with wine red, and a wonderful scent of almonds and pistachios into May.

‘Foxy Foxtrot’ wakes up early in April, opening a lovely lemon yellow then gaining rich, warm tones of apricot, salmon-pink and honeyed gold.

In mid-to-late April, ‘Amazing Grace’ starts in a soft lavender pink with green accents, then shade-shifts into a deep rose tone that lasts deep into May.

In early April, ‘Royal Acres’ blooms open into bowls of intensely-colored, velvety magenta petals, often with a silvery sheen along the ruffled edges.

‘Dream Touch’ takes over the show from late April through May with enormous purple blooms, their striking petals prettily edged in white.

Though officially classified as a late tulip, ‘Mariage’ blooms in mid-April with deeply ruffled flowers in the prettiest pale rose-pink, and lasts into May.

‘Wild Romance’ adds creamy white petals with blushing pink picotee edges from late April through May, opening to reveal dramatic jet-black stamens.

‘Marshmallow’ opens in early April with a splash of bi-colored raspberry pink and ivory white petals, with a few green streaks for good measure.

Keeping the drama going, ‘Black Hero’, blooms from late April through May with ruffles of velvety deep-maroon petals that have a super sheen.

Growing spectacular double tulip flowers from fall-planted bulbs couldn’t be easier, and November is the exact right time to get them in the ground.
