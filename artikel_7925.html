10 “Must Do” Home Projects for November

For many of us, November marks the beginning of a season filled with house guests, nonstop holiday cooking, and cozy nights indoors. It’s also the last chance for cold-region dwellers to finish last-minute outdoor maintenance and garden projects. Get ready for whatever this month brings by taking on these timely tasks.

Installing a programmable thermostat puts savings on automatic: It’s another task you won’t have to remember to do yourself. The U.S. Department of Energy estimates that homeowners can save about 10 percent on their heating costs by turning their thermostats down 7 to 10 degrees for eight hours a day. If you work outside the home, automatically setting your thermostat to a lower temperature while you’re gone should be a no-brainer. Nest learns quickly when you’re home (and want the heat on) and when you’re away (and don’t), and builds an ideal winter thermostat schedule accordingly.

If your home is on a septic system, this month is prime time to give it a little TLC. Dropping a septic tank treatment product into your toilet is an easy, effective DIY. We also like getting our septic tanks pumped in November, to ensure that there are no issues (remember that scene in Meet the Parents?) when we’re hosting company and our toilets are working overtime. In some areas, septic waste disposal fees increase on January 1, which gives you added incentive to take care of the task before the New Year.

Americans are getting ready to put their ovens and stovetops to work this month. Here’s an important reminder as you start your holiday prep: More cooking fires occur on Thanksgiving than on any other day of the year. Make sure you’re ready for whatever comes your way by keeping a fire extinguisher close at hand the kitchen, and letting the cooks know where it’s kept. Be sure to check the pressure of your current units, and replace old or damaged fire extinguishers.

There’s some debate over when to hang the Christmas lights, but many of us waste no time and flip the switch on Thanksgiving weekend. One of the best reasons to hang lights in November is that the (slightly) warmer weather makes this outside job easier to do. Whenever you choose to light up the yard, check all cords for fraying, and dispose of any damaged strings. Plug the lights into ground fault circuit interrupters (GFCIs) to reduce the risk of electrical fires, and turn them off before you go to sleep. When they are exposed to extreme temperatures, under- or non-insulated pipes, especially those that run along an exterior wall or an unheated garage, can freeze and cause major problems. To protect your pipes, first ensure that your walls are adequately insulated. You may also consider wrapping specialized heating cables or heating tape around water pipes that run along exterior walls. Heating tape can be plugged into an outlet, which will heat the water in your pipes to prevent freezing and bursting. Avoid installing heating tape inside walls, ceiling, or floors, where it can pose a fire hazard.

Gathering the family around the fireplace is one of the good things about cold weather. Before you strike a match, make sure you have plenty of firewood on hand for the season. Seasoned hardwood, like oak or ash, is generally the best wood for burning in the fireplace.

If you’ll be playing host to friends and family this season, now’s the time to get the guest bedroom in good shape. A fresh coat of paint or new bedding can make the space more inviting and won’t break the bank. Don’t have a spare bedroom? Alternative guest bed ideas, like cots, air mattresses, or pull-out sofas, can quickly turn any room into a bedroom.

Given the extra time you and your family will be spending in the kitchen through the end of the year, odds are higher that your countertops will see more spills, stains, and scratches than usual. The best ways to protect these surfaces depend on their composition: While materials like quartz and laminate only need regular cleaning with an all-purpose spray, countertops made of butcher block, marble, and granite should be sealed in order to resist stains (and warping, in the case of butcher block).

Once your countertops are protected, move on to cleaning your cutting boards: Plastic boards can be sanitized in the dishwasher; clean wood cutting boards with vinegar or, if stained, salt and half a lemon. Condition wood cutting boards with a food-safe mineral oil.

Your water heater is probably installed in an unheated environment, like a basement or a garage. Insulating your appliance is an absolute must to prevent it from losing heat. Choose an insulating water heater blanket that’s the right size for your tank, and follow the manufacturer’s instructions for installation. This quick task could save you 4 to 9 percent in water heating costs, and pay for itself in about a year.

Your kitchen will likely be getting a lot of use over the Thanksgiving holiday, which is why now is the time to minimize the risk of grease fires. Make sure to clean your stove drip pans and the stove top itself; go ahead and clean the oven, too, and whip up a nontoxic, fume-free mix of baking soda, water, and vinegar. Don’t use the self-clean function on your oven right before the big day: Self-cleaning puts strain on your appliance and could result in a maintenance call, or worse.
