Feds Investigating More Than 1 Million Honda SUVs, Vans, and Trucks Over Engine Start-Stop Concerns

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

NHTSA has received hundreds of complaints that Honda’s start-stop feature failed to do its job properly.

Honda’s automatic engine start-stop feature is under new federal scrutiny, after the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration received hundreds of complaints that engines failed to restart automatically from a complete stop at a traffic light on certain models, prompting the agency to commence an investigation.

The federal probe, announced on Friday, November 13, includes 1.03 million units of the 2024-2026 Honda Passport, Odyssey, Pilot, and Ridgeline. The inquiry also includes 2024-2026 Acura MDX units. NHTSA’s Office of Defects Investigation received 701 complaints related to the Auto Start/Stop feature.

“To help improve fuel efficiency, the Auto Start/Stop system will automatically shut off the engine when a vehicle comes to a complete stop. The engine is intended to automatically restart when a driver releases the brake pedal. However, the complaints allege that the engines are failing to restart once they have been shut off by this system,” the investigation announcement reads.

While automatic start-stop systems were created to improve fuel economy, Honda seems to be having a recurring issue with the feature. Federal safety regulators previously opened a probe into Honda’s automatic start-stop system in 2025. That investigation included 2.2 million late-model Honda Pilot, Odyssey, Passport, and Ridgeline models, as well as Acura MDX and TLX units. However, the new investigation includes 2026 model-year units. Inquiry documents allege that the two issue populations have different root causes and respond differently to repairs.

There have been no injuries related to this issue, according to the feds. EPA boss Lee Zeldin said the agency was investigating improvements or eliminating the technology last year; whether it sticks around in the long run, however, an engineering analysis of the stop-start system should bring Honda and NHTSA together to identify an effective solution to this recurring problem soon.
