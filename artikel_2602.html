Game Preview: Knicks vs. Cavaliers, November 25, 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Writing a preview for a Cleveland Cavaliers game can be an interesting task these days. Not because the upcoming Knicks-Cavs matchup on Wednesday is another NBA Cup game (sorry, Mr. Silver), but rather because it can get a bit dicey with your deadline. After meandering through stats and stories, recaps and rosters, you inevitably stumble upon some post on social media. Just a short video clip of a guy with an extensive beard not trying to play defense. As is common with train wrecks, you can't look away and click on it. Oh, the algorithm shows you another one?! Bang! One more, maybe?? You try to restrain your hand, but it still clicks on it. It could go on like that forever.

Watching James Harden play something that supposedly passes for NBA defense in 2026 from afar is almost like a guilty pleasure. Only for a moment, of course, until you realize that you're seeing one of the great scorers of this generation making a mockery of the game you dearly love. Then you breathe a sigh of relief that he isn't on your team, even if you've seen him score 51 points the other night against the Indiana Pacers. But it's all right; his own coach seems to be more than happy with the bearded fellow:

Analytically, he may be, but Lil' Coach K still seems a bit delusional here. Better said, he seems to have skipped the last few sessions of media training. You can't say stuff like that and then watch Harden in a game getting blown by like he is Andrea Bargnani after too many glasses of limoncello. Yes, he might, just might, be a top-ten scorer of all time (I certainly think he isn't), but you have to point that out specifically. By phrasing it the way he did, he opens himself up to major criticism, which is exactly what you don't need when your basketball team is in a funk. Or when some of your own players see what we're seeing.

If you want to know why things aren't really clicking for the Cleveland Cavaliers (9-9), you only have to connect the dots between these two clips. Your coach is praising one of your big-name players as if he is the second coming of Julius Erving, and then your big offseason acquisition is saying that the effort of said star player, among other things, is the reason why your team can't play a lick of defense. Now, he didn't say the name James Harden out loud, but you don't need the guys from "Mindhunter" to know where Watson is going here. He might just as well be talking about Donovan Mitchell, who is nowhere to be found as a leader these days. Either way, the Cavs are in a world of trouble.

You could argue they were before the last game against the Knicks, too, a contest they won and that figured to get them going a little bit. But in the six games since, it has been more of the inconsistent same for Cleveland. Missing Evan Mobley with a toe injury has been a major problem for their defense, as they now start three subpar defenders in Harden, Mitchell, and sharpshooter Sam Merrill. Their point-of-attack D has been nonexistent and looks even worse without Mobley behind it to at least erase a few mistakes by the matadors on the perimeter. Peyton Watson is working as hard as he can on that end (1.8 blocks per game as a wing), but ultimately, it's not enough.

It can't be that bad, can it? Well, without Mobley in their last six games, the Cavs have been 26th in defensive rating in the NBA. In their most recent outing, they allowed 45 second-quarter points to a Chicago Bulls team playing without Caleb Wilson and Norman Powell. If that wasn't embarrassing enough, they topped it off by giving up 63 points to the Windy City's bench mob, a group highlighted by the infamous star power of Jordan Hawkins, Noa Essengue, and Jaylin Sellers—no offense, in more ways than one.

There is a reason, of course, why the Cavs are still at .500, even if they'd make Tom Thibodeau rip out his last Homer Simpson hairs if he had to watch them play defense every night. That reason is their offense, their firepower. Cleveland has a top-five scoring group in the entire NBA; they've got two guys who can explode for half a hundred on any given night and plenty of role players who know how to put the ball in the basket, as the Knicks saw in their last meeting. If the Cavs win, they do it by outscoring teams, mostly in the half-court (Cleveland and New York are the two slowest teams in terms of pace right now), and by letting their individual talent create favorable situations. That can be enough on a lot of nights in the NBA regular season; it sure doesn't look like a recipe tasty enough for a team that went to the conference finals last year and would love to go a step further this time around.

New York isn't sure yet about the status of the wounded OG Anunoby, Deuce McBride, and Jordan Clarkson, which might be a problem in a game where you will need to score some points to keep up. We also know that big stars want to perform on the big MSG stage, so there is that. In the end, though, I'd say the Knicks will surely feel they owe Cleveland one after that last home defeat. Maybe they can even manage to get into one of those clips with the bearded guy. Who knows?
