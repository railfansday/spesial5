Donate a working computer, phone, or tablet to a group that refurbishes it for someone who needs one, but clear it first by backing it up, signing out of your accounts, and resetting it to factory settings. Choose a group that takes that kind of device, and get a receipt if you’ll deduct the gift.

Americans discarded 2.7 million tons of TVs, computers, phones, and other consumer electronics in 2018, and only about 38% of it was recycled, the Environmental Protection Agency estimates.

Wipe each device yourself before it goes, even when the group promises to erase it. Several of the refurbishers below erase every drive they take in, but they can’t sign out of your Apple Account or Google account for you, and a phone still tied to one can’t be set up by its next owner. Goodwill’s stores around Milwaukee and Chicago recycle any phone that still asks for a password.

Then match each device to a group that takes that kind of device. Laptops, phones, and tablets have the most takers, so check a group’s list before you pack a desktop, a monitor, or a TV. A phone new enough to fetch a good price may be worth selling for cash instead.

Back up what you want to keep to a new device, a cloud account, or an external drive, as the Federal Trade Commission suggests. Then reset each device from its own settings.

Take out a phone’s SIM card, which the FTC notes may store personal information, and any memory card too.

If you’ll deduct the gift, keep each charity’s receipt, which under IRS rules describes the device but leaves its value to you.
