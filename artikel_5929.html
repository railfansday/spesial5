Radisson is joining the paid membership trend, but with a twist as it courts local diners, not frequent travelers. The discounts are generous, so the economics have to work hotel by hotel.

Radisson Hotel Group is the latest hotel company to sell paid memberships, and India is its launch market. But unlike programs aimed primarily at hotel guests, Radisson Dining is designed to bring people living nearby into its restaurants more often.

Launching Dec. 1, Radisson Dining offers restaurant and spa benefits to encourage local customers to visit more frequently.

“Our restaurants already attract local residents for family celebrations, business meals and everyday dining,” Nikhil Sharma, managing director and COO, South Asia, told Skift.

“Discussions with owners highlighted the opportunity to build a more consistent relationship with these customers and encourage repeat visits.”

Radisson Hotel Group is launching Radisson Dining in India on December 1, a paid membership aimed at local residents rather than hotel guests. Members get 25% off food and beverages for up to 10 guests, 25% off spa services, and Wednesday Members Day offers with 50% off food and 25% off drinks. The Classic tier costs INR 4,990 ($54) a year nationally, while Signature and Signature Plus are priced by individual hotels, which set their own dining certificate and stay benefits, and come with Premium status in Radisson Rewards. Radisson aims to have more than 100 hotels honoring member benefits in the first year, with around 40 also selling hotel-based subscriptions. Nikhil Sharma, managing director and COO, South Asia, said success will be measured by "repeat visits and incremental contribution rather than headline membership numbers," since hotels are giving up revenue through discounts. Hotels pay no participation fee; TLC DigiTech built the platform, and subscription revenue is shared between hotels and TLC, with terms undisclosed. Radisson is pitching Dining to owners as a demand-generation tool, particularly for 100-room hotels in smaller cities, where more than half of its 225-plus Indian hotels operating or under development are located. Accor has already built a sizeable paid membership business in India, with more than 100,000 Explorer members at INR 23,199 ($250) a year sold through bank partners including Amex and Axis.
