Rockstar Reportedly Shopping Around Red Dead Redemption TV/Movie Rights, But Has Rejected Grand Theft Auto Adaptation Offers

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Grand Theft Auto VI hasn't even been out for a week, and yet it's shattering records left and right, following in the footsteps of its predecessor, Grand Theft Auto V. Despite that, developer Rockstar Games has reportedly continued to reject Hollywood TV and film adaptation offers for the crime saga series, according to a report from The Ankler. However, the studio is reportedly more open to a TV or film adaptation of its cowboy western series, Red Dead Redemption, according to the same report.

The Ankler reports that Rockstar is looking at possible licensing deals for its long-running western shooter series, which received its last entry in 2018, Red Dead Redemption II, for as much as $100 million. There's heavy emphasis on "possible," though, as there is no confirmation that Rockstar has said yes to any of these offers thus far. It's very likely a TV or film adaptation might never come to fruition – it's common for studios to field offers like this, sometimes with serious intent and other times as a quick look at the ocean of possibilities, as it were.

That said, video game adaptations continue to grow more and more popular, especially compared to their humble beginnings in Super Mario Bros. (1993), DOOM, and Mortal Kombat. Franchises like Sega's Sonic the Hedgehog, CD Projekt Red's Cyberpunk 2077, renewed Super Mario Bros. and Mortal Kombat efforts, and others, including Street Fighter, Resident Evil, Fallout, and Castlevania, have found great success on streaming services and on silver screens. Given how beloved the narratives of Rockstar's Red Dead Redemption series are, it's not surprising that production studios are interested in adapting those stories for TV and movie theater screens.

In the meantime, players have plenty to experience from Rockstar via its latest release, Grand Theft Auto VI, which launched on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S last week on November 19.
