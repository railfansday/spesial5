6 Ways to Spend Less This Holiday Season Without Buying Cheaper Gifts

Spending less on the holidays doesn’t mean buying cheaper gifts. Propose a name draw so each adult in your family buys one better gift, then pay less for the things you’d buy anyway, like decorations on clearance and gift cards with no fee.

Say you buy $50 gifts for nine other adults, or $450 in all. Cutting each gift to $35 saves you $135, while drawing names and buying one $75 gift saves you $375. The one person you buy for then gets a $75 gift instead of a $50 one. In Deloitte’s 2026 holiday survey of 4,000 consumers, 47% said they prefer giving one meaningful gift, even at a higher price, over several smaller ones.

A holiday budget sets what the season can cost you, and the moves below make that money go further.

The more adults in your family who swap gifts, the more this saves, and it’s the one move here that needs everyone’s agreement before the shopping starts.

In a big family, buying a gift for every adult adds up fast. Propose a name draw before Thanksgiving instead, where each person draws one name and buys that person a gift under a price cap you all agree on. Everyone gives and gets one gift instead of a stack, and that one can be better.

Gift cards top the list of gifts people want in the National Retail Federation’s 2026 holiday survey, so the saving should come from skipping a purchase fee, never from a smaller amount.

Give a gift card for a store or restaurant the person you’re shopping for already uses. A Visa or Mastercard gift card costs several dollars more than it holds, and its last few dollars are hard to spend. A $50 card bought straight from the store or restaurant costs $50.

For whoever is still on your list after a name draw, an unopened gift you already own is the one present that costs you nothing.

Regifting is a fine way to save money as long as the gift is new, unopened, and something you’d have bought the recipient anyway. Keep a list of who gave you what, and check it before you wrap anything, so a regift never reaches the original giver or anyone close to them.

Nobody can tell what you paid for an ornament or a roll of wrapping paper, which makes decorations the easiest place to pay less for the same things.

Shop for next year’s ornaments, lights, wrapping paper, and cards in the week after Christmas, when holiday decor goes on clearance at 50% off or more and gets cheaper again by early January. Buy only what you’ll use again and have room to store, and you’ll stop paying full price every December.

Read more: Buy Next Year's Decorations, Wrap & Cards After Christmas - For Half Price or Less

Airfare works the way decorations do for anyone flying home, because the same seat costs less when you book it at the right time.

Holiday fares have historically bottomed out about five weeks before Thanksgiving and seven before Christmas, and this year they’re climbing. Set a price alert today, book Thanksgiving by mid-October and Christmas and New Year’s by the end of October, and take the first fare you can live with.

Giving is part of what the season is for, so the goal here is getting more of your money to the cause, not giving less.

Before you buy something because part of the price goes to charity, find out how much the charity gets per purchase. When the share is small, buy only what you’d buy anyway and give to the charity directly. A $10 gift sends 10 times as much as a $40 purchase that donates $1.

Read more: Holiday Shopping for a Cause - Check the Charity's Share Before You Buy

Together, these moves lower what the holidays cost you without making a single gift you give any cheaper. Start with the name draw this week, and send your family one message that sets a price cap and a draw date.
