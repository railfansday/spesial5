Seven wins later, ex-UFC prospect Nick Maximov still waiting for call back — ‘What do I gotta do?’

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Former prospect Nick Maximov was not technically released by Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC) in 2022.

The promotion simply allowed his contract to expire and decided against offering him another one. Nearly four years and seven victories later, that distinction probably does not make the rejection sting any less.

"After my injury, it just seemed like they didn't want to re-sign me," Maximov told MMA Mania recently. "I fought out my contract, so technically I wasn't released. It was like, 'We just don't want to re-sign you.' I was like, 'F—k! Damn, that sucks.' I didn't know what else to do."

Maximov entered UFC as an undefeated prospect from the Nick Diaz Academy. After defeating a much larger Oscar Cota on Dana White's Contender Series, he joined the promotion and scored consecutive victories over Cody Brundage and Punahele Soriano.

Losses to Andre Petroski (watch highlights) and Jacob Malkoun followed, leaving Maximov with a 2-2 UFC record when his four-fight contract expired.

"I feel like I'm way more mature and just better now," Maximov said. "I'm doing whatever I can, but it's just hard to get in."

Getting into UFC once is difficult. Getting back after the promotion has already moved on is considerably harder.

UFC parts ways with dozens and dozens of fighters every year, and most never receive another opportunity. Those who return often need a lengthy winning streak, several finishes, a short-notice opening, and someone behind the scenes willing to push their name at precisely the right moment.

Maximov appears to have completed most of that checklist.

The 28-year-old did not compete for almost two years after his UFC departure because of nagging injuries, but he has remained unbeaten since returning in Aug. 2024. Maximov has recorded seven victories across eight appearances, with one no contest caused by an accidental eye poke mixed into the run.

He has earned two technical knockouts and one submission during that stretch. Most recently, Maximov moved up to Light Heavyweight and defeated Wildemar Santos by unanimous decision in the LFA 243 main event, improving his professional record to 15-2 (1 NC).

"Seven straight," Maximov said. "It's like, bro, what are we doing? That's crazy. I knocked the dude out. I knocked a bunch of other people out, and it's like, I just don't even know."

Maximov said he has occasionally heard from people connected to the promotion, but those conversations have repeatedly led to another requested fight instead of a contract.

"What they kept telling me was, 'The next one,'" Maximov said. "'Did you get a finish?' Then I get a finish, and it's like, 'Oh, I just don't know right now.' What, dude? I'm doing everything on my end. 4Maybe it was my manager's fault at the time. I feel like I need someone to really push for me."

Maximov believes there may also be some internal hesitation because of how he or his team were previously perceived. He did not offer specifics but acknowledged that MMA matchmaking can involve factors beyond wins and losses.

"There are a lot of weird politics with it," Maximov said. "I think that's a little bit of a play, for sure. I don't know if they think a problem will come with what I bring, but I'm a different person, dude. I don't want problems with anybody. I just want to fight and do my thing."

Despite the frustration, Maximov remains focused on returning to the promotion rather than simply finding the highest available offer elsewhere.

"I want to get back to UFC, for sure," Maximov said. "That's the only place I really want to be."

That leaves Maximov in an uncomfortable position. He can continue accepting regional fights and hope another victory finally changes UFC's mind, but every additional training camp brings more risk without any guarantee of receiving the call.

"I'll keep fighting until I get in there," Maximov said. "I just have to keep showing them. It's a weird spot. No one else has been like eight-and-zero since UFC and not gotten signed. I've never heard of that in my life."

"I feel like I'm at that point right now, but it's like, 'F—k, another one.' All right, I guess we just have to buckle up and do it, but it's not really what I want to do by any means."

Maximov is also considering fighting overseas in hopes that a change of scenery — and potentially stronger opposition — might finally create some movement.

"Maybe that might be the next best thing because, obviously, what I'm doing right now is not working," Maximov said. "Even though I'm winning, it feels like it's not working. So, what do I have to do? I'm open to anything, really. It's frustrating as s—t, dude. But I'll keep being positive, and hopefully something good will come from it."

For now, there is only one option left: win an eighth fight and continue to be undeniable.
