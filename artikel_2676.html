Colts release Daniel Jones: Landing spots for QB include Patriots, Ravens, Eagles

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

Indianapolis Colts (3-8) is finally pulling the plug on its struggling quarterback. The Colts are releasing Daniel Jones, the team announced on Friday, just months after Jones re-signed with the Colts on a two-year, $88 million contract with $49.49 million fully guaranteed this past offseason. According to CBS Sports' ex-NFL agent Joel Corry, the Colts will carry a $35.2 million dead cap hit in 2027 from his $44 million signing bonus that was prorated through 2023 with three void years attached to his contract. The team saved $11.6 million in 2027 by releasing him now, since Jones was scheduled to have a $46.8 million salary cap hit next year. The Colts don't have a first-round pick in the 2027 NFL Draft since they traded both their 2026 and 2027 first-round picks to the New York Jets for cornerback Sauce Gardner at the 2025 NFL trade deadline.

Indianapolis' 26-3 faceplant on Thursday Night Football against the Houston Texans marked Jones' sixth straight game with an interception, the longest streak by a Colts player since Curtis Painter was filling in for an injured Peyton Manning in 2011. Per CBS Sports Research, that also made him the first quarterback since Jim Hart in 1979 to start each of his first 11 games of a season and have no games with more passing touchdowns than interceptions. Jones leads the league in turnovers (16), including a career-high 13 interceptions. His 65.4 passer rating is also the worst in the NFL this season.

Jones has earned $166.9 million over his eight-year NFL career strictly from his player contracts. He did so by peaking just in time to get paid, with decent play in the final year of his rookie deal with the New York Giants and his one-year deal with the Colts. With 2026 not being a contract year for Jones, his play has plummeted in his first year back from suffering a torn Achilles that cut short his 2025 campaign.

In a corresponding move, the Colts named 2023 fourth overall pick Anthony Richardson their starting quarterback going forward. He requested a trade this past offseason after Indianapolis prioritized Jones over his own development. Richardson attempted just two passes in 2025 after suffering a freak eye injury in the pregame of Week 6.

When Richardson was last the Colts' starter in 2024, he led the NFL in yards per completion (14.4) while ranking dead last in completion percentage (47.7%). That made him the first player to accomplish the feat since Heath Shuler in 1994 and just the fourth player to do so since the 1970 AFL/NFL merger.

This was a move the Colts had to make because the defense refused to take Jones' arm seriously, which has severely hampered three-time Pro Bowl running back Jonathan Taylor's production. This season, Indianapolis has faced the eighth-lowest rate of light boxes in the league on run plays, according to NFL Next Gen Stats. With Taylor rushing for 21 yards on 11 carries in Week 11, he now has 18 consecutive games without 100-plus rushing yards, the longest drought of his seven-year career. Richardson and his rocket-launcher right arm, which led the NFL in yards per completion (14.4) in 2024, should at least give opposing defenses pause as they consider whether to crowd the line of scrimmage to keep Taylor down.

So where could Jones land next? There are three teams who are uniquely positioned to be in line for Jones' services for the remainder of the 2026 season. Let's take a look at those three teams below.

With a 5-5 record, the defending AFC champion New England Patriots are just one game back of the conference's seventh and final playoff spot currently occupied by the 6-4 Cleveland Browns. Despite consecutive blowout losses against the Green Bay Packers (45-27) and the Detroit Lions (35-10), they are still right in the mix to reach the 2026 postseason. However, face-of-the-franchise quarterback Drake Maye went down in Week 10 against the Lions with what is reportedly a fractured fibula. That injury might sideline him for the rest of the season.

If New England decides it doesn't want to roll with undrafted veteran Tommy DeVito at quarterback, Jones could be a fit with the Patriots. DeVito has just eight NFL career starts while Jones has 93. Offensive coordinator Josh McDaniels could see Jones as a scheme fit after he had another former Patriots assistant, Brian Daboll, as his New York Giants head coach for three seasons.

The Baltimore Ravens are in a similar spot as the Patriots. With a 4-6 record in 2026, they are still in the hunt for the AFC's No. 7 seed, and they're also without their starting quarterback. Two-time NFL MVP Lamar Jackson is on injured reserve after having surgery for a broken and dislocated right thumb on his throwing hand. The Ravens traded for veteran journeyman Jacoby Brissett, but he doesn't appear to be the answer. Brissett was the only quarterback who couldn't light up the struggling Dallas Cowboys or win against the Los Angeles Chargers this season. He was rough (340 yards passing and an interception on 33-of-49 through the air) in a 23-20 Monday Night Football home defeat against LA, giving the Chargers their first win of the season to close out Week 10. It's no surprise he has the lowest rate of explosive passes per dropback in the league (5%), per CBS Sports Research.

Putting Jones out there instead would give the Ravens a quarterback who is four years younger than the 33-year-old Brissett, who is more willing to push the ball downfield.

The Philadelphia Eagles are now 2-7 and on a seven-game losing streak because of their injury-plagued offense. Super Bowl MVP quarterback Jalen Hurts is out for the season with blood clots, while wide receiver DeVonta Smith (hamstring) and running back Saquon Barkley (hamstring) have also dealt with nagging injuries this year. Barkley and Smith have been on the field for just 14% of the Eagles' offensive plays in 2026, down from 54% in 2024 and 67% in 2025.

If the Eagles prefer Jones's 93 career starts of experience over 2023 sixth-round pick Tanner McKee (5 career starts) and 2024 sixth-round pick Joe Milton (1 career start), he could make sense in Philadelphia.
