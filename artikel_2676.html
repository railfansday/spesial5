Kirk Cousins, Vikings reunion could be just getting started

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Quarterback Kirk Cousins will spend the rest of the 2026 season with the Vikings. That may not be the end of Kirko 2.0.

The Vikings have already expressed interest in keeping Cousins beyond the current season, we’re told. As we understand it, the sentiment was articulated casually and in passing before his rousing debut at Lambeau Field. And it was more of an indication that there will be discussions.

Of course, there will be a greater motivation to do something, now that it’s clear that Cousins is exactly what the doctor ordered for an offense that was ready for a visit from Dr. Kevorkian.

Even though Cousins is under contract through 2028, the deal was structured to spark a release after one season, with Cousins getting a $10 million payment on the back end aimed at sticking the Falcons with $8.7 million in 2026.

A base salary in 2028 of $78.655 million becomes fully guaranteed on the fifth day of the 2027 league year, along with a $1.345 million base salary in 2027. Although that works out to $80 million over two seasons (which puts the average closer to the bottom of the veteran quarterback pay scale than the top), the current structure imposes a ridiculously high cash and cap obligation for 2028, when he will be 40.

So the deal will need to be revised. And Cousins will have leverage, if he continues to do what he did on Sunday.

That said, he possibly will realize that it’s not the time to maximize his leverage. He did that in 2024, when he opted for a multi-year financial commitment that he mistakenly viewed to be a multi-year playing commitment. And then the Falcons pulled the rug out from under him by drafting Michael Penix Jr. roughly six weeks after the ink dried on the new deal.

And, frankly, the Vikings could decide to devise a plan for swallowing the proverbial poison pill and paying $40 million per year for two more season. The impact of Cousins’s return on the Minnesota offense would justify trying to devise a solution.

The best result would be for the two sides to work something out, at least for 2027. After 2023, the Vikings wanted to take a year-to-year approach with Cousins. Cousins wanted something more than that — even though the Rams and Matthew Stafford have been using the year-to-year thing for a while. In hindsight, a compromise would have been much better for both sides.

That lesson from the past should inform the future. As one source put it, sometimes it takes a breakup for everyone to appreciate each other.

It’s clear that, one week after Cousins returned to Minnesota, both sides have significant appreciation for each other. Which gives both sides a very strong motivation to find a way to keep it going at least for 2027.

Unless, of course, the Vikings win the Super Bowl this year and Cousins decides to walk off into the sunset with the trophy that will fit in that spot in his house in Michigan he has saved for it.

That would throw the Minnesota quarterback position back into disarray for 2027. But if it comes with the first Super Bowl win in franchise history, the Vikings would take that.
