Run the car you list on Turo like a small rental business. Start from Turo’s recommended prices, raise them for local events and holidays, and discount weekly trips so the calendar stays full. Offer airport delivery, keep the car spotless, and choose a protection plan by the damage costs your savings could cover.

A Turo car earns only on the days a guest has it, and every rental means a handoff and a cleaning.

So what you keep depends on days filled, cleanings needed, and your share of each trip. Say a compact SUV rents for $60 a day, you keep 80%, each cleaning costs $25, and weeklong trips get 15% off. Five two-day trips fill 10 days and leave you $355 after Turo’s 20% and five cleanings. Three weeklong trips fill 21 days and leave about $780 after Turo’s 20% and three cleanings, more than twice the $355.

Turo’s three protection plans, now called earnings plans, pay more of each trip the more damage you’ll cover. The More earnings plan pays 90% and leaves you the first $2,750 of damage per incident. Balanced pays 80% and leaves $1,500, and More peace of mind pays 70% and leaves $250. In nine markets, among them Austin and Seattle, each plan pays 10 percentage points above its usual share on trips booked four or more weeks ahead and 5 below it on those booked within two days.

Pick the highest-paying plan whose per-incident damage you could cover from savings today.
