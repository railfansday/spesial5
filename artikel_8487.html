Te Araroa day 20: Catching up with friendly faces

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

26km, Ruakākā Motor Camp to Waorahi Campsite

After my day off the previous day, I was keen to get walking. The weather was thankfully looking better – the threat of a few showers, perhaps, but nowhere near as bad as the day before. Feeling energetic, I thought I might end up doing a bigger day and pushing all the way through to Mangawhai Heads, 39km away. Alternatively, I could stay at a free campsite in 26 kilometres. I set out at my usual not-especially-early time, and headed to the beach. The day would start with 5km of beach walking before heading inland.

The tide was pretty much all the way in, so I had to walk on soft sand which was pretty annoying. As I walked, I kept getting whiffs of pee. I couldn’t work out where it was coming from, but eventually realised that my right hand smelled of pee and traced the smell back to the strap of my hiking pole. And then I realised what must have happened. You see, one of the great but not often discussed advantages of the Durston X-Mid tent is that the vestibule is large enough to squat and pee in. On a rainy night when you just don’t want to leave your tent, that can be a great advantage. And so it had gone the previous night. I had spent the day drinking fruit juice, eating grapes, and chugging water. I’m sure this was great for my hydration, but it meant that I needed to pee in the night, and it was raining. Presumably, the strap of my hiking pole got caught in the cross-fire and ended up with pee on it. And now it was on my hand. Excellent.

Somewhat perturbed but with not much I could do about it, I continued along the beach. Far in the distance, I could see some little figures that looked like hikers. I couldn’t figure out who they could be though, as there were only two and the hiker trio I had been with the day before would have been unlikely to split up so soon. I continued along my way, and gradually got closer – I’m a pretty speedy walker, and my curiosity motivated me to walk quickly. As I neared them, I started to recognise one of the figures. It looked like they were wearing Annabel’s distinctive flowy shorts, and once I figured that out I was almost certain that one of them was Annabel. And the other person looked like Lindsay to me. Now it made sense – I had kind of forgotten that these two, and a number of others who I had previously been hiking with, had got an AirBnB in Ruakākā for a day off. I would have joined them had I known that I would also be taking a zero the previous day, but since that wasn’t my plan, I had stayed at the campground.

Annabel and Lindsay in the distance – this was when I recognised them, although they were more distinct in real life

Beach walking with Lindsay and Annabel – photo by Sinéad

Happy to see some friends, I put the boosters on and caught up to them fairly quickly. We soon also caught Sinéad, who had been taking a dip in the sea. I explained my ‘pee stick’ problem and we had a good laugh about it. The others threatened to make Pee Sticks my trail name, but I vetoed that pretty quickly! As we were standing around, a man came jogging barefoot down the beach towards us. He stopped by us, asked the now-familiar question (‘are you doing that big walk?’) and asked why we didn’t go barefoot on the beach. We told him that our feet needed more support for long kilometres of beach walking, especially with weight on our backs. He looked unconvinced, but wished us luck and jogged away. To be fair, he could probably walk the length of 90 Mile beach without shoes. But I certainly couldn’t!

It was good to catch up with the others about their day off, and I was having a great time walking with friends again. I realised that I was not going to go all the way to Mangawhai Heads that day – I would much rather hang out with this lot and have a fun time. We had all heard through the grapevine that there was a cafe in Waipu that gave hikers free coffee, so that was our destination for the morning. After the beach, there was a bit of gravel road and then a kilometre or two of main road to walk down to reach Waipu. I was walking with Sinéad and we were chatting away. At one point I looked behind and the others weren’t there. Had we really walked that fast? ‘They probably hitched,’ said Sinéad. Aha. I had forgotten that hitching was a thing – it would never have occurred to me on this short of a road section! But fair enough, there’s no need to walk on busy main roads if you don’t want to.

We got into Waipu, which was surprisingly popping for a small place on a random Wednesday. I made a beeline for the public toilets to wash the pee smell off my hand and gear, then headed to Logan McClean cafe where legend has it, hikers get a free hot drink. The others were already there, along with the hiker trio from my day off. The lady at the counter at the cafe asked if I was walking too, which saved me from the awkwardness of having to say ‘Sooo, I heard you do free drinks for hikers?’ In addition to my coffee, I got a couple of slices. I couldn’t choose and figured I would take one away with me. We sat outside the cafe for a bit, and Sven turned up too. He hadn’t heard that there was free coffee here, so had bought one at another cafe. But then of course he had to make use of the freebie so got another! A few people came up to talk to us, including an older guy who had done the TA a few years ago. The others had various things to buy at the Four Square and the pharmacy, and then we were back on our way.

Apricot crumble and ginger macadamia slices – I thought I would take one of them away. That didn’t happen, shocker

The next section looked to be mostly road walking. Sven (ever the hitching fan) and Lindsay hopped in a car, but me, Annabel and Sinéad decided to give the road a go. I wasn’t keen to hitch at all, as this would already be a shorter day for me and I was full of energy from my day off. Things started out promisingly, with a pavement to walk on through the rest of the town. But soon, the pavement stopped and it looked as though we would be on tarmac. Then I happened to glance across the road and saw a TA sign leading to a bike path. Great! The bike path turned out to be reasonably scenic, and we were happy we chose to walk.

At one point, I had to stop to change my sock. I had washed the sand out of my shoes and socks from the end of the last day, but with the rain they hadn’t had a chance to dry. This morning, I had wanted a dry pair of socks, but after the recent death of a sock I only had 1.5 pairs to work with and decided to use my bed socks. They’re hiking socks anyway, but on TA so far I had been using toe socks. But I figured it was worth a go. Unfortunately, it was becoming apparent that I needed toe socks – the toes on my left foot were rubbing and I could tell I would get blisters. Happily, the remaining sock from the half pair was a left one, so I was able to change that out and keep my right sock as it was. I didn’t think there was anything particularly odd about this, but when the others saw my odd socks at camp later they seemed to think it was an abomination.

My odd socks at camp later – is this really so strange? It was working for me!

After a bit of effort, I caught Annabel and Sinead again after my sock stop. The bike path went on a bit longer before the trail turned up a side road. We had to walk on the road this time, but it was a quiet road, and quite scenic as it wound its way up the hill. At one point it became a gravel road, and we stopped for a lunch break. After a while on the gravel road, which became progressively narrower and more overgrown, we reached the final 2km to the campsite and the first real track of the day! We knew there was some weather due to be coming in later, and as I walked up (and down) the track, I could hear thunder rumbling in the distance. I was keen to get to camp sooner rather than later, but after the day of gentle climbing on the road, the track felt exceedingly steep and hilly, and my calves were definitely not prepared for such a workout.

Eventually, I popped out of the trees and reached the campsite. Having been here before, I remembered the site as nice but not overly flat and a bit exposed. I was happy to see that there was more flat area than I remembered, although the majority of the site was on quite a hill. The views out across the sea were great. Lindsay was pitching her tent as I arrived, and I pitched mine pretty close, concious of the sloping ground and the other two behind me.

It was funny how the surfaced road stopped so abruptly

Sven had decided to continue on to Waipu after his short, hitching-heavy day. It was only 3.30pm at this point and if it hadn’t been for the approaching weather and the good company at this campsite, I might have done the same. As I expressed to the others, I’ve been feeling a bit of tension between wanting the physical challenge of pushing for some bigger days, and wanting the trip to last as long as possible so I can enjoy it as much as possible. I think as I move southwards I’ll naturally end up with some bigger days, especially where there are more camping options. Right now, most places are spaced 20-25km apart, and I don’t feel ready for 40-50km days yet! But at the same time, 25km days on easier terrain, like this day, are feeling easy and I get to camp feeling like I haven’t done enough.

As well as the people I had been walking with that day, Bart and Anica were at the campsite. I hadn’t met Anica before, as apparently she has been skipping around a bit. But she seemed cool and we all sat around the table in the shelter, chatting and cooking dinner as we watched the storm roll in. It was a fun place to sit, as we could see bands of rain moving across the ocean towards us. It seemed like the worst of it missed us, but we did get a few heavy showers over the course of the evening. There was a logbook at that campsite, and it provided some good entertainment over the course of the evening. We made plans to hike out at 7 the next morning, for some reason, and found a trail host in Mangawhai that offered camping for significantly cheaper than the holiday park.
