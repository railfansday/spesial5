Shirley MacLaine at Home: 10 Photos of the Golden Age Great’s Life Behind the Scenes

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

“A house is really a life…one you’ve had or one you want to have. A new dream.” So wrote Shirley MacLaine on the first page of her memoir Sage-ing While Age-ing. And she should know. The dancer-actor-author has lived everywhere from the family home at 930 North Liberty Street in Richmond, Virginia, which she shared with her parents and younger brother (none other than Warren Beatty) to Manhattan apartments, Malibu beach cottages, and New Mexican desert ranches. And that’s just in this lifetime; MacLaine believes she has come around again through several incarnations, even writing books about her experience as a nongendered being in the Lemurian era, and another go-round on the lost continent of Atlantis.

In the 10 images below, we focus on MacLaine’s current incarnation, stepping into the Oscar-winner’s homes to see the star of Steel Magnolias and Downton Abbey at work, at play, and dancing on air.

MacLaine relaxes with a magazine in 1954.

At 20, MacLaine was a chorus girl on Broadway and the understudy for Carol Haney in The Pajama Game. When Haney twisted her ankle, MacLaine (who had changed her last name from Beaty to an alternate spelling of her mother’s maiden name, MacLean), replaced her for two weeks. Alfred Hitchcock spotted her and cast her in The Trouble with Harry, and Hal Walis signed her to a five-year studio contract, which led her to move to California. This 1954 shot shows her reading a magazine at home in Malibu, where she rented a tiny beachfront apartment. “Each home was a palace and a new jolt. They loomed on cultivated hilltops or nestled in L-shapes and U-shapes on shaded slopes, jewel-like habitations set in acres of jade carpet,” MacLaine recalled in her memoir Don’t Fall Off the Mountain. “We stared at the fairyland until our eyes hurt and our sense of reality reminded us that we were flat broke.”

When MacLaine moved from New York to California, she writes in Don’t Fall Off the Mountain, “sunshine bled from every corner and the dazzling white buildings gave me a permanent squint.” The happy gaze is on full display here in on the beach in front of the Malibu one-bedroom she and husband Steve Parker rented, which was not white, but a weathered wood cottage on stilts.

MacLaine and Parker, making the most of their outdoor shower in 1955.

“When I met Steve Parker, the attraction was immediate,” MacLaine writes in her photo-driven memoir The Wall of Life. She first encountered the businessman in New York, where, before he proposed, he informed her he was “undercover with military intelligence and work in Japan regularly, as well as other places throughout the world.” Here, Parker sneaks up on MacLaine in the one-bedroom, one-bathroom Malibu pad’s bonus outdoor shower.

The newlyweds on their home’s deck in Malibu in March, 1955.

This 1955 shot captures a rare moment of togetherness for MacLaine and Parker at their Malibu beach house. He often lived in Japan while she made films. “I’m not sure I would say that Steve and I had a happy marriage, but it was a satisfied one,” MacLaine wrote in The Wall of Life. “He had a mistress in Japan and I had whoever I was with.” But in their SoCal haven, they experienced their first taste of married life. “I was up every morning at four-thirty trying to be a housewife—ironing cleaning, and experimenting with the cookbook,” MacLaine wrote in Don’t Fall Off the Mountain. “At six Steve drove me to the studio, where we shot the final sequences of The Trouble with Harry.”

MacLaine dancing on the beach in front of her home in Malibu.

MacLaine, shown here leaping on the beach in front of her home, trained as a dancer since age three. “As I recall my life, I have come to understand that the physicality of dance was the most influential force,” she wrote in The Wall of Life. Here, MacLaine shows of her skills at the rental property she described in Don’t Fall Off the Mountain as “a tiny, one-bedroom apartment in a building set high on pilings that shook with each wave that crashed beneath it.”

This 1955 publicity shot shows MacLaine studying a script under Parker’s adoring eye in their Malibu home. While the residence’s knotty pine walls were peak mid-’50s rec room decor, MacLaine recalls Hollywood looking down on her living arrangements as her star was on the rise. She imagines the whisperings about her in Don’t Fall Off the Mountain: “She’s STILL living in a one-room shack at the beach. She’s STILL married to that man, what’s his name?”

MacLaine plays with her dog at home in 1956.

In this March 1956 photo, MacLaine plays with her boxer at home in Malibu. Six months later, on September 1, her daughter Sachi Parker would be born, inspiring MacLaine to move her new family to larger digs “in the Malibu colony with a spare room for Sachi’s new nurse-caretaker,” she wrote in The Wall of Life. “Our first year together felt like we were beach camping. I never wondered if the lifestyle was good for her, and she was never sick.” In 1963, architect Cliff Mays built an eight-unit Malibu apartment building where MacLaine would claim the top floor, which boasted six bedrooms, four baths, and a deck overlooking the ocean. She retained the property for 30 years until relocating to New Mexico, telling The Los Angeles Times, “I feel the high desert calling me.”

Shirley MacLaine in full 1970s style: maxidress, long hair. and aggressive shag carpet.

In 1972, MacLaine was spending most of her time in an apartment at 400 East 52nd Street after filming her short-lived TV series Shirley’s World. The building is part of the Southgate complex, which opened in 1931, but MacLaine did not adhere to the edifice’s Art Deco style—note the thick shag carpeting and the East Asian–inspired art on the walls. Dancer Tommy Tune bought the penthouse of the building after visiting MacLaine in her apartment.

Describing her life in New York in the 1970s, MacLaine wrote in You Can Get There from Here that “on any given day, I would mosey into Serendipity for a frozen mocha, plunder the skirt shop at Bloomingdale’s, walk to the Village and back until I reached Beekman Place at sunset and breathed in the smell of the East River.… I wanted to sit in my apartment for hours, watching all the news shows, and eating cinnamon buns from the automat.”

In this 1980 photo of MacLaine at the Southgate, her hair is different but the floral sofa remains as it was in 1972. In the years between 1972 and 1980, “I did just three films. I don’t think I got any good scripts in that period, but I didn’t care and was honestly more interested in seeing the world and understanding what was going on in it.” She made a documentary in China, campaigned for George McGovern and Jimmy Carter, and did live shows in New York and London. She was drawn back to Hollywood to make The Turning Point, and won an Oscar for her work in 1983’s Terms of Endearment.

Today, MacLaine’s New Mexico ranch is on the market, and, at age 92, she has been spotted out and about in her old stomping grounds: Malibu.
