Here’s what happened in crypto today

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Need to know what happened in crypto today? Here is the latest news on daily trends and events impacting Bitcoin price, blockchain, DeFi, Web3 and crypto regulation.

Today in crypto, former UK Prime Minister Boris Johnson called on Britain to lead in blockchain development despite his persistent skepticism of Bitcoin, Harvard and Dartmouth maintained their holdings in BlackRock’s Bitcoin ETF during the third quarter, and Bitcoin mining services provider Luxor warned of increasingly frequent AI-enabled cyberattacks after its platform was compromised.

Former United Kingdom Prime Minister Boris Johnson has backed blockchain technology and tokenization as opportunities for the UK’s financial sector, despite persistent doubts about cryptocurrencies.

Speaking at Solana’s Breakpoint conference in London on Monday, Johnson maintained his position that Bitcoin (BTC) resembles a Ponzi scheme, following an opinion piece published in the Daily Mail in March. He questioned who investors could hold accountable if funds were stolen.

However, Johnson distinguished his criticism of digital assets from his view of the underlying technology, saying the UK should be at the forefront of tokenization.

Lord Ross Kempsell, who joined Johnson on stage, said although he shared his reservations on cryptocurrencies, “on the parts of the industry that can be brought into a regulated perimeter, which the regulators are working on, there’s a lot of scope here for a high growth industry.”

Harvard University and Dartmouth College reported unchanged share counts in BlackRock’s spot Bitcoin exchange-traded fund (ETF) at the end of the third quarter as a Bitcoin rally lifted the value of both stakes.

Harvard held 3.04 million shares of the iShares Bitcoin Trust ETF (IBIT), worth about $144.1 million at the end of September, while Dartmouth reported 201,531 shares valued at $9.5 million, according to Form 13F filings with the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC).

IBIT gained about 42% in Q3 as Bitcoin rallied nearly 43%. Both posted their strongest quarterly gains of 2026 through September.

Harvard has reported the same IBIT share count since the end of March, after cutting its holdings by 43% in Q1 and 21% in Q4 2025. Dartmouth has reported the same 201,531 shares since first disclosing its Bitcoin ETF position in Q4 2025.

Bitcoin mining services provider Luxor has warned that AI-enabled cyberattacks are becoming increasingly difficult to defend against after attackers compromised its mining-management platform and redirected customers’ computing power to another mining pool over the weekend.

In an X post on Sunday, Luxor CEO Nick Hansen said the attack came through a “sophisticated AI-enabled vector,” without specifying how AI was used in the attack, warning that these types of attacks will only get more frequent over time.

“At this moment, anyone running any sort of substantial business, especially in crypto, is inundated with AI-enabled attacks,” Hansen said in an X post. “This does not seem to be slowing down; rather, it’s speeding up as models get better and more attackers enter the arena.”

The warning comes as security researchers have documented the spread of AI-assisted hacking tools, raising the prospect of faster attacks against businesses holding money or controlling valuable infrastructure.
