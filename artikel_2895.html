ING: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Restructuring wholesale operations and optimizing capital deployment are unlocking efficiency gains that may support both shareholder returns and long-term profitability.

By The Motley Fool Team – Nov 24, 2026 at 11:30AM EST

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Oct. 29, 2026, ING (ING -1.40%) announced an additional 1.9 billion euro capital distribution to shareholders while raising its profitability outlook for 2027. The financial services business continues to expand its primary mobile customer base, driving fee income higher through scalable digital platforms. That operational momentum generates excess capital while keeping cost growth under control.

Three key takeaways from management's comments stand out.

Converting basic banking accounts into primary mobile relationships drives engagement because customers adopt multiple products and generate higher fee income per person. That cross-selling engine expands revenues without proportionally increasing client acquisition costs.

Management is reorganizing its wholesale banking unit into Corporate and Institutional Banking (CIB) to simplify client coverage and trim headcount. The enterprise expects a provision of 175 million euros in the fourth quarter to yield 75 million euros in annual cost savings starting in 2028.

The bank generates excess capital by deploying Significant Risk Transfer (SRT) transactions, which transfer loan risk to institutional investors to free up required capital reserves. Efficient risk management allowed the company to maintain a Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) solvency ratio of 13.3% while funding an additional 1.9 billion euro capital distribution.

Revenue momentum is building faster than anticipated, and full-year fee income will hit 5 billion euros a year ahead of schedule. Scalable digital investment platforms and disciplined pricing maintain profitability across core European markets, while leadership tracks commercial interest rate margins to preserve yield.

Structural efficiency gains continue to support higher profitability across the entire banking franchise. Digital operations and capital velocity put the bank in position for long-term growth.
