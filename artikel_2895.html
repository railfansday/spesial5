Why Joe Mazzulla hugged Neemias Queta instead of yelling at him in win over Hornets

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

CHARLOTTE — Early in the third quarter of the Celtics' 119-80 win over the Charlotte Hornets, Joe Mazzulla called a timeout and walked over to Neemias Queta.

Queta had just gotten tangled up with multiple Hornets players while trying to secure a rebound. And, as is often the case, Mazzulla grew frustrated with his big man's longstanding tendency to foul.

But this time, instead of reaming him out, Mazzulla chose to embrace him.

"I'm just trying to channel my anger into love," Mazzulla said afterward.

"Instead of just yelling at him, I gave him a hug."

It's no secret that Mazzulla is hard on the Portuguese big man, who began his journey with the Celtics on a two-way contract in 2023 and blossomed into the Celtics' productive and reliable starting center last season.

Sometimes, Mazzulla's yelling gets picked up by the TV broadcast. More often than not, his anger is visible from press row.

And, none of that tough love is something that the Celtics head coach denies.

"I'm hard on Neemi," Mazzulla said on Friday night. "I'm hard on our bigs. Those guys, the way they need to play for us, there's a ton of pressure on them. We talk about it all the time — the fives have the toughest job on this team because they got to do a ton on both ends of the floor."

Mazzulla has frequently discussed how difficult the center position is in the team's system, particularly as of late. That's also applied to Mitchell Robinson, who has struggled in his acclimation to the Celtics as he takes on more responsibility in a new system.

Still, Mazzulla wants to make sure that amid his tough coaches style, there's an understanding that all of it is rooted in love. He visits players in the offseason in order to better understand what makes them tick; this past summer, he joined Queta in Portugal, Mitchell Robinson in Nashville, and Luka Garza in Iowa.

In difficult moments, there's a relationship that runs deeper than basketball.

"I appreciate that they allow me to be hard on them, but I also want them to know I appreciate them," he said. "Just trying to do that for them."

Queta is not the only player to get a hug from Joe Mazzulla as of late; Payton Pritchard got an in-game hug from Mazzulla in a win over the Chicago Bulls, after a couple of high-impact plays.

"Joe challenged me," Pritchard said after that win. "I feel like the first four games my defensive activity and my rebounding have been poor… He's challenging me to step up in that area. I think the hug was for me doing that."

On Friday, Pritchard reflected on how Mazzulla has successfully tapped into both sides of the emotional spectrum as a coach.

"Joe, he's a competitor, and he has a lot of emotion, so it brings the best out of us, and it's also cool when you are doing well; he shows the emotion of a loving brother type situation," Pritchard said. "And then we'll have moments where we get into it, and we have disagreements, and it's all right. It's just part of it — we go through love and hate."

Pritchard said his bond with Mazzulla has strengthened over the years; he's been playing for him since he took over as head coach in 2022, and it's come with plenty of variance. Pritchard has gone through stretches in which he's fallen out of the rotation, won Sixth Man of the Year, and was a part of the 2024 championship team.

"It's a journey," he said. "We've definitely gone through a lot of different situations, a lot of ups and downs, and you just grow your relationship through that, and it becomes stronger and stronger."

Mazuzlla echoed that sentiment last week, after his hug.

"I like pushing him, I like coaching him, and I love him like a brother," Mazzulla said of Pritchard.

For Mazzulla, turning anger into love has been the result of an intentional effort, especially when it comes to his relationship with Queta.

"A lot of meditation, a lot of journaling, a lot of just not being so mad at him all the time," Mazzulla said. "But I love him."

Still, Mazzulla's ability to leverage anger hasn't gone away, the Celtics' veterans say.

"It just depends on the day," said Jayson Tatum with a smile. "Some good days, some bad days."

"They're both needed," Pritchard said. "You can't have love without the anger."
