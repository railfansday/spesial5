Trying to make the fantasy football playoffs? Trade for these players now before the deadline arrives

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

I'm pleased to report that Thanksgiving 2026 was a success at the Allen house. Granted, my morning started at 5:30 in the rain with my smoker, but the day improved. My kids were bickering with each other before I got done with my coffee. We had the food prepped before the first kickoff. Dinner was nothing but reminiscing, laughter and regret about eating too much. It couldn't have been better. But all the while, I knew what was waiting for me the next day. The holiday would be over, and it would be time to get back to work.

For fantasy football managers on the cusp of a playoff berth, I'm sure you're feeling the same pull. Yahoo's fantasy trade deadline is at 11:59 p.m. PT on Saturday, November 28. The playoffs start in a few weeks. If you've got a shot, the next day and a half may be your final chance to get your roster in shape for the final push. So, let's talk strategy and find some viable targets to acquire to put you on the path to taking down your league.

I'll start simple. "Temet Nosce" means "know thyself," or it's better known as the phrase on the sign above The Oracle's kitchen in The Matrix. Essentially, it's identifying the strengths and weaknesses of your roster to find areas where a trade would make the most impact. Since Hayden Winks is currently leading our Expert's League, I'll use his roster as an example.

Your draft-day squad compared to what it looks like now is like before-and-after pictures of a prize fighter. You've made decisions to scrape together wins. There's no shame in that. But you'll need the best team to crawl through the playoffs. So, even just identifying the potential liabilities is a strong first step.

Even as a Bengals fan, I'd have to admit that Samaje Perine sticks out as the weak link. Sure, he's liable for a big play and siphons goal-line work away from Chase Brown. But he's scored double-digit PPR points in three games. Admittedly, that's a subjective take, though. So, let's put some numbers to my claim.

I used each player's weekly touch stats to calculate their weighted opportunities per game. Based on the idea that targets are 2.5 times more valuable than a carry, the pass-catchers immediately jump to the front of the line. But without a stable role week-to-week, Perine lags behind the rest of Hayden's starters. Coupled with his lack of weekly upside, finding a strong replacement for Weeks 15-17 would solidify Hayden as the favorite to take down the league.

Let's stick with the Perine example for a second.

While I advocated for Hayden finding another player to take Perine's place in the flex, that didn't mean sending him away. First, Perine has handled over 40% of the carries in two of the last three games and a double-digit target share in consecutive outings. Second, let's take a look at Cincinnati's schedule during the fantasy playoffs.

Week 15 at Carolina: 8 (RB1 Weeks Allowed), T-1st

Of course, Joe Burrow and the passing game remain the focal point of the offense. But, independent of how they get there, we want RBs on teams that get into scoring position more often than not. And the Bengals are one of six teams with top-12 marks in both red-zone plays per game and red-zone drives ending with a TD. So, finding the matchups that would lead to right-tail outcomes for our players would set us up for hitting spike weeks when we need them the most.

Adjusted Fantasy Points Allowed (FPA) is a schedule-adjusted, opponent-strength metric measuring how much more or less an RB scored against each defense. I estimated it by comparing a rusher's expected PPG to their actual output. The results highlight Perine's Week 15 and 16 contests as his best shots at outperforming his projection. And by expanding this quick look at Perine to the other positions, we can figure out which offenses should have our attention.

If only I believed in Cam Ward or Bo Nix more. But remember, this is just a way to gauge a player's situation. And to generate fantasy points, we need the environment along with talent (e.g., earning touches). Regardless, by positioning our starters against weaker opponents, we're giving them the best possible chance to pull out their best performances when we need them the most.

Fantasy football shouldn't be much different. Safe plays and floor options will likely give us median outputs. Meanwhile, our opponents will have one or two players peaking at the right time. Let's go back to 2019 for an example.

Before Week 15 of the '19 season, Breshard Perriman had yet to record a 100-yard game. However, both Mike Evans and Chris Godwin were out. Jameis Winston was slinging the ball 39.1 times per game. Perriman had an absurd 17.0 aDOT. The result was Perriman averaging 116.3 per game, totaling 75.9 PPR points over the three-game stretch. Spinning it forward seven years, let's see if we can find receivers with the possibility of repeating Perriman's feat.

I went back through seasonal data up to Week 11 from 2023 to now, gauging each receiver's target share and aDOT over their prior six games. I kept the WRs averaging at least four targets per game and split the groups into thirds by opportunity and depth. Afterward, I found out how often their next game resulted in a WR1 finish and if it included a 40+ yard catch. Essentially, a play that could make a player's week on its own.

Unsurprisingly, volume is our best predictor of fantasy results. But a high aDOT raises the odds of a week-winning play for the WRs with middling workloads (7.0% to 12.1% and 8.9% to 15.4%). So a low-share, high-aDOT WR is a boom-or-bust big-play bet. Let's apply that profile to '26 and see who comes up.

Adonai Mitchell: 22% (Target Rate), 17.4 (aDOT), 2.5 (Deep Targets per Game)

My knee-jerk reaction was to wince at the names. But that's kind of the point. Nobody believed in Perriman before he went on his tirade. I'd have trouble doing the same for Jaylen Waddle, but we already identified the Broncos as a WR group to target due to their schedule. The Giants (Darnell Mooney) sit right behind Denver. So, don't let the names scare you away. I get they'd be the last guys you'd expect to win you a title. But that's exactly why they could.

I'm going to assume Week 11 was a blip for Trevor Lawrence. On the plus side, he threw the ball 47 times (season-high) but completed just over half of his passes (season-low). However, the volume part is what intrigues me.

Let's say HC Liam Coen gives Bhayshul Tuten some time to heal up. In that scenario, Jacksonville may turn to its passing game to buttress the offense. Lawrence hit a single-game high +6.0% PROE after Tuten reaggravated his ankle injury. If we get the volume plus Lawrence's normal high-end efficiency, we've got a top-6 QB on our hands. Unfortunately, Lawrence will be without his top target, Parker Washington, for at least this week — something to monitor.

What if I told you that Kirk Cousins' outing against the 49ers in primetime wasn't that bad?

OK, first, stop laughing — bear with me for a second. Cousins' completion rate actually increased from his first game against the Packers to his fated MNF result (67.7% to 68.4%). Also, his passing success rate didn't fall too far (47.1% to 43.6%). He only took one sack. Basically, outside of the interception and a couple of miscues, it wasn't a complete disaster. Besides, things get better during the playoffs.

Week 15: Lions (Opponent), +6.5 (Adj. FPA)

All three are top-10 matchups for QBs. Let's give Cousins and the rest of the offense more time to gel and then reap the benefits on our way to a championship.

Tony Pollard has the ideal situation for an RB. His backup is on IR. The options behind him have a combined seven touches on the season. That's why, from an opportunity standpoint, Pollard should be an RB2 on any fantasy squad.

Rushing Share: 77%, 4th (out of 29 qualifiers – min. 250 snaps)

Not only does Pollard have the lion's (titan's?) share of the backfield, he also has the schedule to boost him into the RB1 conversation. That's talent AND situation. With weeks against the Colts, Raiders and Steelers on tap for Weeks 15-17, Pollard should grade out as one of the best RBs to have for the playoffs.

If I'm advocating for Cousins, his WR1 has to be on this list. Hopefully, Justin Jefferson just keeps running into the end zone next time he has the ball instead of thinking he's LeSean McCoy. But at least the rapport with his QB was still intact.

Target Share: 45% (Week 10), 21% (Week 11)

Even with Cousins having to look farther downfield for Jettas, the two only missed on two hookups. T.J. Hockenson is healthy, and Minnesota has a budding WR2 in Tai Felton, forcing defenses to look in multiple directions on passing plays. And when we take into account the aforementioned schedule, those big box scores from Jefferson should be on their way.

I'll be honest. Trading for Josh Downs may require a bit of faith. Despite the initial outburst in Week 10, HC Andy Reid has left us wondering what the plan is for his new receiver.

Route Rate: 21% (Week 10), 32% (Week 11), 23% (Week 12)

The good news is that Downs was still one of the more efficient KC receivers with his targets. Even in the rain against the Bills, Downs had the highest YPRR of any of the receivers with multiple targets. Plus, it was still the case that whenever he was on the field, Patrick Mahomes was looking for him. Downs earned a target on 57.1% of his routes. We just need the role to expand, but a soggy night in Orchard Park wasn't conducive to that situation.

Thursday night was just the third time Mahomes dropped back to pass fewer than 30 times. And the last time Mahomes had a passing success rate at or below 36.7% was in Week 16 of the '23 season. In short, this is a two-leg parlay of the passing efficiency returning and Downs moving up in the pecking order. However, if you can trade for him and you have depth at the position, you can hold out until the playoffs to start him.
