Knicks Player Ratings: Brunson Drops 30, Bridges Nails Game-Winer Against Trail Blazers

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The New York Knicks survived a dramatic finish against the Portland Trail Blazers, escaping with a 97-96 victory after Mikal Bridges delivered the game-winning shot. Jalen Brunson led the way with 30 points, including six three-pointers, while Bridges added 12 points and came through when it mattered most. It was an ugly, hard-fought game for much of the night, but the Knicks found a way to get the job done.

New York's biggest advantage came from beyond the arc. The Knicks knocked down 19 of 42 three-point attempts (45.2%), compared to Portland's 11 of 38 (28.9%). That shooting gap proved decisive in a game where the Trail Blazers actually won the rebounding battle 47-42 and outscored New York 48-26 in the paint.

The Knicks also had a commanding 26-point lead at one stage, only to let Portland back into the contest. Brunson's scoring and Bridges' late-game heroics ultimately saved New York from a frustrating collapse. Here is how every Knicks player performed.

Game Stats: 30 PTS, 7 REB, 5 AST, 3 TOV, 11-23 FG, 6-10 3-PT FG, 2-3 FT, 36 MIN

Brunson was the engine behind New York's offense, delivering a 30-point performance with six three-pointers. He was particularly effective from deep, converting 60.0% of his attempts, and consistently gave the Knicks a scoring option when their offense needed a spark.

His seven rebounds and five assists added to an otherwise strong all-around performance. Three turnovers and a relatively inefficient 11-for-23 shooting line prevent this from being a perfect grade, but Brunson was clearly the Knicks' most important offensive player. New York needed every one of his 30 points.

Game Stats: 20 PTS, 1 REB, 3 AST, 7-14 FG, 6-10 3-PT FG, 36 MIN

Shamet delivered one of his best performances of the night, matching Brunson's perimeter shooting with six made three-pointers of his own. He finished with 20 points on 50.0% shooting, providing the Knicks with a second major scoring threat.

Even more impressive was his ball security. Shamet finished without a single turnover while adding three assists. His minus-three plus/minus was not ideal, but his shooting was instrumental in helping New York build its sizable lead. The Knicks needed his offensive production, and he delivered.

Game Stats: 12 PTS, 2 REB, 3 AST, 1 STL, 3 TOV, 5-8 FG, 2-3 3-PT FG, 28 MIN

Bridges did not have the same scoring workload as Brunson or Shamet, but he made his biggest contribution when the game was on the line. His game-winning shot gave New York a victory that had become increasingly difficult to secure after the Knicks surrendered their large lead.

He was efficient from the field, making five of eight attempts and two of three from beyond the arc. Three turnovers and a minus-six rating are negatives, but the value of delivering the decisive basket cannot be overlooked. Bridges came through in the moment that mattered most.

Game Stats: 7 PTS, 13 REB, 1 AST, 1 STL, 3 BLK, 2 TOV, 3-10 FG, 1-2 3-PT FG, 23 MIN

Drummond made his presence felt defensively, recording 13 rebounds and three blocks in just 23 minutes. His work on the glass was important against a Portland frontcourt that ultimately finished with a five-rebound advantage over New York.

The seven points came on an inefficient three-of-10 shooting performance, but his defensive contributions were substantial. Drummond also added a steal, giving the Knicks some much-needed interior protection. His ability to influence shots and collect rebounds made this a productive outing despite the shooting struggles.

Game Stats: 8 REB, 6 AST, 2 STL, 1 TOV, 0-7 FG, 0-4 3-PT FG, 35 MIN

Hart had an unusual night, finishing scoreless despite playing 35 minutes. He missed all seven of his field-goal attempts, including four from three-point range, and never found an offensive rhythm.

However, he still contributed in other areas. His eight rebounds, six assists, and two steals demonstrated his willingness to impact the game without scoring. Hart's defensive activity and playmaking helped soften the blow of his shooting struggles, but New York needs more offensive production from him in close games.

Game Stats: 8 PTS, 2 REB, 2 AST, 1 BLK, 1 TOV, 3-7 FG, 2-5 3-PT FG, 15 MIN

Alvarado provided a useful scoring contribution off the bench, knocking down two three-pointers in 15 minutes. He also added two assists and a block, showing some versatility in a relatively short appearance.

His four-point plus/minus was a positive, and he gave the Knicks another perimeter option without needing a high volume of possessions. It was a solid reserve performance.

Game Stats: 7 PTS, 3 REB, 1 STL, 1 TOV, 3-7 FG, 0-2 3-PT FG, 1-1 FT, 13 MIN

Kolek made a modest but useful contribution in 13 minutes. He scored seven points, collected three rebounds, and added a steal. Although he did not find his range from beyond the arc, he made three of seven shots overall and converted his only free-throw attempt.

His plus/minus of minus-three was not particularly encouraging, but he gave the second unit some additional scoring support.

Game Stats: 5 PTS, 6 REB, 2 AST, 3 BLK, 1 TOV, 2-3 FG, 1-2 FT, 25 MIN

Wiseman was productive defensively, matching Drummond with three blocks while collecting six rebounds. He also finished with five points on efficient two-of-three shooting and contributed two assists.

His six rebounds and rim protection helped New York manage its frontcourt rotation, although the Knicks still struggled to contain Portland around the basket. Wiseman's minus-one rating was relatively neutral, and his defensive contributions made his minutes worthwhile.

Game Stats: 5 PTS, 2 AST, 1 STL, 2-3 FG, 1-1 3-PT FG, 11 MIN

Dadiet made the most of his limited opportunity, shooting efficiently and knocking down his only three-point attempt. He also recorded two assists and a steal without committing a turnover.

His plus-six rating was the best among the Knicks' rotation players, making his short appearance a productive one. It was an encouraging contribution from the young forward.

Game Stats: 3 PTS, 1 AST, 1 BLK, 1-4 FG, 1-4 3-PT FG, 14 MIN

McBride had a quiet night offensively, making just one of four attempts from the field. His only basket came from beyond the arc, and he added one assist and a block.

He avoided turnovers and finished with a plus-six rating, suggesting his minutes were not damaging to the team. Still, New York could have used more scoring from its bench guard.

Knecht barely saw the floor, playing just three minutes and missing his only shot attempt. There is not enough of a sample to make a meaningful judgment about his performance.
