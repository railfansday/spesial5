New-Home Sales See Welcome Shift, but Not an Overall Improvement

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

With housing construction continuing to face supply chain and affordability challenges, the latest data from the U.S. Census Bureau and the Department of Housing and Urban Development saw new-home sales experience a welcome shift upward.

New-home sales jumped 14.2% in October to a rate of 701,000, up from a revised rate of 614,000 in September (originally 676,000). This was also up 7.5% year-over-year.

While a positive shift for the construction industry, the shift is notably due entirely to growth in the South. While all three other regions were down both month-over-month and year-over-year to varying degrees, the South was up 36.2% month-over-month and 24.7% year-over-year.

Danushka Nanayakkara-Skillington, National Association of Home Builders (NAHB) assistant vice president for forecasting and analysis, noted that while any increase in new-home sales is “encouraging,” it is “too early to interpret the gain as a sustained recovery in housing demand.”

“Elevated mortgage rates and high home prices continue to limit purchasing power, and the outlook for new home sales remains sensitive to financing costs and broader economic conditions,” she continued.

Bright MLS Chief Economist Lisa Sturtevant noted that as mortgage rates remain near a three-year high, “the new home industry is experiencing the same pullback in demand that we are seeing on the resale side.”

“While home builders can help offset some of the pain of higher rates by offering buyers rate buydowns and favorable financing, prospective buyers are cautious as we head into the end of 2026,” she continued. “Some buyers have been priced out of the market, while others are simply waiting until 2027, hoping for more favorable conditions.”

Inventory remained on the downslope, falling 1.5% month-over-month and 4.1% year-over-year to 477,000. At the current sales rate, this clocked in at an eight-month supply, down 14% from last month and 11.1% year-over-year.

Builder activity has been significantly diminished due to aforementioned market challenges, with builder confidence recently tanking to a 14-year low.

“Builders are responding with incentives and pricing adjustments, but a more sustained improvement in affordability will be important for strengthening housing demand,” noted NAHB Chairman Bill Owens.

In line with this trend, the median price for a new home slipped 0.3% month-over-month and 3.2 year-over-year to $391,300. Sturtevant noted that this was near a “five year low.”

“Traditionally, new homes command a premium over existing homes, but in October, the median price of a new home was 6% lower than the median price of an existing home,” she continued.”

Sturtevant concluded that with incentives and lower pricing on the table, “new homes continue to be an attractive option for buyers who remain in the market, particularly when builders can offer a discounted interest rate.”

She noted, though, that the issue at hand is “that there are simply fewer buyers in the market as we head into the last few weeks of the year.”

Claudia Larsen is a senior editor for RISMedia.
