Visa and Mastercard pushed for final approval of $38 billion swipe fee deal

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Free daily briefing on global business news.

A federal judge in Brooklyn withheld a ruling after Monday's hearing as Walmart and the National Retail Federation object

Visa $V and Mastercard $MA urged U.S. District Judge Brian Cogan on Monday to give final approval to their $38 billion deal with merchants, who accused the networks of overcharging to handle credit card transactions, according to Reuters.

Cogan withheld a decision on final approval at the hearing in Brooklyn, New York. He said a decision to turn down the settlement might set off a "protracted process" if the case then went to trial, according to Reuters.

The settlement obliges Visa and Mastercard to trim their swipe fees by a tenth of a percentage point for five years, while limiting charges on standard consumer cards to 1.25% for eight years. It ends the "Honor All Cards" rule, which requires merchants to accept all of a network's cards or none. Merchants would, for the first time, be able to pick which of three categories to accept: standard consumer cards, commercial cards, or premium consumer cards such as rewards cards.

The proposed settlement would apply to over 12 million merchants, although some of its opponents put the number of businesses affected at more than 20 million.

Visa lawyer Michael Shuster described the deal as delivering "potent" relief to merchants, while acknowledging that Visa is hardly "jumping with joy" over the changes. "It's going to change life for the networks," Shuster said.

Two experts retained by the plaintiffs, economist Joseph Stiglitz and Keith Leffler, a University of Washington professor, estimated that the changes could deliver $224 billion in total benefits, including for consumers, with merchants saving $38 billion by 2031.

Swipe fees, which are also called interchange fees, added up to $118.8 billion for Visa and Mastercard across the United States in 2025, according to the Merchants Payments Coalition. The average rate came to 2.36%.

The court logged just over 1,000 objections, among them filings from Walmart $WMT and the National Retail Federation. Some opponents say merchants would keep paying steep fees to take rewards cards and would lack the ability to pick among issuers, for instance by taking one bank's cards while turning away another's.

Mithun Mansinghani, a lawyer for the National Association of Convenience Stores and Circle K, told Cogan that the deal contains no structural changes that would foster real competition among card issuers. He said charges would stay excessive for commercial cards and premium consumer cards, which together account for over 90% of the market for cards. "Competition should be the North Star ... and this settlement is way off course," Mansinghani said, according to Reuters.

The Electronic Payments Coalition, whose members include Visa, Mastercard, Bank of America $BAC, Capital One $COF, Chase and Citibank, supports the settlement.

Cogan granted preliminary approval in June and said he expected to grant final approval, describing the terms as "fair, reasonable, and adequate." He acknowledged that some objections raised legitimate points but said perfection was not the standard.

In June 2024, a different judge, Margo Brodie, refused to approve an earlier $30 billion deal because its size was inadequate. She concluded that swipe fees would not have fallen to pre-violation levels and that the "Honor All Cards" obligation would have remained intact.
