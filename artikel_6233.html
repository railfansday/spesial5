America Needs an Arsenal of Democracies

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

We must combine American and allied innovation and manufacturing to take on the threats we share.

A worker along the assembly line handles large caliber 155mm metal projectiles at the Scranton Army Ammunition Plant in Scranton, Pa., April 16, 2024.

An Israeli-developed missile is now rolling off an American production line. On October 9, Raytheon-Rafael Protection Systems, an American Israeli joint venture, announced the first Tamir interceptors manufactured in Camden, Ark. They are intended for Israel’s Iron Dome missile-defense system, with plans to produce a U.S. variant for the Marine Corps.

The Camden facility builds on decades of allied weapons production, including the AIM-9 Sidewinder air-to-air missile with West Germany as well as the F-16 with multiple partners. But today’s conflicts require an industrial network that produces weapons at scale, maintains deliveries during disruptions, and replenishes forces across theaters. Co-production alone does not deliver that. It changes where a weapon is built; resilience depends on whether production can continue when something breaks.

Mackenzie Eaglen is a senior fellow at the American Enterprise Institute, where she works on defense strategy, defense budgets, and military readiness.

Daniel Flesch is the Executive Director of the Defense Technology Alliance.
