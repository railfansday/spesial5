Alaska Airlines Grows More With 4 New US Cities From Seattle & 1 Seasonal Route To Mexico In 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Alaska Airlines is pressing ahead with its ambitious expansion at Seattle-Tacoma International Airport (SEA), announcing five additional nonstop routes for 2027 despite a challenging third quarter that saw profits fall sharply. The carrier will introduce year-round services to Charlotte, Norfolk, Jacksonville and Des Moines, alongside seasonal flights to Guadalajara, Mexico.

The new additions will bring Alaska's Seattle network to 119 nonstop destinations, nearly double the reach of arch-rival Delta Air Lines, which has also been expanding aggressively at its Pacific Northwest hub. However, the announcement also comes as Alaska Air Group faces mounting pressure to translate network growth into stronger earnings.

Alaska confirmed the expansion on Thursday, reinforcing its commitment to growing its largest hub. The five routes will launch between March and June 2027, with Guadalajara receiving three weekly flights, and all four domestic destinations served daily. Jacksonville, Norfolk and Des Moines will gain their first nonstop links to Seattle, while Des Moines will become Alaska's first destination in Iowa.

Charlotte Douglas International Airport (CLT) represents a different competitive proposition from the other domestic additions where Alaska will face no competition. American Airlines, Alaska's oneworld partner, already operates approximately 20 weekly nonstop flights between Charlotte and Seattle. Guadalajara also has established competition from Mexican carriers Volaris and Aeromexico.

The expansion follows other investments in the Pacific Northwest, including a substantial increase in service at nearby Paine Field (PAE). But these latest additions are aimed squarely at supporting its broader strategy of building Seattle into a more comprehensive domestic and international connecting hub.

Ben Minicucci, Alaska Air Group CEO, emphasized the airline's growth strategy.

"Strengthening our Seattle operation means giving passengers more nonstop choices and additional opportunities to connect across our network. These are the things that earn guest loyalty, diversify our revenue, and ultimately drive stronger earnings."

The choice of Jacksonville International Airport (JAX) and Norfolk International Airport (ORF) appears particularly deliberate. Analysis of 2026 US Department of Transportation DB1C data identifies them as the two largest airport markets without existing Seattle nonstop service. Both currently depend on connecting itineraries, giving Alaska an opportunity to capture established demand and grow it further by offering shorter, nonstop journeys.

However, Des Moines International Airport (DSM) stands out. It ranks ninth among the unserved airport markets examined, behind six other destinations with greater existing demand. Its relatively modest local demand suggests that Alaska is either picking up incentives to fly there, or sees opportunities beyond just the passengers traveling directly between Iowa and Seattle, such as connecting on to Alaska's expanding international services from Seattle.

Late night Seattle departures connect to early morning arrivals in Guadalajara three times weekly aboard 737-800.

The route expansion comes as Alaska pursues its ambitious Alaska Accelerate transformation, targeting $1 billion in incremental annual profit by 2027, including $500 million in Hawaiian Airlines merger synergies. By September 2026, management reported capturing approximately two-thirds of that opportunity. Nevertheless, the airline continues investing in new routes, premium cabins and international operations, seeking to build a more diversified business despite difficult near-term conditions.

Alaska's latest results illustrate the challenge. Operating revenue increased 14% year over year to $4.31 billion, driven by its recent growth, but operating profit fell 45% to just $82 million. The airline also reported a $147 million net loss, compared with a $73 million profit a year earlier. This is directly attributable to fuel expenses, which surged 60%, as the carrier reported notable improvements in passenger yields and premium revenue.

Despite these pressures, Alaska is betting that broader network coverage, stronger connecting traffic and its increased ability to meet premium demand will eventually support higher earnings. Premium revenue rose 25% during the quarter, while corporate revenue increased 30%, and loyalty program cash remuneration increased 21%, reaching $721 million in Q3 2026.

However, management now expects a full-year adjusted loss of $5.00–$5.50 per share, reflecting substantial fuel-related headwinds. The new Seattle routes therefore represent another test of whether Alaska can convert ambitious network growth into sustainable profitability.
