'We have players who have been here for four years and can't speak English': Nottingham Forest boss Oliver Glasner slams stars for failing to adapt in England in press conference rant

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Published: 04:12 EST, 26 November 2026 | Updated: 07:31 EST, 26 November 2026

Oliver Glasner has criticised Nottingham Forest players that cannot speak English after being in the country for several years, as he called for higher standards at the club.

Forest are currently outside the Premier League relegation zone on goal difference after 11 matches, having won just twice in the top flight since Glasner's arrival in the summer.

Speaking ahead of their Friday night clash against Chelsea, Glasner highlighted the failure of some of his squad to learn English to illustrate how standards needed to rise at the club.

'Stability in a company is having standards,' Glasner said. 'Everybody who joins commits to the standards. I think, very honestly, the standards were not high enough.

'We have players who are here for four years, can't speak English. If you go to Italy, after three months, every player gives his interview in the Italian language.

'For me, if I want to manage in England, I have to speak English. If I want to manage in Italy, I have to speak Italian.

Nottingham Forest boss Oliver Glasner has criticised stars for failing to speak English years after joining the club

Glasner highlighted the issue as an example as he insisted standards need to rise at the club

'Otherwise, Oliver Glasner, if you want to speak in your dialect, stay at home in Austria, everybody understands you, but nowhere else.'

Football clubs regularly enlist the services of language experts in an effort to help foreign stars to settle and communicate with their team-mates.

West Brom and Wolves have worked with Language Partners for around 20 years, with the company providing tailored classes to players and coaches, offering specific skills required for their role at the clubs.

Another company, Communicate Well, has worked with Premier League clubs since 1999, with lessons taking place at training grounds, players’ homes and ‘out and about’ to provide real-life tasks in English.

Chelsea goalkeeper Emiliano Martinez previously revealed Arsenal had included a £20,000 bonus in his first contract with the club, which would be paid when he passed the English proficiency test.

Martinez, who signed for Arsenal in the summer of 2010 from Argentine side Independiente, had reportedly passed the test before Christmas.

English stars moving abroad have received praise in recent years for their efforts to learn the native language, with Jude Bellingham, Anthony Gordon and Trent Alexander-Arnold having all made efforts to become fluent in Spanish.

Alexander-Arnold revealed he had worked with a Spanish tutor for five months prior to joining Real Madrid. The tutor revealed he had started ‘from zero’ but worked every day to be able to speak Spanish at his unveiling at the club.

After speaking Spanish at his Barcelona unveiling, Gordon explained he had initially begun learning the language at Everton when working under Rafael Benitez. He later spoke Spanish with a physio at Newcastle as he targeted a move to Barcelona, while the winger also underwent six formal lessons.

Daily Mail Sport reported last month that Glasner had not been happy with some of his players' attitudes and their response to his work since joining the club from Crystal Palace in the summer.

Glasner had explosively hit out at his players after their 2-0 defeat at Brentford at the end of October, with the Austrian having warned players either had to buy into his methods to see a turnaround in form or they would have a sixth manager since the start of last season.

The 52-year-old's warning appeared to have been met with a positive response as Forest earned a stunning 3-1 victory over Man City before the international break, but they returned with a 2-0 loss at Bournemouth last weekend.

Glasner had maintained that his squad will have to work hard and show commitment both on and off the pitch to improve.

'We always try to support the players to get the best version of themselves that they can be. But at the end, the players that have to do it, they have to buy in, they have to listen and work hard on the pitch,' Glasner said.

'There is no other opportunity to improve, it would be easy laying on the sofa and doing nothing and you become a better football player or suddenly speak a language all of a sudden.

'It just doesn't work. It is all about how hard you work and how committed you are.

Glasner has insisted his squad need to work hard and show commitment to improve

'I believe none of our players have reached his personal ceilings so far, there is always bits to improve – physically, mentally, technically, tactically – there is always space to improve.

'The most important thing is that players want to improve, then it can happen.'

Glasner met with Forest owner Evangelos Marinakis in London earlier this week, where plans for the January transfer window were discussed.

Forest are expected to target adding around five players to their squad in the window as they bid to turn around their fortunes.

'He was asking "what do you need, what do you want here to improve in the squad, the staff and the environment",' Glasner said.

'He wants the best for Nottingham Forest, he wants the best for everyone here.

'We need to improve in the squad, in the staff, in the environment and this is our discussion. It was a fantastic meeting and it was worth spending six hours travelling.'

Forest face a Chelsea side on Friday that have lost three of their last four league matches.

Glasner’s side will then begin December with matches against Hull, Brighton and Sunderland as they bid to climb the table.
