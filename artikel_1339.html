Video Assistant Referee (VAR) has its fair share of critics. Introduced to the Premier League at the start of the 2019/20 season, the technology was designed to help the on-pitch officials and ensure matches were officiated seamlessly and without controversy.

However, it has not had the impact that it was intended to have. If anything, the number of refereeing controversies has gone up, and many high-profile names, including Roy Keane and Wayne Rooney, have lost patience and want to see VAR scrapped.

But what would the 2026/27 Premier League table look like if every refereeing decision was correct? After every match day in England's top tier, Squawka asks their followers to vote on controversial VAR decisions and whether the right decision was made or not.

Using that data, they have created a Premier League table without VAR errors following matchday 9, and it makes for interesting reading.

The table has been compiled on the basis that penalties, should they have been awarded, would have been converted.

Sunderland have had a dismal start to the campaign. The Black Cats, who are competing in the Conference League after finishing seventh in 2025/26, are currently rock bottom of the Premier League having picked up five points from their opening nine games. They can't blame VAR for their troubles, either, as they would remain in the same position without its errors.

Crystal Palace would be second bottom, with Manchester United rounding out the relegation places. Nottingham Forest, who are currently 19th in the Premier League, would be two points better off and would be above Palace and United in the table had VAR been perfect.

Back in September, Forest and Tottenham Hotspur drew 0-0 at the City Ground. VAR is adjudged to have wrongly intervened to disallow Neco Williams' goal for the home side. The Premier League released a statement explaining the goal had been ruled out for handball, but many felt the footage was inconclusive and not clear and obvious. Had VAR made the right decision, Forest would have won 1-0.

Fulham and Coventry City will both remain in 15th and 16th. Neither have had a VAR error go for or against them so far this season.

Hull City have had a tremendous start, but they would be a point and three places worse off without VAR errors. Newcastle United would remain in 13th, with their points tally and goal difference unchanged.

Tottenham and Aston Villa are now both in the top half after overcoming poor starts. Both teams have benefited from VAR errors, though. Spurs would drop from ninth to 12th after getting a controversial point against Forest, which they wouldn't have done had Williams' goal been correctly given. Villa would also drop a spot.

Premier League Table Without VAR Errors (20-11)

Leeds United have had a decent start to the season under Daniel Farke, but it would have been even better without VAR errors. They are currently 12th, but would be two places better off in 10th if correct decisions were made.

Ipswich Town can also be happy with their start. They've picked up 11 points from their opening nine matches and are currently four points above the drop zone. Without VAR errors, they would actually be a whopping six places better off and comfortably in the top half.

Everton would remain in seventh, while Liverpool and Chelsea would drop a place to sixth and fifth respectively, with Brentford jumping above them into the top four.

Manchester City would be two points worse off and drop a place from second to third without VAR errors. They won 1-0 at Old Trafford thanks to Erling Haaland's winner. ProRef released a statement revealing that the goal should have been ruled out due to Enzo Fernandez being in an offside position. Without that goal, the match would've ended in a goalless draw and Brighton would be above them on goal difference as a result.

Arsenal have picked up 24 points from their opening nine matches, winning eight and losing one. They wouldn't have beaten Liverpool without VAR errors, though.

Liverpool felt aggrieved that they weren't given a penalty when Alexander Isak's shirt was tugged by Jurrien Timber in the box, preventing him from having a header at goal. If VAR had made the right decision, Liverpool would've been given a spot-kick, and it is assumed they would have scored and that the spoils would have been shared.

Despite that, Mikel Arteta's side would still be well clear at the top of the table without VAR errors.
