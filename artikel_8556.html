You May Qualify for Child Care Assistance – Some States Help Middle-Class Families

If you work or attend school or job training, apply to your state’s child care assistance program even if you think you earn too much, and do it early, since many states keep waiting lists. For a child under 5, apply to free Head Start or Early Head Start as well.

Every state runs a child care assistance program with money from the federal Child Care and Development Fund, and each sets its own income limit, with some limits well into the middle class. Vermont helps families earning up to nearly six times the federal poverty line, and New Mexico has no income limit at all.

Federal rules cover children under 13 whose parents work or attend school or job training (under 19 for some children with disabilities or under court supervision). You choose a provider that takes the subsidy, the program pays the provider, and you pay a copay, your share of the bill, which your state sets by income and family size, sometimes at zero.

Picture a single parent approved for her state’s program, which pays her day care everything above her copay (figures illustrative). Earning $40,000, she pays $200 a month toward a $1,100 bill, so the program saves her $10,800 a year. Her approval lasts at least 12 months, and a raise in that time can end it only by lifting her income past 85% of her state’s median income.

Head Start and Early Head Start are free. Head Start serves mostly 3- and 4-year-olds, and Early Head Start serves infants, toddlers, and pregnant women. Families qualify with income below the federal poverty line or with cash welfare (TANF), Supplemental Security Income (SSI), or SNAP food benefits, and children in foster care or experiencing homelessness qualify at any income. Programs can fill up to 10% of spots with families above the line, and some can fill more with families under 130% of it. For a 3- or 4-year-old, check whether your state offers free public pre-K too.

The National Women’s Law Center counted over 400,000 children on child care assistance waiting lists by fall 2025, so apply now.

To apply, pick your state on Childcare.gov and open its financial assistance page, which links to your state’s program. For Head Start, enter your ZIP code in the Head Start Center Locator.
