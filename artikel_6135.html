“Light Bites”: American Airlines To Reinstate First Class Meals On Flights Between 700-899 Miles

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

American Airlines is bringing fresh food back to First Class on selected shorter domestic flights, introducing a new menu of "light bites" on services between Chicago O’Hare International Airport (ORD) and John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK) or LaGuardia Airport (LGA) in New York. The offerings include smoked salmon, whipped burrata, and poached shrimp, providing passengers with something more substantial than the packaged snacks traditionally available on these routes.

The change introduces a new, intermediate level of catering between snack-only flights and the full meals generally provided on services of 900 miles (1,450 km) or more. It also restores an element of American's pre-pandemic First Class experience, as US airlines compete to attract premium passengers through better seats, lounges, and onboard dining.

According to the airline's November 11 announcement, the 'light bites' will debut this month on flights linking the airline's Chicago hub with JFK and LGA. Passengers will be able to choose between two fresh options, with selections rotating throughout the year. Unlike American's standard First Class meals, the new dishes cannot be preordered, and the selections will only be available to choose from onboard.

The menu includes six potential breakfast dishes and eight lunch or dinner selections. Choices range from familiar sandwiches and fruit plates to more distinctive offerings, including tropical chia overnight oats for breakfast, and blackened chicken later in the day. The initiative follows other catering improvements, including expanded Pecan Lodge barbecue offerings on eligible Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport (DFW) departures, refreshed Main Cabin snacks, and enhanced Flagship Dining facilities at Los Angeles. American's Vice President of Culinary Strategy and Operations, Raphael Girardoni, described the new offering as an elevated experience on a popular route:

"We want to exceed the expectations of even our most discerning customers, and this new, more substantial menu offering is designed to do just that."

American's latest announcement marks a return to a service that first began more than a decade ago. In April 2015, the airline announced improved domestic First Class catering, replacing snack baskets with plated selections on flights of 700–899 miles (1,130–1,450 km). These included charcuterie, hummus with pita bread, and fruits and cheese, accompanied by warm nuts.

By 2019, American maintained a three-tier First Class catering structure for all domestic routes. This meant packaged snacks on the shortest flights, plated snacks on intermediate services, and full meals from approximately 900 miles (1,450 km). Longer flights received more elaborate service, while selected shorter routes qualified for full meals because of competitive considerations. The pandemic brought substantial reductions to onboard catering, and the plated snacks previously available on shorter services largely disappeared. American's decision to restore them fits its wider premium investment strategy, as the airline seeks to improve the passenger experience beyond the seat itself.

DFW to LAX travelers get a reserved buy on board trial with wraps, snack boxes and fruit plates landing in October.

American's light bites offering will create a new distinction from Delta Air Lines and United Airlines. They typically reserve full First Class meals for flights of at least 900 miles (1,450 km), with packaged snacks provided on routes below that threshold. Some routes and departure times are exceptions.

Light Bites on select routes; snacks elsewhere

Alaska Airlines stands out by offering freshly prepared First Class meals on flights as short as 670 miles (1,080 km). Its Pacific Northwest-inspired menus include strawberry burrata salad and lemon pesto gnocchi, with up to five dishes available for preorder. Unlike American's new Light Bites, Alaska passengers can reserve their preferred food before departure, giving the airline an additional competitive advantage.

American could eventually extend Light Bites to other high-volume routes in the 700-899 mile range, such as Chicago–Dallas, Philadelphia–Orlando, or Miami–Nashville. For passengers spending several hours traveling, fresh food could make a meaningful difference to the premium experience. The question is whether American will restore this benefit across its full network, or keep it confined to selected competitive markets.
