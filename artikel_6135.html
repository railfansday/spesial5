Buy Hilton Honors Points With 120% Bonus By November 21: Worth It?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Through November 21, 2026, Hilton Honors is offering a buy points promotion, which is the program’s best-ever bonus on purchasing points! While Hilton Honors ordinarily offers up to a 100% bonus on purchased points, this time around the program is offering up to a 120% bonus, which is a cost of 0.45 cents per point. This could represent a great value for hotel redemptions, especially with an immediate use in mind, as members can purchase up to 528,000 points in one transaction.

As always, I only recommend buying points with a short term use in mind, and if you understand the mechanics of how award redemptions with a program work, including what redemption costs are like.

Link: Buy Hilton Honors points with this promotion with a 120% bonus

Between 12AM ET on October 7 and 11:59PM ET on November 21, 2026, Hilton Honors is offering a bonus on purchased points. Different Hilton Honors members may be targeted for different bonuses, so you’ll want to log into your account to see what you’re eligible for. It would appear that the best offer is for up to a 120% bonus, and that’s structured as follows:

Hilton Honors ordinarily charges one cent per purchased point, including all taxes and fees. That’s before any discounts or bonuses. If you’re eligible for the 120% bonus and maxed out the offer, you’d receive a total of 528,000 points at a cost of $2,400, which is a rate of 0.45 cents per Hilton Honors point.

Ordinarily, Hilton Honors lets members buy up to 160,000 points per account per calendar year, before any bonuses. However, during this promotion, that cap has been increased by 50%, to 240,000 points. With a 120% bonus, that means you could purchase up to 528,000 points.

The purchase of Hilton Honors points is non-refundable, and points purchased don’t count toward elite status. Purchased points will post within 48 hours of finalizing the purchase (though in my experience, much faster than that).

While Hilton Honors has a strict limit on how many points you can buy, note that there are ways to acquire more points than that limit without violating the rules.

Hilton lets members combine points across accounts at no cost, so in reality you could acquire substantially more points by simply buying them across accounts and then consolidating them. Hilton Honors lets members transfer up to 500,000 points, and receive up to two million points, per calendar year. Each member can make up to six transfers to other members per calendar year, and new Hilton Honors members can only transfer or receive points a minimum of 30 days after enrollment.

Hilton also allows points pooling, whereby Hilton Honors members can combine points with up to 10 other members, with the same limitations.

When Hilton Honors offer a promotion on purchased points, it’s most common to see a 100% bonus (the equivalent of a 50% discount). So this 120% bonus is the best-ever promotion we’ve seen from the program, so that’s awesome.

Hilton Honors point purchases are processed by points.com, meaning they don’t count as a hotel purchase for the purposes of credit card spending. That means if you pay with a Hilton credit card, you wouldn’t earn any bonus rewards, so it’s not treated as if it’s Hilton spending.

I’d recommend using a card with which you’re trying to reach a minimum spending requirement, or a credit card that maximizes your return on everyday spending. When in doubt, review which credit cards are best for buying points and miles.

There’s potentially huge value to be had buying Hilton Honors points. Hilton Honors sells points at a reasonable cost, has lots of aspirational properties, has good redemptions with a fifth night free opportunity, and elite status is also easy to earn with the program. Hilton’s partnership with Small Luxury Hotels of the World (SLH) is also awesome, and offers great redemption opportunities.

However, people shouldn’t blindly buy Hilton Honors points, as there are also lots of redemptions where you won’t come out ahead. As I see it, the biggest value with buying Hilton Honors is for aspirational redemptions at expensive properties. Let me give some examples, based on current redemption rates.

For example, take the Waldorf Astoria Maldives, which is bookable for 250,000 points per night for a standard room. At a rate of 0.45 cents per point, you could acquire those points for $1,125. As a point of comparison, the cash rate would be $3,400, so that’s like two-thirds off. That’s pretty amazing!

As another example, take the Waldorf Astoria Los Cabos, which is bookable for 250,000 points per night for a standard room. At a rate of 0.45 cents per point, you could acquire those points for $1,125. As a point of comparison, the cash rate would be close to $2,200, so that’s like half off.

Above I mentioned the Hilton Honors partnership with SLH, so let me give an example there. RiverView Ranch Retreat is located in Montana, and is bookable for 220,000 points per night for a standard room. At a rate of 0.45 cents per point, you could acquire those points for $990. As a point of comparison, the cash rate would be over $3,800 (the screenshot below is for two nights, since that’s the minimum stay), so that’s close to three-fourths off.

Now, while the above are all examples of what a good deal it can be to buy Hilton Honors points, let me of course acknowledge that these are all top-end properties. Unfortunately I find that for your run of the mill Hilton properties, including many limited service hotels, the value just isn’t there in the same way.

For example, take the Hilton Garden Inn Tampa, which is bookable for 45,000 points per night. At a rate of 0.45 cents per point, you could acquire those points for $203. As a point of comparison, the cash rate would be under $180, so there’s absolutely no reason to buy points, only to redeem them for less value than what you paid. You’ll find that to be pretty common, which is why I always recommend crunching the numbers before making a purchase.

Hilton Honors doesn’t have a published award chart, but rather the program has dynamic award pricing. This means that the cost of a standard free night award can vary depending on the day of the week, time of year, etc.

As a general rule of thumb, Hilton Honors free night awards start at 10,000 points per night, and go up to 250,000 points per night (which is a shame, because up until last year, properties maxed out at 150,000 points per night).

Sometimes you’ll see award costs that are way higher than these amounts. In those situations it’s because there’s not a standard room available, so higher pricing kicks in for premium rooms. That’s generally not nearly as good of a use of Hilton Honors points, and you’ll find that some awards even cost one million or more points per night. Obviously that’s a lousy value.

If you have Hilton Honors elite status (which is easy to earn, as I’ll outline below), you receive a fifth night free on award redemptions. This is a way to stretch your points even further, and whenever possible, I’d recommend making your Hilton Honors award stays in increments of five nights.

To take advantage of this, just log into your Hilton Honors elite account, and go through the process of making an award booking for five nights. When you get to the booking page, you should notice that the cost of the fifth night is automatically deducted.

Let me give an example how much value this can add. Say you want to book five nights at the Waldorf Astoria Seychelles Platte Island. At a rate of 150,000 points per night, you’d pay a total of 600,000 points for five nights, which averages out to 120,000 points per night. At a rate of 0.45 cents per point, that’s like the equivalent of paying $540 per night. As a point of comparison, the cash rate would be over $1,800 per night, so that represents absolutely massive savings.

Hilton Honors points don’t expire as long as you have at least some account activity once every 24 months. Eligible account activity includes earning or redeeming points, so buying points would even reset the expiration of your points.

Everyone will value points differently. Based on my latest miles & points valuations, I consider Hilton Honors points to be worth 0.5 cents each, and I tend to value points pretty conservatively. As you can see, the pricing here is potentially better than what I value these points, and there are lots of ways to get outsized value with this promotion.

Hilton Honors doesn’t have blackout dates on award nights, and all standard rooms are available for award redemptions. So this is about as good of a policy as you’ll find with any hotel group when it comes to award room availability.

In the past we’ve generally seen bonuses of up to 100%, so this is a better offer than ever before. In fairness, Hilton Honors points have also been devalued a bit over time. With a short term use in mind, there are plenty of situations where this could represent a good deal.
