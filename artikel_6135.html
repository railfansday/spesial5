India’s Visa Overhaul Fixes the Easy Part of Its Tourism Problem

India’s visa changes remove one hurdle for foreign visitors. But an easier visa alone won’t bring more tourists or get India close to its target of 100 million visitors.

India has simplified its visa system, cutting the number of main categories from 13 to nine and reducing the information required on application forms by as much as 70%, as the government looks to make entry easier for foreign visitors.

The revised system should go live by the end of 2026, the country’s home ministry said.

It is the second overhaul of the visa regime in 13 months. The home ministry said it has also reduced the number of sub-categories from 49 to 23, following an earlier consolidation in October 2025.

The changes come as India struggles to revive inbound tourism even as the government targets 100 million visitors by 2047. The country received 9.2 million foreign tourists in 2025, down 9.4% from 2024 and about 16% below its pre-pandemic peak of 10.9 million in 2019. The overhaul addresses longstanding complaints that India’s visa process is needlessly complicated for ordinary tourists.

India has cut its main visa categories from 13 to nine, reduced sub-categories from 49 to 23, and trimmed the information required on application forms by as much as 70%, with the revised system due to go live by the end of 2026, according to the home ministry. It is the second overhaul in 13 months. A new "Visitor Visa" replaces several narrow categories: V-1 allows multiple entries and stays of up to 30 days covering tourism, business meetings, medical consultations, wellness retreats and short courses, while V-2 carries one-, five- or 10-year validity. Medical and AYUSH categories remain separate but will generally carry one-year multiple-entry validity. The reforms land as inbound tourism falls — 9.2 million foreign arrivals in 2025, down 9.4% from 2024 and about 16% below the 2019 peak of 10.9 million — against a government target of 100 million visitors by 2047 that would require roughly 12% average annual growth for two decades. Tourism ministry additional secretary Suman Billa has pointed to saturated destinations, expensive hotels and gaps in the visitor experience, and India's overseas promotion budget now stands at about INR 35 million, or roughly $380,000. A PHD Chamber of Commerce report estimates arrivals could fall a further 15%-20% this year as the Middle East war disrupts long-haul air corridors.
