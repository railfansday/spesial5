8 of our employee-owners’ favorite family recipes for the holidays  : Celebrate the season with us.

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The holidays are always a big deal here at King Arthur, and this year we’re celebrating with something extra-special: our brand-new Enjoy the Bake campaign, which is all about the joy of baking together.

Enjoy the Bake is a reminder that memories are made around a mixing bowl, and traditions are passed down in the kitchen. In honor of this precious holiday baking season, our employee-owners are sharing the family-favorite recipes that mean the most to them this time of year.

“Every year since I was in grade school, my mom and I would spend the night before Thanksgiving making every type of pie that the family wants. We were a family of six, and everyone's taste was different. What makes it such a lasting memory is how much of a well-oiled machine my mom and I were when working together in the kitchen. She would make pie crust, and I would make all the fillings! Years later, we still make pies for the holidays — but in our own homes — and then come together for the holiday.”

Martina Fleurrey, Customer Experience Supervisor

Get the recipes: Apple Pie, Old-Fashioned Pecan Pie, and Pumpkin Pie

“I grew up with my grandparents making giant batches of pizzelle for the holidays. Their delicate crunch and subtle hint of vanilla are things I'll always associate with the season. My dad now has the pizzelle press, and every holiday, we carry on the tradition, taking turns pouring the batter, pressing, and carefully removing each one.”

“My mom and I entered a gingerbread house contest every year, and making that together was so fun, as was shopping for the candy. More than once, our design was completely themed around a weird gummy shape we couldn't say no to. It was an opportunity to bake gingerbread in increasingly odd shapes, get creative with themes and ingredients, and channel our cheeky humor.” (Learn how to make your own gingerbread house in our new On-Demand Class: Gingerbread House Baking, Building & Decorating.)

Annabelle Shippee, Social Media Strategist

Get the recipe: Construction Gingerbread for Gingerbread Houses

“Each year on Thanksgiving my paternal grandmother, who had 10 kids, would make pies and bring them to each of her local kids. One year, she made two for us, as we were having a big gathering and pumpkin is my dad's favorite pie — one pie was for the gathering and one for just our family. My dad put his personal pumpkin pie upstairs in the closet. About a month later, I hear a screech and come running: My mom found the pie … which was now all moldy. We still laugh about that to this day. The last couple of years, I've been making our Pumpkin Cheesecake Pie as a new elevated version of my grandmother's classic pumpkin pie. It's been a fan favorite, and it's so beautiful to slice.”

Jordan Kilby, Director, Digital Marketing and Customer Data

“One of our family traditions is sausage bread on Christmas Eve. It's served as an appetizer (but to be honest, we all eat enough of it to be a full meal). This is one of my favorite family recipes, not only because it's delicious, but it's also nostalgic. My grandmother made it every year, and my Aunt Millie included it as one of the recipes to pass down in a family cookbook she created. My brother and I have baked it for many years, and now my kids are starting to take over the baking. (We are all still doing the eating.) It's so good that everyone looks forward to it — probably the reason it's lasted four generations.”

“We started having Cranberry Orange Scones on Christmas morning back when I was a little kid. We'd use the King Arthur scone mix and the special scone pan; I loved pressing the dough into the wedge-shaped wells. At one point, we decided that we'd use orange juice as the liquid in the dough instead of milk for extra orange flavor, and that became our ‘secret trick’ that made the scones so delicious. Nowadays, we make the recipe from scratch (even though I still love the box mix) and have scones and coffee while we open stockings on Christmas morning. They're the perfect treat for our family — they feel special without being fussy, so we're able to mix them up and bake them fresh without taking away from our family time.”

“My mom has a late fall birthday, and since childhood she has insisted that instead of a cake she get a pumpkin pie to celebrate. So over the years, in the weeks between her birthday and Thanksgiving, my family ate a lot of pumpkin pie in all varieties: store-bought, homemade, topped with pecans and whipped cream, graham cracker crust and flaky pastry. When my sister's child was born, I went and stayed with her to hang out, clean laundry, and bake comfort food. When my mom showed up to visit, I had a batch of our Test Kitchen's pumpkin pie bars handy. Upon trying them she declared they were ‘the best pumpkin pie she had ever eaten.’ Since then, they have reigned pumpkin pie supreme and been the most requested dessert on the Dyer family holiday table.”

Cecile Dyer, Senior Video Producer, Baking Education
