Trump says FBI investigating Nvidia boss's affair with alleged Chinese spy

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

"He's suffering": Trump answers BBC question about Nvidia's Jensen Huang

Donald Trump has said the FBI is investigating the affair between the boss of Nvidia and an alleged Chinese spy.

The US President said he spoke with chief executive Jensen Huang on Monday following reports that the US believed he had been targeted in a Chinese espionage campaign.

"I asked him that question, 'Did you have any idea she was a spy?' He said 'I had no idea she was a spy' and I really believe him," Trump told reporters.

Beijing has rejected claims that Huang was targeted, with its foreign ministry saying the allegations were "entirely groundless, speculative and intended to smear and malign China".

Nvidia, who has commissioned an independent investigation into the affair involving Alice Huang, alleged to be a Chinese spy, said it had no reason to believe the company's secrets or national security were compromised.

The revelations knocked about $250bn (Â£190bn) off the value of Nvidia during trading on Monday as the firm's share price fell by about 4%.

Trump later told reporters at the White House that the FBI was now looking into the issue, but did not elaborate on the probe or when it was launched. The FBI declined to comment when contacted the BBC.

US intelligence officials believe Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, was a spy for Beijing, according to a New York Times report.

The report said Secretary of State Marco Rubio sent a memo to top officials in Washington last spring warning that the Nvidia boss was in a close relationship with a Chinese spy.

Alice Huang was managing director of Singaporean company Megaspeed, which has had dealings with Nvidia, the company at the centre at the development of AI worldwide through its microchips.

Trump's remarks are the first indication of whether Huang knew about the woman's alleged ties to Chinese espionage.

The American firm run, co-founded by Huang, is also a major focus in the AI rivalry between the US and China, with Washington officials treating the company's exports to Beijing as a matter of national security.

At the White House, Trump was asked multiple times, including by the BBC, on when he was made aware of the relationship. He said he was informed "over a period of time" but was not specific.

Trump said he thought Huang was an "honorable guy and I feel very confident that he will be in good shape" after the investigations.

"I hope there's no problem, I believe he is suffering greatly," the president said.

He said he spoke with Huang on the phone on Monday "just to see if he's feeling alright, how you doing, and I asked him about that and he said he absolutely had no idea that the person in question was a spy".

Nvidia designs advanced computer chips that power AI models and lies at the centre of the US and China's technology race - a key reason why Huang's affair has sparked national security fears.

Trump has raised concerns that allowing China to lead in AI would supercharge Beijing's military and surveillance capabilities.

The president added he was "sure" Chinese leader Xi Jinping would "ask me about it" when they next met.

Huang has admitted he was "unfaithful" to his wife and apologised for hurting her and their children.

He said on Monday that he was "committed to protecting Nvidia and supporting America's national security" and would cooperate with the investigation the company had launched.

Huang has also been requested to appear for a joint closed-door briefing for several Congressional committees next week.

In the letter, the chairmen of the committees said they were seeking a briefing "on Nvidia's security protocols against foreign adversary espionage and your interactions with a publicly reported People's Republic of China (PRC) intelligence asset: Megaspeed executive Alice Huang".

It is unclear whether Huang will appear. Nvidia has declined to comment, while the the BBC has also contacted the committees.

Nvidia has been around for decades, with Huang founding it with two other engineers in 1993, but its rise to one of the world's most valuable and powerful companies has only been recent.

It originally designed computer chips for gaming, but its decision to shift focus to AI development turned it into the powerhouse it is today.

Its chips have become so valuable to the AI-build out that the White House previously imposed controls on where its advanced products could be sold.

The H200, Nvidia's second most advanced semiconductor, had previously been restricted over concerns that it would give China's technology industry an edge over the US.

But Huang has long been against restricting sales to China, arguing that it would be better for the global rollout of AI to be powered by US technology.

In January, the Trump administration said it would allow the company to sell its H200 chips to Chinese customers under certain conditions.
