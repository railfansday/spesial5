Joe Brady’s Bills need a vintage Sean McDermott performance tonight vs. Chiefs

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Some of the gameday columns I've written this season have centered around how Joe Brady's Buffalo Bills need to be different than the Sean McDermott Bills. But not this one, not before this game.

Because after ending the playoff drought, I view the Bills' regular-season success against the Chiefs as one of, if not the crown jewel of the McDermott era.

Five-consecutive wins against the Chiefs was stunning. And how Mahomes struggled vs. McDermott's defense in those games was unfathomable.

And how about Allen vs. the vaunted Steve Spagnuolo defenses in the five contests?

9 passing touchdowns, 7 rushing touchdowns

And if Brady and Co. are to draw any inspiration from a McDermott-era win over the Chiefs, there's no need to look back into the distant past. As the 2025 regular-season victory against the Chiefs was the the finest win in the McDermott era. I genuinely believe that.

Sure, the Bills had the luxury of playing at home.

Other than that, the game had style points galore for Buffalo.

Josh Allen went 23-of-26 for 275 yards with a touchdown pass and no interceptions. That's a completion percentage of 88.4%, a yards-per-attempt average of 10.5, and a passer rating of 123.2.

James Cook had 114 yards rushing at a 4.2 yards-per-carry clip.

Defensively, it was McDermott's magnum opus against Mahomes, the type of dominant performance that certainly evaded Buffalo's defense in playoff meetings with the Chiefs quarterback.

Mahomes went 15-of-34 for 250 yards with no touchdowns and an interception. That's a completion percentage of 44.1%, yards-per-attempt average of 7.4, and a passer rating of 57.2.

The Chiefs averaged 4.0 yards per carry, but only finished with 79 yards on the ground.

At one point, the Bills held a 21-10 lead. There was some semblance of Mahomes magic, as he converted a 4th and 17 in the fourth quarter that led to a touchdown drive to bring the Chiefs within one score. But the Bills were undeniably the better team from start to finish, and we had never witnessed such a sizable dichotomy between Allen and Mahomes in the same game.

Tonight, in what will be a rain-soaked Highmark Stadium, Brady's Bills need to take a page out of McDermott's book against their most bitter rival. A sixth-consecutive win over the Chiefs would be remarkable and move Buffalo closer to something McDermott never did in Orchard Park — earning the No. 1 seed in the AFC playoffs.
