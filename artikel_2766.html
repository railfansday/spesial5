Week 11 Preview: #1 Seed on the Line?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The Minnesota Vikings will travel to Mexico City to face the 8-1 San Francisco 49ers in a game that could very well determine the #1 seed in the NFC playoff bracket in January.

With the Vikings currently tied with the 7-2 Detroit Lions and Chicago Bears in the ridiculously good NFC North, a win over the 49ers could put them in the driver's seat for the rest of the way. After Sunday, the schedule gets much easier, with five of the final seven games at U.S. Bank Stadium. The Vikings also have the benefit of already being 3-1 in the division, with the final two at home. The Lions and Bears each have four division games left. And while the Packers may "just" be 6-4 and destined for last place, they're certainly no pushovers.

A loss to the 49ers certainly wouldn't dash the good guys' hopes for a division crown, as they'd still control their own destiny; it could, for all intents and purposes, slam the door on the coveted #1 seed in the NFC and its lone bye. The 49ers do have some tricky games left, but I'd think the only one they'd be underdogs in would be the Week 16 road game against the Kansas City Chiefs. At effectively two games behind, the Vikings would probably need to be flawless.

Kirk Cousins' return changes the formula dramatically. If the trade didn't happen, I'd give the Vikings offense little to no shot at hitting 20+ points here. Or maybe even 10. And this is with the knowledge that the 49ers defense is kind of living off its reputation a bit from the earlier Kyle Shanahan era.

Don't get me wrong, they're solid. But it's not the unit from the 2019-2022 Robert Saleh-DeMeco Ryans years. That said, new (and highly experienced) coordinator Raheem Morris has them playing well: 11th in points per game (21.8), 6th in total yards (308), 17th in pass yards allowed (226), 2nd in rush yards allowed (82.6), and 20th in 3rd-down conversion rate (41%).

The standout stat is their exceptional success so far at stopping the run. The Vikings average 108.6 per game (19th) but improved in both volume and efficiency last week with Cousins back – e.g., higher YPC (4.2) and seven rushing first downs. Avoiding 3rd-and-long situations will be crucial in this one. Nice gains on 1st and 2nd downs will also open up the play-action game, which I'm sure will be a focus for Kevin O'Connell. Keeping the 49ers defense on their toes will make an already average pass defense even less effective.

Many 49ers sites are focusing on the fact that Cousins already faced this defense a few weeks ago, and the results were…suboptimal: 216 yards, 1 TD, 3 INTs, and a 55.3 QBR. Does this matter? To a certain extent, I suppose. Does it mean history will repeat itself? Definitely not. Different play-caller, better offensive personnel, and almost certainly a more effective game plan, with that game on film to learn from. To be honest, I fully expect this to be a far more challenging unit to face than the Packers were a week prior.

If you're going to go there, it's only fair to point out that Cousins is 2-1 in regular-season games in a Vikings uniform against Shanahan's 49ers, averaging 286.7 yards passing in those games, with 6 TDs and 2 INTs for an overall passer rating of 99.4. One of those was a great home win in 2023, when he passed for 378 yards in a victory without Justin Jefferson against the then 5-2 49ers.

Again, all that aside, getting the running game going early in this one will be critical. Allowing Nick Bosa, Keion White, and company to go wild repeatedly on 3rd-and-long isn't ideal, even if the 49ers' overall pass-rush metrics (e.g., sack rate) this season have been at or below average.

Again, this is your main event of the evening. Strength vs. strength. The NFL's #1 scoring/EPA defense faces the #1 yardage/efficiency offense. We all know how historically dominant the Vikings' defense has been. I touched on it a few days ago. But with apologies to the Bills, Lions, and Bears, this will almost certainly be the season's biggest challenge.

The 49ers are formidable: 2nd in points per game (31.2), 1st in total yards (399.8), 7th in pass yards (255.8), 3rd in rush yards (144), and 1st in 3rd-down conversion rate (52%). The Vikings' rankings in those categories? 1st, 2nd, 2nd, 17th, and 3rd, respectively.

The number that stands out to Vikings fans is that we're 17th in rushing yards allowed per game. There are vulnerabilities, no doubt. This is especially true since it's no secret that Christian McCaffrey is the straw that stirs the drink for the 49ers offense. He's Brock Purdy's security blanket and among the most dangerous dual threats in the NFL. But digging a bit deeper into the numbers, I found that the Vikings are much better (7th) in run EPA/play, at around -0.10. That makes you feel a bit better, but the eye test (for me, anyway) shows the concerns are legitimate.

If the Vikings are effective at stopping the run on early downs, it will play right into our hands. What's unique about Flores' scheme is that it flips traditional schematic reasoning on its head. Logic would dictate that the more aggressive the defense, the more vulnerable a team becomes in the quick, on-time passing game. Instead, the Vikings did a good job of taking that away in the last matchup against Purdy and the 49ers in 2024. ESPN's Jason McCourty and Dan Orlovsky had a great discussion about it that's worth your time.

And here's another post from The Athletic's Alec Lewis that's just ridiculous.

In the end, Kyle Shanahan is 0-5 when Brian Flores is on the opposing sideline, whether as a head coach or a coordinator. 49ers fans will say he's due. It's only a matter of time. Vikings fans will counter that this is the best Flores unit Shanahan has ever faced. Who will be right? We shall see.

The unique wildcard here is the altitude issue in Mexico City and which team's differing methods will prove more effective. I'm not a biologist and have no clue which strategy is better. I'm sure there's science supporting both approaches; why would a team deliberately put itself at such a disadvantage?

What I do know is that after Week 1, the 49ers had the oldest average roster in the NFL (27.49 years). They've since traded for 34-year-old Keenan Allen and 32-year-old DeForest Buckner to play prominent roles. The Vikings have similar issues, specifically with Harrison Smith (37) and Kyle Van Noy (35). You can also note Kirk Cousins (38). But there's a fundamental difference between a quarterback taking three- and five-step drops and a receiver running routes and blocking all game. The same goes for an interior lineman battling in the trenches for four quarters. Depth and scheme comfort/familiarity on both sides of the ball will be important here. Expect more varied snap counts than usual after this one.

I'm cautiously optimistic about this game. Jordan Addison being out again isn't ideal, but it opens opportunities for a Jauan Jennings revenge game, and Myles Price and Tai Felton played well last week, too. The key for Cousins will be to avoid turnovers, and the way to do that is not to press, which I felt was a bit of an issue on his return last week. The interception that counted was one example, and the one that didn't, because his knee was fortunately down, was another. If he takes what's there, nothing more and nothing less, the Vikings offense will be fine. The name of the game is to keep the chains moving and to afford the defense time to recover. Unless there's an unexpected turnover barrage, whoever wins time of possession is likely the victor. That's always true, but it's even more critical given the atmospheric conditions.

On defense, it all comes down to scheming effective pressure on Purdy without committing more than five too often. Listen, Flores is going to bring the heat, but it wouldn't shock me if the blitz rate were slightly reduced from the record-breaking highs if it's not getting home early. Purdy isn't going to be easily rattled and, with time, will quickly make the proper reads to exploit the mid-15 to 20-yard range, where we can sometimes be susceptible. And, of course, having to account for McCaffrey, who can take it to the house from anywhere, on every play could even out the numbers game.

I've got a death grip on the positive vibes since Cousins' return, and I have no plans to let go until the situation warrants it. I want that bye week, and I think we'll stay on track to get it.
