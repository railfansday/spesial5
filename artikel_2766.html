Game Preview: Phoenix looks to carry its defensive momentum into matchup with San Antonio

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Who: Phoenix Suns (3-5) vs. San Antonio Spurs (8-2)

Where: Mortgage Matchup Center — Phoenix, Arizona

The fourth week of the NBA season kicks off Monday night as the Phoenix Suns open a back-to-back by welcoming Victor Wembanyama and the San Antonio Spurs to town for their only visit to Phoenix this season.

The Suns are coming off easily their most impressive victory of the season, having held a potent Houston Rockets offense to fewer than 100 points en route to a much-needed win. While the offense has begun showcasing greater connectivity and accountability, it's the defense that has truly stood out over the past two games. Since last Thursday, Phoenix has averaged 11.0 steals per game, the second most in the NBA during that span.

If the Suns hope to knock off a formidable Western Conference opponent in the defending conference champion Spurs, that defensive intensity will need to continue. San Antonio opened the season with consecutive losses before responding by rattling off eight straight victories, establishing itself as one of the hottest teams in basketball. The Spurs are playing with confidence, executing on both ends of the floor, and beginning to look every bit like the contender many expected them to be.

That being said, Phoenix has been something of a thorn in San Antonio's side over the past few seasons. Since 2023, the Spurs hold a narrow 6-5 advantage in the head-to-head series, illustrating that the Suns have been more than capable of making life difficult for Wembanyama and company.

If Phoenix can carry the defensive energy and collective effort it displayed against Houston into Monday night's matchup, perhaps it can find a way to make things uncomfortable for San Antonio once again.

How can you not have your eyes on Victor Wembanyama in this matchup? The guy is an absolutely domineering presence on the floor, capable of shrinking the court defensively while continuing to expand his offensive skill set with each passing season. Now in his fourth year, he already has a Defensive Player of the Year award on his résumé, and he's making a legitimate case for MVP consideration.

So how exactly do the Suns go about neutralizing one of the premier talents in the NBA? Or is the better strategy to focus their energy on the players surrounding him?

Perhaps the answer lies in limiting the damage inflicted by De'Aaron Fox, Stephon Castle, and Dylan Harper, forcing Wembanyama to shoulder as much of the offensive burden as possible. After all, think back to last week, when Wembanyama delivered one of the most absurd statistical performances we've ever witnessed, pouring in 52 points, grabbing 24 rebounds, and blocking eight shots. And the Spurs won that game by one point.

That's what makes this matchup so fascinating. Even when Wembanyama puts together a performance that looks like something generated by a video game with the difficulty turned all the way down, San Antonio isn't necessarily guaranteed to dominate. There may be an opportunity for Phoenix to survive an extraordinary individual performance if it can prevent the supporting cast from finding its rhythm.

So what I'll be watching tonight is the defensive philosophy Jordan Ott and his staff employ against Wembanyama. Do they dedicate additional resources to containing him, knowing that doing so could create opportunities for San Antonio's other offensive weapons? Or do they accept that Wembanyama is going to get his and concentrate on neutralizing the players around him?

Rebounding, right? Yes, that's generally my default mode. But if I want to dig a little deeper into this matchup, I think three-point shooting will play a significant role if the Suns hope to come away with a victory.

Given Wembanyama's affinity for denying opponents at the basket and his ability to quite literally change the geometry of the game, I expect Phoenix to be firing away from beyond the arc. That's going to present a challenge when Oso Ighodaro is on the floor, because every time he catches the ball above the break, Wembanyama can comfortably sag into drop coverage, knowing he doesn't have to respect Oso as a three-point threat.

While Ighodaro has been a welcome addition to the Suns' starting lineup, his offensive strengths, particularly his effectiveness in dribble handoff actions, become more difficult to utilize when Wembanyama doesn't necessarily have to involve himself in those actions defensively.

So can the Suns consistently generate quality three-point opportunities, and more importantly, can they knock them down? If the answer to both questions is yes, Phoenix has a legitimate chance to win this game.

The Spurs are good. Really good. And it's difficult for me to envision a scenario in which the Suns win this game. What I'm hoping to see is Phoenix maintain a competitive pace and build on what it accomplished against the Houston Rockets, further solidifying an identity that has been defined by early-season inconsistency.

If the Suns can impose aspects of their will, play physically against a San Antonio team that possesses some sizable guards, and remain competitive throughout the game, I'll view that as progress in and of itself. Not a moral victory, but another step in the right direction for a team still trying to prove it can perform with consistency.

But when all is said and done, I expect San Antonio to leave Phoenix with the win.
