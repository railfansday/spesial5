Week 10 Observations: I Like That!

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Vikings began the unexpected second act of their 2026 season with a resounding 30-10 road victory over the rival Green Bay Packers. Between Kirk Cousins' emotional return and the defense's historic season, it's hard to imagine a more memorable and satisfying result.

The Daily Norseman crew did their usual stellar job recapping the rewarding victory here, here, and here. Aside from the obvious candidates (e.g., Turner, Van Ginkel, Redmond, Smith, Wilson, Van Noy, Price), I was particularly encouraged by contributions from Caleb Banks, Elijah Williams, Gavin Bartholomew, Tai Felton, and Jakobe Thomas. Maybe we do have some depth, huh?

Like He Never Left. Kirk Cousins parachuted into Vikings practice on Wednesday and started on Sunday. As unheard of as that is, given the chaos that's defined the past two seasons at quarterback, seeing him run out to the huddle for the opening series felt totally normal, as if time stood still. I was eagerly soaking it all in.

Even with a few days of prep and a playbook that had changed since 2023, Cousins' familiarity with the scheme and system was evident from the get-go. While the numbers weren't overwhelming – 21 for 31, 207 yards, 2 TDs, and an interception – the eye test was passed with flying colors.

Of course, not all passes and not all yards gained on them are created equal. How and when matter. That's where Cousins immediately changed things. EPA per play improved (+0.04), and the Vikings offense converted 8-of-16 third downs. This included a 3rd-and-23 TD to Myles Price and a critical 3rd-and-12 conversion to Justin Jefferson, who also saw a season-high number of targets. Coincidence? I think not. This was a notable improvement over the 3-for-10 third-down conversion rate in the loss to the Lions and the 3-for-11 in the ugly win against the Colts. The Bills game? Let's not go there.

It's also no fluke that Sunday's game saw the Vikings' offensive time of possession jump to over 35 minutes. This was a marked improvement over the abysmal 17 minutes against the Bills and 24 minutes against the Lions, respectively, in the team's two losses. It was also much better than the 28 minutes in the ugly home win over the hapless Colts. It's almost as if the mere threat of an effective passing game suddenly makes running the ball easier. Weird.

It's just one week, and time will tell, but based on interviews and body language, this seems like a different Kirk Cousins to me. One who is not only more confident and comfortable but also grateful for the opportunity to change the narrative of his career forever – something that seemed out of reach just days earlier. All made easier by what's shaping up to be a historic defense by his side. I basically said it all last week:

Cousins realizes the feel-good, storybook ending is within reach. Reports say the Raiders left the trade decision in his hands. He knows. This is his last, best chance. A Kirk Cousins with a Super Bowl ring would not only have a legitimate Hall of Fame resume (the statistics will be there), but he'd also have a statue outside U.S. Stadium to immortalize his role in ending the long-suffering anguish of a tortured fan base.

I've Seen Enough. This Defense is Generational. After another impressive performance against a top-flight offense, it's time to discuss the 2026 Minnesota Vikings' defense in historic terms.

While national media talking heads usually write narrative checks on the Packers that they inevitably can't cash, it was worse coming into Sunday's game because the Packers had won five of their last six, including a dominant win over a fraudulent Patriots team living off last season's rep the week prior. Instead, they got another kind of check: a reality check.

The eight sacks and four turnovers didn't do justice to the torture Brian Flores inflicted on Matt LaFleur, Jordan Love, and the entire Packers offense throughout the afternoon. It was yet another piece of the puzzle that, when complete, could look record-breaking.

The comparison to the 1985 Bears is impressive enough, especially given the changes made to benefit offenses over the past 20 years.

Enjoy the rest of the ride, folks. Soak it all in, because this isn't normal. It's thrilling, but it's not guaranteed to last beyond this season. Brian Flores will get another head-coaching opportunity someday, and key players such as Blake Cashman, Andrew Van Ginkel, Isaiah Rodgers, and Kyle Van Noy will be free agents. Meanwhile, Jalen Redmond is a restricted free agent, and it's almost certainly Harrison Smith's last hurrah.

Third Time a Charm? Since 1990, the Vikings have made the playoffs 17 times, tying for 7th in that span. Not too shabby. Each year, the purple neurons in my brain lit up, distorting reality into something that justified the belief that they could not only make the Super Bowl but win it.

My intuition has proven far more reliable. It's equipped with the right defenses to filter out most delusions and purify it all. It shocks me back into reality, takes off the blinders so I can see clearly, and acknowledges the issues you know deep down will inevitably dash the dream.

They truly aligned only twice: 1998 and 2009. Not 2000. Not 2017. My gut didn't trust the defense in 2000 or the offense in 2017. In the end, both sides of the ball were an issue, but that doesn't matter now.

What matters is… things are close to aligning with this team again after 17 years. The convergence of multiple elements (i.e., the defense and Cousins' return) for a magical season appears to be in place. There are still eight games left, so that could change before the playoffs, and that very well could happen. But I've never been as close to the long-awaited third instance as I was after this impressive win in Green Bay. A win, or even a razor-thin loss, to the 8-1 49ers next week will put it within arm's reach.

If so, let's hope the third time is the charm and that the season ends with a third act, one that culminates in a Lombardi Trophy triumphantly lifted in the air.
