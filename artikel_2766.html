Are 49ers due for a reality check? Red flags emerged in wild win over Cowboys

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The San Francisco 49ers second travelogue trip of the season got off to an exciting start on Sunday, surviving a wild - and somewhat alarming - shootout with the Dallas Cowboys, 46-40.

Now they head from the plains of Texas to the Rocky Mountains and then take a hard left to go south of the border to Mexico City, during their second lengthy, multi-country trip.

The good news is the 49ers are tied for a league best 8-1. The better news - for the coaches - is that, during this long week away from home, the 49ers have plenty to keep them occupied. Like tightening up that problematic defense. Improving the pass rush. And keeping Brock Purdy upright.

Twelve days after Democrats turned Texas blue, 49ers fans turned Dallas red, taking over AT&T Stadium and making Jerry Jones' palace sound like their home stadium. For the fans' next trick, they'll try to turn Mexico red.

"Our fans are awesome," head coach Kyle Shanahan said. "Wish I could give them all game balls."

The 49ers have cruised to an 8-1 record, and the most impressive part of the first half of the season has been vanquishing their most heated rivals away from their home stadium: the Rams in Australia, the Seahawks in Seattle and, on Sunday, the Cowboys, in Arlington, Texas.

But the upcoming second half of the season holds some potential concerns. And Sunday's split personality game underscored those worries.

"It was sort of a weird game," Purdy said.

While the 49ers offense looked like the most elite on the planet on Sunday, racking up 584 yards, the 49ers defense couldn't say the same thing. The 49ers swamped the Cowboys from the end of the second quarter until early in the fourth quarter, outscoring Dallas 28-3 in that stretch.

But then the 49ers found themselves in an uncomfortably tight situation, giving up three Dallas touchdowns in the final 9:08 of the game and having to survive an onside kick, their own fumble (that they recovered), a three-and-out and a Dallas attempt at a Cal Bears-style lateral-fest comeback.

"Anytime you get 40 dropped on you, you're going to be upset," said linebacker Fred Warner. "You've got to look at the tape and see why that was the case."

There will be a lot of tape to watch in the coming week, while getting acclimated to altitude in Colorado Springs. Though the first half of the season has gone almost perfectly, there is still a long road ahead. Next week, in Estadio Azteca, the 49ers will face Minnesota, a team that - unlike the Cowboys - will present a ferocious defense. And, with the addition of Kirk Cousins, a team that suddenly looks like a true Super Bowl contender.

(Cousins, easily sliding back into the Vikings offense and leading them to a victory in Green Bay, and Keenan Allen, with five receptions for 61 yards for the 49ers - both just days after being traded - are belying the cliche that learning an NFL playbook is akin to nuclear physics).

After Minnesota, the 49ers will face the Seahawks at home, go east for another 10 a.m. body clock time at the New York Giants (it didn't go well in Atlanta), and then host the somewhat resurgent Rams at Levi's (both the Seahawks and Rams beat the 49ers at Levi's the past two seasons). They also have a game against the Kansas City Chiefs at Arrowhead in late December.

In other words, their current position is great. But nothing is guaranteed.

The five sacks the 49ers allowed were a concern. After not being sacked at all the first four games of the season, Purdy has been sacked 13 times in the last five games. Conversely, the 49ers have only sacked the quarterback 16 times this season, just once on Sunday and zero times in four of their nine games.

And then there are those big defensive lapses that the team had late in the game. Yes, Dallas has a potent offense. But the 49ers defense is supposed to be better than what it showed.

This is the 49ers' best start since 2019, the first year that Shanahan led the team to a Super Bowl. There are so many of the same key players on this team as were on the roster back then: the team's "get the band back together" theme has brought back critical members of that original team, like Dre Greenlaw, Deebo Samuel and DeForest Buckner.

The biggest difference now is that all those men know just how difficult it is to get to a Super Bowl. How rare it is to even be on a winning team and to have a rewarding playing experience.

There's a lot made about how old this team is. But with all that age and all those reps comes important experience, vital perspective. This is not a season to waste, because it may never, ever come again.

Five days holed up in the Rocky Mountains, learning to breathe at elevation and trying to fix the things that could make a difference between another win and a loss? Sounds like a perfect travelogue opportunity.
