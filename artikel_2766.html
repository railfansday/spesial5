6 takeaways from Detroit Lions’ Thanksgiving loss to Chicago Bears

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Here are six takeaways after the Detroit Lions' frustratingly blown game against the Chicago Bears.

The tipping point of this game rested on a couple of big coaching decisions by Dan Campbell. On the Lions' final drive of the game, Detroit got pretty aggressive, opting to prioritize moving the ball rather than bleeding clock. After a big Jahmyr Gibbs run got the Lions into field goal position with under three minutes left, Detroit opted to pass on second down, resulting in an incomplete pass and stopping the clock. Then, facing a fourth-and-4, Campbell opted to go for it rather than attempt a 51-yard field goal to potentially make it a six-point game. T.J. Edwards made a fantastic play to stop Gibbs short.

Analytics were generally in favor of the fourth down decision, and we've seen the Lions put the game away so many times on offense because of their aggression. Personally, I think it was the right decision, especially with the way the defense was flailing.

If there was a mistake on the coaching staff, I believe it was on the previous series. The Lions faced a third-and-1 in Bears territory, and opted to run a play-action pass that was ultimately intercepted. The Lions have been so good at third-and-shorts lately with the running game and/or a quarterback sneak, and I wish they would've kept on that. But at the same time, the Lions should have been able to convert there with the play call they had, but they didn't execute.

And let's not forget Campbell's decision at the end of the first half to accept the penalty that resulted in a safety, erasing a fumble recovery. It was a controversial decision, but it netted the Lions nine total points—a big gain for Detroit.

You take the good and bad, but there were far more bigger issues that caused this loss, including…

The Lions offense was disjointed early because of an odd string of false start penalties. Yes, maybe Jeffery Simmons was playing some games with the snap, but Juice Scruggs still has to stay disciplined.

The bigger issue is the taunting penalties. Christian Mahogany got tagged for one that moved an extra point back and was ultimately missed. That one point changed how both teams played the rest of the game. Anthony Lucas cost the Lions 15 critical yards of field position after they had just gotten a turnover on downs late in the game, as well. On the final defensive drive, D.J. Reed committed a critical 15-yard facemask penalty.

Nine penalties for 70 yards is too much, but these penalties particularly hurt because they came in critical moments.

Detroit has been doing a decent job keeping the boat afloat without captain Jack Campbell, but the damn broke on Thursday. Caleb Williams was constantly checking down to his running backs and tight ends, and the linebackers were nowhere to be seen in coverage. D'Andre Swift finished with seven catches for 97 yards, Sam Roush had five for 31, and it probably would've been even worse if Colston Loveland didn't get injured earlier in the game.

Detroit also had their worst run defense game in quite some time, and a lot of times it felt the linebackers were out of position. Chicago managed 146 yards on 24 carries for 6.1 yards per carry and three touchdowns.

It sucks the Lions squandered such an incredible game for Jahmyr Gibbs, who rushed for 205 yards and three touchdowns on 21 carries. He was electric, and he finally broke out the explosive runs we've been waiting to see.

He has already surpassed last year's rushing totals with 1,303 yards, his 5.4 yards per carry is currently the second-best in his career, and his 16 rushing touchdown ties a career-high—with six games remaining.

He's going to be fun to watch for a long, long time.

While he—and the entire Lions defense—disappeared a bit in the second half, Lions rookie Derrick Moore had a bigger impact on this game than anyone in the first half. He tallied two sacks—one strip-sack that resulted in a safety—plus a punch-out forced fumble that ended a threatening Bears drive. He was single-handedly carrying the defense in the first half, and he is off to a monster start to his career.

His 6.5 sacks on the season is second only to DJ Wonnum on the team, and he's now tied for the team-lead in forced fumbles. Oh, and his name is now permanently etched with some NFL legends.

For as frustrating as that game was, it was also pretty damn entertaining. The Lions and Bears showed they are very evenly matched, and while Chicago has the edge now, there is still plenty of football to be had. Detroit is just a game back of Chicago, and with a Week 17 matchup at Soldier Field, that can be made up in a literally one game.

Detroit is going to regroup after a tough 11-day stretch here, they're going to win more games and make the playoffs. And everything remains in front of them despite this disappointing outcome.
