The 9 Walking Shoes WH Editors Love the Most

These are the pairs we reach for again and again.

It’s easy to underrate walking as one of the best things we can do for our fitness—after all, it doesn’t get your heart rate up like running or a HIIT workout. But that doesn’t mean you’re not getting tons of physical and mental health benefits from walking, whether you’re following a walking workout plan or not.

And perhaps one of the best things about walking is that it doesn’t require any specialized equipment or gear except a really good pair of comfy walking shoes. Luckily for you, we’re constantly testing walking shoes, and we’ve narrowed down all the options out there to a list of pairs we reach for again and again. We’ve used our testing experience, plus picked the brains of podiatrists and other experts to compile our favorites in this guide.

If you’ve logged even a couple of miles in a not-so-great pair of walking shoes, you know wearing the wrong footwear can make what started out as a nice walk turn into an uncomfortable slog fast (we’re talking blisters, achy arches, plantar fasciitis, and more).

The right walking shoes, on the other hand, can help you walk farther, feel happier while doing it, and gush to friends about your sneakers (ask us how we know). Plus, select styles can help prevent injury or overpronation (when your arches collapse and cause your foot to angle inward), says podiatrist Chanel Perkins, DPM, an assistant professor of foot and ankle podiatry in the Department of Orthopedic Surgery and Rehabilitation at the University of Texas Medical Branch.

After testing dozens of walking shoes for our 2026 Fitness Awards, the Adrenaline GTS 25 took the top spot for its near-perfect balance between cushioning and stability. Our staffers who tested it say it’s comfortable enough to walk in for hours. “It offers just the right amount of cushioning for a bouncy step without compromising on support for my flat, wide feet,” says WH beauty editor Nicole Saunders.

That comfort starts with the 25’s updated midsole, which feels plusher than previous models without losing an ounce of stability. It still features the brand’s signature GuardRails technology—firmer along the sides and softer through the center—to gently lock in your stride. In our testing, Brooks continues to set the standard for stability, making this a standout pick for anyone whose feet tend to roll inward while walking.

When it comes to fit, the 25 has a roomier toe box up front and a secure (but still comfortable) heel lock back, which helps cut down on friction and lowers your chances of blisters during long walks. The higher 10 mm drop creates a slightly forward-leaning stride that takes pressure off the calves and Achilles, making the 25 a smart pick for anyone with tight lower legs or Achilles sensitivity. If you prefer a flatter feel underfoot, though, we recommend something lower profile like the Hoka Mach 6.

At 9.5 ounces, it’s one of the heavier options on this list, but that added weight comes from the stability tech and structured testing. We barely noticed the difference, especially once we got moving.

Diana Rogers, DPM, a podiatric foot and ankle surgery specialist in Crystal Lake, Illinois, and Casey Pidich, MD, a podiatrist and foot and ankle surgeon based in the Bronx, New York, both recommend Hoka’s Clifton series to patients for arch support, so you know it's a good one.

Pidich loves that the design encourages great form—the sole ensures that your feet rock off the ground rather than push off your toes. Rogers specifically recommends the shoe for her plantar fasciitis (a condition where you feel pain in the arch) patients for this exact reason. “The lightweight feel on this shoe propels feet forward rather than making them do extra work,” she says.

WH style editor Shannon Zitz found in her testing that the foam absorbed impact well on both pavement and the treadmill. “My feet—unfortunately plagued by plantar fasciitis—never felt fatigued wearing this shoe,” she said, noting that her stride felt smooth and natural.

The Clifton 11 is available in wide and extra-wide sizing, making it a good fit for a wide variety of walkers.

For those who appreciate a good slip-on shoe, the Skechers Go Walk Flex Azriel’s lightweight, breathable mesh upper is great for sweaty feet, while hovering around a bargain price of about $60 (and on sale for cheaper sometimes). The ultra-cushioned midsole offers great arch support, too.

“This is a solid no-frills walking shoe that’s perfect for errands, quick hot girl strolls, or for walking your dog,” says WH style editor Shannen Zitz. “I love that it slips on easily while still having the appearance of a classic sneaker with bungee laces that never come undone.”

Zitz was skeptical about whether the slip-on would be supportive and secure enough, but the breathable, stretchy mesh offered surprisingly good compression around her feet.

“It keeps my feet locked in and has never caused any pain or blisters,” she says. “While I wouldn’t log miles in this shoe, it is cushioned and supportive enough for long periods of standing or casual wear.” However, she finds the style a bit orthopedic-looking, although the white colorway can look cute with jeans or dresses.

Asics is considered a classic, all-around great brand for walking, especially if you struggle with overpronation (ankles that roll inward) or have flat feet. Take the Gel-Kayano 32, which has thick foam borders on each side to encourage proper alignment and walking gait. Deep heel cups also help keep arches supported—there’s a reason this shoe made our best plantar fasciitis walking shoe guide.

Fitness contributor Nicolette Accardi is a huge fan of the firm, stiffer midsole that still maintains massive plushness with its signature gel cushioning system for superior shock absorption. “My landings feel like I’m hovering above ground, which says a lot given I stomp on hard pavement daily. I actually find it more cushioned than the Hoka Mach 6,” she says. The heel-to-toe drop is 8 millimeters, which Accardi says is a bit high for her liking, but ideal if you want to take all the pressure away from your arches.

The Hoka Mach 7 is Zitz’s personal favorite. While it’s technically a running shoe, she loves it for recovery and walking. “I’ve been living in this sneaker since returning from a week-long hiking trip where I logged about 20K steps per day,” she says. “It’s flexible, lightweight, and cushioned—it basically feels like a hug for my super tired feet.” The cushioning also doesn’t feel bulky compared to other Hoka models, such as the Bondi 8.

The previous iteration, the Mach 6, took home our 2025 Fitness Award for top walking shoe thanks to its blend of responsive cushioning, breathability, and lightweight construction—and the Mach 7 has built on that winning formula with an updated outer and grippier bottom. Zitz tested this shoe after it first dropped, walking more than 150 miles in it—and not once did she feel even a bit of foot fatigue or soreness.

Another brownie point? It comes in wide sizing. Some WH editors find standard Hoka sizing pretty narrow, but the wide fit has adequate space in the toe box, so you can walk for hours without experiencing any friction. For extra room overall, podiatrist Shital Sharma, DPM, a podiatrist at Pinewood Family Care Co. in New Jersey, recommends going up a half-size in all walking sneakers because your feet tend to swell and expand from physical activity.

Lined with mesh to make this an ultra-breathable sneaker, the Pegasus is a winner because it has a slim, low heel that Perkins says is awesome for maintaining a smooth walking cadence. We love that the 42 is lighter than the previous model and has a curvier toe box that gives more toe spring, a smoother ride, and a roomier fit.

WH Fitness Director Jacqueline Andriakos has worn countless versions of the Nike Pegasus for both running and walking and has logged hundreds of miles in it. “I find the sole a bit on the firmer side, and the mesh upper really hugs and forms to your individual foot shape,” she says. “The shoe feels perfectly snug and secure as you step without being too constricting.”

She does flag that the tongue sometimes gets folded up and tucked under the laces in a slightly uncomfortable way. “I fix this by being really diligent when I put the shoes on, making sure to pull the tongue taut and make sure it's lying flat before tying my shoes.”

The shoe is super breathable and lightweight, which makes it a great option for spring and summer, but less so for cold or wet weather. Overall, this is Andriakos’ go-to walking shoe recommendation for her friends and family—and so far she's only heard happy feedback from loved ones who have purchased a Pegasus shoe.

The latest edition of the Fresh Foam X 1080 wows us with its inclusive sizing, with narrow, standard, wide, and extra-wide fits available to accommodate all feet.

“I have a hard time finding walking shoes that are both cushioned and supportive enough for my high arches and sensitive balls of the feet, but that are also narrow enough,” Andriakos says. “This is absolutely perfect if you deal with any foot pain issues or struggle with sneakers being a bit too wide—I have walked over 100 miles in these at this point and have never even had a sore or tender spot from rubbing or chafing (they also required zero break-in period).”Andriakos also finds the laces are very secure and don’t come untied easily. The only downside? “When I’ve worn it on rainy days, I notice my feet will feel damp and it takes a bit to dry out.” To help combat that, she just throws it in the dryer (which, to be clear, isn’t recommended by the brand, but she found it hasn't damaged the silhouette!).

The On Cloudtilt Remix is built for walking, thanks to its sequence of collapsing “cloud” foam to deliver a smooth, plush ride. It has maximum cushioning and bounce, compared to lots of other walking shoes we’ve tested, making it a solid pick for walkers and casual runners who prefer a softer, cushier feel underfoot.

Zitz recently added this pair to her collection and appreciates how lightweight and springy it feels. “I feel like I have such a pep in my step when I wear it—which is basically all the time since it’s so stylish,” she says. “I love the layers of see-through mesh on the upper, which looks super modern and clean. I’ve been wearing this shoe nonstop with capris, leggings, and athletic shorts.”

This style features Helion Superfoam (a blend of firm and flexible foam components) that delivers bounce without a lot of bulk. It also has a moderate 7 mm heel-to-toe drop, meaning it distributes weight evenly throughout the lower body, making it a pretty good pick for most walkers. It’s the perfect shoe that combines all our must-have walking shoe features into one sleek kick.

The New Balance 608 V5 Trainer has over 38,000 reviews on Amazon, and for good reason. The brand integrated its signature ABZORB midsole (a blend of substantial foam cushioning with compression resistance), which is designed to lessen the blow on joints when walking.

The 608 has tons of shock absorption which makes it great for neutral to high arches. But since it lacks stability features, it’s not our top choice for flat feet. We do like that it comes in narrow, standard, and wide sizing.

Ideally, walking shoes will assist with your gait (your walking pattern) instead of just cushioning impact. Your shoe should also be somewhat stiff—the more it bends, the less stability it will have.

To test the flexibility, hold the shoe at the toe box and heel counter and try to fold the shoe in half by pushing on its ends to see how sturdy it is. If it folds in half or twists with ease, it’s too flexible, says podiatrist Elizabeth Daughtry, MD, a podiatrist and foot and ankle specialist with Foot and Ankle Specialists of the Mid-Atlantic in Cary, North Carolina.

She also adds that seeking out a shoe with a boat-like (rockered) shape, rigid midsole, and firm heel counter can help stabilize feet, control overpronation (collapsing arches), and prevent heel striking.

Look for walking shoes that have an outsole with more rigidity in the midsole for stability. Different feet have different needs, so it’s important to have insoles that can be removed in case you use orthotics. “Most shoes don’t offer adequate support alone,” says Dr. Daughtry. “You should supplement most shoes with a more custom-molded orthotic, which your local podiatrist can make for you, as this provides adequate support and helps with shock absorption, while also allowing for additional cushioning.”
