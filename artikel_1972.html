Ex-CEO updates on Leicester City 'terminating' agreement

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Leicester City cannot afford to terminate their deal with their front-of-shirt sponsor BC Game any time soon.

That is according to former Aston Villa chief executive Keith Wyness, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who believes that it will be "very hard" for the Foxes to enforce a "morality clause" in the agreement.

This comes after the gambling company BC Game recently named a porn actress as one of their new ambassadors, a move which has quickly been condemned by fan group The Foxes Trust.

However, Wyness believes that it will be "very difficult" in a legal sense for Leicester to terminate the deal, and also pointed out that they need the money it is providing right now.

The League One outfit are struggling on the pitch and remain in turmoil at board level behind the scenes.

Long-time owners King Power are working to sell the club, but are making slow progress with their move to cash in on the East Midlands outfit.

Villa's former chief Wyness - who served as CEO at Villa Park between 2016 and 2018 and now runs a consultancy business advising elite clubs - believes that Leicester may be forced to just accept the "publicity" that has come with BC Game's move.

The Foxes' fans have raised questions regarding the morality of the deal and the image it presents, but Wyness is adamant that the club are not in a position to be axing partners.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider's Inside Track podcast, he believes that the club must simply sweep the story under the carpet before it becomes a "distraction."

"No, I think they have to stay with it. I think they need the money. And I think that it will be very difficult legally to terminate the deal as well," he said.

"Normally, if there is a morality clause, which often is in there, it would be about the behaviour of the company itself. And if she is legally hired as an ambassador by the company and she's not done anything wrong in her past, it would be very hard to enforce a morality clause, in my opinion.

"So I don't think they'd have great grounds to terminate. Look, if I were them, I'd take the publicity. I'd move on. Leicester need the money. It's a distraction.

"But look, I'm afraid, as I said, we've seen stories of clubs who are desperate for sponsors and revenue. And it's not always easy to dictate the behaviour of your sponsors."

Football Insider revealed back in October that Leicester had held internal talks about potentially replacing Russell Martin as manager.

The Foxes had their eyes set on former Wolves boss Rob Edwards, who recently turned down an approach from Championship strugglers Burnley.

However, Leicester have since embarked on a four-match unbeaten run, which has lifted them up to tenth in League One amid the latest international break.

Things are beginning to look brighter for Martin and his group, but they still have huge expectations to fulfill this season.
