The benchmarks for the new models may be better than before, but people aren’t all that happy with the performance in some areas.

OpenAI dropped two new AI models this week in the form of GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol, each surpassing benchmarks of their predecessors, but people who have begun using them haven’t all walked away very happy.

A new AI model seems to drop almost every couple of days from one of the big tech giants, each improving on the previous version in one way or another. However, OpenAI’s latest and greatest are getting some pushback from developers and early testers. For the everyday user, the 6.2 Sol and Astra should perform more than serviceably, but those looking to dig in, test and use the new models for more professional work may run into some hiccups they’re not used to, according to some feedback.

Ethan Mollick, associate professor and director of the Generative AI Lab at the University of Pennsylvania’s Wharton School of Business, said in a LinkedIn post that GPT-6.2 Astra was “harder to steer and work with,” and “frequently seemed ‘lazy’,” compared with competitor models. Another brutal assessment on LinkedIn came from Jason J. Fleagle, head of AI at NETSYNC: “I think GPT-6.2 has to be one of the worst AI models OpenAI has ever released.”

However, there might be more to the story than that. OpenAI Codex leader Thibault Sottiaux, known as Tibo, mentioned in an X post that early builds of 6.2 Sol and Astra were released to influencers some time ago, that updates and changes were made to the models within the past couple of days, and that he assumes no retesting was done since. Only time and more testing will show whether these improvements make much of a meaningful difference.

OpenAI didn’t immediately reply to a request for comment.
