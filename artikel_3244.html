‘Artificial’ Filmmaker Luca Guadagnino On Whether AI Is Humanity’s Doomsday & Sam Altman’s Arsenal – Crew Call Podcast

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Unlike Sony’s recent theatrical release of The Social Reckoning, which arguably didn’t resonate with the current tech era we’re living in, Neon is unwrapping Luca Guadagnino‘s Artificial about Open AI co-founders Sam Altman, Ilya Sutskever and Elon Musk at a time when we fear the technology will end us.

Now whether that’s in regards to our existence or our financial livelihoods is another question.

Earlier this week, former OpenAI alum and Altman rival Dario Amodei’s Anthropic went public creating a $1.18 trillion company. However, shares fell from its $111 day-of-market price to $88.91 EOD Friday. Neon is shrewdly opening Artificial limited on Christmas Day (the perfect time to launch a sophisticated adult skewing awards contender), and in the run-up to that release, following premieres at New York Film Festival and BFI London Film Fest, along with current awards voter screenings, anticipation is boiling for the pic evident in its current 95% fresh Rotten Tomatoes critics rating. As a filmmaker who knows something about love with the Zendaya starring tennis love triangle pic, Challengers, and Oscar winning gay romance, Call Me By Your Name, we spoke with Guadagnino on the latest Crew Call as to what pulled him into the OpenAI-verse. Watch on Deadline

Well before its theatrical release, Artificial generated a lot of controversy as its original studio, Amazon MGM Studios, backed away from the movie out of fear of OpenAI. That’s because Amazon has a $50 billion investment in the Altman non-profit.

However, unlike the Facebook camp back when Social Network was getting released in 2010, there’s been zero ill will coming from Altman or OpenAI about Guadaganino’s film. In fact, you could say they’re great sports.

In a sit-down with the Vanity Fair podcast, Altman says he “did not” coerce Amazon to drop Artificial. “I don’t know anyone else who did,” he added, “I didn’t speak to Jeff Bezos.”

In fact, Guadagnino brazenly shot the pic outside Altman’s house in San Francisco. The filmmaker tells us about that. For Altman, he was giddy about running into his onscreen doppelgänger, Andrew Garfield: “One morning, I was walking down the street to get coffee and there were all these cameras and there was a guy dressed exactly like me with the same backpack, walking so much, I felt like I was in a weird glitch or a simulation.”

Altman emphasized to Vanity Fair that Garfield “did walk like me.”

While Altman hasn’t seen Artificial, his reaction to the trailer is, “I don’t think they’re going for accuracy, so in that sense it doesn’t bother me. They’re trying to make a good movie and maybe it will be good. It won’t be good as an accurate depiction, except for the walk. I’m sure they’ll get other parts right at least accidentally.”

One of the jaw dropping moments shown in the Artificial trailer is Altman having a bunker in his wine country estate loaded with guns.

“That is an example of an inaccuracy,” Altman told Vanity Fair, “There’s no bunker with firearms on the wall.”

“I have a survivalist mindset,” Altman admitted, “but I don’t have a setup that is depicted in that trailer.”

Guadagnino tells Deadline, “I have validated the validating information that has been vetted: He does have guns.”

While the filmmaker experienced zero pushback from OpenAI and Altman during production on the Simon Rich script, he says “We were very careful that everything that was in the movie had to be approved legally, so that we were not bending facts and reality for our narrative purposes. But, because the truth in this case is more extraordinary than any fiction, that was okay. Because we had the drama, we had the juice.”

When it came to Amazon exiting Artificial, Guadagnino had a Zen mindset: “I was cool because I like disruptions, very much.”

“Their decision to give the movie to us to find another home was truly honorable, because they could have written off the movie and (said) ‘Bye-bye’.”

As far as Artificial being a hot potato that no studio wanted to handle, except NEON, Guadagnino asserts that “All the speculation about nobody wanting the movie; I like to think that there was the need (by the media) to fill blank pages with nothing.”

For off the bat, per Guadagnino, NEON was first, front and center in their passion to takeover the project after CAA screened it for them. Listen above for more details.

When it comes to the paranoia out there that AI is the catalyst for human extinction in ten years, the filmmaker tells Deadline, “I don’t have trust and I don’t have faith in humans.”

His suspicions about AI stem from growing up during the Cold War. “I remember very well in 1985 when Chernobyl happened,” he adds.

And there’s myriad reasons for concerns about AI, from a Chatbot being at the center of 16-year old Adam Raine’s suicide, to AI trespassing and impostering another company’s security space

We also talk with the Call Me By Your Name Best Picture Oscar nominee on why Andrew Garfield was the right guy to play Altman, and the Open AI Boss’ sincerity when it comes to creating guardrails for the technology.
