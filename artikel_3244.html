Indonesian police reportedly found no Nvidia chips at Megaspeed’s unit

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The Straits Times says the Megaspeed raid came at the FBI’s request, before Jensen Huang quit. Companies tied to Alice Huang have also bought into DeepSeek, The Information reported.

Indonesian police searched the local office of Megaspeed, the Singapore cloud company at the centre of the Nvidia scandal, The Straits Times reported on Monday. They found server racks but no Nvidia chips. The FBI had asked for the search earlier this year to trace chips Megaspeed bought.

The paper’s source is a senior Indonesian government official who asked not to be named. It calls the Megaspeed raid the first known enforcement action in Indonesia tied to the case. TNW has not independently verified the report.

Officers from the police’s criminal investigation department visited PT Megaspeed International Indonesia, the official said. Nobody seemed to have unpacked or used the racks.

The official alleged that Megaspeed entities shipped most of their Nvidia hardware to Indonesia. Investigators believe much of it then went on to China, possibly by private jet, he said. The head of the police’s international relations division declined to comment to the paper.

The raid came before Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia’s chief executive on 12 November. He had admitted an affair with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive whom US officials suspect of spying for China. Megaspeed says she left in August 2025 and that it runs a lawful business.

Singapore’s digital minister, Josephine Teo, raised Megaspeed with US Deputy Secretary of State Christopher Landau in Washington last week. Both sides agreed to keep talking, the Straits Times reported.

Singapore has barred Alice Huang from returning, and its inquiry into Megaspeed and six other people goes on. Malaysia is investigating AI chips linked to the company. On 13 November it said it is working with Singapore and the US against chip smuggling.

Companies tied to Alice Huang also invested in DeepSeek, Claudia Chong reported for The Information on 13 November. The two firms are Century Litong and its Shanghai-listed parent, Jiangsu Lettall Electronics. The Information describes them as big suppliers of black-market Nvidia Blackwell chips in China.

DeepSeek staff introduced the two firms to its investors as critical to chip supply, according to the report. They joined DeepSeek’s 105.5bn yuan ($15.8bn) round through vehicles that investors such as Monolith Management set up. Lettall put in 95mn yuan this way. The full amount from the group is unclear.

Eduardo Baptista at Reuters reviewed more than 150 Chinese procurement notices, contracts and disclosures. He found projects worth over 23bn yuan ($3.2bn) built around restricted Nvidia H100, H200 and H800 chips. Reuters found no link between those projects and Megaspeed. Nvidia and China’s commerce ministry did not immediately respond to Reuters.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent says other departments wanted Megaspeed on the US trade blacklist for months, but Commerce had the pen. China denies the spying allegations. No authority has found that Megaspeed broke US rules.
