Homemade Mac &amp; Cheese

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The perfect mac & cheese will have you dreaming about its cheesy goodness for days after you eat it. Obviously, the cheese has to be top-notch, but the real secret to the best version is the perfect crunchy topping. My recipe delivers on all of that and more, so you won't even think about reaching for another mac & cheese recipe for the rest of your gatherings. From weeknight dinners to potlucks and holidays, this is the classic side dish everyone will be reaching for.

First, you’ll boil the pasta. Start with a big pot of salted water (and I mean super salty, it should taste like the ocean), then add your pasta and cook it until before al dente—I’m talking still-has-a-ring-of-hard-white-pasta-in-the-middle-when-you-bite-in uncooked. You want it to meld and absorb with your cheese sauce in the oven, not turn into mush. Undercooking now guarantees the perfect bite when you pull it out of the oven.

Then it’s cheese sauce time, and that means making a béchamel. You’ll start by melting your butter in a pan over medium heat, then sprinkling the flour over the top and stirring everything together until it starts pulling away from the pan slightly. You’re looking for what’s called a “blonde roux,” which has a slightly yellow tint and should smell a bit like baked goods. You don’t want it super dark (so keep an eye on the heat!), but you definitely don’t want it as light as it started.

Next, add the milk in slowly, stirring constantly so you don’t get thick lumps. Want to shortcut this process a bit? Heat your milk ahead of time to room temperature or even slightly warm. You’re going to cook it until it’s thick and bubbly (about 2 minutes), so having your milk warm will help you get there faster. It’s going to get a LOT thicker when you add the cheese, so if it’s still a little thin but you want to get on with it, that’s okay too.

Once you’re ready to add the cheese, it’s very important to take it off the heat before so that the sauce doesn’t start to separate. That said, if you’ve been at it for a minute and are still having trouble getting your cheese to melt, pop your pot back on the heat for just a few seconds to bring the temperature up and help the process along.

Once it comes together, you can stir in the macaroni until every noodle is coated.

At this point, you could eat this mac & cheese right now, and it would taste wonderful. But! If you want exceptional mac & cheese, you’ve gotta bake it. So pour it into a pan, then get ready for the best part—the topping. To make it, you’ll combine some breadcrumbs and the remaining half cup of Parmesan in a bowl, then add the olive oil and mix it all up. Sprinkle it on top of the mac and cheese in the casserole dish.

All you have to do is pop it into your preheated 375° oven and bake it until it's golden brown on top. Go ahead, snag a bite before it's fully cooled—you'll really be able to taste all your extra effort.

The full list of ingredients and directions can be found in the recipe below.

If you’re looking to make this dish ahead of time, prepare the mac & cheese up until step 4, then cover it in your baking dish and refrigerate it up to 3 days (or freeze it up to 2 months). When ready to serve, add the Parmesan and panko mixture, bake, and serve. If baking from frozen, allow to thaw in the refrigerator overnight before baking.

If you have any leftovers, store them in an airtight container in the fridge for around 3 days.
