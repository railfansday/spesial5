Perfect your next hair look with these expert-loved picks

Perfecting an at-home blowout once required contortionist-level abilities combined with a little patience and a lot of skill, especially when styling the back of your head. But nowadays, thanks to the advancement in hair tools—from high-powered blow-dryers to one of the best round brushes—it's easier than ever to craft sleek styles that rival your local salon without dealing with arm fatigue.

Unlike regular boar-bristle or vented brushes, round brushes feature a cylindrical shape in which hair wraps around for a seamless dry. Though many of these tools are crafted from similar materials, they're also available in a range of sizes to suit your hair texture and unique beauty needs. But regardless of the brush you choose, experts say a polished dry isn't achieved with dripping wet hair. "The key is to get all the moisture out," celebrity hairstylist Priscilla Valles tells Bazaar. "To achieve the smooth look, you need to keep drying until the hair is completely dry."

New York City-based hairstylist Jackson Simmonds recommends having two to three round brushes in your collection—just in case you change your hairstyle. “To keep it simple, think, smaller brushes for shorter lengths, bigger brushes for longer lengths,” he explains.

What Are the Benefits of Using a Round Brush and Blow-Dryer?

It’s all about the shape. “The round brush allows the hair to wrap around it, creating a bend in the hair as it dries and cools,” explains Mohapi, who explains that a regular hairbrush should not be used for heat-styling. “Expensive brushes like Masons are not designed for direct heat, and you will ruin a perfectly beautiful brush,” says the stylist.

Several top stylists prefer to use round brushes and blow-dryers separately rather than an all-in-one device, as it enhances control while minimizing hair damage. "Blow-dry brushes tend to be high heat, so if you’re putting that tool on soaking wet hair, you’re going to overdry and damage it," explains Gamez. "A blow-dryer allows you to create space between the heat and the hair shaft as needed. You can control the air direction of a dryer with your nozzle, pointing downward and sealing the cuticle, resulting in shinier, smoother hair, as opposed to a hot brush that blows air outward in all directions."

On the other hand, blow-dry round brushes definitely have their benefits. According to Valles, one key factor is their overall ease of use to craft salon-worthy styles at home. "A blow-dry brush makes the process so much easier," Valles says. "Some people aren't as coordinated handling a brush and dryer simultaneously, but the blow-dry brush gives the same results and is easier to manage." At the end of the day, the best round brush for you is one you actually enjoy using regularly.

“Many people wouldn’t want to use a boar bristle brush for ethical reasons, and there are lots of good alternatives, so boar bristle isn’t the only option,” explains Mohapi. “It is, however, a highly effective material and is used extensively in hairbrush manufacturing globally.” Here’s why: “Boar bristles are highly tolerant of heat and won’t melt or buckle under the intense extremes of a packed Friday at the salon. The fibers are both strong and flexible and have the unique quality of being able to almost grab the hair and hold it in place as you pass it through the brush.”

That said, the stylist adds that some synthetics are nearly as good. They just may not last as long. “Nylon is a different thing altogether,” he notes. “Nylon will close down the cuticle and help create a shiny, slick finish to the hair. This is great for straight hair, but nylon brushes take all the body out of the hair, so when you use a nylon round brush, it has a different type of finish.”
