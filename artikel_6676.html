The Cutest Way to Wear Your Short Hair Right Now? A Flipped Bob.

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

What’s one thing Ariana Grande and Hailey Bieber have in common right now? Their flipped bobs. (Bobiana Grande, anyone?) Bobs in all forms have basically taken over the hair world, from French bobs and Italian bobs to blunt bobs and bell-bottom bobs. I would’ve guessed, at this point, that bobs had bobbed their last bob. Alas, another one has appeared: the flipped bob. (At this point, there are probably almost as many types of bobs as times I’ve said “bob” in this paragraph. Anyway.)

Inspired by the voluminous, flipped-out hairstyles of the 1960s, the fun, bouncy finish on this retro look is super chic. Plus, they’re so in for this time of year: “Flipped bobs are very trendy for the holiday season and a perfect style for holiday parties,” says celebrity hairstylist Nicolas Flores. And the best part? If you already have a bob, you don’t necessarily need a new haircut to get in on the trend. A blow-dryer-and-brush combo or flat iron can do the trick to give your existing bob that sought-after flick. Looking for some flipped bob ideas? Ahead, some expert tips from Flores on getting the look and plenty of inspo pics to reference before you start styling.

A flipped bob is just a classic bob haircut, but with one very important twist: The ends are styled outward rather than straight or curled in. It “focuses more on curling the ends outward and away from the face, giving a fun, flirty look,” says Flores. The result? A playful, totally-bouncy hairstyle that was made popular in the ‘60s by beauty icons like Jackie Kennedy and Mary Tyler Moore and is now being worn by some of today’s beauty icons like Grande and Bieber.

While the original flipped bob had a ton of volume and serious outward curl, the current versions we’re seeing are a little more subtle, sleek, and versatile, sometimes just barely flicked away from your face. The style works well on all lengths of bobs, from chin-grazing cuts to longer, shoulder-length lobs. You can also customize them with bangs, layers, a side part, or however you normally like to wear your bob.

Before any styling happens, think about your haircut. If you already have a bob, you don’t need to worry about cutting it shorter or anything like that. But if you’re getting a bob for the first time with the intention of styling it with the ends flipped out, “tell your stylist you don’t want to go too short,” says Flores, since the styling can make the hair look even shorter. “I always recommend an inch-and-a-half to two inches below the hairline to get that perfect length,” he says.

“I’d start with damp hair, a little volumizing mousse, and some heat protectant,” he says. The Cosmo Holy Grail Beauty Award-winning Nexxus Hy-Volume Weightless Lift Cloud Foam and Roz Air Thickening Spray are two great options. Section your hair and apply two to three pumps of each product at your roots, working it through to the ends.

“Using a medium round brush, blow dry the hair nice and smooth, giving it a little lift at the roots,” says Flores.

Don’t forget to focus on the star of the show: those flippy ends. “Once you get to the ends, turn the brush upward and away from the neck to create that cute little flip,” says Flores. “Give it a quick blast of cool air before releasing the brush so it holds its shape.” That’ll give a more natural-looking flip, but for something more dramatic, reach for a flat iron after you finish the blowout. “If you want the flip to be a little more defined, you can go back in with a flat iron and gently flick the ends outward,” adds Flores. “Just a little turn of the wrist is all you need!”

After that, you’re pretty much at the finish line: “Finish with a light hairspray to keep everything in place without making it stiff,” says Flores. “The goal is to keep the bob looking soft, bouncy, and effortless.” Spritz it lightly all over, but focus on the ends especially to keep that flippy look in place all day.
