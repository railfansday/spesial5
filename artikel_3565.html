Billy Crystal Answers the Proust Questionnaire

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Which historical figure do you most identify with?

My wife, Janice. Fearless, smart, optimistic, hilarious, loving…adored by all.

What is the trait you most deplore in yourself?

Self-doubt. Wait, now I’m not sure of the answer. Was that okay? Can we come back to that one?

What is the trait you most deplore in others?

From my dressing room to backstage—standing alone in the dark, a deep breath, and then feeling it’s time and walking out into the light.

What do you consider the most overrated virtue?

What do you dislike most about your appearance?

I’d rather not say, and thank you for your attention to this matter!

Which words or phrases do you most overuse?

“Sorry, I didn’t see your text till now.”

That my father died when I was 15 and never got to see my family, my career, and the man I think I am.

What or who is the greatest love of your life?

In the hospital waiting room, when my girls were born, and the doctor said everyone was fine.

Which talent would you most like to have?

If you could change one thing about yourself, what would it be?

I’d stop worrying about everyone all the time.

If you could change one thing about your family, what would it be?

That they’d finally understand why I worry about everyone all the time

What do you consider your greatest achievement?

We raised two fantastic daughters who became good, hard-working, honest people and great spouses and mothers in the middle of this business.

If you were to die and come back as a person or thing, what do you think it would be?

If you could choose what to come back as, what would it be?

I lost them all, so I’d have to say my memory.

What do you regard as the lowest depth of misery?

Love, empathy, loyalty, a sense of humor, and a handful of quarters for the parking meters.

Hemingway, David Sedaris, Neil Simon, Herb Gardner, Arthur Miller, Victor Hugo.

Santiago, the fisherman in The Old Man and the Sea.

Picasso, Muhammad Ali, Mickey Mantle, Buster Keaton, Sid Caesar, Mel Brooks, JFK, Alan Shepard—and Jim Redmond, a father who ran out of the stands at the ’92 Olympics to help his son, Derek, who had torn a hamstring in his race and was struggling to stand up and continue. They held onto each other and crossed the finish line together. Perfect.

Holding Janice’s hand. Ready, not frightened, satisfied, and with a smile and a goodbye to my family. And if there’s a laugh, that’s the last thing I hear? That would be great.
