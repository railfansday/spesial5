Holiday Office Hours: A tech editor picks the best wearables to buy as gifts in 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

If you're shopping on a budget, this was not a good year. Thanks to RAMageddon, aka the global memory crisis, many tech products have gotten more expensive, making holiday shopping a little more complicated this year, especially if you're shopping for someone who loves new gadgets.

That's why we've joined forces with our colleagues at the CNET Lab for the Holiday Office Hours from the Lab video series. For our latest entry, we're recommending the best smartphones and wearables to give as gifts in 2026, with options that won't break your budget (and some that might, to be honest).

So, as Mashable's tech editor, it fell to me to name my two favorite wearables of the year. I consulted our friends at the lab and my fellow tech experts at PCMag, Lifehacker, ZDNet, and CNET, and ultimately settled on two new products that are easy to recommend — the Apple Watch Series 12 and the Oura Ring 5.

Coincidentally, both products can set you back roughly $399 (the gold Oura Ring 5 is on sale for $399 as of this writing, while the silver is $299). So, they're not exactly gifts you'll buy for the office Secret Santa exchange. But if you have an early adopter on your Christmas gift list, I think they'll appreciate either of these wearables.

While it looks a lot like last year’s model, the new Apple Watch Series 12 does have some key under-the-hood upgrades. It has a more durable Ceramic Shield front cover that Apple says is 60 percent tougher, paired with IP6X dust and water resistance. The battery also lasts longer, for two days of power on a single charge.

The Series 12 also offers impressive heart-rate tracking thanks to an entirely new electrical and optical heart sensor. On top of that, it offers sleep apnea tracking, exercise tracking, fall detection, crash detection, and tons more. Basically, you have oodles of data to feed into the revamped Vitals app. Plus, now that Siri AI (beta) is finally on your wrist, the Series 12 is a true AI wearable.

I do have to mention the divisive Audio Intelligence tool that listens to your conversations throughout the day to recap what you heard. Now, it doesn't actually record those conversations. Still, a lot of people aren't happy about this one. On the plus side, the same Audio Intelligence tools also put Shazam on your wrist, which is pretty nifty. Basically, some people will love it, and some people will hate it, but you don't have to use it.

If you want to upgrade to an even more special gift, the new Apple Watch Ultra 4 is better than ever (and it's on sale for Black Friday). You can also save some money by getting last year's Apple Watch SE 3 for $249. It’s got an always-on display and a much better battery, so you aren’t making big compromises. For my money, the Series 12 is the Goldilocks choice for most people. It’s pretty thoroughly future-proofed, so it should last for a few years.

In her review of the Oura Ring 5 fitness tracker, Mashable Lead Shopping reporter Samantha Mangino said that after testing the new smart ring, she was finally ready to give up her Apple Watch. And it's easy to see why.

The Oura Ring 5 offers a wealth of health and fitness insights, but in a much smaller form factor. You can even wear it to sleep and forget it's even there.

As a fitness tracker, sleep tracker, health monitor, and AI coach, the Oura Ring goes way beyond counting steps; it also keeps track of how much you're sleeping (or not sleeping), your heart rate, your blood pressure, your blood oxygen, and even your stress levels, which are always high this time of year.

Plus, the latest Oura Ring 5 is super-thin, made from scratch-resistant titanium, and is comfortable enough to wear 24/7/365.

Now, I don't love that it costs $399, or that you have to pay $70 a year for a subscription to get all those perks I just talked about. But because you can wear it for such long periods of time — Oura says it's got a seven-day battery life — it provides extremely detailed health info.

Here's a pro tip: If you don’t know your giftee's ring size, buy the Oura Ring sizing kit to put under the Christmas tree, along with a gift card or a promise to pay for the actual ring.
