Our 5th Time Hosting the Hudson Quattrocento Rally Was Unforgettable

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Our adventure spanned four days, two tracks, and around 480 miles of perfect driving roads.

Road & Track just returned from our fifth Hudson Quattrocento rally, and this year’s festivities were a familiar reminder of why we can’t stay away from Upstate New York. A special group of readers from across the United States and Canada spent four days driving through the Hudson Valley and Catskill Mountains, touring the greatest roads from past rallies and exploring some all-new pavement, too. Fun fact: the “quattrocento” in the rally’s namesake comes from the Italian word for “four hundred”, alluding to the four-hundred-mile route of our first-ever Hudson Quattrocento rally. This year, we gave participants an extra 80 miles, plus a couple of surprises along the way.

Drivers showed out with a diverse lineup of cars: a 2026 Bentley Continental GTC Speed, a 2000 Ferrari 456M GT, a 2026 Lotus Emira, and even a Cinnabar Red 1988 BMW M6. The drive was led by Editor-in-Chief Dan Pund in an Aston Martin Vanquish Volante, Editor-at-Large Matt Farah in a Ford Mustang Dark Horse SC, and Executive Editor Mike Austin in a Cadillac CT5-V Blackwing. Our official event partner, Corient, also came along for the ride to help make Hudson Quattrocento an unforgettable rally.

Day one of Hudson Quattrocento kicked off at Scribner’s Catskill Lodge. Guests checked in at registration, grabbed a light bite, got briefed on the rally’s road rules, and listened to introductions from Road & Track staff and fellow attendees.

Rally participants lined up in their driving groups for the first route—an out-and-back joyride to Rhinebeck, NY. Peak autumn offers a magical driving experience in the Hudson Valley, with winding roads lined with bursts of red, orange, and yellow leaves and the crisp sting of not-yet-cold fall air.

In Rhinebeck, we stopped for lunch at Gigi Trattoria and afforded guests time to explore the charming Hudson Valley town. We hopped back in our cars and completed the loop back to Scribner’s Catskill Lodge, where guests checked into their rooms and explored the woodsy property.

After a little downtime, we gathered for drinks and dinner in the Apex Lounge. Later, we took full advantage of being in the chilly mountains by gathering around a cozy firepit and enjoying s’mores. Guests then tucked in for the night and rested up for the following day’s track activities.

Guests started Day Two with breakfast at the hotel before lining up in our driving groups. The group headed west to Wilzig Racing Manor, a private, Grand Prix-style racing circuit in entrepreneur and semi-pro driver Alan Wilzig’s backyard. This exclusive track is not typically open to the public, so getting a turn at the 1.1-mile road course was a real privilege. After lapping the elevation-heavy road course, we also got the chance to explore Wilzig’s unreal collection of classic cars, race cars, and motorcycles. From the 2006 Ariel Atom and 2008 Lotus 2-Eleven to the 2017 Ginetta LMP3 or 1974 Ducati Sport, we had enough automotive eye candy to keep us busy for a while.

We left Wilzig Racing Manor and pointed west on Highway 28A until we found some twisty back roads. 98 miles later, we pulled into Callicoon Center, NY, and arrived at Callicoon Hills Resort. Attendees were greeted with a lavish charcuterie board and a bottle of wine in their rooms, courtesy of Corient. The evening concluded with drinks and dinner at Callicoon Hills’ rustic Prospect Restaurant. After dinner, guests retired to their rooms to rest up for our second track day.

Miles logged: 130.8 (plus several more laps around the track)

After fueling up with breakfast at the hotel, we rallied to Lime Rock Park for our second track day of the trip. Drivers tracked their cars on Lime Rock Park’s historic seven-turn, 1.5-mile road course. Three-time 24 Hours of Daytona champion and former IndyCar driver Ryan Briscoe also joined us, giving guests hot laps in a Spec Miata. After mastering the track, we amped up the friendly competition with a timed autocross competition. Guests zipped, weaved, and bobbed through a couple hundred cones for the chance to win a coveted Hudson Quattrocento award. (More to be revealed soon…)

We then continued our journey with a short ten-mile drive to Troutbeck Hotel in Amenia, NY—something we were grateful for after giving it our all at the track. Upon arrival, guests settled into their rooms and enjoyed the beautiful grounds.

Before dinner, we gathered in the hotel’s library for an intimate fireside chat between Dan Pund and Briscoe. We rounded out the night with the Hudson Quattrocento award ceremony featuring awards for the Fastest Time, Driver of the Day, Longest Drive, and Best Co-Pilot, presented by Corient.

Miles logged: 138.9 (plus several more laps around the track)

We queued up in our driving groups for one last time as we headed east for the rally’s final stop in Portland, CT. TV personality and master car restorer Wayne Carini gave us a private tour of his personal garage and car collection.

As guests enjoyed their wood-fired pizza lunch, Matt Farah sat down with Carini for a fireside chat about his career, collection, and upcoming projects. Afterward, Carini signed copies of his book Steering Through Life.

Guests exchanged hugs and contact information and said final goodbyes as the rally concluded. Hudson Quattrocento was an unforgettable rally—complete with stunning drives, multiple track days, thrilling pit stops, and fantastic company.

If you would like to get in on the action yourself, there are plenty of opportunities to hit the road with us in 2027. We’re heading to Southern California on February 23-26 for Palm Canyon Run, then heading to New Hampshire, Maine, and Vermont for New England Run on May 17-20. We hope to see you there!
