Major World War 2 Flight Sim Goes Free for a Limited Time

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

News Editor at DualShockers, 10+ years writing experience, OpenCritic-listed reviewer, Master’s Degree in Developmental Psychology

The Il-2 Sturmovik name has become associated with excellence in flight sims since its initial release on November 18, 2001.

Although the team behind the first launch is no longer around, the torchbearers at For-Games are marking the occasion by letting new players try out the cornerstone game of the second generation of the Il-2 Sturmovik series.

Korea: Il-2 Series is a promising start for the next generation of flight simulators.

Starting today, Il-2 Sturmovik: Battle of Stalingrad will be free to play on Steam, as part of the 25th anniversary celebration of the franchise. The flight simulator is also offering an 85% discount until November 15.

Il-2 Sturmovik: Battle of Stalingrad centers on the battle of the same name that raged from July 1942 until February 1943. Although the Germans, led by the 6th Army, managed to enter the city, a massive Soviet counterattack led by Marshall Georgy Zhukov in November trapped Axis forces in.

Codenamed Operation Uranus, this move kicked off a brutal siege that would lead to the annihilation of the 6th Army and the capture of its commander, Field Marshal Fiedrich Paulus. The German defeat marked the end of its expansionism to the East, and is often cited as the turning point of the war in Soviet favor.

The fight on the ground has become the stuff of legends, but airpower played a major part in the battle of Stalingrad.

A massive German bombardment turned the city to rubble in August 1942, killing thousands of civilians and destroying almost all houses in the city. The German Luftflotte 4 averaged 1000 sorties per day between August 23 and November 22, 1942, putting immense pressure on the strained Soviet defenders.

When Zhukov's counterattack closed the door on the German troops in the city, the Luftwaffe pivoted from offensive missions to airlift, using transports and bombers to haul supplies and wounded. German fighters and attack aircraft managed to inflict heavy losses on Soviet units during this period, but were comprehensively beaten on a strategic level.

The fight on the ground has become the stuff of legends, but airpower played a major part in the battle of Stalingrad.

Soviet aviation in Stalingrad was outmatched in both quality and numbers when the Axis offensive hit in the summer of 1942. Reinforcements in August eventually helped tip the scale in the Red Army's favor, especially with the widespread deployment of La-5 fighters, plus Il-2 and Pe-2 attack aircraft.

The siege of Stalingrad allowed Soviet fighters to hunt down German transports trying to reinforce the 6th Army, in turn forcing German fighters to dedicate more and more time protecting these. Eventually, Soviet fighter activity pushed the Germans to primarily fly transports at night, which became increasingly prohibitive once the winter set in.

Meanwhile, Il-2 Sturmovik attack aircraft used the increasingly thin German fighter cover to pound enemy frontlines. Il-2 and Pe-2 units also took the fight to German airfields, hammering aircraft on the ground and helping seal the fate of the trapped garrison.

On its own merits, Il-2 Sturmovik: Battle of Stalingrad is one of the most realistic and advanced flight simulators in the market, and it remains the leading World War 2 game in its category.

Players are able to fly eight different aircraft in the standard edition.

On top of that, the Il-2 Sturmovik structure allows owners of any edition to play across maps in multiplayer servers, which means you’ll get to experience some of the newer maps if you choose to fly online.

Server owners like the Ad Astra group are setting up special events for free weekend players to help them get a feel for the game. This Saturday, Ad Astra is running a Siege of Odessa event set in August 6, 1941. Free weekend players will have access to the LaGG-3, Ju-87 and He-111.
