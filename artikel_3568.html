Creator Dhar Mann Unveils 5 Original Scripted Vertical Series

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Dhar Mann Studios, founded by creator Dhar Mann, is unveiling the first five original scripted vertical dramas from its partnership with Fox Entertainment and Holywater Tech.

“I’m very involved in the overall creative direction, including what stories we’re telling, how we want people to feel, and what they take away from watching,” Mann, 42, exclusively told Us of the process. “We also have an incredible team led by Erin McFarlane, Dhar Mann Studios’ Head of Film and TV, who’s leading the day-to-day work of bringing these verticals to life.”

The first five series, which span romance, action, suspense and music-driven drama, are developed specifically for vertical viewing. For Mann, the romance genre is “exciting” because it “opens up so many stories about trust, heartbreak, forgiveness, and second chances.” Mann noted that “those are themes we’ve always cared about,” and this is “another way to explore them.”

“I think the appeal of vertical dramas is pretty simple: you can get invested in a story quickly, right on your phone,” he said. “You might have a few minutes to watch, but you still want to feel something. The episodes are short, but the emotions can be big. I think that’s going to influence how Hollywood tells stories. People’s viewing habits keep evolving, and there’s a real opportunity to meet them where they are. That’s what we’re excited to do with Fox Entertainment and Holywater.”

Mann gained an online following more than one decade ago, sharing videos while at the lowest point in his life. While reflecting on hitting rock bottom before reaching success, Mann shared that those hardships made him a better storyteller.

“I think people might be surprised by how much that chapter still shapes the stories I want to tell today,” he said. “When you’re going through a really difficult time, you don’t know how your story is going to turn out. You can feel alone and misunderstood. I know that feeling, and it’s something I can connect with when we’re writing a character who’s struggling.”

He continued, “That period also taught me to take responsibility for my own choices and circumstances. That’s why, in our stories, I care about seeing what someone actually does to change. You want to believe they’ve earned that second chance.”

Mann noted that his experience gave him “more empathy,” adding that “you never really know everything someone is carrying.”

“I want our stories to remind people that their hardest chapter doesn’t have to be their last chapter,” he said. “That’s a message that means something to me personally.”

While balancing his online persona, Mann is also navigating parenthood as a father of three daughters. Being a dad — and husband to wife Laura Avila — has influenced the stories that Mann chooses to tell on his platform.

“Having daughters has made me so much more emotional. It’s opened up a part of me that absolutely influences how I connect with stories,” he said. “As they get older, I think about the messages they’re going to receive about who they should be, what makes them valuable and how they should expect to be treated. I want them to know their voice matters, that they deserve respect and that kindness is a strength.”

He continued, “That carries into the characters we create, especially our female leads. I care about them having a voice, making their own choices and discovering their own worth. And being a father keeps me thinking about my own example, too. The lessons I put into our stories are lessons I have to keep practicing in my own life.”

While reflecting on the storytelling process, Mann told Us that he listens to his fans “a lot” about what type of content they want to see.

“One of the biggest advantages of what we do is having that direct relationship with our audience. They tell us what they love, what they want more of, and when something doesn’t connect. We take that seriously,” he said, adding that vertical dramas are a perfect example. “Our community had been asking us to explore this, and we learned that many of them were already watching these shows regularly.”

He continued, “What’s exciting is realizing how open they are to seeing us try different kinds of stories. They connect with the emotion, the characters and the lessons, and they want to see what we can do with that in a new format. Our audience has helped us build this company. It makes sense to listen to where they want us to go next.”

Mann, whose brand has always been associated with uplifting stories and positive life lessons, shared that he is balancing his signature message while also giving the audience entertainment.

“I’ve always believed you can entertain people and leave them with something meaningful,” he said. “A story can have a love triangle, a betrayal or a shocking twist and still say something important about how we treat each other. For example, a romance can also be about learning your worth or understanding that someone wanting you and someone treating you well are two different things. There’s a lot of room for meaningful storytelling there.”

Mann shared that characters who “make mistakes, face consequences and have to earn their growth” is what “makes a redemption story satisfying,” adding, “You’ve seen what it took for that person to change.”

“With The Brothers Who Love Me, there’s the romance and rivalry, but Abby is also dealing with loss and figuring out who she is. That emotional journey matters to us,” he explained of the first vertical drama. “We want you wondering what happens next while also caring about what happens to these people. That’s always been at the heart of our storytelling.”

Mann’s partnership is just the latest accomplishment in his impressive portfolio, having amassed more than 170 million followers across social media platforms. But Mann noted that “celebrity” is “still a funny word” to associate with himself.

“I spend so much of my time thinking about the next story, working with our team and being a dad that ‘celebrity’ isn’t really how I think about my everyday life,” Mann said. “I’m grateful that so many people connect with what we create. When you hear a number like 170 million, it’s hard to even picture that many people. But every one of those people has their own life, their own struggles and their own reason for watching.”

He continued, “That’s the part that means the most to me, the possibility that something we created helped someone feel understood or gave them a little hope. It also makes me appreciate the responsibility that comes with having a platform.”

At the end of the day, Mann is still working toward his mission every day of not just telling stories — but changing lives.

“There will always be someone who needs encouragement, someone who feels alone or someone who needs a reminder that things can get better,” he said. “What matters to me is what someone does after watching. Maybe they treat a classmate differently. Maybe they apologize to someone. Maybe they find the courage to try again. Those choices can seem small, but they can change a relationship or the direction of someone’s life. I want the impact of a story to last longer than the time someone spends watching it.”

He continued, “And I want us to build a company where that work can continue for a long time, where talented people have opportunities to tell meaningful stories and inspire others to do the same. That would make me incredibly proud.”
