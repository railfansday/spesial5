Every Highly Anticipated Fantasy Book Adaptation Trapped in Development Hell

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Book adaptations are a special kind of joy and pain for readers, but that's especially true when you spend years waiting for them to come to fruition. Though terrible adaptations can be devastating for fans, big fantasy hits like Game of Thrones prove they can work both for existing fans and newcomers alike. But though many series have been picked up in the wake of some major hits, several of the most anticipated fantasy book adaptations have really stalled.

The last few years have seen a few major, long-awaited series officially canceled. In 2023, Netflix ditched its Shadow of Bones adaptation after just two seasons. The planned Six of Crows follow-up got scrapped altogether. But while those cancellations are tragic for fans, many fantasy readers are stuck wondering whether their book adaptations will ever cross the finish line.

Some books are optioned for film or TV adaptations, but linger in limbo. So-called "development hell" can happen to projects for many reasons. For fantasy books in particular, the cost-benefit analysis for CGI budgets can take ages. That means series that seem like obvious wins, including ACOTAR, can spend years in pre-production before they ever hit our screens.

Brandon Sanderson's Mistborn series has seen quite a few false starts in the adaptation realm over the years. The wildly popular fantasy series has been optioned a few times, but prior attempts to bring the series to the screen fell through. However, hope for a Mistborn adaptation is far from dead.

Earlier this year, Apple TV snagged the rights for the entire Cosmere universe. Better yet, they asked Sanderson himself to write the script. The author confirmed that he's got a good feeling about this latest adaptation attempt. Even so, the fact that we've been waiting for so long to see an adaptation come to life lands this project solidly in the development hell category.

Given how popular ACOTAR is in the book world, an adaptation seems like an obvious move. Yet Hulu let the rights lapse without ever bringing the series to fruition, leading to a devastating cancellation. But it's not all bad news for fans. Earlier this year, Maas confirmed she's not given up on getting her series made into a movie or TV series. She is working to find another studio willing to take on the project.

The trouble with ACOTAR is that, while the books are popular, they'll require a good bit of CGI to bring to life. The series is set primarily in a fantasy realm, and the big budget attached ultimately scared Hulu away. Though there could be a Game of Thrones in it for whoever steps up to the plate next, more recent fumbles like Shadow and Bone likely have studios scared to spend on such high-budget projects with no guarantee of success.

Brandon Mull's Fablehaven series is a modern fantasy classic by many rights, with over 3.5 million copies sold. And yet, despite a few studios poking at the project over the years, it's yet to get a single adaptation. That could finally be about to change, however.

A recent Fablehaven crowdfunding project on Backerkit soared past full funding and then some. In theory, that means Angel Studios is finally going to make a Fablehaven adaptation a reality. Since the campaign wrapped in April, the company has provided sporadic updates on social media. But it will likely be some time yet before the movie comes to fruition.

Lionsgate Studio optioned the rights to adapt Patrick Rothfuss's Kingkiller Chronicle series over 10 years ago. Since then, we've seen a few false starts, including a Showtime prequel series with Lin-Manuel Miranda. After that series fell through, Lionsgate has resumed its search for a network or streaming service to pick it up.

In part, the hesitation to pull the trigger on The Kingkiller Chronicle could be related to its unfinished status. Though wildly popular, the series hasn't yet released its final book. Showtime, and other studios Lionsgate tries to approach, may be worried about repeating the old Game of Thrones ​​​​​​problem. As such, we might be waiting a while before this project gains any traction.

In theory, the next several years could deliver on some massive fantasy adaptations. There are quite a few big series that have announced TV or movie versions on the way. However, many have stalled out, with few updates since their initial adaptations. There's always hope that they're moving forward behind the scenes, as none of these fantasy adaptations have been officially canceled, either.

As many writers know, books are often optioned without ever getting adapted. This step just means that someone has paid for exclusive rights to adapt a book, if they decide to. It doesn't mean they plan to move forward so much as they see enough promise in a series that they want to avoid letting anyone else get ahold of it. That means these series are even less likely to happen than those that have been formally announced.
