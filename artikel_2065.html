Stefan Borson shares verdict on Celtic paying compensation fee to lure Kieran McKenna

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Kieran McKenna is unlikely to return to football to replace Martin O'Neill as Celtic manager this season.

That is according to former Manchester City financial adviser Stefan Borson, who exclusively told Football Insider that the manager's compensation fee is just too high for the Scottish giants.

McKenna has been out of work since stepping down from his role at Portman Road, desperately wanting to take a break from the sport.

Despite this, Football Insider understands that the 40-year-old is first choice at Parkhead if O'Neill's future comes to a very abrupt end.

When McKenna walked away from Ipswich Town, it was revealed that any interested clubs would have to jump the hurdle of an £8million compensation fee until the summer of 2027.

These types of figures can reduce over time, though, and it is believed that his fee in particular should have done so since his time at Portman Road.

Speaking exclusively to Football Insider, Borson does believe McKenna's compensation can be as high as £8m now, but that would rule out a move to Celtic Park.

"If it's true, is the first thing. I can't see how it could be £8m, especially now. He left Ipswich quite a while ago now," he said.

"But to be honest, that will just rule it out. I think there's no way that they would pay £8m. I just don't see it happening if that is the fee.

"And why, anyway, would he want to come straight back into football so quickly if it was true for the reasons that he left?

"Wasn't this about burnout and about being desperate to have time off? If that's the case, then why would he come back so quickly?"

Right now, the Hoops are on a very mixed run of results, but the most recent win over Besiktas has surely given O'Neill more time.

Football Insider understands that the 74-year-old is on the verge of being sacked, but victory in the Europa League may have changed his fortunes slightly.

Having a replacement ready may even be what's stopping the hierarchy from making a decision, especially if McKenna simply isn't affordable.

This was always bound to happen after O'Neill took time to consider his future during the last international break. Maybe in this one, he'll walk away.
