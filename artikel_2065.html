Can Scott Parker Fix What Nuno Couldn’t? West Ham News

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Following the international break, Saturday’s clash with Preston North End feels like a pivotal moment in West Ham’s season. The Hammers desperately need to start climbing the table, and while promotion remains within reach, time is quickly becoming a factor.

Promotion from the Championship is not won on reputation alone. West Ham began the season as the bookmakers’ favourites for automatic promotion, but that tag has quietly disappeared.

The dismissal of Nuno and the appointment of Scott Parker signalled a fresh start, although our new manager has had limited time with the squad before the break. What already seems evident, however, is that West Ham’s struggles stem from structural issues rather than shortcomings of individual players.

Kretinsky was not prepared to back Espirito Santo any longer -his tactics and playing style were not working

Parker will be fully aware of the challenges facing his side. The Championship is a division where possession statistics mean very little unless they are backed up by purpose, intensity and results.

Goals have not been the problem. Defensive frailties have been the far greater concern. Playing  Morato at left back never looked convincing, and Saturday’s team selection will be fascinating. With Kilman unavailable due to suspension, Parker must decide whether to pair Mavropanos with Golambekis or Morato.

The absence of a prolific, out-and-out goalscorer has also raised questions. Joel Piroe was recruited to fill that role and, on paper, the logic was sound. He is a proven Championship striker. Four goals and two assists in 14 appearances suggest he is moving in the right direction, but when compared to the division’s leading scorer, Will Lankshear, who has 13 goals, the gap is impossible to ignore.

Ironically, West Ham are the Championship’s leading scorers with 37 goals. Encouragingly, those goals are coming from all areas of the pitch rather than relying on a single player. Yet, there is one player, Jarrod Bowen who has been central to that success, contributing seven goals and leading the league’s assist charts with seven in fifteen matches.

He will go if the club don’t secure promotion, an inevitable outcome should the club not succeed this season.

Perhaps the biggest challenge for West Ham, is psychological.

West Ham arrived in the Championship expecting results to come naturally. As fans we expected an automatic promotion place.

At times this season, West Ham have looked weighed down by expectation. Instead of playing with the confidence of promotion contenders, the team have often appeared nervous and overly cautious, afraid of making costly mistakes.

Scott Parker’s biggest task may not be tactical at all. He must build a mentality centred on resilience and belief rather than entitlement.

If he can transform West Ham into a side that is more aggressive, more robust and better suited to the harsh realities of Championship football, promotion is still very much achievable.

If he cannot, the club’s stay in the second tier could last far longer than anyone at the London Stadium ever imagined. Saturday could be the first real indication of which path they are heading down.
