My Kid Wants to Play Grand Theft Auto. Now What?

Posted November 19, 2026 | Reviewed by Gary Drevitch

For the last days, even weeks, millions of parents have been fielding the pleas from their adolescent, even pre-adolescent, children to buy and play what is likely to be the biggest video game release in history – Grand Theft Auto VI.

Overall, it's important to have a balanced perspective. We don't need to get apocalyptic about this release. For most people, GTA6 will be a relatively harmless diversion that will consume a monstrous amount of time and then fade out. The risk that playing this game will turn your overall well-adjusted teen into a murderer or drug dealer is very low. This game, and media in general, is not the primary driver of mental health problems in youth; that would still be the old standbys: genetics, trauma, and adverse child experiences—social drivers of health.

The release of this game to child development and mental health professions is similar to how Halloween is experienced by dentists. Everything about it feels wrong, but few youth will probably experience long-term harm.

That said, we should not be naïve or complacent about some of the real risks.

These come in two main categories. The first is what is sometimes called displacement, which basically refers to the sheer volume of hours that youth will choose to spend on this game rather than on something healthier. Video games, just like gambling, are addictive, so some people are just not going to be able to stop and that can interfere with sleeping, homework, or just about anything else.

The second area of concern is related to the game’s content. GTA6 has everything that might be contained in an R-rated movie including graphic violence, drug and alcohol use and trafficking, nudity, and profanity. So, really, the question if you are a parent wondering about buying the game or letting your child get the game is: Would you be comfortable giving them unlimited and unsupervised access to being immersed in an interactive R-rated movie? I’ve heard they tried to dial down the misogynistic content in this version. We’ll see.

Even if the game doesn’t make your child act violently, it will still be tough to push out the more aggressive and misogynistic thoughts that will be swirling around in their head. There is also the concern that excessive exposure to violent video games can desensitize youth to real world violence.

Of course, adolescents are different and individuals are going to react differently to playing the game. For many it will be a relatively harmless waste of time but for others there could be more serious consequences, and it is important to try and see how playing the game affects your individual child.

Sometimes I wish I could be one of those cool adults who is totally chill with immersive content that glorifies violence and criminal behavior, but I guess I’m not. Good luck, parents.
