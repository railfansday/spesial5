More visitors now arrive from chat assistants and AI answer boxes, but many analytics setups still lump them into "direct" or "referral". Teams then argue about whether AI search matters, using numbers that cannot answer the question.

Measuring this traffic is an engineering task. You need to capture the referrer, classify it, store it safely and know where the data is blind. This article covers seven patterns, including a small classifier you can run.

Explicit tags are more reliable than guessing from a referrer. If you control a link, such as a partner mention or a newsletter, add a standard utm_source and record it on landing. SitePoint's tutorial on building a campaign attribution tracking system covers capturing UTM parameters, storing first-touch and last-touch data, and keeping conversions with no attribution marked as unattributed. Tagged visits should always win over inferred ones.

Browsers limit what they send. MDN's reference for the Referrer-Policy header explains that the default, strict-origin-when-cross-origin, sends only the origin for cross-origin requests over HTTPS and sends no referrer when moving from HTTPS to HTTP.

Save the raw classification inputs, the referrer origin and the campaign tag, next to the result. When the domain list changes, you can reclassify old data. Keep raw events for a limited time and aggregated numbers longer. Avoid storing full IP addresses or fingerprints, since you do not need them for this question.

Analytics can show that visits arrive, but explaining why a brand appears in an AI answer, or what to change in content, is a separate specialty. Compare any outside recommendation with the traffic you can measure yourself.

A good report states what it cannot see. Many AI answers are read without a click, some visits carry no referrer, and Search Console reports AI feature clicks together with other web results. Put those limits at the top of the dashboard. A number with its caveats is more useful than a confident chart that hides them.

Can I identify every visit that comes from an AI tool?

No. Some tools send a referrer and some do not, so your numbers will undercount. Use campaign tags wherever you control the link.

Should I use UTM parameters for AI traffic?

Only where you control the link. You cannot tag a link that a chat tool generates on its own.

Keep it only as long as you need to reclassify and audit, for example about 30 days, and keep aggregated metrics longer.

Measuring AI search traffic comes down to honest data handling: define your questions, prefer explicit campaign tags, understand the limits of the referrer, classify visits in one tested function, store auditable events, bring in specialist advice where you need it, and report the blind spots. The classifier above is short, but it makes the logic explicit so you can review and improve it as new tools appear.
