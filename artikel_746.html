Late last week the UN’s climate conference, COP31, failed to reach a consensus on its headline message. That news came on the heels of the UN acknowledging that the 1.5 ˚C warming limit set out in Paris will be breached. So, it’s easy to feel anxious in a world where it seems that not enough is being done about climate change. But is it possible to overcome such anxiety or even use it?

In the final episode of Cooked? I open up about my own climate anxiety and discover that there are still things that I can do to battle climate change. And how those small individual actions could potentially lead to more systemic change.

Madalina Vlasceanu: There is a big difference between being aware of the problem in principle and being aware of this problem viscerally at a level where you know it's affecting you personally. Madalina Vlasceanu: The top intervention leveraged two processes. So the two processes are efficacy beliefs and positive emotions. And so the way in which you can achieve this efficacy belief induction is by giving people examples of times in which such participation in collective action has actually had meaningful effects.

Nick Petrić Howe: Such examples include the banning of fossil fuel extraction in Quebec due to decades of grassroots movements and Los Angeles moving to ban urban oil extraction, because of years of protests and community organising.

Madalina Vlasceanu: And the other process I mentioned is positive emotions. Nick Petrić Howe: Madalina also found that appealing to ideas like preserving the purity of the environment and respect for authority can also encourage more action — and these interventions were effective across the political spectrum.

Madalina Vlasceanu: It could be, for example, that someone's very motivated to not pollute very much or not use a lot of fossil fuels, but the system doesn't necessarily facilitate these actions. So it makes it much harder for me as an individual not to pollute, even if I wanted to. And so if I participate in collective action to change some of those systemic level decisions then it could be even easier for me to engage in individual level climate action by having an easier time engaging in in individual level behavioural change, such as you know, engaging in ways of transport that are less polluting.

During the civil rights movement in the US, collections of small groups came together and organised protests, civil disobedience and boycotts that eventually led to new civil rights laws and the end of most legal discrimation against African Americans.

Madalina Vlasceanu: the main thing an individual can do to address climate change is to be less of an individual. And so we have to recognize the power in groups in the power of our collective to making change.
