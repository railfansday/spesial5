Warriors finish winless road trip with lopsided loss to Grizzlies

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

It turns out that Steph Curry is pretty important to the Golden State Warriors success. When the Dubs head out on a five-game road trip just over a week ago, they did it with an eight-game winning streak and all the momentum in the world. And then, just a few minutes into the five-city trip, Curry suffered an oblique injury, and a few hours later, the Warriors lost to the Houston Rockets. What followed was brutal: a 38-point loss to the San Antonio Spurs, a 21-point defeat at the hands of the New Orleans Pelicans, a 22-point loss to the upstart Dallas Mavericks, and finally, on Thursday night, a 28-point loss to the surprisingly great Memphis Grizzlies.

The silver lining, if you're into that sort of thing, is that the Warriors looked the best that they have since Curry was injured, despite losing 126-98. The execution was the best it had been in a while, but ultimately, an astounding lack of shooting did the Dubs in.

As had been the case in all of the other Curry-less games, the Warriors got off to a comically slow start. They trailed 5-0, and then 8-2, and Steve Kerr's first timeout came when the Warriors trailed 13-4 just a few minutes in, as the team was racking up the turnovers. Even the good news was bittersweet: Charles Bassey scored the first seven points for Golden State, but also collected three quick fouls.

Still, the Warriors were executing well … mostly. They couldn't resist turning the ball over, with eight giveaways in the first quarter alone, but they were creating things on offense and getting good looks. Unfortunately, the good looks weren't falling, and they weren't able to do much on defense. Even with a nice run to end the opening frame, the Warriors trailed 31-24 after the first.

The Grizzlies came out firing in the second quarter, and quickly led 43-29 with an astonishing 21 points off of turnovers. That, combined with the Warriors' inability to make seemingly any jump shot, made it clear that unless there was a serious reversal of fortunes, the Warriors would not be winning. And there was, unfortunately, no reversal of fortune.

Memphis was turning Golden State's turnovers and missed shots into offens of their own, and used a 14-1 run to turn the game into a blowout at 47-30. But Golden State's response was impressive. The defense really stepped up late, and put the clamps on the Grizzlies with some of their best possessions in the past week (low bar, I know). That led to some much better offensive possessions, with the team getting significantly more aggressive to make up for the unsuccessful shooting. After trailing at one point by 18, the Warriors ran off a 10-0 run, and thanks to a strong defensive stand to end the half, only trailed 55-47 at the break. They had only made three shots from beyond the arc, they had no players in double figures, and Yaxel Lendeborg had yet to make a field goal, yet somehow they were in the game.

It wouldn't last long. For the third quarter in a row, the Grizzlies started the third hot, and quickly pushed the lead comfortably into the double digits. It felt like the Warriors were so close to playing well, but just couldn't quite get over the hump. They would execute well but miss shots, and then they would follow it up with some impressive individual plays supplemented by poor execution. The third quarter was ultimately defined by a distinct lack of easy shots … even when they scored, they simply couldn't get anything easy, and labored over every possession, good or bad. They still couldn't make any threes, and they still had too many turnovers, though credit where it's due: the energy and effort level didn't fade a bit, from opening tip until final buzzer. But energy and effort were no match for talent and execution, and the Grizzlies took a 92-73 lead into the final frame.

From there, it was just window dressing. The Grizzlies again were on fire to start the quarter, and quickly pushed the lead well into the 20s, making it clear that there would be no miraculous comeback for the Dubs. Both teams emptied their benches fairly early, as Memphis rolled to a 126-98 victory, their second win of the season against Golden State.

The Warriors had a serious talent deficit, playing without not just Curry, but also Draymond Green, Jimmy Butler III, Moses Moody, and Brandon Williams. Charles Bassey led the way with 16 points, and also grabbed 13 rebounds for a double-double, while Will Richard had another strong game off the bench, scoring 15 points and playing excellent defense. But Yaxel Lendeborg and Brandin Podziemski once again struggled to carry the offense with Curry out, with the former scoring 11 points on 3-for-11 shooting, and the latter 15 points on 5-for-12 shooting. While Lendeborg struggled on offense, he played a good defensive game, and finished with eight rebounds, seven assists, one steal, and two blocks.

For Memphis, Jaylen Wells (22 points and seven rebounds on 8-for-10 shooting) and rookie Cameron Boozer (16 points, six rebounds, and five assists on 6-for-6 shooting) starred in a total team effort. But the biggest discrepancy, as it has been for much of the road trip, was the shooting. Golden State shot a miserable 5-for-29 (17.2%) from deep, while the Grizzlies went an excellent 15-for-35 (42.9%). That, along with 22 turnovers, is how you lose by 28 points.
