Delta Air Lines Cancels 3-Hour Route After 15 Months: See Map Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Airlines routinely churn through routes, particularly those with lower load factors or poor yields, and Delta Air Lines is no exception. The carrier has withdrawn from several markets this year, including some underperforming international routes.

In its latest weekend schedule update, the SkyTeam member confirmed that it is ending its service between Salt Lake City International Airport (SLC) and Clinton National Airport (LIT) in Little Rock, Arkansas. Delta launched the route in September last year only. Interestingly, it was among several routes the airline temporarily suspended earlier this summer amid rising jet fuel prices.

The route was indeed an interesting addition to Delta’s network when it launched. It has been serving Little Rock for decades, but primarily from its main hub at Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport (ATL), where it currently offers six to seven daily flights. For passengers traveling from Salt Lake City, which is Delta's fourth-largest hub, by scheduled departures, this meant flying east to Atlanta before connecting to Little Rock.

So, the introduction of a nonstop service provided a more convenient option. Currently, the route is served by SkyWest Airlines, which operates a daily flight on behalf of Delta using Embraer E175 aircraft. The service was also scheduled to operate daily next summer using the same aircraft type. However, according to data from aviation analytics provider Cirium, the last scheduled flight on the route will take place on December 31, 2026.

Looking at data from the US Department of Transportation (DOT), it is perhaps unsurprising that the SkyTeam member has decided to axe the route. The latest available figures, which cover the period from September 2025 to August 2026, show that the service carried 27,538 passengers, with an average load factor of 71.6%. However, this is just one factor.

Airlines do not necessarily remove routes simply because of lower load factors, especially if they generate good yields or bring connecting passengers into their hubs. In this case, Delta may have hoped that the route would bring more connecting traffic through Salt Lake City. But despite the size of its hub, around 57% of passengers traveled only between Little Rock and Salt Lake City, rather than connecting onwards to other destinations.

In addition, the route also performed poorly in terms of revenue. After adjusting for flight distance, it ranked fifth-lowest for total revenue per available seat mile (TRASM) among more than 90 markets served by Delta from Salt Lake City. Nevertheless, the airline will continue to serve Little Rock from its Atlanta hub, with six to seven daily flights, as well as from New York LaGuardia Airport (LGA), with six weekly services.

While the Atlanta-based airline is saying goodbye to one destination from Salt Lake City, it is also adding new routes from the hub. Starting June 7, 2027, it will launch twice-daily, year-round nonstop flights to Flagstaff, Arizona, operated by SkyWest Airlines on its behalf. Another addition to its network is Monterey Peninsula Airport (MRY), which the carrier is scheduled to begin serving on December 19, 2026. Notably, this will mark its return to Monterey after nearly two decades.

Overall, according to Cirium data, Delta has scheduled around 56,400 flights from Salt Lake City for summer 2027, compared to 52,859 during the previous summer season (approximately 6.7% more). The airline will offer more than 200 daily departures and over 250 on peak days.

Unsurprisingly, Atlanta will remain its busiest destination from Salt Lake City next summer, with eight daily flights, followed by Los Angeles with seven daily services and Seattle with five to six. Among international destinations, Vancouver will see the most flights, with up to three daily departures on peak summer days.
