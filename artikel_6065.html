As much as I love the holiday season, it's my least favorite time to travel. Flights and accommodations are expensive, airports are at their busiest and winter storms can upend even the best-laid plans.

A credit card can't help your family pack lighter or ensure your flight departs on time. But the right benefits can lessen the sting from some of travel's most predictable pain points.

Here are the cards that can make the biggest difference during a hectic holiday trip, organized by the problem they solve.

Few travel fees are more annoying than paying extra to bring your socks with you — especially now that roundtrip bag fees can feel higher than the cost of the clothes inside.

For a family checking multiple bags, this may be the most immediately valuable benefit on this list. A card with a relatively modest annual fee can pay for itself during holiday travel alone.

Many airline credit cards offer a free checked bag, so start with the airline you fly most.

Higher-fee airline cards often match or exceed the checked-bag benefits shown here.

Many of the same airline cards that waive bag fees also provide priority boarding benefits. On a packed holiday flight, I'll gladly get in line early if it keeps my carry-on out of the gate-check pile.

This is a useful way for existing cardmembers to absorb a holiday expense.

Ever spend more time in airport security than you do on the flight itself? TSA PreCheck, Global Entry and Clear+ earn their keep throughout November and December.

PreCheck provides expedited screening at eligible U.S. airports; Global Entry includes PreCheck and adds expedited processing when returning to the U.S. from abroad; and Clear+ uses biometric identity verification to speed up the document-check step of security (so some travelers use it alongside PreCheck).

And for travelers who frequently cross between the U.S. and Canada, Nexus can speed up passport control.

A lounge won't make a delayed flight leave sooner, but a power outlet, snacks and a drink make the wait easier.

These lounge cards offer different benefits:

* Up to 10 visits per eligible Platinum Card, per year to the Delta Sky Club or to Grab and Go when traveling on a same-day Delta-operated flight

I've had all of these over the years, although lately my favorite card for lounge access is the Bank of America Premium Rewards Elite. The card's Priority Pass membership still provides credits at participating airport restaurants, which can be helpful when traditional lounges are full or there's nowhere left to sit.

There's a broader lesson there, too: don't choose a card based solely on how many lounges its network advertises. Check whether your airports have participating locations, whether there are restrictions on when you can visit and if it costs extra to bring your travel companions.

For holiday travel, the best card isn't necessarily the one with the longest list of premium benefits; it's the one that solves the problem most likely to cost you money or patience. A family checking several bags may save more with a $95 airline card than an $895 premium card, while someone planning a dream trip may want every perk they can get.

Opening an expensive card solely to make one trip less miserable isn't the play here. But if you are already paying a premium annual fee, this is the time to use the benefits you've paid for all year.
