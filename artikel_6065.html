At the Races: It’s almost over

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

By Mary Ellen McIntire and Daniela Altimari

Voters are weighing in on whether to keep Republicans in control of Congress or give some power to Democrats amid mounting signs of dissatisfaction with President Donald Trump.

Democrats, who have expanded both the House and Senate battleground maps, are optimistic about their chances, while Republicans are hoping their base voters turn out — and help them defy the polls once again.

The expanded battleground map was evident in our final rankings of the most vulnerable senators, which included Sens. Roger Marshall of Kansas, Darline Graham of South Carolina and Cindy Hyde-Smith of Mississippi, three states that weren’t exactly expected to be part of the battleground at the beginning of the year.

Some of House Republicans’ best pickup opportunities come in districts that were redrawn in their favor, which is reflected in our final list of the most vulnerable House incumbents. Just two Democrats— Florida’s Darren Soto and Alabama’s Shomari Figures — rank among the 10 most endangered House members, while Republicans from the nation’s most competitive swing seats occupy most of the other spots.

Speaker Mike Johnson has spent the past several weeks crisscrossing the country hoping to save his narrow majority. He appeared Monday night with Rep. Jen Kiggans in the same Virginia district where Hakeem Jeffries, the top House Democrat, campaigned on Saturday. Earlier in the day, Johnson returned to Eastern North Carolina, home to a costly rematch between Democratic Rep. Don Davis and Republican Laurie Buckhout. Johnson also held a rally in a neighboring district that Trump would have carried by 14 points, a sign of GOP vulnerabilities even in conservative terrain. The speaker will begin Election Day in South Texas, where Republicans are hoping to flip two House seats while preserving another.

To mark Election Day, we’ve taken a look back over the past two years and identified 15 key moments that shaped the midterm campaign. We also have five questions we hope the election results will help us answer, including whether redistricting pays off for Republicans and how key constituencies that supported Trump and Republicans two years ago vote this time. And Roll Call photojournalists Bill Clark and Tom Williams shared the most memorable and striking images from their time on the trail this cycle.

Of course, it hasn’t been a quiet end to the campaign. Michigan Rep. Haley Stevens, who unsuccessfully sought the Democratic nomination for Senate this year, sent shockwaves across both parties Sunday when she appeared with and endorsed Republican Mike Rogers over primary rival Abdul El-Sayed.

That followed a major development in the Alaska Senate race, after The New York Times reported that aides to Democrat Mary Peltola helped recruit a candidate who shares a name with Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan. A federal judge on Sunday rejected a lawsuit from the Trump administration, which sought to delay certification of the Senate election if the incumbent Sullivan loses, as our colleague Michael Macagnone reported.

If your preferred way to watch results roll in is with a crowd, CQ Roll Call’s Nina Heller has a rundown of D.C. bars that will be airing election night results, many of which are offering deals on drinks and food.

Stay tuned to the Roll Call website for results and immediate takeaways. At the Races will be back in your inbox as usual on Thursday as we break the results down and look ahead to the next Congress.

After all, 2028 is right around the corner.
