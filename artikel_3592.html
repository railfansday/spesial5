Scott Colom’s Mississippi Votes Are Still Rolling In—and So Are the Views on His Viral Campaign Ads

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

In an era when short-form-video political messaging reigns supreme, it might be a tiebreaker, and even kingmaker, in Mississippi’s Senate midterm race, where Democratic challenger Scott Colom narrowly leads incumbent Republican senator Cindy Hyde-Smith as ballots continue to be counted on Thursday, November 5.

On October 13, Colom’s campaign released an ad that opens with a clip from a televised February interview featuring Hyde-Smith, a cattle farmer and former agriculture commissioner, responding to a question about rising beef prices by offering that Mississippians “have so many proteins to choose from.” The ad then cuts to a man grilling burgers. “Pick another protein?” he asks. “Sheee-it. I’d rather pick another senator instead.” The ad appears to have been made by content creator Ryan Sayles. Vanity Fair has reached out to Sayles as well as Colom’s campaign for comment.

The message, which highlighted the Colom campaign’s commitment to cost cutting, capitalized on Hyde-Smith’s gaffe to the tune of more than 2.8 million views between X (formerly Twitter), Instagram, and Facebook within the first 36 hours, according to the Clarion-Ledger. Former president Barack Obama’s video endorsement emphasizing Colom’s “Christian faith,” released just a day earlier, earned just over 700,000 views on the platforms in nearly double the time.

Colom currently has the edge in the historically deep-red state: If elected, he would become the first Democratic Mississippi Senator in more than 40 years—as part of the blue wave that swept the 2026 midterms, in which Democrats seized back control of both the House and Senate from MAGA-era Republicans. In Mississippi, which President Trump won by roughly 20 points in 2024, Hyde-Smith was favored to win. That is, until mid-October, when one of Colom’s campaign ads went viral, turning the Southern enclave into a surprise battleground state.

The day before Halloween, Colom made waves when he paid tribute to the Oscar-winning Ryan Coogler film Sinners, casting himself and his twin brother, Andrew, as fictional twins Smoke and Stack Moore, both portrayed by Michael B. Jordan in the movie. The film, which was set in the 1932 Mississippi Delta, follows the Black brothers as they try to keep white vampires away from their juke joint. The ad, titled “Sinators,” and released by Colom on October 30 with the disclaimer that “this is a joke!” shows the two men guarding a door as a blonde woman, who seems to be a proxy for Hyde-Smith, tries to enter. Colom says the duo have to “hold her off until the sun rises on Election Day” in the ad, which garnered more than 380,000 views on X alone prior to the November 3 election, as reported by the Sun Herald. If Colom wins, he would be Mississippi’s first Black senator since the Reconstruction era (1865–1877). VF has reached out to Coogler for comment on Colom’s campaign ad.

Colom’s attention-grabbing content hasn’t come without controversy. During the campaign, the candidate was compelled to delete another ad that featured an artificial-intelligence-generated depiction of Hyde-Smith running away from reporters after the state attorney general Lynn Fitch threatened legal action against him. (Fitch, a Republican, helped support Texas’s lawsuit challenging the 2020 election results, and was also counsel for the petitioner on Dobbs v. Jackson Women’s Health Organization, the case which ultimately overturned Roe v. Wade.)

Shortly before the release of the Sinners ad, but after the AI dustup, the Senate Majority PAC, the flagship super PAC for Democratic Senate candidates aligned with Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, announced it would spend $1.2 million on a broadcast TV and digital ad campaign for Colom. “It is clear Colom is running a competitive race that has put Hyde-Smith on her heels,” Senate Majority PAC spokesperson Lauren French said in a statement to The Hill.

Colom, a local prosecutor, and Hyde-Smith, seeking her second full term in the Senate, had a past connection heading into this political stand-off. Hyde-Smith blocked Colom’s nomination for federal judge in 2023, citing his support for transgender rights and his having previously been boosted by a George Soros–linked PAC, according to the Mississippi Free Press. Colom told ABC News this “has nothing to do with the run for me…. As a Christian, I forgive her. It’s about the people in Mississippi.”

Since Mississippi elections do not have an automatic recount process, the opponents face a potential legal challenge over the results of their Senate race, in which Colom holds a razor-thin lead over Hyde-Smith. Colom later echoed a similar sentiment. “It’s not about me. It’s about the people in Mississippi,” the smiling Colom told reporters while speaking to constituents at a local establishment on Thursday morning. “They decided to go in a new direction.”
