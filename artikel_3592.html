World Of Warcraft Forever’s Economy Problems Continue Because Some People Just Can’t Be Normal About Fictional Money

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

World of WarcraftWorld of Warcraft: Forever

We’re two weeks into World of Warcraft: Forever, and the in-game gold economy is already reeling from a massive exploit that created a huge gap between rich players and us regular Joes, while concerns about real-money transactions continue to loom.

As players increasingly reach max level, more and more have been doing a new-to-WoW–Forever dungeon called Blackmaw Hold. And recently, they discovered that one of the rooms in Blackmaw Hold contained a group of 30 or so easy-to-kill enemies that dropped very large amounts of currency, as well as items that can be sold for even more.

So, obviously, everyone was very normal about this, right? Wrong.

Apparently, some players started farming this room specifically for gold: basically running in, sprinting as fast as they could to that room, clearing it, and then leaving the dungeon, only to come back an hour later when the instance “reset” and do it all over again. Blizzard has since hotfixed this so that these mobs no longer drop anything, but not before some players had racked up hundreds, even thousands in gold. And in WoW: Forever, that’s aÂ lot. It’s not like the retail version where everyone has tens or hundreds of thousands of gold on them all the time. I’m level 23 and I think I have, like, four gold. Maybe. At level 40, it will cost me 90 gold to learn to ride a mount, and 900 gold at level 60 to learn to ride a faster one, both of which are considered pretty essential rites of passage within the game.

He’s not the only one. In fact, a number of high-profile WoW streamers could be involved in this mess. Ziqo specifically names Sodapoppin and Brucewayner here, and accusations have also been levied by the community at Jokerd, Liz, and Ahmpy. Ziqo is the only one so far who has admitted publicly to having used this exploit (and included video evidence).

Some, of course, are defending the exploiters, claiming that they didn’t hack or botâthey merely took advantage of something Blizzard itself put in the game. But many members of the community are growing angry at streamers like Ziqo not just for messing up the economy, but also because of a belief that their profiles as popular streamers and their past relationships with Blizzard (sometimes including actual partnerships) are protecting them from consequences. You might recall Ziqo in particular from past reporting on his engagement with real-money transactions (RMT) during the WoW: Forever beta. His gold was wiped for that, but his account was not banned for any length of time.

Meanwhile, some individual players are reporting being banned for allegedly engaging with RMT in the main game, while vehemently claiming they did no such thing. Instead, they say, they tried to share a small amount of gold with a friend, or just tried to sell enchantments on player equipment for normal amounts of money. While being banned erroneously is something that happens in games all the time, it especially stings when popular WoW content creators who are publicizing bad behavior are actively getting away with fewer consequences than regular players who are allegedly doing nothing wrong.

Most people I saw commenting about these issues seem to have no doubt Blizzard will issue some sort of consequences for using this exploit. But a lot of players are calling for Blizzard to really crack down on those involved. Many are asking for Blizzard to, at minimum, remove all gold that was obtained in this way from accounts that used the exploit, as well as to issue bans (temporary or permanent, opinions vary) to those involved, including these high-profile streamers. Some are even calling for those streamers to be named and shamed by Blizzard, to make it clear in a very public way that no one is above the rules. It looks like some temporary bans are already rolling out to accounts that participated in this exploit, but it’s not yet clear whether high-profile streamers, and particularly those with past marks on their records like Ziqo, will suffer similar consequences.
