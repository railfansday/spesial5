Khawaja ready to be frozen out by Cricket Australia after sandpaper storm

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Usman Khawaja says bans handed out to David Warner, Steve Smith and Cameron Bancroft were "grotesquely overboard" following sandpaper scandal in 2018; Khawaja claims every member of Australia dressing room knew about the plot eight years ago, an allegation vehemently denied by Pat Cummins

Usman Khawaja says being frozen out by Cricket Australia would be a "small price to pay" after his claims about the 2018 ball-tampering affair in South Africa.

Khawaja alleged in his autobiography 'Sabr' that every member of the Australia dressing room was aware of the plot to alter the condition of the ball with sandpaper and not just the players who copped bans in Steve Smith, David Warner and Cameron Bancroft.

The 39-year-old's account has been backed up by former team-mates Warner and Chadd Sayers but vehemently denied by current Test captain Pat Cummins, who was part of the bowling group in the series against the Proteas eight years ago.

Khawaja retired from international cricket after Australia's 4-1 Ashes thrashing of England earlier this year but continues to play for Brisbane Heat in the Big Bash League.

Speaking on an episode of the They Used to Be podcast, which was recorded before Cummins called Khawaja's claims about 'Sandpapergate' "utter nonsense" and accused him of being flippant with people's lives, the batter said he weighed up the "cost" of what his allegations would mean for him and those still playing.

"I still have a very good relationship with (Cricket Australia chief executive) Todd Greenberg," Khawaja explained.

"What happens to that relationship in the future, honestly [I don't know]. If Cricket Australia for some reason doesn't want to [continue], it's a small price to pay in my mind for doing what I did.

"I have stood up for three guys I feel were treated unfairly (Warner, Smith and Bancroft).

"But there are other guys that are still playing in the team right now that there might be some sort of cost. I have tried my best to balance that out.

"There is always a cost in everything you do. There has been a cost to my family, too. But I am also not playing the victim here, because I knew what I was getting into.

"I knew what was coming. I just didn't realise it would be so personal."

On the podcast, Khawaja spoke of an "awkward" conversation with Smith before the release of his book.

He also called the penalties dished out following the sandpaper storm as "grotesquely overboard" and "over the top" with then-skipper Smith and vice-captain Warner hit with year-long suspensions and Bancroft banned for nine months.

Warner was also told he could not captain again before that sanction was lifted years later.

Cricket Australia CEO Greenberg said recently that there was "no compelling reason" to re-open any investigation into the scandal.

Khawaja added: "I'm not here to say 'he did this', 'that person did that, or 'he knew this or he knew that'.

"It was more a case of 'we win as a team, we lose as a team, we take chances as a team'. I think if there is an opportunity to do that, we can move forward once and for all.

"But I don't think we've ever done that, and this is why it keeps coming back to us over and over again. Speculation after speculation just keeps rearing its ugly head."
