Europa Fight League! Marseille hooligans descend on Levski Sofia end after 'Nazi salute' - while Besiktas match is SUSPENDED due to pro-Palestine propaganda... and away fans infiltrate Sunderland

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Published: 16:06 EST, 26 November 2026 | Updated: 19:16 EST, 26 November 2026

European football has endured another night of chaos with fan trouble at Marseille, Besiktas, and Sunderland.

Fans of Marseille and Levski Sofia clashed violently at their Europa League match, with home hooligans descending on the away end with bats and metal pipes after being provoked by a Nazi salute.

Turkish side Besiktas' clash with Israeli team Hapoel Be'er Sheva was brought to a temporary halt after fans of the former unfurled a pro-Palestinian flag, leading to a clash with police.

And Sunderland's home end was infiltrated by supporters of Jagiellonia Bialystok, who had been banned for the fixture by UEFA - with some of the visitors starting brawls.

The three Europa League disturbances cast another dark shadow over football.

Marseille saw the worst events as an estimated 200-300 home hooligans entered the away end with bats, pipes, and flares.

Marseille's supporters descended on the Levski Sofia end with bats and flares

It is believed that the Marseille ultras were provoked by a Nazi salute by a Levski Sofia fan

One supporter at the Marseille-Levski Sofia match nursed a bleeding head injury

Besiktas fans unfurled a Palestine flag at their fixture against Israeli side Hapoel Be'er Sheva

They had reportedly been provoked by some of Levski's ultras, who have a long association with neo-Nazism, and Marseille's supporters responded with an antifascist chant.

Some people kicked and punched each other - another even used a fire extinguisher, according to L'Equipe.

Bulgarian news agency Bgnes reported that the French hooligans also hurled bottles, flares and firecrackers.

Women and members of Levski's entourage were among the crowd, and several injured supporters had to be escorted out of the stadium, according to reports.

'Olympique de Marseille condemns the incidents that occurred on the sidelines of the match against Levski Sofia,' the hosts said in a statement.

'The swift intervention of the club's security teams restored calm, without affecting the match itself.

'The club is currently working to establish the precise circumstances of these incidents, in conjunction with the relevant authorities.'

Meanwhile, trouble spiked in the 25th minute of Besiktas' 2-1 win over Hapoel Be'er Sheva.

One fan was seen being taken away on a stretcher at the Besiktas game, which was played in Batumi, Georgia

Besiktas full-back Kassoum Ouattara hung the Palestine flag over the advertising hoarding, covering the UEFA lettering

Police had to remove some Jagiellonia Bialystok fans who had infiltrated the home end at Sunderland

The match had been moved to Batumi, Georgia due to security worries surrounding ties between Turkish and Israeli clubs. There was even tension on the pitch, as Besiktas refused to shake hands with their opponents.

After a pro-Palestine banner was revealed by Besiktas' fans, the Georgian police appeared to react with tear gas or pepper spray and batons. One fan was seen being carried away on a stretcher.

The game had to be briefly suspended, with both sets of teams and the officials going to the dressing rooms.

After the match, Besiktas full-back Kassoum Ouattara hung the Palestine flag over the UEFA lettering on the pitchside advertising hoardings.

Besiktas condemned the local police, saying they had used 'disproportionate force'.

The club also wrote: 'Upon learning that security forces were obstructing our fans' entry into the stadium, we immediately contacted the UEFA delegate, who arranged for the stadium's evacuation gates to be opened, allowing our fans to enter the stadium quickly and safely.

'We announce that steps have been taken with our Consulate General in Batumi and UEFA regarding the disproportionate force used by security forces against our fans, and state that we will closely monitor the situation until each of our fans returns safely to our country.'

Turkey and Israel have long been simmering with tension amid the conflict and numerous Turkish clubs such as Galatasaray, Fenerbahce, and Mohamed Salah's Trabzonspor came out in support of Besiktas later - despite usually being bitter rivals.

Jagiellonia Bialystok's supporters had been banned - but some bought tickets in the home end

Meanwhile, at the Stadium of Light, events were of a lower scale, but some away fans were seen brawling with the home faithful and police waded in to eject them.

Jagiellonia Bialystok's fans had been banned from attending the game by UEFA following disturbances between them and Levski Sofia fans earlier this month.

But around 20 of them managed to secure tickets, Sunderland believe, and squeeze into the stadium.

The club had already caught some out in advance, writing: 'The club is actively monitoring ticket purchases and has identified and cancelled tickets purchased by, or on behalf of, individuals identified as Jagiellonia supporters.

'All affected tickets will be refunded to the original purchasers.

'This restriction applies across all areas of the stadium, including home sections, and regardless of how tickets have been purchased or transferred.

'Any attempt to circumvent the restriction, including using tickets purchased through third parties or belonging to other supporters, will result in refusal of entry.

'Jagiellonia supporters without a valid entitlement to attend the fixture are strongly advised not to travel to Sunderland.

'No Jagiellonia supporters without valid permission to attend will be permitted entry into the Stadium of Light, and we strongly advise they stay away from the immediate surrounding vicinity.

'The club will continue to work closely with the relevant authorities to ensure UEFA's ruling is fully enforced and that the fixture takes place as a home supporter-only event.'
