Colin Farrell fights back tears after receiving 'family heirloom' gift of his late football star father and uncle

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

By BEN WILLCOCKS, ASSISTANT SPORTS EDITOR

Published: 11:11 EST, 27 November 2026 | Updated: 11:12 EST, 27 November 2026

Hollywood actor Colin Farrell was moved to the verge of tears after being presented with a remarkable gift honouring his late footballer father and uncle.

The Irishman's father Eamon, who died aged 83 after a long illness in April 2025, played for Shamrock Rovers during his football career before he became a successful businessman in later life.

Colin, 50, joined YouTubers Josh Carrott and Ollie Kendal, better known as JOLLY, for lunch at Burger & Lobster in Mayfair this week, where he opened up about his glittering career and latest film The Slightest Touch.

Towards the end of the podcast, Colin was presented with an extraordinary parting gift - a matchday programme and newspaper cutting from the 1962 FAI Cup final, in which his father Eamon and uncle Tom both featured.

The brothers played a key role for Shamrock Rovers as they helped the Hoops beat Shelbourne 4-1, lifting the FAI Cup in front of 32,000 fans.

Colin was visibly moved as he was presented with the gift, which Ollie explained he bought after seeing a listing on eBay.

The remarkable gift was a matchday programme from the 1962 FAI Cup final, which starred both his father and uncle. He was given it while appearing on the JOLLY podcast this week

'Your dad is listed in there, as is your uncle,' Ollie said as he pointed to the matchday programme.

'When it arrived - they didn't say this in the listing - there was a newspaper clipping, and it's an account of the match, and there's actually a photo of your dad and uncle.'

Amazed, the Oscar-nominated actor replied: 'Sneaky f***er, you. Clever f***er.

'Jesus, thanks very much. This means, like obviously, it means a lot. That's incredible. This will be framed and then go up on my wall in Los Angeles.

'I won't talk anymore because I'll start... I'll start giving you some really good s***!'

He added, jokingly: 'Go see the film if you want to really see me cry.'

Before the video ended, the Hollywood star thanked JOLLY for giving him a 'family heirloom' that he can pass down to his children, describing the gift as 'extraordinary'.

He admitted the bonus newspaper clipping was particularly special, as he had lost his own collection of cuttings documenting his father's career in football.

'I used to have so many newspaper cuttings of my father. And a great picture of him heading the ball. Black and white picture of him,' he said.

'I don't know where they went. Literally. So I have nothing. But now I have this.'
