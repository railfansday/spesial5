What We Learned from the Spurs win over the Jazz

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Those of us who have been roaming the beat of hyper-focused amateur basketball blogging for long enough know that, at any given moment, we are prone to spouting off the odd exaggeration or two. An embellishment, if you will. Saying something as innocuous as "that's one of the coolest plays I've ever seen" is a part of the set decoration around here and, if I could take you behind the curtain even further, I'd venture to say that unsheathing our hyperbolic sword to wax poetic about the tragedies and triumphs of regular old basketball moments is actually the whole gig. It's our mandate from heaven. From the mundane, we must divine the remarkable.

Now, taking that rather large grain of salt and tucking it away in your subconscious, I invite you to entertain the idea that the 1st quarter of basketball the Spurs played against the Warriors on Friday night was maybe the greatest 12 minutes of basketball I've seen this group play. Ever? I mean, who has the time to research forever, but for the sake of this conversation, let's say, yeah, that was the best stretch I've ever seen. Everything worked. They opened the game on an 18-0 run and the quarter ended 38-14. The offense seemed to flow in and out of infinite possibilities at random. A three, a dunk, a layup, a middie. The basket glowed in a shining iridescence of joy and wonder at the possibilities before it.

The defense, on the other hand, appeared to absorb the deep grey of the NBA Cup court and became a void into which offense flattened out into a shadow of itself. The Warriors, 5-for-25 in the quarter, were swallowed up in this cloud, shrinking and disappearing into nothingness while our silver-clad demons continued to gleefully romp through this realm of dreams and nightmares.

That quarter was so insane that I'm still sitting here thinking about it four days later. In fact, as I watched that very same group of basketball gods look exceedingly mortal out there in Utah yesterday afternoon, the vividness of their display against the Warriors only became more stark in comparison. As inevitable as the Spurs seemed the other night, the letdown was equally so.

Sunday afternoon was a slog. An uphill battle against a team desperate to find a foothold in this season. You could feel the wild energy radiating off the Jazz the entire game. A burbling, untethered sort of raw power that seemed equally likely to hit the target or harm innocent bystanders. Mo Bamba went 6-for-6 from three and 0-for-3 at the line. There was nothing precise or controlled about their approach; instead, they seemed animated by an unseen force trying to make something, anything, out of what is rapidly becoming a lost season.

Into this tempest rode the good ship Spurs, and immediately the goal was to weather it. Survive and endure. Perfection is not something that can be found in a storm, and so they abandoned it. Everything became less fluid and more practical. They held back, they picked their spots, and they took the opportunities provided to them.

Even as the Jazz surged ahead again and again, the Spurs calmly stayed the course. They applied the pressure slowly and deliberately until the end when, their opponent's frenzied blows finally slowing, they steadily pushed through and saw it out. No real theatrics. No otherworldly displays. Just a group playing with a sort of dogged determination and certainty that, in the end, their process would take them where they needed to go.

The contrast of these two performances is fascinating to observe side by side, although, as we've discussed ad nauseam, it's hard to know exactly how much, if any, of this translates toward the ultimate goal of redemption at the end of the season. The Warriors were without Curry. The Jazz were without hope. Every team the Spurs face has some sort of flaw or hindrance that keeps them from revealing the true nature of these Spurs, and so we are left to our own devices. Forced to spend the next few months reading tea leaves and trying to understand what exactly these guys might be capable of.

The idea that the Spurs have the ability to touch perfection, to wield it, if only for a moment, is a tantalizing one. It's an exciting new dimension to their ceiling, and the thought of them unleashing it in a playoff atmosphere sends chills down my spine. You can conjure that vision in your mind, can't you? The crowd feeding off it. The sounds growing louder. The players bouncing and vibrating off each other in a kind of combustion designed to obliterate their opponent from the face of the earth. Somewhere in there, the light goes strange and your voice drops an octave. We would be conquerors. We would be kings. We would be gods, beautiful and terrible as the Dawn!

But we've seen how that goes in the playoffs, haven't we? We've seen this group in their bag against elite competition and we've seen how quickly that high can dissipate until all of a sudden there's a vacuum. It's cold and dark when that power deserts you and, in that void, you can lose track of how you climbed that mountain in the first place.

These Spurs didn't have an answer last June when they reached for divine inspiration and came up empty. They became shells of themselves and watched as the tide washed over them, extinguishing what was left of their dream.

As Stephon Castle casually loped down the court before throwing down a nonchalant windmill dunk to officially put this game to bed yesterday, I felt a profound satisfaction come over me. In the 4th quarter, they calmly erased the Jazz's 5-point lead. Dylan Harper, quietly and competently, poured in 18. Victor controlled the paint, Steph, Devin and Keldon repeatedly took advantage of Utah's desperation, and before you knew it the game was over. It was always over. It was over before it started.

It wasn't the greatest 12 minutes of basketball I've seen this group play, but, in a way, that was even more impressive.

Sure, so I maybe downplayed the extent to which Dylan Harper's 4th quarter was amazing up there because it fit the narrative I was trying to sell. So sue me! The hyperbole sword cuts both ways! But that was main body Charlie, and we're in Takeaway land now, where we're free to say whatever the hell we want about Harper's 4th quarter takeover. It's telling that the Spurs don't need him to become an apex predator all the time, but it's a remarkable card that they get to play in certain spots. The Spurs had spent the night being mildly competent, getting mostly the same looks they're used to and simply not executing. Shots weren't falling. Passes were slightly off, cuts were a step slow. The beautiful game wasn't so beautiful at the moment, and what it needed was a tool to cut through the red tape. Harper turned into a machete. He looked fast, thorough, and sharp as a tack. Put simply, he just relentlessly looked like the best player on the court. It was a career high in points for him, a record that I'm sure will be fleeting. What a player.

I'm almost sad that the Spurs relative competence erased the 6 of 6 miracle night from Mo Bama. Genuinely incredible thing to witness.

The Luke Kornet situation is so fascinating. The effect of having Wembanyama around makes everything else really difficult to contextualize. Is Kornet bad at basketball, or is he just normal and the dropoff from Vic's insanity so steep that it makes you feel like you're getting the rug pulled out from under you? You can even see it with our guys. They're out there throwing weird, very Wemby-coded lob passes to this poor guy who's just trying to crash the basket, and he looks silly when the ball glances off his hands or the backboard or whatever. Meanwhile we're all sitting there like, "Geez, we gotta figure out this backup center situation." But I truly don't know what there is to be done. Vic can't play 48 minutes. Whoever comes in is never going to be him.

I can feel the walls closing in a little bit on Keldon Johnson's time as a Spur. Maybe it won't be this year, but it feels like something eventually might have to give where we can't afford to pay him we he deserves to get paid. I dunno. I'm trying not to think about it and I'm hoping I'm wrong. I'm so glad he has a mustache this year.

Aren't mustaches sort of played out at this point?

Yeah, like, weren't we doing ironic mustaches in the hipster community around 2009? People were tattooing little mustaches on their finger as a bit? You don't think we've overplayed that hand as a society?

I don't think we, as a society, are capable of speaking out as one and declaring anything definitively good, bad, cool, dumb or otherwise. If you don't like a mustache, that's your business. I think the courage and self confidence it takes to only grow out the hair on your upper lip reflects something about what's going on in your soul. Mustaches can be evil. They can be goofy. They can be stoic. They can be boring. They can represent the apex of what it means to be middling, no nonsense, run-of-the-mill accounting type or it can be the pièce de résistance on the most interesting man in the world. Masculinity is a spectrum upon which the mustache squarely sits.

What do you think Keldon's mustache says about him?

Says he can pull off a hook shot in traffic, I'll tell you that much.
