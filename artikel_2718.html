Iowa State will have ‘enhanced monitoring’ of Bill Fennelly’s women’s basketball program

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

After receiving a letter signed by six former players, Iowa State said on Saturday, Nov. 21 that it has "instituted enhanced monitoring" of coach Bill Fennelly's women's basketball program.

The former players sent the letter to Iowa State president David Cook and athletic director Jared Benko on Friday, demanding an "independent, trauma-informed review" of the program, led by Fennelly for 32 years. The players said they "experienced or witnessed conduct we considered to be verbal, emotional, and psychological abuse from" Fennelly.

Four players remained anonymous, but two — Maggie Espenmiller-McGraw and Izabella Zingaro — publicly identified themselves. While a spokesperson for the group of former players told USA TODAY Sports they are not considering legal action at this time, they are being organized and represented by Lori Bullock, a Des Moines-based attorney.

In a statement provided to USA TODAY Sports on Saturday, Iowa State said it "cares deeply about the health and well-being of our student-athletes and takes seriously any claims of conduct contrary to our values and standards," and that the university has reached out to Bullock for details about the players' claims.

"In the meantime, to best live up to our mission and to support our student-athletes as well as Coach Fennelly and his coaching staff, we have instituted enhanced monitoring of the women's basketball program," the statement said.

The letter from former players comes on the heels of Fennelly serving a one-game suspension for making "inappropriate contact" with one of his players. During a timeout in Iowa State's 69-64 win over Illinois-Chicago on Sunday, Nov. 14, Fennelly scolded sophomore guard Sienna Harvey and then pushed her head with his hand.

Fennelly later apologized for the incident and "for allowing my emotions to get the best of me." The 69-year-old — who is the second-longest tenured Division I head coach in the country, trailing only UConn's Geno Auriemma — began coaching at Iowa State in 1995. Fennelly announced last month he is retiring at the end of this season.

After Iowa State's 72-59 win over Northern Iowa on Wednesday, Nov. 18, Harvey and teammate Mya Babbitt publicly defended Fennelly.

"I can see how it was perceived on the internet, but, yeah, like, he loves us and cares about us more than anyone," Harvey said. "And I like being coached hard, so I think it was perceived differently than what it was."

In their letter, the former players said they understand tough coaching, but that Fennelly's behavior crossed a line.

"We accepted the demands of hard work, discipline, and competitive excellence. But demanding excellence is fundamentally different from undermining an athlete's dignity, well-being, and sense of safety. For some of us, the consequences did not end with our playing careers. We continue to deal with anxiety, depression, diminished self-worth, and lasting distress that we associate with our time in the program," the former players said. "We also believe the systems and supports meant to protect student-athletes repeatedly failed. Some of us felt dismissed, discouraged from speaking openly, or afraid of the consequences of raising concerns."

Fennelly has led the Cyclones to 24 NCAA Tournaments.

His suspension and this letter from former players comes after the Cyclones experienced an offseason exodus where nine players transferred out of the program. The departures included the nation's second-leading scorer in Audi Crooks and All-Big 12 selections in Addy Brown and Jada Williams. Crooks transferred in-conference to Oklahoma State, Brown went to UCLA and Williams landed at LSU.

The Cyclones were picked to finish 11th in the Big 12 preseason poll. They're 6-0 to start the season.

During Fennelly's suspension, associate head coach Caroline McCombs took over. Fennelly is expected back on the sidelines for Iowa State's game at home on Sunday, Nov. 22 against Drake.

"We live in a world where, when you see something on video, everybody's going to make an opinion," Fennelly told reporters on Friday, before the letter from ex-players became public. "... I am who I am. I'm not going to apologize for that. Our kids know how much I love them, how much I care about them, how hard we work to do what we do, and try to do the right things. Certainly, I understand both sides of it."
