10 takeaways as Celtics steamroll Mavericks for 6th straight win

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The Celtics have learned this season that no lead is safe in the NBA.

They blew a 22-point advantage against the Pistons in their opener and overcame a 22-point deficit versus the Raptors on Wednesday.

When they built a 33-point cushion through 1 in Sunday's 131-94 victory over the Mavericks, it would have been easy to accept that the game was definitively theirs. Instead, they continued to play with urgency and didn't let Dallas come anywhere close to a comeback.

Here are 10 takeaways as Boston played one of its most complete games to date and earned its sixth consecutive win.

To say the Celtics dominated the first quarter would be putting it lightly. They built a 42-9 advantage through one, shooting 59 percent from the floor and 54 percent from 3. It was the largest lead after the first quarter in franchise history.

The Mavericks were at 11 percent from 3 and 0 percent from distance, and they turned it over six times. Not much went wrong for Boston, and not much went right for Dallas. It was complete and total domination.

Jayson Tatum had the second-highest plus-minus (plus-33) in a single quarter in NBA history.

Joe Mazzulla wasn't ready to assume the Celtics were on their way to a stress-free victory.

"It tests your ability to remain focused on the process," Mazzulla said.

There was a brief moment in the second quarter where it felt like maybe, just maybe, the Mavericks were onto something. They cut it to 24, then Boston finished the quarter strong to take a 67-37 lead into halftime.

Runs are going to happen, but the Celtics are learning how to counteract what the opposing team does with their own move to maintain a comfortable cushion.

"It goes back to the formula that we're trying to create," Mazzulla said. "It's not about hanging onto leads. That's a passive mindset. It's not about building on them. It's just understanding the ebbs and flows of the game."

Tatum was solid in the first half, then exploded for 21 in the third quarter to finish with 32 for the game on 11-of-18 shooting.

It was one of his best performances of the season and the kind of showing where you look up from your screen and suddenly he has 25.

"Obviously it's easy to really bring it when it's a close game, or things like that," Tatum said. "Just trying to challenge our mindset of we're up 25 to 30, can you try to put the game away? I think we did a really good job of that today."

Tatum continues to make the game look rather easy. Efficiency is always key with him, and he was extremely efficient Sunday night.

Jordan Walsh was aggressive from the opening tip, scoring 8 points in the opening five minutes to help the Celtics build an early 22-4 advantage.

"Jordan is continuing to develop the mindset that we need him to have versus great players," Mazzulla said. "A great team has to have a group of guys that they can rely on make it difficult for the other team's best player every single night. It has to be by committee. Jordan is on that list. He's on that committee. He's taking ownership of that."

Walsh hit a corner 3 and finished in traffic twice, looking confident and in command against a disorganized Mavericks defense. It was his most shot attempts (nine) on the season and his most points (11) since Oct. 27 against the Nets.

On the other end, he locked up Cooper Flagg at times, got in various Mavericks' faces and rotated well defensively. When asked where he stacks up in that respect, Walsh didn't hesitate.

"I think I'm the best defender," he said.

He continues to provide energy, versatility and burst for the Celtics, and the additional offense is a nice bonus.

Walsh said he is not graded by the box score, but rather how much he affects the other team's primary ball handler and other pest-like metrics. At the same time, when he gets the ball, he is as confident as ever and it shows.

"I'm just trying to find pockets to be aggressive," Walsh said. "I don't get a lot of shots, so when I do catch the ball, I'm looking for an opportunity to score instead of trying to passing it. I'm trying to create a real threat."

Neemias Queta swished a corner 3 with ease early in the third quarter. Queta, who also hit one Wednesday against the Raptors, is now 4 for 5 on the season.

He's not going to magically morph into Steph Curry (though his percentage is higher), but his ability to keep defenses honest goes a long way for this team. When he's open, he should let it fly.

Queta finished with 8 points (3 of 3), 6 rebounds, 2 assists, 1 block and 0 turnovers (plus-31) in 15 minutes and took the rest of the night off.

"His growth and his role has gone kind of under the radar," Mazzulla said. "He's more important than just with defense. He's important in the locker room, he's important on the offensive end. He continues to get better and better. It's easy to overlook it because of how easy he makes it."

Derrick White helped set the tone Sunday with a sizzling first quarter and ended the night with 16 points on 6-of-11 shooting.

White, who added 19 against the Raptors (7 of 15) is averaging 13.4 points in November. While his efficiency isn't quite where he wants it to be, it is trending positively. Whether the shots go in or not, White needs to keep taking them as long as they are high-percentage looks.

Baylor Scheierman contributed 12 points, Ron Harper Jr. 10, Hugo González 9 and Chris Cenac Jr. 6. Devin Carter and Luka Garza also fared admirably.

Harper Jr. has the talent of a rotational player. Whether that opportunity comes with the Celtics or a different team remains to be seen.

Cenac showed some bounce and looked like he belonged. Carter hustled on both ends and was a steady presence.

Flagg, playing in front of a sea of supporters, was scoreless in the first quarter but grew progressively stronger as the game progressed.

He finished with 17 on 5-of-15 shooting and was a game-low minus-30. Flagg had his moments, with a silky step-back 3 and a thunderous dunk, but overall it was a lukewarm New England homecoming for the Maine native. Even so, despite the outcome, he enjoyed the environment.

"It's special," Flagg told reporters. "Just being able to come here and play and feel that love and support every time is going to be really special. It's something that I'm not going to take for granted. It's such a blessing."

It was too bad Kyrie Irving didn't play. I'm sure he would have been showered with love from the Boston faithful. But seriously, it was a shame he wasn't out there.

The game may have unfolded differently. Irving's tenure in town was complicated, but the man can hoop like few others in NBA history.

A Tatum versus Irving showdown, with both back from injury and in a groove, would have been fun to watch.

The Celtics are one of the hottest teams in the NBA and are showing no signs of slowing down.

It's still early, but this team may have a higher ceiling than last year's team. The top of the East is anyone's game, and the Celtics are very much in that mix with the 76ers, Pistons and Knicks.

After a home game against the Magic on Monday, a grueling stretch of six of seven games on the road will test them. If they can go 4-3 or 5-2, that should be viewed as a success.
