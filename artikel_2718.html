Luka Doncic leadership on display as Lakers unexpected starter went bonkers

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Luka Dončić recognized what was happening.

He has been at the center of so many scoring flurries. He can tell when someone is about to have a hot hand. When an explosion is about to happen.

So when he saw the telltale signs coming from one of his teammates, he jumped into action, waving off Laker coach JJ Redick's play calls in the fourth quarter.

"No, I'm trying to get Q the ball," Doncic told Redick. "I'm trying to get Q."

Luka Doncic of the Los Angeles Lakers reacts after scoring against the Charlotte Hornets on Tuesday, Nov. 17, 2026, at Crypto.com Arena in Los Angeles. Getty Images

Quentin Grimes went on to have the most spectacular performance of his career, scoring a career-high 47 points in the Lakers' 118-114 overtime win over the Hornets.

"I got going and caught fire a little bit," Grimes said.

The 26-year-old guard had 21 points in the fourth quarter, singlehandedly outscoring the Hornets (17 points) in that period to help the Lakers claw their way back from an 18-point deficit.

On a night when the Lakers were without Austin Reaves and Walker Kessler, Grimes took over. On a night when Doncic was in foul trouble, Grimes played the hero.

It was obvious that Grimes was having a different kind of night at the top of the fourth quarter.

In a 23-second stretch, Grimes made a 3-pointer, stole the ball from Ryan Kalkbrenner and then drained another shot from beyond the arc to cut the Lakers' 12-point deficit to 6 points with 9:04 left.

Los Angeles Lakers guard Quentin Grimes reacts after making a 3-point shot during a game against the Charlotte Hornets on Tuesday, Nov. 17, 2026, in Los Angeles. Getty Images

From that moment forward, it was clear the ball was flying at a higher arc than usual when it left Grimes' fingertips. The rim had seemingly opened a little wider. He was having one of those nights where the ball seemed magnetized to the bottom of the net once it left his hands.

He went on to score 15 points in a row for the Lakers.

So, when the Lakers found themselves down 111-108 with 12.1 seconds left, there was no doubt who was going to take the final shot.

It wasn't the Slovenian superstar who's leading the league in scoring with 39 points per game.

It was the journeyman who had been traded three times since the LA Clippers selected him with the 25th pick in the 2021 draft, playing for the New York, Detroit, Dallas, Philadelphia and the Lakers over six seasons.

Quentin Grimes (5) of the Los Angeles Lakers dribbles during a game against the Charlotte Hornets in Los Angeles on Tuesday, Nov. 17, 2026. NBAE via Getty Images

After Matisse Thybulle chased down a missed free throw by Coby White, he passed the ball to Doncic, who dribbled down the court and stopped at the top of the 3-point line. Doncic pump-faked, then passed it to Grimes.

Grimes swished a 26-foot 3-pointer with 1.7 seconds left, sending the game into overtime with the score knotted at 111-111.

The shorthanded Lakers went on to pull off their ninth straight win, improving to 13-2, the best record in the league.

"What an amazing game from him, so it was unbelievable to watch," said Doncic, who had 36 points, six rebounds, seven assists and five fouls.

Quentin Grimes #5 of the Los Angeles Lakers shoots a three-point basket during a game against the Charlotte Hornets on Tuesday, Nov. 17, 2026, in Los Angeles. NBAE via Getty Images

Grimes shot a stunning 10-for-14 from beyond the arc, becoming the only Laker to score 10 or more 3-pointers in a game other than Kobe Bryant.

"Man, I'm going to sleep good knowing that one tonight for sure," Grimes said. "But that's just crazy. Kobe's one of my idols growing up watching him. You try to model your game after him and his intensity, his passion for the game, that's just something that you don't see often.

"He's one of the reasons that I wanted to play this game. My dad was a Lakers fan from here and everything. So that's just crazy to have my name in a conversation with a legend like that."

After missing two games because of a sprained right pinky, Grimes returned Sunday against the Suns and went on to bank a game-sealing 3-pointer with 32.2 seconds left to lift the Lakers to a 129-121 win.

When asked if he called bank, he joked that he called game.

Then against the Hornets, Grimes had his magnum opus, surpassing his 46-point performance with the 76ers in March 2025.

"The hot-hand theory, I believe was prior debunked," Redick said. "Now it's been confirmed that it's a real thing. It definitely was a real thing tonight."

For Grimes, it was the type of game that can imbue him with confidence for an entire season.

It was the type of game that can tilt his trajectory.

"I'm calling this a statement game," he said.
