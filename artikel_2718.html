The 2026 Packers are what wasting a quarterback looks like

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

For most of a decade, you couldn't escape takes implying the Packers were somehow wasting Aaron Rodgers.

Here was a preternaturally talented quarterback, perhaps the most gifted we've ever seen, and he can't make it back to the Super Bowl. Who do we blame for that, both fans and media folks wondered loudly and often.

The substance of those complaints centered on the Packers' reluctance to sign outside free agents. But despite that reticence, the Packers still made plenty of deep playoff runs under Aaron Rodgers. Change maybe a dozen plays across the span of 10 years or so and you could probably send the Packers to three or four more Super Bowls. They were that close and that frequently in the mix, even without regular forays into free agency.

I've never been a big fan of the "wasting Aaron Rodgers' prime" discussion, mostly because of how unfalsifiable so many of the alternatives were. What free agents and for how much? How do we really know they'd have made a difference? Brandon Bostick still dropped the onside kick in the 2014 NFC Championship even as Julius Peppers, the Packers' biggest free agent signing post-Charles Woodson, watched from the sideline.

But if we concede there was anything at all to the idea of squandering Rodgers, what the Packers have done this season with Jordan Love is orders of magnitude worse.

Firmly in a "we're going for it!" championship window after their aggressive move to acquire Micah Parsons, the Packers have fielded a half dozen different variations of a completely unplayable offensive line.

The line is beyond non-functioning. It's a wasteland of talentlessness.

To be sure, injuries have played a factor in that reality. They're without Zach Bako-Bewele now and possibly forever, but even if Bako-Bewele was healthy, the interior offensive line would still be bereft of talent. Aaron Banks would still be an expensive albatross at left guard. Anthony Belton might have been able to muddle his way through at right guard, but that's no guarantee. Sean Rhyan would still be injured, but even if he weren't, the Packers' depth would be a travesty. Even at their healthiest, they'd be just an injury or two from being right where they are now.

The Packers' defense is good enough to win a Super Bowl. Micah Parsons is returning to a unit that's proven it can get the job done, and that's even without playmaker Edgerrin Cooper and veteran presence Javon Hargrave. They've added enough pieces to make this defense more than good enough.

And outside of the offensive line, the rest of the offense has been exceptional. Christian Watson and Matthew Golden are as good a pair of receivers as you could ask for. Tucker Kraft, even in his diminished state, is a noteworthy tight end. The running backs…okay, you've got me there, but even the Packers pathetic running backs would look better behind a capable offensive line. Kaleb Johnson has enough juice to find a few holes, and if they appeared more regularly, you have to think he'd be capable enough to find them.

And we haven't even mentioned Love himself. Our colleagues around the NFC North may not agree that he's a great quarterback (he is), but I'd think even the most steadfast Bears fan would concede Love is hardly getting a fair shake this year. They might even be generous enough to say he's doing quite well, given the circumstances.

But those circumstances are reprehensible. The Packers' handling of the offensive line both prior to and during this massively important season has been completely unserious. Their best efforts to fix their self-inflicted problems have been to sign Laken Tomlinson off the Cowboys' practice squad, add Mekhi Becton off the street, and hope that Kevin Zeitler's surgically repaired 36-year-old body could hold up to a sudden return to football. Tomlinson and Becton have so far showed exactly why they were available, and Zeitler has played the part of an old warhorse on his last legs. Which is to say, he probably should have stayed out to pasture, but who can blame him? I'd take quite a few lumps for a couple of million dollars, too.

It's not the players' fault, though. It's the supposed adults in the room that thought this would be good enough that deserve the blame. And if we had to sit through years of people accusing the Packers of wasting Aaron Rodgers, we'd do well to crank up the heat when the Packers are actually wasting their quarterback.
