Molly Miller Goes Viral For Sideline Outfit As Fans Discover Arizona State Coach’s Remarkable Basketball Success

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Molly Miller is turning heads both on and off the basketball court. The Arizona State women's basketball coach has gone viral after stepping onto the sidelines in a stylish black outfit, leaving fans asking where they could buy her top. But there's much more to the 41-year-old than her game-day wardrobe. Miller has become one of college basketball's most talked-about coaches after transforming a struggling Arizona State program into an NCAA Tournament contender in just one season. Now, with a new contract and a reloaded roster, she's giving fans plenty of reasons to pay attention.

A post shared by Sun Devil Women's Basketball (@sundevilwbb)

Miller made an impression during Arizona State's season opener against Jackson State on November 2, wearing an all-black ensemble that quickly attracted attention online. The coach paired a fitted black top with matching trousers, keeping her look sleek and polished as she directed her players from the sidelines. On November 4, the Sun Devils women's basketball account shared photos of Miller with the caption, "Stepping into Year 2 in style."

The post sparked enthusiastic reactions from fans who were just as interested in her wardrobe as they were in the team's performance.

"Ok but where is the top from???" one person asked.

"Molly is the best," another wrote, while a third commented, "If you look good you play good it's simple."

According to The Spun, Miller's fashion choices have become a talking point among basketball followers, adding to her growing popularity on social media. Her outfit wasn't the only thing worth celebrating that night. Arizona State opened its season with an impressive 84-41 victory over Jackson State, giving Miller a winning start to her second year in charge.

Thomas Fernandez / Image of Sport Photos/Newscom/MEGA

Long before she became a viral sensation, Miller had already built an impressive reputation in women's college basketball. Born in Springfield, Missouri, she played basketball at Drury University, where she later began her coaching career. Miller spent six seasons leading Drury's women's basketball program, compiling 180 victories and establishing herself as one of the most successful coaches in Division II. Her success eventually brought her to Grand Canyon University in 2020.

During five seasons with the Antelopes, Miller recorded a 117-38 record and transformed the program into a conference powerhouse. Her final season at Grand Canyon was particularly memorable. The team finished 32-3, went undefeated in Western Athletic Conference play with a 16-0 record, and captured both the regular-season and conference tournament championships. The achievement secured Grand Canyon's first NCAA Tournament appearance in women's basketball. Arizona State took notice, hiring Miller in March 2025 to revive its struggling program.

When Miller arrived in Tempe, Arizona State was coming off a disappointing 10-22 season. The Sun Devils had also gone several years without an NCAA Tournament appearance, leaving the new coach with plenty of work to do. Miller wasted little time changing the team's fortunes. In her first season, Arizona State finished 24-11, setting a school record for the most victories by a first-year head coach. The Sun Devils also opened the campaign with 15 consecutive wins and returned to the NCAA Tournament for the first time since 2019. Although their tournament run ended with a First Four loss to Virginia, the turnaround represented a significant achievement for a program that had struggled to find consistency.

According to Arizona State Athletics, the team also set a program attendance record during the 2025-26 season, drawing 61,695 fans. Miller's coaching record placed her among the winningest active women's college basketball coaches by career winning percentage. By the end of her first season at Arizona State, she had accumulated 321 career victories against just 66 losses across her collegiate coaching career.

Thomas Fernandez/Image of Sport/Newscom/MEGA

Miller isn't relying solely on last season's success to keep Arizona State competitive. Ahead of the 2026-27 campaign, the coach and her staff worked to strengthen the roster through the transfer portal. According to Arizona State Athletics, the Sun Devils added Ayanna-Sarai Darrington, Rashunda Jones, Averie Lower, Ella Ryan, and Ruby Whitehorn. The recruiting effort attracted national attention, with ESPN recognizing Arizona State's transfer class as one of the strongest in the country.

The new additions are expected to help Miller build on the momentum established during her first season. Her team's opening performance against Jackson State offered an early glimpse of what the retooled roster could accomplish. Despite falling behind 11-2 in the opening minutes, Arizona State recovered to secure a commanding 84-41 victory. Heloisa Carrera led the Sun Devils with 15 points and nine rebounds. For Miller, the result provided an encouraging start as she attempts to establish Arizona State as a consistent contender in women's college basketball.

Thomas Fernandez/Image of Sport/Newscom/MEGA

Arizona State has already made it clear that it wants Miller leading its women's basketball program for years to come. In August, the university announced a contract extension keeping the coach in Tempe through 2031. Athletics Director Graham Rossini praised her immediate influence on the program.

"Molly's impact has been immediate, creating real momentum and putting Sun Devil Women's Basketball in the national spotlight," Rossini said in the university's announcement.

He added that the extension reflected confidence in Miller's leadership and her ability to build a team capable of competing for championships. Miller also expressed her appreciation for the university's support.

"My family and staff are incredibly grateful for the opportunity to continue building at Arizona State," she said.

The coach, who shares two children with her husband, Derek, has spoken enthusiastically about her future with the Sun Devils. With her team winning games, her coaching reputation growing, and her sideline style attracting a new audience, Miller is entering her second Arizona State season with considerably more attention than when she arrived.
