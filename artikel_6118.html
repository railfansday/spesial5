<i>Grand Theft Auto VI</i> and How We Spend Our Time

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Your time on earth, no matter how long, is shorter than you think. Do you want to spend it doing chores in a simulated environment?

A gamer plays Grand Theft Auto VI during an unofficial launch party at OS Gaming Lounge in New York City on November 19, 2026.

Donald Trump just met with Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping in China for a tripartite global summit — and nobody has any real news to report, except for Trump, so desperate for media attention that he’s giving interviews to anyone holding a hot mic and a video camera. The lack of news is not just because of a (predictable) blackout enforced by the Chinese Communist Party, but because the collective consciousness of America has shut down for the next few days. If you thought the traditional four-day Thanksgiving Day Weekend was typically slow, get ready for a one-and-a-half-week productivity dip in the economy as the video game Grand Theft Auto VI goes live across the nation. Even National Review, as sober-minded as it is, has more than a few fans of the series on its staff.

Every periodical you might care to read — even the political or news-based ones — has brought forward its big-think “review” of the game, now available on PlayStation and Xbox. For all I know we’ll be publishing one too. But I am not a gamer — not in the modern sense of the word, although I did whip out my copy of Battletoads for the NES the other day to prove that I’ve still “got it” — and this will not be a review of GTA VI. Instead, I want to talk about time spent — and time wasted.

A gamer plays Grand Theft Auto VI during an unofficial launch party at OS Gaming Lounge in New York City on November 19, 2026.

Jeffrey Blehar is a National Review staff writer living in Chicago. He is also the co-host of National Review’s Political Beats podcast, which explores the great music of the modern era with guests from the political world happy to find something non-political to talk about.
