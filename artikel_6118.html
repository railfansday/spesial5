This is only the third nationwide ground stop in 25 years. The last one, during a 2023 system outage, lasted about two hours.

The Federal Aviation Administration halted all departing flights nationwide Friday after a communication issue affected the country’s Air Route Traffic Control Centers, the high-altitude air traffic control facilities that manage flights between airports, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said.

The FAA “issued a nationwide ground stop for all U.S. airports following a communication issue impacting all Air Route Traffic Control Centers,” Duffy said in a post on X. “FAA technicians are investigating the issue. We will provide immediate updates as we have them.”

The agency said all departing flights would be grounded, and inbound international flights would be managed on a case-by-case basis.

The FAA halted all departing flights across the U.S. on Friday after a communication issue affected every Air Route Traffic Control Center, the high-altitude facilities that manage flights between airports, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said. Duffy said on X that the agency "issued a nationwide ground stop for all U.S. airports following a communication issue impacting all Air Route Traffic Control Centers," and that FAA technicians are investigating. The agency said all departing flights would be grounded and inbound international flights would be handled case by case, citing "extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety." Nationwide ground stops are extremely rare: the FAA issued one on 9/11 after the terror attacks, and again in January 2023 over problems with a pilot alert system. The story is breaking, and the FAA has not said how long the stop will last or what caused the communication failure.
