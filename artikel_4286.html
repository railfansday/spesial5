Rockstar Moves Up GTA 6 Pre-Load Date, Confirming Massive File Size

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Rockstar has brought Grand Theft Auto 6's pre-load date forward by two days and finally confirmed how much storage we'll need. According to a new Newswire announcement, pre-loading begins on November 10 instead of November 12, and the file could require up to 212 GB of space, depending on the device.

According to a new rumor, Rockstar Games could release the GTA 6 PC port just a few months after the highly anticipated console launch.

With the game still scheduled to launch on November 19, that gives you a little more time to get the download out of the way. The earlier start is welcome news for anyone with a slower internet connection, although finding enough free storage might be the bigger problem.

In its Newswire announcement, Rockstar says the pre-load file will require "up to 212 GB depending on your device." The developer also advises everyone to clear enough room before pre-loading begins. That's a substantial amount of storage, but the wording is worth paying attention to.

The phrase "up to" suggests that 212 GB is an upper limit rather than a fixed requirement for every console. Rockstar hasn't provided a platform-by-platform breakdown, and we still don't know whether the final installed game will take up exactly the same amount of space.

The figure is close to the 210 GB size reported on October 27 by PlayStation Game Size, an account that tracks information from the PlayStation Store. That earlier estimate concerned the PS5 version and wasn't an official confirmation from Rockstar. We now have a confirmed upper limit for pre-loading, although the final installation size remains unclear.

The distinction matters if you're trying to decide how many games to uninstall. The amount of storage required for pre-loading and the game's eventual installed footprint can differ, especially with updates around launch. For now, making at least 212 GB available, preferably with some extra room, seems like a sensible precaution.

I can already see this becoming a problem for me. My drives are usually left with just 10–20 GB of free space, mostly because I refuse to uninstall games I might suddenly get hyperfixated on again. I always tell myself I'll return to them someday, even if that means keeping them installed for months. If that sounds familiar, you might want to start clearing some space now.

Rockstar originally announced November 12 as the pre-load date, giving everyone a week to download the game before release. Its latest Newswire post moves that forward to November 10, with slightly different timings for each platform.

On PlayStation 5, digital pre-loading begins at midnight local time on November 10. Xbox Series X|S pre-loading, meanwhile, begins globally at 12 a.m. GMT on the same day. That gives console owners several days to finish downloading what could be one of the largest games in their libraries.

Rockstar's information hasn't been entirely consistent, however. Its online store listing still mentions November 12, while an older section of its customer support article also retains that date. The same support article now includes the updated November 10 timings further down, matching the latest Newswire announcement. For anyone planning their download, those newer instructions are the ones to follow.

The change also raises questions for anyone buying a physical copy. Rockstar previously confirmed that boxed editions of GTA 6 will contain a download code rather than a game disc, with retailers expected to begin distributing them on November 12. That schedule remains unchanged in the latest announcement, so anyone waiting for a boxed copy will still need to receive and redeem their code before downloading the game.

Pre-loading won't unlock GTA 6 early, of course. The game is still scheduled for November 19 on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. But after years of waiting, having the download finished ahead of release is one less thing to worry about. I'd certainly rather spend launch day playing GTA 6 than staring at a progress bar.
