Blade Runner 2099 sneaks in a book detail that never made it into the movies

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

As adaptations go, Blade Runner is a fairly loose take on Philip K. Dick's Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?

The 1982 film replaces the novel's androids (or "andys") with replicants. Characters from the novel were cut, including Iran, the wife of Rick Deckard (Harrison Ford), and John Isidore, who houses the androids Deckard hunts. (William Sanderson's J.F. Sebastian effectively replaces him in the film.) These are just some world-building details from Dick's work that Blade Runner couldn't fit into a feature-length film.

However, one of these missing elements just made its way into the Blade Runner 2099 TV series.

In Blade Runner 2099's sixth episode, replicants Julius (Tom Burke) and Clotilde (Amy Lennox) drive past a large billboard that advertises "the power of Mercer." The display is a reference to the fictional religion of Mercerism, built around the martyr Wilbur Mercer. Much of the rest of the episode takes place at the abandoned island Mercer Monastery where Cora (Hunter Schafer) grew up.

Mercerism was initially conceptualized in Dick's 1964 short story "The Little Black Box." Followers connect with Mercer by gripping the handles of an empathy box. This device allows the user to experience Mercer's climb to his death: a slow ascent up a hill during which he's stoned repeatedly.

Dick revisits Mercerism in Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?, in which Deckard uses an empathy box and sees visions of Mercer. However, radio host Buster Friendly exposes that the Mercer seen in the empathy boxes is simply a character played by an actor. Buster is omitted from Blade Runner and its sequel Blade Runner 2049. In Blade Runner 2099, however, he is played by Hugo Hamlet, and can be spotted in TV broadcasts.

Mercer plays a key role in Blade Runner 2099 episode 6, which is named "Kill the Killers" after Mercer's major tenet: "You shall kill only the killers."

When Cora and Olwen (Michelle Yeoh) investigate the monastery,  they encounter the lone Mercerite (David Dencik) living there. He's keeping up a religious telecast devoted to Mercer, and he claims the signal is strong enough to reach Los Angeles. But given that no one else has joined him, it appears his efforts are just shouting into the void.

The Mercerite has an empathy box, and he prompts Olwen to use it while he holds her at gunpoint. Within, she sees Mercer (Tomas Rydval) making his last climb. As unseen forces pelt him with rocks, the empathy box shocks Olwen's hands to mimic the painful experience.

Olwen eventually uses a loose handle from the box to stab her captor. Then, she and Cora escape the island without becoming Mercer converts. (Sorry, Wilbur.)

The episode functions both as a tribute to this piece of world-building and as a key subplot that was initially lost when Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep? leaped from page to screen.

Buster Friendly and Wilbur Mercer may not have gotten their time to shine in Blade Runner or Blade Runner 2049, but in a TV series like Blade Runner 2099, there is space to expand on the world and give them a moment in the spotlight, no matter how brief.
