Predicting Awards Season’s First Big Week: Who Wins at the Gothams, NYFCC, NBR and More

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The starting gun of the Oscar season starts Monday, and by next Sunday, it will have a shape that many of us will be able to see clearly.

For months, the Oscar race has run on festival premieres, studio screenings and ever more elaborate predictions, mine included. This week, the people with ballots finally speak.

The Gothams, the New York Film Critics Circle, the National Board of Review, the American Film Institute and LAFCA each answer to a different electorate and each has its own complicated history with the Academy. None of them pick the Oscars, but they all shape the conversation that follows.

The question isn’t only who wins. It’s who wins where, and what each victory says about the corner of the electorate that handed it out.

Here’s how we see the opening week unfolding, with some commentary for each.

The Gothams have long been a launching pad for independent cinema, a place for films that might otherwise get flattened by the studio machine. In 2023, the organization scrapped its $35 million budget ceiling and opened more categories to international titles. The result is a more eclectic ballot, one that puts established auteurs and first-time filmmakers side by side. This year, that means 42 features and 25 performances across 11 categories.

That makes the Gothams a natural first stop for “The Invite,” which we expect to win best feature. Wilde’s ensemble piece, starring Seth Rogen, Penélope Cruz and Edward Norton, has the kind of dinner-party-argument appeal that plays well with this voting body. “Josephine” is my alternate and could follow some jaw-dropping selections like “A Different Man” two years ago. The drama took both the U.S. Grand Jury Prize and the Audience Award at Sundance in January, and the Gothams have never lost their appetite for championing a discovery.

Notably, a different set of industry professionals, five jury members each, for each category, pick the Gotham winners, while Gotham members vote on the audience award. A film can win a jury on artistic grounds while another finds its people in the wider membership.

Founded in 1935, the New York Film Critics Circle is the country’s oldest film critics organization and one of the most influential early stops on the calendar. Its history is full of choices that challenged the Academy’s conventional wisdom. Many of them went on to become landmarks, even when they diverged from the eventual Oscar winner.

This year, we’re predicting “You Can See Everything” to show its strength by winning the top prize for best film, with “The Odyssey” as the alternate. Fielder and Oppenheim’s 174-minute portrait of Elizabeth Holmes premiered as Telluride’s secret screening and has drawn the kind of critical fervor usually reserved for narrative features. A best film win from New York wouldn’t automatically make it an Oscar best picture contender, but it would move the film into a different tier of consideration. “The Odyssey” is the more traditional choice: a major filmmaker, a sweeping production and a likely force in the technical categories.

In directing, Wilde could pull off a deserved citation ahead of Nolan, who has never won from the group before.

Andrew Scott is my choice for actor, with Robert Pattinson’s combined work in “Primetime” and “The Drama” as the alternate. Julianne Moore could win actress for “The Debut,” with Sandra Hüller for “Rose” as a real threat. In supporting actor, Guitarricadelafuente could keep building momentum for “La Bola Negra,” with a surprise citation for someone like John Cho of “Your Mother Your Mother Your Mother” (much like Regina Hall or Tiffany Haddish surprised in their respective years). Kate O’Flynn (“Tender Loving Care”) and Amanda Seyfried (“You Can See Everything”) could be in a battle for supporting actress, or someone else completely.

The screenplay prize could go to Jordan Firstman for “Club Kid,” with Cristian Mungiu’s “Fjord” as the alternate. “Tangles” is my animated film pick over Laika’s “Wildwood,” an interesting split between independent animation and a more established studio shop.

Founded in 1909, the National Board of Review has named a Top 10 every year since 1929. Unlike the critics’ groups, it’s made up of New York-area film enthusiasts rather than working critics. Its selections land just as studios are deciding which campaigns deserve more fuel, which makes it one of the earliest industry signals of the season.

Variety is predicting “Project Hail Mary” for best film, with “Artificial” as the alternate. That would be a fitting choice for a group that has long rewarded accessible, ambitious filmmaking. Phil Lord and Christopher Miller could also take director, ahead of Denis Villeneuve for “Dune: Part Three.” That would follow the three-year current trend of the same film winning both prizes.

If both happen, “Project Hail Mary” has an instant entry for best picture.

The acting picks could be just as revealing, but they don’t always match up with Oscars, so you have to consciously pick an acting winner that probably won’t translate (i.e., Elle Fanning in “A Complete Unknown” or Will Forte in “Nebraska”). Seth Rogen is my actor prediction for “The Invite,” with Matt Damon for “The Odyssey” as the alternate. Inde Navarrette could win actress for “Obsession,” although she can win breakthrough performer. Rachel Zegler won best actress for “West Side Story” (2021) instead of breakthrough her respective year.

John Leguizamo could follow veteran winners who tend to win supporting actor with “The Odyssey.” A “no guts-no glory” pick exists with Elizabeth Debicki could taking supporting actress for “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth,” with Samantha Morton for “The Odyssey” projected as the runner-up.

The screenplay categories offer openings for two very different films. James Gray could win original screenplay for “Paper Tiger,” ahead of Curry Barker for “Obsession.” In adapted, Sian Heder and Rebekah Taussig could be recognized for “Being Heumann,” with “La Bola Negra” as the alternate.

The specialty awards could be telling, too. I’m predicting “Wildwood” for animated feature, Anthony Ippolito for breakthrough performance in “I Play Rocky” and Barker for directorial debut with “Obsession.” “La Bola Negra” could win international film, while “You Can See Everything” is my documentary pick. Hoyte van Hoytema could take cinematography for “The Odyssey,” and “Spider-Man: Brand New Day” could win stunt artistry. “Naza” is my choice for the NBR Freedom of Expression Award.

The lists may matter as much as the prizes. For the Top 10, look for glaring omissions but don’t put too much stock in them. “The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King” (2003) settled for the NBR’s ensemble prize without a Top 10 slot, and Guillermo del Toro’s “The Shape of Water” (2017) was left off entirely. Both won best picture. The NBR’s lists are an early endorsement, not a verdict.

The American Film Institute’s annual selections live in a different space from the critics’ prizes. There are no individual performances and no craft citations, just 10 films the institute considers culturally and artistically representative of the year. The Top 10 is restricted to American productions. Works that fall outside its criteria can receive an AFI Special Award, as Kenneth Branagh’s “Belfast” and Questlove’s documentary “Summer of Soul” did in 2021 and Jafar Panahi’s “It Was Just an Accident” did last year.

That makes the AFI list a measure of which films have entered the industry’s broader conversation. There’s no single winner, and because the list is alphabetical, it isn’t a ranking.

We’re thinking: “Artificial,” “Dune: Part Three,” “Elsinore,” “I Play Rocky,” “The Invite,” “Michael,” “The Odyssey,” “Project Hail Mary,” “Wild Horse Nine” and “Wildwood.” “Obsession” is my alternate.

“You Can See Everything” is my Special Award prediction, with “La Bola Negra” as the alternative since neither can win.

AFI Special Award: “You Can See Everything” (A24)Alternate: “La Bola Negra” (Netflix)

Founded in 1975 by critics working in print, radio and television, LAFCA has become one of the most respected critics’ groups in the country. It votes and announces in December, then presents its awards at a ceremony in January.

We’re seeing “The Odyssey” for best film, with “You Can See Everything” as the alternate. That would hand Nolan a major critical endorsement in a week when New York may have gone the other way. That’s exactly why the opening stretch matters: different groups can watch the same year of movies and come away with very different pictures.

For director, I’m going with Andrey Zvyagintsev for “Minotaur,” ahead of Ryusuke Hamaguchi for “All of a Sudden.”

With non-gendered acting categories, the group chooses two lead and two supporting performances.

Lead performance could produce one of the more distinctive outcomes of the season, with Jeon Do-yeon for “Possible Love” and John Malkovich for “Wild Horse Nine” sharing the prize. Virginie Efira for “All of a Sudden” and Andrew Scott for “Elsinore” are the alternatives.

In supporting performance, Andrew Garfield could win for “Artificial” alongside Amanda Seyfried for “You Can See Everything.” Penélope Cruz for “The Invite” and Anne Hathaway for “The Odyssey” are the alternate selections.

The screenplay award could go to Jones and McCormack for “The Invite,” with Firstman’s “Club Kid” as the alternative. Add it up: a Gotham best feature, a possible New York director win and an LAFCA screenplay prize would give “The Invite” a broad critical footprint before the guilds have said a word.

On the craft side, I’m predicting Linus Sandgren for cinematography for “Dune: Part Three,” Adam Locke-Norton for editing for “You Can See Everything” and Ludwig Göransson for music score for “The Odyssey.” “Backrooms” could surprise in production design, with Danny Vermette ahead of Donald Graham Burt for “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth.”

If “You Can See Everything” wins both editing and documentary, pay attention. A documentary competing in a category usually owned by narrative films would strengthen the argument that it belongs in the larger best picture discussion.
