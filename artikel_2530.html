Everyone on the internet loves to overreact, and that's especially true when we're talking about the NFL. Zach Pereles looked at several things that happened during Week 11, so decide for yourself whether we're all overreacting.

Statement: Jesse Minter will be one-and-done in Baltimore.Overreaction or reality: Overreaction. Lots of the Ravens' issues are out of first-year coach Jesse Minter's control. The Lamar Jackson injuries, obviously, threw the season way off course. Even Sunday's game was another example of the things Minter can't control going wrong. Justice Hill fumbled 1 yard from the end zone. LaJohntay Wester muffed two punts, one of which the Panthers recovered. Derrick Henry also lost a fumble. It's been a season from hell. But there are plenty of issues Minter has been responsible for — or at least hasn't fixed — this year. Regarded as a defensive whiz after two years as the Chargers' defensive coordinator, he now oversees a defense that ranks in the bottom half of the league in yards per game and yards per play, and in the bottom 10 in yards per pass attempt and takeaways. Simply put, it's been underwhelming at best for a team expected to contend for a championship. But the Ravens won't fire Minter at season's end. There are big-picture questions about Jackson and the entire roster, but Minter should get an offseason to tweak and improve his defense and fit it better to his defenders.

Statement: The Buccaneers have a serious Baker Mayfield problem.Overreaction or reality: Reality. The Buccaneers extended Mayfield with a three-year, $165 million deal just before the season began, after it seemed like an extension wouldn't happen. Tampa Bay must be wishing it hadn't made the deal. Mayfield is constantly banged up - he's dealing with plantar fasciitis in addition to the thumb injury that caused him to miss several games already this year - and he can't physically make his play style work. How the Buccaneers overcome this is TBD, because there are no realistic easy answers.

Statement: Jahmyr Gibbs will win Offensive Player of the Year.  Overreaction or reality: Reality. It wasn't always pretty, but Jahmyr Gibbs ground out 118 yards from scrimmage and two touchdowns in the Lions' 31-21 win over the Buccaneers. He's up to 1,502 yards from scrimmage this season and on pace to finish with 2,553. That would break Chris Johnson's all-time record of 2,509 from 2009. That, one would think, would clinch an Offensive Player of the Year award for Gibbs. Even if he doesn't break the record, Gibbs would be a deserving winner, so long as he keeps producing. He's had at least 110 yards from scrimmage in all but two games.
