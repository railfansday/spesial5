15 Best Arch Support Shoes for Long Travel Days

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

I grew up believing that sore and throbbing feet were a necessary evil of long travel days, until a physical therapist said three magic words: arch-support shoes. She’d noticed the inside forefoot and outside heels of my shoes’ soles were completely worn through—a sign of overpronation because I have no natural arch. With that kind of misalignment, it was no surprise that even a simple day of set-jetting through The Sound of Music sites in Austria or strolling through the compact center of Prague was wreaking havoc on my feet.

As it turns out, as much as 37% of the population is believed to have some degree of flat foot, according to a National Library of Medicine study. But how it affects everyday life can vary by person. Initially, I only noticed the pain on high step-count days, but when I started long-distance running 10 years ago, I knew it was time to invest in supportive shoes capable of going the distance.

So I visited my local Fleet Feet in Hoboken, New Jersey, where nowadays its complimentary Fit ID 3-D scan can get granular, most recently showing me that my two feet have different lengths, as well as varying ball and heel widths. Most blaringly, both my arch heights are on the lowest possible end of the scale.

What I’ve learned more than anything is that there is no one-size-fits-all supportive shoe that works for everyone, which is why individual fittings are so crucial. (In extreme cases, like when I got posterior tibial tendinitis after running a half marathon, be sure to consult a healthcare professional.) But for a dose of stability, here are 15 arch-support shoes that are primed for long days on your feet.

For the last decade, the Brooks Adrenaline GTS has been my go-to shoe—that’s what GTS stands for, after all. The shoe’s GuideRails technology works its magic not by trying to correct your stride, but by guiding it in the direction that feels natural. Not only did it get me through my first few races with New York Road Runners, and eventually NYC Marathon training, but it’s always the shoe I go back to when I crave stability with just a sprinkle of cushioning. Whenever I slip these on, I feel locked in. The new version, which launched on November 1, retains its trusted features, while refining the heel tab and tongue to be sleeker and more functional.

During the most wrenching times of my foot pain, when every step I took hurt, an orthopedic doctor encouraged me to get a pair of Hokas and start running again. The brand may have led the way with oversized cushioned soles, but one of its bestsellers is this stability shoe, with an H-frame to prevent arch-less feet from turning inward. The newest version features a tighter mesh upper on the inside portion of the shoe, adding support to its arch side while retaining that soft cushiony feel. It even bears an American Podiatric Medical Association (AMPA) seal certifying that it’s beneficial to foot health.

Oofos may be known for its slides and recovery footwear (I wore a pair for a week after the 2024 Boston Marathon), but the same lightweight support strategies are applied to this casual sneaker. Its trademark high-impact absorbing Oofoam soaks up 37% of impact compared to regular shoes. Best of all, it’s machine washable with a closed-cell foam that prevents odors.

Let’s face it: Hiking shoes can be a pain to pack—which is what makes this Columbia hiking boot that's disguised as a sneaker so brilliant. Without any breaking in, they were immediately comfortable yet firm, and have kept my feet dry through sudden downpours and even river crossings on a hike in Thailand. This fall, they doubled as a sporty shoe, as I ran and biked the 32-mile Moose Loop Trail around Anchorage over three days, including a drizzly four-mile run on the final day.

Most impressively, during my Intrepid Travel Kenya Safari Adventure earlier this month, rain started falling while we were planting trees in the Mt. Kenya region’s Intrepid-adopted forest until we were trudging through mud. But the Konos’s sophisticated underfoot system provided support with maximum cushioning and traction so that I never doubted my stability any step of the way.

During the worst of my foot issues, I thought I'd be confined to a life of bulky orthotics. That’s when Vionic saved me. Every shoe in its collection—from sandals to dress shoes—is powered by its podiatrist-approved Vio Motion technology with a deep heel cup for stability, shock-absorbing cushioning, flexible movement, and most noticeably, strong arch-support that realigns the body while improving balance. This casual sneaker merges all that into a lace-up design that’s as supportive as it is cute. Not only was there zero break-in period for instant comfort, but it also meets the One Planet sustainability standard.

As I learned during my most recent Fleet Feet fitting, arch support and heel stability are inherently linked—and Kuru’s own patented sole hones in precisely those two traits. Together, they turn these lightweight court shoes into a secret weapon, able to endure the miles without an inkling of fatigue. Plus the versatile style can be dressed up or down.

The 90-year-old brand from the Italian Dolomites is best known for its mountaineering gear—and it’s that kind of technical expertise that was used to develop its Pedrock hiking shoe. The visible 3F System ties together flexibility, fit, and support. The adjustable laces ensure that the shoe fits like a glove while an EVA plate provides stability, along with reinforced heel and toe caps. All together, the urban-to-mountain shoe transitions perfectly through every step of your travels.

When it comes to technical gear, there are so many bells and whistles to consider that it’s easy to overlook the basics—namely, support. Teva never goes wrong in that realm and uses its own Lite-Comf midsole for a cushiony step, along with a patented universal heel lock system and Vibram grip for traction on the outer, so there’s no reason to teeter, even on multiple-day hikes over varied terrain. Those looking for less bulkiness with the same support can also opt for the lightweight road-to-trail Aventrail R2T shoe, which launched earlier this year.

The dependability that comes with Merrell’s high-performing outdoor wear is merged with Gore-Tex’s gold standard of waterproofness in this simple yet powerful hiking boot. It's easy to see why it's become my go-to for the last few years. At 16.36-oz. per shoe, it’s a hearty design, which equals stability throughout. The Kinetic Fit–blend contoured footbed strengthens the heel and a molded nylon shank adds arch support, while Vibram traction on the soles prevents slipping on slick surfaces, eliminating any flat-footed hiker’s worries—and taking me on adventures I thought were out of my range.

Cold weather footwear has long been a struggle for me—until I met this travel-friendly ankle boot from my trusted Vionic. Its classic design is easy to pair with anything from dresses to jeans, and the full-length zipper makes it easy to slip on and off. But don’t underestimate its durability, equipped with a completely waterproof leather upper and sealed seams to ensure not a drop seeps in. Plus, it conquers my fear of slippery sidewalks with articulated pods that provide traction for steady steps while still feeling cushiony.

The Kizik brand may seem like a novelty, focused on the ability to step in and out of shoes hands-free—yes, even these boots with laces (that don’t actually need to be tied). But the Juno 2 goes beyond just speeding you through TSA checkpoints. It’s chock full of support, boasting a molded EVA foam midsole for comfort and an Arch Form insole that molds to your foot shape.

Here’s what Vionic does best—take the patented footbed it's known for with orthotic-grade arch support and apply it to styles that don’t disclose your foot problems. This everyday loafer, made with post-consumer recycled materials, combines that solid support with extreme flexibility and a grippy sole. But the best part for travelers is how packable these loafers are—the upper (which comes in either super soft leather or suede) squishes easily, yet retains its shape and support. I’ve worn these to conferences and events, and often get asked who makes them, as it’s rare to find business casual shoes that are so supportive. I stocked up with three pairs waiting in my closet for when my first ones wear out—though after more than a year, they’ve shown no signs of aging.

A ballet flat with actual arch support? Only Birkenstock can make that possible with its trademark cork-latex footbed known for its firm arch support, along with a natural leather insole and suede lining. All that support may sound stiff, but it’s evened out with EVA cushioning. All together, it’s that free-flowing feeling from its famous Big Buckle sandals wrapped in a cozy exterior.

This leather slip-on may look like an everyday flat, but its comfort features are where it shines, starting with sturdy arch-support designed for all-day wear. Unlike most shoes of this genre, it has a removable footbed to slide in custom orthotics (I’m used to having to tear them out of flats!), plus a lightweight rubber outsole to keep you on your feet. Available in regular and wide, the Larisa also proudly wears an APMA acceptance seal.

The first time I slipped on the Aetrex Collette loafers, I immediately stood up straighter. There’s something about its sloping arch-support that directed not just my feet but my entire posture into a more confident stance. It turns out the footbed is designed to follow the natural curvature of each person’s foot, alleviating issues from plantar facilities to heel pain. The brand puts an emphasis on personalized fit, with the strap across the shoe cleverly doubling as an adjustable Velcro and, making them feel more like snug sneakers than loafers.

While it may seem like only people with flat feet or low arches require arch support, that’s not the case. “Anyone with tired feet or discomfort may benefit from a more supportive shoe,” Jenny Simpson, three-time Olympian and Fleet Feet’s chief running officer, tells Traveler. She adds that the specifics of your foot will dictate the support that works best. Generally, those with low or flexible arches can benefit from stability shoes, while those with high and rigid arches might do better with more cushioned shoes.

What to look for in an arch-support shoe?

Simpson cautions not to choose a shoe based on arch height alone: “Support should feel like it is meeting your arch, while the heel should feel secure,” says Simpson. Only you can tell what feels right once it’s on your foot, as “how you move and what feels comfortable to you” are the ultimate factors.

No singular shoe will address soreness for everyone. “The right choice depends on what is causing the discomfort, how your foot moves, and whether you need more cushioning, more stability, or a combination of both,” says Simpson. Since no product photo or descriptions can replicate how shoes will feel on your feet, in-person fittings are the most valuable.

Are arch-support shoes good for heel pain?

Simpson says that insoles can definitely be “an excellent tool for anyone looking for more tailored solutions for their footwear.” In particular, for those dealing with plantar fasciitis-induced heel pain, the extra support can reduce strain. Alternatively, cushioned shoes—like Hoka is known for—can make standing and walking more comfortable.

What brands design the most supportive shoes?

Running and walking shoes are built to endure the long days that travel requires, as Simpson calls out Brooks, Hoka, Asics, New Balance, and Saucony as strong options. She suggests trying different models since two styles from the same brand can have totally different fits. Other lifestyle shoes aren’t necessarily designed for the same level of wear. “My strongest advice is not to shop by label alone,” Simpson says. “Many shoes are labeled ‘supportive,’ ‘stability,’ or ‘orthopedic.’ They can be useful descriptions, but your foot gets the final vote. The best shoe is the one that fits your anatomy, supports your activity and allows you to move comfortably.”
