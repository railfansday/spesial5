The Best Frame TVs Bring a Discreet, Tasteful Look to Your Living Room

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

These alternative screen designs feature matte, anti-glare displays that make static art look just as good as your favorite movie.

Unlike conventional TVs, which display a black screen when powered off, frame TVs display museum-quality artwork, family photos, and other media. With customizable bezels in various materials and colors, they look like picture frames and blend better with your home’s aesthetic.

While early frame TVs were all style and no substance, the technology behind these displays has evolved significantly. Today’s models feature matte screens that reduce glare, brightness sensors that automatically adjust to ambient lighting, and advanced display technologies that match the best 4K TVs on the market.

When shopping for a frame TV, factors like screen size, display quality, and style play a role just as they would with the best gaming TVs. However, frame TVs introduce additional considerations. Some premium models offer interchangeable bezels in various materials, while installation is a bit more challenging. They may also include free access to curated galleries, though some require monthly subscriptions. Below, we’ll dive into all the features to consider when searching for a solid frame TV, along with a few recommended picks.

For this roundup, I established clear criteria that each TV had to meet to qualify as a true “frame TV.” First and foremost, they had to have an elevated design beyond the traditional black rectangle—a design that can blend into your existing décor. Second, each model needed a dedicated art mode with specialized screen technology—like anti-glare—that minimizes reflections. Lastly, I considered options with some form of customization, including interchangeable bezels and curated art libraries. Samsung’s The Frame earns our top recommendation for effectively pioneering this category, offering a beautiful design, matte display, and extensive art collection. That said, each model in this roundup offers unique advantages. Whether you’re looking for a premium art display, a design statement, or a more budget-friendly option, our selections cater to various preferences and price ranges.

Samsung’s The Frame made a splash when it debuted in 2017, creating a TV that perfectly disguised itself as a piece of art. A few generations later, the TV looks even better and has the technology to match. The 2025 model brings years of refinement, featuring a QLED 4K display, an anti-reflective matte coating, and quantum dot technology that delivers vibrant colors and deep contrast.

The Frame’s anti-glare coating gives the TV a canvas-like appearance, so you can place it in a bright room without the picture washing out. When the art mode is activated, the matte finish helps diffuse ambient light, creating the illusion that you’re standing before an actual painting in a gallery. Samsung’s adaptive brightness sensor further enhances this effect by automatically adjusting the display’s brightness to match the room’s lighting conditions throughout the day.

Additionally, the TV features a built-in motion sensor that detects when you enter or leave the room, automatically turning its art mode on/off to save power. The Frame further helps save energy by lowering its refresh rate from 120 Hz to 60 Hz when art mode is activated. To help eliminate cable clutter, The Frame comes with an external hub that houses all the TV’s ports and connections, so you can hide everything from view.

Samsung's Art Store, which provides access to more than 2,000 curated works of art, requires a monthly subscription. Meanwhile, the TV's customizable bezels are sold separately and typically range from $100 to $200, depending on size and material. This can add to the overall cost of the TV, which already comes at a premium.

TCL’s frame TV line, Nxtvision, brings the art display concept to a more accessible price point while still offering advanced features such as a 4K QLED display, a high refresh rate, and next-generation gaming capabilities. At just 1.1 inches deep, the Nxtvision TV is incredibly thin, and it comes with a mount so it sits flush against the wall.

Like other options on this list, it features an anti-glare screen, which creates a convincing paper-like look, maintaining the illusion of physical artwork. The TV’s processor can also optimize color, contrast, and clarity, ensuring you get the best picture quality no matter what’s being displayed.

In addition to a curated gallery featuring various styles and periods, there’s an AI art capability that generates custom artwork from prompts. Additionally, you can display your personal photo gallery, giving you a huge picture frame for your favorite memories. The only drawback is that the Nxtvision comes with its own proprietary mount, so it’s not VESA-compatible, which may limit your mounting options.

If you’re in the market for another value pick that doesn’t scream “technology” when not in use, the Hisense CanvasTV is a mid-range lifestyle TV with an art mode that showcases Renaissance paintings, family photos, and more. Its matte display reduces reflections and gives artwork more depth and texture, so pictures look like actual paintings.

Additionally, the ultra-slim wall mount lets you hang it flush against the wall for that coveted gallery look. The magnetic frame system (teak comes standard) also lets you easily change the look of the CanvasTV to match your décor. Just note that it only comes in just two sizes, 55 and 65 inches, limiting placement options.

The CanvasTV doesn’t sacrifice performance for aesthetics, delivering an excellent picture with its 4K QLED display. Gamers will also appreciate the 144 Hz refresh rate and Auto Low Latency Mode, ensuring smooth gameplay without screen tearing or motion blur. Together, the combination of design and features makes the CanvasTV an excellent value.

The LG G5 isn’t necessarily a gallery TV like Samsung’s The Frame, but it features a slim design with a barely noticeable bezel, plus a gallery mode for showcasing artwork and photos. For these reasons, and others, including best-in-class picture quality, we believe this pick is a good entry point for those who want to upgrade their current model to a hybrid lifestyle TV.

On the G5’s OLED display, images look stunning thanks to perfect blacks, infinite contrast, and vibrant colors. It also offers better brightness—a major weakness of OLED—with some clever engineering that helps the screen stay vivid. However, as good as the OLED display is, it can still appear slightly washed out in bright ambient light, which may influence where you place it in your room.

While it doesn’t offer customizable bezels like The Frame, the LG G5’s minimalist aesthetic naturally complements modern interiors without sticking out like a sore thumb. Without a stand, the TV is just 1 inch thick, creating a sleek, floating appearance that doesn’t protrude from your wall.

Beyond its aesthetics, this pick also offers excellent functionality. Its four HDMI 2.1 ports allow you to enjoy gaming at 4K resolution with a smooth 120 Hz refresh rate. Dolby Vision support ensures that movies look their best, and Filmmaker Mode helps preserve the director’s original vision, providing the optimal viewing experience.

As frame TVs have grown in popularity, their size range has increased, too. When Samsung’s The Frame launched in 2017, it was available in only a few sizes. But now, the TV is available in sizes ranging from 32 inches to 85 inches. Frame TVs from other manufacturers also come in a variety of sizes, allowing you to get the option that best fits in your space.

When you’re shopping for a frame TV, I recommend resisting the urge to go for the largest size you can afford. Instead, think about how far you’ll be sitting from the screen. A huge TV in a small room can actually make viewing uncomfortable, because you’ll need to constantly move your eyes across the screen to take in all the action—it’s like sitting too close to a theater screen.

On the flip side, a TV that’s too small can force you to squint to make out details. Finding the sweet spot is crucial, especially with frame TVs designed to blend seamlessly with your décor. That sweet spot will vary depending on the available space, décor, etc., but generally, you should measure the area where you plan to place the TV before purchasing it.

Whether you’re shopping for a premium TV or something more affordable, there are different display technologies you should know about, including QLED and OLED. In the TV space, QLED is a common option among top manufacturers. This technology employs quantum dots to enhance color vibrancy and brightness, making it perfect for viewing art or pictures throughout the day.

Meanwhile, OLED is still widely regarded as the best display technology because of its ability to deliver true blacks and infinite contrast. Each individual pixel is self-emitting, meaning it can turn completely off when displaying black, providing a stunning viewing experience for high-contrast content.

While OLED is making its way into some high-end frame TV options, it offers lower peak brightness than QLED. This can make content appear less vibrant in well-lit rooms, so it's worth considering if you’d like to display bright content on your TV.

While frame TVs are generally wall-mounted, they come in a few installation styles. Wall mounting creates the illusion that your TV is a picture frame (hence the name), allowing it to hide in plain sight.

Most manufacturers include the appropriate mounting hardware, but the customizable bezels that allow you to match your TV’s frame to your décor are typically sold separately. This extra hardware generally comes in different wood tones and colors to best match your taste.

While a frame TV mostly serves as a decorative piece, there are a few extra features that sweeten the deal. Most models include dedicated art modes featuring curated galleries with thousands of classic and contemporary artworks.

However, if you want to access the full galleries with artwork from the world’s leading museums and artists, you may have to pay a subscription fee. For example, Samsung’s The Frame requires a subscription to its art store to access more than 2,000 iconic images

Frame TVs also incorporate auto-brightness features that use built-in sensors to detect your room’s ambient light. These sensors automatically adjust the screen brightness, similar to how smartphones adapt their displays between indoor and outdoor environments.

Many models also include motion sensors that detect when someone enters the room, automatically activating the display to show artwork without requiring you to turn the TV on manually. Additionally, frame TVs typically incorporate anti-glare technology that reduces reflections, creating a more authentic, canvas-like appearance.
