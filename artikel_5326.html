Bitcoin ETF investors are pulling nearly $5 billion in November's historic selloff

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust has lost more than $4 billion this month as bitcoin trades near $58,300, down about 23% in November

U.S.-listed bitcoin exchange-traded funds are poised for their steepest monthly withdrawals since launching in 2024, according to Bloomberg. Investors pulled a net $4.94 billion out of the funds during November, through Tuesday. Bitcoin is trading below $60,000.

BlackRock $BLK's iShares Bitcoin Trust, the biggest vehicle for holding bitcoin, has experienced withdrawals topping $4 billion so far in November, according to Bloomberg. That leaves the fund headed toward the largest monthly withdrawal in its own history.

Bitcoin has shed roughly 23% since the start of November and recently changed hands near $58,300, having slipped about 2.5%. The decline is on track to be the token's weakest month since June 2022, according to Bloomberg.

The funds, which debuted in the U.S. in January 2024, gave a broader range of institutions and everyday investors access to bitcoin and helped fuel demand throughout the bull market, according to Bloomberg. Record redemptions are compounding the slide now that the buying has faded.

Bitcoin faces headwinds including hacks that have raised doubts about its security, the possibility of rising rates and tougher regulatory scrutiny after the U.S. midterm elections, according to Bloomberg.

"The ETFs and other institutions were an important source of demand on the way up, and there is little interest from them at these prices," Glassnode said in a note, according to Bloomberg. "A return to ETF inflows, together with net buying on Coinbase, would be the first sign that this is changing," the data analytics firm said.

Ayesha Kiani, chief operating officer of Monarq Asset Management, called the ETF exodus "concerning" in an interview. Bitcoin is "competing for the same risk capital that is going into equities right now," Kiani told Bloomberg Television.

"There are better yield-bearing assets out there at this point," Kiani told Bloomberg Television. "So, like, why Bitcoin?"

Bitcoin slid to a low of $55,142.16 last Saturday and has barely bounced back. It has fallen for seven weeks in a row, a streak that has happened twice before in its history. The cryptocurrency peaked at a record $126,279.63 in October 2025.

U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs had shed close to $3 billion on a net basis between Oct. 30 and Nov. 13. A $6.3 billion hack at Binance and fading institutional demand for the funds have weighed on prices.
