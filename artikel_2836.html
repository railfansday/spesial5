F1 May Ditch the New Engines That Drivers Have Loathed

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Prepare for Formula 1 to get a lot louder come the turn of the decade.

Motorsport's premier competition looks set to welcome back the V-8 engine by 2030. No official announcement has been made yet, but those involved in the competition seem to be taking the mill's return as a given.

On Monday, representatives for F1's five engine manufacturers—Audi, Ferrari, Honda, Mercedes, and Red Bull-Ford—met with officials from the competition's ruling body, the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile, or F.I.A. to discuss the matter, according to The Athletic. That meeting followed another that involved all 11 current F1 teams.

Then, on Tuesday, the F.I.A.'s president Mohammed Ben Sulayem posted audio of the screaming eight-cylinder engine on social media, along with the caption, "You spoke, we listened. [V-8s] coming soon…"

Lewis Hamilton is one of just five F1 drivers who raced during the sport's last V-8 era

Expect cheers from F1's massive global fan base when an official announcement is finally made. F1 cars have used turbocharged V-6 hybrid powertrains since the 2014 season, but the setup has struggled to win over the diehards who watch every race, many of whom miss the engine roar of the sport's earlier eras. Changes were made to the technology before this season, and more are planned for the 2027 campaign, but the electrified engines remain as unpopular as ever.

The fans aren't the only ones excited about the potential return of V-8 power. F1 drivers, who seem to hate the current hybrid V-6s even more, are, too. "I don't know if they'll be exactly like the engines that I grew up watching necessarily, but it's great that we've been listened to," McLaren's Oscar Piastri told reporters before this weekend's Las Vegas Grand Prix. Ferrari's Lewis Hamilton, who is one of just five drivers who raced in the sport's last V-8 era, was also cautiously optimistic, saying, "I think the return of a great engine sound is something we've been missing since we entered the V-6 era."

Plenty of details still need to be worked out, though. The last V-8 engines that F1 used were naturally aspirated, as were the V-10s that predated them. The next generation of eight-cylinder mill will likely be electrified, with manufacturers said to be favoring using small hybrid systems similar to those used by today's road cars. Expect more details as 2030 grows closer.
