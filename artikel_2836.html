Game Preview: Knicks vs Trail Blazers, November 21, 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

As we all know, Madison Square Garden isn't just the most glorious gymnasium on the planet and revered in the world of sports; it's also famous for the long list of famous musicians who have performed under its unique bicycle-wheel ceiling. Now the Portland Trail Blazers are dropping by for a quick game of hoops on Saturday night, but they might actually be the one franchise that would be adept at bringing out the microphones as well. I mean, who wouldn't want to hear the newest rendition of all-timers like "Bust A Bucket" or "Rip City Rhapsody"?!

Not to disappoint anybody, but the days of Terry Porter and those shiny lycra dance outfits of the Blazers cheerleaders that would have made Cory Everson proud are long gone. Today's Rip City Rascals (coming in at 10-5) have their own way of entertaining a crowd, though, which is more than many would have expected before the season. With the return of Damian Lillard and a highly questionable move for "Pistol Petey" Ja Morant, the Blazers looked to be about five years late to the modern NBA party but atleast early it turns out they are anything but.

New head coach Micah Nori is making one of the more funky-looking rosters in the Association work so far, and he does it with a very modern, analytical approach. The Blazers shoot and make way more threes than their opponents (outscoring them by about 12+ points from beyond the arc), defend downtown better than 25 other teams in the NBA, and make sure Tony Brothers and his brothers-in-arms in the striped shirts have a living by drawing the second most fouls in the league.

Most of those foul numbers might be attributed to Deni Avdija. Only Luka from the Lakers and that feaky Greek in Miami stand at the charity stripe more frequently than the Israeli All-Star from Portland, leading to some fans questioning the ethics in his basketball bravado. But make no mistake about it, he is a serious talent, big-bodied and confident with a well-rounded game that can give every opponent problems. If he will be of any concern to the Knicks is not clear yet though; Avdija missed Portland's last two with a foot injury and figures to be a game-time decision.

But even without Avdija, Portland (road record 7-3) will present a very interesting matchup for your Knickerbockers. Damian Lillard seems to be rejuvenated after his Achilles injury, and a lot about his game feels a bit more wholesome in the jersey in which he always belonged. Behind him you have a Jrue Holiday coming off the bench, probably one of the more professional, classy guys you can find in the NBA these days. Cheekily, he also still packs quite a punch in his game, giving Nori first-class playmaking, tough D, and leadership in spades. If you think rattling these two old wily pros might get you anywhere, think again.

You could have more luck with some of the younger Blazers, but many of them bring certain elite skills to the table, too. Belgian baller Toumani Camara is one of the best defenders in the entire league, long, lanky, and extremely athletic. Seemingly plodding big man Donovan Clingan is getting more mobile by the minute and still hasn't lost his knack for gobbling up boards like a motivated Moses Malone did back in the day. Sidy Cissoko is another promising young wing, and former number two overall pick Scoot Henderson, while having a nightmarish season overall, can still break out for a big one as he did against Milwaukee the other night with 27 points.

What all those Blazers have in common is that they buy into Nori's system so far and operate with a clear-cut plan on the shiny hardwood. They extend their defense, making it hard to find anything downtown but also possible to do some damage in the midrange. On offense, they move the ball well, play five-out as much as they can (using Clingan as a high screen setter), and rely on balanced scoring night in and night out. It's a good formula, especially without Ja Morant, who has missed all but four games with a hamstring injury so far this year. The Knicks will have to touch the paint plenty to get that Portland defense moving, and they have to attack Lillard whenever possible on that end. Their superior depth could be a factor as well.

If the Blazers then throw a curveball and try to actually upend the Knicks' three-game winning streak by dragging them into a singing competition after all? No need to worry – we still got Tyler Kolek!
