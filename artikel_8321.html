21. The Wall

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

They say the journey of 1000 miles starts with a single step, but what about the final step?

I felt myself slowing down as I walked over the broken earth. Once I get there, I’m there, and the hike is over.

Would the journey end at the official terminus? The escalation of border conditions had left us all uncertain. First it had been the National Defense Area visitor pass, applied for weeks ago and received. We would be checked in and allowed to reach the spike of rock that marked the end of the trail.

Then the word had come – no access at all. The terminus had been walled off on the US side. The great plan was, they said, to build a double wall to trap any would-be border crossers between them. The thirty-foot rim of steel that ran from one horizon to another wasn’t cutting it, apparently. A second one would surely do the trick, never mind that anyone can see straight through the fence, and never mind that any ladder or rope or anything of that nature could easily be passed between the bars.

Never mind that jaguar and oryx and all sorts of wild, rare and beautiful creatures need freedom of movement through the region in order to survive. People aren’t the only things that live on the land. The species of animals that have lived here since before recorded history do not live in “Mexico” or “The United States.” They simply live on the land. Only people have use for nations or walls.

With the uncertainty of our ending place, two talented hikers had built a replica of the terminus out of cardboard, and had been carrying it for over 100 miles. This was to be placed against the border wall at whatever location we were able to get to, to be our terminus if we couldn’t reach the official one.

Then the word had come from some hikers just a couple days ahead: the terminus could be accessed by walking around to a cattle guard at the side of the construction site. Neither border patrol nor the military was anywhere to be seen, and the men of the construction teams were easygoing and sympathetic to our cause. Maybe we could get through, maybe we could get there. Maybe we could finish at the terminus.

It was with these hopes and somewhat muted expectations that I walked over the last hill of rocky ground, and saw the wall for the first time, ten miles away. Dust clouds from construction. A small farming community of the far side. A black line that cut the visible world in half.

I reached the cattle guard where other hikers waited. A security man said we could go in as soon as the digger had moved on far enough down the line. More hikers arrived and waited. We sat and ate snacks and threw pebbles and looked up at the massive beams of the wall. The two parallel lines of fencing creating an optical illusion, gaps and solids that blurred with movement to disorienting effect. Any sound that was loud enough – the clapping of hands, for example – ricochetted off the angle of the beams with a sound like a laser gun in a movie. We had a little fun making the “beeowmmmm” sounds shoot down the border wall.

Then it happened. A garbled voice on the radio, somebody gave a thumbs up, and we were in. All the chatter of our wait was gone now. We marched with an inward silence for the last hundred yards of dirt to the end. The end.

The stone was warm from the sun. We spent a long time taking it in, and taking photos. There we were. It was neither good nor bad, it simply was. The obelisk, the end of the journey. There it was.

Some in our group were finishing their triple crown, and although we all were excited by our achievement, they earned a special place among us. The group of them stepped up to the monument for a group picture, and the rest of us moved away. I stepped to the older wall, the one that stood on the actual border. A cool wind moved in between the bars from Mexico. The truth is, if there had been a gate there I would’ve passed through and kept on walking. But there was no gate. There was only the wall.

A few weeks ago the trail led me past the front gate of a prison. There were inmates in the yard walking laps as I walked past. I remember being surprised at how taken aback I was – I have seen prisons and prisoners before, yet this felt hard to witness. People fenced in, walking in circles while I walked free.

I don’t know that I’ve ever been called an optimist, or a pessimist. An idealist, sure, certainly. It is not for me to say that people in prison shouldn’t be there. It is not for me to say that the border between two nations shouldn’t be protected.

Using the methods of man’s mind to separate and divide people just feels wrong. No matter how right it may be, locking people up is wrong.

Freedom of movement is the entire basis of thru hiking. Taking someone’s freedom to move is taking freedom itself. “The Trail Unites Us” was carved into one side of the monument that stood between two walls. Literally set in stone, and literally overshadowed by the powers that be.

When the time came to leave, we did so with slight bewilderment. Now what? Even though we all knew what came next in our lives, or at least had an idea, the feeling of purpose that comes with a thruhike was just…gone. There was relief at the success, excitement for the future and peace from the accomplishment, but the hike was over, and the purpose had gone with it.

We loaded up into the vehicles. Some family members had come to aid their loved ones – and the rest of us – in the finale. I climbed into the bed of a pickup with a number of backpacks. There was room for me to sit in one of the cars, but I wasn’t ready for that. I wanted to stay outside, and I was happy to have a moment alone.

Today I am thirty years old. I’ve made it this far – through my life and through the journey south. One is ending, and the other continues for however long it may. I am ready for what comes next. In the near future there will be time with friends and family, the chaos of cities, buying a car and moving into a new apartment and going back to work and all sorts of events of “regular life.” In the further future, there will certainly be another thruhike. For as long as I can, for as long as it is up to me, I will live free. For now, I sit in the solitude and peace of the windy truck bed and watch the evening lightning crackle and blaze over Mexico.

Until next time, dear readers. Live free.
