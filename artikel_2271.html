AI predicts Sheffield Wednesday's League One finish under Dave Challinor

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Sheffield Wednesday have their man, as Dave Challinor was officially confirmed as the new Owls boss earlier this week.

The 51-year-old seemed almost destined for the post at Hillsborough ever since his hugely successful stint at Stockport County came to an end following defeat in the play-off final against Bolton Wanderers.

County's CEO Simon Wilson departed at the end of the season to become the new sporting director at Wednesday, and now the two will work together once more in South Yorkshire to get over the line and into the second tier after falling short at Stockport.

The Hatters finished third in consecutive League One campaigns under Challinor, who'd overseen their rise from the National League to the third tier in the four-and-a-half years he spent in charge.

Promotion will be the goal, and this demanding fanbase, who called for Henrik Pedersen's head despite sitting eighth in the division at the time, will be wanting it at the quickest possible moment, but what does AI think?

Henrik Pedersen's dismissal came after just 11 games of the League One campaign. At that time, Wednesday were two points removed from a play-off place with a game in hand, and had won games 7-2 and 5-0 against Bromley and AFC Wimbledon, respectively.

Ultimately, Challinor isn't coming in to a club in crisis, even if just two points in a run of games against Notts County, Doncaster Rovers, Cambridge United and Blackpool isn't exactly play-off form.

The ex-Stockport boss has 33 league games to help gain, at worst, two points on the side in sixth place, or six points on his former side, who occupy the second automatic promotion spot, which seems a doable task.

According to ChatGPT, he'll succeed in the former, but fall just short of finishing in the top two. Indeed, AI feels that Sheffield Wednesday will finish 4th this season under Challinor.

While that wouldn't be the ideal scenario for some Owls fans, they can feel reassured that ChatGPT went on to state that it predicts play-off success at Wembley, with Challinor's recent history of struggling to get through the postseason at Stockport not bleeding into his tenure at Hillsborough.

Ultimately, the appointment of Dave Challinor won't just mean that Sheffield Wednesday will stroll up the League One table and finish in the top two with ease. The 51-year-old has good credentials, but even he knows how unforgiving this division can be.

Additionally, this is a job like no other for Challinor. The Owls are a giant of English football that, ultimately, the likes of AFC Fylde, Hartlepool United and Stockport aren't.

Especially at Stockport, there wasn't as much fan pressure if things weren't going as well. Ultimately, he's brought the club closer to the Championship than they have been for 25 years, and that gave him plenty of credit in the bank.

At Sheffield Wednesday, though, he'll be under pressure to oversee winning performances in front of 30,000 at Hillsborough every other week and packed away ends elsewhere, and if bad results creep in, as they did under Pedersen, it won't take long for negativity to set in.

This is a job that Challinor has earned, but there's still a tiny bit of scepticism surrounding his fit from AI, hence the haste in putting them amongst the top two immediately.

Challinor was in the stands at Hillsborough on Sunday in their draw against Blackpool, observing and writing notes. He'll have plenty of time to get to work and implement some of those ideas in the coming weeks.

Wednesday are in action against Oldham Athletic in the FA Cup first round on Sunday in front of the TV cameras, giving the Owls a chance to showcase any changes immediately in front of a national audience.

From then, though, they have an EFL Trophy matchup at Accrington Stanley, which will serve almost as a free hit with the Owls already through to the knockout phase, and then likely a nine-day break due to the upcoming international window.

Dave Challinor's side haven't had their game against Plymouth Argyle postponed just yet, but it will likely happen. Kieran Morrison has already been called up for Northern Ireland, and their two games over the last break had to be rearranged, too.

So, with only one "meaningful" game against an out-of-form League Two opponent in his first 17 days officially in charge, it presents a good opportunity to stamp his authority on this new side.

All eyes will be on Oakwell on November 20, as Wednesday travel to Barnsley in what could set the tone for a promotion charge. Perhaps then, a fourth-placed prediction could be selling them a little short.
