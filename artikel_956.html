Bayern Munich hold ‘constructive’ talks over Jamal Musiala’s health amid contract row

Discussions between Bayern Munich officials and Jamal Musiala’s representatives on Tuesday focused on the star player's health rather than his salary, according to the club's board member for sport Max Eberl.

The talks took place shortly before Bayern flew out for Wednesday's Champions League fixture against Lille in France. They follow recent criticism from global players' union Fifpro, which accused Bayern of "a coordinated strategy to put pressure on Musiala" after he suffered seizures.

Characterising the meeting as "very, very constructive”, Eberl said the focus was entirely on Musiala’s recovery from a recent injury, in comments reported by the dpa news agency.

"Today it was about just how we want to get Jamal back on the field, just like before his muscle injury in the international break," Eberl said. "That's the only task FC Bayern has currently, to give Jamal this trust and not some topics about money or something."

Prominent figures at Bayern had previously questioned whether Musiala could return to his previous form, suggesting the contract extension he signed last year might no longer reflect the player he has become.

The idea that terms could be renegotiated due to poor health drew rare, sharp criticism from Fifpro.

"Contracts are there to be respected," the players' union stated on Saturday. "An injury or a dip in form gives a club no grounds to reopen terms it agreed to, just as a run of outstanding performances gives a player no automatic right to a pay rise."

Musiala has featured rarely since collapsing during pre-season games in August and has also dealt with a muscle problem. He was nevertheless included in Bayern's 20-player squad travelling to Lille on Tuesday.

Social media posts previously published by Musiala stated he had been diagnosed with "absence seizures caused by a neurological dysfunction”, noting the condition was not a threat to his health and confirming his intention to continue playing.

Bayern coach Vincent Kompany, who has experienced his own health issues this season before returning to the touchline last week, publicly backed Musiala in September, stating it would not be "a big deal" if the seizures were to return.
