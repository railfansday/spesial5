Leicester City are dropping their BC.GAME sponsor, following pornography backlash from fans

Leicester City have binned their front-of-shirt sponsor aftere a controversial partnership with a pornographic actor.

Leicester confirmed in a statement on their official website that they reached a mutual agreement with cryptocurrency casino BC.GAME to terminate their affiliation with immediate effect, following “constructive discussions” that “recognised our differing commercial priorities”.

This comes after the Belize-registered brand named Russian porn actor Eva Elfie as an ambassador, posting a video of Elfie and a delivery driver on social media, with the caption, “Let the fun begin.”

The Foxes Trust, which represents Leicester supporters, released a statement claiming that the League One club were “ditching its values” by linking up with a betting company in the first place, before condemning this latest partnership from BC.GAME.

“From the moment the club decided to take sponsorship from a betting company we felt Leicester City were ditching its values,” the statement read.

“Now throw into the mix that BC GAME have entered into a partnership with someone who continues to make their name via adult content.

“Partnerships like these bring these two worlds to a larger audience and, by association, link them to Leicester City. Is this who we are now?”

Leicester have confirmed that fans who bought a shirt within the last 28 days through official channels are eligible for a full refund or exchange, even if the shirt has been worn or printed with a name on the back.

It was recently claimed that the club would struggle to terminate their partnership with BC.GAME, from a legal standpoint – while there may be further financial issues from losing the sponsorship.

This mishap is the latest in a long line of events at the King Power Stadium that fans would rather forget, with the 2015/16 Premier League champions now languishing in League One and making the headlines for all the wrong reasons.

Following relegation from the top flight in 2024 – the first campaign that BC.GAME adorned Leicester's shirts for – the Midlands outfit suffered a shock back-to-back relegation last term to return to the third tier for the first time since 2009.

Life in League One has been a struggle under Russell Martin, too, with the club sitting 11th in the table, failing to win their first three fixtures and suffering a notable 4-0 home defeat to Aaron Ramsey's Oxford United.

Leicester head to Huddersfield Town this weekend, for their first match in a sponsorless shirt.
