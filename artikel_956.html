Football Daily | Football’s political gamers face off at Fifa with the real-life final boss

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Although the millions of gamers who are pulling sickies would beg to differ, the release of Grand Theft Auto VI wasn’t the most exciting midnight launch involving a sprawling enterprise full of shady characters. Over in Zurich, the clock also struck 12 on the candidate deadline for next year’s Fifa presidential election, leaving those of us who have no interest in video games with our own chaotic, multi-billion-dollar sandbox where untouchable bosses bend the rules to suit themselves. While online carjackers are busy exploring a “most extraordinarily, indulgently detailed virtual world”, football’s real-life power brokers are gearing up for a March showdown in the actual city of Rabat.

For years, Gianni Infantino has looked nailed-on to glide into a fourth term of office completely unopposed, but following his disastrous Fifa Forward Enterprise wheeze to flog stakes in the World Cup to his American pals on the cheap, many of the emperor’s former allies have finally turned against him. In the wake of the revolt, European national associations withdrew their backing for Infantino and, on Thursday morning, Fifa confirmed the 56-year-old will face his first contested election since parking his backside on the throne a decade ago. Sadly for all those who would like nothing more than to see him deposed, Infantino remains the red-hot favourite in what will be a three-horse race.

Leading the charge for the mutineers is Philippe Diallo, the 63-year-old French Football Federation president who was unanimously anointed as Europe’s preferred candidate after their actual preferred candidate, Arsène Wenger, decided not to throw his chapeau in the ring. His billing by Uefa’s vice-president, Laura McAllister, as a man “who can instigate real, meaningful and lasting reform” at Fifa conveniently overlooks the fact that, as recently as five months ago, Diallo’s own federation signed a letter formally endorsing Infantino again. Joining Diallo in the battle to prise Gianni’s fingers off the levers of power is Kozo Tashima, the 68-year-old honorary president of the Japan FA. He could yet stand aside to avoid a split of the vote from Fifa’s 211 members who want Infantino booted to the door marked “Do One!”. Unfortunately, Ramon Vega, the former Switzerland and Tottenham defender, won’t be on the ballot. He had again announced his candidacy but proved unable to scare up the five member-association endorsements required.

Between now and Fifa’s March congress, all three candidates must pass a Fifa eligibility and integrity check, a feat which shouldn’t be beyond the two pretenders, given Infantino has repeatedly come through it with flying colours while helping to overhaul the committees tasked with marking his papers along the way. Assuming he once again clears the hurdle of being vetted by his own organisation, the next four months promise a familiar blur of private jets, palm-greasing and disingenuous crawling. While a generation of gamers will spend winter pulling off virtual heists in Vice City, when it comes to bending the rules in your favour, nobody plays the game quite like the Fifa final boss.
