Vance scraps schedule to address tech outages as Trump admin insists incidents aren't linked

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Vice President JD Vance on Wednesday abruptly canceled his scheduled appearance at The New York Times DealBook Summit to instead attend meetings on a recent wave of high-profile technology outages.

The cancellation was announced shortly after a senior Trump administration official insisted that the outages, which have affected a wide range of industries and have been felt across the country and around the world, are not the result of a cyberattack or any form of coordinated action.

"We have a great deal of confidence in our assessment that these incidents are the result of upgrades of the systems, systems being patched, hardware being applied" or other "disparate" events, the official said on condition of anonymity to discuss the government's intelligence assessments.

The official briefed news outlets by phone in the wake of numerous major disruptions at large corporations and federal agencies in recent days, including a nationwide aviation ground stop, a protracted Microsoft Outlook outage and a reported blackout of the National Security Agency's classified communications network.

Other incidents include software issues at JPMorgan Chase, an outage at United Airlines that caused some flight delays and product issues at Apple.

On Tuesday, Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told CNBC that the outages are not a cyberattack and "not a coordinated effort by a nation-state."

He gave a similar assurance on Wednesday to the House Homeland Security Committee.

"We have no indication that these incidents are related, that they're coordinated," Clayton said. "We also have no indication that they're result of a state-sponsored malign actor."

But the sheer volume of incidents in the past week alone has raised alarm bells among consumers and tech experts alike, with some wondering if rapidly advancing artificial intelligence models are to blame.

The senior Trump administration official, asked on Wednesday's call to what degree AI was involved in the outages, said it is "challenging to answer that question across all of these incidents that have been reported."

Sen. Mark Warner, D-Va., vice chairman of the Senate Intelligence Committee, told CNBC on Wednesday morning he wants "better coordination" on AI safety.

"I understand why people are freaked out," Warner said. "The one way we could lose this AI race and [see] all of these companies' valuations plummet dramatically is if we have an AI tool run amok, or an AI-sponsored cyberattack that creates harm."

His comments came hours before before Anthropic is scheduled to price its initial public offering for the first major AI industry IPO.

Warner advocated for a "pre-testing regime" for new AI models that establishes guardrails and helps ensure a "culture of safety built in" to the industry."

He said the U.S. is "lucky" that it has so far avoided a major AI-generated cyberattack or a crisis with a rogue agent that goes beyond incidents like the Hugging Face breach earlier this year.

— CNBC's Samantha Subin contributed to this report.
