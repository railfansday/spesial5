Giants-Jaguars matchup: Will New York defense live, or die, by the blitz?

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The 5-3-1 New York Giants host the red-hot 7-2 Jacksonville Jaguars on Sunday. The game was flexed to a 4:25 EST window, and the Jaguars are currently favored by 6 points with a 46.5-point Over/Under. The Jaguars finish their three-game road trip in New York; they've beaten the Colts, Ravens, and Titans, scoring 115 points while allowing 40 over their last three games.

Jacksonville is a true test for the Giants, who lost at home in a disappointing Thursday Night Football matchup to the Washington Commanders, 24-21.

Liam Coen's Jaguars rank fourth in points per game, averaging 29.9. Only the Bills, 49ers, and Bengals score more points per game than the Jacksonville Jaguars. They're 9th in total yards per game, with a 358.9-yard average, 241.1 passing yards per game (10th in the NFL), and 117.8 rushing yards per game (12th in the NFL). The Jaguars have rested starters in the fourth quarter in multiple contests this season.

Jacksonville has a 46.8% third-down conversion rate, third in the NFL, and a 65.2% red-zone touchdown rate, fifth in the NFL. The Jaguars are an efficient offense with explosive weapons and a well-designed rushing attack led by second-year running back Bhayshul Tuten, who has 609 rushing yards (4.1 YPC) and five touchdowns.

Wide receiver Parker Washington is breaking out in his third season. He has caught 36 of 64 targets for 654 yards wtih six receiving touchdowns from Trevor Lawrence, who is having an excellent second season under Coen. Lawrence has completed 66% of his passes for 2,284 yards with 21 passing touchdowns and five interceptions. He's been sacked 14 times and has 106 rushing yards.

The Jacksonville Jaguars lean on Tuten but do keep a mild rotation behind the budding star running back. Chris Rodriguez Jr. was brought in to be the hammer near the goal line. He maintains a role on the offense when the game is neutral and is the back in clean-up duty as well. He has 233 yards (3.9 YPC) and four touchdowns this season. Ameer Abdullah is a change-of-pace back who still provides explosive upside, but did not play an offensive snap in Week 10. LeQuint Allen Jr. is a backup third-down back who sees 10-15% of snaps a game.

Jacksonville has a deep rotation at wide receiver. Washington is the star emerging into his own, but long-time veteran Jakobi Meyers leads the team in snap share. He has caught 29 of 43 passes for 408 yards with two scores. Brian Thomas Jr. plays just less than half of the snaps, and he competes with rookie Josh Cameron for snaps; the latter sees some snaps in 12 personnel due to his blocking, and he caught a touchdown in Week 10.

Brian Thomas Jr. looked to be an elite weapon after his stellar rookie season, but the former LSU product has been relegated to a situational role, and he was heavily rumored to be traded at the deadline, but he remains a Jaguar. He has caught 12 of 25 passes for 146 yards with two touchdowns. He'll be on the field in 11 personnel.

The Jaguars did an excellent job identifying Josh Cameron as an asset for them in the sixth round of the draft. The former Baylor product has caught 12 of 17 passes for 190 yards with three touchdowns. Travis Hunter is a full-time — and difference-making — cornerback for Jacksonville, but he still plays in certain offensive packages. He played just 8% of snaps on Sunday; he hasn't eclipsed 13% of snaps on offense this season.

The Jaguars have invested in their tight end room. Brenton Strange is their starter and plays over 70% of snaps. He has secured 32 of 40 targets for 365 yards with two touchdowns, including one last week against Tennessee. Jacksonville spent a second-round pick on Nate Boerkircher, and he provides solid blocking; he has caught 10 of 14 targets for 156 yards wtih two touchdowns. Quintin Morris and Tanner Koziol are the third and fourth tight ends on the roster.

Starting left tackle Anton Harrison is on IR, and Walker Little has stepped into the lineup. Little has played excellent football in lieu of Harrison.

Rookie Emmanuel Pregnon is playing good football in the run and pass game; he's their starting left guard. Robert Hainsey is solid at center, but is out with an injury, and Ezra Cleveland is solid at right guard. Cole Van Lanen is the starting right tackle.

Jacksonville is a problem. I expect Dennard Wilson to lean on the blitz and other creative ways to generate pressure, as he has almost all season. Jayden Daniels burnt the Giants for three third-down touchdowns when the Giants blitzed; Washington accounted for the Cover-0 approach well after New York effectively employed the approach in their Week 5 tie. Nevertheless, the Giants may attempt to bring extra pressure on Lawrence. According to SumerSports, up until Week 7, Trevor Lawrence was the QB most affected by pressure:

Jacksonville's last three opponents—the Colts, Ravens, and Titans—did not apply much pressure to Lawrence, but the former Clemson Tiger improved when he faced pressure. This season, Lawrence has been above-average overall against the blitz, though. Per NFLNextGen, and Travis Holmes on X, here are Lawrence's numbers against the Blitz:

Lawrence's ability to burn a defense that sends extra rushers is undeniable, but not sending extra rushers is a PRECARIOUS situation for the opponents of Jacksonville. The Giants can rely on deception to manipulate and waste blockers to facilitate mismatches and/or free rushers into the pocket if they don't blitz. Lawrence EPA per dropback from a clean pocket ranks first in the NFL.

The Giant problem that arises if New York sticks to heavy pressure is Lawrence's ability to quickly find a solution, like Washington did in Week 10. New York must be sound in tackling and avoid dumb penalties — playing efficient and disciplined football is the key to victory against a talented team like the Jaguars. Jacksonville shouldn't struggle to run as Washington did, so New York must stay gap-disciplined; both Micah McFadden and Arvell Reese have done well in this regard over the last two weeks.

If Lawrence quickly finds solutions early against pressure, Wilson should look to bail into coverage/contain looks on some third downs, focusing on slot-threat Parker Washington, who will have an advantage against Dru Phillips. It was years ago, but Wink Martindale defeated Jacksonville for the Giants in 2022 by dropping eight often-out-of-pressure looks; this could be a wise curveball for Wilson to use.

Carter is maligned, and it's mostly unfair; he is double-teamed often and is constantly affecting the game — the Giants need more of that and then some on Sunday against Lawrence. Four-man rushes should consist of twists and simulated pressure. Expect Wilson to align Carter in some advantageous looks when/if Coen allows for that; if not, someone else on the Giants will have to step up against this solid offensive line.

New York should contain to run zone match in three-deep looks, for their communication was impressive against Washington; it was heavy blitz plays and Cover-0 that burnt them in high-leverage situations in that tough Week 10 loss. I would mix up the coverage on the back end to keep Jacksonville guessing: middle-of-the-field closed and middle-of-the-field open looks.

I wouldn't fully stray away from Cover-0 post-snap, but I would be judicious with its usage against Lawrence. But still, New York can bring pressure in other ways.

This is the toughest matchup for the Giants since Week 2. Jacksonville is one of the league's hottest teams, and they're concluding a three-game road trip at MetLife Stadium. The Jaguars are on a three game winning streak against three teams that are down bad: the Colts, Ravens, and Titans. They lost to Houston overseas prior to their Week 7 bye. I believe the Giants show up and make the game competitive, but ultimately lose down the stretch of the fourth quarter. Jaguars, 27-20.
