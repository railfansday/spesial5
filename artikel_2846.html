NASA Is Handing Out Moon Contracts Again. Intuitive Machines, Firefly, and Redwire All Got Paid. Only 1 Has the Balance Sheet to Keep Winning.

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Not all NASA contractors are equally positioned to succeed.

By Jeff Siegel – Nov 23, 2026 at 10:27PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Firefly Aerospace ›

NASA is investing more in its lunar exploration program, and three publicly traded space companies in particular are positioned to benefit: Intuitive Machines (LUNR +11.11%), Firefly Aerospace (FLY +8.20%), and Redwire(RDW +13.63%).

On Nov. 7, NASA announced a new round of awards supporting its planned moon base, including lunar transportation, communications, and power infrastructure. All three companies have secured positions in NASA's expanding lunar program, but their financial situations are quite different. Let's take a closer look.

NASA recently selected eight companies, including Firefly and Intuitive Machines, to compete for contracts under its next commercial lunar payload services program, known as CLPS 2. The program has a maximum value of $10 billion over 10 years. NASA also increased the ceiling of its existing CLPS program from $2.6 billion to $4.2 billion.

Redwire was selected to provide power-generation technology for NASA's planned mini-communications and solar tower, which will support operations on the lunar surface. The value of that award wasn't disclosed.

These announcements follow approximately $590 million in lunar delivery contracts announced in June, including $144.2 million for Firefly and $148.3 million for Intuitive Machines. Of course, the $10 billion CLPS 2 figure represents a spending ceiling, not guaranteed revenue for participating companies. Individual contracts will still need to be awarded.

Intuitive Machines has been growing quickly. In Q3 2026, revenue reached $269.3 million, up from $52.4 million a year earlier. The company also generated $66.2 million in operating cash flow and approximately $33.5 million in free cash flow. It also finished September with $463.8 million in cash.

Those are encouraging numbers, especially for a company operating in an industry where developing spacecraft and launching missions can quickly burn through hundreds of millions of dollars. However, Intuitive Machines carries $336.9 million in long-term debt. That's a substantial obligation for a business still expanding its operations and investing in new technology.

Redwire manufactures spacecraft components, solar arrays, and other technologies used in space infrastructure.

In Q3 2026, revenue reached $109 million, an increase of 5.4% from the previous year. Contracted backlog climbed to a record $619.7 million, and the company also reported $488.9 million in cash, cash equivalents, and restricted cash against approximately $47 million in debt. That's a considerable improvement in its financial position.

But Redwire remains unprofitable. It reported a $23.2 million net loss in Q3 and an adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) loss of $5.3 million. Indeed, its growing backlog is encouraging, but converting those contracts into profitable operations remains a challenge.

Firefly's latest financial results make a compelling case for why it could be the strongest of these three companies. At the end of September, Firefly held $534.7 million in cash and short-term investments, compared with just $25.1 million in financial debt. That works out to approximately $510 million in net cash.

Firefly is also growing quickly. Third-quarter revenue jumped 259% year over year to $110.7 million, while backlog reached a record $1.7 billion. And unlike many companies competing for NASA contracts, Firefly has already demonstrated its ability to land a spacecraft on the Moon.

In March 2025, its Blue Ghost lander completed the first fully successful commercial lunar landing. Firefly is now preparing additional lunar missions while expanding its launch and national security operations. That gives it an established track record, a substantial backlog, and considerable financial resources to pursue additional contracts.

To be sure, Firefly does have a history of operating losses and negative operating cash flow. Its cash and short-term investments also declined from approximately $893 million at the end of 2025 to $535 million in September 2026.

Developing launch vehicles and spacecraft isn't cheap, and future missions will require substantial investment. And there's also no guarantee Firefly will secure a meaningful portion of NASA's new $10 billion program. Still, Firefly has the largest net cash position among these three companies, a $1.7 billion backlog, and a successful lunar landing already under its belt.

Intuitive Machines and Redwire have legitimate opportunities to benefit from NASA's expanding lunar ambitions. But based on their latest financial results, Firefly looks best positioned to compete for additional contracts without taking on substantial new debt.

That doesn't automatically make it the best stock to buy. Valuation and execution still count. But if I were choosing one of these three companies based on financial strength and its ability to pursue future lunar contracts, Firefly would be my pick.
