Tajikistan follows US call, withdraws from ICC

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Tajikistan has initiated its withdrawal from the International Criminal Court as the US calls for it to be "dismantled." The decision was made during a recent visit from Russian President Vladimir Putin.

Tajikistan has now become the fourth country to announce its formal withdrawal from the International Criminal Court (ICC) in the Hague, Netherlands since the US announced its intention to "dismantle" the body — joining Chad, Naoero [formerly Nauru] and Venezuela.

US President Donald Trump and Secretary of State Marco Rubio have railed against the court as abusing its jurisdiction, with Trump publicly attacking the ICC during remarks at a trilateral meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin — who is wanted by the court for war crimes committed during the course of Russia's full-scale invasion of neighboring Ukraine.

Trump's public attacks on the world's permanent war crimes tribunal prompted an amused smirk from Putin during their meeting in Shenzhen, China.

The ICC prosecutes individuals accused of genocide, crimes against humanity, crimes of aggression and war crimes in instances when national cannot or will not act.

The ICC issued an international arrest warrant for Putin in March 2023.

Tajikistan's government said it made the decision to initiate the one-year withdrawal process on Friday, when Putin made a stopover visit after leaving his trilateral meeting with Trump and Xi.

The ICC said it "regrets" Tajikistan's decision, adding that the move undermines "the collective pursuit of justice" and weakens "global efforts to end impunity."

"The ICC stands at the heart of the international system of accountability, and its effectiveness depends on sustained and unequivocal support from the international community," an ICC statement continued.

Tajikistan signed the Rome Statute that established the ICC back in 1998 and ratified it in 2000.

Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger have also announced their withdrawal from the court, blasting it as a tool of "neocolonial repression."
