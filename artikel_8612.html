Start with a no-fee flat 2% cash back card such as the Citi Double Cash, which pays about $480 a year on $2,000 a month. Switch to a card that pays more in one category only when a large share of your spending goes there. Either way, pay in full, since balances charged interest average over 22% a year.

The average rate on bank credit card accounts that were charged interest was 22.36% in the third quarter of 2026, by the Federal Reserve’s preliminary figures. A dollar left unpaid for a year at that rate costs more than 22 cents in interest, several times what any of these cards pays back on it. So every pick assumes you pay the full balance each month.

Every yearly figure below is for one sample household, which puts $2,000 a month on the card: $500 on groceries at supermarkets, $300 at restaurants, $200 on gas, $300 on utility and phone bills, and $700 on everything else. The figures subtract any annual fee and leave out welcome offers. Each pick is the strongest of its kind on its rates, spending caps, payout rules, sign-up offer, and 0% interest period taken together.

The sample household collects about $480 a year with no categories to track and no cap, more than most category cards on this list pay on a budget this spread out.
