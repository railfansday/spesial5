Continuity has bred success for Wave back line

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Back four of Kristen McNabb, Kennedy Wesley, Mimi Van Zanten and Perle Morroni ready for playoffs

Late in the San Diego Wave’s September 6 match in Seattle, veteran defender Kristen McNabb began to cramp. Head coach Jonas Eidevall went to the bench and replaced McNabb with Tatum Wynalda. It was the 6th minute of stoppage time. The 32-year-old defender was credited with 89 minutes in the 1-0 loss to the Reign. That stray minute was the only one she would miss all season.

“I don’t care deep down about the awards and stuff,” McNabb told The Equalizer about being so close to ironwoman status in the longest NWSL season on record. “But when you’re so close to one like that, you’re like ‘ugh, I had to cramp in the 92nd minute of one game.’ It’s a bummer. But whatever.”

As it turned out, McNabb’s near-perfect 2,699 minutes this season was not even the most played within her central defense pairing. Kennedy Wesley, 7 years her junior, started every match alongside McNabb during the regular season. Wesley finished them all. The outside backs in Eidevall’s four-back, Mimi Van Zanten and Perle Morroni, both started 29 out of 30, each missing one for card suspensions. It is that familiarity, along with the skill sets of the quartet, that has turned the Wave defense into one of the top units in the league.

“We’ve really built a solid chemistry together,” Wesley said. “We’ve been fortunate that a lot of us have stayed healthy, and I do think that has helped us a lot (to) build our relationships with each other. I think that’s a big portion of it.”

Of course, it takes more than good health to stay on the field. Strong performance is also critical.
