Ledger Brings Clear Signing to Solana, Ending 'Blind' Signing for Jupiter Swaps

Ledger has brought clear signing to Solana, allowing users of its hardware wallets to read exactly what they are approving before they sign a transaction.

"Until now, most Solana DeFi on a Ledger meant signing blind," the company tweeted on Monday. Users can now verify a swap on decentralized exchange aggregator Jupiter "in plain words, showing what you send and what you get before you approve," it said.

Blind signing, in which users approve transactions displayed as unreadable data, has long been a weak point for hardware wallet users, since it leaves them unable to spot a malicious request. Ian Rogers, Ledger's chief human agency officer, likened it to "signing a blank check" in a presentation at Solana's Breakpoint conference.

A Jupiter swap that previously meant scrolling through 13 hard-to-read screens on a Ledger device now takes three, showing what the user is giving, what they're getting and who they're dealing with, Rogers said. "It's a huge step for every Solana user," he added.

Clear signing now covers nine Solana apps and 18 of the 22 most common actions users take on the network, according to Ledger. It was built with the Solana Foundation and Anza, the developer of Solana's Agave validator client, with Jupiter and lending protocol Kamino as the first supported apps.

Rogers also pitched the company's tools for AI agents. Ledger's approach is that an agent "never holds the keys; you do," he said, comparing it to a teenager who has to ask parents for the family car. The agent requests approval when it needs to move funds, and the user signs off on a Ledger device.

Because that means an agent has to stop and wait, Ledger last month launched Agent Intents, a single queue where approval requests collect so agents can keep working while users approve them when convenient. It works with Pay.sh, a service that lets AI agents pay for APIs on Solana, Rogers said.

He also showed off Ledger Atom, the touchscreen device based on Android's open-source code that Ledger announced last month. When it's time to sign, the device switches its screen to Ledger's secure chip, so secrets stay hidden from Android, Rogers said. Atom will be the first hardware beyond the Solana Seeker phone to carry the Seeker platform, bringing its more than 1,500 apps to the device, he added.

Ledger has previously teamed up with MoonPay on tools to let users control AI agents' crypto spending with its hardware wallets.

The launch comes weeks after Ledger investigated a suspected $87 million drain of user funds, which it later said was tied in all confirmed cases to a reseller, Cryptobilis.
