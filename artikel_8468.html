Te Araroa day 15: New friends and laundry drama

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

18km, free camp 307.5 to Whananaki Holiday Park

Using earplugs worked pretty well, and I slept decently. I poked my head out and saw that I was still in a cloud, but surprisingly, my tent didn’t have much condensation inside it. I went into the forest to do my business, and as I was emerging I heard the sound of running feet. A whole bunch of hunting dogs wearing radio collars came zooming out of the woods towards my tent, thankfully closely followed by some humans. The dogs started barking when they saw me, and the hunters called over to me that they were friendly – but I was happy to have a fence between them and me!

The weather was a bit mizzly with some heavier patches of rain, unsurprisingly since I was literally inside a cloud. The track went up and down a bit, and became a farm road before meeting a gravel road. After crossing that I went down an old ox track, which abruptly ended at a gateway and TA swung to the right over a stile into some bush. This track looked newly-marked, and wasn’t on the base maps yet – I guess there was probably some landowner dispute about the use of the original track, and it was re-routed to use the land beside it. It was a nice track through the forest though, and gradually I emerged down out of the cloud. Once again, the weather down there was hot and muggy. The track ran alongside some cow pastures and back and forth across a stream a few times before emerging at yet more tarmacked road, which I would be walking along for 7.5km. I put my headphones in and got stuck in.

The road was boring, but at least it wasn’t raining. After a while I reached the turning for a track that went alongside some more fields before reaching the Whananaki estuary. After a kilometre or so, I reached the back entrance of the holiday park in Whananaki. I had a booking for that night, but it was only 12 and I wanted to try to get lunch at the general store first. The accomodation options are limited along this stretch of trail, so I chose to have a short day and treat it as a semi-rest day where I would be able to do laundry and resupply a bit.

I found a path that headed towards the general store and the ‘town’. It went past the school, where there was a kapa haka festival on (Kapa haka is the traditional Māori performing art, probably best known from the All Blacks performing a haka before rugby games). I would have stopped to watch, but I was on a lunch mission!

Chips and caramel slice – the lunch of champions!

At the store, I got some chips and a caramel slice for lunch. So healthy! On the same site as the store and cafe was the local radio station, Whananaki FM. It was amusingly strap-lined ‘The greatest radio station in the world,’ in this tiny town (permanent population 330, according to Google). The radio was playing outside the store where I ate, and mentioned thunderstorms for the afternoon. It was quite nice at the time, so I didn’t think too much of it.

After my lunch, I headed round to the holiday park to check in. However, it turned out that the general store was also the office for the campsite, so I had to walk back to where I had just come from. Then I walked all the way back to the far end of the campsite to get set up at my spot. Once my tent was up, I gathered my laundry and got ready for a shower. The shower was coin operated, and $1 only got you 3 minutes. By the time I figured out where the ‘hot’ part of the dial was, my three minutes was almost up. So I had to go for another $1.

Then I tried to do laundry. I had bought a sachet of detergent when I checked in, so I found a machine, loaded it up, and tried to use the payment terminal. It refused to load. I tried again, and again. Nothing. I went out to the shop, and the staff there said it was because the wifi (which the machines need to accept payment) is Starlink, which doesn’t do well with cloud cover. Apparently it should come right in about 10 minutes. After 15 minutes, it still didn’t work and I went back to the shop. I must have seemed particularly upset, because they took pity on me and showed me where their ‘behind the scenes’ laundry was. That was nice of them, but I think perhaps it isn’t smart to have laundry machines that rely on wifi when the wifi is unreliable if it’s cloudy…

Once my laundry was finally underway, I went back to the shop once again to get a small resupply. Unsurprisingly for such a small place, the options were limited and expensive. But I had enough ‘proper’ food and really just needed a top-up of snacks. Aside from the laundry debacle, I liked this holiday park. They had humorous signage and the staff were helpful in my plight.

There was lots of this kind of amusing signage

Some rain started coming in towards mid-afternoon, and so did some other hikers. Harry, who I had met a couple of times, was the first person to arrive, followed by fellow Trek blogger Lindsey – who I was very happy to see back in action after her injury! – and Lucy, plus a number of people I hadn’t met yet. Cody, Eve, Sven, Issy and Riv all seemed nice and they had pretty much all decided to share some cheap but tiny cabins in view of the torrential rain and thunderstorms. I was sat in the kitchen charging stuff and working on some blog posts, and three seperate people offered me a beer that they had bought. Eventually I ventured outside to join them in the covered patio, where everyone was cooking dinner. They were a fun bunch and we had a good laugh. Sven had bought some postcards to send to various people back home, and one in particular was hilarious:

One of Sven’s postcards… note the chocolate fish, a Kiwi icon!

I went to bed a little later than usual, and with my brain running on overdrive from the unaccustomed social interaction. I lay awake for absolutely ages, watching the lightning and listening to podcasts to try to turn my brain off. But eventually, I slept.
