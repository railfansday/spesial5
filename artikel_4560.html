A Tesla Aftermarket Shop Wants to Build the Cybertruck Under License

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Unplugged Performance took to Tesla boss Elon Musk’s preferred social medium of choice, X, to make the offer.

The Tesla Cybertruck, as you may well have heard by now, is on death row. How the news hits you will likely depend on what your opinions on the truck that company CEO “Technoking” Elon Musk once described as “what Bladerunner would have driven.” Those who dislike the steel-bodied machine range from pleased to gloating; fans, meanwhile, are rending garments and clutching their vowel-less vanity plates. Alas, poor Cybertruck; I knew him, Elon!

But one group of Tesla enthusiasts are aiming to take matters into their own hands. Unplugged Performance, an aftermarket shop specializing in Teslas, has taken to social media to entreat the automaker not to kill off the slab-sided pickup, but rather, to license its production out to them instead.

The company took to X—which is owned by Musk, who is also, shall we say, a vocal user—to make the offer. “An open offer to Tesla: We fully support putting Cybercab first, and we’d be glad to take the Cybertruck line off your hands. We’d fund it with a consortium of investors and run it at no cost to Tesla’s floor space, headcount, or focus,” Unplugged Performance wrote.

That offer, unsurprisingly, evoked a reaction amongst Internet-active fans of the Cybertruck, as well as quite a few questions for specifics. To their credit, Unplugged Performance responded with a comprehensive amount of detail regarding the plan so far. “A fair question we’re hearing: can UP really run the Cybertruck vehicle line? Not alone, and not overnight,” the brand tweeted (X-ed?), before elaborating that it intends to round up investors and manufacturing partners in the months leading up to Tesla’s planned changeover of its Texas plant that will use the capacity currently occupied by the truck to build autonomous Cybercabs.

“We believe this is highly fundable because one production platform serves three markets: existing demand for the pickup, pent-up demand for a seven-seat SUV, and Robotaxi network vehicles in useful all-terrain form factors (seven-seat people movers, cargo haulers, and pickups),” Unplugged Performance continued.

“Should add,” the shop added, “the number of Tesla alumni who have reached out offering to help has been overwhelming. For anyone questioning talent or experience, we already have a strong and resourceful team, and many people who helped build these vehicles want to participate and see the platform live on.”

As of this story’s Wednesday afternoon publication, Tesla and Musk don’t appear to have offered a public response to Unplugged’s offer. That said, Musk did post on Wednesday that pickup production is being extended through summer of next year to “work through more of the Cybertruck order backlog.” And given his follow-up, who knows? Maybe he’d be open to letting someone else take over after that.
