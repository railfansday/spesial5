The Art of Showing Up

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Voting stickers adorn memorials to ICE victims, Jeremy Shaw talks grief and ecstasy, and a portable archive of East LA.

It was raining like crazy when I cast my vote yesterday in Iowa, a blood-red state so battered, beleaguered, and embittered by an affordability crisis and farm debt and crumbling infrastructure that it just might — just might — swing. It’d been a steady stream all day, a poll worker told me, and she didn’t mean the rain.

The vox populi doesn’t just speak from the ballot box. In Houston, for instance, a memorial’s been talking: Dozens of “I Voted” stickers now adorn a roadside tribute to Lorenzo Salgado Araujo, a man ICE murdered in July, in an impromptu participatory artwork that is now being echoed in shrines to others around the country. “I voted for you,” someone wrote.

It makes me think of the ways we make ourselves heard, the ways we honor one another. The day after a deadly attack on Berlin’s Pride Festival this summer, about 200 people gathered at Jeremy Shaw’s video installation of dancers moving toward ecstatic revelation, he told writer Eileen G’Sell in a profile today, turning the gallery into a space of collective mourning, defiance, and solidarity. And a new project by Guadalupe Rosales, writes Carolina A. Miranda, is a tribute to her Eastside Los Angeles upbringing, and to the friends and relationships that offered a respite from rising violence and anti-immigrant sentiment. It’s what we do — create meaning, build a community, find a way forward.

Last month, Paul Ramírez Jonas invited the people of Toronto to sit down with a stranger in a public space, make a promise, swear on it, see it spelled out on a large-scale marquee, and then memorialize it in a one-of-a-kind contract they kept. One of hundreds of such vows was: “I promise to disagree better.” | Anna Gallagher-Ross and Kate Gilbert
