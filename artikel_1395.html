Four years ago, scientists reported findings from the OneK1K project1. This large-scale initiative involved sequencing RNA from cells donated by around 1,000 people with the goal of identifying genetic variants that affect gene expression and disease risk. An expansion of that project, TenK10K, now combines single-cell RNA-sequencing data with other kinds of molecular profiling with the aim of mapping and characterizing genetic variants in 50 million cells from a diverse cohort of 10,000 individuals. Writing in Nature, Cuomo et al.2, Xue et al.3 and Tanudisastro et al.4 introduce findings from TenK10K’s first phase.
