Japan bond yields surge to 3.75%, hiding a quiet Bitcoin trap

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Demand for Japan’s 20-year bonds fell sharply as yields rose, putting yen-funded Bitcoin trades under scrutiny.

Demand fell at Japan’s Nov. 18 auction of a 20-year government bond, with investors offering to buy 3.64 times the amount the Finance Ministry accepted in the competitive auction, putting pressure on traders who borrow yen to hold Bitcoin.

The average accepted yield rose, while the yen barely moved between the Bank of Japan’s two 5 p.m. Tokyo readings.

In the Nov. 18 results, the ministry recorded ¥1.914 trillion in competitive tenders for issue 198 and accepted ¥525.8 billion. At the October auction, it received ¥3.139 trillion and accepted ¥537.1 billion.

Demand fell by much more than the amount sold in the competitive auction, and the average accepted yield rose 3.3 basis points to 3.756% from 3.723%.

Competitive coverage fell from 5.84× on Oct. 20 to 3.64× on Nov. 18 for Japan’s 20-year issue 198.

Source: Finance Ministry, Oct. 20 result · Finance Ministry, Nov. 18 result

At 5 p.m. in Tokyo, the midpoint of the Bank of Japan’s dollar-yen quote was 159.35 yen per dollar on Nov. 18, up one hundredth of a yen from 159.34 on Nov. 17.

Money-market broker Tokyo Tanshi reported a weighted average unsecured overnight call rate of 1.227%, unchanged from the previous day. The rate measures one-day borrowing in Japan’s call money market, for which the Bank of Japan publishes a daily series.

The currency and the overnight rate track different parts of a yen-funded trade.

The dollar-yen midpoint moved one sen between readings. The overnight call rate was unchanged.

A trader borrowing yen short-term to hold Bitcoin faces higher costs to renew the loan if funding rates rise. If the trader sells Bitcoin for dollars and leaves the yen debt unhedged, a stronger yen makes repaying the loan in dollar terms more expensive.

Bank for International Settlements researchers documented that feedback during the August 2024 unwind in leveraged currency and equity trades.

Bitfinex reported that aggregate Bitcoin futures open interest fell to 645,750 BTC over Nov. 14 and 15, 8% below Nov. 11. The futures total shows how much exposure was outstanding but gives no breakdown by funding currency.

BIS research showed that although it identified $271 billion in yen-denominated bank loans to non-bank borrowers outside Japan in the first quarter of 2024, it could not determine how much was used for carry trades.

The Finance Ministry plans to offer about ¥300 billion at the Nov. 25 reopening of a 40-year bond, the next test of long-dated demand.

A stronger signal for Bitcoin would be rising short-term yen borrowing costs or a sustained strengthening of the yen, alongside shrinking futures exposure and evidence that traders cutting positions had yen liabilities.
