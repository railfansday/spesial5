Nearly 7,000 flights across the United States were delayed or canceled on Friday, according to flight-tracking service FlightAware. Planes already ⁠in the air have been allowed to land, Reuters reports, but those on the ground could not take off.

The disruption is the latest and most dramatic sign of strain on a US air traffic ⁠control system beset by equipment and communications failures, controller shortages and ageing infrastructure.

The FAA’s ground stop order, halting departures from all US airports, runs through at least 11pm ET Friday.

Speaking to reporters outside the White House on the nationwide ground stop at all US airports, the US homeland security secretary, Markwayne Mullin, said: “Just to make sure we understand, there is no indication, and we had no reason to believe as of right now, that this was a threat to the United States. This was a telecommunication outage throughout FAA.”

The secretary added that there was no evidence “yet that this was a nefarious attack; it is just a telecommunication outage, as of right now”.

Asked whether it might have been caused by AI or a cyber-attack, Mullin incorrectly said that the last nationwide groundstop was “on 9/11” and sought to reassure the families of travelers that this outage was not part of a terrorist attack. “There is no reason to be concerned about your loved ones if they’re flying,” he said.

“As we investigate what caused this, you always got to assume that as many cyber-attacks that take place, that you make that assumption – so, but as of right now, that’s not known.”

After restoring communications so all airborne flights can land, Mullin said, “we’re going to continue to work to find out what the root of the cause is”.

“We’re not ruling out anything at this point,” Mullin added.
