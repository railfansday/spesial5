Robert Pattinson As Bond Or Bond Villain: What Did The Actor Say Years Ago?

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Robert Pattinson today described being cast as James Bond as a “dream come true”.

The actor enthused: “James Bond is one of the most legendary characters in cinema, and to be entrusted with 007 is the honour of a lifetime. I’m deeply grateful to Denis, Amy, David, Tanya, and everyone at Amazon MGM Studios for their belief in me. To say this role is a dream come true is an understatement. I couldn’t be more excited about this partnership and the work ahead with this fantastic team.”

But what has the Twilight, Tenet and Batman actor previously said about starring in the iconic franchise? Watch on Deadline

Speaking way back in 2010 during publicity for movie Remember Me, Pattinson was asked by Fandango for his thoughts on acting in the franchise. The Dune actor replied: “I’d quite like to play a villain in a Bond film. That would be fun. Or maybe play… kind of play a male, like, Bond girl. A Bond boy.”

Speaking on the London red carpet for the same film, Pattinson was asked whether he could see himself as Bond: “Ooh, I don’t know,” he responded at the time. “That’s quite big shoes to fill”.

Two years later, in an interview with the U.K.’s Shortlist, Pattinson was a little less demure:

“Yeah definitely, but more like [in] 20 years,” Pattinson said of whether he’d like to play Bond.

He expanded: “I think there’d be nothing worse than, like, ‘Let’s get a fresh-faced Bond!’ That would be the worst idea in the world. After Daniel Craig, you have to have some baggage. It would be ridiculous to reinvent [him] as some young posh kid. Or, if [he] was, I’d have to get so tortured in the first few scenes. I’d have to do the first film with one arm or something.”

There has been plenty of speculation in recent years that Amazon would go for a younger Bond as it looks to refresh the franchise. In the end, they’ve gone for a slightly older Bond. Pattinson turns 41 next May. By the time his first Bond is released, he will likely be the second oldest Bond on debut after Roger Moore, who was 45 when Live and Let Die was released.
