‘Obsession’ Director’s Next Movie Officially Casts a Stephen King Favorite

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Hannah covers horror, sci-fi, and games at Collider, where she writes news and features, reviews new releases, and interviews the people who make them. She’s helping build out Collider’s gaming coverage, with Pokémon as one of her specialties. If she isn’t writing about a monster, she’s probably watching one on a tape from her VHS collection.

Matthew Lillard, who has now worked on more than a few Stephen King adaptations, has spent enough time in haunted houses to know how badly this can go. In Thir13en Ghosts, he plays one of the few people who understands what’s trapped inside the house, which is a miserable thing to know when everybody else is still getting a tour. A year later, he was Shaggy in Scooby-Doo, trying to survive a trip to a resort where the monsters really did turn out to be monsters. There’s a lot of room between those two performances, and Curry Barker’s next movie sounds like it could use both.

Barker got the biggest horror hit of 2026 out of a guy making one selfish wish. Obsession has cleared $500 million worldwide and arrives on Netflix in the U.S. today, just as its director is adding another familiar face to his next movie. This time, Barker and longtime collaborator Cooper Tomlinson are playing two guys who have made the mistake of telling people they know how to handle a haunting. Eventually, somebody takes them at their word.

Lillard has joined the cast of Curry Barker’s Anything But Ghosts, a horror comedy set in the same universe as Obsession that Focus Features will release in theaters on May 7, 2027. Lillard’s role in the movie currently remains under wraps. He joins Barker, Tomlinson, Aaron Paul, Bryce Dallas Howard, Violet McGraw, and Chris Reinacher.

Four franchises, four completely different ways of getting underneath your skin. This is the one built out of your particular fear, why it works so well on you, and what it would leave behind.

Your nightmare does not wait outside the door. It waits for you to close your eyes, which you are going to have to do eventually, because nobody has ever won that particular argument.

Your nightmare is not really the doll. It is the face of the person you tell, and the way they decide, very kindly, that you have imagined all of it.

Your nightmare has your number, knows your name, and has almost certainly been inside your house before. The worst part is that you already know them.

Your nightmare is not really about you. It is about the people asleep down the hall, and the feeling that whatever is here is not interested in leaving.

Barker and Tomlinson play fake ghost hunters who are hired by a couple with an actual ghost problem. Barker directs from a script he wrote with Tomlinson, and putting the two of them in front of the camera gives the movie a different setup from Obsession. They’re the ones who have to stand in somebody’s house and pretend they know what they’re doing, even after they realize their clients have every reason to be scared.

The fun of casting Lillard in Anything But Ghosts is how much of his career already points to how perfect he is for a scenario like the one Barker and Tomlinson have cooked up. He spent two Scooby-Doo movies as the guy who wanted out of the haunted house before anybody had finished explaining why they were there. In Scream, he got one of the movie’s biggest laughs out of Stu realizing he might actually bleed to death. That timing could do a lot for a comedy about two frauds who have to keep up the act once the ghosts show up. Lillard’s more recent work in Mike Flanagan's Carrie and Daredevil: Born Again shows how much darker he can go, and his William Afton in Five Nights at Freddy’s is a reminder that the same guy who’s great at panicking can be just as good at giving everybody else a reason to panic.

Anything But Ghosts premieres in theaters May 7, 2027.
