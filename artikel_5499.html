New Research to Analyze Pet-Related Housing Policy Landscape

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Human Animal Bond Research Institute Awards Grant to the University of Tennessee

WASHINGTON, Nov. 16, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- The Human Animal Bond Research Institute (HABRI) today awarded a new grant to the University of Tennessee, Knoxville for a comprehensive, publicly accessible database on pet-related housing policies across the United States. The database will be designed for renters and housing providers to access plain-language information regarding state statutes, administrative codes, regulatory guidance and municipal ordinances related to pets in rental housing. Researchers will also conduct in-depth interviews with relevant stakeholders to better understand the experiences, strategies and challenges associated with designing and implementing pet-inclusive housing policies. This research will be led by Claire Schuch, PhD, Associate Director of Research at The University of Tennessee, Knoxville Center for Pet Family Well-Being (CPFW) and Courtney Cronley, PhD, Professor and PhD Program Director in the College of Social Work.

"Research indicates that restrictive pet policies in rental housing contribute to housing insecurity for renters with companion animals and may elevate eviction risk for low-income tenants as they struggle to find and maintain pet-inclusive units," said Dr. Claire Schuch, study Co-Principal Investigator. "By improving transparency and understanding of pet-related housing policies, our project will support pet owners in maintaining housing stability, assist housing providers in adopting pet-inclusive practices and inform policymakers seeking evidence-based solutions."

This mixed-methods study will proceed in three phases. The first phase will involve a systematic review of state-level statutes and regulations related to pets in housing. The second phase will analyze these policies and examine their implications for pet ownership in rental housing. The third phase will consist of interviews with key stakeholders across diverse communities to better understand how these policies are interpreted and implemented in practice. This work will inform a replicable toolkit based on the successes and challenges within representative municipalities that have addressed pet-inclusive housing policies.

"Pet-related rental housing policies vary widely across different states and jurisdictions, yet there is currently no centralized database tracking these regulations," said Steven Feldman, HABRI president. "This important project will address this gap and allow researchers, advocates and policymakers to better understand the national landscape and remove barriers to pet-inclusive housing."

About HABRI HABRI is a not-for-profit organization that funds innovative scientific research to document the health benefits of companion animals; educates the public about human-animal bond research; and advocates for the beneficial role of companion animals in society.
