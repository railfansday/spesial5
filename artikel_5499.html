IEA said global oil supply is topping demand for first time since Iran war

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Observed oil inventories rose by 52 million barrels in October, the first increase since June, as Gulf exports recovered

The International Energy Agency said on Friday that global oil supplies are surpassing demand, a first since fighting erupted in Iran, as Gulf exports rebound while consumption lags. In its November oil market report, the agency described the market as "showing tentative signs of rebalancing."

Observed global oil inventories rose by 52 million barrels, or 1.7 million barrels a day, in October, the first increase since June, according to the IEA. Global supply rose by 2.3 million barrels a day to 105.4 million barrels a day in October, lifting output by 5.3 million barrels a day since August.

The IEA expects the market to tip back into oversupply during the fourth quarter, with a larger build in 2027. According to Business Recorder, the agency sees fourth-quarter supply averaging 105 million barrels a day, 1.7 million barrels a day above forecast demand. The IEA’s earlier forecast showed a fourth-quarter deficit, according to Business Recorder.

Gulf oil exports, excluding Oman, reached 18.4 million barrels a day in October, 26% below pre-war levels, the IEA said. Crude flows rose to 15 million barrels a day, or 82% of pre-war levels. The agency also lifted its outlook for supply in the last three months of the year, adding 3.3 million barrels a day.

The IEA forecasts that worldwide consumption of oil will shrink by 2.7 million barrels a day in 2026, to 102.3 million barrels a day, and then climb back by 2 million barrels a day in 2027. It projects supply will rise by 6.6 million barrels a day to 108.1 million barrels a day in 2027. That outlook was lowered because of constrained Iranian shipments. Looking further out, the agency puts the 2027 glut near 3.8 million barrels a day, a narrowing from the 3.94 million barrels a day it projected last month, according to Business Recorder.

North Sea Dated crude fell 32%, from a high of $136 a barrel in early October to $93 when the report was written, the IEA said. That price is $22 a barrel above pre-war levels. European diesel prices fell 11% over the same period but, near $180 a barrel, remained 77% above pre-war levels.

The IEA said tightness in product supply, especially diesel, is the overriding concern for the market and consumers. It warned that attacks on shipping threaten the recovery. The agency said an accelerated release of its emergency diesel stocks will bridge the supply gap until refineries recover. The IEA said that 71 million barrels from its emergency reserves release, 15 million of them diesel, will reach the market before March, according to Business Recorder.

In early October, OPEC+ core members held November production targets unchanged while the war kept Gulf shipments at 60% to 80% of normal levels. Brent crude was above $100 a barrel at the time.
