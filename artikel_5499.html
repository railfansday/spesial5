One hand on the controller, one on the pizza roll: How America fed itself through GTA VI’s $3.79 billion launch night

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The biggest video game launch in years left millions of players with a practical problem: how to eat without putting down the controller.

Rockstar Games, a unit of Take-Two Interactive, said the game brought in an estimated $3.79 billion in retail sales worldwide on its first day, which it called the biggest entertainment launch in history. With that demand, there are surely some tired people holding onto those controllers but hungry for a snack.

It seems like the delivery apps already planned for it. Uber Eats picked several of its Grand Theft Auto VI launch-day deals for food that could be eaten with one hand. In the U.S., the list included buy-one-get-one McNuggets at McDonald’s, boneless wings at Wingstop, pizza at Papa John’s and Whopper Jr. burgers at Burger King.

“We even picked some of our food deals with one-handed eating in mind, so fans can keep their focus on the game,” Uber President and COO Andrew Macdonald said in a statement to Fortune.

Gamers ordered accordingly. Between midnight and 5 a.m. on launch night, Uber Eats orders in Miami, the real city behind the game’s Vice City, rose more than 30% from the prior two Thursdays, Macdonald said on X. Nationwide, orders of Red Bull Watermelon rose 95%, Totino’s Pizza Rolls 54% and Ghost Sour Strips 46%. Uber told Fortune it had no separate data on one-handed food orders.

DoorDash reported a similar pattern. Between 9 p.m. and 2 a.m. ET, orders for chips, burgers, fries and pizza each rose more than 10% compared with the average of the six prior Wednesdays, the company told Fortune. Energy drinks rose more than 20%. Miami led all major markets, with late-night orders up nearly 25%, and Philadelphia, Detroit and Atlanta also saw double-digit increases, according to DoorDash’s data dashboard. DoorDash said it could not compare the food increases with its overall order growth.

Some of the biggest jumps were for gear, not food. Orders for controllers and gaming accessories rose 450% on DoorDash, HDMI cables 440% and headsets 70%. Searches for GTA VI on the app rose more than 940% in the week before launch.

“GTA 6 is more than a game launch. It’s a cultural moment that fans have been waiting years for,” Gina Igwe, DoorDash’s vice president of brand, creative and consumer marketing, said in a statement to Fortune.

Players who weren’t playing were watching. In its first two days, GTA VI drew more livestream watch time than any game has in its entire launch week, more than Elden Ring, Hogwarts Legacy and Cyberpunk 2077 drew in their first weeks combined, according to Streams Charts.

Total Twitch watch time rose 61% compared with the two days before launch, the firm said, while World of Warcraft and Counter-Strike lost about 31% of their Twitch watch time and Fortnite fell 60% on YouTube. Kick’s “Just Sleeping” category rose 176% as streamers ran round-the-clock GTA VI marathons.

Restaurant chains also moved to get a share. Popeyes is selling a Spice City sandwich, named after the game’s setting, through Nov. 22 and told players to order it through its app “between missions.” McDonald’s began taking delivery orders on Discord, the chat app many gamers use while they play. DoorDash ran offers with Wendy’s and the Cheesecake Factory, and Grubhub offered $10 off to its first 1,000 customers.

Analytics firm Sensor Tower expects the game to drive more than 50 million additional visits to U.S. food delivery and restaurant apps in its first 30 days, Reuters reported.

Neither company put a dollar figure on launch night. A rough Fortune estimate suggests the extra takeout spending was small next to the game itself. Uber and DoorDash together handled about $680 million in delivery orders a day worldwide in the third quarter, based on their earnings reports. If late-night hours make up roughly a tenth of that, and launch night lifted those orders 10% to 25%, the extra spending comes to roughly $7 million to $17 million. That’s about 1% to 2.5% of a normal day’s delivery business for the two companies, and less than half a percent of the $3.79 billion Rockstar says the game brought in on day one.

That shift fits what economists say about events like this one. Much of the money moves from one kind of spending to another rather than appearing from nowhere. “People just rearrange how they’re spending their free time,” Victor Matheson, an economist at the College of the Holy Cross, told Fortune before the launch.

Restaurants also had fewer hands. Missed restaurant shifts rose 92% week over week on Nov. 19, according to data from scheduling platform Harri shared with Fortune, covering 280,000 shifts. One in 14 shifts went unworked, compared with one in 27 the Thursday before, while sales at those restaurants fell less than 2%.

Grand Theft Auto VI came out Nov. 19 on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, more than 13 years after its predecessor. Before the launch, a widely shared estimate claimed workers skipping their jobs to play would cost the U.S. economy $1 billion, a figure Matheson told Fortune he doesn’t believe. Office attendance barely moved. Wi-Fi logins at the more than 250 U.S. locations of Industrious, a coworking company owned by CBRE, fell about 5% on launch day compared with the previous three Thursdays, the company told Fortune.
