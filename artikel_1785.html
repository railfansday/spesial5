Process of Elimination

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Over the course of the November international break, news of contract extensions for some of Arsenal’s ‘pillar’ players has begun to filter through. David Raya, Jurrien Timber, Declan Rice and Piero Hincapie are all reportedly on the verge of new deals, with Riccardo Calafiori’s deal also in the in-tray for Andrea Berta too.

Arsenal have assembled an elite core and it didn’t happen overnight. Back in the summer of 2021, the club enacted a policy of buying players under 24 years old. I always felt like this was the true year zero of the Arteta project. That always felt like the window that built the foundations.

In hindsight, it’s interesting that only Ben White and Martin Odegaard have ‘survived’ since then, with Ramsdale, Tomiyasu, Lokonga and Tavares all having drifted away ever since. Emile Smith Rowe also signed a new contract and took the number 10 shirt that summer and now he is scratching around for minutes at Fulham.

The summer of 2022 saw the arrivals of Gabriel Jesus and Alex Zinchenko, as well as Fabio Vieira. The former pair briefly elevated Arsenal to a new level before becoming quickly obsolete. Vieira was a fairly pricey gamble that didn’t pay off. It was the summer of 2023 that truly established Arsenal among England’s elite. David Raya, Jurrien Timber, Declan Rice and Kai Havertz’s arrivals turned Arsenal from caterpillars to butterflies.

Three of those players are about to sign new contracts. Curiously, we have little to no reporting on whether Kai Havertz is lined up for a new deal as yet. Arsenal are banking on their well assembled core to deliver more silverware as they enter their peak years (Raya is 31 which feels close to peak age for a goalkeeper).

Undoubtedly, there are some players who will drift away in the coming years. Mikel Merino, Martin Zubimendi and Ben White feel unlikely to get new Arsenal contracts at this particular moment in time. (Though these things can and do change). Like Havertz, Odegaard’s deal runs until the summer of 2028 and we have yet to hear rumblings of a new deal.

Odegaard and Havertz have negotiated significant fitness challenges over the last two seasons, which probably explains why they have found themselves lower down Berta’s to do list. Timber has had his own physical challenges too, but he is 25. Havertz and Odegaard are 27. Their next contracts will be their last ‘big’ contracts in terms of wages and their resale value will drop to close to zero if Arsenal tie them down. It makes sense for the club to take its time a little more with both players.

Both are also defining attacking and / or creative players in the Arsenal team. At some point, Arteta is going to have to plot an evolution within this core and it feels like that is more likely to happen in the final third of the pitch than within the core of players who operate behind the ball. Behind the ball, Arsenal are absolutely tip top and have some of the most elite players in the world in their positions.

That is not as true ahead of the ball. Odegaard and Havertz remain, for my money, elite, defining players for Arsenal. For the purposes of this piece; I am going to speculate that Odegaard is going to be offered a new contract given his status in the team and because his role is a little more holistic and he doesn’t solely operate in the final third.

Which leaves us with Havertz. I rate Havertz really highly and believe he is integral to Arsenal’s style, he has the technical quality to play one touch football and the physical quality to act as a release valve and hold the ball up. I can’t think of many forwards who offer both sides of the game to such an extent.

There are several Arsenal attackers I would trade in before Havertz. However, the market doesn’t always bend to your will. Arsenal remain a self sustaining club and in the PSR / SCR era, player trading has never been more important or more voluminous. Keeping money swishing between the cost and revenue columns is a necessity in this environment.

Who you would ideally like to sell and who the market wants to buy are often not the same things. I wouldn’t think twice about selling Eze or Gyokeres before I would sell Kai Havertz. But Eze and Gyokeres will be 29 next summer and even putting aside their age, I don’t think they would command the same fee that Havertz would. Havertz is also on a pretty sizeable salary at the moment and players rarely sign up for anything other than a pay rise, which is a big consideration.

Clearly, the number one impediment to Havertz getting a new deal is his fitness. He is still clearly preferred by the manager to Viktor Gyokeres. I have to say I am surprised (a little unpleasantly) at how quickly the tide of opinion has turned against Havertz. The run he went on very recently that saw him score in the Champions League Final, score the goal that clinched the title against Burnley, a critical goal against Chelsea and the winning goal at Anfield seem to have just bought him absolutely no credit within the fan base, which I find a little surprising.

However, Arsenal need to boost their attacking prowess overall at some point very soon. Like I said, I would consider Havertz a superior player to all of Eze, Gyokeres, Tzolis and Madueke. The squad is well served for numbers in attack; it doesn’t have any gaps in that respect. It has a gap in elite quality.

The key to boosting that might not be ‘who is the least effective player?’ it might be ‘who is the most saleable?’ Sometimes you do just have to cash in your chips on a player to evolve and move forwards. It doesn’t always have to be because the player is obsolete or not good enough. Arsenal have such numbers in midfield that there also isn’t really a route into the team there for Havertz either.

If someone offered £60-70m for Gyokeres or Eze, I would accept it and give Havertz a new contract the next day (assuming he does prove his fitness). But I think those scenarios are unlikely. I am not arguing that we have definitely reached the ‘we must sell Kai’ stage and I will reiterate that, at this moment in time, I consider him a very key player who should pretty much always start when fit.

While he isn’t the most prolific finisher as a centre-forward, crucial goals this season against Chelsea and Liverpool demonstrate his worth. The conversation about moving on only becomes live on condition that the money is used to immediately buy an obviously elite attacker- preferably a centre-forward in that case since that is the gap a Havertz departure would leave.

But I am certain it is a conversation Arteta and Andrea Berta are currently entertaining. Arteta will want to push the level of the team even further and, to fund what that is going to entail, potentially painful sales will have to be made somewhere. The vigour with which the club pursued the sale of Gabriel Martinelli this summer shows the manager has the stomach for that conversation too.
