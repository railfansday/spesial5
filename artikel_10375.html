Is another crypto winter upon us? Why Bitcoin, XRP, and Ethereum fell more than 20% in a month

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The crypto markets are down again today, with major tokens suffering significant losses over the past 30 days.

As of his writing, Bitcoin is trading below $65,000, Ethereum is nearly down to $1,800, and XRP is below 96 cents.

Those are more than 20%, 25%, and 26% drops, respectively, over the past month.

Bitcoin hit an all-time high of more than $126,000 a token in October 2025, meaning its value has roughly halved since then.

Those falls are causing some to wonder whether another crypto winter is upon us. The term describes extended bear markets across cryptocurrencies, in which digital tokens drop significantly in value and stay down. Major crypto winters previously hit in 2018–2019 and 2022–2023. 

It may be too early to tell whether this is the start of another one, but several factors have been driving cryptocurrencies lower in recent weeks. Here’s what you need to know.

Expected Federal Reserve interest rate hikes

One of the greatest factors likely weighing on cryptocurrencies is the expectation that the Federal Reserve will raise interest rates in December. As Reuters reported, a majority of economists expect the Fed to hike rates next month. The hike is expected to be by 25 basis points, to a range of 4.00%- 4.25%.

Rate hikes make money more expensive to borrow. This can encourage people to save, which can dampen spending and thus help fight inflation.

But when money is more expensive to borrow, those who lend it can earn more. So, when rates are higher, investors tend to pull their money from non-interest-bearing assets, like crypto, and put it into interest-bearing assets like U.S. treasury bills.

This reallocation leads to a selloff in crypto, driving the basket of digital currencies lower.

AI stock rut

Cryptocurrencies aren’t the only assets having a tough time lately.

Big Tech stocks—especially AI-adjacent ones—are having a rough few weeks. Last week was perhaps the worst thanks to the Jensen Huang scandal that sent shares of Nvdia Corporation (Nasdaq: NVDA) down more than 12% during the last five trading days.

The tech-heavy Nasdaq is also down about 1% during the same period.

AI stocks and cryptocurrencies often tend to move in tandem. Both are highly volatile and act as speculative assets. When one falls, investors tend to get jittery and sell off the other class in an effort to prevent further losses and lock in gains.

The 2026 midterm elections

A blue tsunami swept across America in early November’s midterm elections. In some ways, that is very good for the markets, as a large political majority brings a certain amount of certainty.

Investors may or may not like that party’s policy positions—but knowing what’s coming can help them plan financially.

Unfortunately for crypto, the incoming Democratic Congress may be much less friendly to the industry.

Crypto investors fear that this new political leadership could propose stronger regulation, which may stymie the industry’s growth—or at least impose checks it otherwise wouldn’t have had if the GOP had retained control of Congress.

Crypto Fear and Greed Index falls to “Extreme Fear” level

Unsurprisingly, after a 20-plus percent selloff in major tokens over the past month, investors are fretting about crypto’s future—at least in the near term. 

And nowhere is that worry more evident than when you take a look at CoinMarketCap’s Crypto Fear and Greed Index. The index tracks investor sentiment surrounding the crypto markets. It ranges from 0 to 100.

The higher the index, the more optimistic (and potentially greedy) investors tend to be about Bitcoin and other tokens. The lower the index, the more fearful they tend to be.

Right now, the index stands at a paltry 13, placing it in the “Extreme Fear” range—its lowest range. It has not been at this level since February 2026, when Bitcoin fell from around $87,500 to around $60,000 in the first few days of the month.

Of course, despite the index’s low rating—and the many factors weighing crypto down over the past month—it’s still too early to tell whether the crypto markets are on the cusp of another prolonged downturn. But the longer the rout lasts, the more the specter of another crypto winter will weigh on investors’ minds.
