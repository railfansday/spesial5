Two years at a community college and two at a state university get you the same bachelor’s degree for thousands less, but only if the university counts your credits. Before your first class, find your state’s transfer guarantee or the university’s transfer agreement with your college, and choose your courses from it.

The College Board puts average 2026-27 tuition and fees at $4,210 a year for a community college in your district and $12,290 for an in-state public university, before financial aid.

Pay those prices for two years at each school, and the same bachelor’s degree costs $16,160 less than four years at the university. Grants narrow that gap. On average, they cover all of community college tuition and fees for first-time, full-time students, and all but about $1,920 a year at an in-state public university, the College Board found. Compare each school’s net price, what you’d pay after grants, before you count on the full $16,160.

Living at home instead of on campus during the community college years also skips two years of housing and food charges, which average $14,430 a year at public four-year schools.

Those savings hold only if your credits transfer. A course the university won’t count toward your degree is one you pay for twice, and it can push graduation past four years. You also give up some campus life, and the transfer itself isn’t a given. Of first-time students who started at a community college in 2018, fewer than a third transferred to a four-year school within six years, the National Student Clearinghouse Research Center found.

Book your first meeting with your community college’s transfer adviser before you register for classes, and name the university and major you’re aiming for.
