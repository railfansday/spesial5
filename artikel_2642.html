Celtics throttle Mavericks, 131-94, thanks to magnificent 42-9 1st quarter

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Some NBA games are won late in the fourth quarter, with dramatic twists and turns in the final seconds and elite shotmaking from the game's brightest stars.

Sunday was not one of those games. This one was over before many fans settled into their seats, as the Celtics led by 33 through 1 en route to a dominant 131-94 victory at TD Garden.

"We were playing well, forcing them into shots we want them to take, and they missed some shots," said Celtics coach Joe Mazzulla.

Boston (11-2) has now won six straight, including four by 20 or more, and is still in first place in the Eastern Conference. Dallas (7-7), playing without Kyrie Irving (knee) on the second night of a back to back, looked outmatched in every facet early.

Jayson Tatum led the way with 32 points (11-of-18 shooting), 6 rebounds and 6 assists. Boston shot 51 percent as a team, leaving no doubt and improving to 6-0 at home on the season.

The Celtics absolutely dominated the first quarter, building a 42-9 advantage through 1 with a truly spectacular performance. Yes, it was the largest lead after the first quarter in franchise history. It felt like it, too.

Boston shot 59 percent from the floor and 54 percent from 3, while Dallas was at 11 percent from the field and 0 percent from distance. The Celtics racked up 14 points in the paint, compared to 2 for the Mavericks, and won the rebounding battle, 16-10.

Boston could do no wrong. Dallas could do no right.

"We tried to shrink the floor as much as possible and cause havoc," said Celtics wing Jordan Walsh.

Walsh played with conviction and purpose and helped set the tone early, and Derrick White (16 points) found a rhythm early to help extend the lead. Seemingly every shot the Celtics threw up went in, as the action resembled a varsity team scrimmaging a junior varsity team.

The Mavericks found their footing in the second quarter and chipped away, but the Celtics preserved a 67-37 lead heading into halftime following a relatively even second quarter.

"Obviously it's easy to really bring it when it's a close game, or things like that," Tatum said. "Just trying to challenge our mindset of we're up 25 to 30, can you try to put the game away? I think we did a really good job of that today."

Tatum, Pritchard and White continued to flourish, and Sam Hauser provided strong minutes off the bench. Zaccharie Risacher (18 points) paced Dallas with 9 in the half, but Cooper Flagg (17) struggled and the Mavericks turned it over six more times in the quarter.

Eleven players saw the floor for Boston and nine scored, led by White with 11.

The Celtics kept their target practice going in the third quarter, as Paul George and Walsh hit back-to-back corner 3's to push the margin to 34. Neemias Queta added one of his own from the other corner to improve to 4 of 5 from distance on the season.

"His growth and his role has gone kind of under the radar," Mazzulla said of Queta. "He's more important than just with defense. He's important in the locker room, he's important on the offensive end. He continues to get better and better. It's easy to overlook it because of how easy he makes it."

Flagg got going in the third, but Tatum continued to cook and kept the lead hovering around 30 most of the way.

Tatum drilled a step-back 3 with 4.3 seconds left to make it 104-71 through three. He finished the quarter with 21 and continued to look like pre-injury Tatum.

The Celtics started the fourth with Baylor Scheierman, Devin Carter, Ron Harper Jr., Hugo González and Luka Garza, and they continued to play the brand of basketball that got Boston to that point.

Harper Jr. converted inside, Scheierman and Harper Jr. swished consecutive 3's and Garza delivered inside to push it to 40, 117-77, with 8:58 to go.

"The start of the fourth quarter, we made them call the first timeout," Mazzulla said.

Gino Time came with 2:51 remaining and the Celtics up 36. The win streak is alive, and the Celtics are looking very much like contenders.
