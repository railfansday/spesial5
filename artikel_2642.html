Ex-Cowboys DL believes Christian Parker change ‘obviously’ has to happen

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

How long can the Dallas Cowboys go on like this? Can an actual solution be found before season's end? First-year defensive coordinator Christian Parker has been skewered since Week 1, and the condemnation is only intensifying following a 46-40 loss versus the San Francisco 49ers. Dak Prescott and the non-George Pickens members of the offense probably deserve more blame than they are getting for this latest defeat, but one cannot ignore the gaping holes that always seem present on the other side of the ball.

Former Cowboys defensive end Marcus Spears is growing tired of trying to dissect the unit's problems. He believes the first order of businesses is crystal clear. He also thinks the issues run far deeper than one area of weakness.

"I would actually love to have the enthusiasm and energy it takes to go after this team, but we've been seeing it every week," Spears said on ESPN's "NFL Live." "We've been seeing this defense do this every week. It's gotten to a point now where you shouldn't ask if a coaching change needs to be made. The answer is, obviously. There are players that look worse in Week 10 than they did in Week 1, 2, 3 and 4.

"I don't want to be hyperbolic. It's cool to get all excited about Dallas' defense. They've been awful for two years…. The problem is you haven't had a head coach that determines a culture on the football team, because they are always limited to how the hierarchy is in Dallas… This is a microcosm of how this organization is run."

It would not be a proper Cowboys rant without taking an implied shot at Jerry Jones, would it? Many fans share in Spears' frustration. The owner is not going to discipline himself, however, so that leaves Parker as the primary target, at least for now.

Dallas surrendered a pitiful 584 total yards to the 49ers, allowing its opponent to average 7.4 yards per rush and 11.2 yards per pass. No defense has allowed more total yards per game than this one (393.8). The Cowboys have tried working around Christian Parker, but the situation is not improving. Heck, they combusted against the Arizona Cardinals.

This is not new, though. Matt Eberflus also struggled profusely in the DC role last season and Mike Zimmer was not deemed the answer either. Fans can say that Parker is in over his head, but respected play-callers have also failed in Jerry World. When such a trend occurs, it is time for an organization to consider a philosophical shift.

At minimum, a serious reflection period is required. If the Cowboys' defense cannot withstand the Tennessee Titans in Week 11, then there may not be anything left to say.
