Mauricio Pochettino bemoans ‘unfair’ Liverpool incident in ‘painful’ Manchester United defeat

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Mauricio Pochettino has called Manchester United’s 4-0 defeat by Liverpool “so painful”.

The Argentine, making his bow as the Red Devils’ latest head coach at Anfield, watched on as his new side were thrashed by Andoni Iraola’s hosts in a one-sided affair.

Leny Yoro was dismissed on eight minutes for denying Alexander Isak a clear goalscoring opportunity, and Dominik Szoboszlai, Florian Wirtz, Rio Ngumoha and Victor Munoz netted the goals that handed the Reds a huge three points, and deservedly so.

Reflecting on the heavy loss, which demonstrated the sheer size of his task ahead, Pochettino bemoaned the red card as a key turning point at such an early moment in the encounter.

"So painful. So disappointed,” the former Tottenham and Chelsea head coach said.

“It's not ok to start a game in the Premier League with this situation. The game changed completely after [eight] minutes. Now we can explain everything but the damage is done, it's so painful.

"The players are so, so feeling the pain of the result. I told them to be calm. It was an unfair situation that happened. We need to accept that reality. We need to try to increase our focus, concentration and make better decisions on the field.

"We also need to be critical of ourselves but some situations happened in the game that we are not happy with."

The 54-year-old identified two reasons why he believed Yoro should not have been sent off, despite VAR validating referee Sam Barrott’s on-field decision to give the French defender his marching orders.

"The ball was for the goalkeeper,” Pochettino explained.

“In that situation, if you review the VAR you can see for sure, he never had the intention to connect with Isak.

“When the control was long with the striker, the ball was going to the goalkeeper, he runs and goes down.

"In Leny's case, it wasn't intentional. It was an unusual situation that is normal, everyone who plays football can feel it.
