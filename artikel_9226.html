This New Website Aims to Document Every Film Camera Ever Made

Film camera repairer and enthusiast Pierro Pozella has launched a website with an ambitious aim: to document every film camera ever made.

Pozella of PPP Cameras has spent 15 years repairing, restoring, and selling analog cameras. So there is perhaps no one better placed than him for this project that he calls the Camera Index.

“I’ve always wanted to find a way to organize that knowledge into something accessible to everyone,” Pozella tells PetaPixel.

“There’s a tremendous amount of information out there, but it’s scattered across old books, websites, forums and manuals. Some cameras are incredibly well documented, while others have very little information available.”

Camera Index features illustrations, technical information, instruction manuals, and external links to sample shots.

Pozella says that it is easy for technical knowledge of something niche like film cameras to disappear — Camera Index is his attempt at centralizing that knowledge into one resource.

“I also wanted something that was straightforward to navigate. Being autistic, I personally find information much easier to process when it’s clearly organised and presented without unnecessary clutter. That has influenced how I’ve approached the design and structure of the website,” he says.

“Ultimately, I wanted to create the sort of resource I wish had existed when I first started working with cameras.”

Pozella has already included 1,300 cameras on the Camera Index, and that’s just for the launch. The website is a long-term project that he intends to work on for a number of years.

“Nobody seems to have a definitive answer to exactly how many analog camera models have been produced,” he says.

“We’re talking about well over a century of camera manufacturing, with thousands of manufacturers producing everything from mass-market consumer cameras to extremely obscure models that were only manufactured in small quantities.

“The number quickly grows when you start considering different variations, regional models and cameras sold under alternative brand names.”

Pozella has opted for illustrations rather than photos since he doesn’t have access to every single camera model ever made.

“The Camera Index is about making camera information accessible and signposting people to useful information under one roof,” he explains.

“As well as the practical limitations of having to source and photograph over 10,000 plus cameras — I opted for illustrations as they provide a standardized way of identifying each camera. They function as simple visual references or thumbnails for each camera and are not intended to replace photography or be the main focus of the project.”

Pozella says the Camera Index is for anyone with an interest in analog photography: from people completely new to it and looking to buy their first camera, to seasoned collectors looking for information about a particular model.

“More than anything, I hope that the Camera Index will become a lasting reference for analog photography,” he adds.

“A lot of the knowledge surrounding older cameras exists in ageing publications, discontinued websites or in the heads of people who have spent their lives working with them. As time passes, there’s a genuine risk that some of that information will disappear.

“I’d like to help prevent that by bringing together as much information as possible and making it freely accessible.”

“In the longer term, I also want to expand the archive beyond cameras themselves, documenting accessories and more obscure photographic equipment,” he adds.

“I’m already building a useful links section to connect visitors with camera repairers, specialist shops, manuals and other valuable resources. I think it’s important that the archive doesn’t just document cameras, but also helps people find the information and services needed to keep using them.

“If Camera Index eventually becomes somewhere people instinctively go when they want to learn about an analog camera, then I’ll feel I’ve achieved what I set out to do.”
