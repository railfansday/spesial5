Double Fine Is Updating a Game With Almost No Players on Steam, And We’re Just as Confused

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Covering the video games industry since 2017, with experience in news, articles, and reviews. Currently a writer at DualShockers and gaming magazines.

It truly is more admirable to watch a developer who never gives up on a project, even when things aren't exactly going according to plan. But sometimes, I have to wonder if a little stubbornness is involved, too – and that's pretty much how I feel about Double Fine's latest announcement, as the studio has just revealed an update for a game that can barely keep anyone playing on Steam.

The game in question is Kiln, a rather unusual multiplayer title that launched earlier this year. And while I certainly appreciate developers continuing to support their games after release, announcing a major content update for a title that regularly has almost zero players online is certainly an interesting decision. So, what exactly is Double Fine planning here?

For those who might have missed it, Kiln is a multiplayer party brawler developed by Double Fine Productions, the studio behind the beloved Psychonauts franchise. Its premise is pretty unusual: players use a pottery wheel to create their own ceramic fighters, with the size and shape of each pot determining their abilities and playstyle during team-based battles.

It's a genuinely creative concept, and one that sounds like it could lead to some pretty chaotic matches with friends. Unfortunately, the game hasn't managed to attract a particularly large audience since its April 23 launch.

Double Fine Productions has released its game, Keeper, on PlayStation 5, after the port was highly requested by fans.

According to SteamDB, Kiln reached an all-time peak of just 193 concurrent players on Steam, and its numbers have been considerably lower ever since. At the time of writing, the game currently has only one player online, with its 24-hour peak sitting at just 13. That's not exactly the kind of multiplayer community you'd hope to see for a game built entirely around online matches.

Still, Double Fine doesn't seem ready to throw in the towel. The studio has announced Kiln: Reshaped, a major update arriving on November 12 for PC and consoles, bringing a handful of new features that might give players a reason to return.

At the time of writing, the game currently has only one player online, with its 24-hour peak sitting at just 13.

Basically, this upcoming update will introduce two new online modes, along with a local co-op multiplayer option called “Mini Quench”. That's not all, either, as players will also get access to a brand-new Photobooth in the game’s hub, allowing them to pose their ceramic creations and take screenshots with friends. Double Fine also promises plenty of additional decorations and more content, although the full patch notes (which you can read here) will only arrive alongside the update.

The addition of kernel-level anti-cheat last week has resulted in a fresh campaign of outrage against Helldivers 2.

Interestingly, the studio is also turning the launch into a little community celebration called “Day of Clay”. Double Fine is inviting everyone to jump into Kiln on November 12 after 10:30 AM PT, with the possibility of running into some members of the development team during matchmaking. And honestly, I kind of love that idea! There's something charming about a developer wanting to spend time with the remaining players rather than simply moving on to the next project.

Of course, whether these additions will be enough to bring Kiln back to life is another question entirely, but I’m certain that at least one player is extremely happy today! Also, it is worth remembering that Steam numbers don't tell the whole story, especially when the game is also available on consoles, so its community is probably bigger than that.

For now, Double Fine seems determined to keep its pottery brawler spinning. Let's just hope there are enough players around to actually enjoy all that new content.
