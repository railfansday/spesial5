Forget Ganon, This Is What’s Actually Killing Players in Ocarina of Time Remake

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Covering the video games industry since 2017, with experience in news, articles, and reviews. Currently a writer at DualShockers and gaming magazines.

You'd think that after spending dozens of hours exploring Hyrule, fighting terrifying monsters, and eventually taking on Ganondorf himself, players would have some pretty impressive causes of death in The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Remake.

Well, think again! Thanks to the game's new Zelda Notes companion, we can now see what has been giving players the most trouble, and some of these statistics are honestly hilarious.

Released alongside the remake, Zelda Notes is a feature within the Nintendo Switch app designed to accompany players on their adventures. It offers a real-time map, additional lore, a photo editor, daily activity summaries, and, perhaps most interestingly, personal and global gameplay statistics. And while these numbers are still evolving as more people play, they've already revealed a few amusing details about the community.

According to these global statistics on November 11, Big Skulltulas are currently the most defeated enemies in the game, accounting for 15% of all recorded kills. Stalchild, Tektites, and Keese share second place with 8% each – considering how often these creatures appear while freely exploring Hyrule, those numbers aren't particularly surprising.

The causes of death, however, are a different story! You might expect players to lose most of their hearts against a difficult temple boss or during the final showdown with Ganondorf. Nope – the biggest culprit is actually ReDeads and Gibdos, responsible for 18% of recorded deaths.

The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Remake has finally been released, and fans have already discovered several game-breaking glitches.

And honestly, I can't blame anyone for that one! These enemies are already pretty terrifying, but their increased speed in the remake makes encounters with them considerably more dangerous. Getting caught by one can quickly turn into a nightmare, especially if you're not prepared.

The second most common cause of death is more embarrassing, though: fire. Yes, just fire. Not a particularly powerful enemy or a complicated boss encounter, but simply getting burned by environmental hazards. I'm sorry if you contributed to that statistic, but I can't help finding it funny!

You might expect players to lose most of their hearts against a difficult temple boss or during the final showdown with Ganondorf. Nope – the biggest culprit is actually ReDeads and Gibdos, responsible for 18% of recorded deaths.

At least the third most common cause is a little more respectable. Iron Knuckles, which serve as challenging miniboss encounters, have also claimed plenty of victims. Losing a fight against one is certainly easier to forgive than walking into a flame.

The companion's statistics also track what causes damage without necessarily resulting in a Game Over. Environmental traps, including moving hazards found throughout temples, are responsible for 9% of recorded damage, while ReDeads and Gibdos come in second with 7%.

Some of the original's most charming quirks from Zelda: Ocarina of Time didn't make the journey.

Thankfully, not every statistic is quite so grim. The Hookshot is currently the most-used weapon, accounting for 31% of player usage, while the Lens of Truth dominates the magic item category with 40%. That second result isn't particularly shocking, considering how essential the item is for certain sections of the adventure.

Finally, Zelda Notes reveals that most players are taking between 20 and 30 hours to complete Ocarina of Time. That's a pretty reasonable amount of time for such a beloved adventure, although I apparently took things a little more slowly. My own playthrough lasted around 45 hours, so I guess I really wanted to savour every moment in Hyrule!

Either way, these statistics are a fun reminder that even after all these years, Ocarina of Time can still surprise us. And if you're currently playing the remake, maybe keep an eye out for those ReDeads. Ganondorf might be the big bad, but he's clearly not the one you should be worrying about most.

Link has always been known as the silent hero of Hyrule, but the remake appears to have given him a very brief spoken line.
