Warhammer Survivors Review: A Brutal New Bullet Heaven Game

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Warhammer Survivors is a brutal spin-off of Vampire Survivors, pitting heroes from Warhammer 40,000 and Age of Sigmar against endless hordes of enemies while maintaining the addictive gameplay loop.

Few games have a title as accurate or descriptive to its core content as Warhammer Survivors. In a nutshell, itt’s Vampire Survivors gameplay, but with Warhammer 40,000 and Age of Sigmar characters, monsters, and levels, but with the difficulty turned up to eleven.

Well, that’s a bit of an unfair introduction. If Warhammer Survivors was just a cosmetic upgrade, they would have just released it as DLC in Vampire Survivors, rather than giving it its own dedicated game. After all, Vampire Survivors has invited Castlevania to its roster and added Contra too, so surely Space Marines wouldn’t look out of place?

Thankfully, Warhammer Survivors shakes up the gameplay and adds new tweaks to the familiar formula that makes it a worthwhile purchase for people who aren’t familiar with the tabletop game. Long-time Vampire Survivors fans just need to be aware that its Warhammer equivalent is a fair bit harder than the content they’ve beaten before.

At its core, Warhammer Survivors has the same gameplay loop as Vampire Survivors and its many clones. The player picks a hero, who then has to survive against endless hordes of enemies. The main gameplay gimmick is that the player cannot control how the character shoots: just how they move across the battlefield.

Throughout each level, the player earns experience points from killing enemies, and when they level up, they are given new weapons and upgrades that make it easier to destroy more foes. As time goes on, the enemies get stronger and grow in numbers, meaning careful movement becomes even more important.

There are also bosses that appear throughout the stage, which are outlined in a neon green aura. Slaying these bosses results in crates being dropped with level upgrades. These are important, as if a weapon is sufficiently levelled, then a crate could carry its improved version, such as the Boltgun transforming into the Heavy Boltgun, which has a drastically improved fire rate.

Dying on a run isn’t the end of the world, as the player can find coins strewn throughout each stage that can be spent on static buffs and upgrades. These make future runs easier, so the player has a greater chance of surviving later stages.

Warhammer Survivors keeps this gameplay loop intact, though it splits everything across two worlds. On the main menu, the player selects the “Universe”: Warhammer 40,000 and Age of Sigmar, the latter of which is the modern replacement for Warhammer Fantasy (RIP). They have their own stages, characters, and enemies, with no direct crossovers between the two.

Technically, there is content that connects the two, as the upgrades unlocked are shared between the two, meaning working hard in one helps out the other. There are also non-franchise-specific levels that they share, like the Hobby Table, but for the most part, the Warhammer 40,000 and Age of Sigmar parts of the game are separate.

So, if Warhammer Survivors is just Vampire Survivors with Ork Boyz, then is it worth buying? Well, there are some key differences that justify it existing as its own game, rather than DLC for Vampire Survivors.

In Vampire Survivors, all the weapons are tied to the player character. Warhammer Survivors changes this with its Follower system, as characters are now unlocked alongside their weapon when the player levels up. This means they follow the selected hero around, and their attacks will originate from them.

The Follower system might not seem like a big addition, but needing to account for the changing starting position of attacks makes the gameplay considerably more challenging. This is especially true for the Garlic-type weapon users, which have a static area-of-effect centred on their hero. In Warhammer Survivors, the party members move around after the player, meaning the player has to guide them, like a kite, into the enemy hordes.

The other big difference in Warhammer Survivors is how it handles end of stage bosses. In Vampire Survivors, if the player spends too long in a stage, they’ll eventually be attacked by the Grim Reaper and quickly killed. Warhammer Survivors replaces the manifestation of death with the Extremis Bosses: foes that appear at the twenty-five minute mark for an epic fight.

The Extremis Bosses can’t be faced without preparation, however, as the player needs to unlock a specific item in each stage to be able to damage them. If the player doesn’t have this item, then the boss will be invincible, and it will automatically kill the hero in a cutscene.

The enemies the player faces on each stage can also have unique gimmicks, usually tied to their bosses. It’s possible for Tyranids to buff their weaker units, making them considerably faster, while the followers of Nurgle can summon massive poison-spewing chimneys, creating damaging areas for the player to walk through.

The Orks of Warhammer 40,000 easily have the best gimmick, as they can call a mighty “Waaagh”, bringing endless waves of units against the player until it ends. Surviving a Waaagh can be one of the trickiest parts of the Ork stage, unless the player prepares lots of AoE attacks to survive the horde.

The new additions to Warhammer Survivors make the game considerably harder than the source game. And make no mistake: I adore Vampire Survivors and have purchased it on multiple platforms. I have endured countless runs with my boy Poe, facing the creatures of the night with nothing but a piece of Garlic and a dream, and came out the other side.

Warhammer Survivors ramps the difficulty up in a few ways. The Follower system means the origin point of your weapons keeps moving around, making it harder to accurately fire at enemies. This isn’t as much of a problem with targeted weapon users, like grenades, but AoE and shooter characters are harder to predict, as they keep moving while shooting.

The changes to bosses are also a point against the game, because Warhammer Survivors does an extremely poor job of conveying exactly what items are needed to be “Prepared” for facing the Extremis Bosses. The best clues the player gets is seeing what items are always available in each stage, as well as blanked out menus that fill out if they happen to use the correct upgrades.

There’s far too little information about what items are needed to face the Extremeis Bosses, especially as the blanked out menu only appears after a twenty-five minute run, which is hard to reach on a good day. Most players aren’t going to bother doing trial and error: they’ll just Google the answers as soon as the game launches, so why bother obfuscating it?

It also bears mentioning that the Age of Sigmar characters are way less useful than their 40,000 equivalents. The Warhammer 40,000 roster includes gun and grenade users, while the ranged Age of Sigmar characters tend to be much slower. Thankfully, the upgrades menu stays the same between rosters, so it’s possible to grind with the Warhammer 40,000 cast for stat upgrades, in order to help out their fantasy equivalents.

There are also times when the game will just kill the player with no recourse. The Age of SIgmar stages are particularly bad for this, with enemy cavalry units randomly appearing and rushing across the stage, pushing the player into a wall and instantly killing them in a way that’s impossible to avoid.

The sub bosses fought in each stage also have far, far too much health. I’ve sat there for what felt like an eternity with maxed out weapons, stunlocking a boss into place, while damage numbers just rack up, and they won’t die. It’d be fine if their gameplay made them challenging foes to match their mighty health bar, but many of them are just regular enemies with bigger sprites.

The changes to Warhammer Survivors do provide an extra layer of challenge for those coming from the source material, and I’d be lying if I said I didn’t enjoy the new additions and wrinkles: it just needs a few more tweaks and balance checks for the game to be as good as it can be.

The Warhammer franchise has a reputation for being extremely dark in tone, especially 40,000. The Imperium of Man isn’t exactly a lovely place to exist, between the constant threat of Xeno invasion, the corrupting influence of Chaos present in the souls of mankind, and the rampant corruption within society itself.

Warhammer Survivors is a rare example of Warhammer being tongue-in-cheek and referential. This can be seen through its many nods to the actual game and its history, especially the weapons, such as exploding d6s hitting the board, Nuln Oil slowing foes down, the measuring tape increasing damage AoE, and the experience point booster being called White Dwarf.

The highlight of this approach is the Hobby Table stage, where the player fights off monsters on a crafting table, the kind used by many real-life fans of the tabletop game. The developers had mentioned in the past that the Hobby Table stage is a nod to Micro Machines, and it shows, being easily the most fun level in Warhammer Survivors.

The visuals and overall aesthetic of Warhammer Survivors are close to that of Vampire Survivors, and like the Boltgun series, the approach is mired in retro goodness. Few Warhammer games have managed to cram as many foes onto the screen as this one, making Warhammer Survivors one of the few titles to ever capture the essence of the beloved artwork from the series, save shown through a 16-bit filter.

Warhammer Survivors doesn’t try to tell a story: it’s about throwing iconic heroes from the franchise against familiar foes. It’s clear the people who made the game are huge fans of the source material, and that love shines throughout the experience.

The new layer of difficulty in Warhammer Survivors can be frustrating at times, but it also feels necessary for helping distinguish it from Vampire Survivors. If the player can soldier through the hard moments, then they’ll get another massive dose of Survivors gameplay and content, which is likely a huge selling point on its own for people who are still addicted to the original.

Warahmmer Survivors justifies itself as a separate entity throughout plenty of new characters and stages, with enough gameplay gimmicks to ensure it’s not just a reskin. The difficulty can be frustrating at times, but people don’t sign up for a Survivors-like to have an easy time. Warhammer Survivors will satisfy people looking for more of the familiar but different enough to not be a rehash, while still paying tribute to the universes that won over fans worldwide.
