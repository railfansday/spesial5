Jensen Huang’s Departure from NVIDIA Sparks Hope of a Gaming Pivot

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Contributor at DualShockers, former Esports Insider DACH content lead, former Escapist Magazine Germany content lead, software engineering background.

NVIDIA has announced that co-founder Jensen Huang has resigned as president, CEO and board member, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress has been named interim CEO whilst continuing as CFO, with Harvey Jones becoming the board’s first-ever chair. The announcement didn’t give a reason, although it obviously arrived after very public reports of extramarital affairs, one of them reportedly with a woman US intelligence officials suspect of being a Chinese spy. Neither NVIDIA nor Huang has confirmed the reporting, but investors reacted regardless, with shares falling as much as 14% at the open.

Ultimately, I’m not that interested in the gossip. What interests me far more is what happens to a company that has spent the last few years being defined by one man’s bet on AI, and whether that opens the door for the side of the business that first made NVIDIA a household name. For anyone who has tried to buy a graphics card recently, that is a rather important question.

Huang co-founded NVIDIA in 1993 and ran it for more than 33 years, with the move into AI being very much his decision. It has paid off enormously, because data centres now make up roughly 91.5% of the company’s revenue. The flip side is that nobody else has been quite as closely tied to that strategy, which makes for some interesting possibilities for NVIDIA’s future.

Kress has been CFO since 2013 and is a well-respected voice with the market, and eToro’s Josh Gilbert described her as a familiar and trusted presence, whilst adding that a company known for its technical edge might want an engineer in the chair. Analyst Dan Ives expects her stint as interim CEO to last around a year before NVIDIA looks outside. NVIDIA’s company structure is interesting in that it doesn’t make finding a solution any easier: Huang had more than 60 people reporting to him directly.

NVIDIA has no COO, and reports suggest there was no succession plan in place. The internal names analysts have put forward, such as Ian Buck, Bill Dally, Michael Kagan and Bryan Catanzaro, are largely AI and data centre figures, but none of them has been the face of the strategy in the way Huang was. Constellation Research’s Ray Wang pointed out that many of NVIDIA’s partnerships and investments came about because people trusted Jensen.

Which brings me to the hopium. NVIDIA started out making graphics cards for gamers, and CNBC has pointed out that those gamers helped keep the company afloat back in 1999 by buying the GeForce 256. These days they account for under 10% of revenue, and it shows: RTX 50-series production was reportedly cut by up to 40% earlier this year, the Super refresh was reportedly shelved, and the next generation has reportedly drifted towards 2028. Meanwhile, the RTX 5090 launched at $1,999 and third-party sellers have been asking far more than that ever since.

Nvidia DLSS 5 is officially supported in 2K27, but eager fans have found their own way to enable the software in a myriad of titles.

So it isn’t hard to see why some gamers are hoping the board promotes someone with roots on that side of the business. Jeff Fisher, SVP of GeForce, has been at NVIDIA since 1994, but it is obvious that Kress is probably the safer bet for investors. Jonah Alben, who has led GPU engineering since 2008, and co-founder Chris Malachowsky might also throw a hat into this race. It is however important to note that all of this is still very much speculation: nobody at NVIDIA has hinted at a gaming pivot, and whether that will happen with any likeliness will be heavily dependent on other factors.

Truth be told, I wouldn’t expect a new CEO to change much straight away, even one with a soft spot for gaming. The memory shortage squeezing GeForce is affecting the entire industry, with Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron reportedly prioritising AI customers for their high-bandwidth memory, and that won’t disappear because of who is sitting in the corner office.

Whoever takes over will also inherit a company that has just lost its founder and a fair amount of investor confidence, which tends to make boards cautious rather than adventurous. Even so, I’d quite like to see someone in charge who remembers where GeForce came from, and who has some sympathy for the people still trying to buy one. For now, Kress is running both the company and its finances whilst the search for a permanent CEO gets under way.
