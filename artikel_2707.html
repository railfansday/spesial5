Cowboys RG Tyler Booker, CB DaRon Bland to miss several weeks with high ankle sprains

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Though the Dallas Cowboys' defense might be the greatest obstacle to their fading playoff hopes, their offense suffered a challenging blow in Sunday's 46-40 loss to the San Francisco 49ers.

Starting right guard Tyler Booker sustained a high ankle sprain during the second quarter and is expected to miss at least four weeks, according to several reports.

He was seen wearing a walking boot on his left foot at the team facility on Monday. Such an injury usually requires a recovery timeline of four to six weeks, but the Cowboys are hopeful Booker can return in three to four weeks.

The team is also considering whether to put Booker on injured reserve with a possible four-week absence. However, Dallas has a bye for Week 14, which could allow Booker to stay on the active roster.

Starting cornerback DaRon Bland also suffered a right high ankle sprain during the Cowboys' first defensive series while trying to tackle 49ers receiver Deebo Samuel Sr. He had dealt with an injury to his left ankle during the previous week of practice.

As with Booker, the Cowboys are deliberating whether to put Bland on injured reserve. In 10 games, Bland has 7 passes defended, 38 total tackles and 2 tackles for loss.

Booker, 22, missed three games last season with a high ankle sprain, though it's not certain if this injury is to the same ankle. He was the Cowboys' 2025 first-round pick (No. 12 overall) out of Alabama and soon drew attention for saying, "I make guys not love football anymore" during the NFL Scouting Combine.

Fourth-year guard T.J. Bass is expected to replace Booker on the Cowboys' starting offensive line. He has made 14 starts during his NFL career.

"T.J. is a really good football player that stepped in and did a nice job and he's been Mr. Versatility for us," Dallas head coach Brian Schottenheimer told reporters, including the Dallas Morning News' Calvin Watkins. "I don't have any questions when he has to go in there."
