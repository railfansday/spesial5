Game Preview: San Antonio Spurs vs. Los Angeles Clippers

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The win streak was nice to watch while it lasted, but the San Antonio Spurs will look to quickly rebound from their speed bump when hosting the Los Angeles Clippers on Thursday. The Spurs only lost back-to-back games five times last year, and it happened once to start this season.

It hasn't been smooth for the Clippers as they are one of two winless teams on the road (0-5), the other being the Milwaukee Bucks. This season, they are one of the bottom-third passing teams, which makes them predictable and easier to guard without too many players who can bend the defense on the bounce. Their reliance on screen rolls hasn't produced good results either, scoring 0.83 points per possession.

The Spurs have the third-ranked defense, mainly by doing a good job of helping the helper and neutralizing the action in the lane, permitting just 44.9 points. If they are keyed in, not taking the Clippers lightly, and hit the glass as hard as usual, it could be an easy night so the starters don't work too hard and the role players get plenty of burn.

Spurs Injuries: Dylan Harper: out – left hip soreness; Tobias Harris: out – left calf strain; Maliq Brown: out – G League/two-way; Ja'Kobi Gillespie: out – G League/two-way; Jayden Quaintance : out – right knee surgery.

Clippers Injuries: Bradley Beal: out – right knee management; Brandon Ingram: out – right heel surgery; Nick Martinelli: out – G League/two-way; Jordan Miller: out – left rotator cuff surgery.

One of the Clippers' weaknesses is allowing 15.4 open 3-point attempts per game. Notably, the Spurs allow the fewest open 3-pointers (10.7) and are top six in limiting the wide-open attempts, too. Additionally, some of the Spurs' best 3-point shooting nights have come against weak defensive teams, so it wouldn't be unreasonable to expect at least 14 makes.

Brook Lopez is no slouch, having broken 20 points thrice in his last five games, so Victor Wembanyama has to guard him. The idea is not letting him get comfortable when the Clippers run their pick-and-pop. On top of that, this big-man battle will be worth the watch since Lopez is making 80.8 of his attempts at the rim, and Wembanyama is averaging 4.1 blocks. Even if Lopez is stronger, Wemby should be able to take care of him without help, which should help protect the arc.

Stephon Castle will presumably get most of the minutes guarding Darius Garland. This might be a matchup that Castle dominates with his length and strength since Garland is significantly smaller and has been careless with the ball this year, averaging 4.6 turnovers per game.

The Clippers' bench is the second-highest scoring unit in the league (45.8). Despite most of their reserves logging low shooting percentages, the Spurs cannot lose track of Max Strus and Keaton Wagler. Most of the work here will be ensuring that they don't receive too many clean catches.

Wemby put up numbers against the Detroit Pistons, but his efficiency was unremarkable. Lopez is the only player with the size to guard him, but he doesn't want to come out on the perimeter too much, so expect to see open jumpers galore.

Castle or De'Aaron Fox will take on more of the playmaking duties following Dylan Harper's absence caused by nasty slip in the Detroit loss. Each option getting more usage is dependable and should be able to get a step past almost every defender. Considering Julian Champagnie's fit with the main rotation, he will presumably start the game.
