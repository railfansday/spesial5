How a win or loss to the Steelers affects Eagles' 2027 NFL draft

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The Philadelphia Eagles enter Sunday's game against the Pittsburgh Steelers with the No. 5 pick in the projected 2027 NFL draft order. A loss would put Philadelphia at 2-8 and give it a chance to move up. A win would make the Eagles 3-7 and pull them into a larger group of teams competing for position.

Tankathon's draft slots ahead of Indianapolis playing Houston, had Tennessee, the Los Angeles Chargers and Miami at 1-8 in the first three spots, followed by the New York Jets at 2-8 and Philadelphia at 2-7. Philadelphia's strength of schedule was .503 in that snapshot. When teams have the same winning percentage, the team whose opponents have the lower combined winning percentage picks first. That number can change as opponents across the full schedule win or lose.

The Jets' visit to the Chargers is the clearest opportunity for movement. If the Eagles lose and the Jets win, Philadelphia would have a worse record than New York, 2-8 to 3-8, and could move up to No. 4 or better. If the Chargers win, the Jets would stay ahead at 2-9, and the Chargers would join Philadelphia at 2-8. The Eagles had the weaker schedule in Thursday's snapshot, .503 to .528, which would favor Philadelphia if that order holds.

Tennessee at Dallas and Miami at Buffalo also matter. A win by either 1-8 team would put it at 2-8 alongside Philadelphia after an Eagles loss. Tennessee's .481 strength of schedule was lower than Philadelphia's, while Miami's .588 was higher. If those gaps hold, the Eagles would pick after Tennessee and before Miami in a tie.

A win would put Philadelphia behind the Jets by record regardless of what happens in Los Angeles. Indianapolis and Atlanta could also join the 3-7 group, depending on the Colts' Thursday result. On Sunday, an Arizona loss at Kansas City, a Tampa Bay loss at Detroit or a New Orleans loss at Chicago would put each losing team at 3-7. Those results could move the Eagles down from No. 5, with strength of schedule sorting the tie.

Philadelphia's second first-round pick came from Jacksonville in the Jordan Davis trade. The Jaguars enter Sunday's visit to the New York Giants at 7-2. A Giants win would drop Jacksonville to 7-3 and could improve the outlook for that selection. If Jacksonville makes the playoffs, its postseason finish will determine where the pick lands.
