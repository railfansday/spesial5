What Jensen Huang’s return means for Nvidia’s Colette Kress

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Nvidia finance chief Colette Kress led the company for 11 days during Jensen Huang's sudden but brief departure. When Nvidia's board officially reinstated Huang as CEO on Monday, the company also announced that Kress would continue in her role as CFO.

The 59-year-old Kress currently has roughly 4.775 million shares directly and indirectly held, which makes her worth approximately $778 million at Monday's market close.

The 63-year-old Huang resigned earlier this month following revelations of extramarital relationships, including one involving an alleged Chinese spy.

Nvidia stock soared 31% and the Nasdaq surged more than 6% Monday after the company announced that Huang would return as CEO, president and board director, effective immediately. Huang returned to the office Monday to crowds of Nvidia employees cheering his return.

Kress, who stepped in as interim CEO during Nvidia's moment of crisis, joined the chipmaker as finance chief in September 2013 following stints at Cisco, Microsoft and Texas Instruments.

As CFO, Kress oversees the balance sheet of a company aggressively financing many of the infrastructure deals that power the artificial intelligence buildout. Those agreements also raise concerns over circular financing and Nvidia's AI exposure in the event of a slowdown.

In the first earnings call after Huang's resignation, Kress sought to reassure investors, raising the company's fiscal 2028 revenue-growth forecast to roughly 75% from 70%.

She told analysts during a Q&A portion that it was "business as usual" at the company. She later told CNBC's Jim Cramer that customers have stood by the chipmaker and that demand continues increasing.

"The board has determined that they will not reinstate Jensen, and that is our statement and what the board has decided to do," Kress said.

During the company's first all-hands meeting after Huang's resignation, Kress also struck an optimistic tone, telling employees to "move forward" and mentioning pay raises.

But Kress didn't manage to impress many market participants and insiders, who compared the interim chief to Huang. Last week, the stock recorded its worst week and longest weekly losing streak since the 2008 financial crisis as AI stocks plummeted.

"She just wasn't him," The Futurum Group CEO Daniel Newman told CNBC. "I think she would have had to strengthen her bench around her a lot, because you have a visionary, product technology driven CEO that was literally the epicenter of the company. He was the entire identity."

One person CNBC spoke with said Kress handled the situation with "skill and grace" while managing a tough job.

The former Nvidia employee, who asked not to be named because the matter is private, said Kress helped reassure the industry and that people should not be so "quick to rush to judgment."

In the week since Huang's exit, The Wall Street Journal reported that Huang belittled Kress, calling her "a deer in the headlights."

Huang said in the press release announcing his return that he was "grateful" for Kress "leading NVIDIA during this period."

"I think she is a fantastic CFO. I think Jensen probably has great confidence in her. I hope she stays," the former Nvidia employee said. "I don't think that she's going to be on thin ice when Jensen returns."

Nvidia did not immediately respond to comment for this story.
