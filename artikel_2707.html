Bears vs. Lions: Best odds, bets, and parlays

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

On Thanksgiving, people are surrounded by loved ones, enjoying food, and most importantly, watching Detroit Lions football. It's a classic tradition across Michigan, but it doesn't always end well, as the franchise has a losing record on the holiday. This time, though, they aim to make it a happy Thanksgiving with a win over the Chicago Bears, and what better way to do it than with some extra cash coming to you too!

It was another dominant week for me on the bets last week: I went 3-0 on all my straight bets, and if the refs hadn't called some horrible defensive pass interference penalties on the final Lions defensive possession, it would've been a perfect day on the bets. We also hit our first bold bet parlay, so I can't complain too much. Stay with me here as I try to keep the hot streak going, so make room on that plate for these bets.

Here are three wagers to watch in this matchup. The odds are with FanDuel Sportsbook.

Hope you saved room for some offense because this game should have it. In their first battle last year, the Lions dominated the Bears 52-21 in Bears head coach Ben Johnson's first game back in Detroit. Detroit won the season finale, but it wasn't as explosive, 19-16. This year, I expect it to be more of a fight, and both offenses will be humming along.

Detroit enters at 28.8 points per game, while Chicago averages 26.2. Now the Lions defense has been playing better lately, holding their last four opponents to an average of 12.7 points per game, while the Bears defense started strong but has allowed an average of 22 points per game in their last four games. I expect Johnson will want revenge from 2025, and doing so on Thanksgiving will make it that much sweeter. This is the same coach who wants to lead the NFL in all-time scoring offense.

I think this game could be a high-scoring, close contest, with each team putting up at least 30 points. Each team has plenty of weapons, and as a divisional game, the stakes are higher. The winner takes control of the NFC North and, given how contested the division is, could determine how the standings fall come January.

Since I could see this game being high-scoring, I think Lions quarterback Jared Goff will have a dominant performance against the Bears. The reason isn't the Bears defense itself; it's also how Goff has performed against them recently.

Last season, Goff threw for over 300 yards in both games: 334 yards and five touchdowns in their dominant Week 2 victory, and 331 yards in the low-scoring season finale. Despite the Bears' defensive line upgrades, Detroit's offensive line is a big improvement from 2025. If Goff can throw for over 300 with that line, how can he not do it again this year?

What also helps Goff this year is the Bears' inconsistent pass defense. They're either shutting down passing attacks, holding six teams to under 200 yards, or they're letting them air it out, with three games where they've allowed over 300 yards. Goff has thrown for over 300 yards in four games so far this season, and if teams keep paying attention to running back Jahmyr Gibbs, Goff will gladly slice and dice them upfield.

It wasn't just Goff who went off on the Bears last season; wide receiver Amon-Ra St. Brown had some strong performances. In the first game, St. Brown had six catches for 115 yards and three touchdowns, then snagged 11 balls for 139 yards in the last game. St. Brown made it a point to go off against his former coordinator in Johnson, and as long as he's the head coach of the Bears, I think St. Brown will try his best to play well.

He's averaging 84 yards a game this season, so I think hitting the over on this game is a must. Detroit lost its Thanksgiving game last year too, and I don't think St. Brown wants Johnson to rain on the Detroit parade. He will do whatever it takes for Detroit to win this game, and with the offense humming, I wouldn't put it past St. Brown to have another great performance this week.
