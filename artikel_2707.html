The Solar Report, Week 5: Phoenix has escaped the West’s basement

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Week 5 is now behind us, and the Phoenix Suns find themselves sitting at 8-7, slotted into the tenth seed in the Western Conference.

I know it's too early to start putting much stock into conference standings, but I do think there's some merit to examining them because we're already seeing a clear separation develop. There are the top 11 teams in the Western Conference, and then there's everybody else. 4.5 games separate the Warriors at 11 and the Pelicans at 12.

It was important for the Suns to find some stabilization and avoid becoming part of that bottom group, especially considering there's no benefit to finishing near the bottom of the standings. There's no first-round lottery pick waiting for Phoenix at the end of the season. Stabilization was needed, and that's exactly what we received in Week 5.

The Suns not only secured their first road victory of the season, but they rattled off three consecutive wins away from home, sweeping the week and doing so relatively comfortably. Given the schedule ahead, Phoenix has an opportunity to put together a productive November and early December, potentially continuing its climb up the Western Conference standings.

The key to that success will be maintaining momentum and using these opportunities productively. This is a team that can't afford to take nights off, and thus far, they've taken care of business against inferior competition. The Suns are 5-0 against teams below .500 and 3-7 against teams above .500. Those numbers tell us something. Phoenix is doing what it should against teams it's expected to beat, but there's still work to be done when facing quality opponents.

Continue using those games against teams below .500 as opportunities to experiment, but do so responsibly. Too much experimentation can ultimately cost you a victory, while the right amount can help prepare your team for the challenges that await against better competition.

All in all, Week 5 was exactly what the Phoenix Suns needed. After an unbelievably rough start, they've stabilized their record, stabilized their position in the Western Conference, and perhaps most importantly, stabilized our hopes as a fan base.

It was an interesting week for Khaman Maluach as it pertains to the restocks per 36 minutes statistic. After one of his most impactful games against the Nuggets, he was virtually nonexistent from a defensive standpoint against the Kings, which is ultimately what this statistic is designed to measure.

We're not looking at offensive production. We're examining how he impacts the game through rebounding, steals, and blocks, recognizing that those are the areas where he's most likely to earn his minutes. When you're 7'1″ and not a primary component of the offense, you have to make your living filling up the stat sheet on the defensive end. In Week 5, Maluach finished the week averaging 11.0 restocks per 36 minutes.

What did I see from Maluach this week? More of the same, quite honestly. A young player who is still getting his bearings defensively, with moments that offer a glimpse of what he could become and others that remind you where he currently stands in his development.

What did get me excited, however, was what I saw on the offensive end. Maluach averaged 7.7 points in 16.4 minutes per game this past week, which translates to 16.9 points per 36 minutes. What I'm seeing is a team becoming more comfortable with his presence and learning how to get him the ball. More importantly, his hands appeared ready and waiting when he was positioned in the paint when his teammates attacked the basket.

That's been something we've seen him struggle with throughout the season. It feels like there have been countless possessions where a teammate drives into the lane, attempts a wraparound pass through traffic, and Maluach simply isn't prepared to receive it. This week? I felt like he was. His hands were ready, he anticipated those opportunities better, and he did a quality job securing the basketball when it came his way.

When we go big picture and examine how the Phoenix Suns generate their offense, there are plenty of different data points worth exploring. You can look at points per game on field goals in the restricted area, where the Suns rank 21st in the league. You can examine midrange scoring, where Phoenix sits fifth, shooting 43.3% from that area, the eighth-best mark in the NBA.

You can even dig into second-chance opportunities, where the Suns average 3.23 points per game from beyond the arc. They don't generate a ton of those opportunities, and when they do, they're not exactly lighting it up from deep. Phoenix ranks 26th in the league in second-chance three-point scoring, connecting on 29.1% of those attempts. Yeah, there's data for everything.

What I appreciate about this Suns team, however, is how they create offensive opportunities for themselves. One of the primary ways they accomplish that is through screen assists. In other words, they're utilizing the screener to create opportunities for teammates to get shots off.

So, after throwing a smorgasbord of statistics at you this morning, here's my Stat Me of the Week: The Phoenix Suns rank second in the NBA with 8.3 screen assists per game, trailing only the Oklahoma City Thunder.

Who's doing the dirty work? Oso Ighodaro has recorded 41 screen assists this season, averaging 3.2 per game, which ranks ninth in the league. Khaman Maluach isn't far behind with 39, averaging 2.6 per game and ranking 16th. That's particularly impressive when you consider Maluach is averaging only 18.5 minutes per game.

It's another example of why the center position is so important to Phoenix. The Suns rely heavily on their big men to create offensive opportunities, using screens to open up the floor and generate quality looks for their teammates.

And who leads the league in screen assists? Domantas Sabonis. So, for those of you who belong to the club hoping the Suns can somehow pull off a transaction that brings an unhappy Sabonis out of Sacramento and into Phoenix, here's a little more fuel for your fire.

Not only would Sabonis provide the quality rebounding this team desperately needs, but his affinity for setting screens would fit right into what Phoenix is already doing. He's recorded 68 screen assists thus far this season. Essentially, he'd be an upgraded version of Oso Ighodaro.

I'm not advocating for a Sabonis trade. I'm simply pointing out that there are legitimate reasons why some fans have climbed aboard that train.

Week 6 brings us Thanksgiving, a time to spend with family. And sports. Between what the NFL is throwing at us these days, with Wednesday night games and Friday matchups, combined with the NBA schedule, try not to get into any fights with your family over who's hogging the television.

The Week 6 slate begins tonight against the New Orleans Pelicans, who currently sit at 5-13, have lost three consecutive games, and are 1-9 over their last 10. Their only saving grace is that four of their five victories have come at home.

Following that, Phoenix wraps up its five-game road trip with a visit to Houston to take on the Rockets, who sit at 12-5 but have already lost to the Suns once this season. While we might look at that victory as a potential turning point in the Suns' season, Houston probably views it as an outlier. The Rockets shot 38.3% from the field in that matchup, their lowest mark of the season thus far.

Thursday brings a day off before Phoenix returns home Friday for its third NBA Cup game, this time against Cooper Flagg and the Dallas Mavericks. Dallas currently sits one game ahead of Phoenix in the Western Conference standings at 10-7 and has looked impressive under rookie head coach Dusty May. Flagg, now in his second season, is averaging 24.3 points, 6.4 assists, and 6.3 rebounds per game. That should be a good battle.

The week concludes with the Suns heading back to Utah to face the Jazz for the second time in 10 days.

That's it for this week, Bright Siders. Stay frosty.
