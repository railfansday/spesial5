Liverpool’s expensive attack feast on Palace to leave Sage looking doomed

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Crystal Palace are playing this season as if afflicted by a curse. They seem to have made a terrible deal, possibly involving a monkey’s paw. As a result, Pierre Sage’s team were able to defend credibly for around 44 minutes of each half, on one condition: They must switch off completely, at least twice per game.

This being the Premier League, those lapses in concentration are usually punished. A mostly compact performance out of possession briefly became as porous as a bath sponge when Liverpool eased into a 2-0 lead, nine minutes after half-time. Florian Wirtz did not even need to finish cleanly to score his first Premier League goal of the season, striding on to an unopposed low cross from Bradley Barcola and killing the match as a contest.

Major signings need to lay down a marker when arriving at Liverpool. Barcola has his hat-trick at LASK in the Champions League, but we are still waiting for a stirring big-match-defining contribution from him since his summer arrival. Arguably the same could be said for Wirtz, nearly 18 months since he joined.

Neither left Selhurst Park looking like the world-beaters they did occasionally for their previous clubs. Still, one misfiring attacker creating a goal for another represents progress and all great partnerships must start somewhere. Lennon and McCartney would improve after 1958’s Too Bad About Sorrows.

If there was ever a place for the least-productive expensively assembled attacking group in the league to find form it was surely south London on this Sunday lunchtime. Liverpool came in with two defeats in their previous two but, European trophy aside, it has been a lousy 2026 for Palace.

They have the most defeats of any ever-present Premier League team and began (and ended) the weekend with the worst defensive record. They have conceded twice as many goals from headers as any other team. Even their most loyal fan group has disbanded.

Alexis Mac Allister opened the scoring in gut-punching fashion, after a series of Palace near-misses. Chairman Steve Parish rubbed his face in the directors’ box, in the style of peak-stress era Guardiola. None of this looked good for Sage, who appears on the brink of dismissal, with Thomas Frank his most likely replacement.

Asked if he expected to remain in his job on the other side of the international break Sage said he deemed his prospects “better than you, I think”.

“We deserved better today,” the Frenchman said. “But football is not about what you deserve it is about how efficient you are and today we had a lack of efficiency”.

Counter-intuitively, it was Palace’s flashes of promise which suggested the Frenchman might be doomed. There is enough to work with here, and plenty going marginally against Palace to suggest a change in leadership might prompt a turnaround, however unfair it would be on their manager. Yéremy Pino hit the bar, Jorgen Strand Larsen had his composed close-range goal struck from the record by VAR, Daichi Kamada stung Alisson Becker’s palms and the Brazilian saved well again from Adam Wharton’s feathered header.

The other factor which seems likely to condemn Sage is his team’s boneheaded defensive lapses. Liverpool were awarded a debatable free-kick out wide, Quinten Timber let Mac Allister run off him too easily and he duly nodded Dominik Szoboszlai’s delivery past Dean Henderson with ease. Another header conceded.

Mac Allister, formerly of Brighton, endeared himself to the Holmesdale Road Stand with a priggish celebration. There was some blowback from the seats closest, but a more dominant sense of resignation. Someone in the adjoining block took out their phone and snapped a picture. Formerly hated rival scoring against you, rubbing it in with his celebration: truly one to show the grandkids.

Liverpool had put together little of substance until then, with Wirtz drifting through another half, Alexander Isak looking keen but flawed and Barcola spooning a promising shot into low-Earth orbit. The second half was better, but Isak looked to have wasted his best chance, a one-on-one with Henderson thumped too close to the goalkeeper.

Isak had his moment in the end, checking back to finish calmly at the end of a counter-attack against a tiring defence. Eight for the season for him and an assist for Wirtz, too, although it was hardly a pass of Molbyesque majesty.

“Happy for him,” Iraola said of Wirtz, also praising Isak’s movement for the German’s goal. “I think [Wirtz] has been very unlucky in the Premier League, a couple of goals disallowed and situations where he deserves honestly to score.”

So it was Liverpool’s blip righted here, despite all of Palace’s pressure. Alisson, who took some flak for his performance at Fenerbahce, was hard to fault. He may not have reached Eddie Nketiah’s stoppage-time penalty, but made a series of brave saves and deserved a clean sheet. For all that attacking talent, the long-serving goalkeeper still looks like one of Iraola’s most important players.
