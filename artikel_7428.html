On OnlyFans, one-time transactions are winning out over subscriptions

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Back in August, OnlyFans announced that 5,076 creators have brought in at least $1 million each since the NSFW hub kicked off in 2016, with total creator payouts adding up to $30 billion. About 20% of that $30 billion—roughly $6.2 billion—was delivered to creators during the year that ended on November 30, 2025.

That last figure represents a solid increase in how much creators are making on OnlyFans—but the way they’re making money on the platform has evolved in recent years, too.

According to a recent report from Giggster, the vast majority of the $6.2 billion paid to OnlyFans creators in 2025 stemmed from non-subscription-based transactions. To compile its report (titled “Behind the OnlyFans Paycheck: Creator Payments, Platform Fees and U.S. Rankings”), the online marketplace combined “illustrative payment calculations with third-party estimates of OnlyFans creator accounts across all 50 states and Washington, D.C.” and examined “company-level revenue by payment type and revenue attributed to the U.S.”

Overall, the report estimated that 700,000 OnlyFans creator accounts are active in the U.S., a number that makes up roughly 28% of the platform’s global total. And despite representing less than 30% of OnlyFans accounts, Giggster found that ~62% of OnlyFans’ platform revenue ($965.4 million) was attributed to the U.S. in the 2025 fiscal year.

One of the report’s most significant findings, however, relates to the rising importance of non-subscription-based revenue—aka payments from one-time transactions like tips, paywalled posts, paid direct messages, and custom content requests. According to Giggster, those payments generated 73% of OnlyFans’ platform revenue in the 2025 fiscal year (up from roughly 40% in 2020 and 60% in 2024).

OnlyFans isn’t the only creator-driven platform leaning into one-time payments, either. In a 2024 end-of-year post, Patreon noted that creators on its platform “increased monthly revenue of one-time purchases by 4x” year-over-year. More recently, a study from Ipsos commissioned by TikTok predicted that over 60,000 U.S.-based TikTok LIVE creators would make “more than the median part-time monthly income” from gift earnings in 2025.

YouTube has devoted significant resources to one-time transactions, too. In a January blog post, CEO Neal Mohan reassured creators that the video platform would “continue to invest” in “fan funding features like Jewels and gifts,” adding that “we know these are important revenue streams, especially outside the U.S., for creators like Santiago Matias, who uses Super Chat as a way for fans to influence the outcome of his hit reality series.”

At the end of the day, the rising importance of one-time transactions demonstrates an important truth for creators: if you want to earn more revenue, give fans more ways to pay you.
