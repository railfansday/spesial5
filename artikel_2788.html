Chiefs News: Sunday is Travis Kelce’s 100th game at Arrowhead Stadium

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Kansas City Chiefs have a lot of history with their home venue since 1972: Arrowhead Stadium. It has seen heartbreak and triumph over the years, and many loyal fans in Chiefs Kingdom have sat in the crowd over 100 times.

However, only three Chiefs players have ever played in 100 regular-season games at Arrowhead — and tight end Travis Kelce is slated to be the fourth this Sunday.

Kansas City Chiefs tight end Travis Kelce's top moments at Arrowhead Stadium | ESPN

"You come through the facility, and you just see Arrowhead [Stadium] pop up out of nowhere," Kelce said recently. "That's the best feeling for me."

Few players in the Chiefs' 67-year history have entered Arrowhead to put on the team's red jersey, helmet and cleats as many times as Kelce. On Sunday, against the Arizona Cardinals (1 p.m. ET, CBS), Kelce will become only the fourth player in franchise history — joining Hall of Fame right guard Will Shields, kicker Nick Lowery and punter Dustin Colquitt — to play 100 regular-season games at Arrowhead, a venue that opened in 1972. Kelce considers Arrowhead a revered place, even before the venue was the site where his relationship with Taylor Swift, his wife, became public.

Chiefs terminate veteran LB, two-time Super Bowl champion in Week 11 | Chiefs Wire

The Kansas City Chiefs terminated the practice squad contract of veteran linebacker Cole Christiansen on November 18.

Christiansen started his career as an undrafted free agent with the Los Angeles Chargers in 2020 and signed with Kansas City in 2022.

A member of the Chiefs' Super Bowl LVII and LVIII rosters, Christiansen exclusively saw action as a special-teamer for Kansas City and Los Angeles, logging a total of 399 NFL snaps without a single defensive down played.

Diego Pavia, Heisman finalist and former Vanderbilt star, picked No. 1 in UFL draft | The Athletic

The Columbus Aviators picked the 24-year-old Pavia after his brief offseason stint in the NFL. Pavia went undrafted in April but later signed a three-year deal with the Baltimore Ravens. The Ravens, who have played four quarterbacks this season, waived Pavia in July to make room for center Ethan Pocic.

At Vanderbilt, Pavia led the Commodores to their first 10-win season in 2025 and became the school's first Heisman Trophy finalist. He finished second in voting to Indiana's Fernando Mendoza, the eventual No. 1 pick in the 2026 NFL Draft.

Cowboys' Mike Pettine to take over defensive play-calling duties from DC Christian Parker | NFL.com

Pettine has been a coordinator with the New York Jets, Buffalo Bills and Green Bay Packers. He also served as head coach of the Cleveland Browns from 2014 to 2015.

The Cowboys will keep the 34-year-old Parker in the defensive coordinator role, but hope putting play-calling in the hands of the more experienced Pettine will help turn around a defense that has been torched in 2026.

Parker is "a critical piece of what we're trying to do as we search for answers and solutions," Cowboys coach Brian Schottenheimer said Wednesday, confirming the decision. "I think he has an incredibly bright future not just this year but beyond with us.

Cam Skattebo says he is retiring his backflip celebration after what happened to Aidan Hutchinson | Yahoo! Sports

"It's hard to see," Skattebo told a couple of reporters after Monday's practice. "I'm saying yeah (no more). Mentally, I'm like, 'I'm not doing that.' That ain't going to happen to me."

Skattebo has become known for his backflip celebration. Andy Reid and Chiefs will be cautious with Kenneth Walker this week

It would feel shortsighted for the Chiefs to put Walker on the field against the (3-7) Arizona Cardinals in a game where Kansas City is favored by 15.5 points, according to FanDuel Sportsbook. Four days after that matchup, the Chiefs will be on the road to play their AFC rivals, the (8-1) Buffalo Bills, in a pivotal Thanksgiving night game.

Reid acknowledged that the quick turnaround could factor into Walker's availability this weekend.

"That goes into the equation," Reid said about the coming short week. "I'm not saying that's the answer, but that goes into [it]. You look at the distance between games, you look at what the symptoms are and where he's at."

"If he's not going to further injure it, then he'll go play," Reid stated. "If he can, if it looks like he's on the edge there, then he doesn't play. Keep it simple."
