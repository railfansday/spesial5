Luka Doncic versus Victor Wembanyama: Buckle up for the MVP race

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

In one corner, there's the 6-foot-8 Slovenian superstar Luka Doncic who recently surpassed Michael Jordan for the best start in modern NBA history.

In the other, there's the 7-foot-4 Victor Wembanyama who's doing things on a basketball court that have never been done.

It's Luka Magic versus The Alien. It's offensive wizardry versus an inhuman skill set. It's a guy who uses pace and brakes to make himself unguardable versus a guy who seemingly flies above his competition.

And they're about to meet for the first time this season when the Lakers play the Spurs in San Antonio on Friday night.

Both of them would call this just another game. They'd be lying.

In tight MVP races, head-to-head contests are heavily weighed. This is a measuring stick game. There are implications that could resonate months later when awards voters make their decisions.

Doncic has been on a revenge tour since the Mavericks stunningly dealt him to the Lakers in February 2024, marking the first time in modern day NBA history that a player of his caliber in his prime was traded against his will.

Luka Doncic of the Los Angeles Lakers shoots a free throw against the Utah Jazz in Los Angeles on Monday, Nov. 23, 2026. NBAE via Getty Images

Then there's Wembanyama, who's widely considered the next face of the league but ascended faster than anyone anticipated, carrying the Spurs to the 2026 NBA Finals. Once they arrived, his team unraveled.

The Spurs blew double-digit leads in all four of their losses to the Knicks, punctuated by allowing New York to claw its way back from a 29-point deficit in Game 4, the largest comeback in Finals history.

Wembanyama blamed himself. He was demoralized. Crestfallen.

Both Doncic and Wembanyama want to change the narrative. They each want to be MVP. They know they have to get through each other to accomplish their goal.

The 27-year-old Doncic has never finished higher than third in MVP award voting despite leading the league in scoring twice and carrying the Mavericks to the Finals in 2024.

The 22-year-old Wembanyama became the first ever unanimous Defensive Player of the Year and finished second in last year's MVP race behind Shai Gilgeous-Alexander.

San Antonio Spurs center Victor Wembanyama walks up court during an NBA basketball game against the New Orleans Pelicans on Sunday, Nov. 22, 2026, in San Antonio. AP Photo/Darren Abate

Doncic is averaging a league-leading 36.6 points, 6.6 rebounds, 7.7 assists and 1.8 steals per game for the Lakers.

Wembanyama is averaging 29.9 points, 12.3 rebounds, 3.2 assists and 3.9 blocked shots for the Spurs.

Their teams have the two best records in the league, with LA 15-3 and San Antonio 15-4.

Doncic and Wembanyama are the it guys. They're dominating headlines. Dropping jaws. Taking over the narrative.

Doncic scored 40 or more points in four straight games. Wembanyama had a 50-point, 18-rebound and five-block performance against the Pelicans on Nov. 21. They're both putting up video game numbers.

Doncic all but refuses to advocate for himself for MVP. Wembanyama made the case for himself last season as though he were an attorney arguing in front of a jury.

Luka Doncic of the Los Angeles Lakers runs out before a game against the Utah Jazz on Monday, Nov. 23, 2026, at Crypto.com Arena in Los Angeles. NBAE via Getty Images

But what Doncic won't say in words he has made clear with his actions. He wants this. Bad. He's playing more defense than ever in an attempt to dispel the negative narratives around him. He has already taken four charges this season.

Wembanyama wants this just as bad. And he's taking a different tack. He's leaning into the negative chatter. He turned into a villain (at least in New York) during the Finals. Instead of balking at that label, he told reporters he's choosing to view it as "a great sign of respect."

Both players have unique things fueling them and completely different styles, but they also have something very important in common.

They're sprinting next to each other for the most prestigious individual award in the sport.

And on Friday, we'll get our first glimpse of who's really in the lead.
