The 6 Best Smart Thermostats to Keep Your House Comfortable and Save You Money

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Get more efficient heating and cooling with these set-it-and-forget-it models.

Just like Wi-Fi-enabled lights and plugs and locks, smart thermostats give you the ability to automate an element of your home for increased convenience. In this case, it’s your temperature controls. These devices tend to be a bit pricier than conventional thermostats, but it’s an upgrade that can pay for itself over time thanks to increased energy efficiency.

Using a companion app on your phone, you get intuitive and nuanced controls, including the ability to customize temperature schedules remotely. That comes in handy when, let’s say, you want to warm up the house earlier than usual if you head home from work early. You’re also able to track your usage with monthly energy reports, which can help you bring down your bills.

For the past few months, my colleagues and I have been steadily testing smart thermostats throughout in our homes, including models from Google Nest, ecobee, and Honeywell Home. These are the models we tried, plus a few more we recommend based on our research and reviews. We’ve also included some expert advice on what to consider before you buy.

The ecobee covers the standard lineup of smart thermostat features, with climate control and money-saving that are commonplace among any of the top options, but sets itself apart with a wider range of smart features.

It doubles as a security base for your home security, serving as the arming and disarming mechanism. It also features a built-in air quality monitor that alerts you to poor air quality and sends reminders when you need to replace air filters in your furnace.

The ecobee comes with a separate smart sensor for precision control, allowing it to fine-tune the temperature in the rooms where you spend the most time. Though it comes with a single sensor, you can purchase more to add that precision throughout the house.

It also provides free Eco+ Software which works on your smartphone and generates energy reports that further maximize your energy savings.

The smart thermostat also comes with a host of standard smart features. After connecting to a Google assistant or Alexa, the smart speaker built directly on the thermostat itself can take voice commands and even play music. It also comes with a three-year warranty from date of purchase.

Each purchase comes with a full converter kit as well, so if you aren’t currently in a home with a C-Wire, you can add one by following the kit instructions.

For a smart thermostat that better fits a tight budget, Amazon has its own option that comes with many of the same energy-saving features at a lower overall price tag. Through the connected app, the thermostat tracks and reports your energy usage and peak time periods throughout the week and month.

Through Alexa, you can also set home, away, and sleep temperatures that you can schedule throughout the day and week in order to further maximize savings on your energy bill and optimize your air conditioning to your exact schedule. You can also set the temperature remotely from your phone through the Alexa app.

Beyond the controls, by using an Amazon product, the company will pass along potential rebate and saving opportunities that you could take advantage of by owning a smart thermostat.

As for upfront costs, the device is designed specifically for easy DIY installation so there is no need to hire an HVAC technician unless you want one.

The Honeywell T9 is a great choice if your primary concern is reducing energy use and lowering your bills. You need to pair it with one of Honeywell’s smart room sensors to take advantage of the thermostat’s full functionality, which is an extra hurdle, but the sensor only costs about $30, so it’s a short one.

One of the key ways the T9 saves money is through virtual fencing. Using your phone’s GPS, the thermostat automatically switches between home and away mode as you come and go. So, when you leave your house, energy usage is reduced across the board.

Honeywell also provides monthly usage reports that point out areas where energy can be reduced for a lower upfront cost. Adding more room sensors can increase the accuracy of those reports, giving you more chances to optimize your heating and cooling system.

The Honeywell T9 doesn’t have the same wide spectrum of features as our other recommendations, but it’s core functionality is very solid.

The newest generation of the Google Nest Thermostat has impressed our team as an easy-to-install, easy-to-use option that reduces energy use and is compatible with most HVAC systems. Like previous models, the smart thermostat builds a schedule, learns your temperature preferences, and reduces energy when it isn’t needed.

Beyond the basic features, Perez also pointed out how much he likes the actual display and design of the thermostat. “Sure, other thermostats and window a/c units have similar smart features, but they don’t blend into my home the way the Google Nest thermostat does. I genuinely enjoy looking at it. I light up when I pass by it.”

It’s a versatile option, compatible with multiple smart home assistants. Like other models, it’s Energy Star-certified, which means that using one can help you save even more money through local rebates.

While the smart thermostat is great for easy scheduling and reading, it lacks the learning software of the Google Nest or the virtual fencing adjustments that many other smart thermostats have. That said, it’s also considerably cheaper.

Most smart thermostats aren’t compatible with baseboard heating and cooling systems. This Mysa unit is the rare exception, and designed specifically to work with electric baseboards. Combine that with compatibility across every major smart home system, and the Mysa becomes one of very few thermostats with such capabilities.

While the compatibility alone makes this a tempting choice, it also has many of the same high-end smart features as the competition. This includes virtual fencing, so when you head out for the day, the thermostat automatically reduces energy use.

Another key feature is its temperature monitor. Unlike many other devices, which only provide you with degrees, the Mysa smart thermostat also measures and reports humidity levels.

Traditional thermostats only require power through the four classic wires while smart thermostats typically require low- voltage power all day in order to run the display screen and some basic functions. This low-voltage power comes from an additional fifth wire called the “common” wire or C-Wire. The biggest potential issue you may have with installation is if your home lacks this wire.

If this is the case, it’s probably time to step away from your weekend home project and call an HVAC technician. While installing a common wire isn’t an incredibly difficult undertaking if you’re somewhat handy, it’s one that likely requires the expertise of a trained professional.

Some smart thermostats include installation kits that provide equipment and step-by-step instructions for adding a C-Wire to your current wiring. This can be a helpful addition to the handier among us, and may circumvent the need for an HVAC technician entirely, though you may want to keep one waiting in the wings if anything goes wrong.

Once you've wired and installed your smart thermostat, you can connect it to other devices in your home via Wi-Fi or occasionally Z-Wave. Most units are compatible with many major smart home ecosystems. While they’re compatible with many systems, I would still take a moment to check and make sure that a thermostat works specifically with the smart home setup you use at home.

Along with scheduling heating or cooling programs from the convenience of your phone, there are a handful of features that set smart thermostats apart from their traditional counterparts. Most smart models automatically adjust the temperature based on peak usage and some automatically respond to outdoor temperatures and set ideal temperatures indoors.

Other models use geofencing, or virtual fencing technology. This creates a radius around your home and detects how close or far away your smartphone is from it. With this, the smart thermostat eases energy use when you aren’t home, and ramps back up as you reenter the virtual boundary.

Several smart thermostats we cover use external sensors to expand the reach of temperature readings. Setup is typically no more complicated than placing the sensors around the house and syncing them up with the rest of the system.

They’re great for maintaining different temperatures in rooms, like keeping bedrooms and bathrooms warm and offices and laundry rooms cool. More precise temperature control also increases the potential for saving energy. Smart thermostats that include sensors as part of their systems often come with one, though some models require purchasing sensors separately.

We gave them our seal of approval based on ease-of-use, installation process, programmable features, and how well the devices integrated with our smart home assistants, mobile devices, and tablets.

We also prioritized options that were highly compatible with other smart devices and offered convenient features for everyday use. Price rice was a major consideration, as well: We chose models ranging from roughly $160 to $250, ensuring that we recommend a range of options that fit every need and budget.
