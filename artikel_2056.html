Louie Barry drops 'unreal' claim in message that Sheffield Wednesday fans must love

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Bethany Patrick is a football writer based in Manchester, UK.

Her work focuses on the EFL, with particular interest in football governance, supporter culture, and fan experience and access.

Bethany is a director of the award-winning Sheffield Wednesday Women’s Supporters Group, where she works on fan engagement, strategy and campaigns for greater inclusion across the game.

Despite Sheffield Wednesday’s injury crisis, the Owls were able to welcome back a fan favourite on Saturday.

Louie Barry returned as a second half substitute in Wednesday’s 3-0 win over Plymouth Argyle.

The winger - who joined on a permanent deal from Aston Villa in the summer - had endured two months on the sidelines after an ankle injury at Wycombe Wanderers at the beginning of September.

Barry was understandably pleased to be back among the action, taking to Instagram after the final whistle to share his excitement.

“Unreal feeling to be back playing in front of you fans at Hillsborough after a couple months out,” he wrote on his story.

His return represents a timely boost for an injury-hit Wednesday squad.

Barry had made an immediate impression following his arrival, scoring three goals and providing an assist in his first four league appearances under Henrik Pedersen.

For a side that has struggled for consistency and attacking options in recent weeks, having a player of his directness and quality available again could make a considerable difference.

Barry's comeback was not the only reason for optimism on Saturday. Challinor's first League One victory also offered some early indications of how he intends to shape this Wednesday side.

The Owls were far from convincing in the opening 45 minutes, with Plymouth creating the better opportunities.

Mitchell's deflected opener, arriving just before half-time, gave the hosts a lead their performance had not entirely warranted.

The second half was a different proposition. Wednesday looked more competitive, imposed themselves physically and began to find greater purpose in possession.

Further goals from Davis Keillor-Dunn and Di'Shon Bernard gave the scoreline a convincing feel, even if Challinor was quick to acknowledge the shortcomings in his side's performance.

Saturday suggested Challinor’s principles are beginning to take hold. With several senior players still unavailable, a three-goal victory against promotion rivals was an encouraging starting point.

For Barry, the timing of Challinor's arrival could prove particularly significant.

The pair already have a successful working relationship, having enjoyed an impressive spell together at Stockport County - Barry scored 29 goals in 56 appearances under the Wednesday manager.

That previous understanding offers an intriguing prospect for the Owls.

Challinor knows how to utilise Barry's strengths, while the winger has already demonstrated that he can make an impact in League One.

There is little reason to believe that his latest manager will be unable to get even more out of him.

However, of course, there is a need for patience. Barry has only just returned from injury, and Wednesday will be keen to avoid rushing him back into regular action.

But with a manager who has already brought the best out of him, there is genuine cause for excitement.

Saturday's cameo was only a first step, but the prospect of seeing the pair reunited at Hillsborough could yet become one of the more important developments of Wednesday's season.
