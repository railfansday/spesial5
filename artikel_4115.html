GTA 6 Doesn’t Have The Internet Because It Wasn’t “Necessary”

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Grand Theft Auto games are time capsules, and I'm not just talking about the anachronistic entries like Vice City that unravel the glitz and seedy glamour of '80s Miami movies.

Between sending mob bosses to swim with the fish and bowling with his cousin in GTA 4, Niko Bellic visits an internet café to chat with women online. The entire interface is a relic of early naughties forums and crude online shopping, something that GTA 5 would iterate on and bring to mobile. Part of making the game world feel real meant tackling the online world, and Rockstar understood that—right down to emailing, an infamous LCPD sting operation, and in-game news sites. GTA 6, however, is bucking that trend, getting rid of the internet entirely for something much more 2026.

In an interview with Kotaku ahead of the game's launch, Rockstar explained why it removed the internet, and why in-game social media was a better fit for Leonida; "It didn't feel necessary for us," head of development Rob Nelson said. "We definitely wanted to lean into apps and create these apps."

Nelson assured that just because GTA 6 doesn't have an in-game internet to browse, that doesn't mean the idea won't return in the future—and Rockstar has hinted that sequels won't take nearly as long to get out the door. But for Leonida, the team wanted players to experience the "meaningful stuff" by focusing on the open world, rather than navigating an interface on their phones. They wanted you to fixate on "doing Grand Theft Auto," engaging with the world's economics and systems instead of mindlessly online shopping for lavish cars.

It's taken approximately 35 minutes for GTA 6 to start breaking records.

"They don't really need websites to be buying a lot of sh*t, you know what I mean, and so everything grows very organically," he continued. "It was never a top-down, 'We're not going to have websites.' I remember asking Stu Petri, our head of 2D and UI, about websites, and he was like, 'For what? What do you need it for?' So, that was it."

Jason and Lucia won't bumble into a sting operation, and you can't order an Oppressor from your phone, but the in-game social media app Snapmatic—Rockstar's answer to Instagram. It has entire feeds with actual pages and posts, so a lot of work has gone into making the online side of GTA 6's world feel as alive and realistic as past entries, just with the focus shifted entirely to social media instead of car dealerships and pyramid schemes.

The most anticipated video game of all time is a triumph on every level that exceeds years of unbelievable expectations. You're not ready for Leonida.
