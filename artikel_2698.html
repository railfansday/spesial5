NBA Game Preview: Timberwolves vs Blazers Thread

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Julius Randle returns to the Target Center in his first game against his former team. The storyline of giddiness vs moodiness has been writ large over this whole season and Randle has been pointed to as the source of the latter. This is one of two games that the Nets will be showcased on national TV as they expect a Randle revenge performance.

Given how Brooklyn has rallied around Randle, which is to say, they haven't, this is a game the Wolves should demonstrate the power of positive thinking, or at least consistent effort.

Who: Minnesota Timberwolves (12-5) vs. Brooklyn Nets (3-12)

When: Monday, November 23rd at 6:30 PM CT

Some of the stats above are the usual suspects when comparing teams. Others are a bit cherry picked for the sake of comparing these teams. The Nets are bad. They had five draft picks last year and only three of them are getting playtime.

They are young or disinterested in playing defense. Noah Clowney is the one of few players bringing energy but that can quickly bubble over into earning a technical. Conspiratorial podcaster Michael Porter Jr. is close to, if not their best player and he has never been known to be much of a deterrent. We know that Julius Randle, when dialed in, can be a bulldog on defense. So far this season he has been mostly checked out and only looking for his shot.

The Wolves offense, while backsliding in rank over the past few games, should make mincemeat of this Nets squad. I half-expect the Nets to put Porter Jr. on Jaden McDaniels. MPJ has all the physical tools to hound McDaniels and none of the want-to. The rest of the Wolves should be looking to feed Jaden when he cuts, which should be often.

The Nets do offer a bit of size. Their frontcourt starters of Randle, Porter Jr. Day'Ron Sharpe, and Danny Wolf are all 6'9" and bigger, not to mention they bring one of my least favorite players Mo Wagner off the bench (if Mo so much as lays a finger on Joan Beringer, I riot.) And yet, Brooklyn are bottom in the league at rebounding. This is a team loaded with talent and little want to. Nets head coach Jordi Fernández has his work cut out for him helping the team's youthful rebuild while managing his vets bubbling tempers or conspiracy theories.

The Wolves, who traded away the "size" of Randle and Naz Reid, have been a top rebounding team this season mostly for the fact that when the ball is loose, they are scrambling for it.

To tie this into one of my favorite quotes from Minnesota Lynx head coach Cheryl Reeve on KFAN a number of years ago:

2. Defensive Rebounding: If you are willing to put in the work to make the opponent miss, then finish the play.

In his two years in Minnesota, Julius Randle was instrumental in making deep playoff runs. His game was not pretty. Some offensive possessions he seemed to spend whole shot clock drilling for oil under Target Center, using the ball as his shovel. He was able to use his size to wear down defenders before barreling to the rim, pulling away for a mid-range two, or dishing out to a cutting shooter beyond the arc.

As a point-forward, he offered the Wolves a much needed dimension when Anthony Edwards was out. There are few defenders in the league that can stifle Randle one-on-one on the block. He rarely shrank from the moment, though on several occasions, he missed the moment all together.

His defense would morph from bodying up his man to full matador, sometimes in the same game. If his matchup could hit a three-point shot (thinking about some of those Jaren Jackson Jr. games) his closeouts were always lackluster. Those burly shoulders would shrink when he didn't get the ball and he'd jog back to the other side of the court on a fastbreak. His replacement, Jonathan Kuminga, has been the polar opposite.

Some of that is due to role. Randle was asked to be the engine of an offense. JK has been asked to be the oil that allows the engine to run smoothly. Still, Kuminga has offered up defensive resistance against the best forwards in the game no matter where the attack is coming from. He gets his shots in the flow of the offense and sprints back to defend regardless if he touched the ball or not. What it really comes down to is consistency and that is where Randle's struggles truly lay.

He is an All-NBA talent on any night of the week, just not every night of the week.

Fingers crossed that the Wolves do not come out overconfident like their game against the Bulls. The Randle element should likely add some fire to this matchup and if the Wolves start hot, they should run laps around the Nets. Randle will likely have some move against Rudy or a full shot clock burning possession before he spins for a pull-up jumper. He will jaw at the bench, and the Wolves lead will be shrunk to 24. Maybe Ben Saraf and Drake Powell will get some burn by the end.
