49ers game review: Nick Bosa in career-worst drought. How bad did it look in Dallas?

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Nick Bosa's bull rush remains bruising. Just ask Terence Steele.

In the second quarter Sunday, Bosa, the San Francisco 49ers' All-Pro pass rusher, extended his hands into Steele's chest and drove the Cowboys' 6-foot-6, 320-pound right tackle back into the lap of quarterback Dak Prescott.

As Prescott threw, targeting wideout CeeDee Lamb on a slant, Bosa hit Prescott's throwing arm, causing the ball to sail into the breadbasket of linebacker Dre Greenlaw, who dropped the gift-wrapped potential interception in the 49ers' 46-40 win.

It was a vintage Bosa. For many of his other 41 pass-rush snaps: Invisible Bosa?

Bosa earned the lowest Pro Football Grade (40.8) of his 89-game career in a game in which he didn't have a sack and the aforementioned play was his lone QB hit. Bosa, who missed five games with a calf injury, doesn't have a sack in four games he has played, the only time during his career he has gone sack-less in four straight games in the same season.

Bosa has two QB hits in 109 pass-rush snaps this season and his lack of stats is inspiring questions about whether his medical file is a contributing factor.

In September 2025, the ACL in Bosa's right knee was torn for the second time. The Chronicle spoke with two orthopedic surgeons in August about a second ACL surgery on the same knee, what they termed a "revision ACL reconstruction," and both said players often don't return to normal pre-surgery performance for 12 to 18 months.

After reviewing Bosa's snaps Sunday against the Cowboys, however, some context is required for a performance that wasn't up to his standards, but also didn't suggest he's a shell of himself.

Bosa did have four QB pressures - he twice had quick wins against Steele that forced Prescott to scramble - and the Cowboys devoted an extra blocker to Bosa on nine of his 41-pass rush snaps, using a tight end or running back to deliver a chip block on six plays. In the third quarter, two series after Bosa hit Prescott's arm in mid-throw, Bosa beat Steele with a speed rush, forcing Prescott to step up in the pocket before he fired a deep incompletion on 3rd-and-6.

A contributing factor to Bosa's relatively silent Sunday was the 49ers' shredded secondary, which routinely failed to hold up in coverage. As a result, Prescott rarely had to hold onto the ball for a few extra beats in a game in which he completed 30 of 51 passes for 343 yards with five touchdowns and an interception and was sacked only once.

Bosa had three offsides penalties, which was a factor in his career-low PFF grade. His pass-rush grade (60.4) was the 13th-lowest of his career.

The verdict? We obviously didn't witness the best of Bosa, but he wasn't far removed from landing a sack Sunday that would have quieted much of the noise. In fact, if Greenlaw hadn't dropped the would-be interception at the Cowboys' 45-yard line, Bosa would have had the 49ers' defensive play of the day.

But the play didn't result in a takeaway. And Bosa is still without a sack. A price of his $170 million contract is that the criticism, some of which may be unfair, will mount as long the drought continues.

QB Brock Purdy's flee-the-pocket, 74-yard touchdown toss to running back Christian McCaffrey was his most spectacular off-schedule play. Maybe? Purdy had two other great-escape completions while throwing for 135 yards on scrambles, matching the most in the NFL since 2018, according to Next Gen Stats.

In the third quarter, on 2nd-and-12, Purdy dashed to his right out of a fast-crumbling pocket, pointed to wide receiver Jordan Watkins in mid-scramble, seemingly directing him to run to the sideline, and delivered a 27-yard strike.

In the second quarter, on 3rd-and-12, Purdy escaped the grasp of All-Pro defensive tackle Quinnen Williams, darted to his left with three defenders in pursuit and fired an on-the-run, 23-yard completion to wideout Keenan Allen.

Fox play-by-play man Kevin Burkhardt: "… Running for his life, throws across his body and …"

Purdy couldn't always escape. He was dropped for five sacks and took a hellacious hit in the third quarter when he was sandwiched by linebacker Dee Winters and Jaishawn Barham. Purdy remained on his back for about five seconds and walked around to collect himself after slowly rising.

"There were a couple times it seemed like he was hurt," head coach Kyle Shanahan said. "He never told me that anything was wrong, so I'm assuming not. But he took some hits in that game."

The primary reason for Purdy's punishment: Connor Colby.

The second-year left guard had a woeful PFF pass-blocking grade (21.4) while often lined up against Williams. Not a fair fight. Williams beat Colby for a second-quarter sack and easily blew past him again later in the period, forcing Purdy to step up in the pocket and get dropped for a sack by defensive tackle LT Overton. Purdy absorbed the linebacker sandwich after Barham beat Colby.

The early returns on the Allen trade: positive. And they could be even better if Purdy didn't appear to misfire on two passes - 14 and 23 yards downfield, respectively - in which the just-acquired wide receiver was wide open over the middle.

Allen caught five passes for 61 yards on seven targets. If not for the off-target throws: seven catches for more than 100 yards on seven targets.

Shanahan said Allen was also open in the end zone in the second quarter when Williams dropped Purdy.

"Missed him in the red zone on a possible touchdown when we got sacked," Shanahan said.

With the 49ers leading 46-40 with 42 seconds left, it was notable that rookie running back Kaelon Black was on the field instead of McCaffrey and had the first two carries of the possession as the 49ers tried to run out the clock.

It will be interesting to see if that happens the next time the 49ers are in a similar situation.

On his second carry, Black fumbled after Williams ripped the ball from his grasp, but Black avoided a potential disaster by recovering the fumble. Not surprisingly, McCaffrey replaced Black on the next snap.

Black also nearly fumbled at the end of a reception earlier in the game, but he was ruled down before the ball popped up in the air after it was ripped away (Black caught it while on his back).

The Cowboys appeared to have made note that Black lost a fumble in a Week 7 loss at Atlanta. On Black's 25-yard touchdown run in which he bulldozed safety Caleb Downs at the 22-yard line, cornerback Cobie Durant made no attempt to tackle Black at the 12-yard line, swiping at the ball instead.

Speaking of fumbles: Linebacker Fred Warner clearly forced a fumble in the third quarter when he punched the ball from running back Javonte Williams' grasp, but Williams was ruled down. The 49ers didn't challenge the play because it wasn't clear who made the recovery.Warner indicated the 49ers would request a stat correction.

"It felt clean on the field," Warner said. "We'll see. We'll turn it in."

Warner is tied for the NFL lead with five forced fumbles and, if he's awarded another from Sunday's game, he'll remain on pace to break the league record of 10 held by Osi Umenyiora (2010) and Charles Tillman (2012).

"Just got to keep punching," Warner said.
