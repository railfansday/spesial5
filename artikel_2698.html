Do Cincinnati Bengals have enough to win Super Bowl? | Williams

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Ask columnist Jason Williams anything − sports or non-sports – and he'll pick some of your questions and comments from his inbox and respond on Cincinnati.com. Email: jwilliams@enquirer.com

Message: What a game (Nov. 15)! There's a lot of Bengals Super Bowl talk going on. I still wonder if the Bengals can overtake the Jaguars, Chiefs and Bills and win the AFC. All those teams made moves at the trade deadline. Do the Bengals have enough to win the Super Bowl?

Reply: The Bengals have proven the last two weeks that they should be considered a Super Bowl contender. But there's a difference between being a contender and actually winning it.

The Bengals simply aren't good enough on defense to win the Lombardi Trophy.

This is not to throw a wet blanket on how well the Bengals are playing right now and all the excitement about them around town. Last weekend's dismantling of Pittsburgh was a thing of beauty, and the performance certainly reiterated that the Bengals have the most potent passing attack in the league.

What really helped the Bengals is their offense also was their best defense. The Bengals held onto the ball for all but 8 minutes of the first half. But the Bengals don't run the ball enough to consistently control the clock, so playing keep-away likely isn't a sustainable strategy.

There's a reason Bengals player personnel director Duke Tobin tried (and failed) to make a move to upgrade the defense at the trade deadline. You know, there's something to that defense-wins-championships cliche.

Look at the last three Super Bowl champions: Kansas City (2023 season), Philadelphia (2024) and Seattle (2025). The Chiefs and Eagles ranked No. 2 in fewest points allowed in those championship seasons. The Seahawks only gave up 17.2 points per game last season, tops in the NFL.

The Bengals rank a respectable 10th in the league in that category, giving up 21.4 points per game. But let's not forget the Bengals have benefitted from one of the weakest schedules in the league. Only one of their wins has been against a team with a winning record (Pittsburgh on Nov. 15). And five wins have come against teams that rank in the bottom half of the league in total offense.

Let's see how the Bengals play on Dec. 13 against Kansas City at Paycor Stadium. That game could be a barometer for gauging the Bengals' chances of winning a championship. The Chiefs (7-2) are ranked second in the league in total offense and have a potent ground attack with reigning Super Bowl MVP Kenneth Walker.
