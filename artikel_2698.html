Jay volleyball's historic season ends with state championship

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Jay volleyball's march to history is complete.

The Royals are state champions for the first time in school history after beating Branford in five sets (25-22, 22-25, 25-22, 13-25, 15-8) to win the Rural state championship.

"We still feel like we're on cloud nine," Jay head coach Carey Locklin told the PNJ In a phone interview after the match. "We still feel like we're in a dream, and it's a really good dream."

Jay senior Darby Godwin was a monster in her final high school game, finishing with 26 kills on a .333 hitting percentage and 21 digs. Aubreigh Nelson also finished with a double of 14 kills and 19 digs as did libero Lanie Stephens with 34 digs and 10 assists and setter Bailey Lowry with 44 assists and 16 digs. Kylei Martinez totaled five kills and 18 digs, while Abigail Persichini added six kills. They hit .210 as a team.

For Branford, senior Anabella Dicks left it all on the court with 31 kills and 24 digs. Paisley Knight finished with 13 kills and Ava Alcorn a match-high 57 digs. The Buccaneers hit .222 as a team.

The match was played in front of a raucous crowd at the Polk State College's Winter Haven Health Center, as Branford chartered a spirit bus full of students to make the trip south. A Jay parent ordered noisemakers on Amazon last night, Locklin said, and she told her players to imagine that Buccaneer blue was also Jay blue.

Jay (28-2) trailed by at least four points in the first four sets, yet still managed to turn things around in the first and third set to stay afloat before Branford (29-3) steamrolled through the fourth set, where Locklin said it looked like her team forgot how to play volleyball.

The defending Rural state champions who were also making their fourth straight state championship appearance, led 13-9 before Godwin and Nelson helped flip the script. Jay went on an 11-2 run to take a 20-15 lead and hold the Buccaneers late charge.

Branford then led 17-12 in the second set before Jay battled back to cut the lead to 20-19, but Dicks and the Buccaneers found enough holes in the Royals defense to tie the match. They parlayed that into a 12-5 lead to start the third set before Jay chipped away. Branford still led 20-16 in the third set, before the Royals closed on a 9-2 run, with Nelson getting three kills in the final four points.

The Buccaneers stormed back with their season on the line, leading 13-3 in the fourth set and never sweating. Branford was 4-0 in five set matches entering today, while Jay lost it's lone five set match against T.R. Miller on Aug. 27.

But Jay had come too far to fall short of its mission and sprinted ahead with a state championship hanging in the balance. They led 4-1, then after Branford rallied for a 5-5 tie scored six straight points and were never threatened again. Godwin clinched the championship with a push to the back, catching Branford on its heals, with the Buccaneers failing to return the free ball.

"She's just a phenomenal athlete," Locklin said of Godwin. "She's been up and ready to play since seven o'clock this morning. She's locked in and serious. There ain't a lot of playing around with Darby. She's all business...I told her in that fifth set if they start shutting you down then I'll move you to middle or opposite side and she said 'I got this, I'm good. They're not shutting me down.'"

The title continues Jay athletics impressive run in the last five years. The Royals won a softball state championship in 2022, a baseball state championship in 2024 and played for the girls basketball state championship in February.

In the Jay high school gym, pictures of the 1995 and 1996 girls basketball state championship teams hang on the near wall. Soon enough, the 2026 Jay volleyball team will join them.

"Several of these last 10 years...it was oh we were really close to winning a district championship," Locklin said. "Then it was oh we almost won the regional and we missed it. Last year winning the district and getting thrown out in the regional fired us up like 'we're going to win the district again' and this time we're going to put a regional championship under our belt. Once we got that we were like 'oh let's go.'"

"I think it sets a precedent for our volleyball program at Jay High School about what can happen with a lot of hard work. But it takes years. There's building years, that's what coaches do. You get a group for four years and you try to get the best you can out of them.
