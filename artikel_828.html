'Rolls-Royce' Iqbal's 'dream' journey from Old Trafford to Tannadice

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Jim Goodwin's side are eight games unbeaten, with Zidane Iqbal pivotal

Given the superlatives being thrown in the direction of Zidane Iqbal by the Scottish football fraternity, one would think he was a replica of a certain France great instead of just sharing his name.

So much so that BBC Radio Scotland pundits were suggesting on Saturday's Sportsound that the 23-year-old is the answer to Celtic's hunt for a long-term successor to captain Callum McGregor as their midfield general.

It is just as well for Dundee United that the Iraq international is tied to them until at least the summer, his early season appearances for Utrecht ruling out a transfer during the January window.

So, where has he sprung from and what is all the fuss about?

Born in Manchester to a Pakistani father and an Iraqi mother, Iqbal came through the Old Trafford youth system.

He signed his first professional contract with United in 2021 and became the first British-born South Asian and first-ever British South Asian to play in the Champions League when he came on as an 89th-minute substitute against Young Boys that December.

However, that was to be his one and only first-team appearance and, although he had already made his international debut in a World Cup qualifier at the age of 19, he was sold to Utrecht for a reported fee of Â£1m in summer 2023.

Starting off in their Jong Utrecht second string, he would go on to make 49 appearances for the Dutch top-flight outfit before making a surprise switch to the Scottish Premiership this summer.

On the one hand, United were getting a player who played all three of Iraq's games at this summer's World Cup, two of them starts, albeit all three were heavy defeats.

On the other hand, Iqbal had only managed seven appearances last season for Utrecht and had been a second-half substitute in two of their four matches at the start of the new campaign.

However, after a pivotal display in what was being described as a midfield "masterclass" by United in Saturday's 4-0 dismantling of hosts Hibernian, manager Jim Goodwin revealed he always had belief the Tangerines were unearthing a "Rolls-Royce".

"The head of recruitment's job is to go and identify good talent for us," Goodwin told BBC Scotland. "It's my job to give him the profile and then he goes out and does the groundwork.

"We could see immediately when we watched the footage of Zidane and when we had the Zoom call with him and his representatives, we not only had a fantastic player but a really good person with the right characteristics to come and fit the culture here."

Zidane Iqbal was also a marked man at the World Cup finals

While on-loan Rangers man Lyall Cameron was among the goals and fellow 24-year-old Dylan Tait provided a stylish assist, it was the third leg of what some regard as the best midfield triumvirate in the Premiership who was singled out for most praise at Easter Road.

With former Celtic winger Aidan McGeady again pondering the question about McGregor's likely successor, transfer market expert and former United wing-back Allan Preston opined: "A six for Celtic? Look no further than young Iqbal.

"He's been magnificent since he's come to Scottish football, absolutely brilliant. Running the show again.

"If Celtic are looking for a young boy at his age with experience, he is a full international, knows the game, played in Scotland, you can see what he can do, there's your man in front of you.

"Other players' hair's on fire, but he's just nice and calm. He's at a different level. He doesn't panic. When the ball comes to him, he is so calm on it, he takes his time, he's got great peripheral vision."

The former Dundee United wing-back pointed out, though: "He's had his two clubs that he can play for in a year of football, so he is going nowhere until at least the summer, so enjoy him while you can because I think you'll get serious money for this young boy."

McGeady pointed to Iqbal's "good pedigree", having started with Manchester United and already having 32 caps for Iraq, but wondered if that would be enough for Celtic to consider him.

"I don't even think, if he's performing this well, Celtic will go and get him," said McGeady.

"Why are Celtic not looking at players like this? Do they feel they have to prove themselves somewhere else first?"

Former Celtic captain Scott Brown was similarly impressed.

"He's been a delight to watch - sharpness to get on the ball and willingness to drive forward," the former midfielder and manager pointed out.

"He beats a man in the middle of the park as well and there's not too many midfielders willing to do that.

"Watching him there talking to the media, he can't get the smile off his face. He enjoys football, he takes the ball under pressure, he does things that other people don't do, especially in midfield.

"He brings composure, but he can also drive through as well. His willingness to keep getting on the ball, even if he makes that slight mistake - that's what I really enjoy watching about him."

While Goodwin reckons there will be likely interest in the summer, Iqbal has no thoughts of so quickly leaving a club - and manager - who have resurrected his club career.

"I'm not even looking at any other clubs," he told BBC Scotland. "I'm fully focused on Dundee United.

"They've given me a platform to enjoy myself and play football. I'm really enjoying my football again.

"They've given me faith and confidence. The gaffer and the coaching staff have a lot of belief in me.

"The bare minimum I can do is pay them back with strong performances and hopefully it carries on."

Iqbal recalled being at Manchester United's "development centre since I was four or five - I signed when I was eight", explaining that he "then left for some first-team football".

He stressed that his lack of games last season came as a result of "a couple of injuries" and, while "I learnt a lot in Holland", he is "happy to have left that behind me", adding: "That book's closed now."

Iqbal also recalled how Goodwin's enthusiasm persuaded him to make the switch to Tannadice on deadline day.

"After the World Cup, I had one year left on my contract and I was in touch with my agent," the midfielder said. "We both wanted a move and I had a few chats with a few clubs.

"To be fair, I left it a bit late for Dundee United, but after the first call with the gaffer, I straight away called my agent after we ended the call.

"I said, 'look, I genuinely feel like the guy really believes in me'.

"It happened very late. I had to drive from Holland to Scotland. It was a tough drive but worth it in the end."

That World Cup was also a defining experience, with representing his country in the United States described as surreal.

"Having your friends and family there to watch you and playing on the world stage, with so many people, was a dream," he said.

"Playing against top players gave me that confidence boost I needed. I thought I did well in the three tough games.

"I knew, wherever I'd end up, with the faith of the gaffer, I could really kick on and push because I've learnt so much from the World Cup."

Goodwin is the manager to have done that and Iqbal added: "He's such a top guy, not just in football.

"His tactics are amazing. Everyone's comfortable. Everyone wants to fight for him."
