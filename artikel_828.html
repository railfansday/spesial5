Championship roundup: Strelec sends Middlesbrough top; 10-man Stoke go second

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Boro beat Bristol City 2-0; Stoke 1-0 Birmingham

Scott Parker still winless as West Ham held by Preston

Middlesbrough scored a goal either side of half-time to climb to the top of the Championship with a 2-0 win that piled further pressure on the Bristol City manager Michael Skubala.

Adilson Malanda’s header five minutes before half-time was followed by David Strelec’s finish in the 54th minute to secure victory. The three points were effectively secured before Middlesbrough head coach Kim Hellberg introduced the division’s top scorer, Will Lankshear, for the final 14 minutes from the bench after injury. Defeat heightened the frustrations being felt by Bristol City, who have won just one of their last eight matches, as they dropped to 18th.

Hellberg later called Strelec “a brilliant human being” after the Slovakia striker’s first goal since May. “It was a massive moment for David. He has been very good since coming back, he’s shown how good he is in these two games. That goal was a beautiful team goal and he is a top player – you could see how happy he was.”

George Hirst scored the only goal as 10-man Stoke moved into the automatic promotion places with a 1-0 victory against Birmingham. Hirst broke the deadlock in the early stages of the second half before Luca Bombino was shown a second yellow card for the hosts in the 63rd minute. The two teams were level on points at the start of the day, with both having won their last three games, but it was Stoke who prevailed as Hirst scored for the first time since early September.

Adam Armstrong scored his fourth goal in five games and Rafiki Saïd his first for the club as Wolves beat Charlton 2-1 at Molineux. The dominant hosts put their first-half frustrations behind them to score twice in four minutes shortly after the break – both set up by Matheus Mané – before Matty Godden’s 81st-minute consolation rattled a few home nerves. Victory extended Wolves’ unbeaten run to seven games.

Ashley Williams began life as Swansea’s interim head coach with a draw against the club who showed him the door as a 16-year-old 25 years ago. West Brom led twice through goals from Isaac Price and Rabby Nzingoula, but Swansea levelled each time thanks to Moussa Yeo and Elijah Just. All the goals came in an eventful first half, with the crucial moment being a penalty save by the Swansea goalkeeper Lawrence Vigouroux to deny Price.

Stockport took over top spot in League One with a 3-0 victory away at Cambridge. Second-half efforts from Oliver Norwood and Ethan Ennis and an own goal from Gassan Ahadme sent County three points clear of Huddersfield, who lost by the same scoreline against Leicester, with Louis Page netting the only goal.

Oxford are level on points with the Terriers after dramatically beating nine-man Wycombe 4-3. Wanderers led 3-0 but Dan Casey and Luke Leahy both saw red in the second half, and Gatlin O’Donkor’s goal five minutes into injury time completed the comeback.

A Nahki Wells double helped in-form Luton come from behind to beat Burton 2-1 while a very late goal from Sean Grehan helped Doncaster draw 2-2 against high-flying Bradford. Managerless Blackpool were beaten 2-1 by Wigan following Ian Evatt’s sacking and Peterborough beat Plymouth 2-0. There was a notable away victory for struggling Bromley against Reading, Victor Adeboyejo scoring the only goal, while Leyton Orient dispatched AFC Wimbledon 4-1. PA

Tonda Eckert urged Cyle Larin to keep channelling his inner Robert Lewandowski after his goal helped Southampton to a 2-0 victory against Derby. The Canada forward bundled in his eighth goal of the season on his return from a hamstring injury before Divin Mubama settled the contest in stoppage time.

Eckert who crossed paths with Poland great Lewandowski when coaching at Bayern Munich, said: “I think it was an unbelievable finish. Those are the finishes you can’t teach a striker. You have that in your blood. I think Lewandowski said once that if the manager tells you how to move in the box as a No 9 you should do your own thing. Larin knows what to do in the box, there is nothing to tell him.”

Alex Neil fumed at what he called “over-zealous” officiating in Millwall’s 1-0 loss against QPR. While Neil had no complaints about the red card shown to left-back Zak Sturge after just 15 minutes, he took issue with the punishments meted out to him and his backroom staff. The Scot was shown a yellow card following an altercation with the QPR forward Paul Smyth, with his assistant Martin Canning also being booked later on after the goalkeeping coach, Dean Thornton, had been sent from the dugout. Nicolas Madsen’s goal proved decisive at The Den as QPR won away from home for the first time since August and inflicted a third straight defeat on Millwall.

Neil said: “I’ve been coaching for well over a decade at this level and I’ve been booked less than a handful of times. Any time we spoke to the fourth official, he was dishing out bookings like confetti.”

Sheffield United returned to winning ways as they breezed past struggling Watford with a 4-0 victory at Bramall Lane. Two early Ben Brereton Díaz goals and second-half strikes from Jordan Ayew and Sydie Peck sealed a win that was all too easy for the hosts. It was a feisty affair, with referee Tim Robinson handing out 13 yellow cards – much to the disapproval of both sets of fans.

Norwich came from behind to beat Cardiff 2-1 and make it five successive league wins. Yousef Salech put Cardiff ahead three minutes into the second half but the hosts quickly regrouped and were back on level terms on 62 minutes when the in-form Paris Maghoma curled in from just outside the area. The winner arrived six minutes later when Mathias Kvistgaarden’s penalty was saved by Nathan Trott but the Danish striker reacted quickly to stab home the rebound.

League Two leaders Salford held on to a 1-1 draw against Accrington despite being reduced to nine men. All the action came in the second half, with Elliot Bonds putting Salford ahead just after Daniel Udoh had been sent off. Donald Love equalised for Stanley before Bonds also saw red.

Aaron Lewis scored twice as Newport piled the pressure on Ian Holloway with a 2-0 win against Swindon at Rodney Parade. It was a 10th defeat in the last 15 games in all competitions for the visitors.

Barnet closed the gap to four points with two games in hand thanks to a 2-1 victory over Port Vale. Onel Hernández opened the scoring for Vale but was later sent off and Tom Knowles netted what proved to be the winner.

Dylan Duffy scored twice as third-placed Chesterfield defeated Shrewsbury 4-1 while Grimsby and York were both held to goalless draws. A more dramatic draw saw sixth-placed Crewe held 3-3 by Gillingham, with veteran Garath McCleary scoring an injury-time equaliser. Bottom side Crawley claimed their first home win in the league, beating Fleetwood 2-1, but fellow strugglers Rochdale lost a topsy-turvy encounter 3-2 to Northampton. PA

Scott Parker’s stuttering start as West Ham manager continued in his first match at the London Stadium as bottom club Preston battled their way to a 1-1 draw. Parker is yet to taste victory in three games since he returned to the club. After Konstantinos Mavropanos headed home on the hour to cancel out Liam Lindsay’s early goal for the visitors, it seemed that West Ham’s attacking power would force a victory to keep pace in the promotion race. Instead, despite having 27 efforts on goal, they could not break Preston’s resistance and are now winless in their past five games.

Parker said: “I got here really early today and I had a vision of ending the day with three points and a brilliant performance. I got the brilliant performance, but not the three points, so in that sense, I am devastated. But with a rational head, there were a lot of good things, and if we played that game a hundred times, we would win every other time.”

Sam Dalby’s fine effort cancelled out Josh Murphy’s powerful header as Bolton held Portsmouth to an entertaining 1-1 draw, while George Thomason struck a late winner as Wrexham secured a welcome 1-0 victory at Lincoln. It was a first away league victory for Phil Parkinson’s side since the start of September when they beat Millwall 2-0.
