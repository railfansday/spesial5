On the Couch with Silka Rittson-Thomas

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

The creative mind behind Le Monde Béryl’s New York flagship shares a collection of personal favorites.

Every item on this page was chosen by an ELLE Decor editor. We may earn commission on some of the items you choose to buy.

Curator and collector Silka Rittson-Thomas has run her own independent art advisory for almost two decades. The Phillips alum has worked with scores of galleries and auction houses and established an art residency program at famed Positano hotel Le Sirenuse in 2015. Now, she’s turned her attention to a more unexpected space: a footwear flagship in Manhattan.

In collaboration with architecture studio Tutto Bene, Rittson-Thomas designed the new Upper East Side store for Le Monde Béryl, a luxury shoe brand founded in 2016 by Lily Atherton Hanbury and Katya Shyfrin. It’s the brand’s first permanent location, and the neutral-toned space is filled with design details like 20th-century Murano twin-branched wall sconces, aluminum easy chairs by Mats Theselius, and a Ransom & Dunn “Adler” sofa with custom upholstery. “On the one hand I was thinking about the principles of the Renaissance, specifically around lighting, reflections and proportions, while the idea was to create a space which feels theatre-like, a place of discovery,” Rittson-Thomas tells ELLE Decor.

Now open on 67th and Madison, Le Monde Béryl is Rittson-Thomas’s most recent project, but she has plenty in the pipeline. She’s renovating a private apartment, also with Tutto Bene, in London, and, she says, “I’m also passionate about a seed project that my friend Lena Evstafieva—the proprietor and mind behind Villa Lena in Tuscany—and I are scheming about.”

Below, we had the art advisor take our On the Couch survey, sharing her own go-to galleries, favorite artists, and destination museums.

Define your studio in three words: Unexpected, poetic, collaborative.

Greatest influences: The natural world, artists’ minds, my eyes, and light.

Favorite eras of design: Neoclassicism; the 1930s and 1940s; the 1980s; Brutalism; weathered design and architecture.

Favorite sofa: Cy Twombly’s elongated Roman sofa is, indefinitely, my favorite sofa. I have a tendency to consider sofas as objects rather than objects of comfort. I love a heavily patinated Kaare Klint sofa, the delightful yellow velvet Armand-Albert Rateau sofa for Lanvin, and Regency sofas.

Most beloved buildings: My parents’ modernist bungalow, a folly built in the 1970s. I like to think of gardens and landscapes as buildings, and among my favorites are geometric and spiritual gardens, such as those designed by the late Spanish landscape architect Fernando Caruncho, or the “musical garden” by the Danish landscape architect Carl Theodor Sørensen.

Other favorites include the Ibn Tulun Mosque in Cairo; Hagia Sophia in Istanbul; the Querini Stampalia in Venice; and the Summer Palace in Bukhara, built in the late nineteenth century by the last Emir of Bukhara as a summer residence for his beloved princess. It is a magical, bejeweled Arts and Crafts retreat.

Architects you admire: Fernando Caruncho, Carlo Scarpa, Mayan architecture, traditional and contemporary Korean and Japanese architecture, barn architecture, vernacular architecture, and mountain refuges.

Destination museums: Museo della Frutta in Turin, Italy, and Insel Hombroich in Germany.

Go-to galleries: I have focused on London and its extraordinary energy among a younger, resilient gallery community: Emalin, Rose Easton, Heidi, Ilenia, Edel Assanti, Tramps, Ginny on Frederick, Philippa Pflug, and A Squire.

Favorite artists and artworks: Zurbarán’s Rose; a Joan Mitchell Grand Vallée; a Jannis Kounellis Rosa Nera painting from his mid-1960s exhibition at Galleria L’Attico in Rome; Graham Little’s wondrous gouaches; Victor Man’s recent still lifes; Suzan Frecon’s monochromatic canvases; Simone Fattal’s bronzes; still life by the French Impressionist painter Marie Bracquemond. I would also love to work on a commission with Milena Muzquiz and her vibrant ceramics and installation work.

Vintage piece of your dreams: A large eighteenth-century Sicilian Baroque giltwood mirror with sinuously carved foliage and its unusual original turquoise paint, from Tarquin Bilgen on the Pimlico Road. A pair of Spanish-colonial Viceregal Peruvian mother-of-pearl-inlaid bureau-cabinets sold by Zebregs & Röell.

Favorite getaway: Always Paris via Eurostar; lake swimming in the Austrian and Swiss Alps; and Genoa.

Hotels to check into: My favorite new Parisian hotel is Hôtel Masse in Pigalle. I would like to check into Hope by Wildland, set within the vast coastal landscapes of Sutherland; Reschio in Umbria; The Tuba Club. Le Sirenuse always, of course.

Favorite jeweler: The artist jeweler du jour, Julia Muggenburg of Belmacz; antique Jules Wièse pieces; and Art Deco Jean Després.

Fashion designers: 1990s and 2000 vintage Prada, 1970s and 1980s YSL. Contemporary: Róhe, Attersee, and RIER.

Best host/hostess gift: Beautiful napkins or seeds with a story—Gayle Warwick linens and seeds from historic gardens like Great Dixter are my go-tos.
