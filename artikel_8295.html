Table Rock Tenting

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

My final section in Pennsylvania is on the docket for today. I press along 3 mountains and find a top tier campsite.

Can it be? Just one section of Pennsylvania remaining, and then I’m off to Maryland to attempt the 4 state challenge and get off trail for good. I am starting to feel a tugging at my emotions. Its part relief to be sure, but any time you switch away from something you’ve been doing for a long stretch of time you can’t help but feel that you’ll miss it. I felt the same way when I made the decision to leave my former employer. Sure, it could drive me nuts at times, just like any job can, but it had its moments and its good people. Soon I’ll be off to something new soon, it will have new benefits, and new things to stress over.

I had a short drive to the trail as I attempted to find a quieter place to car camp last night. It was fine until just before 5:00AM when traffic picked up considerably. I will have more time to catch up on sleep tonight on the trail, so getting an earlier than desired wake-up call wasn’t much of a problem. I attempted to jolt myself into action by stopping for a quick coffee on my way back to Swatara Gap. I had a long day of hiking ahead of me with more elevation gain than I had been treated to most of my recent hiking stints. I headed south around 7:30AM with roughly 25 miles to Peters Mountain Road. It had been nearly 4 months since I got trapped at the trailhead there. Ah, fond memories indeed. I recall there being campsites a short walk from the trailhead, but, as always, I would be keeping my pace up and my eyes peeled for other options.

The trail briefly crosses to the far side of Route 443 before re-crossing and heading through a meadow. From there it spends time on 3 parallel mountain ridges. The northernmost is Peters Mountain. To its south is Third Mountain, and then Second Mountain. There is a First Mountain, but the Appalachian Trail doesn’t traverse it. From Second Mountain I could clearly make out Swatara Gap through the trees. Stony Creek Valley sits between Second and Third Mountain and it appears a long gravel road runs for miles along the creek. A sign along the trail stated that the village of Rausch Gap used to occupy the space with about 1,000 people. I couldn’t help but notice how clear Rausch Creek was. I wasn’t sure but there seemed to be water being treated in some manner just north of the bridge over the creek.

The trail heads up Third Mountain, a tree lined ridge without much to see. I did notice a number of trails connecting to the AT. The entire area is quite remote. The long mountain ridges make access tricky, so it makes sense that the section wasn’t as settled as the surrounding areas despite the proximity to Harrisburg.

By early afternoon I was making my way down to route 325 and up to the third and final mountain ridge, Peters Mountain. Through the trees I could make out a familiar sight to the north of spread out farms. As usual, water was quite scarce up on the mountain ridges. My only option for water near the end of my hike was from the spring at Peters Mountain Shelter. “At” is a rather generous preposition to describe the relative location of the spring to the shelter. It requires a 0.2 mile hike down the steep ridge. A sign posted at the shelter indicates 300 steps are required. I’d put it at closer to 400, none of which were easy.

Down at the spring, movement in the distance caught my attention. I caught something that I was sure was a darker, dulled shade of brown than the white-tailed deer I’d been seeing constantly. I thought for sure it must be a coyote and was glad it had decided to trot off in the opposite direction. Upon closer inspection it did seem to be just another deer, the color was certainly unusual though. I climbed back up the steps to the shelter and collected my pack. Staying at the shelter was enticing, it had a loft and loads of space. I figured I would see what the trail ahead would offer and make a decision.

Looking at Third Mountain from Kinter View.

0.9 miles from the shelter is the Table Rock viewpoint. The large rocks at the crest of the ridge provide an open view across to Third Mountain. However, it looked like the sunset was likely going to be just out of view. Sunrise, however, would be quite clear just to the left hand side of the view. Looking ahead, it seemed like the best I could do, and there seemed to be a spot for a 1 person tent. It was sloped, but I was confident I could orient my tent in such away that I would sleep with the slope running down toward my feet. With the campsite boxes checked, I stashed the 2 liters of water I had collected and headed the last 2 miles south to the Peters Mountain trailhead to connect to the point where I’d ended my southern AT hiking back in July.

With almost no minutes to spare I made it back to table rock to catch the last bits of sunlight hitting Third Mountain. I was exhausted and eager to scarf down my dinner and crawl into my tent. My tent theory was spot on, the slope followed the length of my sleeping pad and didn’t wash out right or left. It is not an easy thing to eye since the inside of my Durston tent runs at a funky diagonal angle relative to the rectangular footprint of the rain fly. I didn’t bother setting an alarm for sunrise. There are about 14 hours between sunset and sunrise. Even if I slept 10 hours I’d be up well before the sun would be making its daily appearance.
