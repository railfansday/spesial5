The Garmin CIRQA Is the Health Band for People Who Hate Wearing Watches

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Overview: The Garmin CIRQA is an excellent screenless health band, but it suits people who dislike watches better than current Garmin watch wearers. After more than two months of day-and-night testing, Outside columnist Graham Averill found it tracks sleep, heart rate and HRV, auto-detects workouts and needs charging about once a week, all for $200 with no subscription. The trade-off: you can’t see your data in real time without opening your phone.

I like wearing a big watch. Not a fancy watch, but a big, adventure watch with large numbers and all sorts of data on the face like the altitude of my current location and how many steps I’ve taken during the day. I like wearing a big adventure watch because it sends a statement to the world. It says, “I might be standing in the grocery store checkout line with a shocking amount of ground beef in my cart, but I do cool things in the outdoors.”

Also, my big adventure watch tracks all of my health data. I am a man of a certain age (50), so the world tells me constantly how important it is to monitor the hours I sleep and my resting heart rate. But Garmin recently released the CIRQA, a new health band that promises to track every aspect of my life, and it had me wondering if I should ditch the big, ostentatious watch and opt for a more minimalist health band. One without a face. One that sends a different statement to the world, probably along the lines of “please ask me about my meditation practices.”

So I spent the last two months wearing Garmin’s new CIRQA health band every day, and I discovered that it is an excellent health tracker that does everything it claims to do. I also discovered that these minimalist health bands aren’t for me. But the CIRQA might be exactly what you’re looking for. Let me explain.

Pros and Cons + Comfortable design + Great battery life + Automatic workout tracking + Awesome sleep score detail + No subscription required – Can’t track data in real time

I’m all in on Garmin’s ecosystem. I wear the Garmin Fenix 7 watch, I use Garmin’s computers on my bike, and I even track my golf game with Garmin’s app and launch monitor.

As I inch my way closer to death, I’ve become obsessed with knowing every aspect of my health, so I’ve been curious about comprehensive health bands like Whoop. But Whoop is expensive, with an annual subscription that starts at $199. Garmin’s CIRQA promises all of the data of a Whoop without the annual subscription.

So I spent more than two months testing the Garmin CIRQA, wearing it every day and night and charting my health on Garmin’s everything app, Connect.

The CIRQA tracks all the major data that a health-conscious athlete could want, continuously monitoring your heart rate and heart rate variability (HRV). It tracks your sleep quality, counts your respiration rate and measures your pulse ox and your skin temperature. The smart band then uses all of these metrics to deliver a “Body Battery” score that puts a number on your sleep quality, workout difficulty, and stress levels.

It’s comfortable and understated. The band is made from a soft, stretchy poly fabric that isn’t waterproof, but dries fast. I wore it on my right wrist most days—I kept my watch on my left wrist—and found it dries fast after a workout, swim or shower. It does as good a job picking up your heart rate as any wrist-based heart rate monitor I’ve tried, and the details about your sleep quality are awesome. You get a Sleep Score every morning, which contributes heavily to your Body Battery score, but you can dig deeper on Garmin’s Connect app to learn how much deep sleep or REM sleep you got, and how many times you were restless and awake throughout the night.

So far, I’m restless and awake an average of 40 times a night. I think my wife is pinching me in my sleep.

You can also self-report lifestyle choices to the app, like alcoholic beverages and late-night meals or snacks. This option has helped me draw a direct link between booze and poor sleep. I always suspected it—because there’s so much science to back up the theory—but I never wanted to believe it. Unfortunately, you can’t argue with math. A typical sleep score for me is in the high 70s or low 80s. Add a couple of beers toward the end of the night, and that score drops to the 40s.

Yes. I’m impressed with how well the CIRQA automatically picks up an activity. Start walking or hit the gym and the CIRQA tracks every movement, heartbeat and calorie without any prompts or button pushing. If you’ve ever forgotten to click the “start” button before an epic effort, you know that this automatic tracking is awesome. There’s nothing worse than going for a six-mile run that you didn’t track. If you don’t know your total calories burned, did it even happen?

You’ll get an alert on your phone after the workout that takes you into Garmin’s Connect app, where you can name the activity. The Connect app is a big part of CIRQA’s user interface. That’s where you can get details about each workout or nap, and it’s easy to use. Connect even has a cool coach layer, so you can design workout plans, and it tells you how much sleep you need to get to recover from your day’s effort.

Smart bands like the CIRQA are designed to simplify your health tracking, giving you the data you want to monitor every aspect of your life, but with one less screen to look at—the watch. But you have to open your phone to see any of that health data, which means you can’t dig into your metrics in real time.

I like wearing a watch when I work out because it tracks my heart rate and calories burned in real time. It keeps me honest and has made my workouts more effective.

My Garmin Fenix doesn’t automatically pick up my workouts, and my wife complains that her Apple Watch doesn’t do a good job of that either. The CIRQA’s battery life is great, too. I charge it once a week, about the same as my Garmin Fenix.

And that’s my takeaway from two months’ of testing the CIRQA: it’s great. The CIRQA does exactly what Garmin says it will do, but it’s not an upgrade from my watch, so I don’t see any reason to switch.

But plenty of people don’t like wearing bulky watches, and many athletes don’t want or need to know their heart rate while they’re working out. If that sounds like you, the CIRQA might be a good option, especially since you won’t be on the hook for a $200 subscription every year.
