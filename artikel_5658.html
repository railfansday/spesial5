Leading With Purpose

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

https://www.ironvalleyrealestate.com/region/hamptonroads

Region served: Coastal Virginia and Northern North Carolina

Work philosophy that you live by: Together, we’ll go further.

What drew you to Iron Valley Real Estate that made you want to step into ownership, and how does the brand’s structure and vision help your team stand out in the market?

What resonated most was the model, which was very agent friendly, where agents kept the majority of their money. But the company leaders are what ultimately attracted me to ownership. Mike Little and Eric Edwards, the original founders, allowed me to have a platform where I was able to train and mentor a big group of agents, giving me free reign. I got to incorporate my own philosophies into what was already a successful brokerage, and I came with a diverse group of agents who added to the culture of Iron Valley. The fact that the ownership embraced that diversity is what ultimately led to me wanting to be a part of it.

How has your 23-year experience in the Navy translated to your real estate career, especially when it comes to mentoring agents?

A large part of my success has been the leadership training that I received in the Navy—the years of guiding and leading a diverse group of individuals. The military is very diverse, where people come from different races, religions, ethnicities, parts of the world and even parts of the United States. Bringing all these different people together, the military teaches you how to interact with others at a large scale. On top of the diversity, the leadership training I had throughout my time in the Navy helped catapult my real estate career.

What is your best piece of advice for those considering brokerage ownership or leadership roles?

Understand that your job satisfaction isn’t going to come from finances or money. This has to be a person whose job satisfaction comes from helping other people excel. A lot of people get into the brokerage and real estate ownership business thinking that it’s profitable—and while it can be, if that’s your sole reason for opening a brokerage, you’re never going to attain that job satisfaction. I want to excel with the people I enjoy being around; I don’t want to make it to the top alone.

When onboarding new agents or taking experienced agents to the next level, how do you foster accountability while maintaining a supportive environment?

I remind them of their “why.” In order to hold someone accountable to reach the goals they’ve set for themselves, you have to remind them why they want to achieve those goals in the first place. For me, my why is seeing everyone I encounter achieve whatever success means to them.

How do you train your team to handle the fast-paced timelines, permanent change of station moves and VA loan nuances that come with serving military families in your community?

Hampton Roads is the biggest military concentrated area in the United States. Every agent knows someone who served, has a family member who served, or served themselves. And if that’s not the case, which doesn’t happen very often, I actually teach military jargon. We also go over the processes associated with military members moving and selecting orders—and if they have to leave the area, we examine what that process looks like as well. Our owners are all veterans, so we train to those experiences to ensure our agents are better equipped to serve the military population.
