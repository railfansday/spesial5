99th Oscars: Every Single Contender — All 104! — for the International Feature Race

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

THR's cheat sheet of all the record-setting submissions for the Best International Feature category at the 2027 Academy Awards.

By Scott Roxborough, Patrick Brzeski, Lily Ford, Georg Szalai, Etan Vlessing, Kevin Cassidy November 26, 2026

The Best International Oscar race is the biggest ever — a record 104 international features are in contention, beating the previous high-water mark of 93 set in 2020 — and one of the most open in years.

There is no out-and-out favorite this season, although European cinema again looks strong, with Javier Calvo and Javier Abrosso’s Spanish entry La Bola Negra; Cristian Mungiu’s Palme d’Or winner Fjord, in the running for Romania; and Pawel Pawlikowski’s Polish contender Fatherland all expected to make the short list, alongside Asian faves including South Korea’s Possible Love, from Lee Chang-dong; and Japan’s All of a Sudden from Ryusuke Hamaguchi.

Behind that top five is a long list of maybes, including French entry Minotaur from twice-nominated Russian director Andrey Zvyagintsev; Lukas Dhont’s WWI drama Coward, Belgium’s entry; Austria’s Rose, which like Fatherland stars Sandra Huller; and Rehearsals for a Revolution, Pegah Ahangarani’s documentary on her family and their battles against the Iranian regime, which is the U.K. official Oscar submission.

One hundred films were submitted by countries or regions. Four qualified through a new method where movies that win top awards at leading festivals are accepted as long as they meet the language, creative input and theatrical-release requirements for the category. According to Academy rules, an international feature film is defined as a feature-length motion picture (more than 40 minutes) produced outside the United States with a predominantly (more than 50 percent) non-English dialogue track. New Zealand’s submission, Taratoa Stappard’s gothic horror tale Mārama, was dropped for falling short of the language requirement.

Academy members from all branches who meet the minimum viewing requirements can participate in the preliminary round of voting for the shortlist, taking place between will take place Dec. 7-11. The shortlist of 15 international films will be announced on Dec. 15. Oscar nominations voting will take place Jan. 11-15, 2027, with the nominations announcement on Jan. 21.

The 99th Academy Awards will be held on March 14, 2027.

THR has exhaustively compiled the full list of international contenders, with trailers, reviews and talent interviews where available. Enjoy!

Roland Sejko’s documentary uses archival footage, newsreels and state-produced cinema to examine how Enver Hoxha’s communist regime used film as an instrument of propaganda. Tracing Albania’s isolation and political transformation over four decades, the film explores how the country’s official image was manufactured, controlled and disseminated by the state.

In 1993, a car crash leaves Ahmed amnesiac, so that not even his family feels familiar. His youngest son fears him and his bandaged face, while Ahmed feels uneasy about his neighbor. Yanis Koussim’s debut feature — the Arabic title means “exorcism” — reimagines the traumas of Algeria’s bloody “Black Decade” (1992–2002), a civil war between the government and Islamist rebel groups, as a horror film, blending memory, trauma and possession.

Teenage angst meets the supernatural in Laura Casabé’s coming-of-age horror film, set over a single lazy summer day in 2001, at the peak of Argentina’s great depression. Amid the backdrop of social unrest, adolescent desire turns deadly in this slow-burn rage thriller, which debuted at Sundance in 2025.

Tamara Stepanyan’s narrative feature debut follows Céline, a French woman who travels to Armenia to settle the affairs of her supposedly deceased husband, Arto. There she discovers that he concealed his past as a soldier and is regarded by some as a deserter. As she investigates his life, Céline encounters a country still haunted by successive wars and their unresolved consequences.

James J. Robinson’s debut feature is the country’s first-ever Tagalog-language submission for the Oscars. The meditative film focuses on an aging woman who questions her faith as the world crumbles around her. After a young construction worker dies under suspicious circumstances, an elderly nun whose life has been shaped by discipline, service and belief, gets drawn into a world of crime and corruption.

Sandra Hüller is everywhere in this season’s awards conversation, but the German star gives her most transformative performance playing a 17th-Century woman passing as a male farmer in Markus Schleinzer’s period drama. Hüller won the Best Performance trophy in Berlin for the role, which was based on hundreds of similar recorded cases throughout history. Read THR’s review of Rose here.

Zaur Gasimli’s historical drama follows Azerbaijani philanthropist Haji Zeynalabdin Taghiyev and his efforts to establish Baku’s first school for Muslim girls in 1901. Facing fierce opposition from religious conservatives, Taghiyev enlists the support of his young wife, Sona, in a campaign that becomes a test of his influence, diplomacy and determination to expand educational opportunities for women.

Mejbaur Rahman Sumon reimagines Adam and Eve in the monsoon-soaked Bengali countryside in this dark folk tale. A tenant farmer (Mostafizur Noor Imran) wants out of his arranged marriage to a woman (Nazifa Tushi) the villagers regard as insane. Promising her a trip to the circus, he rows her into the forest and slips away in the dark. Yet however far he takes her, the thud of a palm fruit beside their hut announces her return. Sumon’s follow-up to Hawa, Bangladesh’s 2022 Oscar submission, premiered in Rotterdam’s Tiger Competition.

Lukas Dhont’s queer World War I drama follows two Belgian soldiers, Pierre and Francis, who find an unexpected refuge from the brutality of the trenches in the world of theatre. Assigned to organize entertainment for their fellow troops, the men develop an increasingly intimate relationship as they put together a revue behind the front lines. Leads Emmanuel Macchia and Valentin Campagne shared the Best Actor prize at Cannes.

This Turkish-language political drama from German Academy Award nominee İlker Çatak (The Teacher’s Lounge) qualified for Oscar submission by winning the Golden Bear for Best Film at the Berlinale. Özgü Namal and Tansu Biçer star as Derya and Aziz, a successful theatrical couple whose lives are upended after their latest production brings them into conflict with the authorities in Ankara. Dismissed from their jobs and forced to leave their home, they find their loss of professional standing and financial security puts their marriage under pressure, testing the convictions that once united them.

Read THR‘s interview with Çatak here and THR‘s review of Yellow Letters here.

The coming-of-age drama about tradition, ambition and choosing your own destiny follows a young woman in the Bolivian Andes who takes up the sacred ways of midwifery, but dreams of escaping to the city to become a singer. Directed by Álvaro Olmos Torrico, The Condor Daughter had its world premiere at the Toronto Film Festival.

A 60-something electrician has his very ordered life shattered by the sudden death of his wife. Left alone and forced to fend for himself in a strictly patriarchal society, he begins to recognize how much he took her devotion for granted. His attempts to reconnect with his estranged children are further complicated by his discovery that his son is living with another man.

Allan Deberton’s queer coming-of-age drama, which debuted in Berlin as part of the Generation Kplus section, follows a 12 year-old boy growing up in a small rural community trying balance his affection and desires with the fear of rejection from his father and surrounding community.

Check out a THR interview about the film here and the trailer below.

Nearly a decade after well-received 2017 feature Western, German director Valeska Grisebach returns with another deconstruction of a male-dominated genre. This slow-burning crime thriller, set in the Bulgarian borderlands, works as a low-key arthouse take on The Godfather, as archaeologist Veska (Yana Radeva) returns to the rural community she grew up in and reconnects with old friends, getting pulled back into criminal activity. Winner of the Jury Prize in Cannes.

Majid Majidi’s social drama, about Solan (Mohammad Rahimi), a poorly-paid temporary teacher in a remote, drought-stricken Iranian village was the stand out at this year’s Busan Film Festival, where it won the top prize, directly qualifying it for the Oscar race. When the death of the benefactor funding the school’s meal programme threatens to drive away the pupils, Solan takes matters into his own hands, turning to oil smuggling to keep the meals coming.

Check out the trailer for Midday Meal below.

Even the dead face eviction in Polen Ly’s elegiac ghost story about the cost of redevelopment in Cambodia. Thida (Savorn Serak) has watched over a movie house in Battambang since her death during the Khmer Rouge years. With the building marked for demolition, she must choose between wandering as a homeless spirit and risking the pain of another human life. She befriends Hai (Piseth Chhun), a young journalist who remembers going to the movies there as a boy. In the shuttered theater, the two watch a film together on his phone. Ly’s fiction feature debut premiered in Venice’s Biennale College Cinema program and won the Golden Hanoman, the top prize at Indonesia’s Jogja-NETPAC Asian Film Festival.

Nchini Sylvia-Bright is back in her second Cameroon Oscar submission after Ngang Romanus’ 2021 movie Hidden Dreams. In Mabanda, she plays Mojoko, a pregnant teenager who, after the death of her mother, is left alone with her abusive father, played by director Quintus, and faces rejection by her community. Shot in Cameroonian Pidgin English, it’s the eighth submission by the country for the Academy Awards.

This transatlantic drama from Montreal-based director Geneviève Dulude-De Celles bowed in Berlin, where it won the Silver Bear for Best Screenplay. It centers on an art expert, Mihail, who fled Bulgaria 30 years earlier and returns to authenticate the work of Nina, an 8-year-old Bulgarian artist and possible child prodigy, whose paintings have gone viral online. As Mikhail studies her work, he is forced to confront the ghosts of his past.

Check out the trailer for Nina Rosa below.

The period mystery, the director’s second feature, premiered at Cannes. The follow-up to Martelli’s General Augusto Pinochet-era political thriller Chile ‘76is set in 1992, after Pinochet’s regime ends and a 9-year old girl is staying in an Andean ski resort where she befriends a German teenager aiming to be a competitive skier, only to vanish without a trace.

Chinese comedy star Shen Teng plays a chef who takes a job in Bahata, a fictional stand-in for Baghdad, to pay off his family’s debts. In this war drama loosely inspired by the 2003 U.S.-led invasion of Iraq, the Chinese restaurant where he works becomes both a refuge for local orphans and a bar for American soldiers. Director Wen Muye previously made a blockbuster out of the high cost of cancer drugs in China with 2018’s Dying to Survive. This latest social drama has grossed $358.4 million worldwide, making it by far the highest-grossing entry in this year’s international feature Oscar race.

A group of misfits converging at Babel, a legendary Colombian dive bar that doubles as purgatory, and where La Flaca — the city’s Grim Reaper — presides. Here, souls gamble years of their lives with her, daring to outwit Death herself. With his feature debut, which debuted at Sundance in 2025, director Gala del Sol has created a fantastical Colombian fever dream where life and death collides and the fate of people is left to chance.

With her debut feature, Costa Rica’s Kim Torres has created a rural chiller, combining story combining a coming-of-age drama with supernatural and thriller elements. The film follows 13-year-old Laura, who feels suffocated by her new family, despite the lush forests and palm plantations that surround her. She soon discovers the supposed village paradise hides a shadowy secret that preys on the women living in it. Torres’ drama world premiered at San Sebastian’s New Directors sidebar in 2025.

Director Hana Jusic took inspiration from Wuthering Heights and gothic horror in this period drama, set in rural Croatia, circa 1910, to tell a surprisingly modern tale of female liberation. Teresa, a Chilean woman dressed all in black like an avenging angel, arrives in an isolated, and deeply religious community of Croatian shepherds in the early 20th century, claiming to be the widow of a local man who emigrated to South America.

Her arrival unsettles the tightly ordered society, challenging its traditional relationships and expectations. Unable to speak the language, she becomes a blank canvas for hopes and fears, particularly of the women in the community.

Carlos Larrazabal’s debut feature explores the pull of home and the longing to leave. It follows Elvis, who returns to Havana after spending a decade in prison for murdering his abusive stepfather. As he reconnects with childhood sweetheart and falls back into life in the city’s criminal underworld, Elvis must decide whether to stay in the world he knows or escape Havana for a new life elsewhere.

Cyprus’ first-ever international Oscar contender is also the feature debut of writer-director Myrsini Aristidou. Newcomer Maria Petrova and Christos Passalis, who played the son in Yorgos Lanthimos’ Dogtooth, star as an 11-year-old and her absent, petty criminal father who returns home after a family death. The film won The World Cinema Dramatic Audience Award at Sundance.

Prague-based filmmaker Pepa Lubojacki scoured through family photos and diary snapshots for this intimate, heart-rending look at her family’s struggles with alcoholism, addiction and homelessness. The film premiered in Berlin, where it won the best documentary honor as well as the independent Caligari award.

This historical thriller from directors Yohane Dean Lengol and Hamed Mobasser is set in 1959, in the final months of Belgian colonial rule, in Club Rumba Royale, a popular nightclub where white Europeans and Congolese citizens rub shoulders as dance mixes with debates of independence. Featuring African rumba star Fally Ipupa, the feature is the first submission by DR Congo since 1997 and only the second overall.

Check out the trailer below (no English subtitles).

May el-Toukhy’s Golden Lion-winning Woman Unknown is backdropped by a Danish city free from Nazi occupation, mere months after World War II has ended. It tells the story of Marie (Volpi Cup winner Mathilde Arcel), a young nanny and housemaid about to marry her wealthy, older, and widowed employer, Christian (Carsten Bjørnlund). But Marie guards a shameful secret.

The Danish filmmaker deftly scrutinizes post-war patriotism and the era’s chastisement of women and their sex lives. “There have been so many films about the Second World War, and that is true,” el-Toukhy told THR ahead of Venice. “But they’re all based mostly on men’s experiences and recollections of war. So that really energized me.”

The second feature film from director Andrés Farías centers on Sonia (Mercedes Morales), a widow who trades her family home for a cramped apartment and finds unexpected intimacy with Aimé, a working-class Haitian man, defying her family and the racial tensions between Dominicans and Haitians. The drama played Havana, Miami and Chicago and won best film, director and actress at the 2026 Dominican Film Festival in New York.

Director Cristina Barragán, who represented Ecuador in 2016 with Alba, continues her exploration of childhood wounds in this drama starring Simone Bucio as Azucena, a woman in her 30s who approaches Julio, a teenager in a group home, played by first-time actor Francis Eddú Llumiquinga. As they spend time together, an uneasy closeness grows, changing the personal bond in ways two outsiders from different worlds, but with a shared experience of trauma, The film won the best screenplay award in the Orizzonti section at the Venice Film Festival.

The Egyptian-Austrian filmmaker Abu Bakr Shawky is back in the international Oscar race after his 2018 feature Yomeddine, also submitted for Egypt. The Stories, loosely inspired by his own parents’ relationship, follows aspiring Cairo pianist Ahmed and his long-distance romance with Austrian pen pal Liz, from the 1960s through the 1980s. Starring Amir El-Masry (The Crown) and Valerie Pachner (Fantastic Beasts: The Secrets of Dumbledore).

Arturo López Rodas wrote, directed and produced this debut feature, a 60-minute drama in which three old friends reunite for a funeral after decades apart, musing on life, loss and time. It won best screenplay at the Ícaro Awards and best international feature at the Cobb International Film Festival. Vértices represents El Salvador’s first entry into Oscar’s best international feature competition.

In his feature film debut, director German Golub charts the real-life story of Erika Salumäe, a track cyclist who overcomes personal and professional hardship — and the pressure of the dwindling Soviet regime — to become a two-time Olympic gold medallist in 1988 and 1992. From troubled young woman to national hero, Salumäe’s tenacity makes this biographical sports drama a story of inspiration and grit, with Estonian actress Karolin Jürise in the titular role.

Check out the trailer for Our Erika below.

Finnish filmmaker Markus Lehmusruusu (2019’s Ricky Rapper and the Strong Man) turns to science fiction in his sophomore feature. In a future, sterile world without nature or animals, Pasi (Miro Lopperi) is a lonely and compassionate designer of synthetic pets.

When by chance he stumbles upon the last surviving squirrel, he decides to defy the very strict regulations of interaction with what little nature is left in the world and help the squirrel back into its natural habitat. Lehmusruusu tackles the intersection of humanity’s instinctive need to both survive and provide in this Fantastic Fest debut (which used real, trained squirrels over animatronics or VFX).

Check out the trailer for The Squirrel below.

The first feature film from exiled Russian dissident director Andrey Zvyagintsev (Loveless, Leviathan) in nearly a decade is a taut psychological thriller set in Russia shortly after the invasion of Ukraine. Loosely inspired by Claude Chabrol’s The Unfaithful Wife (1969) it follows a wealthy businessman caught between government demands that he recruit employees to send as cannon fodder in the war effort, and his growing suspicions that his wife is having an affair. As pressure mounts on both fronts, his carefully controlled domestic and professional life begins to unravel. Like the Russian state, he goes to extremes of violence to maintain the appearance of power and control.

Levan Koguashvili’s dramedy is set in a Georgian village struggling to recover from the economic upheaval following the collapse of the Soviet Union. At its center is Zuriko, a widowed hazelnut farmer trying to secure a fair price for his harvest while his debts mount. With the help of his friend Gela, he also embarks on a search for a new partner, despite the objections of his formidable former mother-in-law.

Birgit Minichmayr plays Ella, a mother struggling to process the death of her daughter Jessie, in Sandra Wollner’s drama about grief and the complicated bonds left behind. Ella begins spending time with Lux, Jessie’s boyfriend, who was with her on the night she died. As the two grow closer, their relationship becomes increasingly entangled with memories of the young woman. An impulsive family holiday to Tenerife becomes an increasingly surreal attempt to escape grief and reconnect with the past.

A favorite in Cannes, where it won Best Film of the Un Certain Regard section, Everytime has been nominated for Best Film and Wollner for Best Screenplay at the 2027 European Film Awards.

Read THR‘s interview with director Sandra Wollner here.

Yannis Economides crafts a modern Greek tragedy out of the story of a businessman who devises what appears to be a foolproof scheme to save his family home from falling into the hands of a loan shark. But his plan sets a series of increasingly unpredictable events in motion, threatening to destroy the very life he is trying to preserve.

Check out the trailer for the film below.

Guatemalan auteur Jayro Bustamante returns with an action-tinged drama inspired by the true story of Guatemala’s 2018 Volcán de Fuego tragedy. María Mercedes Coroy plays a volcanologist who tries to warn and evacuate Indigenous communities as a new volcano forms amid corruption and economic interests. This is Bustamante’s fourth Oscar submission, after 2015’s Ixcanul, 2019’s La Llorona (shortlisted) and Rita from 2024.

Check out the official trailer (no subtitles) below.

Snow falls on Hong Kong in Juno Mak’s underworld epic, in which a hospital bombing kills a pharmaceutical magnate and leaves his younger son with an unusual ambition for a family drug empire — to shut it all down. A long-in-the-making saga starring Asian industry heavyweights Takeshi Kaneshiro, Tony Leung Ka-fai and Louis Koo, the film premiered in Cannes and won eight Hong Kong Film Awards, including one for its score by the late Ryuichi Sakamoto.

Tony Leung Chiu-wai, Léa Seydoux and fast-rising German star Luna Wedler feature in this century-spanning contemplative drama from Hungarian auteur Ildikó Enyedi (On Body and Soul, The Story of My Wife). Set in three different eras — 1908, 1972, and 2020 — it follows three very different people, a pioneering female student, a lovesick young man and a Hong Kong neuroscientist who share a connection to an ancient ginkgo tree growing int the botanical garden of a German university town. Enigmatic but never self-serious, Silent Friend leavens its big ideas and visual poetry with a healthy dose of deadpan humor.

Yrsa Roca Fannberg turns her camera on the residents and caregivers of a Reykjavík nursing home in this observational documentary about ageing and the final stages of life. Everyday activities, from dancing and exercise classes to painting nails and sharing quiet moments, become the focus of an intimate portrait of a community negotiating the realities of old age. Rather than concentrating on decline, the film finds moments of companionship, humour and pleasure in the routines of institutional life.

Set in a remote Naga village near the Myanmar border in the early 1960s, Theja Rio’s debut feature follows Anghba, the community’s last surviving chief. With most of the villagers having left, he remains with his ailing wife and speech-impaired son, struggling to preserve the traditions of his ancestors. The arrival of an American missionary introduces Christianity and new social customs, deepening the generational divide between father and son. The film won the Platform Award at Toronto, qualifying it for the Oscar race.

Check out the trailer for the film below.

In Santosh Davakhar’s Marathi-language folk thriller, a young bride in rural Maharashtra plots her groom’s murder during the traditional ceremony meant to bless their marriage. As the village gathers for the gondhal, an all-night ritual of devotional song and dance, Suman (Ishita Deshmukh) recruits the groom’s cousin, a former suitor, to do the killing so she can run away with one of the performers. The film won Davakhar the Silver Peacock for best director at the International Film Festival of India in Goa.

Director Kamila Andini (Yuni) drew on the testimony of sexual assault survivors for this magical realist drama about the patriarchal structures of a Javanese mountain village. Putri Marino plays Pertiwi, who spent 20 years in prison for killing an accomplice to her rape at 16. She comes home to find the men who sent her away now running the village. When she meets a deaf teenage girl whose ordeal echoes her own, Pertiwi rallies her fellow outcasts to bring the abuse to light. The film premiered in Toronto, and Marino shared the best performance prize at the Singapore International Film Festival.

Babak Khajehpasha’s anti-war drama captures the lives and dreams of children and adults in Gaza against the backdrop of an ongoing conflict and crisis with Israel. The film cast child actors of Arab, Iranian, Syrian, and Lebanese backgrounds alongside Iranian performers.

Rather than depict violent battlefield scenes, the Iranian filmmaker focused on the all-too human impact of war on children as they learn fear, death and waiting are part of their everyday lives. The film also strikes a note of hope that love and life can be kept alive during horrific war and strife.

The Iraqi feature, which premiered out of competition in Venice, follows a female Kurdish sniper, Vyan, who returns to Mosul to try and rescue her younger sister. She has survived war by suppressing her emotions. When she learns that her younger sister, missing since the ISIS assault in 2014, may still be alive, she goes all out to rescue her. Mustafa, who was born in Iraqi Kurdistan and fled to Norway as a child, found inspiration for the film from an encounter with a female Peshmerga sniper during the 2014 frontline battles, as female Kurdish fighters countered Islamic State attacks.

A robbery at a rural credit union ends in tragedy, but Damian McCann’s Irish-language thriller works backwards to reveal how three women became involved in the botched operation. Carrie Crowley, Bríd Brennan and Eva-Jane Gaffney star as the unlikely accomplices at the center of a story involving desperation, criminal ambition and a woman on the verge of a breakdown.

The Israeli film, set in 1987, centers on a 12-year-old boy, Boaz, who while preparing for his bar mitzvah is shocked to witness his father having sex in a swimming pool locker room. The boy’s family life is upturned just as the AIDS epidemic takes hold. But a decade later, the father and his older son attempt to reconcile and accept one another.

Rosenthal’s film, titled Independence in Hebrew, premiered at Sundance and won the top best picture award at Israel’s Ophir Awards, automatically making it the country’s Academy Awards entry.

Check out the trailer for Tell Me Everything below.

Paolo Strippoli’s psychological thriller is one of the rare genre romps among the mostly serious dramas in this season’s international Oscar race. Jasmine Trinca plays a housewife so desperate to have the perfect family she makes a faustian bargain with Valeria Bruni Tedeschi’s chain-smoking stranger, triggering a series of events that, over the course of decades, will bring her family crashing down. New Line has already snatched up English remake rights to this twisty crowd pleaser.

Ryusuke Hamaguchi’s first French feature begins as a workplace drama set in a nursing home outside Paris and blossoms into a love story about two women falling for each other’s minds. Marie-Lou (Virginie Efira) asks her overstretched nurses to employ a more humane system of care for residents living with dementia. Watching a play about life in a psychiatric institution, she recognizes her own convictions in the work of its Japanese director, Mari (Tao Okamoto), who is dying of cancer.

The women connect and talk deep into the night, slipping between French and Japanese, and there’s such pleasure in their exchanges that even Mari’s impromptu whiteboard lecture on capitalism feels like a moment of deep emotional connection. Efira and Okamoto shared the best actress prize at Cannes. After winning the international feature Oscar for Drive My Car in 2022, Hamaguchi is again considered a frontrunner this season.

So the mother follows her son about with devotion, hoping her guidance at attention will prove a cure. Abel is a shepherd living on a collective farm in Kazakhstan whose life in thrown into turmoil in the 1990s when, following the collapse of the Soviet Union, collective farms were dismantled and divided up among corrupt leaders. The aging Abel has a choice: play along for a piece of the action or stand up for what he believes is right.

Writer-director Vincho Nchogu serves up a mix of comedy and drama in her feature debut. Sarah Karei plays Star, an orphaned and unmarried 38-year-old woman in a rural village who risks losing her home unless she can prove her family lineage and claim her land deed. Along the way she has to deal with hostile neighbors, shady organizations and the promise of potential romance. Winner of the audience award at Tribeca and the Best First Feature prize at the BFI London Film Festival.

Blerta Basholli takes a coming-of-age story into the increasingly volatile atmosphere of late-1990s Kosovo. Thirteen-year-old Dua is navigating the usual uncertainties of adolescence when an incident turns her into a target within her community. Her friendship with the outspoken Maki offers a new perspective on the world around her, even as the escalating conflict threatens to overwhelm their lives.

Bekzat Pirmatov blends psychological horror, folklore and family drama in this supernatural tale about a grieving woman plagued by a recurring nightmare in which an unseen presence appears to strangle her in her sleep. Seeking an explanation, she turns to a traditional healer who discovers ominous markings on the bodies of pregnant women. Their investigation leads to the ancient Kyrgyz legend of Albarsty, a malevolent female spirit associated with pregnancy and childbirth.

A biographical sports drama from Viesturs Kairišs, who co-wrote Ulya with Kārlis Arnolds Avots, Livia Ulman, and Andris Feldmanis. The Cannes-premiering, black-and-white feature is set in rural Soviet Latvia in 1964, where Uļjana “Ulya” Semjonova’s (Kārlis Arnolds Avots) attracts the attention of basketball scouts due to her exceptional height and strength. She’s pulled from farm life into elite sport in this mise-en-scène-embracing portrait of a Latvian legend.

Lebanon’s Oscar hopeful is a heist thriller about two sisters who concoct an elaborate plan to break into a bank to steal their own money — the cash frozen by the government during the 2019 Lebanese liquidity crisis — to fund a life-saving operation. Director Rami Kodeih co-wrote the film with his wife Nora Mariana inspired by their own experience at the time, when Mariana needed surgery and their savings were inaccessible. George Clooney and Grant Heslov are executive producers for Smokehouse Pictures.

How to Divorce During the War is exactly as it sounds — a high-flying executive Marija (Zygimante Elena Jakstaite) chooses the worst possible moment to inform her struggling filmmaker husband Vytas (Marius Repsys) she wants to split up: just one day before Russia’s invasion of Ukraine. The Vilnius-set, soul-searching drama won director Andrius Blaževičius a directing award upon its Sundance premiere.

This jump scare-heavy psychological horror from debut feature filmmaker Cyrus Neshvad (already an Oscar nominee for 2023 short The Red Suitcase) follows a family in dire straits. With their young son not having much time left, Anne and Greg are ready to sacrifice everything to give him one last chance at salvation. But after crossing an unforgivable moral line, they find themselves trapped in a nightmare where every choice comes with an ever-higher price. Child earned its world premiere at the 2026 Raindance Film Festival.

Woo Ming Jin drew on his own childhood as a twin for this coming-of-age drama about two teenage brothers in a Malaysian coastal town. At 15, Ali is the only person who understands his twin brother Amir’s way of communicating. When their father remarries, he packs the boys off to work in a salted-fish factory. But it’s their shared crush on their new English teacher (Indonesian star Dian Sastrowardoyo) that turns the brothers into rivals. The film premiered at the Toronto International Film Festival.

Żejtune is a visually rich Maltese-language drama from the New York-based Alex Camilleri, led by Michela Farrugia as Mar. When her estranged mother passes away, Mar sees her way out of Malta by selling off her inheritance: three pieces of farmland in the countryside. Mongolia’s struggle to end more than two centuries of Qing rule takes the shape of an espionage thriller in Tamir Bat-Ulzii’s sweeping historical epic. The actor-director casts himself as Namnansuren, the statesman who joined forces with the Buddhist leader Bogd Khan in 1911. The conspirators seek help from Russia while building a network of spies at home. Made to mark the 115th anniversary of Mongolia’s declaration of independence, the film became one of the country’s biggest local hits this year.

His wife (Jeon Do-yeon) befriends a documentary filmmaker (Cho Yeo-jeong) whose tycoon husband (Zo In-sung) patiently supports her interest in workers’ rights.

The Netflix film won Venice’s Silver Lion Grand Jury Prize. Former child star and documentarian Pegah Ahangarani charts over 40 years of turbulent political history in Iran with Rehearsals for a Revolution, crafted entirely around her own family archives. The final product, which won Ahangarani Cannes’ Golden Eye award for best documentary, is a portrait of a country shaped by political repression and in constant hope for radical change.

“I realized, ‘Okay, these are my materials, these are my tools,’” Ahangarani told THR about drawing inspiration from her relatives’ photos, videos and voice recordings. “Because I think these personal archives can stand against the dictatorship [and] the official version of history that the dictators want us to believe.”
