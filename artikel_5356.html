Screen Printing Expert Chris Murray Examines When Embroidery Is Better Than Screen Printing for Custom Apparel

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Chris Murray compares embroidery and screen printing based on durability, fabric type, appearance, and cost.

HONESDALE, Pa., Nov. 25, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- When is embroidery a better choice than screen printing for custom apparel? That question is answered in a HelloNation article featuring insights from Chris Murray of Platform Industries in Honesdale, Pennsylvania.

The article explains that both embroidery and screen printing are widely used for customizing garments, but the better option depends on how the apparel will be used. Embroidery, which uses thread to stitch designs into fabric, offers long-lasting results with a raised, textured look. It is especially well-suited to thicker garments such as polos, jackets, hats, and work uniforms. Because the thread is sewn in rather than applied on top, embroidery resists fading, cracking, and peeling, even with frequent wear and washing.

Screen printing, on the other hand, involves pressing ink directly onto fabric. This allows for large, colorful designs and is typically the more cost-effective option for high-volume orders. The article notes that screen printing works best on lightweight materials like cotton t-shirts and is ideal for bold graphics or event apparel. However, printed designs may wear down over time, especially when washed often or if the curing process was incomplete.

According to the HelloNation article, the decision between embroidery and screen printing often comes down to durability. For businesses in Northeastern Pennsylvania that need custom apparel to last, such as work uniforms or branded jackets, embroidery provides a more resilient finish. Thread maintains its shape and color better than ink, which can be affected by temperature, washing methods, and fabric quality.

Embroidery is also preferred when a more formal or polished look is required. The article explains that a stitched logo conveys professionalism and is commonly used for corporate uniforms and staff apparel. This can influence how a brand is perceived by customers, especially in roles with direct client interaction.

That said, the article outlines clear limitations of embroidery. It does not perform well on thin or stretchy fabrics, where the tension from stitching can cause distortion. Embroidery is also priced by stitch count, so larger or more complex designs can become costly. Logos with gradients, small text, or great detail are often better reproduced with screen printing, which handles intricate digital artwork more effectively.

Screen printing shines in situations where visual impact and cost-efficiency are key. The HelloNation article highlights that schools, sports teams, and community groups often choose screen printing for spirit wear or event t-shirts. It allows for vibrant, eye-catching graphics over a wide area of the garment. While screen printing is generally more affordable for large batches, its durability is not as strong as that of embroidery for heavy-duty use.

The article advises considering the intended use of the garment when selecting a method. Embroidery is ideal for long-term wear and garments that need to endure frequent laundering, such as custom work uniforms in Northeastern Pennsylvania. Screen printing works best for short-term or promotional use, where appearance and volume matter more than extended durability.

Cost considerations also factor in. The article notes that while embroidery may cost more for larger designs, it adds value when lasting quality and professional appearance are important. By contrast, screen printing offers budget-friendly options for temporary or casual apparel.

Ultimately, the HelloNation article concludes that each method has distinct strengths. Embroidery offers superior resilience and a refined look, making it the preferred choice for apparel that must hold up under regular use. Screen printing offers flexibility in design and volume, especially for simpler, short-term projects.
