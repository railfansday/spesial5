Discover this year's Dezeen Awards Designers of the Year

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Dezeen has announced the six winners of Dezeen Awards 2026 Designers of the Year at this evening’s ceremony in London, which include Lina Ghotmeh, Keiji Ashizawa and Yinka Ilori.

Our Designers of the Year awards recognise the best emerging and established talent whose innovative work has made a notable impact on the industry across architecture, interiors and design.

This year, Ghotmeh took home the architect of the year award, while studio duo Shohei Kuma and Elsa Escobedo were named emerging architect of the year.

Interior designer of the year was awarded to Tokyo-based Ashizawa, and Australian designer Yasmine Ghoniem was named emerging interior designer of the year.

London-based Ilori won designer of the year with Stockholm-based Simon Skinner named emerging designer of the year.

Read more about the Designers of the Year below:

Her poetic approach to materials, memory and landscape brings buildings to life with a rare sensitivity to people, place and planet.

Ghotmeh, who is the founder of architecture practice Lina Ghotmeh Architecture, is one of the world’s most sought-after architects. Among her high-profile projects was the 2023 Serpentine Pavilion and she was recently appointed to renovate the British Museum’s Western Range galleries.

“From Beirut to Paris and beyond, Lina Ghotmeh creates architecture that feels both rooted in history and entirely of the future,” said the judges.

“Her poetic approach to materials, memory and landscape brings buildings to life with a rare sensitivity to people, place and planet. We anticipate her studio continuing to go from strength to strength.”

Ghotmeh is known for her sustainable and historically rooted designs, including the Stone Garden apartment block in Beruit, which was named architecture project of the year at Dezeen Awards 2021. She has received multiple accolades, including being named on the Time100 Next list in 2025 and winning the 2023 Great Arab Minds Award.

Working between Japan and Europe, Kuma and Elsa are creating an architectural vocabulary that feels both regionally distinctive and deeply connected to its surroundings.

Emerging architect of the year: Shohei Kuma and Elsa Escobedo

Founded by Japanese architect Shohei Kuma and French‑Spanish architect and urban designer Elsa Escobedo, Kuma&Elsa is an emerging architecture studio with offices in France, Spain and Japan.

“Shohei Kuma and Elsa Escobedo’s emerging practice brings a fresh sense of curiosity to architecture, balancing bold forms and material experimentation with tactility and unexpected gestures,” said the judges.

“Working between Japan and Europe, Kuma and Elsa are creating an architectural vocabulary that feels both regionally distinctive and deeply connected to its surroundings.”

Among the studio’s varied and intriguing work is Nakano House in Japan’s Fukuoka prefecture – an apartment block informed by the design of a traditional Japanese engawa.

His distinct spaces reveal the beauty in restraint, proving that the most powerful interiors are often those that will quietly stand the test of time.

Interior designer of the year: Keiji Ashizawa

Interior designer Ashizawa runs his interdisciplinary, craft-oriented studio based on the ideal of “honest design”. Along with interior spaces, his Tokyo-based studio designs everything from lighting to furniture and products.

“Prolific designer Keiji Ashizawa brings a quiet confidence to interior design, where natural materials, considered details, refined craftsmanship and a sense of calm take centre stage,” said the judges.

“His distinct spaces reveal the beauty in restraint, proving that the most powerful interiors are often those that will quietly stand the test of time.”

Recent work from the studio includes a tea shop in central Shanghai, a holiday home clad in charred cedar in central Japan and a showroom for Japanese furniture brand Karimoku.

Often challenging convention, her interiors are expressive and distinctive, and full of character, warmth and individuality that defy easy categorisation.

Emerging interior designer of the year: Yasmine Ghoniem

Australian designer Ghoniem has found great success since founding her interior studio YSG in 2020 with the aim of creating bold, holistic design experiences.

“Yasmine Ghoniem takes a bold, instinctive approach to interior design, bringing together rich textures, unexpected colour and cultural references,” said the judges.

“Often challenging convention, her interiors are expressive and distinctive, and full of character, warmth and individuality that defy easy categorisation.”

Among her recent – and varied – designs are a wellness hotel in Bali, the revamp of a Sydney apartment, a warehouse conversion in Sydney designed to evoke the Mojave desert and a bar for gin brand Four Pillars.

Rooted in his British-Nigerian heritage, his exuberant work reminds us that design can be as joyful as it is meaningful, bringing people together through shared experiences and bold creative expression.

Ilori designs pavilions, installations, pop-ups, products and furniture, all united by a bold use of colour informed by his Nigerian heritage.

“Yinka Ilori injects design with an irresistible dose of colour, humour and optimism, turning everyday objects and spaces into lively celebrations of culture and community,” said the judges.

“Rooted in his British-Nigerian heritage, his exuberant work reminds us that design can be as joyful as it is meaningful, bringing people together through shared experiences and bold creative expression.”

Ilori has designed numerous pavilions, including the Colour Palace in London and an events space for the Estrel Berlin hotel in Germany. On a smaller scale, he has designed products including a watch for Swiss brand MB&F and his own range of branded homeware.

He also recently created an extensive collection of furniture, fabrics and homeware for Dunelm.

Simon Skinner is a burgeoning talent who brings a fresh perspective to contemporary Scandinavian design, pairing a thoughtful approach to materials with a refined, understated visual language.

Emerging designer of the year: Simon Skinner

Swedish designer Skinner is well-known for his furniture, sculpture and installations that explore how materials can be rethought or reused.

“Simon Skinner is a burgeoning talent who brings a fresh perspective to contemporary Scandinavian design, pairing a thoughtful approach to materials with a refined, understated visual language. His work subverts the familiar, creating distinct new objects that hint at an exciting new direction for design.”

Recent examples of his varied, multidisciplinary work include a range of sculptural lights made from second-hand bowls, ashtrays and cat figurines.
