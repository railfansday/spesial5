2,000-Year-Old Wall Paintings and Mosaics Depicting Animals and Mythological Figures Unearthed at Ancient Roman Site in Mérida, Spain

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Fifty years after the Huerta de Otero site in Mérida, Spain, was first excavated in 1976, archaeologists are still making new discoveries. According to Hoy, a local daily newspaper, excavations have been intermittent, only picking up steam in 2019.

The latest discovery at the approx. 1.2-acre site, of which only 15 percent has been excavated so far, involves mural paintings from around the mid-1st to mid-2nd centuries CE, depicting animals like lions, deer, and various types of birds, along with mythological figures like a satyr. Archaeologists also uncovered a new, well-preserved mosaic to go along with one of Huerta de Otera’s most well-known discoveries, a large, multicolor Medusa mosaic floor uncovered in 2023. Ancient Romans established a colonia, known as Augusta Emerita, in the area around 25 BCE. The site is made up of remains of a sprawling Roman-era residence, baths, and roads. The latest discovery involved excavating more of the remains of an opulent home belonging to a wealthy family. “We’ve encountered a tremendous surprise: extraordinary mural paintings,” Pepe Vargas, who oversees archaeological work at Huerta de Otero, told Hoy. Vargas also noted a courtyard peristyle covered in mosaics.

Excavation work at the site was possible due to a vocational training program, the Barraeca Professional School, overseen by the Mérida City Council. The current cohort included 12 local students trained as archaeological laborers for three months and then worked in the field at Huerta de Otero for nine months. A new group of trainees will likely start in January, according to Hoy.
