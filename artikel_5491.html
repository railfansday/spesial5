FDA declined to approve Summit Therapeutics' lung cancer drug

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The agency said ivonescimab lacks a statistically significant overall survival benefit; Summit said it will continue development

Summit Therapeutics announced on Sunday that the U.S. Food and Drug Administration issued a complete response letter declining to approve ivonescimab, its lung cancer drug, in its present form. Summit sought approval of the drug with platinum-containing chemotherapy for patients with locally advanced or metastatic non-small cell lung cancer who had received prior EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor therapy.

The FDA determined that substantial evidence of effectiveness is not established without a statistically significant improvement in overall survival, the company said. The agency also indicated that a progression-free survival benefit without a significant overall survival benefit does not support a positive benefit-risk profile. Summit included the announcement in an SEC filing.

The application rested on HARMONi, a global Phase 3 trial that compared ivonescimab plus chemotherapy with placebo plus chemotherapy. The trial had two primary endpoints, progression-free survival and overall survival. It met the progression-free survival endpoint. In the primary overall survival analysis, the ivonescimab combination showed a positive trend that fell short of statistical significance, Summit said.

In a post-hoc analysis of longer-term follow-up, the overall survival hazard ratio was 0.76 in the full population, in Asian patients and in western patients, according to the company. Summit said no FDA-approved regimen has shown a statistically significant overall survival benefit in this patient setting.

On safety, 16 patients (7.3%) stopped ivonescimab because of treatment-related adverse events, compared with 11 patients (5.0%) who stopped placebo. Four patients (1.8%) in the ivonescimab arm and five patients (2.3%) in the chemotherapy-only arm died of treatment-related adverse events. Summit said the trial showed no new safety signals.

“We are disappointed for patients and their families awaiting additional treatment options in this setting,” Robert W. Duggan, chairman and co-chief executive of Summit, said in a statement. He said the company’s belief in ivonescimab is unchanged and that it will continue its clinical development program.

Summit is developing ivonescimab in lung, colorectal, urothelial and renal cell cancers. The company said ivonescimab is not approved by any regulator in its license territories, including the U.S. and Europe, and that no PD-1 x VEGF bispecific antibodies are approved in the U.S. Akeso invented the drug, which was first approved in China in May 2024.

Summit obtained ivonescimab from Akeso in 2022 through an agreement valued at as much as $5 billion, which covers the U.S., Canada, Europe and Japan, according to MarketWatch.

AstraZeneca $AZN completed a $2 billion equity investment in Summit last month, giving it a roughly 12% stake while Summit kept full development and commercial rights to the drug. The investment came with a clinical collaboration to test AstraZeneca's sonesitatug vedotin alongside ivonescimab.
