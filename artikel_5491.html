Wendy's rejected Trian's $1.4 billion bid to take the burger chain private

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Wendy's board also adopted a one-year shareholder rights plan as its independent committee called the $7.35-a-share offer too low

Wendy's $WEN rejected a $7.35-a-share cash proposal from an investor group led by Trian Fund Management on Thursday, concluding the offer undervalues the burger chain.

A committee of independent directors, unaffiliated with Trian, reviewed the unsolicited, non-binding bid with outside financial and legal advisers, the company said. The committee's vote was unanimous. It found the proposal "substantially undervalues" Wendy's, does not reflect the value expected from the company's Dave's Way strategic plan, and is not in the best interests of Wendy's and its shareholders.

The investor group also includes Flynn Group LP, BlueFive Capital and Sixth Street Partners. The proposal covers all Wendy's stock the group does not own.

"The Independent Committee thoroughly evaluated this Proposal and unanimously concluded that it substantially undervalues Wendy's standalone prospects and long-term earnings potential," Chairman Art Winkleblack said in a statement. He expressed confidence in the ability of CEO Bob Wright and his executive team to carry out the plan.

Wendy's board adopted a limited-duration shareholder rights plan on Nov. 4, the company said. Rights become exercisable if a person or group acquires beneficial ownership of 15% or more of Wendy's stock, or if a holder of 15% or more buys additional shares, without approval from the board or the independent committee. The plan expires Nov. 3, 2027, and the board may consider ending it sooner.

The board said it weighed the investor group's rejection of a company offer to enter a standstill agreement when it adopted the plan.

Wendy's announced Dave's Way on Thursday alongside its third-quarter results. The multi-year strategy focuses on growing customer traffic and market share while strengthening restaurant profitability. Wendy's stock slipped by under 1% ahead of the opening bell. Trian declined to comment, according to CNBC.

Trian submitted the proposal on Oct. 28, valuing the deal at about $1.4 billion and offering a premium of about 20% to the prior day's closing price. Trian and affiliated parties own about 16.3% of Wendy's stock. Flynn Group operates more than 340 Wendy's restaurants in the U.S. The proposal had no financing condition, and Trian reserved the right to withdraw or change the terms.

Wendy’s board set up the independent committee in February, prompted by a securities filing Trian had made, according to CNBC. Trian had been assembling investors for a take-private bid since August 2025, around the time Wendy’s cut its quarterly dividend in half and withdrew its full-year outlook. Wright became permanent president and CEO in May.
