Airspeed Launches Airbots, Turning WhatsApp and iMessage Into the New Sales Dashboard

A founder's most important sales update rarely arrives in a dashboard. It arrives in a message: the champion at a key account has left, a big deal is about to slip a quarter or a call in an hour needs fresh prep. Software has not caught up with that habit. Salespeople still spend only 40% of their time selling, while the rest disappears into research, admin and tools that wait to be opened.

The interface is changing fast. Gartner expects 60% of B2B seller work to run through conversational user interfaces by 2028, up from under 5% in 2023, as sellers move from clicking through dashboards to asking an AI and having it act. More than half of sellers have already used AI agents, while nearly nine in ten plan to by 2027.

Airspeed, the AI-native go-to-market platform founded by former DeepMind researchers, has opened early access to Airbots, an always-on AI chief of staff that lives in WhatsApp and iMessage. Airbots connects to the tools teams already use, spots the deals, calls and follow-ups that need attention and offers to handle them before anyone asks. There is nothing to set up, since it learns how each user and company sells from every reply and correction.

Gartner's forecast marks a shift in how sales software works. Conversational experiences are expected to handle up to 15% of customer meeting prep, 14% of sales planning and 14% of deal negotiation, with the share of seller work running through chat rising roughly twelvefold in five years (our analysis). Gartner also expects a third of chief revenue officers to fund dedicated teams to run generative AI in sales.

The channel matters as much as the model. WhatsApp alone has more than 3 billion monthly users, while founders, investors and salespeople already run much of their working day from their phones. An assistant that sends updates where people already read their messages removes the need to open one more app.

Salesforce's 2026 survey of 4,050 sales professionals found that sellers spend only 40% of their time selling, while 60% goes to research, data entry, admin and internal work (our arithmetic). Sellers expect AI to cut prospect research time by 34% and email drafting time by 36% once it is fully in place, while top performers are 1.7 times more likely than underperformers to use AI agents for prospecting.

More tools have not always meant less work. 51% of sales leaders using AI say disconnected systems slow their projects, so each new bot can add another feed to check. Airspeed's view is that teams need one assistant that sees across the stack, not one bot per job.

Some of the most important signals in sales come from outside the CRM. Lusha's 2026 data found that 12.6% of US sales leaders change roles each year, while 21.7% of UK sales leaders changed roles over twelve months. Its tracking logged about 13,600 company changes every working day in the first half of 2026.

At about 1% a month, a team with 1,000 contacts on active deals should expect around 31 of them to go stale each quarter. When the departing contact is a deal's champion, the internal advocate who pushes the purchase through, the deal can stall quietly unless someone notices the change and finds the replacement in time.

Airbots replaces separate bots for separate jobs with one proactive assistant. Users choose which tools to connect, from Google Workspace to Slack and Salesforce. They then receive tailored text updates about what is happening and when. They can reply to ask a question or hand off a task, just like messaging a colleague.

A typical morning message tells a seller they have five calls that day, warns that their champion at a key account left last week, says Airbots has already found her replacement and offers to draft an introduction before 2pm. Behind each message sits a team of agents that keeps working across email, CRM and connected tools without waiting to be asked.

Airbots learns as it goes. It needs no configuration, since it picks up each user's selling style and their company's playbook from every reply and correction until it works like a second version of the user. The more tools it can see, the more it can do, from spotting a lost Salesforce deal worth reopening to checking that every large deal sits in the right forecast category across HubSpot and Gong.

Users always stay in control. Airbots never sends an email unless a user asks it to, while users choose which tools it can access and can disconnect any of them at any time. Airspeed is SOC 2 Type II compliant, an independent standard for how a company protects customer data.

For sales reps, Airbots points out when the deals they are focused on will not get them to quota, finds a new stakeholder to re-engage stalled deals and spots accounts they have not touched this quarter, with a reason to reach out and a draft ready to send. Sales leaders see where the quarter is likely to land and which deals could slip, while founders can set their priorities for the week and let Airbots keep them on track as new requests come in.

The assistant reaches beyond the sales team. Marketers can ask whether a campaign is coming up on sales calls, while post-sale teams can get suggested next actions, look up what a customer pays straight from the contract, catch hanging threads and refresh call prep at the last minute. When a contract champion leaves, Airbots can flag it immediately and find the new contact on LinkedIn.

Airspeed was founded in 2022 by chief executive Adam Liska and chief technology officer Devang Agrawal, who left Google DeepMind together to start the company in London. Liska holds a PhD in brain and cognitive sciences and previously worked at Spotify and Facebook, while Agrawal studied engineering at Cambridge.

Airspeed describes itself as the execution layer for revenue teams, turning what is said in calls, emails and meetings into the next action that moves a deal forward, from CRM updates to deal risk to coaching. It serves more than 180 customers in 20 countries, including Persona, Qdrant and Pricefx. The company is headquartered in London and New York.

The company raised a $20 million Series A in June 2026 led by DN Capital, taking total funding past $25 million. Its investors include Framework Venture Partners, Vi Partners, Atlassian Ventures, Point72 Ventures and Creator Fund.

Growth has been strong. Airspeed's revenue grew fourfold year on year at the time of its Series A, while customers built thousands of custom agents in the first four months of 2026 and monthly agent runs nearly tripled between January and April. Airbots extends that platform from the desktop into the place many users spend most of their day, their messaging apps.

Airbots is available in early access, with a waitlist open at Airbots.ai. Founders, investors and sales teams can join to connect their tools and start receiving updates in WhatsApp or iMessage.

The direction of sales software is clear. Gartner expects most seller work to run through conversation within a few years, while sellers are adopting AI agents faster than their tools can coordinate them. Airspeed is building one proactive assistant that knows the whole pipeline and speaks up first, giving every seller and founder a chief of staff that lives where they already work.
