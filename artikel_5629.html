The final rule, issued by the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services Office for Civil Rights (OCR), strengthens and clarifies the right of an individual to access their protected health information and reduces regulatory and administrative burdens, the agency said.

The proposed rule was issued more than five years ago, in January 2021. An unpublished version of the final rule is available here.

Under the final rule, healthcare organizations must respond faster to patient requests for copies of their health records. HHS said covered entities will have no more than 15 calendar days to respond to a request, reduced from the current 30-day period. Organizations are allowed one extension of no more than 30 calendar days, HHS said in the final rule.

The rule also establishes that patients can access their protected health information through personal health apps when the covered entity has the technical capacity to deliver the records in that form and format.

The rule also expands circumstances when patients must receive copies of their health records for free, requires advance notice of fees and modifies identity verification requirements.

To make it easier for healthcare organizations to share data for care coordination, the final rule clarifies that health plans may use and disclose protected health information for care coordination and case management for individuals. The rule also expressly permits covered entities to disclose PHI to social services agencies, community-based organizations and similar third parties for care coordination and case management for individuals.

“Americans should never have to fight for access to their own health records,” said HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy, Jr in a statement. “Under President Trump’s leadership, we are putting patients in control, requiring faster access to their information, and cutting red tape that gets in the way of coordinated care. This rule modernizes HIPAA to serve patients and help providers spend more time delivering care and less time navigating bureaucracy.”

Provisions in the final rule will become effective 60 days after publication in the Federal Register, and compliance will be required 180 days after that date, according to HHS in a fact sheet.
