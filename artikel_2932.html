The stealing, running Mavs are the perfect cast for a high-IQ duo that could change basketball

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The Mavericks just beat a very good Denver Nuggets team through aggressive defense, forcing steals, and running on offense. It is indeed a new day in Dallas.

Kyrie Irving, a player that has pretty much seen it all by now, didn't even have an efficient night (5/19 on field goal attempts, 1/8 from three). He did, however, lead the team to a victory that shows the whole league that the Dallas Mavericks now are the exact opposite of who they used to be.

In a gesture as moving as it was telling, Kyrie showed the world that he and Cooper Flagg have the rarest of connections. This is a high-IQ duo who could change the trajectory of what basketball looks like. And they may be on the way to doing that as we speak.

It is not only the gesture that's important here, it's the ultimate look away pass and the back door cut by Flagg that shows a connection between the two, usually built through years of ups and downs. It was almost like they could read each other's minds. This is what real chemistry looks like.

Kirk Henderson said it best: "There are lookaway passes, then there's something like this where I feel as if Kyrie's head never even moved the direction where Coop cut to."

Jamal Crawford interpreted the gesture as "me and you", and either way this is a gesture of connection, we – as in us – we got this. And even though Kyrie meant Cooper, we all feel it a little bit.

You may be watching from across the globe, you may not even be a Mavs fan, but anybody watching, knowing what this team and city have gone through recently, felt it.

Because no one has worked harder to get the Dallas Mavericks back together than Kyrie Irving. After it was torn apart, he did everything he could to hold it together. He mentored, he was everyone's vet, he led, he coached when injured – he showed the way and the light in the dark times.

And Dallas is reaping the benefits right now. That is also why it felt so touching, seeing the vet who worked so hard to hold it all together, make this gesture to the young superstar with the highest ceiling imaginable.

They took away his running mate, his brother and a genuine friendship and on-court chemistry, resulting in the most negative period in Dallas, in which he forced himself to play too much and carry more than he should have. His resulting ACL injury took him away from his craft for more than a year, and now – finally – his hard work is really paying off. Kyrie Irving deserves this.

And despite the spiritual experience it can be to watch Irving play, especially when he has chemistry with his teammates, he didn't carry the team to this extraordinary win. Neither did his co-star Flagg really.

It was a cast of teammates who decided to outwork, outhustle and believe in their connection, along with a well-prepared coach, who had clearly done a lot of work to get this team ready. It was no easy feat. But the Mavs made it look easy.

They had 13 steals. The league leader averages 10.6 (Los Angeles Lakers). The Mavs average 9.44 this season, but led by the surprising hero of the last games, Caleb Martin, with five, they kept the pressure on the passing lanes all game, stressing the Nuggets in a way we don't see every day. Not only that, they had 16 steals last game versus Utah, and a whopping 50 steals the last four games (which all happen to be wins).

Martin started this game on fire on both ends, secured the first six points by knocking down two threes in a row and continued to force five steals in the first half, constantly the first to run in transition.

This is exactly the leadership a team needs from a role player next to superstars, and with Zaccharie Risacher next to him, the two have the potential to cause havoc defensively on every team in the league, followed by easy transition points, if they keep this up.

Caleb Martin explained the game plan in the half:

"High sense of urgency. A high level of respect for a great team," he said during the broadcast.

He went on to explain that steals were an emphasis, as well as just being active. "The more active we are," Martin said, "sense of urgency, the better a team we are, positive things happen".

And not even interview questions were about to distract Caleb Martin, who is on a mission in Dallas right now.

When asked in the half whether the loud colors of the court, which are usually a little on the bright side during Cup games, was giving him a headache, too, he answered deadpan:

"I don't know, I'm trying to win a game right now."
