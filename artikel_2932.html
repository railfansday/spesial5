5-Year Outlook: Can Domino's Pizza Deliver?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

This under-the-radar consumer discretionary stock boasts a Superscore of 85 from our Hidden Gems Primary database, part of The Motley Fool's Moneyball Database system. Here's why.

By Sarah Sidlow – Updated Nov 24, 2026 at 6:43PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Domino's Pizza ›

Picture a local franchise owner managing a dozen units across a city. She relies on a centralized supply chain that delivers dough and ingredients overnight, a digital ordering system that processes over 85% of her transactions, and a consistent, predictable royalty structure. This is the operational engine behind Domino's Pizza (DPZ +0.36%), a global food giant that has scaled to over 22,700 locations since its founding in 1960. As of Nov. 19, 2026, the stock trades at $352.12, having returned -12% over the past year as the business navigates a complex environment of slowing consumer discretionary spending and intense competition.

Our proprietary Hidden Gems scoring system assigns Domino's Pizza an overall Superscore of 85 out of 100, placing it in the Strong category. The Superscore is an AI-powered score that evaluates a company's overall strength by combining financial performance, product market position, technological capabilities, leadership quality, and relative valuation. It represents the unification of all our scores into a single score for public companies, with five rating bands: Exceptional (90-100), Strong (75-89), Above Average (60-74), Average (40-59), and Cautious (0-39). An 85 places the company in the top ~4% of every company we score. This article pairs the reasons the score is high with the risks that keep it from rising further, so you can decide whether the business fits your investment goals.

The company maintains a high return on net tangible assets, which measures how much profit the business earns for every dollar of its hard tangible assets. This efficiency means that even moderate revenue growth can translate into meaningful returns for shareholders, partially offsetting the risks of a higher valuation multiple in a competitive market.

Domino's asset-light, franchise-heavy business model has been a recipe for success over the past few decades. Since 2006, the pizza stock has delivered more than 2,600% gains for investors as the company expanded its geographic footprint and leveraged technology to serve customers more efficiently. It has grown its dividend for 12 consecutive years, now paying out 2.23%. These characteristics are all reasons Domino's has been a solid defensive investment, but where could the stock go from here?

Domino's next phase of growth will likely rhyme with its first. Geographic expansion, this time internationally, will be critical. The company already operates in more than 90 international markets, with more international stores than U.S. locations. Markets like China and India still offer significant room to expand. But international growth also adds operational and financial complexity. Digital leadership will also continue to drive DPZ's performance. The company that brought us the "pizza tracker" now relies heavily on digital orders, which typically carry higher average tickets and lower error rates. Continued innovation and investment in this area will be important for its next five years of growth.

Domino's provides a tantalizing combination of value, convenience, and consistency. And growth is still on the table: According to Mordor Intelligence, the pizza foodservice market is expected to grow at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of more than 10% through 2031, with Asia-Pacific expected to grow even faster. Meanwhile, continued share buybacks and dividend payments will reward patient investors while they wait -- no tracker required.
