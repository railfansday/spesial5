Taylor Swift’s CMA Return Helped Disney Sell More Ads

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Taylor Swift doesn’t work for the Walt Disney Company, but she may just have played a key role in helping the company carve out a little extra revenue.

Swift hasn’t performed on the Country Music Association Awards, televised on ABC, since 2013. On Friday, after the musician announced she would return to the program, Disney’s sales force opened some new streaming ad inventory tied to the event. “We were sold out.  We opened up additional opportunities because of Taylor, and we got additional interest, and we were seeing brands come in” all this week, says John Campbell, senior vice president of entertainment and streaming solutions at Disney Advertising, during a recent interview. “We can create demand 24 hours before the show.”

Such activity may be encouraging not only for Disney, but for the broader media sector, which is placing a growing emphasis on live events in a bid to keep the large audiences advertisers crave coming to its screens. Disney has in the last year put together a large schedule of sporting events and spectacles, including the CMAs, the College Football Playoffs, Dick Clark’s New Year’s Rockin’ Eve, the Grammys and the 2027 telecast of the Super Bowl — a string of big-ticket events that come with a large amount of advertising time that needs to be monetized.

Disney’s Campbell says ad revenue tied to the CMAs is up 20% compared to 2025’s telecast, and cites Madison Avenue’s desire for tentpole extravaganzas that are different from the rest of the TV schedule, as well as growing interest in creating bespoke promotions for social media and streaming video, along with traditional TV.

General Motors’ Chevrolet is returning for its tenth year as a presenting sponsor of the program. Its vehicles will be seen during the program, and country music artist Zach Top will appear in moments that celebrate country music and military service, among other things. Top will give fans a behind-the-scenes look at the creation of some of the vignettes he’s in via ABC and CMA social channels.

In a new venture, retailer Bed Bath & Bodyworks will be the presenting sponsor of live CMAs coverage on TikTok. Country star Lauren Alaina and digital creator Haley Baylee will serve up real-time content to fans, including branded moments, host integrations, interactive polls, and social engagement opportunities. “We know that fans want to interact with the show in so many different ways,” says Campbell, who notes this new type of sponsorship “provides additional content and more value for fans.”

Financial-services provider Ally is also new to the program’s ad roster. A new campaign will feature entrepreneur and reality show star Kristin Cavallari, and will surface during the show, on social and streaming venues and via linear TV commercials.

Advertisers interested in live specials are eager “to go beyond just the 30-second spot,” says Campbell. He believes new opportunities that weave marketing alongside musical content is driving “increases in overall commitments to our live specials.” There are, Disney no doubt hopes, a lot more to come.
