Red-Wine-Braised Short Ribs

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

These red-wine-braised short ribs have fans. “This has been my go-to recipe for short ribs for as long as I can remember,” says one commenter. “It never fails.” It’s the kind of special-occasion dish you make once and return to forever.

Flavor builds in layers: The bone-in beef is browned, then the onions, carrots, and celery cook in its drippings. A full bottle of dry red wine reduces by half before the broth goes in; a whole head of garlic and plenty of fresh herbs round out the braise. After that, a few hours in the oven turn the meat fall-off-the-bone tender.

Even better: You can make the ribs a day ahead, leaving little to do before dinner but reheat them. Serve over mashed potatoes, soft polenta, or wide egg noodles to soak up every drop of the rich sauce.

Look for meaty, bone-in English-cut short ribs, with a single rib bone beneath each piece of meat. Ask your butcher to cut them crosswise into 2" lengths—you’re not expected to saw through the bones at home. Avoid the thin strips sold for grilling.

What’s the best wine for braised short ribs?

Choose a dry red wine you’d enjoy drinking. Cabernet Sauvignon is the recipe’s first choice, but Merlot or a Rhône-style red also fits the bill. There’s no need to splurge; save your special bottle for the table.

For a more rustic approach, pluck out the herbs and garlic skins (leave the cloves behind) and use an immersion blender to purée the cooked vegetables into the sauce.

Start checking after 2 hours in the oven. A fork should slide into the meat easily, and a gentle twist should pull it apart. If the meat still resists, keep braising. The listed cooking time is a guide, not a hard stop.

Can I make these short ribs ahead, and how should I reheat them?

Yes. Finish cooking the ribs and strain the sauce, then refrigerate the ribs with their sauce in shallow containers within 2 hours of cooking. The next day, lift off the hardened fat. Transfer the ribs and sauce to an ovenproof pot or baking dish, cover, and reheat in a 350° oven until hot throughout—the meat should reach 165° in the center.

Staub Enameled Cast Iron Round Dutch Oven 7-Qt.

Preheat oven to 350°. Season 5 lb. bone-in beef short ribs, cut crosswise into 2" pieces by your butcher, with kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper. Heat 3 Tbsp. vegetable oil in a large Dutch oven or other heavy pot over medium-high. Working in 2 batches, cook short ribs, turning occasionally, until browned on all sides, about 8 minutes per batch. Transfer to a plate. Pour off all but 3 Tbsp. drippings from pot.

Cook 3 medium onions, chopped, 3 medium carrots, peeled, chopped, and 2 celery stalks, chopped, in same pot over medium-high heat, stirring often, until onions are browned, about 5 minutes. Add 3 Tbsp. all-purpose flour and 1 Tbsp. tomato paste and cook, stirring constantly, until combined and deep red, 2–3 minutes.

Stir in one 750-ml bottle dry red wine (preferably Cabernet Sauvignon), then add short ribs along with any juices that have accumulated on the plate. Bring to a boil; reduce heat to medium and simmer until wine is reduced by half, about 25 minutes. Add 1 head of garlic, halved crosswise, 10 sprigs parsley, 8 sprigs thyme, 4 sprigs oregano, 2 sprigs rosemary, and 2 fresh or dried bay leaves; stir in 4 cups low-sodium beef broth. Bring to a boil, cover, and transfer pot to oven. Braise until short ribs are tender, 2–2½ hours.

Uncover pot and transfer short ribs to a platter. Strain sauce through a fine-mesh sieve into a heatproof measuring glass; spoon fat from surface and discard. Season sauce with salt and pepper. Arrange ribs in shallow bowls, over mashed potatoes if desired, and spoon sauce over.
