Consistently inconsistent? Ten things we've learnt about Alonso's Chelsea so far

Xabi Alonso's Chelsea project is 10 Premier League games in. Here's what we've learnt so far...

Goalkeeper has been a long-standing problem position at Chelsea. Xabi Alonso spoke positively about his keeping options after taking charge but a night to forget for Robert Sanchez at Fulham in the Spaniard's first game in charge pushed the club into action. The 2022 World Cup winner Emiliano Martinez seemed like a no-brainer. However, the 34-year-old let three goals in on his debut against Brighton and hasn't looked totally assured.

An injury to the Argentine has created an opportunity for the highly rated Mike Penders. A calamitous own goal for the 21-year-old against Spurs was a terrible way to mark his first Premier League start but his response has been impressive. It is food for thought that Penders has a positive Expected Goals Against vs Actual Goals Conceded ratio (4.49 vs only three let in). Martinez's return is negative - 11 goals conceded from shots worth 9.17.

Petr Cech was just 22 when he made his Chelsea debut and went on to achieve legendary status. Alonso evidently didn't believe the Belgian international was ready at the start of this campaign but Penders has certainly given his manager something to consider.

Chelsea have been consistently inconsistent so far. They bounced back from a poor draw with Hull and even worse thumping at Brentford by dishing out a 5-1 thrashing of Bournemouth, while losses to Spurs and Sunderland have tempered expectations. Three games in, their trip to the Emirates was billed as an opportunity to show they could be a real threat - potentially even title contenders. Instead, after 10 games, they're 11 points behind Arsenal and unlikely to trouble the Premier League champions.

They've won back-to-back league games just twice and have yet to demonstrate evidence of a clear identity forming. Injuries to key personnel have hampered Alonso. The Chelsea boss has continually stated his side will look different in May, compared to what we are witnessing now. However, the journey and the eventual destination isn't obvious. Yet in spite of four league defeats already, the Blues are relatively well-placed for Champions League qualification.

We'll come onto Joao Pedro's brilliant start but Alonso's curious approach to his recent injury issue has sparked debate. Chelsea started without a recognised striker against Spurs, Man Utd and Sunderland, with Danny Welbeck given just a few minutes off the bench in the first two of those games before going on after an hour as Chelsea chased an equaliser at the Stadium of Light. With summer signings Welbeck and Emmanuel Emegha not trusted and Liam Delap, Nicolas Jackson and Marc Guiu sold, Chelsea look short up front.

Morgan Rogers and Pedro Neto have been used as false nines. The former Aston Villa man offers a presence when deployed there but Neto's usage is difficult to comprehend. Welbeck has been ineffective in starts against Brentford and Liverpool in the cup but Chelsea lack a focal point without a natural forward. They also have the biggest positive difference between goals scored (19) and Expected Goals (15.9). While that is the result of some stunning strikes, it also raises questions about the long-term sustainability of their attacking threat after blanks against Tottenham and Sunderland.

In pre-season, there was much debate about whether Alonso would utilise the back-three set-up which served him so well at Bayer Leverkusen or go with a four as he did at Real Madrid. The answer is: a bit of both. There have been six matches when Chelsea have started with a four and four when they've gone with a three.

Alonso has been keen to stress that in-play shape is more significant than how the names are laid out in a graphic but his variations suggest he is still searching for the right formula - and Chelsea's inconsistent performances and results reflect that uncertainty. Levi Colwill and Wesley Fofana have failed to convince at the start of this season and Maxence Lacroix is not injured. Academy graduate Josh Acheampong has been the only major positive defensively so far. He has mightily impressed.

Beyond formations, preconceptions about the style of play under Alonso have also been challenged in these opening 10 games. In half of them, Chelsea have had more possession than their opponents and bossed the ball. But it's been a different story in the other matches.

In fact, it was stunning to see Chelsea register just 26 per cent possession at home to Brighton in a game they won 4-3. They had just 31 per cent of the ball at home to Spurs. A performance which deeply frustrated the home support.

Part of the plan or a stat which speaks to deeper issues around controlling matches and winning midfield battles? Alonso has admitted that he wants his team to be better with the ball, a message he delivered at half-time to spark an upturn in performance against Manchester United.

Alonso was a midfield maestro in his playing days and he has made no secret of declaring the central area as key to his team's success. But he has tried six different midfield combinations so far. Moises Caicedo's early-season injury problem was unhelpful, as was the subsequent Reece James lay-off. Romeo Lavia has at least been fit and impressed, while Jordan Henderson seems to have struck up a relationship with Caicedo. All three started in the defeat at Sunderland. It remains, though, an area of debate.

The sale of Enzo Fernandez late in the transfer window without an adequate replacement coming in has proved to be a problem. A January priority?

Such is the modern game, transfers of set-piece coaches is now a thing, and Chelsea's capture of Aston Villa's Austin MacPhee - an A-lister in the category - this summer drew attention. The results so far are best described as mixed. Only Brighton have scored more than Chelsea's six from set-piece situations, before you add in Cole Palmer's penalty against Man Utd. But no side has conceded more than their six from set-pieces, with Enzo Le Fee's penalty for Sunderland making the dead-ball for/against tally level.

Their resilient display at Everton suggested progress had been made. However, Alonso, MacPhee and Chelsea have work to do if they are to master this important department at both ends of the pitch.

With JP Morgan (are we sticking with that name for Chelsea's first-choice front three?) making a flying start to the season, the knock-on effect has been reduced opportunities for Estevao. In fact, Alonso's persistent reluctance to throw the teenage Brazilian into the action has become a frustrating issue for Chelsea supporters, given the electric winger is a firm fan favourite. Like fellow 19-year-old Geovany Quenda - who signed an eight-year contract in the summer - Estevao has made just one Premier League start so far.

The absence of European football hasn't helped, with Chelsea's number of matches reduced this term, and Estevao was out to prove a point when he was given his shot against Man Utd. Despite his best efforts, he was unable to add to the eight goals he scored last season. A narrow offside call prevented him from adding to his tally.

He returned to the bench for the defeat at Sunderland. It's a situation Alonso needs to manage. Estevao has been seen as "untouchable" and unavailable for sale. However, the situation seems unsustainable if the Brazilian spends more time warming the bench than racing up the touchline.

The Chelsea No 9 jersey has weighed heavy on its wearer for some time. From Radamel Falcao and Alvaro Morata a decade ago through to Romelu Lukaku, Pierre-Emerick Aubameyang and Liam Delap in more recent seasons, strikers have failed to repeat their prolificacy at other clubs when they've been marked out as Chelsea's main forward. But Joao Pedro has bucked the trend.

He has five goals and three assists in his six Premier League appearances so far and Chelsea's attack looks far better connected with him leading the line. Joao Pedro has emerged as perhaps Alonso's most important player. They need to keep him fit. Chelsea have scored just two goals in the four league matches he has missed - both coming in the win over United.

Cole Palmer and Morgan Rogers are the only players to have started all 10 Chelsea Premier League games for Alonso.

Chelsea No 10 Palmer has shown glimpses of his best form and looks close to full fitness. He appears to have found his spark after his struggles last season, although his returns have been inconsistent. His three goals and three assists only tell part of the story. He looks a different player when he has Joao Pedro ahead of him. At times he has looked unplayable. Without the Brazilian striker, he has struggled to impact games as much. He let his frustration show with his sending-off against Spurs.

His status as an England international remains a major topic of debate. A small injury forced him to withdraw from Thomas Tuchel's squad in September. He appears to have fallen down Tuchel's pecking order as a result.

Palmer's good friend Rogers has fiercely defended his desire to play for England and on the pitch they have worked well in tandem, too.
