'Standout candidate Murray should be Davis Cup captain one day'

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Great Britain's outgoing Davis Cup captain Leon Smith hopes "standout candidate" Andy Murray will one day take the role he is vacating after more than 16 years.

Smith took over from John Lloyd in 2010 following a humiliating loss to Lithuania that left Britain facing relegation to the bottom level of the historic competition.

Harnessing the talents and commitment of Murray, who he coached as a junior, Smith led Britain back to the top tier and, only five years later, to a first title since 1936 when they defeated Belgium in 2015.

Murray won all 11 singles and doubles rubbers he contested in 2015, as well as 33 out of 36 in singles in the competition overall.

The 39-year-old, who is currently head coach to Jack Draper, paid tribute to Smith, saying: "Winning the Davis Cup in 2015 with Leon as captain was one of the proudest moments of my career.

"He did a great job of bringing everyone together and making sure we all felt we had a part to play.

"I've obviously known Leon a long time, since he coached me as a kid. I think the most fun we had together was when I was playing Davis Cup and Leon was on the bench.

"I wish Leon all the best as he steps down, and good luck to the new captain."

Smith, whose final tournament will be next week's Finals in Bologna, where Great Britain face Germany in the quarter-finals, hopes Murray will one day take the role.

"I think it is timing for him, he's busy with his life," Smith said. "He obviously is the standout candidate, isn't he? For all the attributes and more.

"Hopefully it's something he's interested in the future.

"The Davis Cup, it's not just the ties itself. It's being at the events. It's supporting the players and their personal coaches on their journeys.

"And it's also the Olympics and his history in the Olympics is second to none.

"So I do hope it's something he does in the future, but I respect that it's not for him right now, and I can understand it.

"But I really hope at some point in the future that he does take it on."

Speaking on Sky Sports Tennis, Andy's older brother Jamie discussed the role with Gigi Salmon and Tim Henman.

The seven-time Grand Slam doubles champion said: "I think that's an interesting way to see how they're going to go about it. I think there's probably quite a few people that would quite fancy it.

"I think Greg [Rusedski] as well, with everything that he's done in his career, would definitely deserve to be in that conversation.

"I'm sure Andy will get asked about his interest levels. He certainly should be given everything that he's done for tennis in our country."

Henman reflected on Smith's tenure by saying: "A big part of that role was making sure that Andy turned up to play and then giving him the environment to go out and perform as well as he could.

"Andy at that time was loving playing Davis Cup, representing his country and the team went from strength to strength.

"When Leon started, they were at the bottom of professional tennis, but they worked their way up to lifting the trophy in Belgium."
