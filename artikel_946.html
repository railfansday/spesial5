Khawaja: Entire Australia team knew about ball-tampering scheme

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Usman Khawaja says “all of us” knew about ‘Sandpapergate’ plot in Cape Town Test eight years ago and not just Steve Smith, David Warner and Cameron Bancroft as previously asserted by Cricket Australia; former batter speaking ahead of the release of his new book

Former Australia opener Usman Khawaja has claimed that the side's entire dressing room knew about the ball-tampering scheme in Cape Town in 2018.

That declaration, made by Khawaja in the Sydney Morning Herald ahead of the release of his book 'Sabr', is at odds with Cricket Australia's assertion that only Steve Smith, David Warner and Cameron Bancroft were aware of the plot to use sandpaper to alter the condition of the ball in the third Test against South Africa eight years ago.

Khawaja, who retired from international duty after Australia's 4-1 Ashes thrashing of England last winter, said: "It was all of us. Not the batters, not the bowlers, not the staff. All of us."

Captain-at-the-time Smith, Bancroft and Warner, who was found to have been the architect of the 'Sandpapergate' plot, all received bans after the incident, with Smith and Warner suspended for one year and Bancroft nine months.

Bowlers Pat Cummins, Mitchell Starc, Josh Hazlewood and Nathan Lyon issued a joint statement in 2021 insisting they had no prior knowledge of the ball-tampering.

That came after Bancroft, who had aimed to conceal the sandpaper down his trousers after using it on the ball to try and generate reverse swing, replied "I think it's probably self-explanatory" when asked in a newspaper interview whether the bowling attack knew about the scandal.

Head coach Darren Lehmann tearfully resigned in the aftermath and, following Khawaja's new claims, he said: "I don't think everyone would have known [about the use of sandpaper]."

He told ABC Sport's Summer Grandstand: "You do know ball tampering is going on because it's been happening for 150 years. It's just how it's being done, and how would you know to that extent? That's an individual case by case, to be fair."

Cummins, Starc and Hazlewood have all been rested from this month's white-ball series at home to England while Lyon is not in the limited-overs set-up, so the quartet may not be quizzed on Khawaja's claims just yet.

Khawaja wrote in his book that ball-tampering plans were first discussed in the second Test of the 2018 series in Port Elizabeth, now Gqeberha, and that he urged the team to take collective responsibility after the scandal was exposed.

He continued: "In an ideal world, I don't want to do this.

"I don't want to be the one who comes out and talks about the truth. The issue is that I genuinely thought after eight years the truth would have been out by now.

"Maybe I thought Davey [Warner] would have exposed it, or someone else would have talked about it more.

"There has been a great deal of speculation in the eight years since the 2018 Cape Town Test, including extensive media coverage throughout last month's series in South Africa.

"During this time there has been the ongoing opportunity to present any relevant information, and this remains the case.

"All relevant information presented to CA to date has been properly considered and we remain comfortable with the findings of our integrity process."

"At the end of the day, I think it's the right thing to do, particularly for those three guys. And I think most of the guys in the team, at some level, will be relieved we can finally move on.

"Even in the change rooms when I was talking to them after the game. I said: 'Take this as a team. We did this as a team. We knew what was going on. We have to take it as a team'.

"I was always of that stance. Obviously, it didn't work that way, and those three guys got crucified.

"I've seen Davey over the last eight years just get nailed bad over and over again. It's taken a big man not to say anything and just to stay quiet. He's just copped it and copped it and copped it.

"I've always felt like there's more to life than just cricket, and these guys, in some respect, deserve that justice. And it just doesn't feel like anyone's going to give it to them."

Reflecting on the first chats about ball-tampering, which led to sandpaper being seen as an "effective agent", Khawaja said: "It was at this point Smith essentially threw his hands in the air and said he didn't want to know."

The left-hander also expressed his surprise that he was never asked to give evidence despite playing in the Cape Town Test and sitting next to Bancroft in the dressing room.

Khawaja scored over 6,000 runs for Australia's Test team in 88 matches between 2011 and 2026, notching 16 hundreds and 28 fifties with a best of 232 in Sri Lanka in January 2025.

He feels good came out of 'Sandpapergate' in that the side found "humility" in the aftermath.

Khawaja added: "The best things come out of the worst times. I genuinely believe, for the Australian cricket team, the best thing that came out of Sandpaper is we found humility again.

"JL [Justin Langer] came in as coach and he was honestly probably the best coach we could've had at that time. He put humility back in the Australian cricket team. It was just lovely to see. It was beautiful.

"I really enjoyed it. I really enjoyed seeing where the Australian team went from that point onwards and obviously Cummins, who ended up becoming captain, was a part of that process and journey."

Speaking ahead of Australia's recent Test series in South Africa, which they lost 2-1, Cummins said he had given Khawaja his blessing to revisit the past after taking a phone call from his ex-team-mate.

"(I said) tell your story. It's fine," he said.

"It's obviously a long time ago and all the investigations, everything, was done back then. We answered every question a million times. It was dealt with. All I can say is that as a group, we have moved on."
