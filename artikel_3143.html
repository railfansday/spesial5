The Eight Sleep Pod 6 comes in two parts: a control tower and a cover... plus an app but no mattress. The cover is fitted with a series of water tubes (to heat and cool) and sensors (to track the temperature) which feed back to the control hub to keep your sleep climate even throughout the night.

There are two things worth calling out about the Eight Sleep Pod 6. First, the control tower. It's small and compact but it does still take up vital floorspace. However, it's also whisper quiet. Apart from the occasional jump scare when it starts calibrating itself during the working day, I'd hardly know it was there.

Second, the mattress cover. It doesn't have a huge impact on the feel of your bed but I can tell it's there. I'm sleeping on the Leesa Original mattress and with the Eight Sleep on, the surface has less 'sink'. That's not a deal breaker for me but if you love a bed that 'hugs' the body, the Eight Sleep might get in the way.

Temperature is an important signal to our circadian rhythms, as we naturally cool at night. Increased temperatures — whether through environment our physiological causes — can disrupt your sleep quality.

That's why active climate control is the main focus of the Eight Sleep Pod 6. Its raison d'être, if you will. The integrated water tubes are at work throughout the night to subtly adjust the temperature of the surface.

I'm not a regular hot sleeper but hormonal shifts mean a few times a month I can expect a stuffy morning. Since using the Eight Sleep, I haven't woken up sweaty once. I have on occasion felt my body temperature rise — but the Eight Sleep responds with a flood of cool.

Before bed, I've been enjoying the opposite. The Eight Sleep gently warms in the minutes leading up to my bedtime, so I climb into a cozy bed And I don't have to warm the entire house to do so — I've often found turning the thermostat up before bed results in overheating around 3 a.m.

The Eight Sleep provides a gentle ebb and flow. It warms around 10 p.m., cools as I fall asleep, maintains that cooler feel until the early hours, then gradually warms again so I wake up cozy. And if at any point my body temperature or the room diverts course, the Eight Sleep can adjust to bring me back to the baseline.

The Eight Sleep has a temperature range of 55°F/12°C to 110°F/43°C. I haven't pushed it to the extremes yet but I have gone outside my comfort zone to sample either end of the scale and I've noticed the a significant difference each time. This isn't the placebo effect; the Eight Sleep really is changing the feel of your mattress.

You control the Eight Sleep via the Autopilot app, which is where you set alarms, adjust temperature and view your sleep tracking data.

Overall, I've been impressed with the sleep tracking and how it's displayed in the app. Autopilot shines when it comes to data comparison, showing how your sleep is changing night by night. It's a good way to identify patterns and spot issues before they become major problems.

However, you don't have to use the app to control the Eight Sleep (ish). While the tracking and set up is all done via app, you can adjust the temperature in the night using a panel on the side of the cover. At 3 a.m. when your room is a touch chiller than you want but you don't want to cope with the glare of your smartphone, this is a real benefit.
