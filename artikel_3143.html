IonQ vs. Alphabet: Which is the Better Quantum Computing Stock to Own for Next 5 Years?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

IonQ is getting closer to producing a viable product.

By Keithen Drury – Nov 21, 2026 at 5:35AM EST

While AI gets all the headlines, quantum computing is starting to become a technology that's becoming more and more interesting. Several companies are making strides in quantum computing endeavors, and the technology is only years away from being commercially viable.

The big question investors must answer in this space is whether it's best to go with a massive company that has huge resources that can make a successful quantum computer, or if it's better to back a pure-play company that is much smaller.

Two of the most popular options in this segment are Alphabet (GOOG -2.59%) (GOOGL -2.70%) and IonQ (IONQ -8.76%), with Alphabet being the giant and IonQ the smaller player. It's the classic David versus Goliath matchup, but which one makes the most sense? It all depends on your risk tolerance.

When comparing Alphabet's and IonQ's quantum computing technologies, it is a bit difficult to draw direct comparisons. IonQ releases information about every breakthrough it makes and keeps the investing public well informed of its progress. Alphabet is more silent, although it does release updates about its quantum successes from time to time.

At the surface level, some may think that Alphabet is being silent because it's behind and doesn't want to publicly display that information, but I don't think that's what's going on.

IonQ doesn't have a business to fund its operations. It must find partners and raise capital to continue its operations. Unless IonQ can show these entities that it's making progress and that its technology is coming along nicely, it won't be able to drum up enough interest to keep its company going.

On the flip side, Alphabet is self-funding its quantum research. It only has to keep internal leaders happy with progress, and staying under the radar is a smart idea on their part. So, it's natural that Alphabet doesn't release as many updates as IonQ.

But that doesn't mean IonQ is behind by any means.

Next year, it's releasing its Superion 256-qubit quantum computer. With IonQ's backlog rising to $1.2 billion, it has generated a lot of interest. While this machine isn't quite ready for commercial-level workloads, it's a step in the right direction, and several clients are ordering the machine so they can start to develop quantum computing programs for future implementation. Depending on how this launch goes, it could vault IonQ into a leadership position. It also has plans to build a 10,000-qubit computer, which would be the first commercial-level computer ready for purchase. That's still a few years out, but with quantum technology progressing rapidly, it will be here before investors know it.

But does that make it a better buy than Alphabet for a quantum-focused investment?

IonQ isn't a stock that everyone should own. In fact, I would argue that most shouldn't. Why? Its chance of success is low. While Alphabet hasn't been public with its quantum computing products, the reality is that its products are competitive with IonQ and boast similar fidelity. Alphabet isn't the only big tech company, either. Microsoft (MSFT -2.77%) and IBM (IBM +0.25%) are also heavily investing and represent well-funded competition to IonQ.

If IonQ works out, it will be a huge winner. But there's high risk involved because if it loses to any of its peers or the big tech companies, the stock could plummet to $0. IonQ is a high-risk, high-reward investment. If investors want exposure to quantum computing without the risk of extreme failure, then Alphabet makes for a great stock pick. If you're OK with more risk in exchange for upside, then IonQ is a smart option. Just be sure to size the position appropriately to account for the risk.
