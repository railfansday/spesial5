Bitwise CIO Predicts Hyperliquid Could Become "the Next Amazon." Here's Why He's Right.

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

One of Hyperliquid's strengths is that it gets other people to pay to broaden and deepen the markets on its platform.

By Alex Carchidi – Nov 20, 2026 at 7:30AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Hyperliquid ›

When speaking on a podcast earlier this month, Matt Hougan, chief investment officer of crypto asset manager Bitwise, said that he thinks Hyperliquid (HYPE -8.76%) could become the next Amazon (AMZN -0.63%) thanks to its habit of expanding beyond crypto perpetual futures into real-world assets and prediction markets. That stance meshes cleanly with the view of Hyperliquid Labs' co-founder and Chief Executive Officer Jeff Yan, who wants it to become the "house all of finance." His preferred method for accomplishing that goal closely mirrors Amazon's marketplace model, in which sellers use the company's infrastructure to connect with their target buyers, facilitating transactions from which the platform takes a cut of the proceeds.

Hougan is probably correct about Hyperliquid's potential. Here's why, and how realizing that potential is likely to play out.

Hyperliquid is a decentralized exchange that specializes in perpetual futures contracts, or perps, which are a type of financial derivative. Users pay fees to trade them, and nearly all of the resulting revenue is used to buy back HYPE coins on the open market, like a stock buyback. The exchange earned $72.6 million in revenue during the 30-day period ended Nov. 19, and its coin is up by about 75% during the past 12 months.

Third-party merchants do much of Amazon's selling. Hyperliquid's equivalent is letting other companies commit the capital that's needed to create new markets on its platform, using its infrastructure. For now, new perpetuals markets and prediction markets are supported on Hyperliquid, and to launch one, a developer must stake 500,000 HYPE, worth about $34.4 million as of Nov. 19, which must stay locked for at least 183 days and can be rejected by validators if the market developer misbehaves. Then, those third parties are entitled to a portion of the fees generated by trading on the markets they funded.

Hyperliquid also offers a marketplace of "vaults." Vaults let anyone with $100 open an investment fund that others can invest in, almost like a mini hedge fund. The fund's manager is entitled to their portion of the fund's profits, and investors can easily screen hundreds of vaults to find those with the performance record and asset mix they prefer.

So in many ways, Hyperliquid is already following Amazon's business plan within its own domain.

As of October, more than 261 decentralized crypto exchanges offering perpetual futures trading have launched since Hyperliquid opened in 2022. But it still holds a 57% share of open interest in perps (the value of contracts outstanding) across decentralized perpetual futures exchanges.

This is to say that Hyperliquid's competitors are myriad and growing in number, and have always been so, and likely always will be. Plus, there are many powerful competitors aiming to achieve much the same outcome as Hyperliquid using other approaches. That means its margins may always be under pressure, and its growth may always be contested by competitors.

But the same was also true for Amazon in its early days, and that didn't stop it. So while it's probably not for the best to bet the farm on Hyperliquid becoming the Amazon of decentralized finance, it's certainly on track to accomplish just that.
