Why Garden Clogs Are Suddenly Fashionable and What to Know About the Trend

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

If you’ve been shoving your feet into a pair of rubbery slip-ons every time you step outside to yank weeds or haul mulch, congratulations. The fashion world has officially caught up. Here’s what practical gardeners should know about the garden clogs trend and whether the trend could make your trusty yard shoes pricier or harder to find.

Garden clogs made the jump from the potting shed to fashion feeds through boutique buzz, a runway appearance and word-of-mouth demand in Brooklyn, downtown Manhattan and London.

Plasticana’s Gardana clogs had already built a quiet following among shoppers in Brooklyn and downtown Manhattan, where people started wearing them with everyday streetwear, according to GQ’s May 2024 reporting. Brooklyn boutique Salter House began importing Plasticana footwear after opening in 2018, drawing local chefs, dog walkers and curious shoppers. Designer Emily Adams Bode Aujla then put the shoes on a bigger stage by including Plasticana clogs in her Fall 2020 runway collection. British Vogue’s November 2025 coverage traced the shoes’ path from that early fashion interest to London, where demand reportedly created a months-long waiting list at one retailer.

The honest answer is the same reason gardeners have loved them for years. They slip on in a second, they handle mud and they don’t look like every other plastic shoe on the rack.

That quick-on, quick-off design is exactly what makes them useful for a dash out to the compost bin or a quick trip to grab the mail. Their shape leans unconventional rather than sleek, which is part of why fashion shoppers have embraced them, as British Vogue reported in November 2025. Plasticana’s Gardana style also works hemp fibers into its construction, giving it a look that stands apart from the usual plastic yard shoe, as GQ noted in its earlier coverage. The good news for your wallet? The Standard’s May 2025 reporting noted that Plasticana remains relatively affordable compared with designer alternatives, and that more brands are rolling out their own takes on the gardening clog.

Signs point to staying power, thanks to brand collaborations, the broader workwear moment and the simple fact that these shoes earn their keep outside fashion.

JJJJound teamed up with Plasticana on a subtly branded version of the Gardana, as documented in GQ’s March 2025 reporting. Collaborations like that suggest the audience now stretches well beyond the original gardening crowd. The trend also fits into fashion’s broader embrace of functional footwear, with The Standard’s May 2025 coverage tying it to the rise of workwear-inspired styles and partnerships between fashion labels and outdoor footwear brands. Even if the hype cools, garden clogs will still do what they were built to do. That quiet usefulness is probably why gardeners kept buying them long before anyone called them a trend.
