Marathi Crime Drama ‘Hurda – That Tight Green Top’ Puts a Journalist’s Masculinity Under the Microscope at WAVES Film Bazaar

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Apurv Gaikwad is presnting his debut feature “Hurda – That Tight Green Top,” a Marathi-language crime drama adapted from Atharva Pandit’s novel, to the Co-Production Market at WAVES Film Bazaar.

The film follows Chitranshu, a journalist investigating the mysterious deaths of three young sisters in a village in Maharashtra. When he returns to the village six years later, his search for the truth takes an unexpected turn, forcing him to confront uncomfortable realities. Gaikwad says he was drawn to the story because he “was fascinated by its polyphonic narrative, the way multiple characters coexist, each with their own distinct voice.” He adds, “Even months after reading the novel, there was one particular aspect of it that refused to leave me – the protagonist, Chitranshu.”

“It is as much a mystery as it is a character study and an exploration of the society we live in. While the audience may find the answers they initially seek, I hope they leave the film with an entirely different set of questions,” says Gaikwad, who previously made the short mockumentary “The Last Auteur.”

Gaikwad hopes to challenge the binary understanding of masculinity and “the comfortable distinction we make between good men and bad men. I hope the film encourages audiences to question their own prejudices and examine how caste, gender and social hierarchies shape their understanding of truth, justice and morality,” he says.

The film is being produced by brothers Apurv and Akshay Gaikwad and Nina Bhatt of Disha Cinema and Khan & Kumar Media (“Songs of Forgotten Trees”). In an advanced stage of pre-production, the film is scheduled to start shooting in January 2027. Akassh Ghosalkar leads the cast, which also includes Rohit Kokate, Jitendra Lad, Gayatri Bansode and Gauri Deshpande.

Producer Nina Bhatt says she was drawn to Gaikwad’s examination of ordinary masculinity. “The contradictions between how men see themselves and how they exercise power in everyday relationships, his exploration of the entitlement and complicity that can exist within familiarity, affection and even empathy, felt particularly compelling to me. What made me want to back the project was his willingness to turn that examination inwards, rather than simply locate violence in others.”

Producer Vikas Kumar says Khan & Kumar believes in nurturing and supporting new and important voices in cinema, and that he strongly believes Gaikwad is one of them. “Supporting ‘Hurda’ felt like a natural progression of that belief.”

At Film Bazaar, the team hopes to engage with programmers, sales agents and other industry professionals and explore potential pathways for the independent production.

WAVES Film Bazaar runs as the market component of the International Film Festival of India (IFFI) in Goa.
