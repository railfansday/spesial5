Effective networking does not come naturally to everyone. Credit: sanjeri/Getty

Networking is not “random socializing”, says Nessa Carey. It’s a professional skill that can be learnt and developed.

Carey, a UK-based entrepreneur and consultant, explains in a six-minute video why scientists often struggle with networking, drawing on her own experiences while working in industry and academia.

Both Carey and Caroline Dunne, an academic careers coach based in the United Kingdom and Australia, describe how to identify good people to chat to, ask insightful opening questions and circulate effectively in a room packed with strangers. In her six-minute video, Dunne recommends arriving early and says it is fine to leave an event before it formally ends.

The two videos formed part of a session on networking at Nature’s flagship Reframing Precision Medicine conference in London last month.
