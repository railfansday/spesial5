‘Roma’ Producer Nicolás Celis, Warwick Thornton Films Set for Rotterdam Market IFFR Pro

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Berlinale award winner and auteur Teona Strugar Mitevska will bring 'Petra Doesn't Want to Die' to the market.

The International Film Festival Rotterdam (IFFR) Pro has unveiled the selection of 20 feature projects for the 44th edition of CineMart, its flagship co-production market, running Jan. 31-Feb. 3, as well as 10 projects for its work-in-progress platform Darkroom. And such big names as Aboriginal Australian filmmaker Warwick Thornton, Roma producer Nicolás Celis and North Macedonian auteur Teona Strugar Mitevska (God Exists, Her Name Is Petrunya) will be represented.Additionally, IFFR has shared the selected projects for the second edition of its Safe Harbour strand of CineMart for filmmakers who have experienced displacement or forced migration.IFFR Pro brings together filmmakers, producers and international industry partners around a carefully curated program of projects and opportunities. At its center is CineMart, which connects projects in development with potential partners and collaborators, alongside dedicated pathways for filmmakers supported by the Hubert Bals Fund and those participating in Safe Harbour. Darkroom extends this support to films approaching completion, while Rotterdam Lab invests in the next generation of producers and filmmakers. “Together, these initiatives reflect IFFR’s commitment to building lasting connections between artistic talent, international networks and the opportunities needed to bring distinctive cinematic visions to audiences,” organizers said.“Among the notable filmmakers and producers featuring at CineMart 2027 are Caméra d’Or winner Warwick Thornton, Berlinale and Lux Audience Award winner Teona Strugar Mitevska, Nicolas Celis, producer of Academy Award-winning Roma and Emilia Perez, treble Cannes Critics’ Week winner Myroslav Slaboshpytskiy, Toronto Platform Award winner Carlos Marqués-Marcet and Tiger Award winner Paz Fábrega,” IFFR Pro said. Said Marten Rabarts, head of IFFR Pro: “From contemporary reinventions of the Western and working-class musical fables to personal and urgent stories of displacement and identity, this year’s CineMart, Darkroom and Safe Harbour selections unites an extraordinary range of cinematic voices – filmmakers reclaiming histories, subverting familiar genres and finding fresh ways to tell stories about our world. That we can bring together celebrated auteurs and festival alumni working in independent cinema with filmmakers embarking on their first features is something quintessentially Rotterdam. It is the strength, relevance and originality of the cinematic vision, rather than the stage of a filmmaker’s career, that draws us to a project.”
