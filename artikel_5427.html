FDA is stepping up unannounced inspections at Indian pharmaceutical facilities

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The agency conducted 87% of its India audits without prior notice in the year ended March 31, up from 47% a year earlier

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration is conducting unannounced inspections at Indian drug facilities, an agency official said at an event in Mumbai, according to Bloomberg.

"Previously, an inspection had to be coordinated through our local counterparts or public health authorities. Now those are unannounced," said Neil Bonzagni, an international relations specialist at the FDA.

The agency conducted 87% of its audits in India without advance warning during the fiscal year that ended Sept. 30, versus 47% a year earlier, according to Bloomberg. Bloomberg cited Elizabeth Miller, associate commissioner at the FDA’s office of inspections and investigations, for the figures.

India has more FDA-approved facilities than any other country outside the U.S., and its manufacturers provide roughly 40% of the generic drugs consumed by Americans, according to Bloomberg.

Quality concerns have followed Indian drug exports, according to Bloomberg. Dozens of children in Gambia and Uzbekistan died following their use of Indian-made cough syrups, and four Americans were killed by eyedrops from the country that were contaminated with bacteria. Those deaths occurred in 2022 and 2023.

Bonzagni said the FDA assesses the supply chain "from cradle to grave," from the sourcing of ingredients through production and the distribution of final goods, according to Bloomberg. "The unannounced inspections really do help us ensure safety there," he said.
