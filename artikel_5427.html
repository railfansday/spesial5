German carmakers face their first 24-hour strikes in a wage round since 2018

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Free daily briefing on global business news.

IG Metall wants a 5% raise for about 3.7 million metal and electrical workers; employers offer 1.3% from summer 2028

The IG Metall union called on workers in Germany's metal and electrical industries to stage 24-hour strikes starting Friday, the union said in a statement. It expects work stoppages at numerous companies nationwide following member votes.

IG Metall is set to shut down output on Friday at Volkswagen's Audi facility in Ingolstadt and at BMW's plants in Munich, Dingolfing and Landshut, according to Bloomberg. Workers at Bavarian locations of Robert Bosch, ZF Friedrichshafen and other industrial companies are due to down tools for 24 hours.

The union plans to call on several hundred companies nationwide to join the strikes, it said.

“The frustration and concerns of the employees are so great that we are now escalating the situation with these full-day warning strikes,” IG Metall Chair Christiane Benner said in a statement.

IG Metall is demanding a 5% wage increase, a collectively agreed profit-sharing scheme and job security for about 3.7 million employees in the sector, the union said. Employers have rejected a proposal from IG Metall that included limits, concessions and guarantees, according to the union. They have offered a one-time payment that can be scrapped and a 1.3% wage increase starting in summer 2028, the union said.

The IG Metall districts conduct the negotiations for the sector. A fourth round has not been scheduled, the union said.

In northern Germany, IG Metall has won support for full-day walkouts at certain plants in Bremen, Hamburg, Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania, Schleswig-Holstein and northwestern Lower Saxony, according to Bloomberg. Companies based in that area include Mercedes-Benz Group, which has a big car-making factory in Bremen, along with Airbus and TKMS. The union has not identified which sites it will target.

Employers want more room to adjust what they pay for labor and how long staff work, according to Bloomberg. According to Bloomberg, IG Metall has proposed allowing companies to depart from collective agreements if they pledge to invest in German plants and safeguard products and jobs. The union said employers have not accepted the offer.

More than 118,000 workers joined a prior round of briefer walkouts in Bavaria that continued through last week, according to Bloomberg. The full-day walkouts mark the first time since 2018 that IG Metall has stepped up its action in a wage round.
