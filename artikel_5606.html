Johnson & Johnson is backing a psychedelic-inspired depression startup in a $100 million round

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Delix Therapeutics plans to start a placebo-controlled Phase II trial of its depression pill zalsupindole in the first half of 2027

Johnson & Johnson $JNJ led a $100 million Series C financing in Delix Therapeutics, a Bedford, Massachusetts, developer of psychedelic-inspired depression treatments, the company said on Monday. J&J invested through its venture capital arm, Johnson & Johnson Innovation – JJDC.

SV Health Investors' Dementia Discovery Fund co-led the round. Existing investors including RA Capital, UC Investments and Artis Ventures took part, as did new investors including Adage Capital Management and Hamilton Square $SQ Partners Management, Delix said.

The money will fund Phase II development of Delix's lead drug, zalsupindole, in recurrent major depressive disorder, along with the company's other neuroplastogen programs, the company said. Delix expects to start the placebo-controlled Phase II trial in the first half of 2027.

The trial will enroll 240 patients, who will be dosed either every day or two times a week for a month, according to Bloomberg. Data should arrive in the first half of 2028, Bloomberg said.

Zalsupindole is an oral compound inspired by 5-MeO-DMT and designed to promote neuroplasticity without hallucinations, Delix said. In a Phase Ib trial in adults with recurrent major depressive disorder, patients had a mean reduction of about 50% in depression scores after one week of treatment. The improvement held through day 36, four weeks after the final dose.

That study involved 18 patients, and a bigger trial that includes a placebo group will be needed to verify the findings, according to Bloomberg.

"This capital enables us to take Zalsupindole through Phase II Proof of Concept," Delix CEO Mark Rus said in a statement.

Christian Jung, a partner at SV Health Investors, and a JJDC representative have joined Delix's board, the company said.

Delix has taken in $115 million across previous financings but would not share what the company is worth, according to Bloomberg. Rus told Bloomberg the company intends to look into an initial public offering next year, though that hinges on how markets behave and how its clinical work goes.

The financing follows other big-company moves into psychedelic medicine. Eli Lilly $LLY agreed in July to acquire AtaiBeckley, a developer of psychedelic-based mental health treatments, for roughly $2.8 billion in cash, with up to roughly $1 billion more tied to development and regulatory milestones.

The FDA also held a public hearing in September on the therapeutic use of psychedelic drugs, where speakers called for clear safety and access standards.
