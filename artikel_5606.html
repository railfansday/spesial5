'Opioids Cause Pain. Full Stop.': What We Heard This Week

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

— Quotable quotes heard by MedPage Today's reporters

"Opioids cause pain. Full stop." -- Jeffrey Glassberg, MD, of the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai in New York City, commenting on the use of opioids to treat sickle cell disease pain.

"This is not a frivolous exercise for the boycotters." -- Christian Kohler, who resigned from his position as vice president of publications at the American Diabetes Association, on the ongoing boycott of the organization.

"We can't ignore the elephant in the room." -- David Goldberg, MD, of the Miller School of Medicine at the University of Miami, who during a session at the annual Liver Meeting referenced a whistleblower lawsuit that alleged safety concerns about the metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis drug resmetirom (Rezdiffra).

"Treat the answer as its own symptom." -- Anne Marie Morse, DO, of Geisinger Medical Center in Danville, Pennsylvania, on how sleep disorder patients describe brain fog.

"Science is supposed to be apolitical." -- Georges Benjamin, MD, CEO of the American Public Health Association, discussing a survey showing that roughly 1 in 4 U.S. adults believe myths about autism risk.

"It will be more textual squid ink that accomplishes nothing other than reinforcing unfounded doubts about vaccines that have been cultivated by the HHS secretary for decades." -- Elizabeth Jacobs, PhD, a founding member of Defend Public Health, criticizing an overdue report from a new HHS vaccine task force.

"Now [the American Cancer Society is] taking a much clearer position." -- Mark Ashamalla, MD, of Episcopal Health Services in New York City, on major updates to prostate cancer screening guidelines.

"We think use of pregabalin is still far too high." -- Rose Cairns, PhD, of the University of Sydney, commenting as the gabapentinoid surge in Australia showed signs of reversing.
