10 states with kindergarten vaccination rates below 90% in CDC data

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Kindergarten measles vaccination rates, plus one all-vaccines figure, sat below 90% in these 10 states in CDC data for 2025-26, with Idaho lowest at 75.2%

Kindergarten measles vaccination rates in the CDC's 2025-26 state table run from 75.2% in Idaho to 98.9% in West Virginia. That 23.7-point spread separates the lowest and highest figures the agency lists for the school year.

The CDC table covers about 50 jurisdictions. Kansas and Montana show no data for 2025-26. They cannot be placed in the order used here. This selection covers 10 reporting states with figures under 90%. All 10 fall in the CDC's "Less than 90%" category. Several other states land in the agency's "95%+" category.

The slides run from the lowest percentage to the highest within this group. Idaho comes first at 75.2%. Wisconsin follows at 85.1%, a 9.9-point gap from Idaho. North Dakota closes the selection at 88.5%, 13.3 points above Idaho. The other seven states — Minnesota, Utah, Arizona, Colorado, Oklahoma, Ohio and New Hampshire — range from 85.7% to 88.2%.

Three more states sit just outside this group. Florida and Iowa each reported 88.9%. Georgia reported 89.1%. Those figures are also under 90%. This article is a selection of 10 states with low rates, not a full list of every state below that line.

The CDC lists each state's rate alongside a count and a second percentage. The count appears to show how many kindergartners were assessed. The second percentage appears to show how complete the reporting was. The slides treat both readings with that caution.

States also collected their numbers in different ways. Six states in this selection used a census. Wisconsin, Arizona, Oklahoma and Ohio used a census for public schools and voluntary response for private schools.

New Hampshire needs a separate note. The state did not assess coverage for individual vaccines. Its 88.2% is the share of kindergartners who received all doses of all vaccines required for school entry. It is not a measles-specific figure.

The table does not explain why any state's rate is low. It carries no exemption data, no outbreak data and no policy details.

Idaho reported a kindergarten measles vaccination rate of 75.2% for the 2025-26 school year. No reporting state posted a lower figure in the kindergarten coverage table from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.

That rate leaves 24.8% of Idaho kindergartners outside the share the CDC recorded as covered.

Idaho is also the only reporting state in the table with a rate below 80%. Every other state that supplied data cleared that mark.

The CDC lists Idaho's survey method as a census. The entry shows no separate approach for private schools. Several other states in the table pair a public-school census with voluntary response from private schools. Idaho's entry names a census alone.

The table pairs Idaho's rate with a count of 21,728. If that count reflects the kindergartners assessed, the 75.2% figure describes a group of that size. The column label was not confirmed, so the number is described here only as the count the CDC lists beside the rate.

The entry also shows a second percentage of 100%. If that column measures how complete the reporting was, Idaho's figure would rest on full reporting rather than a partial return. That reading is also unconfirmed.

Wisconsin's kindergarten measles vaccination rate was 85.1% in the 2025-26 school year, according to CDC data. Among the states that reported figures, only Idaho posted a lower rate.

That leaves Wisconsin 4.9 percentage points short of 90%. It sits in the CDC's "Less than 90%" category.

Minnesota, the next state up the table, reported 85.7%. The two states are 0.6 points apart.

Minnesota reported a kindergarten measles vaccination rate of 85.7% for the 2025-26 school year, according to CDC data. That is the third-lowest figure among the states that reported numbers for the year.

The statewide figure does not show how coverage varies within Minnesota. Clearwater County has the lowest overall measles vaccination rate of any county in the state, according to tucson.com. Neighboring Wadena County has the second-lowest.

In Clearwater County, many children are recorded as partially vaccinated. They lack one of the two doses. They also have no exemption on file. That pattern ties the county's low rate to incomplete dose series rather than to recorded exemptions.

Utah reported measles vaccination coverage of 87.2% among kindergartners in the 2025-26 school year, according to CDC data. That figure places the state in the CDC's "Less than 90%" category.

Utah's rate falls 2.8 percentage points short of the 90% line. Among the states that reported data for the year, only Idaho, Wisconsin and Minnesota posted lower rates. Minnesota, the next state down, sits 1.5 points below Utah.

Utah's figure sits 11.7 points below West Virginia's, the highest in the CDC table. It is 12 points above Idaho's, the lowest. The two gaps are close to equal. Utah sits near the midpoint between the table's highest and lowest rates.

Utah gathered its numbers through a census of kindergartners. That approach differs from the mixed method some states used, which pairs a census of public schools with voluntary responses from private schools. Under a census with full reporting, Utah's rate does not depend on which private schools chose to respond.

Utah requires each child enrolled in kindergarten to receive specific immunizations, including a two-dose series of the MMR vaccine, according to The Salt Lake Tribune.

The CDC table does not explain how Utah's coverage relates to that state requirement. It offers no breakdown of kindergartners by number of doses received. It also carries no exemption counts for the state.

In the 2025-26 school year, 88.2% of New Hampshire's kindergartners had received all doses of every vaccine required for school entry, according to CDC data. The state did not assess coverage for individual vaccines. That makes the number broader than a measles rate.

New Hampshire's number measures something different from the other nine states in this selection. Each of those states reported a measles figure. New Hampshire's figure counts only children who were fully up to date on the whole school-entry schedule. A kindergartner missing a single required dose of any required vaccine falls outside the 88.2%.

That difference limits what the number can show. It does not reveal how many New Hampshire kindergartners had measles vaccination specifically. It also does not show which vaccines or doses the remaining children lacked. A gap in one required vaccine registers the same way as gaps in several.

The figure is also not a direct match for its neighbors in this list. The CDC places New Hampshire in its "Less than 90%" category. Its 88.2% sits 1.8 percentage points below that line.
