SFA to deliver revised proposal after young player vote delayed

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The Scottish FA says they will continue consulting with SPFL clubs in a bid to get more game time for homegrown talent after initial plans put forward by the governing body failed to reach a member vote.

Following an all-club meeting at St Andrews in September, the SFA proposed financial incentives - and penalties - along with quotas, in a bid to get more youngsters playing for clubs.

Initially, these proposals were to have been voted on at a SFA general meeting in October and the SPFL's annual meeting this month.

But the SFA now say they are aiming to put refreshed proposals to the clubs in the first quarter of 2027, in time for the start of the 2027-28 season.

Despite the governing body saying they are "encouraged", they add that "recent discussions have spawned a range of additional potential innovations that all parties agree will require further consultation."

"The passion we all have for Scottish football has been demonstrated by the public debate sparked since the all-club meeting in St Andrews," a Scottish FA statement read.

"We will continue to engage in dialogue with the aim of delivering a proposal that will achieve the stated objective of improving opportunities for young homegrown players to fulfil their potential at the top end of the Scottish professional game."

"It is an objective everyone benefits from: players, clubs, supporters and Scotland national teams."

'Not fit for purpose' - Craig Mulholland on youth pathway

Craig Mulholland, SFA chief football officer, has engaged with clubs to encourage more playing time for younger players, however media speculation suggests those plans may have been diluted.

At their initial consultation with clubs, they put forward three suggestions to increase minutes for young Scottish players:

Two under-21 Scotland-eligible players start in all Scottish Cup matches, with one player on the pitch at all times.

Two under-21 Scotland-eligible players start in all Premier Sports Cup matches, with one player on the pitch at all times.

Clubs should field Scotland-eligible Under-21 players for an average of 60 minutes in each game across all four SPFL divisions (6% of available minutes).

The proposals were introduced following a dramatic dip in Scottish-born players being played by Premiership clubs and suggestions of a fear by managers and owners to take a chance on academy players.

Statistics also reflect a huge increase in young players being tempted south to club academies in England, prompting widespread concerns they could be lost in a vast youth system rather than breaking into first teams domestically.

"Given the implications for our game, and our young talent, it is imperative that we take the necessary time to get those proposals right," the governing body adds.
