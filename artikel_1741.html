The one Crystal Palace tradition that Thomas Frank must change if former Tottenham boss is to succeed

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Crystal Palace sacked Pierre Sage and hired Thomas Frank because they needed change. They need more than their new manager seems to realise.

The Dane revealed on Thursday that the 3-4-3 formation Palace have become so used to playing under Oliver Glasner and then briefly under Sage will “definitely be one of our systems, a go-to - and then we'll see what we change.”

The truth, though, is that while flexibility is good, the formation is one of the things that does need a rethink.

Frank was right about one thing, at least. “It is obvious that this squad is recruited to 3-4-3”, he said. “That's why we have very good players in their positions.”

But those “very good players” are not still at Selhurst Park. The Eagles have been persevering with a system that suits players no longer at the club, something the new boss must put right.

Wind the clock back less than a year and after a 2-1 win over London rivals Fulham, Palace sat fourth in the Premier League table in a season destined produce historic European silverware.

Adam Wharton was firing. Ismaila Sarr and Jean-Philippe Mateta were doing well to cover up the loss of Eberechi Eze to Arsenal. Maxence Lacroix and Marc Guehi were two-thirds of one of the finest centre-back crews in the division. Daniel Munoz was the most enjoyable high-flying wing-back to watch in England. In charge was Glasner, a man who knew exactly how to eke out the best of Palace’s 3-4-3. Life could not get any better.

Now, it is an entirely different landscape.

Though Jaydee Canvot and Chelsea-bound Honest Ahanor are promising players, Palace also have Axel Disasi - who still has a mistake in him - and there is therefore an awful lot of pressure on the shoulders of the one central defender who lived through all the good times: Chris Richards.

In their last game, midfielder Jefferson Lerma started there while actual centre-backs sat on the bench: which is never a good sign. A 3-4-3 system involves playing one more centre-half than most teams, and yet for Palace that means one more player in a position where the quality just isn’t there anymore since Guehi and Lacroix’s departures.

In midfield, Wharton has lost his way this season and is being overrun alongside Daichi Kamada. Switching to a 4-2-3-1 - a formation Frank knows well - would allow three midfielders to get a better handle on things, provided the No10 adopted defensive responsibilities. The likelihood of such a porous Palace midfield would drop.

At wing-back, only Anan Khalaili suits a back five any more than a back four. Oscar Mingueza is the more defensively astute right-back. The pair should share game time depending on whether Palace are hunting a leveller or preserving a lead. On the other flank, Tyrick Mitchell has a claim to being the best one-v-one defender in the Premier League. Scaling him from wing-back to left-back would be no issue.

Palace must remember where they are. They sit bottom of the Premier League by a point and have the worst defence in the division.

Frank has been hired to save them, with plenty of time to do so. That means plenty of time to find a way of getting the best out of the squad as it is now, with the players available. Not getting the best out of a team that no longer exists.
