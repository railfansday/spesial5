Four Takeaways From Dodgers Losing Tarik Skubal to Mets in $450 Million Deal

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The Los Angeles Dodgers will not be keeping Tarik Skubal after all, as the New York Mets get their man in free agency.

Mets owner Steven Cohen is giving Skubal $450 million over the next nine years, along with a no-trade clause and a $45 million signing bonus, according to reports.

The Dodgers, while not officially confirmed, likely fell short of that massive offer, which sets a new record for the most money ever for a starting pitcher per season and in total.

Here are some thoughts on what is a blow for the Dodgers, no matter how the pizza is sliced.

The Mets were in the race for Yoshinobu Yamamoto before he went to the Dodgers; the same for Kyle Tucker and even their own free agent, Edwin Diaz.

New York, after missing out on Tucker, went big after Bo Bichette, a deal that definitely didn't work out or prove to be the game-changer the team needed.

This time, with Skubal, Cohen was resolute about not coming in second place. He gave Skubal the ball, going far beyond what the records were for pitchers, to help bolster their biggest area of need.

The Dodgers still have a ton of quality pitchers in the bullpen who can be starters, not to mention Tyler Glasnow, Blake Snell, Yoshinobu Yamamoto, and Shohei Ohtani, along with an emerging Roki Sasaki.

The Mets, meanwhile, really have nothing behind Nolan McLean that comes close to the Dodgers. New York's need was urgent; beyond avoiding a loss to the Dodgers in free agency, they needed an ace who could mentor McLean and give them a true one-two punch to build around.

The Mets have not been good over the last two years despite Cohen's investment, and when you look at the Dodgers' and Mets' contender status, it is no contest who gives him the best shot at winning.

However, Skubal is a pitcher who just had elbow surgery, along with Tommy John in the past, and knows the risk of pitcher injury.

To have $450 million over nine years guaranteed in a big market like New York, with the chance to help usher in a new era with the Mets, was a better pitch than possibly getting less money for the Dodgers, even if the odds of winning a title are higher.

The opportunity with the Mets is good enough to warrant the additional money, along with the No-Trade clause, massive signing bonus, etc., but made the decision to leave the Dodgers clear in the end.

Now that Skubal has officially pitched in just 12 games for the Dodgers after they gave up two legitimate top-100 prospects, it is easy to think the Dodgers made a mistake in trading for the ace.

But the reality is that the deal was worth it — Shohei Ohtani did not pitch after the trade deadline, while Tyler Glasnow was not himself after coming back from injury.

Skubal was elite for the Dodgers, posting a 2.61 ERA in the regular season, along with a 1.05 WHIP and a 2.35 FIP.

It wasn't Apex Skubal, but in the end, he delivered as expected, and with a three-peat on the line, the Dodgers needed to do everything possible to win the World Series.

They were swept in the NLCS, but LA made the right call.
