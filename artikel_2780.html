Melton hit 8 threes on Kings, joined an exclusive Warriors’ club

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

De'Anthony Melton nailed eight threes against Sacramento on Wednesday night and sent me down a rabbit hole (brought to you by Stathead) that ate up a lot more of my Thanksgiving than I planned.

Melton tied his career high with eight threes and scored 24 points in the 129-110 NBA Cup win over the Kings, and he did it with Steph sitting out with a torn oblique. He went 8-for-12 from deep, 8-for-14 overall, and never took a free throw.

With those eight treys, Melton walked into one of the most exclusive shooting clubs in franchise history. All of which got me wondering how often the Warriors lose when somebody does that. The answer is: almost never.

Since Coach Steve Kerr took over in 2014, there have been 120 regular-season performances where a Warrior hit eight or more threes, spread across 119 games because Curry and Wiggins share one, and the Warriors are 92-27 in those games. That's a .773 winning percentage, a 63-win pace, and the average scoring margin across all 119 games, wins and losses together, is almost plus-10.

Melton is the eighth different Warrior to do it under Kerr. The other seven are Curry, Klay Thompson, D'Angelo Russell, Jordan Poole, Wiggins, Brandin Podziemski and Moses Moody, and if you squint, that list doubles as a decade of Warriors history.

By the way, if I told you two Warriors once hit eight threes apiece in the same game during the Steve Kerr era would you bet on that being the Splash Brothers? Wrong, dear reader. Twas' Curry and Andrew Wiggins.

But let's find some more fun facts. During the first five seasons of the Kerr era, 2014-15 through 2018-19, the Warriors went 50-4 when somebody hit eight threes. Since then they're 42-23. A 92.6% winning percentage turned into 64.6%. Remember when Steph would hit his sixth three and you'd start figuring out how to beat the traffic out of Oracle? Nowadays a sixth three means you're hoping the Warriors can get a stop at the other end.

In the 73-9 season they went 20-1 in these games, which means somebody hit eight threes in roughly one out of every four games that year. The one loss took history to pull off. On April 1, 2016, Curry hit eight threes against Boston and the Celtics still won 109-106, ending the 54-game home winning streak, an NBA record. Even the losses from that era came with a plaque.

Those dynasty teams had the defense, depth and talent to turn a hot shooting night into an early bedtime for everybody watching on the East Coast.

Curry has 88 of the 120 performances, and Steph and Klay have a combined 111 of them, 92.5% of the entire club. The Warriors are 68-20 when Steph hits eight. Through age 34, the Warriors were 56-10 in his eight-three games. Since he turned 35, they're 12-10. Since he turned 37, they're 3-3.

He's still having these nights, and that's what gets me. In January 2025, at 36, he went 8-for-8 from three against Philadelphia, the only perfect night in the entire dataset. He dropped 31 with eight threes on the Lakers a month ago at 38 years old. The miracle nights keep coming, but they used to be a guaranteed win and now they're closer to a coin flip. The worst one to look back on is February 2024 in Atlanta, when he scored 60 with 10 threes and lost in overtime. He's lost 57-point and 50-point games too. The Warriors are 10-3 when Curry scores at least 50 points and makes at least eight threes, and they went 4-0 when Klay did it. When Steph has gone nuclear lately, he's usually been making up for everything around him.

Across his whole Warriors run under Kerr, 2014-15 through 2023-24, Klay went 19-4 in his eight-three games and 13-1 at home. He scored 60 points in 29 minutes against Indiana and sat out the fourth quarter. He hit 14 threes in 27 minutes in Chicago, which is still the franchise record, and he once went 10-for-11 from deep against the Lakers.

Not counting Steph and Klay, the record is 6-3. Two of those losses belong to D'Angelo Russell, who hit nine threes against Dallas on Dec. 28, 2019 and nine more against Indiana on Jan. 24, 2020, and lost both. The Dallas game was a 20-point loss, still the worst in the whole dataset. That's the most 2019-20 Warriors thing imaginable, and I say that with love for everyone who suffered through that season.

Poole went 1-1 and Wiggins went 2-0. Podziemski went 1-0 against the Lakers in April 2025, and Moody went 1-0 last November in New Orleans when he hit seven threes in one quarter and joined Steph and Klay as the only Warriors ever to do it.

A 6-3 record for the non-Splash Brothers isn't a big enough sample to call it a new formula, and one bench explosion against Sacramento doesn't change the math from the last few years. These days the shooting is sometimes the only thing keeping the Warriors in the game.

That's why Wednesday night felt so good. Steph was in street clothes nursing a torn oblique, somebody else got to be the reason an opposing coach was staring at the ceiling wondering what he did to deserve this.
