The Washington Commanders run game against the Giants wasn't particularly noteworthy on the face of things. The Commanders totaled 105 rushing yards on 33 carries at 3.2 yards per carry. That number is boosted by quarterback Jayden Daniels running the ball too, both on designed runs and on scrambles. When you narrow it down to just the running backs, the Commanders backs totaled 77 yards on 22 carries at 3.6 yards per carry, which is still not particularly great.

However, there was a shift in the second half run game that saw the Commanders yards per carry jump pretty drastically. Under new offensive coordinator David Blough, the Commanders have transitioned away from former coordinator Kliff Kingsbury's heavy use of gap scheme runs into more of a zone run scheme. Kingsbury liked to use various gap scheme runs with different lineman pulling in different directions to confuse the defense, but Blough's foundation has been built on zone runs that have every lineman blocking in the same direction together.

The results have been hit and miss. Early in the year the offensive line looked good blocking zone and running back Jacory Croskey-Merritt hit some nice cutbacks. But in recent weeks, the zone run scheme has struggled to become a trustworthy scheme to keep the offense on or ahead of schedule, frequently leading to second and/or third and long situations. That was the case against the Giants too.
