FLEX Finder Week 11: Young wideouts offer hope amid the byepocalypse

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Here's a weird thing: There are six teams on bye, but this was actually a really easy week to do FLEX Finder. Maybe it's the lack of choice that's freeing. Maybe it's the fact that so many of the offenses in the league have proven to be so fantasy-uninteresting that we can't possibly rely on them. Maybe it's the fact that the league's good defenses are easy to understand at this point. Maybe it's all three of them. Either way, there wasn't much deliberation necessary here.

1) I will not recommend quarterbacks — There are plenty of good ones. You're on your own there. 2) I will not recommend any running back who costs more than $5,000, wideout who costs more than $4,500, or tight end that costs more than $4,000 in DraftKings' DFS slate — This was an easy cutoff for what a FLEX really is. 3) I consider any player who garners over 50 yards or scores a victory for the column — anything more you get out of that, well, great for you. Let's set expectations low because we are combing for outliers.

Xavier Hutchinson, Ja'Kobi Lane highlight Week 11's Regression Files

Denny Carter takes a close look at players who might be due for positive or negative regression headed into Week 11.

Braelon Allen, NYJ -- $4,900 – The recipient of nine and 10 touches the last two weeks despite the return of Breece Hall, Allen continues to get reasonable FLEX-level volume. The Chargers, despite somehow glomping on the Ravens on Monday night, are 24th in run defense DVOA. The line has moved heavily in favor of the Jets, and the Chargers may be favored here but they are also 1-8.

Justice Hill, BAL -- $4,700 – The recipient of at least 11 touches each of the last three weeks, including a long touchdown in Week 9 during garbage time, Hill seems like a fit for how the Ravens will operate with Jacoby Brissett. The Panthers are in theory susceptible to being crockpotted by Derrick Henry, but he hasn't crossed 60 yards since Sunday Night Football in Week 5. Hill's the first of three Ravens I like this week for counting stats in a game with two defenses that have each trended towards being rough.

J.K. Dobbins, DEN -- $4,500 – He hasn't been trending well over the last three weeks, garnering an average of just six touches a game while Jonah Coleman has taken over as the main back. But I think some distance will be good for the Broncos and that the idea of establishing it against a Ravens team with a rookie quarterback will probably win out. The Broncos give Dobbins one more chance to show he belongs in a committee with Coleman here.

Seth McGowan, IND -- $5,000 – His role has steadily grown, I always trust backup running backs a little bit more on a short week, and he's running well. Could the Texans defense rise up and actually stuff the run here? I guess it's in play. But Shane Steichen kept them plenty off-balance in Week 3 and the Houston's defense will be light Azeez Al-Shaair yet again. I'm going to trust that the Colts definitely don't want to throw the ball. Perhaps McGowan will find another touchdown.

Caleb Douglas, MIA -- $4,400 – The Dolphins don't have a real quarterback with Kyle McCord back there, but they had to throw plenty against the Colts last week and Douglas was heavily involved with eight targets. They project as huge underdogs against Buffalo. I think the threat of the Malik Willis run game changes this offense enough that they'll have to throw more, and I think that's good news for Douglas in a game where the Dolphins might have to throw plenty.

Tank Dell, HOU -- $4,400 – My grand unified theory of Texans wideouts in this current setting: You start Dell when you expect zone coverage, and you start Xavier Hutchinson ($4,200) when you expect man coverage. The Colts are down their top two cornerbacks here. I'd say that there is some chance of the Texans running clock in the second half based on how the Colts have played and a large Vegas line, but the Texans are going to have to prove to me that they can blow out a bad team without a big passing game before I believe it.

Tre Harris, LAC -- $4,000 – It's hard to recommend a player in this dysfunctional of a passing offense, but let's try: For whatever faults Justin Herbert has, he's playing out of structure a lot and Harris can get there with one busted coverage. Ladd McConkey (back) feels unlikely to be a major factor on a short week. Harris easily led the Chargers in yardage last week and even if McConkey does come back, could be on the field over Quentin Johnston. The Jets defense is so bad that Aaron Glenn is trying to shift playcalling duties to someone you've only heard of if you've marinated in the league.

Elic Ayomanor, TEN -- $3,700 – The recipient of 22 targets over his last three games in an offense that has crossed 23 points in all three of those games – words I didn't think I'd be typing about the Titans given everything they've done over the past three years – Ayomanor already was scratching fringe FLEX value. Now he's playing the Cowboys in the highest-totaled game of the week. I think this might be a bounce-back game for the Cowboys defense (lol) against a more reasonable opponent, but there's room for that to be true and for Ayomanor to deliver as a FLEX play.

Ja'Kobi Lane, BAL -- $3,400 – Jacoby Brissett seemed to really like targeting him, throwing him 11 targets against the Chargers. He only caught three of them, but it's his first game with a quarterback who was just acquired. I just can't quit the idea that Lane will be good, and I think you're getting a pre-determined price here that is too good to ignore after last week's game.

Chig Okonkwo, WAS -- $3,500 – So let's see: The Bengals offense is on a major heater, the Commanders will have to play catch up, and Cincinnati always lets opposing tight ends go off. I'm afraid I won't be able to quit Chig for yet another week, even if Ben Sinnott ($2,500) is coming on and is a reasonable punt play here.

Mark Andrews, BAL -- $3,500 – Just when I thought I was out, Jacoby Brissett brings me back in by peppering the tight ends with targets in his first start. Andrews set season-highs in targets, receptions, and yards. He's a reasonable PPR play even if Josh Cuevas starts to encroach on his ceiling cases.
