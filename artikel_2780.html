Aaron Rodgers rips Mike McCarthy, says Steelers coach fell asleep in meeting and mistakenly called team 'Packers'; other players reportedly corroborate quarterback's account

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Pittsburgh Steelers quarterback Aaron Rodgers did not stay silent for long following his surprise benching. And shortly after Rodgers publicly accused Steelers head coach Mike McCarthy of everything from sleeping in meetings to mistakenly calling the team the "Packers," the franchise released the veteran quarterback on Wednesday.

Rodgers, a four-time NFL MVP, joined the "Pat McAfee Show" on Wednesday afternoon to address his benching … and he did not hold back when it came to sharing his side of how the events of the week unfolded — or when it comes to his feelings about McCarthy.

"I found out that I was gonna be inactive on a phone call last night from Pat Freiermuth," Rodgers said in the interview. "I was talking to Muth and a couple of the guys, and he said, 'I can't believe they're doing this.' And I said, 'Doing what?' I didn't know. And he told me, and obviously, there had been a lot of things in motion at that point. I didn't hear from Mike [McCarthy].

"Mike and I talked for a long time the last few days, and that word [benching] never came up," Rodgers continued.

After that, Rodgers released a windfall of scathing accusations against McCarthy, ripping into his head coach for a series of events over the past week that "ultimately led to where we're at now."

Rodgers said McCarthy arrived late to a quarterbacks' meeting last Wednesday, in which the coach fell asleep and was snoring.

"Last Wednesday, we're in a quarterback meeting. Mike came in late, sat down and within 10 minutes began snoring," Rogers said. "Woke himself up, was rattling his papers. We all looked around. Five minutes later, you hear the snoring again. Less than five minutes after that, he walks out of the room like nothing happened."

Rodgers accused McCarthy of missing an offensive meeting the following day and also missed the Friday walkthrough.

"Doesn't come to the first walkthrough," Rodgers said. "Doesn't come to the protection walkthrough on Thursday. Saturday, he misses the morning meeting, and we don't have a team meeting Saturday night for whatever reason in Philly. And then comes Sunday, when obviously we struggled, and the product wasn't great."

Rodgers also ripped into McCarthy's relationships with players on the team, alleging that he didn't care to get to know the defense and had lost many of those players well before the end.

"There were some guys early on that were … didn't like the way things were going, especially defensive guys. Mike didn't want to get to know the defense," Rodgers said. "At one point, I told him, 'You should really lean into the defensive guys a little bit more and talk to those guys,' and he said, 'Well, I don't think I need to.' And I said, 'Well, I disagree with that.'

"He already kind of lost a lot of those guys, and I think the nail in the coffin for so many of us was, at halftime, toward the end of it … he says, 'Alright, 1-2-3-Packers!' That's what he broke it down on in the locker room," Rodgers said. "We all looked around, going, 'What the f***? What is going on? Where's his head at, at this point?"

The Steelers went on to lose that game, 19-16, snapping a seven-game losing streak for the Philadelphia Eagles. Rodgers took five sacks in the loss, completing 30-of-44 pass attempts for 227 yards and a score.

Clearly, the loss was a tipping point for Rodgers.

"I went in on Monday, and I just said, 'Hey, this last week, this is not what we talked about.' We've had these conversations for months now. And it began as a concerned friend about somebody who's unhealthy. If you're sleeping in meetings, you're unhealthy. If you're not coming to everything you should be coming to as a play-caller, [you're unhealthy].

"I mean, how many quarterbacks around the league are dealing with the same type of stuff? I gave you one week. I could go week-by-week throughout the season on stuff that was going on.

"Monday, we're having this conversation, and I said, 'We need to make a change for the betterment of the team.'"

The change Rodgers was hoping for was a new play-caller. But instead, the team made a change at quarterback.

"I felt like I was talking to him at the behest of the team," Rogers said. "It was pretty wild. I'm saying these things to him that I told you. He's denying all of it. He's denying sleeping. I go, 'Mike, there's five guys in the room that would sign an affidavit.' There's no hyperbole. There's no trying to stick it you. What's best for the team is you give [play calling] up to somebody, you take care of yourself, you take care of your health, do the schedule and do the team meetings and let that be that."

"When I talk to him on Tuesdays, he says I haven't seen the film yet, I haven't seen the film." Rodgers said. "I don't know how that's possible on Tuesday when you play on a Sunday. That part's disrespectful to the game. You have peoples' livelihoods involved, players and coaches."

"I left it with him that if you try and bench me or don't change the play-calling, then you should just release me because I feel like at this point I'm doing what's right for the organization," Rodgers said. "He responded with, 'I'm going to do what's right for the organization,' and, in his mind, that's getting rid of me."

How do Aaron Rodgers and the Steelers move forward after this?

Well … apparently, they don't. Rodgers acknowledged as much nearing the end of his McAfee appearance, saying the "ship has sailed" on a return to the team. Soon after Rodgers' interview concluded, it was reported that the Steelers were releasing Rodgers, according to Pittsburgh Post-Gazette beat writer Gerry Dulac. The team later confirmed Rodgers' release.

The real question now is how the Steelers will move forward with McCarthy, after multiple league sources have corroborated Rodgers' account to ESPN.

Following Rodgers' release, McCarthy spoke to the media, insisting the team was focused on Friday's matchup against the Denver Broncos. But of course, he was not able to go long without acknowledging the elephant in the room.

McCarthy owned the call to bench Rodgers and start backup Mason Rudolph in Week 12 against the Denver Broncos, saying that the team made "consistent efforts" to contact Rodgers' agent about the benching on Tuesday evening. He said, "There was an ultimatum made that was clearly out of his job description, so I made the decision to bench him."

While he seemingly rebuffed Rodgers' claims that he lost the locker room, he said he came from a meeting with the team's leadership council where they had a "very direct, very honest" conversation.

He continued that string of honesty, addressing Rodgers' claims that he had broken down a team huddle by saying, "1-2-3, Packers!" saying, "Halftime was very emotional; there were conversations prior to that embarrassing moment. We work a lot of hours; there was definitely a difference of opinion from what I was told about the comments."
