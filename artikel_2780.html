Raiders Week 11: Fernando Mendoza’s arm, legs key against stingy Broncos

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

With just 186 total points allowed in nine games, the Denver Broncos boast the ninth-best defensive unit in that regard, which will make it tough sledding for the Las Vegas Raiders this Sunday.

The Silver & Black Express rolls into Mile High with a new conductor in prized rookie quarterback Fernando Mendoza a road divisional matchup against the first-place Broncos (tied with the Kansas City Chiefs at 7-2 overall). The No. 1 overall pick in the 2026 NFL Draft, Mendoza made his first career start against the stifling Seattle Seahawks defense and Las Vegas fell 21-13 in Week 10.

Another formidable defense awaits Mendoza in Week 11 as Vance Joseph's Denver squad comes off a bye week after a wire-to-wire Week 9 26-21 win at the Carolina Panthers two Sunday's ago.

To hear the 23-year-old budding franchise quarterback tell it, however, he wouldn't have it any other way as he preps for his second-career NFL start this Sunday in Denver.

"I believe getting thrown in the fire is the best way to learn, and the Seahawks and the Broncos both have considerably elite defenses," Mendoza said when asked what it's like getting thrown into the fire against two quality NFL defenses. "I really believe every NFL defense is elite because when you're playing at this level, the margin for error is so small, and all the defensive coordinators are geniuses.

"So, I'm honored and blessed to go against a great defensive coordinator in Coach Robbie (Raiders defensive coordinator Rob Leonard) every single day, especially in OTAs and fall camp and training camp. So, I believe that's helped prepare me, an now I'm excited to embark on this journey with my teammates."

How Mendoza settles during his second start against an aggressive Broncos defense bears watching. The Seahawks produced a blueprint of how to and how not to defend the Indiana product last week. When protected with a clean (and sometimes noisy) pocket, Mendoza was a smooth operator going through progressions and using his accurate and strong arm to deliver the football to Raiders pass catchers.

In the first half, Mendoza went 11-of-13 for 90 yards including his first-career touchdown run. When Seattle saw enough of that, head coach and defensive play caller Mike Macdonald went to his bag of tricks of blitz, simulated pressure, and exotic alignments to take advantage of the neophyte signal caller and Mendoza went 6-of-16 for 53 yards in the second half while absorbing six sacks and 12 hits.

Expect Joseph to stick to a similar script and bring the pain on the 6-foot-5 and 225-pound Raiders quarterback. Especially considering the Broncos have 84 quarterback hits on the season alongside 26 sacks. Zach Allen is an ultra-productive defensive lineman who plays both inside as a tackle and outside as an edge rusher and paces Denver with 4.5 sacks and 23 quarterback hits. Edge rusher Nik Bonitto is right there with him with 4.5 sacks and 11 QB hits while defensive tackle Malcolm Roach is disruptive with four sacks and 13 quarterback hits.

Heat-seeking inside linebacker Alex Singleton is a quality clean-up man and is tied for the league lead in tackles at 107 (Raiders inside linebacker Nakobe Dean is who he is tied with) and also has two sacks and six quarterback hits on his stat sheet.

All of this is presented to note Mendoza's accurate arm and fresh legs are integral to Las Vegas' aspirations to walk out of Denver with an AFC West win. That dual-threat ability is essential to counter the Joseph's defense that is every bit as creative and chaotic as Macdonald's group. All it takes is a few successful scrambles to make a defense bite — Seattle did this — and with Mendoza lauded for his cerebral ways and ability to not make the same mistake again, watching the film from last Sunday's loss will showcase just that.

The second-quarter 22-yard dart Mendoza zipped to wide receiver Cody White is an example of this. Mendoza felt pressure, began to scramble but kept his eyes downfield, and his ability to thrown on the arm and velocity on the fast ball were on full display.

"We have a great strength and conditioning staff here, and performance staff as a whole, whether it's the training staff, nutrition, strength. There's so many different aspects of it, so I'm blessed to be a part of it," Mendoza began when as he answered a question about his mobility being an underrated aspect of his game, "I believe I've gotten faster since being here, and I'm grateful for that because in the NFL, those players are fast."

Those fleet feet will come in handy to escape the exotic blitzes Joseph is expected to deploy. Especially with Las Vegas ground game grinding to a halt consistently over the course of the 2026 season, much to the chagrin of Raiders rookie head coach and offensive play caller Klint Kubiak. Mendoza's ability to run helps provide an alternative run game and scrambling for hidden yards and first downs can frustrate the opposing defense.

Kubiak should again go play-action heavy with designed rollouts and bootlegs to vary the launch point of the ball and give his talented rookie cleaner passing lane and run-pass options. Mendoza has the arm talent to push the ball downfield and by getting the ball into the hands of his pass catchers quickly or downfield, Las Vegas can take advantage of Denver's penchant to whiff on tackles as the team has a high 16.2 percent missed tackle rate. Of course, it'd help to have a healthy Brock Bowers available as the tight end is the Raiders' de facto WR1.

Mendoza is keen on balancing staying in the pocket and winning with this arm while also knowing when to run and gain positive yards against Denver.

"There were a lot of moments in the game that we reviewed after in the film, whether it's Coach Kubiak, Coach (Andrew) Janocko or Coach (Mike) Sullivan, they made comments on whatever the situation was, whether it was some inefficiency or a play that in the future we want to change. So, I'm taking those day by day and trying to improve on them," Mendoza said regarding staying in the pocket longer to deliver a throw instead of taking off as quickly as he did in his first start.

While the Broncos boast the ninth-best red zone defense — teams have visited the space between the 20-yard line and end zone 29 times for only 15 touchdowns for a 51.7 percent conversion rate — they are susceptible to getting yards piled upon them allowing 3,195 yards for 27th most amongst the 32 teams.

That right there is a textbook example of a bend-but-not-break defense.

Sunday's Mile High tilt marks the 134th time the Raiders and Broncos go toe-to-toe. The Silver & Black lead the all-time series 73-58-2 (72-5702 in regular season play, 1-1 in the playoffs) but are riding a four-game losing skid against the Horsemen. The last time Las Vegas beat Denver was in January of 2024, a 27-14 victory inside Allegiant Stadium. Since that tilt, the Broncos have won the next four meetings 34-18, 29-19, 10-7, and 24-17. The pair of longtime AFC West foes will meet again in Week 15 in Las Vegas.

—Las Vegas Raiders defensive coordinator Rob Leonard on Seattle Seahawks wideout Jaxon Smith-Njigba's 71-yard touchdown on a broken play

"I think with Brock (Bowers) and anyone in the tight end, wide receiver, anyone, they're constantly talking and as they grow and mesh together it's something that will continue to benefit us as an offense."

—Las Vegas Raiders offensive coordinator Andrew Janocko on the chemistry between pass catchers and quarterback Fernando Mendoza
