The highly anticipated Anthropic IPO is set to test investor appetite for frontier AI companies amid explosive growth and heightened market caution. We look at how high ANT could go in 2026 and into 2027.

Will the ANT stock launch spark a fresh wave of bullish sentiment across the AI sector, or could it mark the top, triggering a death spiral as pressure builds on these tech companies?

Anthropic just dropped the IPO numbers:• 825M shares• $70–$78 range• Up to $64B raise• Ticker: $ANTPricing next week. Possible debut around Nov 19–20.Big one incoming. https://t.co/orSq5RbwKh

Anthropic, the company behind the Claude family of AI models, is expected to price its initial public offering on November 18, 2026, with shares beginning to trade on the Nasdaq the following day under the ticker ANT.

The company is offering 825M Class A shares in a range of $70 to $78 each. At the top of the range, the public offering would raise approximately $64.35Bn.

In addition, Anthropic has arranged a concurrent $26Bn private placement of non-voting Class N shares with strategic partners including Google, Nvidia, Microsoft, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, and TSMC.

Combined, the deals could bring in more than $90 billion in total proceeds. The offering implies a valuation of roughly $1.0–1.02 trillion on shares outstanding after the deal, or up to about $1.4 trillion on a fully diluted basis.

Analyst views span a wide range. Morningstar has assigned a fair-value estimate of $92 per share (implying roughly $1.7 trillion in equity value) and describes the IPO range as undervalued, with a bull-case target of $188.

Other independent models produce base-case valuations near or slightly above the IPO range and more optimistic scenarios well above $100 per share if enterprise adoption and revenue growth remain strong.

Skeptics point to the company’s enormous compute commitments (nearly $900Bn) and intense competition as reasons to be more cautious.

Anthropic IPO enters the public markets with exceptional momentum. Its annualized revenue run-rate reached approximately $81 billion by the end of October 2026, reflecting explosive growth from enterprise customers.

In early November, the company released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2, its most capable models yet for coding, finance, scientific work, and security research.

Enterprise traction continues to deepen, with thousands of customers now spending six or seven figures annually. The company’s Public Benefit Corporation structure and emphasis on AI safety set it apart from purely commercial rivals.

Post-IPO catalysts that could drive a rally include further model advances, expansion of high-margin enterprise products, evidence that the heavy compute investments are translating into durable competitive advantages, and any broader recovery in AI-related stocks.

Assuming the IPO prices near the upper end of the $70–$78 range and the company continues its rapid revenue expansion while managing costs, shares could trade in a range of $110–$150 by the end of 2027 in a constructive environment.

This would reflect continued strong enterprise adoption, successful deployment of new model capabilities, and a more stable macro backdrop for high-growth technology names.

Upside beyond that range remains possible if Anthropic cements a clear leadership position in enterprise AI or if the broader AI investment cycle reaccelerates sharply. Conversely, intensified competition, slower-than-expected monetization, or renewed risk-off sentiment in tech could keep the stock closer to its IPO range or below.

Overall, the Anthropic IPO marks a major milestone for the AI sector, and Anthropic’s execution in the first 12–18 months as a public company will be decisive for its longer-term valuation trajectory.
