Coinbase opens crypto options in a market that accounted for just 2% of Deribit’s trading

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Coinbase brings Deribit options to non-US users as USDC-settled contracts account for just 2% of the exchange’s options turnover.

Coinbase has opened a portion of Deribit’s options market to eligible customers outside the US, with contracts in Coinbase Advanced settling in USDC and falling into a product category that accounted for about 2% of Deribit’s October options turnover.

In two matched calls sampled on Nov. 19, USDC contracts had narrower spreads, while coin-settled contracts offered buyers more size at the best price.

On the same day, Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong announced on X that crypto options were live for non-US users.

The Coinbase Advanced options guide names Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana, Avalanche, Hyperliquid, and XRP. All six use linear contracts, where traders pay the premium in USDC and in-the-money positions ultimately settle in the same stablecoin.

Coinbase directs customers to check eligibility in the app and complete derivatives onboarding where the service is offered. Coinbase had described a phased rollout in its earlier integration guidance.

The options rollout follows Coinbase’s Oct. 1 migration of its International Exchange (ICE) business to Deribit. The latest change lets eligible Coinbase Advanced users trade options from their existing Coinbase account, including through the app, on the infrastructure that already powers their international perpetuals.

Deribit’s October turnover report puts USDC options at $1.35 billion of $68.6 billion in total reported options turnover, or 1.97%. The BTC- and ETH-settled categories accounted for about $67.25 billion combined.

Deribit reports the inverse categories in dollar terms even though the contracts settle in their underlying coins.

October turnover in billions of U.S. dollars was 58.71 for BTC-settled options, 8.53 for ETH-settled options and 1.35 for USDC-settled options across assets.

The USDC category covers multiple assets, so 1.97% describes activity for the venue’s full linear category before the announcement. The price and size a customer can trade depend on the orders available for the individual strike and expiration.

CryptoSlate sampled two Nov. 27 calls on Deribit between 20:11:50 and 20:12:36 UTC on Nov. 19, matching underlying asset, strike and expiration across settlement currencies. In both cases, the USDC book had the narrower quoted gap between the highest bid and lowest ask. The coin-settled book showed more size at the price a buyer could take.

For the $64,000 Bitcoin call, the USDC book showed a 25 USDC spread and 10 BTC of underlying exposure offered at the best ask. The BTC-settled book showed a spread equivalent to $31.03 and 25.9 BTC available at its best ask.

For the $1,800 Ethereum call, the USDC book showed a 0.80 USDC spread and 150 ETH at the best ask. Its ETH-settled counterpart showed a spread equivalent to $0.88 and 1,642 ETH offered at its best ask.

The size advantage flipped for sellers trading at the best bid. At that price, buyers were seeking 20.01 BTC and 116.1 ETH of exposure in the USDC books, compared with 12.8 BTC and 29 ETH in the coin-settled books.

Coinbase said on Nov. 4 that global options and perpetuals were live on Prime for eligible institutions in the US and other supported markets. Its Prime product list includes USDC-settled linear options and inverse options priced and settled in BTC or ETH, both allowing long and short positions.

Institutions must qualify as Eligible Contract Participants, complete the application and portfolio activation, and sign the clearing and Deribit agreements. Orders route through Coinbase Financial Markets and a foreign broker’s risk controls to Deribit’s matching engine.

The Prime venue terms list USDC, BTC, and ETH as collateral, with standard or portfolio margin by portfolio and settlement currency. Daily settlement takes place at 08:00 UTC, and the Global Derivatives portfolio’s margin is kept separate from spot and US derivatives.

Armstrong pointed to Coinbase Pro for US retail traders, but his post left open when those customers can trade and whether they’ll be able to reach the coin-settled books.
