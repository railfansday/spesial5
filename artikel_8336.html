700 Miles of Dust: ✔️

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

“How long do I have to climb up on the side of this mountain of mine?”

Today we are aiming for 18 miles. We were hoping it would be a beautiful day packed with memories. At 6 am just like clockwork we were staring the tramp.

The scenery was indeed spectacular. The first 2.8 miles were quiet other than a couple people passing us. At a water cache we stocked up and ate a warm breakfast. I haven’t had a warm breakfast the whole hike. Thanks to Backpackers Pantry for giving me energy!

We saw a border patrol truck pass on a dirt track, which means we’re getting close to the border. We are soooooooooooooooooooo close to finishing. This brings me to the mantra that I had been saying in my head.

brings me closer to that fine finish line”

There was a bit of ascending.  I was not really noticing though, because I was yapping away about our cats to my mother. What a good listener. 😅

We stopped close to our high point for another morning break. Landslide Lizard seemed very wrapped up in the map. A couple moments later she told me if we did 8 more miles today (not too hard because it was no later than 11:30) then there is a chance we could finish tomorrow, a day earlier than expected and spend a night in Sierra Vista. We aren’t sure yet.

Lunch was in Parker Canyon and we got more water. I had some Trader Joe’s tuna with NutThins and my super good sourdough bread from yesterday. To top all that greatness off I had a Stroopwaffle and a chewy. Tended to my 4 blisters. Lil fuckers.

The next 4 miles were broken into two parts. The first was the 2.5 miles to the jugs of water. Then an additional 2.4 to our campsite. We walked along so peaceful. It was not nearly as warm as yesterday and not nearly as difficult. Super sweet deal we’ve got going here! We walked through grassland.

It was 5 when we pulled into our spot. Love getting in early. I even walked down to the stream to wash off. Dinner is next. Dal Baht by Peak.

In the morning I tried to get a bit of rest. Then at lunch Gunnar and I did a photo-op on a log. See in picture below. 👇🏿

Water is super important for us to live. Without it we all go bye bye. As far as I’m concerned, if the water is running then we should be happy. See you tomorrow.

This morning I really did not want to deflate my sleeping pad. My eyes were trying to open but I was TIRED. The first couple of miles weren’t too hard. At some water trough (3 miles in) we sat down for breakfast. Same as yesterday, Backpackers Pantry.

Then we had the climb up Miller Peak. I wasn’t sure what to expect. 4-5K of elevation gain could be easy or impossible! 😩 There were switch-backs. The climb didn’t kill me. It was windy, but not like cold. I was still in my Nike top and bottoms that did very little in the way of warmth.

As we climbed a bit higher I remembered that when we were on the Tahoe Rim Trail I’d say only Taylor Swift could get me up the hills. I was such a Swiftie then. I tried it though. It worked great, but not quite as good as when we got up to the ridge line. Up there I turned on Clocks by Coldplay (my favorite band!) It felt like I was in the scene from Peter Pan where the two fly off to Neverland. I was on my way to Neverland, minus the wings. 🫠

At Bathtub Springs we got some water for ramen later. On the last ascent I compiled a Tucson bucket list.

It’s a lot for someone who hiked 115 miles in 6 damn days. 😝

After getting as close to the top of Miller Peak as the trail took us we could see into Mexico on one side. Literally, we saw the wall. If we went on to the other side of the ridge line we looked into Sierra Vista and had cell service, but not on the other side. At this point we were up around 9K feet with big views.

Just as we started descending it started raining. I popped my rain coat on over my top and changed into my pants.

We were really out in the open and then heard thunder and we even saw lightning. Not super safe because we were high up on the side of a mountain with not much in the way of trees. It got pretty scary as we contoured around hills down to the end. Rain pelting my body, wind blowing madly, NOT HAPPY.  😠 There was no point in waiting out the storm, because there was no place to hide out and our trail was going down, which made more sense than staying up high.  I was leading and we basically ran the last 3-4 miles.  My Aries “get it done” attitude kicked in. I am not a fan of the elements especially lightning on a mountain top. Just stick me in some 65 degree weather and I’ll be jamming.

We finally made it to Montezuma Pass, which is about as close to the southern terminus as people are allowed currently. At the parking lot there was a pavilion that we sheltered under while waiting for Joelle, the trail angel, to pick us up. I was freezing from being in the cold rain for two hours, so I pulled out my sleeping bag and ate some lunch at last!

Well, that finishes 700 of the 800 miles of the AZT for now! The remaining section will be a bit of time, because it involves waiting for trail repairs in the Grand Canyon after the flooding.

Next up, a night in Sierra Vista at Rodeway Inn and then Gabrielle, a different TA, will bring us to Tucson tomorrow.

I was blowing everywhere all day. PJ hiked with such vigor I was nervous I would bounce right off the pack. Then the rain started and just wouldn’t let up. My fur was all wet and Gunnar was cursing about his pants getting soaked. Rough day over all, but I’m tough!
