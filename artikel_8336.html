British Adventurer Karl Bushby Completes 28-Year, 31,000-Mile Hike Around the World

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

British explorer Karl Bushby has officially crossed the finish line of a 28-year-long hike around the world, returning to his hometown of Hull, England on Monday after walking more than 31,000 miles from the southern tip of South America to the United Kingdom.

The former paratrooper set out on the self-funded trip, which he called the Goliath Expedition, on November 1st, 1998 from Punta Arenas, Chile. He was 29 years old. Bushby had two strict, self-imposed rules: no motorized transport of any kind to advance his path, and no returning home until the entire continuous line was finished.

Now, at 57, Bushby has made it back home.

Originally projected to take roughly 12 years, extreme geographic hazards and geopolitical stalemates pushed the timeline to 28 years, as Bushby crossed four continents and nearly 30 countries. He navigated the infamous Darién Gap between Colombia and Panama, survived an unprecedented 2006 foot crossing of the frozen Bering Strait from Alaska to Russia, spent nearly a decade negotiating Russian visas and Siberian winters, and was jailed twice for illegal crossings.

To maintain the unbroken continuity of his route around conflict zones like Ukraine, Bushby completed a 31-day open-water swim across the Caspian Sea. He then secured clearance from French authorities to swim the English Channel, landing at Shakespeare Beach near Dover in October 2026 before completing his final 250-mile leg north to Hull.

Karl Bushby at his recent homecoming celebration in Hull. Photo: Hullian111, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

While the homecoming was celebrated internationally, Bushby has spoken candidly about the psychological weight of finishing. After spending nearly three decades on this project, adjusting to life back home without the overarching purpose of his trip will be a different kind of challenge.

“My purpose and reason for living is about to end,” he admitted prior to his arrival. “It’s going to be difficult … trying to blend back into a society that you just don’t know anymore.”

A crowd of hundreds gathered outside Hull City Hall and lined local streets to greet Bushby as he pushed his trademark gear cart through town. He completed the final steps of the route at his mother’s home before sharing an emotional reunion with her on the sidewalk after a decade apart.

“They say it takes a village, but literally it took a world to get me here,” Bushby said to the crowd after finishing. “The kindness of strangers, so many, from individuals to nation states to get me back here today. I didn’t do this alone. There was a big team. The biggest team: Earth.”

With the trek concluded, Bushby becomes the first person on record to complete an unbroken, continuous foot journey of this scale across the globe. Artifacts, route logs, and gear from the 28-year expedition will go on public display in Hull starting this week.
