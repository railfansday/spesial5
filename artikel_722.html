Mega Tours by Oasis, Dua Lipa and Coldplay Drive U.K. Music’s Economic Contribution to Record-Highs

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The This Is Music report provides a comprehensive assessment of the U.K. music industry's performance domestically and on the world stage.

Headline-making tours by the likes of Oasis, Coldplay and Dua Lipa helped drive the U.K. music industry toward generating a record £8.4 billion ($11.1 billion) for the country’s economy in 2025. The figure marks a 5.7% increase on the previous year, according to the newly-released This Is Music report from UK Music, the umbrella organization representing a range of industry bodies, including the BPI and collection society PRS For Music. Published annually, This Is Music provides a comprehensive assessment of the U.K. music industry’s performance domestically and on the world stage. The report examines employment figures, global music sales and revenue generated through live ticket sales, tourism and other industry activity, offering an overview of music’s contribution to the country’s overall GDP.

The 2026 report points to the international success of British artists as a key driver of growth, with major overseas tours by Oasis, Coldplay, Lipa, Sam Fender and Robbie Williams helping boost figures. Recorded music exports also reached a new high of £800 million ($1.05 billion), up from £794 million ($1.04 billion) in 2024.

The Oasis Live ‘25 Tour, which was recently immortalized in Stephen Knight’s blockbuster Oasis: Don’t Look Back In Anger documentary, hit cities such as Seoul, Tokyo, Mexico City, Melbourne, Buenos Aires, Chicago, Toronto and Los Angeles following its initial U.K. and Ireland run in July and August. Lipa’s Radical Optimism tour, meanwhile, took in locations such as Seattle, San Francisco and Amsterdam, Madrid, Munich, Auckland and Sydney, alongside U.K. shows at Wembley Stadium and Liverpool’s Anfield Stadium.

In the U.K., 2025 total music tourism revenues — covering expenditure including the cost of a ticket for a concert or festival travel, accommodation and meals while traveling to events — were £11.2 billion ($14.7 billion), up 11.3% from the previous year.

Overall music exports also reached a record £5 billion ($6.9 billion) in 2025, up 3.6% on the previously reported figure of £4.8 billion ($6.33 million), with U.K. remaining the second-biggest recorded music exporter after the U.S. The figure includes revenues generated outside the U.K., such as sales and streams of recorded music by British artists and labels, performances of U.K.-copyrighted compositions and master recordings, merch sales and brand endorsements.

Central Cee’s 2025 LP Can’t Rush Greatness spurred a stateside breakthrough for the 28-year-old, becoming the first album by a U.K. rap artist to hit the top 10 on the Billboard 200 back in January. Progressive metal group Sleep Token topped the chart with Even In Arcadia later in the year, while the likes of RAYE, PinkPantheress and Sienna Spiro all scored hit singles in the U.S., with the latter’s “Die On This Hill” peaking at No. 19 on the Billboard Hot 100.

Back on home turf, Olivia Dean’s second LP, The Art Of Loving, spent eight non-consecutive weeks at No. 1 on the Official U.K. Albums Chart and scooped four BRIT Awards, while the sector benefited from chart-topping albums by the likes of Yungblud, Sam Fender and Wet Leg. However, despite breakthroughs for Lola Young and Myles Smith in 2025, it also reports that fewer new U.K. artists have emerged since 2020.

Employment levels lifted by a marginal 0.3% in 2025, with the U.K. music industry supporting 220,500 full-time equivalent jobs in 2025, compared to the 219,770 recorded the previous year. The report states that “encouraging” growth was recorded in roles across music merchandising and professional services, including music law and accountancy.

Elsewhere, This Is Music also sees UK Music urge the Government to secure long-term funding for the BBC as part of its upcoming Royal Charter renewal. The issue was recently highlighted at UK Music’s Our BBC: Our Music campaign launch at the House of Commons on Oct. 19, which brought together musicians, BBC presenters including Nick Grimshaw and Greg James and industry execs to celebrate the broadcaster’s wider impact on the industry.

Another area where UK Music said action is needed from the Government is to ensure that threats from generative AI usage across the industry are addressed. The report references a 2026 survey by Berklee College of Music which found that AI was used in 18.5% of occasions to “generate a soundalike when a client insisted on using a specific popular song they could not afford to license”, and says that composers across film, TV and advertising or music production libraries are particularly vulnerable to having their work replaced by generative AI.

In a statement, Tom Kiehl, UK Music’s chief executive, said: “Our music industry has once again shown why it is one of the UK’s greatest economic and cultural success stories. This year’s This Is Music report reveals a sector that continues to grow and innovate despite significant challenges.

“We should rightly celebrate what has been achieved over the past year, but we must also ask what is needed to ensure that this success continues. As a matter of priority, the Government must use the upcoming U.K, EU summit on Nov. 20 to work to remove barriers to EU exports. Also, it must not give AI a free pass and should support a sustainably funded BBC — and deliver economic growth for music in every postcode.“We are not simply asking the Government to act. We stand ready to be part of the solution too. The U.K. music industry has never lacked talent, ambition or creativity. The challenge now is ensuring the right environment exists for those strengths to flourish. If we get that right, the opportunities ahead are enormous.”

Dr. Jo Twist OBE, CEO of the BPI, added: “2025 was another banner year for British music, with a brilliant new generation of talent emerging and being supported to success by our world-class recorded music industry and the wider ecosystem.

“As ever, the U.K.’s label businesses had a big part to play in contributing to British music’s overall growth, with the U.K. remaining the world’s second-largest exporter of recorded music and British annual recorded exports topping £800 million for the first time ever. Musical success came from all across the U.K., proving once again that with the right infrastructure and the support of industry, local scenes can create global impact.”

Responding to the findings, Mark Davyd, CEO of the Music Venue Trust, welcomed the international success of British artists but warned that the economic growth highlighted in the figures was not being felt equally across the industry. He said: “It is obviously welcome news for successful British artists, companies and organizations that the U.K. Music Report continues to demonstrate a robust and resilient music sector. Sadly, the experience at the grassroots level remains very different, as will be shown when the MVT Annual Report for 2026 is released in January.

“Venues continue to close, employment continues to decline, and the U.K,’s grassroots music sector desperately needs to be brought into the wider industry’s success with a comprehensive approach to reinvestment. The way to do that is already in place; the long-discussed Grassroots Levy. Adoption of that mechanism is lagging far behind both the sector’s needs and the Government’s stated ambitions. A successful industry needs solid foundations. Let’s build them.”

David Martin, CEO, Featured Artists Coalition, concurred: “Given the challenges facing U.K. music, today’s headline figures are extraordinarily positive and a testament to our remarkable talent. But growth is slowing, and success is not being felt evenly. For all the stadium-sized successes, emerging and mid-tier artists face increasingly difficult conditions.“This report must be a catalyst for further action from Government and industry to ensure artists can develop, reach new audiences and build sustainable careers. The future growth of our industry depends on it.”
