Gorillaz Celebrate Mexico’s ‘Revolutionary Spirit’ at Corona Capital, Bring Out Johnny Marr and Trueno

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The British band returned to Mexico City for the first time in eight years to headline the first day of the popular festival.

Gorillaz returned to Mexico City on Friday (Nov. 20) for the first time in eight years, headlining the opening day of Corona Capital. During the long-awaited show, frontman Damon Albarn praised the country’s “revolutionary spirit.” Guitarist Johnny Marr and Argentine rapper Trueno joined the band as special guests — and Albarn also gave a shoutout to Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum. Last week, Sheinbaum traveled to Shenzhen, China, for the 2026 Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit, flying economy alongside other passengers. The decision made waves on Chinese social media — and came up again Friday at the popular Mexican festival, where 72,000 people packed the Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez, according to promoter Ocesa.

“I f— with Mexico because you have a revolutionary spirit here,” Albarn told the crowd. “This is an inspirational country, and I don’t know too much about your president, but the fact that she traveled economy class, for me, is a really important… It’s a statement, you know? So, I salute you all.”

Albarn made the comment while wearing a red beret and military-style jacket, an image that fit the occasion: Mexico’s Revolution Day, observed every Nov. 20 to mark the start of the 1910 armed uprising against Porfirio Díaz, who had been in power for more than 30 years.

Earlier in the set, Johnny Marr joined the band for “The Empty Dream Machine” and “Casablanca”; he played on the studio versions of both on Gorillaz’s The Mountain (2026). He also performed “The Plastic Guru” and “The Sweet Prince,” which also appear on the album.

Trueno, meanwhile, joined the collective — formed 28 years ago by Albarn, former frontman of Blur, and illustrator Jamie Hewlett — for “THE MANIFESTO,” their collaboration from The Mountain. He also freestyled during “Clint Eastwood,” the song that closed the set.

When Trueno shouted “¡Viva México, cabrones!” during “Clint Eastwood,” Albarn burst out laughing and kept singing offbeat as he tried to hold it together. Trueno also used the stage to hype up his favorite soccer team, Club Atlético Boca Juniors, proudly wearing the club’s blue-and-gold jersey.

Mexican singer Luisa Almaguer was another special guest, joining Gorillaz for “Latin Simone (¿Qué Pasa Contigo?),” the 2001 song originally sung by the late Cuban star Ibrahim Ferrer for the band’s self-titled debut album. Her appearance made for an emotional moment with the crowd. Almaguer previously took part in Albarn’s Africa Express project and was chosen to open Gorillaz’s Latin American tour in November and December.

The British collective also welcomed singer Kara Jackson for “Orange County,” U.S. rapper Bootie Brown for “Dirty Harry” and “Stylo,” and MC Black Thought for “The Sad God.”

Alongside Gorillaz, other acts performing on the first day of Corona Capital included James Blake, Mumford & Sons, CHVRCHES, Yung Lean, The Kooks, Violet Grohl, Jamie xx, Durand Jones & The Indications, CMAT and Grace Ives.

The festival’s second day, Saturday (Nov. 21), is set to feature Twenty One Pilots, The Offspring, Pierce the Veil, Underworld, Sébastien Tellier and Peaches, among others.
