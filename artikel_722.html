Josephine Vaccarello on Booking Harry Styles, Artist Residencies & the Future of Concerts | Billboard On The Record

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

From Radio City Music Hall to Madison Square Garden and Sphere, Josephine Vaccarello has had a front-row seat to the evolution of live entertainment. The president of MSG Entertainment and Billboard’s 2026 Touring Executive of the Year joins Billboard On The Record host Kristin Robinson live from the Billboard Live Music Summit to discuss how the live-music landscape is changing, from the rise of immersive concert experiences and artist residencies to growing fan expectations. Following a record-breaking summer at Madison Square Garden, Vaccarello offers an inside look at what it takes to bring the world’s biggest artists to iconic venues and book long-term residencies.

Kristin Robinson: Hello, guys, and welcome to a special live episode of Billboard On The Record. This is our music business podcast at Billboard, and I’m joined by Josephine Vaccarello. Thank you so much for coming, Josephine. I also want to say you were the first person to ever come on this show, so thank you for taking a chance on us last year when we were first getting started, back then, you know, the Sphere was really kicking off. Wizard of Oz was about to hit the Sphere, and sorry, Sphere. I always get that wrong. It’s just Sphere, not the Sphere. Pro tip.

But I wanna, I wanna take it back to the beginning part of your career to start our conversation. As our Touring Executive of the Year, I find it really inspiring that you got to the level you are today as the president of MSG Entertainment by starting as an administrative assistant, and I’m wondering if you can kind of just lay the groundwork for us. What was the job back in 1998? What was that first entry level role at MSG for you?

Josephine Vaccarello: So I started at Radio City as an admin assistant, and I think for the first six months of the job, OK, I’m aging myself. I typed up Rolodex cards and I answered phones. That was the job. Wow, I loved it.

So was that always your goal? You were like, “I have to be in live music,” or was it one of those happy accidents?

It was definitely a happy accident. I mean, I’ve always loved live music. I mean, I performed when I was younger. I had a job at Jones Beach during the summer at concessions, so I could see shows for free.

So I mean, it was always you know it’s always been part of my life. But I had a friend who was a headhunter and said she had a job at Radio City. “Do you want to go?” And I was like, “Sure.” And that’s that was it.

I mean, Radio City. I was just telling her this backstage. Radio City is one of my favorite venues. It’s just so gorgeous.
