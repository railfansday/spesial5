There’s a New Push at COP31 in Turkey: Electrification

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

With war squeezing global oil and gas supplies, leaders at COP31 are backing a plan that would mirror China’s rapid grid expansion.

Reporting from the COP31 climate talks in Antalya, Turkey

If the leaders of this year’s United Nations climate conference achieve one of their big goals, countries will rally behind a new global target on expanding the reach of electric power.

The implication: The rest of the world will have to look a lot more like China.

Over the past two decades, China has raced ahead of other industrialized countries, building ultrahigh-voltage power lines, constructing the world’s largest high-speed rail system and filling its roads with electric vehicles. It is sometimes called the first electrostate.

At the same time, countries across the West and in Africa are scarcely more electrified than they were a generation ago.

That disparity is one of the things the climate conference, known this year as COP31, is campaigning to change. The event’s leaders say the shift is well-suited for a year when the United States’ war in Iran has pinched the global oil and gas supply.

“Electrification is the answer,” said Murat Kurum, the Turkish environment minister, who is leading the 12-day conference in Antalya, Turkey. “Everyone now understands that energy that you must import is energy that you do not control.”

Electrification involves replacing technologies that directly burn fossil fuels. That means swapping gas-powered vehicles for E.V.s, for instance, and trading oil-fired furnaces for heat pumps. It also entails looking for electric alternatives to generate the steam and intense heat needed by factories.
