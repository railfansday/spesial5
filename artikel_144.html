In other news, France’s national blood service has said hackers accessed control systems and boosted temperatures at three cold stores stocking vital blood products, in an apparent escalation of recent cyber-attacks on fridges and freezers across Europe.

The French blood service (EFS) said temperature and defrosting thresholds had been raised to “very high levels” in the attack, adding that its alarm systems had allowed technicians to intervene before the products were compromised.

The EFS is France’s public blood transfusion operator, collecting, testing, preparing and distributing products such as blood, plasma and platelets to more than 1,900 hospitals around the country. It supports more than a million patients a year.

The attack, which EFS said had occurred overnight on Monday, came as the temperatures of refrigerators and freezers in supermarkets in almost a dozen European countries soared as high as 50C (122F), causing millions of euros of food damage.

German foreign minister Johann Wadephul has said the results of the US midterm elections will mean some “continuity” in Germany’s relations with the Trump administration, but also a “change” in ties with the US Democrats as he hoped to see some breakthrough on Ukraine.

Berlin will “continue to seek common ground” with the Trump administration, “striving for compromise” on key issues in the face of global challenges.

But these elections “also signal change” as the Democratic Party’s success – which he noted was “of a magnitude rare in American history” – will “likely seek to shift the political discourse in the US.”

He said he did not “any complications” arising from this result, and noted that the political engagement will now need to be “broader” and “perhaps even more interesting in certain aspects.”

He said many foreign policy issues featured prominently in the US elections, including tariffs and the war in Iran.

Wadephul added that he hoped for “more dialogue in Congress, across party lines, about what can be done collectively to support Ukraine.

“I think they’ve been holding back on that issue for quite some time ‌now during the election campaign,” he said.

“I also know that in both chambers – and within both parties – there are staunch supporters of Ukraine who clearly recognise that this Russian war of aggression threatens not only security and freedom in Europe but also in the United States.”
