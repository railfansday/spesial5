After Testing the Noco Boost Plus Jump Starter, I Appreciate How Well It Works In the Rain

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

It’s safer than many others thanks to its watertight ports, though it lacks some nice-to-have features.

The only thing worse than discovering your car has a dead battery is having it happen when it’s raining. If you live in a wet environment as I do in Oregon, you’ll be glad you own a portable jump starter like the Noco Boost Plus GB40. With its three watertight ports closed, it’s impervious to dust intrusion—but more importantly, its IP65 rating means it can withstand heavy rain and even low-pressure blasts with a hose.

Besides its consummate performance in wet conditions, the GB40 is worth considering because it’s compact, lightweight (just over 2 pounds), and relatively easy to use. I also appreciate that it includes spark-proof and reverse-polarity protection, an output port for charging portable electronics, and a six-mode flashlight.

That said, the GB40 isn’t perfect. While I found that it punched above its weight in jump-starting power and its battery life is adequate, it isn’t the most powerful portable jump starter among those I’ve recently tested, and its 2,150 milliampere-hour (mAh) capacity is definitely on the lower side. It also doesn’t have an LED screen but rather uses lights as indicators of its status and operation. Even more crucially, it uses a manual override function to boost severely depleted batteries instead of a dedicated boost button.

I tested the Noco Boost Plus GB40 over several months in Oregon spanning the wet and dry seasons. When testing a portable jump starter, I evaluate how quickly it starts a vehicle, how many jump-starts it provides before needing to be recharged, how easy it is to use, its features, and the number and effectiveness of its auxiliary power ports to charge portable devices such as a smartphone.

The Noco GB40 easily fits in the palm of my hand, and the rubberized case and ribs built into each side make it easy to grip, which is important when working around an engine with moving parts. Its small size also means it’s easier to store in a vehicle’s glove box than a larger device like the Gooloo GT6000, which I recommend as the best portable jump starter for most people.

The Noco GB40 doesn’t have an LED screen like many of the portable jump starters from Gooloo, its main competitor. But the touch-based control panel on one side makes it relatively simple to operate. This consists of a large power button with a white on/off LED next to it, a four-segment multicolor battery-level indicator that shows the state of charge in 25 percent increments, an error indicator, a boost LED, a manual override button, and a button for the flashlight.

With a peak output of only 1,000 amps, the Noco Boost Plus GB40 is the least powerful portable jump starter among those I’ve tested. Its 2,150 mAh rating also makes it the lowest in reserve-power capacity. That said, it had no issues jump-starting my Honda Element when I left the lights on and drained the battery. It even cranked the 6.8-liter V10 in my RV when I came back to it after months in storage and found the battery depleted.

I also intentionally left it powered on (since it doesn’t have an automatic shutoff like the Astro AI S8, which I recommend for its value) and it lasted about 24 hours before shutting down. Via a 5-volt/2.1-amp USB-C input, it charges about as fast as other portable jump starters with the same specs, and it took just under four hours to charge from 0 to 100 percent using the supplied USB-C cable.

Among features, the flashlight on the GB40 is the most compelling. It has six modes, including an SOS and strobe, which puts it well ahead of the competition. It also has two lenses, one on each side of the output ports, whereas most portable jump starters use a single flashlight lens. While the flashlight isn’t as bright as some others at 100 lumens (for comparison, the $70 Gooloo GP2000, has a 400-lumen flashlight), I found the Noco GB40 more than adequate for use at night.

The Noco GB40’s USB-C charging input doesn’t double as an output port to charge portable devices, which puts it at a disadvantage. It has just one 5-volt/2.1-amp USB-A output port for charging portable electronics (the Gooloo GP2000 has two) and charged my iPhone 16 from 0 percent to 80 percent in about two hours.

Instead of a boost button that supplies a bit more amperage to jump-start a very dead battery, the GB40 has a manual override button that, when pushed and held for three seconds, detects a severely depleted battery (below three volts).

It’s a far less user-friendly feature, plus it disables the Noco’s automatic spark-proof and reverse-polarity protection. As such, users need to be extra careful not to allow the cable clamps to touch, which can cause dangerous sparking. To complicate things further, the GB40 has an LED labeled “boost” that’s simply an indicator light to let you know the device is properly connected to the battery.

There are certainly much more powerful portable jump starters than the Noco Boost Plus GB40 and much of the competition offers features that are far more convenient (albeit for a higher price point), perhaps most significantly, a boost button. Above all, the GB40’s waterproof rating is the best reason to buy it, plus its compact size, six-mode flashlight, and emergency charging for small devices make it an appealing choice. It also provides ample and reliable power to give a dead battery in most vehicles a boost and start the engine.

Other Portable Jump Starters We Recommend

The Gooloo GT6000 packs 27,000 mAh of reserve power capacity and 6,000-amp peak current. This allows it to jump-start vehicles ranging from a small motorcycle to a heavy-duty truck with a 12.0-liter diesel engine. The GT6000 offers a standby time of two years, and it’s ready to jump-start a vehicle after just seven minutes of recharging. It’s got a heap of charging ports, including a 100-watt USB-C in/out that can top off the internal battery from empty to full in 1.4 hours. The device’s 3.2-inch screen makes checking battery level, charging output, and more easy and foolproof.

With 4,000 amps of peak current and 24,000 mAh of capacity, the Wolfbox can jump-start up to 10.0-liter gas or 6.0-liter diesel engines. It holds a charge for more than a year. The 1.5-cubic-foot-per-minute air pump takes one minute to inflate a 17-inch tire from 30 to 36 psi. It also has a 65-watt USB-C in/out port to quickly recharge the internal battery, two USB-A ports to charge other devices, and a 400-lumen, three-mode LED flashlight.

The Noco AX65 combines a portable jump starter and a portable air compressor into a single device. The jump starter has an impressive 16,000 mAh capacity and delivers 2,000 amps of peak power. The tire inflator pumps out 2.3 cubic feet per minute of airflow. The jump starter cranks gas engines up to 8.0 liters and diesel engines up to 6.0 liters. The tire inflator has a digital display, auto shutoff, and memory settings like other air compressors I’ve tested but also pauses and then resumes inflation when a set pressure is reached to ensure proper inflation.
