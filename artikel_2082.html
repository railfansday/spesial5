Andy Burnham and his comments on the 3pm Saturday blackout got me thinking

The headline declared that Andy Burnham has urged caution on scrapping the 3pm Saturday TV blackout.

Ahead of this latest international break I was at the Fulham v Newcastle United game, the novelty of it being a rare 3pm kick-off for NUFC.

The BBC article that I was reading, stated: “The blackout originated in the 1960s when Burnley chairman Bob Lord encouraged the EFL to oppose television coverage of 3pm Saturday matches.”

I understand the sentiments expressed by Andy Burnham, as he doesn’t want to be seen as upsetting the lower league clubs who are dependent on match day receipts and the peripheral spending.

However, when you look at the amount of games in the Premier League played outside the Saturday 3pm kick-off, you begin to wonder.

The working man’s game where you could rock up to St James’ Park with a belly full of ale for a 3pm kick-off and pay at the turnstiles are a thing of the past and long gone.

Take a look at the Newcastle United fixtures so far this season and you will see Fulham really was novelty.

In these first five months of the 2026-27 Premier League season only three 3pm Saturday kick-offs for Newcastle United, home to Hull and away to Fulham, plus Ipswich away next month.

All of these Premier League games played outside of the 3pm Saturday slot is a boon for lower league clubs. Due to technology, I can watch Newcastle play at 3pm on a Saturday even when I don’t get a ticket for the match.

But the 3pm Saturday kick-off is such a rarity it has become an irrelevance.

As the Newcastle United games are now shown at an increasingly ridiculous range of times, that frees up Saturday afternoon for me and my mates to go down to Dulwich Hamlet on a Saturday afternoon and have a pint of Peckham pale ale on the terracing while watching live football in the Isthmian Premier League.

So as me and my lad fail to get home tickets for Newcastle United on a regular basis now (no need for tiny violins), we are down the Hamlet on a Saturday afternoon with hundreds of other like minded Premier League team supporting people, who simply want to see live football kicking off at a proper time.
