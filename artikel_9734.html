Rounding is a business rule: what your fee calculation actually charges

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

A fee calculator is mostly arithmetic, and the arithmetic is mostly correct. The part that produces support tickets is the last step: rounding. It looks like formatting. It is actually a business rule that decides whether the number on screen matches the number that was charged, and in a fee-heavy product those two disagree more often than anyone expects.

Here is what rounding actually does in a fee pipeline, and the three places it silently changes the answer.

Every language gives you several: half-up, half-even (banker's), ceiling, floor, truncation. They differ only on ties and on negatives, which is exactly where money lands when a refund is involved.

The important thing is that this is not a technical choice. Ceiling on a fee means the platform always collects a little more than the formula says; floor means it collects a little less. Over ten thousand small trades, "a little" is a line item. So the rounding mode belongs in the fee schedule as data, next to the rates — not hardcoded in the formatter where nobody will look for it.

If the mode lives in code and the rates live in the database, then a venue changing its rounding rule is a deploy, and until that deploy your numbers are wrong by a fraction of a basis point that nobody can see.

Fees are frequently computed as amount * rate, then converted, then rounded, then summed. Each of those steps is a place to round, and rounding early loses money in one direction and invents it in the other.

The failure mode is a total that does not equal the sum of its parts, which is the single most common complaint about any fee report.

0.1 + 0.2 != 0.3 is famous enough that most people avoid floats for money. The subtler trap is mixing: a Decimal rate applied to a float amount silently becomes a float, and the precision you carefully preserved is gone. If the amount arrives from JSON, it arrives as a float or a string, and which one it is depends on the client.

So the boundary matters: parse to Decimal at the edge, from the string, and never let a float into the calculation. Decimal(str(value)), not Decimal(value).

Users compare rates and then check the total. If the rate is 0.08% and the total implies 0.083%, the conversation is not about rounding — it is about trust. The fee page is where a trading product is most likely to be accused of something it did not do, and rounding is usually the actual explanation.

Rounding is a business rule, so store it with the rates. Keep full precision until the money boundary, round per line item, reconcile the sum, and test the ties. The rate is the part everyone argues about; the rounding is the part that is actually wrong.
