After Daniel Jones' Colts release, four NFL landing spots for veteran QB

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Daniel Jones, it turns out, won't be the one to break the Indianapolis Colts' post-Andrew Luck retirement quarterback woes.

Jones was released by the team on Friday, Nov. 20, 24 games into a two-year stint with the Colts that started with an 8-2 record in 2025 before injury and inconsistency led to a spectacular derailment of the franchise's hopes of contending in the wide-open AFC South.

The Colts moved on from Jones, in favor of uber-athletic but raw backup Anthony Richardson Sr., one day after falling to 3-8 on the season with a 26-3 loss to the Houston Texans. The Colts offense continued to sputter with Jones at the helm against the Texans. He passed for 144 yards on 12-for-27 passing and threw his league-leading 13th interception.

Jones couldn't rediscover the promise that he showed in 2025 before a suffering a torn right Achilles tendon in Week 14. The eighth-year quarterback was lauded by coaches and teammates in recovering quickly enough to be the Colts' Week 1 starter in 2026, but his 58.4 completion percentage this season is the worst of his career, and his 7-to-13 ratio of touchdowns to interceptions tanked his quarterback rating to a career-low 30.3 before his release.

Still, Jones fits the mold of a competitive NFL quarterback at 6-foot-5 and 230 pounds, and despite this year's struggles with accuracy, he has thrown 96 career touchdowns to 68 interceptions. At 29, Jones has plenty of potential years left to fill a meaningful role with an NFL team.

Jones' long-term outlook under center isn't likely to shake out until the offseason, but here are four options where he could fit for the remainder of the season.

With franchise quarterback Drake Maye potentially lost for the season to a reported fracture to his right tibia, the Patriots are suddenly 5-5 and staring down an eight-game stretch with Tommy DeVito under center. You tell us that head coach Mike Vrabel isn't looking around the league for options. Jones is only slightly older than DeVito but has 93 career starts to DeVito's eight, and would be a viable option to quickly pick up the Patriots' playbook if DeVito doesn't grasp the opportunity.

We'll take the layup here, as the Vikings were said to be interested in Jones before trading with the Las Vegas Raiders for current starter Kirk Cousins ahead of the NFL trade deadline. With Cousins in place, Jones would simply be an insurance piece to break in case of injury. But at 7-2 and locked in the toughest divisional playoff race in the NFL, the Vikings would be wise to hedge against a future with either injured Kyler Murray or ineffective Carson Wentz steering the ship.

With the Ravens at 4-6, this season may be all over but the shouting. Injuries to star Lamar Jackson and backup Tyler Huntley forced the Ravens to trade for journeyman QB Jacoby Brissett ahead of the deadline, only to see him flop in a 23-20 loss to the previously winless Los Angeles Chargers. Adding Jones to the island of misfit QBs of Brissett and Joe Fagnano might help stabilize things for a proud Ravens franchise. Or it might just be time to ride with Brissett and look toward 2027.

How about a return to the NFC East, where Jones started his career in six seasons with the New York Giants? At 2-7 and with starter Jalen Hurts out indefinitely with blood clots, the Eagles' season is already lost. But suffering through the next eight weeks with Joe Milton III and Tanner McKee, neither of whom is seen as "quarterback of the future" material, might be untenable in front of Philly's famously prickly fans. Would Jones change Philly's long-term outlook? Probably not. But could he be a passable Hail Mary for first-year offensive coordinator Sean Mannion to call up? Perhaps.
