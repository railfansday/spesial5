Forget the Market's Hottest Stocks. This Boring Dividend Stock Is Where I'd Put $1,000 Before 2027.

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

This unloved Dividend King isn't performing well right now, but history suggests it will get back on track.

By Reuben Gregg Brewer – Nov 26, 2026 at 8:32AM EST

Stock market volatility has ticked up dramatically in recent weeks, with the hottest stocks suddenly becoming some of the most volatile. If you are a long-term investor with a dividend focus and $1,000 to invest, you're better off taking a look at deeply unloved PepsiCo (PEP +0.88%). That will get you about seven shares.

There are good reasons for Wall Street to be downbeat about PepsiCo. However, the company's long and successful history suggests that investors who think in decades, not days, will be well rewarded. Here's why you should buy it before the start of 2027.

To get the bad news out of the way right up front, PepsiCo's business isn't doing particularly well right now. It isn't falling apart, either, but inflation, increasingly budget-conscious consumers, and shifts in consumer buying habits have taken a toll. In the third quarter of 2026, organic revenue growth was 3.1%. Coca-Cola (KO -0.03%), the company's primary competitor in the beverage space, saw organic sales rise more than twice as fast, at 7%, in the same quarter.

Simply put, investors are focusing on the industry leader and taking a show-me attitude with PepsiCo. You can see that in their valuations. For example, PepsiCo's dividend yield is 4.2% compared to Coca-Cola's 2.2%. Looking at more traditional valuation metrics, PepsiCo's price-to-sales ratio is just under 2x compared to a five-year average of 2.5x. Its price-to-earnings ratio is nearly 18x versus a five-year average of 25x. Coca-Cola's P/S ratio is 8x compared to a five-year average of 6.4x, and its P/E ratio is 28x compared to a five-year average of 26x. PepsiCo is cheap compared to its own history and relative to Coca-Cola.

That said, there are many reasons to like PepsiCo beyond valuation. For starters, as noted above, it isn't performing as well as Coca-Cola, but the company isn't doing poorly. It is probably best to view it as muddling through a difficult period. It has done that many times before over the last 54 years. If that seems oddly specific, it is. That's the number of years this Dividend King has increased its dividend. Sure, that's a decade less than Coca-Cola, but it is hard to complain about PepsiCo's streak.

Moreover, PepsiCo can stand toe-to-toe with Coca-Cola as a business. They are both industry leaders in brand strength, marketing prowess, innovation capabilities, and distribution. You aren't exactly stepping down to own PepsiCo. Meanwhile, PepsiCo offers you diversification that Coca-Cola does not. Coca-Cola is a beverage giant, but PepsiCo is a beverage giant (Pepsi), a snacking giant (Frito-Lay), and operates a large, well-respected packaged food business (Quaker Oats). For investors who value diversification, that may actually give it a leg up on Coca-Cola.

Meanwhile, it isn't like PepsiCo is sitting still and hoping conditions improve. It is actively working to shift its business in line with changes in consumer preferences. For example, it has acquired on-trend brands (Siete and Poppi), and it has been bringing out updated versions of existing brands, with a focus on healthier fare and protein. These are exactly the types of things that you would expect the company to do, but such moves can take time to play out.

If you are looking to buy a stock and see it go up the next day, PepsiCo won't be a good option for you. The turnaround could take a year or more. However, you'll be collecting an attractive 4.2% dividend yield backed by a Dividend King with an industry-leading business while you wait. For patient dividend investors, this could be a very compelling long-term opportunity.
