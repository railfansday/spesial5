Billionaire Stanley Druckenmiller Says He's 'Staying Away' From Stocks. Here's What He Bought and Sold Last Quarter

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The billionaire investor says he's focused on not losing money right now. His newest SEC filing shows how much he had already reshaped his portfolio by the end of September.

Stanley Druckenmiller, the billionaire founder of Duquesne Family Office, says he's sitting out the stock market for now.

"We are basically staying away," he said Tuesday at the TomorrowX Summit in Austin. "We are trying to not lose money as opposed to make money."

It's not clear exactly when he made that call. But a filing his firm sent to the SEC on Monday provides a detailed look at what he owned as of Sept. 30, showing a portfolio that was already shrinking.

Duquesne's latest 13F filing lists about $4.47 billion tied to U.S.-listed stocks and options as of Sept. 30, down from about $5.21 billion at the end of June, according to its previous filing. The number of positions fell to 65 from 95.

The firm sold out of dozens of companies and funds during the quarter, including Alphabet, AMD, Lam Research, Sandisk, Reddit, Delta Air Lines and United Airlines.

Perhaps most telling, it no longer listed call options on the S&P 500, the Russell 2000 and an equal-weight S&P 500 fund. Call options are bets that prices will rise. In June, those three positions covered about $341 million worth of the underlying funds.

The biggest holding by far is Natera (NTRA -0.35%), a genetic-testing company. It makes up about 29% of the disclosed portfolio, worth about $1.3 billion.

Druckenmiller didn't buy more to get there. Duquesne held about 3.16 million shares, slightly fewer than in June. The position grew because the stock did: Natera's shares gained about 50% during the quarter, and he let the winner run.

Among big shifts was one in semiconductors. Duquesne sold about 94% of its Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing (TSM -3.69%) shares, a stake worth about $282 million in June, and cut its STMicroelectronics shares nearly in half.

At the same time, it nearly tripled its Seagate Technology (STX +1.08%) shares and added call options on the stock, making Seagate its second-largest holding. It also opened a new position in Western Digital. Both companies make hard drives, which store much of the data that AI runs on.

Other notable moves include purchases of Unity Software (U -2.93%) (Duquesne's stake grew more than fivefold to about 4 million shares) and gold miners (with new stakes in Newmont, Barrick and Agnico Eagle, worth over $100 million combined). He also made new, smaller bets on Palantir, AppLovin and SpaceX, and bought more shares of Amazon, Sea Ltd and Fox.

A 13F has limits. It lists only the U.S.-listed stocks, funds and options a firm owns. It doesn't include short bets, currencies, bonds, or commodities, which are a big part of how Druckenmiller trades. And it's a snapshot from Sept. 30, seven weeks before Tuesday's comments.

That's how the filing and his "staying away" comment can both be true. His positions may have changed a lot since then.

By the time a 13F is public, the trades may already be undone. But the filing shows three habits worth borrowing.

First, conviction matters. Druckenmiller let Natera grow to nearly a third of his disclosed portfolio instead of trimming it.

Second, he seems to cut quickly when he loses confidence. In one quarter, his position count fell by 30, and his bullish bets on the overall market disappeared.

Third, cash can be a position. When one of the most successful investors of his generation says the outlook is unclear, it's fine to hold more cash and wait for a better opportunity.

For investors loaded up on semiconductor stocks, his quarter is worth a closer look. He didn't abandon tech, but he moved away from the chipmakers and toward the companies that store the data AI needs.
