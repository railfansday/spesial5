Williams handed penalties after minor breach of cost cap

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Williams "unintentionally committed" spending and procedural breach of 2025 budget cap after overspending by £627,000; Alpine found in procedural breach; watch the Qatar GP on Sky Sports F1 this weekend, with qualifying at 6pm on Saturday and the race at 4pm on Sunday

Williams have been given financial and aerodynamic testing penalties after committing a minor overspend breach of Formula 1's cost cap for the 2025 season.

The FIA announced Williams had accepted an Accepted Breach Agreement after being found to have "unintentionally committed" a minor overspend and procedural breach of the sport's spending rules for last year.

Williams overspent by £627,000 - 0.48 per cent over the 2025 cost cap - and in addition to a financial penalty, will receive a 1.5 per cent reduction in wind tunnel and CFD testing time over the next 12 months. The 2025 spending cap was $135m (£102m) plus inflation.

An FIA statement read: "The Cost Cap Administration recognised that WGPE [Williams Grand Prix Engineering Limited] has acted cooperatively and in good faith throughout the review process, provided additional information and evidence in a timely manner when so requested and that there is no evidence that WGPE has sought or obtained any advantage [sporting or otherwise] as a result of the identified breaches."

Explaining the nature of the breach, Williams, who finished fifth in last year's Constructors' Championship but are ninth with two races of this year to go, said in a statement: "The breach relates to administrative costs we incurred making changes to our company structure. We believed these costs fell outside of the cap." A minor breach is defined as an overspend of less than five per cent of the cost cap.

"Following discussions with the FIA, we have agreed to resolve the matter in line with their interpretation that the costs should have been in cap.

"The FIA has noted that Williams did not gain any sporting or financial advantage, and nor did we ever seek to. It has also noted that we acted cooperatively and in good faith throughout the review process.

"Integrity is central to what Williams stands for and we can now close this case and remain focused on our future. We have always been and will continue to be strong supporters of the cost cap, which naturally requires accurate policing to be effective."

Alpine were found to have committed a procedural breach, related to inaccuracies in their full year reporting documentation.

The FIA noted that they had also "acted cooperatively and in good faith throughout the review process and has sought to provide additional information and evidence when requested in a timely manner and that there is no accusation or evidence that Alpine has sought or obtained any undue advantage". Alpine's spending was below the cost cap limit.

Alpine will pay a financial penalty to the FIA after also entering into an accepted breach agreement for the procedural breach.

All nine other teams, which included 2026 newcomers Cadillac, were found to be in compliance for 2025, along with all five power unit manufacturers for their respective spending cap.
