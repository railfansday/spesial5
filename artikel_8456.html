Paying Your Mortgage by Credit Card – Worth It Only Fee-Free or for a Bonus

Paying your mortgage by credit card through a payment service costs a fee of about 3%, more than the 2% a good card pays back. Do it only through a no-fee route such as Bilt’s credit cards, or to reach a sign-up bonus’s spending requirement with a payment you can pay off in full.

Plastiq, a service that pays your lender and bills your card, adds 2.99% to each payment plus at least 99 cents to deliver it. On a $2,000 mortgage payment, that’s about $61 to earn $40 from a card that pays 2% back, so you lose about $21 every month.

Few lenders accept cards directly, which is why the fee is hard to avoid. Bilt’s credit cards, such as the no-annual-fee Bilt Blue Card and the $95-a-year Bilt Obsidian Card, are the exception. They can pay your mortgage, whichever company collects it, with no transaction fee and earn up to 1.25 points per dollar on the payment. The rate rises with your other spending on the card, so earning 1 point per dollar on a $2,000 payment requires about $1,500 in everyday purchases that month. Bilt takes the money from your bank account within a day, though, so the payment buys you no time.

The fee pays for itself only when the payment earns a sign-up bonus you’d otherwise miss. Say a new card pays $500 once you spend $4,000 in its first three months. Two $2,000 mortgage payments through Plastiq get you there for about $122 in fees, so the $500 bonus covers them about four times over.

Check two things before the first payment. Plastiq’s terms warn that some card brands and banks limit what a card can pay, so confirm yours can pay a mortgage there. Then ask the bank that issued your card whether the charge will count toward the bonus. Banks decide how to classify the charge, and some leave cash advances and payments on installment loans such as a mortgage out of a bonus’s spending.

A payment you can’t pay off by the card’s due date becomes debt at the card’s interest rate, and the bigger balance raises your credit utilization, the share of your credit limits in use. Charge a mortgage payment only when the cash to pay off the card is already in your account.
