We Really Need to Talk About the <i><em>MobLand</em></i> Season 2 Finale

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

A certain character’s fate hangs in the balance.

Nothing is simple in MobLand. Two seasons in, the plot has racked up more lines than Eddie put up his nose the night he stabbed Tommy Stevenson in season 1. Actually, that’s not strictly true. One thing is simple in MobLand: Eddie. He is a simpleton of truly Homeric (as in Simpson) proportions: a smarmy, sneering, simpering Freudian mess, channelled with gimlet-eyed precision by Anson Boon. All he wants is his God-given right to be respected.

Trouble is, when you’re an anti-Midas who turns everything he touches to shit, respect is a dish that never leaves the kitchen. Eddie is, in Kevin’s words in season 1, “a knifey little twerp.” That stabbing set the whole bloody saga in motion, so it’s only right that two seasons later we end with Eddie the Incompetent. Full circle, then, like a roundabout made of corpses.

MobLand opened with Eddie turning a stabbing into a gang war, so it seems fitting that we end with Eddie turning an assassination into an own goal. But we’ll get to that.

It seems Conrad’s chickens are coming home to roost, and his marbles are jangling around his head like loose change. We open the episode with his card being declined at a petrol station. In frustration, he shoots the till boy dead. But he knows what it means.

“We’re getting lifted,” he tells Maeve in the car. Police are raiding the entire business: bank accounts are frozen, warrants are out, and Tattersall is telling reporters the Harrigans have links to terror groups across the globe. The family convenes at a dingy old safe house out of town.

“We’re terrorists,” says Seraphina. “We’re looking at life in Guantánamo. It’s now or never.”

Her plan has been bubbling for a while. She calls it Harrigan 2.0: going global.

Remember the diplomatic suitcase of diamonds Seraphina collected from her billionaire stepdad Charlton in the last episode? It was meant to fund her and Bella’s takeover of the Harrigan operation. Now, it seems, Harry has it. He takes her aside to let her know he knows. “How did you know?” she asks, incredulous.

Then he says the three words he says more than any others in MobLand: “It’s my job.”

Inside the safe house, the family are all there, and everyone has a plan to pitch. Kevin wants to crawl back to Kat and hand over Conrad, Maeve, and Eddie. “Clean the house,” he growls to Harry, out of earshot of the others.

Conrad, meanwhile, isn’t sure what to do. “What are our options?” he asks Harry.

“We don’t have any,” comes the terse reply. “Either we run seven ways and it’s every man and woman for themselves, or we go back to Kat and try to strike some kind of deal.”

Kevin proposes offering her 50 percent and first working out who’s a liability. “And right now I’m seeing a lot of liabilities. Right, Harry?”

Bella has had it up to ’ere with Kevin’s pusillanimity. “Oh, you nearly got to the end of a sentence without saying ‘Harry,’” she taunts. “I know he saved you from a bumming once but… Jesus.”

Kevin pulls a pistol and fires, missing her ear by millimeters. Conrad draws. “Put the gun down, boy,” he hisses. Bella steps forward. “Go on then, big boy.”

You could cut the tension with piano wire.

“Fuck this, I’m getting us out of this mess,” says Seraphina, defusing the standoff by fetching a fistful of diamonds from the car. “Fifteen Type IIa brilliants. North of ten million euros,” she tells Conrad, placing them in his hand.

The plan: offer Kat a cut of the empire. “This time tomorrow we’re back in business.”

Remember that glorious, long-winded speech Conrad gave in Spain about the good old days, how he came from nothing, and how Maeve has been his compass through the carnage of his life? “We were born outlaws and we’ll die outlaws,” he bellowed.

Well, now he knows the jig is up. Looking balefully at the floor, he grunts: “Do it.”

So how will Harry play this? He slips out to his 4x4 and rings Kat. “At 5 p.m. I will meet your diplomatic courier. I will pass to said courier €10 million in untraceable diamonds. Upon authentication, I will give you the location of the Harrigan family.”

A double cross? It must be a ruse. He simply MUST have a plan… Then, hanging up, he mutters: “Fuck this, I’m done.”

He returns to the family and lies: “Kat will take the ten million and 50 percent.” Too easy by half.

As Frankie put it two episodes ago: “Is that it? Unless you’ve got some brilliant plans, that is actually a bit shit. What are you up to, Harry?”

We’re more than halfway through and haven’t heard a peep from Frankie, our laconic, crocodilian villain. Ah, but here he is, getting the news of Harry’s volte-face from Tattersall. “How do we know it’s not a bluff?”

“He’s not stupid,” says Tattersall. “He knows when the game’s up.”

“Well, you know me,” leers Frankie. “I can’t resist a gamble.” Guns are grabbed from car boots, and off he pootles with a gaggle of tooled-up goons to send the Harrigans back to Hell.

It’s all unfolding very quickly now, as denouements always should. Back at the safe house, Conrad is still seeing the dead, including the petrol station lad he just murdered. Maeve says he’s been saying their names in his sleep.

The old loon’s marbles are now departing his head like passengers from a cancelled flight. But there’s one thing that’ll straighten any man out: a black pearl of Maeve wisdom. “We’ve been stuck up tighter holes than Kat McAllister’s,” she reassures him. “But when you’re stuck up someone’s hole, there’s at least two ways out.”

Quite so. And who better to lead the way than Harry? As ever, he picks the exit that leads straight back to where he came in. Unbeknownst to us, he has gone up Kat’s backside, rummaged about, and come out the other end holding her entire career. The “betrayal” was a ruse from the start.

So when Frankie’s goons arrive armed to the teeth, it’s a trap: flash grenades, smoke grenades, poison gas, and Zosia on the exit with a machine gun for anyone daft enough to run. “Oh, Harry,” concedes Frankie, before a mighty gunfight erupts and Harry tears through the house like Keyser Söze in a gas mask. “So this isn’t the beginning of a beautiful friendship,” smiles Frankie, choking in the corner. Harry shoots him in the knee.

Then Harry rings Kat. Those “untraceable” diamonds? Very traceable indeed, straight to a terrorist organization. He has bribed a low-ranking diplomatic courier to deliver them along with a “little present, just for you.”

She opens the box, ashen-faced. It’s Donnie’s blue and rotting head. “This one you might find harder to justify,” he tells her.

“My family are gonna hunt you down and feed you your balls,” she spits.

“Probably,” says Harry. “But not before they feed you yours.”

Checkmate, Kat. The black queen has no more squares to go.

But wait … Harry can offer her one square. “I can fix this,” he says.

Here’s what she’s going to do. She’ll resign. She’ll kill the police investigation. The Harrigans’ accounts will reopen and they’ll come off the terror list.

Cut to a London corridor of power, where a faceless civil servant informs DS Kavanagh that “continuation of your terrorism financing line will interfere with an active joint intelligence operation between British and American security services. You will stand down at the close of business.”

And so, full circle. Maeve is electrocuting Frankie’s balls with a car battery. Kevin is building bridges with Bella. Conrad is going madder by the day. And Eddie?

The Semtex he blackmailed Paul for was meant for Harry, at Maeve’s behest. In the hands of any other Harrigan, this would be a doddle. But not Eddie. Not the knifey little twerp, a man who could trip over his own foreskin in an empty car park.

And he does. But first, the hints. Something strange has been going on with Jan all episode: the soppy Damien Rice soundtrack, the wistful dream sequences of the good times she shared with Harry. Her Harry. The one she fell for in the first place. She seems to have forgiven him. They’re even taking a family holiday.

But there’s one last stop before they go: Harrigan HQ, where Eddie is lying in wait. While Harry negotiates the terms of the holiday with Conrad, Gina and Jan stroll over to the lake. It’s Eddie’s moment, his chance to shine. He plants the Semtex in their car, hides around the corner, arms the bomb, finger hovering over the detonator … then a phone call.

By Finn McCool’s cobblers, it’s Kelly “Mad Dog” O’Connor, the lunatic son of the Medusa, whom Maeve sent Eddie to kill. “I’m coming for you,” growls the voice. “The Mad Dog is coming.”

Gina, Jan and Harry return to the car. Only Jan gets in. In his quivering, fearful state, Eddie trips and drops the detonator.

That’s Jan up in smoke, along with Harry’s plans for a quiet life. It may spell the end of Jan and all her problems, but it’s only the beginning of Eddie’s. He now has Mad Dog O’Connor to answer to, and Harry.

Then the credits roll, and what a gloriously cruel place to leave it.

The only other loose end is Frankie. We’re left to assume he met a long, painful death at the business end of Maeve’s car battery, but we never see a body. I truly hope he does a Harry and wriggles out of one last tight hole—Maeve’s hole—with one good knee and a long memory.

Season 3 can’t come soon enough. When Harry works out whose finger was on the detonator, Eddie’ll wish a knifey little twerp is all he could be.
