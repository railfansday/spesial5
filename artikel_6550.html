I Tried Ina Garten’s Ranch Dressing, and It Changed My Mind About Ranch

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

I thought I knew exactly how I felt about ranch dressing. Then I made Ina's version.

I have a confession to make. I’ve never been particularly fond of ranch dressing. I realize this puts me at odds with a substantial portion of the American population, but I’ve always preferred a good balsamic vinaigrette or a creamy Caesar. And despite making countless salad dressings over the years, I’ve never once felt compelled to make ranch.

I suspect my aversion began during childhood, watching my friends dunk perfectly good slices of pizza into enormous puddles of cheap ranch. I found the whole business unappealing, an opinion that has apparently skipped a generation seeing as both of my children now do exactly the same thing. There’s always a bottle of ranch in our refrigerator that was purchased specifically for pizza night, and it has absolutely nothing to do with me.

Then I came across an Instagram reel of Ina Garten making her buttermilk ranch dressing for a wedge salad. I’ve trusted Ina with everything from roast chicken to panna cotta, and if anyone could persuade me to reconsider my longstanding position on ranch, I figured it would be her. So, after decades of avoiding it, I finally made a batch of ranch dressing.

Ina’s recipe makes about 3 cups of dressing, which initially struck me as an ambitious quantity for someone with my history of distatte. Fortunately, it comes together in just a few minutes, and the ingredients are far more fresh and interesting than anything I’d previously associated with ranch.

Combine the scallions, basil, lemon juice, Dijon mustard, olive oil, garlic, salt and pepper in a food processor fitted with a steel blade. Puree them for 15 to 20 seconds, until the mixture is smooth.

Add the mayonnaise, Greek yogurt and buttermilk, then blend the dressing again until everything is thoroughly combined. Transfer the dressing to a covered container and refrigerate it for an hour to let the flavors develop.

That’s really all there is to it. No complicated technique or careful emulsifying, and very little cleanup. The hour in the refrigerator is important, though, since it gives the garlic, herbs and other seasonings time to mingle with the creamy ingredients.

Of course, I expected Ina’s ranch to be better than the bottled varieties I’ve tried. What I didn’t expect was for it to taste like an entirely different dressing.

The first thing that struck me was how fresh it was. The basil and scallions give it a wonderful, almost garden-like quality, while the garlic and Dijon add savory sharpness that keeps all that creaminess interesting. There’s plenty of richness from the mayonnaise, but the buttermilk, Greek yogurt and lemon juice provide enough acidity to balance it. It’s tangy, herbaceous and wonderfully flavorful—without the heavy, slightly cloying quality I’ve always associated with ranch.

And the basil! It’s not an herb I would have thought to put in ranch dressing, but it works beautifully. Its flavor comes through clearly without overwhelming the other ingredients, giving the dressing a character all its own.

In her video, Ina serves the dressing over a wedge salad topped with tomatoes and onion. Having already entrusted her with the dressing, I figured I might as well follow her lead with the salad, too. I made my own wedge with Flavor Bombs tomatoes (my favorites), pepitas and crispy pieces of bacon, then spooned a generous amount of ranch over the whole thing. The cold, crunchy lettuce was the perfect backdrop for the dressing, while the sweet tomatoes, salty bacon and nutty pepitas made the whole thing particularly satisfying. I’ve always loved a good wedge salad, but this was easily one of the best I’ve had.

By the time I’d finished testing and photographing the recipe, nearly half the dressing was gone, and I was already making plans for the rest. I have to admit, I’m even curious to see how it tastes with pizza, which is not something I ever expected to say about ranch dressing.
