NASA Invites Media, Public to View Roman Telescope’s First Images

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

NASA will release the first images from its Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope during a live stream from the American Geophysical Union (AGU) Annual Meeting in San Francisco at 3:30 p.m. EST (12:30 p.m. PST) Monday, Dec. 7.

The images will showcase the unique scientific capabilities of Roman, which will soon capture deep, panoramic, never-before-seen views of the universe to explore the mysteries of dark energy, dark matter, and worlds outside our solar system.

The agency also will offer embargoed interviews following the conclusion of the briefing to registered media. A recording of the media briefing will be available on the AGU website after the embargo lifts at 3:30 p.m. on Dec. 7.

NASA invites the public to claim a corner of the universe with one of Roman’s select first images. When Roman reveals its first images, each pixel will represent a unique piece of the cosmos. Once the image is revealed, participants can look up their personal pixel and receive a digital trading card with key information they can share with NASA social accounts.

Get involved by hosting your own event for the reveal of Roman’s first images.

Roman launched Aug. 30 from NASA’s Kennedy Space Center in Florida on a SpaceX Falcon Heavy rocket, beginning a three-month, million-mile trip to its final orbit at the Sun-Earth Lagrange Point 2. During that journey, Roman has undergone a commissioning process where its systems are turned on, adjusted, calibrated, and prepared to conduct science. Roman will use its large field of view and crisp infrared vision to study vast swaths of the sky and probe deeply into cosmic history. In addition to helping scientists explore dark matter, dark energy, and exoplanets, Roman’s surveys will support a broad range of research extending far beyond the mission’s main science goals. The mission also will feature a cutting-edge coronagraph technology demonstration to directly image planets around other stars.

The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope is managed at NASA’s Goddard Space Flight Center in Greenbelt, Maryland, with participation by NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory in Southern California; Caltech/IPAC in Pasadena, California; the Space Telescope Science Institute; and a science team comprised of scientists from various research institutions. The primary industrial partners are BAE Systems, Inc.; L3Harris Technologies; and Teledyne Scientific & Imaging.
