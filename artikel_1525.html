Celtic boss O'Neill seeks Europa League stand-in as Baur set for lengthy spell on the sidelines

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

By JOHN MCGARRY, SCOTTISH CHIEF SPORTS FEATURE WRITER

Published: 17:30 EST, 20 November 2026 | Updated: 17:48 EST, 20 November 2026

Martin O'Neill will weigh up replacing Mika Baur in the Europa League squad after the midfielder was ruled out for up to 10 weeks with an ankle injury.

The German hobbled off late on in Celtic's win over Kilmarnock almost a fortnight ago with a scan subsequently revealing that he sustained ligament damage.

One of the Parkhead side's star men since signing from Paderborn in the summer, Baur's absence is a major blow for O'Neill ahead of a defining period in the season.

While the manager will look to the likes of Oliver Sorensen and Joel van den Berg to step up domestically, he will also consider using a UEFA rule to make a change to his European squad ahead of Thursday's match away to Torreense in Portugal.

The rule allows clubs to make an alteration to the squad they submitted for the group stage if the injury is expected to sideline the player for more than 60 days from when it occurred.

Van den Berg, Jota, Landon Emenalo and Shim Mheuka weren't on the original 25-man list so would be options to be drafted in.

German midfielder Mika Baur could miss up to 10 weeks of action after injuring his ankle

'I would definitely check with the medical staff and Mika, for a start,' said O'Neill.

'And speaking to him yesterday, he's really upbeat about it. Obviously, he will have that boot on for a few weeks.

'I suppose deep down, if it was, let's say, 10 weeks, that's taking you right through to the end of January. I can't see us seeing him in the next six weeks.'

Baur's form following his £4.5million move has been good enough for him to win his first full cap for Germany after being called up by new manager Jurgen Klopp.

'He was doing great for us,' added O'Neill. 'It is a blow. And it seemed so innocuous at the time.

'I thought that he'd been kicked, but it was the other foot he'd landed on. It's just gone over. It's a real shame. He was playing great for us.

'There's things that he can't do for a while.'

O'Neill revealed that Benjamin Nygren is also an injury doubt for Saturday's match with St Mirren.

'He didn't train today, but we'll see how he is in the morning,' he explained. 'He's got a sore heel. He missed out the second game in Sweden's run.

'He's been feeling it for a couple of weeks, so we'll see how he is tomorrow.'

19-year-old summer signing Joel van den Berg could make his first Celtic outing this weekend

Van den Berg hasn't played for the first team since joining from PSV Eindhoven for £3.4m in the summer with an injury impeding his progress.

But the 19-year-old could be in line for his first-team debut against the Buddies.

'I had a little chat to him,' said O'Neill. 'He trained this morning, he didn't train yesterday. We played a bounce game during the week, Tuesday against St Johnstone.

'He played in that game, came off and he was definitely limping for a while. He had a couple of days' treatment.

'In the overall scheme of things, whether it's tomorrow or in the next week or two, I'd hope to see him make his mark.

'As I said to him yesterday, "When you get in, it's really up to you".'

O'Neill anticipates a stern test from a St Mirren side who often have not picked up the positive results their performances have merited under Craig McLeish.

'The overall job he's done has been terrific, really terrific,' said the Celtic manager.

'They're having a wee tough time at this minute, obviously, with results, but his overall contributions, I'd say it's been excellent.

'There are some really fine coaches in Scotland, genuinely, and I don't want to sound patronising or sycophantic, but there are.

'We were up against one two weeks ago against Kilmarnock [Neil McCann], so I've got a lot of time for those boys who are coming through and who, to my mind, are every bit as good of coaches as quite a number down south.'
