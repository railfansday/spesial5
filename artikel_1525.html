​Arteta’s ‘dynasty’ dream collides with Arsenal’s harsh reality

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Mikel Arteta says he wants to create a “dynasty” at Arsenal as he threw down the gauntlet to his players to retain their Premier League title and reach a “different level”.

Arsenal are on the verge of completing a £75m deal for Brazil midfielder Bruno Guimãraes from Newcastle and have not given up hope of landing Real Madrid winger Vinícius Júnior, who has been offered an improved contract by the Spanish giants.

The Premier League champions slumped to a 3-1 defeat to Real Betis in Dublin on Wednesday night, when they paid the price for individual errors and a lethargic, sloppy defensive display.

But that did little to quell Arteta’s ambitions for the new season. “I’m even more excited [about this season], and we’re going to go and attack every possible title because that’s the ambition that we have,” he said.

“We know that we have the ability. We will go from a strength to a strength as well, whether it’s with the players that we have to take them to a different level, and with the market that is still open to recruit as well, so we are very optimistic about it.

“All the work that we’ve done – that I’ve done through the summer – is to understand what we need to do to win again and to create a dynasty in this football club. That’s the aim.”

Despite Arteta’s bullishness, he conceded Arsenal would have to be a lot better than they were against Betis. When asked about the mood in the dressing room, he said: “Very upset, given a hard time. They are fuming, but this is good. That’s what we need.”

Telegraph Sport analyses some of the talking points from a disappointing evening at the Aviva Stadium.

The importance of goalkeeper David Raya and centre-backs William Saliba and Gabriel Magalhães to Arsenal hardly needs overstating. Their title success was built on the foundations provided by that triumvirate at the back.

Raya has yet to report back from holiday after Spain’s World Cup triumph and Saliba is facing an extended spell on the sidelines with a back injury. Gabriel did appear for the second half but, watching this, Arteta is unlikely to have been dissuaded from the belief he needs another top defender to compensate for the loss of Saliba.

It may have been only Arsenal’s second game of pre-season, so a degree of legginess was understandable.

But the ease with which they were cut open and overloaded by the Spanish side, and the volume of individual errors, did not sit comfortably with Arteta with just over a fortnight until they kick off their title defence at home to newly promoted Coventry City.

Only once in 63 matches last season did Arsenal concede three goals in a single game but they were fortunate Betis did not score more. “In the first half there are two different things: to play well and to compete,” said Arteta, who saw his side trail 3-1 at the interval.

“We did the first one quite well in a lot of moments but the compete part – especially in and around our box – was not to the standards to win a football match against a good side. It’s better to give them [the goals away] in a friendly match than an official match. It’s always good when you lose a match in pre-season because it creates a bit more fire in the belly.”

Kepa Arrizabalaga will not want to watch Betis’s first goal back in a hurry. The Arsenal goalkeeper may have got a slight nudge as he came for the ball at a corner but he was all over the place and never looked even in the vaguest control of the situation. Rodrigo Riquelme scored at the far post after the ball sailed over the keeper. Arteta called it a “really sloppy goal”. With former Leeds goalkeeper Illan Meslier having arrived on a free transfer as back-up to Raya, Kepa faces an uncertain future at Arsenal. Moments like this will not help his cause.

He did not feature much last season but is probably best remembered for his torrid showing in the Carabao Cup final defeat by Manchester City in March. He looked as unconvincing here as he did then, as the former Chelsea goalkeeper also sliced a clearance in the build up to Betis’s third goal. Before then, Kai Havertz had turned over possession cheaply from which Nelson Deossa set up former West Ham midfielder Pablo Fornals to score.

Arsenal need more creativity and firepower in attack, hence the interest in Vinícius and Atlético Madrid’s Julián Alvarez. Viktor Gyokeres again looked rather limited here and the likes of Bukayo Saka and Eberechi Eze are also still to report back. Yet one of the few, brighter aspects of Arsenal’s performances was the sight of the regal looking Riccardo Calafiori marauding forward from a very nominal left-back role.

If Arsenal are going to play more adventurously this season and try to ratchet up things in the entertainment stakes then Calafiori could have a big role to play, as long as Arteta can keep him fit. He can be a potent weapon galloping forward and there were clear glimpses of that here. Harnessing that talent while not compromising too much defensive security may be one of the manager’s challenges.

Louie Copley had only been on the pitch for nine minutes – a half-time substitute for Ben White – when the 19-year-old midfielder went down largely unchallenged with what looked like a troubling knee injury. Arteta admitted afterwards that the way the teenager fell to the ground was a concern and he looked in visible pain and distress as he was treated by the medical staff before being helped off the pitch.

The fear will be of some kind of ligament damage and Arteta confirmed Copley would be scanned on Thursday. “Quite worried to be fair for the kid because the mechanism doesn’t look very positive and the symptoms that he already has,” the manager said. “So we are going to have to wait and see for tomorrow’s scan. Touch wood that is not a bad one.”

Premier League champions Arsenal continue their preparations for the new season as they take on Spanish side Real Betis at the Aviva Stadium in Dublin, with this being the first-ever meeting between the two sides. This is the first time Arsenal have played a pre-season game in Dublin since August 2018, when they drew 1-1 with London rivals Chelsea, with Arsenal winning on penalties.

Arsenal beat Girona 4-1 in their opening game of pre-season, with new signing Christos Tzolis on the scoresheet in his first appearance for the club. After today, Arsenal have three more games, including the Community Shield, before they kick off the defence of their Premier League crown at home against newly-promoted Coventry on opening night of the new season. They are closing in on a deal for Newcastle captain Bruno Guimaraes, who is set to become one of Arsenal’s most expensive signings. Mikel Arteta’s side have been dealt a blow ahead of the season with the news that France centre-back William Saliba will miss an extended period due to a back injury. The likes of Declan Rice, Bukayo Saka and Noni Madueke are still yet to return after their World Cup exploits.

Real Betis, under the management of former Manchester City boss Manuel Pellegrini, finished fifth in La Liga last season and have therefore qualified for this coming season’s Champions League after a 20-year absence from the competition. Pellegrini has led Betis into European competitions for five consecutive seasons, which includes losing the 2025 Europa Conference League final to Chelsea. They have been busy in pre-season action already having played five games up to this point. They have won four of the five, including a 4-0 victory over Lyon, with just one defeat coming against Granada.

They have a number of former Premier League players in their ranks, including Antony, Giovani Lo Celso, Pablo Fornals and former Arsenal man Hector Bellerin. Real Betis have already played a number of friendlies as they build up to the start of their season, which begins on August 21 when they take on Real Sociedad.
