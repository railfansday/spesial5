Wispr Flow improves dictation accuracy with new Canto-2 speech-to-text model

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Popular AI dictation app Wispr Flow is rolling out a new speech-to-text model for Mac, iPhone, and other platforms, promising fewer transcription errors and better recognition of names and technical terms. Here are the details.

Wispr Flow this week launched Canto-2, its newest speech-to-text model, which the company says reduces transcription errors by 36% across 14 languages and by more than 15% in English compared to its predecessor, Canto-1.

In practice, that means the average error rate dropped from 9.9% to 6.3% across the 14 languages, and from 3.2% to 2.7% in English, according to Wispr’s internal benchmarks.

According to the company, teaching a model to recognize specific names and terms comes with a trade-off: the more it relies on that user-specific context, the more likely it is to insert those words even when they weren’t actually spoken.

Wispr notes that Canto-2 strikes a better balance between context and accuracy, improving its ability to recognize user-specific terms without substantially increasing the frequency of incorrect substitutions:

Canto-2 recovers hard names and terms accurately nearly 7 of 10 times, without more false insertions. It makes a third fewer mistakes across 14 languages, and has the lowest Hindi error rate of any multilingual model on Hugging Face’s open benchmark. It is 10 times the scale of Canto-1, and our biggest step yet toward dictation that’s ready to send without a single edit.

According to the company, the percentage of difficult names and terms correctly recognized went from 41% to 69% between Canto-1 and Canto-2, “without inserting them more often.” It added that this improvement holds up even as the user’s custom dictionary grows, with the new model correctly recognizing 92% of easier terms even with 1,000 saved entries.

Wispr also noted that it trained Canto-2 to handle real-world dictation conditions, including background noise, laptop and phone microphones, and sentences that restart halfway through, rather than relying solely on clean recordings of scripted speech.

When tested using Hugging Face’s public multilingual benchmark, Canto-2 achieved results that would have placed it third overall as of November 9. However, Wispr conducted the evaluation itself, and the model has not yet been officially added to the leaderboard as of today, November 25.

In its own internal benchmarks, the company says Canto-2 achieved the lowest average error rate among the competing models it tested across 14 languages.

Canto-2 is now available to Wispr Flow users on Mac, Windows, iOS, and Android in the Americas, with the rollout to other regions underway.
