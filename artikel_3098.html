What we learned as Warriors' 3-point shooting stays silent in loss to Grizzlies

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

What we learned as Warriors' 3-point shooting stays silent in loss to Grizzlies originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

A 28-point loss to the Memphis Grizzlies on Thursday night salted the Warriors' wounds and capped a winless five-game road trip.

The Warriors lost Steph Curry in the first few minutes of the trip and never found an answer after. The final straw was a 126-98 loss at FedExForum. The Warriors failed to reach 100 points in the final four games of the road trip and lost by at least 20 points each time.

Charles Bassey was the Warriors' surprise leading scorer, finishing with a 16-point, 13-rebound double-double.  Thursday marked the first game all season where the Warriors didn't have a single 20-point scorer.

They continued to be doomed by poor defense and terrible starts.

Here are three takeaways from the Warriors' fifth straight loss.

In summer league, Yaxel Lendeborg exacted his revenge on Cam Boozer by avenging his one loss in Las Vegas with a win against the Grizzlies in the championship game. Boozer got the best of Lendeborg in their first regular season game against each other, and it was the same result the second time.

Lendeborg was a game-low minus-31 with 11 points on 3-of-11 shooting, but he did have eight rebounds, seven assists and two blocked shots. Boozer was much better as a plus-16 with 16 points on a perfect 6-of-6 shooting and made all four of his free throws. The much-younger Boozer also added six rebounds and five assists.

The game looked easy to Boozer, and a struggle for Lendeborg – mentally and physically.

After using size to his advantage in Dallas, Lendeborg floated far too often around the 3-point line. It's his teammates' job to find him on mismatches. It's up to Lendeborg first to create them, though.

As the Warriors suffered yet another blowout loss, Will Richard again was a silver lining.

Richard the night before in Dallas scored 20 points in 27 minutes. He was a plus-8 in a 22-point loss, going 9 of 13 from the field with four steals. That same energy followed Richard to Memphis, where he scored 15 points off the bench on 5-of-8 shooting.

Without Curry's gravity and with Lendeborg struggling, the Warriors' best offense has been a heavy dose of cutting. Richard is among the Warriors' best cutters, and it's how he immediately made an impact. His teammates should take note.

In a back-to-back where not much went right for the Warriors, Richard scored 35 points off the bench on 14-of-21 shooting (66.7 percent).

Al Horford met with media members during Warriors shootaround in San Antonio and said the Warriors' best bet of survival without Curry is defense. The Warriors have to lean on defense and build off strong starts if they want to compete. They couldn't accomplish either in Memphis, or the rest of the road trip.

Awful starts continued with the Warriors down 31-24 after the first quarter, which is an improvement as of late. But their process was not. The Warriors in the first quarter made eight shots and turned the ball over eight times for 13 points by the Grizzlies.

Golden State surrendered 22 turnovers, which led to 35 points on the other side. The Warriors' entire starting five had multiple turnovers, including three from Lendeborg and Gui Santos. Turnovers are just one part of how the Warriors were outscored by 80 points in the first quarter (174-94) on an 0-5 road trip.

The offense taking a tumble in Curry's absence is understandable. The Warriors' defense being this bad isn't. The Grizzlies shot 52.2 percent from the field, 42.9 percent from three and made all 17 of their free throws while barely breaking a sweat.
