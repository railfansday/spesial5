Rosie Huntington-Whiteley on the Quiet Power of Saint Laurent

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

The latest Women’s Ready-to-Wear collection and the house’s new Simone bag speak to a style built on restraint, tension, and silent confidence.

Rosie Huntington-Whiteley has one rule when she gets dressed: “I never want to feel as though the clothes are wearing me.” It has served her well for more than two decades, first as one of fashion’s most recognizable faces, then as muse, and now now as an entrepreneur in her own right. It also embodies why Saint Laurent has been a constant.

“I have always felt like a Saint Laurent woman,” she says. Part of the pull is the house’s history, a way of dressing that Yves Saint Laurent defined in the 1960s that still feels charged today. Part of it is simpler: she has always wanted clothes that let her feel like herself, and Saint Laurent does that artfully.

For Fall 26, the collection harkens the house’s founding codes with tailoring that feels looser and more sensual than ever. It was the first thing Huntington-Whiteley noticed. “There’s something incredibly powerful about that Saint Laurent silhouette, particularly the way it feels so fluid and sensual this season,” she reflects. “I’ve always loved a beautifully cut suit worn against bare skin.”

The season’s other headline is Simone, a new bag drawn from Monsieur Saint Laurent’s personal letters. The envelope pouches he favored in the 1970s and 1980s were made for evening. This iteration of the shape is made for every day, with a softer, more structured build, two compartments, a zipped pocket inside, and a magnetic snap that keeps the line clean. On the front sits the new double Cassandre, a raised, dimensional take on the house monogram.

It suits the way Huntington-Whiteley thinks about her own closet. Her most treasured pieces are the Saint Laurent leather jackets she has collected over the years. “There’s something about a Saint Laurent leather jacket that only gets better with time,” she says. “Each one begins to hold memories and becomes part of your identity.” Simone is made in that same spirit. Cut in supple leather and meant to be carried every day rather than saved for a night out, it is a bag designed to be lived in, and to collect a few memories of its own.

Huntington-Whiteley’s own style comes is built on constants. “I’m drawn to a sense of restraint, clean lines, strong silhouettes, beautiful materials and pieces that have a timeless quality to them,” she offers. “There’s often a tension between the masculine and feminine in the way I dress.”

That push and pull is what keeps her coming back to Saint Laurent. “Masculine and feminine, restraint and sensuality, strength and vulnerability,” she says. “Those contradictions have always been at the heart of Saint Laurent.” More than six decades after Yves Saint Laurent founded his house in Paris, it is still finding new ways to make those codes feel modern.

Ask her to sum up the Saint Laurent woman and she doesn’t hesitate. “She’s sensual without being overtly feminine, powerful without losing her sexuality, and there’s always an element of mystery,” she says. “It’s an aesthetic that doesn’t ask for attention, but inevitably commands it.”
