Anthropic’s IPO is a bellwether—and a warning

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Good morning. Anthropic just pulled off a record-breaking IPO, with the five-year-old company debuting at a value of $2 trillion. Is the creator of the Claude chatbot worth it? Will customers get enough value from its products to pay prices that justify that valuation? Is confidence in AI moving faster than companies’ ability to capitalize on it? After all, there are two ways to vote on AI: Buy the stock or buy the product. The first tells us how much capital investors are willing to commit. The second tells us whether customers get enough value to keep paying.

There are plenty of data points to discuss. Start with the org chart, which is the opposite of founder mode: CEO Dario Amodei has one direct report (Jensen Huang had 60 at Nvidia), with most of Anthropic’s senior team reporting to his sister, President Daniela Amodei. The average Anthropic employee is now worth more than $40 million. (Daniela, lunch is on you.) Anthropic’s novella-length list of risk factors about its products still reads like a dystopian sci-fi thriller. The company’s ownership structure, from the role of the Long-Term Benefit Trust to the Class F shares, can warm your heart and hurt your head. Who will decide when to speed up or slow down?

For now, let’s look at this debut as both a bellwether of current AI sentiment and a harbinger of what’s to come. As IPOs go, this one was kind of choppy. The stock was priced at $83 a share on Wednesday and began trading at $111.50 a share on Nasdaq before noon under the symbol ANT. Then it surrendered a lot of those gains during intraday trading before closing at $97.17.

What’s interesting to me is who wanted in. About 1,200 institutional investors submitted orders at the $83 IPO price, more than half of whom didn’t get shares. Retail demand was the largest for any IPO on record, with individuals getting about 15% of the offering. Anyone who got a piece of that started yesterday with a 34% gain. Those who paid a premium at the open are betting it’s still worth more. Confidence in the technology and confidence in the stock price are two different things.

As a vote on the prospects for AI, Anthropic’s IPO was a success. Despite the pushback on data centers, the scandal around Nvidia, the outages, the job jitters, and the doomsday critics, there’s something real here. Investors think the fuss around frontier labs is justified because AI will transform how we work, live, play, and learn. I stole that line from Cisco, which used it as a slogan to describe the internet’s impact during the dot-com boom, when Cisco was briefly the most valuable company on earth. Cisco’s still here, with annual revenue that’s 3x what it was in fiscal 2000 and a total market value that’s a third less than at its March 2000 peak. The technology can be right and the stock can still be wrong. Businesses and markets move at different speeds.
