BYD apologized for installing wheel rims backward on its Great Tang SUVs

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The carmaker blamed a supplier error, said handling was not affected and restarted the warranty for affected owners

BYD apologized on Wednesday after a factory mix-up swapped the wheel rims between the front and back axles on certain Great Tang electric SUVs, according to Bloomberg.

BYD said in a Weibo statement that its investigation pinned the fault on a supplier based in Hubei, according to Bloomberg. The supplier, Hubei Boyu Industrial, made a code entry error that led to the wrong installation, according to CnEVPost.

BYD said it has corrected the faulty system, and the supplier has begun carrying out extra visual checks along its assembly line, according to Bloomberg. BYD's own testing found no effect on handling and no safety risk, the company said. It did not say how many vehicles were affected.

Owners with affected vehicles can have the rims replaced, and warranty coverage on their cars starts over as of Wednesday, according to Bloomberg. BYD will ask owners to bring their cars to nearby service centers, where the inspection and rim swap cost nothing, and it will supply replacement cars on loan or reimburse them for getting around, according to CnEVPost.

The mistake surfaced when a number of owners posted complaints on the internet, according to Bloomberg. They said the rear rims, which are the wider pair, were sitting on the front axle, while the narrower front pair had ended up on the rear axle. Some owners said they feared excessive tire wear and safety risks while driving.

The factory specification lists 8J×20 rims wrapped in 255/50R20 tires for the front axle and 9J×20 rims wrapped in 275/45R20 tires for the rear axle, according to owners cited by CnEVPost.

The Great Tang is the flagship of BYD’s Dynasty lineup and is priced from 239,900 yuan (about $35,800), according to Bloomberg. The model plays a key role in BYD’s move upmarket, and the company said it sold about 14,000 units in October.

No safety incidents were reported, according to Bloomberg. The error strikes a nerve for China's auto industry, where regulators are paying increasing attention to quality and durability.
