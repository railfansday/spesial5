Jensen Huang is the AI boom’s key man. Now, that’s a problem

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

It’s just about the most relentless and pulpy headline imaginable: Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang admitted to an affair, with a woman who’s an alleged Chinese spy no less.

The news bombshelled on Sunday, and the market reacted viscerally on Monday: Nvidia closed down 4.3%, with about $230 billion in shareholder value wiped out, as other tech companies like Google and Meta saw share price upticks (welcome after Thursday’s Nasdaq nightmare). And yes, the luridness certainly drew attention, as did the national security concerns now subject to investigation.

The underlying question of it all is simple: Where do Huang and Nvidia go from here? Huang is AI’s ultimate key man. But more importantly: Nvidia—indelibly associated with Huang—is AI’s arms dealer, controlling north of 80% of the global market for AI and data center GPUs.

So, Huang’s affair isn’t just a public markets story, it’s a private markets story, with many tendrils. For one, functionally every AI startup and LLM giant is dependent on Nvidia GPU clusters to train and run models. This, naturally, includes the biggest names, including OpenAI, Elon Musk’s xAI, and Anthropic (on the eve of its IPO, expected to be historically large).

Nvidia’s also widely, and I believe correctly, described as AI’s central bank. The company’s financial footprint is stamped across the AI-pilled private markets. Nvidia’s been a key investor in startups, including Ilya Sutskever’s Safe Superintelligence and robotics unicorn Figure AI, and in key neoclouds like Nebius—not to mention an acquirer, as was the case with Nvidia’s September $12.93 billion announced acquisition of open-source startup Hugging Face, which had its own host of venture backers, including Lux Capital and Salesforce. (Nvidia has also backed OpenAI and Anthropic, and at least 400 other startups, a look at PitchBook says.)

It goes even further: Nvidia’s existing partnerships across the private markets include deals with  Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, and KKR to create $500 billion in financing opportunities for (you guessed it) compute and data centers.

It’s the ultimate “key man risk” story. I’ve never typed this sentence before, but it’s true: There are trillions at stake, and it all ties back to Huang, who decisively helped create the AI boom. And he’s now the reason the AI status quo is, at best, imperiled.

But Huang is also impossibly entrenched and vital, and there’s just too much financial infrastructure at stake. This story is still developing, as they say, and new revelations may yet come to light. But my guess: Whether it’s right or wrong, Huang survives this furor and remains CEO of Nvidia.
