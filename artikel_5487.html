Hackers breached the U.S. Air Force's flagship cloud computing platform

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The intrusion at Cloud One, found during a system upgrade, has disrupted the platform and prompted a Cyber Command damage assessment

Hackers breached the U.S. Air Force's Cloud One computing platform, an intrusion that could expose sensitive information on tactics, personnel and logistics, according to Reuters.

Reuters, citing unnamed sources, reports that the intrusion turned up roughly within the last month, while the system was being upgraded, and that the discovery has disrupted the platform. Reuters could not establish who the hackers are, how far the intrusion reached or whether any data was stolen.

The Air Force declined to discuss the matter. It "does not comment on the security posture, vulnerabilities or operational status of specific networks and systems," the service said in a statement to Reuters. It added that it takes steps to safeguard the information it holds, the systems it runs and its ability to carry out missions.

Investigators have turned to Cyber Command, the cyber arm of the U.S. military, to gauge the extent of the harm, according to Reuters. Cyber Command did not respond to Reuters' requests for comment.

The Air Force started Cloud One in 2017 as a single source for its IT needs, according to Reuters. The platform combines resources from Microsoft $MSFT, Amazon $AMZN, Oracle $ORCL and Alphabet $GOOGL. It runs applications that manage personnel data, pay, logistics and maintenance, including the Air Force's Cloud-Based Command and Control system and the Theater Integrated Combat Munitions System, Reuters reports. The first brings together radar from air defense systems and additional data streams covering North America. The second keeps tabs on bombs and missiles at upwards of 200 Air Force sites globally.

The breach has led to several disruptions, according to Reuters. Ongoing efforts to shift back-office software over to the platform have been put on hold. Updates to programs already hosted on the platform have been blocked, so Air Force personnel cannot apply fixes at a moment when fresh software flaws are coming to light throughout the tech sector. Some users also saw their admin-level access withdrawn.

One source told Reuters the intruders got to the platform's control plane by taking advantage of a flaw in the software. That person likened the control plane to a universal remote that could, in theory, give intruders a view of whether military aircraft are operational or where spare parts are headed. It remains unknown if the hackers actually took that step, Reuters reports. Reuters could not identify the vulnerability.

SAIC served as the lead contractor on Cloud One until 2025, when Leidos assumed the role, according to Reuters. SAIC did not respond to a request for comment, and Leidos referred questions to the Air Force.

Reuters also reports that the U.S. government is dealing with no fewer than six other cyber incidents that were disclosed recently. They include two at the FBI, a previous hack of Pentagon data, personnel information from the Department of Homeland Security that was leaked, at least two spy agencies hit by a single breach, and a hack at the Treasury Department.
