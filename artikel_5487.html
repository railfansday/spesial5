Mortgage rates ease as buyers gain leverage with concessions, ARMs

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Mortgage rates slipped to 7.35% this week but remain high enough to keep buyers extremely payment-sensitive. Economists say hotter inflation data could delay meaningful rate relief, limiting demand to needs-based movers. In many markets, leverage is shifting toward buyers as inventory builds, prompting sellers and builders to offer larger concessions to fund temporary and permanent rate buydowns and adjustable-rate mortgages.  AI Summary

Mortgage rates eased slightly this week but stayed above 7.3%, keeping affordability tight and forcing loan officers to lean on seller concessions and adjustable-rate mortgages (ARMs) to close deals.

At HousingWire’s Mortgage Rates Center on Tuesday, 30-year fixed-rate mortgages averaged 7.35%, down from 7.38% one week earlier. Rates for 30-year jumbo loans averaged 7.56%, a 6-basis-point decline, while 30-year Federal Housing Administration (FHA) loans were up 6 bps to 7.22%.

“Since early October, rates have improved about 100 basis points, which can equate to an eighth of a point to 0.375, so that’s helping affordability,” said Shannon Hoff, a California-based mortgage broker at Hoff Home Loans.

Inflation remains a key obstacle. Data released Tuesday showed the Consumer Price Index rose 0.4% on a monthly basis in October, hotter than expected, keeping the door open for the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates at its December meeting.

“Because that’s creating uncertainty, I am seeing buyers are still extremely payment-sensitive,” Hoff said. “However, during the COVID-19 pandemic, people were having to pay $100,000 over for the listing price. If your payment is going to change $100 or $200 from where you want the rate to be, in the big picture, that’s not so worrisome.”

First American senior economist Sam Williamson said hotter inflation is likely to prolong the wait for real mortgage rate relief.

“Stronger job growth may give some households the income to move, but elevated borrowing costs raise the hurdle for everyone,” Williamson said in a statement. “Until mortgage rates ease, life moves will keep the housing market moving, while discretionary buyers wait on the sidelines.”

In this context, Optimal Blue‘s October 2026 Market Advantage mortgage data report showed that rate-lock volume fell 19% month over month and 38% year over year. But ARMs represented 17% of total lock volume, the highest share in the report going back to 2019.“ARM share reached another high, FHA gained share and first-time buyers took a larger share across the major agency programs,” Brennan O’Connell, director of data solutions at Optimal Blue, said in a statement. “For lenders, that means the opportunity is increasingly in helping borrowers find the product and payment structure that works for their circumstances.”

As fewer borrowers are able or willing to buy at today’s rates, inventory is starting to build in parts of the country. That’s giving buyers more leverage, while pushing sellers and builders to participate more heavily in financing costs.

That’s the opposite of a few years ago, when rock-bottom rates, intense competition and limited supply forced buyers to bid far above list price and waive contingencies.

“I’m licensed in nine states, I get to see various markets, and a lot of them are leaning toward the buyer,” said Gay Veale, a 30-year U.S. Air Force veteran and owner of Guidon Mortgage Group. “Because of that, there’s often seller concessions involved, and I am seeing people use those seller concessions for as many different ways to do rate buydowns or get their mortgage to be cheaper in the beginning, at least, if possible.”

Veale cited one deal in which a borrower received $58,000 in seller concessions — an unusually high figure — and locked a 6% rate. With a 3-2-1 temporary buydown, a permanent buydown and an adjustable-rate mortgage, the borrower’s effective initial rate fell to 2.75%.

“Around 75% of my clients are getting seller concessions. They’re not all that much, but at least $10,000 is an average for me,” Veale said.

Veale said most buyers using ARMs today are opting for products that provide about five years of lower rates before they reset. That roughly matches borrower expectations that rates could ease somewhat over the medium term.

She added that most of her current clients are “need-to-move” borrowers rather than investors, such as people relocating for a job, dealing with a death in the family, welcoming a new child or trying to get closer to relatives. Investment purchases are far less common than during the pandemic period, she said.

Veale said her business volume this year looks very similar to last year, an outcome she called “very atypical of the market” and not something she expects to see again under normal conditions.

In Northern California, Hoff said weaker demand is allowing some buyers to negotiate steep price cuts and substantial credits.

She pointed to a client scheduled to sign on Wednesday for a new home after securing $50,000 off the purchase price and $10,000 in seller credits. That $60,000 swing would have been unattainable in a hotter market, she said.

“That $60,000 is much more than paying a half of a point or a point higher in rate,” she said. “Not only is it the uncertainty affecting the buyers, it’s also affecting the sellers.”

Down payment dynamics have shifted as well. During the pandemic, borrowers using down payment assistance or putting 3% down struggled to compete with cash offers and large down payments. Today, Hoff said, these buyers are reappearing.

She is seeing “more first-time homebuyers jump in the market with challenged credit, down payment assistance, outside-the-box loans, bank statements. My loans are definitely a lot more challenging now.”
