Quitting Smoking or Vaping Can Save Thousands a Year – and the Help Is Free

Quit smoking or vaping and you stop spending on packs or pods, often thousands of dollars a year, and later pay less for life and health insurance. Most health plans must cover quit-smoking counseling and medication at no cost, so ask your doctor to prescribe nicotine patches, and call 1-800-QUIT-NOW for free coaching.

Work out your own yearly cost. A pack a day costs your local pack price times 365, and vaping costs a week’s pods or e-liquid times 52.

Take a pack-a-day smoker whose plan covers the patches they quit with. At $9 a pack, they keep about $3,300 in the first year, before any insurance savings. Most life insurers charge nicotine users, vapers included, higher premiums than nonsmokers pay for equal coverage. Insurers selling plans on HealthCare.gov and state marketplaces can add up to 50% to a tobacco user’s premium.

Smoke also counts against a car or a house when you sell it. Its odor can be hard and expensive to clean out of a car. A 2008 San Diego State University study found nonsmokers asked 7% to 9% more for their used cars than smokers did for comparable ones. In a Drexel University survey, 96% of Philadelphia-area realtors said a house where people smoke is harder to sell.

Under federal guidance, a job-based or marketplace plan complies by covering at least two quit attempts a year at no cost. Each attempt includes four counseling sessions and 90 days of FDA-approved medication, from nicotine patches and gum to prescription pills.

That coverage includes over-the-counter patches and gum only with a prescription, so ask your doctor for one instead of buying them off the shelf. Then call 1-800-QUIT-NOW, which connects you to your state’s free quitline. Its coaches help vapers too, and some state quitlines give callers free medication.

The insurance savings take longer. For marketplace plans, tobacco use means regular use in the past six months, so once you’ve gone six months without tobacco, you can answer no to the tobacco question on your next application and drop the surcharge. Most life insurers want 12 months without nicotine before they’ll rate you a nonsmoker, and patches and gum count as nicotine.

So write down the day you stop all nicotine, and a year later, ask your life insurer to reprice your policy at nonsmoker rates.
