Why I’ll Never Fly Ryanair, Even If It’s The Only Option

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Love them or hate them, low-cost airlines dominate the skies in some parts of the world. Nowhere is that more true than in Europe, home to Ryanair, the largest low-cost airline in the world (per 2025 passenger numbers).

Back in my late teens and early 20s, I was all for booking the cheapest ticket to a place I’d never heard of, just because I could.

That said, I still do still love to find a bargain, and can spend hours researching the best value option even when booking the simplest of itineraries. The buck stops, however, at Ryanair. My last flight was in 2023, and despite nothing glaringly awful happening, I vowed it would be the last.

Part of the low-cost airline business model is choosing airports far from the cities they serve, since landing fees are often cheaper. One of the best examples of this is Frankfurt Hahn (HHN), located about 80 miles from Frankfurt am Main — the city the airport’s named after.

After a quick check on the Uber app, a one-way Uber ride would likely cost around $180. A more wallet-friendly alternative would be this €24 bus. The journey time would likely be longer than the flight itself…

Even Ryanair’s largest base at London Stansted (STN) is around 40 miles from London, and requires a relatively expensive train or lengthy bus journey to the city.

This is the part I hate the most about Ryanair. They are so stingy that they built their planes with built-in steps at the front boarding door so they don’t have to pay for air bridges. Whether it’s a sizzling 110+ degrees in the middle of the day in Seville, or deep in the minuses at Rovaniemi in Finland (the gateway to Lapland and Ryanair’s northernmost airport), you’ll have to brace the elements while boarding and deplaning.

Getting a deal on a flight departing at daft o’clock in the morning isn’t limited to just Ryanair or low-cost airlines. Who would ever want to set an alarm before 6 a.m.? However, Ryanair can charge such a premium to fly at a respectable time of day, that its fares end up almost the same as flag carriers.

The screenshot below is a great example. British Airways’ one-way fares of 88 pounds from Heathrow (LHR) are over twice as much as two of Ryanair’s options from Stansted to Berlin.

However, Ryanair charges £74 for its midday departure (without a cabin bag). Compare that to BA’s 1:20 p.m. departure for £88 including a cabin bag; it’s a wonder why anyone would even consider Ryanair’s option if they needed to fly in the middle of the day.

Ultimately, airline preference is very subjective, and I know there are some die-hard Ryanair fans who won’t hear a bad word said about the airline. In its defense, if you’re heading somewhere off the beaten track, you can usually rely on Ryanair to get you as close as possible.

Personally, I’d rather enjoy status benefits (and earn miles and status-earning points) by flying a legacy carrier like Air France-KLM, British Airways, or Lufthansa to the closest airport, then figure out ground transportation the rest of the way.

Well, at least Ryanair is honest about its business model. You know exactly what you get when you book a ticket with them. You know that you will most certainly fly to a remote airport not even close to your final destination. On the other hand, booking a flight on Condor or Eurowings doesn't guarantee that you can board the plane directly from the terminal (that happens to me pretty often in HAM). And if you are so desperate to sit next to your travel companion, you better pay for seat selection. But again, they are honest about it; in contrast to some "legacy carrier" in Europe.

I recently needed to get from Tirana in Albania to Naples in Italy. My choices were the legacy airlines who were charging upwards of €200 and had 5+ hour connecting itineraries that had ridiculously early departures or really late arrivals. Or Ryanair with a €19 fare and €15 carry on fee plus a departure around 9.00am. Tirana and Naples have no airbridges so all airlines board outside. Also, Ryanair operates out of the same airports as the legacy carriers on this route. Only a pompous fool would not pick Ryanair in this situation. Also consider that while they sometimes operate out of more remote airports, British Airways only operate international flights out of London. Ryanair operate out of 22 UK airports which I’d say makes them more convenient for the vast majority of UK passengers. Also, Heathrow has a choice of taking the Piccadilly line which is cheap but takes longer than the train to Stansted, or the Lizzie line or Heathrow express which costs about the same as Stansted express.

I once took Ryanair and it was fine. I flew out of Milan Bergamo airport after a vacation in northern Italy. The airport was accessible via public transit, we flew into the main airport for the destination I was going to, and it was less than half the price of a mainline carrier on that route.

I think the only circumstance under which I have (and would) fly them is in a market where they're the only nonstop operator. For example, a few years back I needed to go from Bari - Palermo, and as much as I tried to rationalize a routing through Rome in my head, sucking it up on Ryanair for the short flight and saving 3 or 4 hours in total travel time were worth it.
