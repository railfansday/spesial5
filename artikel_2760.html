Marnus Labuschagne dropped for NZ Test series: George Bailey confirms AUS star's disastrous run reached breaking point in SA

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Marnus Labuschagne dropped from Australia's NZ Test squad after a poor South Africa series.

George Bailey confirmed the decision on November 9, following Labuschagne's return of just 131 runs at 21.83.

The batter will return to Queensland's Sheffield Shield side to rebuild his form.

A Test comeback remains possible ahead of Australia's 2027 tours of India and England.

Australia have finally taken a decisive stand when it comes to the Test future of Marnus Labuschagne, as the former No. 1 Test batter will not play the upcoming four-Test series against New Zealand starting December 9.

As the Chairman of selectors George Bailey said on Monday, Labuschagne will not be taken into account for playing the home series against Black Caps after the batter's disappointing series in South Africa where he scored only 131 runs from six innings with an average of 21.83.

This is another blow for Labuschagne, whose form has been deteriorating for quite some time now, costing him a spot in the team yet again within the span of one year.

With Labuschagne not part of all four Tests against New Zealand, Chairman of selectors George Bailey explained that this will also bring clarity for the batter after weeks of speculation.

"At this stage, we won't be considering Marn for selection for certainly that first Test but for the New Zealand series," Bailey told reporters.

He said this step would help the batter to concentrate on his game in the upcoming Sheffield Shield games.

"There's been a lot of speculation around his place in the team and so I think this gives him a little bit of space and clears up a bit of conjecture," Bailey said.

Bailey also admitted the batter's performance has been below the mark lately.

"We all know how good and how effective Marnus can be at his best, but that performance hasn't been there," he added.

"He knows that the fundamental nature of being a batter in the top six is to score runs and that just hasn't happened."

Labuschagne's recent failure comes on the heels of a disappointing tour of South Africa where he aggregated 131 runs in six innings at an average of 21.83. His scores included 37, 37, 14, 39, 0, and 4 while his failures in the final Test match contributed to Australia's 2-1 series loss.

The bad form has been an extension of what occurred in his recent Bangladesh series where he hasn't got any half-century in his last 16 Test innings. In his last 10 innings, he has accumulated 204 runs at an average of 20.40.

He had made his last Test century against England in July 2023. It is now 49 innings since his last Test hundred. Once regarded as the number one Test batter in the world, his batting average now stands at 42.64 in 68 Test matches.

Labuschagne has not crossed 39 runs in any of his last 10 Test innings. In four of them, he did not even score 10 runs and in his best innings of 39 runs in Gqeberha also, he failed to make an impact.

Bailey hasn't completely closed the door on Labuschagne making a comeback, saying he can still find his best form and be back in the Australian team's plans.

"It's still possible, absolutely. And I think we all know how good and how effective Marnus can be at his best," Bailey noted.

"But I don't think we've ever ruled anyone out permanently ... and that's no different in this case."

Labuschagne will get a chance to return to the Queensland Sheffield Shield team, the next round starting from November 11.

Good performances in the domestic format may help him get back into the team before Australia's hectic 2027 schedule, which includes a five-match Test series in India and an away Ashes series in England.

Labuschagne earned a lot of recognition in previous games, but having a good reputation is not enough to secure a position in Australia's Test team.

The warning bells rang very loudly against Bangladesh, and yet they gave him another chance to prove himself in the South Africa tour. His poor record and his inability to make runs after scoring starts did not give the selectors any reason to keep trying.

Hence, selecting Bailey makes sense, as they need solid performances from their top batters rather than relying on Labuschagne's past performances.
