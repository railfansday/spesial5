Devin Booker’s 30 points lift Suns past Hornets, 101-96

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

The Phoenix Suns led 48-31 halfway through the second quarter, and it looked like they were on their way to another stress-free win against a depleted Charlotte Hornets team. Without the franchise cornerstone players Kon Knueppel and Brandon Miller, and with a superb stretch from the Suns bench, the Hornets looked dead in the water, but ultimately fought back and made this game a slog for the final two and a half quarters for the Suns, who won 101-96.

Two things were apparent in this game: the Phoenix Suns could not rebound, and the Charlotte Hornets could not stop turning the basketball over. The Hornets outrebounded the Suns 64-to-43 and outscored the Suns 16-7 in second-chance points. The Suns forced 27 Hornets turnovers (each Hornets starter had at least four turnovers) while the Suns committed eight turnovers. So the rebounding numbers are skewed in favor of the Hornets because they had 13 fewer shots on goal than the Suns did. However, 22 Suns defensive rebounds compared to 15 Hornets offensive rebounds is not getting the job done at all for a Suns team that can be much better than they have been this season at rebounding.

Because the Suns could not rebound the ball and play as fast as they needed to get the offense going, the game became a half-court slog in the second half. The Suns shot 13-for-42 from the 3-point line, and a lot of the looks that went in on Tuesday did not fall for the Suns against the Hornets. Overall, the Suns were 41% from the field, and only one player had a field goal percentage higher than 50%. Devin Armani Booker scored 30 points on 12-of-20 shooting, 4-for-8 from the 3-point line, and had seven assists with just two turnovers. He made every big shot for Phoenix in clutch time, including a deep dagger 3 with 23 seconds left to give the Suns a decisive six-point lead.

All eyes were on Miles Bridges, Grayson Allen, and Royce O'Neale in this game because of the Bridges trade that happened this offseason. Bridges scored 11 points, grabbed six rebounds, and had three assists, and was effective playing with the bench units, but continued to struggle to make an impact with the starting group. Allen scored 14 points, while O'Neale scored 7 points, all coming in the fourth quarter. The Hornets were led by Coby White, who scored 21 points but did not play in the fourth quarter due to what appeared to be a minutes restriction. That certainly helped the Suns win this game, because White was getting wherever he wanted against the Suns' defense.

A win is a win, but the Suns' starting unit continued to not find any type of consistency in this game. The best stretches of basketball continue to come from this team at the beginning of the second and fourth quarters when there are at least four bench players in. The Suns closed the game with a Booker-Luke Kennard-Jordan Goodwin-Dillon Brooks-Oso Ighodaro lineup, while Jalen Green played just 21 minutes and none in the final quarter.

Green's continuing to show growth in the areas the Suns need him to grow in, but the fit with everyone else on the roster continues to be a problem, and the trust in him is obviously dwindling as he continues to not see minutes in fourth quarters. The Suns' record is now 5-6, and sometime in the near future they are going to have to change the starting lineup if it continues to look this disconnected.

All eyes were on Miles Bridges, Grayson Allen, and Royce O'Neale to start this game, and it was Grayson Allen who got things going for the Hornets early, connecting on a pair of triples. The Suns' defense lacked focus and gave up multiple wide-open layups and threes early, with Coby White hitting a wide-open 3 in semi-transition to go up 15-10. Jordan Ott called a timeout and subbed out Bridges for Ryan Dunn. The Suns' energy immediately changed on that end of the floor, as they quickly jumped on the Hornets defensively, leading to a 7-0 run engineered by Jalen Green knocking down two free throws and a corner 3 off of Suns stops.

There were two big takeaways from the Suns' first quarter: defense forced 10 turnovers and Devin Booker's pull-up 3-point shooting. Booker was dialed in, scoring 13 points on 3-of-4 shooting from 3. His comfort level shooting from long range continues to grow, and it is opening up everything for the rest of his game. The Suns led by six to end the first quarter because the Hornets did just enough on the offensive glass to stay in it, down 28-22 after one.

The Suns bench took over in the second quarter because of their hustle and tenacity on both ends of the floor. Jordan Goodwin forced multiple turnovers, leading to a wide-open dunk for Bridges and free throws off of an excellent out-of-bounds play. Collin Gillespie got free for multiple long-range bombs that went in as the Suns pushed their six-point lead out to 15 four minutes into the second quarter, 46-31.

The Suns led by as much as 17 before putting the starters back in, but there was some slippage. The Hornets went on a 14-2 run to cut the Suns' lead to 50-43. A couple of defensive breakdowns and Bridges fouling Grant Williams on a 3-point shot, leading to a 4-point play, were the catalysts for the Hornets getting back into the game. Williams scored 9 points in the second period, and another Allen 3 got the Hornets' deficit down to five at halftime, 55-50.

Even though the Suns were out-rebounded 28-to-15, the Suns still won the possession battle because the Hornets had 15 turnovers compared to the Suns' three total turnovers in the first half. The two worst stretches of the game came when the majority of the Suns' starters were on the floor, as they continued to get in their own way of being a good team.

The Suns' sloppiness from the starters continued to start the second half. As the Hornets jumped out to a 12-to-4 run to start the third quarter, the Suns' poor offensive flow led to easy shots and even more offensive rebounds for the Hornets, who led 62-59 with 8:22 left to play in the third. Since the Suns' 17-point second-quarter lead, the Suns were outscored 31-to-11 across both halves, and Coby Whites 19 points up to that point were a big reason why.

For the rest of the quarter, the Suns started to play better once Dunn, Gillespie and Maluach checked in for Ighodaro, Bridges and Green. But the Hornets responded immediately after two tough Booker midrange buckets. Both teams finished the quarter cold from the field, but Christian Anderson's solid play off the bench for the Hornets put the Hornets up 76-72 after three quarters. The Suns continued to look allergic to rebounding and playing consistent, mistake-free defense, and now a game that was once in cruise control completely slipped away from them. At no point should Jalen Green ever lead the Suns in rebounding with four total rebounds after three quarters.

It wasn't the prettiest 11-3 run ever to start the fourth quarter, but the Suns bench just never relents on their pressure. It started with the Suns grabbing a defensive rebound and a Bridges dunk in transition. The Suns attacked the rim and glass early in the quarter, capped off by a Gillespie midrange pull-up after a travel from Reid. Next play, the Suns force a turnover, Gillespie misses another midrange shot, but Goodwin chases down the rebound and lobs it up to Ighodaro to give the Suns an 83-79 lead.

The Suns continued to string together good possessions for the most part but could not create much separation. Luke Kennard scored multiple midrange shots, and a Gillespie drive-and-dump off to Maluach should have ended in a dunk, but he mishandled it, and O'Neale sprayed in a triple on the other end.

Booker, Brooks, and Ighodaro checked and left Goodwin and Kennard in. After Williams got open for a layup, Booker found Goodwin for an open 3 that just rimmed out. Booker connected on a midrange jump shot to give the Suns a three-point lead. The Suns continued to get multiple stops, and finally a Brooks 3-point shot gave the Suns a 92-86 lead.

The Suns and Hornets traded multiple empty possessions until Reid broke the seal with a 3 of his own, but Booker responded with a tough floater in the lane to get the Suns back up five with one minute to play. A cutting Diabate layup on the other end got it right back to a one-possession game with the Suns up 94-91. Then it was Devin Booker time as he hit a deep, deep 3-point pull with 23 seconds left to give Phoenix a decisive six-point lead. That is, until Brooks fouled O'Neale on a 3-point shot, which, of course, went in for the four-point play.

The Suns inbounded the basketball to Booker. He quickly passed it back to Bridges, who was fouled by Allen, and he made both free throws. Then Booker poetically stole an Allen pass for the Hornets' 27th turnover of the night, and sank both free throws as the Suns went on to win 101-96.

The Phoenix Suns finish up their homestand with the Los Angeles Lakers on Sunday afternoon.
