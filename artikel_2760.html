Interview with the Enemy: Chicago Bears

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

This week, the New Orleans Saints will head to Solider Field to face the Chicago Bears in Week 11 of the 2026 regular season. To break it down, Bill Zimmerman of Windy City Gridiron joins us to preview this weekend's matchup.

NJ: How legitimate are the Bears' Super Bowl aspirations at this point in the season? Does it feel like this could finally be the year Chicago gets it done?

BZ: The Jeffery Simmons trade changed everything as far as I'm concerned. The Bears defense was the reason they weren't a true Super Bowl contender. Dennis Allen was largely doing it with smoke and mirrors. Yes, he had really effective blitz packages on 3rd down, but that wasn't sustainable in the playoffs against great teams.

The offense was never a question; the defense was. With Simmons, he's a multiplier. In just two games, we've seen the play improve of every defensive lineman and linebacker around him. If their front 7 can play like this, it will help a banged-up secondary that is the weakest part of the team.

No one can guarantee a Super Bowl, but I do think the Bears are on the short list of true Super Bowl contenders this year (and that's very foreign to write).

NJ: Who are some notable names who will be sidelined for the Bears on Sunday against the Saints? Is there a particular area in which New Orleans could exploit?

BZ: The Bears are the healthiest they've been in a while heading into this game. They have RB Kyle Monangai returning, which will be a huge boost for their running game, and rookie CB Malik Muhammad may not be the biggest name, but he is a key starter in their secondary.

TE Cole Kmet is out for the year, and S Coby Bryant has been out since training camp. The only other notable starter who will be out is CB Kyler Gordon, who injured his shoulder against Tampa; he did finish the game, but he is now listed as out for this one.

The key to stopping the Bears is to attack their cornerbacks. It's the weakest part of the roster, but you have to make sure you are getting the ball out quickly before Jeffery Simmons collapses the pocket.

NJ: How much of an impact has Dennis Allen had on turning around the Bears' defense throughout his tenure? Have there been any decisions or coaching tendencies that have rubbed fans the wrong way?

BZ: I think Dennis Allen is very popular among Bears fans, and I think he was the perfect DC hire with Ben Johnson. I think Dennis Allen is at a point where he almost certainly isn't going to get another head coaching job. That means he should be able to pair with Johnson and be the top coaches on this coaching staff for years to come. If things go well, they'll have an Andy Reid/Steve Spagnuolo type partnership.

I think, before the Simmons trade, people had been frustrated with how Dennis Allen wanted to build his defensive line, but other than that, I think Bears fans are very happy with how he calls a game, and I think most of us understand that he got a lot more success out of his defense than the level of talent that was on the field.

The best thing he's done in Chicago is create a phenomenal third down defense. The Bears have been quite strong on third downs and it's forcing teams to get big chunk plays on the early downs or end up punting the ball back to the Bears.

NJ: Who is one Bears player you think Saints fans should keep an eye on Sunday—someone who may not receive much national attention but could have a strong performance?

BZ: Offensively, I don't think there are many names to give because Caleb Williams, Luther Burden, Colston Loveland, and Rome Odunze are their main weapons, and I think football fans are pretty familiar with all of them. I do think Kyle Monangai is going to be a big part of the offensive game plan and split carries with D'Andre Swift so Monangai is one returning from injury that I think can have a strong game.

Defensively, I mentioned Muhammad, but I'll give you Austin Booker. He was a 5th-round pick in 2024 out of Missouri, and he's been coming into his own this year as an improved run defender and a solid pass rusher. You should hear Booker's name at least once on Sunday, either making a TFL or a sack.

NJ: According to FanDuel Sportsbook, the Bears (-720) are currently 11.5-point favorites over the Saints (+520) in Sunday's matchup; what is your final score prediction and how do you see the game playing out?

BZ: It's really hard to imagine that the Bears don't win this one. Ben Johnson is great on extra rest, and the Bears are healthy, and I don't think it's an insult to say the Bears are simply a better team than the Saints and are playing them at home. I think the question isn't who will win or whether the Bears cover the big spread?

I think the offense is going to have success regardless. I think it comes down to if you think the Saints can get points on the Bears' defense. If you do, then there's no reason to think this one couldn't be like 31 to 21 or so, and the Saints cover. Personally, I do think the Saints will struggle on offense against the Bears, and I think the Bears will win this one comfortably by 2 or 3 scores. I'll call it 31 to 13.
