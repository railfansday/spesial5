FCS Week 12 Game of the Week: Montana Grizzlies @ Montana State Bobcats: How to Watch, Preview, Predictions

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The third Saturday in November is almost upon us once again and for those who follow the FCS closely that means two things; it's almost playoff time and it's Brawl week. Montana and Montana State renew their heated rivalry this weekend and, as is almost always the case when the 'Cats and the Griz clash, there are some major postseason implications at stake. #2 MSU comes in at 10-1 and already has a share of the Big Sky title under its belt. One more win will give them the conference championship outright and all but ensure they get homefield advantage in the playoffs again this year.

The #8 Grizzlies at 8-3, meanwhile, wouldn't just play spoiler if they get it done in Bozeman. They'd get to share the Big Sky championship with at least Montana State (and maybe a few others depending how the rest of the league games go). They would also be in play for a top eight seed and bye week in the playoffs. A loss would likely not end their season but would absolutely ensure that they're playing on Thanksgiving weekend. November is about to reach its crescendo in the Treasure State.

Just last week Montana State head man Brent Vigen became the program's all time winningest coach when he picked up his 71st career win. Vigen's resume is as impressive as any coach in the FCS. He's taken MSU to the playoffs in each of his six seasons, has four Big Sky titles on his mantle and won the national championship last fall. Vigen's team has lost just four conference games since he's been in charge and he's never lost a league game at home. Save for two defeats (both in Missoula) to these Grizzlies, Vigen has also never lost in the month of November. The accolades are almost endless for the 2024 Eddie Robinson Award winner and his team is consistently one at or near the top.

For Montana it's Bobby Kennedy calling the shots for his first time as a head coach. Kennedy, who has been almost everywhere there is to be in college football, took over after the abrupt departure of longtime coach Bobby Hauck last winter. He was UM's receivers coach in 2025 but has served as an assistant in various roles everywhere from Texas to Rice to Washington to Penn State (among many other high-level institutions). Kennedy's first season with the Griz has been an up-and-down one but it looks like it will result in a playoff trip no matter how Saturday goes.

Montana State's offense is brimming with weapons and it starts with their senior quarterback Justin Lamson. Lamson led MSU to the national championship last year and was the game's MVP when all was said and done. There is seemingly no moment too big for him. Lamson comes into this one with a 71.7 completion percentage and just one interception. He seldom makes a poor decision and passing isn't his only forte. Lamson will call his own number when he needs to as he's rushed for 278 yards and seven touchdowns on the season. He's as tough as they come and it's hard to keep him down.

Lamson isn't alone in the Montana State backfield. In fact the 'Cats have an armada of running backs that can gash opposing defenses. Adam Jones is at the top of that billing. Jones, a junior, is 15 yards away from cracking the 2,000 mark on his career. Last season he ran for 575 yards and this year he's at 698 with 12 scores. Jones is complimented by both Jared White and Colson Coon. White has been seeing an increased workload as of late with two 100-yard games over the past month (his most recent coming last week at Northern Colorado). Coon is averaging 6.8 per carry as well. Mix all that in with Lamson's running ability and that ground game is lethal when it gets going.

This is where Montana linebackers Peyton Wing and Nai Mose will need to be at their best. Wrapping up is vital against the Bobcats and mistakes in tackling can lead to gash plays and touchdowns in a hurry. Mose is the Grizzlies' leading tackler with 70 stops and Wing is right behind him with 55. Those two will spearhead the effort over the middle of the field for the Griz D. They usually are the most active players when it comes to swarming on that side of the ball and they'll need to be again this weekend.

The biggest name missing for Montana State this Saturday will be last year's hero Taco Dowler. The star receiver has missed the majority of this season while dealing with concussion issues and he is not listed on the two-deep again this week. Instead it will be standout freshman Carter Dahlke and Dane Steel hauling in passes and both have proven themselves many times over this year. Dahlke has burst onto the scene as one of the more talented youngsters in the whole country with 759 yards on 51 catches. Steel is right there, too, with 711 yards on 53 snags. The duo has 11 touchdowns between them. And that's not even mentioning tight end like Rocky Lencioni who has also shined this fall. Lamson will not be short on weapons even without Dowler.

Montana has made it clear all season long that it butters its bread on offense. The Griz offense can score points almost at will, averaging 39.9 points per contest. At the heart of it is star running back Eli Gillman. Gillman, who now holds the Big Sky record for career touchdowns with 80, is the best breakaway threat in the entire country. Idaho State did a good job last week of containing him but that's no easy feat and no one knows it better than this Bobcats defense. Last year between their two matchups, Gillman rumbled for 238 yards and two scores against MSU. Stopping #10 has to be top priority for Vigen's team.

Doing that, though, will leave quarterback Keali'i Ah Yat a little more free to sling it around and that's something he does extremely well. Ah Yat has thrown 3,195 yards this year (third in FCS) along with 30 touchdowns. With receivers like Brooks Davis at his disposal that comes as no surprise. Davis is the best pass catcher in the Big Sky right now with 1,314 yards and 12 touchdowns. If Ah Yat places the ball anywhere in his vicinity, there's a good chance he's coming down with it. Between Gillman, Davis and Ah Yat, the Montana State defense is going to have its hands full.

One thing the Bobcats have going for them on that side of the ball, though, is a very stout defensive line. Led by defensive ends Zac Crews and Seth Brock, the trenches have been a game-wrecking part of the equation of MSU this year. Crews and Brock have a combined 11 sacks between them this year along with 15 tackles for loss, five QB hits and two forced fumbles. That pair will look to rough up a Montana O line that has only allowed ten sacks this year. Getting pressure on Ah Yat will not be easy but guys like Crews and Brock are more equip to do it than most that the Griz have seen up to this point.

It's the back end of the defense that's been suspect for the 'Cats at times this year. Allowing nearly 230 yards per game through the air this year, Montana State will have to shore things up against Davis and company. The good news for them? Last year's Big Sky Defensive Player of the Year is back there. Safety Caden Dowler, who authored a pick six against these Grizzlies last November, will be patrolling the secondary and seldom is he out of position. Dowler will make his plays. It's guys like Takhari Carr, Tayden Gray and J.J. Dolan that will have to step up and solidify what has been the shakiest part of MSU's game.

This has the chance to be one of the higher scoring Brawls there's been in a while. Both defenses have struggled at times this season to keep opposing offenses off the field and off of the scoreboard. It could be a track meet of a game but it may very well also turn into a game of keep-away. The best defense for both units might end up being long, sustained offensive possessions. If that is indeed the case, that style of play favors MSU more than it does UM. Guys like Lamson, Jones and White will be able to keep the chains moving and the Griz offense on the sideline. Montana will be able to strike quickly when they do have the ball and that will result in some fast points but in the long run it may end up actually hurting their defense more. Last season Montana State was able to salt the game away at the end with a 14-play, seven-minute drive. That run game paired with homefield advantage will serve strong for MSU in the late stages and the Bobcats will take a close one.

Score Prediction: Montana State: 37 – Montana: 31
