Devon Dampier, No. 2 Utes on cusp of something historic

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

There's a lot on the line for the University of Utah in its trip to Fort Worth.

Not only would a win keep an undefeated season alive, but it would clinch No. 2 Utah's first-ever berth in the Big 12 championship game.

The Utes can punch their ticket to the last-ever Big 12 title game by beating TCU or West Virginia. A BYU loss at Kansas would also secure the Utes' place, regardless of Utah's Saturday outcome.

After getting through the Houston-Cincinnati-BYU-Arizona gauntlet unscathed, Utah has a real shot at finishing undefeated in the regular season for the first time since 2008.

Standing in its way are a pair of 5-5 teams — TCU (3-4 in Big 12 play) and West Virginia (2-5 in Big 12 play).

ESPN's matchup predictor gives the Utes a 73.5% chance of winning in Fort Worth, and Utah is favored by 8.5 points on the road.

It's Utah's first trip to Amon G. Carter Stadium since 2009, a visit most Ute fans would like to forget. Back then, No. 4 TCU kept its own undefeated season alive with a 55-28 win over the No. 16 Utes.

Now, 17 years later, the circumstances couldn't be more different.

Both teams are in the Power Four Big 12 Conference, but TCU is having a rare down season after going to the national championship game in 2022 and posting a pair of 9-4 seasons in 2024 and 2025.

The Sonny Dykes-coached Frogs have been on the wrong side of one-score games in all five of their losses this season, including a 17-10 loss vs. BYU and a 29-28 loss at Arizona that saw the Wildcats recover a pooch onside kick and kick a game-winning field goal.

"I mean, we're two recovered fumbles away from this game being for who's going to be in the championship game," Dykes said. "You know what I'm saying? I mean, we had a chance to recover a fumble against West Virginia and we had two chances to recover a fumble against Arizona.

"That even takes away the lack of productivity we've had on offense. Just those two plays right there, we're sitting here at, what, 7-3 and two losses in the league and this game is for an opportunity to get in the Big 12 championship."

As Dykes said, TCU's offense has been fairly unproductive. The Horned Frogs have what Dykes thinks is his best-ever defense, but the coach known for his offensive teams has been unable to find answers this season.

TCU is averaging just 23.4 points per game.

The Horned Frogs are averaging 372.9 yards per game, No. 74 in the nation and No. 15 in the 16-team Big 12, and have one of the nation's worst rushing offenses at just 106.5 yards per game.

This is a pretty one-dimensional offense that will try to beat teams through the air, and that's where the Frogs have found some success, throwing for 266.4 yards per game.

"They're throwing the heck out of it on the offensive side of the ball," Utah coach Morgan Scalley said.

Harvard transfer Jaden Craig, who is battling an ankle injury, did not practice all week, per 247Sports' Jeremy Clark.

ESPN's Pete Thamel reported that sophomore Adam Schobel is expected to make his first-ever start on Saturday.

If it is indeed Schobel, it's a tough spot to be in for a debut.

The former four-star quarterback will have to have a great night against a Ute defense that has given up explosive plays, but is still No. 10 in pass efficiency defense and is holding opposing quarterbacks to just a 53% completion rate. Utah also has 14 interceptions on the year.

The Utes will have another opportunity to get pressure on an opposing quarterback that doesn't have a top-tier offensive line protecting him. The Horned Frogs have allowed 28 sacks, one of the highest rates in the nation, and Craig is being pressured on nearly 33% of his dropbacks.

For Utah to have success against a good TCU passing offense, it needs to get to the quarterback.

Craig or Schobel will have one of the top tight ends in the country in Kari Ashley to throw to. Ashley has 684 yards and four touchdowns on the season, and he's complemented by receivers Ed Small (633 yards, 2 TDs) and Jordan Dwyer (580 yards, 1 TD).

It's yet another test for Utah's secondary against a talented receiving corps.

"Wide receiver Dwyer I think is fantastic, really good skill players. The quarterback's been efficient. So that's going to be a test for our guys for sure," Scalley said.

Scalley and the Utes know they can't keep giving up explosive passing plays during the most important part of the season, especially against an offense like TCU's that has one of the better explosive play rates in the conference.

"We're giving up chunk yardage plays on the defensive side of the ball that we can't continue to give up. We've got to clean some stuff up on the back end," Scalley said. "Some of it's just plaster coverage. Sometimes when the quarterback escapes, guys trying to do too much, leaving their coverage.

"Some of it's pre-snap identification and communication. So there's enough there that we've got to get better at in the secondary for sure."

Devon Dampier has turned in Heisman moments in each of the past two weeks, and Saturday's game will provide another opportunity.

TCU's defense is one of the best the Utes will face all year.

"They run to the football. They're athletic, those defensive ends, the outside backers, and they put some pressure on you," Scalley said. "Anytime you're relentless and when the ball is thrown, you're turning and running and you've trained your body to do that, those are the defenses that usually end up making a bunch of plays."

TCU is No. 22 in the nation in total defense (304.4 yards per game), No. 23 in rushing defense (109 yards per game) and No. 41 in passing defense (95.4 yards per game).

The most points the Horned Frogs have given up this season is 28 against Noah Fifita and Arizona.

Simply put, it's going to take another phenomenal performance by Dampier.

The health of running back Wayshawn Parker will be key, though Daniel Bray has proven that he can step up in his absence. Parker is probable to play after getting just one carry last week with a heel injury.

If Utah can run on the Frogs' excellent rush defense, it will do a lot to open up the pass game for Dampier.

It's tough to bet against a Ute offense that has shown up in nearly every game and is averaging 37.9 points. If Utah can have offensive success against a tough TCU defense, it will be tough for the Horned Frogs to win in a shootout.

If TCU is going to hand the Utes their first loss, it will be because they slowed down Dampier and Utah's offense, something that a lot of teams have been unable to do.

Dampier is playing at a very high level against some tough defenses in the past two games, averaging 190.5 yards and two touchdowns through the air and 94.5 yards and two touchdowns on the ground.

Once again, Dampier's rushing ability will come up big to help break through a TCU defense that has been stingy against running backs.

In a primetime game on the road, the Utes have a chance to earn a trip back to Dallas in two weeks to compete for their first-ever Big 12 championship.

"Every game is a championship game. We don't have anything solidified. We got to go 1-0 this week. All of our attention is on TCU," Scalley said.
