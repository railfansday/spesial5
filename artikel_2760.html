How high can Alperen Sengun climb Rockets historical hierarchy?

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Some NBA teams don't have a distinct best player in franchise history.

The Houston Rockets are not one of those teams.

I don't have to say his name. You know who I'm referring to.

Joking. I wasn't born after 2000. It's Hakeem "The Dream" Olajuwon, and if the answer ever changes, something unspeakably beautiful has happened.

Alperen Sengun will not usurp Olajuwon. There's no sense in even qualifying that statement. It's ironclad.

But how high can he climb in this organization's historical hierarchy?

First, we'll disqualify some "also-ran" candidates.

Sengun has already surpassed Steve Francis. Next. He's inevitably going to surpass Yao Ming. Next.

Accounting for both health and production, Ming's best season was 2008-09, when he averaged 21.1 points, 10.6 rebounds, and 2.1 blocks per game with a 3.1 Box Plus/Minus (BPM). If not an injury, his 2006-07 season may have been the benchmark, when Ming averaged 25.0 points, 9.4 rebounds, and 2.0 blocks per game, although his 2.4 BPM was pedestrian.

As of this writing, Sengun is (almost in) arguably having a better season than Ming ever did. He's averaging 22.0 points, 9.3 rebounds, and 7.2 assists per game with an 8.7 BPM.

A few quick points before this whole article is dedicated to comparing Sengun and Ming. Comparing players across eras is always a questionable process. On a related note, we know that BPM heavily weighs assists from bigs. If Ming played in 2026, he'd likely get more dimes (he was a very good passer, and it would be a way for him to create threes).

Also, my brain wants to say Ming was a better defender, but he wasn't. Ming was a deep drop specialist who'd quite possibly get tarred and feathered in today's game. Sengun is defending at the level, even switching at times, and doing a lot of stuff Ming couldn't dream of.

I think Sengun has probably passed Ming already, but it's highly debatable. What's clearer is that he's on a trajectory to do so.

Who are the best Rockets in history (after Olajuwon, of course)?

For me, Harden has a firm grip on the number two spot, but some would give it to Moses Malone. After those three, it's Calvin Murphy and Elvin Hayes.

There's a quick, tidy, sledgehammer of an argument to make here. If Sengun is the best player on a Rockets championship team, he automatically gains a strong argument for the number two spot.

Yes, "rings culture". I'm a proponent of the notion that the NBA championship is the existential purpose of the NBA, and that winning a championship, while not the exclusive measure of a player, is the most important one. So, if the Rockets won the title this year:

I would consider Sengun the fourth-best Rocket in franchise history.

Why fourth? Well, Harden did accomplish some lofty statistical feats. A 10.9 BPM in 2018-19. Thirty-two straight 30-point games, second only to Wilt Chamberlain. A 53-point, 17-assist, and 16-rebound performance (not a typo).

And he took the Rockets to Game Seven of the Western Conference Finals against one of the most dynastic teams in NBA history, arguably stifled only by Chris Paul's hamstring. That's where ring culture can be myopic: There are variables outside of a player's control.

Similarly, Moses Malone led the team to the NBA Finals and won an MVP. One championship probably doesn't erase that either. Two would, but let's all calm down.

Elvin Hayes never made it out of the first round during his Rockets tenure. Calvin Murphy did, but he was practically a role player during their 1981 run. I'm not so old as to have seen either (or Malone, just so we're clear), but statistically, Sengun has surpassed Murphy already. Even a Conference Finals run in 2026-27 could put him ahead of Hayes.

If anything, this piece is a testament to the Rockets' franchise legacy. We (yes, we) can be easy to overlook. We're not the Lakers, Celtics, Spurs, or Warriors, but we're in the next tier with the Pistons, Heat, Bucks, and Thunder. We're a proud middle upperclass team, so the fact that Sengun is even forcing us to consider his place in franchise history is meaningful.

If this Rockets core explodes, he can get as high as second. There's no exact science here, but a pair of titles should get him there. Otherwise, one title, sustained team success, and marginal individual improvement would, in my estimation, get him as high as third. If the Rockets never win a title during his tenure, and he stabilizes at his current level of play, I'd put him fourth or fifth depending on your view of Hayes.

Second place is almost surely his ceiling, but there are some bars nobody should expect to clear.
