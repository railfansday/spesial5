Where Is Caroline Ellison, Portrayed by Julia Garner in <i><em>The Altruists</em></i>, Now?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The cryptocurrency executive spent 11 months in prison.

This story contains spoilers for The Altruists.

Netflix’s The Altruists tells the true story of FTX, the celebrated cryptocurrency exchange, and the trading firm Alameda Research, which both collapsed amid accusations of wrongdoing by executives. Anthony Boyle and Julia Garner play Sam Bankman-Fried, who founded the company, and Caroline Ellison, the co-CEO of Alameda, respectively. The two, who met while working for the New York City firm Jane Street, are said to have been a couple at one point. Both were sent to prison on charges of fraud, with Ellison having been released this year. Here’s what you need to know about where she is now.

A native of Massachusetts and a Stanford grad, Ellison joined Alameda as a trader in 2018. She was promoted to co-CEO in 2021.

Ellison was sentenced to 24 months in prison in 2024 after pleading guilty to seven charges, including wire fraud and money laundering. Prosecutors praised Ellison for her cooperation, which included testifying against Bankman-Fried. “In her many meetings with the government, Ellison approached her cooperation with remarkable candor, remorse and seriousness…and she persevered despite harsh media and public scrutiny and Bankman-Fried’s efforts to publicly weaponize her personal writings to discredit and intimidate her,” they wrote.

Ellison served about 11 months in a Connecticut prison before being moved to what was referred to as community confinement, which typically means being under house arrest or living in a halfway house. She was released in January 2026.

While in college, Ellison became interested in effective altruism. The movement, which is popular within tech circles, urges people to use evidence to make decisions that will yield the most good for society. That can mean choosing to take a high-paying salary rather than volunteering, believing that the money earned will provide more benefit.

The nonprofit Manifund, a grant-funding organization that was inspired by the effective altruism movement and had deep ties to FTX, hired Ellison. The nonprofit’s CEO, Austin Chen, announced in September that she had begun working over the summer. “I believe in redemption. Caroline has admitted her faults, worked to make creditors whole, and served her time in prison. I’d like to give her the opportunity to contribute back to the world; I hope that the world will concur,” he wrote.

Chen quoted Ellison in his announcement. “I’m deeply sorry for what I did, and I completely understand why people may not want to work with me. But I’m very grateful to Austin for giving me a second chance, and judging me on my current work performance rather than my past. I feel very lucky not only to have a job, but to be working somewhere that I think is doing great work and has a mission I believe in,” she wrote.
