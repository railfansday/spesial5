Review: Godzilla Minus Zero joins the list of truly stellar sequels

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Director Takashi Yamazaki ups the ante by pitting Godzilla against one of the monster’s greatest foes

It’s a rare thing for a film sequel to match or even surpass its predecessor; The Godfather Part II and Terminator 2: Judgement Day are the best known examples on a pretty short list. Go ahead and add Godzilla Minus Zero to that list. Director Takashi Yamazaki’s sequel to his critically acclaimed Godzilla Minus One (2023) is everything one could want in a Godzilla movie: human pathos, great special effects, and, ultimately, two giant kaiju duking it out over who gets to destroy Japan (and eventually the world) the most.

(Spoilers for the first film below. Spoilers for Minus Zero but no major reveals until after the gallery. We’ll give you a heads-up when we get there.)

As I wrote previously, Yamazaki wanted to return to Godzilla’s filmic roots with Minus One, setting the events in postwar Japan and tapping into the monster’s symbolic representation of the Japanese perspective on the 1940s nuclear holocaust—while also incorporating all-too-human themes of guilt and redemption. That first film focused on kamikaze pilot Kōichi Shikishima (Ryunosuke Kamiki), who struggled with survivor’s guilt after he failed to take action when Godzilla attacked the Japanese base on Odo Island in 1945.

Knocked unconscious, Kōichi survived and awoke to find his parents and many others were killed in the World War II bombing of Tokyo. Kōichi rebuilt a life with a woman named Noriko (Minami Hamabe) and the orphaned baby she rescued, Akiko (Sae Nagatani). But then Godzilla re-emerged, and Kōichi resolved to die fighting the monster to atone for Odo Island. Kōichi saved the day and lived to tell the tale. He reunited with Noriko, who had a strange black bruise on her neck. And we saw a chunk of Godzilla’s remains start to regenerate, leaving things open for a sequel.

Godzilla Minus One was a massive hit, both in Japan—where it remains one of the highest grossing Japanese films ever—and worldwide, racking up $116 million globally and winning an Oscar for best visual effects. Yamazaki said in interviews at the time that there had as yet been no talk of a sequel—while also hinting at future directions a sequel might explore. He later admitted it was way of planting a seed for what would become Godzilla Minus Zero.

Godzilla Minus Zero is set two years after the events of the first film, with Kamiki and Hamabe reprising their roles as Kōichi and Noriko, along with Nagatani as Akiko. The family is just trying to live in peace after the devastation Godzilla inflicted on their country. Alas, a new kaiju emerges, arriving via meteorite and using its tentacles to feed on local wildlife before morphing into a dragon-like creature that causes fresh rampaging destruction. Fans of the genre will recognize this creature as Ghidorah, whose appearance in the film was teased in the various trailers.

It isn’t long before Kenji Noda (Hidetaka Yoshioka)—i.e., the dude with the magnificent wavy gray hair who is director of the Large-Scale Disaster Response Bureau—seeks out Kōichi for help in dealing with this new kaiju threat. His team is able to track Ghidorah’s movements via electrical storms and other pertinent weather patterns, thanks to meteorologist Makoto Aoyama (Masami Nagasawa). But the team has no idea how they might defeat this new threat to life and infrastructure. It’s hardly a spoiler to note that at some point, a regenerated Godzilla will come into play.

(WARNING: significant spoilers appear below. Stop reading now if you haven’t seen the film.)

Yamazaki has retained the visual tone that made the film’s predecessor so striking; it feels like classic Godzilla fare of yore, just with 21st century cutting-edge visual effects. If anything, he’s upped his game on the special effects front, reflecting the larger production budget he had to work with. (It’s also the first Japanese movie filmed specifically for IMAX and it’s well-worth seeing in that format.)

Much of that budget went toward bringing the central monsters to cinematic life, and Godzilla Minus Zero does not disappoint. Ghidorah, for instance, responds to a bombing attack by mutating into its famous three-headed King Ghidorah form, complete with a spiffy new superpower: a “gravity beam” that lets the creature levitate entire houses (and, at one point, Godzilla) into the sky before dropping them back to the ground. And the kaiju vs. kaiju battle is spectacular in both scope and execution.

Yamazaki even managed to squeeze in what he called his “bucket-list” special effects shot inspired by a scene in 2008’s Cloverfield: Godzilla looming out of the Atlantic Ocean in front of the Statue of Liberty to wreck havoc on the statue and New York City in general. Granted, it occurs in a dream sequence of biologist Kanji Murakami (Min Tanaka), who survived the Hiroshima blast that killed his wife and children and longs for revenge. And it’s not just indulgence on the director’s part: the scene reinforces the traditional theme of linking Godzilla to nuclear radiation and the horrors of the atomic bomb.

Nor does Yamazaki ignore the human factor via the dilemma of his nuclear family trio. Kōichi must wrestle with his conscience: how can he risk his life again to battle a kaiju when it could mean leaving Akiko an orphan yet again should he fail? Noriko is infected with Godzilla’s radioactive G-cells—the only human to survive the mutations in her own body for so long. But her time could be running out, plus they’ve kept her infection secret for fear she will be taken away by the government for experimentation. Sometimes doing the right thing comes at great personal cost, and watching these quite ordinary people grapple with that reality brings emotional depth to the film.

The film ends on a suitably bittersweet note, and while Yamazaki hasn’t dropped any hints about a possible third film in the series, we’d love to learn more about Noriko’s strangely symbiotic connection to Godzilla via the G-cells in her bloodstream. Long may he reign.

Godzilla Minus Zero is still playing in theaters.
