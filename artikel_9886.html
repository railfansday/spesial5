Norway links energy firm cyberattacks to critical Azure API Management flaw

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Microsoft says mitigation is in place, but admins must investigate possible compromise and rotate secrets

Microsoft has issued a critical vulnerability advisory after attackers were caught raiding Azure API Management (APIM) instances for secrets and performing admin operations.

According to a nonpublic advisory, seen by The Register, Microsoft said the attacks, which have already affected two major energy companies, only impact customers on Azure API Management Classic tiers, not Azure API Management v2.

It said of the now-patched vuln: "Our investigation found that customers were exposed and an unauthorized party could have used the issue to access secrets stored in Azure API Management Classic instances before Microsoft identified the security issue."

Microsoft linked exposure to Classic-tier configurations that did not follow its network security guidance or stored secrets directly in APIM rather than referencing them from Azure Key Vault.

Microsoft directs customers to its security guidance for the recommended configuration.

The advisory lists potentially exposed secrets, including named values, client secrets associated with identity providers and OpenID Connect providers, subscription keys, and tokens used to call direct management APIs.

Security expert Anders Kristiansen warned that stolen credentials could also give attackers access to services behind APIM.

"If someone obtains a credential from that configuration, they may be able to use it directly against the service it protects, outside APIM," he wrote. "What they can reach depends on the credential's permissions and whether it is still valid."

Norway's National Security Authority (NSM) urged organizations using APIM to assume their secrets and named values had been exposed and rotate all tokens and secrets.

The agency also advised mapping the information accessible through each APIM instance to assess the potential scope of any compromise.

NSM credited Norway's energy sector with first detecting the vulnerability last week. It named gas giant Equinor and hydropower company Skagerak Energi among the affected organizations.

The authority commended Equinor for its early detection and rapid information-sharing, and warned that growing use of AI increased the risk of further exploitation.

"The vulnerability affects several sectors in Norway and also beyond Norway's borders," it said. "NSM has close dialogue and cooperation with national and international partners and businesses about the situation.

"When a vulnerability becomes known, multiple actors will be able to attempt to exploit it. Rapid escalation in the use of artificial intelligence is affecting the situation. This poses a risk to Norwegian businesses."

Customers who spoke to The Register warned that forgotten development and test instances could leave more organizations exposed. The extent of exploitation remains unclear.

Microsoft said it identified the security issue on November 18 and automatically applied a mitigation to close the access path the following day.

The Windows giant said customers still needed to investigate whether their instances had been compromised before the mitigation was applied.

NSM nevertheless advised organizations to check their instance's runtime version against its published patch thresholds and contact their provider if it remained unpatched.

Microsoft did not immediately respond to our request for comment. ®
