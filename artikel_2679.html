The NHL is not what it is today without Ovechkin and Crosby, and they entered the league together in 2005. They have faced off 100 times in the NHL, including 25 games in the playoffs. In those 100, they have combined for 231 points. A total of 90 games saw either find the score sheet and 49 had both record a point in the same game.

They have met in four postseason series, each in the second round. Crosby and the Penguins won the first three series and won the Cup each time. The Capitals won in 2018 and won their first and still only Stanley Cup. They have played 75 regular-season games in the series. Crosby leads 43-28-4 and has 35 goals and 62 assists in those. The 39-year-old Crosby is in the final year of his contract but has said he has no plans to retire. He and Ovechkin have joined Howe, Alex Delvecchio, Stan Mikita and Steve Yzerman as the only players in NHL history to play 22 seasons with one franchise.

"Over the years, obviously, I've developed a friendship with him and have a ton of respect for him," Crosby said to the media Monday with the Ovechkin news. "It's tough, you come into the league at 18 years old, and before we even met, we were built up against each other."
