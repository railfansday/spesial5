Browns coach with deep ties to Brock Bowers, will like Raiders’ latest injury update

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Brock Bowers practiced on a limited basis for the second straight day Thursday, with one caveat for anyone encouraged by the sight of the Raiders tight end back on the field.

The ankle that kept him out of last week's game in Denver was heavily wrapped.

Whether that was a precaution or a sign the ankle still has a ways to go remains to be seen. At this point, Bowers' availability for Sunday night's game against the Browns in Cleveland appears headed toward a game-day decision.

Either way, there's someone in Cleveland who'd love to see him. You get the idea Browns coach Todd Monken would just prefer their reunion take place during pregame warmups, with Bowers in street clothes.

Monken was Georgia's offensive coordinator when he and Bowers won back-to-back national championships. He spent those two seasons dreaming up ways to get Bowers the ball. Given a week to figure out how to keep it away from him, Monken fondly recalled what he was up against.

At Georgia, they had a name for the kid who would chase down every ball you threw him, bring it back and look at you like he couldn't think of a reason you'd stop throwing it.

Brock Bowers of the Las Vegas Raiders looks on prior to a game against the Seattle Seahawks at Allegiant Stadium in Las Vegas on Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. Getty Images

"You could play ball all day and his tongue would be hanging out, and he never wanted to come out, man," Monken told reporters this week. "He would just want to keep playing."

As for the prospect of seeing his old tight end Sunday, Monken allowed himself a little wishful thinking.

"And I would tell anybody that listened to him, I said, this guy's just different, you know," Monken said. "And hopefully he's still hurt."

Good luck selling Buddy on another Sunday off.

"We all really want to play," Bowers said. "It's really hard mentally on me to miss time."

Imagine how Fernando Mendoza feels. The rookie quarterback makes his third NFL start Sunday and has barely had a chance to find out what life is like with Bowers. They connected twice for 18 yards on the opening series against Seattle in Mendoza's debut. Bowers hurt his ankle on that drive. That's the extent of their work together on game day.

Brock Bowers, left, and Fernando Mendoza of the Las Vegas Raiders wait to be introduced before playing the Seattle Seahawks, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026, in Las Vegas. Getty Images

Consider what Mendoza stands to get back.

Bowers caught 112 passes for 1,194 yards and five touchdowns as a rookie, then added 64 catches for 680 yards and seven scores in 12 games last year. In eight appearances this season, he has 50 catches for 676 yards and six touchdowns. That's 226 catches, 2,550 yards and 18 touchdowns in 37 career games.

Thirty-five of his 50 catches this year have moved the chains. For a young quarterback trying to get the ball out before the rush gets home, there's considerable appeal in throwing a five-yard pass to a guy who can get you 10.

Georgia's coaches got their first look at that extra gear before anyone threw Bowers a pass. The COVID-19 shutdown had cost the kid from Napa, California, his entire senior football season. On the first day of winter conditioning in Athens, all the freshman had to do was finish a timed run with the other tight ends.

"He's like 10 yards in front of all of them," Monken said.

Monken figures he'd have stayed with the pack his first day, lest an older teammate get the idea the new guy was showing off. Georgia tight ends coach Todd Hartley remembers what happened when they lined up again.

Cleveland Browns head coach Todd Monken watches the third quarter against the Houston Texans on Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026, at Huntington Bank Field in Cleveland. IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect

"This dude was winning every sprint," Hartley said. "The fastest dude out there, the hardest worker. And we're like, 'Oh, my God.' I mean, we knew he was fast, but we didn't know he was fast-fast, or had a work ethic like this."

Before Bowers won either of his two Mackey Awards as the nation's best tight end, he had changed what a day's work looked like in Athens. The guys trying to beat him out could do the math.

"Brock Bowers was the best thing to happen to us. Not just because he had a million catches and won two Mackey Awards. But because he set a standard for work," Hartley said. "And from the very beginning, [other players] saw that kid and said, 'If I'm going to see the field, I got to step my game up.'"

Brock Bowers of the Las Vegas Raiders leaves the field after being injured during a game against the Seattle Seahawks in Las Vegas, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. Getty Images

Long before Georgia, Bowers and his dad had their own Sunday night game. With football on TV, Bowers drew a line in the carpet for a sideline. His dad threw him a Nerf football, and Bowers tried to come down with his feet in.

All these years later, somebody else still has to tell him when it's time to quit.

"I'm always pushing to play no matter what," Bowers said.

Monken could have told the Raiders that much.
