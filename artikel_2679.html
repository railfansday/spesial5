Las Vegas expansion focus turns to new arena (which could be new Summer League home)

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

In the coming weeks (maybe), the NBA is expected to choose between three groups looking to own and run a new franchise in Las Vegas. While the biggest factor in the winning bid will always be the money — with bids expected to be at or above $10 billion — another topic has come to the forefront:

Specifically, one of the groups trying to win the bid — Golden Knights owner Bill Foley and former Suns owner Jerry Colangelo — has big plans for a new arena, which they detailed to Mike Vorkunov and Joe Vardon of The Athletic.

Foley's group reached out to USA Basketball and the NBA Summer League to discuss its interest in hosting them in a possible new arena if the group were to win the bid for a new Vegas franchise, according to multiple league sources briefed on the pitch.

There are two parts to that. As noted in the original report, USA Basketball is not moving its headquarters from Colorado Springs to Las Vegas. That said, the USA Men's National Team often starts its Olympics/World Cup training camps in Las Vegas, using UNLV's facilities and playing an exhibition game in the city. A new arena with top-flight facilities could be a draw for that kind of situation for USA teams.

The NBA Summer League is also held annually on the UNLV campus, with games played in the Thomas & Mack Arena and the Cox Pavilion, which are connected by a shared lobby (allowing Summer League to have two games going at once that fans can bounce between). Warren LeGarie, who runs the Summer League, told The Athletic he would be open to a new venue if it met the league's needs.

In the coming weeks, and certainly by the end of the year (per NBA Commissioner Adam Silver's previous comments), the league is expected to pick one of the three potential ownership groups in Las Vegas: Foley and Colangelo; Nancy Walton Laurie and her husband, Bill Laurie, with Lauri being a Walmart heir and Bill having owned the St. Louis Blues; former Bucks owner Marc Lasry, former Turner Broadcasting president David Levy and Canadian billionaire Steve Apostolopoulos.

All three potential ownership groups have talked about a new arena, potentially as an anchor for a resort/casino complex and entertainment district. Exactly where this new arena and resort would go is undecided, which is part of the delay. Dallas Mavericks governor Patrick Dumont — the CEO and chairman of Las Vegas Sands — led part of the discussion around the Las Vegas team at the Board of Governors' meeting before the season.

Getting a new arena designed, approved and built takes time and could mean the Las Vegas team will not tip off until the 2029-30 season.

While Las Vegas has the 10-year-old T-Mobile Arena just off the Strip — home to the NHL's Golden Knights, the WNBA's Aces, and where the NBA has hosted the final rounds of the midseason NBA Cup the past two years — it would need up to $400 million in renovations to host an NBA team, Silver has said. While not off the table, that is less likely than building a new arena.
