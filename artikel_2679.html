The Lakers are an NBA title contender because Luka Doncic is completely out of control

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

I'm something of an expert on one-man teams. And the 2026-27 Los Angeles Lakers and Luka Doncic look like they're taking my theories to heart.

The Lakers didn't necessarily look like they would be a completely Luka-centric roster this season, and they did spend money and draft capital upgrading the team around him this offseason; they have a second scorer in Austin Reaves, and this is hardly the least-talented roster Doncic has ever had to play with. But he's just taken every bull by every horn and become the immovable center of a 12-2 Lakers team, and he singlehandedly makes them a contender to win the West. Yes, we're at that point.

Doncic is completely out of control this season. He's averaging 39.0 points per game and leading the league in all sorts of hilarious things: shot attempts, 3-point attempts, free-throw attempts. Basically anything that ends with "attempts," Luka is attempting it. And he's making it too, notably making 41 percent of his 3-pointers on over 13 attempts per game — if you don't intuitively understand how ridiculous that is, that's fine, let me spell it out:

If you make 41.3 percent of your 3-pointers, you inherently produce the best offense in NBA history simply as a function of points per 100 possessions. The only team that's ever done that as a team (with a normal 3-point line) is the 73-9 Golden State Warriors, but lots of players have shot at or above 41 percent. The problem is doing that at volume, but Doncic is almost doing it at over 13 threes per game, which is not supposed to be a thing; he's never shot that well in his career, nowhere near that actually, and so we'd expect some regression, but even a 38-39 percent-shooting Doncic at this kind of volume is going to bring a serious amount of points to the table.

And he's doing it with a pantheonic ball-hog season so far. In terms of the most one-man seasons in NBA history by usage rate, there are three seasons ever over 40 percent: 2016-17 Russell Westbrook is in a special category; he won the MVP and averaged a triple-double but also thought about his stats more than any player I've ever seen on a basketball court. 2018-19 James Harden is number two, when he threw down over 36 points per game on a Rockets team that had no other scoring to speak of … and then it's this Luka Doncic season.

Interestingly enough, he has the highest career usage rate of any player in NBA history, a function of how every team Doncic has ever been on has basically been built entirely around him. But getting and staying above 40 percent usage puts you in the Twilight Zone, where we have to start asking the hard questions: does this actually work?

The 2017 Westbrook team went 47-35 and the 2019 Harden team went 53-29. The Lakers are currently on pace to go, well … 70-12, which I don't expect will happen, but I truly believe these kinds of heliocentric experiments can and do work mathematically. And I think Luka's play this season absolutely makes the Lakers a serious contender, and perhaps one of the favorites — it is totally possible, even feasible, for a single player to carry an NBA team through the playoffs with proper efficiency, numbers Luka has. Michael Jordan won two championships with over 37 percent usage in the playoffs, and he did not even really shoot threes in either of those seasons.

Okay, it's time for "basketball theorycrafting that you'll just have to trust me on", since the research on this does not (yet) exist in a way that I can link, though I am working on it. Scroll down in this old post about soccer to find a graph about the so-called "Usage-Efficiency Boundary" which was from the work of Evan Zamir, the original publication of which I cannot find anywhere. Basically, there is a causal relationship between usage rate and efficiency in the NBA that shows that efficiency necessarily decreases as usage increases in basketball; this could be due to fatigue, simple statistical regression from volume or (probably) a mix of both. But Ian Levy, noted basketball nerd, told me once that he found that 3-point efficiency doesn't necessarily follow that same trend, and you might be able to blow through this usage barrier with enough 3-pointers. Hmm…

I'm currently looking into that and how to have a full project on it soon … but what that means is that Luka Doncic could potentially do way better than Harden and Westbrook did with their usage rates if he can keep his 3-point volume up and keep that shooting up, then we could be in business. Stay tuned for updates on this theory.

The only thing Doncic hasn't been this season is the MVP favorite, somehow, since Victor Wembanyama still somehow leads in that department having one of the most destructive two-way seasons I could have possibly imagined. Luka really could become one of the greatest players ever never to win an MVP if this just never breaks for him, which is certainly something to monitor historically, but for this year? I think this Luka could both win his first MVP and win the Lakers a title. I buy this hype, every bit of it, even if it's just because I'm a nerd.
