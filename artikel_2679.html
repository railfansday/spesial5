Week 12 Fantasy Football Holiday Slate: Will Chiefs rotate Kenneth Walker III and Emmett Johnson?

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

My daughter retired from the military at the start of the season and came home to start her civilian life. We love having her back. Our sons are excited to hang out with their big sister. My wife and I get an extra driver for the boys. Win-win.

Well, until we started talking about the Thanksgiving menu.

She hasn't been home for the holidays in the better part of a decade, so we're pulling out all the stops for Thursday. I'll have at least two types of meat in the smoker. Multiple veggie dishes. Not to mention the family members coming over to spend time at the new house. Between tomorrow's game, the Thursday tripleheader and a Black Friday special, I'll probably get roped into so many conversations I'll only be able to watch the game in pieces.

If your situation is anything like mine, here's one thing I'll be watching for in each game. If that's all you've got time to check before food's ready, you'll know what matters for your fantasy football lineups.

I'm glad Green Bay got an extra week to reset after going up against the Vikings. Unlike Brock Purdy and the 49ers, Jordan Love didn't fare so well.

To be fair, Love isn't the first to fall victim to DC Brian Flores' unit. Purdy is the only QB whose efficiency metrics improve with a bunch of purple in front of him. But what might be more concerning for the Packers is if the slump lingers into the next game. Because it wouldn't be the first time.

Five of the seven QBs with a game before and after facing Minnesota posted a lower EPA per dropback in their next game than their average before the matchup. Of course, you can poke holes in the narrative I'm suggesting. Daniel Jones, Tyler Shough and Malik Willis aren't world beaters. But Josh Allen posted his first game under 200 passing yards after getting pummeled by Minnesota in primetime (10 hits, season-high). Jared Goff was an outright disaster in Miami, notching his first game of '26 without a passing TD and his third with three or more sacks. Again, this is why the Packers bye couldn't have come at a better time, as the Rams have their own way of creating havoc for enemy QBs.

Week 10 was just the second game where L.A. could feature the vaunted duo of Myles Garrett and Aaron Donald. Turning them loose against the Commanders and Cardinals seems like some sort of crime. But it paints a similar picture to Love's environment the last time we saw him.

Time to Pressure (Weeks 10-11): 2.2, T-1st

The Rams put QBs in a bind, but not by sending the house on seemingly every snap. In their two games together, Garrett and Donald have combined for 14 quick pressures. Braden Fiske and Byron Young have four each, too. Meanwhile, on the backend, their secondary has allowed just four TDs over a six-game stretch. They're especially stingy on intermediate throws, giving up three or fewer receptions on attempts of that depth in all but one game.

Josh Jacobs is still recovering. The offense will have to rely on Love's shoulder yet again. So, if you look up and see him struggling to connect with his receivers, or Matthew Golden having another ill-timed drop, just hope one connects for a TD and we can move on to Week 12.

I talked about Chicago's lackluster start to the second half of their season on Sunday. In truth, the Bears defense was the real MVP. The runner-up was the Saints' first-quarter offense. They've scored a total of nine points in the first 15 minutes in 2026. But after Chicago mounted a multi-score lead, Tyler Shough found a rhythm.

Drive #4: 7 (Plays), 80 (Net Yards), 3:47 (Drive Time)

Now, on the one hand, just three of these drives turned into points for New Orleans. And only one resulted in a TD. But look at the number of plays run and the time Caleb Williams had to spend on the bench. Surprisingly, Chicago generated just one more first down on offense than the Saints (18 to 17). Its defensive success rate (57.7%) was actually worse than the Saints' (60.3%). If Shough could do that, think about what the Bears' upcoming opponent will do.

Jared Goff blacked out against the Bucs. HC Todd Bowles blitzed him on 36.7% of his dropbacks. Goff didn't even take a sack while completing 7 of 11 attempts. Tampa's defensive front put Goff under duress 43.3% of the time, and that didn't faze him either. In fact, Sunday afternoon, given the stakes, was Goff's best performance when pressured since coming to Detroit.

Success Rate (when pressured): 58.3%, Highest (of 17 games with +40% pressure rate)

OC Drew Petzing's game plan was perfect. They attacked the seams of the Bucs' zone coverage for multiple chunk gains. With Jahmyr Gibbs stepping up to block DT Vita Vea, the Lions could flood the exterior of the defensive coverage with four receivers out on a route (2 WRs, 2 TEs) on 28.6% of Goff's dropbacks. Or, as the above clip highlights, he could beat them the old-fashioned way with Jameson Williams.

Chicago just let the Saints dink-and-dunk their way into a close game. And the Lions can play that way, too. But the explosive element always lies in wait. So, if the Bears stall on either side of the ball, the Lions can pounce on them and maintain their lead in the NFC North.

I didn't turn on the PIT-PHI game until late in the fourth quarter. My thought was Pittsburgh would pull out the win, and the race in the AFC North would stay close. But on just 21 passing attempts, Joe Milton got his first win as a starting QB. But it wasn't all pretty.

To say the Eagles are trying to ease Milton into the role (i.e., hiding their passing game) would be kind. Philadelphia went from a -1.1% PROE in his first start, had the bye week to plan and came out throwing even less (-10.6%). Don't get me wrong — Milton didn't acquit himself of any poor play. He ranked second-worst and fifth-worst, respectively, in CPOE across both starts. Somehow, at 6'5"-246lbs., Milton is averaging five sacks a game. Luckily, he's using his speed as a runner (3.6 FPPG on the ground), but the arm talent could use a boost.

Only three QBs have squared off with the Cowboys and not walked away with one of their best efficiency marks of the season. Dallas even switched defensive coordinators, and it was still in the bottom 10 in defensive success rate. Admittedly, Cam Ward's TDs were unethical at best. But as a Bengals fan, expecting your offense to score 30+ is akin to thinking hope is a plan. However, with DeVonta Smith back, Dak Prescott might need to do just that.

Outside of Smith coming back to a laughable 57.1% target share, he also took 61.1% of the air yards. Milton tried him on throws of 25 and 53 air yards, but the two couldn't quite hook up. If I see that on Thursday, we're in for a wild ride on Thanksgiving.

The Chiefs have two problems they'll (hopefully) solve on Thursday. Well, honestly, the issue belongs to us fantasy managers. But Kansas City's offense does have something of a dilemma on its hands.

Watching Emmett Johnson come in relief of Kenneth Walker has been great for both the Chiefs and fantasy managers. Nothing changed about how they do business. HC Andy Reid only opted to run on 46.7% of their early-down plays, but Johnson gained four or more yards on 53.8% of those carries. From a utilization standpoint, Walker and Johnson look interchangeable.

Early-Down Rushing Share: 79.1% (Walker W1-9), 81.3% (Johnson W10-11)

But something has to give, assuming Walker is back from his groin injury. When both were healthy, the Chiefs ran six plays with both on the field. Mahomes is already averaging the 12th-most dropbacks per game, so there's little chance of Kansas City becoming a rushing team. However, if there's one area where the two RBs could split the workload, it's in the passing game.

Yards per Route Run (as the starter): 1.80 (Johnson), 1.31 (Walker)

It should be no surprise that the younger, faster rookie can churn out more yards with the ball in his hands and a clear runway. Regardless, if both are active, I'd expect to see more of a rotation between the two dynamic runners.

However, the Chiefs' passing game has its own concerns.

I realize I'm going to come off as a Chiefs' hater, but the last two weeks have been preseason games for Patrick Mahomes. The last time he threw for more than 300 yards in back-to-back games was in 2023. Sure, the offensive line is still an issue, but his hits and sacks have been down, too. And the timing couldn't be better, as it's allowed him to find the best ways to use his receivers.

HC Andy Reid hasn't unleashed Josh Downs yet. He was up to a 32.5% route share and five targets after making a splash in his debut. The good news for gamers rostering Downs is that when he was on the field, Mahomes was looking for him. His 38.5% TPRR led the Chiefs' pass-catchers. The downside is that on the eight routes that Downs wasn't the primary target, Mahomes was making magic with the other receivers.

The above clip highlights how No. 15 used Downs' gravity to pull defenders to the sticks, leaving space for Rashee Rice. Two plays later, instead of going to the intermediate part of the field again, Mahomes hits Emmett Johnson on a screen to set up an easy 4th and 1. The deep shots to Xavier Worthy or Cyrus Allen didn't always work, but the tendency to look 15+ air yards downfield hasn't been an element of the Chiefs' passing game for a while. And if Week 11 was any indication, Kansas City went from having limited reliable receivers to multiple.

In Week 10, six different pass-catchers earned three or more targets. That was up to seven against the Cardinals. Mahomes ranked 2nd and 9th in PROE in those games, respectively, but without a clear WR1, we're hoping someone other than the RBs gets into the end zone. That should change on Thursday night.

Buffalo is first in offensive PPG and fourth in YPG. The Chiefs shouldn't have time to test things out or see what works best. Playoff seeding is on the line. So, I'll be seeing which pass-catchers become Mahomes' top options in one of the biggest games of the regular season.

I believed Jonah Coleman would take over the backfield in Denver. But I've been waiting for HC Sean Payton to do what he's done for years: split the backfield. Through the first six weeks, J.K. Dobbins played on 54.8% of the early-down snaps. But he was mostly off the field on third downs. Tyler Badie and RJ Harvey handled 77.8% of the RB touches in obvious passing situations. And everybody got the ball in the red zone. But maybe the rookie has made Payton change his ways.

Thinking back on it, if there were ever a matchup for Coleman to take advantage of, it'd be against the Raiders. Their run defense was allowing a whopping 131.9 YPG to opposing RBs. And to make matters worse, the short-area coverage ranked 32nd in completion rate to rushers (91.7%). Essentially, any way that someone out of Denver's backfield got the ball, they'd turn it into a positive result. I just didn't think so many of those touches would go to Coleman.

Going back to my thoughts on Payton's tendencies, how often he turned to Coleman isn't something we can wave away. His 88.9% share of the carries is the most of any running back since Payton came to Denver in '23. Third downs? All Coleman. Targets to RBs? 100.0% to Coleman.

I have no doubt we'll see No. 20 start the game. But I'll be watching for his workload. And if we don't see any more Payton backfield hijinks, Coleman will have the OROY award locked up before the stores close.
