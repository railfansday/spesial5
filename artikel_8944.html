JR assembles 2,500 portraits into a collective image of thailand’s creative scene

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

photographed across eleven professional sectors, the installation brings together artists, designers, architects, craftspeople, and the many people working behind the scenes of thailand’s cultural landscape.

JR’s participatory art project Inside Out comes to Cloud 11 with Thailand Creative Archive, a large-scale photographic installation featuring more than 2,500 black-and-white portraits of people working across the country’s creative industries. Opening on November 11th, 2026, the largest presentation of the Inside Out Project in Southeast Asia brings together artists, designers, architects, performers, craftspeople, and behind-the-scenes professionals in a collective portrait that places the people shaping the cultural landscape of the country at the center of public space.

Founded by JR, the Inside Out Project uses large-scale black-and-white portraits to give individuals and communities a platform to express their identities, beliefs, and causes. Since its launch, the initiative has reached more than 150 countries, inviting people to become active participants in shaping the visual identity of their communities.

The approach has informed several of JR’s best-known projects, including Portrait of a Generation, which brought the faces of young people from the Paris suburbs into public view, and La Caverne du Pont Neuf, which transformed Paris’s historic bridge into a cave-like installation.

For Thailand Creative Archive, the focus shifts to the people behind the creative economy of the country. Between May and September 2026, an Inside Out Photobooth truck traveled across the country, photographing more than 2,500 participants from eleven professional groups. These include music, art, film, software, gaming and animation, advertising, design, architecture, fashion, culinary arts, crafts and handicrafts, and other creative professions.

People whose contributions to the creative sector take different forms and levels of visibility stand in front of the French artist‘s camera lens. Artists, performers, and established creatives appear alongside photographers, production crews, designers, and professionals whose work many times remains outside the public eye. Presented together, the photographs shift attention away from individual authorship and toward the wider network of people that makes cultural production possible.

The archive presents Thailand’s creative identity through the people who collectively sustain the industry. The portraits document a particular moment in the cultural landscape of the country, raising a question about how creative communities are represented.

At Cloud 11, the photographs occupy several parts of the development, including its main facade, the exhibition space on Level 2, and the retail area. ‘We believe the success of the creative sector is never the achievement of one person alone. It is built on the collective efforts of everyone involved, whether in the spotlight or behind the scenes,’ says Paul Sirisant, CEO of Cloud 11. He adds that the project reflects the diversity of Thailand’s creative industry and the importance of recognizing the contributions of its wider community.
