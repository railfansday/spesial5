After Testing 122 Pieces of Gear, We Found the Best Climbing Gear of Fall 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Gear testing above 12,000 feet on the golden granite of Chamonix's Aiguille du Midi. It's a hard job, but someone's got to do it. (Photo: Anthony Walsh)

Rock climbing season doesn’t end when snow hits the high country. Some of the best conditions of the year are right around the corner: cool temps, ample shade, and just the right amount of humidity in the air. Unless you’re sharpening your ice tools, don’t give up on the rock (or plastic) season just yet—with the right gear, you’re in for the best season of your life.

Climbing’s gear-testing team has spent all summer sweating it out with the latest and greatest so you don’t have to. We tested 28 harnesses, 27 pairs of climbing shoes, 30 pairs of approach shoes, 9 ropes, and 28 backpacks—a healthy mix of new items and longtime favorites. Some of the gear was good, some was great, and a lot of it missed the mark: bruised hips, sore feet, exploding packs—it’s all part of the gear-testing game.

2026 was the year of the hyper-specific shoe. Although notable all-arounders made it onto our list, the real highlights were the shoes who didn’t try to do too much. One shoe felt about as stiff as a crampon, with its new-age carbon insert, and powered us up numerous vert testpieces; another had about as much support as a woolen sock (but, like, in a good way!); and the best shoe I’ve ever used for offwidths is absolutely terrible for finger cracks. Perhaps 2026 is the year of the shoe quiver.

The harness is the one piece of gear that will make or break your day on the wall. In an effort to find the best climbing harness available, we put everything from fast-and-light alpine G-strings to beefy big-wallers to the test this year, taking them on trips from the cold Canadian alpine to the steep walls of Devil’s Tower to overhung sport routes at The Red.

Unlike this year’s crop of rock shoes, my favorite climbing packs worked hard to serve multiple niches. One versatile alpine-climbing pack carried anywhere from one to four days of mountain kit with ease. And a new haul bag might just be the last pack (and airplane carry on) a rock climber will ever need.

The best climbing ropes are a tough category of gear to review because, these days, almost all are well made and perform great right out of the package. It’s only after hard wear—climbing pitch after pitch in rain, snow, and ice, coiling and uncoiling on dirt and sand, and taking some big whippers—that a rope reveals its true colors.

Helmets are our best defense against rockfall and weird whippers while climbing outdoors. And after years of writing accident reports—often of climbers who took a seemingly benign fall, before something weird happened—I regularly note how fortunate the climber was to be wearing one. When shopping for a helmet, take the time to find one that’s comfortable, lightweight, breathable, and fits properly—because actually liking your helmet makes you far more likely to wear it.
