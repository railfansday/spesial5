Norwich hit back to hold QPR

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Norwich City vs Queens Park Rangers. EFL Championship.

Report and free match highlights from the Sky Bet Championship match between Norwich City and Queens Park Rangers at Carrow Road on Tuesday night; Canaries draw their first game of the season as Anthony Musaba cancels out Harvey Vale’s opener.

Please use Chrome browser for a more accessible video player

Norwich's impressive run of victories came to an end as QPR held them to a 1-1 draw at Carrow Road.

The Canaries had won their previous five Championship fixtures but had to come from behind to pick up a point this time around after Harvey Vale had fired the visitors in front just before the break.

Philippe Clement's side were quickly back on level terms in the second half through a well-taken Anthony Musaba effort, but were well contained after that as Rangers registered a well-deserved point.

After a slow start, the match burst into life with a flurry of activity around the mid-point of the first half.

Norwich thought they had taken the lead on 19 minutes when Harry Darling got a touch on an in-swinging Jacob Wright free-kick but the effort was ruled out for offside.

Andre Brooks then saw a low shot from the edge of the area come back off the upright, while at the other end Dan Grimshaw did well to palm away a goal-bound effort from Vale.

Rangers were giving the hosts plenty to think about and took the lead four minutes before the break when Vale made another opportunity count.

The Republic of Ireland international had plenty to do after latching on to Kealey Adamson's pass but despite having Ruairi McConville and Darling in close attendance, he worked himself some space in the area before firing a powerful shot past Grimshaw.

Norwich were back on level terms within three minutes of the restart after a poor clearance by goalkeeper Pierce Charles was punished. It went straight to Musaba, who played a quick one-two with Mathias Kvistgaarden before confidently finding the roof of the net.

Vale fired inches wide as Rangers sought a quick response while substitute Oscar Schwartau was foiled by a good save from Charles as the game entered its final 10 minutes.

Norwich continued to press for a winner but could find no way through as they drew a game for the first time this season.

"This team will never settle for a draw - they will always keep going for the win but in football it doesn't always happen.

"After going behind we showed the right mentality, got our goal and kept pushing them back, with the fans playing their part, but unfortunately we couldn't get the second goal we deserved.

"We were up against a well-organised side and knew it was going to be tough but I thought the momentum was with us in the first half too and we just needed a bit of luck.

"We had a goal ruled out through a very tight offside decision and then Andre (Brooks) hit the post.

"When we went behind it became an even tougher task because we know how difficult they are to break down but our response was really good with everyone playing their part, including the lads who came off the bench."

"That's four points from two very tough away games, Millwall and Norwich, which is a very positive outcome and all credit to the players for that.

"We were up against a side who had won their previous five games, scoring 17 goals, and it was a very good performance, with a draw a fair result I feel.

"We started very well, both with and without the ball, playing with a good tempo, and scored a very good goal.

"Obviously we were disappointed to concede so early in the second half but the lads showed great character to stick to the game plan. They defended properly as a team and we also had some chances.

"I am lucky to have a special group of players here and although they are a young group I thought they showed great maturity out there to come away with a deserved point."
