Fiona the Hippo Undergoing Medical Care After Eating Hat

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Fiona the Hippo, the Cincinnati Zoo animal who went viral for her premature birth in 2017, is undergoing medical care after eating a knit hat that was thrown into her enclosure.

The Cincinnati Zoo confirmed that its famous semi-aquatic animal, who gained worldwide fame for her premature birth in 2017, is receiving medical treatment after eating a knit hat that was thrown into her habitat earlier this month.

“Her care team is cautiously optimistic that she will pass it and return to her usual self,” the zoo said in a statement Nov. 17, noting that Fiona is currently getting, “24-hour care and attention.”

After the zoo consulted with “veterinary and human specialists,” Fiona’s care team concluded that she is unable to undergo surgery to remove the hat. As a representative from the zoo acknowledged, “Our best chance for success with Fiona would be if she can pass it on her own.”

So far, Fiona is moving in the right direction. The hippo received a colonoscopy courtesy of The Columbus Zoo earlier this week as well as an ultrasound, which showed that her intestines are bunching, proving that she is working on passing something through.

In the meantime, Fiona is of course receiving all the love and support from her Cincinnati Zoo family, including “all of her favorite foods,” anti-nausea medicine as well as ensuring she stays hydrated.

“Fiona will remain off habitat until this is resolved,” the zoo added in a separate statement. “We will share updates as we have more information. Please help us keep our animals safe by securing your belongings and taking extra care near habitats.”

This isn’t the first time Fiona has fought an uphill health battle. The now-9-year-old hippo was born six weeks early back in 2017, weighing in at just 25 pounds, the lowest recorded birth weight for her species ever.

Ever since, Fiona has remained beloved in online circles, even participating in a virtual safari show as part of The Cincinnati Zoo’s programming during its shutdown for the COVID-19 pandemic.

As the hippo continues her latest health fight, the Ohio zoo expressed gratitude for all the love and support she’s received.

“As you can imagine, we are rooting for her,” the zoo added in a statement, “and hoping that she proves to be as strong and resilient as she was at the beginning of her life.”

The zoo's 17-year-old hippo named Bibi gave birth to little Fiona six weeks early on January 24, 2017. She weighed 25 lbs. less than the lowest recorded birth weight for her species, prompting critical care from vet staff.

Fiona's health made steady improvements in the weeks after her arrival, and by mid-March she had reached the 90 lb. mark, was eating hay and enjoying time in the pool!

Ever so curious about her surroundings, Fiona couldn't help but get an up-close look at herself on a computer screen.

By May, Fiona was well into the 200 lb. range and spending plenty of time lounging in her kiddie pool. What. A. Life.

Thanks to Fiona's tireless spirit and determination, she graduated to deeper-level pools after learning how to propel herself through the water.

It wasn't until early June that little Fiona began spending time with her mama. In the beginning, Bifi (as they were so lovingly dubbed) interacted during brief, supervised play dates but have since begun adjusting to life in the outdoor pool enclosure.

Fiona's dad Henry was reunited with his daughter one month later and from the looks of those hippo grins, their first time interacting went swell!

Fiona embraces all her curves and all her edges, and it's a much-needed reminder that we all should too.

It took much longer than anticipated, but Henry, Bibi and Fiona are finally cohabitating as a happy and healthy bloat. She can't get enough time exploring her dads' mouth, which is apparently normal behavior for a growing baby hippo!

Without a finalized schedule in place, Cincinnati Zoo visitors should cross their fingers in anticipation of seeing Fiona out and about. Here she is laying a wet one on her caregiver. Mwah!

This mother-daughter pair spends their afternoons splashing in the pool like any hippos should. And aren't those faces priceless?
