'Why not have a go?' - the halfway line goal phenomenon

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Diego Gomez's shot against Crystal Palace soars over Walter Benitez

David Beckham's goal from the halfway line against Wimbledon in 1996 is one of football's iconic moments.

It helped to catapult the Manchester United midfielder to global stardom and remains a staple of highlight reels.

The impudence to shoot from such a distance highlighted the exceptional talent and character of the future England captain.

Premier League players are now trying their luck from long range more regularly, testing their instinct, precision and technique.

Diego Gomez's stunning 51-metre (56-yard) strike for Brighton against Crystal Palace last month added to the spectacular list in the Premier League, while players in the EFL and FA Cup have contributed their own.

There have already been 19 attempts from over 50 metres this season, on course to beat last season's Premier League total of 26.

Of the 2,733 shots in the Premier League this term, 0.7% of them have come from over halfway - up on 0.3% over the course of last season.

This sample size is admittedly small. And in the case of goals scored from distance lower down the football pyramid, this is data that isn't readily available.

But why are we seeing a growing number of shots being taken from such extravagant distance?

Gomez tried to score from deep in his own half during Brighton's 2-0 win at Sunderland on 10 October.

A week later, he beat Walter Benitez with the longest-range goal in the Premier League since Wayne Rooney's 57.6m strike for Everton against West Ham in 2017.

The Paraguayan took advice from Lionel Messi, when the pair were at Inter Miami, to always look to play the ball forward to another level.

It is not just Premier League players who are trying their luck from outrageous distances.

Charlie McNeill scored from halfway for Crewe in their recent 2-1 FA Cup win against AFC Wimbledon.

The on-loan Sheffield Wednesday forward, who played once for Manchester United before leaving Old Trafford in 2024, beat goalkeeper Joe Bursik to open the scoring.

Having done it while at Manchester City and Manchester United's academies, the 23-year-old felt his latest attempt was instinctive.

He told BBC Sport: "Goalies nowadays are always quite high up the pitch - playing a sweeper-keeper role - and I always have a thought if I ever got into that situation to have a go.

"You see it more and more. I had the opportunity so I thought: 'Why not have a go?'

"It was one of those goals. I turned and saw him off his line and in that split-second made the decision to shoot.

"I knew I caught it sweetly and I was following it the whole way in so I knew it had a good chance, but as the keeper was going back I thought he was going to get it.

"When I watched it back it dropped quite early, but as it was getting closer and closer I thought it was going to go over because it went quite high but it dropped in."

Crewe Alexandra's Charlie McNeill talking about the rise in halfway line goals

McNeill joins a growing number of players to have scored from the halfway line or inside their own half over the past 18 months.

Preston Fearon, now on loan at Forest Green, scored two goals from around the halfway line for West Ham Under-21s in their win against Burnley last month.

Gbemi Arubi was barely a stride over the halfway line when he scored for Burton against Peterborough in October.

Last season, Abdul Fatawu (Leicester), Ryan Ledson (Huddersfield), Reyes Cleary (Barnsley), Regan Hendry (Mansfield) and Sammy Braybrooke (Newport) all scored from the halfway line or inside their own half in the EFL.

Two years ago, Chelsea's Moises Caicedo scored from the centre circle against Bournemouth.

Is it all about the goalkeeper's position, as they stand higher up the pitch to either stop opposition attacks or get their own team moving?

"You've got to execute it properly, but it plays a big part. Most keepers are like that, high out the net, because we are playing over the top so they are there first, to get the ball," said McNeill.

"I have always had it in my head and have tried it a couple of times. Although it's not like I do my homework and see he is always off his line. It's one of those instinctive moments."

Paul Robinson scored two goals in his career, one for Tottenham and one for Leeds

Asmir Begovic holds the record for the longest-range Premier League goal when the goalkeeper scored from 95.7m for Stoke against Southampton in 2013.

Two other goalkeepers have scored from their own half: Tim Howard for Everton against Bolton in 2012 and Paul Robinson, who netted for Tottenham against Watford in 2007.

Yet they were flukes rather than by design - similar to Barrow goalkeeper Ben Winterbottom's goal from his own half in their 3-0 National League win at Gateshead on Saturday.

Robinson, who also scored for Leeds against Swindon in 2004, external, feels conceding from distance is now a risk managers are willing to take.

The former England international said: "We've seen the game evolve in the way where the goalkeeper becomes a key figure in starting a pattern of play. The manager wants the goalkeeper at the base of the back three, four or five.

"But then also we're seeing a lot more teams playing with a higher line and the goalkeeper then has to take a higher line of starting position to read the through ball.

"When your team are playing a high line, you obviously have to be further up the field and you're concentrating on where your defensive line is, as opposed to where the football is at times.

"It's one of those things modern-day managers are willing to accept if and when it does happen, because of the way they want their goalkeeper to play.

"We see so many managers come out now and say: 'It's OK. I'm quite happy with them making mistakes. This is the way I want them to play. Blame me because that's what I've asked them to do.'

"We're probably entering that type of territory with it."

With attackers aware of the higher starting position of opposing keepers - a result of teams pressing high - and likely to have been encouraged by the success of other long-range efforts across football, attempts from extreme distance are becoming more frequent in the Premier League this season.

The conversion of efforts from more than 50 metres have increased too - up to 5.3% this season, compared to 1.1% across the previous three seasons combined, with Gomez's strike the first Premier League goal scored from over 50 metres since the 2023-24 season.

It's a small sample size, so more time is required for clearer trends, at least in the Premier League to be established.

But with more long shots being taken and keepers starting higher, there is a suggestion (and hope) we may see more Goal of the Season contenders before the term is out.
