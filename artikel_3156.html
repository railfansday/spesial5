CAVA vs. On: Which Consumer Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Fast-casual Mediterranean chain CAVA and footwear maker On both post rapid growth, but their valuations sit far apart on forward earnings.

By Seena Hassouna – Updated Nov 21, 2026 at 2:34PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Cava Group ›

Choosing between high-growth consumer favorites can be difficult for long-term investors. Both CAVA Group (CAVA -4.67%) and On (ONON -2.76%) are scaling quickly, but which stock is the better addition to your portfolio?

CAVA is disrupting the fast-casual dining space with its Mediterranean-focused menu and expanding grocery line. Meanwhile, On is challenging established footwear giants by blending performance technology with high-fashion appeal. While both companies target premium consumers, their financial profiles and valuation multiples vary significantly as they approach the next several years of growth.

CAVA operates a Mediterranean fast-casual restaurant brand that prioritizes fresh ingredients and customizable bowls. The company serves a diverse audience, with significant engagement from Millennial and Gen Z consumers across its 500 restaurant locations. Beyond physical dining rooms, the business expands its brand reach by selling dips and spreads through grocery distribution channels.

In the 12 months ended Oct. 4, 2026, revenue reached about $1.4 billion, which represents a 27% increase over the previous year. This rapid top-line growth helped the company generate a net income of approximately $64.7 million. The upward trend in sales highlights the successful scaling of its restaurant network across 30 states and Washington, D.C.

The balance sheet shows a debt-to-equity ratio of nearly 0.6x, which is entirely attributable to restaurant lease obligations, as CAVA has no borrowings. As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the current ratio is roughly 2.7x, indicating the company has $2.70 in current assets for every dollar of current liabilities. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 28, 2025, free cash flow was nearly $26.1 million, representing the cash remaining after a company funds its operations and capital investments. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 8.2% of operating cash flow in the fiscal year ended Dec. 28, 2025.

On designs and sells premium performance footwear and apparel to a global customer base. The company leverages a multi-channel strategy that includes its own e-commerce platform, 67 physical retail locations, and a vast network of wholesale partners. By focusing on engineering and distinctive design, the brand has established a strong foothold in the Americas, Europe, and the Asia-Pacific region.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached close to $4.0 billion, an increase of about 17% compared to the prior period. Net income for those 12 months was approximately $526.8 million, resulting in a healthy net margin of roughly 13.1%. This double-digit net margin, which measures the percentage of revenue remaining as profit, suggests strong pricing power in the sportswear market.

The company maintains a conservative capital structure with a debt-to-equity ratio of approximately 0.4x as of December 2025, driven by lease obligations rather than borrowings. Its current ratio of nearly 2.7x suggests a strong ability to cover short-term obligations with liquid assets. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, On generated about CHF 281 million in free cash flow, providing ample capital for further international expansion.

CAVA faces intense competition from established restaurant chains and other fast-casual brands. The company relies on a limited number of suppliers and single-source partners, which could lead to disruptions if those partners face operational issues. Scaling 500 existing restaurants to a national footprint target of around 1000 also requires significant capital and carries risks related to food safety incidents or cybersecurity compliance.

ON Holdings must contend with fierce rivalry from major global competitors such as Nike and Adidas. Because the brand relies on discretionary spending, any downturn in consumer confidence could negatively affect premium footwear sales. Expanding rapidly into markets like China and Hong Kong also introduces geopolitical and regulatory risks that could affect its global supply chain.

On appears to be the more conservatively priced option because it carries a significantly lower Forward P/E and P/S ratio than CAVA Group. The P/S ratio measures the company's market value against its sales over the past twelve months.

*Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

On earns the edge because it charges less for the better business. It trades at a lower price-to-sales multiple than CAVA while maintaining several times the net margin, and that gap persists even after you strip out the one-time U.S. tariff refunds that padded its latest quarter. CAVA grew sales last quarter almost entirely by adding restaurants, while guest traffic at existing locations barely moved, and its stock price already assumes that expansion will continue to work. Management blamed part of the slowdown on a produce-safety scare that may fade, but the valuation leaves little cushion if it lingers.On carries its own risks. Wholesale partners in the Americas are ordering more cautiously in a heavily promotional market; new Section 301 tariffs are expected to raise costs for its products; and it sells a single discretionary category. Both stocks also trade partly on being trendy, which cuts both ways when consumer tastes turn. I would buy On for 2027, but I would not back up the truck. Build a starter position, add only if the fourth-quarter report shows how much the refunds were hiding and wholesale holds up, and keep CAVA on a watch list until same-restaurant traffic recovers.
