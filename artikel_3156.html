BYD vs. Ford: Why the Cheaper Automotive Stock Isn't Necessarily the Better Investment for 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The global transition to electric vehicles is reshaping the economics of the automotive industry, but leadership in electrification doesn't necessarily translate into superior financial performance.

By RJ Fulton – Updated Nov 18, 2026 at 5:30AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Ford Motor Company ›

The global automotive industry's transition toward electrification is creating new opportunities for manufacturers while challenging the profitability of established business models. For investors considering BYD (BYDDY +0.37%) and Ford Motor (F -0.27%), the two companies offer fundamentally different approaches to navigating this transition.

BYD has emerged as a global leader in electric vehicles by leveraging its vertically integrated supply chain and competitive pricing. Meanwhile, Ford continues to rely on its established truck, SUV, and commercial vehicle businesses while investing in electrification.

Although both companies face declining revenue and significant financial pressures, their contrasting business models, capital requirements, and growth prospects could make one a more attractive investment heading into 2027.

With a presence in more than 120 countries, BYD is increasingly looking beyond its domestic Chinese market to drive growth. One of BYD's primary competitive advantages is its vertically integrated business model, which allows it to manufacture critical components, including batteries and semiconductors, in-house. This provides greater control over production costs and vehicle pricing.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached approximately $120.4 billion, representing a 4.5% year-over-year decline. Despite falling revenue, BYD generated $4.7 billion in net income, translating to a profit margin of roughly 3.9%. Maintaining profitability is particularly noteworthy given intensifying price competition in China's electric vehicle market and the costs associated with international expansion.

As of December 2025, BYD maintained a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.7 and a current ratio of 0.8. Perhaps more concerning, the company recorded negative free cash flow of approximately $14.6 billion in fiscal 2025.

Ford maintains an established global automotive business supported by a network of more than 8,200 independently owned dealerships. Unlike BYD, Ford's competitive advantage stems largely from its established position in the truck, SUV, and commercial vehicle markets. Its Ford Pro division also provides fleet management, maintenance, and software services, creating revenue opportunities beyond traditional vehicle sales.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached approximately $187 billion, representing a 1.4% year-over-year decline. However, Ford recorded a net loss of $7.8 billion, translating to a negative profit margin of roughly 4.2%. The company's deteriorating profitability highlights the challenges of maintaining its traditional automotive business while investing in electrification and navigating an increasingly competitive industry.

As of December 2025, Ford maintained a debt-to-equity ratio of 4.7 and a current ratio of 1.1. While its debt burden appears substantial, a significant portion is associated with Ford Credit, which provides financing to customers and dealerships. Additionally, Ford generated approximately $12.5 billion in free cash flow during fiscal 2025.

One of BYD's primary risks is growing trade protectionism. As the company expands internationally, tariffs and restrictions on Chinese-made vehicles could undermine its pricing advantage and limit access to lucrative automotive markets. Meanwhile, intense price competition in China threatens to compress profit margins, particularly as BYD continues to allocate substantial capital to manufacturing capacity, battery technology, and overseas expansion.

Ford faces the challenge of balancing investments in electrification with the profitability of its traditional vehicle business. Uncertainty surrounding EV demand, government incentives, and emissions standards complicates long-term investment decisions and increases the risk of misallocating capital. Ford also remains exposed to costly vehicle recalls, warranty expenses, and rising manufacturing costs. Additionally, its reliance on truck and SUV sales makes the company particularly vulnerable to weakening consumer spending and elevated borrowing costs.

Ford appears to be the more affordable option based on its lower earnings and sales multiples, which reflect the financial hurdles it currently faces.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

Ford is considerably cheaper from a valuation standpoint, trading at a forward price-to-earnings ratio of 6.1 compared to BYD's 13.7. Ford also trades at just 0.2 times sales, while BYD trades at 0.6 times sales. Despite this valuation disparity, I believe BYD offers the more compelling long-term investment opportunity heading into 2027.

My preference for BYD comes down to the direction of the global automotive industry. As electric and hybrid vehicles become more affordable, BYD's vertically integrated business model positions it to compete aggressively on price without relying as heavily on third-party suppliers. Its diverse vehicle lineup and expanding international presence also provide multiple avenues for growth, particularly in markets where vehicle affordability remains a primary consideration for consumers.

Ford's attractive valuation and positive free cash flow shouldn't be overlooked. However, the company faces the difficult task of protecting its traditional automotive business while developing a profitable electric vehicle operation. Although BYD's substantial cash outflows and exposure to international trade restrictions warrant caution, I believe its competitive advantages in battery technology, manufacturing efficiency, and affordable electrification justify its valuation premium over Ford.
