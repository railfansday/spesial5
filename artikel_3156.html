Cardinal Infra (CDNL) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Margin pressure and a CFO exit followed a sharp profit drop at the contractor.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

Cardinal Infrastructure Group Inc. (CDNL -62.38%) reported a significant decline in quarterly profitability despite high revenue growth, leading management to withdraw its full-year 2026 financial guidance. The company experienced material margin compression driven by cost estimate revisions on a small number of residential projects in the Carolinas alongside rising fuel and transportation expenses. Management is responding with a leadership transition in the finance department, the postponement of a planned acquisition to focus on internal execution, and the implementation of more rigorous project selection and review standards.

Operator: Good morning, and welcome to the Cardinal Infrastructure Group Third Quarter 2026 Earnings Conference Call. [Operator Instructions] As a reminder, this conference is being recorded. I would now like to turn the conference over to Tiffany Gidley, Cardinal's General Counsel. Please go ahead.

Tiffany Gidley: Good morning, everyone, and welcome to Cardinal's Third Quarter 2026 Earnings Conference Call. Joining me today are Jeremy Spivey, our Chairman and Chief Executive Officer; Benji Wood, our Chief Operating Officer; and Mike Rowe, our Chief Financial Officer. Please refer to the earnings release and presentation issued this morning and available on the Investor Relations section of our website. Today's call will include certain non-GAAP financial measures. Please refer to our earnings release for definitions and reconciliations to the most comparable GAAP measures. Today's call will also include forward-looking statements that are subject to risks and uncertainties that could cause actual results to differ materially. Additional information regarding these risks is available in our SEC filings.

Cardinal undertakes no obligation to publicly update or revise any forward-looking statements, except as required by law. With that, I'll turn the call over to Jeremy.

Jeremy Spivey: Thanks, Tiffany, and good morning, everyone. Revenue grew 65% this quarter to $202 million, but our third-quarter adjusted EBITDA margin totaled 4.2%, well below our expectations. The decline primarily reflected project estimate revisions, residential pricing pressure, lower initial bid margins on certain commercial and industrial work, and higher fuel and transportation costs. Mike will provide the detailed financial bridge, but I want to be direct. These results are not acceptable, and they make clear that we need to strengthen how we estimate, manage, and execute projects across Cardinal. As a second-generation operator with more than 30 years of experience in this industry, I know what strong project execution requires, and I recognize when we need to raise our standard.

We are taking several actions to do just that. Benji Wood, our Chief Operating Officer, is a fourth-generation operator with more than 30 years of experience and a demonstrated record of success leading ALGC. Benji will now take a more direct role in establishing and implementing consistent operating standards across the Cardinal platform, focusing on project selection, estimating, project reviews, and accountability. We will also engage an experienced independent third-party adviser to support implementation of these operational changes and assess our estimating and project review processes. Separately, in collaboration with Hughes Brothers, we decided to postpone the proposed acquisition.

We continue to view Hughes Brothers as a strong strategic fit, and our near-term priority is strengthening our existing platform, and we will revisit Hughes when appropriate. We believe the right priority today is strengthening execution across our existing operations and realizing the benefits of the businesses we've already acquired. We also remain focused on capital allocation. Given the strength of the balance sheet, our Board of Directors has authorized a share repurchase program up to $50 million of our Class A common stock over the next 12 months. Although our third-quarter performance exposed areas where our execution and operating processes must improve, we have made meaningful progress this year in building a more diversified, vertically integrated platform.

We expanded our self-performance capabilities, added complementary services in Atlanta, and brought an asphalt plant online in the Carolinas. Our strategy remains intact. Our immediate priority is strengthening execution and building the operating discipline and supporting infrastructure necessary to translate growth into more consistent profitability. With that, I'll turn over the call to Benji to discuss the operational changes we're making.

Benjamin Wood: Thanks, Jeremy. My immediate focus is applying the operating practices that have worked well at ALGC to the broader Cardinal platform. Our operating priorities are improving project selection, project execution, and accountability. We have identified several actions that are already underway. First, we are tightening project selection and estimating through more consistent bid review standards, greater management oversight, and increased selectivity around project risk and expected returns. Second, we are strengthening project management and field execution through more frequent and rigorous project reviews, standardized estimates to complete, and earlier identification and escalation of changes in productivity, schedule, scope, and expected profitability. Third, we are strengthening operating accountability through consistent estimating, reporting, and financial control standards across the platform.

These changes are intended to provide earlier visibility into project performance and allow us to act sooner when a project deviates from plan. We will implement them while continuing to invest in our employees and safety programs, bringing greater discipline and consistency across the Cardinal platform. With that, I'll turn the call over to Mike.

Mike Rowe: Thanks, Benji. I'll briefly review the financial results, balance sheet, and the outlook. Revenue grew 65% to $202 million in the third quarter, an increase of $80 million, of which $24 million was organic growth. Backlog reached $816 million. Revenue growth and backlog demonstrated continued demand across the platform. However, that growth did not translate into acceptable levels of profitability during the quarter, as adjusted EBITDA was $9 million compared to $21 million last year, while adjusted EBITDA margin declined to 4.2% from 17.5%. Gross profit for the third quarter was $11 million, resulting in a gross margin of 5.4% compared to 15.9% last year.

As Jeremy referenced, on an adjusted EBITDA margin basis, project estimate revisions accounted for approximately 6.5 percentage points of the decline. Residential pricing pressure and lower initial bid margins on certain commercial and industrial work accounted for approximately 3.6 points, and higher fuel and transportation costs accounted for approximately 3.5 points. These impacts were partially offset by approximately 0.3 points from other factors, resulting in a 13.3 percentage point decline in adjusted EBITDA margin. G&A increased to $15 million for the third quarter. This increase reflected acquired businesses, stock-based compensation, acquisition-related costs, and investment in personnel, systems, and infrastructure to support the growth and increased complexity of the organization.

Net loss for the quarter was $11 million compared to $10 million of net income in the same period in 2025. For the year-to-date results, revenue for the first 9 months of 2026 increased 92%, from $286 million to $596 million. Organic growth contributed $144 million, or about half of the increase, with the balance attributable to acquired businesses. Gross margin was 11.5% compared to 15.4% in the prior year period, primarily reflecting higher fuel and transportation costs, residential pricing pressure, and lower initial bid margins on certain commercial and industrial projects, as well as project estimate revisions recorded in the third quarter.

Net income for the first 9 months was $12 million compared with $46 million in the prior year period. Adjusted EBITDA increased to $63 million compared to $56 million last year, while adjusted EBITDA margin declined to 10.6% from 17.9%. We ended the quarter with $335 million in cash and cash equivalents and approximately $136 million of net cash, along with substantial additional borrowing capacity. Net cash provided by the operating activities was $30 million for the first 9 months of 2026, and capital expenditures were $42 million. Turning to our outlook.

Given the magnitude of the project estimate revisions recorded in this quarter and the work underway to strengthen our project-level forecasting, we are withdrawing our previously issued full-year 2026 revenue and adjusted EBITDA margin guidance. While we currently expect fourth quarter adjusted EBITDA to improve from third quarter, we are not providing a specific revenue or margin outlook. Our priority is to complete the necessary project reviews, strengthen our forecasting processes, and establish greater confidence in our timing and profitability of the work in our portfolio. With that, I'll turn the call back to Jeremy.

Jeremy Spivey: Thanks, Mike. I also want to share a leadership update. Mike Rowe will be stepping down as Chief Financial Officer and will remain with Cardinal to support an orderly transition. We have engaged Spencer Stuart to conduct a comprehensive search for his successor. I want to thank Mike for his contributions to Cardinal and for his commitment to supporting the transition. I'll close with this. Our long-term strategy continues to center on vertical integration, end market diversification, and acquisitions, supported by disciplined capital allocation. Right now, our focus is clear: strengthen execution, improve project visibility, and translate our growth into more consistent profitability. We understand that credibility will be built through execution and demonstrated results.

Our management team is focused on delivering those results. With that, Operator, let's open the call for questions.

Jeremy Spivey: Yes, the 3 were residential projects.
