Which 18th generation Pro Max phone has the best cameras?

The iPhone 18 Pro Max isn't the only 18 Pro Max to be released this year, now that Xiaomi has just revealed the Xiaomi 18 Pro and 18 Pro Max flagships. The two phones share more than just a name, though, and it seems Xiaomi has adopted a very Apple-looking camera bump on the back of its phones. But considering the camera specs are totally different, I started wondering what the actual quality differences were.

The iPhone 18 Pro Max is one of the best camera phones, sporting a trio of 48MP camera lenses: one main, with variable aperture, an ultrawide angle, and a telephoto lens with 4x optical zoom. The zooming camera offers up to 16x "optical quality" and has a maximum magnification of 40x. The Xiaomi 18 Pro Max, on the other hand, has a 200MP main lens, a 50MP ultrawide and a 200MP telephoto with 3.2x optical zoom.

You'd think that the higher resolution camera would produce the best photos, right? Well we've seen time and again that resolution doesn't always equal quality, especially now that camera phones rely on software just as much as they do hardware. That's why phones like the iPhone 18 Pro Max perform so well in our photo shootouts.

But obviously the only way to find out for sure is to actually take some photos and compare the results from each phone. So, out of iPhone 18 Pro Max and Xiaomi 18 Pro Max, which 18 Pro Max actually has the best cameras?

The latest and best iPhone, the iPhone 18 Pro Max comes with a new variable aperture camera, a larger battery, incredible performance from the A20 Pro chipset and all those other things that make the iPhone worth owning.

The main camera shot is quite different on both phones. The Xiaomi certainly excels in taking a brighter photo, but the result means that the lighter areas look considerably more washed out compared to the iPhone. The overall quality isn't as good either, since the iPhone can pick up more detail in the surroundings, such as my dogs' fur and the gravel on the path. In contrast, the Xiaomi tends to blur those finer details together, especially in the background.

Shooting indoors, things are a little more balanced, but it's clear that the Xiaomi's camera isn't quite as focused as the iPhone's, and most of the Lego set is blurry in comparison to Apple's camera. However the coloring and detail seem to be much closer than they were with the outdoor shot.

The Xiaomi camera's habit of brightening up the image seems to work in its favor on the ultrawide angle lens, with the iPhone adopting a more washed out and drab-looking color temperature. There are reasons for that, and it all comes down to Apple's automatic white balance being somewhat anti-Fall.

However, coloring aside, the detail on the iPhone-captured shot just isn't there. You can even see it in the grass if you look closely, with all the stalks looking pixelated and blurred, compared to the much sharper detail captured by the Xiaomi.

When it comes to color, the Xiaomi does edge the top spot with slightly more vivid coloring of these berries, in part thanks to the slightly brighter images. The iPhone hasn't washed out the coloring in this instance, and manages to offer a nice bright image with solid contrast between the different colors.

The Xiaomi 18 Pro Max's front camera sports a 50MP sensor, compared to the 18MP one on the iPhone 18 Pro Max, but how much of a difference does it make? As was the case with the rear cameras, the photo is a lot brighter, which really highlights the fact that the sun is shining directly onto my face. Meanwhile the iPhone's shot is a little darker, but the tones seem more true to life and don't make it look like this is my first time outside in several months.

The quality is good on both, even going so far as to pick up the reflection of my other dog in my sunglasses. However, zooming in shows how much of a difference high resolution can make. Xiaomi's rendition of my reflected dog is clear and almost perfect, while the iPhone's is pixelated and grainy. Then again, the rest of the photo is fine, and zooming right in isn't something a lot of folks will be doing.

Xiaomi's Portrait mode is, honestly, incredibly disappointing. The bokeh effect is barely noticeable, and it doesn't really emphasize myself in the same way that the iPhone does. The IPhone 18 Pro Max ramped up that effect to the point where the background isn't that visible, and it makes me pop out a little more. The quality of the photo isn't quite as good, but with the extra bokeh and the return more accurate coloring, it's the clear winner.

Zooming into 4x magnification and things are fairly even. The iPhone's coloring is a little more drab than Xiaomi's, but the overall quality of the zoomed-in shot is basically the same, to the point where it's more about personal preference than anything else.

Jumping up to 8x magnification and you can see how Apple's "optical quality" feature really shines. The shot of my dog at this magnification is much more clear than that of the Xiaomi 18 Pro Max, which noticeably merges the finer details of my dog's fur. The contrasting colors on the Xiaomi's telephoto is more noticeable than the iPhone's, but the loss of quality really lets the team down.

If you increase the magnification to 40x, there's absolutely no question about which camera is better. It's Xiaomi's, simply because the phone produced a clear and crisp shot that the iPhone just can't compete with. I assume some AI upscaling was involved, as is the case with so many other phones, and it's something that Apple consistently fails at. Maybe that's something Apple can include on the iPhone 20.

Going right in with the Macro mode, and the iPhone takes the crown once again. As with before, the picture isn't as bright as Xiaomi's shot, but that doesn't actually matter in this case. The iPhone managed to produce a much crisper photo, with bright colors and details that Xiaomi's camera didn't quite match.

Low light performance can make or break a camera phone, and I'm honestly a little torn over which camera is better in this situation. The iPhone 18 Pro Max produced a shot that is brighter and captured more detail, but it also appears somewhat overexposed in the process.

The Xiaomi 18 Pro Max managed to capture less detail on the church and its surroundings, despite the higher resolution camera, but produced a shot that looks slightly nicer — so long as you don't zoom in too much. And, honestly, I can't tell you which one I like better.

Overall, the iPhone 18 Pro Max managed to beat out the Xiaomi in more categories, though, the two phones drew in several rounds. That shows that Xiaomi 18 Pro Max's camera is no slouch, but not enough to beat the iPhone's camera in those core categories. The quality of the 8x zoom in particular was especially disappointing, since a 200MP lens should have the resolution to be able to cut down a high-quality image to simulate extra magnification — just like Apple does.

Given the choice, and purely based on camera performance, I'd pick the iPhone 18 Pro Max over the Xiaomi. Still the iPhone 18 Pro Max didn't dominate, and the Xiaomi camera certainly manages to produce some great shots. The fact they tied in four different categories just goes to show that these two phones are both very capable camera phones.

But Xiaomi obviously needs to work on fixing its shortcomings, including capturing more detail in low light, improving the bokeh effect and all those other things that could make the camera even better.
