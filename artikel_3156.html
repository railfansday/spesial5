Move Over, Amazon! Billionaire David Tepper of Appaloosa Has a New No. 1 Holding.

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Appaloosa’s billionaire boss pared down dual-industry leader Amazon for a foundational company in the artificial intelligence (AI) data center build-out.

By Sean Williams – Nov 20, 2026 at 7:06AM EST

With the exception of earnings season, few data dumps are more valuable to investors than the quarterly filing of Form 13Fs with regulators. A 13F allows investors to track which stocks Wall Street's brightest and most successful money managers purchased and sold in the latest quarter (in this instance, during the third quarter).

Billionaire David Tepper, who oversees nearly $7.6 billion in assets under management at Appaloosa, is among Wall Street's most revered fund managers -- and he was a busy bee in the third quarter. Tepper modestly pared down what had been his fund's top holding, dual-industry leader Amazon (AMZN -0.63%), and bulked up Appaloosa's stake in another foundational artificial intelligence (AI) stock, Micron Technology (MU -6.22%).

Appaloosa's David Tepper was a busy bee in the third quarter. Image source: Getty Images.

According to Appaloosa's newest 13F, its billionaire boss disposed of 525,000 shares of Amazon, reducing his fund's remaining stake to 4,475,000 shares. Nevertheless, it still accounts for roughly 15% of Appaloosa's invested assets.

Tepper's modest selling may be due to simple profit-taking. Appaloosa has been a continuous shareholder of Amazon stock for more than seven years, with shares effectively tripling over that time.

But it's also possible that Tepper is concerned about Amazon's aggressive capital expenditures (capex) tied to the AI infrastructure build-out. The company logged a staggering free cash outflow of $33.4 billion during the September-ended quarter, with capex more than doubling from the comparable quarter in 2025. If fundamentals come back into focus, Amazon (and other hyperscalers) could take it on the chin.

However, for investors playing the long game, it's been business as usual for Amazon. In addition to dominating online retail sales, revenue growth in its higher-margin cloud infrastructure services segment, Amazon Web Services (AWS), is accelerating. The integration of generative AI and large language model solutions lifted AWS's year-over-year sales growth to 39% in the third quarter.

Whereas Appaloosa's billionaire chief reduced his fund's stake in Amazon by 10%, he scooped up another 275,000 shares of memory and storage solutions leader Micron Technology, increasing the position by 28% to 1,250,000 shares from the June-ended quarter and making it the new No. 1 holding.

The (likely) lure for David Tepper is Micron's foundational position in the AI data center build-out. The company is a key provider of high-bandwidth memory (HBM), which is packaged with graphics processing units to transfer data at jaw-dropping speed.

Micron has benefited from two dynamics. Firstly, the overwhelming demand for HBM and solid-state drives has boosted its pricing power and enabled the company to lock in more than $100 billion in strategic customer agreements (SCAs) through 2030.

Secondly, despite the best efforts of Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing (TSM -4.11%), HBM supply shortages persist. Ongoing supply shortages are affording Micron exceptional pricing power and its highest-ever gross margin.

However, history has repeatedly shown that strong pricing power for commoditized tech products, such as memory, tends to be short-lived. While Micron's SCAs should be able to offset potential price weakness for the foreseeable future, historical factors point to earnings multiple compression and the possibility of an AI bubble-bursting event taking place.
