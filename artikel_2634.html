MLB rumors: Mets to meet with Tarik Skubal, Rangers' Corey Seager plan, plus Blue Jays, Cubs, Red Sox, more

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

This is an important week on the baseball calendar. First and foremost, it is awards week. Kevin McGonigle and Sal Stewart were named the Rookies of the Year on Monday. The Managers of the Year will be announced Tuesday, Cy Youngs and Relievers of the Year will be announced Wednesday, and finally the MVPs will be announced Thursday.

Also, the GM Meetings are this week in Las Vegas. The GM Meetings are not the Winter Meetings in terms of the sheer volume of rumors and transactions, but they do usually provide some news. There could be more than usual this year given the near certainty of a lockout on Dec. 1, which would also mean there are no Winter Meetings. This may be the only time everyone is in the same room this winter. The three weeks between now and Dec. 1 could be quite active.

We've already ranked the top 50 free agents and the top 25 trade candidates.

With that in mind, here are Tuesday's hot stove rumors.

The Mets have a meeting scheduled with two-time Cy Young winner Tarik Skubal, the clear top free agent avaiable this offseason, later this week, reports the New York Post. Skubal will meet with teams in California, presumably at the offices of agent Scott Boras. Owner Steve Cohen is expected to push for Skubal, according to SNY; president of baseball operations David Stearns has long been reluctant to sign big-money, long-term deals for pitchers, though pitchers of Skubal's caliber are rarely available. The Mets are also seeking first base help and bullpen reinforcements.

Nolan McLean and Christian Scott are the only locks for New York's 2027 rotation at the moment. Justin Hagenman, Sean Manaea, Kodai Senga, Zac Thornton, and Jonah Tong are next in line to make starts. Tylor Megill is expected back from Tommy John surgery early next year as well. The Mets have some depth but clearly need more impact.

The Rangers signed Corey Seager to a 10-year, $325 million deal prior to the 2022 season and it paid off quite handsomely with a World Series title in 2023 (Seager was the World Series MVP). With five years and roughly $155 million left on the deal, however, the Rangers appear ready to trade him.

The Rangers "intend to explore moving Seager at the general managers' meetings," reports The Athletic.

Seager, 32, was stellar from 2023 to 2025 in spite of a litany of injuries, averaging 6.2 WAR per season with a .294 average and 158 OPS+. He actually averaged 8.7 WAR per 162 games. Only the injuries kept him from making multiple serious MVP runs. Last season, though, was a step back. In 102 games, he hit .227/.312/.424 with 20 home runs, 51 RBI and 3.1 WAR.

The injury history keeps getting more extensive, too, as he's dealt with injuries to his thumb, hamstring, hip, other hamstring and back in the last four years.

With a lackluster, at best, shortstop class in free agency, now might be the perfect time for the Rangers to move on from Seager, assuming a deal can be worked out with the years and dollars left on his contract. Texas also has Sebastian Walcott, one of the top prospects in baseball, ready in the wings.

The Blue Jays are among the teams with interest in free agent second baseman Brandon Lowe, according to Sportsnet. Ernie Clement, Toronto's primary second baseman in 2026, crashed hard in the second half and has always fit better as a super utility man who plays regularly at different positions. Josh Smith is the team's other second base option.

Lowe would bring needed power to a team that finished dead last in home runs this past season and had just one player, Kazuma Okamoto (33), clear 15 home runs. Only four Blue Jays hit even 10 homers. Also, Toronto's lineup is mostly right-handed. Lowe would add power and also a lefty bat to provide balance. We ranked him as the 13th-best free agent available.

Adding rotation help is a "top priority," White Sox GM Chris Getz said during an MLB Network Radio interview on Tuesday. "We're going to exhaust the starter market, both free agency -- we'll take a look -- along with trades. That is our top priority," Getz said. He also confirmed that Grant Taylor, the team's postseason closer, will move into the rotation. Hagen Smith will remain in the bullpen.

The White Sox have solid enough rotation depth with Taylor joining Sean Burke, Luis Castillo, Anthony Kay, Davis Martin, David Sandlin, Noah Schultz, and perhaps Shane Smith and Drew Thorpe. There is room for improvement, though, particularly at the top. Chicago could use a bona fide ace, not that one is easy to acquire or abundantly available.

Add the Padres to the list of teams that have shown interest in Skubal, reports MLB Network. San Diego has a major need in the rotation -- Joe Musgrove, who hasn't made a start since the 2024 postseason, and Randy Vásquez are their only rotation locks -- and a new ownership group that seems willing to spend in its first offseason.

Skubal is far and away the offseason's best free agent. He is also a Scott Boras client, and, historically, top Boras clients take the largest contract offer regardless of team situation. That isn't to say the Padres are a bad situation. Not at all. They're a very good team that is in the postseason most years, plus they play in San Diego. Who doesn't love San Diego?

The Padres are also connected to several Japanese players, according to The Athletic. That includes MLB free agents Seiya Suzuki and Shota Imanaga, as well as players coming over from Nippon Professional Baseball, like third baseman Teruaki Sato.

Free agent lefty Adrian Morejon is drawing interest as a starting pitcher, but he prefers to remain in the bullpen, according to The Athletic. Even during the season, there were rumblings that Morejon would like to be given a chance to start in 2027. He came up through the minors as a starter and only remained in the bullpen because he was so dominant in a late-inning role.

We ranked Morejon as the offseason's second-best free agent on the idea that he would move into the rotation. The belief within the game is that he has a chance to be an excellent starter, not just a back-end piece. Either way, Morejon is a very good pitcher, and at 28, he's also one of the youngest free agents available. He should have several peak years remaining.

Although they are disinclined to pursue Tarik Skubal, the Cubs hope to add three established starting pitchers this offseason, reports The Athletic. Of the nine pitchers to make at least nine starts for Chicago in 2027, seven are free agents. Only Ben Brown, who recently had neck surgery (per ESPN), and Edward Cabrera, who had a 5.96 ERA and was demoted to the bullpen, remain.

Left-hander Justin Steele is expected back from UCL surgery next year, though he suffered a setback this summer and wasn't able to return as hoped in September. Others like Javier Assad and Jordan Wicks are depth options more than full-time rotation pieces. This free agent class offers plenty of second- and third-tier starters. That's where the Cubs are expected to shop.

Unlike last offseason, the Mets are not interested in a major overhaul despite their disappointing year, reports MLB.com. The Mets went 74-88 and finished in last place in 2026. The only silver lining to the disappointing season was the ability to give a long runway to rookies like Carson Benge and Nolan McLean, both of whom finished in the top four of the Rookie of the Year voting.

The Mets went 83-79 in 2025 and missed the postseason on the final day of the season. They made significant changes last winter, most notably jettisoning stalwarts Pete Alonso and Brandon Nimmo, then had a worse season in 2026. In September, president of baseball operations David Stearns admitted that much turnover created an "uncapped downside scenario," which is executive-speak for "there was too much risk."

Not surprisingly, the Braves are uninterested in moving former National League MVP Ronald Acuña Jr., according to MLB Network. It does feel like Atlanta's offense has gone stale and that they could move a core piece, but it won't be Acuña, nor is it likely to be Drake Baldwin, Michael Harris II, or Matt Olson. Ozzie Albies and Austin Riley are the more obvious players to move. Albies is entering the final season of his wildly team-friendly contract. Riley has six years remaining on his deal, which figures to make a move difficult.

On Monday, Red Sox chief baseball officer Craig Breslow declined to commit to Trevor Story as his starting shortstop next season. "We still have a ton of confidence in Trevor, but any decisions like that are going to take place between Trevor and me far before I speak to anything publicly," Breslow told reporters, including the Boston Globe. Boston has one of the top prospects in the minors in shortstop Franklin Arias, who is very likely to make his debut early next year. Arias could push Story into a utility role or off the team entirely.
