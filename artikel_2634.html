Two Backup Plans for the White Sox If They Miss Out on Freddy Peralta

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

‎No Chicago White Sox fan will like to hear this, but there's a growing expectation that the organization may miss out on the signing of Freddy Peralta. After the New York Mets reportedly signed Tarik Skubal on a nine-year, $450 million contract, the Toronto Blue Jays are now the favorites to sign Peralta after missing out on acquiring the two-time Cy Young Award winner. Along with Toronto, the San Diego Padres are also in the mix for Peralta.

The Blue Jays and Padres have the money to pay Peralta, unlike the White Sox, who might not match the $35 million+ per year deal projected for him. That now reduces the chances of the Sox getting the top-of-the-rotation ace in the coming days. But all hope is not lost for the White Sox getting another MLB-proven pitcher.

There are still some top-tier starters who have not made headlines in November but have the quality to match the likes of Skubal and Peralta. Michael King is one of them, but rumors are that the White Sox have been losing interest in him in recent days.

That now takes Chris Getz back to the drawing board on which high-profile, fundamentally different starting pitcher can be signed. One is Trevor Rogers and the other is the San Francisco Giants ace, Logan Webb. On paper, both represent aggressive moves to anchor the rotation alongside new starter Dustin May. However, while both are good pitchers, under the hood, one represents a free-agent trap, while the other is the exact type of workhorse Chicago's clubhouse needs.

Trevor Rogers, a former All-Star, has been linked with the White Sox since October after hitting free agency. In 30 starts for the Baltimore Orioles during the regular season, Rogers posted a 4.03 ERA with 157 strikeouts and 2.2 WAR in 169 2/3 innings. He allowed just 39 walks.

Sep 25, 2026; Bronx, New York, USA; Baltimore Orioles starting pitcher Trevor Rogers (28) pitches against the New York Yankees during the first inning at Yankee Stadium. Mandatory Credit: Brad Penner-Imagn Images

In 2025, Rogers posted a 1.81 ERA in 18 starts and had a 3.60 Expected ERA (xERA), but playing almost double what he did the previous season saw a drop in his usual level. Looking closer at his profile, there appear to be red flags in some areas, particularly having doubled his outings in 2026 compared to 2025.

‎Apart from that, Rogers' biggest flaw has been his vulnerability to right-handed hitters, which accounts for his lowest strikeout percentage against and his heavy reliance on fastball zone command. Furthermore, because Rogers is coming off a qualifying offer, signing him carries a hidden tax for the White Sox, who might not be willing to carry the financial burden.

Rogers turns 29 this November. He has an estimated free-agent market value between $20 million and $24 million for a one-year contract. He will be demanding at least a three-year deal from the White Sox, which would likely tally to $70 million-plus. But his checkered injury history might make the White Sox turn away.

Logan Webb offers the exact opposite Rogers could offer for the White Sox. Webb is already a frontline starter, a three-time All-Star with proven durability. For years, he was seen and treated as untouchable by San Francisco, but the organization has finally cracked the door open for trade talks for Webb.

From 2021 to 2025, the right-hander posted a 3.19 ERA across 159 starts, establishing himself as one of MLB's premier inning eaters. Even after a relatively down 2026 campaign by his standards, in which the Giants failed to make the playoffs and Webb dealt with right knee bursitis and a right middle finger blister, he still put up decent numbers.

‎In 27 starts last season, he posted a 4.31 ERA, 134 strikeouts, and a 1.170 WHIP across 160 2/3 innings. Webb is widely expected to have a healthy bounce-back in 2027 given his elite profile. And that makes him a more enticing pitcher to acquire than Rogers.

But unlike Rogers, Webb requires a large financial commitment, and he's already 30. He has a two-year contract with the Giants, $23 million in 2027 and $24 million in 2028. The figure matches Luis Castillo's $23-$24 million annual payroll slot, which has been vacated after he was traded away to the Colorado Rockies.

‎However, the caveat in getting a deal for Webb is that the White Sox will have to part ways with one or two of their top-tier prospects as part of the trade. The Giants would not sell their frontline ace for just a good prospect while contenders like the Atlanta Braves and Baltimore Orioles are looming as suitors. Chris Getz will likely offer an upper-minors talented pitcher to bring Webb to the South Side

‎The final verdict is that Freddy Peralta is the ideal Plan A for Getz, but to get him requires Jerry Reinsdorf's splashing over $100 million, and that isn't likely to happen. Maybe if Skubal had been signed by Toronto, that would be a different story, as the Mets would never go for Peralta after what happened in 2026. So, Getz is left with the next choice: either Rogers or Webb.

Neither player will be cheap, as they will be looking for a multi-year contract, and investing in the right one will be the biggest bet the White Sox have to take this offseason. Webb brings top-tier frontline starter experience, while Rogers offers complex risk but quality pitching metrics. Surely, Rogers will be cheaper than Webb, and the easiest choice is to go for the cheaper player, but the gamble could backfire next season.
