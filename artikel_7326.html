Bumble cozies up to Alix Earle in attempt to convert Gen Zers

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The influencer, who has previously stated her dislike of dating apps, appears in a two-minute video alongside Pete Davidson to encourage Gen Z users to give the platform a try.

Bumble is looking to appeal to Gen Z users as the demographic continues to ditch apps for real-life alternatives. User retention continues to be a problem for the dating app, which saw the total number of paying users decline from 2.5 million in Q2 2025 to 2.1 million in Q2 2026, according to an earnings statement.

The casting choices for the campaign are interesting ones as Earle has routinely rejected dating apps and Davidson is known for his string of ill-fated relationships with high-profile individuals, including a short-lived engagement to pop star Ariana Grande.

The video is self-aware regarding the history of its stars. For example, when Davidson plays an inconsiderate first date, Earle asks what Davidson does for work. He responds "Nothing much. I just kind of talk to camera.” Earle responds that she does something similar, to which Davidson replies, “yeah, but when I do it, it’s different,” a reference to controversial comments the comedian made regarding large influencer salaries and the amount of work involved.

Earle’s character is similarly aware of her past with dating apps. When confronted with the fact that she has publicly stated she hates dating apps, she responds: “I have said that publicly at least five different times on three different platforms. But sometimes I can be a little dramatic.”

Over the course of the sexual innuendo-laden spot, Earle convinces her fellow Gen Zers to try Bumble, showing off features such as searching for specific people. The women are impressed by the quality of men they find on the site, with one participant being impressed they all have bedframes.

The campaign was created in partnership with OBB Bolded and is supported by Bumble data that found 81% of singles between the ages of 18 and 25 are most interested in a relationship that adds value to the life they’re building.

Bumble has worked to attract more users to its app in recent months, including allowing anyone in a match to send the first message, a deviation away from the previous model where women had to send the first message, which was once a selling point for the brand. It also expanded the response window from 24 to 72 hours and introduced handles, a tool that allows verified users to more easily find one another on the app.

This isn’t the first time Bumble has turned to influencer marketing. In June, the app launched “Bee Line,” a talk show hosted by comedian and influencer Jake Shane that tackles dating anxiety.
