Klook’s AI Agent Handles the Admin, but Operators Want Help Selling

Klook’s K.ai doesn’t just advise small operators. It makes the changes for them, and an early user says it is already saving time. The bigger payoff, for operators and for Klook, could come from the tools still in development, such as pricing advice that helps them sell more.

Every morning, staff at MyProGuide used to check inventory by hand, spending at least 30 minutes on each product. The tour-guide and day-tour supplier, which has sold on Klook for more than a decade, now hands much of that work to a recently released AI agent built into Klook’s merchant portal.

Founder Jo Wong said the agent, K.ai, already helps his 35 staff review bookings, spot products nearing capacity and pull together complex data more quickly. What he wants next is harder to deliver: pricing recommendations, market price comparisons and booking-trend analysis, the tools that could help him sell more. For now, he is using K.ai to run daily operations rather than to drive bookings.

Klook has released K.ai, an AI agent built into its merchant portal that takes plain-language instructions from tour and activity operators — pulling booking reports, previewing and applying price changes, drafting product listings, and flagging shifts in performance using review and sales data. Co-founder and CTO Bernie Xiong said Klook is rolling it out in phases worldwide ahead of the year-end travel season and aims to reach all merchants by the end of the year, free for now, because many experiences merchants don't have dedicated teams for technology or back-office work. Klook said early users completed individual pricing and inventory updates 95% faster, and that merchants using K.ai recorded 3.4 times as many monthly bookings as those who didn't — a comparison drawn from a randomly selected early-rollout group in which merchants chose whether to use the tool, so it may partly reflect differences between the merchants themselves. The pitch lands in the least digitized part of travel: Klook, citing Euromonitor, said about 34% of experiences bookings are made online, against 66% for accommodation and 81% for flights, and small and midsize operators make up about 95% of the sector. MyProGuide founder Jo Wong, who used to spend at least 30 minutes a day checking inventory by hand for each product, said the agent now handles daily operations for his 35 staff. What he wants next — pricing recommendations, market price comparisons, and booking-trend analysis — is still being developed.
