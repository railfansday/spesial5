Ras Al Khaimah’s airport is betting on Wynn’s new integrated resort and other luxury hotels to bring in five-star travelers. For now, it is upgrading the terminal it has and holding the new one until airlines commit. The resort’s delay pushes any payoff to late 2027.

Ras Al Khaimah International Airport in the UAE is upgrading its existing passenger terminal and building a dedicated terminal for ultra-wealthy travelers. Both will be ready for the opening of the Wynn integrated resort. The resort’s opening has been delayed from spring 2027 to September.

“We are doing a soft uplift in our current passenger terminal by improving processes and using new technology as we increase our share of leisure passengers. Ras Al Khaimah International Airport is upgrading its existing terminal and building a dedicated private-jet terminal for ultra-wealthy travelers, with both ready for the opening of the Wynn casino resort, now pushed from spring 2027 to September 2027. CEO Manish Seth told Skift the soft uplift of the current terminal will lift capacity from 1.2–1.3 million passengers a year to 1.4–1.5 million, while construction on a planned new passenger terminal has not started and will wait until airline relationships materialize. Falcon Aviation, which runs a similar facility at Al Maktoum Airport in Dubai, is developing the ultra-high-net-worth terminal under a concession, with about 1,500 square meters of terminal space, 8,000 square meters of hangar, and apron room for four Boeing or Airbus corporate jets. The airport is courting full-service carriers for long-haul widebody flights to markets such as the UK and China, which Seth said could start in a year and a half to two years, as 75% to 80% of new hotel inventory in the emirate is five-star. The near-term picture is weaker: Seth said traffic will likely finish the year 25% to 28% below 2025 as Middle East carriers avoid Iranian airspace, Russia flights and European seasonal charters are not operating, and the airport no longer expects 1.5 million passengers in 2027. Its 2030 target of 1.8 million to 1.9 million passengers remains on track.
