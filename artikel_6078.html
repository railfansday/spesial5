Two major U.S. disruptions in four days add pressure on airlines and the FAA to show their systems can hold up heading into Thanksgiving.

United Airlines said it resolved a systemwide outage that lasted for more than an hour on Monday, just days after a nearly five-hour-long ground stop halted most travel in the U.S.

United said there was no indication that the outage was a cybersecurity issue.

“We have resolved a technology issue that delayed flight departures,” United said in a statement. “While some flights were delayed, there was no impact on flights in progress, or on United Express flights. There is no indication that this was a cybersecurity-related issue.”&n

United Airlines said Monday it was experiencing a systemwide technology outage affecting all departing flights, days after a nearly five-hour FAA ground stop on Friday forced close to 18,000 flight cancellations over the weekend. United said the issue did not affect flights in progress or United Express flights, and said it was troubleshooting. The carrier did not link the outage to the FAA ground stop, and other airlines did not appear to be affected. As of Monday evening, United had canceled 21 flights and had 418 delays, according to FlightAware, against an industry total of roughly 300 cancellations and more than 8,000 delays as airlines recovered. United had been one of the stronger performers coming out of the ground stop, canceling around 3% of flights over the weekend. Raymond James estimated the four largest U.S. airlines — American, Delta, United, and Southwest — could take as much as a $1 billion hit from Friday's ground stop. United CEO Scott Kirby said he met with Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy, FAA chief Bryan Bedford, and lawmakers in Washington about the impact on the company's operations, and has advocated for more funding to modernize air traffic control.
