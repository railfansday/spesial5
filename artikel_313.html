Yellowjackets Season 4 soundtrack: Every needle drop you need to know

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

As the story of a soccer team gone cannibal kicks off in 1996, Yellowjackets has plumbed the depths of '90s music to make one banger soundtrack season after season. This year, if you're looking to play (or headbang) along, we've got you covered with the needle drops of Season 4 as each episode hits.

But that's not all. Sure, you can use this collection to built a killer playlist. (Buzz, buzz!) We're also going to dive into a bit of what it means and why each song might have been selected for its particular Yellowjackets moment.

Let's kick things off with the Season 4 premiere:

Sample lyric: If I could find that Heina / And that Sancho that she's found / Well, I'd pop a cap in Sancho / And I'd slap her down

Season 4 opens with this hit song that had a total chokehold on teens in 1996. The lyrics sing of a hapless dude making messy decisions.

Fittingly, it plays while teen Jeff (Jack DePew) is getting high on the job at Taylor's Furniture Store with his doofy bestie Randy (Riley Baron). Despite some violent lyrics, "Santeria" is a upbeat tune that speaks to Jeff's laid-back attitude, post-graduation.

But once he sees the news of the Yellowjackets rescue on TV, the theme song cuts in and dramatically changes the episode's tone.

Sample lyric: It was cool / Nothing fire / Nothing broke / Keep simple / Nothing tired / Nothing old / Same as you / Same as you

For four seasons now, this song's cryptic lyrics have played as a pitch-perfect distillation of the show's female rage, rooted in lust, jealousy, and even love.

Sample lyric: My girl, my girl, don't lie to me / Tell me, where did you sleep last night?In the pines, in the pines / Where the sun don't ever shine / I would shiver the whole night through

After the opening credits, Yellowjackets picks up with the titular team in a hospital, where they are safe but look worried, likely about the world learning how it is they made it through their ordeal.

Playing over the sequence is Kurt Cobain's cover of the American folk song also known as "In the Pines," which is recognized as a murder ballad. The lyrics reflect a haunting story of homicide in the woods. The particular song has been covered by a variety of acts since its creation in the 1870s, including the father of Bluegrass Bill Monroe, Blues singer Lead Belly, and Dolly Parton.

Cobain famously covered the song on his MTV Unplugged appearance, on Nov. 18, 1993. So, it's a cover the Yellowjackets themselves might recognize. Moreover, his grunge rendition reflects the trauma and terror of the team's return to civilization.

This '90s song plays in the present timeline, but in a meaningful way. Showing up to Shauna's (Melanie Lynskey) door in episode 1, Melissa (Hilary Swank) presents a bottle of wine along with the news that she's moved in next door, and that she's called the press. Shauna looks on in shock asking, "What did you do?" as "The Freshmen" plays.

From 1997, this hit song from the Verve Pipe tells the tale of two young men, reflecting on the damage they've done to a shared lover. In this context, "The Freshmen" plays as if Shauna's subconscious is making an excuse ("we were merely freshmen").

Or perhaps, this song's use use here is meant to reflect Melissa's perspective. As the wounded lover, her story could show what might happen if that lover struck back with the poetic justice of reclaiming her narrative and her future.

Sample lyric: Words like violence / Break the silence / Come crashing in / Into my little world / Painful to me / Pierce right through me / Can't you understand / Oh my little girl?

Needle drops are often non-diegetic, meaning the characters within the film or TV show don't hear them. But "Enjoy the Silence" is played within the world of Yellowjackets, while teen Shauna (Sophie Nélisse) is listening to the radio with her mom (Gabrielle Miller). Still covered in bruises from the beating she took at the hands of her teammates in the wilderness, she's subjected to the salt in the wound of the wider world's speculations.

Her mom clicks from one station to the next, and each one features someone squawking about the team, including two radio DJs who make crass jokes about the team "eating each other." In response, Shauna's mom switches off the radio, and says, "I feel like a little quiet." But the quiet is too much for Shauna, so she clicks the radio on, and "Enjoy the Silence" plays.

The Depeche Mode song from 1990 is a love song, so it's fitting that it plays as a photo of Jackie (Ella Purnell) swings from the vehicle's rear-view mirror. Even out of the wilderness, in silence or song, or her mother's car, Shauna can't escape her first true love. But even more haunting is the following lyric: "Words are very unnecessary / They can only do harm." In Yellowjackets, this song, which plays while Shauna returns to her house before a captive audience of newscasters, speaks to how silence is how the team can protect themselves from the civilized world.

Sample lyric: Thirty-eight chrome to the dome / It's on. You better be strapped when you're rolling in my zone.

This hip-hop track plays when teen Shauna visits teen Jeff at the Woodbury Apartments, where he shares a place with Randy. The music begins as a non-diegetic tune, but then becomes part of the diegetic soundtrack of their bachelor pad, which also features a Tommy Boy poster and empty beer bottles.

It's a song that sets up the bro-hang environment that Shauna's crashing, which is reflected in how it stops as she enters the apartment. Sharp-eared Yellowjackets fans might remember this song also played in Season 2, episode 3 ("Digestif") when Jeff (Warren Kole) picked a fight at a gym with the late Kevyn Tan (Alex Wyndham).

Sample lyric: You're my angel, sticking on me / You're my angel, bringing me to my knees

This 1994 track from comes from the Los Angeles rock band that dog. In episode 2, it begins playing in the current timeline as Jeff approaches one of his billboards for Sadecki Furniture. It's been defaced by Shauna, who painted out letters so it reads "Sad Unit" next to Jeff's smiling face.

Then, the song continues as the episode leaps back to the wilderness, where Natalie (Sophie Thatcher) brings blankets to the pit. There, the spikes and Mari's (Alexa Barajas) remains have been cleared away, and Lottie (Courtney Eaton) and Shauna are being held within so they can't harm others or themselves. The song ends as Shauna asks if the radio call to civilization worked. When Nat assures her it did, she begins to weep, then yells, "We're going to be so totally fucked!" As Nat walks away looking spooked, "Angel" kicks back in.

In both instances, the song is directly tied to Shauna, who is certainly an angel of vengeances, no matter how petty.

Yellowjackets Season 4 debuted on Paramount+ with Showtime on Nov. 20, with new episodes weekly.
