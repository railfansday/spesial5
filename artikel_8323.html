Piolet d'Or Climber Bags New Route on Kangchung Shar

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Hiroyoshi Manome of Japan and his partners, Koji Shiogai and Subaru Takeda, are back in base camp after climbing a new route via the southwest pillar on Kangchung Shar in the Everest region.

The three climbed the line in four days and three nights from their base in Gokyo, Manome’s home team told ExplorersWeb. They summited in good weather and clear skies yesterday at noon. “The route was particularly interesting, with substantial technical rock climbing,” they explained.

The team believes it is the first ascent of this particular line and, as far as we know, they are right. The Himalayan Database has no record of any previous ascent of the southwest pillar.

Manome during the climb. Photo: Subaru Takeda

Kangchung Shar, located in the Gokyo Valley, has an altitude that varies between 6,030m to 6,103m, depending on the source. The Japanese team has shown that the peak still has plenty of adventurous terrain.

Manome explained before departure that their primary goal was the formidable 1,000m mixed north face, which has been climbed only once, with the east ridge as a backup.

“It will be a technical climb, which suits me just right, and there is little information available, so I think it will be an adventurous climb,” he said. The climber illustrated his plans with the hard-drawn sketch below. However, in the end, the climbers found a third option.

The Japanese team’s two potential routes. Photo: Hiroyoshi Manome

Kangchung Shar was first climbed in 1984 by Franci Knez of Slovenia, on a solo up the northwest ridge from Kangchung La. The 1,000m north face of Kangchung Shar was first climbed by  Zdenek Hak and Jaroslaw Bansky of the Czech Republic on their third try in 2021. No one has repeated it. After summiting, the Czech pair traversed to the south side for their descent.

Another Japanese team climbed neighboring Kangchung Nup (6,090m) in the spring of 2022. The American Alpine Journal describes Kangchung Shar and Kangshung Nup as “a bit like two Matterhorns side by side but two kilometers higher.”

An alpinist for 30 years, Hiroyoshi Manome focuses on technical, alpine-style climbs of 6,000m Himalayan peaks. He received a Piolet d’Or for the first ascent of the 2,200m south pillar of Kyashar (6,770m) in Nepal, with Tatsuya Aoki and Yasuhiro Hanatani. His latest big climb was another first ascent in November 2024, this time of the northeast ridge of Nepal’s Chhopa Bamare (6,109m), with Akitugu Konishi and Kotaro Kunimune.
