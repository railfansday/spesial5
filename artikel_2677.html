Game Preview: Suns seek continued road success against struggling Pelicans

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Who: Phoenix Suns (8-7) @ New Orleans Pelicans (5-13)

Where: Smoothie King Center — New Orleans, Louisiana

The road trip marches on as the Phoenix Suns head to the Big Easy to take on a New Orleans Pelicans team that is struggling to find its footing.

The Pelicans are 5-13 on the season, with four of those victories coming during a four-game winning streak in late October and early November. Since then, they've gone 1-9 over their last 10 games. In their most recent outing, they surrendered 50 points to Victor Wembanyama, who assured us he wasn't stat padding despite continuing to chase 50 with his team leading by 24 points in the fourth quarter.

But the Pelicans remain the conundrum they always seem to be. On paper, this is a formidable roster, one built around size and length. Three-point shooting, as expected, remains a challenge, but when you look at the personnel, there's plenty to like.

As usual, however, injuries continue to derail their potential. Trey Murphy III has appeared in only 10 games this season, starting center Yves Missi has played seven, and Dejounte Murray has suited up for nine.

Teams possessing length and defensive versatility have typically given the Suns problems over the past couple of seasons, but New Orleans has been the exception. Phoenix swept the four-game season series last year, winning by an average of 10.3 points. Granted, that margin was inflated by a 23-point victory last November, but the Suns consistently found ways to handle the Pelicans.

On the other side, Phoenix enters this matchup feeling pretty good about how its road trip has unfolded. Regardless of what happens over these final two games, the Suns have already secured a winning road trip by taking the first three. Win tonight, and you're heading into Houston feeling really good about yourself. Find a way to beat the Rockets on Wednesday, and you'll feel spectacular.

While the Suns' starting lineup still hasn't fully hit its stride, the team as a whole is beginning to look and play better. There's an opportunity to take another positive step tonight by getting quality minutes out of that starting unit against a Pelicans team that currently looks discombobulated. That's the kind of opportunity Phoenix needs to take advantage of.

Typically, I'd say I'm watching to see what the starters do, because we need that unit to gel as the season progresses if the Suns are going to become a complete basketball team capable of competing in the Western Conference. But tonight, I'm actually going to be keeping a closer eye on the bench.

While the Suns rank eighth in the NBA in bench scoring, the Pelicans rank fifth. There's talent on this New Orleans roster, of that there is no doubt. There's a reason why, whenever hypothetical trades start making the rounds, people look toward New Orleans to see which pieces they might be able to pry away. It's because the Pelicans have numerous players worth pursuing. And no, Dejounte Murray isn't one of them.

You have Saddiq Bey, Derik Queen, who could probably average a triple-double if he were starting, and Jordan Poole. I'm not suggesting the Suns should go out and acquire any of those players, but they represent quality offensive talent that can put together a run in a hurry if you're not prepared.

So while my eyes will naturally gravitate toward how effective and efficient the starting unit looks, it's the defensive intensity of Phoenix's second unit that I'll be particularly interested in watching tonight.

The Pelicans possess one of the worst defenses in the NBA, ranking 27th in defensive rating and 29th in points allowed per game. Simply put, they're going to give you opportunities to score.

So how do the Suns win this game? Beyond making sure rebounding doesn't become an issue, as it seemingly does every night, Phoenix needs to take advantage of what New Orleans gives them. The opportunities will be there. It's a matter of recognizing them, capitalizing on them, and making a struggling defense pay. Efficiency is your friend. The Pelicans allow 48.7% shooting for their opponents. Go ahead and shoot 50% tonight.

The road trip has been laden with subpar competition thus far, and to the Suns' credit, they've stepped up and taken care of business against every opponent. In fact, of the 144 minutes played on this road trip, Phoenix has trailed for only 13 of them.

I expect that trend to continue tonight, perhaps without the same level of dominance when it comes to controlling the game, but with the same result.
