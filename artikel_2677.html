Rutgers stuns Penn State with touchdown, 2-point conversion as time runs out [updated]

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

STATE COLLEGE – Anthony Donkoh slowly sat his 6-5, 324-pound frame into the chair in the Penn State interview room.

He looked stunned. He looked exhausted. He looked devastated. He looked confused, trying to digest what happened moments earlier.

"It sucks," Donkoh said. "I don't really have anything else to say. We have to move forward. We have to learn from today. We just came up short."

Prohibitive underdog Rutgers scored a touchdown on a lateral with no time left and then connected on a 2-point conversion pass, shocking Penn State 25-24 and ending its 18-game losing streak to the Nittany Lions Saturday at Beaver Stadium.

It was the Scarlet Knights' first win over Penn State since 1988. The Lions (3-5 Big Ten, 6-5) squandered a 16-0 second-quarter lead.

"It starts with coaching," Matt Campbell said, shouldering the blame for the brutal loss. "It starts with the head football coach. Obviously I didn't have us prepared to play our best football game on Senior Day. That really hurts me. That puts a lot of responsibility on my shoulders. It starts there."

Trailing 24-17, Rutgers (2-6, 3-8) took over at its 33-yard line with 3:28 left and took advantage of two defensive pass interference penalties. The second one was called against cornerback Audavion Collins in the end zone on fourth-and-8 from the 9 with four seconds left.

On the next play, A.J. Surace threw a short pass to running back Antwan Raymond, who was grabbed by linebacker Kooper Ebel. Raymond then flipped the football back to wide receiver Ben Black III, who took it into the end zone.

"That was obviously gut-wrenching," linebacker Tony Rojas said. "You never really see those things. When it happens, you're in awe. It's on all of us. We have to rally to the ball and not put it in the hands of the refs to call it a touchdown."

Campbell said he thought it was possible that an official had blown the whistle to stop the play before the lateral.

"We thought maybe there was a whistle," he said. "It felt like that. At the end of the day, you can't leave it to chance. Unfortunately, we left it to chance. If you leave it to chance, you get a chance to get burned.

"That has nothing to do with officiating. That has everything to do with our inconsistencies."

Rutgers coach Greg Schiano, not surprisingly, decided to go for the win. Surace then tossed a pass to running back Clay Thevenin, who easily scored the game-winning points in front of shocked Penn State fans.

"It was the right play for them against the defense we were in," Rojas said.

The game was reminiscent of Penn State's 24-20 loss to Wisconsin in September at home when the Lions blew a 17-0 lead and allowed the game-winning TD in the final 75 seconds.

They controlled most of the game until the Knights went on an 19-play, 75-yard drive that lasted 9:38. Raymond capped it with a 13-yard TD that cut it to 24-17 with 10:19 to go.

Penn State had the ball two more times and gained only one first down, enabling Rutgers to rally. Its season-long second-half woes continued.

"The offense goes down and scores in the third quarter (to give the Lions a 24-10 lead) and you feel like you're in a great spot," Campbell said. "You give up two long drives. You never had the ability to put the final blow in. That's continued to be a challenge for this team. We continue to work on it, but we haven't figured it out."

The Lions have been successful on fourth down most of the season. In the second quarter, they had six snaps from inside the Rutgers 10 and couldn't score. Rocco Becht was stopped inches short of the goal line on a quarterback sneak on fourth-and-1.

Even though they scored a safety on the next play, they lost five points.

One series later, Cam Wallace was stopped for no gain on fourth-and-1 at the Knights' 29.

"The goal-line and short-yardage (offense) hurt us big time," Donkoh said. "We have to do a better job in those situations because we left points on the board."

Becht completed 14-of-21 passes for 233 yards and one touchdown for Penn State, Cam Wallace carried 14 times for 95 yards and one score and Chase Sowell caught six passes for 123 yards and one TD.

For Rutgers, Raymond rushed 23 times for 101 yards and two touchdowns and Surace, playing with three fractured ribs, passed for 178 yards and the game-winning score.

Immediately afterwards, Rojas and Donkoh were crying on their way to the locker room.

"I wouldn't say it's frustration," Donkoh said. "It's just disappointing because we had opportunities to close it out."
