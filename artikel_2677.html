Warriors feeling psychological boost of having injured Steph Curry around team

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Warriors feeling psychological boost of having injured Steph Curry around team originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

SAN FRANCISCO – Warriors guard Brandon Williams scrimmaged Tuesday and is expected to return Wednesday against the Sacramento Kings after missing the last six games because of a mild left hamstring strain. Williams might as well have been listed with an illness, too.

Williams was injured in fewer than two minutes against the New Orleans Pelicans on Nov. 9. Steph Curry, in only 21 minutes, scored 25 points on 6-of-10 shooting, 4-of-7 from 3-point range and a perfect 9-of-9 from the free-throw line in a 138-87 win. The Warriors' 51-point beatdown put the cherry on top of a 6-0 homestand that extended their win streak to eight straight games.

The Warriors' perfect homestand ended on a Monday. The next Monday, in a game without injured Williams and Curry, the Warriors lost by 21 points in New Orleans to a Pelicans team without Trey Murphy III.

Curry grabbed at his right side in the opening minutes of the Warriors' five-game road trip in an eventual seven-point loss against the Houston Rockets. Curry was ruled out the next day for the rest of the road trip with a right abdominal oblique strain. His two-week re-evaluation is expected within the coming days.

Golden State lost its next four games by a combined 109 points.

Williams traveled with the team and watched the offensive struggle from the bench. The same can be said for his view of the Warriors scoring 88 points in their fifth straight game below 100 points, yet found a way to beat the Boston Celtics by one on Sunday night.

"Yeah, I ain't gonna lie, s–t's been tough watching that," Williams said Tuesday at Chase Center after practice.

Watching the eyesore reminded Williams of why he's here, and why he has been so valued early this season.

"I know exactly what I need to do. Just watching from afar, you see a little bit more," Williams said. "The offense gets stagnant at times, and I know that's what they brought me in here for – to keep it rolling, keep it going, keep everybody organized and stuff. So hopefully we see that next time on the court for sure."

Even with Curry, Williams was going to be missed. Without the two-time NBA MVP, Williams' importance has been amplified as a playmaker who has a scorer's mindset and can make defenses react to him from beating them off the dribble.

Williams scored 20 points when Curry missed his first game to rest this season. He's the Warriors' fourth-leading scorer at 10.4 points per game, but ranks second in points per 36 minutes (23.3).

The Warriors will take any offensive help they can find until Curry is back, and Williams is sure to bring life to a unit that has looked listless as of late.

While Williams will be an instant boost to the Warriors, Curry already was one against the Celtics, despite his injury.

Curry was at Warriors shootaround in San Antonio after his injury but flew home the same day hours before the game and missed the massive FAA flight interruptions. He's the sun in the Warriors' solar system, as coach Steve Kerr often says, and that includes emotionally as well.

The Warriors ached for Curry's return right when he went down. His presence was sorely missed outside of box score stats during the rest of a historically bad road trip, and it was again felt rooting from the sidelines Sunday night as the underdog Warriors upset the Celtics.

"Definitely helpful," Kerr said Tuesday. "I mean, he's just always a breath of fresh air. And the humor and the confidence that he has, you saw it the other night. Draymond [Green] hits a three from the corner, he's flying all over the place on the bench.

"There's just a different vibe when Steph is in the building, and it's good to have him here, even if he's not playing."

Curry got out of his chair and turned to the Warriors' bench in celebration when Green, hours after going at it all day on social media with former Sixth Man of the Year and 17-year NBA vet Eddie Johnson, opened the game with a three from the right corner.

Curry threw three fingers up in unison with teammates when Green drained his second three of the first quarter, again letting it fly from the right corner.

And when Curry's longest-tenured teammate made his third three from the right corner in the first six minutes of the game, he almost melted into his chair and fell onto the court.

Sunday's win that snapped the Warriors' five-game losing streak started with Green making threes, and ended with him stopping Jayson Tatum's game-winning attempt. Green and Kerr shared an extended, emotional hug. Curry waited behind for Green and embraced him as the two enjoyed another countless moment of their competitive spirits intertwined.

Rookie Yaxel Lendeborg needed Curry in every way during the Warriors' skid. He needed Curry's gravity that made the start of his career so smooth. He also needed his calming presence that inspires confidence.

Curry wasn't there when Lendeborg gave everyone an inside view into his mind and said he's "mentally f–ked up" after the Warriors lost by 38 points against the Spurs the game after losing their superstar. He wasn't there as Lendeborg hung his head in New Orleans, or when he looked lost in Dallas and Memphis.

But Curry was there Sunday in San Francisco sharing knowledge with Lendeborg, who liked a post about the moment on Instagram.

"Yeah, it's huge," Kerr said about Lendeborg being around Curry. "And I've said this, but I love the internal leadership we have on this team. Our vets have been fantastic. Draymond's leadership every day has been brilliant. Al and Steph are more the quiet leaders by example, but pull a guy aside and talk to him.

"Our veteran players have provided such great leadership for us this year, and it's really helpful, especially for the young guys, Yaxel in particular, given how much he's playing."

This is all new to Williams. His prior teams, the Portland Trail Blazers and Dallas Mavericks, saw stars like Damian Lillard, Anthony Davis, Kyrie Irving and others miss time due to injuries. Curry is different, and so is the culture he has built for the Warriors.

Having him on the bench and around the team is personally helpful to Williams, and Curry's backup point guard is impressed by the Warriors' resolve in recovering from being hit in the mouth.

"It helps a lot, specifically for me," Williams said. "Those teams that I've been on, like, Kyrie went down, the morale around the team just went down. It's the complete opposite case here. Everybody's the next man up. I think the thing out here, what is it, Strength In Numbers? I think that's a great motto to live by, and it's perfect for us right now.

"Everybody's got to step up, whether it's Gui [Santos], Will [Richard], Graham [Ike], [Malevy Leons], all of us got to step up. It's a collective group effort."

Narratives have run rampant since the Warriors' 2022 championship season. The numbers still tell an obvious story. The Warriors have gone 39-53, a 42.4 win percentage, without Curry in the regular season the last five years. They're 2-5 this season, and 2-6 if you include their loss in Houston.

Curry was in attendance for the Warriors' first loss without him this season, a hard-fought nine-point defeat in Phoenix on the second night of a back-to-back after playing the night before in San Francisco. He went wild on the sidelines during his second rest game as Lendeborg unleashed 37 points in a home win over the LA Clippers and could hardly contain himself, bringing energy to the bench as the Warriors got back in the win column against the Celtics.

Greatness is contagious, even if it's felt in different ways than usual.
