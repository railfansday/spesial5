Scouts: Nets biggest problem is roster construction

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Things are bad in Brooklyn. The team isn't just 3-10, tied for the second worst in the Eastern Conference and fans are up in arms, justifiably. After two years of tanking and a lot of hope, the Nets aren't just losing. They look disorganized even unprepared. The head coach and general manager are facing the brunt of fans' frustration with calls for both to be fired. That, at the moment, seems unlikely.

So, Brian Lewis spoke to a number of NBA scouts. What, he asked, is the problem? The answer for almost all of them was poor roster construction which, of course, is Marks's responsibility. Of course, as (most) people inside will tell you, this was always supposed to be a development season and working out rotations should be expected. Still, tell that to fans.

"The lineups don't really fit," one Eastern Conference scout told Lewis, writing for New York Post Sports+. "It's like super jumbo right now. Sometimes it's super small, you have like three guards out there. The roster just isn't very clean. … There's small guards or some big guards, but they're guards who need to ball. The lack of shooting on the roster, that can be an issue."

Part of the issue, Lewis writes, is the team's recent spate of injury and illness. It's handcuffed Jordi Fernandez's line-up decisions. As Lewis notes, Monday's game against the 76ers marked just the fourth time Egor Dëmin, Mikel Brown Jr. and Nolan Traoré were all available, and they never had shared the court at the same time.

"It probably eventually gets to … they need to start Brown and Dëmin. So, yeah, eventually that's what it has to get to," a veteran scout said. "The back end is what the back end is. But yeah, it's tough when you have five firsts and it looks like two sure misses and probably three, more than likely three misses."

Indeed, the Nets 2025 draft haul was a frequent refrain. Of the five — picks at Nos. 8, 19, 22, 26 and 27 — there's a lot of complaint about both the selections and use. Scouts agree, as Lewis wrote.

Other than Dëmin and Traoré, the other three have yet to prove themselves as viable NBA rotation pieces. Danny Wolf has shown improvement this season, starting four games as an emergency two-guard. Ben Saraf and Drake Powell are out of the rotation and in the G League.

One scout suggested that Demin, taken at No. 8 in 2025, is not fulfilling the Nets hopes.

"There's not a lot of identity," the scout said. "In terms of guys that I like, I think Traoré actually has gotten better. He was pretty rotten last year. I know he was young, but he couldn't handle [the] physicality of the game. He couldn't handle ball pressure. He couldn't throw it upcourt. But he got better.

"Egor, he had a really good preseason, but it hasn't really translated. His shooting has not really shown that much. You just wonder. It's great to have size, but if you can't break the paint as a lead guard that makes it kind of hard."

The same scout also questioned whether Powell, Saraf and even Wolf are going to work out.

"And the other ones, the other first rounders of that five, you wonder are they NBA players. Saraf? Powell? And then Wolf. They probably should have consolidated that and tried to move up. I don't know [with] who.

Marks & Co. did try to move up, but ultimately wound up settling for quantity. Just last week, the Nets extended the Flatbush Five through 2027=28 so they've not given up on development. As for Brown Jr., the scout likes what he sees so far and isn't bothered by the so-called "babying" of the 20-year-old. Nor, he says, it is that unusual.

"I like Brown. I mean, he's been wild, but he's showing [ability]. He at least has something. There were four guards [drafted] back-to-back-to-back [in 2026], [Keaton] Wagler, him, [Darius] Acuff and [Kingston] Flemings. He's been in-and-out. He's on a minutes limit, which is actually kind of good sometimes to work your way into it. I saw Flemings start the other night, and he played 17 minutes, so that's fine."

As for the team's two veteran bigs, it's become clear that Michael Porter Jr. is the big contributor … and that he won't be around after the February trade deadline.

"It's a tough watch," the Eastern Conference scout said. "Michael is the engine to all of it. He's the hardest guy to guard in terms of actions, ball movement. He's so big, it's hard to switch on him. It's a challenge to guard that dude.

"There's an action where he's like in the paint and [curls], a Spanoulis action. It's a really hard thing to guard, because if they switch a small on him, he's so big, he shoots it over him. And also based on body position, they can throw a lob over the top. Even the SLOB [sideline out-of-bounds] they run, he'll swing. He's just hard to guard because he's so big and his release point is so high. … And he's good at the late air passes to people. And he has good chemistry with [Day'Ron] Sharpe because Sharpe sets good screens, so he opens it up. And then if X5 [the opposing center] shows up, he has a drop down to Sharpe, so you get rewarded."

The plan, it appears, is to use MPJ to not to secure a superstar to make everything well and good, but to garner yet more assets so they can outbid anyone for a big player next year. Will it work? Seems risky but no rebuild is without a lot of it … and at the moment, it's a very mixed bag.

As for the team's only All-Star, Julius Randle, the scout is a bit mystified about how he's being used.

"Julius actually looked good earlier in the year. And they were doing some of the inverted pick-and-roll stuff. Doesn't really seem like he's in a great rhythm right now," the Eastern Conference scout said. "And they're not really running a lot of actions for him. They were running [stuff]. But they were great in the preseason. Which also tells you, yeah, it's the preseason. There's a reason."

That's on the head coach of course. The same scout also takes a lot at the team's atrocious defense, not untypical for such a young team — youngest in the NBA again this season. Still, it can't be ignored.

"How many guys do they have that can actually guard?" one scout asked. Not a lot.

Fernández has largely eschewed playing drop coverage for more aggressive defenses designed to pressure the ball and create turnovers, having the Nets play up at the level of the screen. Fernández has correctly asserted that the best championship contenders play that way, leaning into the modern possession game.

Anyone thinking that Joe Tsai will make a dramatic move before season's end. That's the general impression NBA executives have shared. Marks of course has been GM for 11 years, the third longest in the NBA but there's every indication the owner and his GM are on the same page — the long-term rebuild defined by a strategy build around smart drafting and development. Building organically for a brighter day.

There are however rumors of Nets long-term planning beyond Marks. Not surprising of course but don't expect much in terms of detail for a while.
