Justice Department is suing Lyft for $53 million over alleged disability discrimination

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The lawsuit alleges Lyft and its drivers refuse rides to passengers with service dogs and stowable wheelchairs

On Monday, the Justice Department filed a lawsuit against Lyft $LYFT, accusing the ride-hailing company of discrimination toward riders with disabilities, among them people traveling with service animals and stowable wheelchairs.

The suit, filed in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, seeks $53 million on behalf of people with disabilities who lodged complaints with Lyft or the department, the department said. The department is demanding that Lyft pay a civil penalty as well.

The department contends that Lyft broke Title III of the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990, a provision that prohibits private transportation companies from discriminating on the basis of disability. The complaint says Lyft and its drivers turn away passengers who have disabilities and then bill cancellation fees to riders who were denied rides. It also says Lyft declines to modify its policies where needed, including by denying riders with mobility disabilities the option to sit in the front seat.

The department wants a court order compelling Lyft to halt the alleged conduct, bring its policies into line with the ADA and provide legal instruction to employees and drivers.

"Individuals with disabilities should be able to rely on rideshare services free of discrimination," Acting Assistant Attorney General Jesus A. Osete of the Justice Department's Civil Rights Division said in a statement. "We intend to uphold the ADA's promise that people with disabilities are entitled to equal access in every part of public life, including essential transportation services."

U.S. Attorney Craig H. Missakian for the Northern District of California pointed to the government's case against Uber $UBER. "We take these allegations of misconduct seriously and, like our case against Uber for similar discriminatory conduct, we will devote all the resources necessary to make things right," Missakian said in a statement.

Lyft disagrees with the allegations, according to Reuters. "We have long-standing policies requiring drivers to accommodate riders who travel with service animals or use foldable mobility devices, and drivers who violate these policies face serious consequences, including permanent deactivation," the company said in a statement, according to Reuters. Lyft said it cooperated with the Justice Department during its review.

The government sued Uber last year under the same federal law, seeking $125 million for people subjected to alleged discrimination who had complained to Uber or the Justice Department, according to Reuters. Uber rejected every one of the accusations.
