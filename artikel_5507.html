Zcash Upgrade Could Triple Transaction Speed as ZEC Faces Crypto Governance Turmoil

Zcash is preparing to triple transaction speed as its NU7 network upgrade approaches November 5, but governance tensions and falling prices are testing investor confidence.

The Zcash upgrade will reduce target block times from 75 seconds to 25 seconds, making transaction confirmations significantly faster. The activation is planned for block height 3,543,405, according to the network’s deployment schedule.

Zcash Network Upgrade 7 mainnet activation height is being set for Block 3,543,405 (~November 5th 2026) pic.twitter.com/C4D5Dch61l

The technical changes arrive as Zcash faces a separate debate over its community-grants funding system. ZEC has also suffered recent price weakness, leaving traders weighing network improvements against growing uncertainty over its future direction.

The upcoming Zcash upgrade introduces several changes beyond faster block production. Under ZIP 218, the network will target a new block every 25 seconds, down from 75 seconds. That could reduce the waiting time for three transaction confirmations from nearly four minutes to around 75 seconds.

The upgrade also introduces limits on shielded transaction activity, designed to improve network efficiency and protect wallets against spam.

Meanwhile, ZIP 235 will remove 60% of transaction fees from circulation and place them into a reserve. The mechanism is intended to support future mining rewards as block subsidies decline.

Node operators must also upgrade their software to remain on the network’s consensus chain. at present, Zcash Foundation has released Zebra 7.0.0, which supports NU7 on mainnet and includes additional synchronization and security improvements.

The Zcash Upgrade comes as the project faces renewed debate over how its development ecosystem should be funded. Zcash co-founder Sean Bowe recently proposed ending the automatic allocation of 8% of newly issued ZEC to the Zcash Community Grants program.

Bowe said that the program already holds approximately $125 million after existing commitments, enough to fund years of development without continuing automatic inflows.

His proposal would redirect the allocation toward a reserve supporting future mining rewards. The committee would retain its existing funds and continue distributing grants.

The proposal does not directly cite that controversy, instead focusing on the size of the grants treasury and long-term funding sustainability.

ZEC has also faced selling pressure ahead of the upgrade. Recent market coverage placed the token near $1,135 after an approximately 11% decline, highlighting the disconnect between improving network fundamentals and short-term price performance.
