Democrats flipped 7 Senate seats in 2026 — Republicans held Kansas for 94 years

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The Senate seats Democrats flipped in 2026 span seven states, from Kansas to Maine, ending Republican holds that in some cases lasted decades

The last time a Democrat had won a U.S. Senate seat in Kansas was 1932. That streak ended in the November 3, 2026 midterm elections. It also closed out a Republican hold on the seat that The New York Times put at 94 years.

Kansas was one of seven Senate seats Democrats flipped in 2026. Those wins gave the party control of the chamber, according to The New York Times. The seven states were Kansas, Texas, North Carolina, Maine, South Carolina, Ohio and Iowa. Republicans held each of those seats going into the vote.

In North Carolina, Republicans had held the seat for 18 years. Democrats last won a Senate seat in the state in 2008. Republicans then won five consecutive Senate elections there.

In Maine, the defeated incumbent was Susan Collins, a five-term Republican senator. Her loss ended 30 years of Republican tenure in the seat.

Ohio's seat returned to Democratic hands after an appointed Republican had held it on a temporary basis. The Times described the Iowa result as a "battleground shift." Democrats won there in an upset that secured their route to a Senate majority.

The Times did not report how long Republicans held the seats in Texas, South Carolina, Ohio and Iowa.

The national count was not complete when these results were reported. In a tally timestamped 10:16 p.m., The Associated Press had called 33 of the 35 Senate seats up for election. Democrats stood at 54 seats in that count. The figure combined 20 races won, two races in which Democrats were leading and 34 seats carried over. It also included independents who caucus with Democrats.

Republicans stood at 44 seats in the same AP count. That total came from 13 races won and 31 seats carried over. The AP showed a gain of seven seats for Democrats and a loss of seven for Republicans.

Those totals were a snapshot, not a final result. The two races in which Democrats led had not been called. The 54 and 44 figures add up to 98 seats, leaving two of the Senate's 100 seats unaccounted for in that count.
