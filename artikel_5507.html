The cost of manual handoffs: Where homebuilder margins slip

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Connected homebuilder data can help reduce pricing gaps, contract errors and the rework that puts pressure on homebuilder margins

Homebuilders have spent years digitizing individual departments, from plan design to purchasing and sales management. Yet moving information between those departments often still depends on people. Changing a plan, option or price can require several teams to update their systems, interpret what changed and confirm everyone is working from the same information.

That repeated work carries a cost. An outdated price can reach the sales center, a buyer’s selection can be missed in the addendum, or a discrepancy can delay a home’s progress toward construction. Across multiple homes and communities, those gaps can quietly erode homebuilder margins.

Higharc and ECI built their integration to help builders navigate those handoffs. By connecting the home a buyer configures in Higharc with the pricing and contract information managed in ECI MarkSystems, the companies address margin leakage that occurs when departments must recreate and reconcile the same information.

“Most builders don’t have one version of a home; they’ve got three,” said Jad Buckman, Director of Business Development at Higharc. The architectural team maintains the plan. Marketing maintains the version buyers see online or in the sales center. Another version takes shape when employees enter the selected home and options into the ERP. When a plan, option or price changes, employees must decide where that change needs to go and ensure it arrives accurately. In a webinar poll cited by the Higharc team, 75% of respondents said builders touched two to three systems for each product or price change. Some reported touching six or more. The financial consequences accumulate. An option might sell at last quarter’s price. A selection discussed with the buyer might never make it into the addendum. A mismatch between the contract and the buyer’s expectations can lead to rework or costs the builder absorbs to make things right. “None of these costs show up as one big line item,” Buckman said. “It’s like death by 1,000 cuts.” Delays create another exposure. Waiting for information to be entered or corrected can keep a home from becoming start-ready and add carrying costs. Builders also carry the labor cost of those handoffs. Employees spend time rekeying information across disconnected systems, limiting how much additional contract volume the back office can support without adding staff. Why Higharc and ECI MarkSystems built the bridge The push to connect the platforms came from builders themselves. Customers working across both systems designed and sold homes in Higharc. MarkSystems managed pricing, purchasing and downstream operations. People in the middle moved information between them. The integration gives that handoff a defined path. MarkSystems serves as the financial and operational backbone, maintaining pricing, lot information, customer data and contract records. Higharc serves as the design and sales system, where plans are developed, options are configured and buyers experience the home in real time. “Builders don’t think about their businesses in terms of software platforms. They think about designing homes, selling them and getting them built. Technology should make that process easier, not create more work between departments,” said John Wood, Lead Product Manager of Higharc. “By connecting Higharc and MarkSystems, we’re removing the friction between design, sales and operations so builders can focus on what matters: selling and building homes.” That division of responsibility is central to the collaboration. Builders can continue using each platform for its intended work while reducing the manual effort required to keep information between them. For builders evaluating a homebuilder ERP integration, that connection between departments is as consequential as each system’s capabilities. Carrying the buyer’s decisions into the contract The workflow starts with pricing and lot data moving from MarkSystems into Higharc. That includes base plan, elevation and option pricing, along with relevant lot information. During implementation, builders validate the information across their plans to ensure homes are presented and priced as intended. Buyers can then configure options through Higharc’s interactive 3D Showroom and see the associated pricing maintained in MarkSystems. Once the buyer is ready to turn the configured home into a contract, the sales rep can create the MarkSystems contract record from Higharc. Buyer information, the configured home, selected structural options and their prices move into that record. Instead of entering 15 or 20 options separately while working with a buyer, the sales team can transfer those selections with a button click. From there, downstream processes continue within MarkSystems. Finish selections and addendum workflows vary by builder, so the connection supports existing processes. The practical change is fewer touches between the buyer’s decision and the contract record. Connected homebuilder data reduces the need to re-enter selections, helping teams avoid missed options and incorrectly entered prices. Connecting product data with operational data Connecting the platforms has also prompted builders to examine the information inside them. For the first beta customer, implementation prompted a closer review of how data was organized in the ERP. Bringing that information together with product data revealed opportunities for greater efficiency and better business control. That is a meaningful part of connected homebuilder data. A plan, its available options and pricing need to relate consistently across departments for information to move reliably. Manual processes can obscure inconsistencies because employees repeatedly interpret and reconcile them. Connecting the systems brings those relationships into focus. By eliminating manual handoffs and keeping pricing and selections aligned across systems, the integration reduces administrative work, minimizes the risk of errors and accelerates the path from home selection to contract. Sales teams can move buyers forward with confidence, while back-office teams gain capacity to support more contracts without adding overhead. Building a more connected foundation Buyer expectations add urgency to shortening sales-to-contract turnaround. Consumers can configure other purchases online and see pricing in real time. Builders, however, can hesitate to offer that visibility when prices change, and updates must be maintained across multiple systems. Connecting the buyer experience in Higharc with pricing maintained in MarkSystems helps address that concern. The opportunity also extends beyond individual sales. Once plans and pricing are validated for an initial community, that work can support rollout to additional communities. The longer-term value will depend partly on how quickly builders can bring more plans and communities live without recreating the same administrative burden. The buyer experience matters, too. Showing available configurations alongside current pricing can provide greater transparency and help avoid surprises later in the contract process. “In a market where every point of margin matters, builders need to look beyond material costs and construction efficiency. There’s significant opportunity in eliminating the administrative work that happens between selling a home and getting it into production. Connecting design, pricing and contract workflows is a critical part of that,” said Buckman. For builders, the broader question is how much repeated work accompanies each new sale, plan or community. Protecting homebuilder margins means examining those handoffs alongside each department’s performance. A homebuilder ERP integration that connects design, pricing and contract information helps builders reduce that work as the business grows.
