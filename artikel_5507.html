Novartis CEO warned U.S. biotech curbs on China could slow drug development

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Vas Narasimhan said Treasury's proposed investment limits would hinder moving technologies between U.S. and Chinese operations

Chris J. Ratcliffe / Bloomberg via Getty Images

Novartis CEO Vas Narasimhan told Bloomberg TV on Friday that U.S. proposals to restrict biotech investment in China might slow how quickly new drugs get developed.

"The current approach would limit our ability to bring these technologies into our U.S. factories for U.S. patients and vice versa, U.S. into China," Narasimhan said. "I am hoping more sensible approaches will come out of these discussions."

"Finding a new treatment for cancer or heart disease is not a national security issue," he said. Narasimhan added that patients in China and the U.S. alike ought to have access to new discoveries.

The Treasury Department proposed in October that biotech join the sensitive technologies already covered by caps on investment tied to foreign adversaries, according to Bloomberg. The rules would broaden an existing program under the Comprehensive Outbound Investment National Security, or COINS, Act, which currently covers artificial intelligence, semiconductors and quantum computing.

Foreign drugmakers such as Novartis, headquartered in Switzerland, could be covered by the restrictions whenever they employ people or run operations in the U.S., according to Bloomberg. The U.S. ranks first among markets for Western drugmakers, with China in second place. China is also supplying a rising share of novel drugs and technologies.

Novartis rolled forward its mid-term guidance on Thursday at its Meet Novartis Management event in London, the company said. It expects net sales to grow at a 5% constant-currency compound annual rate from 2026 to 2031.

Novartis has signed a licensing agreement with Chinese developer Abogen Biosciences for an mRNA-encoded autoimmune therapy worth up to $7.8 billion. The deal was announced Oct. 2.
