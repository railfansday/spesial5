AI inference servers shrink to fit the air-cooled racks enterprises already run

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Power-efficient inference chips are becoming the entry price for enterprises that want to run AI on hardware they own.

As AI shifts from building models to running them every day, costly graphics processing unit racks and the liquid cooling they demand keep many companies renting compute instead. That gap is opening room for challengers to the incumbent chipmakers. Axelera AI B.V. built its business at the edge with low-power chips and edge AI development tools, and it is now taking that technology into data center infrastructure, according to Manuel Botija (pictured), vice president and head of product at Axelera.

“Enterprises cannot own their own infrastructure. They’re locked out because of the cost and the power,” Botija said. “We are enabling them now to own it. It’s not that we are helping the world save power or save money — which we are — but it’s that we are unlocking use cases that were simply not possible before.”

Botija spoke with theCUBE Research’s John Furrier and Rebecca Knight at SC26, during an exclusive broadcast on theCUBE, SiliconANGLE Media’s livestreaming studio. They discussed how power-efficient inference chips are heading for the data center and how enterprises locked out by the cost and power of GPU racks can own their own AI infrastructure instead of renting compute. (* Disclosure below.)

Axelera’s Europa chip has been shipping since September. Ahead of SC26, the company unveiled Titania, a third-generation architecture that scales the design up to a 400-watt chip. The resulting six-kilowatt server is built to perform like leading data center GPUs while fitting into the existing 20- to 30-kilowatt air-cooled racks of an ordinary enterprise data center, Botija explained.

“Having a six-kilowatt server that can perform like a data centre GPU is a pretty big deal,” he said. “It basically means that they have some storage equipment, some networking equipment, some traditional CPU equipment, and next to all of that, they can also insert some AI inference hardware acceleration without needing to go and rebuild another server room or another data center.”

For enterprises that would rather own their AI hardware than pay a recurring cloud bill, the barriers have been steep. A GPU rack costs at least $1 million, often far more, and usually requires liquid cooling, Botija noted. Axelera’s answer rests on digital in-memory computing, a technique that grew out of its founders’ research and gives the company’s power-efficient inference chips a performance-per-watt advantage.

“Now you can own your own infrastructure,” Botija said. “You can amortize those investments and … reduce your opex, and therefore be more profitable as a company.”
