Home Depot vs. Uber: One Stock Is Betting on a Housing Recovery, the Other on the Future of Transportation. Which Is the Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Strong earnings growth, attractive valuations, and an uncertain economic outlook are creating two very different opportunities for investors heading into 2027.

Home Depot (HD -0.69%) and Uber Technologies (UBER -1.57%) are two market leaders facing very different challenges heading into 2027. Home Depot is navigating a prolonged housing slowdown, while Uber continues to deliver strong growth despite a struggling stock price and growing uncertainty about the future of autonomous transportation.

For investors, the distinction between temporary economic headwinds and potential long-term disruption could be critical in determining which stock offers the better opportunity in the upcoming year.

Home Depot is a home improvement retailer that serves homeowners, contractors, and construction professionals. The company has increasingly focused on expanding its professional contractor business through acquisitions to strengthen its presence in roofing, building materials, and specialty construction products. This strategy helps diversify revenue beyond traditional do-it-yourself home improvement spending.

In fiscal 2025, Home Depot generated $164.7 billion in revenue, an increase of 3.2% year over year. However, net income declined 4.4% to $14.2 billion as elevated borrowing costs and sluggish housing activity weighed on demand. More recent results highlight continued challenges. In the second quarter of fiscal 2026, revenue increased 5.7% to $47.9 billion, but comparable sales grew just 1.7%, indicating relatively modest underlying demand despite contributions from acquisitions.

Home Depot remains a substantial cash-generating business, producing approximately $12.6 billion in free cash flow during fiscal 2025. However, its debt-to-equity ratio of roughly 5.1x reflects a leveraged balance sheet, while its current ratio stood near 1.1x. Although its cash generation provides financial stability, meaningful growth will likely depend on an improvement in housing activity and renewed spending on larger renovation projects.

Uber operates a global mobility and delivery platform connecting millions of consumers with drivers, couriers, and merchants. The company is positioning itself for the transition to autonomous transportation, focusing on integrating self-driving vehicles into its existing network rather than developing its own autonomous driving technology. Management expects more than 2,000 fully driverless vehicles operating on its platform by the end of 2027.

In fiscal 2025, Uber generated $52 billion in revenue, an 18% year-over-year increase. Net income reached approximately $10.1 billion, although a substantial portion reflected a one-time tax benefit. More recent results highlight continued operating momentum. In the third quarter of 2026, gross bookings increased 20% to $59.8 billion, while revenue climbed 10% to $14.8 billion. Adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) rose 31% to nearly $3 billion, demonstrating improving profitability as the platform expands.

Uber also maintains a strong financial position. In fiscal 2025, the company generated $9.8 billion in free cash flow, an increase of 42% year over year. At the end of September 2026, it held approximately $10.1 billion in unrestricted cash and short-term investments, providing substantial flexibility to invest in new technologies and expand its operations.

Home Depot's biggest challenge remains the housing market. Elevated mortgage rates and sluggish home sales have weighed on renovation spending, while competition from other home improvement retailers adds pressure on pricing and margins. Although lower interest rates could eventually revive demand, a prolonged housing slowdown could continue limiting growth.

Uber faces a potentially more significant long-term challenge from autonomous transportation. While the company is positioning its platform to accommodate self-driving vehicles, competitors could increasingly offer rides directly to consumers, threatening Uber's market share and pricing power. Regulatory uncertainty surrounding driver classification, along with competition from Lyft (LYFT +1.55%) and other mobility providers, introduces additional risks.

Uber appears to offer better value based on future earnings estimates, though Home Depot remains the more affordable option when comparing its price to trailing sales.

Home Depot and Uber offer two very different investment opportunities heading into 2027. The distinction comes down to the nature of their challenges. Home Depot's struggles are largely cyclical, meaning a recovery in housing activity could revive demand for home improvement projects. However, with mortgage rates remaining elevated and remodeling spending expected to grow modestly, a meaningful rebound may take time.

Uber, meanwhile, is already delivering strong operating results. Its latest quarterly gross bookings increased 20%, while adjusted EBITDA climbed 31%. Despite that momentum, the stock has struggled as investors question how autonomous vehicles could reshape its business. That uncertainty is legitimate, particularly as self-driving operators explore offering rides directly to consumers. However, Uber's established customer network, growing delivery business, and substantial cash generation provide competitive advantages as the industry evolves.

For investors looking toward 2027, Uber is my preferred buy. Trading at 20.4 times forward earnings compared with Home Depot's 23.3 times, Uber offers a compelling combination of faster growth and a slightly lower earnings valuation. Autonomous transportation poses meaningful long-term risks, but I believe the company's current operating momentum and financial strength make a stronger investment case than waiting for an uncertain housing recovery.
