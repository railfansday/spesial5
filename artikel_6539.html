This Genius Trick Makes Perfectly Portioned Cinnamon Rolls—No Ruler or Guessing Required

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

This clever little move turns your knife into a measuring guide for cinnamon rolls.

I have no objection to a shortcut in the kitchen. I’m happy to let boxed cake mix handle dessert or a great jar of pasta sauce get dinner moving. But I just can’t make peace with cinnamon rolls from a can. I want to love them. I appreciate the convenience, and I understand the appeal of having warm cinnamon rolls before the coffee is finished brewing. They just never quite satisfy that craving for a warm, deliciously gooey cinnamon roll.

Some viral upgrades make a compelling case—particularly the heavy cream bath and generous additions of butter and brown sugar. But my long-standing love for cinnamon rolls has much more to do with the soft, pillowy dough, so I’m willing to wait through a rise or two.

Having committed to that, though, I’m still interested in anything that makes the process easier. So when I saw a video of someone flipping a knife over a log of cinnamon roll dough to mark perfectly even portions, I wanted to try it. Cutting equal slices by eye can involve a surprising amount of second-guessing when you’d really just like to get them in the pan. I made our classic cinnamon roll recipe and put the trick to the test.

The clever part of this trick is that the knife becomes its own measurement. Rather than judging the thickness of each slice, you use the width of the blade to mark equal portions along the dough roll before slicing it into pieces.

Once your dough is filled and rolled into a log, gently lay the blade flat across the top at one end, with the knife running crosswise over the log. Then flip it onto its opposite flat side, moving along the dough one blade-width at a time. It looks a little like the knife is doing small somersaults, with its edge leaving a light indentation at each turn.

Continue down the log, using just enough pressure to leave visible marks. Then go back and slice through the dough at those marks.

This worked beautifully with our classic cinnamon rolls, but the same technique would work with just about any cinnamon roll recipe. The turning motion was simple, and the knife left an evenly spaced cutting guide along the dough. Once the marks were there, I could slice the rolls without measuring each one or estimating where the next cut should go.

I particularly liked being able to see all the portions before I started cutting. It made the job feel settled: The spacing was already worked out, and all I had to do was follow it. For something that takes so little effort, it’s a useful bit of precision.

This is the kind of cinnamon roll shortcut I can get behind. I still get the homemade rolls I wanted, but there’s one less thing to fuss over before the pan goes into the oven.
