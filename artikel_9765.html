Getting Traffic From aocr.org? It Could Be a ByteDance Crawler

If you have noticed a burst of visits with the referrer www.aocr.org in GA4, PostHog, Plausible, Umami, Fathom, or Cloudflare, you are not alone. The short version: the referrer is forged. AOCR is not involved, and the fingerprint points to a likely ByteDance crawler stack. Who operates it is not proven.

This post adapts the findings of Tin Analytics, "Getting traffic from aocr.org? That's ByteDance crawling your website" (29 Oct 2026).

Since about 24 Sep 2026, many sites show visits with the referrer www.aocr.org (American Osteopathic College of Radiology). The pattern is consistent:

On the sites Tin monitors: clawmessenger.com had 187 visits (27-29 Oct, still active), tin.computer had 3, and two other sites had 74 (28 Sep-28 Oct).

Requests carry Sec-Fetch-Site: none, which means the page was typed in or opened by a program. A real click from another site would be cross-site. The referrer is forged.

AOCR does not link to your pages and shows no sign of a hack. Do not contact them. They have nothing to fix and nothing to tell you.

Tin confirmed several independent signals that line up.

The TLS ClientHello sizes (1722/1754/1786/1818 bytes), HTTP/2 priority weight 256 exclusive, and an Accept header including image/heif match Bytespider, ByteDance's declared crawler. The numbers are stark:

The browser timing data includes extensionTiming with forestResFrom and forestResLoadStart, plus htmlPredictorHit. "Forest" is ByteDance's internal resource loader. Of 37 real-visitor recordings, 0 had these fields.

The real servers are in AS132510 (China Mobile, Shanxi). They run Linux x86_64 with 4 cores, 32 GB, and Chinese fonts, yet claim to be Windows. The time zone is Asia/Shanghai, the screen is 800x600, and each page gets a wait and scroll of about 28 seconds.

The IPs you see are not those servers. They are rented US proxies: 187 visits came from 186 different IPs (Datacamp/CDN77, Web2Objects, Ace Data Centres, EGIHosting, M247, and others) on ports 1180, 40000, and 60000, likely a commercial proxy pool.

It is not established whether a ByteDance team runs this or a customer of a ByteDance service (for example, Volcano Engine) using the same browser. Tin asked ByteDance for comment on 29 Oct 2026.

Say "likely ByteDance crawler stack", not "ByteDance did this".

The stack is old: Bytespider has existed since 2024. What is new since late Sep 2026 is a Windows Chrome 146 disguise, a US proxy pool, and the forged referrer. The referrer made previously invisible traffic visible.

There is precedent. In 2025, Cloudflare found that Perplexity ran undeclared stealth crawlers.

Do not block IPs. There is a new one per visit, so a blocklist is useless. Do not contact AOCR either.

Expect the referrer to change; the fingerprint probably will not. That is why the Accept/sec-ch-ua rule and the Asia/Shanghai + 800 screen-width filter matter more than the aocr.org referrer rule.
