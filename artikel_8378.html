Personal Consumer Issues • Re: Super automatic espresso machines

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

An espresso machine may not be what you want if you drink coffee and not espresso. A good drip brewer, e.g., Fellow Aiden, and separate grinder with high quality flat burrs you can clean, e.g., Fellow Ode Gen 2, will give you better drip coffee although with a slightly more involved workflow since you need to grind the beans yourself. Separating the brewing and grinding functions also allows for continued use of the suriving function if one device fails, and you have the flexibility to upgrade each function separately if you ever want to.

I had a Krups superauto which failed after 8 years. It produced decent drip-type coffee as well as espresso and milk-based espresso drinks. The Aiden makes far superior drip coffee, akin to a pour-over, while my Ninja Luxe Cafe semi-automatic espresso machine makes better espresso and espresso drinks. Portafilter espresso machines (semi-automatic like the Ninja or manual) generally make better-tasting espresso than superautomatic bean-to-cup machines when paired with a good grinder and operated with care. It must be acknowledged, though, that superautomatics are more convenient.

If you do go the superauto route, oily beans can be problematic since most superauto grinders are not removable for cleaning. You can, however, use Urnex SuperGrindz periodically to help eliminate oil residues and their negative effects of function and tatste.
