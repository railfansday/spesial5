Tiorati Trials

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

One thing leads to another resulting in a rough day on the trail.

Six weeks into the season, and it might be over. The football season, that is. My rag-tag team of fantasy football players have been doing their best, but luck hasn’t been on my side this year. I got the big performances I needed during the Monday Night Football game, but ended up less than one point from a victory. I now find my team amongst the basement dwellers with 5 wins to 9 losses. A team that has just 7 points more than me, representing a difference under 1%, has 8 wins to 4 losses.

It shouldn’t matter, but this narrow loss took the air out the room for me this morning. It can’t be rationalized, when sports aren’t going your way, it affects your mood and often spills over into the rest of your life for a time. At least for today I’d be carrying that with me.

At least the weather was on my side, it was comfortably around 45 degrees. That made packing up a pleasant experience and I was on the trail before 8:00AM, already eager to get the day over with. At least I had a shortcut on the agenda just ahead. A trail marked by a rectangular white square with a red circle in the middle led the way over Goshen Mountain. I followed the Japanese-flag looking blazes to Tiorati Road, which runs past Lake Tiorati. Along the shore of the lake is a beach and just further up is a picnic area. After that the trail rejoins the AT by the two water towers I passed yesterday. It is a nifty shortcut. I suspect many hikers take this route as the picnic/beach area has loads of amenities for hikers; water bottle filling, swimming, restrooms, showers, and vending machines.

I was still bothered by my team’s narrow loss and hiking with disillusionment. The trail was starting to respond in kind. This section of New York is rocky to be sure. Foot placement takes concentration and constant corrections when you find that your foot isn’t as stable as you’d ideally like it to be. Rock steps are a regular presence. Then there is the route of the AT. While the trail stays low, this isn’t a mountainous area, it still climbs and drops endlessly. I found that I was rarely looking ahead, always up or down. The constant barrage of terrain was making the day unpleasant. I didn’t want to talk with any of the hikers I passed on the trail. I thought about “cancelling” this post, writing something to the effect of it being a day that I’d rather forget. My brain could only conjure up all the things I didn’t like about the trail today. That was a long list. But writing it all out would make this post really whiny. I didn’t want to insult the good people of New York by writing a long negative post. I wondered how I was going to write anything worth reading.

I rumbled along, eventually reaching the road where I rejoined the AT after getting a bit lost yesterday. This would be new territory for me. The trail climbed steeply to Buchanan Mountain. I was reaching a new low. Getting lost yesterday put me on a far easier path than going up and over the mountain, even though the route was substantially shorter. I came upon a group of hikers at an overlook who warned me to be careful on my way down the mountain. I probably should have paid the warning more mind, but I’d seen miles and miles of this type of terrain before. I navigated my way down through the rocky descent. The leaves took away any hint of what the rocks buried below might look like. My trekking poles skipped off the rocks, offering no aid. I had to rely on my feet and ankles. It went well until it didn’t. I don’t know what I stepped on, but it wasn’t flat. My left foot slipped, bending my ankle at an awkward ankle. I cursed, maybe loudly enough for the group I had passed to hear. There was no lingering effect from the event, but it didn’t help my mood at all.

Rock steps on the section of trail known as “Agony Grind”.

A bird flies near Arden Mountain over I-87.

I came to a stream right around the point where I lost the trail yesterday and stopped to filter some water. I needed to regroup and find some way to redeem the day. My only idea was to hike back to Route 5 and save myself a bit of climbing by road walking back to my van. It looked to be roughly the same distance, but considerably flatter. Just down the road I saw the Albert Wisner Public Library, which would remain open until 8:00PM. I’d feel better if I could get my blog posts for yesterday and today scheduled and reduce some of my weekend workload. I pulled on my pack, worked through the rest of my trail snack, finished up the water I had just filtered, and hiked on.

Just above Fitzgerald Falls was a large pool fed by the same creek. A sign read simply “AT SPA”. I could have done with a spa day, but given the recent overnight temperatures I think this one might not have done me a whole lot of good. In the summer though, it would have been a glorious stop.

Eventually I reached the road and turned left. About an hour later I was back at my van getting cleaned up. The library was a short 7 minute drive away, but when I walked up to the doors I found that it was closed due to flooding. I had planned on going to the grocery store afterward for some town food anyway, but I was annoyed that I’d probably end up falling behind on the blog. In the first bit of good fortune all day, the Shop Rite had a seating area where I could get my work done. Plus, it was open until midnight. I could enjoy a bit of food and then work through my write-ups before heading back to the trail. The store even had the TV in the seating area set to the World Series, so I got to watch baseball for the first time in months. It had take a while, but the day finally managed to turn a corner.
