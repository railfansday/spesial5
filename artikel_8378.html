Pennsylvania Pebbles

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

It hasn’t rained for weeks. That changes today, just in time to join forces with the Autumn leaves and northern Pennsylvania rocks to brew up a wicked hiker test.

They can’t help themselves, these white-tailed deer. They like to visit us campers. At 3:00AM this morning a deer, which had almost certainly sensed the rain coming, perhaps curious if there was space inside for it to keep dry. I told it that to save about $50 I bought a 1 person tent, it muttered a snort before prancing off.

The next thing I remember rain was pattering onto my tent. Shucks, I wasn’t going to escape with a dry tent this morning. I had been clever enough to tuck my groundsheet behind the rain fly so that the water would fall to the ground rather than pool up. The fact that I was camping on a layer of leaves was a boon as well, I wouldn’t have to deal with mud getting on everything. I fell back asleep but emerged from my tent shortly before sunrise to an eerie scene – the forest was enveloped in a thick mist. My tent looked sorry, the water weighing down the rain fly, the stakes no doubt shifting from their precarious posts during the night. Usually this is the time where I come up with some excuse to stay in my tent for half the morning, but not this time. The rain had brought about 10 degrees of warmth along with it, it was in the mid fifties. It wasn’t currently raining, so I certainly didn’t want to dilly dally only for it to start raining, making packing up a mess. I wolfed down my breakfast and set about packing. By 7:00AM I was making my way northbound.

I had made it a grand total on 1 mile north of the Leroy A. Smith shelter yesterday, leaving a little over 18 miles back to my van at the Delaware Water Gap. I knew rain could arrive at any time, there was a decent chance of it throughout the day. I was liking the foggy trail conditions, though it came at the expense of the valley views I had yesterday.

On the north side of Wind Gap a rocky yet mercifully short climb awaited me. Once the trail leveled out, I found that the area was still consumed with fog. As I continued my meandering, a small flock of birds, perhaps starlings, took off from the ground near the trail and disappeared into the white mist. Several chipmunks scampered into their burrows, revealing little more than a flash of brown fur to me. My thoughts ran to the interaction between these two very different species of animals. Where are the birds searching for food, potentially at the expense of the chipmunks? Had I stumbled upon nature’s version of capture the flag?

The scene on the trail early this morning.

I wouldn’t watch a scary movie before wandering into this part of the forest.

For a while the rain held off. I enjoyed more of the trail surrounded by fog from all sides. It creates some fascinating scenes for sure. I didn’t miss the valley views, I had something completely different to experience. Nature was about to throw something different to experience at me. Right after I turned onto the Wolf Rocks bypass trail, the mist that had been on and off all day turned into a light rain. I tossed on my poncho for the first time since Vermont. I had made excellent progress, most of the bad rocky sections were behind me. I trudged on through the fog and rain determined to stay on track for an early finish.

Suddenly I wasn’t alone, ahead of me a figure was descending the trail. On a day like this, it could only be a long distance hiker. A large yellow pack and sky blue sun hoodie confirmed as much. Then I realized that I knew those items and the face of the hiker well, Pebbles! I called out to her, yet again struck by the encounter as I was sure that this time she would have been far down the trail. In fact, she had spent Halloween trick-or-treating back in Unionville, NY. That explained why I only saw her once last week, jumping ahead on the trail should have put us on track to meet again the next day. She reported the town was very friendly to her and her thru-hiker costume. She may be a genius, I kicked myself for not thinking of the idea. Maybe I’ll buy some candy for myself at the post-halloween discount.

Pebbles and See You Tomorrow catch up on the trail.

I didn’t envy Pebbles, who was heading straight into the rocky sections that tested me yesterday. I, on the other hand, reached the gravel road near Mount Minsi. I could really make time on the wide open path. After a brief rocky descent on the AT I switched back onto the gravel road. I figured it would be a much smoother descent, and it also led straight back to the AT just before the trailhead where my van was parked. It was not perfect, there were plenty of rocks on the primitive road, but there were none of the step downs that I would have had on the AT. My ankle has taken a lot of abuse this year, but it feels up to finishing off the hike and I want to keep it that way. Just before the trailhead, I noticed and abrupt change in the color of the gravel on the road. It turned into a mix of turquoise and gray, a combination I felt was worth admiring for a few moments before finishing out my hike.

I was back at my van around 1:30PM and quickly got myself ready for town. The local chain cafe was open until 10:00PM giving me time to get all 4 blog posts from this week ready for publishing. With luck I could have another light Sunday coming up, just what I need as I enter the closing stages of my year long hike!

Yes, I caught myself admiring the rocks in northern Pennsylvania of all places!
