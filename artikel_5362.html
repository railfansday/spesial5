Holiday Shoppers Beware: AI and Spoofed Websites Are Making Online Scams Harder to Spot

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

eMazzanti Technologies warns consumers to verify before they buy, as AI, fake retail sites and look-alike domains make holiday scams more convincing

HOBOKEN, N.J., Nov. 25, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- That Black Friday bargain in your social feed may not be real. Neither may the website offering it.

As holiday shopping accelerates, eMazzanti Technologies is warning consumers that artificial intelligence is helping cybercriminals create more convincing scams, while spoofed websites, look-alike domain names, fake delivery notices and phishing attacks make it increasingly difficult for shoppers to distinguish fraud from legitimate commerce.

The problem is particularly troublesome during the holidays, when consumers are moving quickly, hunting for deals and receiving a flood of legitimate order confirmations, promotional emails and delivery notifications. Criminals can exploit that activity by impersonating familiar retailers, financial institutions and delivery companies.

"The old advice was to look for something that didn't look quite right. That's no longer enough," said Carl Mazzanti, president of eMazzanti Technologies. "AI can make a fraudulent store, email or message look remarkably legitimate. Consumers need to verify before they trust, and businesses need to know when criminals are impersonating their brands. Taking 30 seconds to stop and verify before clicking can prevent a tremendous amount of damage."

A Familiar Brand Doesn't Guarantee a Legitimate Website

One threat a holiday shopper may overlook is domain spoofing.

Cybercriminals can register look-alike domain names designed to resemble those of legitimate retailers. A fraudulent site may use a familiar logo, brand colors, product images and professional-looking content while directing consumers to a web address that differs from the legitimate company's domain by only a few characters.

Generative AI raises the stakes by making it easier to create convincing content and communications at scale. Current fraud warnings have also highlighted AI-enabled impersonation, including cloned voices, as part of the evolving scam landscape.

When consumers arrive at a fraudulent ecommerce site, the objective may go beyond collecting money for merchandise that never arrives. Fake sites can also be used to capture credit card information, passwords and other personal data.

Retailers Have a Role in Protecting Shoppers, Too

Consumers aren't the only victims when a legitimate brand is impersonated.

A fraudulent site can damage the reputation and customer trust of the company being spoofed, even though that business had nothing to do with the scam.

That's why brand protection has become another component of cybersecurity.

eMazzanti Technologies' Brand Protection services help organizations identify digital impersonation and online threats directed at their brands, providing another layer of defense against criminals attempting to exploit the trust businesses have built with customers.

For ecommerce businesses, that protection is particularly important. eMazzanti combines cybersecurity, monitoring and technology services to help its ecommerce customers protect the environments their businesses depend on. The objective is not simply keeping an ecommerce site operating. It is helping maintain the security and reliability consumers expect when they visit a legitimate online store.

For eMazzanti ecommerce customers, those protections help create an important distinction: shoppers are doing business with a legitimate organization whose technology and cybersecurity are being actively managed, rather than an unknown operator behind a convincing copy of its brand.

Trust Has Become Part of the Online Shopping Experience

The consequences of an online attack increasingly extend beyond stolen data or a fraudulent purchase. For legitimate retailers, an impersonation attack can undermine customer confidence in the brand itself.

"Retailers spend years earning their customers' confidence, and a criminal can try to exploit that trust in minutes," Mazzanti said. "Protecting an ecommerce business today isn't just about securing servers and transactions. It's about protecting the customer's confidence that when they interact with your brand online, they are really interacting with you."
