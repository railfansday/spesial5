The Fastest Shoes at the 2026 NYC Marathon

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Eight brands cracked the top 10, but Nike and Adidas “hyper shoes” took the overall top spots.

We earn a commission for products purchased through some links in this article. Why Trust Us?

The defending champs were in the mix all the way to Central Park, but in the end there were some surprising upsets. Andreas Almgren, in his debut, became the first European man to claim victory at the NYC Marathon in 30 years. And Jess McClain outsprinted Hellen Obiri to take second place overall. There were even bigger surprises—at least in the eyes of the RW gear team—as there was good parity among shoe companies among the fastest runners. Even Xtep got into the mix. Let’s take a look at what the top 10 men and women laced up for their dash from Staten Island to Central Park.

You can’t get this shoe until January 2027, but Nike’s top pros are already having some good success on the roads with the $360 racer. It has two Air units stacked under the forefoot and a zero drop. Lighter and more aggressive than an Alphafly, the Apex is being positioned as Nike’s elite-level marathon shoe, while the Alphafly 4 is being turned into a marathon racer for the masses. I took an Apex demo pair out for a short run last month and found they feel aggressively propulsive—you’re immediately tipping forward off those Air units as soon as you touch down.

On had a big day on the streets of the Big Apple. Even though Hellen Obiri didn’t repeat as champ in the women’s division, the brand’s top racing model with the robot-sprayed upper was seen a lot among the pro fields.

Personally, I love that the collar of the upper is designed more like a normal shoe—the taller cuff of the first version rubbed my ankle and I had to cut the upper.

Xtep might be a new name to you, but it’s one of a handful of exciting brands coming out of China right now. The shoes are tough to get here in the U.S., but we’ve received a few of Xtep’s models for testing recently, including the 160X 8.0 Pro. “This is a rocket ship of a shoe,” said RW shoe tester Josh Davis. “It really shined in intervals where I was pushing it at 5:30+ pace, but it is downright uncomfortable at easy paces.” Mofolo rode this thing in the sweet spot today.

This shoe has been cleaning house at the World Majors Marathons. Today was one of the rare days when the featherweight, $500 speedster didn’t land on the podium. Bonus: Adidas just refreshed stock so a few pairs are still available. Hurry. If that shoe sells out, the next fastest option is the Adios Pro 5.

The Ray is Asics’s ultralight racer, using an exceptionally soft A-TPU midsole foam with a see-through upper that really does little more than hold the foam to your sole. It’s a lot of fun if you’re dashing in a straight line, but gets squirrelly if you have to turn.

Muhumed laced up a black-on-black prototype that Hoka got approved by World Athletics within the past week. It’s hard to see much detail here but, given the silhouette of the sole, it looks like it might be the successor to the Rocket X 3 (the Cielo has a more pronounced rocker shape).

Nike recently released the Alphafly 4 and announced the Apex, which you saw finish further ahead in the pack. But Kamworor reached for the Alphafly 3, which has been discontinued. That’s the beauty of this sport: Our preferences are highly individual and you have enough choices to reach for the shoe that works best for you.

The blue color looks like the Ray we saw earlier, but zooming in on the image it appears there are two layers of foam—the top layer is a little more yellow-tinted—and the upper looks to be the slightly thicker mesh found on the more traditional racing duo. The use of two foams (PEBA and A-TPU) gives the Sky better stability and a firmer feel underfoot.

As we expected, Chepkirui (the 2024 champ) and Hellen Obiri (2025 champ) went toe-to-toe all the way into Central Park. In the end, it was the Pro Evo 3 once again taking the top spot in a WMM race.

The World Athletics site lists two development prototypes, one named “hyperion Green2” and one simply called “RED.” That latter shoe was approved for use at the end of September, ahead of the fall marathon season. We’re not sure which of these is the shoe McClain wore to claim a podium spot, but earlier in the week Brooks told us that she was planning to race in a prototype disguised to look like the currently available Hyperion Elite 6. That’s the same tactic Clayton Young used at the Chicago Marathon last month when he ran the second-fastest marathon ever by an American.

The 46-year-old delivered another stunning performance in NYC. On her feet were Puma’s speediest racing shoes with a carbon-fiber plate that extends out past the toes for extra leverage.

The Cielo is Hoka’s bounciest marathon shoe, with a much more aggressive rocker shape. If you prize a slightly firmer or more stable experience, the Rocket X 3 may be more your speed. The Cielo carried Chelangat to a top-five showing.

The second American woman finisher laced up the Edge, a marathon racer that is designed for “cadence” runners—those who speed up by taking faster steps rather than lengthening their stride.

The two layers of PEBA foam in this racer are just a little firmer and more controlled than found in the Cielo. The rocker, too, is more easygoing and doesn’t feel quite so propulsive. But it’s clear that the shoe can still get up and move.
