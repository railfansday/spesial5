Mauricio Pochettino endured the worst possible start to life as Manchester United manager as his side were thrashed 4-0 by fierce rivals Liverpool.

United set the tone for a disastrous afternoon early as they went a man down inside eight minutes, with Leny Yoro bundling into Alexander Isak as the last man to be shown a straight red card.

Dominik Szoboszlai fired in from the resulting free-kick to ensure Yoro’s blunder was fully punished, and Liverpool doubled their lead three minutes later when Florian Wirtz excellently improvised a volley to score.

Rio Ngumoha got a deserved goal after the break to put the game to bed, converting from Bradley Barcola’s cross, before Victor Munoz put the cherry on a ruthless performance for the hosts late on.

Welcome to Manchester United, Mauricio. For a man who had coveted the United job for years, this was an ignominious introduction. Mauricio Pochettino got a 13-minute crash course in the craziness of his new club. His first game was lost before he made his first substitution, and that came as early as the 22nd-minute. His debut brought the joint heaviest ever defeat by a United manager in his first game in charge, and the biggest in 100 years.

“So painful,” Pochettino said. “So disappointed.” As can be the way with United of late, most things that could go wrong did. When their new head coach’s name was sung, it was by the Kop. “Poch is at the wheel,” they taunted. A first meaningful day of work showed the scale of the task to steer United in the right direction.

Liverpool captain Virgil van Dijk remains uncertain of whether he will remain at the club beyond the end of the season.

The 35-year-old centre-back is approaching the final six months of his contract, which expires next summer.

He believes that he can keep playing at the top level for “many more years” but says he has “no idea” whether that will be at Anfield as Liverpool’s decision-makers continue to ponder over a fresh offer.

“We see Wirtz move around so much, going left, going right, running around. I talked about his pressing actions before the game - that's the most impressive stat he has. It's his running ability, his pressing,” said Daniel Sturridge on Sky.

“But what we're seeing now is he's bringing his real game together. I don't think he's truly at his best yet. I think he's finding his groove. He's understanding what it takes to make things happen.

“People will give credit to Jurgen [Klopp], who would be in his ear telling him about little things, but I have to give credit to Iraola, of course, because ultimately the manager has shown a lot of faith in him,” he explained.

“There's been a lot of question marks about is he good enough? Should he be playing? Now he's really showing his level.

“In football, there is a sense of immediacy because of a price tag. But he's slowly but surely showing people why they paid the money.

“Over the course of his career at the club, we would like to think there will be more performances like this,” he added.

The job of Manchester United head coach has become something of a poisoned chalice for a succession of managers but even new boss Mauricio Pochettino could not have expected how quickly he would be taunted with jokes about him being sacked.

His reign began in humiliating fashion after an early red card and two goals led to the Kop chanting “Sacked in the morning” just 13 minutes into the Argentinian’s first game.

Chants of “Going down with the City” when the third goal went in – followed by “You’re not famous any more” and “Poch is at the wheel” – will only have made the trip back along the M62 more uncomfortable.

New Manchester United manager Mauricio Pochettino has denied that Leny Yoro should have been sent off in his side’s 4-0 loss to Liverpool, calling it an “unfair situation”.

Yoro was given a straight red card in the eighth minute of the game at Anfield to give Pochettino the worst possible start to his life as United boss, with the young Frenchman fouling the Liverpool striker after he had gone clean through as last man.

Roy Keane accused visiting Manchester United supporters at Anfield of “applauding rubbish” after Liverpool romped to a 4-0 win.
