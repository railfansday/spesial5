Erasmus running risk of breaching World Rugby ban with watchalong

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Sky Sports understands Rassie Erasmus would be running risk of breaching six-Test World Rugby suspension with planned watchalong of Saturday's Ireland vs South Africa Nations Championship Test; part of Erasmus' ban covers "direct or indirect contact with team management on matchdays"

South Africa head coach Rassie Erasmus would risk breaching the terms of his six-Test ban if he holds his planned online watchalong of Saturday's Nations Championship clash with Ireland, Sky Sports understands.

Erasmus is serving a six-match suspension handed down by World Rugby following extraordinary claims, saying "match-fixing" and "manipulation" was occurring in rugby after his side's defeat to France in Paris last week.

The 54-year-old was suspended for "public criticism of match officials, undermining the integrity of individuals or the sport, and fuelling online abuse", but has since taken to X to confirm he will watch Saturday's Test from his Dublin hotel and has organised a free watchalong for anyone to sign up and view him react to the Test match as it is unfolding.

As of Friday, over 100,000 people had signed up to watch. Erasmus' ban covers the following:

"The suspension applies with immediate effect, applies to international matches, and includes any matchday involvement including coaching, direct or indirect contact with team management or players on matchdays; entering the stadium, coaches' box, playing enclosure, technical area, approaching and/or communicating with match officials on matchday; engaging in any 'feedback' process with match officials; and engaging in media duties during the suspension."

Regarding the "direct or indirect contact with team management or players on matchdays" element of his suspension, Sky Sports sought guidance on the potential for one of South Africa's coaching staff or management set-up signing up and listening to and watching the reactions and comments of their suspended head coach during Saturday's contest - something which could have a bearing on prospective replacements and tactics in the Test.

While this specific scenario is not covered by the guidelines, Sky Sports understands Erasmus' plan could be considered a breach by rugby's governing body.

It is also understood there would be a delay to any broadcast feed Erasmus will be using.

South Africa Rugby released a statement requesting a full misconduct hearing in an immediate response to the sanction, but it is understood formal confirmation of an intention to appeal is yet to be received at World Rugby.

Erasmus has now been suspended three times in five years for these sorts of incidents.

During the 2021 British and Irish Lions series in South Africa, after the Springboks lost the first Test, Erasmus broadcasted an hour-long critique of Australian referee Nic Berry, and was eventually handed a two-month ban from all rugby activity and 10-month matchday ban.

In 2022 he received a two-Test ban after sarcastic social media posts following back-to-back South Africa defeats to Ireland and France. A video critical of referee Wayne Barnes led to the official revealing he and his family had received death threats.

On Wednesday, SA Rugby released a statement "condemning the online abuse of officials" following confirmation of Erasmus' ban.
