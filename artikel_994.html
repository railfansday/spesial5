The last 15 fixtures played in this slot immediately after an international break have averaged a whopping 3.5 goals per game. All 15 have cleared the over 1.5 goals line, while 11 have produced three or more goals.

That's a sizeable gap. And one I'd expect to narrow as the season progresses such is the attacking talent on show.

Manchester City remain dangerous enough to win this comfortably if their attacking combinations click but their ability to control games without allowing opponents opportunities has been questionable.

But admiration doesn't pay the bills in this game. Finding value does.

And I'm not convinced Villa have done enough to justify being odds-on favourites here.

They remain a team with a few too many rough edges to be trusted at short prices, especially at Villa Park where they've failed to deliver for favourite-backers in each of their last four Premier League home games.

That's a worrying trend for anyone steaming into the home win.

Sunderland, meanwhile, are a profitable betting proposition when the market writes them off. Backing them to win with a £1 level stake in every Premier League away game since the start of last season when priced bigger than 2/1 would have returned a handsome £7.70 profit.

Bournemouth don't get nearly enough credit for just how good they are without the ball. There's a perception that they're all about high-octane pressing, attacking intent and causing chaos. But dig beneath the surface and you'll find one of the most disciplined defensive units in the Premier League.

No team has conceded fewer shots inside their own penalty area this season than Bournemouth's 59, while their expected goals against figure of just 1.13 per 90 is the third-best in the division.

And who's sitting second in that particular metric? Nottingham Forest at 1.10.

So we're dealing with two teams whose defensive foundations are among the strongest in the league. That's usually my cue to start sniffing around the unders market.

Bournemouth home matches have been a profitable hunting ground for under 2.5 goals backers since the start of last season, returning a healthy £6.56 profit to £1 level stakes. Five of their last six Premier League home games have also failed to produce three goals.

The managerial merry-go-round is spinning again. Crystal Palace have jumped aboard, handing Thomas Frank the keys to Selhurst Park. New hope. But a new manager doesn't necessarily mean new results.

In fact, recent evidence suggests quite the opposite for teams in trouble.

The famous 'new manager bounce' is one of football's most romanticised theories. A fresh face walks through the door, standards supposedly rise overnight and points start rolling in.

Of the last six Premier League teams sitting in the bottom eight to change manager between November and March, five actually saw their points-per-game average drop across the following five matches.

Vitor Pereira at Nottingham Forest. Igor Tudor at Tottenham. Rob Edwards at Wolves. Graham Potter at West Ham. Ivan Juric at Southampton.

It's a fascinating trend that should serve as a warning to Palace supporters expecting Frank to arrive waving a magic wand, especially with Frank having plenty to prove in that he can operate to the level expected without the comfort blanket of the Brentford structure. The Evens on offer for a Palace relegation is real tempter.

Tottenham are 8/15 with Sky Bet to beat Ipswich. Spurs have won just five of last 28 Premier League home games, albeit they are searching for three wins on the spin in front of their own faithful. Things are looking up but are they a trusty steed just yet? No.

The markets still haven't quite cottoned on to what Roberto De Zerbi is cooking up either. The reputation remains one of chaos, risk-taking and goals galore. But the reality? Something very different.

Across his 17 matches in charge, Spurs games are averaging just 2.1 goals.

This isn't De Zerbi-ball of old. It's a more controlled and pragmatic version. Tottenham are prioritising defensive structure over attacking fireworks and the market seems reluctant to adjust.

Lewis Hall's performance for England in that Nations League victory over Spain was outstanding. He's made the left-back position his own for Thomas Tuchel and is now carrying himself with the swagger of an established international.

That's filtering into his Newcastle performances, too.

Hall isn't just content with doing the dirty work defensively. He's adventurous, positive and increasingly willing to get himself into shooting positions. He's registered a shot in 11 of his last 15 Newcastle starts, averaging 1.1 shots per 90 minutes - very respectable numbers for a left-back.

Hall is also the most fouled defender in the Premier League, drawing an average of 2.2 fouls per 90 across his last 12 matches in all competitions. His willingness to carry the ball into dangerous areas and take opponents on makes him a magnet for contact.

Brighton have quickly developed into one of the Premier League's most exciting outfits, playing with a level of confidence and attacking invention that makes them extremely difficult to contain.

And at the heart of it all is Pascal Gross. The man has a football brain that operates on an entirely different frequency to most mere mortals.

Only Erling Haaland and Evanilson have registered more Premier League goal involvements than Gross, who has eight to his name, four assists and four goals. There are quicker players. More explosive players. But very few who think the game quite like Gross.

The prices surrounding this influence in the goalscorer markets haven't adjusted fully with the 5/2 with Sky Bet about him scoring in a game that Brighton are heavy odds-on to win certainly offering up some value. Gross is also the designated penalty taker for this goal heavy Brighton team, too.

Anfield. Liverpool. Manchester United. Maurico Pochettino making his Premier League return. This is a fixture with enough history, pressure and emotional baggage to turn an ordinary Sunday afternoon into complete bedlam.

Pochettino has walked into a mess at United though. A team find themselves in the relegation zone at this stage of a season for the first time in 40 years. A footballing catastrophe for a club of their stature.

The defensive numbers are particularly frightening. United have shipped 30 goals in just 15 matches across all competitions this season. Only Lens (31) have conceded more among teams in Europe's major leagues.

Two sides who are comfortable in their own skin, two managers who understand the value of defensive organisation and a fixture where the first goal could completely change the tactical landscape. It's classic Premier League where the margins are so fine and trying to dig out a prediction on the outright market is a tough task.

The numbers behind this particular scheduling sweet spot are rather tasty.

The last 15 Monday Night Football fixtures played immediately after an international break have averaged 3.1 goals per game. The latest instalment, Coventry's meeting with Newcastle, served up five goals for our entertainment.

Unlike the dreaded Saturday lunchtime slot, where managers are often scrambling to get their international players back into shape, Monday night offers an extra couple of days on the training ground. More preparation time, sharper attacking patterns and potentially more fluid football.
