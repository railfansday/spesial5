What Cameron Fens' eligibility means for UNC basketball

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

UNC basketball center Cameron Fens will make his debut for the Tar Heels against Marshall after receiving emergency relief against the NCAA in Orange County Superior Court on Thursday, Nov. 19.

Judge Alyson Grine granted a temporary restraining order that makes Fens immediately eligible after months of uncertainty. Fens filed a lawsuit Wednesday, Nov. 20, seeking a temporary restraining order and preliminary injunction that would allow him to compete while his case proceeds in court.

A hearing on the preliminary injunction is scheduled for 2 p.m. on Nov. 30, but Fens will remain eligible at least until that process is complete. The order will remain in effect until the judge rules on the motion for the injunction or until 5 p.m. on Dec. 1, whichever comes first.

Following its game against Marshall, UNC hosts Monmouth (1-3) on Monday, Nov. 23, before playing West Virginia (4-2) in the Dick Vitale Invitational on Friday, Nov. 27, in Charlotte.

The NCAA denied Fens' waiver request in late October after the case had been pending for roughly nine months. In late July, Fens was among a group of Class of 2022 athletes who received temporary relief through an injunction challenging NCAA eligibility restrictions. He committed to the Tar Heels on Aug. 7.

Fens averaged 14.4 points, 8.7 rebounds and 1.4 per game last season at South Dakota. The 7-foot, 255-pound graduate student from Dubuque, Iowa, started all 32 games as a senior for the Coyotes. He was a first-team and all-defensive player in the Summit League, playing an average of 25 minutes per game and finishing 29th nationally with 12 double-doubles.

He made 57.9% of his shots and was the only player in the Summit League to average a double-double (17.6 ppg, 10.3 rbg) in conference games. His 47 blocks last season were the seventh-most swats in a single season for the Coyotes.

When he committed to UNC, Fens called it "one of the most surreal moments of my life" in a social media post.

"Humbled to wear Carolina Blue and to represent this university," Fens wrote. "Excited to get to work. Go Heels! AGTG."

To be clear, this is not Sean May, Tyler Hansbrough or Caleb Wilson getting cleared to play for UNC. Being an All-Summit League player doesn't necessarily mean Cameron Fens will be an All-ACC caliber contributor for the Tar Heels.

Still, Fens gives UNC something it sorely needs because of his knack for crashing the glass and banging around in the post. The Tar Heels desperately needed to add some bulk with a bruising center to join a group that is more suited for a finesse style. Fens can be the "dirty work" player for this roster.

Fens was fourth nationally last season in defensive rebounding percentage at 28.8%, trailing only UNC Greensboro's Justin Neely (32.1), Lamar's Braden East (31) and Florida's Rueben Chinyelu (30.7). Fens also had a 11.9% offensive rebounding percentage, which was 134th nationally.

UNC has been outrebounded by 17 total rebounds across its first four games and owns a minus-4.5 rebounding margin. The Tar Heels are allowing opponents to post a 37% offensive rebounding percentage, which is 293rd nationally. If that holds, it would be the worst mark in the KenPom era (1997). With Fens eligible, UNC is getting some much-needed reinforcements on the glass.

UNC basketball coach Michael Malone expressed strong support for Cameron Fens in an affidavit filed before Thursday's ruling.

"Since he arrived, Cameron has practiced with our team every day," Malone said.

"He lifts and conditions with the team, travels with us and sits on our bench during games. He is a part of this team in every way but one: He has not been allowed to play in a game."

Malone went on to describe as "physical and strong," as well as a "skilled player," adding that "if he were eligible, he would be a regular part of the rotation" for a UNC team in need of frontcourt help.

"If a court order bars the NCAA from treating Cameron as ineligible and from penalizing UNC, or Cameron's own records, because he played, UNC will certify his eligibility," Malone said in the affidavit. "Cameron is ready to play now. I will play him in our first game after he is certified."

Malone ended his affidavit with a final endorsement.

"Cameron has done everything we have asked of him since he arrived," Malone said. "He belongs on this team, and he should be allowed to play for it."
