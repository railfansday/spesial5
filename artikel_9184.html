Student Wrecks Scottish Exhibit in Attempt to Sit on Stone of Destiny

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Executive Editor, ARTnews & Art in America

A French student who smashed a vitrine protecting the storied Stone of Destiny in Scotland said he did so by right, claiming to having been born in Scotland and thus having been bestowed the privilege of sitting on a relic that had been used to crown Scottish monarchs until the 13th century.

As reported by the BBC, “Arnaud Hercules Harixcalde Logan, 36, pleaded guilty to a charge of maliciously damaging the historic artefact’s glass casing at Perth Museum in July last year. Perth Sheriff Court was shown CCTV footage which showed Logan using a blue and white hammer to strike the reinforced glass cabinet 24 times—causing £9,000 [around $12,000] worth of damage.”

The incident, which forced the closure of the Stone of Destiny exhibit for three months, led to Logan being tackled by a museum visitor and a member of its staff. After an initial court appearance last year, the perpetrator fled to Australia and failed to check in with police for several months, as required by law. To that and the smashing incident, Logan pled guilty, with sentencing to follow on January 6.

About the jarring day at the museum, a public prosecutor said, “Members of the public appeared distressed and panicked. Staff evacuated everyone from the building. He said he was called Logan and that the Logan clan had been betrayed and that the Stone of Destiny had been stolen from him.”
