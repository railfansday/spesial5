First off, banks are different in Japan. For example, you are forced to open an account at the closest office. When you log in, you enter your branch number and account as part of the process.

To send money with internet banking, you have to pair a phone with a one time password. To do this you have two options.

1. Scan the IC chip if your driver's license or my number card (which is a fancy social security number.) The standard is not the same as NFC, so you still need a specific phone to scan it. Japanese phones generally work, but international iPhones work as well.

2. You can ask for a code to be sent by mail. it will usually arrive within 5 business days.

I use international phones because Japan only sells large slabs nowadays. You can only have one office registered at a time, so I have to do the mailed code for every phone switch.

You may have noticed, but many services are starting to required registering with a phone number which helps to reduce bots and fraud. I don't really see an option for anonymous accounts in the future.
