How many times are you paying to store the same file?

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

EVENT: Peers compare how they're cutting duplicate storage without ripping anything out, over dinner with The Register.

Pick any busy project folder and count the copies. There's one on the file server, one in a cloud bucket and one on each laptop that needed it at a usable speed. If a team got fed up with sync and built its own workaround, there's another in there too. The storage bill pays for every one of them.

The Register and LucidLink are hosting senior technology, infrastructure and data leaders at the Covent Garden Hotel on Tuesday 24 November to compare how they're bringing that number down. Joe Fay of The Register is in the chair.

Nobody keeps duplicates for fun. They're what people do when files have to move between sites faster than sync allows. Every copy IT can't see into is a security question as well as a cost, so the duplicates you don't know about matter most.

What the table wants to hear is how others got the copies down without ripping out storage they've already paid for, or the governance built around it. The evening runs under the Chatham House Rule, so people say what worked and what didn't. Alan Marshall, Strategic Director, EMEA at LucidLink, joins a small group of peers. Nobody presents.

Drinks are at 6.30pm and dinner around one table runs until 9pm. Places are limited.
