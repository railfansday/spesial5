Iowa football vs. Illinois: Who wins? Final score predictions

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

For the final time in the 2026 regular season, the Iowa football team will hit the road for a game.

The Hawkeyes (8-2, 5-2 Big Ten) are set to take on the Illinois Fighting Illini (6-4, 4-3 Big Ten) in Champaign, Ill., on Saturday afternoon. Bret Bielema's Illini are a banged-up group, but will be playing with a lot of pride against their border rivals. At home, this is a win they desperately want.

Iowa needs a win to keep their slim College Football Playoff hopes alive. With enough chaos around them, a 10-2 Hawkeyes team could sneak into the CFP. But none of that is possible without a win over Illinois. Kirk Ferentz's team is 3-1 on the road this season. They'll look to continue being road warriors this weekend.

Before these two rivals hit the gridiron, it's time to predict who comes out on top. Here are the Hawkeyes Wire team's official predictions for Iowa vs. Illinois.

Iowa needs a win to keep its CFP hopes alive. The Hawkeyes meet a well-coached Illinois squad standing in the way that would love to play spoiler.

While early-week indications were that Illinois starting quarterback Katin Houser may be sidelined, it seems like it's trending toward Houser giving it a go. Illinois head coach Bret Bielema said on Thursday that Houser was still in concussion protocol after undergoing surgery earlier this week to repair a broken nose.

But, Houser was upgraded from doubtful to questionable on Friday's availability report, indicating that he could be trending toward playing.

At this point, I'd expect Houser to start for the Illini.

Ultimately, even with Houser in the mix, Iowa should be able to find some explosives between Hank Brown and Tony Diaz. Look for DJ Vonnahme to have a big day, too. And I like Iowa's defense to make life difficult for Houser.

Hawkeyes win a tight one and head home for a regular-season finale against Nebraska with CFP implications on the line.

While Illinois is no easy opponent, the Fighting Illini have major injury concerns at quarterback.

Illinois allows 128.9 rushing yards per game, which should give running back LJ Phillips Jr. a chance to have a big day. Illinois allows the most passing yards per game in the Big Ten (246.3), so Hank Brown should be able to take advantage through the air.

With Iowa the better, healthier team in this matchup, I believe Iowa will look to make a statement to the College Football Playoff committee and win big on the road.

It's hard to look past Illinois' injury situation entering this game. Starting quarterback Katin Houser is questionable after undergoing surgery for a broken nose earlier this week. The backup quarterback, Michael Clayton II, is unavailable for this game due to a throwing-hand injury. That leaves Carson Boyd as the potential starter.

Boyd has thrown four passes in his entire collegiate career. Making his first career start against a Phil Parker defense is very favorable for the road team. If that's the case, the Hawkeyes should be in a good spot here. They'll have to shut down Collin Dixon, who's been one of the best wide receivers in the country this season. Iowa does that. This should be a low-scoring affair for Illinois.

Offensively, Iowa's running game is finding a rhythm and has a nice matchup. The Illini are giving up 128.9 yards per game on the ground. This should be a huge LJ Phillips Jr. day. But Hank Brown will also have an opportunity to shine. Illinois is last in the Big Ten in passing yards allowed per game, giving up 246.3 per contest. Iowa needs to make them pay through the air.

Ultimately, Iowa is the better, and healthier, team here. Throw in the fact that a third-string quarterback may be making his first career start, and this is a game the Hawkeyes can't afford to lose. Illinois hangs around early, but Iowa pulls away to improve to 9-2.
