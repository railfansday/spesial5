What does life after Kevin Durant look like for the Rockets?

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Alperen Sengun is 24. Amen Thompson and Jabari Smith Jr. are both 23. Tari Eason is 25. Kevin Durant is…

So is Fred VanVleet, but that feels inconsequential. Depending on where you stand, the Houston Rockets either need to replace him with a more dynamic offensive guard, solidify a succession plan that puts Reed Sheppard in his place, or replace him with a comparable low-turnover, game-managing point guard.

No matter where you land on VanVleet, replacing him is not a pressing imperative. It will be relatively easy.

Durant is averaging a career-low in field goal attempts per game by a considerable margin. He's shooting 13.4 shots per game, and his next-lowest career mark was 16.0 in 2022-23 with the Phoenix Suns.

That's either intentional or serendipitous.

For reference, that's effectively the same as, for example, the Minnesota Timberwolves' Jaden McDaniels (13.5). Let's use him as an example. Here's the existential question the Rockets need to be asking themselves:

If they replaced Durant with McDaniels, right now, how much worse would they be?

That's the type of player they can acquire relatively easily. Not McDaniels specifically: he's the premium model. He'll likely never be a Rocket, and he's just an example. More broadly, with their assets, the Rockets could find a high-end three-and-D wing tomorrow.

The offense might not be as different as you'd think. In 2026-27, Durant has a 65.4% True Shooting% (TS%) to McDaniels' 63.8%. The most devious devil in the details is that there's no massive gap between the two in shot creation this year. In 2026-27, 60.2% of Durant's two-point field goals have been assisted, and 86.7% of his threes, compared to 55.7% on twos and 93.1% on threes for McDaniels.

The Rockets don't seem to need Durant to create offense at a high volume. The Rockets' 121.1 Offensive Rating is the best in the NBA. Surprisingly enough, defense has been an issue this year, as their 113.0 Defensive Rating ranks just 11th. Wait a minute:

Durant has been the best (and most expensive) decoy in the NBA in 2026-27. His gravity is important to Alperen Sengun's functionality as a hub, especially when so many of the team's actions revolve around Amen Thompson cutting while he's in the high post. That partly works because there's a guy on the perimeter that the defense absolutely cannot leave.

So, let's assume the Rockets don't need Durant's creation, but still need his gravity. This gets tougher. Defenses won't guard McDaniels the way they guard Durant. They would scramble to make sure that, say, Corey Kispert isn't open, but he can't defend well enough to find regular minutes anywhere.

We know the Rockets will lean defense. Suppose they draft the next McDaniels in 2027, or sign someone, or whatever: Imagine they bring in a high-end three-and-D who doesn't create much offense to replace Durant when he retires.

They will take a step back, but I propose that it won't be too substantial a step. I can live with that:

Fundamentally, that's what this boils down to. If the Rockets can stomach a quick fix, replacing Durant with a young(er), low-usage, high-impact wing should suffice. If not, they'll need to be star-hunting as Durant approaches the end of his career. By that logic, he'll need to be replaced both positionally and functionally, so they could get that low-usage wing and a star guard, or vice versa.

Only then can this room be elephant-free.
