As LeBron soars up all-time assist list, his teammates need to have their hands ready

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Fourteen games into the season, the Sixers are noticing the LeBron James effect in just about every facet of life. After a win over the Indiana Pacers, Tyrese Maxey zoomed to the podium to knock out his media availability with the majority of the Sixers beat in the locker room interviewing James.

"I love LeBron James," Maxey said as he sat down in front of a room with only a couple reporters.

Of course, they're noticing it in plenty of ways on the court as well. That win saw LeBron record 11 assists, good enough to move him up ahead of Jason Kidd for third in all-time career assists. He now only trails Chris Paul and John Stockton.

It may not sound like it would be, but playing next to such a prolific passer is something they have to get used to. The fact that your teammate could hit you with the ball at any given moment is an adjustment.

On the first play of the game, Maxey was cutting down the baseline just for the sake of cutting through. He thought all he was doing was helping create space on the side of the floor he was vacating, not realizing that LeBron had found him wide open under the basket.

"I was not planning to get the ball and the ball hit me in my chest and I looked up I was at the rim so I had to lay it in and I missed it," Maxey said, "but it's just like plays like that where you got to be ready at all times."

The idea of being ready for the ball at any time when James is on the floor is something that the Sixers are trying to make a point of emphasis. As was the case with Maxey's smoked bunny, the process of learning that can look ugly at times.

James joked that the key to guys figuring it out is to "hit 'em in the head a couple of times." According to James, VJ Edgecombe was a particularly frequent victim of that during training camp.

"Even when you don't think you're open, you're gonna be open," is what James told Edgecombe and, frankly, all of his teammates. "I just tell my guys, always be paying attention to me when I got the ball because I definitely see plays develop before they happen."

Off-ball movement is often looked at as "dirty work" in the NBA, something the Sixers haven't excelled at in recent years. Because of James' ability to reward cutters, it's made the Sixers not just buy into it, but happy to do so.

"He makes you want to cut. He makes you want to play without the ball," Maxey said. "He makes you want to always have your hands ready and be ready to score the ball because he's that unselfish and he's going to find you."

They say raw assist numbers are not the truest mark of a passer or a facilitator. One of James' best assists of the game against Indiana was not given to him in the stat sheet, but it was entirely created by what he saw from outside the perimeter.

Dean Wade cut out of the strong side corner to open that up from the ball handlers, before hearing James tell him to run back. James saw that Wade's defender would help on Edgecombe once Edgecombe drove to the basket, leaving Wade wide open for a corner three. Steve Jones, a brilliant Xs and Os guy for The Dunker Spot, clipped the play.

After being around James for training camp and a handful of regular season games, Wade said he's not really surprised by LeBron's ability to make that kind of read in the moment.

"He sees everything that's going on," Wade said. "He knows what their defense is doing."

James has had a reputation for being able to diagnose opposing team's plays as they're being called. Pacers head coach Rick Carlisle, who called James an "NBA basketball AI machine," said that LeBron is the reason he doesn't have play calls anymore. James said that ability is something he's just always had with him.

"I've always, since I was a kid, had the ability to see plays before they actually happened," he said, "seeing the movement, being able to see like cracks in the defense before they was even open. It's something that's been with me since I was a kid."

The Sixers have struggled so far to put together all of this absurd offensive talent in a way that really clicks. Even after beating the Pacers, they are still 19th in the league in points per 100 possessions, per Cleaning the Glass.

Getting used to the tendencies of one of the best passers of all time should help things look smoother as the season goes on.
