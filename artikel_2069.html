West Ham v Preston Preview: Team News & Lineups as Parker Faces Must-Win Home Debut

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

West Ham United v Preston North End EFL Championship London Stadium Saturday 21 November 2026, 3pm GMT

Scott Parker’s first home match as West Ham United head coach is massively important. Preston North End are bottom of the Championship and, while Saturday’s fixture is not mathematically must-win, realistically it feels very close to it.

West Ham have collected one point from Parker’s opening two games and must start closing the gap to the automatic-promotion places. After two uninterrupted weeks with much of his squad, this is the first proper opportunity to judge whether the new head coach has begun improving the organisation and intensity of the team.

There is also the troubling matter of West Ham’s home form. I worried before relegation that the vast gaps between the London Stadium crowd and the pitch might turn it into a disadvantage rather than an advantage, and we now desperately need to begin winning there consistently.

The London Stadium has not remotely resembled a fortress this season. Opponents have arrived believing they can frustrate West Ham, exploit the anxiety around the ground and capitalise when the players become nervous.

As one former Championship striker recently observed, visiting teams have been enjoying the London Stadium experience. That situation has to change if West Ham are serious about challenging for automatic promotion.

Parker needs his players to begin quickly and give the crowd something to respond to. The longer this remains goalless, the more Preston will believe their long-ball approach and deep defensive shape can earn them something.

Scott Parker will need to make the London Stadium feel like home

West Ham will dominate possession, so the challenge will be using it properly. Passing sideways in front of an organised defence will achieve very little; we need quicker movement, runners beyond the striker and enough tempo to pull Preston out of position.

I expect West Ham to be considerably tighter defensively following Parker’s first extended spell on the training pitch. However, the international break has not been quite as useful as it initially appeared because several important players were away representing their countries.

Konstantinos Mavropanos was among those absent, while Max Kilman is suspended after reaching the disciplinary threshold at Blackburn. Parker has therefore been unable to work throughout the fortnight with the central-defensive partnership he will need on Saturday.

I suspect Morato will move into his natural centre-back position alongside Mavropanos. That is probably the safe and predictable selection, although I would prefer to see Airidas Golambeckis given the opportunity.

The teenager has generally impressed when called upon and deserves serious consideration. As our examination of Parker’s defensive conundrum against Preston highlighted, the decision appears to be between Morato’s experience and Golambeckis’ encouraging performances.

Joël Veltman should start at right-back, while Ezra Mayers deserves to retain his place on the left. Kyle Walker-Peters continues to be assessed, but Parker has two natural and capable full-back options without needing to rush him back.

The biggest selection question surrounds the goalkeeper. I am finding Parker’s team difficult to predict primarily because I do not know whether he will retain Mads Hermansen or turn to Alphonse Areola.

Hermansen has produced an inconsistent sequence of performances. He excelled for Denmark against Portugal before making another clanger during the defeat by Norway, which will not have made Parker’s decision any easier.

There has been considerable pressure for West Ham to make a change in goal, but Areola’s own fitness has reportedly been an issue. Until Parker confirms otherwise, my best guess is that Hermansen will retain his place.

I suspect Parker will stick with Hermansen

This is an ideal fixture in which to rebuild the goalkeeper’s confidence, but Preston’s likely use of long balls and set pieces means he must command his penalty area properly. Another nervous display would only intensify the debate but my own choice would be to select Finn Herrick.

I expect Parker to continue with the 4-2-3-1 formation. Arne Engels and Soungoutou Magassa should form the central-midfield partnership, with Divine Mukasa operating further forward.

Magassa gives West Ham a recognised holding midfielder and should help protect a changed defence. That will allow Engels to move the ball forward and support Mukasa rather than both central midfielders occupying similar positions.

Preston’s victory over Southampton was built around an aggressive pressing approach, something Parker will have worked on during the break. I previously argued that West Ham must learn to play through Preston’s press, and this midfield should contain enough composure and energy to do it.

Jarrod Bowen will naturally start on the right, with Edvin Austbø on the left. In my opinion, Austbø has moved ahead of Manor Solomon in the pecking order and deserves a proper run in the team.

Joël Piroe should lead the attack. Parker needs to give West Ham’s most proven Championship goalscorer an extended opportunity rather than continually rotating strikers who have struggled to find any rhythm.

I expect five players who were away on international duty—Hermansen, Mavropanos, Mukasa, Austbø and Piroe—to start. The key defensive change should see Morato move inside to replace the suspended Kilman.

Preston earned their first victory under Graham Alexander by beating Southampton before the international break, so they should arrive with renewed confidence. However, both of their Championship wins this season have come at home.

Their away record is dreadful. Preston are winless on the road, having drawn once and lost six of their seven away league matches, including some heavy defeats.

I expect them to use plenty of long balls, remain compact and try to frustrate West Ham. Even so, the Hammers have far better players and will enjoy the lion’s share of possession.

Quite frankly, it is time they showed the difference in quality. If West Ham cannot beat the Championship’s bottom club at home after Parker has been given two weeks to prepare, serious questions will remain about our promotion credentials.

Preston may make the opening stages awkward, but West Ham should eventually have far too much attacking quality. Parker’s team need to play with intensity, move the ball quickly and turn possession into genuine chances rather than merely dominating the statistics.

I expect a much more organised performance following the international break. Preston’s direct approach may still produce a goal, particularly with West Ham fielding a changed central defence, but this should be the afternoon Parker claims his first victory.

Prediction: West Ham United 4-1 Preston North End
