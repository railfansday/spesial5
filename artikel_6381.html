If you’re looking for an efficient way to heat your home during the cold winter months, pellet stoves offer several advantages over wood stoves or fireplaces.

The novelty of a wood burning fireplace wears off when you have to trudge outside to grab a bundle of logs. Installing a wood burning stove can be expensive, and once it’s running, it produces ash that needs to be cleaned out. Pellet stoves, however, are an efficient, convenient option for heating a room while appreciating the glow of a live fire.

Powered by compressed hardwood pellets stored in a hopper that can hold over 100 pounds, pellet stoves can provide a fire that burns for dozens of hours without needing to be tended. Those pellets drop into a combustion chamber where they are burned to produce heat. Exhaust is then vented through a flue pipe out a sidewall or your roof.

A pellet stove offers consistent, reliable heat without a lot of fuss. More expensive models will cost several thousand dollars, but often come with programmable digital thermostats and larger hoppers that allow a fire to burn longer without the need for additional pellets being added.

Wood stoves require a bit more effort and knowledge—you’ll have to learn about the different burn rates of wood and clean out ash more regularly. Still, wood burning stoves are typically quieter and may last longer than pellet stoves because they don’t have electronic components.

As for installation, freestanding pellet stoves need an available outlet with an open plug and a vent to the outside, which may require the installer to cut a hole in the wall. If your power goes out often during storms, that means your pellet stove won’t work without a generator or separate power source. Pellet stoves that insert into your fireplace require a chimney liner, a pipe that vents gas outside.

A pellet stove can be a big investment—multiple thousands of dollars to cover the stove and installation—so it’s important that you find one that fits your room and heats it efficiently.

Pellet stoves come in three main styles: freestanding, wall mounted, and insert. A freestanding stove—which can be larger or more powerful than the other options—evokes a classic cabin feel and sits on the ground or on feet. A wall-mounted stove hangs free of the floor and kicks out heat like the heads of a mini split. Inserts are meant to slot inside your fireplace and could take a non-functioning fireplace and make it a focal point of your room once again.

Pellet stoves are efficient, producing less creosote (the tarry byproduct of burning wood) and ash than wood stoves. The compact pellets—thanks to their small size and low moisture content—burn hot and quickly. Pellet stoves that receive certification from the United States Environmental Protection Agency typically offer between 70 and 83 percent fuel efficiency. In some cases, certification comes with a tax credit so check before you purchase a given model.

Pellet stoves often have multiple settings. Higher settings release more pellets so the stove can burn hotter, whereas the lowest setting will enable you pellets to last longer, but you might not get as much heat. A stove with a smaller hopper running outdoors may only last 6 hours per full hopper, like the Even Embers Pellet Stove, which is fine if you’re looking to gather in the backyard at night. Larger pellet stoves can burn unattended for more than a day, like the PelPro Pellet Stove which can run for 40 hours.

The larger the hopper, the longer the burn time, and the longer you can go between pellet refills. Most hoppers will need to be refilled once a day. But larger hoppers (60 pounds and up) typically mean bigger stoves, so you’ll have to consider whether or not it will fit your space. Likewise, it can be cumbersome to pour heavier bags—50 pounds at a time—into the hopper.

The heating ability of pellet stoves is measured in British Thermal Units (BTUs) per hour. The higher the BTUs, the easier it will be for a stove to heat a room or multiple rooms. If you’re considering heating multiple rooms or a whole home, a pellet stove rated at 60,000 BTUs can heat approximately 2,000 square feet.

Warranties typically cover parts and repairs for several years. A well-maintained pellet stove should last longer than the warranty—a decade or more if properly maintained; but it’s a comfort to have some financial coverage in the first few years after investing a decent amount of money in a pellet stove.

Empty your ash pan and clean the firebox once or twice a week. If your pellet stove has baffles, clean them every few months. And you’ll need to do a deep clean of the venting probably once a year.

Check your manual for the specific instructions, but cleaning can follow a simple rule—if you see ash build up, clean it out once your stove has cooled.

Pellet stoves heat up quickly—it’s one of their best features. They’ll be raising the temperature of your room often inside of 30 minutes.

Pellets may be more expensive; but they’re convenient. Pellets take up less space and produce less ash than firewood. Wood stoves can be used for cooking (even if it’s just s’mores) and require tending that you’ll either see as a nice part of the ritual or a chore that you’d rather not do.
