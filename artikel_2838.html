Northern Bedford knocks off Meyersdale in D5-1A football title game

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

WINDBER — A key injury, a snow squall, and turnovers proved too much for Meyersdale to overcome, falling to Northern Bedford County 21-0 in the District 5 Class 1A football championship game, Friday, Nov. 13, in Windber.

For Meyersdale head coach Ryan Donaldson's Red Raiders, who were held to under 100 yards of offense, this is one that will hurt for a while.

"It stings right now, especially for the kids," said Donaldson. "They have been working hard through the 12 weeks of the season. I am proud of them for getting here, but we fell short tonight. First half, it's a 0-0 ball game. It was a dog fight, and in the second half, things didn't go our way, and it got out of hand quickly. Losing quarterback Spencer Whitfield was key. One of our guys we relied on for both sides of the ball, but we still had a game to play with the guys that were in there, but they left it out on the field."

An 18-play drive by Northern Bedford County ended when the Meyersdale defense batted away a scoring opportunity for the Black Panthers inside the 10-yard line as play moved into the second quarter.

The teams traded punts, and then an injury to Whitfield on defense seemed to change the complexion of the game.

Northern Bedford County (7-5), the No. 4 seed, had an opportunity late in the half. However, a Trace Baker pass was intercepted by Meyersdale's Carter Vogt inside the 5-yard line to end the threat, sending the teams to the locker room in a scoreless tie.

Disaster struck for the Red Raiders on the opening kickoff of the second half as snow began to fall. The ball was fumbled and recovered by the Black Panthers at the Meyersdale 32-yard line.

Two plays later Baker found a wide-open Jordan Free for a touchdown.

After Meyersdale (7-5), the No. 2 seed, turned the ball over on downs on its next series, Baker used his legs this time, running off right tackle and making a few players miss on the snow-covered field, racing 67 yards for the touchdown to extend the lead to 14-0.

As the game moved to the fourth quarter, Northern Bedford County's Brett Cottle added an 8-yard touchdown run for a 21-0 advantage.

Meyersdale backup quarterback Ryder Reese was intercepted by Cottle on the first play of the ensuing series, and the Black Panthers controlled the ball the rest of the way. This was the second time Northern Bedford County defeated Meyersdale this season.

Northern Bedford County meets the District 3 champion in the first round of the PIAA playoffs.

Northern Bedford County head coach Garry Black tipped his hat to Meyersdale.

"That is a team that doesn't start a single senior on their offensive line, and that is a good young football team over there," said Black. "The fumble to start the second half was huge. In the first half we just didn't finish plays, but starting with that play to start the second half, it was a different story. I am just so proud of these guys for adding that '26 to the banner in the gym."
