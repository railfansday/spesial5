3 thoughts ahead of the Mavericks’ back-to-back bonanza at the Utah Jazz

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

It may feel like the season just started, but the Dallas Mavericks (9-7) are already in the middle of the team's fourth back-to-back set as they turn the team charter toward the great Salt Lake, with a quick turnaround before facing the Utah Jazz (3-14) at the Delta Center. The Mavericks beat the Los Angeles Clippers (4-11) at home on Saturday, 110-100.

Dallas salvaged a clunker on Saturday, and if there is one thing the Jazz are accustomed to this year, it's clunkers.

Let's take a swim in the salty waters out west and get a look at what lies ahead for the Mavs in Utah.

The back end of a back-to-back set for the Mavericks is the front end of one for Utah in this one. The Jazz are coming off of Friday's 111-101 home loss to the Phoenix Suns. Rookie guard Darryn Peterson was gaining some steam earlier in November with five straight games of 21 or more points before a three-game lull preceded his 20-point night in the loss to the Suns. Still, he's shooting just 36.3% from the field and just 26.1% from 3-point range in his last four.

While Peterson is averaging 16.7 points per game through the first 17 games of his NBA career, Keyonte George leads the team at 21.1 points and 7.1 assists per game, while key contributors Lauri Markkanen (hand), Jaren Jackson Jr. (knee), and noted Mavs killer Jusuf Nurkic (foot) will all miss Sunday's game with injuries. As a result, the Jazz come into the game against Dallas with an offensive rating of just 106.9, next to last in the league, ahead of only the Charlotte Hornets (103.1). They're 25th in the NBA in net rating, at -7.7.

The Mavs had some trouble putting the struggling Clippers away in the front end of their back-to-back set on Saturday, but Sunday's game in Utah presents another opportunity to hone that edge to their game that has been hit-and-miss since the team's 7-4 start to the year.

Keep an eye on who is available for the Mavericks ahead of Sunday's game in Utah. Kyrie Irving, who scored 25 points on 8-of-24 shooting in 35 minutes in Saturday's win over the Clippers, has played in both legs of one of the three previous back-to-back sets so far this season. We're just guessing, but the established trend and the opponent offer the Mavs another opportunity to do the "injury management" dance.

The Mavs were also without PJ Washington (illness) on Saturday, and while the hope would be that one game off would be enough to get Washington right, we won't know for sure until about noon on Sunday for sure on their status. If those two are unable to go, the viewing experience could end up anywhere from "meh" to downright brutal.

Lost in the slog of Saturday's squeaker over the Clippers was a rare good shooting night from Max Christie. On a night when Cooper Flagg's shot diet (6-of-16 from the field) was downright unhealthy and the rest of the offense sputtered around him, the Mavericks needed all 13 of Christie's points in his 24 minutes on the floor after he had worked his way into the Dusty May Phantom Zone after a slow start to the year.

Both Christie and Walter Clayton Jr. (16 points, 5-of-8 shooting, 2-of-3 from 3-point range) were positives off the bench for the Mavericks, and they will both likely be called upon to do it again on Sunday, especially if Irving is inactive.

The Mavericks and the Jazz tip off at 6 p.m. on Sunday at the Delta Center. The game will be televised locally on KFAA Channel 29, or you can catch the stream on MavsTV or NBA League Pass where available. The radio call can be heard on KRLD 105.3 The Fan.
