Who’s the Detroit News Boys High School Team of the Week? Vote now!

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Every week, Michigan high school teams across the state are winning championships, breaking records, reaching milestones and putting together seasons worth celebrating.

The Detroit News is recognizing those standout squads as Team of the Week. From nominations submitted by coaches, parents and fans, we've selected five boys teams as this week's finalists.

Any boys team, in any sport and from any region of Michigan, is eligible. Now it's your turn to decide who deserves the honor.

Voting is open from 10 a.m. Monday through 5 p.m. Thursday. Readers can vote multiple times, so rally your school community and get everyone involved.

The winner will be announced Friday morning in a special Detroit News post celebrating its accomplishments.

Who deserves to be named the Detroit News Boys Team of the Week? Cast your vote below.

Birmingham Seaholm didn't just repeat as Division 2 boys tennis state champion. It dominated the competition, winning 35 matches at the state finals, the most of any program.

Seaholm claimed five flight championships, with all eight flights advancing to at least the semifinals.

Senior Kalil Wahab and freshman Hunter Helfman went unbeaten in five matches, winning every one in straight sets. That included a 7-6 (1), 6-4 victory in the No. 1 doubles final.

Sophomore Charlie Griffith also came through, winning his second consecutive individual title at No. 3 singles. He battled through a three-set final, 6-2, 5-7, 6-3, and finished the season with a 20-4 record.

It was another dominant performance for a program that continues to set the standard in Division 2.

Holland Calvary Christian didn't leave much doubt about who belonged at the top of Division 4 boys cross country.

The team put all five scorers in the top 15 Saturday at Michigan International Speedway, cruising to its second consecutive state championship with 35 points. No boys team in any division scored better at the state finals.

Senior Noah Schipper led the way with a fifth-place finish in 16:03.7. Senior Christian Getz wasn't far behind, taking seventh in 16:06.1.

Senior Brody Van Tuinen also delivered, finishing 12th to earn his first All-State honor.

With all five scorers in the top 15, Holland Calvary Christian put together one of the day's most impressive performances and defended its state championship in convincing fashion.

Troy put together a season nobody else in Michigan boys soccer could match.

The team finished 19-0-2, becoming the state's only unbeaten boys soccer program. It capped its historic run with a 2-1 victory over Livonia Stevenson in the Division 1 state championship Saturday at Grand Ledge High School.

It was Troy's second state championship and first since 2003.

Senior defender Faris Ibrahim and sophomore midfielder Connor Hotts each scored on a set piece in the championship game. Hotts finished the season with 20 goals and 14 assists.

Troy also took down Salem, 5-2, in Wednesday's semifinal before closing out its unbeaten season with another championship performance.

Warren De La Salle has turned winning Division 2 state championships into an annual tradition.

The team captured its third consecutive state title Saturday at Grand Ledge High School, holding off St. Joseph, 3-2, in a back-and-forth championship game.

Senior forward Andrew Corder was the difference-maker all weekend. He scored twice in a 3-2 semifinal victory over DeWitt before adding two more goals and an assist against St. Joseph.

Senior goalkeeper Dominic Baldarotta also came up big, making several acrobatic catches on long passes into the box as St. Joseph pushed for a late equalizer.

The championship was De La Salle's eighth overall, giving the program the third-most state titles of any boys soccer team in Michigan, regardless of division.

After going without a district championship since 2002, Jonesville finally broke through Friday, Nov. 6, with a 52-30 victory over former Big 8 rival Union City in the Division 7 playoffs.

The win capped a remarkable turnaround for a team that lost four consecutive games early in the season before winning five straight.

Senior running back John Milks led the way with 190 yards and three TDs on 26 carries, pushing his season total past 2,300 yards. Senior quarterback Carson Young threw for 114 yards and three TDs, connecting with senior Reece Rundquist for two scores.

The defense also delivered three takeaways, highlighted by sophomore Lucas Stanton's 75-yard fumble return for a TD. Stanton added five tackles, while senior Harper Griffiths led the team with nine.

At 7-4, Jonesville is making its deepest playoff run since that 2002 championship season.
