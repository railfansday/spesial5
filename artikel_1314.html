Former Professional Game Match Officials Limited (PGMOL) boss Keith Hackett has taken aim at an ex-Premier League referee after he admitted to having struggled at two stadiums.

Hackett, 82, began officiating back in the 1980s, going on to become one of the top 100 referees listed by the International Federation of Football History and Statistics (IFFHS).

He has also taken charge of matches in the Olympic Games as well as the 1984 and 1988 Euros. Having also refereed the FA Cup final, the Milk Cup final and the FA Charity Shield.

Recently, Hackett took to social media to criticise former Premier League referee Graham Scott, who admitted to having struggled at two English grounds.

While speaking to The Athletic, Scott, 58, picked out two stadiums in the Premier League that were tough to ‘stay neutral’ at.

He explained that Everton’s former home, Goodison Park, was the most hostile atmosphere, while Manchester United’s Old Trafford also proved to be challenging. Speaking about referee bias, Scott began:

“Of course it could be a thing… It’s subconscious; I may not even know it’s there! I don’t know where the truth lies.

"The evidence of VAR would actually suggest there is some home bias going on, in that VAR tends to be correcting more in favour of the away team. So there is something in it."

“I’m not sure about the bigger club, but then I would say that, wouldn’t I? There’s certainly no organised sense of, ‘We need to look after these people’ or not look after them.”

Scott, who worked in the Premier League between 2015 and 2025, was then asked whether there were stadiums where he found it tough to ‘withstand pressure and stay neutral’ while officiating a match.

He replied: “The most hostile ground used to be Goodison. Partly because of its geography, everything was very tight.

“And partly because people in the crowd were all up for the game. But Goodison was always really tight; I guess in your own mind you need to be strong, be resolute, and make sure you do the right thing. A great place to referee, and a great atmosphere.

“Old Trafford became very sanitised for a time, where it just felt like everyone was waiting to be entertained, or only there to complain about owners rather than the referee. From a refereeing point of view, it could be quite straightforward.”

However, Scott’s quotes have not impressed ex-PGMOL boss Hackett, who wrote on social media: “Oh dear no wonder Graham didn’t hit the Elite level of officiating.”

Scott’s refereeing career started with the National League in 2006 before he moved into the English Football League in 2008, rising to the Premier League in 2015.

The 58-year-old stated that Manchester City star Jeremy Doku would be the player he would most like to referee.

“Of course, he appeals for free kicks and penalties, but I have not seen him do so unreasonably and I am yet to see him dive.”
