Ipswich Town agreement with Chelsea must make Will Lankshear think twice

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Will Lanskhear is in the midst of one of the best individual scoring seasons the Championship has ever seen, to the point where there are murmurs of him being tipped to make a move to an elite club as soon as January.

Middlesbrough are reportedly dismissive of those links, which see the 21-year-old admired by the likes of Arsenal and Manchester United, and over the weekend, the Sun stated that Boro had slapped a £70 million price tag on the exciting frontman's head.

Ultimately, that price tag is an indication that Lankshear isn't going anywhere anytime soon, but it won't be long before those links to the traditional 'big six' sides gain serious legs. The 21-year-old was being suggested for the England squad before injury struck just before the international break.

But he wouldn't be the first young English frontman to make the step to a top club early in his career if he did move on from Middlesbrough in the next 12 months, and he'll have to be wary of being caught up in the expectation that would surround such a move.

It's been a much-discussed question over the past couple of years or so, as Harry Kane entered his 30s, as to who the 'next Harry Kane' would be.

Luckily enough, the now all-time record appearance maker for the Three Lions hasn't slowed down; if anything, he's gotten better. But just last summer, that successor looked to be Liam Delap, after he was one of the better performers in Ipswich Town's sole Premier League campaign in 2024/25.

The frontman shone under Liam Rosenior at Hull City during the 2023/24 Championship season, and earned a Premier League move off the back of that with the newly-promoted Tractor Boys.

Ipswich went straight back down, owing mainly to poor defensive numbers. Going forward, Kieran McKenna's side only scored six fewer goals than Everton and eight fewer than Manchester United. Delap was the main source of those, netting 12.

He only netted one brace, too, so he scored in 11 of his 37 league games that year. For his first year at the level in a relegated side, the then-22-year-old was being tipped for big things, and elite Premier League clubs were interested in him.

After relegation, Delap moved from Ipswich to Chelsea for a £30 million fee.

The Blues fended off interest from Manchester United, Newcastle United and Everton for his signature, and many expected him to net much more than the 12 he did at Portman Road in a side playing in the UEFA Champions League.

Delap contributed to Chelsea winning the Club World Cup in America during the summer of 2025, appearing in every game he was available for and scoring against Esperance.

He was sidelined just three games into the new season with a hamstring injury, though, and upon his return a couple of months later against Wolves in the EFL Cup, he was sent off after receiving two yellow cards in the space of seven minutes.

Then-Chelsea boss Enzo Maresca called the sending-off "very stupid" and "embarrassing," and he couldn't really recover after that, even despite his former manager Liam Rosenior coming in after the Italian left in January.

Delap ended the season with one Premier League goal and two in 41 appearances in all competitions. His time at Stamford Bridge was quite a disaster, and he left this past summer. Somehow, Chelsea managed to bank £50 million for his services from Nottingham Forest.

Lankshear already has five more goals in around 1,200 fewer minutes this season than Delap did in his last Championship campaign in 2023/24.

It's evident that he's further along in his development at this stage than his compatriot, but the 21-year-old could look at Delap's trajectory in the past couple of years as an example of what not to do.

Joining these big clubs is always a risk, as the amount of pressure on these strikers is massive. It's not just young strikers who fail, either. Look at Viktor Gyokeres, for example; a goalscoring machine at Sporting is now playing back up at Arsenal.

The weight of expectation is even bigger when you're an English striker, too, especially one who has been talked about for a call-up while in the Championship. There'll be a demand for Lankshear to hit the ground running, and if he didn't, he'd be the one being shot at online and amongst pundits.

Honing his craft and not running before he can walk would be wise for Lankshear. It seems as if Middlesbrough are set on keeping him past January, and the likelihood would be that, if they were promoted, he'd do at least one Premier League season at the Riverside, too.

That's not to say that Lankshear should remain out of the spotlight for the next five or so years, however. It's evident that the 21-year-old knows how to handle the mantle of being a line-leader for a side expected to win games at Middlesbrough.

His purpose in Kim Hellberg's side is to get on the end of the many chances that those behind him create, and he's not only gotten into the right positions consistently, but he's finished opportunities exceptionally.

Lankshear could probably make the jump to a Premier League side in the next year or so, but at Middlesbrough, he's in a position where he can afford to gain more and more confidence as the focal point of a good team, with there still plenty of time for him to make that step-up.

If he does go to an Arsenal or a Manchester United in the future, doing so as a 23-year-old with a hundred-odd appearances for Middlesbrough, some of which coming in the Premier League, would suit him much better in the long term.
