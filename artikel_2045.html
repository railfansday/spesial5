Sven Botman can transform Newcastle's defence as Matthias Jaissle's answer to new trend

Newcastle have been poor this season, but Sven Botman will be able to hold his head high.

Matthias Jaissle is undoubtedly under pressure, with his football not producing the results that fans want.

Heading into this international break, the Geordies are 12th in the table with just three wins after 10 games.

Botman has been one of the few positives, with his physical presence giving Newcastle incredible value at the back.

He has consistently shown he is willing to put his body on the line, protecting his goal, even if the results don't ultimately end up in his favour.

And he may prove even more key as the latest trend in the Premier League emerges.

The Premier League is seeing a growing trend of tall and physical strikers becoming the norm.

The era of the big man up top looks to be returning, with the top four goalscorers in the English top flight this season all coming in at over 6'2".

Erling Haaland (nine), Alexander Isak (seven), Thierno Barry (six) and Nicolas Jackson (six) are all proving their worth down the middle this season.

That trend will mean that teams must be more proactive in dealing with the threats they pose, with aerial battles in particular being important.

Botman, though, is already proving to be the ready-made solution at Newcastle, with his data showcasing his quality.

The Dutch defender wins more aerial duels per 90 than any other Premier League centre-back with 5.24, according to Data.MB.

He also competes in the second-most aerial duels per 90 in England's top flight, with 6.81.

Things have not gone well for Jaissle at Newcastle, with his direct and vertical football not providing the results.

Botman could be a key player in turning things around, as his ability to head the ball clear could end up creating counter-attacking opportunities for Newcastle.

If the Geordies can force the opposition to commit many players into the box, while leaving a threat of their own, Botman's clearances could see them generate chances.

Of course, that would also rely on the Dutchman directing his headers in the right direction as well.
