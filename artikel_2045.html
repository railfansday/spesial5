West Ham Stadium Concert Decision Raises Questions Over £15m Forecast

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Earlier this week, Newham Council resolved to grant planning permission for changes that will enable the London Stadium to host more major concerts and entertainment events.

London Stadium released a statement saying:

“The decision by Newham Council represents a significant milestone in the Stadium’s ambition to attract more world-class entertainment and sporting events to East London, delivering increased opportunities for visitors, businesses and the local community.”

The permission will allow London Stadium to increase its annual allocation of concerts and other non-sporting leisure events from 10 to 24, alongside the ability to host up to eight events each year at a maximum capacity of 85,000.

There will be no changes to West Ham United’s fixtures or the club’s current matchday capacity of 62,500. No physical changes to the stadium are required.

The application was supported by an economic impact assessment forecasting the potential to generate an additional £15 million for the Newham economy through up to 800,000 additional visits to the stadium each year.

New London Stadium CEO Darren Raczkowski said:

“This is a hugely positive outcome for London Stadium and for East London. We are delighted that Newham Council has recognised the opportunity these changes present, both for the Stadium and the wider local economy.”

However, on closer scrutiny, you have to ask what the point of increasing to 24 days is and whether the claimed benefits will ever be realised.

Before planning permission was granted, the London Stadium could host up to 10 days of concerts each year, a limit it has never reached in the ten years since opening.

To date, since 2016, no more than five days (half the previous planning permission allowance for concerts) have been used in any single year, so the change seems a bit of wishful thinking to go over ten and reach the dizzy heights of 24 days.

The cost of hosting concerts outside the summer break, while the seats are moved, is likely to be prohibitive. The cost of moving seats remains in the millions, and laying out protective flooring for one concert in an international break could make the venue commercially unattractive, even if the seats remained in place.

The concert venue marketplace is competitive, with Wembley, the O2, Spurs Stadium and Emirates. Brentford are also looking to use their Gtech Community Stadium for future concerts.
