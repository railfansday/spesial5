This Tech Wreck Looks Just Like 2018. Back Then, Here's What Happened Next.

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

By Billy Duberstein – Nov 23, 2026 at 9:30AM EST

10 stocks we like better than NASDAQ Composite Index ›

The last two months have been brutal for the technology sector, especially semiconductors. Today, everyone has an opinion on when the bottom might be in.

Is the AI bubble popping, like the dot-com bubble in 2000? Or is the current downturn like other difficult corrections that preceded big rallies, such as the COVID downturn or the post-COVID inflation scare?

In this investor's eyes, the 2026 tech wreck looks almost exactly like a different year. Here's why today's decline looks so similar, and how the market reacted after that example.

The parallels between this sell-off and that of late 2018 are striking:

The parallels are very striking, though the composition of the decline has nuanced differences. In 2018, the Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC +6.76%) crashed about 23.6% peak-to-trough in late December, finishing the year down roughly 3.9% overall.

Currently, the Nasdaq is down about 13.9% from its Oct. 6 high and is now down 2.7% for the year. While the current top-to-bottom downturn hasn't been as deep, the semiconductor index has seen a larger drawdown than in 2018, over 43% from its highs, versus roughly a 23% drawdown in late 2018. That's in line with the more severe drawdowns in the chip sector in 2022 and early 2025, likely due to concerns that the massive AI build-out will run out of steam.

So while the Nasdaq hasn't seen as big a top-to-bottom drawdown as in 2018, it is down similarly for the year, and certain components, such as the chip subsector, are actually down more.

There are, of course, no perfect parallels, even if history does rhyme, and today's drawdown has both positive and negative differences relative to 2018.

On the positive side, the AI build-out appears to be far larger and earlier in its life cycle than the "boom" of 2018, which was centered on traditional cloud computing. GPU rental prices remain resilient, and token consumption is growing rapidly.

According to OpenRouter, an API service that allows developers to consume tokens from multiple models, token consumption is still rising exponentially, up 25x since January. And the trajectory doesn't seem to be slowing down; October's consumption of 790 trillion tokens grew nearly 37% over September, in just the span of one month!

Of course, there is also the big elephant in the room that wasn't present in 2018: Nvidia (NVDA +32.52%) and the recent scandal surrounding CEO Jensen Huang's untimely resignation.

As of this writing, the SEC has launched an investigation into Nvidia's accounting practices and whether the company knowingly funneled its advanced GPUs to China. Nvidia had also provided financing or an implicit backstop for a web of customers, including start-ups that use all these tokens, as well as the "neoclouds" that provide computing for them.

Whether Nvidia will continue down the path of aggressively funding start-ups and neoclouds remains unknown. If it doesn't, the ultimate impact on the AI trade, which is partly dependent on these start-ups and neoclouds, both of which consume and produce tokens, remains unclear.

The good thing is that the three big hyperscale cloud providers have strong balance sheets to fund the build-out, so it isn't as if their continued investment will stop as long as token demand is there, which it appears to be. However, it remains to be seen whether Nvidia will continue being as aggressive in funding some of its customers and neoclouds as it was before.

The good news for investors is that after the 2018 decline, 2019 was a great year for the stock market. The Nasdaq Composite not only rebounded 35.2% but also grew over the two following years.

Of course, that rally was somewhat dependent on the U.S. not falling into a recession, combined with the Federal Reserve backing off its interest rate increases and then later returning to interest rate cuts. The Fed raised rates four times in 2018, weakening the economy. However, investors took comfort in the 2019 "dovish pivot," which included three rate cuts, and the market rallied over 35% despite S&P 500 (^GSPC +4.85%) earnings growth of only 5.4% that year.

In September, the Federal Reserve began raising interest rates again, with officials recently saying more hikes are likely. Therefore, all eyes will be on new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh at the next Fed meeting, scheduled for Dec. 9.

It is often a fool's errand to try to time the market's ups and downs, especially amid this unprecedented AI boom. Still, the many parallels to the late-2018 downturn offer hope for next year.

Amid all the turmoil and uncertainty, investors should stick with their regular investing plans, allocating a portion of their savings to high-quality stocks each month, with an eye toward the long term -- not just next month or next year.
