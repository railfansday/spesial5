The Expanse Season 7 Isn't Happening, But Prime Video Has Found The Next Best Thing

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Dani Kessel Odom (they/them) is an autistic lead writer and TV critic who covers sci-fi shows like Doctor Who and Pluribus, fantasy shows like The Magicians and Percy Jackson, and horror of all kinds. They also cover Marvel and DC TV shows and movies, with a robust knowledge of comic books. Their TV reviews can be found on Rotten Tomatoes.

Dani is also part of ScreenRant's books team, using their fiction writing and literature studies as the backbone for their book analyses.

They have covered events, such as the Denver Fan Expo. Professionals in the field, such as Damien Leone and Lucy Hale, have shared their articles. Their review of Ponies was quoted in the show's TV trailer.

In university, they majored in English Writing with a minor in psychology. They have always had a passion for analyzing TV and movies, even taking filmography and scriptwriting classes in university. They also studied and participated in onstage and onscreen acting extensively from the ages of 7 to 18.

Aside from working at Screen Rant, Dani has worked as a freelance editor and writer over the past decade, often in a ghostwriting capacity.

Whether we like it or not, The Expanse isn’t bringing the final three books to the screen, but Prime Video has luckily found the next best option. The streamer canceled the show because of increased costs during the pandemic and lower viewership. Plus, the books have an almost 30-year time jump between Babylon’s Ashes and Persepolis Rising, which would have made continuing the adaptation difficult. If they were going to end the show early, this was the most natural stopping point.

In a perfect world, Prime Video would revive the TV show and give us The Expanse seasons 7 through 9. However, we don’t exist in an ideal world. The cast and crew have moved on to other projects. The streamer hasn’t given any indication that The Expanse could return. What’s more, they’re working on other high-budget projects like Fourth Wing and War of God, which makes it less likely they'll bring back a show that costs an estimated $2 to $5 million per episode.

That being said, there’s still reason to be happy. If Prime Video isn’t going to give us The Expanse season 7, the next best option is to bring the newest James S.A. Corey book series to the screen, and the streamer has jumped at the opportunity.

After finishing The Expanse series, writers Ty Franck and Daniel Abraham, the duo who write under the James S.A. Corey pen name, moved on to a new sci-fi series, which will have three novels and two novellas. So far, two novels and one novella are out, and it’s thrilling. The new James S.A. Corey series, The Captive’s War, centers on a group of human scientists from the planet Anjin who are kidnapped and enslaved by the Carryx Empire, a fascist interstellar empire of insectoid aliens with no mercy. They must prove their worth to survive, but they’re secretly plotting a rebellion. If this sounds nothing like The Expanse, that’s because it is.

The space opera shares major themes, including human survival under intense pressure, navigating complex political systems, and existing in morally gray areas. Everything else, from the pacing to the tone, differs drastically. However, I’m willing to still give the TV show a vote of confidence because it has the same brilliant creative minds behind it.

Ty Franck and Daniel Abraham will be directly involved in the adaptation as executive producers and writers. The Expanse’s showrunner, Naren Shankar, will serve as an executive producer, showrunner, and writer on The Captive’s War, and Breck Eisner, director of The Expanse, will be executive producer and director on the upcoming show.

In anyone else’s hands, I would not trust an adaptation of The Captive’s War. If anyone can bring the new trilogy to the screen, it will be Franck, Abraham, Shankar, and Eisner. Even that group will have to jump through hoops to get it to the screen, though. Franck and Abraham admitted to Polygon that they made The Captive’s War “freaking hard to adapt into a TV show.”

They cited the characters' interiority and writing the many types of aliens as major challenges. They also acknowledge that Dafyd’s personality will be off-putting in a TV show, so they’ll need to find a way to make him compelling onscreen without losing core parts of the character. They haven’t even started thinking about how to bring the story to the screen visually.

Additionally, I foresee difficulty because The Mercy of Gods starts extremely slowly, and there isn’t much momentum until two-thirds of the way through the book. A book can do this because they don’t have to worry about retention. If a reader gets halfway through a book and DNFs it, the publisher still makes money from the book sale. TV shows have to captivate audiences immediately because they make money based on views and watch time.

Ultimately, The Captive’s War has the most potential as a follow-up to The Expanse, but it will be an uphill battle. Hopefully, the team can rise to the occasion.
