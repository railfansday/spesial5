Employers want AI skills. Here’s how colleges are adding them without new hires.

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Ask a college president what students need to graduate ready for work, and AI is now near the top of the list. More than a third of entry-level jobs now require AI skills, nearly triple the share six months earlier. Yet 63% of faculty say recent graduates aren't prepared to use AI in the workplace.

Most academic leaders agree AI belongs in the curriculum. The harder question is who will teach it, especially at small, tuition-dependent institutions.

So many institutions stall, and students graduate without the skills employers are hiring for.

Rize Education helps colleges lower costs and grow enrollment through a shared program model, letting them launch high-demand, career-focused programs faster and for far less than building them alone. Its two AI programs have been adopted 94 times across the network of institutions.

With more than 1,800 students enrolled through these courses, Rize Education can finally answer the question provosts ask most: do students actually value an online AI course?

Rize surveys students at the middle and end of every course. Here’s what they said:

Students rate these courses as highly as others. 89% of Applied AI responses and 83% of AI Literacy responses rate the course as good as or better than other online courses students have taken. Against on-campus courses, the figures are 76% and 73%.

Limited AI experience didn’t hold students back. AI Literacy students rated their prior familiarity with AI just 3.9 out of 7, yet rated their confidence in finishing with a good grade 6.2 out of 7.

A well-run course shapes the experience. Students gave their highest marks to clear assignment instructions, reasonable grading and helpful instructors. As one AI Literacy student put it: "This course is challenging but all of the assignments work together to give a clear, functional foundational knowledge of AI that can immediately be put to use in professional and private settings."

For more than 1,300 students, an AI course was their first Rize-powered course, a sign these programs draw students in rather than just checking a box.
