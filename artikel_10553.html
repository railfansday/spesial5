YouTuber’s food festival promising 50,000 visitors left vendors with huge losses after just hundreds showed up

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

A food festival in Tottori, Japan, led by the YouTuber known as Sayoto has sparked backlash after an event promoted around 50,000 expected visitors reportedly attracted only hundreds of people.

The Local Food Fight in Tottori took place on November 7 and 8 in Kurayoshi, with food vendors split into eastern and western teams competing based on their sales. Promotional material reportedly listed an expected attendance of 50,000 and an economic impact of 1 billion yen.

Sayoto served as one of the event’s executive committee chairs alongside TOSU representative Yasumi Matsubayashi. Around 40 to 50 vendors reportedly traveled from across Japan to attend, with standard booth fees set at 50,000 yen and VIP packages costing 100,000 yen.

Reports from Japanese outlets placed attendance anywhere from roughly 500 people to fewer than 2,000 across the two-day event. TV Asahi reported that one visitor described the venue as having almost no customers around the stalls, while FNN said the site appeared largely empty.

A fried chicken restaurant from Matsue reportedly prepared around 200 kilograms of chicken, enough for more than 1,600 servings, after being told to expect a major crowd. The owner said the restaurant expected first-day sales of between 700,000 and 800,000 yen but made only about 10,000 yen. The business reportedly suffered losses of more than 330,000 yen and canceled its second day.

Another vendor, Matsui Sake, reportedly told Yomiuri Shimbun that it lost around 700,000 yen over the weekend. Vendors also complained that promised tents were not ready, electricity and washing facilities were unavailable, and the event’s 10 a.m. start was delayed until around noon for some businesses.

The festival also featured a performance from Pokemon theme singer and voice actor Rika Matsumoto. She later said that her microphone cut out and the music failed to play properly during the show, though she tried to turn the problems into a joke for the audience.

After the event, TOSU’s website and social media accounts reportedly became unavailable, while some vendors said they struggled to contact the organizers. Matsubayashi later said the 50,000 figure had been set as a goal but was never promised.

Sayoto has also since privated the event-related Shorts on his YouTube channel. The videos had promoted the festival as a 50,000-person event and encouraged viewers to attend, but their links now show the uploads as unavailable. Sayoto has not publicly explained why the Shorts were removed.

Sayoto apologized on X and Threads, saying he would explain the event’s attendance, preparation problems and operational failures by the end of November. He also said he was not trying to avoid explaining what happened and would address questions about the festival’s planning.

Matsubayashi apologized on November 11 and said the event committee believed attendance had exceeded 500 people. She blamed a combination of preparation problems, contractor shortages, criticism surrounding the festival and a sudden cold snap for the poor turnout.

She also said the committee was working with lawyers on payments to vendors and planned to contact businesses whose banking information had been confirmed. The organizers have not publicly released a final attendance figure or a complete compensation plan.
