Spurs winning streak driven by Victor Wembanyama, but he is far from alone

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Earlier this month, Victor Wembanyama, still in his uniform, sat at the podium and seemed almost disappointed.

He had just made history, generating a stat line that had only been replicated one other time since 1974. His 52-point, 24-rebound, 8-block masterclass on Nov. 4 propelled the San Antonio Spurs past the Dallas Mavericks in a one-point win.

But Wembanyama had been dealing with a cold, and it was only that night when he felt like he had finally gotten his legs back.

"It should be more like this," Wembanyama said. "More often."

The Spurs are the hottest team in the NBA, winners of 12 consecutive games. They've climbed to the top of Western Conference and are tied with the Los Angeles Lakers for best record in the NBA. San Antonio ranks within the top three in offensive rating (119.2), defensive rating (106.6) and net rating (+12.6). And one season after falling short in the NBA Finals, the Spurs are looking like a dominant team, one that could be poised for a championship run.

How exactly is San Antonio doing it? Throughout it all, it has been Wembanyama carrying the Spurs.

On Monday, Nov. 16, Wembanyama earned Western Conference Player of the Week, his second such honor in the regular season's first month. He's averaging career highs in points (28.6), rebounds (11.6) and blocks (4.3). But more than just the numbers, it's the way Wembanyama is playing the game that is elevating San Antonio.

Wembanyama is either attacking the rim and using his height to get high-percentage looks, or he's using his range to stretch the floor with his 3-point shot. He ranks second in the NBA in points in the paint per game (15.3), trailing only Miami Heat forward Giannis Antetokounmpo (17.9). Yet, Wembanyama leads all players in dunks with 65, which is 19 more than Antetokounmpo, the next closest player.

Wembanyama is getting to his spots down low and either slamming the ball home on his own, or giving his teammates an indomitable lob threat. Put another way: Wembanyama is not settling.

When the Spurs struggled against the New York Knicks in the Finals, it was typically because they fell in love with the 3-point shot and settled for perimeter jumpers. When San Antonio is its best, it's deploying its speedy guards in transition to attack the paint.

And this is where San Antonio's trio of guards come into play. With the promotion of Dylan Harper into the starting lineup, the Spurs now have the fastest starting backcourt in the NBA. De'Aaron Fox, Stephon Castle and Harper can each sprint up the floor on the break. They can each slash to the rim, and they can each kick out passes to open teammates. They also protect the ball.

The Spurs lead the NBA in fewest turnovers, committing just 12.3 per game. That has increased the efficiency with which the team plays, and that should only get better. Free agent addition Tobias Harris (calf) is expected to be sidelined through November, but he'll provide a perimeter shooting presence once he returns.

But even without Harris, teams have had to calculate which defensive assignments to prioritize. In a 38-point drubbing of the Warriors Friday, Nov. 13, Golden State practically dared Castle to shoot 3s, instead focusing on Wembanyama, Fox and Harper. Castle dropped 32 points and laced 5-of-8 shots from beyond the arc.

"Seeing teams have to scramble and switch matchups and over-rotate – I think that's when we're at our best," Castle said after the game. "When we have the ball popping and have them in rotations and we're getting drives and wide-open shots."

Granted, this 12-game winning streak has not come against the stiffest competition, so San Antonio will need to show it can sustain this level against elite opponents, especially when they're at full health. Tuesday night's matchup against the Detroit Pistons and a three-game stretch (Portland Trail Blazers, Lakers, Celtics) later in November should be telling.

Another thing that's telling is that the winning streak came after San Antonio dropped its first two games of the season and began Oct. 24 with a win against the Mavericks. Despite that victory, coach Mitch Johnson made it clear that more was to be expected from this group, challenging them to "get back to their standard."

So far, that challenge has been accepted.
