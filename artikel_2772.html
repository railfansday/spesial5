Game Preview: Knicks vs. Heat, November 27, 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Back in the 1990s, one way to preserve basketball games for the future was to record them on a VHS cassette. For all the younger folks, this was a big, chunky, rectangular thing that had magnetic tape on the inside. Whenever a game was on TV, you popped your cassette into the video recorder, pressed "REC," and from that point on, you could rewatch anything you recorded whenever you wanted. Or for as long as the tape held up, for that matter.

My favorite recorded event back then was Game Five of the first round of the playoffs in 1999 between your New York Knicks and the Miami Heat. It ended with Allan Houston's historic leaning jumper with 0.8 seconds to go, sending the top-seeded team coached by the Godfather of Schenectady packing and the underdog Bockers on an improbable run all the way to the NBA Finals. The tape wasn't just special to me because of the historic shot or the basketball played with bad intentions back then. It was also awesome because it showed the Knicks beating the Heat. Sorry, Grandma, I know you taught me not to hate anyone, but those were the Heat, my favorite team's biggest rival for years.

Now, before A'ja Wilson's boyfriend thinks he can hold a candle to the guy Patrick Ewing once offered one of his kidneys to, let me make it clear: That was a different time, a different rivalry. Whoever experienced it firsthand knows what I mean by that. When the Knicks meet the Heat on Friday night for their fourth NBA Cup game of the season, it will be different, much different. Different players, different coaches. Heck, it all seems like a different world. But make no mistake about it—it's still Knicks versus Heat. Which means the stakes are just a bit higher than in a game versus the magicians from the nation's capital or the royalty from "The Big Tomato."

Friday's game will be particularly interesting because it gives the Knicks a first glimpse at the newest version of the Heat, featuring Greece's national hero Giannis Antetokounmpo, who also happens to be one of the greatest and probably most unique basketball players this generation has seen. The project started pretty badly, with Giannis continuously reminding everyone who cared and everyone who didn't that he dearly missed Milwaukee, the city he had called home for every second of his storied NBA career until then. Then the schematic issues befell the not-so-hot Heat, who struggled with spacing, players playing in new roles, and the daunting task of implementing a headstrong player with immense gravity into a completely new system. Defense? Haha, good one!

But after a 3-6 start, the second-oldest team in the league decided this wasn't the way to ride off into the sunset. Or rather, their famous coach made plenty of adjustments. Erik Spoelstra, as we all know, is one of the best in the business. Not only at taking average rosters into the Play-In Tournament but indeed at coaching that sport with the orange leather. He inserted young Pelle Larsson into the starting lineup, found a spacing solution for the Heat, doubled down on their identity as a full-court, offensive-minded team, and then watched his guys reel off seven mostly impressive victories in a row (among them wins over Houston and Detroit) before falling to Philly on Wednesday night.

What can we expect from the Heat specifically? They love to get up and down the court and play at the third-highest pace in the entire league. Currently, only the Memphis Grizzlies score more points than Spoelstra's squad, and the Heat do it by shooting a high percentage from the field, drawing boatloads of fouls, and making use of two post players. Yes, you read that right—post players. Both Giannis and A'ja Wilson's boyfriend can effectively score with their backs to the basket, and despite many worries, they can actually coexist next to one another. One of them fills the dunker spot almost all the time, they rotate decisively in screen actions, and overall, Miami runs its sets fast and ferociously to get them rolling downhill. They don't just set screens to see where it leads them; they want to get into an action quickly before any lanes can close.

It works, partly because the players around Giannis and that guy who made a habit last year of disliking the team that won a championship before him are all doing a good job in their roles. Klay Thompson adds shooting (43.2 percent from downtown), Swedish Swiss Army knife Pelle Larsson provides a little bit of everything, and playmaker Davion Mitchell has all of a sudden turned into one of the better point guards in the league at age 28, averaging career highs of 17 points and 8.6 assists per contest while also shooting 51 percent from the floor and 43-plus percent from behind the arc. The bench has some older vets more or less collecting paychecks so far, but Bobby Portis and Andrew Wiggins clearly have some talent. Apparently, that also describes Ryan Conwell, a stocky second-round guard who has been a pleasant surprise for Miami.

That whole group will definitely look to waltz into Madison Square Garden on Friday with the idea of showing the champs that they are a team to be reckoned with. Whether they truly are remains to be seen; there are plenty of things that could uproot the positivity currently sprawling across South Beach. But the Knicks better be prepared for a tough test, and not because they might still be missing some of their injured guys or because they are coming off an emotionally draining victory against Cleveland on Wednesday. Not even because A'ja Wilson might have taught her boyfriend a few more tricks this offseason after winning her fifth WNBA MVP trophy.

No, it's going to be tough because it's Heat versus Knicks. And even if that doesn't mean what it did in the days of the VHS cassette, it still means something.
