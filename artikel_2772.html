Mike Brown pushing for faster offense from sluggish Knicks

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

"Last-last," Brown asked, "or second-to-last?"

Entering Saturday, the Knicks sat at the very bottom of the NBA in pace, averaging 94.91 possessions per game. Brown repeatedly has urged his team to play with pace since taking over last year, but it's a rare desire that has not come to fruition.

New York Knicks head coach Mike Brown reacts during the fourth quarter against the Portland Trail Blazers in New York, Saturday, Nov. 21, 2026. Getty Images

The reality is that this is not an overly athletic roster that thrives in transition. This group of veteran players never will be a unit that loves to get up and down the floor. Jalen Brunson, Karl-Anthony Towns and OG Anunoby are more methodical scorers who prefer to set up themselves in the frontcourt.

Brown knows that, so while he will continue to preach pace, he values something else that serves as an indication of whether his offense is functioning properly, something that doesn't necessarily require the natural athleticism they lack.

"We have to make sure that every time we cut, it's a scoring cut and our decisions are quick," Brown said before a 97-96 Knicks win, which required a buzzer-beating 3 from Mikal Bridges to escape after blowing a 26-point second-half lead.

When the Knicks offense kicked into gear during their championship run last year, it was evident.

With Towns operating as a hub, the four other Knicks on the floor constantly cut and set off-ball screens around him. The ball moved quickly, swinging side to side constantly. Towns, along with Anunoby and Jordan Clarkson, did not play Saturday.

Landry Shamet, who scored 20 points, shoots a jumper over Toumani Camara during the Knicks' buzzer-beating win over the Trail Blazers on Nov. 21, 2026, at the Garden. Jason Szenes for New York Post

"You can tell when a guy goes from Point A to Point B with a little bit of velocity," Brown said. "A guy like Landry Shamet, he is doing it every single time. With him shooting the ball the way he's shooting it, that gravity that he carries when he moves, you can't really see it or feel it if you don't know the game well. But when he moves that quickly from Point A to Point B, everybody is just leaning just a little bit in that direction, which may create an opening or an advantage for your teammate that's away from him."

It's a reason why Shamet's role keeps growing. Among the five starters, though, it's something they've had to reestablish after a sluggish first few games of the season.

The Knicks aren't quick in transition. Once they're set in the frontcourt, though, they're capable of playing quickly.

It's when they are at their best. It takes pressure off Brunson having to orchestrate everything. It sets up open catch-and-shoot opportunities for Anunoby and Mikal Bridges. It allows Josh Hart to utilize his passing ability and set up teammates.

"Trying to get everybody to do that all the time is huge," Brown said. "Because the defense doesn't have a chance to catch up, they don't have a chance to get set, and they just gotta react. And sometimes they'll overreact because you're going so hard. And you can see it as the game goes along. If guys aren't doing it, we try to mention it to him."

A slow team finding a way to play fast — that's crucial to the Knicks' success.
