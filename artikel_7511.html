LinkedIn adds another in-stream game

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Let Social Media Today's free newsletter keep you informed, straight from your inbox.

The math-based puzzle, called “Sumbreak,” is part of the platform’s push to increase user retention and engagement.

LinkedIn has launched its ninth in-app game, with a new math-based game called "Sumbreak."

As shown in these images, "Sumbreak" gets players to use math in order to merge numbered tiles and eventually get to a single tile.

As explained by LinkedIn Head of Games Helen Smith: “Each time you merge matching numbers on the grid, they add together, bringing you one step closer to clearing the board. The challenge is figuring out how to combine the numbers so you're left with a single final tile.”

LinkedIn members will be able to challenge others to "Sumbreak," offering another means to prompt friendly rivalry in the app and drive more engagement using games instead of relying on professional connections.

In June, when the platform launched its eighth puzzle game, called Wend, Smith said that billions of puzzles had been solved in the app since games were first added in-stream in May 2024.

LinkedIn hasn’t shared specific numbers on how many of its users are playing its puzzle games, but in March 2025, the platform reported that “millions of professionals” were playing its in-stream games each day, and that 84% of users who play a game return to try again the next day.

The idea is that these in-stream games will increase user retention and engagement and improve LinkedIn’s usage stats while also driving more opportunities for conversation and connection.

On another front, LinkedIn added favorites to gaming leaderboards, making it easier to compare performance against chosen connections and rivals.

LinkedIn also added bonus puzzles to reward players who return every day, and made its games easier to find in its updated UI.

Integrating games into a professional connection site may still feel like an odd fit, but the data suggests that LinkedIn users are benefitting from the feature.
