The Hotel’s Member Rate Often Beats the Booking App – Check It Before You Book

Before you book a chain hotel through a booking app, check the chain’s own site, signed in to its free loyalty program. Members often pay no more there than on the app, and booking through the app forfeits the chain’s points and credit toward elite status. Compare the same room, dates, and cancellation terms at the final price.

A booking app doesn’t sell a big hotel chain’s member rate. The chain sells that discount to members of its free loyalty program on its own site and app.

Hilton takes at least 2% off eligible rates for members who book on its site or app, and Marriott’s member rate is at least 2% below the hotel’s lowest public rate for a standard room on weekdays.

Booking through an app also costs you the chain’s rewards. At Hilton, a reservation made through a third-party site earns no points and no credit toward elite status, the program’s higher tiers. It also gets none of the member perks, such as free Wi-Fi and late checkout when available. Changes and cancellations go through the site you booked with, not Hilton.

On the app and on the chain’s site, signed in, choose the same room type, dates, and cancellation terms. Then compare the totals on the last screen before payment, taxes and fees included. Say the app’s total comes to $189 a night for a room you can cancel up to a day ahead, and the chain’s site comes to $176 for the same room on the same terms. Booking direct at $176 rather than $189 saves $13 a night and earns points.

When the app still comes out cheaper than the chain’s site, the big chains offer a price match. Hilton’s Price Match Guarantee, for one, matches another site’s public price at least 1% below Hilton’s for the same room and terms when you book direct and claim within 24 hours. It then takes another 25% off the room rate. It doesn’t cover travel packages, opaque deals that name the hotel only after you book, or prices shown only after signing in or only in a mobile app.

The app can still win on a flight-and-hotel package, an opaque deal, or a last-minute discount, and at an independent hotel with no loyalty program. Equal totals favor the chain’s own site, since the same price there also earns points and elite credit, and the hotel itself handles any change.
