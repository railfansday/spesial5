Wealthfront vs. Chime – High-Yield Savings or a Paycheck Account With Overdraft Cover

Open a Wealthfront Cash Account for savings, since it pays 3.55% on every dollar with no direct deposit required. Choose Chime® for your paycheck if you want fee-free overdraft cover and free cash deposits at Walgreens. Unless $3,000 a month would reach Chime, keep your savings at Wealthfront even if Chime gets your paycheck.

Which app pays more on savings depends on your direct deposits. Wealthfront pays a 3.55% annual percentage yield (APY) on every dollar in its Cash Account, money you’re about to spend included, with no minimum to meet. Chime’s savings rate rises with the pay you direct deposit into Chime Checking within 34 days. You earn 1.00% with none, 3.00% with Chime Plus™ status (at least $200 in one deposit or $400 in total), and 4.00% with Chime Prime™ status (at least $3,000).1 Only Chime Prime’s 4.00% beats Wealthfront’s 3.55%.

Chime’s SpotMe® covers card purchases and ATM withdrawals past your balance with no fee once $200 a month arrives by direct deposit, though not automatic bill payments or transfers.2 Its limit starts at $20 and can grow to $200. Wealthfront charges no overdraft fees but offers no overdraft cover.

Chime takes cash deposits at Walgreens for free and at other partner stores, where a fee may apply. Wealthfront’s debit card can deposit cash only at retailers that accept it, for a fee of up to $5.95.

Wealthfront and Chime can both post a direct-deposited paycheck up to two days early,3 so early pay doesn’t decide it.

Neither app is a bank, and each holds your money at partner banks, where deposit insurance for app accounts applies.4

Pick Wealthfront for savings that earn a high rate with no paycheck to move. Pick Chime as your paycheck account, with overdraft cover and a free way to deposit cash. Below $3,000 a month in pay, split your direct deposit so what you save goes to Wealthfront and at least $200 a month still reaches Chime to keep SpotMe on.
