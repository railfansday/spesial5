Why 'inspiring' Van Gaal influences Scotland head coach Pocognoli

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Sebastien Pocognoli played for Louis van Gaal at AZ Alkmaar

Scotland head coach Sebastien Pocognoli says he draws on the influence of legendary manager Louis van Gaal in his own coaching career.

The Belgian played under the Dutchman during their time at AZ Alkmaar, a special period for Pocognoli during which he won the Eredivisie title in 2009.

Van Gaal also previously took charge of the Netherlands, and now that he has moved into international football himself, the Scotland boss reflected on the 75-year-old's influence.

"I take a lot from all of my gaffers, but probably he was the one with the most experience," Pocognoli told BBC Scotland.

"I was very young and you know in that moment he had all the Barcelona young players between his hands, also Ajax players.

"The way that he treats the young players in a direct way, honest way and hard way was for me really inspiring in my daily job now.

"I was lucky to have a lot of good coaches but he was probably the most that impressed me day by day, and I felt progress under him.

"That I think is the best thing for players as a feeling."

Progress is exactly what every Scotland fan is yearning for. Under Steve Clarke, the amount of that very commodity plateaued.

Years in the international wilderness were expelled with two European Championships and a World Cup appearance, but they were punctuated by disappointing results and dour tactics.

Pocognoli's arrival signified not only a new era, but a new approach, formation, selection and outlook.

It has been far from straightforward for the Belgian as he approaches 100 days in charge next week. Five games, two wins over North Macedonia, a draw in Slovenia and two humbling defeats mark his tenure so far.

Within it, a fresh system and injection of different faces to offer a break from the norm, but also plenty signs that a transformation will not be an immediate reality.

Scotland's failings in front of goal has remained, while it is clear the Scots are still getting to grips with a back three, albeit with enforced changes in personnel.

"Everything in life takes time to build if you want to achieve something," said Pocognoli.

"So we know that in the performance world everything goes faster and quicker, but we have an objective and we know that we have to go through the up and down and the good moments. At the end I hope that all this experience can make us stronger.

"I don't ask the players to be patient, it's just to believe in what we are doing."

Pocognoli acknowledges he's not had much opportunity to immerse himself in Scottish culture after taking his first foray into international management.

A wander around Glasgow is about all he's managed. And any further exploring will have to wait until after Monday's Nations League test in St Gallen.

Finishing in the top two of their group seems a tall order for Scotland, who are requiring a win and snookers in Slovenia. It seems progression to the next Euros will instead roll on to the qualifying campaign next year.

"We don't have the control of what will happen because Slovenia will decide," Pocognoli said. "But if we want to have a chance to go through to the group stage and to go to the play-offs we have to go with the bravery and try to play with courage and try to force something.

"I don't believe that we are waiting for something then it will happen, so let's go through our quality and try to be aggressive and wait what happens in Slovenia."
