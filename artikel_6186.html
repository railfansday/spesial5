Amadeus has the scale and airline relationships, but those advantages don't guarantee it a direct role in AI-agent bookings.

Amadeus has spent decades connecting airlines with travel sellers. AI agents may change how customers shop, but somebody still has to find flights, complete bookings, and handle changes. On its Q3 2026 earnings call on Friday, November 6, an analyst asked why Meta's Muse was using Duffel rather than Amadeus to do that work.

CFO Carol Borg said the Muse-Duffel partnership supports Amadeus' belief that AI platforms will use aggregators rather than build booking systems themselves. She doesn't think it displaces the GDS model: "It actually reinforces it."

But Duffel, not Amadeus, has the direct relationship with Muse. Other companies are pursuing similar roles across airline distribution, hotel booking and payments.

Amadeus used its Q3 2026 earnings call on November 6 to argue that AI platforms will plug into aggregators rather than build their own booking systems, a position CFO Carol Borg said the Meta Muse–Duffel partnership "reinforces." The detail behind that claim is that Duffel, not Amadeus, holds the direct relationship with Muse, even though Duffel's inventory includes direct airline connections, NDC content, and traditional GDS inventory from Travelport. A growing set of intermediaries is taking similar positions: Selfbook powers hotel booking and payments for Perplexity, Nuitée supplies hotel booking infrastructure to Grab and Revolut and is building APIs for AI applications, Katanox has partnered with Selfbook on distribution and settlement, and AirGateway and Kyte offer API-based airline connectivity. CEO Luis Maroto pointed to roughly 3 billion daily searches and said servicing complex itineraries at that scale is harder than connecting to individual airlines. The financials show pricing holding up as volumes slip: bookings fell 2.8% in the first nine months of 2026, which the company attributed largely to Middle East disruption, while revenue per booking rose 5%. Group revenue rose 5.2% at constant currency to €5.08 billion (about $5.67 billion), with adjusted EBIT up 5.5%, and management expects full-year results near the lower end of guidance while maintaining its 2026–2028 targets.
