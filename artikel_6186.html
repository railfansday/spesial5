White House Urges Congress To Approve $30 Billion In Upgrades To US Aviation Infrastructure

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

The White House is urging Congress to approve an additional $30.1 billion for US aviation infrastructure upgrades. Reuters reports that Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy and budget director Russell Vought are pushing for action before Democrats take control of Congress in January. The request follows the Republicans' defeat in November's midterm elections, leaving the administration with a narrowing window to secure funding.

The appeal comes as the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) has reportedly ordered a nationwide ground stop on November 13, citing extraordinary circumstances and safety concerns. The administration first requested approximately $30 billion in additional funding in September, on top of the $12.5 billion previously approved by Congress.

Duffy and White House Office of Management and Budget Director Russell Vought have written to congressional leaders requesting swift approval of the additional investment. With Democrats preparing to assume control of Congress on January 3, 2027, the administration is seeking to advance the proposal during the remaining weeks of Republican control. However, the legislation's prospects remain uncertain, and a change in congressional leadership does not necessarily mean the funding would be rejected.

The administration argues that the funding is essential to replacing outdated air traffic control equipment, improving airport capacity, and strengthening the safety of the national airspace system. The latest proposal builds on a modernization program announced in 2025, following years of concern about aging communications infrastructure, controller shortages and operational disruptions. The investment is intended to accelerate upgrades that officials say cannot be completed with existing appropriations.

In their November 13 letter to Congress, Duffy and Vought said:

"This investment is critical to complete the modernization of our national airspace"

$12.5 billion in initial funding approved

Duffy requests approximately $30 billion more

The funding campaign began well before this week's political developments. Duffy called for additional investment on September 22, shortly after a major Northeast air traffic control disruption. Senator Eric Schmitt subsequently introduced the Modern Skies Act on September 28, supported by major airlines and aviation industry organizations. The latest White House intervention represents a renewed effort to secure congressional approval rather than an entirely new funding initiative.

The proposed investment would bring federal aviation modernization funding to approximately $42.6 billion, including the $12.5 billion originally approved in 2025. Unlike the earlier allocation, which concentrated on ATC improvements and controller recruitment, the latest request includes substantial funding for airports. Of the $30.1 billion, $18 billion would support airport infrastructure, including $10 billion to modernize or replace aging terminals.

The FAA's infrastructure problems have accumulated over decades. Some facilities still depend on aging computers, with replacement components reportedly purchased through auction websites. The agency recently announced plans to eliminate floppy disks used to distribute weather and other critical information at more than 400 ATC facilities by the end of 2028. These technological limitations have compounded existing concerns about staffing shortages, congestion and delays.

Nevertheless, modernization is already underway. According to Duffy, 68% of the FAA's legacy copper telecommunications network has been replaced with high-speed fiber infrastructure. The additional funding would accelerate upgrades to controller workstations, automation platforms and telecommunications, while expanding airport capacity. The challenge is implementing these improvements across one of the world's busiest aviation networks without creating further operational disruption.

A severed backup fiber line triggered ATC Zero conditions, grounding major Northeast airports and sending disruption toward Boston.

The funding appeal coincides with an extraordinary disruption to US aviation. Earlier on Friday, the FAA issued a nationwide ground stop, instructing departing aircraft to remain on the ground until further notice. According to Axios, the advisory cited "extraordinary circumstances and safety reasons," with international arrivals to be handled individually, although at the time of writing, the cause of the disruption had not been officially established.

The incident follows September's major Northeast ATC meltdown, when simultaneous telecommunications failures disrupted thousands of flights. A circuit failed at an air traffic facility in Philadelphia, while an Amtrak construction crew accidentally severed a fiber-optic cable in New Jersey. Ground stops affected several major airports, including Newark, Philadelphia and New York's airports, with knock-on delays extending to Boston.

Although the causes of today's nationwide ground stop still need to be established, the September incident demonstrated how communications failures can spread disruption across multiple airports. The FAA's modernization program aims to reduce these vulnerabilities through more resilient telecommunications, upgraded technology and improved infrastructure. With billions of dollars awaiting congressional approval, the latest disruption provides a timely backdrop to the administration's argument that further investment cannot wait.
