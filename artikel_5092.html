Strategy’s Bitcoin purchases have slowed sharply, and its preferred shares appear to be getting priority. During the latest reported week, the company spent approximately $147 million buying back STRC while allocating just $29.6 million to additional BTC.

The shift comes as the market struggles. BTC reportedly plunged to $55,319 on November 21, around 56% below its $125,761 all-time high, before recovering to approximately $62,777 by November 23.

Strategy purchased only 457 BTC during the latest reported week, according to its filing. The average purchase price was $64,898, above the reported market low.

STRC, meanwhile, is a preferred share carrying a 12% annual dividend. Strategy aims to keep it around $100 and has been buying shares when they trade below that level.

STRC declined from $99.50 to $93.20 during the week. Strategy continued buying.

Across its last four weekly filings, the company allocated $836 million to STRC buybacks, compared with $211 million for BTC purchases.

Strategy funds these activities through sales of its common stock, MSTR. But that funding source weakened as shares fell to a reported 52-week low of $77.80.

Last week, MSTR sales raised $176.7 million, down from $283.8 million two weeks earlier. Only $29.6 million went toward new BTC purchases.

Strategy has moved $1 billion from its dividend reserve into a flexible cash pool that could fund buybacks or purchases.

Strategy has acquired 457 $BTC and repurchased $212M of $STRC. We increased our Digital Credit Securities Repurchase Program by $1B and reallocated $1B from our USD Reserve to USD Cash. As of 11/22/26, we hold 851,305 BTC and $4.8B of USD Assets. $MSTR https://t.co/jqhYrehyTl

However, its board also increased the STRC buyback budget from $3 billion to $4 billion.

Strategy Bitcoin purchases could recover if cash shifts back toward accumulation. Next Monday’s filing should reveal which priority wins.
