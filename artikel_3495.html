Netflix's 6-Episode Western Thriller Masterpiece 'Territory' Is 'Yellowstone' Done Right

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Taylor Sheridan’s Yellowstone may know a thing or two about running a ranch empire, but the Duttons aren’t the only family ruling the countryside. All the way in the Australian outback, Netflix’s Territory depicts a different kind of ranch life, where human threats are just as vicious as the unforgiving wild. Instead of the Duttons, there's a different family at the story's heart, one that has passed down their cattle ranch for generations.

While Yellowstone and Territory share similar threads — dealing with grief, greedy corporations, and Indigenous groups — the latter does it better. Territory tells a fleshed-out story of succession without reducing its characters to one-dimensional tropes, while offering thoughtful representation of Aboriginal communities. If anything, Territory manages to outdo Yellowstone at its own game.

Territory follows the fictional Marianne Station in Australia’s Northern Territory, currently helmed by the ruthless, aging patriarch Colin Lawson (Robert Taylor). The series is told through the eyes of Emily Lawson (Anna Torv), who is married to Colin’s eldest son, Graham Lawson (Michael Dorman). However, Colin has long detested Emily, seeing her as a threat because of her background as the daughter of cattle thieves. Like Yellowstone, the series begins with a family death that throws the ranch’s future into question. The sudden death of Daniel Lawson (Jake Ryan) is a blow to Colin, who has always favored his younger son over Graham.

This Netflix series has united two neo-Western giants, and it's about time.

Daniel is supposed to be the inheritor, but his death directly complicates the station’s succession, forcing Colin to choose between the alcoholic Graham and his grandson Marshall (Sam Corlett), who wants nothing to do with the family. Emily believes she’s up to the task, but given her bitter relationship with Colin, convincing the Lawsons to put Marianne under her leadership is easier said than done. With rivals surrounding them and looking to take what they have, the family doesn't have the luxury of time to make that choice.

One of the major flaws with Yellowstone is how Sheridan writes its female characters, typically shaping their personalities in relation to the Dutton men around them. Given her tough upbringing in the Dutton household and fueled by her hatred for her brother Jamie (Wes Bentley), Beth is the de facto “badass” woman. This is portrayed through both her smaller habits, from her cussing to her chain-smoking, and more serious traits, such as her penchant for physical altercations. The same one-dimensional writing applies to Kayce's (Luke Grimes) wife, Monica (Kelsey Asbille), who is primarily defined by the endless trauma caused by her marriage into the Dutton family.

Territory, on the other hand, doesn't box its women into one mold. Instead, it recognizes that women can be many things at once. Emily has all the brains in the world to run Marianne, but she also makes a brash personal decision involving her husband when her heart gets in the way. Her daughter, Susie (Philippa Northeast), is naive enough to waste her university education working at the ranch, but she's also quick to learn the ropes. Meanwhile, Keeley Redford (Tuuli Narkle) serves as a strong-willed community spokesperson who advocates for uplifting the Indigenous community, which continues to lack resources, from proper facilities to job opportunities.

For a show that heavily features a fictional Indian reservation (the Broken Rock Reservation), Yellowstone is occasionally guilty of questionable Indigenous representation, something Lily Gladstone herself has publicly criticized. Besides mining Monica for trauma, the show also portrays Indigenous characters as antagonists intent on taking land that “rightfully” belongs to the Duttons. Case in point: Thomas Rainwater (Gil Birmingham). Despite his credentials as a Harvard graduate, former finance worker, and current Chief and Chairman, his fight to reclaim the land is overshadowed by bureaucratic maneuvering and casino politics, ultimately framing him as a greedy chief who takes advantage of the reservation's gambling crisis.

Meanwhile, Territory handles Indigenous representation with greater consideration, thanks to the involvement of Indigenous writers Steven McGregor and Kodie Bedford. Rather than focusing primarily on high-level bureaucracy, Territory gives working-class Aboriginal Australians a central role in its story— particularly through the experiences of Nolan Brannock (Clarence Ryan), a half-white, half-Aboriginal cattle rancher. Caught between two contrasting worlds, Nolan struggles to succeed in the white-dominated cattle industry while remaining loyal to his community. His efforts to create economic opportunities put him at odds with Indigenous elders like Uncle Bryce (Hamilton Morris), who fear financial advancement will come at the cost of exploitation and of Nolan turning his back on his roots in the name of progress.

With all the elements above, Territory has outdone all seasons of Yellowstone in just six episodes. While that makes it all the more disappointing that the Netflix series was abruptly canceled after just one season, it also means that the show is a satisfying binge from start to finish.
