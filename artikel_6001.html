Trump admin announces sweeping measure to cut Iran’s ‘last financial lifeline’

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Tensions escalate in the Middle East as President Donald Trump signals his willingness to resume military strikes against Iran next year following recent drone attacks. Fox News chief foreign correspondent Trey Yingst reports live from Tel Aviv on the retaliatory claims, while retired Navy SEAL officer Mike Sarraille discusses the conflict during 'The Faulkner Focus.'

FIRST ON FOX: The Treasury Department on Monday imposed sweeping new sanctions against more than two dozen Iranian exchange houses, overseas front companies and financial operatives accused of moving billions of dollars through a global "shadow banking" network to keep Iran connected to international trade.

The action, carried out by the Treasury’s Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC), targeted "financial plumbing" spanning Iran, Dubai, Hong Kong and Turkey that officials say enables Tehran to bypass U. S. restrictions, sell oil and petrochemicals, and settle transactions in Russian rubles and Chinese yuan.

"Iran’s economy is underwater, and its leaders are panicking," Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent wrote in a statement Monday before the news release. "Iran’s shadow banking system is the last financial lifeline of a bankrupt regime. Treasury will continue dismantling these illicit networks until we have buried the head of the snake."

TRUMP’S IRAN CRACKDOWN ‘SUFFOCATING’ REGIME AS OIL WELLS COULD SHUT WITHIN DAYS, BESSENT SAYS

Monday’s designations mark the latest escalation under Operation Economic Outcast, a maximum financial pressure campaign Bessent launched on Aug. 24, to cut off the regime's remaining revenue streams and heighten secondary sanctions risks for foreign banks and brokers that continue doing business with Tehran.

TRUMP'S FIRST IRAN WAR ESCALATION POST-MIDTERM LOSSES SEEKS TO CHOKE OFF TEHRAN’S TRADE

According to the Treasury Department, Iran's "shadow banking" apparatus is overseen by the Central Bank of Iran and relies on domestic currency exchange houses paired with bank-affiliated "rahbar companies" that manage sanctions-evasion networks through thousands of foreign shell firms. As declining oil revenues and existing sanctions squeeze the Iranian economy, Treasury said shadow banking facilitators have increasingly failed to repatriate export earnings while enriching themselves with state funds.

The Treasury also revealed Monday that its Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) "identified approximately $14.7 billion of financial activity from January 2025 to August 2026 potentially linked to Iranian shadow banking."

MILLIONS OF BARRELS OF IRANIAN OIL SIT BEYOND TRUMP BLOCKADE AS HE VOWS ‘ZERO LEAKAGE’

Among the highest-profile targets in Monday’s package is the Iran-based Iran Center for Exchange of Currency and Gold, which matches Iranian exporters holding foreign currency with importers seeking to buy goods abroad across tens of billions of dollars in annual trade. Treasury noted that the Central Bank of Iran also uses bonds issued by the exchange center to facilitate cross-border trade in Russian rubles and Chinese yuan without relying on the U.S. dollar.

BESSENT WARNS IRAN'S OUTSIDE BACKERS TO 'STAY AWAY' AS TRUMP SEEKS TO 'ASPHYXIATE' TEHRAN'S ECONOMY

The OFAC package sanctions Rahbar Bazargani Nesim Baharan Qeshm Company — the rahbar affiliate for Iran’s Bank Sarmayeh — along with three of its senior officials, Sayyed Mohammad Jalali, Valiollah Hemmati and Ali Kalhor, for facilitating transactions on behalf of major Iranian oil companies. Two Iran-based currency dealers, Mina Fashki and Partners Exchange Company and Mellat Exchange Company, were blacklisted alongside five of their owners and officials.

US TARGETS CHINESE REFINERY IN SWEEPING IRAN OIL CRACKDOWN, SANCTIONS ‘SHADOW FLEET’ TANKERS

To choke off Tehran's overseas payment channels, Treasury targeted 10 exchange house front companies across the United Arab Emirates, Turkey and Hong Kong accused of moving billions of dollars for Iranian clients. Those include Dubai-based Recaz Star General Trading LLC, which sent and received hundreds of payments totaling more than $170 million tied to Sadaf Exchange, and OGA Global General Trading L. L. C, which oversaw millions of dollars in payments for the state-owned National Iranian Tanker Company.

Other newly sanctioned front companies include Hong Kong-based Grandez Trading Limited, Turkey-based Bogazici Gumrukleme Ithalat Ihracat Turizm Petrol Urunleri Sanayi Ve Ticaret Limited Sirketi and several Dubai-based trading firms — including Gespl International General Trading LLC, which Treasury alleged was used by the National Iranian Oil Company to launder oil and petrochemical proceeds, and Robust Trading LLC for processing payments for the sanctioned National Iranian Copper Industries Company.

Treasury also invoked counterterrorism authorities to sanction Dubai-based H N B Trading Co LLC, alleging it received hundreds of thousands of dollars from a front company tied to Iran’s Armed Forces General Staff to help pay for the charter of a vessel alongside sanctioned shipping firm Dragon Road Ltd.

Under Monday's order, any U.S.-based assets or property interests belonging to the designated individuals and entities — as well as any businesses 50% or more owned by them — will be frozen, and Americans are barred from transacting with them.

Treasury also warned that foreign financial institutions risk losing access to U.S. correspondent accounts if they knowingly facilitate significant transactions for the sanctioned networks.

Whistleblowers who report sanctions violations resulting in penalties exceeding $1 million might ultimately qualify for federal awards.
