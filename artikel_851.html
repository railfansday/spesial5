KJ Biermann Accused of 16 Sex Offenses, Including Incest in Prior Case

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Prosecutors in the sexual assault case against Kim Zolciak and Kroy Biermann's son KJ shared insight into an April 2025 sexual assault case, in which he was charged with 16 offenses, including incest.

Content warning: This story discusses allegations of sexual assault.

More has come to light about Kroy "KJ" Biermann's legal history.

As the prosecutors in the sexual assault case against the 15-year-old son of Kim Zolciak and Kroy Biermann sought to block the transfer of his case to juvenile court, they detailed 16 previous offenses against the teen from a separate sexual assault case, according to court documents obtained by E! News.

In the response to the defense's motion to transfer the case—KJ was indicted on Sept. 3 on seven counts including aggravated child molestation, aggravated sexual battery, aggravated sodomy, sexual battery against a child under 16 and false imprisonment following an alleged assault on a 15-year-old girl in April—filed with Fulton County Superior Court on Nov. 16, the prosecutors described the previous case as "a very serious one, with probable cause found for incest." (KJ pleaded not guilty to the 2026 case and his attorney Jason Sheffield denied the allegations.)

Prosecutors in the current sexual assault case noted that the 2025 case is currently with the Fulton County Juvenile Court. KJ was charged with incest, eight counts of child molestation, sexual battery, statutory rape and five counts of the sexual exploitation of children in April 2025 after the Milton County Police Department discovered "videos and photos" in a hidden folder on his phone that February.

Sheffield previously addressed the 2025 charges—which he denied on behalf of his client—in a September statement to E! News, accusing the prosecutors on the case of "grossly" misrepresenting the facts.

"The matter arose from inappropriate, crude behavior involving two middle-schoolers," the statement continued. "It did not result in an adjudication for statutory rape, sexual intercourse, or any comparable offense. Any suggestion otherwise is inaccurate and creates a materially misleading impression of how the juvenile matter was ultimately resolved."

E! News has reached out to KJ's attorneys for comment and has not yet heard back. His final plea in the April 2026 case will be entered Feb. 10, according to legal docs.

Prosecutors acquiesced to the goal of the 2025 case being filed through the juvenile legal system was for KJ to "receive the appropriate treatment and rehabilitation to avoid further future crimes or assaults committed by this defendant."

However, the response highlighted that the most recent case—in which the teenage girl, only identified as L.T. in the docs, alleged that she was sexually assaulted by KJ in a Lifetime Fitness gym's changing room—occurred one year after the first.

"This behavior has become part of a deeply troubling pattern when it comes to this defendant forcing sexual compliance through a personal relationship," the response read. "The closeness in time between the two incidents is significant."

"Based on the repetitive pattern of offenses," prosecutors continued, "this defendant has proven to be beyond the rehabilitation offered within the Fulton County Juvenile Court process."

The docs argued that it was "even more concerning" since KJ had completed "all or the vast majority" of his court-mandated therapy sessions at the time of his second alleged offense.

The filing added, "Protection of the community is at the greatest risk when it comes to repeated sexual offenses, regardless of the defendant's age."

The prosecutor's response came two months after Sheffield, who told E! News in September that KJ is "100 percent innocent," requested that his client's case be moved to the juvenile court system due to his age.

"He is an adolescent whose development is ongoing and can demonstrate his ability for understanding, growth and change," the filing, obtained by E! News in September, argued, "and whose future cannot reliably be predicted merely from the labels attached to the allegations against him. The evidence reserved for the transfer hearing will demonstrate that KJ remains amenable to treatment, supervision, structure, education, and rehabilitation within the juvenile justice system."
