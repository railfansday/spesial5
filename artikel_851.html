Wolves are clicking, Stoke are dreaming - but could QPR be dark horses?

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Wolves are improving defensively, Stoke look strong and QPR look resolute; Andy Hinchcliffe picks out the key stories in The Championship catch-up; a Sky Sports column focusing on the second tier.

Welcome to The Championship Catch-up, a new Sky Sports column in which Andy Hinchcliffe rounds up some of the key stories from the second tier. This week:

With the players Wolves have got and the way they play, they were always going to score goals. They've averaged around 1.7 goals per game across those periods, so they're still good for a goal under Cesar Peixoto.

But in the last seven games they've conceded only five, compared to the 14 goals they shipped in their first nine. They're not playing defensively, they're just defending well. They're keeping the ball and keeping teams away from their penalty area.

At the start of the season they were too open. They were great to watch and scoring goals, but conceding too many. They needed to find a balance. They didn't have to become ultra-defensive, but they did need some defensive strength.

They've got plenty of attacking threats. Raul Jimenez is still missing, but Adam Armstrong is doing really well and is a proven goalscorer at this level. They've got width, dynamism and options, while Andre plays a really important role sitting deep in midfield.

All I was looking for was more balanced performances. They didn't need to let the goals dry up just to keep clean sheets. They've managed to find that balance and do both. They're dominating games, keeping teams out and still scoring goals.

Even when they change the right-back, centre-half or left-back, they can maintain that defensive strength. They're clearly moving in the right direction and are now a much more balanced, complete team.

Kieran Trippier's experience has been invaluable. He cares about the club getting promoted back to the Premier League. Individually, he wants to do well, but he also wants the team to succeed.

He's perhaps not the player he was five years ago, but he can still have a huge effect. He'll have to get used to the fact that he might not play every game, but Wolves have the depth to manage that.

With Trippier, it's not just about his ability but also his leadership and experience. He can reinforce the standards they should be setting and what they're looking to achieve this season.

Stoke City have won 10 of 16 games, more than any other Championship team. Would you have expected them to have won more games than Wolves, West Ham, Middlesbrough or Southampton after 16 matches? Probably not.

They're still overachieving in terms of the data, but Mark Robins spoke during the international break about the need to improve their performances. The league position doesn't really tell you how good you are. You have to monitor performances and look at how to develop and improve.

There's still work to do at both ends of the pitch, but they're getting goals from different players. George Hirst came in for big money and had been struggling for goals and not starting games. He won them the game against Birmingham, which is a lift for him and the team.

Against Birmingham, they were down to 10 men for a long time and showed the resilience to hold on to a 1-0 lead. When you look at everything, the foundations are there for them to get better.

How many experienced, very good coaches have gone to Stoke and not been able to make it work? We all hoped Mark Robins would succeed because of the work he did at Coventry, but there was always the question of whether he would become another coach who fell foul of a club where nobody seemed able to get things going.

I just think it's wonderful that it's him because he's such a brilliant coach. He's very understated and humble. I saw him after a game recently and asked him a couple of tactical questions. He was saying he was glad I'd noticed certain things he'd done. He's very engaging, but it's not all about him. It's about the job he's there to do.

Player for player, this Stoke side is better than last season's team. That's what I'm saying about their recruitment and the shape Mark is playing. They're better, and the fact they can improve further makes things look really positive.

But we're only 16 games into a long season. Mark will know winter is really tough for every team, particularly when you're up near the top and everyone is looking to knock you off your position. They'll be looking at their performances and adjusting their training because that's all you can do.

Could Stoke possibly be in the mix for a top-two finish? With the way they're playing and the players they've got, who could improve further, very possibly.

Defensively for QPR there's been the big shift this season. They were 23rd last season for goals conceded and are now in the top four. That's the foundation. If you don't concede too many goals in the Championship, you've got a chance.

I don't think they're a defensive team. They're a team that defend well. It doesn't mean they're sitting everybody behind the ball and playing on the counter-attack. They can dominate possession and force opponents backwards. They defend well, but they're not defensive.

Jimmy Dunne, Ronnie Edwards and Pierce Charles are absolutely crucial, but the team structure is there too. They've got a solid base and the nice problem now is working out how to be more creative.

It was important to win at Millwall and maintain their momentum in the top eight, but they won't always find themselves in such a dominant position. There's still work to do going forward to become more threatening and dangerous.

They need to find a regular goalscorer, but they also need to create more chances. Getting Ilias Chair back into the team regularly is important. He's one of those players who can affect games in the Championship.

This has to be a big season for Chair. He undoubtedly has the ability, but at times he's coasted along and hasn't become the dominant force Rangers need him to be.

If they want to improve going forward and finish in the top eight, he's key. He's got the movement, intelligence and creative quality, and he can score goals. It's not just about what he can do himself, but what he can do for the players around him.

Looking at his performance against Millwall and what he could bring for the rest of the season, I think he's key to improving their attack. It's down to him to drive that improvement.
