Australia complete record T20I chase at MCG to edge England in thriller

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Harry Brook hits 68 off 25 balls after being dropped twice, as Australia put down six chances in the field; England add only 68 from final 10 overs, as hosts chase 188 with a ball to spare; second T20I takes place in Carrara on Tuesday (play starts 8.15am UK time)

Australia pulled off a record T20I run chase of 188 at the MCG to beat England by three wickets with a ball to spare in a thrilling series opener in Melbourne on Saturday.

England captain Harry Brook had tonked 68 off just 25 balls - hitting six sixes - as England posted 187-9, though his side wasted a dominant position of 119-4 at the halfway point of their innings by adding only 68 from the last 10 overs.

Nikhil Chaudhary, who had helped drag Australia back into the contest with the ball - the hosts having dropped six chances - then top-scored with a magnificent 68 off 38 with the bat.

He was dismissed with 20 still required off the final two overs, Australia seven down, but Jack Edwards (25no off 11) struck a couple of crucial sixes in a clutch cameo before finishing the job with a boundary.

England 187-9 in 20 overs (inserted at toss): Harry Brook (65 off 28 balls), Tom Banton (39 off 22), Jordan Cox (27 off 20); Nikhil Chaudhary (2-21), Jack Edwards (2-27)

Australia 191-7 in 19.5 overs (target 188): Nikhil Chaudhary (66 off 38 balls), Mitch Marsh (37 off 32), Sam Harper (32 off 15), Jack Edwards (25no off 11); Will Jacks (2-29)

After being inserted by the hosts, England's innings had got off to the worst possible start as Phil Salt picked out mid-off first ball, while Aneurin Donald (10) - put down two deliveries prior - perished soon after.

But Brook, dropped on 2 and 3, made Australia pay for their profligacy as he smoked three sixes in a row in taking Riley Meredith's fourth over for 24, the touring captain romping to a 20-ball fifty.

He and Tom Banton (39 off 22) - shelled twice, taking Australia's tally to five inside seven overs - put on 107 for the third wicket, with Adam Zampa also taking some tap as his opening over went for 20.

Both were gone in the space of six deliveries though, Chaudhary's wrist spin accounting for Brook, caught behind, and Banton gone to a sensational diving catch in the deep by debutant Lachlan Shaw.

England stumbled their way to a rather underwhelming score thereafter, despite the best efforts of Jordan Cox (27 off 20) and Sam Curran (21 off 20), the visitors falling short of the 200-run target they'll have surely been eyeing.

That said, it was still a record T20I chase at the MCG for Australia to reel in, and their assignment was made all the tougher when Liam Dawson (1-30) struck in the opening over - Jake Fraser-McGurk (1) bowled when charging the left-arm spinner, deceived in the flight.

Mitch Marsh hammered Henry Crocombe's first ball in T20 international cricket for six over deep backward square for quite the introduction, but the young quick responded well to remove fellow debutant Sam Harper (32 off 15) for his first wicket in his second over.

Harper had provided some impetus to the Australian innings, Jamie Overton's opening over plundered for 21 runs, and Chaudhary maintained that momentum, with useful support from his captain Marsh (37 off 32) and Tim David (20 off 13).

David failed to cash in a huge slice of luck when the bails failed to dislodge as he inside-edged one back onto his stumps, as he departed a ball later to the returning Adil Rashid (1-30).

It prompted a rather sizeable Australian wobble, with three wickets tumbling for 13 runs in little more than an over, and when Chaudhary finally fell - caught in the deep - it looked like England might be in the ascendency, only for Edwards to have other ideas.

In other worrying news for England, Will Jacks left the field late on, splitting the webbing on his hand as Ben Dwarshuis drilled a ball back at him in the penultimate over.

He is due to captain the team in the fifth and final T20I of the series as Brook departs for the Test tour of South Africa.

Speaking afterwards Brook said: "I don't know [how bad Jacks' injury is]. He obviously came off with a lot of blood but I couldn't quite see what happened.

"Hopefully he is alright because he has been such a valuable player for us in the last two years. It would be sad to see him go home early.

"I've not even thought about [the captaincy] yet. That's a decision we'll have to discuss but I don't have an answer yet."

Sam Curran, Tom Banton and Phil Salt could be captaincy contenders for a squad already missing Jacob Bethell (knee).

Hampshire's former Scotland seamer Scott Currie has been called up for the final T20I in Canberra.

"I was just trying to hit the wickets at the start and got the big wicket of Harry [Brook]. I wanted to bowl that five, six-metre length. That was my goal.

"With the bat, you knew it was going to be a big game and that there was going to some chirp. I just had to stay stable and play my game.

"Hopefully I did that and won the game for the team, which was great. There is nothing better than playing at the MCG and winning."

"It was a very entertaining game, going down to the last couple of balls. It's what the fans want to see and everybody watching at home.

"We were probably 10 runs short of what we needed on that surface. If we get those, we probably end up winning the game."

"I was worried when we dropped the best batter in the world [Brook] a couple of times. Most of the time you don't deserve to win.

"But outside a few tough chances, I thought the energy and speed was great to watch. They are all good kids.

"It has been a pretty crazy 12 days but right there is the future of the Australian cricket side with all these young guys. Hopefully we can get back to enjoying that.

"They are a great bunch. I want them to not care about making mistakes and enjoy the environment. Hopefully a win like that shows them they belong."

All T20 internationals to start at 8.15am UK time
