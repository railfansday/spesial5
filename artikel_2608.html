Officials who were bumped by reconfigured Week 10 schedule won't lose pay

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The four officials who worked two games in Week 10 got paid accordingly. The officials who found themselves without an assignment after the deck was reshuffled due to the ground stop won't be docked.

NFL Referees Association executive director Scott Green tells PFT that the Collective Bargaining Agreement between the NFL and the NFLRA "requires that all active officials including anybody out with an injury are guaranteed 16 games of pay during the regular season regardless if they work all 16 games."

Of course, it's possible that the NFL will reconfigure upcoming assignments to balance out the total assignments, putting an official who was supposed to work a given weekend on a bye while assigning an official to work when the official was supposed to be off. (That's not something Green said; it's our own assessment of how the imbalance can be rectified.)

Hopefully, that won't happen. Those who were supposed to work but didn't should get credit for the game, since they were prepared to show up and do the job. And those who worked when they weren't supposed to shouldn't lose the opportunity to work their otherwise normal schedule — unless they choose that.

And the four who worked two games in one day should absolutely get some sort of a bonus. As Green said, "[T]hat's pretty remarkable as you are generally pretty wiped out both mentally and physically after working a game."

The fact that Don Willard, Terry Killens, Jabir Walker, and Justin Larrew went above and beyond the call makes a reward not just appropriate but necessary.
