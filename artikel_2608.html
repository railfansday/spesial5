Stephen Jones say the "time is nigh" to make a decision on the Bears' next stadium

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

As the Bears continue to do an awkward Bossa Nova with Indiana and Illinois over the location of a new stadium, a key executive from another team has chimed in.

“The time is nigh to get something done,” Cowboys COO and co-owner Stephen Jones told Eric Fisher of Front Office Sports. “This definitely feels like it’s coming to a head and getting to the point of a decision.”

On the surface, it seems unconventional (to say the least) for an owner of one team to speak publicly about the business affairs of another team. The fact that Jones has a seat on the league-wide body responsible for stadiums makes it somewhat less unusual.

“As a [stadium] committee member, I want the Bears to have the very best, and what is now clear is that the Bears are going to have great options, and ultimately, a special building,” Jones told Fisher. “We need the Bears to be one of the top franchises in the league. . . . It’s getting time to make a decision.”

The comments likely aren’t intended to put the squeeze on the Bears. They seem to be part of the calculated effort to get Illinois to pass legislation that would lay the foundation for a new venue that would keep the team from crossing the border to Indiana — especially with the current legislative session ending on December 3.

The problem seems to be that the powers-that-be in Illinois don’t view Indiana as a viable option for the Bears. Illinois likely sees it as a leverage play aimed at getting Illinois to do what needs to be done.

That will change only when the Bears and the NFL persuade Illinois that they’re serious about Indiana. Jones did his part in addressing the dynamics of a regional relocation across state lines.

“We’ve seen that across the league,” Jones told Fisher. “It’s hard to develop and build these buildings, but they’ve done it or are doing it now, and it’s been working out great.”

It’s one of the most basic realities of negotiations. The “or else” alternative isn’t taken seriously until it’s regarded as a viable alternative. Which can result in an ongoing game of chess morphing into a short-fused game of chicken.

Because one or more of the NFL’s teams are, at any given time, trying to do deals for stadium construction or renovation, the Bears’ situation gives rise to broader concerns. Other state and local governments need to take future huffing and puffing seriously.

Which means that the Bears may have to blow down the house in Illinois and build one in Indiana, if only to prevent public entities negotiating subsequent stadium deals from chronically calling the NFL’s bluff.
