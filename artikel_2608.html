Position Grades: Minnesota

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Tim is out today, so it's a substitute teacher for this week's grades. The good news is that Penn State behaved very well in its 42-14 win against Minnesota. So, I don't have to leave a nasty note about any position groups.

Rocco Becht threw three touchdowns passes and avoided any catastrophic mistakes. He also ran for a score. Alex Manske threw for a score, too. Overall, I'm boosting this grade a bit because with the conditions as they were, it wasn't an easy night to throw and the quarterbacks were better than fine.

Carson Hansen ran for more than 120 yards on 10 carries, showing the explosiveness that he didn't the first half of the season. Hopefully the injury that kept him off the field in the second half heals quickly. Cam Wallace was good in a secondary role, too. His tough fourth down run early in the third quarter will be an underrated play in a 28-point win, while he also caught a touchdown pass. Lastly, James Peoples scored on a wheel route and a good wheel route is worth some bonus points.

The weather. The game script. Those things meant that the wide receivers were hardly targeted at all. Chase Sowell nearly caught another long pass if not for a great play by a Minnesota defensive back. Brett Eskildsen caught the one ball thrown to him and it was the same with Amarion Jackson.

Ben Brahmer only had two catches, but he made them count. His used his large frame to grab a couple of touchdowns and nearly had a spectacular back-of-the-end-zone grab in the second quarter. Andrew Rappleyea had four catches, including one where he may have been as open as any Penn State pass catcher ever.

This group was trending to A++ through the first two and a half quarters, but then Minnesota made some adjustments. Early on, there were massive running lanes for Wallace and Hansen to run through. Meanwhile, check out the pocket that Becht had on his first touchdown throw. It's clear this group has gotten healthy and gotten comfortable playing with one another over the course of the season.

The Gophers had just 41 yards rushing. Meanwhile, there were some disruptive plays from this group. Alexander McPherson had two tackles for loss, including a sack. Dallas Vakalahi had a sack, while Yvan Kemajou opened the game with a beautiful tackle for loss.

Kooper Ebel had his best game in a Penn State uniform, leading the team in tackles and coming up with an early fumble recovery. It's great to see Cam Smith back and healthy – he was active and had a great special teams play, too. The only thing keeping them from an A was it seemed they were attacked a bit late in the third quarter when the Gophers started to make things a little interesting.

Sort of rinse and repeat for the secondary. Overall, Minnesota had less than 200 yards passing on the night. But, they did have those back-to-back scoring drives in the third quarter that came nearly entirely through the air. Therefore, it's hard to give the secondary a full A grade.

Ryan Barker was 5-of-5 on extra points. Nathan Tiyce averaged 44.6 yards per punt and two were downed inside the 20 (thanks Cam Smith for the one). The kickoff coverage was great. Zay Robinson was good with his two punt returns. The lone bad part? There was a costly personal foul penalty on one punt that gave the Gophers better field position ahead of a touchdown drive.

I know there will be a lot of hand wringing about the third quarter – Penn State was outscored 14-7 and it felt like Minnesota was sneaking back in the game. Third quarters have clearly been an issue for the Lions, dating back to the disastrous one against Wisconsin. But, some credit is due to Campbell and staff for how last night's third quarter started. Up 28-0, the Lions stayed aggressive and went for it on a fourth down play with Wallace gaining just enough to earn a first down. That play allowed Penn State to continue on a long drive that ate up nearly half of the quarter and it was a drive that ended with the Lions up 35-0. Even though things stalled from there, Penn State was never truly in danger of losing that game.

It's been a weird year in Minneapolis. Their offense hasn't clicked, but they've played a lot of good teams tough. This game, they dealt with weirdness even getting to Happy Valley – flying in the morning of the game. P.J. Fleck, who I normally find to be hard to listen to, said that was no excuse in seeing Penn State build a four touchdown halftime lead. The Gophers did play hard through the end and, to an extent, made things interesting in the early part of the fourth quarter.
