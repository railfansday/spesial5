Open Thread: A second generation of NBA players has taken over the league

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Today's NBA is loaded with talent. Children today grow up competitive in the sport from a young age. For many, the familiarity begins at birth. Stephen Curry and Klay Thompson, two of the best shooters to play the game, both grew up on the hardwood. Curry's father Dell played sixteen seasons in the NBA. In 1994, he was the league's Sixth Man. Mychel Thompson, Klay's dad, was the #1 draft pick in 1978. He played thirteen seasons and picked up two rings with the Showtime-era Lakers.

Today, the Spurs have Dylan Harper, son of five-time NBA star Ron Harper. He was the second overall pick in 2025. Cameron Boozer, this year's third overall draft pick, is the son of 2-time NBA All-Star Carlos Boozer. But the ties of current players whose fathers played in the NBA is a much deeper well.

Current NBA players including Jalen Brunson, Andrew Wiggins, Domantas Sabonis, Devin Booker, Jaren Jackson, Jr., Al Horford, Jerami Grant, Luke Kornet, Gary Payton II, Tim Hardaway, Jr., Scotty Pippen, Jr., Jabari Smith, Jr., Gary Trent, Jr., Ron Harper, Jr., Trayce Jackson-Davis, Cameron Carr, Devin Carter, Reed Sheppard, Nic Claxton, Larry Nance, Jr. and his brother Pete, Jase Richardson, Jabari Walker, Darius Garland, and Bronny James all have fathers who played (or in Bronny's case still play) in the NBA.

And if you look at a younger generation, you see the trend is most likely to comnintue:

With so many of today's NBA stars growing up around the game through their fathers, it's a good time to ask — could the children of the San Antonio Spurs classic Big 3 line-up join forces someday in the NBA?

Tim Duncan's son Draven is nineteen and currently playing for Pomona-Pitzer. If that name sounds familiar, Gregg Popovich served as the head coach of their basketball program from 1979-1987. And a former basketball player by the name of Mike Budenholzer also belongs to the alma mater's Hall of Fame.

Recently, Manu Ginobili's twins Dante and Nico, age sixteen, were seen shooting hoops for their high school:

The duo, who currently play for Saint Mary's Hall, are in their junior year. Depending on their talent, they could be NBA prospects in a couple of years.

Finally, Tony Parker's sons Josh and Liam are age 12 and 10 respectively. While neither have shown an interest in basketball (and Parker does his best to keep them out of the public eye), one has to believe that the apples don't fall far from the tree. A player as talented as Parker has to have passed on some of those basketball jeans. But then again, not every offspring gets their father's talent for the game…just ask Michael Jordan.

But, just for fun… imagine a Spurs lineup ten years from now featuring the next generation of Duncan, Ginobilis, and Parkers. A San Antonio Spurs being elevated by the sons of the three stars who helped bring four championships together. I realize that even if all five kids grew up with the skills to be in the NBA, it would be a long shot to get even three of them on one team. After all, there are twenty-nine other teams who'd forbid that from ever happenin….again.
