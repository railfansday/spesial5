Asha Bhosle and S. Janaki Remembered by Singer K.S. Chithra and Suhasini Maniratnam at IFFI’s Lata Mangeshkar Talk

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Asha Bhosle and S. Janaki, two of Indian cinema’s most celebrated playback singers, were remembered at the 57th International Film Festival of India (IFFI) in Goa by singer K.S. Chithra and actor-filmmaker Suhasini Maniratnam.

Bhosle, a two-time Grammy nominee whose name was referenced in Cornershop’s 1997 hit “Brimful of Asha,” died in April at 92. Janaki, known as the Nightingale of South India, died in July at 88. Chithra and Suhasini shared their memories of the two at the annual “Lata Mangeshkar Talk: The Voice That Echoes Forever,” named for Bhosle’s elder sister, the singer Lata Mangeshkar. Chithra, who has recorded more than 25,000 songs in several Indian languages over a career spanning nearly five decades, spoke about the singers who shaped her musical sensibilities, among them Mangeshkar, P. Susheela and Janaki. She imitated Janaki as a child, while acknowledging that the veteran singer’s distinctive style was impossible to reproduce. Mangeshkar’s devotional songs, she said, had particularly impressed her with their emotional depth.

Suhasini recalled an episode from the making of “Iruvar” (1997), directed by her husband Mani Ratnam. Composer A.R. Rahman, an Oscar winner for “Slumdog Millionaire,” had invited Bhosle to record a song that needed to evoke the style of Tamil playback singer L.R. Eswari, but was unsure how to explain the reference to her. He asked Suhasini to help. She demonstrated Eswari’s style, and Bhosle went on to deliver “Venilla Venilla.”

Chithra spoke about Bhosle’s versatility and her ability to move between musical forms, including ghazals, and about her desire to establish her own musical identity rather than be compared with her sister. At their first meeting, Chithra said, Bhosle asked her why she was singing in such a high register.

Janaki, Suhasini said, could inhabit a character through her voice, singing like a child or a teenage boy. Chithra recalled that Janaki was a devoted fan of Mangeshkar. She would play Mangeshkar’s songs on the radio, turn down the volume during the vocal portions and record herself singing along – “like a karaoke on her own,” as Chithra described it. Offstage, Chithra said, Janaki would feed her by hand when she visited her home.

The session also touched on the demands of recording and on recognizing one’s vocal limitations with age.

Chithra sang snippets throughout, including songs from her own repertoire and numbers popularized by Janaki and Bhosle. In a closing quiz, film clips were played without their background scores and Chithra was asked to identify each song by singing it. She identified all five.

The session ended with Chithra singing “Paadariyen” from “Sindhu Bhairavi” (1985), which won her the first of her six National Film Awards, as Suhasini, who starred in the film, joined her, lip-syncing.
