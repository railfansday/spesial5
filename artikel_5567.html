SpaceX is launching Starship in its first attempt to catch both rocket stages today

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Free daily briefing on global business news.

SpaceX said success would mark the first full recovery of an orbital launch system, with the ship catch set for about 22 hours after liftoff

SpaceX plans to launch Starship Flight 15 from Starbase in Texas on Friday in its first attempt to catch both the Super Heavy booster and the Starship upper stage with its launch tower.

The company said the 75-minute launch window opens at 5:45 p.m. ET. SpaceX has said the schedule is dynamic and could change, and a live webcast is planned to start about 30 minutes before liftoff.

If both catches succeed, it would be the first time an entire orbital launch system has been brought back intact, SpaceX said. Reusing both stages would allow the company to load fresh propellant and send those same vehicles back up, according to Bloomberg.

SpaceX set out a catch timeline for the flight. The booster catch is scheduled about 6 minutes and 35 seconds after liftoff. The ship is slated to circle the planet about 14 times at a height near 275 kilometers (171 miles) before its catch, which is scheduled about 22 hours and 32 minutes after liftoff.

This will be the first return and catch attempt for a Super Heavy V3 booster. Three V1 boosters were caught on earlier flights, and two of them flew again. SpaceX has not attempted a ship catch, which is harder than a booster catch because the ship flies a longer route at greater altitude and velocity.

The ship's return to the launch site depends on the flight control team confirming that the systems needed for reentry maneuvers, the landing burn and the tower approach are healthy. The flight profile lets SpaceX cut the flight short and bring the ship down in a splashdown if an engine problem or another issue arises, according to Gizmodo.

Starship will deploy 26 Starlink V3 satellites. Each adds 1 terabit per second of capacity to the constellation, and the satellites should begin serving customers a few weeks after launch at the earliest. Three of them carry cameras that will scan the ship’s heatshield and send imagery to operators.

Flight 14, on Sept. 28, was the first to send the ship to orbit, where it released 26 Starlink V3 satellites. The booster made a soft splashdown in the Gulf of America on that flight instead of returning to the tower, as SpaceX tested changes to improve engine relight reliability.

CEO Elon Musk outlined the double-catch plan on SpaceX's third-quarter earnings call last month. "I'd say it's more likely than not that this succeeds next flight," Musk said. SpaceX is aiming for Starship to reach full capability around the end of 2027.
