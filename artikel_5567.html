Rate’s Jesse Allen: Reverse mortgage lenders should plan for the ‘down case,’ not FHA relief

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Jesse Allen of Rate said lenders should expand reverse mortgage product menus to reduce reliance on FHA-backed HECMs. He said proprietary reverse is about 40% of Rate originations and that Rate is also growing its HECM volume year over year.  AI Summary

With mortgage lenders continuing to cut expenses, including personnel, as the originations forecast for 2027 dims, Jesse Allen said the reverse mortgage business at Rate is still growing and he isn’t waiting for federal housing leaders to offer help.

At last week’s National Reverse Mortgage Lenders Association Annual Meeting & Expo, Allen said he’s planning for the “down case” on Federal Housing Administration policy regarding upfront mortgage insurance premiums (MIP) for Home Equity Conversion Mortgages (HECMs). Allen’s comments came not long after Ginnie Mae President Joe Gormley said the Trump administration is “very comfortable” with current MIP structures for FHA-insured single-family home loans and reverse mortgages.

“In an uncertain environment, you have to plan and execute to your down case scenarios, and then when something better than down case happens, you’re that much better,” Allen said.

Allen, the president of the 55+ lending division at Rate and a NRMLA board member for 2026-27, sat down with HousingWire‘s Reverse Mortgage Daily at NRMLA Annual to discuss some of the hot topics of interest for industry professionals. He said that lenders should look to expand their product menus to insulate themselves from any obstacles to federally insured loans, and he pitched responsible home equity release as the next big opportunity for the wider mortgage industry.

NRMLA and other trade groups have spent the past year arguing that the FHA could lower the upfront mortgage insurance premium for certain borrowers. Allen said that cutting some fees would pull more people into the FHA’s Mutual Mortgage Insurance Fund, which he called “basic insurance economics.” NRMLA has also laid out the math to lower premiums for borrowers who utilize less of their HECM proceeds while lobbying to eliminate second appraisals.

But with the political transition underway after the recent midterm elections, Allen does not expect quick action. “I’m not building my business model or business case around that,” he said. “If positive things happen, it’s wind in the sails.”

Still, a lower upfront MIP could be “powerful for older homeowners” who want to access their equity responsibly, he said, as they might tap 30% to 40% of their available line of credit now while saving the rest for later in retirement.

When asked whether he thinks a more balanced Congress is coming in 2027, Allen said everyone’s definition of balanced differs. But he also believes that if Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-Mass.) takes over the Senate banking committee, “we’re going to see more regulatory interest.” Allen’s approach, he added, is to operate daily as though auditors are showing up, which should be the standard for a business that serves a protected class of senior homeowners.

Allen clarified that Rate is a nondelegated correspondent lender for proprietary reverse mortgage products and is fully delegated on FHA-backed HECM products. Proprietary loans make up about 40% of Rate’s reverse mortgage originations, he said, and he estimated them at 55% to 65% of its volume based on their typically larger loan sizes.

Similar to the industry at large, private-label loans are where growth is happening at Rate, although its HECM volume is rising too, Allen said, with the company outpacing the industry year over year. According to data compiled by Reverse Market Insight, Rate was the 13th-largest HECM lender in the country through the first nine months of the year, with its 228 endorsements up 41% compared to the same period in 2025.

Proprietary loans often serve borrowers that FHA products can’t, including owners of high-value homes and those who want to pay off consumer debt without it counting against them in qualification — a guideline NRMLA has pushed to change. But Allen said his team spends time reminding loan officers that the HECM is “still an awesome product.”

Compared with a traditional home equity line of credit, he said, a HECM line of credit mitigates the risk that falling home values lead to frozen lines, leaving borrowers without access when “they need their equity the most.” HECM lines of credit can grow for the life of the loan if the borrower doesn’t exhaust funds and meets requirements like property tax and insurance payments. Proprietary lines of credit typically stop growing after seven to 10 years, Allen added.

Allen pointed to reverse for purchase financing as a tool for addressing affordability. He said that one of Rate’s top loan officers recently closed a reverse for purchase for the parent of one of his real estate agent partners. The LO had a couple of reverse mortgages under his belt but had never done a purchase deal, and he worked with one of the company’s specialists to get it done.

“Specialty lending in general is a big priority for most mortgage companies today,” Allen said, naming non-QM and builder programs alongside reverse.

Allen thinks that today’s retirees are less willing to sacrifice their lifestyles. Reverse mortgage proceeds can fund a variety of uses — from family vacations to in-home care that eases the load on adult children, or even a move into a 55-plus community without draining savings.

“That’s why I love this business, because it’s infinitely customizable,” he said. “Every client is unique, every situation is unique, and we have this expanding suite of products to really help deliver for the clients … so that they can get the joy out of the transaction.”
