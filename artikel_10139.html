Not long ago, a synthetic voice was easy to spot: flat, rushed and strangely stressed. Today, the best AI text to speech tools can read a script with natural pacing, switch accents, whisper or laugh on cue, and even speak in a voice cloned from a short recording. That makes them useful far beyond screen readers. Developers use them for product voice prompts, marketers for video voiceovers, teachers for course narration and writers for listening back to their own drafts.

This guide explains how modern text-to-speech works, what to look for when choosing a tool, and compares six options: OpenArt, ElevenLabs, Murf AI, Speechify, WellSaid Labs and Amazon Polly. Each section covers strengths, limits and the best use case. The details come from each vendor’s own pages at the time of writing, and plans, models and prices change often, so confirm current terms before you commit.

OpenArt’s AI text to speech tool lives inside a wider creative platform. You open the Audio tab, choose Create voice-over, select a model, paste your script, pick a voice, generate, listen and download. It runs in the browser with nothing to install. According to OpenArt’s page, you can switch between models for each script, including Fish Audio Drama 3, Eleven v4 and Eleven v4 Turbo.

The direction system is what makes it practical for storytelling. You add notes in square brackets, such as [whispering], [laughs] or [pause], directly in the script. OpenArt says Fish Audio Drama 3 gives close control over single words and mid-line changes of mood, while Eleven v4 reads the whole script to infer the emotion of each line and keeps the voice steady when you regenerate a line. Preset voices are labeled by accent, gender and age, and you can upload a recording of your own voice to clone it.

Because OpenArt also makes video and background music, you can build the voiceover, visuals and soundtrack in one place. It describes a Director mode for planning stories up to five minutes, and the voiceover can also be added in any video editor.

Pricing: New accounts get 40 free credits. Paid plans add monthly credits and watermark-free downloads, and commercial rights apply on eligible paid plans. Check the pricing page for current plans.

Best for: creators who want expressive voiceovers together with AI video and music.

ElevenLabs is one of the best-known names in AI voice. Its text to speech page describes a large voice library, voice design and both instant and professional voice cloning, with the latest model, Eleven v4, positioned as the most expressive. It also lists Eleven v3, Multilingual v2 and a low-latency Flash and Turbo family aimed at real-time use, and it claims support for more than 90 languages and accents, with the exact count varying by model.

For developers, there is a text to speech API with SDKs, which makes it practical for apps, games, agents and automated pipelines. The free plan includes 10,000 credits a month, and paid plans start with Starter at $6 a month, then Creator at $22 and Pro at $99, according to the vendor page, with introductory offers on the first month.

Pricing: Free plan with 10,000 credits a month. Paid plans from $6 a month as listed on the vendor page.

Best for: expressive narration, multilingual content and apps that need a voice API.

Murf AI positions itself as a studio for voiceovers. Its page lists more than 200 voices, built with permission and partnership from professional voice actors who earn royalties, and support for 35 or more languages for text to speech, with dubbing in 40 or more. The Studio editor offers pitch, speed and intonation controls and a custom pronunciation library, which helps with brand names and technical terms.

Murf also has an API. Its page describes Falcon 2 for conversational use with time-to-first-audio under 100 milliseconds, priced at $0.01 per minute, and Gen 2 for controllable voiceovers. Gen 2’s “Say it My Way” feature lets you record your own rendition so the model reflects your tone and inflections.

Pricing: Free plan with limits. Paid plan prices are on Murf’s pricing page.

Best for: marketing videos, training and presentations where you want to fine-tune delivery.

Speechify is built around reading aloud. You paste text or upload a PDF, DOCX, TXT or EPUB file, pick a voice, adjust playback speed and listen or download the audio. Its page lists more than 1,000 voices across 60 or more languages, including several regional English accents, and the tool works in the browser and in apps for iOS, Android, Mac and Windows, plus Chrome and Edge extensions.

The use cases lean toward productivity and accessibility: studying, going through emails and documents, language learning and support for people with visual impairments, dyslexia or ADHD. A free account saves your documents and syncs them across devices.

Pricing: You can start free. Paid plans are listed on Speechify’s pricing page.

Best for: students, professionals and anyone who wants to listen to documents and articles.

WellSaid Labs focuses on professional voiceover for businesses. Every voice is built from recordings of a real voice actor who consented and is paid when the voice is used, and the voices are licensed for commercial use. Its page describes hundreds of voices across languages, accents, tones and styles.

Pronunciation control is a highlight. You can highlight a word to see suggested pronunciations and save your choice to a team library that applies to every project, and set pacing, emphasis and pronunciation line by line. WellSaid also describes a Producer product that connects AI assistants to WellSaid through MCP and its API.

Pricing: Contact sales for a quote based on your voiceover volume.

Best for: corporate training, product marketing, courses and advertising where licensing and consistency matter.

Amazon Polly is Amazon Web Services’ text to speech service, and it is aimed squarely at developers. According to AWS, it supports more than 40 languages and language variants with over 100 male and female voices, and it works through APIs in your code. It supports SSML, the W3C standard markup for phrasing, emphasis and intonation, and custom lexicons that fix the pronunciation of acronyms and terms. Output formats include MP3 and OGG.

Best for: apps, IVR systems, notifications and any product that needs automated speech at scale.

Treat this table as a starting point. Features and limits change often, so test two or three tools with the same script.

Modern text-to-speech systems turn written words into audio through several stages.

Newer expressive systems go further by reading the whole script to infer emotion and context, and by accepting direction in the text itself, such as a bracketed note that says to whisper. That is why a single script can produce very different reads depending on the model and the instructions.

What is the best AI text to speech tool? It depends on the job. OpenArt suits voiceovers with video and music, ElevenLabs suits expressive multilingual audio, Murf AI suits studio-style control, Speechify suits listening, WellSaid Labs suits licensed corporate work and Amazon Polly suits developers.

Is AI text to speech free? Several tools offer free plans or credits, but limits are common. Examples include OpenArt’s free credits for new accounts, ElevenLabs’ free monthly credits and Murf’s limited free plan, which has no commercial licence.

Can I use AI voices commercially? Often yes on paid plans, but the rules differ by tool. Always check the current licence before publishing.

Can AI text to speech clone my voice? Some tools support it, including OpenArt and ElevenLabs, usually from a short recording of your own voice. Only clone voices you have permission to use.

What is SSML? Speech Synthesis Markup Language is a W3C standard that lets you control pauses, emphasis, pronunciation and pitch in synthetic speech. Amazon Polly is one service that supports it.

Which tool is best for developers? Amazon Polly and ElevenLabs both offer APIs, and Murf describes API options as well. Evaluate latency, languages, pricing and SDK support for your use case.

How do I make AI voices sound less robotic? Write conversationally, add pauses and emphasis, correct pronunciations and split long scripts into short sections. Choose a model that is designed for expressive reads.

AI text to speech is now good enough for real projects, but the right tool depends on what you are making. OpenArt is a strong first stop for expressive voiceovers alongside video and music, ElevenLabs offers a deep voice library and API, Murf AI and WellSaid Labs suit polished, controlled narration, Speechify turns documents into audio and Amazon Polly fits straight into developer workflows. Test two or three with your own script, check the licence and always listen to the full result before it goes live.
