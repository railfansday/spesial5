PFT's Thanksgiving and Week 12 2026 NFL picks

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

After flirting a couple of times with landing under .500 in a given week of picks, it finally happened. For Week 11, I went 6-7.

That’s what I get for trying to pick upsets. In Week 11, it was Chalk City — and 12 of 13 home teams won.

And so, from now on, I’m not getting cute. I’m sticking with my first impulse. I’m not overthinking it. The first number that pops into my head will be the pick.

For the year, I’m at 102-60-1. The success rate is still 62.5 percent. Here’s hoping I can find a way to push it a little higher with the 16 games to be played from Wednesday through Monday.

Sean McVay is 0-5 against Matt LaFleur. A loss on Wednesday night would put the preseason favorites in grave danger of missing the playoffs, especially in light of an upcoming schedule that includes games against the Chiefs, the 49ers, the Cowboys, the Seahawks (twice), and the Buccaneers.

It’s the biggest early-window Thanksgiving game ever, with both teams at 8-2. Ben Johnson’s Bears gave up 52 points during last year’s visit to Ford Field. They’ll be determined to achieve a different result this time, in what becomes a true litmus test for the new-look Bears. If they’re truly contenders, this is a game they win.

It won’t be a cakewalk. For Philly, it’s the closest they’ll get this year to a game with a postseason vibe. But the Cowboys have a chance to make a run at the Giants — if Dallas can also hold off the Commanders.

Josh Allen is 5-0 against Patrick Mahomes in the regular season, 0-4 against Mahomes in the postseason. The trend continues, thanks to conditions that are more conducive to Allen making the right throw at the right time.

This one had the feel of a game the Steelers would find a way to win, until the benching of Aaron Rodgers threw everything into disarray.

The Bengals need to get back on track, quickly, after Monday night’s sluggish performance. A slump could open the door for the Browns to steal the division.

It feels like an elimination game in Houston, even if the winner will still be facing an uphill climb to the postseason. When in doubt, ride with the clearly better quarterback.

This will be a far cry from the 1958 NFL Championship. The Giants are surging, and the Colts are slowly disintegrating.

As the saying goes, it’s not the size of the dog in the fight but the size of the fight in the dog. Unfortunately, this fight is round two of Doberman vs. Chihuahua.

The Dolphins are running out of chances to get a second win (if they even want one).

Kirk Cousins gets the double whammy — his first home game in his second stint with the Vikings, and his first shot at revenge against the team that signed him and then drafted his replacement.

It’s fitting that a game pitting two struggling teams coached by former Ohio State and Michigan players happens one day after a game between struggling Ohio State and Michigan teams.

Monday night’s upset of the Bengals means nothing if the Commanders bungle a winnable game at Arizona.

Having an extra week to prepare, coupled with the urgency of a 6-4 record, is just what the doctor ordered for the Seahawks.

The NFL will be happy it flexed this one into primetime. (For reasons other than getting Patriots-Chargers out of that window.)

Tampa Bay is running out of chances to pull themselves back into contention.
