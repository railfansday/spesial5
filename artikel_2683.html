Red Sox DFA Connor Wong: A Look Back and What It Means

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The Boston Red Sox made a trade for former Tampa Bay Rays catcher Nick Fortes today, but they gave up a minor leaguer, meaning a corresponding move still had to be made on Boston's end. This move was later revealed to involve Connor Wong, the team announced.

The last remaining piece from the 2020 Mookie Betts trade was designated for assignment this afternoon, making this (potentially) upcoming season's catcher room look completely different from the start of the 2026 season. Here's a bit on Wong's Red Sox career, as well as what this move means for the Red Sox now.

Jun 5, 2026; Bronx, New York, USA; Boston Red Sox catcher Connor Wong (12) singles during the third inning against the New York Yankees at Yankee Stadium. Mandatory Credit: Vincent Carchietta-Imagn Images

Acquired in the Betts deal in February 2020, the Red Sox called Wong up to the 40-man roster that season, after the minor league season was canceled due to COVID-19. They would not call him up to the active roster until midseason the year after, and he made his MLB debut on June 22nd, 2021.

Wong became the full-time catcher in 2023, after Christian Vázquez was traded to the Houston Astros at the 2022 deadline. He would have his best statistical season in that stretch in 2024, where he hit .280 with 13 homers and 52 RBI.

His issues were always defensive, though. On that front, he was either inconsistent or flat-out bad. In 2023, Wong had a DRS of four, followed by a DRS of -14 in 2024. When Carlos Narváez broke onto the scene with his arm behind the plate after being called up in 2025, combined with Wong's struggles at the plate, Wong slowly got shifted out of the regular lineup. Even though he would still catch every fifth day with Brayan Bello pitching, it just felt like they were slowly but surely moving on.

This move means that the Red Sox officially have a completely new catcher room compared to last Opening Day, with Adley Rutschman, Fortes, and maybe Mickey Gasper, although he saw more time at DH despite being able to catch. On paper, this may be a better catcher situation for the Red Sox, considering multiple instances of the catcher room being a major topic of discussion last year.

As mentioned before, not only does this affect the catcher room, but it might affect part of the rotation as well. Bello would rely on Wong this season in his starts, and relied on him in past years too. After Bello's struggles last season have led to questions about whether he'll return to the rotation, the question becomes: if he does, who will be his guy behind the plate?

Wong was one of the longest-tenured Red Sox, after Tanner Houck and Garrett Whitlock. The team will surely take an impact from this, but this move was seen as one in the making for a few months now, due to his constant struggles. With Fortes on the team, Boston took the chance to move on. It's sad to see Wong go, but this could be seen as a move that's beneficial to the team moving forward.
