High school football schedule, score predictions for Week 14 Games of the Week

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

We're not even a few weeks into the high school football postseason in most states, and for many, the easy games are over, with massive matchups now lining up.

This week is a perfect example, as several huge showdowns from around the country could decide who goes on to win state championships.

But first, here are the USA TODAY High School Sports staff's prediction standings heading into Week 14:

High school football rankings: Two powerhouses fall in Super 25

The only two unbeaten teams in Indiana's Class 6A field square off in the semifinal round for the second time in the last two years. Last year, Brownsburg rolled to a 31-0 win over the Warriors en route to their second consecutive state championship.

Key for the Warriors this year will be Iowa running back Keyon Thomas, who was limited last season with an ankle injury. Thomas has been on a tear this season, rushing for 1,800 yards and 24 touchdowns. Defensively, the Warriors are led by Kentucky linebacker commit Sean Fox and Purdue safety commit Kaleb Elkins.

Brownsburg enters this game riding a 31-game winning streak. Former Warren Central head coach John Hart has turned the Bulldogs not only into the state's best team, but also a name to know on the national scene. They're led by Indiana receiver commit Branden Sharpe, Illinois State quarterback commit Oscar Frye and senior running back Brady Lewis.

Alex Martin prediction: Brownsburg 28, Warren Central 17

Alyce Brown prediction: Brownsburg 31, Warren Central 21

Ansley Gavlak prediction: Brownsburg 28, Warren Central 21

Ehsan Kassim prediction: Brownsburg 42, Warren Central 3

Joe Spears prediction: Warren Central 31, Brownsburg 28

Logan Newman prediction: Brownsburg 38, Warren Central 24

The top-ranked team in the country leaned heavily on its defense to beat San Clemente 7-0 after star sophomore running back Adrian Petero suffered an apparent leg injury in the first quarter and missed the rest of the game. He left the field under his own power, and his status is unclear for this weekend's matchup.

If he's not available, the Eagles will need more offensive production to beat St. John Bosco for a second time this season. Their Oct. 16 matchup ended with a 21-7 Santa Margarita win.

Offensively, St. John Bosco had no issues last week, picking up an impressive 38-0 home win over Orange Lutheran. It was a good bounce-back win for the Braves after they blew a late lead against Mater Dei on Oct. 30. Over their last three games, the Braves are averaging 42.3 points per game.

For the first time since September, the Trailblazers were held to fewer than 40 points in a game as they held off Mission Viejo 21-7. Jaxsen Stokes had a night in the win, rushing for 170 yards and two touchdowns.

It may not have been another blowout, but it was the sixth consecutive game in which they allowed seven points or fewer.

That Trailblazers defense will face its biggest test since playing Chaminade-Madonna (FL) back in September as they travel to Centennial.

The Huskies opened postseason play with a statement win, crushing Mater Dei 45-15. Senior quarterback Jaden Jefferson was instrumental in the win, passing for four touchdowns and adding another on the ground; the Jaden Walk-Green has been a difference-maker since returning from injury.

It could be argued that no team in the country had a more impressive win last week than Lakeland, as the Dreadnaughts knocked off St. Thomas Aquinas 27-24 on the road in overtime.

That win marked their ninth straight win after losing to Venice (FL) 32-25 in double overtime back on Sept. 4.

This week their defense will face arguably its biggest test of the season. Chaminade-Madonna enters this game after beating Venice 35-0, led by another stellar performance from junior quarterback Brady Quinn. This season, Quinn has completed 73% of his passes for 2,873 yards and 34 touchdowns to just four interceptions.

The only two teams that beat Mount Carmel (IL) this season square off in a massive Class 7A semifinal showdown.

East. St. Louis has consistently been ranked as Illinois' top team this year and for good reason. Despite losing Auburn running back commit Myson Johnson-Cook to injury, the Flyers have put up video-game-like numbers. They're averaging 50.9 points per game and winning by an average of 44 points.

Brother Rice has two losses to Lincoln-Way East (IL) and Loyola Academy (IL), but those were the only two single-digit games the Crusaders have played in this season. Freshman quarterback Sam Holcomb has emerged as a star, passing for more than 1,435 yards and 23 touchdowns with no interceptions.

Alyce Brown prediction: East St. Louis 31, Brother Rice 24

Ansley Gavlak prediction: East St. Louis 28, Brother Rice 24

Date: Friday, Nov. 20, 7 p.m. ET at Zanesville High

For the second straight week, Massillon is facing one of Ohio's top three programs. Will the team move to 2-0 in those matchups?

The Tigers were stout defensively last week, shutting out Big Walnut 21-0. After a scoreless first half, Massillon scored all its points over the final 24 minutes.

Defensively, few teams in the state have been better than Bishop Watterson this season. All year, they've allowed only 57 total points, for an average of 5.2 points per game. Despite being two of the state's top two programs with 12 state titles between them, this will be the first-ever meeting between the programs.

Alex Martin prediction: Massillon 17, Bishop Watterson 13

Alyce Brown prediction: Bishop Watterson 21, Massillon 17

Ansley Gavlak prediction: Bishop Watterson 24, Massillon 21

Utah's Class 6A championship is on the line Friday afternoon after Corner Canyon and Skyridge each picked up dramatic semifinal wins.

Corner Canyon intercepted seven passes to beat top-ranked Mountain Ridge 34-28, while Skyridge outlasted Herriman 35-34 behind a pair of fourth-quarter touchdowns.

This is not the first time these two have met for a state championship: Skyridge won the 2022 meeting, and Corner Canyon won the 2023 game by 14. Corner Canyon is aiming to win its fourth straight state championship and seventh overall.

Alyce Brown prediction: Corner Canyon 28, Skyridge 21

Ansley Gavlak prediction: Corner Canyon 21, Skyridge 17

Ehsan Kassim prediction: Corner Canyon 21, Skyridge 18

Nearly a month after St. John's shut out DeMatha 12-0 on the road, the Cadets get another shot to make it 2-0 against their crosstown foe.

St. John's has consistently been one of the best defensive teams in the country, especially since mid-October. After giving up 28 points to Cornerstone Christian (TX), the Cadets have allowed only 10 points over their last five games.

The shutout loss to St. John's was the only game this year in which DeMatha failed to score. The Stags responded to that loss by beating Gonzaga (MD) in back-to-back games, scoring 30 or more points in each.

Dowling Catholic's road to reestablishing itself as the team to beat in Iowa successfully added another chapter last Friday as the Maroons avenged their only loss of the season, cruising past Southeast Polk.

Now they get the only other team that managed to keep it within a possession.

The Maroons beat Johnston 41-35 on Sept. 4, and the Dragons responded by winning nine of their next 10 games. Their lone loss during that stretch came by three to Southeast Polk. In their three playoff wins, the Dragons outscored their opponents 98-21.

Can the Pirates one-up the Ironmen again? That was one of the biggest shocks in New Jersey's high school football scene on Oct. 23, when St. Peter's Prep beat Don Bosco 13-10 on a last-second field goal.

St. Peter's Prep followed up that win by beating Red Bank Catholic by three the next week. The Pirates were much more dominant in their postseason opener, crushing Seton Hall Prep 40-7.

The loss to St. Peter's Prep only seemed to wake up Don Bosco. A week after the loss, the Ironmen had no issues in a 42-10 win over Delbarton and last week they beat St. Joseph 56-0 in their first playoff game.
