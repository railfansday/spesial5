NAZA documentary on Gaza killings becomes one of year’s most watched

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

The film, which details Israel’s surveillance of Palestinian civilians, has been seen by more than 1.5m people in less than two months

NAZA, the award-winning documentary that details Israel’s mass killing of civilians in Gaza, has been seen by more than 1.5 million people since being released in cinemas across the world last month.

The film, which is directed by the Oscar-winning Israeli film-makers Yuval Abraham and Rachel Szor and produced by the Guardian, has reached a worldwide audience of 1,547,033 as of Friday 6 November, making it one of the most-watched documentary films with a theatrical release in 2026.

NAZA won the Special Jury prize at the Venice film festival in September, where its premiere was met with a record 24.5 minute standing ovation, and has received widespread critical acclaim. It drew worldwide attention after Israel’s culture minister threatened to strip its directors of their citizenship and prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu ordered the identification and prosecution of the film’s interviewees.

After a cinematic run in 47 territories to date, the investigative documentary’s free global streaming release on theguardian.com is now planned for early December, as the Guardian works to prepare the online video release for a large global audience in multiple languages.

The film has already made cinematic history in Italy, where it was released in September. With 717,517 admissions, NAZA is the third most-watched documentary with a theatrical release of all time in Italy, behind Michael Moore’s Fahrenheit 9/11 and The March of the Penguins.

Other European regions with a notably large interest in NAZA include the UK and Ireland, where the film has garnered a total 102,508 admissions so far, and the Benelux, where distributor Cherry Pickers has counted 132,323 admissions across the three states Belgium, the Netherlands and Luxembourg.

The film has clocked 83,673 admissions across the US and Canada, where it was initially released on 30 September through a self-release model but was picked up in October by distributor Neon. Earlier this month, it won the Critics’ Choice documentary award for best political documentary.

In France, NAZA has been shown in cinemas only over a limited 48-hour period due to the country’s strict rules around films that are also streamed online. The film nonetheless clocked up more than 82,000 admissions over nearly 500 screenings, ranking top of the box office on both days.

There have been multiple reports of audiences sitting in stunned silence as the film’s credits roll in cinemas. “Rarely does a film screening give way to such silence when the lights come back on,” said a review on Radio France.
