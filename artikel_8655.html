Use your commute to learn something new without paying for it. Borrow audiobooks and language courses with your library card, and add a free university podcast for ideas you can use at work. Stick to audio when you drive, and read or study flash cards on a train or bus.

The average one-way commute in the U.S. took 27.4 minutes in 2025, according to the Census Bureau. For someone who commutes five days a week for 48 weeks, the trips there and back add up to about 219 hours a year, more than five 40-hour workweeks.

A library card covers the books and the language lessons. Libby, OverDrive’s free app, lets you borrow your library’s audiobooks and ebooks, and some libraries also offer audio language courses in it, lessons you can follow without looking at a screen. If your library offers Mango Languages, the same card gets you its app lessons in more than 70 languages and dialects. Each library chooses its own titles and services, so what your library card covers depends on which one issued it.

For your career, Stanford posts its Entrepreneurial Thought Leaders seminar as a free podcast, with founders and business leaders talking about how they built their companies.

Match the format to how you travel. Behind the wheel, stick to audio, and start it before you pull out. On a train or bus, you can also read an ebook, work through a lesson in a language app, or study flash cards. Either way, download each title over home Wi-Fi the night before, so a tunnel or a gap in cell service can’t interrupt it.

Say your train ride takes 30 minutes each way, five days a week, 48 weeks a year, and your audiobooks run about 10 hours each. That’s 240 hours a year, enough for 20 audiobooks and a beginner Spanish course of 30 half-hour lessons, with about 25 hours to spare. Borrowed with a library card, none of it costs anything.

Tonight, sign in to Libby with your library card and borrow one audiobook, so tomorrow’s commute starts with chapter one.
