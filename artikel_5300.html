The next Fed rate decision looks settled after Wednesday’s inflation report. On November 25, the October PCE price index, the Federal Reserve’s preferred inflation gauge, showed prices up 3.8% from a year earlier. Core inflation, which strips out food and energy, rose to 3.3%, above forecasts.

Traders on Polymarket now see about a 97% chance that the Fed raises rates at its December 8-9 meeting. That would be the Fed’s second hike of 2026, after September’s.

Stocks fell hard. The Nasdaq dropped as much as 4.4% in the first hour of trading, led by Nvidia, and bitcoin slid to $57,300. The 10-year Treasury yield fell to 4.82% as investors moved into bonds.

So what does a December hike mean for crypto?

The Bureau of Economic Analysis report gave the Fed little reason to wait:

Strong incomes and sticky prices are the mix that worries a central bank. Polymarket’s December market puts a quarter-point hike at 94% and a half-point hike at 2.6%, with $58 million traded on it.

The year has turned around for the Fed. In January, two officials voted for a rate cut. By September, under Chair Kevin Warsh, who took over in May, the Fed raised its target range by 0.25 points to 3.75%-4%. In October, it held, but two officials wanted another hike. A December move would lift the range to 4%-4.25%.

The inflation report landed on a market already shaken by the AI sell-off. Early on Wednesday, CNBC’s market blog showed the Nasdaq down 4.4%, the S&P 500 down 3.1% and the Dow down more than 1,000 points.

Higher rates hit expensive growth stocks hardest, because they make future profits worth less today. Bitcoin, which has traded with tech stocks all autumn, fell more than 4% in 24 hours.

A hike sounds like bad news for crypto. This year’s data tell a more mixed story. We measured bitcoin’s move in the week after each 2026 Fed rate decision, from the open on decision day to the close seven days later.

The pattern fits an old market saying: sell the rumor, buy the news. By the time the Fed acts, a hike is often already in the price. Seven meetings and one hike is a small sample, so treat it as a clue, not a rule.

Bitcoin trades at $58,244, below its 50-day exponential moving average (EMA), an average that gives recent prices more weight, at $71,927, and its 200-day EMA at $74,695.

The December Fed rate decision is now about the size of the hike and what officials signal for 2027. Until then, $57,800 is the line to watch for bitcoin.
