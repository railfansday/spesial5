Shiba Inu (SHIB) recorded 496.3 billion SHIB in inflows in the latest CryptoQuant snapshot, which suggests a possible increase in selling pressure. That pressure could lead to additional selling volume sooner than investors expect.

About 155 billion SHIB in net inflows is an important factor here and, unfortunately, a bearish one. Simply put, more SHIB is being added to exchanges than removed from them. This is not a bullish sign and makes the available supply for selling even larger than it already is.

About 341.3 billion SHIB left exchanges over the same period. Exchange reserves increased by 0.18% to about 85.89 trillion SHIB. The dollar value of those reserves fell by 5.21% to around $316.9 million. Gross inflows were up 2.21% in the table’s 24-hour comparison, while net inflows were up 5.87%. The ten largest deposits accounted for about 10.86 billion SHIB.

The seven-day moving average of mean inflow rose by 86.2%, pointing to a substantial increase in the average deposit size. The moving average reached about 1.72 billion SHIB per deposit. The latest unsmoothed mean inflow was roughly 2.76 billion SHIB per deposit.

From a price perspective, the SHIB chart shows a clearly bearish pattern, with the downtrend continuing rather than a recovery taking shape. Falling trading volume alongside the price could suggest that selling pressure is easing, but it should not be seen as a sign of a reversal.

SHIB was trading at $0.00000370 on the Binance daily chart on November 20. The session low at the time of the snapshot was $0.00000365. The asset was below all the moving averages shown on the chart, and the RSI was near the oversold area. The brief bounce after the latest drop also stayed below $0.000004.

For SHIB to make a lasting recovery, it needs to break above the $0.000004 price level. Price moves below this level are unlikely to provide a solid base for recovery.
