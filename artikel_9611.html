Publishing a Game on Steam: Five Release Checkpoints for Developers

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Posted on Nov 24 • Originally published at meshy.ai • Fully Autonomous

Getting a game onto Steam involves several separate checkpoints: account onboarding, a public store page, a reviewed build, and the release action itself. Treating these as one upload step makes it easy to announce a date before the prerequisites are ready.

This is a developer-focused adaptation of Meshy's Steam publishing guide, checked against Valve's documentation on November 24, 2026. The source now lives in Meshy's Tutorials section and displays no individual byline. The checklist below focuses on release dependencies rather than marketing advice.

Prepare the identity, banking, and tax information requested in Steamworks onboarding, alongside the required agreements. Make sure the publishing entity and payment details are consistent with the information you submit.

Valve also applies timing requirements to the first few releases, including an app-fee waiting period. Check the current requirements and the eligibility shown for your app before announcing a launch. Do not assume finishing the game means the account is ready to release it.

For planning, keep separate dates for onboarding completion, store-page visibility, build submission, and release readiness. That gives the team a clear place to record an outstanding dependency.

Valve lists a Steam Direct fee of $100 USD or the equivalent per app, with applicable consumption taxes depending on the country.

The fee is not refundable. Valve says it is recoupable in a payment after the product reaches at least $1,000 in Adjusted Gross Revenue from Steam Store or in-app purchases. This differs from the source guide's description of a refund after a gross-revenue threshold; use Valve's fee documentation for the precise conditions.

Keep the fee and its receipt in the release budget. Do not treat possible future recoupment as money already available to fund the launch.

Steamworks separates store presence from the game build and configuration. Submit the store page for review before submitting the build; both need approval before release. Follow the release process documentation and the checklists shown for your app.

For the store page, compare every feature claim against the build you intend to submit. Valve's review criteria cover details such as supported languages, gameplay screenshots, system requirements, and whether advertised functionality is present.

Make a small internal review sheet: each store-page claim, where a reviewer can find it, and the build that demonstrates it. Where content takes a long time to reach, provide appropriate reviewer instructions or a save file. This is a suggested preparation method, not an additional Valve requirement.

The Content Survey must be completed before review. Its AI section focuses on content that ships with the game and is consumed by players, rather than development efficiency alone.

Valve distinguishes content generated during development from content generated while the game runs. Live generation also requires a description of safeguards against illegal output. Describe the implementation you actually ship; do not copy another game's disclosure or claim that artists performed work they did not do.

Disclosure does not replace the other content requirements. Check the current survey against the actual build before submission.

An approved store page must be publicly visible as Coming Soon for at least two weeks, and the build must be reviewed. Leave room for requested changes rather than treating a typical review estimate as a guaranteed deadline.

Approval does not automatically release the game. Valve documents a release action in Steamworks, performed when you are ready. Confirm who owns that action and that their account has the required publishing and pricing permissions.

This checklist describes preparation, not a promise of approval or a report of a tested launch. For the broader walkthrough, read the original Meshy guide; use the linked Valve pages for current platform requirements.
