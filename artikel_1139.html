The boyish, Lego-obsessed son of a farmer who quit Man City to become England's No 1: How James Trafford - with £95m spent on him - is maturing to star for Leeds and the Three Lions

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Published: 14:00 EST, 16 November 2026 | Updated: 16:35 EST, 16 November 2026

'It was like meeting a kid at a sixth-form careers day'. That was the verdict, slightly unflattering but endearing all the same, of one Premier League chief after encountering James Trafford.

When, at England's World Cup base in Kansas City, I then sat down with Trafford and he revealed that his partner had packed some Lego for him to build in camp, you wondered if sixth-form was a grade too high.

He is the son of a Cumbrian farmer and the Lego was of a tractor, which he would save for later in the tournament. 'You don't want to go too early,' he said.

None of this is a criticism. Far from it. There was a boyish charm, an innocence, even, and Trafford was engaging company. Nonetheless, he did not sound ready for the likes of a World Cup semi-final against Argentina. Thomas Tuchel agreed.

But every boy will one day become a man. On a ferocious and febrile night in Madrid, no place for adolescents, we may just have witnessed James Trafford's graduation ceremony.

Without his double save in stoppage-time, England would have left Spain without a famous, and deserved, victory. One Spanish title labelled it 'la actuacion de su vida', the performance of his life. You wonder how much they saw of his loan spell at Accrington Stanley, but the sentiment stands.

Trafford pictured with his farmer father, David, in the cab of his tractor with their dogs (Source: James Trafford)

Madrid daily AS exclaimed: 'Magisterio de Trafford!' Translated, 'A Trafford Masterclass!'. They claim he was the reason Spain could not complete their comeback, and they would be right.

When Tuchel stood with us in a corridor of the Metropolitano Stadium on the eve of the game, he had no issue confirming that Trafford would start. His message, though, was clear - it was sink or swim for a goalkeeper who turned 24 last month.

Not only did Trafford keep his head above water, it was he who pulled the team ashore. It was he to whom his senior colleagues ran in celebration. Ezri Konsa nearly took the shirt off Trafford's back, such was the force of his gratitude. But maybe this was the occasion when Trafford took the No 1 jersey from the back of Jordan Pickford, the holder for nearly a decade.

Pickford has done nothing wrong. Were Trafford to still display that maturity deficit, Pickford would be a reliable custodian heading to Euro 2028. This is not Peter Shilton post Italia 90, an uncomfortable truth of a great in need of the retirement barn. The 32-year-old has plenty to give on the back of all he has already given.

This, admitted Tuchel after the 3-2 win, is a selection dilemma that allows a manager to sleep well at night.

'There is no headache at all!' he boomed. 'I'm so far away from a headache, I cannot imagine.

Trafford left Manchester City with the plan to usurp Jordan Pickford as England No 1

Jude Bellingham certainly seemed impressed with the performance of the goalkeeper

'This is what we wanted. This is the best thing that can happen to all of us, also to Pickers. It is amazing how they push each other, and if then Traff has a performance like this, he has it because he knows he's well prepared. He gets the push from his colleagues and competitors.'

Tuchel has seen enough of Trafford in training and around camp to believe he is ready for promotion. He has watched him for Leeds, where he rates as the best performing goalkeeper in the Premier League. His 'net goals prevented' (4.4) and 'save percentage' (81.4) are higher than any rival this season, including Pickford.

'You always have to deserve your place,' added Tuchel. 'You will never get a gift and just get a game (for the sake of it). So he deserved to play and we expected this kind of performance. It was not only the last save, but also the build-up play and not losing focus. He has proved it on this stage, so this gives him a lot of belief. Now, it's on him to keep going for it.'

It, you presume, is the No 1 jersey. Trafford has made no secret of his desire to take it from Pickford, and he told us in the summer that such ambition was the motivation behind him wanting to leave Manchester City for a second time.

That day, he flipped his room key with a magician's sleight of hand throughout the interview, a dexterity useful for Lego. Better still for fingertip saves such as the one to deny Aymeric Laporte in the 91st minute.

Also in the gift pack from his girlfriend were two face masks. 'Very thoughtful,' he quipped. But that is another observation, bordering on concern, that some prospective buyers have made in recent years - on the goalkeeping scale of uber professional to joker, he resides too close to the latter. We have seen, even this season, the odd concentration lapse that speaks to that thought.

Trafford has played a starring role in goal since moving to Leeds United in the summer

But there was also a feeling that Trafford needed more exposure and responsibility before the penny dropped and his value was realised. Already, £95million has been spent on him in transfer fees.

At Leeds, and now with England, he has been given that stage, as Tuchel calls it. The head coach maintains that Pickford is his first choice, but it was Trafford he picked for both Nations League fixtures against Spain, the ones that really mattered, the ones that would strengthen or threaten his own employment.

As for Trafford's career prospects, to revisit our opening line, England's No 1 sounds like a fair vocation.

Thomas Tuchel wants England to use victory in the Nations League finals as a springboard to winning Euro 2028 at Wembley.

The head coach admits it has taken him a while to recover from the trauma of his side’s World Cup semi-final defeat by Argentina in July, for which he was heavily criticised.

But after beating world champions Spain 3-2 on Sunday night, topping their Nations League group ahead of March’s quarter-finals and next summer's finals, Tuchel is finally able to look forward with positivity and belief.

‘I saw the celebrations in Portugal when they won the Nations League,’ he said. ‘It’s just the best, the best preparation we can have for the Euros. We want to have these matches on the highest level to prove ourselves and to gain confidence, to see where we are and to adjust our game where we need to, that’s what this Nations League is perfect for. We want to win it.’

England have won five of their six matches since the World Cup, losing only to Spain at Wembley in September. In the build up to that game, Tuchel was involved in heated exchanges with observers who continued to question his tactics in the final half hour against Argentina.

’It took me a while to digest (the World Cup exit) and see the way forward, how we can help and adjust and improve, and how we can do this together,’ he said.

‘We had reviews and so on, but clarity then came together with the players. Straight away we had the energy right to take the next steps. The ideas became more clear for me, even if it was not so easy the first week, to overcome and arrive with different emotions. Some were still upset from the World Cup. Some were new in the team. Some had digested it, some had not digested it. But it was important to have this kind of process together.

‘(Spain) was a complete performance that makes me very proud. It's a full package of belief, of courage, of commitment, of togetherness, of relying on each other and stepping up in this moment.’

Tuchel, though, has rejected the idea that seeing out the win Madrid was proof that he has learned from his mistakes in Atlanta

‘Not at all, I think we would not have been in this situation if it was 11 against 11,’ he said. ‘For me, it was Mexico reloaded (not Argentina). It's one man down, but what can you do? It is their strength to have ball possession, to overload and find the gaps. It just plays totally into their hands and it comes wave after wave. It felt like three hours and thirty minutes!’
