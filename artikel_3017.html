With the Nasdaq in a Correction, Vanguard's Tech ETF Is the Fund I'd Buy Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The fund's price has slid back to its late-April level. Its earnings didn't slide with it.

By Daniel Sparks – Nov 22, 2026 at 8:23PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Vanguard Information Technology ETF ›

The tech sell-off that started in October has become a correction. As I write, the Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC -5.28%) has dropped around 13% from its all-time closing high, hit on Oct. 6.

The Vanguard Information Technology ETF (VGT -7.00%) has dropped even more. It finished at $104.99 on Nov. 19, its lowest close since May 4 and around 19% below its own Oct. 6 record of $129.80.

That said, the fund's still up around 12% in 2026 as of this writing, counting dividends.

When the fund was about 10% below its peak in late October, I didn't think it was a bargain yet. At today's price, I do.

The businesses in it have kept growing their earnings as the share price dropped, and I'd be glad to pay the resulting valuation for a fund I plan to hold for years to come.

The fund owns 315 stocks, but a handful of them drive it. As of Oct. 31, Apple was around 18% of its assets, Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%) about 17%, and Microsoft about 12%.

These three haven't moved in step. Apple stock is within about 2% of its record as of this writing. Nvidia, though, has fallen about 29% since Oct. 30 alone. By my math, Nvidia by itself is behind about half of the fund's 10% fall since then.

The slide came with a leadership change. Nvidia said on Nov. 12 that Jensen Huang had resigned as CEO, with CFO Colette Kress taking over as interim CEO. On Nov. 19, California's attorney general followed with an investigative subpoena over whether Nvidia's advanced chips or confidential information might have gone to China, and if the company misled investors about that risk.

But Nvidia's business is still booming. In the third quarter of its fiscal 2027 (the period ending Oct. 25), the company's revenue more than doubled year over year to $115.8 billion. Data center revenue climbed 110%, and non-GAAP (adjusted) earnings per share surged 111%. Management also forecast fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of about $130 billion.

In other words, much of the fund's fall has come from investors paying less for the same chip profits, not from those profits dropping.

Vanguard lists the average price-to-earnings ratio of the stocks in the fund, and that number has dropped a lot in the last year and a half. The fund's holdings traded at about 39 times earnings at the end of June 2025 and about 35 times earnings on April 30, 2026. By Sept. 30, the number was about 33 times earnings, and on Oct. 31, it was 29.5 times earnings.

Granted, that last reading came before the latest 10% slide. With earnings held flat, today's price would put the ratio closer to 26 or 27 times earnings.

That's a big shift for a share price that is now right back where it was in late April, when it closed around $103. By my rough math, every share of the fund now carries about a third more earnings than it did then.

The gap to the broader market has shrunk, too. On Oct. 31, the stocks in the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF traded at around 24 times earnings, so the tech fund's premium had fallen to about a quarter. For businesses whose profits are still growing this fast, I think the price is fair.

Sure, the fund might keep sliding. A correction can become a bear market (a drop of 20% or more), and the fund is already near that mark. And with three stocks making up almost half of its assets, a tough patch for just one of them can drag on the whole fund.

But a long holding period changes how much a fall like this matters. After all, this year has already shown how quickly the mood can turn.

The fund closed at around $84 on March 30 (adjusted for its 8-for-1 stock split in April), and it then rose about 55% to its Oct. 6 high in just over six months.

No one knows where this sell-off stops. But an investor planning to hold for five or 10 years is largely buying the earnings these companies produce in that stretch. And those earnings seem to have kept climbing through both of this year's sell-offs.

Is the fund worth buying at around $105 a share? I think so. Its valuation has fallen far more than its price, and its largest companies are still growing. I'd buy the fund now, maybe in a few chunks over the coming months, because the sell-off might go further first.
