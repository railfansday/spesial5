If SpaceX and Tesla Merge Next Year, Here's What a $5,000 Investment in the Combined Company Could Be Worth by the End of 2028

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

An investment in Tesla could double in the event of a Space Exploration Technologies merger.

By Geoffrey Seiler – Nov 25, 2026 at 6:04PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Space Exploration Technologies ›

The odds of Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +1.30%) and Tesla (TSLA -1.18%) merging sometime next year have been growing. Prediction market odds jumped earlier this month after Elon Musk's biographer, Walter Isaacson, told CNBC he was confident a merger is coming, since keeping the companies separate makes no sense. This was followed by the two companies picking board members to begin official merger talks.

Isaacson's comments raised the odds that SpaceX would announce a merger with Tesla before March 1, 2027, from 27% on Nov. 11, 2026, to 62% two days later. They now sit at 67%, as of Nov. 24 on Kalshi. Meanwhile, the odds of a merger announcement before the end of 2027 are at 89% on Polymarket.

Now, a merger isn't a foregone conclusion and does face some hurdles, but it does seem very likely at this point. The Democrats' sweeping victories in the midterm elections complicate matters a bit, as Texas Attorney General-elect Nathan Johnson told Bloomberg that a potential merger would face "tremendous scrutiny." Meanwhile, more than half of all Tesla shareholders would need to vote to approve the merger, not just those who decide to vote. Musk owns nearly 20% of the company, but Tesla also has a significant retail shareholder base that would have to vote on the deal for it to pass.

Despite these obstacles, there is still a very good shot that the companies announce a merger next year and complete it by the end of 2028. In such a scenario, it would be best to purchase shares of Tesla, the acquired company. With a little over $5,000, an investor could buy around 13 shares of Tesla.

An acquisition of Tesla by SpaceX ultimately makes a lot of sense. First, it unites Musk's two largest companies and will allow him to focus on a single vision instead of splitting his time between the two. Second, the companies already have an important joint venture in the form of their massive chip manufacturing facility, Terafab, so it would make sense to have this under one roof. Finally, the companies' businesses complement each other.

More than anything, both SpaceX and Tesla are about the future, and together they give Musk and investors more shots on goal. Meanwhile, SpaceX could ultimately need some of Tesla's technology to build its planned data centers in space, including solar and Optimus robots. As such, the deal makes sense from a synergy perspective, and the company would be able to cut some overlapping corporate costs.

Any merger between SpaceX and Tesla is likely to be an all-stock deal in which Tesla is acquired at a premium. Analysts at HSBC recently pegged the most likely premium around 33% based on past similar deals, which makes sense.

Based on that premium and current prices, investors would get a little over 4 SpaceX shares for each Tesla share they hold. That would turn investors' 13 Tesla shares into 52 SpaceX shares after the merger, worth $5,000.

For a combined SpaceX-Tesla, the stock would likely trade based on a forward price-to-sales (P/S) ratio. For 2029, the Tesla revenue consensus estimate is $146 billion, while SpaceX's is roughly $336 billion. Combined, that's $482 billion in estimated annual revenue for 2029.

Place a 12-times multiple on that, given its strong growth outlook, and the combined company would be worth $5.8 trillion. Meanwhile, following the merger, it would have roughly 29.7 billion shares. That would value SpaceX at approximately $195. Those 52 shares would then be worth about $10,150, approximately double a $5,000 initial investment in two years.

This makes Tesla a top growth stock to buy ahead of a potential SpaceX merger.
