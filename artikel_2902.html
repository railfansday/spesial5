Jensen Huang Is Back. Is It Safe To Buy Nvidia Stock Now?

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Nvidia brought its trillion-dollar man back in.

By Jeremy Bowman – Nov 23, 2026 at 11:14AM EST

Investors wondering how much Jensen Huang was worth to Nvidia (NVDA +32.52%) got their answer Monday morning.

Nvidia stock jumped 30% at the open, adding roughly $1 trillion to the company's market cap.

Huang's return comes after President Trump said on social media that Nvidia should bring him back, and after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on Face the Nation Sunday morning that Nvidia should consider reinstating him, reversing his earlier position that the company should tap an outsider CEO to lead the company.

It's not clear why Bessent changed his mind, but the stock market's plunge on Friday, President Trump's support for Huang's return, and calls from a number of top investors and business leaders for Huang to return likely contributed to his reversal.

The news comes as a shock to investors, as just six days earlier on the company's earnings call, board Chair Harvey Jones told investors, "The Nvidia Board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO."

In the statement reinstating Huang this morning, Jones said, "The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead Nvidia, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners, and customers. We have every confidence in Jensen and look forward to working with him as he resumes his leadership of the company."

The board's decision was unanimous and came after extensive discussions with Huang. It also discussed the matter with senior U.S. government officials who indicated that reinstating Huang would not pose a national security risk.

The board said that the investigation it had previously announced into Huang's involvement with a Chinese intelligence agent will continue until it's concluded. Additionally, it's forming a committee on national security and export control to oversee trade compliance and national security risks.

Nvidia investors are certainly enjoying the surge in the stock on Monday, but shares are still down more than 10% from where they were when the news about Huang's affair with a Chinese intelligence agent first broke on Nov. 8.

Huang's return seems to solve one set of problems for the AI chip leader. It no longer needs to search for a new CEO, and any concerns about how the loss of Huang's leadership and his network of relationships with customers, suppliers, and governments would impact the company are removed.

Huang may not be a national security risk, but the investigations announced in the wake of his scandal will still proceed, and he's still expected to testify before Congress, including a closed-door interview with three House committees on Dec. 1.

There's also a question of whether Huang will face any meaningful reputational damage both within Nvidia and externally that could undermine his effectiveness as a leader. The company will likely face more scrutiny from the media in the coming months as well.

In other words, Huang's reinstatement doesn't erase the events of the last two weeks.

Nvidia continues to look unbelievably cheap even after the 30% surge today, and the guidance hike it gave for fiscal 2028 carries more credibility with Huang back in the CEO chair. Analyst estimates on Nvidia jumped after that report, and even after today's pop, the stock trades at just 10 times next year's expected earnings per share, a year in which Nvidia expects 75% revenue growth.

Overall, Huang's return is a net positive for Nvidia, and during his absence, the board may have realized it had no good options to replace him. However, Nvidia still faces an elevated risk, which now includes an SEC investigation into accounting issues, according to Reuters.

Depending on your current exposure to Nvidia, I think buying the stock here makes sense. However, it's a good idea to get exposure to related AI stocks that don't face the same investigative risks as Nvidia as well, such as TSMC, which jumped 16% this morning on the Nvidia news. Another option worth considering is the VanEck Semiconductor ETF, which counts Nvidia as its biggest holding but offers exposure to a wide range of chip and AI stocks.

The worst appears to be over for Nvidia, but investors shouldn't discount the risks still facing the company. The Huang episode is a good reminder of why diversification matters.
