3 things that led to the Mavericks’ cathartic 116-94 blowout win over the Golden State Warriors

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Dallas Mavericks (8-7) handled business Wednesday night, beating the shorthanded Golden State Warriors (8-7) 116-94 in a game Dallas controlled almost from the start. Cooper Flagg led the way with 33 points, five rebounds, nine assists and three steals on 11-of-15 shooting, while P.J. Washington added 21 points and hit five threes. Kyrie Irving finished with 17 points, six rebounds, five assists and three steals, and Dallas shot 50.6 percent from the field and 41.5 percent from distance. Golden State never found much offense without Stephen Curry, Jimmy Butler and Kristaps Porzingis, and once Dallas pushed the lead to 20 by the end of the third quarter, the game was essentially over.

Dallas jumped all over Golden State in the first quarter, starting with Flagg and Irving controlling almost everything. The duo scored or assisted on 22 of the Mavericks' first 25 points, setting the tone for a 19-2 Dallas run that quickly created separation. Flagg was perfect in the quarter, scoring 10 points on 3-of-3 shooting, while Kyrie added six points and two assists. P.J. Washington also caught fire early, hitting all three of his 3-point attempts, and Dallas shot 57.1 percent from the field and 50 percent from deep. Golden State, meanwhile, looked lost offensively for long stretches and shot just 38.5 percent. The Warriors found a little life late, but Dallas still led 35-25 after one.

Golden State showed more resistance in the second quarter, but Dallas remained firmly in control. The Warriors forced a few sloppy turnovers and cut into the lead, but Flagg quickly settled things down. He scored 12 points in the quarter and reached 22 by halftime, attacking the rim, getting to the line, and knocking down another three while shooting 7-of-9 overall. Washington was just as important, finishing with 16 points, four threes, and another reliable source of offense for Dallas. The Mavericks shot 53.8 percent from the field, 45.5 percent from three, and went 10-for-10 at the line in the half, taking a 62-49 lead into the break.

Dallas blew the game open in the third quarter and turned a comfortable lead into a rout. The Warriors were shooting just 41.4 percent from the field and 25 percent from three through three quarters, with Brandin Podziemski struggling badly from deep. Dallas kept stacking stops, moving the ball and getting quality looks, and by the end of the quarter the Mavericks had pushed the lead to 20 at 92-72. At that point, the game was over.

The fourth quarter was basically a formality. Dallas had already put the game away in the third, so the final 12 minutes were mostly about getting through it healthy and letting the bench finish things off. Flagg stayed in just long enough to finish with 33 points, five rebounds and nine assists, and the Mavericks cruised to a 116-94 win. Golden State never seriously threatened again, and Dallas emptied the bench to close out a pretty uneventful final quarter.

Flagg had been in a rough stretch by his standards over the last few games, so this was about as emphatic of a response as Dallas could have asked for. Flagg was in control from the opening minutes and finished with 33 points, five rebounds, nine assists and three steals while shooting 11-of-15 from the field, 3-of-5 from three and 8-of-8 at the line. He was aggressive without forcing anything, got downhill whenever he wanted, and looked much more comfortable creating for everyone around him.

The nine assists were especially encouraging because they showed he wasn't just hunting his own shot to break out of the slump. He read the floor, made the right play, and controlled the game. After a few quieter performances, this looked much more like the version of Flagg Dallas has come to expect.

Washington needed this one too. His jumper had gone cold during the road trip, but he looked completely different against Golden State, knocking down five threes and finishing with 21 points on 7-of-10 shooting. He hit three threes in the first quarter alone and helped Dallas create separation before the Warriors could settle into the game. When Washington is making shots like this, the Mavericks become so much harder to defend because teams cannot load everything toward Flagg and Kyrie Irving. It was only one game, but seeing the ball go through the net again should be a nice confidence boost after the way he had been shooting.

There really is not much more complicated than this — Golden State doesn't have much if the Warriors don't have Curry in the lineup. The Warriors were without Curry, Butler and Porzingis, but Curry's absence was especially obvious because Golden State had almost no reliable way to generate offense. The Warriors shot 42.7 percent from the field and an ugly 23.7 percent from three, with Brandin Podziemski going 0-of-5 from deep and Yang Hansen Lendeborg finishing 6-of-18 from the field.

Dallas deserves credit for taking advantage, but there were long stretches where Golden State simply looked incapable of creating a good shot. Once the Mavericks pushed the lead out in the third quarter, nobody on the other side could drag the Warriors back into it. This was a shorthanded team that looked exactly like one.
