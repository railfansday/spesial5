Delaying Retirement By 1 Year? Here's What It Might Do for Your Finances

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Waiting just a bit longer to end your career could do you a world of good.

By Maurie Backman – Nov 24, 2026 at 11:18AM EST

When you set a retirement date, it's natural to want to stick to it. After all, the idea of stopping work and reclaiming your free time can be appealing.

But if you're not feeling all that confident in your retirement finances, it could pay to be flexible as your planned workforce exit date approaches. In fact, delaying retirement by just a year could do you a world of good. Here are two potential benefits.

The amount of money Social Security pays you each month in retirement is based on your personal wage history coupled with your filing age. If you wait for full retirement age to claim benefits, which is 67 for anyone born in 1960 or later, you'll get your monthly Social Security in full. But waiting could yield greater benefits.

Each year you delay your claim past full retirement age up to age 70 boosts your monthly benefits by 8% -- for life. So if your planned retirement date is 67 but you work one more year and claim Social Security 12 months later, you could score a nice boost to your monthly checks.

And remember, boosted benefits also mean larger cost-of-living adjustments. You may have an easier time keeping up with inflation in retirement if your annual increases are more generous thanks to having larger benefits coming your way.

Working one extra year may afford you the opportunity to modestly boost your retirement savings. But even a modest last-minute contribution to your IRA or 401(k) could give you more options or peace of mind.

Let's say you work an additional year and contribute an extra $1,000 a month to your 401(k) during that time. Will another $12,000 make or break your retirement? Maybe not.

But could an extra $12,000 take some pressure off your first year of retirement? Probably. That sum could easily pay for a vacation, some surprise home repairs, or other costs you may not have anticipated.

This, of course, is just one example, and the amount of extra money you can save by working another year depends on your personal situation. The key is to not discount the benefit of a last-minute contribution.

It's not easy to delay retirement for years when your heart is set on a specific date. But working one extra year may be doable. And it could benefit you financially in more ways than one.
