Why Sal Stewart Rookie of Year honor bolsters Reds' optimism for 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

LAS VEGAS – When Cincinnati Reds officials talk openly, without irony, about trying to go from last place in 2026 to the playoffs in 2027, this is one of the biggest reasons why:

The hard-nosed, hard-hitting, durable first baseman who just became the Reds' ninth Rookie of the Year to cap off a 32-homer, 112-RBI All-Star rookie season. Stewart is the fifth Reds player to achieve the feat during an All-Star season, joining the likes of Johnny Bench (1968) and Frank Robinson (1956).

Trade Elly De La Cruz with three years left of club control? Tear down the group the Reds rolled out as rookies in 2023 with visions of long-term success and try another rebuild?

That's a lot harder to fathom after getting a high-caliber glimpse of the young crop right behind that group, led by the ultra-competitive pair of 2026 All-Stars in Stewart and starting pitcher Chase Burns, who won 15 games with a 2.65 ERA for a 75-win team.

Stewart, the Miami-raised slugger taken under the wing of Manny Machado as a rising teen, beat out finalists JJ Wetherholt of the St. Louis Cardinals and Carson Benge of the New York Mets.

Reds CEO Phil Castellini backed his front office in its ongoing competitive effort because of players such as Stewart, and suggested optimism for next year.

"Specifically, I think you've got some really standout performers in the year," Castellini said in the immediate aftermath of the bitter 2026 season. "You've got Sal, who's got a pretty good shot at Rookie of the year. Elly's had a fantastic year, a 30-30 year. Burns. … There's a lot of things to highlight. There's a great core here."

Machado, the San Diego Padres superstar third baseman, predicted during spring training that Stewart would win the award that became official with the Nov. 9 announcement.

"Just knowing how he studies his game, that he got a little taste of what it takes from last year. Now he knows what to expect up there," Machado said, explaining the rationale for his prediction.

Stewart, who led the Reds with five home runs during his September debut in 2025 (which culminated in a 2-for-4 playoff performance), made his mentor look good out of the gate, winning National League Player of the Week the first week of the season.

Stewart, a 32nd overall draft pick in 2022, later added Rookie of the Month awards in April and August, with a players-ballot All-Star selection in between.

His monster April provided most of the Reds' offensive production during a hot start for the team that had them in first place before a tailspin in May.

He fought through inevitable adjustments by league pitchers, along with various aches and pains, to wind up as one of only seven players in the majors this year to play all 162 games. He's the only rookie on a list that also includes presumptive NL MVP Pete Crow-Armstrong of the Cubs and stars Bryce Harper of the Phillies and Junior Caminero of the Rays.

When asked after the final game of the season about his accomplishments, Stewart ignored the offensive numbers that won him the award and focused on the 162.

"I played every game. People outside the game kind of take that for granted," he said. "But sick, tired, hurt, sore, rough patch, no matter what, I stayed in there grinding. And that's the most important thing.

"That's also thanks to Tito as well," he said, referring to manager Terry Francona, "because (a manager can) maybe give a break or this and that, but he rode with me the whole way. Playing 162's a huge, huge, huge thing. And I'm proud of that."
