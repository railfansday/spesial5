Chevron Says AI Has Already Captured Over $100 Million in Value. Here's Where It Might Find an Even Bigger Payoff.

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Chevron isn’t a tech company, but it’s already accruing benefits from use of artificial intelligence (AI).

To say that artificial intelligence (AI) stocks are prominent and top of mind for many investors is an understatement. Still, hefty market capitalizations and popularity don't always paint the full picture.

Sure, plenty of companies and high-ranking executives at those corporations are using AI, but many currently struggle to generate tangible profit benefits from the technology. The harsh reality is the vast majority of corporate AI adopters are spending on the technology with little to show for it. Chevron (CVX -0.47%) isn't part of that group.

Chevron is a prime example of a major company benefiting from AI adoption. Image source: Getty Images.

When it reported third-quarter earnings last month, Chevron told analysts and investors it had generated $100 million in year-to-date "operational efficiency gains" backed by AI initiatives. Chevron's AI benefits include a 50% reduction in loss production events in the Denver-Julesburg Basin. Those data points may be signs that Chevron is onto something with its AI ambitions.

No one will mistake Chevron for a technology company, and that's OK. Its primary business is the exploration and production of oil and natural gas, but that makes Chevron (and some of its peers) ideal for broader AI deployment.

Getting down to the heart of the matter, the lifeblood of the energy industry is finding new reserves and efficiently extracting more oil and gas from already tapped basins, offshore deposits, and oilfields. Oil and natural gas prospecting is data- and labor-intensive, time-consuming, and not cheap. AI can improve those scenarios so much that Chevron has its own AI platform, ApEX.

Two interesting facts about ApEX for Chevron investors to consider: First, this is very much an "in-house" product, meaning the oil major is saving some money by not farming out its most important AI needs to a third-party vendor. Second, ApEX was initially tested in deep waters in the Gulf of Mexico. That shows confidence in the platform, as deep-water offshore drilling is often cost-intensive and potentially risky.

Those are points to keep in mind as Chevron looks to solidify long-term earnings growth by probing new fields in locations such as Venezuela while potentially lifting output at domestic oilfields such as the Bakken Shale and the Permian Basin. If there's one thing oil companies love, it's the ability to pull more oil and gas than previously expected from the locations in which they're already operational.

So, in answering "where" in terms of the locations where Chevron can drive AI benefits, the answer could well be "everywhere."

Some industries are struggling with AI adoption, but as Chevron proves, that's not the case in the oil and gas space. Some experts believe one reason for successful AI deployment in the energy sector is that it's a real-world, not a virtual, application.

Beyond that, there's a belief that dedicated energy-sector AI adopters will leverage the technology to improve efficiencies, cut costs, and deliver tangible earnings before interest and taxes (EBIT) benefits. And for those worried about the environmental impact of the oil and gas industry, companies such as Chevron can dramatically reduce their carbon footprints by using AI.

The good news is that Chevron is already reaping benefits from AI use, and there's likely more to come in the long term.
