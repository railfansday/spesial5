Apple Intelligence, decoded: How to make sense of Apple’s confusing AI lineup

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Apple has received much criticism for being late to the AI game. While OpenAI helped define the AI boom beginning in late 2021, and Google and other tech giants quickly jumped on the AI train the following year, Apple didn’t make its first real foray into artificial intelligence until 2024 with Apple Intelligence.

Though the initial capabilities of Apple Intelligence were rather lackluster compared with its peers, Apple spent the next two years rapidly refining its offerings. In 2026, Apple made its AI system much more capable and expansive by introducing more advanced AI tools, including the Siri AI chatbot and Audio Intelligence. And just last month, Apple also debuted an additional AI tool dubbed “intelligent assistant.”

The thing is, Apple now has so many AI offerings that it’s getting hard for the average consumer to keep track of what each one does, which devices support which AI tool, and which tools require a fee to access. So, here’s a cheat sheet for making sense of Apple’s increasingly confusing AI lineup.

Apple Intelligence

Apple Intelligence is the oldest of the company’s modern AI offerings, introduced in 2024. Think of Apple Intelligence as the foundational artificial intelligence layer integrated into Apple’s operating systems, including iOS, macOS, and watchOS.

Apple Intelligence includes a suite of smaller AI tools tailored to specific tasks. For example, Apple Intelligence powers Visual Intelligence, which lets Apple’s AI see and understand what’s on your screen or through your camera and take action on it; for example, it can tell you the nutritional information of the food on your plate.

Apple Intelligence powers Audio Intelligence, a tool that can help you identify sounds and music and even recap your spoken conversations on some Apple Watches. It is also the engine behind numerous other AI features across your Apple products, including image generation, live translation, select photo editing tools, message summaries, and more.

Not every device that supports Apple Intelligence supports all its features. Any Mac with an Apple Silicon chip will run Apple Intelligence, but you’ll need a Mac with an M3 chip and 12 GB of RAM or more to use some of its most advanced features, including custom Siri Voices and advanced AI dictation.

For the iPhone, you’ll need an iPhone 17 Pro or later to use those advanced Apple Intelligence features (iPhone 15 Pro and later is required for most Apple Intelligence features). As for Audio Intelligence on the Apple Watch, that’s limited to the new Apple Watch Series 12 and Apple Watch Series 4.

Siri AI

Siri AI is Apple’s new chatbot. Though people often use the terms “Siri AI” and “Apple Intelligence” interchangeably, they are actually distinct.

Apple Intelligence is the foundational AI system integrated into Apple’s operating systems. Siri AI is the natural-language gateway to Apple Intelligence—it lets you communicate as you would with a person to give it commands or find information, which it then carries out using Apple Intelligence’s capabilities.

Siri AI runs on the cloud on any device that supports iOS 27, macOS 27, iPadOS 27, visionOS 27, watchOS 27, and (soon) tvOS 27. However, if you want to run some aspects of Siri AI locally on your device, you’ll need a Mac with an M3 chip and 12GB of RAM or more, or an iPhone 17 Pro, iPhone Air, or later.

Intelligent Assistant

This is Apple’s newest major artificial intelligence tool, the most impressive, and the one that most muddies Apple’s AI branding. 

“Intelligent assistant,” introduced last month and released this month as part of the updated Logic Pro, Pages, Numbers, and Keynote apps in Apple’s paid Creator Studio suite, is a new natural-language AI tool aimed at helping creators be more productive.

The tool is integrated into the above apps in the form of a chatbot. There’s a text field where you type instructions to the intelligent assistant (“make this piano track sound like it was recorded in a concert hall” or “fix the formula errors in this spreadsheet”). The changes happen like magic—all very impressive stuff. 

As a matter of fact, it’s way more impressive than the other capabilities Apple has shown off for Apple Intelligence and Siri AI. So why hasn’t Apple branded these new AI features as Apple Intelligence?

The answer: Intelligent assistant is not powered by Apple Intelligence or Siri AI. Instead, the new AI tools use Apple Foundation Models combined with additional models from third-party providers, in this case, OpenAI and Google. This explains why intelligent assistant is so capable (both OpenAI and Google have a huge lead over Apple in the AI space) and also why Apple has eschewed the Apple Intelligence branding for the work it carries out.

Does Apple charge for AI?

This is where things get really muddied for people. Apple’s AI pricing runs from free, to a monthly subscription fee, to higher usage limits with select iCloud Plus plans.

Currently, using Apple Intelligence and Siri AI is free for all users. However, Apple says this won’t last. With the release of the 27.3 versions of its operating systems in December, customers will have daily limits when using Siri AI and Apple Intelligence. If they exceed those limits, they won’t be able to use any Apple Intelligence features that require cloud processing (which costs Apple compute power).

If users do want higher Apple Intelligence and Siri AI limits, they can get more by paying for an iCloud Plus plan (if they don’t already have one). The 50GB iCloud Plus plan, which costs just 99 cents per month, doesn’t increase your AI limits. However, the other iCloud Plus plans do:

  • 200GB ($2.99 per month): 2x limit increase
  • 2TB ($9.99 per month): 5x limit increase
  • 6TB ($29.99 per month): 10x limit increase
  • 12TB ($59.99 per month): 20x limit increase

As for intelligent assistant access in Logic Pro, Pages, Keynote, and Numbers, users already need to pony up. Intelligent assistant is available only as part of Apple’s Creator Studio subscription bundle, for which it charges $12.99 per month or $129 per year.

This means that if you want as much access as possible to Apple’s AI offerings, you’ll need to subscribe to two subscription plans.
