West Ham’s Answer Was There All Along – So Why Ignore It?

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

West Ham United’s lack of cutting edge against Preston North End was concerning, but perhaps the most frustrating aspect of Saturday’s 1-1 draw was that Scott Parker already appears to have the answer to the Hammers’ creative problems.

At the risk of sounding like a scratched record, West Ham have an emergency, break-glass solution to breaking teams down. It’s called Jarrod Bowen, and I’m not entirely sure I’ve ever seen our captain play wider than he did at the London Stadium yesterday.

What’s particularly puzzling is that I genuinely believe the best we’ve seen Bowen play in the last couple of years came during the second half against Cardiff City. On that occasion, Parker was effectively handed a blueprint for getting the best out of West Ham’s most important player, yet we’ve barely seen it since.

During that extraordinary second-half comeback against Cardiff, Bowen was given a certain amount of freedom, perhaps encouraged by Parker or maybe he just took it upon himself to drift into the middle of the pitch and cause havoc.

Whatever the reason, he had a hand in all three West Ham goals, and the difference was remarkable.

For the first, he drifted into what might be regarded as the number 10 position and chipped a lovely ball over the Cardiff back line for Divine Mukasa to finish.

The second was similar, with Bowen again creating the opening from a central position. His pass found Joël Piroe, whose flick released Arne Engels to score.

The third goal saw Bowen back in his more familiar position on the right-hand side, where he supplied a wonderful inswinging cross for Max Kilman to equalise.

Jarrod Bowen was given license to roam against Cardiff

But that only went to show quite how much freedom Bowen had. In essence, he was everywhere.

As a blueprint, Scott Parker couldn’t have been given a better gift on his first day in charge than discovering that West Ham’s best player could operate with devastating effect from a free role.

Indeed, I was so encouraged by what I saw that I subsequently wrote about how Scott Parker must restore West Ham’s most dangerous weapon following the defeat at Blackburn.

Because for a brief period against Cardiff, it looked as though we’d stumbled upon something rather special.

Therefore, I was a little underwhelmed that we didn’t see it repeated against Blackburn and very worried when we probably saw our captain more straightjacketed than ever against Preston North End.

If I could offer Parker one piece of encouragement or advice, it would be to get the best out of Bowen.

I’m quite sure our new head coach will improve the defence, and I think we saw signs of that yesterday, albeit against the league’s worst team.

But it can’t come at the expense of shackling our best player, who potentially could only be here for a few more months.

As I argued in my assessment of the Preston draw, West Ham had plenty of possession, shots and territory, but very little magic.

And that’s the really bloody annoying part. We have a player who can provide exactly that, but we’re asking him to operate in a way which makes life considerably easier for the opposition.

I know one thing. If I was head coach of an opposition Championship team and I saw Jarrod Bowen shackled to the right wing, I’d breathe a huge sigh of relief.

If we don’t succeed in getting promotion, Bowen could leave for a pittance of £15m. That’s the release clause in the revised contract he agreed following relegation.

We do know that Bowen flew to Prague to meet Daniel Křetínský before pledging to remain at West Ham for another season, as he explained in his interview with Sky Sports.

And I’m now very fearful that we are going to waste what should really be something of a cheat code for the division.

Bowen is comfortably our best attacking player, and we’ve already seen how devastating he can be when allowed to roam. Yet rather than building upon that discovery, Parker appears to have returned him to a position where he’s easier to mark and less able to influence the game.

I wish it weren’t so, but I’ve now come to the conclusion that the second half against Cardiff was a pure accident rather than a window into what we could begin to see from Scott Parker as he got his teeth into the West Ham job.

But decisions like that could well make the difference between automatic promotion and a play-off place, if we’re lucky.
