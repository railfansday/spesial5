When Is Lindsay Clancy's New Trial? Retrial Date Set by Judge, More Updates

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Lindsay Clancy is heading back to court, and Judge William Sullivan has set a new trial date of March 29, 2027, for the Massachusetts mother accused of killing her three children. The decision was made weeks after the judge denied her legal team’s attempt to dismiss the murder charges altogether.

Clancy appeared in Plymouth Superior Court on November 16, 2026, to discuss the future of her case after her September 2026 mistrial, with prosecutors using the hearing to formally share their plans to retry her.

The commonwealth pushed for a retrial to begin in May 2027, but Sullivan opted for an earlier March 2027 date. The judge has previously announced plans to retire in 2027.

Plymouth County District Attorney Tim Cruz holds the ultimate authority over how Clancy is charged the second time around. Cruz has the power to retry her on first-degree murder — the same charge she faced in the first trial — or to pursue a lesser charge, such as second-degree murder or manslaughter.

The scheduling decision marked the latest in a series of rulings Sullivan has issued since the previous hearing on November 9, 2026, when he denied Clancy’s motion to dismiss the murder charges tied to the deaths of her children: Cora, 5, Dawson, 3, and Callan, 8 months, in January 2023.

Leading up to the new trial are pretrial hearings on December 30, 2026, and February 18, 2027.

Sullivan rejected the defense’s bid to throw out the case on November 9, 2026, after Clancy’s attorneys argued that a retrial would violate her constitutional protection against double jeopardy, which prevents a person from being prosecuted twice for the same offense.

“Because the Court concludes that there was a manifest necessity for a mistrial based on the jury’s deadlock, a retrial on the murder indictments does not violate Clancy’s right not to be placed in double jeopardy,” Sullivan ruled, according to ABC News.

The judge also denied the defense team’s motion to investigate the lone holdout juror from her murder trial and turned down a gag order sought by prosecutors.

The jury deliberated for seven days before Sullivan declared the mistrial. Eleven of the jurors voted to find Clancy not guilty by reason of insanity. Holdout juror Michael P. Desronvil stood firm that she was guilty.

Before declaring the mistrial, the jury foreperson handed the judge a note stating that the person later identified as the holdout juror was “unable to follow the instructions regarding reasonable doubt,” Boston 25 News reported.

Clancy’s defense team argued that Sullivan should have questioned the juror to determine whether there were grounds for removal and replacement — rather than declaring a mistrial.

In his November 9, 2026, ruling, Sullivan wrote that questioning the holdout juror mid-deliberations could have pressured the juror to change his stance. “The Court’s primary concern must be protecting the fairness of the proceedings and avoiding the possibility of a coerced verdict,” the judge wrote.

Desronvil has publicly maintained his belief that Clancy was guilty. He broke his silence in a September 28, 2026, interview with Fox News.

“I know the whole focus has been on me, but we want to reel the whole thing back and put the focus on the three angels [Clancy’s three children],” he said at the time. “[They] are the ones who can’t speak for themselves. The ones that can’t defend themselves. The ones that can’t fight their own battles.”

Desronvil continued, “I want to thank God for my Catholic faith. I want to thank God for giving me the ability to love my daughter. Foremost, I would like to thank everyone — every individual — that has supported me throughout this difficult situation. Your prayers, your emotional support, your psychological support is tremendous [for] my heart.”

Clancy was charged in connection with the deaths of her three children at her Duxbury, Massachusetts, home in January 2023. Prosecutors alleged she strangled her children in the basement of the residence.

Clancy’s defense has consistently argued that she should not be held criminally responsible for the deaths of her children because she was suffering from postpartum psychosis, along with additional mental health struggles, at the time of the deaths.

Meanwhile, prosecutors maintain that Clancy was aware of her actions. They have pointed to her suicide attempt immediately after the children’s deaths as evidence that the killings were intentional rather than the product of a psychotic break.

Those competing narratives — a mother in the throes of a severe postpartum mental health crisis versus a parent who understood what she was doing — will form the heart of the second trial, just as they did the first.

Before the retrial was officially declared, Clancy’s attorneys told CBS News that she was dreading the possibility of a second trial and has been on suicide watch since her children’s deaths.

“She wants to be with her children. … And if given the opportunity, I have no doubt in my mind at all that she would take her own life,” one of her attorneys, Rosemary Scapicchio, told the outlet.

With the March 29, 2027, trial date now locked in, both sides have months to prepare for a second round of proceedings that could ultimately hinge on the same core question that deadlocked the first jury: whether Clancy’s mental state in January 2023 meets the legal standard for insanity, or whether she can be held criminally responsible for the deaths of Cora, Dawson and Callan.

Any decision to pursue a reduced charge — rather than first-degree murder — will rest with Cruz as the retrial approaches.
