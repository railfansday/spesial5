Apple TV's 10/10 Spy Thriller Just Made a Totally Ambitious Move

The best news for fans of spy thrillers this week isn't what you'd expect. While everyone was celebrating a new James Bond, MI5 actually had the biggest shake-up as Apple TV announced the renewal of Slow Horses for Seasons 9 and 10. You read that right — Season 6 has barely wrapped up, and viewers can already look forward to four more seasons of Jackson Lamb's (Gary Oldman) trademark grumpiness. This comes as a great surprise, not just the number of seasons itself, but given the greater circumstances. Back in September, Apple had already renewed Slow Horses for Season 8, and now, less than two months later, has confirmed an incredible Season 10 update. It's a really unprecedented move for any streamer, so is there a catch — or are things just that good at Slough House?

Gone are the golden years of cable TV, when viewers could expect their favorite shows to return year after year without fail. Getting lost in series like Friends, Supernatural, and Grey's Anatomy was a pleasure, but, with the dawn of the streaming age, it became harder and harder to find such long-running shows — so much so that people usually point to these behemoths of old when looking for long-term commitment on TV, as those titles remain available on most streaming platforms.

Because of the usual budget constraints, long-term casting commitments, and ever-changing business models, scripted live-action shows rarely go beyond six or seven seasons on streaming, so Slow Horses earning this type of renewal is completely unprecedented in the current landscape. Its now-confirmed 10-season run makes it Apple TV's longest-running series — a title it previously shared with For All Mankind, which is slated to end with Season 6.

There are a few reasons that have resulted in Slow Horses getting this kind of treatment. Each season has only six episodes, making it much quicker to produce, and every season finale delivers a preview for the next batch of episodes. Most important, though, is how well it performs among audiences and critics. On Rotten Tomatoes, every season of Slow Horses has consistently performed above 95%, so, from a business perspective, what's not to love about it?

On its own, this unexpected renewal is bound to keep Slow Horses and Apple TV at the top of viewers' minds for a while, especially with a new season set to premiere next year. Its consistent release schedule is another reason this series has performed so well since the beginning. (Longer gaps between seasons are another streaming staple, unfortunately.) To keep the pace, a quick production is important, but what's really vital is the fact that there has always been source material available to adapt for each new season.

Slow Horses famously adapts Mick Herron's Slough House book series, which currently comprises nine entries. From the beginning, each season has adapted one book, with the exception being Season 6 — which adapted two novels, Joe Country and Slough House. Right now, the expectation is that Season 7 will adapt Bad Actors and Season 8 will adapt Clown Town, Herron's most recent novel published in 2025.

The line between reality and fiction might be clear, but these misfit spies' pulse-pounding missions are definitely rooted in truth.

Now that Slow Horses has been renewed for more, though, Seasons 9 and 10 don't yet have a published Herron novel to adapt — at least for now. The expectation is that a tenth Slough House novel will be announced, although there has been no confirmation as of this writing. Herron has previously mentioned that he already knows "why, when, and where Jackson Lamb dies," so it stands to reason that the Apple TV series won't run forever, and its end may be steadily approaching.

Even if Herron's tenth novel takes longer to reach publication than anticipated, there are still five Slough House novellas for the show to draw from, all compiled in Watch the Quiet Snow. It's still way too early to know where Slow Horses is headed in Seasons 9 and 10, but it seems unlikely that Apple TV would take such a bold step in renewing it without a clear plan.

Curiously, the timing of this renewal news couldn't have been better. Mere days prior, Olivia Cooke, who plays Sidonie "Sid" Baker, said in an interview that Season 8 would be Slow Horses' last, even mentioning that the cast has been "all melancholy" while filming. Apple TV's renewal immediately refutes this, adding another two seasons to the previously announced seventh and eighth back in September. (Hopefully, Season 8 isn't Cooke's last season, though.)

Finally, there is the "James Bond" of it all. Jack Lowden, who plays down-on-his-luck protagonist River Cartwright, was among the top contenders for 007, but although fans rooted for him until the very end, the role ultimately went to Robert Pattinson. Ironically, hoping to get out of Slough House for a better position in a government agency only to ultimately get shot down is such a Cartwright thing that we can almost hear Lamb laughing in the background. So far, only Oldman's return has been confirmed, but it's hard to imagine Slow Horses without the rest of Lamb's joes, no matter how many seasons the show ultimately runs for.
