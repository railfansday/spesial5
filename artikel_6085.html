Caring for Aging Loved Ones Is Not an Injustice

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

SNL’s ‘Sister’ skit has ignited a national discussion about caregiving, but are we taking the right lessons from it?

John Mulaney appears in Saturday Night Live’s “Sister” sketch

Saturday Night Live struck a nerve with its recent “Sister” skit, a spoof advertisement for a supposedly revolutionary elder-care service that is free, available around the clock, and conveniently happens to be your sister. The skit is funny because it captures something many families recognize: When an aging parent needs help, it’s often the women who step up, while male relatives carry on with their lives largely uninterrupted.

There’s certainly some truth to this, but there’s a difference between objecting to an unfair division of caregiving responsibilities and treating those responsibilities as inherently oppressive. The suggestion that our elder-care challenges are largely a product of sexism, or that women who take on these responsibilities have simply been conditioned to accept their own exploitation, misses the bigger issue. This interpretation not only diminishes the contributions of female caregivers but also feeds into a troubling cultural narrative: that caring for the people we love is an injustice rather than a responsibility inherent to family.

Heather Madden is the vice president for policy initiatives at Independent Women.
