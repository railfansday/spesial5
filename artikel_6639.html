This Is the Best Way to Use Leftover Canned Pumpkin

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Half a can of pumpkin leftover pumpkin may not be enough for another pie, but you can definitely put it to use in other fall recipes.

Fall baking has a way of leaving you with awkward amounts of leftover canned pumpkin. So many recipes call for half a can or some other quantity that leaves you wondering what exactly you’re supposed to do with the rest. You put the open can of Libby’s pumpkin puree in the refrigerator with every intention of using it, yet a few days later, there it is, still waiting for you to come up with a plan. It’s not as though you can make half a pumpkin pie.

Fortunately, you don’t need much pumpkin to make something wonderful. In fact, one of the best things I’ve made with leftover pumpkin requires less than a cup, a handful of pantry staples and very little effort. It’s the sort of recipe that makes having leftover pumpkin feel less like a nuisance and more like a stroke of good luck.

These are a little different from the dense, chewy blondies you might be accustomed to. They have golden edges and a wonderfully soft, almost cakey center, thanks in part to the pumpkin puree. Brown sugar gives them their rich, buttery sweetness, while cinnamon and pumpkin pie spice make them taste unmistakably like fall. The recipe lists the chocolate chips as optional, though I’d argue strongly in favor of including them. Chocolate and pumpkin are excellent together, and the little pockets of melted chocolate are delicious against all those warm spices.

The pumpkin blondies also almost suspiciously easy to make. Everything comes together in one bowl, using ingredients you probably already have on hand. There’s no frosting to fuss with, no dough to chill and no pie crust to negotiate. Just spread the batter into a 9-inch pan and bake it for 25 to 30 minutes. You’ll have a dozen soft, spiced bars that are equally at home on a Thanksgiving dessert table as they are next to your morning coffee.

Best of all, the recipe calls for just 1/2 cup of canned pumpkin. That’s a particularly convenient amount when you’ve got the remains of another baking project taking up residence in the refrigerator. And if you have a full cup left, making a double batch seems like a perfectly reasonable course of action.

And there’s no reason to limit pumpkin to sweet recipes. A few spoonfuls can add body and richness to soups, pasta dishes and risotto, especially when paired with sage, garlic and Parmesan. Just be sure you’re using plain pumpkin puree rather than pumpkin pie filling, which already contains sugar and spices and isn’t as versatile.

If you’re not quite ready to commit to another pumpkin project, you can always freeze your leftover pumpkin puree. That way, the next time a recipe calls for an odd amount of pumpkin, you’ll have some on hand.
