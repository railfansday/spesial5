10 RPGs That Are Unexpected Masterpieces

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Ronald is a video game journalist with more than 10 years of experience in the industry, working on several of the most important Spanish websites.

Thanks to the diversity with which the genre can be expressed, role-playing video games constitute a large part of my favorite experiences in the interactive medium, as they encompass a practically unparalleled plethora of possibilities.

Both an isometric horror title and a first-person medieval fantasy can be RPGs, giving all players, regardless of their tastes, the opportunity to find tailor-made masterpieces within the genre.

Of course, being among the most popular categories in the entire industry, finding an adventure curated to suit our tastes is no easy task, as it's common to need dozens of hours to fully enjoy each of its journeys.

Therefore, in order to save you many afternoons and with the aim of appealing to as many gaming interests as possible, I invite you to read this list of ten RPGs that are unexpected masterpieces.

If you like Paper Mario, you already know exactly what Bug Fables: The Everlasting Sapling has to offer, though I say this as one of the highest compliments I can give.

It's a game with very traditional foundations but an execution brimming with personality, offering a compelling world and story that truly keeps you engaged, especially due to its unique theme.

However, its greatest marvel lies in its gameplay. From exploration to turn-based combat combinations, side quests, bosses, and customization, the overall mechanics work flawlessly, captivating both newcomers and veterans of this type of game.

This independently created gem from Panama deserves all the recognition it can get. Rest assured, Bug Fables: The Everlasting Sapling is such an outstanding work that, at times, I think it surpasses its main inspiration.

Speaking of surpassing your muses, I've read many discussions about Path of Exile already surpassing Diablo, and frankly, comparing the most recent performance of both franchises, I find it difficult to argue otherwise.

Although they have different approaches, it's undeniable that Grinding Gear Games' creation, both the original and its sequel, has reached such levels of content density and attention to detail that they ceased to be "a clone" many years ago.

Instead, we're talking about one of the greatest representatives of the isometric RPG genre, with a consistently captivating experience that puts your job at risk because, once it hooks you, it only lets go after several hundred hours.

Imagining in 2013 that Path of Exile could go head-to-head with its category's benchmarks seemed impossible, but today, what's truly unbelievable is calling it anything less than a masterpiece of its kind.

Since the end of the last decade, efforts to find a true successor to Disco Elysium have tripled year after year, but even so, I couldn't have anticipated that a game like Esoteric Ebb would be its true spiritual heir.

Although it boasts enough distinctions to stand on its own merits, it's clear that the foundation was laid by the 2019 marvel, which allowed Christoffer Bodegård to craft an outstanding adventure from the dialogue to the worldbuilding and immersion.

Each narrative path is more captivating than the last, and even without voice acting, which forces you to read for hours on end, the cadence and rhythm between exploration and conversations, both internal and with others, are measured with truly commendable precision.

If you're a fan of Disco Elysium or Dungeons & Dragons, Esoteric Ebb strikes a perfect balance that, I can say with complete certainty, no other title this decade has even come close to achieving.

If you're looking for a 16-bit adventure, these SEGA Genesis RPGs are a great place to start.

A decade and a half ago, The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim's release sparked a subsequent odyssey to become the next great medieval first-person RPG, and I believe that title finally belongs to Tainted Grail: The Fall of Avalon.

The mere comparison is the highest honor that can be bestowed upon this wonderful dark fantasy, which nevertheless knows perfectly well how to distinguish itself from Bethesda's icy landscapes without losing an ounce of immersion.

Moreover, the title manages to compensate for its idol's weaknesses by including choices that truly feel meaningful, as well as a much more fluid and demanding combat system that not only increases the challenge but also the reward of leveling up and becoming gradually more skilled.

Considering the remarkable post-launch support that its developers provided for free, Tainted Grail: The Fall of Avalon is a blessing for the genre that deserves to be rewarded with a chance.

Dread Delusion looks further back than the 2011 Game of the Year and draws its inspiration from two superb titles, The Elder Scrolls III: Morrowind and King's Field, and the result is as outstanding as you'd expect from that combination.

Its distinctive aesthetic, blending a feverish PlayStation 1 dream with a lush color palette, immediately captivates the eye, yet the experience truly enchants for hours thanks to the sheer satisfaction of getting lost in its picturesque settings.

That said, you need a very high tolerance for its combat, as it lacks depth and isn't particularly rewarding. Rather, it's a necessary sacrifice to immerse yourself in its splendid world, which is worth every second.

It's a tougher recommendation, especially in modern times, given how much better it feels when you're not using your weapons, but make no mistake: Dread Delusion is one of those titles that, if it's right for you, will win you over thoroughly.

There are certain video games whose ultimate goal is to hook you for hundreds of hours without you even realizing it or being able to stop, and Grim Dawn is undoubtedly one of them.

I went in expecting to find a good RPG and little else, and ended up finding a lifelong companion that I would spend countless afternoons with, mainly due to its absurdly deep and satisfying replayability.

The art is superb, the music is tremendous, the story and atmosphere stand out, the creativity with which it allows you to experiment with builds is impressive... It has everything you could expect from an ARPG, and even more.

For how magnificent it is, I'm afraid Grim Dawn isn't talked about enough, and precisely for this reason, you wouldn't expect that behind it lies one of the most magnificent isometric role-playing games ever made.

There are still numerous RPG gems to enjoy before the year ends.

Appearances in the video game industry are easily deceiving because the essence lies in interaction. This allows a pixel art game like Look Outside to appear harmless, while beneath the surface, it's a masterpiece of cosmic horror.

However, despite its themes inducing fear through impressively disturbing designs, it's a turn-based RPG where your decisions shape both the party and the story, which quickly spirals into madness.

Despite its modest origins in RPG Maker, the game compensates for its technical limitations with outstanding art and writing, aided by a functional gameplay component that ensures the campaign flows smoothly, so the protagonist—the atmosphere—never suffers in the slightest.

Look Outside is among the finest indie games of the last couple of years, although it seems the community hasn't quite grasped the greatness with which it represents the work of H.P. Lovecraft... Yet.

We all know that everything Obsidian touches turns to gold, though for reasons that are hard to understand, the gaming community seems to have overlooked this when it came to Alpha Protocol.

Yes, the action sequences are decent at best, and technically speaking, it's a subpar product, but the stealth, writing, and decision-making are so magnificent that I struggle to imagine a world where the negative outweighs the positive.

You can experience the story multiple times in diametrically opposed ways and have campaigns that feel unique, with memorable characters, plot points that truly make you think, and a worldbuilding that lures you to explore every last detail.

Since I know there are two types of RPG fans, one who prioritizes gameplay and another who prioritizes narrative, I must emphasize that only the latter will truly enjoy Alpha Protocol, though their lives will be completely changed once they play it.

From Mass Effect 2 to Elden Ring, these RPGs make putting off the main quest worthwhile—and sometimes even essential to the outcome.

Kingdom Come: Deliverance was a very pleasant surprise, but it appealed to a limited niche due to the specificity of its medieval setting. Therefore, I don't think any of us expected Kingdom Come: Deliverance 2 to become the new gold standard for RPGs.

All future titles of this genre that prioritize immersion and the complexity of their systems will have to look to Warhorse Studios' masterpiece, which demands a titanic yet worthy effort to show why it's one of the finest works the genre has produced this decade.

By polishing absolutely everything from its predecessor and improving upon it as much as humanly possible, it's a perfect sequel that doesn't fall short in any area, being extraordinary in its narrative, gameplay, interactivity, audiovisuals, and even its overall atmosphere.

Kingdom Come: Deliverance 2 is the blueprint that every first-person RPG should take into consideration for years to come, which is an astounding affirmation to say considering that the original was just very good.

Over the years, I've made it my mission to champion Dragon's Dogma as the most underrated video game of the century, and unfortunately, not even the sequel's success has allowed me to hang up my gloves in that endeavor.

Capcom has numerous masterpieces to its name, though this one in particular stands out for how far ahead of its contemporaries it was at its release, offering an epic fantasy scope barely explored by a couple of other titles within the genre in all its history.

The scale at which Gransys allows us to develop a heroic tale against colossal and captivating beasts is breathtaking, compelling you to lose yourself in a classic mythology that enchants you from its goblins to its dragons.

Thanks to the spectacular gameplay and customization options, where skirmishes are remarkably large and visually stunning regardless of the class you choose, it's a title that could hardly have been anticipated, especially considering what its competitors were doing at the time.

Years go by, and Dragon's Dogma keeps aging like the finest wine, waiting for the video game industry to finally recognize it as one of the past decade's generational titles.
