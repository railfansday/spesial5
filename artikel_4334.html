The Ratchet & Clank Game You Didn’t Know Existed is Shutting Down

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Daniel is a lifelong gamer with a love for various genres and gameplay styles who enjoys exploring games and playing from various angles.

I’ll admit, I have a tendency to hyperfixate on games and franchises that I care about, occasionally to the detriment of others. Even so, I like to consider myself sufficiently plugged-into this industry, especially when it comes to relatively big-name IPs like the Ratchet & Clank series. You can imagine my distinct confusion, then, when I heard today that Ratchet & Clank: Ranger Rumble was shutting down, to which my response was, “what the heck is Ratchet & Clank: Ranger Rumble?”

Apparently it is, soon to be “was,” a PvP live service shooter available for iOS and Android. The game saw a soft-launch in mid-2025 in limited territories like Canada, France, and parts of Asia, which probably explains why I have literally never heard of it alongside the fact that I don’t often pay much mind to mobile games in general.

What’s especially interesting about this particular discontinuation, however, is that the game wasn’t even actually finished yet. That soft-launch was as far as the game had ever managed to get, with its planned global launch officially being scrapped and further development ceasing as of the game’s most recent update today, according to its official home page.

As the home page announcement explains, with the newest update pushed out today to mobile storefronts, no further updates for Ratchet & Clank: Ranger Rumble will be released, and on November 21, the game will be pulled from said storefronts entirely.

This is a feature that looks at how live service games are dying as a popular choice in the industry.

For those who are still actively playing the game, you don’t have to stop playing it right away. While new players won’t be able to download the game from the Google Play or Apple App stores anymore following the delisting, anyone who already has the game downloaded will be able to keep playing multiplayer matches until the servers are officially closed in May 2027.

On November 21, the game will be pulled from storefronts entirely.

The game’s “Qwark Pass” seasons will continue running until the server closure, while the game’s “Super Pass” has been made free, with all of its related content now available to earn throughout the remaining seasons. Of course, said content will vanish into the ether once the game goes offline, so whether or not you can be bothered is up to you.

This also goes for any in-app purchases made on things like currency, cosmetics, upgrades, and so forth. If you’re looking to get a refund for any of that, the developers suggest you contact the Apple App Store and Google Play Store’s respective support lines to determine eligibility. Again, absolutely everything is being erased when the servers shutter in May, so whether you want a refund or to keep playing, you’d better act now.

In its final announcement, the developers offered some words of thanks to the players who joined them for the soft-launch process. “On behalf of the dev team and everyone helping to make the game, thanks to our amazing community for all your support, especially to those who have spent time playing, sharing feedback, and helping shape Ranger Rumble throughout its development,” they wrote.

While not as high-profile as some other historic live-service flops over the years, the discontinuation of Ratchet & Clank: Ranger Rumble is another good sign that live-service games are reaching the end of an era. Aside from a very fortunate few that manage to keep attention with consistent updates and player-grabbing content like Fortnite’s One Piece event, the majority end up crashing and burning well before they can take off in earnest.

Sony in particular has been putting the kibosh on live-service games left and right for the last couple of years, mostly following the disastrous launch of Concord, and it seems even its major first-party PlayStation IPs like Ratchet & Clank aren’t safe from the proverbial axe.
