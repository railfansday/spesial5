How Taylor Swift Taught Keltie Knight to Make the Whole Place Shimmer

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Ahead of E! News’ Keltie Knight releasing her new self-help book The F--k Them Theory, she detailed how a gig as Taylor Swift’s backup dancer taught her to truly shine in an exclusive interview.

What’s a girl gonna do? Taylor Swift taught Keltie Knight that a diamond’s gotta shine.

Ahead of the release of her new book The F--k Them Theory: Self-Help for People Who Are Done Being the Bigger Person on Nov. 24, the Live from E! host detailed how her past experience as a backup dancer for the “Bejeweled” singer taught her a valuable lesson.

“At the time, I was a blonde,” Knight told E! News in an exclusive interview at a New York launch event for her upcoming book, recalling her 2009 VMAs performance alongside Swift. “Taylor was feeling a way—I don’t know if it was her or her team—but they made me wear a hat because I was blonde and I just had a little blang—a little blonde bang coming out. They were like, ‘No this is her moment.’”

And for the Lady Gang podcast co-host, that moment made her realize that she’s not doing all the extra credit to get graded on a curve.

“Being unapologetically like, ‘I want to be the brightest thing in the room because this is my moment,’” Knight recalled. “I think that is something I’m trying to teach in this book which is like, you’re allowed to shine.”

The 44-year-old emphasized that Swift struck a perfect balance of basking in her moment while also championing those around her.

“You’re allowed to be the star of your own life,” she explained. “If you don’t want any other blondes, if you don’t want bridesmaids, if you don’t want if you want to just own your moment yourself, you can.”

Emmy Award-winning TV personality and New York Times bestselling author Keltie Knight's latest release is half memoir, half self-help book, inspiring readers to transform rejection into power, healing, and reinvention.

In the book, Knight also detailed how Swift was the consummate professional during the 2009 performance, sharing that she not only thanked everyone involved in the project, but also was extra conscious of minor details that helped others do their jobs more effectively.

“I really wanted to tell this story particularly in the book because I saw the Eras Tour documentary and everyone was saying, ‘Oh well, if I had a billion dollars I would treat people this nice and do the handwritten notes,’” the former dancer shared. “And it’s like, no. I was, in 2009, a backup dancer for a girl who had never even won a Moonman yet and she was exactly the same person.”

As Knight put it, “The story is really about showing up and being your best and the way you treat people when you’re nobody is the way you treat people when you’re somebody.”
