Smithsonian Head Assures Staff of Independence After Glimpse of White House Overhaul Plan

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Outgoing Smithsonian Secretary Lonnie G. Bunch III reasserted the institution’s independence in a letter to his staff after a leaked document from a Trump aide laid out a sweeping overhaul of its programming and personnel.

“The intended use of this list is unclear,” Bunch wrote in the letter, which the New York Times reviewed and first reported. “What remains clear is that the Smithsonian is an independent institution with the responsibility and privilege to present accurate information to millions of visitors through our scholarship and educational programs.” Related Articles Did a Trump Staffer Accidentally Reveal White House Plans to Revamp the Smithsonian? The document, titled “Smithsonian Institution Renewal Plan,” was photographed on a laptop that a Trump aide was using on the Washington Metro. As first reported by ProPublica, its 25-item action list includes plans to dismiss top curators and administrators, among them Anthea M. Hartig, director of the Smithsonian’s National Museum of American History, whom the White House has accused of promoting “radical activist ideology.”

The plan also calls for a new permanent exhibit, “The Riches of Our Western Civilization Inheritance,” and another on “Great Families of American History.” It envisions a “Colossus Statue” of George Washington at the museum’s entrance, which would fulfill a request Trump made in September.

The plan appears to be a work in progress: Photos show the aide brainstorming additions in a notebook. One handwritten entry seems to read “No X-rated exhibition policy,” an apparent callback to a particularly contentious stretch of Hartig’s testimony, when lawmakers questioned her about images of bondage wear in an LGBTQ history exhibition. On Monday, ProPublica identified the staffer as an associate policy adviser with the Domestic Policy Council, the White House office behind a 162-page report that accused the Smithsonian of “extreme political activism that seeks to transform our country.”

A central complaint of the report was what its authors characterized as anti-white bias at the museum, which they said showed in its promotion of diversity, equity, and inclusion. The report accused one exhibition, “Many Voices, One Nation,” of trying “to convince visitors that illegal aliens are entitled to citizenship, voting rights and ‘belonging’ in America.” (Two weeks after the report’s release, Trump signed an executive order directing that signs be installed outside the museum warning visitors of its “ideological capture.”)

The document glimpsed on the staffer’s laptop laid out new interpretive guidelines for exhibits, including an explicit end to the Smithsonian’s use of a diversity style guide. The Smithsonian’s strategy for traveling exhibitions would be reviewed, and one show now on tour, “The Bias Inside Us,” would be halted. Grants to “woke third parties,” such as the American Alliance of Museums, would also be reviewed.

The Smithsonian’s online K-12 curriculum would be overhauled to “realign educational materials with patriotism, not ideology.” The “Becoming US” module on immigration, for example, would be deleted and replaced with a “Founders-focused curriculum titled something like Architects of Liberty: The Heroic Journey of America’s Founders OR Profiles in Liberty: Washington and America’s Heroic Men of Courage.”

The Smithsonian, a network of 21 museums, is structured to operate independently of the White House. It’s governed by a 17-member Board of Regents: the Chief Justice of the United States, John G. Roberts Jr.; the Vice President, JD Vance; six members of Congress from both parties; and nine private citizens. Legally, the Smithsonian answers to Congress, not the White House, but federal money makes up about 60 percent of its budget. Trump proposed cutting that funding by roughly 12 percent for fiscal year 2026, a threat that ultimately went unrealized.

Bunch, who became secretary in 2019, has repeatedly asserted the institution’s legal independence as pressure from the White House has mounted. The administration has demanded that the Smithsonian turn over internal materials and pushed for the firing of Kim Sajet, the director of the National Portrait Gallery, whom Trump had called “highly partisan.” Sajet ultimately resigned. Bunch has drawn similar attacks: a White House aide reportedly called him a “rabid partisan.”

In a May interview with the New York Times, Bunch seemed to acknowledge that recent vacancies on the Board of Regents had weakened his position, though he still appeared to have the support of a majority of the board. In September, he announced that he would retire at the end of the year.

In his letter to staff on Tuesday, Bunch wrote, “As I have said before, we welcome scrutiny of our work, and even disagreement. The Smithsonian will continue to be driven by scholarship, research and reflection, free from outside influence and in service to the American people.”
