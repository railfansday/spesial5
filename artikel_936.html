WTA chief Valerie Camillo says private comments made by Jessica Pegula about Mirra Andreeva being overheard on a YouTube livestream is "not bad for viewership".

Camillo personally apologised to Pegula, describing the broadcast of on-court conversations during a practice session at the WTA Finals in Indian Wells as "regrettable and unacceptable".

The Tour, who operated the livestream, had intended for the microphones to capture ambient sounds from the court but failed to inform participants.

A broadcast showed the American in conversation with her coach Mark Knowles, calling the 19-year-old a "diva" and saying she has lost respect for Andreeva's coach Conchita Martinez for not calling out her behaviour.

"Someone actually asked me if we planned it or did it on purpose to draw attention to the event," Camillo told Sky Sports' lead tennis commentator, Jonathan Overend.

"I wish that we were so crafty and strategic but we can't put ourselves in any situation where we put our athletes at risk. Our number one objective as a member services organisation for our players is to protect our players.

"What Jess said is competitive banter. I don't think there was anything particularly salacious in it. But having mics lying around that aren't being properly managed is not how the WTA expects to execute and operate.

"It created a viral moment. It created a lot of dialogue. But that's not intentional and not something you should expect to see us doing in the future.

"I've had this conversation here that I come from a variety of different sports. Before joining the WTA, I exclusively worked in male sports. I worked at the NBA and majorly baseball and it's quite common for there to be competitive banter that is brought into the forefront.

"It's up to the athletes for what they feel comfortable with and what they don't. But a little drama isn't bad for ticket sales.

"It's not bad for viewership. It's not bad for commentary. That's just a reality."

Andreeva laughed off the hot mic moment and went on to defeat Pegula 6-3 6-4 on Saturday, in a match that gained an extra layer of intrigue to reach the WTA Finals title match.

She concluded a stunning week with a second victory over Elina Svitolina to win the season-ending tournament.

The WTA was forced to replace the venue for the season-ending event from Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, in July, which represented a major financial setback for the governing body.

The deal with the Saudi Tennis Federation (STF) included record prize money over a three-year period, but the ongoing tension between Iran and the United States made planning for the Finals more challenging, eventually forcing the tournament to move to Indian Wells in California.

The body is projecting operating losses of £17.3m ($23m) for 2026 and expects to hold roughly £11.3m ($15m) in cash by the end of the year, according to The Telegraph. Camillo remains confident of finding extra revenue streams through in-house media rights and sponsorship growth to create financial stability.

"Our ambition for the WTA Finals is equal prize money [with the men's tour]. That's our ambition, just as we expected of the Grand Slams. All of our combined 1000 events will have equal prize money in 2027," she said.

"We've been building fandom. We've been operating globally. We've been creating superstar athletes through our platform for many, many decades, and so the athletes have the opportunity not to just earn on the court, but also to control their own brands and monetise that off the court.

"WTA intends to remain in its position as number one in the hearts and minds of fans, in the mindset and investment of our sponsors, and we want to be building an even greater platform on which our great athletes can compete, not only one that they can enjoy from the ferocity of the crowd, but also one that enables them to maximise their financial opportunity to the best of our ability."

One of the WTA revenue streams comes from selling data, match data, court feeds to data companies with the betting industry a large part of that.

British tennis star Jodie Burrage called on social media platforms to hold people accountable for sending death threats and abuse.

Burrage, who was playing her first WTA Tour match since Wimbledon last summer, received death threats and online abuse following a first-round defeat at a tournament in India.

The 27-year-old shared a raft of abusive messages sent to her Instagram account. Alongside the video, Burrage wrote: "Nice welcome back to the tour. I remember why I left."

Camillo said: "The complexity of official data to these partners is sort of an industry inside thing. But the reality is that through having official partnerships, you actually can have more integrity and controls.

"If you don't have relationships with these variety of platforms, you can turn into a little bit of the wild, wild west. It's not to say that there isn't a financial element to these relationships. That would be disingenuous. Of course there is. But it does also enable us to have a partnership that leads to better controls."
