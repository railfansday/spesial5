Meta begins internal tests of prediction markets app

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The app would offer similar functionality to Kalshi and Polymarket without involving cash transactions, per the Wall Street Journal.

Meta is seemingly pushing ahead with a controversial plan to introduce a prediction markets element on its platforms. The move comes amid an ongoing debate about the potential harm caused by its apps.

As reported by the Wall Street Journal on Friday, Meta has begun internal testing of its own prediction market app.

As per WSJ: “Employees have begun testing a prediction-market app under development, according to people familiar with the matter, a step the company often takes ahead of a public release.”

WSJ reported that the app, called “Takes,” allows users to wager on the outcome of questions or events. The system does not enable users to place cash bets, but instead uses a system called “cred” that lets users boost their standing in the app by making accurate bets.

Meta has reportedly been developing its own prediction markets tool for the past few months, with the New York Times reporting in June that the company was considering a push into this sector.

Prediction markets have become big business of late, with platforms including Polymarket and Kalshi enabling users to place bets on all sorts of events.

According to the June NYT report, Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg had “dispatched a small team at his company to create a smartphone app similar to Polymarket and Kalshi.” NYT said the app would use “a video-game-like points system” instead of letting users bet real money, with Meta hoping to tap into the compulsion of live event betting while avoiding any regulatory scrutiny that would come with financial stakes.

The plan would be for Meta to launch its separate prediction markets app by leveraging Facebook and Instagram for promotion. As such, this wouldn’t be introducing actual gambling into its core apps, but could still provide another way for Meta to benefit from gambling in order to boost engagement.

Which, no matter how Meta frames it, seems like a risky bet.

The company is already facing regulatory scrutiny and legal action around the world due to the harms that its apps have allegedly caused, in particular to younger users.

Younger users are also increasingly likely to engage with prediction markets.

As reported by Reuters: “Just over 60% of users on prediction markets Kalshi and Polymarket are under the age of 35, according to a recent S&P Global Market Intelligence Kagan survey, with sports being the most popular category.”

It would be risky for Meta to go anywhere near this, even without actual money being involved. But, for whatever reason, it seems that Meta is going to try, which could be another example of how the company prioritizes business benefit over user well-being.

Even without involving cash, introducing a betting-like option could be habit-forming, and could inspire people to up their stakes in other apps.
