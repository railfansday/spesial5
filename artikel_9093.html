VENICE VICTORS ANNOUNCED. After a Venice Biennale encumbered by bitter controversy, the exhibition has at last revealed the winners of its top honors, which were chosen this year by public vote instead of a jury, reports ARTnews’s Alex Greenberger. They went to the American artist Nick Cave for his participation in Koyo Kouoh’s main exhibition and India’s national pavilion. The so-called Visitors’ Lions replaced the traditional Golden Lions because the five-person jury for the latter award resigned in May, ahead of the show opening, after stating they would not consider pavilions for the award, which represented countries whose leaders are being charged with crimes against humanity by the International Criminal Court. SMITHSONIAN TAKES A STAND. Lonnie Bunch, the secretary of the Smithsonian Institution, is among the leading figures who responded to a leaked document outlining the Trump Administration’s plans to fire top personnel at the Smithsonian, including the director of the institution’s National Museum of American History, Anthea M. Hartig, reports the New York Times. Though it’s unclear whether the White House intends to carry out the leaked plans, in a letter to staff, Bunch insisted on the Smithsonian’s autonomy. “What remains clear is that the Smithsonian is an independent institution with the responsibility and privilege to present accurate information to millions …” he stated. The memo about the Smithsonian overhaul was photographed on the Washington, D.C. Metro by a fellow traveler who spotted it on the laptop of an aide, who has since been identified by ProPublica as an associate policy adviser within the White House’s Domestic Policy Council. The White House has not responded to requests for comment.

SORRY, NOT SORRY. In this descriptive profile for the Financial Times, New York dealer Marianne Boesky looks back as her eponymous gallery turns 30. For one, we learn that she plans to open a gallery in Paris next year, her first in Europe. She also reflects on how mothers have had to struggle to make their way in the art world over the years. One memory in particular still stings: In 2006, when Takashi Murakami left Boesky for Gagosian, she recalls that after her daughter Ellie was born, he told her he couldn’t be represented by someone who was “lactating.” “It was devastating for me because it was still so early in my career,” she said. “He didn’t honor his commitments that we had made to each other. That’s when it went south.” Murakami’s studio said the artist had no recollection of saying the comment about breastfeeding. But things were also different then, said Boesky. “At that time, the idea of being a woman in business and feeling fully liberated and autonomous meant that you just kept your kids hidden,” she said. “The next generation, they are present and unapologetic.”
