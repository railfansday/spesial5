The 10 most unfootballery footballers ever: Where does James Trafford rank in weird and wonderful list?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Manchester United, Arsenal, Chelsea, Everton and Leeds United have all employed footballers who you always felt somehow stumbled into the wrong profession.

We’re talking former NASA interns, qualified doctors and those with a keen interest in literary theory. Footballers aren’t supposed to be this interesting away from the pitch, but this lot have broken the mould in weird and wonderful ways.

Here are 10 of the least footballery footballers in history.

You remember him. The 6’3″ man mountain who went viral for looking more like a minor Game of Thrones character than a professional footballer.

With a club career away from the limelight in South Korea, Greece and his native Hungary, Euro 2024 catapulted the striker in front of a whole new audience.

“Of course, one or two get to me, I usually have a laugh about it,” he said about becoming a social media sensation.

“I was born this way, I have this body shape. I’m not saying that I was this big when I was born, but I have a basic physique. Genetics – I can’t change that.”

No shade here. In a sport increasingly populated by chiselled physiques and carefully curated Instagram accounts, it’s refreshing to see a full international who looks like he should be crushing skulls in Westeros. More of them, please.

A bit landfill indie, a bit Mod revival. You can picture Baines in Paul Weller’s backing band, wearing a parka and looking mildly unimpressed on stage for two hours.

That’s not to say he was some great footballing eccentric, but Baines had a certain individuality that stood out a bit in the identikit culture of the Barclays era.

Back in Batty’s day, it wasn’t remotely unusual for England internationals and title-winning midfielders to look and sound like blokes you’d encounter at your local.

Media training wasn’t yet a finely tuned industry, sanding down every rough edge until footballers sounded like they’d been assembled on a production line of bland platitudes. Three points, take each game as it comes, you know the rest.

Batty wasn’t alone in that respect, but his almost total disappearance from public life since retiring is a pretty good indication of how little he ever cared for the circus.

Just look at his sensationally understated response to missing a vital penalty in England’s World Cup defeat to Argentina in 1998. You’d have thought he’d misplaced his car keys.

“You know what the position is before you step up and take a pen,” he said.

“I’m disappointed it didn’t work out right but I’m pleased I took one.”

Nevin isn’t the only footballer with a penchant for indie music, but his credentials go well beyond owning a few Smiths records.

He’s a brilliant broadcaster and his genuine enthusiasm for the game always shines through, but we still get the feeling that running down the wing for Chelsea and Everton denied him his true calling of playing keyboards for a Glaswegian dreampop band.

Once did a Peel session, supported the Cocteau Twins, reunited for a run of shows at 200-capacity venues back in 2016. He can but dream.

“I had been attending university in the US on a football scholarship, and had been doing a summer internship at NASA the year before I graduated,” Hislop reminisced in an interview with FourFourTwo.

“After getting my degree in Mechanical Engineering, I was drafted by an indoor team called the Baltimore Blast. This was before the days of MLS. As it turned out, Baltimore were touring England that summer.

“We played Aston Villa in two indoor games. Dwight Yorke – another Trinidadian – had signed for them a couple of years previously.”

The Jacques Brel-inspired crooner album he released? The Ken Loach film he appeared in? Suddenly retiring at the age of 30 just because? Basically every word he’s ever uttered?

None of it at all footballery. We’re still not entirely convinced he was a real person. Some sort of post-rave collective fever dream apparition.

In a recent appearance on the Second Captains podcast, Bellerin delved into his admiration for Stefan Zweig, the Austrian literary giant known for his nuanced character studies and literary explorations of obsession, morality and the human psyche.

This guy used to line up alongside Andrea Pirlo and David Villa.

Socrates was destined to be different from the very beginning. His father was a self-educated intellectual with a passion for philosophy and literature, and named his first three sons after figures from ancient Greece. His brothers were called Sostenes and Sofocles.

One formative childhood memory was watching his father dispose of his treasured library following Brazil’s 1964 military coup. He was 10 years old at the time.

As an adult, he became a qualified doctor, was an outspoken political activist and helped spearhead a revolutionary movement at Corinthians. He’d have been just as comfortable discussing Marxism over a bottle of red as he was dictating the tempo in midfield.

He smoked two packs of cigarettes a day, sported a magnificent beard, captained Brazil at two World Cups and briefly came out of retirement to rock up at Garforth Town.

Football has produced plenty of superstars, but precious few quite like Socrates. Almost certainly the coolest footballer in history.
