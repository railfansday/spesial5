United Airlines Passenger Accused Of Storming Cockpit Had Been Deported Twice And Granted Asylum, DHS Says

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

A passenger accused of claiming to have a bomb and trying to enter the cockpit of a United Airlines flight had previously been deported twice, then received asylum, according to the Department of Homeland Security. The newly disclosed immigration history adds another troubling layer to an incident that thankfully ended with the aircraft landing safely and the suspect in custody.

Rony Alexander Gomez-Alberto, 34, was arrested after United Airlines flight 2174 from Newark (EWR) to New Orleans (MSY) landed on November 9, 2026.

According the Jefferson Parish Sheriff’s Office, Gomez-Alberto allegedly told cabin crew there was a bomb onboard. DHS separately alleges an attempt to enter the cockpit. DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin said:

“This criminal illegal alien attempted to storm the cockpit, hijack a plane and claimed he had a bomb. Thanks to law enforcement, he is now behind bars. This twice-deported criminal illegal alien was allowed into our country because of a weak asylum system that allowed him to game the system.”

He went on to claim Gomez-Alberto was a “fraudster” trying to scam his way into the USA, but President Trump is cracking down.

The Boeing 737 MAX 9 carried 46 passengers and six crew members. Deputies arrested Gomez-Alberto after arrival, with local booking charges including communicating false information of planned arson and terrorizing. The FAA confirms that the flight landed safely around 11:10 pm following a passenger disturbance and says it will investigate.

The immigration history has attracted considerable attention, and understandably so.

According to DHS, Gomez-Alberto was apprehended after entering the United States illegally in 2011 and again in 2014, and removed following both encounters. A third encounter with Border Patrol occurred near San Ysidro, California, in 2019.

An immigration judge ordered release from custody pending removal proceedings in January 2020. DHS now says an immigration judge subsequently granted asylum. The asylum claim cited fear of persecution for being transgender.

Immigration and Customs Enforcement has lodged a detainer with the local sheriff’s office, which is a a legal notice or hold placed on an individual or property to prevent their release or transfer.

Two deportations followed by another entry and an eventual asylum grant certainly warrant a closer look at the record. What information did the government have? What did the judge consider? Were there warning signs relevant to public safety? Was there anything more than this?

But the reported asylum grant is also an important part of that record. Describing the earlier illegal entries does not, by itself, establish the person’s immigration status at the time of this flight, not that I am trying to defend him here.

DHS has accused Gomez-Alberto of exploiting the asylum system. That may well be the case, but the subsequent alleged conduct aboard an airplane does not establish that the original persecution claim was fabricated…we have not seen the full immigration decision or the evidence considered.

Thus, we need those answers before declaring exactly where the system failed.

Meanwhile, imagine being aboard that flight. A claimed bomb and an alleged attempt to reach the pilots are about as frightening as an onboard disturbance gets.

I am thankful this flight reached New Orleans safely and that law enforcement took the suspect into custody. Whatever the eventual criminal findings or immigration consequences, the allegations concern conduct that could have endangered everyone onboard.

There is no basis here to conclude that United should have refused transportation simply because of his asylum history. If authorities had information that should have prevented boarding, that would be a separate and very important question…

I expect this to be counterproductively politicized, but I also think this person is very sick to engage in such an act.

That history deserves scrutiny, including a clear account of what officials knew and when. But at least he is in custody and I am grateful the aircraft landed safely and this frightening incident ended without a successful cockpit breach.
