This year’s El Niño is unlike any other on record

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

El Niño’s hallmark tongue of warm water extends thousands of kilometers from South America's Pacific coast. This map from November 14 shows the tongue’s average surface temperature was at least 3 degrees Celsius (dark red) above normal.

It was an unprecedented year for El Niño, the weather-changing ocean pattern defined by warmer-than-normal surface waters in the central and eastern equatorial Pacific. By October 18, the ocean area that researchers use to monitor El Niño’s severity climbed to an average temperature of over 30° Celsius (86° Fahrenheit) for the first time in history. From there, it kept on rising.

In September, the region’s daily average sea surface temperature reached the most extreme level ever recorded: more than 3.03 degrees Celsius above the 30-year average (dashed line). The World Meteorological Organization predicts that sea surface temperatures may peak at 3.7 degrees above that average by January. The two previous strongest El Niños on record emerged in 1997 and 2015.

All that hot water has fueled the most active Pacific hurricane season on record. By mid-October, 331 units of cyclone energy had accumulated, beating out 2018’s record of 318 units. It has also unleashed heat into the atmosphere, affecting distant circulation patterns and leading to an extraordinarily quiet Atlantic hurricane season.

2026’s El Niño will be felt well beyond the New Year. Precipitation and drought impacts are expected across the world through spring 2027. And the global heating effect could compound with human-caused climate warming to make 2027 the hottest year ever recorded.
