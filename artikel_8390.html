SHT Days 15, 16, 17: A New Hiking Partner and New Plan

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Today was a wonderful zero day. There was no hiking planned, though we did have a short list of things to do. We slow rolled it out of Lamb’s with a stop at the nearby bakery first. Baked treat and fancy coffee in hand, we started our drive back down to Duluth. We needed to retrieve my vehicle from the church parking lot. We will need it to shuttle ourselves to and from trailheads in the coming days. Bill was happy to have my company, as he just drove all the way up here yesterday by himself.

We ran a few errands together, then he dropped me off at the church and we began our separate drive back to Lambs. On our way back we made an ultra-important stop at Castle Danger Brewery, the one in Two Harbors. We spent a few chill hours there having a drink and food from the taco truck parked outside. Later this evening, we chilled by the fire back at Lamb’s to complete our day of relaxation.

After a much needed day of rest, today I got back after it. It was a beautiful day, and I had a treat in store for Bill. The iconic Bean and Bear Lakes are in the next section north of Penn Boulevard. Bill likes a good day hike, and if there’s one to do up here, this would be the one. He had the brilliant idea to go ahead and stash one vehicle at the Penn Boulevard trailhead last night on our way back from Duluth, as it was on our way to Lamb’s. We would make that our end point for today’s SOBO hike.

We started at the Tettegouche State Park trailhead, the one inside the park, and began our journey. The morning was cool, and we didn’t see any other hikers for the first few miles. We climbed up “the drainpipe”, a spot shown in videos of the past that is an incredibly steep rock scramble, but has been made much easier today with the addition of wooden steps.

Not the drainpipe, just another steep section with rock steps.

The day warmed up quickly, and I was just in pants and a t-shirt, though I could have easily worn shorts and been comfortable. Eventually we started seeing a ton of people. I mean hordes of hikers. I knew Bean and Bear Lake were probably very pretty, but today was a gorgeous fall Saturday, and the trees were at their autumn prime. Everybody and their mom was on trail today, and it was a shock to my system to see so many people after having the trail practically to myself for the past week.

Our first big views of this beautiful landscape.

Bill and I approached the main view of Bean and Bear Lakes (yes, very pretty!), squeezed in for a selfie, and departed. We hiked on to a less crowded area to sit down for a lunch break. I’m beginning to realize why the Penn Boulevard trailhead parking area is so large. This section of trail is incredibly popular for day hikers, and there are a handful of campsites in this section too.

Approaching the iconic Bean and Bear Lakes.

We continued on, constantly moving aside for oncoming traffic. Eventually we wound down to the trailhead where we were absolutely amazed to see that, not only had the parking lot filled up completely, but it had spilled out onto Penn Boulevard as well. Cars were lined up on both sides of the road as far as the eye could see. Wow! Good thing we parked our vehicle here last night!!

We hiked a little over 10 miles today. The terrain was surprisingly challenging for a section that so many people are willing to walk. To be fair, most people are only doing an out and back to Bean and Bear, which is probably more like 6 miles.

The plan today is for short mileage, then a bit of playtime later. We are on a vacation after all! Bill brought his golf clubs up here with the hopes of hitting some courses in good fall weather, and today is calling for just that.

Bill dropped me off at the Minnesota Highway 1 trailhead. He will pick me up at the Tettegouche trailhead we started at yesterday. Shortly after beginning my hike today, I find a puzzling sign before a three-way trail intersection. The sign says something like “SHT re-route” with an arrow. The arrow appears to point to the opposite side of the intersection, but there isn’t another sign on the other side confirming this. Furthermore, Avenza does not have the SHT heading that direction. At first, I do head down that path, but it’s severely downhill and I have reservations about this. Do I want to go downhill only to find out that this isn’t the right way and have to climb back out and backtrack? I vaguely remember something about a bridge being out across the river here, but I don’t remember if it’s been repaired or not.

I decide to just be safe and follow Avenza. At least this way I know I’ll make it! The trail follows the course of the Baptism River down to the Tettegouche State Wayside on Highway 61. The trail dips under the highway, then switches to pavement as it heads toward the Wayside, crosses the river on a bridge that cars drive on, and then basically follows the paved road back up the other side to the parking lot at the trailhead. Not the world’s most exciting 3.5 miles.

View from the bridge of the Baptism River emptying into Lake Superior.

My guess is that the trail bridge over the Baptism River had been repaired, but Avenza had not been updated to reflect this. It’s funny because the guidebook (or rather, databook) I’ve been using described the mileage between my two trailheads today as 1.4 miles. Yes, cutting across the river rather than going around it would definitely shorten the mileage. If I’m ever back in this area, I’m going to have to check out this bridge. It’s described as a swinging bridge over the highest falls entirely in Minnesota. I will definitely want to experience that. Did I just waste time on garbage miles this morning? Maybe. Nevertheless, I’m grateful I was healthy and able to enjoy a beautiful fall day outside.

Sometimes I see a tree that just begs to be photographed.

I found Bill at the trailhead and we drove to the local coffeeshop in Silver Bay. There’s not much in Silver Bay, but this coffeeshop was hopping and had delicious coffee and food. I highly recommend.

We continued on to the golf course in Silver Bay, where I played cheerleader to Bill and enjoyed the sunshine and gorgeous setting and weather conditions. I like to golf as well, but opted to just be a passenger today.  Today was essentially a zero, as the miles this morning were short and easy.

Beautiful weather was par for the course this weekend!

As I sat in the golf cart I came to the realization that my plan to complete the remaining mileage in the intended time frame was very aggressive. Especially considering I’ve already taken two zeros and tomorrow the weather is supposed to be terrible. If there was any chance of finishing, I would have needed to not take the two zeros I’ve already taken, and buckle down with serious mileage per day for the remainder, regardless of the weather. While this is definitely doable, I have my husband here, taking time away from his job, and I want him to enjoy his vacation as well. He’s willing to help me get this done, but I think the smart thing to do is to just intentionally slow down and pick a spot to stop and leave a small chunk of miles for some other time. In my opinion, even with a day pack 20+ mile days on the SHT is really ambitious. With this new plan I can hike shorter mileage and still sightsee and explore with Bill.

So, last year’s hike was derailed by an injury, and this year’s was derailed with intention. Who knows, pushing myself to the limits to finish might cause injury anyway. I am utterly not upset by this change. I still have miles to complete while I’m up here, and I’m going to make the best of it!
