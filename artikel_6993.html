Grok Users Turned My Selfie Into AI-Generated Sexual and Religious Abuse. Now, I’m Suing xAI Because of It

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

This is undeniably all of Elon Musk’s fault. So now I’m coming for him.

Content warning: This story contains religious slurs and details about sexual and religious abuse.

I was violated by a group of strangers who took turns stripping me of my hijab. It started with them exposing my hair and making hateful remarks about the hijab. Then it quickly escalated. They removed my clothes to expose my body. They wrote degrading words on me. They encouraged each other. My protests only made them do it more.

It started out as an otherwise happy moment for me. I was attending the historic inauguration of Mayor Zohran Mamdani and celebrating the first Muslim mayor in New York City’s history. It was an occasion being celebrated by not just New Yorkers but also fellow Americans and people around the world for the symbol of increasing visibility and inclusion that the moment represented for so many of us. I snapped a quick selfie in front of the stage with the inauguration banner billowing behind me.

In the selfie, I’m bundled up in a burgundy fur-trimmed coat, my hijab clinging to my head against the cold winter wind. I’m proudly smiling at the camera. Despite the cold, I’m so happy. It was this photo, uploaded to my X account, that users prompted Grok to regenerate in rapid succession in ways that violated my consent and religious autonomy. They used an AI chatbot to digitally take control of my body to inflict their abuse.

“I’ve always wondered what you all whackos look like without all that mess.”

I founded Muslim Girl, a digital media platform, as a teenager to reclaim our narratives and identities online. I wanted Muslim women to speak for ourselves, on our own terms, instead of allowing others to define us. I’ve dedicated my career to empowering women to take control of their own choices. But in a matter of moments, Grok robbed me of mine.

Elon Musk, the owner of xAI, essentially removed all the guardrails from Grok to compete in the AI race. By allowing Grok’s image generation feature to remove clothing and promote its “spicy” capabilities, Musk was banking on users using it for sexual purposes without any regard to the harms it would cause.

The person I was most afraid to tell was my dad. After my mom passed away a few years ago, he’s become way more protective of his only daughter, and for an old-school Arab Muslim immigrant, I didn’t know how he would react. He was furious. At first, I was afraid he was going to be mad at me for being in this situation since I have always been a vocally public woman online. But he understood very quickly that technology was being used to inflict the same abuse I had gotten used to online in more advanced and extreme ways.

Most of my elders don’t know the difference between AI-generated images and real photos, especially those who habitually forward chain messages in massive WhatsApp family group chats. My dad had to figure out a way to preemptively and sensitively explain to them what happened. Even though the images weren’t technically real, the humiliation of having to explain them to family and friends remained the same.

Coming from a religiously conservative background heightened the feeling of violation I experienced. The hijab is meant to be a woman’s choice. I’ve worn mine since I was 13 years old, so no public photos of me exist without me wearing it. I wear the hijab not out of heightened strictness or chastity, but rather as a guard against the male gaze in a hypersexualized society that commodifies women’s bodies and pressures us into how we should look or present ourselves.

Once the choice to cover or uncover is taken from a woman, it can easily become a tool of control and dominance over her body. In France, we’ve seen the proposal of a hijab ban that would fine women for covering, at the same time as we’ve watched countries like Afghanistan force women to cover. In all cases, the point is asserting power over women’s bodies.

Technology has created new ways to violate our autonomy while our current laws struggle to keep pace with protecting our rights in the AI era. Our bodies are still a battleground. Only now, the battleground is digital.

When the notifications started pouring in, I didn’t know what to do against the faceless mob that was using Grok to replicate and violate my identity in such shocking ways. I called the police, and when they came over, I had to endure the first humiliating experience of showing them the photos. They took a police report, said there was nothing they could do, and suggested that I make my account private. I refuse to diminish myself from the public at risk of having my basic rights and dignity violated online. None of us should have to.

That’s why I’m suing Elon Musk’s xAI. This isn’t just about my hijab or even about me. It’s about what we will and will not allow AI to do to us. I hope this becomes a landmark case that draws a line for the rights we are entitled to online. Even if it means standing up to the richest man on Earth.
