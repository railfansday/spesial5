Leonardo’s UK Loss Shows the Cost of Renting Hotels

Leonardo's UK hotels are making money from guests, but not enough to cover the rent. With costs rising and more rooms coming onto the market, the lease-heavy model that let hotel groups expand fast in the UK is starting to look like a liability.

Rising wages and payroll taxes are squeezing Leonardo Hotels' UK business, but the bigger problem is the rent on its hotels, according to accounts filed with Companies House, the UK company register.

On paper, the drop is steep. The hotel chain swung to a £55.6 million ($74 million) pre-tax loss in 2025 from a £50.1 million ($67 million) profit. But the 2024 profit included an £89.8 million ($119 million) one-off gain from revaluing its leases. Without it, 2024 would have shown a loss of about £40 million ($53 million), so the loss widened by about £16 million ($21 million) rather than appearing from nowhere.

The filing shows revenue at Leonardo Hotel Management (UK) Ltd was steady, rising 1.3% to £350.1 million ($466 million), with no new hotels opened. Gross profit fell 12% to £37.6 million ($50 million), and the gross margin slipped to 10.7% from 12.4%. Staff costs rose 6% to £102.5 million ($136 million), and employer social security costs rose 34%.

Leonardo Hotels' UK operating company swung to a £55.6 million ($74 million) pre-tax loss in 2025 from a £50.1 million ($67 million) profit, though the prior year included an £89.8 million ($119 million) one-off gain from revaluing leases — stripping that out, the loss widened by about £16 million ($21 million). Revenue was essentially flat at £350.1 million ($466 million) with no new hotels opened, while staff costs rose 6% and employer social security costs rose 34%, a complaint echoed across UK hospitality filings. The heavier bill is rent: Leonardo leases almost all of its UK hotels, carrying £1.43 billion ($1.9 billion) of lease liabilities, and the interest portion alone came to £78 million ($104 million), more than twice its £31.7 million ($42 million) of operating profit. Net liabilities reached about £167 million ($222 million), and the directors said the UK business will continue to be supported by parent Fattal Holdings. Ronen Nissenbaum, who runs the UK business, told Skift in July that Leonardo's 11 London hotels earn 15% to 20% more revenue per room than local rivals, which suggests that, in London at least, the problem is costs rather than guests. The same pressure is visible at Travelodge, where inflation-linked rent reviews — around 60% of them uncapped — helped cut pre-tax profit to £59.6 million ($79 million) from £96.9 million ($129 million), and both groups have started buying hotels outright. Whitbread is moving the other way, planning £1.5 billion ($2 billion) of sale-and-leasebacks to fund growth.
