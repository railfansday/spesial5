Chase Ink Business Unlimited Bonus: 75K Points Welcome Offer

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The Ink Business Unlimited® Credit Card is part of the Chase Ink Business card portfolio, and is one of the best no annual fee cards out there. It’s one of the rewarding Chase cards for everyday spending, and a great complement to some of Chase’s more premium cards.

In this post, I’d like to take a look at the Chase Ink Business Unlimited bonus, as it’s one of the best welcome offers you’ll find on any no annual fee card.

Link: Learn more about the Ink Business Unlimited® Credit Card with 75K bonus points

The Chase Ink Unlimited Card is offering a welcome bonus of 75,000 points after spending $6,000 within the first three months. You’ll see the Ink Unlimited bonus is marketed as offering cash back, rather than points, so it’s described as being worth $750. Indeed, you could redeem the rewards as cash back.

However, if you have the card in conjunction with a card earning Ultimate Rewards points, like the Chase Sapphire Reserve® Card (learn more) or Sapphire Reserve for Business℠ (learn more), then rewards can be transferred to Ultimate Rewards airline and hotel partners.

Based on my latest miles & points valuations, I consider Ultimate Rewards points to be worth 1.7 cents each, and that increases the value of the points by 70% right there, as far as I’m concerned. I consider the 75,000 points to be worth $1,275. That’s a great bonus, especially for a no annual fee card.

As you can see, the Chase Ink Unlimited publicly available welcome offer typically ranges from 75,000 to 100,000 points. The offer of 75,000 points is excellent, but is the standard offer. If you are willing to wait, we might see another offer of 90,000 points, or a return of the best-ever offer of 100,000 points.

However, the offer of 75,000 points is still great, and I’d decide whether to apply now or wait based on how much value you’d get from the card in the coming months. For a no annual fee card, the offer of 75,000 points is still industry leading.

When it comes to getting approved for the Chase Ink Unlimited Card and earning the welcome bonus, what rules should you be aware of? Well, there are the card specific policies, and then also the more generic Chase policies. When you pull up the application, you’ll see the following terms:

The new cardmember bonus may not be available to you if you have ever had this card or any other Chase for Business card without an annual fee. We may also consider factors pertinent to your business in determining your bonus eligibility.

As you can see, officially the Ink Unlimited bonus isn’t available to you if you’ve had this exact card, or another Chase business card without an annual fee, including the Ink Business Cash® Credit Card (learn more). So there is an element of cards being mutually exclusive.

However, the key here is that you “may” not be eligible, and not that you absolutely won’t be eligible. I don’t love that level of vagueness, though the good news is that if you apply for the card and aren’t eligible for the bonus, you should receive a pop-up message informing you of this, before there’s a hard pull. So that at least means the downside of applying is limited.

The good news is that eligibility for the bonus is completely unrelated to whether you have a Chase business card with an annual fee, like the Ink Business Preferred® Credit Card (learn more) or Sapphire Reserve for Business℠ (learn more).

It’s fine to apply for Chase business cards as a sole proprietorship, corporation, etc. If you apply as a sole proprietorship, you can just enter your Social Security Number in the section where you’d otherwise put your EIN.

There’s not a consistent credit score you need to get approved for the Chase Ink Unlimited, though I’d hope to have a score in the “good” to “excellent” range, ideally 700+ points. However, people with lower scores have been approved, and people with higher scores have been rejected, as it’s one of only many factors that impacts card eligibility.

The Chase Ink Unlimited Card has no annual fee, and there are several reasons to consider picking it up. The way I view it, there are a few points that are most important.

For one, the card has a solid rewards structure, as it offers a minimum of 1.5x points on all spending, making it one of the best Chase cards for everyday spending (the card does have foreign transaction fees, though, so I’d only recommend using it for US purchases). Furthermore, the card offers primary rental car coverage, which can come in handy if you rent cars with any frequency, and is incredibly rare for a no annual fee card.

Also, since the card has no annual fee, there’s really no opportunity cost to holding onto it, and it allows you to create a great portfolio of cards to maximize rewards.

The Chase Ink Unlimited Card is one of the most lucrative no annual fee cards out there, and it’s currently offering a welcome bonus of 75,000 points upon completing minimum spending.

Not only does the card have a great bonus that’s too good to turn down, but it offers rewards that can make it worth holding onto, like 1.5x points on everyday spending, plus rental car coverage. If you’re eligible for this card, I think there’s a lot of merit to picking it up. Taking advantage of this bonus is a no-brainer, if eligible.
