Malaysia Airlines Orders Up To 16 Boeing 787-10s, Deliveries Start 2032

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Malaysia Airlines has just placed a new aircraft order, as the airline will be adding a new wide body aircraft type to its fleet. This seems logical enough, even if the carrier’s long haul fleet seems rather diversified at this point.

Malaysia Airlines has placed its first-ever Dreamliner order, as the airline will be taking delivery of up to 16 Boeing 787-10s. This includes a firm order for 10 jets, plus options for six more, with deliveries expected between 2032 and 2035.

For context, the 787-10 is the largest variant of the Dreamliner. While it has the least range of any 787 variant, the per-seat economics are great, given how it’s “stretched.” While some airlines have started to use the 787-10 for high capacity regional routes, Malaysia Airlines is taking a different approach.

The oneworld carrier intends to use these planes to support its long haul fleet modernization and international expansion, including routes to Europe, along with destinations throughout Asia-Pacific. The airline points out how long haul demand to and from Southeast Asia has increased by more than 50% over the past decade.

Here’s how Malaysia Airlines Group CEO Nasaruddin Bakar describes this:

“The acquisition of the 787 Dreamliner represents a strategic investment in the future of Malaysia Aviation Group. As we look ahead to the next phase of growth, these aircraft will strengthen our ability to serve key international markets with greater capacity, efficiency and flexibility, while delivering the elevated travel experience our customers expect. Beyond fleet renewal, this investment reinforces our commitment to maintaining a modern, competitive airline that supports Malaysia’s connectivity and global aspirations.”

A few things stand out here. First of all, it’s cool to see Malaysia Airlines growing again, after a rather rough… what, nearly 15 years? At this point the airline has enough planes on order to both replace its previous generation fleet, and expand. I’d expect the 16 A330ceos will be retired at some point in the coming years, and more than replaced by the A330neos that remain on order. Furthermore, the 737-800s will all be replaced by the 737 MAXs.

The 787-10 is an incredible plane in terms of economics, especially as Boeing has been able to make incremental range improvements, turning it into a true long haul aircraft.

At the same time, I can’t help but feel like it’s unusual how the airline has just seven A350s, with no more on order, and is now switching to the 787 for fleet renewal. Admittedly the A350 was ordered back in the day under a completely different management team, which also at one point thought the A380 was a good fit for the airline.

So I am curious how the carrier’s fleet evolves. Will the seven A350s stick around, or will they eventually just be replaced by 787-10s? Even with airspace restrictions and headwinds, it seems like all of Malaysia Airlines’ routes should be within range for the 787-10, though admittedly London (LHR) might be on the cusp, depending on how airspace closures evolve, how dense of a configuration the airline selects, etc.

The big challenge with the airline industry is that it’s never static, and decisions aren’t made in a vacuum. One management team makes one decision, and then a management team later on can’t just undo what the other management team did, without it being costly. So I get that they’re doing what they can…

Malaysia Airlines is the newest customer for the Boeing 787, as the airline has ordered up to 16 787-10s, including 10 firm orders and six options. The airline plans to use this for a long haul expansion to Europe, as well as for flights in Asia-Pacific.

It’s cool to see Malaysia Airlines continuing to grow, as the carrier has quite the order book at this point. I am curious if the airline keeps around the seven A350s in the long run, or how the airline manages its three wide body aircraft types.
