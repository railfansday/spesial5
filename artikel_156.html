Guinea orders residents to clean up the capital months after deadly rubbish dump collapse

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Dozens of people were killed in August after a rubbish landfill collapsed in Conakry

Guinea has ordered residents to do a mass clean-up of the capital, Conakry, months after a rubbish landfill collapsed, killing dozens of people.

Prime Minister Amadou Oury Bah on Wednesday announced a deadline of 31 December to clear rubbish in the city and its suburbs, which has seen an increase in illegal dumping in recent months.

In August heavy rains caused a landslide at the capital's main waste dump, with the rubbish collapsing onto homes killing 31 people and injuring others.

The site in the city's Dar es Salaam area was subsequently closed permanently, which has led to piles of rubbish accumulating outside homes and on the streets.

At a public forum on sanitation, Sanitation Minister Aboubacar Camara said Conakry was generating about 3,000 tonnes of waste every day.

He said much of the garbage was ending up in the streets because of weaknesses in the collection system.

The prime minister called on local authorities to become more involved in organising waste collection, saying this could become a way to create jobs.

"The state will do its part, but the municipalities must also organise themselves to ensure waste collection and provide jobs for young people," the prime minister said.

"Conakry must be completely clean by the end of December," he added, relaying a directive from the country's president, Mamady Doumbouya.

He cited measures the government was taking to improve waste collection and recycling, including a national sanitation fund that would guarantee financing for the sector.

Camara said talks had been finalised with a waste collection operator, with a plan for equipment to move waste from transit and sorting centres to a new disposal site in BaretodÃ©, outside the capital.

The August tragedy at the Dar es Salaam landfill was not the first - 10 people were killed at the same site in 2017.

The government had ordered people living close to the site to move, but many residents chose to stay.

According to the prime minister, the government now plans to transform the closed waste site into an urban park.
