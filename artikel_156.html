Burnham urges Badenoch to withdraw ‘crass’ comment defending disability cuts on grounds ‘amputees run marathons’ – UK politics live

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

PM and leader of the opposition clashed at PMQs over welfare

Badenoch says that reply makes it sound like Burnham is not planning cuts. He says young people cannot wait 10 years. With Burnham, everything takes 10 years, she says.

She says working-age welfare is set to rise.

Keir Starmer could not make welfare cuts because MPs now in Burnham’s cabinet led a rebellion against it. That shows they are the welfare party.

Burnham says Badenoch only ever asks for cuts.

He says Helen Whately, the shadow DWP secretary, said hundreds of thousands of disabled people would lose benefits under Tory plans.

Andy Burnham has said that Kemi Badenoch should withdraw her “crass and insensitive” comment justifying cuts to disability benefits on the grounds that “double amputees run marathons”.

After Badenoch used this line in a speech last week, the Paralympian Richard Whitehead posted a message on Facebook criticising her. He said:

double quotation markDisability should never be judged through isolated examples of individual achievement, but through an understanding of the barriers people and families face every day.

The fact that some double-leg amputees can run marathons does not mean that disability support is unnecessary. Equally, the fact that some people with ADHD thrive despite limited support does not mean that others do not need meaningful assistance.

In subsequent remarks, Whitehead said that Badenoch’s comments were “divisive” and that he was “gobsmacked that somebody’s sporting success would be weaponised in this way”.

At PMQs today, the Labour MP Charlotte Nichols asked Burnham whether he agreed with Whitehead that “the achievements of our sports men and women with disabilities should never be weaponised against others”.

double quotation markI thought the comments by Richard Whitehead do deserve a response from the right honourable lady [Badenoch]. They were crass and insensitive, and I think she should withdraw them.

Whitehead has run more than 100 marathons, set world records and won four Paralympic medals.

Guardian readers probably don’t need much persuading that PMQs is a terrible forum for reasonable debate, and there was a good example today that shows why. In his final response to Kemi Badenoch, Burnham accused her of adopting a “patronising tone”. This was uncharacteristically personal, and it lit up on social media as about the most interesting line from the exchange, with some commentators implying it was a gaffe. In reality, of course, it was probably one of the least contentious things Burnham said. Badenoch is patronising, particularly at PMQs. If there were a world championships for being patronising, she would be leading the UK team. Yet Burnham will probably conclude that it was a mistake, and something he shouldn’t have said.

Why? Because it made him look a bit rattled. Burnham came into office determined to pursue “problem solving, not point scoring”, but increasingly that is looking like a lost cause and the centrifugal power of PMQs yanks him back into a slanging match. (He is not always like this with opposition backbenchers, but he is – inevitably – with Badenoch.) Today, speaking to the Tory leader, he seemed to devote more time than ever to attacking her party’s record, rather than seeking common ground. On the plus side, the points he was making about the Conservatives’ record, and about her policy agenda now consisting of little more than welfare cuts, were forceful. On the negative side, he sounded like someone on the defensive.

Just as she did last week, Badenoch was reasonably good at delivering the sort of broad-brush ‘You’re rubbish’ messaging that would work as a headline for a Daily Express op ed. But it was hard to discern what particular attack line she wanted to land. There was a time when she would arrive at PMQs with a pre-prepared gotcha question and afterwards, normally following Keir Starmer’s refusal to answer, CCHQ would send out a press release highlighting the alleged implications of the question-dodging. There was none of that today, or last week.

One of Badenoch’s most interesting claims was that Burnham is just offering the “vibes” of welfare reform, not the real thing. But that claim is so wide of the mark it is unlikely to work even with a Tory audience. The Milburn plan may have its drawbacks, but it is absurd to dismiss it as just vibes.

Sky News also set Badenoch up for another attack, highlighting Alan Milburn saying yesterday that some people would lose from the reforms, while Burnham said in a separate interview he did not want anyone to lose out. It was a classic case of apples and pears; Milburn was saying people will lose out from the benefit cuts, while Burnham was saying that, because those cuts are part of a strategy intended to divert off benefits into work, in the long run they wouldn’t lose out. Mindful, perhaps, of the ‘if you’re explaining, you’re losing’ theory of political communications, Burnham did not want to clarify this, and he just sidestepped her question. Badenoch then did not pursue the point, which was strange because a follow-up question could have made it much more awkward for him.

Layla Moran (Lib Dem) asks if the government will act to stop non-disclosure agreements being used by rich and powerful people to silence vulnerable victims.

Burnham says there was action in the Employment Rights Act to tackle this, but the government could go further, he says. He says they will look to see if this can be extended to freelancers.

Ayoub Khan (Ind) asks about someone who posts online as Habibi, but who has been identifed “as Ben Tait, who has deep connections in the House of Lords”. He says this person has smeared MPs and peers, and incited religious hatred. But he has been praised by a shadow justice minister, he says.

Burnham says he does not know the details. This sounds like an issue for the Tories, he says. But he says he will arrange a meeting for Khan about this.

Andrew George (Lib Dem) says the budget will reward second home owners who let their homes. They will get a tax benefit. This is public money that could be better spent helping people in housing need.

And he says he was disappointed his private member’s bill on this did not get government support.

Burnham says he asked officials to look seriously at the bill. “It was felt that it wasn’t the best way to address the issues, but we don’t disagree with what he’s trying to do,” he says. He says he will arrange for the housing minister to meet George.

Shivani Raja (Con) says Leicestershire police has been “issuing dual identity warrant cards for gender fluid officers”. She says that is absurd, and she urges the government to stop this.

Burnham says the government has been “relentless in tackling violence against women and girls in every single respect”. He says he is not aware of this issue, but will look into it.

Martin Vickers (Con) asks if Scunthorpe will be a priority for the strategic sites accelerator programme?

Burnham says he would encourage the area to put in an application.

Andrew Ranger (Lab) asks about the flytipping problem in Wrexham. Will the PM urge the Welsh government to give Wrexham council more powers to tackle this?

Burnham says he is encouraging the Welsh government to devolve powers further down.

Caroline Voaden (Lib Dem) says fishermen in Brixham can barely afford to go out because fuel costs are so high. Will the government help them?

Burnham says the government has acted on fuel duty. But there is probably more to be done, he says.

Charlie Dewhirst (Con) asks if the government will prioritise British jobs when investing in military satellite capability.

Burnham says he cannot comment because that is a question about a live procurement decision.

Gill German (Lab) asks the government to invest more in the rail system in North Wales.

Burnham says he thinks the rail system in North Wales “does need to be better”.

Dave Doogan, the SNP leader at Westminster, says Brexit has been a disaster. But he claims Burnham is being queasy about moving away from Keir Starmer’s red lines. When will he commit to rejoining the EU?

Burnham says he is leading on Europe. But he is also sticking to what he promised at the Makerfield byelection.

Ed Davey, the Lib Dem leader, says the Vote Leave zombies are back in force. They are trying to scare people with a new made-up number. But people can now see the true Brexit horror. Will the PM get a new deal?

Burnham says the zombies have been shouting at him. The Tories don’t like it when he says their Brexit deal did not work. Exports are down as a result. So there is a deal to be had. He says he would like to get cross-party support for a deal.

Davey urges Burnham to ditch the trade and cooperation agreement, and to negotiate an entirely new trade deal.

He asks about Nvidia, and says Donald Trump sold share in it before news of the Jensen Huang scandal broke. That is fresh evidence of corruption, he says.

Burnham says, when Trump is meeting President Xi in China tomorrow, he will be in Berlin for a meeting of the coalition of the willing. He says he is glad the Commons is united in standing up to Ukraine.

double quotation markWell, I think we heard from some of those zombies just before. They were shouting at me. But the truth is, they don’t like it when we say the truth in this House.

The truth is, the Brexit deal that they negotiated wasn’t good enough for Britain. It’s just a fact, and the vast majority of the public know that.

It’s really important that we face up to that. They burned bridges with our largest market, old friends and neighbours. They just destroyed all of those relationships.

Exports are down from their constituencies, and they know that. They know that as well.

So there is a deal in front of us on Friday. I am looking for support across the floor of this House for what we are trying to negotiate.

He accuses Badenoch of asking self-serving questions and adopting a “patronising tone”.

Burnham says under the Tories they had years of no growth. The deal with the EU will be good for growth, cutting red tape, cutting the cost of exporting, and bringing food prices down.

Families are struggling. The Tories used to back cutting red tape. But the Tories cannot stand up for this because they are split, he says.
