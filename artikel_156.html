Trump turns White House press conference into a 2028 succession audition

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The president appeared to put Vance and Rubio through their paces as potential successors, though Stephen Miller unexpectedly stole the spotlight

“Which of you shall we say doth love us most?”

The ageing King Lear’s question to his daughters, expecting them to compete for his favour and carve up his kingdom, hovered in the White House press briefing room on Friday.

There at the lectern stood Donald “Lear” Trump. Behind him stood his potential heirs, JD “Goneril” Vance and Marco “Regan” Rubio, and to the side sat Trump’s favourite offspring, Stephen “Cordelia” Miller (more of him later). As for Lear’s Fool, the choices were endless.

Ostensibly this was a press conference to trumpet the president’s trip in Asia, but in practice everyone knew it was the first audition for the 2028 presidential election (and boy, is Trump going to enjoy turning the race into a contest for his approval). Vance is widely expected to seek the crown, though it was Rubio who came out on top in the latest Emerson College national poll.

In a jam-packed room, Trump stood centre stage as if hosting The Apprentice all over again. Behind him were the two contenders expressing their aspirations through matching suits, matching Maga red ties, matching US flag pins and matching leather shoes.

The first trial: could Vance and Rubio stand still for 90 minutes without rolling their eyes, yawning or silently mouthing “Kill me!” to the press? Could they force Trump’s word salad down their throats and look as if they were enjoying it? Even when the boss was rambling at length about potholes?

“When you fix a pothole”, began one detour by the octogenarian president. “I don’t want to bore you – they’ll say, oh, he rambled, he rambled terribly. I don’t ramble. I don’t ramble at all, but when you fix a pothole, you have brand new asphalt against old white asphalt that doesn’t look good, but at least you don’t ruin your car, but these roads were flawless …”

But first, the whole thing got off to a strange start. Trump appeared at the podium and remarked: “That’s a big, big a crowd. I’ve never seen this before,” but reporters crammed in the room could barely hear him above the whoosh of an air conditioning unit. An engineer tweaked the sound, but it only gave Trump’s voice an eerie echo.

Had the gremlins that grounded flights and frozen banking sites finally reached the White House? So much for America the hi-tech superpower. Eventually order was restored, though not necessarily in Trump’s brain.

He was evidently flattered by the media comparing his trilateral summit with the leaders of China and Russia to the Yalta meeting between Winston Churchill, Franklin Roosevelt and Joseph Stalin (he did not say which of the three he identified with, though critics could take a guess). Like a gushing teenager, Trump was so thrilled that Chinese president Xi Jinping had come to the airport to greet him, that he mentioned it twice (“He didn’t meet Sleepy Joe with the plane, that I can tell you.”)

The US, Russia and China hold 92% of global nuclear power, he noted, which gave him an excuse to bring up his uncle John Trump, an electrical engineer and former Massachusetts Institute of Technology professor. “A member of my family spent his life at MIT who they say is now the greatest professor in the history of MIT, so I have great understanding of- I guess maybe genetically I’m blessed,” Trump riffed.

At that remark Rubio could resist no longer. A smile spread across his lips and he shot a look at Vance, who felt obliged to smile back. We’re not laughing at you, we’re laughing with you, Mr President.

Indeed, Rubio had been the more fidgety of the pair. As Trump chuntered on, the secretary of state had shifted from foot to foot, fiddled with his tie, played with his lower lip, touched up his hair and given the odd wry smile. Vance, meanwhile, hid behind his beard and miasma of smugness.

But then, at last, came the moment for Lear’s daughters to make their public declarations of love. Vance went up the lectern and pointed out that he had stayed in Washington rather than joining the trip, comparing himself to Macaulay Culkin’s character in Home Alone – a child who is accidentally left behind at Christmas as his family rushes to catch a flight to Paris. “I missed you, sir,” he said to Trump, before praising the president for “securing historic trade deals” and reorganising the global economy to benefit the American worker and economy.

Not bad, but a little wonky. Marco, you’re up next. The secretary of state took aim at the critics who accused Trump of cosying up to dictators at the expense of Democrats, raising his eyebrows and shaking his fist: “These three countries possess the overwhelming majority of nuclear weapons on this planet. The idea that these three leaders should not engage on a regular basis is not just absurd, it’s dangerous.”

Trump began taking reporters’ questions. One young whippersnapper posed a question that will be asked many times over the next two years. “Because you have the vice-president, the secretary of state right behind you, I’m curious which of them do you think would make a better president after you?”

Trump swatted it aside without answering. Vance remained stony faced. Rubio smiled knowingly.

The same reporter put Vance in an uncomfortable spot by asking about his post-midterm tweet. “Would you argue that the war in Iran was worth it to lose the Senate?” The vice-president agreed with the goal of Iran not getting a nuclear weapon but added carefully: “I think the president is the one who makes the decision and the president has said he would do it again. And my view is that my goal is and my job is to support the president of the United States.”

In other words, I hate this war and would never have started it but I have to back the boss. Expect a lot more of this needle-threading in the upcoming campaign.

Later, when Trump asked “Do you want to keep this going or not” amid a cacophony of shouted questions, he turned around to look at Rubio, who laughed, then at Vance, who did likewise with folded arms. The two rivals patted each other playfully. But neither was prepared for the man who would steal their thunder.

“We have the most powerful man in the world in the room right now,” said Trump, referring to Miller, the recently appointed White House chief of ideological zealotry, who stepped up to the lectern with bald pate gleaming under the bright lights.

“I just wanted to say that what President Trump has achieved over the last two years is more than any president has achieved by far in American history!” Miller roared, before offering the kind of vainglorious soliloquy that would make even a Kremlin or Chinese Communist party spokesperson blush. “What you’re going to see over the next two years, as astonishing as that has been under his leadership, the next two years are going to be the greatest two years in American history!”

Trump, who loves nothing more than flattery with exclamation marks, returned to the lectern and announced: “We have breaking news. We have breaking news.” Waving his hands, then with thumbs pointed at Vance and Rubio, he said of Miller: “He was better than these two guys. Do you want to run for president?”

That was a joke, right? Trump was stirring the succession pot again, making his potential heirs uncomfortable. It was fitting that he ended the press conference reminiscing about the night he hosted Ultimate Fighting Championship bouts on the White House South Lawn. He is going to relish every moment of presiding over a Republican cage match for the next two years.
