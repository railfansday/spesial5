Investing - Theory, News &amp; General • Re: [2026 Boglehead] Conference experiences?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Let me add my thoughts on the conference. I always review things from the "new investor" perspective, meaning for those who may not be familiar with the concepts at-hand.

I thought that there was a bit too much emphasis on "TIPS vs. bonds" - it was constantly mentioned throughout the conference. Mike Piper summed it up nicely - as long as you get your asset allocation right, it really doesn't matter all that much. I realize that the conference needs to cover both new and experienced investors, so perhaps the balance was tilted towards experienced investors (pun intended).

Paul Merriman's presentation (with Jim Dahle) on the "Tilting vs. total market" had a number of charts showing historical performance. The "S&P 500 vs. Value/Growth" smackdown was screaming at me to simply link to the wiki: Callan periodic table of investment returns New investors should read this article. It's all you need to know.

For next year, I would suggest those technical "deep-dives" start with a simple example and summary, then dive into the weeds for those who want more.

In one of the presentations, the concept of a "Financial Power of Attorney" was mentioned. POAs are not titled as "Financial", as you can do so much more. For example, I used a POA for both my mom and husband to admit them to hospitals.

As to the point of using one type of POA over the other ("Durable" vs. "Springing"), that decision needs to be made on a case-by-case basis in consultation with an attorney. Regardless of the type, the person chosen to be POA must be absolutely trusted. I have personal experience with both types.

-------------------
I don't like giving poor reviews, as the presenters are experts in their fields. However, Sunday's last session "Money and Memory" with Christine Moriarty got me going. There was absolutely no need to discuss the medical aspects on the relationship between medical conditions and dementia. To me, throwing up a bunch of medical conditions and claiming they all had a part in dementia was over-the-top. From personal experience, I disagreed with a number of those claims. There may be some relationship, but certainly not in the manner presented. All it did was get the audience emotionally hyped thinking that dementia is pervasive and everywhere. No. Please let the medical experts present these aspects. Medical discussions have no place in a financial conference.

Otherwise, the key point of recognizing dementia early-on is a good one. I also thought that this presentation somewhat duplicated earlier ones.
