94-Year-Old Becomes Oldest Person To Hike the Grand Canyon Rim-to-Rim

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Until he turned 94, Ernest “Ernie” Scheuer had only ever seen the Grand Canyon from the window of an airplane. When he finally visited the park in person, the retired Los Angeles mathematician decided to walk all the way across it.

Guinness World Records has officially recognized Scheuer, who completed the trek on October 11, 2025 at 94 years and 75 days old, as the oldest man to cross the Grand Canyon from rim to rim on foot. He broke the previous mark held by Spain’s Alfredo Aliaga Burdio, who completed the crossing at age 92 in 2023.

Scheuer’s route began on the North Rim and descended into the canyon along the North Kaibab Trail before climbing out to the South Rim via the Bright Angel Trail. In total, the journey covered nearly 21 miles and more than 10,000 feet of combined elevation change.

The view of the Grand Canyon while on the Rim-to-Rim hike. Photo via Katie Jackson

Accompanied by a group of longtime hiking friends, his grandson from Virginia, and a cinematographer documenting the trip, Scheuer completed the crossing in 33 hours and 45 minutes. Partway through the attempt, another friend, Lanny Deremer, hiked down from the South Rim in the middle of the night to meet the team on the trail. The group camped overnight at Bright Angel Campground and logged roughly 15 hours of actual moving time.

“It was not a Rosie Ruiz event,” Scheuer told Reed College, his alma mater, referencing the runner who infamously faked her finish in the 1980 Boston Marathon. “I really did it.”

The idea for the record attempt started on a hike when one of Scheuer’s friends mentioned Burdio’s record. Born in July 1930, Scheuer had never tackled an overnight hike of that scale, though he stays active by hiking in his local mountains once or twice a week.

“The hike is straight-forward,” Scheuer told Guinness World Records. “There are some steep segments both going down and coming up. The trail is a bit rocky, not entirely smooth, it’s important to watch one’s step. I moved slowly much of the time, but I followed my motto ‘one foot in front of the other’ and completed the hike.”

While climbing the final uphill miles out of the canyon on the Bright Angel Trail, Scheuer said he felt a second wind rather than exhaustion.

“I was perfectly fine when we reached the end of the hike,” he said. “I think that I was energized doing the last few uphill miles, knowing that I was going to accomplish my goal”.

After submitting photographs, video footage, and witness statements to verify the crossing, Scheuer received official confirmation of the title, which Guinness World Records publicized in November 2026. Next on his itinerary is a trip to Berlin, where he plans to meet Burdio, the 92-year-old record holder whose hike inspired his own.

Footage from the hike is featured on Youtube, which documents the successful attempt.
