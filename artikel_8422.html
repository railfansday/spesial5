Apply for nonprofit adoption grants and interest-free loans once your home study, a social worker’s review of your household, is approved, and before you put fees on a credit card or take out a personal loan. Some grant funds won’t repay a fee you’ve already paid, and several stop accepting applications once your child moves in.

Helpusadopt.org, for one, applies each grant to an adoption’s final fees and won’t reimburse fees you’ve already paid, even with a card or loan. Charge the last $10,000 to a card, and a $10,000 grant has nothing left to cover.

Helpusadopt.org, Show Hope, and Lifesong all need your application before your child moves in. Apply to several at once, since Helpusadopt.org still considers families who have won another grant.

Keep saving and fundraising while you wait, since Gift of Adoption looks for families who have saved and raised what they can. Raise money through a nonprofit’s page, such as Lifesong’s, rather than GoFundMe, so donors get a receipt. Then borrow only what’s left, on a monthly payment you’ve budgeted for.

Say a $5,000 grant and a $7,000 interest-free loan cover a $12,000 gap, and you repay the loan over four years. That’s $146 a month, $7,000 in all. Put all $12,000 on a card at 22% instead, near the average the Federal Reserve reports for cards that charge interest. Paying it off over the same four years takes $378 a month, about $18,150 in all.

Before your home study is approved, gather your latest tax return, two months of pay stubs, and a list of your assets and debts, and line up two references. Then your first applications can go out the week the approval arrives.
