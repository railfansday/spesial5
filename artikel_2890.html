2 Reasons Anthropic’s IPO Is a Bigger Risk to the Stock Market Than SpaceX’s

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Anthropic’s IPO is a market-disrupting event, but it could also create opportunities in the long run.

By Daniel Foelber – Nov 23, 2026 at 11:15AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Anthropic ›

Anthropic (ANT -1.73%) jumped 17.1% on Nov. 19 -- surpassing Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +6.91%) as the largest initial public offering (IPO) in history after raising roughly $81.3 billion. However, SpaceX remains far more valuable, with a market cap of $1.88 trillion compared to $1.27 trillion for Anthropic.

Here's why Anthropic could have an even larger impact on the broader stock market than SpaceX and how the IPO is impacting investors even if they don't plan to buy Anthropic directly.

Dario Amodei. Image source: Getty Images.

The Nasdaq-100 is the 100 largest non-financial companies listed on the Nasdaq exchange by market cap. SpaceX joined the Nasdaq-100 less than one month after going public thanks to the index's new fast-tracked rules that allow mega-cap companies to be added to the Nasdaq-100 after their 15th trading day. However, the S&P 500 (^GSPC +4.85%) still requires companies to be public for at least a year before they can be added.

Anthropic is likely to be added to the Nasdaq-100 in early December, which will impact index-linked products like the Invesco QQQ Trust (QQQ +7.16%). Several passively managed, high-profile exchange-traded funds (ETFs), including nine Vanguard ETFs, began buying SpaceX within weeks of its IPO. And I would expect the same for Anthropic.

However, Anthropic's impact on the major indexes could be even larger than SpaceX's if it's classified as a tech stock.

The tech sector makes up a staggering 64.4% of the Nasdaq-100 -- as measured by the Invesco QQQ Trust -- and 37.4% of the S&P 500. Despite it being a tech-focused company, Vanguard classified SpaceX in the communications sector alongside Alphabet (GOOG +6.58%) (GOOGL +6.92%) and Meta Platforms (META +6.26%). Similarly, Amazon (AMZN +4.00%) and Tesla (TSLA +12.71%) are in the consumer discretionary sector despite being tech-focused companies.

If Anthropic is classified as a technology stock, it will make the Nasdaq-100 (and eventually the S&P 500) even more concentrated in the tech sector. It would also have far-reaching effects on market-cap-weighted tech sector ETFs -- which are larger than other sector ETFs. For example, the Vanguard Communications Services ETF (VOX +3.84%), which has added to its SpaceX holdings every month since it went public, has $5.4 billion in net assets, whereas the Vanguard Information Technology ETF (VGT +9.06%) has a staggering $145.1 billion in net assets. And that's just one investment management company's ETFs.

Anthropic plays a far more important role in the artificial intelligence (AI) value chain than SpaceX. SpaceX owns xAI and has plans to build constellations of orbital data centers. But for now, most of its revenue comes from its Starlink network of broadband and mobile satellites. Anthropic arguably makes the most important family of large language models -- Claude.

Anthropic is driving massive demand for compute capacity. It is a big reason why hyperscalers are aggressively investing in AI data centers, with ripple effects for chip companies and data center pick-and-shovel plays such as specialty machinery companies, industrials, utilities, and energy. In addition to Anthropic’s own demand for compute, many third-party AI agents and AI tools rely on foundational models like Claude, thereby amplifying the full impact on data center demand.

Anthropic is much more important to the market than its individual weighting will be in the major indexes. It's a similar dynamic to what we've seen play out with Nvidia (NVDA +32.52%). Jensen Huang's sudden resignation sparked a massive sell-off in companies that depend on or do business with Nvidia. And beyond that, Huang has been a major proponent of AI innovation and dealmaking, impacting financial markets far beyond Nvidia's own deals.

So just as Nvidia is arguably the most important company in the AI infrastructure layer of the value chain, Anthropic is probably the most vital company in the foundational model layer.

With the market in a steep sell-off, some investors may view the Anthropic IPO as yet another factor that could add to volatility. And while the Anthropic IPO is arguably an even bigger risk to the market than the SpaceX IPO, it also has the potential to be an incredible opportunity.

If Anthropic can continue delivering excellent revenue growth, it could be a catalyst for the broader tech sector and indexes. Especially if that growth coincides with more demand for even better AI chips, which would benefit the broader semiconductor industry.

Investors interested in gaining exposure to Anthropic without buying the stock directly may want to consider a low-cost tech sector ETF like the Vanguard Information Technology ETF or the Vanguard Growth ETF (VUG +7.05%), which invests in growth stocks from all stock market sectors. These ETFs will likely gradually accumulate a larger stake in Anthropic as more shares are unlocked and sold by insiders and early investors on the Nasdaq.

On the other hand, investors looking to avoid Anthropic altogether may want to stick with high-conviction individual stocks or value and non-tech sector ETFs.
