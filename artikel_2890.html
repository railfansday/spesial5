Could there be an award for Al Horford?

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The number 40 in the NBA almost always refers to points scored and never a player's age. Al Horford was born about three months before the Nintendo Entertainment System (NES) was sold nationwide in the United States. His birthplace being the Dominican Republic makes this an imperfect example, but Dude Is Old. And 40 is rarity. But how rare?

Not only is he just the 13th player to play a season at 40, he's 1 of just 5 centers age 40+ to get at least 2 starts in a season .

That is certainly some impressive company. I know that Horford is typically compared against LeBron these days because of their forties status (careful: it's a Facebook link!), but let's account for Horford's shorter height relative to most centers to rope in some forwards:

But, to be clear, to go with the first, better list, Al Horford is 1 of 5 centers in NBA history (post-merger) to start at least 2 games in a season at the age of 40 or older. Or, 1 of only 10 forward-center players 40+ to start 2+ games in a season. Is it an all-time "No way!" stat? No, but it's a really surprising result.

We're nearing the quarter mark of the season, so, he's unlikely to get that games started number up much more at this pace, especially as the Dubs get healthy and make a trade for a center. However, if he does get to, say, 8 starts, that's a really small group of players he'll share a stat with, and a notable one.

As Dalton Johnson has been reporting for NBC Sports Bay Area, not only is he now the oldest to ever play for Golden State, he's shocked the league with his 20-year career.

Mike Budenholzer coached Horford his final three seasons with the Atlanta Hawks from 2013 to 2016. Those were Horford's seventh, eighth and ninth NBA seasons. The thought of Horford ever reaching 20 seasons is instantly met with a "no" from Budenholzer.

[…] "In some ways I can because he takes such great care of himself," Budenholzer says. "He is so meticulous with his body and his nutrition and diet and such a pro. […] I'm just happy for him. He's just one of the all-time great guys."

Credit to Horford for still having that fire and putting in the work. The aging curve takes pro athletes from attaining peak performance with ease to a struggle to maintain 80% of that peak. Of course, it's not just keeping the body firm. He crested the wave. In the same Johnson report, he quotes Harford's adaptation to the evolving game: picking up a three-point shot. It was great fun seeing him block and shot and hit a three against Boston the other night.

So… when this season ends, the Warriors should consider some kind of award for Al Horford. No, I did not come up with the title of this post first and work backwards. When he had his first start back on the 11th, I thought about how unusual it was to see a 40-year old in a starting NBA lineup.

Of course, he already has several awards. A 5-time All-Star, an All-NBA team, an All-Rookie team, and All-Defensive team… and, of course, a potential Hall of Fame induction. But the Warriors signed him to a 2-year deal specifically because of what his age brings to the table. There's a perseverance and a dignity to his contributions. The team doesn't have to do this, but maybe The Horford is something the various outlets that cover the team could consider. After review, this could just wind up being the Best Old Guy Award, but there is something to be said about a Best Veteran type of accolade.
