Winners and Losers from the Broncos’ 38-24 victory over the Raiders

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

In honor of the late and great Tom Jackson, the Denver Broncos defeated the Las Vegas Raiders at home, infront of the Mile High Crowd.

Now, the game shouldn't have been as close as it was at times, but the Broncos won a rare two-score game and improved their record to 8-2 and retook the lead in the AFC West. Rookie Jonah Coleman had three rushing touchdowns and topped 100 yards on the ground, and Ja'Quan McMillian had the game sealing pick 6 of rookie quarterback Fernando Mendoza late in the 4th quarter.

The Broncos had a 17-0 lead at one point but allowed the Raiders to tie it up at 17-17 due to some poor defensive plays and errors on the offensive side of the ball. Honestly, they even left more points on the field and should have been up 24-10 at halftime if Troy Franklin had not doinked the ball off his helmet.

With all that said, here are your winners and losers from the Denver Broncos' 38-24 victory over the Las Vegas Raiders.

Broncos rookie running back Jonah Coleman had a breakout party vs. the Raiders. Coleman topped 100 yards for the first time in his career and had a hat trick with three rushing touchdowns.

In total, Coleman had 24 carries for 111 yards and 3 rushing touchdowns while adding 4 receptions for 18 yards. That is 28 touches for 129 yards and 3 touchdowns.

After a trade deadline period that featured the Broncos being connected to Jonathan Taylor and Tony Pollard, Coleman emerged from the bye week and proved to the team and the fans that he is the guy. He averaged 4.6 yards per carry, ran hard, and was tough to bring down. He ran through and over Raiders defenders, stiffed armed DBs, and had a nose for the end zone.

If the Broncos want to have success in January, they need a run game like this. Add in the explosive element of RJ Harvey, who will be returning in a few weeks, and the Broncos could have a solid 1-2 punch in the backfield.

This was one of the best Broncos rushing performances in a long time. Love to see it.

The Broncos signed CB Riley Moss to a four-year extension this week, but pending free agent slot corner Ja'Quan McMillian once again proved his worth to the team with a big-time play vs. the Raiders.

It was late in the 4th quarter and the Raiders. led by rookie number one overall pick Fernando Mendoza, had a chance to tie the game up, or even for the win with a two-point conversion. Well, Ja'Quan McMillian, who is known for his clutch plays, did it again. He made an impressive one-handed interception and took back for a touchdown.

Yet another late-game INT and pick 6 that sealed a victory for the Broncos.

Hear me Broncos? Do not let this man leave the building.

Engram, who was supposed to be a gamechanger for the Broncos offense, has been mostly non-existent, including this season. However, the veteran tight end showed up and showed up in a big way tonight.

He led the offense with 6 receptions for 74 yards and made multiple big time plays for the Broncos passing offense.

If we can get this sort of production from Engram more often, that could be a gamechanger for this offense. I have my doubts that this will happen, but it was good to see and I am hopeful it continues.

Troy Franklin has had his issues with tracking a deep ball and that, unfortunately, continued tonight vs. the Raiders. He got open, wide open actually, and had a walk in touchdown due to a beautiful Bo Nix pass. It was all there for a big-time score for the Broncos to retake the momentum and control of the game before halftime. All he had to do was make the catch and…

If you're going to be a situational deep threat wide receiver, you need to make these plays. We have seen Franklin struggle with the deep ball throughout his short career and this one might be his worst blunder of the bunch.

His stat line does not look all that impressive, but he should have at least three touchdown passes. He finished the game going 19 of 35 for 225 yards, 1 touchdown and 1 hailmary INT.

If you add in Marvin Mims getting touched down at the 1 yard line (near TD) and Franklin's drop, you're looking at a near 300 yard 3 touchdown game. These box scores never tell the entire story and that is the case with Bo Nix.

I thought this was Nix's best game of the season. He looked in command of the offense, threw the ball down the field and did it well, looked confident, and moved the ball up-and-down the field.

This has been the storyline with Nix throughout his young career. Starts slow, for whatever reason, gets a bye week, and comes out firing. His best games last season came post bye week and I think we'll see that again this season.

This has been a glaring red flag for me the past three games. The Broncos defense is allowing big plays, giving up first downs on third/fourth and long, and keep letting teams get back into the game.

That happened again tonight vs. rookie quarterback Fernando Mendoza making his second career start. The defense started out hot, but errors, poor plays, and bad play calls allowed them to get back in the game.

How many times have we seen a running back wheel route kill this defense? Why is edge rusher Que Robinson one on one with Ashton Jeanty down the field? Also, the run game continues to allow chunk plays. They allowed a wide open running lane for Jeanty to go 45 yards untouched.

This is a Raiders rushing offense that has been struggling and yet, they had success vs. the Broncos.

They allowed another first down on third and long, which has been annoying me personally, and had a blown coverage late in the game that could have went for a long walk in touchdown if Mendoza threw a better ball.

Despite this, they had sacks, swatted the ball, and had a pick 6 which is great, but these issues remain. Plenty of good, but the bad keeps showing up and has shown up in multiple games in a row now.

I am bunching these two together because the Broncos rotated the two in and out at left guard tonight. Powers has started at left guard all season, but today, Peart got the start. However, Powers still played and had multiple series in a row out there before being replaced by Peart.

So, it's a win for Peart to get the start and extended playing time, but he was still being subbed out. Meanwhile, Powers lost his starting gig, for at least one week, but still saw plenty of playing time.

I am interested to see the snap counts and discuess this situation out more tomorrow.

While I said the defense was a loser due to their issues with big plays and on third/fourth and longs, the defense still made some impact plays in this one.

They had 5 sacks in total. Two by Jonathon Cooper, 1 by Alex Singleton, 1 by Malcolm Roach, and half sacks by Zach Allen and Nik Bonitto. Also, Allen had multiple pass deflections while DJ Jones and Malcolm Roach also were able to get their mits on the ball.

Impact special teams plays win you ball games and the Broncos had two of them tonight.

First, defensive lineman Sai'vion Jones blocked a punt which led to a Broncos field goal followed up by edge rusher Dondrea Tillman getting his hands on the ball and allowing the Broncos to attempt to score before halftime.

Darren Rizzi's unit has been solid this season and we can add these two plays to the list of impact plays they have made this season.

LOSER – RB JK Dobbins: 1 carry for 0 yards. Yikes.

LOSER – WR Courtland Sutton: 2 targets for 0 catches.

WINNER – TE Adam Trautman: Caught a touchdown.

WINNER – WR Jaylen Waddle: He had a bad drop, but he bounced back for 3 receptions for 61 yards. The drops need to be cleaned up, but he showed his potential with a few impact plays in the passing game.

LOSER – The broadcast: They were tough to listen to at times, especially Logan Ryan.

WINNER – WR/KR Marvin Mims: Made a nice play on the flea flicker and nearly scored a touchdown, had a jet sweep for 11 yards and 22 yards as a punt returner. I'd rather see Mims get the deep shots that Franklin is getting and doinking.

WINNER – Penalties: Only 2 flags for 10 yards which triggered Raiders HC Klint Kubiak to whine that the refs weren't calling the game fairly at halftime.

WINNER – Red Zone offense: Went 4 for 4 in the red zone which is a winning formula.
