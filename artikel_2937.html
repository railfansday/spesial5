MLB, MLBPA both vow ‘no compromise’ on salary cap/floor

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

At the completion of the Owners' Meetings in New York on Wednesday, MLB Commissioner Rob Manfred appeared before the media to make a statement and take questions. He took the hardest line so far regarding the owners' stance on a salary cap and floor as the basis for a new Collective Bargaining Agreement with the MLBPA. Citing the overwhelming support of their fans, Manfred stated that the lack of competitve balance is the only priority and there is room for a lot of compromise between the two sides but there will be no compromise on the cap and floor.

Claiming that the entirety of the business done in the meetings this time was regarding the labor situation, the commissioner reiterated that the owner's prevailing priority is establishing a salary cap and floor. There was a lot of rhetoric regarding how bad the other side is for their unwillingness to bargain.

While claiming to represent the fans in these negotiations, the owners are trying to sway fan support to their side. The pending lockout is another device used to ramp up pressure on the MLBPA. Using pressure from a lockout and the fans is designed to force the MLBPA to serious negotiations, which they claim has not occurred so far.

On the other side, Bruce Meyer, interim executive director of the MLBPA, issued a response.

"There can be no clearer example of the league's lack of seriousness than their insistence that they will not negotiate over the economics outside of a radical change to the system that would dramatically reduce overall player compensation for all categories of players and strip fundamental rights from players like the ability to have truly guaranteed contracts," Meyer said.

"There should be no illusions about the owners' true motivations in pushing for this system. They want to make more money in profits and franchise values by artificially suppressing player salaries," Meyer added.

The two sides have been negotiating since May and the rhetoric seems to indicate that no progress has been made. These posturing tactics are not unusual in a situation like this, where labor and management are on opposite sides of a big contract negotiation. The commissioner's job is to represent the needs and desires of the owners. The MLBPA is the voice of the players.

The problem is that they both claim to represent the best interests of the game and the fans.

We don't have a seat at this table. Millionaires and billionaires are fighting over their profits. There is no voice protecting the game or the fans.

It remains to be seen if the two sides are devoted enough to their stances to sacrifice games or the goodwill of the fans.
