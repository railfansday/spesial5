Berkshire Hit a Record High While AI Power Stocks Sold Off. Here’s What That Says About Where Energy Money Is Going.

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Berkshire Hathaway rose as stocks like Bloom Energy and Caterpillar dropped, suggesting safety is increasingly important.

By Reuben Gregg Brewer – Nov 24, 2026 at 6:42PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Berkshire Hathaway ›

Over the past three months, Berkshire Hathaway (BRKA +0.73%)(BRKB +0.85%) is up roughly 15% as of this writing. By comparison, Bloom Energy (BE -6.98%) and Caterpillar (CAT -1.17%), both headline-grabbing artificial intelligence (AI) energy plays, are down 29% and 38%, respectively. Investors aren't giving up on the companies that provide energy for AI, but they may be shifting toward more conservative investments. Here's what you need to know.

Artificial intelligence is really just a fancy computer program. Without electricity, it can't exist. But AI uses a tremendous amount of power, resulting in consumer backlash against the data centers that host it. This is why investors have been so attracted to Bloom Energy and Caterpillar.

Bloom Energy makes hydrogen fuel cells. These factory-produced items can be placed wherever they are needed to provide off-grid power. Caterpillar, while best known for its heavy earth-moving equipment, also makes generators that provide off-grid power. However, given the backlash against AI, investor enthusiasm for AI stocks has diminished, and these stocks have fallen alongside the broader AI trade despite still-high expectations for power demand.

Meanwhile, Berkshire Hathaway's shares have been on the rise. That is at least partly a flight-to-safety trade. Berkshire Hathaway is a giant conglomerate with businesses ranging from insurance to homebuilding, and much in between. It also has more than $370 billion in cash sitting on its balance sheet to cushion it against a market downturn. That cash also provides it with the ability to buy if a bear market opens up attractive investment opportunities.

That's a good start, but there's another small wrinkle here. Over the last three months, Southern Company (SO +1.07%), a large regulated U.S. utility, is up nearly 5%, and Kinder Morgan (KMI +1.67%), a large North American pipeline operator, is down just 2%. These businesses will both be integral to powering AI. They aren't as exciting as Bloom Energy or Cat, but that's the point right now. Investors are looking for a safe haven as AI enthusiasm hits a road bump and are shifting toward boring, reliable businesses.

Notably, Southern offers a reliable 3.2% dividend yield, while Kinder Morgan's yield is 3.9%. Berkshire Hathaway doesn't pay a dividend; however, two of its largest divisions are its utility and energy segments. Add that to the rest of the company's owned business portfolio and its large collection of publicly traded stocks, and you have diversification along with continued exposure to the growing demand for electricity. This is not a small matter, given that electricity demand increased by 10% between 2005 and 2025 and is projected to grow by 60% between 2025 and 2045.

Basically, Berkshire Hathaway could offer the best of both worlds. You own a diversified and financially strong company that is prepared to take advantage of a bear market. And you continue to have exposure to AI's growing appetite for power. All in one investment. Investors may not be dumping AI power companies; they may just be getting more selective and conservative.

The caveat here is that Berkshire Hathaway is now trading near all-time highs. However, the company has been buying back its own stock over the last two quarters, which it has historically done only when it believes the stock is trading below its intrinsic value. While it probably isn't fair to suggest Berkshire Hathaway is a deep-value opportunity, if you are looking for a safer way to play long-term energy demand from AI, the company itself thinks its stock is attractive. It's probably worth a deep dive, even if you only end up putting it on your wish list.
