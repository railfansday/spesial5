Bill Simmons found a lowlight. Jalen Green found the reply button

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The internet is forever. The internet never forgets.

One of the pitfalls of social media is that we often utilize it to express opinions in a heightened emotional state. Typically, you can fire off a tweet, think better of it, and delete it before many people even notice. That luxury diminishes considerably when you have a sizable following, as is the case with Phoenix Suns guard Jalen Green, who has over 180,000 followers on Twitter. Sure, it's not Kevin Durant territory. Durant has 27.6 million followers, and as we know all too well here in Phoenix, very little of his social media activity goes unnoticed.

An interesting exchange occurred today after Bill Simmons tweeted the following. The accompanying video features Jalen Green standing wide open for several seconds before missing a three-pointer against the Sacramento Kings during the Suns' victory on Wednesday night.

I'm sure Bill Simmons was being somewhat tongue-in-cheek when he captioned the video, "This is the official video of the 2026–27 trade deadline."

Was it a great moment for Jalen Green? No. But in the same breath, it would suggest that Simmons didn't watch the basketball game. Green had a productive night, scoring 18 points to go along with 7 assists and 7 rebounds. The miss Simmons chose to highlight was one of only three Green had from beyond the arc, as he went 4-of-7 from downtown. His defensive intensity, which is becoming increasingly apparent as he continues to find his role within the Phoenix Suns, was also on full display Wednesday night.

Given the non-existent defense Zach LaVine played on the play and the drop coverage trade machine All-Star Domantas Sabonis was playing, I see the point Simmons was making. Green's name has been in the hopper since Rich Paul decided Phoenix would be the next destination for a disgruntled Dejounte Murray. And LaVine? His name is always in trade machines as people believe that adding a score-first, defense-last player who was incorrectly awarded a dunk contest a decade ago can be a solution. The video highlighted two players with large contracts who have underdelivered and personify the methodology of shuffling decks on the Titanic.

Ultimately, emotions got the best of Green, who responded with the following:

The tweet was soon deleted, and quite honestly, I don't know exactly what it means. With phraseology like that, you could interpret it a number of different ways.

What's interesting is that as recently as yesterday, Jalen Green acknowledged that he's aware of the noise surrounding him.

"I mean, I see a little bit (of the reports)," Green stated. "This is people's jobs. They think they're doing something with the internet and feel like they got to post things like this. I've seen a couple of things, but I don't really care. I've already been traded once. I'm focused on getting better and winning games."

It's one thing to hear the noise locally from those who watch you play every night. It's another when a prominent national voice cherry-picks one moment from an otherwise productive performance and uses it as the proverbial promotional video for the trade machine that will inevitably dominate NBA discourse over the next few months. That's more than noise. That's an annoyance.

Perhaps the best response Green can provide moving forward is more of what we witnessed Wednesday night. He's beginning to demonstrate that his value to this team extends beyond his ability to score, and that's a development worth paying attention to. Unfortunately, a missed three-pointer generates considerably more engagement than a productive night of facilitating and defending. Such is the nature of NBA discourse in the social media age, where context is often the first casualty of a good tweet.
