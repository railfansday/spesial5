5E Advanced (FEAM) Q1 2027 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Early V-BOR ramp and grid-independence plans take center stage.

By Motley Fool Transcribing – Nov 18, 2026 at 9:53AM EST

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

5E Advanced (FEAM +5.23%) transitioned from a development-stage entity into an operating producer of borates and industrial minerals following the acquisition of Searles Valley Minerals on Oct. 1, 2026. Management reported that the acquisition includes three processing sites, 54 megawatts of cogeneration capacity, and established infrastructure acquired free and clear of legacy liabilities. The company is currently executing a strategy focused on commercial resets, cost optimization through grid independence, and volume recovery across its California assets. Management stated that boron's status as a critical mineral strengthens the company's strategic position as a domestic supplier to energy and defense markets.

Paul Weibel: Good afternoon, and thank you for joining us today. The first quarter of fiscal year 2027 marks a defining moment for 5E Advanced Materials. On October 1, one day after quarter end, we closed our asset acquisition of Searles Valley Minerals, acquiring them free and clear of the prior owner's legacy liabilities and historical claims. With that closing, 5E transitioned from a development stage company into an operating revenue-generating producer of borates and industrial minerals. It is worth pausing on what we now own.

Searles Valley includes 3 processing sites, 54 megawatts of on-site cogeneration capacity, the Trona Railway short-line railroad, water utility, approximately 25,000 acres across 3 California counties and mineral tenure across more than 9,000 acres at Searles Lake, operated by an experienced workforce of approximately 250 people. The operation produces V-BOR, which is borax pentahydrate, sodium sulfate and salt, and it includes boric acid production capacity that is currently idled. Together with our Fort Cady project, 5E now holds 2 boron assets in Southern California at a time when boron has been added to the U.S. Department of the Interior's Critical Minerals list. We believe that positions 5E as a strategically important domestic supplier of critical minerals.

Because the acquisition closed just after quarter end, today, I want to focus on our first 5 weeks of ownership and our plan to grow the value of this platform moving forward. That plan is built on the 3 levers we laid out on our acquisition call, price, cost and volume. The focus is now on operational excellence and execution. Each one is already underway, and I will walk you through our progress. First, I'll look at our first month of operations. In October, we produced 6,200 tons of V-BOR, adding to approximately 3,000 tons of V-BOR inventory we carried into the month, and we sold approximately 2,500 tons.

We prioritized our domestic customers in October, while we build out export logistics through the Port of Long Beach. That network is now established, opening container shipments into Asia and the inventory we built in October is positioned to ship into that channel in November. With logistics in place and product on hand, we expect V-BOR sales volumes to ramp as we move through November and into December. Now to the levers starting with price. Through the bankruptcy process, the legacy below-market sales contracts were rejected. That gives us a clean slate on commercial terms, and we have transitioned customers onto new take-or-pay and fixed-price V-BOR offtake agreements.

In our first month, we achieved a V-BOR netback of approximately $767 per ton. Importantly, many of our November V-BOR orders have been prepaid, which speaks to the demand we are seeing for our product. Turning to cost. Power is one of our largest opportunities, and the solution is already on site. We plan to reduce the Westend plant's reliance on purchased power through greater use of our existing 54 megawatts of on-site cogeneration capacity. To enable that, we have engaged a third-party engineering firm to finalize the specifications for a capacitor. We are targeting having that capacitor on order by January with the objective of reducing our reliance on the grid by next summer.

We expect this initiative to require approximately $0.5 million of capital, and it would save approximately $38,000 per day on grid electricity purchases or roughly $14 million gross on an annualized basis, which we expect to reduce operating expenditures by approximately $8 million to $9 million. Beyond power, we are resetting the cost structure through organizational and procurement efficiencies and the elimination of duplicative corporate costs. On to volume. Current V-BOR production is approximately 82,000 short tons on an annualized basis.

Our objective is to recover production towards approximately 100,000 short tons annually through 2 initiatives: the restart of the SAC plant and an engineering-led well field development program, together with incremental byproduct production, including sodium bicarbonate and additional boric acid. We also plan to restart the idle boric acid production at our Trona facility, adding another product line from assets already in place. Reliability underpins that growth. On November 1, we commenced our first planned 5-day maintenance turnaround at the Westend plant. The team completed the work and restarted production on Friday, November 6. This planned downtime allowed us to address equipment reliability and maintenance items that are important to sustaining higher operating rates.

And it is one part of a broader effort to reduce unplanned downtime and strengthen operating performance. During our last update call, we emphasized the restart of the SAC plant, and it remains our most immediate volume catalyst. For those new to the story, SAC stands for Soda Ash Consolidation. It is a small existing facility that was idled as part of the soda ash exit last February, and it can operate independently of the legacy soda ash operation. The SAC plant is designed to capture natural gas emissions into the brine and reduce brine pH, improving brine chemistry and V-BOR output. Bringing it back online is a capital-light way to add volume.

I'm pleased to report that our team has begun the necessary prep work, including maintenance, readiness activities and the steps required for a safe startup. Our target is to have the SAC plant operational in January. Sustained volume growth starts with the resource. So we are also focused on the brine and well field system that underpins production. October brine grade was in line with our modeled expectations, consistent with the assumptions behind our production plan. Today, we kicked off a drilling and workover program addressing 205 wells with new and reworked wells targeting fresh tincal ore with the goal of recovering brine grade towards historical levels.

We also improved heat delivery on the lake by bypassing an underperforming heat exchanger, resulting in more consistent heat. Together with the SAC restart, these initiatives are intended to support stronger brine grades and higher V-BOR production. Beyond borates, our industrial salt and sodium sulfate businesses give us a diversified revenue base serving established industrial customers. Industrial salt operations transitioned seamlessly, averaging approximately 28 trucks per day, delivering industrial salt to key oil and gas customers in Bakersfield, California. In sodium sulfate, we used October to establish the rail and export logistics serving our customers in Mexico.

That work is done, and we are now moving product by rail to the Union Pacific line and onward into Mexico, setting up volumes to build from here. Searles Valley platform also gives us opportunities beyond our current operating plan. We have commenced 2 independent work streams focused on lithium and tungsten, both of which have the potential to become important byproducts that enhance our economics. Reputable third parties have received brine samples and will work to perform initial testing, analytical procedures and process recovery work. These opportunities are at an early stage, but we see them as meaningful optionality on top of our core borates business. We also see longer-term potential in borates expansion and sulfate of potash.

At Fort Cady, we continue to advance our advanced boron material strategy. In August, independent x-ray diffraction analysis confirmed the formation of iron boride in our ferroboron samples. We have since initiated testing at 1,400 degrees Celsius and are planning an approximately 500-gram batch with a third-party metallurgical processing partner as next steps towards producing samples for prospective customers. All of this is being done alongside the integration of the Searles Valley organization into 5E. We held our first employee town hall, and we are working closely with our employees as they transition on to 5E systems, and we have identified critical positions that we intend to fill. I want to personally recognize the Searles Valley team.

Their experience operating this asset is a real advantage for 5E, and I'm grateful for the work they have done during this initial transition. On financing, we ended the quarter with $15.6 million in cash and cash equivalents. And we received $7 million under our bridge facility at closing and are diligently working on the remaining CPs to draw on the remaining $3 million. Customer prepayments on November orders are supporting our working capital and our go-forward plan includes a working capital debt facility supported by our contracted offtake structure, which we are working now to finalize. We remain focused on solutions that optimize 5E's cost of capital.

To sum up, our first 5 weeks have been about establishing a reliable operating platform and a foundation for increasing production, and we have made strong progress. We completed a successful first month, streamlined logistics to drive sales, began resetting our commercial terms, completed a planned maintenance turnaround and advanced the SAC restart that can unlock additional production and sales volumes.

Looking ahead, our milestones are clear: ramping V-BOR sales through November and December, transitioning customers onto new take-or-pay and fixed-price offtake agreements, restarting the SAC plant in January, restarting idle boric acid production in Trona, placing the capacitor order by January, reducing our reliance on the grid by next summer and recovering V-BOR production towards approximately 100,000 short tons annually. Our focus remains on safely increasing volumes, improving reliability and cost and strengthening commercial execution. We believe the combination of Searles Valley's operating platform today and Fort Cady's long-term resource opportunity positions 5E for its next phase of growth, and we are excited about the road ahead.
