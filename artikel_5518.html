The best travel gear of 2026, according to Shop TODAY

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Free daily briefing on global business news.

A selection of 15 winners from Shop TODAY's 2026 Travel Awards, from a $39.90 Uniqlo dress to a Quince carry-on and a Qi2 charger.

Every trip starts with a zipper and a hope. A traveler closes the bag, heads for the door, and trusts that nothing inside will give out between the curb and the destination. Gear draws little attention until it breaks. A strap snaps in a terminal, a phone dies before the boarding call, or an outfit that looked fine at home turns sticky and heavy in the afternoon heat. Small objects carry outsized consequences once a traveler leaves home behind. The right pieces fade into the background and let the trip unfold. The wrong ones become the story people tell after they unpack.

The friction piles up in ways no packing list captures. Airlines tack fees onto bags, and each additional one adds to the bill. Outlets abroad come in unfamiliar setups that an everyday charger may not fit. Necklaces and earrings knot themselves into clumps inside a pouch. A delayed layover can stretch into an unplanned overnight stay that leaves a passenger too anxious to sleep. Stacked over a week, these snags drain the energy, money, and patience a person on the road would prefer to spend on the place itself. Many of them yield to better equipment, provided someone has put it through real use before departure.

Shop TODAY editors spent months putting clothing, tech, luggage, and accessories through trials for the outlet's fourth annual Travel Awards. Candidates came from new launches, editor recommendations, products trending on social media, and long-trusted favorites, and the team tried 281 products in all. Hands-on studio experiments probed each one for durability, function, and quality. The testing also moved onto trains, planes, and cars, and editors flew to countries such as Norway and Japan. Apparel and shoes went out in New York City, around the country, and overseas. A panel of experts reviewed some of the winners and offered feedback, and most winners carry an overall score on a five-point scale. Executive Editor Adrianna Brach presented highlights on TODAY. Prices reflect the moment of publication. Some categories crown more than one winner, and the 15 that follow form a selection of the winners in an order that implies no ranking.

The Uniqlo Ultra Stretch AIRism Dress won the best travel dress award with a score of 5 out of 5, and at $39.90 it can serve as a whole outfit in a suitcase. Sneakers pair with it during the day. Sandals and a shawl carry it into the evening. The shoes and the wrap change the look, while the dress itself stays the same. Shop TODAY tagged the dress as a bestseller, and Uniqlo sells it at that price, putting a top mark within reach of a modest budget.

Quick-drying, sweat-wicking polyester suits the dress to hot destinations. Editor Shannon Garlin, who tested it this spring, said the fabric dries fast and soaks up sweat on hot days. Testers rated the material comfortable as well. A fabric that sheds moisture keeps the dress from clinging over a full day out, an essential trait in clothing worn for hours on end.

A longer hem gives the dress a second job on itineraries that include sacred sites. On Garlin's 5-foot-4-inch frame, the hem fell to about mid-calf. She liked that length, which she said makes the dress a good option for visiting sacred and holy places on a trip. Her height gives taller wearers a reference point. On a longer frame, the hem will land higher on the leg. The team favored its airy cut as well. Extra coverage, in this case, does not mean a garment that hugs the body.

The dress runs large, and editors recommend ordering a size down for a closer fit. Uniqlo offers it in five colors and sizes XXS through XL. Shoppers who want the relaxed drape can keep their usual size. A single dress that handles sweltering heat, a night out, and a visit to a place of worship lets a traveler pack fewer pieces for a varied route.

The Case-Mate Soap Bubble Waterproof Floating Pouch keeps water out to a depth of 3.3 feet, and it won the best all-weather phone protector award with a score of 4.25 out of 5. Its bestseller label adds a second endorsement, this one from shoppers spending their own money.

Adrianna Brach, Shop TODAY's executive editor, pitched the pouch as cover for more than a handset. In her description, it guards keys, an ID, cash, and other belongings against water, sand, and dirt. A day at the beach or the pool exposes all of those items at once, and a loose pile on a towel invites loss. One waterproof sleeve keeps the whole kit together.

A clear face lets an owner use the phone without breaking the seal. A fingertip can work the touchscreen through the transparent panel, so a traveler can check a map or snap a picture by the water without opening anything. The opposite face hides pockets that keep valuables out of sight, letting the pouch double as a waterproof wallet. An adjustable lanyard keeps it within reach.

Buoyancy covers the worst case. A bare phone that slips off a dock or a boat sinks, while this pouch rides on the surface until someone fishes it out.

Case-Mate built the pouch to fit all phones, which lets one model serve a group of travelers carrying different devices. It comes in 13 colors, enough for companions to tell their pouches apart. Case-Mate and Amazon $AMZN each list it at $20, and Target $TGT charges $29.99, a $9.99 premium for the same item.

The promo code TODAY takes 20% off sitewide at Case-Mate. Applied to the $20 price, the code makes the brand's own site the cheapest of the three retailers named. The sitewide reach also means the discount extends to anything else a shopper adds to the same order.

The Halfday Garment Tote won best travel tote for garments with a 5/5 score at $158 by tucking a full garment bag inside an everyday carry-all. A side zipper releases the garment bag, which holds suits, dresses, and shirts. Formal pieces ride in their own section, away from the shoes and toiletries that could crease or soil them. The setup spares a traveler from juggling a separate hanging bag alongside a tote on the way through a terminal.

The main compartment swallows a laptop, clothes, shoes, and toiletries. Editors pitched the tote for wedding weekends, quick business trips, and adventures of up to 48 hours. Each of those outings tends to call for one polished outfit next to casual basics, and the split interior lets a single bag cover both needs. On a work trip, a computer and a suit share the same piece of luggage.

A 1680D ballistic nylon exterior resists water and abrasion. The shell can shrug off a rainy curb or a scrape against an overhead bin, the kind of wear a short trip brings. Testers praised the sturdy handles and found the tote easy to haul, a meaningful result for a bag loaded with formalwear, footwear, and electronics.

The tote measures 19 inches long, 15 inches wide, and 8 inches deep, and reviewers flagged the width as a drawback. The broad profile buys room for a dress and a weekend's gear, though it can feel bulky in a crowded aisle or a cramped train car. Shoppers can pick from three colors.

Halfday has collected a Travel Award before, for its two-in-one duffel. A second win for a different bag suggests the brand's strength reaches beyond a single design. Top marks despite the complaint about its girth show that editors weighed the drawback against the two-section layout and judged the trade worth making.

The Kodak Charmera earned a 4.67 out of 5 on its way to Shop TODAY's best viral travel gadget award. The word viral in that award title marks a product whose fame spread online. It sells for $34.99 at Amazon $AMZN, Target $TGT, and Walmart $WMT. One button captures both still pictures and video clips. The keychain shooter promises the charm of old cameras with no rolls to develop.

Seven vintage-inspired filters and four frames give each shot a retro finish before it leaves the device. Behind those extras sits an f/2.4, 35mm lens. Editors listed its compact build as the lone strength in their notes, which suits a camera that hangs from a key ring.

CNBC reports that Kodak released the palm-sized point-and-shoot in partnership with the camera company Reto. Marketing casts each of the camera's seven styles as a working fashion accessory, according to CNBC. Every version carries settings that mimic the look of analog film prints. The blind box packaging hands each buyer one of seven designs at random, according to Time. Two shoppers who buy the same box can end up with different designs. A traveler set on a particular design has to accept the luck of the draw.

The Charmera arrives without an SD card, the one drawback editors flagged. Owners transfer their files from that card to a computer. The gadget needs a card before it can pull its weight on a trip. Shop TODAY recommends adding a Lexar 64GB Micro SD Card 2-Pack for $39.99 and an SD card reader for $16.99. The Lexar bundle works out to about $20 per card. Its second card leaves a spare for a longer vacation. The reader alone runs close to half the camera's price. Together those add-ons run $56.98, lifting the full kit to $91.97. The accessories end up costing more than the keychain camera they serve.

The Satechi Vegan-Leather FindAll Passport Cover, which editors named best passport cover with a score of 4 out of 5, gives a $59.99 sleeve the power to report its own whereabouts. Bluetooth connects the cover to Apple $AAPL's Find My network, and an iPhone can display the cover's location in real time. The tracking hardware lives inside the cover, so owners have no reason to slip a separate tag between the pages.

Satechi left off a charging port. The cover recharges without a cable and works with Qi chargers. Anyone who packs a Qi pad for a phone can top up the cover on the same surface. Owners can make it play a sound and share it with other people, so a companion can join the search. The cover lacks Apple's Precision Find feature, though it reports a last known position.

Executive editor Adrianna Brach tied the cover's usefulness to travel within the U.S. Travelers $TRV without a Real ID need a passport even for domestic trips, she noted. Under that requirement, the document rides along on in-country itineraries as well as overseas ones. Each added trip hands the passport another chance to go missing, the exact risk the Find My link addresses.

Slots for credit cards, cash, and boarding passes line the interior, enough for the cover to replace a wallet. RFID blocking shields the cards inside. The passport attaches to the cover by its final page, and a boarding pass can tuck in behind the cards. Satechi made the shell from vegan leather and stitched the edges to reinforce the spot most prone to wear.

The cover measures 5.6 by 4.27 by 0.66 inches and comes in three colors at Amazon $AMZN and Satechi. Folding a tracker, a wallet, and a passport holder into a package less than an inch thick leaves one fewer item to misplace on the road.

The Ekouaer Swim Dress earned Shop TODAY's best value swimsuit award with a score of 4.67 out of 5, and Amazon $AMZN lists it at $32.99, down 15% from $38.99. The markdown trims $6 from a suit priced at the low end of the award-winning swimwear. Shop TODAY tags the dress as a bestseller, a sign that readers have put money behind it alongside the testers who scored it.

The Ekouaer costs less than The Ruffle Backflip from Summersalt, the best one-piece swimsuit winner, including at the sale price Dick's lists. Deputy partnerships editor Francesca Cocchi Zabloudil found the Summersalt supportive for bigger busts, and testers credited the Ekouaer with the same strength. A shopper who needs that lift can get it at the Ekouaer's lower price. Testers flagged the Summersalt as running small, and the Ekouaer's spec sheet lists no sizing caution.

A high-rise fit carries the dress's coverage up through the midsection, beyond what a low-cut suit offers. Testers liked the slight compression in the fabric, a trait that helps a suit stay put through a long afternoon at the beach or pool.

Fixed straps marked the single drawback testers recorded. Swimmers who like to tailor how the top sits will have to live with the stock fit. The limit weighs more on a suit sold through Amazon, where a buyer cannot try it on before it arrives.

The dress comes in 24 colors and sizes S through XXL. Among all the winners, only a pair of Project Cloud flip-flops offers a wider palette, and the range lets a buyer coordinate the suit with a vacation wardrobe. At its list price, the dress undercuts the Soma Swim Caftan Cover-Up's sale price, which makes the swimsuit the cheaper half of a seaside outfit assembled from this year's winners.

Towel Crabber Towel Bands won the best beach hack award with a 4.67 out of 5 score, and a six-piece set costs $11.99 at Amazon $AMZN. Each silicone loop stretches around the top of a lounge chair and pins a towel in place. A towel anchored this way stays put through a gust that would lift a loose one off the seat. The bands appeared among the winners featured on The 3rd Hour of TODAY.

Adrianna Brach, Shop TODAY's executive editor, judged the bands a clear improvement over clips, which she called annoying. She added that the loops fit most standard chairs up to 25 inches wide. An oversized lounger beyond that span comes with no assurance of a fit.

Brach took a set on a cruise and called the bands genius for that trip as well. The upper deck, she noted, never stops blowing. Her verdict carries the product past the shoreline its award names. A strap that tames a towel on a windy ship earns space in more than one kind of suitcase.

Reviewers singled out the bands as compact and secure. Both traits serve the trip at opposite ends, one at the packing stage and the other beside the water. Each set contains six pieces, and buyers can choose from six colors. A handful of thin silicone bands claims little room in a packed bag.

Towel Crabber sells no smaller packs, the lone drawback in the review. A solo traveler who needs one band still buys all six. Split across the set, each band works out to about $2. The extra pieces add little to the bill and leave spares for a travel companion's chair.

Peak Design's $29.95 Packable Tote shrinks to 5.9 by 3.5 by 1.6 inches once folded, and it won the best packable travel tote award with a 4.67 out of 5. At that footprint, the bag can ride in a suitcase corner on the outbound leg without crowding out clothes. A spare carryall that takes up little room on day one leaves the main luggage's full capacity for the things a traveler planned to bring.

Souvenirs explain the appeal for executive editor Adrianna Brach, who said she always reaches the end of a trip needing an extra bag to hold them. She added that the tote doubles as a personal item on a plane, a role she said lets fliers sidestep extra fees. In her telling, the same object that saved space on the way out earns its keep on the way home by carrying the overflow aboard.

A honeymoon purchase sharpened her view. Brach recalled buying a bag in an airport during that trip and paying a steep price for it. She described the chance to pick up this one for $30 as a "travel PSA." Her story casts the tote as a hedge against a predictable expense. Travelers $TRV who wait until the airport to find extra space pay whatever the terminal charges, at the moment they have the fewest alternatives.

Testers praised the tote's ultra-light weight and comfortable strap, two traits that count for a bag meant to be carried full on a travel day. Its shell uses Versa Heal 70d nylon fabric, and buyers can choose from five colors. Testers recorded a single drawback. The tote cannot go in a washing machine. Owners must find another way to clean it.

Its only recorded flaw surfaces at home, after the trip ends. The lone complaint falls outside the work of hauling gear, which suggests the tote handled what testers asked of it on the road.

Shop TODAY editors approached travel pillows as skeptics, yet the Dot&Dot Twist Memory Foam Travel Pillow won them over with a 4.67 out of 5 and the award for best travel pillow. Amazon $AMZN sells it for $29.99 in five colors. A high mark from doubters says more about a product than praise from fans of the category. Judges who expected little from the category had every reason to mark this one down, and they rewarded it.

The twisting design gives one cushion three jobs. A traveler can wear it around the neck in the familiar donut shape, drape it over one shoulder, or wedge it against a window or wall. Each position suits a different way of resting in a seat. A sleeper who starts a flight upright and ends it slumped toward the cabin wall can reshape the same pillow without packing a second one. An aisle passenger with no wall within reach still keeps the neck and shoulder options.

Memory foam keeps all three shapes useful. The material springs back each time its owner switches positions, and testers listed that easy rebound among the pillow's strengths. Foam that stayed flattened after one twist would leave the other configurations limp. Quick recovery holds the whole concept together.

Reviewers gave the washable cover its own praise, a practical feature for an item that spends hours pressed against someone's neck and face. A cover that survives washing lets the pillow return from a long flight and set out clean on the next one.

The team flagged the pillow as hard to store, a fair complaint about something that still has to ride in a bag between connections. A cushion thick enough to cradle a head claims carry-on space that clothes, chargers, or snacks would compete for. The Twist solves an in-seat comfort problem and hands travelers a packing problem in exchange.

Twelve South's ButterFly SE 2-in-1 Qi2 Charger scored 5 out of 5, and Amazon $AMZN listed it for $70 at publication, 30% below its $99.99 list price. The device unfolds to reveal two charging surfaces. One holds a phone. The other powers AirPods or an Apple $AAPL Watch. A traveler can refill both gadgets overnight from a single cord.

A tester likened the folded charger to a makeup compact, and its listed dimensions run 9 by 2.4 by 2.4 inches. Buyers can pick from six colors. One folding unit that does the work of two spares a carry-on the weight and tangle of a separate stand for each gadget.

A meter-long cable ships in the box, and any standard 30W USB-C adapter will run the ButterFly SE. Editors suggested Anker's Nano USB-C Charger Block $SQ at $15.99, down from $19.99, and Apple's 30W USB-C Port Power Adapter at $39, down from $59. Pairing the Amazon deal with the Anker block brings the total to $85.99. Even with a new adapter, the package undercuts the charger's $99.99 list price on its own.

Twelve South listed the ButterFly SE at $79.99, and Apple listed it at $79.95. Amazon's price ran $9.99 below the brand's own store and $9.95 below Apple's. Testers warned that the charger may overheat. A traveler who owns a 30W USB-C brick can skip the adapter and hold the full outlay at $70.

The Ban.do 3-in-1 Game won best boredom buster with a 4/5 score by putting checkers, chess, and backgammon on a single $34.95 roll-up board.

A two-sided design lets one surface host all three games. Chess and checkers share the front, and backgammon fills the reverse. Flipping the board switches games. One flat piece takes the place of three separate boxes in a bag.

The set ships with 32 game pieces and four dice, 36 items altogether, and a zippered pocket in the board holds every one of them. Testers counted the built-in zip compartment among the set's strengths. With the pieces sealed inside, the game moves as one object. No separate pouch can go missing between a hotel room and a departure gate.

Smooth leatherette wraps the board, and Ban.do says the material wipes clean and holds up under repeated rolling. Both points matter for a game that a traveler unrolls at every stop. A surface that flexes without wearing out suits that routine. Wipe-clean material fits tray tables, picnic blankets, and other spots where crumbs and spills collect.

Spread flat, the board measures 14 inches long by 10 inches wide. Testers flagged it as hard to store, the one drawback they recorded. The complaint sits oddly beside a roll-up design, and the dimensions explain why. Rolling shrinks the footprint, yet the tube runs at least 10 inches from end to end. Packed, the game needs a slot that long, which can crowd a small personal item.

Split across three games, the price comes to less than $12 apiece. At that cost, money stops being the main hurdle. Suitcase space decides whether the set makes the trip. A traveler with room to spare gets three ways to fill a long delay from one purchase.

Soma's High-Waist Wide-Leg Pants earned 4.83 out of 5 and the award for best overall travel pant by holding their form from morning through night. The sale price of $55.30 sits 30% below the original $79.

The waistband supplies much of the appeal. A high rise applies mild compression, and the fabric gives the pants a "second skin" feel. Gentle pressure at the midsection offers support through hours in a seat without the bite of a rigid closure. The cut keeps its lines over long stretches, a trait that counts when one outfit has to carry someone from the departure gate to the dinner table.

Lower down, the leg opens into a roomy silhouette that leaves space to move in cramped rows and on city walks. Side pockets keep small essentials within reach. Three inseam lengths let shorter and taller wearers decide where the hem lands, so the wide leg can fall as intended instead of bunching at the ankle or dragging along the ground. Testers praised the light weight, an advantage for anyone trying to keep a bag lean.

Writer Jannely Espinal wears them herself, an endorsement from a staffer who chose the pants for her own closet. Soma sells the style in six colors, enough range to pair with most tops a traveler packs, and in sizes XS through XXL. The pants run big, a quirk worth weighing before choosing a size. Reviewers warned that the fabric may pill, a cosmetic drawback for a packer hoping to rotate one pair through a whole trip. Neither concern touches the comfort or shape behind the score, which points to fit and fabric wear as the probable reasons the pants stopped a fraction short of full marks.

The New Balance 1080v15 won best sneaker for long walks with a score of 4.67 out of 5, and testers praised both its padded sole and its resistance to blisters. They rated the shoe comfortable too, a quality that counts for a lot on a sightseeing day measured in miles.

Writer Emma Stessman came to this pair as a longtime fan of the line. She has tried several earlier iterations of the 1080 and ranks them among her favorite everyday sneakers of all time. Her history with the shoe gives her verdict on the new model a clear point of comparison. She can measure the latest release against predecessors she knows from experience.

New Balance retired the Fresh Foam material it had used for years and swapped in an all-new type of cushioning, according to Stessman. She describes the result as bouncy. In her testing, the padding springs back to its original form with each step. Stessman framed that praise as an early read, saying she had been enjoying the bounce so far. A sole that recovers its shape stride after stride suits the long walks the award names.

The 1080v15 weighs less than the previous version, Stessman found. She called the lighter build a welcome change, the kind of improvement she always appreciates. A lighter shoe asks less of a traveler's legs over a full day, and it adds less heft to a packed suitcase.

Amazon $AMZN and Zappos list the sneaker at $169.95, and New Balance charges $169.99. It comes in 13 colors and sizes 7 to 16, and the shoe uses mesh and rubber. The upper end of that size run reaches 16, room for travelers with larger feet. Editors flagged one weakness. The materials do not resist stains.
