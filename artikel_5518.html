Google agreed to add employee privacy safeguards to clear Spirit Airlines data deal

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The revised agreement resolves union objections over AI training use of employee files and still needs bankruptcy court approval

Spirit filed the revised agreement Monday in U.S. Bankruptcy Court in New York, according to The Wall Street Journal. It settles complaints raised by the unions for Spirit's pilots and flight attendants, which had cautioned that workers' personal records would be used to train commercial AI tools. The deal still requires bankruptcy court approval.

Under the new terms, the sale leaves out a range of worker records, including medical files, drug and alcohol test results, investigations tied to labor matters, records of grievances and arbitration, performance reviews and payroll, among other employment-related materials, according to The Wall Street Journal. An independent verifier will examine how the data is put to use and confirm that confidentiality and security obligations are being met.

A Spirit spokesperson said the carrier welcomed settling the unions' disputes about the "sale of deidentified data" while it works to get the most value it can for creditors, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Google won the August bankruptcy auction for Spirit's digital archives, according to The Wall Street Journal. The archives contain decades of payroll, travel and recruiting files, along with about 100 million emails, 80,000 email accounts and millions of other digital items. Google has said the purchase excludes personal information, and that it intends to apply the data toward improving its products and AI models.

Google agreed to pay $10 million for Spirit's internal business data after winning the auction. AI hiring platform Mercor was the runner-up with a $7.5 million offer. Google said a third party would scrub personally identifiable information from the data before handoff.

Spirit ceased operations earlier this year after a government rescue package collapsed, leaving roughly 17,000 employees and contractors without work.
