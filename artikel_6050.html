ASRA Q. NOMANI: Old-guard Dems face Frankenstein moment as leftists come for them

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Democrats cultivated far-left activist networks to mobilize voters and defeat Republicans. Now, a "red-green-green" alliance of socialist, anti-Israel and climate activists challenges party leaders.

WASHINGTON, D.C. — Ice cream magnate Ben Cohen and activists from the far-left nonprofit CodePink packed into a lawmaker's office in House Longworth Building Room 1236 last week, demanding an end to the war in Iran, a cutoff of U.S. military ties to Israel and the removal of Pentagon chief Pete Hegseth.

Next door, in House Rayburn Building Room 2267, Capitol police officers arrested far-left, anti-Israel demonstrators, including prolific street theater activist Hazami Barmada, cloaked in symbolic blood red, as they packed another lawmaker's office, condemning complicity in Israel's alleged "genocide" of Palestinians in Gaza.

Across Capitol Hill in Senate Hart Building Room 322, police officers arrested young adults from a far-left nonprofit and political action committee, Sunrise Movement, as they packed a senator's office, condemning his politics, including his support of Israel.

The next day, an activist from a far-left nonprofit, Climate Defiance, screamed at a high-ranking lawmaker speaking at a political conference, calling him a "traitor."

But they weren't going after Republican lawmakers.

They were far-left activists with a special target: The Democratic Party's leadership.

The target of Cohen, the co-founder of Ben and Jerry's, was California Democratic Rep. Nancy Pelosi, former speaker of the House. The anti-Israel protesters had filled the office of Democratic Rep. Hakeem Jeffries of New York, nominated Wednesday by fellow Democrats to be the next House speaker despite the protests.

The Sunrise Movement agitators had flooded the office of top Senate Democrat Chuck Schumer, and the activists of Climate Defiance were attempting to shame Jeffries at a conference of the Democratic Party.

The groups protesting top Democrats represent the full spectrum of a "red-green-green-blue" alliance – made up of socialist and pro-communist "red" organizations, Muslim and Arab "green" groups, environmental "green" activists and traditional "blue" groups. The alliance has emerged in recent years to press a far-left agenda in U.S. politics, particularly on the issue of ending U.S. support for Israel.

A Fox News Digital investigation reveals that 22 groups from the network – harnessing about $342 million in annual revenues – are now waging a coordinated effort, dubbed "CHUCK CHUCK," aimed at ousting Schumer from his expected role as Senate majority leader, creating the myth of the marching millions with shared messaging, social media campaigns, influencer videos and staged arrests.

The campaign is operating on several fronts: activists "bird-dogging" lawmakers in the halls of Congress, national advocacy organizations endorsing Maryland Sen. Chris Van Hollen over Schumer, local chapters organizing demonstrations and supporters mobilizing phone calls to senators ahead of a Dec. 1 secret-ballot leadership election.

On Monday, Brooklyn activists from a traditionally Democratic group, Indivisible Project, plan to "celebrate" Schumer's 76th birthday with a protest against him at his Third Avenue midtown Manhattan office.

Schumer maintains that he has the votes to win, and he most likely does.

A Fox News Digital anti-Schumer tracker shows that Van Hollen only has the support of five sitting or incoming senators: Bernie Sanders, Abdul El-Sayed, Jeff Merkley, Peggy Flanagan and himself. The emerging dynamics are reminiscent of the Frankenstein story, with the Democratic establishment empowering the powerful activist movement to defeat its Republican opponents. But now, parts of that movement are turning on the very political establishment that helped bring them to power.

Earlier this year, the Sunrise Movement's executive director, Aru Shiney-Ajay, led an online discussion, published on YouTube, headlined "How to Take Over the Democratic Party."

Neera Tanden, a longtime ally of Bill and Hillary Clinton and now executive director of the Center for American Progress, a Democratic nonprofit created by former Clinton administration chief of staff John Podesta, has another term for the rebels from within who challenge Jeffries: "chaos agents."

On Tuesday, she wrote on X, "I personally would like the names of everyone who wins an historic election and then immediately goes into a circular firing squad so we can tag the chaos agents for the future."

Meanwhile, witnessing the targeting of Jeffries as a "traitor," Texas Democratic Party advisor Kurt Bardella wrote on X, "I can tell you there are times when the tactics and venom coming from the activist left are completely indistinguishable to the MAGA right. They just come off as unhinged a--holes who are throwing tantrums."

PROGRESSIVES FACE ACCUSATIONS OF RACISM OVER ATTACKS ON HAKEEM JEFFRIES AS HE SEEKS SPEAKERSHIP

When Code Pink co-founder Medea Benjamin emerged from Pelosi's office last week, trailing behind Capitol Police officers called by staffers to assess the situation, she told Fox News Digital: "See, we're bipartisan. We yell at Democrats as much as we yell and scream at Republicans."

Importantly, for some of them, the goal of the "red" in the red-green-green-blue alliance isn't Western free-enterprise democracy.

Benjamin's fellow CodePink co-founder, Jodie Evans, gave Fox News Digital a more ideological explanation for rejecting Democrats alongside Republicans, as she stood outside Pelosi's office.

"I'm a socialist, because I believe in equality, so I teach it. It's the peace economy," Evans said. "Socialism is the peace of economy. I read Marx to try to understand why we were at war with communists. Like, well, how bad were they?... They wanted equality for everyone! What insanity!"

The encounters last week captured a fault line running through the opposition to President Donald Trump. Some activists want to push the Democratic Party further left. Others who are openly pro-socialist and pro-communist reject both major parties and see a Democratic victory as another opportunity to organize against the political establishment.

QUIET PART OUT LOUD: ANTI-ISRAEL MOBS WANT ONE STATE CALLED 'PALESTINE,' ERASING ISRAEL

Most importantly, a connective thread runs through the protests by the "red-green-green-blue" alliance: anti-Israel messaging.

Within the broader pro-Palestinian movement, activists advocate different goals, including a Palestinian "right of return" to Israel, a single democratic "one state" encompassing Israel and the Palestinian territories, an end to "Zionism" and Israel's status as a Jewish state, and the emergence of a "free, free Palestine" "from the river to the sea."

Proponents of a "one-state" solution argue that Palestinians and their descendants displaced following Israel's creation in 1948 should be permitted the "right of return" and receive "equal rights" in Israel. Critics contend that such a demographic transformation would end Israel's character as a Jewish homeland. Supporters invoke "rules-based international law" to describe their proposals as a framework for "human rights," "equal rights" and "self-determination" for Palestinians.

These fissures with centrist Democrats are being expressed by the leaders of groups including CodePink, BreakThrough BT Media, the People's Forum and Tricontinental, funded by American-born communist tycoon Neville Roy Singham, who lives in Shanghai, supporting the Chinese Communist Party. CodePink's Evans is married to Singham. Congressional investigators and Justice Department prosecutors have investigated Singham's activities and funding as possible malign foreign influence. No charges have been filed against Singham or the groups he has funded.

Even with Jeffries' leadership survival, their targets include the two Democrats positioned to lead the incoming congressional Democratic majority – Schumer and Jeffries – with disputes over U.S. support for Israel among the grievances raised by activists interviewed by Fox News Digital.

The confrontations harken back to the protests surrounding the 2024 Democratic National Convention in Chicago, where demonstrators gathered at Union Park and marched toward the convention, denouncing Vice President Kamala Harris as "Killer Kamala" and President Joe Biden as "Genocide Joe" for supporting Israel in its campaign against Hamas' Oct. 7, 2023, attack on Israel.

Some of the same organizations and activists challenging Democrats today were gathered in Union Park back then.

Two years later, Democrats’ midterm victories have brought another round of demands, demonstrations and threats to withhold support from Democratic Party leaders.

In an April 2025 interview with New York magazine, Alexander Soros, son of billionaire Democratic Party funder George Soros, expressed his frustration with the leftist environmentalist group Sunrise Movement, which has been heavily funded by Soros organizations.

"What the hell did they do, by the way?" Soros said in the interview. "We gave them money, and now all they do is talk about Palestine. It’s ridiculous."

The dynamics of the "chaos agents" ramped up even as votes were still being counted from the Democratic Party's "blue tsunami" wins in the midterm elections.

On Nov. 4, the day after the election, BreakThrough News host Rania Khalek, in D.C. last week for lobbying organized by CodePink, led a panel discussion with co-host Zoe Alexandra featuring the top Singham network personalities — BreakThrough News editor-in-chief Ben Becker; People's Forum executive director Manolo De Los Santos; and Tricontinental executive director Vijay Prashad — blasting the Democrats.

That day, the Democratic Socialists of America declared that voters had "emphatically endorsed socialism" and urged Americans to reject "the complacent center and the fascist right."

The next day, BreakThrough published a Khalek segment titled "Schumer & Jeffries reap the backlash to the genocide they enabled," accusing the Democratic leaders of benefiting politically from opposition to policies they had helped sustain.

Khalek followed the next day with a written appeal linking to her "BT Live" segment. She accused Democratic leaders of serving wealthy interests and argued that sustained organizing could force policy concessions.

"Do not go back to brunch. Get organized," she wrote.

Also on Nov. 5, Our Revolution, a 501(c)(4) political nonprofit backed by Sen. Bernie Sanders, an elder statesman revered by members of the Democratic Socialists of America, made clear that its leadership challenge extended to both chambers. "Real question: how do people calling for Schumer to step down justify giving Hakeem Jeffries a pass?" the group posted.

By Saturday, Nov. 7, that pressure was visible on Manhattan’s streets.

Demonstrators marched from Times Square toward Seventh Avenue and 14th Street in a rally organized by groups including the Democratic Socialists of America’s New York City chapter. The protests were cast as anti-Trump but there was another theme in the crowd: anti-Democratic Party.

Among their chants: "Jeffries, Schumer let’s be clear, ICE is not wanted here."

Demonstrators held signs reading, "Remove Jeffries / Schumer" and "CALL JEFFRIES & SCHUMER / TELL THEM TO STEP ASIDE."

One sign repeated the thread of anti-Israel activism, aimed also at the pro-Israel lobbying group, the American Israel Public Affairs Committee: "They are not capable of meeting this moment. We need FIGHTERS with BACKBONE & VISION – not corporate & AIPAC hacks."

By Sunday, Nov. 8, the Party for Socialism and Liberation, a communist group working out of the headquarters of the People's Forum on Wesr 37th Street in Manhattan, attacked Schumer directly.

"Chuck Schumer isn’t an ‘architect’ of anything except the genocide in Gaza," the organization wrote, adding that Schumer had no place leading a Senate "which should be abolished anyway."

By early last week, the pressure campaign had reached Schumer’s and Jeffries' offices.

At 12:51 p.m. on Tuesday, Nov. 10, CodePink's Benjamin tried to enter Schumer's office in Hart Room 322, and posted a video with the message: "Chuck Schumer locked his office on us!"

The next day, at 10:35 a.m., the activists reached Jeffries' office in Rayburn Room 2267 and Benjamin recorded a staged statement outside the door for CodePink's social media account.

On Thursday, Nov. 12, Barmada, wearing her red costume, joined a new confrontation at Jeffries’ office, filming as Capitol Police escorted other activists away. Iran's state-run Press TV later promoted the protest on its anti-U.S. propaganda platform. Barmada didn't respond to a request for comment.

Across the Capitol complex, at 2:55 p.m., Schumer's staff faced the sit-in staged by Sunrise Movement's protest organizers, chanting on script, "Hey hey, ho ho, Schumer has got to go!"

Capitol Police arrested 54 activists during the Sunrise Movement action, and a battalion of influencers splashed the news across social media in a series of similar posts.

By Thursday night, amid the coordinated campaign by the left, the campaign to replace Schumer had a declared candidate. Van Hollen announced he would seek the majority leader’s job, and El-Sayed endorsed him.

Just days earlier, El-Sayed had smiled quietly as the board president of the Arab America Foundation led a supportive crowd in chants of "Free, free Palestine," "from the river to the sea."

El-Sayed previously served on the board of the Sunrise Movement, whose organizers have been demanding Schumer's removal. The relationship illustrates how the pressure campaign extends beyond street demonstrations to elected Democrats with histories in activist organizations.

That day, Democratic Socialists of America published a message, "SAY NO TO SCHUMER," over images of the leader. Soon after, the "World Socialist Web Site," representing a pro-communist "world Trotskyist movement" and "Marxist world outlook," promoted the "SAY NO TO SCHUMER" campaign, touting Van Hollen as a "lifelong servant of the Democratic establishment."

The next day, CodePink's D.C. organizer, Olivia DiNucci, regularly seen heckling public officials and lawmakers, responded with a triumphant video, calling on activists to lobby against Schumer in the halls of Congress and flood congressional officials with the message: "VOTE CHRIS, NOT CHUCK."

And, wearing a hot pink T-shirt with the graphic of a watermelon, a quirky symbol of Palestinian "resistance," and "Falasteen," the Arabic word for a Palestinian homeland, CodePink's Benjamin responded that day by urging support for Van Hollen, citing as her first reason: "He has been a forceful critic of Israel's assault on Palestinians."

The campaign to oust Schumer is now drawing support from two of the Democratic Party’s most established "progressive" political nonprofits: Indivisible Project and MoveOn.org Civic Action, both 501(c)(4) nonprofits.

Indivisible Project says it opposes Schumer's "coronation," and Indivisible Brooklyn is promoting an "Even New Yorkers Don’t Want Chuck" week of action, including a Times Square rally on Thursday, co-sponsored by other Indivisible chapters.

MoveOn reports that 75% of participating members who voted support Van Hollen.

Meanwhile, last Friday, Barmada posted a video with one of the activists arrested at Jeffries’ office. She wore a T-shirt that said, "FREE AMERICA FROM ISRAEL," with the image of an Israeli flag over the mouth of the Statue of Liberty.

The back of her shirt read: "ISRAEL GETS BILLIONS / AMERICANS PAY THE BILL."

She accused Jeffries of being "a mouthpiece for Israel, AIPAC, the Israeli lobby," and declared: "The Democrats are spineless."

After the vote paving the way for Jeffries to become House speaker Wednesday, the activists in the red-green-green-blue alliance weren't relenting in their anti-Schumer campaign.

At 2:05 p.m., the Council on American-Islamic Relations issued an "Action Alert" to support Van Hollen against "AIPAC's toxic influence over our politics." The appeal followed an endorsement of Van Hollen by the U.S. Council of Muslim Organizations, an umbrella coalition of American Muslim institutions.

At 4:49 p.m., a far-left 501(c)(4) political nonprofit, Showing Up for Racial Justice, which organizes "white people for racial and economic justice," announced it is hosting phone banks to "FIRE CHUCK."

Around then, Sunrise Movement posted a video of actor Mark Ruffalo, speaking to the camera as he blasted Schumer's support of an environmental bill, gripping his forehead with his hands and saying, "God bless him," as he spoke about Schumer.

A follower responded: "Sorry folks but red and blue are two sides of the same bloody coin."
