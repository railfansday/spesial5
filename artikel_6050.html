Abu Dhabi is defending its F1 rate premium on a thinner base of bookings, while Doha has already cut. Late, local-heavy demand will decide whether that holds or forces last-minute discounting.

Abu Dhabi hotels are pricing this year’s Formula 1 weekend at last year’s level despite the U.S.-Iran war, while Doha’s race-weekend rates have fallen 29%.

Abu Dhabi hotels are advertising race-weekend rooms (Dec. 3-5) at an average of AED 2,692 ($733) a night, up 2.4% on last year, according to hospitality analytics firm Lighthouse. That is 184% above the weekend before (AED 948) and 116% more than the weekend after (AED 1,244).

In Doha, asking hotel rates for the Nov. 26-28 race weekend average QAR 1,206 ($331), down 29% from 2025. The rates are 67% above the weekend before (QAR 720) and 78% above the weekend after (QAR 677).

Gulf hotels are heading into the final two races of the 2026 Formula 1 season with sharply different pricing. Abu Dhabi hotels are advertising race-weekend rooms for Dec. 3-5 at an average of AED 2,692 ($733) a night, up 2.4% on last year and 184% above the weekend before, according to Lighthouse. Doha's asking rates for the Nov. 26-28 race weekend average QAR 1,206 ($331), down 29% year over year, though still 67% above the preceding weekend. Short-term rentals show the same split: race-weekend ADR on the books is up 44.7% in Abu Dhabi and down 8.6% in Doha, while demand nights on the books are down about a third in both cities, according to AirDNA data as of Nov. 10. Search patterns have changed too. Lighthouse's Blake Reiter said last year's steady October and November search build-up has not happened this year and weekly searches have stayed flat, with people booking later in the cycle. Local residents account for a larger share of hotel searches in both cities since the second week of September, U.S. search volume for Abu Dhabi is down 67%, and the U.S. share of Doha searches has fallen to 1.4% from 4.8%. Formula 1 said on Nov. 10 that it had stood down Imola as a backup venue and that the Qatar and Abu Dhabi Grands Prix would go ahead as planned, after the cancellation of races in Bahrain and Saudi Arabia in April.
