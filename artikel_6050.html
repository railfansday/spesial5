White House blasts 'Trumpcare for All' pitch from far-left El-Sayed who called president 'unserious fool'

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Michigan Sen.-elect Abdul El-Sayed references an AI-generated image depicting President Donald Trump as Jesus Christ while sarcastically calling the president "Habibi" to embrace "Medicare for All." (Credit: X @AbdulElSayed)

The White House blasted far-left Michigan Democrat Sen.-elect Abdul El-Sayed's pitch to President Donald Trump to embrace "Trumpcare for All," warning that the Medicare for All proposal would threaten Americans' existing health coverage.

"The Radical Democrats’ socialist proposal for a government takeover of healthcare should really be called ‘Medicare for None’ as it destroys access to the life-saving care Americans rely on daily," White House spokeswoman Allison Schuster told Fox News Digital.

The sharp response came after El-Sayed, who recently called Trump an "unserious fool," posted a 16-second video Tuesday urging the president to embrace the progressive proposal.

Democrat Senator-elect Abdul El-Sayed has said he'll call Medicare for All proposal "Trumpcare for All" if the president agrees to go along with it. (Evan Cobb for The Washington Post via Getty Images, File)

"I think there is a responsibility that we have to engage with [Trump] as the unserious fool that he is," El-Sayed said during a recent appearance on "Pod Save America," according to earlier Fox News Digital reporting.

Despite those remarks, the senator-elect struck a more conciliatory tone in his latest appeal.

"Donald. Habibi. Midterms were rough. I get it," El-Sayed said in the video posted Nov. 17, addressing Trump with the Arabic term "habibi," commonly translated as "my dear" or "my beloved."

"Trumpcare for All. It's really popular. People really want healthcare. You can put your name all over it."

EL-SAYED ADMITS FREE HEALTHCARE MEANS PAYING 'A LITTLE BIT MORE IN TAXES' REGARDLESS OF INCOME

Despite those calling President Trump "unserious," El-Sayed has seemingly struck a more conciliatory tone in his latest appeal. (Asra Q. Nomani/Fox News Digital, File)

The video featured a mock "Trumpcare" card and an AI-generated image depicting Trump as Jesus Christ, which El-Sayed, a Muslim, referenced while urging the president to embrace socialized, taxpayer-funded healthcare.

"Donald, this is your chance to heal the sick (like in that one AI image. You know the one)," El-Sayed said.

The White House instead pointed to Trump's efforts to lower prescription drug prices, contrasting those policies with the single-payer system advocated by El-Sayed.

"President Trump is focused on taking on the special interests that are driving up the costs of healthcare for all Americans including through his historic efforts to ensure American patients never pay more than those in other developed nations for all prescription drugs, which has already saved Americans well over $1 Billion," Schuster said.

In response to El-Sayed, the White House said, in part, "The President will continue delivering real results that lower costs and put Americans first." (Chip Somodevilla/Getty Images, File)

"The President will continue delivering real results that lower costs and put Americans first, while Democrats push ideas that have devastated economies and ruined healthcare quality anywhere they have been implemented."

The administration also cited a U.S. Council of Economic Advisers' analysis estimating that Medicare for All would increase the federal budget deficit by approximately $47.4 trillion between 2027 and 2036 in the absence of additional taxes or revenues.

The White House also cited a CEA projection that Medicare for All would shift large numbers of Americans away from employer-sponsored insurance and into a new government-run coverage system.

Medicare for All, a sweeping single-payer health care proposal championed by Democratic socialists, would replace much of the nation's private health insurance system with federally financed coverage. Supporters argue it would guarantee coverage, while critics warn of substantial tax increases and massive federal spending.

Sen.-elect Abdul El-Sayed and other far-left Democrats are in favor of Medicare for All, a single-payer health care proposal that would replace much of the nation's private health insurance system with federally financed coverage. (Erin Kirkland/Bloomberg via Getty Images, File)

El-Sayed's public appeal was not his first attempt to recruit Trump to the idea.

During a CNN appearance earlier this month, the incoming senator suggested that the president could attach his name to a universal health care proposal.

"Donald, if you're watching, we can put your name on it. I know you like to put your name on stuff," El-Sayed said, according to a Nov. 10 social media post by CNN anchor Kaitlan Collins.

The unusual overture follows a contentious Michigan Senate race in which El-Sayed defeated Republican Mike Rogers.

The Democrat also mocked Trump, Vice President JD Vance and Rogers during his election victory remarks, adding to the contrast between his earlier criticism of the administration and his latest invitation to collaborate.

El-Sayed is scheduled to take office Jan. 3, 2027.

El-Sayed's office did not immediately respond to Fox News Digital's request for comment.
