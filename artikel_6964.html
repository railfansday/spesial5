Engineering Manager - Pipelines at Supabase

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Supabase is the Postgres development platform, built by developers for developers. We provide a complete backend solution including Database, Auth, Storage, Edge Functions, Realtime, and Vector Search. All services are deeply integrated and designed for growth.

We're looking for an Engineering Manager to lead the Pipelines team at Supabase. Supabase Pipelines is a managed change data capture product. It copies the tables you select from Supabase Postgres to other systems, then streams later changes. The product is in public alpha, with BigQuery, ClickHouse, DuckLake and Snowflake destinations.

Pipelines uses Postgres logical replication and Supabase ETL, our open source replication engine written in Rust. The team works close to the database. That means replication slots, WAL retention, initial table syncs, and the load they place on a customer's source database. The team also builds and runs the destination connectors. Correctness, ordering and recovery matter there as much as speed.

The Engineering Manager is a people, delivery and technical role. We don't expect you to write production code. We do expect you to join design discussions and help set the bar for engineering quality, testing and operations.

Build a healthy, high-trust team culture in a remote-first, globally distributed environment.

Run 1:1s, performance management, career development, and onboarding for your direct reports.

Handle people admin (leave, time off, etc.) for your team.

Own the hiring plan for your team and act as Hiring Manager. First round of interviewer for all roles on the team.

Partner with Talent Acquisition to source, interview, and close strong candidates.

Contribute to headcount planning, org design, and the evolution of engineering leadership at Supabase.

Own the team's roadmap and project management (Linear), driving accountability and clear milestones.

Manage workflow intake and Slack triage. These are the team’s primary inboxes for cross-team requests. You decide what gets routed where, prioritized, or pushed back on.

Own the team's weekly updates and keep stakeholders aligned without creating overhead.

Organize effort against support tickets and maintain the team's SLO.

Surface risks, dependencies, and tradeoffs early and clearly to leadership.

Train as an Incident Commander. Drive coordination, resolution, and post-incident follow-up.

Have 5+ years of engineering background, with enough depth to be credible with engineers building complex systems.

Have 3+ years managing remote engineering teams.

Have managed a team that built and ran data pipelines, change data capture, replication or streaming systems.

Have strong technical credibility in data systems. We look for delivery guarantees, ordering, idempotency, schema changes and backfills.

Know Postgres replication well, including logical replication, the WAL and replication slots, and how they affect a production database.

Know analytical destinations such as BigQuery, Snowflake, ClickHouse or lakehouse table formats like Iceberg.

Have led a team through production incidents on a system that holds customer data. You ran the follow-up work and kept the review blameless.

Have shipped a developer-facing data product to customers outside your company.

Have experience with Rust (not required but a plus)

Have managed remote, globally distributed teams that work asynchronously across disparate time zones.

Are technical enough to be credible with strong engineers, but genuinely passionate about people management. This is the work you want to do, not a track you're settling for.

Build psychological safety and a feedback culture. You give feedback often and in the moment, ask for it back, and create space for the team to do the same.

Operate as a multiplier. You help individuals achieve more than they could alone, and you don't need to be the best technical voice in the room.

Coach rather than direct. You remove blockers, connect people to context, and trust your engineers to own how the work gets done.

Have a real track record of performance management and can speak to specific examples of difficult conversations.

Are comfortable with ambiguity and can build structure where there isn't much.

Communicate clearly and write well. You default to async and written, set context early, and surface risks early.

Are collaborative, low-ego and adaptable. You thrive with high-autonomy senior engineers who don't need you to be the smartest person in the room.
