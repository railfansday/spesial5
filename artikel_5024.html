Galaxy Withdraws Call That Bitcoin’s Bear Market Ended in June

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Galaxy has withdrawn its October call that bitcoin’s bear market ended in June, saying the technical signal behind it failed after seven straight weeks of losses and that the bear market is “very likely not over.”

Alex Thorn, Galaxy’s head of firmwide research, laid out the reversal in a report published Wednesday and sent to clients a day earlier. “We withdraw our call that the bear market ended in June,” the report said. “The signal it rested on has failed.”

The firm’s Oct. 26 note had argued that bitcoin had entered an accumulation phase. Galaxy said at the time that a weekly close back below the 50-week moving average that was not regained within a month would invalidate its technical case.

That is what happened. Bitcoin closed the week ended Nov. 8 at $74,968, 0.7% below the 50-week average, after seven weekly closes above it. It then closed the week ended Nov. 15 below the 200-week average, which Galaxy has described as the floor of past bear markets.

The week ended Nov. 22 closed at $61,104, 17.9% below the 50-week average and 10.4% below the 200-week average of $68,159. Bitcoin traded below its July 1 intraday low on Nov. 20 and reached $55,100 on Nov. 21, but the June 30 closing low of $58,525 held on a daily basis through Nov. 22.

Galaxy now treats that level as the line to watch. A daily close below $58,525 would be “the start of a move to a materially lower low,” the report said, and the firm would not treat such a close as a buying signal by itself.

History is not kind to breaks like that. In the seven prior bear market cases where bitcoin closed below a low that had stood for at least 60 days, the eventual low was at least 17% lower, with a median of 52%. Applied to the June low, that works out to about $48,500 and $28,200.

Bitcoin briefly slumped below $57,500 on Wednesday morning as the Nasdaq sank, but it ended the day back above $60,000 and has not posted a daily close below $58,525. It traded near $60,800 on Thursday morning.

Galaxy said two readings of the market disagree. By the calendar, this bear is old: Nov. 22 was day 412 since the Oct. 6, 2025, record, longer than the 163 to 406 days from high to low in the four prior bear markets.

By depth, it looks unfinished. Bitcoin’s 53% decline compares with drops of 77% to 93% in earlier bears. It also has not closed a day below its realized price of $53,205, something every prior bear did for at least 140 days, and only two of the 13 bottom signals Galaxy tracks have been met.

“The clock says this bear is overdue to end, the depth says it has further to fall, and we do not believe the price record alone can settle which,” the report said.

Flows have not helped. Galaxy estimates U.S. spot bitcoin ETPs hold about 1.24 million BTC, 9.7% below their October 2025 peak, after shedding 71,339 BTC, or $4.95 billion, since Oct. 4. November is shaping up as a record month for ETF outflows.

Galaxy said the other three parts of its October thesis, covering positioning, fundamentals and sentiment, are stronger now than when it published. Its proprietary Bitcoin Fear & Greed Index fell to 5.2 on Nov. 21, its lowest reading since June 30, 2022.

To swing back toward its October view, Galaxy wants a weekly close above the 200-week average and then the 50-week average, now $74,401, with the June weekly closing low of $59,517 holding. A weekly close below realized price would point to a deeper bear.

“We are not trying to call the low,” the report said. “Until one gives way, we will judge the market on weekly closes rather than headlines.”
