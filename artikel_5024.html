Wells Fargo More Than Doubles Its Bitcoin ETF Stake to $2.35 Billion

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Wells Fargo more than doubled its holdings of BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust in the third quarter to 49.6 million shares worth $2.35 billion at Sept. 30, though bitcoin’s slide since then has cut the stake’s value to about $1.82 billion.

The bank disclosed the position in a Form 13F filed Monday, covering the quarter ended Sept. 30. It reported 49,577,094 IBIT shares across five of its units, up from 24,396,222 shares worth $812.2 million in its filing for June 30. That is an increase of about 103% in share count. The reported value rose about 189%, because IBIT climbed from $33.29 to $47.34 a share during the quarter.

The fund closed at $36.79 on Monday. At that price, the same 49.6 million shares are worth roughly $1.82 billion, about $520 million less than the value in the filing.

The filing does not break out how much of the position belongs to the bank and how much to its customers. But the largest single block, 32,483,714 shares worth about $1.54 billion, sits under Wells Fargo Clearing Services, the brokerage entity behind Wells Fargo Advisors. Wells Fargo Advisors Financial Network reported another 9.5 million shares, and Wells Fargo Bank reported 7.4 million.

That points to a position built largely by wealth-management clients buying the ETF in brokerage and advisory accounts, rather than a directional trade by the bank itself. A 13F lists securities over which a firm has investment discretion, which at a big bank includes managed client money.

Wells Fargo Securities, the investment-banking and trading arm, also reported IBIT options: calls on 717,200 shares and puts on 540,000 shares, valued at about $34.0 million and $25.6 million. Those figures reflect the value of the underlying shares, not the premiums paid, and they may hedge other positions. Both lines were smaller than at midyear, when the unit listed calls on 1.12 million shares and puts on 845,000.

Wells Fargo also added to its other spot bitcoin funds. Grayscale’s Bitcoin Mini Trust rose to 3.09 million shares, worth $114.1 million, from 2.56 million. Bitwise’s bitcoin ETF rose to 1.6 million shares, worth $72.6 million, from 1.38 million.

The bank moved the other way on Strategy (MSTR). Wells Fargo’s long position in the bitcoin treasury company fell to 1,339,018 shares, worth $205.0 million at Sept. 30, from 1,994,253 shares at midyear, a drop of about 33%. Wells Fargo Securities also listed Strategy puts on 795,000 shares, down from 1.52 million. Strategy closed at $103.03 on Monday, against $153.09 at the end of September.

The filing lands as big banks disclose larger bitcoin exposure through ETFs and treasury stocks. JPMorgan reported $540 million in bitcoin ETFs in its own third-quarter 13F last week. Wells Fargo’s IBIT stake is more than four times that.

The timing also stands out. Since the quarter ended, U.S. spot bitcoin funds have been losing money fast. IBIT bled about $2 billion over nine straight sessions through mid-November, and bitcoin fell to about $62,000 over the weekend before trading near $64,000 on Monday. The fund took in about $76.5 million on Monday, breaking an 11-session streak of outflows that had pulled roughly $2.35 billion from IBIT since Oct. 30. That is almost exactly the value Wells Fargo put on its own stake at the end of September.

Wells Fargo’s filing is a snapshot as of Sept. 30. It does not show whether the bank or its clients bought or sold IBIT shares in October or November, the period in which bitcoin fell from above $83,000 to the low $60,000s. The next round of 13Fs, covering the fourth quarter, is due in February.
