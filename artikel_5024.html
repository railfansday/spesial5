German Court Offers to Drop Movie2k Case for 40,000 Bitcoin

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

A Leipzig judge last week offered to dismiss the criminal case against the alleged operator of the defunct Movie2k piracy site if he hands over 40,000 bitcoin, a stake worth about $2.56 billion at current prices. The presiding judge at the Leipzig Regional Court, Karsten Nickel, made the proposal in a trial that began in February.

A spokesman for the Dresden prosecutors’ office confirmed it after German newspapers and the public broadcaster MDR first reported the offer. Heise later published a detailed account.

The trial is before the court’s economic criminal chamber, and the Leipzig court itself had not issued its own confirmation when prosecutors spoke to reporters. Prosecutors believe the defendant still holds about 56,000 bitcoin, and transferring 40,000 would leave him roughly 16,000 coins. At a bitcoin price of about $64,000, those 16,000 coins would be worth about $1.02 billion, and forty thousand bitcoin would be worth about $2.56 billion at the same price. The dollar figures are approximate.

The proposal also calls for him to pay €38 million in compensation for potential victims, including film rights holders, MDR reported. He would also have to give up any claim to the roughly €2.64 billion Saxony already raised from bitcoin he transferred in 2024, the newspaper Freie Presse reported. Even after handing over most of the remaining coins, he could leave court a billionaire.

Movie2k.to ran from 2008 until it was shut down in May 2013. Prosecutors say its operators made millions from advertising contracts and used the proceeds to buy bitcoin when the price was far lower. The defendant is charged with commercial money laundering in 146 cases. The court has already ruled that about 220,000 copyright counts are time-barred and are no longer part of the Movie2k case.

A co-defendant, a Polish national, is charged with money laundering in 46 cases and especially serious tax evasion. Prosecutors say he received bitcoin from the main defendant in May 2013 as pay for work on the site. Nickel has said that proof of some of the charges would face “great difficulties.” It is conceivable, he said, that legally and illegally obtained bitcoin were mixed in the accounts.

German authorities had sought the defendant internationally since late 2019. He was arrested abroad in May 2023 and held in pretrial detention in Saxony until mid-January 2024. On Jan. 16, 2024, he voluntarily transferred about 49,858 bitcoin to wallets provided by the Federal Criminal Police Office. At the time, the coins were worth about €1.96 billion, and he was released from detention around the time of that transfer.

Saxony sold the coins between June 19 and July 12, 2024, with support from the Federal Criminal Police Office, for €2,639,683,413.92, or about €2.64 billion. Prosecutors described the sale as an emergency liquidation required by bitcoin’s volatility. The proceeds are held by the state’s justice treasury and still belong to the defendant until there is a final confiscation ruling. Bitcoin Magazine has covered that sale.

Two other men in the Movie2k investigation were already convicted in 2023 and received suspended sentences. One was a co-operator of the site. The other handled payments.

A co-defendant in the Leipzig trial was offered terms of 600 bitcoin and €17 million, and his lawyer said she could imagine accepting that. The main defendant’s lawyers said they wanted to confer. The Dresden prosecutors’ office and the defense are still reviewing the Movie2k proposal, and neither side has announced a decision.

If both sides agree, the trial could end before Christmas. If they do not, the case will continue, and the court will still have to decide whether to confiscate the 2024 sale proceeds and any bitcoin he still holds.
