Antonov An-124 Flies 102-Tonne Power Plant Rotor From Germany To Malaysia With 5 Stops

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Aircraft charter specialist Chapman Freeborn has announced its involvement in an Antonov An-124 charter from Stuttgart, Germany to Malaysia in July 2026. Executing the route meant making five intermediate stops, and overcoming high temperatures and conflict-related complications in the Middle East.

Chapman Freeborn completed this cargo flight to move a powerplant generator rotor and its transport frame. The total cargo weight was 226,739 lb (102,847 kg). Along with the enormous cargo aircraft, a specialist road transporter and lifting equipment was needed to complete the mission, with the component also needing to visit a facility in the United Kingdom.

The reason Chapman Freeborn was contracted for this specialist heavy-lift operation was that a powerplant generator motor from Malaysia had been undergoing repair work in Germany, along with high-speed balancing in the United Kingdom. Air transportation was needed because the powerplant's generating capacity was out of operation while the motor was away

Despite needing five stops, the time required for Chapman Freeborn to complete the mission was still significantly shorter than the two months each way required to use sea freight. Anton Lomakin, the VP of Cargo Air Chartering (ASEAN) at Chapman Freeborn, said:

"With a shipment of this scale, the challenge is not just finding an aircraft capable of carrying the cargo, but managing the operational constraints that come with it."

Taking this large and heavy powerplant component across the world required Chapman Freeborn to put together specialist equipment and expertise. This begins with the aircraft, an An-124, which has a maximum takeoff weight of 864,000 lb (392,000 kg) and a maximum payload of 264,500 lb (120,000 kg). Chapman Freeborn also had to enlist specialist equipment on the ground. A heavy-duty road transporter successfully moved the generator rotor within Europe. Top-of-the-range lifting equipment carried the rotor and its transport frame from the road transporter onto the aircraft while on the tarmac in Stuttgart. Ground crews had to pay close attention to positioning, ensuring that the aircraft's center of gravity wasn't thrown off balance.

Carrying cargo of this mass and with unique characteristics brings extreme challenges at the best of times. The component was so close to the aircraft's maximum payload that the amount of fuel it could carry was limited. Furthermore, Malaysia and Germany are in very different time zones. Chapman Freeborn claims that Max Pätzel, Cargo Charter Broker, was working from 5:00 or 6:00 AM local time.

Meanwhile, associates in Singapore weren't leaving their desks until 1:00 or 2:00 AM, due to the number of different time zones that stakeholders were spread across. Furthermore, this July offered far from optimal conditions for the bespoke freight mission, leading to the required five stops to complete the mission, despite the aircraft having a maximum range of 8,900 nautical miles (16,500 km).

Firstly, this July saw extreme summer temperatures. Highs in Stuttgart reached 99°F (37°C) on peak summer days. Daytime highs in Malaysia were around 88°F to 95°F (31°C to 35°C), with problematic humidity also a factor. The conditions meant that the Antonov could not fly at its peak performance.

The ongoing conflict in the Middle East also caused problems for Chapman Freeborn's work. The available routing options were cut down substantially, with the An-124 having to stay well clear of conflict zones. That meant more international borders had to be crossed as the aircraft made each stop.
