My husband and I have always dreamed of visiting London, but we knew we wanted to do it the right way: comfortable flights and a chic hotel stay, with room left in our budget for things like afternoon tea, souvenirs and even a day trip.

All of that can sometimes require some pretty deep pockets, which is why we spent the past year allocating our spending strategically, using my to earn points toward both our flights and our hotel stay.

But like anyone on a budget, we wanted to get the most bang for our “point.” So when American Express last offered a 30% transfer bonus to the Virgin Atlantic Flying Club for eligible U.S. cardmembers, I knew I had to jump on the opportunity and book our trip asap. The good news? Amex is offering this same transfer bonus to Virgin again, now through Nov. 30, 2026.

Here’s how I made the most of the transfer bonus, including the redemptions I made and how you can do the same with the current offer.

First, let’s break down how the bonus works:

The American Express Membership Rewards® program is offering a 30% transfer bonus to Virgin Atlantic Flying Club for eligible U.S. cardmembers until Nov. 30, 2026. That means every 1,000 Membership Rewards points you transfer becomes 1,300 Virgin Points®. Terms apply. Note that Amex points must be transferred in multiples of 1,000, with a minimum of 1,000 points.

Now I’m no math whiz, but I do know that means 100,000 Membership Rewards points would convert to 130,000 Virgin Points, instead of the standard 1:1 ratio. That's one of the best transfer rates Amex offers.

The best part about Virgin as a transfer partner is its surprising breadth. You can redeem your points for flights on Virgin Atlantic, as well as stays at Virgin Hotels and cruise sailings with Virgin Voyages.

I had built up a solid balance of Membership Rewards points through a combination of the Gold Card's welcome offer and my everyday spending, but the limited-time window of that bonus meant I couldn't afford to think about it for too long.

I live in Central Florida, and luckily for me, Virgin Atlantic offers nonstop flights from Orlando

International Airport (MCO) to London's Heathrow Airport (LHR). However, Virgin Atlantic also flies nonstop to LHR from airports across the U.S., including:

Personally, I’ve always been curious about Virgin’s premium economy cabin after hearing so many great things about it from my fellow TPG colleagues. So for our outbound flight, we booked premium economy, which comes with perks like extra legroom and an elevated meal service.

It cost me 35,000 Virgin Points per person, plus taxes and fees. With the 30% bonus, I only needed to transfer 54,000 Amex points for the two of us, which means I saved 16,000 Amex points on the outbound leg alone.

Note that taxes, fees and charges apply in addition to the points price for reward flights in all classes. These vary depending on factors such as your destination and travel dates, and the applicable amount is shown at the time of booking.

From check-in to landing, the premium economy cabin proved to be the perfect balance of value and comfort. Extra touches like a dedicated check-in desk, a glass of bubbly after boarding, upgraded dining and amenity kits made the experience feel so special. The wide leather seats with extra legroom meant we arrived in London well-rested and ready to explore. I could see why so many people rave about it; it was the perfect middle ground between budget-friendly and comfortable.

The real splurge was our return flight, which turned out to hardly be a splurge at all because I found a nonstop Upper Class flight home from LHR to MCO for just 29,000 Virgin Points per person, plus taxes and fees. Thanks to the bonus, I only needed to transfer 46,000 Amex points total for two people. That's 12,000 Membership Reward points saved on an already incredible redemption. Needless to say, I felt like I’d won the lottery.

I’ll be the first to tell you that it doesn’t get any better than Virgin’s Upper Class for R&R on a nine-hour flight across the Atlantic. The direct-aisle-access seats converted into lie-flat beds, and the amenity kits were well curated. Not to mention that the dining and inflight service made me feel like, well, royalty.

By the time we touched down in Orlando, I had slept through most of the flight, which is a rarity for me as a nervous flyer. We arrived home feeling restored rather than depleted.

Combined, our flights cost us 128,000 Virgin Points plus taxes and fees, which took 100,000 Amex points to cover. The transfer bonus saved my husband and me 28,000 Membership Rewards points across both flights, and we used them to redeem on Virgin Red for a rewards stay at Virgin Hotels London-Shoreditch.

Once I had committed to the Virgin Atlantic booking, staying at Virgin Hotels London-Shoreditch was a no-brainer with that 30% transfer bonus. And yes, when you transfer Membership Rewards points to the Virgin Atlantic Flying Club, you can use them toward a stay at a Virgin Hotels property.

Editor tip: Once you’ve transferred your Membership Reward points to Virgin,  check out our easy-to-follow guide to booking Virgin Hotels with Virgin Points.

On our dates, the stay came out to about 60,000 Virgin Points per night. For our four-night stay, I was looking at 240,000 points in total, which felt like a lot to give up. With the 30% transfer bonus, though, I only needed to transfer 185,000 Membership Rewards points, which was much easier to wrap my head around.

When I booked the hotel, I had a feeling using my points would be well worth it, and from the moment we arrived at Virgin Hotels London-Shoreditch, my suspicions were confirmed. The hotel sits in trendy East London, far enough from the crowds and tourist traps, but close to the Old Street Tube station for easy access to activities and dining across the city. It’s also a convenient 10-minute walk from Liverpool Street Station, which has direct access to LHR.

The hotel has quintessential Virgin design, with red accents everywhere, from the lobby carpets to the Smeg fridges in the guest rooms. Beyond the rooms, the property has a rooftop pool for the warmer months and a retro hi-fi listening bar called Hidden Grooves, which serves creative cocktails that are perfect after a long day exploring London.

As for how it compares to cash pricing, a comparable room size runs around $450-$550 per night. Our four-night stay on Virgin Points was equivalent to saving roughly $2,000 in cash, which suddenly made the Amex transfer bonus feel like money in the bank.

My Membership Rewards transfer to Virgin Atlantic Flying Club covered nearly every big-ticket item on our London trip: flights in both directions, including a lie-flat Upper Class seat home, and four nights at Virgin Hotels London-Shoreditch. With the 30% transfer bonus stretching every point further, we were able to spend our cash on experiences, dining and souvenirs.

If you've been sitting on a stash of Membership Rewards points, this is one of the best ways to use them right now, and you don’t need to go to London. Virgin Atlantic also flies nonstop from the U.S. to Manchester and Edinburgh and connecting through Heathrow opens up destinations like Barbados, Cape Town, Dubai, Mumbai and the Maldives.

Your points aren't limited to flights, either. You can put them toward a stay at Virgin Hotels or even a Virgin Voyages cruise. Just keep in mind that the 30% transfer bonus, available to eligible U.S. cardmembers, ends Nov. 30, 2026, so whether you're dreaming of a lie-flat seat across the Atlantic or days in the sun at sea, now is the time to book your next trip.
