Delta’s Amazon Leo Wi-Fi Plans Face Uncertainty After Rocket Failure

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Inflight Wi-Fi is currently undergoing quite the glow-up, thanks to the widespread rollout of Starlink Wi-Fi. Not only are most airlines offering this service for free, but it also represents a massive upgrade in terms of speeds.

While carriers like United, American, Southwest, and Alaska, are all rolling out Starlink Wi-Fi, Delta is countering the trend, and went with Amazon Leo, which is Amazon’s competitor to Starlink. This has of course made quite some headlines, due to Elon Musk’s public ridicule of Delta, plus what Delta CEO Ed Bastian reportedly said behind closed doors. Along those lines, there’s some potentially bad news for Delta.

On Sunday, November 15, 2026, a Vulcan rocket operated by United Launch Alliance (ULA), a joint venture between Boeing and Lockheed Martin, failed about 10 minutes after lifting off from Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida. This was the first mission failure in ULA’s roughly 20-year history, and the company has issued the following statement in response to this:

Based on data received by our Launch Director, we confirm that the Vulcan vehicle launched from Space Launch Complex 41 suffered an anomaly 10 minutes into the flight and failed to deliver the Amazon mission to orbit.

Every flight generates data, and every flight teaches us something. Since the anomaly just occurred, we don’t have any specific data yet to understand what occurred today.

ULA exists to provide assured access to space for our nation and for the customers who depend on us. That purpose is why we take on some of the hardest missions in spaceflight, and why we hold ourselves to the standard of mission success. Today, we did not meet that standard.

We will work closely with our customer and other appropriate organizations to find the cause, determine the appropriate corrective action, and return to flight as quickly as possible.

ULA is committed to full transparency with our customers, and they will have visibility into the investigation process, which utilizes rigorous methodology and corrective action.

We will provide further updates when new information becomes available.

This isn’t about the loss of 40 satellites as such, as that’s only a tiny percentage of the satellites that were supposed to be deployed as part of Amazon Leo, across dozens of missions. Instead, the issue is that this will now delay future launches, while an investigation is performed, improvements are made, etc.

Prior to this, 398 satellites had already been deployed for Amazon Leo, across 14 launches, making up around 12% of the 3,236 satellites that are needed for global coverage.

This rocket failure is absolutely a setback for the rollout of Amazon Leo, though it remains to be seen just how much of a delay it represents. Amazon has released a statement saying it remains confident that ULA will learn from the mission and deliver on their long term plans.

We know that Amazon Leo was targeted for an initial service rollout before the end of 2026 in select regions, with a more widespread introduction in the months that follow.

Rockets are far from my area of expertise, but based on my understanding from those who know more than I do, it seems that this likely represents a delay of several months to the program, at an absolute minimum, if not more. As you’d expect, a thorough investigation needs to be performed, any learnings need to be implemented, etc.

Keep in mind that JetBlue was supposed to introduce Amazon Leo as of 2027, and Delta was supposed to introduce Amazon Leo as of 2028. I think it’s safe to say that the timeline looks worse today than it did 24 hours ago.

One of the risks with picking a service that’s still in development is that the timeline is really quite fluid, and this is an example of that. Delta has of course made quite the headlines over its lack of selecting Starlink, and I imagine this will only make matters worse for the airline.

United expects to have Starlink on all planes by the end of 2027, while Delta was only going to start introducing Amazon Leo on less than half of its planes as of 2028. So the airline was already at a disadvantage there, and that disadvantage has just grown even bigger.

A Vulcan rocket operated by United Launch Alliance (ULA) suffered a failure on Sunday, as it was on a mission to deploy dozens of Amazon Leo satellites. While this was only a small part of the overall rollout of this, the failure will absolutely delay additional rocket launches, which are needed to get this network functioning. This was the first mission failure in ULA’s roughly 20-year history.

It’s anyone’s guess how much of a delay it will cause for the introduction of Amazon Leo, and what impact that will have on Delta and JetBlue. It’s absolutely certain that the timeline looks worse now than 24 hours ago, though we don’t know much more as of now.
