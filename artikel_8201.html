Anyone with a backyard the size of the storied “back 40” has oodles of choices when it comes to flowering shrubs – because space isn't an issue. But those of us with smaller yards need to make every inch count.

And the truth is, you don't need a large yard to enjoy a spectacular display in every season. Planting compact flowering shrubs in fall gives roots time to establish before winter, rewarding gardeners with an explosion of color the following spring. What types of shrubs should you consider? You’ll want space-saving shrubs that punch well above their size.

Below is my shortlist of compact flowering shrubs to plant in fall for spring blooms. While you may want to add a few more bushes to fill up your space, these gems are a great way to get started.

The old saying goes that April showers bring May flowers, but in fact, the best spring flowers come from all that autumn rain. Fall is a wonderful time to plant shrubs, perhaps the very best time, if you want spring blooms.

First, the air grows cooler as October arrives while the soil is still warm. This combination supports root growth and, combined with shorter days, reduces heat stress and evaporation. Add the likelihood of fall rain, and you’ve got a wonderful window to plant shrubs.

These fall conditions help your plants establish root systems during the cooler months. That means the shrubs can invest their energy in foliage and flowers when spring arrives.

So, ready to get started? Here are seven selections that might work well for you.

Yuki Cherry Blossom is a gorgeous, low-growing deutzia with adorable pink flowers. It grows to a foot or two tall and wide. Yuki Cherry Blossom makes a fabulous groundcover and is ideal for mass plantings. It delights with elegant pink-and-white bell flowers in spring, but the second act is just as dramatic: rich burgundy foliage in autumn. Try planting it as groundcover on a sunny slope.

The original Yuki Cherry Blossom (Deutzia × rosea ‘NCDX2’) is available to buy online from Nature Hills. But a newer Yuki Cherry Blossom 2.0 (Deutzia × ‘SMNDXI’) was released this year and is available at Plant Addicts. It has the same pink-and-white flowers, but they hold their color for longer, and it has a wider, tidier growing habit.

Regular lilacs can grow into imposing plants, too big sometimes for a compact yard. But you can still have a delightfully fragrant lilac if you pick a dwarf variety like Baby Kim. This small lilac tops out at 3 feet tall and wide, but boy, does it pack in a lot of beauty in spring, with shiny, dark green leaves and a cloud of fragrant purple flowers with a rounded shape that work well in almost any sunny corner. The plus? Butterflies love this!

Baby Kim lilac is available at Trees.com, which is running a temporary 20% site-wide discount.

Forsythia is no shrinking violet – it’s a tough, easy-growing, resilient, free-flowering plant. “Compact” is not a word usually associated with the species, but Show Off Sugar Baby, available at Plant Addicts, breaks the rule that forsythia shoots up quickly and gets taller than the gardener. It stops at 2.5 feet tall and wide, and – with its dense, egg-yolk yellow blooms – is a little spot of sunshine and joy in any sunny corner. Plant one as a stand-alone or a handful for a low hedge.

You want flowers? I mean oodles of flowers from late spring through the first frost? Check out Double Play Doozie (available at Nature Hills), a compact spirea shrub that grows in a mounding shape and is about 2–3 feet tall and wide. This cool cultivar doesn’t need deadheading or shearing for repeat blooming, offering vibrant crimson flower clusters all season long. This drought-tolerant, non-invasive plant is perfect for containers, mixed borders, foundations, and mass plantings.

This colorful native shrub offers shining white flowers that look like small fireworks and is THE shrub for a shady corner or mixed borders. The pure white blossoms are scented and stand out on the tidy mound of dark green foliage that grows to around 4 feet tall and wide.

ollinator-friendly, Little Henry sweetspire offers strong landscape appeal for a low-to-no-maintenance plant and provides a fiery fall display. It prefers moist soils and will tolerate wet conditions. Order Little Henry online at Trees.com.

Here’s the compact plant that will light up your garden from the first days of spring. Cameo quince, available from DAS Farms via Amazon, puts on a spectacular show starting in spring, with lush, lovely double blossoms, peachy petals, and dark green, shiny leaves. Since the flowers arrive on bare branches, the show is exceptional. Later, expect small, fragrant yellow-green fruits in late spring and summer. They become bitter when they ripen in fall, but they're best used for preserves.

Cameo flowering quince reaches about 4 feet tall and wide and prefers moist, well-drained soil and at least 5 hours of sun per day. Use it as a specimen to highlight its star power, or plant it in flowering shrub borders.

Okay, warm-zone gardeners: here’s the compact flowering shrub for you. Mexican honeysuckle offers stunning bright orange flowers through most of the growing season, pleasing hummingbirds and butterflies, as well as the gardener. These tubular flowers against the light green foliage add a soft, natural, tropical look to your landscape. It grows in a mounded shape in filtered shade. Order Mexican honeysuckle at Walmart.
