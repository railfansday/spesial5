Senegal confirmed AFCON 2025 champions after appeal

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The Court of Arbitration for Sport has ruled Senegal to be the winners of the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations, which concluded in January; Senegal beat Morocco 1-0 in the final but were stripped of the title in March, which led to their appeal

The Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) has announced Senegal as the winners of the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations (AFCON).

In response, the Royal Moroccan Football Federation said it was 'astonished' by the decision and 'affirms its intention to pursue all available legal procedures to appeal the decision'.

Senegal beat Morocco 1-0 in the final in January but were stripped of the title by a Confederation of African Football (CAF) appeal board in March for breaking competition rules by walking off the pitch because of a late disallowed goal and a controversial penalty awarded to their opponents.

Senegal's protest caused a delay of over 15 minutes and when their players returned to the pitch, Morocco's Brahim Diaz missed the penalty to force the match into extra time, with Pape Gueye going on to score the winner.

"The Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) has upheld the appeal by the Senegalese Football Federation (FSF) against the Confederation of African Football (CAF) and the the Royal Moroccan Football Federation (FRMF) concerning the final of the Africa Cup of Nations Morocco 2025 (AFCON 2025)," a CAS statement read.

"The decision rendered by the CAF appeal board is set aside and the decision by the CAF disciplinary board is reinstated. Consequently, the result obtained at the end of the AFCON 2025 final is confirmed (1-0 for Senegal)."

Morocco's appeal was initially rejected but a CAF panel did uphold it and found Senegal's walk-off meant they had forfeited the match, awarding a 3-0 win to Morocco.

CAF said Senegal infringed on article 82 of AFCON's regulations, which states if a team refuses to play or leaves the ground before the end of regulation time without the referee's authorisation, it will be considered the loser and eliminated from the competition.

Article 84 states any team that violates article 82 will be permanently eliminated and lose the match 3-0.

However, the IFAB laws of the game state: "The decisions of the referee regarding facts connected with play, including whether or not a goal is scored and the result of the match, are final."

Senegal appealed to CAS, calling the decision "a travesty", and have now been reinstated as champions.

The Moroccan federation wrote to the CAS earlier on Tuesday asking them to postpone the announcement of their decision. They wanted CAS to investigate allegations that one of the members of the CAS panel - Ulrich Haas - spoke on the telephone to the president of the Senegal FA Abdoulaye Fall on Sunday.

CAS confirmed it had received a challenge petition 'a few minutes' before the announcement but decided 'there were insufficient grounds at this stage to postpone the notification of the award'.

In April, IFAB - who are football's law makers - introduced a law stating that any players who leave the field of play in protest at a referee's decision can be sent off. The adoption of this law is at the discretion of the competition organiser.

Sky Sports News' Chief Correspondent Kaveh Solhekol:

"Common sense has prevailed because the Court of Arbitration for Sport have effectively confirmed that Senegal did win the 2025 AFCON.

"Morocco were very unhappy because they felt that the rules of the tournament had been broken by Senegal when they left the pitch. They felt that at that stage Senegal should have forfeited the game.

"They went to the African Confederation Appeal Board and there was an appeal hearing in March. That appeal decided that yes, Morocco were right and they should be the winners and the result of the game was changed from a 1-0 win to Senegal to a 3-0 win to Morocco.

"Then we had the next chapter in this story because Senegal were also very unhappy with the decision of the appeal board, so they went to CAS in Switzerland. There was a hearing in October and now we have had the decision - a unanimous decision from the three judges.

"I don't want to tempt fate, because let's wait and see what the Moroccan Federation say, but what I would say to you is that they have fought this all the way, they believe that this is an injustice and they are one of the most powerful football associations in Africa.

"They are co-hosts of the next World Cup in 2030 and they have a very close relationships with the leadership at FIFA.

"I'm not suggesting that anything untoward is going on, but they still do have options. For instance, if they feel there has been something wrong in the procedures at the CAS hearing, they could go to the Swiss Federal Tribunal.

"I think the Moroccan Federation, according to what Reuters are reporting this afternoon, are unhappy with the way CAS handled this appeal and they have complained to CAS because they believe that one of the judges actually spoke on the phone to the president of the Senegal Federation on Sunday afternoon, so perhaps that is an avenue that they are going to explore."

The Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) has upheld the appeal by the Senegalese Football Federation (FSF) against the Confederation of African Football (CAF) and the the Royal Moroccan Football Federation (FRMF, in French) concerning the final of the Africa Cup of Nations Morocco 2025 (AFCON 2025).

The decision rendered by the CAF Appeal Board is set aside and the decision by the CAF Disciplinary Board is reinstated. Consequently, the result obtained at the end of the AFCON 2025 final is confirmed (1-0 for Senegal).

The FSF appealed to CAS against a decision of the CAF Appeal Board declaring that the Senegal national team forfeited the AFCON 2025 final and recording the Moroccan national team as the winners.

In their appeal, the FSF argued that the decision of the Referee, Mr Jean-Jacques Ngambo Ndala, to resume the match at the 109th minute (90+19) during the AFCON 2025 final and to allow it to be concluded with a sporting result could not be reviewed, pursuant to the 'field of play' doctrine.

The FRMF argued, on the contrary, that the Referee's decision did not fall under the 'field of play' doctrine, as it cannot be applicable to the disciplinary consequences of the Senegalese team's behaviour. The FRMF alleged that, by leaving the pitch temporarily, the Senegalese team breached Articles 82 and 84 of the AFCON Regulations, which should have led the Referee to bring the match to an early conclusion. The FRMF also argued that the Referee erred in law and that his decision was arbitrary.

After having examined the arguments raised by the Parties, the Arbitral Panel considered that the Referee's decision to resume the match or to stop it fell definitively within his sole authority. By blowing his whistle to restart play, the Referee considered that the conditions for resuming the match had been met - both teams were on the pitch again, and both expressed their willingness to resume.

In the Panel's view, there is no doubt that the effects of the Referee's decision were limited to the pitch. As such, the Panel considers that the Referee's decision was covered by the 'field of play' doctrine. Since Articles 82 and 84 of the AFCON Regulations are intended solely to determine the outcome of a match when a team's conduct leads the referee to call off the game permanently, they are therefore not applicable in this case. In conclusion, the Panel considers that in resuming the match, the Referee did not err in law.

The Panel acknowledged that the Referee had to make a decision in an atmosphere of extreme tension. However, these circumstances do not justify describing the decision as "arbitrary". On the contrary, the Referee's judgment did not appear to have been affected by factors external to the game.

Regarding the disciplinary aspects of the case, the Panel noted that the FSF, the FSF coach Mr. Pape Thiaw, and certain players had already been sanctioned by CAF following separate disciplinary proceedings.

Consequently, the appeal by the FSF was upheld. This was a unanimous decision by the CAS Panel.
