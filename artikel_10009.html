In line with our announcement to cease issuance of emSign TLS certificates, eMudhra wishes to provide an update regarding the scheduled phase out of certificates issued under the emSign G1 hierarchy by major browser root programs.

The respective browser root programs have indicated the following effective dates for phase out of emSign G1 chained certificates:

For Chrome and Mozilla, SCTNotAfter implies that after these dates, certificates issued under the emSign G1 hierarchy will not be trusted by the respective browser and platform trust programs, but does not impact any existing certificates issued before these dates.

For Apple it means all certificates issued under the G1 hierarchy at any point in time will cease to be trusted on their platforms.

In alignment with these time lines, we have already migrated all emSign TLS certificates to alternative providers in all geolocations with the exception of the US region where a large community of customers is still in-progress. It is anticipated that the remaining emSign TLS customers will all be transitioned to alternative providers in advance of the date set by Apple.

eMudhra Limited today announced the appointment of KPMG as its independent external reviewer following the incident that led to the distrust of select global TLS intermediate certificate authorities earlier this month. The appointment fulfills the commitment the company made in its press release of October 21, when it said an independent entity would examine the matter. KPMG is among the leading global networks of professional services firms.

The company said the review is intended to bring clarity on the incident and to help it address any issues arising from it, in keeping with its approach to governance and compliance.

This update follows Update 5 and gives more detail on where the customer transition stands.

Affected G3 certificates and the alternate G1 pathway

The roughly 30,800 certificates issued under the affected G3 hierarchies belong to a client ecosystem in the United States. We had already given this ecosystem an alternate issuance pathway through eMudhra's G1 RSA root, and they are now using that pathway.

Orderly transition out of the G1 TLS Roots

We have asked the browser and root-store programs for time so that the impacted client ecosystem in US can move over in an orderly way without disrupting its member institutions. We are in advanced discussions with leading SSL/TLS providers to support the move. We will keep working with our Client, these providers and the root programs until the transition is complete.

G1 issuance is being used only to support this transition. As part of our voluntary wind-down of publicly trusted TLS services, eMudhra will stop issuing TLS certificates from G1 once the federation's transition is complete. A vast majority of our remaining TLS customers have already been transitioned to alternate TLS providers and we intend to complete this activity in the coming days.

Free migration to alternate providers for unaffected TLS customers

eMudhra immediately provided (and continues to provide) alternate options at no charges to all TLS customers who were using eMudhra TLS certificates without any additional charges. We have already moved these customers to alternate providers at no cost to them, and our teams remain dedicated to support them through the switch.

Global eSignature (DSC, eSignature, eSign), and Enterprise Solutions business unaffected

None of eMudhra's Global Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) or eSign business is affected by this incident. They continue to run normally throughout and remain unaffected. Neither is there any impact to our enterprise solutions (emSigner, CertiNext, SecurePass, Privatrust). Where applicable, SaaS solutions provided by eMudhra also remain unaffected.

We remain committed to transparency, responsible remediation and continued cooperation with the global WebPKI community. We thank our customers, partners, and root store programs for their understanding as we work our way through this transition. Further updates will be published on this page as material developments occur.

Last week, a security researcher reported that the private keys associated with eight of our emSign G3 intermediate TLS CAs were recoverable. They were right.

The flaw was ours. A script error resulted in issuing CA’s with weak keys.

We want to apologise directly to our customers and to the broader WebPKI community, both of whom were entitled to expect better from us. Also to our partners, who have had to field questions that are ours to answer.

The affected intermediate CAs were revoked within 14 hours of our confirming the issue. Based on our investigation to date, the affected intermediates had not issued publicly trusted end-entity certificates, and there has been no compromise of our infrastructure or customer data.

Our digital-signature & eSign/eSignature in India, and Globally, along with all of our enterprise applications operate on separate infrastructure and were not impacted in any manner. We are already in the process of moving our impacted G3 customers and our broader non-impacted TLS customers to alternate certificates at no cost.

We will publish a full root-cause analysis through the CCADB process. It will explain what went wrong, why our controls failed to detect it, and the changes we are making as a result.

We are also commissioning an independent expert to review the incident and the procedures and controls followed by us. Nothing is more important in our business than the trust placed in us by our customers and the broader digital-trust ecosystem.

We will do all that is necessary to maintain that trust and confidence through our actions in the coming weeks.

eMudhra is making certificates issued by Sectigo available as an alternate option to all of our customers.

Our G1 root certificates remain active and continue to be fully supported. Despite this, we are offering Sectigo-issued certificates to give customers additional peace of mind and flexibility in managing their certificate deployments.

What happened, who is affected, and what we are doing about it

On October 17, 2026, an independent security researcher reported that the keys of eight (out of several) eMudhra Global TLS intermediate Certificate Authorities (CAs) were cryptographically weak. We revoked all eight intermediates the following day. No certificates had been issued from any of them, our infrastructure was not compromised, and no data was breached.

We take this incident seriously. We believe an organization's character is shown in how it responds when something goes wrong, so this post sets out plainly what happened, who is affected, and what we are doing about it.

Eight intermediate (issuing) CAs in our Global TLS business were revoked because their published keys were weak. These intermediates sit under eMudhra's ECC root. As a consequence of the incident, the browser root programs have also distrusted that one ECC root.

To be clear about what was and was not affected:

The keys were generated on FIPS 140-2 Level 3 certified Hardware Security Modules (HSMs). Suggestions that HSMs were not used are incorrect. The problem was a flaw in one step of the ceremony procedure, not the absence of HSMs.

During the ceremony, a key wrap/unwrap step produced a second, weak set of keys alongside the original strong keys. The weak keys were the ones published to the browser root stores. The issue went undetected for about 18 months, through two independent audit cycles. The researcher was able to reverse-engineer the weak keys, reportedly with the help of AI-assisted tooling.

We have not yet confirmed the exact root cause. We will publish it in full once our investigation is complete. We are not aware of any evidence that the weak keys were recovered or exploited by anyone before the issue was disclosed.

The impact is limited to TLS certificates chaining to the distrusted ECC root, and it is contained to a single customer ecosystem in the United States.

These products and services run on separate infrastructure and continue to operate normally. Customers using them do not need to take any action.

Our first priority is making sure the affected customer's services keep running without disruption.

CA redundancy for all Enterprise TLS customers. We are offering every Enterprise TLS customer certificates from an additional publicly trusted CA. If one hierarchy ever has a problem, customers can switch without interrupting their business. Keeping our customers running is one of our top priorities.

A full review of our key generation process. We are reviewing every step of our Key Generation Ceremonies, starting with the wrap/unwrap step that caused this incident. We intend to deeply study how we can enhance the governance around our processes, and adopt AI based tooling in addition to other conventional means available to mitigate such occurences.

We acknowledge this incident and take full responsibility for it. Trust is the foundation of everything we do, and we intend to earn it back through action.

We thank the independent researcher who reported this issue responsibly, and our customers and partners for their trust.

eMudhra is actively investigating a security incident involving a specific set of intermediate CA certificates created as part of a Key Generation Ceremony (KGC) conducted in April 2025 within its public trust infrastructure.

The incident is limited to these specific intermediate CA certificates and does not affect eMudhra's root CA private keys or other intermediate CAs.

Importantly, none of the affected intermediate CAs were deployed in production, with the exception of one. Furthermore, none of these intermediate CAs, including the one deployed in production, have issued any end-entity certificates. This has been confirmed through our internal analysis and corroborated by publicly available Certificate Transparency records on crt.sh.

Upon becoming aware of the incident, eMudhra initiated its incident response procedures, took measures to contain the affected certificates, and began working closely with browser and root-store operators to safeguard users and maintain the integrity of the broader WebPKI ecosystem.

We are conducting a thorough investigation into the circumstances surrounding the incident and reviewing our security controls and operational procedures to identify and implement any necessary improvements.

The security and integrity of our trust services remain our highest priority. We are committed to transparency, adherence to industry standards, and continued collaboration with the global WebPKI community.

To avoid any misunderstanding, the removal of our Root CA’s C1 and C3 with Mozilla was a voluntary and planned activity and does not pertain to this security incident.

We will provide further updates as our investigation progresses and additional findings are confirmed.
