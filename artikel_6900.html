Why Do Fans Hate to See an Up-and-Coming Male Actor With a “Normie” GF? Unpacking This Parasocial Phenomenon

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

From Belmont Cameli to Christopher Briney, fans are fully losing the plot.

Amid breakup rumors (which were almost certainly manufactured), Belmont Cameli stepped out with his TV writer girlfriend Raina Morris for a West Hollywood date night on November 10. The two were together before Off Campus premiered, catapulting Belmont into the public eye, but his fans remain convinced that (1) Belmont is meant to be with his costar Ella Bright and (2) Raina must have an ulterior motive in dating him—that motive, of course, being tangential fame.

Here are a few select comments from Just Jared’s post about the outing on Instagram, to give you an idea of what I’m talking about:

As a reminder: This all comes a couple of months after Raina was forced to release a statement asking Off Campus fans to stop harassing her family. “If you are a fan of my boyfriend: Yay!” she started her message, adding, “Please stop messaging my family members. They are so confused. Write in your diary and then go on a walk instead! Love u.”

Raina is not the only non-actor girlfriend of a rising star to experience this kind of cruel harassment. Similarly, when it came to light that Heated Rivalry star Hudson Williams was not dating his costar Connor Storrie in real life (as much as fans wanted that to be true) but was instead dating tattoo artist Katelyn Rose Larson (and had been for years), some people felt a certain type of way.

Hudson first introduced fans to his girlfriend with an Instagram Story post on Valentine’s Day 2026 (two months after Deuxmoi revealed her identity to the public), noting that Katelyn has been with him “since my 2000 gold Mazda Protégé smoked and squealed and I had no job.” But still, when Katelyn joined Hudson on the red carpet at an Oscars after-party the next month, some fans weren’t shy about making their disapproval known...and they’ve been keeping it up ever since.

Again, I present a few comments from a Deuxmoi post showing the two hugging before Heated Rivalry season 2 filming kicked off back in August:

A month after those paparazzi shots of their hug went viral, Katelyn took to her Instagram Stories to write, “Obviously a lot of you have found the studio because of a certain show and that’s more than okay. This is where I draw a very hard line: booking an appointment with me or anyone else purely because of the connection to the show crosses a huge territory.”

She added, “Showing up to your appointment and asking my coworkers any sort of questions about myself or about Hudson is hugely uncomfortable for everyone.”

And of course, we can’t have this conversation without mentioning Christopher Briney’s girlfriend, fellow actor Isabel Machado, who he started dating in college and who has faced no shortage of harassment from The Summer I Turned Pretty fans...much of it due to the fact that she kiiiiinda looks like his onscreen love interest, played by Lola Tung.

I present to you: select comments from a Deuxmoi post announcing Chris and Isabel’s engagement (though that rumor turned out to be totally false).

And while Isabel has never released a statement directly calling out fans, she seemed to respond to harassment in her own subtle way with an August 2025 Instagram post reminding everyone that she’s a “person with feelings.”

So...why is it that fans hate to see an up-and-coming male actor with a less-famous girlfriend? For starters, I don’t think it’s a coincidence that all three of these men star in beloved TV romances...or that their fandoms all want them to be with their onscreen love interests IRL.

But Ella Bright and Belmont Cameli are not actually Hannah Wells and Garrett Graham. And while the actors seem to have great chemistry both onscreen and offscreen, fans must remember that they’re actors for a reason: They’re good at fooling audiences. And dating someone other than their costar doesn’t mean they’re fooling themselves.

These three rising stars have also all been in relationships since before their breakout roles, so to accuse their partners of social climbing seems not only rude, but straight-up false. While they may not have millions of followers, these supposed “normie” girlfriends are all successful in their own right, and it’s been made clear that none of them are trying to steal the spotlight.

“She’s not trying to be out there or be perceived,” Hudson said of Katelyn to ETalk Canada back in March. “She’s always like, ‘I don’t want to be perceived!’”

While Belmont doesn’t talk about Raina directly, he did address the “real venom” of “fandom these days,” telling The Hollywood Reporter, “You can say whatever you want about me or people in my life, but it’s not going to affect me because I’m fully aware of what my reality looks like and feels like.”

Chris also expressed a similar sentiment to Elite Daily when talking about his relationship with Isabel, saying, “People have definitely crossed lines and stepped on personal boundaries...I’ve learned to separate myself from social media and understand that I have my own life. If people have opinions about it, all right, that’s fine.”

Still, it seems fans cannot stop hurling accusations and spreading breakup rumors whenever they’re reminded that Conrad Fisher is not their boyfriend...or Belly Conklin’s boyfriend...or even a real person. Yes, these so-called nepo girlfriends have gained more followers than they may have if their boyfriends were still struggling actors. But chances are, they would have stuck with their men even if they never landed their breakout roles (and are still with them despite what may be totally unwanted attention).

Point being: You can ‘ship your fave fictional relationship all you want. You can even think, “Why not me?” when you see your fave TV heartthrob with someone else. But when you come for the very real people in their lives, you’ve done more than lose the plot; you’ve crossed into parasocial behavior.

To quote Hudson: “RPFing gotta stop, y’all.”
