Should the Denver Broncos shake-up their starting offensive line?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

One of the things that has been irritating many of us this season has been specific areas of offensive line play. I have seen people call out poor performances by Mike McGlinchey and Luke Wattenberg, but the real problem fans seem to see along the offensive line has been from interior guard Ben Powers.

Personally, I've seen him whiff on blocks and get turned around so frequently that I wonder if he is the primary reason Bo Nix has been quick to bail to his right as often as he does. It's not much better in the run game either, which is a source of constant frustration when watching these games.

Head Coach Sean Payton was asked about making changes to the offensive line and he seemed to downplay it a bit, but left the door open too.

"I made sure to make a point about evaluating all assets of what we're doing in our front in the offense," Payton said on Monday. "So I want to make sure one story doesn't lead to another. We'll spend an inordinate amount of time looking at mid-season cutups where we're at relative to scheme and personnel."

Frankly, I think they should make a change. If it gets a lot worse, then you can always change it back. I have nothing against Powers, but he has struggled and it is problematic for sustaining offensive drives. Making a change shouldn't be so hard to do. And maybe there are other things I don't see or cannot understand due to being on the outside looking in. If it's not Powers, then its someone in there not doing a good enough job and a change is certainly warranted.

What do you think: is Payton waiting too long to take action along the line?

NFL Power Rankings for Week 11: How did the Denver Broncos hold up over the bye?

The Denver Broncos didn't play last weekend, but a good chunk of prognosticators didn't move them an inch up or or down in their weekly power rankings.

On what changed with the anticipated trade for Chargers LB Daiyan Henley

"Nothing changed. It was already stated. Everything was pretty clearly spelled out. I'm not going to get into the specifics. He's a prospect we thought—and still do [think]—very highly of. It just didn't work out and it's not uncommon with trades. That's kind of where it went, and I'll leave it at that."

On his message to the team after the bye week and the areas of focus going forward

"I think collectively, we just finished a team meeting talking about the improvement in certain areas where we feel like we're going to have to be better at in this, call it, second half of the season. Specifically, I mentioned this last week, we need to block better on offense and we need to be in the right place. I thought we did an average job against Carolina, and I think that's an area of focus relative to more consistency in our run game. Then there were some third-down, fourth-down-and-longs defensively that was an Achilles heel for us a year ago, and the other day became one. Those are just a few things."

On if there is a loophole in the trade process

"I saw it all. There's no loophole. It just is part of the trade process every step of the way."

On the inside linebacker depth and if there are opportunities for more players to get snaps

"I don't view it the same way you do. We're going to play the starters and then guys that earn reps certainly will earn playing time. Yes, that's all I could say to that."

On the process for handling travel issues with the nationwide flight stoppage

"It's not that complex. [Vice President of Player Development] Ray [Jackson] does a good job of just finding out who's out of town, who may need assistance. I think he communicated several e-mails. Probably by yesterday, there were three players that were just finalizing their plans."
