UK sanctions top Myanmar military leaders after Rohingya genocide ruling

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

More than a million people were forced to flee the violence across the border to Bangladesh in 2017

The UK has sanctioned five senior Myanmar military leaders after the International Court of Justice (ICJ) ruled that the country had carried out genocide against the Rohingya minority â marking the first time a state has been found guilty of the crime.

A military crackdown in 2017 killed thousands of Rohingya and forced more than 700,000 to flee Buddhist-majority Myanmar, formerly known as Burma.

The UK is joined by several other countries, external in acknowledging the ICJ ruling, including Australia, Belgium, Portugal, Spain and Canada.

Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband said: "Our response to a crime as profoundly shocking as genocide must reflect our values and must be matched by clear action."

The senior military leaders facing sanctions are Myo Htut Hlaing, Myo Min Tun, Myat Thet Oo, Kyaw Soe and Kyaw Swar Oo.

Miliband added that Myanmar's President Min Aung Hlaing, who was sanctioned by the UK in 2021, is "directly responsible for many of the atrocities that have happened" as he was head of the military at the time of the genocide in 2017.

He said: "Myanmar must stop ongoing violations, investigate, prosecute and punish perpetrators, and prevent such an atrocity from ever happening again."

Miliband also called for an "immediate pause in hostilities to enable humanitarian aid to get through, a stop to air strikes on civilians, and the release of political prisoners, as well as dialogue towards an inclusive political settlement".

The foreign secretary confirmed that new legislation would be passed to stop the supply of aviation fuel to the country, which was known as Burma until 1989.

He told MPs on Monday the UK would raise the issue at the UN Security Council this week, and there would be a dedicated meeting in a fortnight.

The court found on Thursday that "genocidal intent is the only reasonable inference that can be drawn from the evidence" it had seen.

The verdict was reached by a majority of nine votes to six among a panel of judges from around the world. The ICJ also found in favour by majority that Myanmar had violated several other parts of the 1948 Genocide Convention, external.

In its ruling last week, the court determined that about 10,000 Rohingya were murdered during the clearance operation in the western Rakhine state that borders Bangladesh and the Bay of Bengal. Myanmar denied the claims against it.

Reports said that entire villages were burnt down, and that women and girls were raped.

Miliband said more than 1.2 million refugees fled and were being supported by Bangladesh.

Myanmar's leader during the genocide, Aung San Suu Kyi, was deposed by the military in 2021.

MPs heard that air strikes and violence from the Arakan army were still happening.
