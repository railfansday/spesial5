JPMorgan taps 20-year company veteran Bregje de Best as its next CFO

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

JPMorgan Chase has named Bregje de Best, who has spent two decades at the company, as its next chief financial officer.

The 43-year-old will lead the finance organization at JPMorgan Chase, No. 12 on the Fortune 500. The company had $5.1 trillion in assets and $372 billion in stockholders’ equity as of Sept. 30. She will succeed Jeremy Barnum following the company’s first-quarter 2027 financial reporting, JPMorgan announced Tuesday.

At that time, she will join the firm-wide operating committee, and Barnum will become a vice chairman. Barnum informed the company of his intention to conclude his tenure as CFO after six years in the role.

The appointment will make de Best the latest woman to serve as JPMorgan’s CFO. Her predecessors include Marianne Lake, who held the role from 2013 to 2019, and Jennifer Piepszak, now chief operating officer, who served as CFO from 2019 to 2021.

Since June 2025, de Best has served as CFO of JPMorgan’s commercial and investment bank (CIB). The Dutch banker joined the company in 2006 and previously served as co-head of global corporate banking. DeBest has also held senior roles in investment banking and markets in the United Kingdom and Asia.

JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon described de Best in the announcement as an “extraordinary leader,” citing her experience in finance and across the CIB’s businesses. “She has a deep understanding of how our company operates,” Dimon said.

Her investment banking background is shared by a notable number of current CFOs. According to executive search firm Crist Kolder Associates’ 2026 midyear Volatility Report, 22% of sitting CFOs at companies in its combined Fortune 500 and S&P 500 sample have investment banking experience. Of those with that background, 20% work in the financial industry.

Research also finds that companies increasingly want CFOs with operational experience. De Best’s experience extends to the operational side of the business: As CIB CFO, she oversees business management and controls across JPMorgan’s global banking, markets, payments and securities services businesses.

In a LinkedIn post earlier this year, de Best described attending a firm-wide senior leadership conference focused on resilience, global growth and the rapid pace of innovation. “It’s clear that AI and digital assets are transforming every aspect of our business, and I’m energized by our commitment to building for the future and leading the industry forward,” she wrote.

Barnum and de Best will work closely during the transition. He will lead the January and April earnings announcements and the company’s Investor Day in February, according to JPMorgan. After becoming a vice chairman following first-quarter reporting, he will remain at the company until early 2028, working with Dimon and other senior leaders on firm-wide initiatives.“We see this as more of a continuity choice, particularly because her background at JPMorgan Chase has been fairly similar to Barnum’s, highlighted by both serving as CFO for the commercial and investment bank segment prior to their appointment as firm-wide CFO,”  Austin Taggart, an equity analyst at Morningstar told Fortune.

The company will announce de Best’s successor as CFO of the CIB early next year.

Sheryl Estrada is a senior writer at Fortune, where she covers the corporate finance industry, Wall Street, and corporate leadership. She also authors CFO Daily.
