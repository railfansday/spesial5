Kalshi and Polymarket claim 98% accuracy in calling midterm races

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Free daily briefing on global business news.

A New York Times analysis found prediction markets did no better than election forecasts for most of the race

Kalshi said the candidates its traders favored won 98% of resolved races in Tuesday's midterm elections. The company announced the figure on Wednesday.

The tally covers 479 of 487 races resolved as of 6 a.m. ET on Nov. 4, Kalshi said. Favorites won 356 of 360 House races, 32 of 33 Senate races and 33 of 34 governor races.

Polymarket said that, judged against the day before the election, 98.4% of its settled contests had pointed to the correct winner, according to The Associated Press. Polymarket put its hit rate at roughly 97% for the Senate and gubernatorial contests and at 98.6% for those in the House.

Kalshi said its markets for House control passed 99% likelihood at 8:10 p.m. ET and its Senate control markets passed 99% at 9 p.m. ET, hours ahead of the networks. Trading volume on its 2026 midterm markets topped $2.5 billion, compared with $1.3 billion for the 2024 cycle, the company said.

In a separate analysis, Kalshi said directional accuracy for races called by 6:26 a.m. ET on Nov. 4 rose from 93.8% three months out to 98.2% when polls closed. Brier scores, on which lower is better, fell from 0.040 to 0.016, the company said.

Outside experts urged caution. Ross Dahlke, a University of Wisconsin-Madison professor, said the figures include only resolved markets, which may be easier to predict, and that many races were fairly certain going into Election Day, according to The Associated Press.

An analysis by The New York Times found that prediction markets fared about the same as election forecasts through most of the midterm campaign. Markets gained an edge in the closing days, the Times said, once polls and forecasts had gone out of date. Kalshi told the Times that its ability to post odds on par with seasoned forecasters well ahead of voting demonstrated the markets’ forecasting strength.

Polymarket did not respond to a request for comment from The New York Times.

Koleman Strumpf, an economics professor at Wake Forest University who studies prediction markets, gave the markets a B, according to Bloomberg. He said the markets pointed the right way but underestimated how large the Democratic wave would be.

Kalshi said on X $TWTR that its markets outperformed forecasting models including those of Silver Bulletin, FiftyPlusOne and The Economist, according to Bloomberg. Nate Silver, who runs Silver Bulletin, said his model and others showed the Democratic wave sooner and more aggressively.
