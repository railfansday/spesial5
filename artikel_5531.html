Navigating the AI Shift

What AI developments do employees and agents need to be paying attention to?

The big one is knowing which models give you facts and which ones are people pleasers. Take listing remarks, for example. ChatGPT is excellent at them, but you need strict guardrails on it, because a people-pleaser model prioritizes making you happy over the facts and the rules. Left alone, it will write you a Fair Housing violation and never tell you it did. Beyond that, ask the questions most people skip: How are my competitors using it? How are my vendors using it?

Give us your top tips for HR leaders as they prepare people to successfully navigate the AI shift.

First, keep an open mind, because AI is the newest employee at your business. In addition to needing policies and procedures to follow, it also needs training and oversight just like an employee does. Second, give current employees the chance to utilize AI, and when making future employment decisions, build AI capability into job descriptions. Third, have employees use the paid versions, making sure they’re turning off the learning capabilities. Otherwise, competitors could unintentionally learn closely guarded secrets.

How will AI change the way brokerages approach recruiting, onboarding, training and employee development?

Real estate is a people business, and the relationship part is the part AI doesn’t touch, but everything around it changes. Recruiting is already moving that way. Sourcing, screening, scheduling, the first follow-up are all getting automated. My caution as an HR professional is that you still own the outcome. If a vendor’s tool screens someone out, that’s your decision, and you need to be able to explain how it was made. Onboarding is where you can save administrative time. New people ask the same 20 questions in their first week, and they ask them at night and on weekends when there’s nobody there to answer. AI answers instantly, and it answers the same way every time. Training and development are where the real gains are. With AI, you can give every agent individualized coaching. You can let them practice a listing presentation, review their own scripts, get feedback at 10 p.m. when they actually have time. None of that replaces person-to-person interactions, but it helps bridge gaps.

What role do you see HR playing in helping people adapt, learn and take advantage of new technologies?

HR has to be at the table early. Historically, HR gets brought in at the end of a technology decision to write the policy after somebody else already picked the tool. With AI, that’s backwards, because almost every risk is a people risk. HR needs to be part of the decision, not the cleanup.

The second piece is making it safe to learn. People won’t experiment with something they think is going to replace them. If your employees believe AI is a threat, they’ll quietly avoid it, and you won’t find out until you’re two years behind. HR helps set that tone.

Third is guardrails: policies, approved tools, what can and can’t go into a prompt, what gets reviewed before it leaves the building. This gives people something to work inside of so they’re not figuring it out alone and making different decisions than you would about your data.
