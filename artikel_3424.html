Rian Johnson Leaves the Door Open to Star Wars Return: ‘It’s a World I Deeply Love’

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Rian Johnson isn’t writing a Star Wars movie, but he isn’t ruling one out, either.

Responding to a question from IndieWire during an in-conversation event at the 23rd Marrakech International Film Festival, the writer/director of “Star Wars: The Last Jedi” was clear that his next project lies elsewhere: “I’m already writing the next thing I’m going to do, and it’s not a Star Wars thing, or in that genre,” he said.

“I’m very much enjoying just making my own stuff, but I mean, if the opportunity presented itself,” Johnson said, nodding. “It’s a world I deeply love. It’s also a group of people I love.”

“I love Star Wars more now than I ever have,” he added. “And it’s exciting to me that there are a lot of different voices now taking cracks at it.” The timing is hard to ignore. Johnson’s remarks land just as Lucasfilm appears to be turning back toward the corner of the saga he helped shape. Polygon reported that two long-gestating features, “Star Wars: New Jedi Order” and an untitled film from Dave Filoni, are still listed as in development by Production Weekly, despite months of silence and the soft box office performance of “The Mandalorian & Grogu.”

“New Jedi Order” would theoretically bring back actress Daisy Ridley as Rey roughly 15 years after “The Rise of Skywalker,” rebuilding the Jedi Order that “The Last Jedi” so memorably called into question. Sharmeen Obaid-Chinoy is attached to direct, with George Nolfi now writing after earlier drafts from Damon Lindelof and Justin Britt-Gibson, and a stint by Bond 26 screenwriter Steven Knight. The film was pulled from its December 17, 2027 release date in May 2025. Meanwhile, “Star Wars: Starfighter,” the Shawn Levy-directed film starring Ryan Gosling and set five years after “The Rise of Skywalker,” remains the only Star Wars feature with a firm date: May 28, 2027.

In 2017, the year “The Last Jedi” hit theaters, Lucasfilm announced that Johnson would create a new Star Wars trilogy. Those films have yet to materialize, but Johnson had warm words for Filoni, who took on the role of Lucasfilm’s president and chief creative officer in January: “Dave Filoni I got to know while making ‘The Last Jedi’, and he’s a wonderful guy who really has the spirit of Star Wars in him,” he said. “All the Lucasfilm folks are awesome.”

Later, answering questions from the audience, Johnson described “The Last Jedi” as “the most joyful process of any movie I’ve ever made”, despite the challenge of stepping from his own original films into what he called a contested mythology.

“I’m a huge, huge fan of George Lucas, and I’ve read a lot about when he was writing the original Star Wars, how personal it was for him,” Johnson continued. “I think especially with something that has a deep cultural impact, and that belongs to everybody, that makes it even more important… to make sure it’s coming from a personal perspective and something that’s deeply on fire inside you, as opposed to thinking, ‘what’s gonna please this person and this person and this person’. That leads to homogeneity, to repeating yourself and to something that feels dead on screen.”

As a middle chapter, “The Last Jedi” drew directly on Johnson’s childhood experience of “The Empire Strikes Back,” though not as something to copy. “I didn’t want to imitate ‘The Empire Strikes Back’,” Johnson said. “What I wanted to get to was the heart of what that movie has, which is this center part of life where your job is to fail, and your job is to learn from that and to get up and keep moving forward.”

He drew a distinction between a film having a moral compass and having a moral. The compass, he said, “comes from the entire mass of every single creative decision made by the writer, or director, or whole team.”

Johnson also reflected on the only two composers he has worked with. His cousin Nathan Johnson has scored his films since the pair started making movies together at age ten: “Nathan is the first person to read the script; he’s on set for the entire shoot so it’s very much a collaboration.”

John Williams, he said, is also “a miracle of a human being”, and working with him was the opposite experience. “You give him the movie and then the first time I hear any music is when we’re on the scoring stage, and there are 200 musicians playing it.”

If Johnson does return to Star Wars, it seems he won’t be thinking about franchise strategy. “For me it’s not about thinking about IP, it’s about stories,” he said. “I deeply believe the only way to actually make a movie that matters and is worth something is to have your eyes nowhere else except on making that movie. What is the story we’re telling? Why are we telling it? Why do we care about it?”

“Outside of that bubble,” Johnson added, “I’ll leave it to the executives.”

With those executives now steering Star Wars back toward Rey and the post-“Rise of Skywalker” era, the bubble may be closer to his than it has been in years.
