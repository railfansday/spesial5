QLED. OLED. Mini-LED. The TV world can be super overwhelming, so let’s do a quick rundown of the differences between the three “premium” panel types.

OLED TVs have lots of mini diodes inside the panel that emit light individually. This means there’s no backlight, unlike older TVs. As a result, OLEDs are better for higher contrast, better blacks, better color, and smooth motion for gaming.

QLEDs also use a light-emitting diode. Unlike OLED, a QLED panel has a backlight, which illuminates each diode individually. QLEDs are better for brighter rooms, as the panel itself tends to be brighter, but OLEDs can reach better contrast levels and color accuracy.

Mini-LED is something else entirely: it uses tiny LEDs (light-emitting diodes, like both QLED and OLED) — but thousands of them. That means each diode can pack a major color and brightness punch. Mini-LEDs can be brighter, blacker, and more colorful — all at once.
