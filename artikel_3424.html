Why the Audi S6 e-tron is the sporty luxury EV to beat (despite an awkward flaw)

The 2027 Audi S6 Sportback e-tron pairs its luxury aesthetics with a sporty drive that makes it fun on the road, all while remaining whisper-quiet on the inside thanks to its air-suspension ride and solid soundproofing. While its nearly $95,000 as-tested price tag is a hefty amount, it’s accompanied by premium features and surprisingly efficient for drivers who want sports-sedan thrills without sacrificing everyday versatility.

When I got into the driver's seat of the Audi A6 Sportback e-tron last year, my expectations weren’t all that high. But it ended up impressing me so much that it quickly became one of the best electric cars I’ve tested. With the 2027 Audi S6 Sportback e-tron, it’s pretty evident that Audi has dialed up the aggression and sportier flair.

While there are similarities between the two, the S6 Sportback e-tron differentiates itself with standard dual-motor quattro all-wheel drive, sport-tuned adaptive air suspension, and up to 543 horsepower. Quite simply, it’s a mid-size luxury sedan that trades some range for extra muscle.

I tested the 2027 Audi S6 Sportback e-tron Prestige for a week, which carries a $94,490 as-tested price — putting it squarely in the crosshairs of similar models I’ve tested, like the BMW i5 and Lucid Air Touring. And after seven days behind the wheel, I discovered this pricier performance variant still holds a few surprises up its sleeve.

I drove the 2027 Audi S6 Sportback e-tron Prestige for a total of 152.4 miles and recorded an overall average efficiency of 3.6 mi/kWh. While that overall figure trails slightly behind the results I got out of the BMW i5 and Lucid Air Touring, it’s well within striking range of its main rivals.

Personally, I think it’s a valiant result given how the A6 Sportback e-tron yielded 3.7 mi/kWh in my previous test. On longer highway stretches, I’ve recorded a best mark of 3.9 mi/kWh with the A6 Sportback — while short trip efficiency reached 3.6 mi/kWh.

When plugged into a standard household outlet for Level 1 charging, the S6 Sportback e-tron added range at a rate of 4.05 miles per hour. That places it squarely in the middle of its rivals, beating the Lucid Air Touring while trailing the BMW i5 xDrive40.

I’ve always been a fan of Audi’s signature design philosophy, and the 2027 S6 Sportback e-tron takes that familiar luxury aesthetic and injects it with an aggressive, sporty edge. Between its low-slung fastback silhouette, razor-sharp LED lighting, and glossy front grille, it strikes an athletic stance that never feels overdone — and makes me feel young at heart.

Behind the wheel, the 2027 Audi S6 Sportback e-tron delivers a refined, whisper-quiet ride that glides effortlessly over rough pavement thanks to its standard sport adaptive air suspension. Yet the moment I bury my foot in the accelerator, it pins me back in the seat with blistering off-the-line speed.

That effortless torque makes overtaking slower traffic on the highway feel almost instantaneous, giving me serious passing power without ever sacrificing its composed demeanor. It actually offers a better ride than the Lucid Air and matches the BMW i5 on open roads.

One of the standout tech features on the S6 Sportback e-tron is Audi's augmented reality head-up display, which projects crisp visuals directly into my line of sight on the windshield. Beyond showing me all the usual stats — like my current speed, turn-by-turn navigation arrows, and speed limit signs — it also visualizes adaptive cruise assist and following distance in real time.

It’s one of the most comprehensive and advanced HUDs out there, even turning red when the gap to the vehicle ahead gets too short.

In 2026, it’s still very rare for any vehicle to come with an integrated dashcam, but the S6 Sportback e-tron is one of them. Ingeniously, it’s built into the auto-dimming rearview mirror — keeping the windshield free of clutter, unlike third-party camera solutions with dangling power cables. Not only does it offer automatic loop recording for convenience, but it also doesn’t require an extra fee or subscription just to work.

Another practical advantage of the S6 Sportback e-tron is its rear liftback design, which opens up a massive cargo hold that puts conventional sedans to shame. Unlike the traditional, narrower trunk openings I dealt with on the BMW i5 and Lucid Air Touring, the Audi’s entire rear glass lifts with the power tailgate — making it far easier to load bulky gear, luggage, or larger items that simply wouldn’t clear a standard trunk lid.

With roughly 26 cubic feet of space behind the second row (and even more when you fold the rear seats flat), it gives you crossover-like versatility wrapped in a sleek sport-sedan profile.

I’ll be the first to admit that its $95,000 as-tested price is hefty, but Audi compensates for it with a serious tech suite of luxury features you don’t traditionally find. Overhead, the panoramic glass roof features variable light control that electronically lets me switch between transparent and opaque at the touch of a button, while LED interior ambient lighting wraps across the dashboard and brings the cabin to life at night.

On the outside, I love small details like the animations of the LED headlights and OLED taillights, which give it a slick look when turning the vehicle on and off. There are also dual charging ports on either side of the vehicle, giving me the convenience of parking however I want for Level 1 and 2 charging — albeit with Level 3 DC fast charging only supported on the driver’s side.

Entertainment and audio get just as much attention, thanks to a crisp 10.9-inch front passenger display and a booming Bang & Olufsen 3D sound system that even builds speakers directly into the front headrests.

While the S6 Sportback e-tron’s sleek, low-slung roofline looks fantastic from the outside, it comes at the expense of a narrower rear windshield compared to its rivals. As a result, it creates a somewhat letterbox-like view.

Between the low roofline, chunky A-pillars, and the large sensor housing behind the rearview mirror, spotting overhead traffic lights can be challenging when pulling up close to an intersection.

I’m the kind of driver who religiously activates adaptive cruise control, and while I appreciate how the S6 Sportback e-tron keeps steady in its lane, it lacks automatic lane-change assist. That means I’m still forced to manually take over whenever I want to pass slower traffic on the highway — a glaring omission for a pricey $95K luxury vehicle.

Compounding that frustration is how Audi continues to put all of the adaptive cruise control functions on a dedicated stalk tucked behind the lower left side of the steering wheel, which forces me to move my hand slightly away from the wheel. I’d much rather have them integrated into one of the control pads on the steering wheel itself.

There’s a lot to love about the 2027 Audi S6 Sportback e-tron, starting with its sharp and sporty design, all the way to its genuinely practical hatchback-like utility. Add in a rich roster of high-end comfort tech — from its augmented reality HUD to its built-in dashcam — it delivers an upscale daily driving experience.

Still, at nearly $95,000 as tested, that steep price tag makes it a tough pill to swallow for most buyers, especially when smaller quirks like a narrow windshield and inconveniently placed cruise-control stalk hold it back from perfection. If you don't need the extra cargo space or blistering acceleration and are willing to go a little smaller, the 2027 Mercedes-Benz CLA 250+ delivers exceptional luxury EV value at a dramatically lower price.
