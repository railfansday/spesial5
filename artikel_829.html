Ex-Iceland goalkeeper Ingvar Jonsson who was part of Euro 2016 squad that stunned England dies aged 37

Ingvar Jonsson, the former Iceland goalkeeper who was part of the squad that defeated England at the 2016 European Championship, has died at the age of 37.

His former club Víkingur announced his death on Thursday. The team did not provide a cause of death, although Jonsson had reportedly been diagnosed with cancer earlier this year.

In a statement, the Icelandic club said: "It is with great sorrow and deep sadness that we announce that our dear friend and teammate Ingvar Jonsson, goalkeeper for Víkingur, passed away on Wednesday, November 25, in the arms of his loved ones."

They added: "The club and all of Víkingur are thinking of his family and loved ones. We send them our deepest condolences."

Jonsson served as an unused substitute during Euro 2016, when Iceland created one of the greatest shocks in tournament history by knocking England out in the round of 16. The team were later eliminated by France in the quarter-finals.

Paying tribute on X, European football’s governing body Uefa wrote: "We extend our deepest condolences to Ingvar’s family, friends and former team-mates. Our thoughts are with them at this difficult time."

Jonsson earned eight international caps for Iceland, having made his first squad back in 2012. He had to wait another two years before making his first appearance for his country in a friendly against Belgium.

His final cap for Iceland came in 2019, but he continued to make the squad through until 2022.

His club career saw him start at Njardvik in his homeland before moving to Norwegian outfit IK Start in 2015. He would go on to play for Sandnes Ulf and Sandefjord prior to leaving Norway in 2018 to join Danish side Viborg.

After two years in Denmark, Jonsson returned to Iceland to play for Vikingur and remained at the club until his passing.
