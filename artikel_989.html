Cox: Three hundreds not enough to keep Buttler out

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

England's Jordan Cox ahead of series-deciding third ODI vs Australia quips scoring three hundreds would not be enough to keep Jos Buttler out of the side when latter returns, hailing him 'England's best ever'

Jordan Cox insists Jos Buttler has earned the right to come back into England's ODI side, even if he was to score three hundreds while the country's "best ever" white-ball player recovers from injury.

Cox will continue to bat and keep wicket in the ODI series decider against Australia at Hobart's Bellerive Oval on Wednesday, having scored a lively 67 in England's series-levelling win in Adelaide last time out.

He owes his place in the XI to Buttler's torn hamstring and is pragmatic when it comes to his prospects of keeping the shirt for next year's World Cup campaign.

Asked about his chances of winning that particular selection battle, he said: "I don't think so, no. I think what Jos has done for English cricket in general [is too much].

"Even if I score, let's say three hundreds - if I was head selector I'd be like: 'Coxy, I'm really sorry but our best ever English white-ball player is coming back in.'

"That's absolutely fine. I'm not saying I would like that to happen, because I would love to be in that team. Playing ODI cricket, playing cricket for England is the best thing ever, but Jos is a freak and I feel like he's earned the right to come back into any team."

England are set to stick with an unchanged side for their winner-takes-all decider, with Sonny Baker's new-ball showing last time out enough to keep him in ahead of Jamie Overton. That also means Adil Rashid is expected to miss out again and will need to wait for the five-match T20 series to see action.

Essex batter-wicketkeeper Cox has enjoyed a breakout year in international cricket, making his Test debut in the summer against New Zealand at The Oval and earning a spot in both white-ball sides for the current trip Down Under.

The 25-year-old looks increasingly well placed to play a significant role in England's future plans but his Adelaide-born mother, Roslyn, made sure he was always aware of his options growing up.

When the Cox family followed England's victorious 2010/11 Ashes tour as fans, she ensured there was a home kit on hand as well as one from the touring camp, but his own allegiances were clear even at the age of 10.

He recalls collecting signatures from Sir Andrew Strauss' squad and still has a picture of himself playing tennis at a hotel court with Yorkshire seamer Tim Bresnan.

"Life throws you in so many directions and Mum's always gone: 'Oh, you've got an Australian passport, it would be pretty nice if you played for my country'," Cox said.

"Mum's a big Aussie lover, obviously, being brought up here. And you know how Australians are: very passionate. My dad's very passionate with England, so I don't know how they managed to make it work!

"We came to watch the Ashes out here back in the day, I remember my brother and I used to wear different shirts (one Australia, one England) at the games.

"But when I joined Kent at the age of 10 I never thought about playing for Australia. I was like: 'My dream is to play for England', and it's been no different since."

Australia all-rounder Cooper Connolly said on Tuesday the ongoing 'Sandpaper-gate' controversy has not affected the team ahead of facing England in Hobart.

Of Australia's form heading into the contest, Connolly added:

"Probably we just put ourselves in a bit of a hole at the start, being 4-29. So it's always pretty hard to come back from there.

"It's probably just that, but still continuing to maintain the way we play at the top there, and still being aggressive because that can change your game quite quickly as well.

"It's always exciting to play in a series-decider, and especially to have it at home in Australia is always nice. And hopefully, we can put on a show for the fans tomorrow.

"We're all looking forward to it. Obviously, the last game wasn't the result we were after. But we'll look to learn from that. We're looking forward to the opportunity."
