Barbie Rewind Review: A Treasure Trove of Classic Barbie Video Games

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Barbie returns with a spectacular collection of 16 classic, retro games, including an unreleased title, to celebrate her nearly 70-year history.

For almost 70 years, Barbie has dominated the toy industry and pop culture in ways that are almost unfathomable. After watching her daughter play with paper dolls and assign them adult roles and lives, Ruth Handler convinced her husband Elliot to start creating dolls that broke away from the usual infant or toddler designs.

They named her Barbie after their daughter Barbara, launching what would become one of the most iconic pop culture icons of all time. As any good pop culture landmark does, Barbie eventually started making her way into video games. I’m a bit of a Barbie fanatic, and I suspect one of the main reasons for that is playing Barbie video games during a lot of the 1990s.

TMNT fans are in for a real treat with Digital Eclipse's retro compilation of classic games.

To celebrate the rich longevity of the legendary doll, Digital Eclipse and Atari have teamed up to produce Barbie Rewind. This spectacular collection brings together 12 of Barbie’s most beloved games, all of them packed together in what has become a rather common feat for Digital Eclipse. This celebratory package of Barbie’s endearing legacy of empowerment is one that every fan of the iconic doll is going to want to add to their dream home.

Barbie Rewind uses a really fun framing device to sort of justify the existence of the games in the collection. That was entirely optional, and Digital Eclipse didn’t have to actually do that, but what they’ve done is incredibly cute. Normally, games just exist in a collection and that’s it, but Barbie Rewind makes a direct effort to explain why all these games are together.

In the “Let’s Decorate” mode, you are tasked with designing a Barbie Dream Home with assistance from Barbie herself. In order to unlock more stuff for the house, you have to play the games in the collection. It’s a fun little incentive to get you playing more of the collection, which helps keep the game from feeling like a museum, and instead infuses it with the classic Barbie personality.

Like so many of Digital Eclipse’s previous collections, Barbie Rewind is built around a genuine love for the IP it’s celebrating. Though it sadly lacks some of the more comprehensive video documentaries and historical documentation that have made previous Digital Eclipse collections even more engaging, there’s still a lot to love here as a Barbie fan.

Each game does include beautiful scans of the manual, and the lack of video documentaries isn’t exactly the worst thing in the world, but after seeing the effort that was put into them from previous collections, them not being here in some form is a bit disappointing. Even so, there are a grand total of 16 Barbie games here, even including an unreleased game known as Barbie: Vacation Adventure, so there’s quite a lot of content to sort through here.

Among the 16 Barbie games included with Barbie Rewind, so many of them hit me in ways that I wasn’t quite expecting. Growing up loving Barbie was one thing, but aside from the unreleased game, which I wasn’t aware of until this collection came around, I have strong, distinct memories from each title in the collection. It’s been quite an experience to explore these games again.

Beginning with a game known simply as Barbie that released on the NES in 1991, Barbie Rewind chronicles her outstanding run of video games all the way through the SNES and the PS1, all the way up to the GBA in 2007. Through nearly two decades of Barbie video games, Digital Eclipse has done a fabulous job of bringing so many iconic games back to the forefront.

Like so many of Digital Eclipse’s previous collections, Barbie Rewind is built around a genuine love for the IP it’s celebrating.

These games come from a very different time in not just video game history, but Barbie’s history as well. Barbie was continuously built on making sure girls knew they could be literally anything they wanted, whether that was a world champion rollerblader or a detective. It often feels like some sort of bizarre mash-up of WarioWare and Barbie, as you’ll go from designing a dream home to being a secret agent.

Many of the games are also charmingly bite-sized in their minute-to-minute gameplay, so you can seamlessly jump between each game to your heart’s content. Like a lot of retro collections, Barbie Rewind also has a rewind feature, but the buttons for that rewind feature also tend to pop off and on randomly at the beginning of each game in some rather annoying ways. It isn’t an outright disaster, but it was enough to get rather irritating after a while.

While Barbie has dominated TV and movies, including a big budget blockbuster adaptation in 2023 starring Margot Robbie, one of her more successful ventures over the years has been video games. Each decade of Barbie has seen her try her hand at everything from being an astronaut to a rock star, leaning heavily into the pop culture that defined that era.

The games in Barbie Rewind are indicative of that, as well. If you grew up with Chris Anthony, Mattel’s very own in-house voice of Barbie for over a decade, as the voice of Barbie, you’ll be right at home. Many of these games, such as Barbie: Race & Ride and The Barbie Diaries, are intrinsically tied to a very specific era of the character.

They feel like time capsules, and revisiting them in this collection is going to be an absolute delight for anyone who grew up with this wonderful time for her. This was right at the height of Mattel figuring out that Barbie needed to be and feel more connected to the young people who collected her toys. You can still feel some essence of Barbie’s previous life in the early games, but as each new title comes along, you’ll notice a distinct evolution that’s impossible to ignore.

Though the games in the collection vary in quality, all of them are worth jumping into in their own unique ways. The early platformers are all fairly basic, although some of them actually have fun physics that felt unique for the time, but the real draw is in the later PS1 and Game Boy games. You never really know what each game is going to throw at you, and for a character who made her name on being anything she wanted to be, that feels quite in character for the ambitious woman they call Barbie.

Barbie Rewind brings together 16 classic Barbie games, including an unreleased Genesis and SNES game, for one spectacularly nostalgic package. The quality of each game is wildly different, but that tends to come with the territory of these retro collections. Each game feels uniquely fun in its own way, but the package itself has a strange issue with the rewind buttons, as well as a lack of video documentaries that have defined previous Digital Eclipse collections.
