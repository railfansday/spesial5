Is the Galaxy S26 Ultra still a good buy at the end of 2026?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Yes, the Samsung Galaxy S26 Ultra remains a good buy in late 2026 if you want a large-screen Android phone with versatile cameras, an integrated S Pen, and long-term software support.

The Galaxy S26 Ultra has aged very well, benefiting from Samsung’s mature hardware and software, along with a host of other quality-of-life improvements gained from its recent update to One UI 9 running Android 17.

We’re going to take a deeper dive at what the S26 Ultra is, and how it compares to its cheaper siblings, in a quest to figure out whether it’s still worth your time and money this holiday season.

The Samsung Galaxy S26 Ultra is the highest-end model in Samsung’s 2026 Galaxy S26 lineup. Released back on March 11, it pairs a 6.9-inch display with a Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 processor, a built-in S Pen, and a four-camera system led by a 200MP main sensor.

It’s a powerful device, built for photography enthusiasts and professionals who want to get the most out of their phones while on the go. Its large screen size belies a relatively compact, portable device that’s a pleasure to do work on, from writing emails to taking photos to taking meeting notes using the S Pen.

We also found dependable everyday endurance from the 5,000mAh battery, and now that the phone supports 60-watt wired charging, quick top-ups become more useful. Samsung also promises seven generations of OS upgrades and seven years of security updates from launch, an important consideration if you plan to keep your next phone for several years.

At launch, US pricing started at $899.99 for the Galaxy S26, $1,099.99 for the Galaxy S26+, and $1,299.99 for the Galaxy S26 Ultra, each with 256GB of storage. But those prices have dropped over the past few months, and you may be able to get them for less. We’ve seen the S26 Ultra, for example, as low as $999.99 during Prime Day.

Having different lenses gives you more options for portraits, travel photos, and subjects you can’t easily walk closer to.

The regular Galaxy S26 is the more natural choice if you prioritize a smaller phone and a lower purchase price. The Galaxy S26+ makes sense if you want a large-screen Galaxy without paying for Ultra-specific features.

The biggest improvement since launch is the move from Android 16-based One UI 8.5 to Android 17-based One UI 9.

One UI 9 offers several practical additions. Now Nudge suggestions extend beyond the keyboard into conversation notifications and a floating button. The quick panel offers more customization, and Samsung DeX gains easier movement between desktops and desktop previews in Recents.

Samsung has also made Smart Switch even better, which allows you to quickly and easily port over your apps, data, and media from another device.
