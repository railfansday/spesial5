Meta expands Muse to the U.K.

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Let Social Media Today's free newsletter keep you informed, straight from your inbox.

The launch comes amid ongoing security concerns about what kind of user data the artificial intelligence agent may be accessing.

Meta has launched its Muse artificial intelligence agents in the U.K., which marks the next stage in the company’s expanded push to make Muse its key access point for consumer AI.

In order to launch its latest AI-powered tools in the U.K., Meta had to align with reporting and transparency regulations, as outlined by the U.K. Competition and Markets Authority. The CMA said that it had worked with Meta on its terms and conditions relating to Muse, in order to ensure that they align with existing consumer laws.

That could ensure greater protection for U.K. users, with the CMA also monitoring usage of the new app.

As per Meta: “From doing the Christmas shopping and searching for a cheaper energy deal, to sorting the school calendar or turning your saved travel Reels into a holiday itinerary, Muse can get it done for you. And from today, Meta’s personal AI agent, which proactively helps with your goals and suggests ideas, is available in the UK.”

Meta has partnered with a range of U.K. retailers and other businesses to maximize the utility of Muse right off the bat, including Marks & Spencer, Debenhams Group, Currys, Ocado Retail, Argos, Sainsbury’s, ASOS, JD Sports and more.

“With some [of these partners], you can connect your personal account so Muse can reorder your usual shop or pull up a booking,” Meta said. “With others, Muse can find its way around their websites from day one, so it can help you find what you need and get it done.”

As noted, Meta’s U.K. expansion is the latest in the company’s push to make Muse its next big platform, and realize its broader vision for consumer AI. The company launched Muse agents in Australia last week, while Muse is also available in the U.S., Canada, Japan and Singapore, with more regions set to come soon.

Though questions remain about the security of Muse, amid reports of unintentional misuse and vulnerabilities.

Shortly after Muse launched, a user in Toronto empowered his Muse agent to list and sell his items on Facebook Marketplace. The user then found that Muse had negotiated a deal, and organized for the buyer to pick up an item from his home address, without the user being aware of this process.

Further investigation found that the user had given Muse permission to do all of these tasks, though the user himself didn’t realize the level of authority that he had granted the app.

Which is at the core of a more recent security issue related to the AI bot.

According to a report from Reuters, Muse agents have been accessing users’ encrypted DMs in other apps, such as Signal, even when they don’t have explicit permission to do so. The Muse agents have been using systematic workarounds to avoid restrictions, and access more information than the user may have authorized.

As per Reuters: “Specifically, on Android, [Muse] used its permission to access the device's push notifications to retrieve the messages… On Apple’s macOS, it used a combination of the computer’s accessibility and screenshot tools, repurposing settings Apple offers to help people with disabilities operate their computers.”

So rather than directly accessing people’s private, encrypted DMs, which users may have restricted, and may feel are comfortably safe from the tool, Meta’s Muse agents can find other ways to track people’s private messages. This means that even without explicit access being granted to these tools, Meta’s AI bots can still gather private data.

Signal CEO Meredith Whittaker responded to these reports by further restricting Muse access to its platform.

As per Whittaker: “We also see a growing need for tools that can prevent third-party agents, like Meta’s Muse, from accessing application data using the deep permissions that users are encouraged to grant them during setup. In many cases these agents are leveraging accessibility settings that were meant to empower actual people to get the most out of their apps and services, not to act as a skeleton key that provides access to AI agents.”

The Signal incident highlights significant potential security vulnerabilities that could be exposed by Meta agentic tools, with Meta's AI systems able to problem-solve their way through access restrictions. The implications of that could be significant, especially if Muse can find workarounds to access banking information or health data that users may not actually want to share.

In response to these reports, Meta reiterated that users remain in control of the access that they grant Muse. But if those users don’t understand the technicalities of these processes, that assurance is largely meaningless.

In this context, it’s also worth noting that, according to data provided by Apptopia, around 46% of Muse users in the U.S. are over age 46.

According to data from the FBI, older users are more susceptible to online scams, and are seemingly less aware of the tools and processes that are in place to protect them. The FBI reported that in 2025, victims over 60 lost more than $7.7 billion to online scams, a 59% increase over 2024.

There's obviously some gap between the digital literacy of users aged under and over 50, but even so, the fact that older users are seemingly more willing to trust Muse could be setting the stage for further concerns.

According to data tracking conducted by First Page SEO, Muse is now up to 2.74 million daily active users, with shopping and research as its primary use cases.
