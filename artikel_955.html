Mauricio Pochettino has agreed to become the new Manchester United manager after Michael Carrick’s sacking.

A second Old Trafford comeback of the week, this time battling from two goals down twice to rescue a 4-4 draw with Aston Villa on Sunday, was not enough to save Carrick’s job and the club announced on Thursday that it had parted ways with him after just 10 games of the season and with his side 18th during the international break.

Former Chelsea and Tottenham boss Pochettino has now been confirmed as Carrick’s replacement, bringing his reign as USA manager to a close at the end of the international break.

He was tearful about leaving the USA in a press conference on Friday but said an opportunity the size of United is “difficult to turn down”.

Manchester United have appointed Mauricio Pochettino as their new manager.

The club confirmed their decision to sack Michael Carrick after just 10 games of the season on Thursday, with the club dwindling in 18th heading into the international break.

United have paid US Soccer compensation worth an initial $7m (£5.3m) to get Pochettino out of his existing contract, which ran until the 2030 World Cup. Additional compensation will be required for Pochettino’s trusted coaching staff, including long-time assistant Jesus Perez.

Mauricio Pochettino said he received contact from Manchester United on Sunday about possibly taking over as manager.

Michael Carrick was not officially sacked until Thursday.

"All was so quick,” he said. “Sunday afternoon, there started to be some contact. We were very transparent [with US Soccer].

"We’ll have time next week to talk about the job at Manchester United. It’s much better to talk now about US Soccer."

Mauricio Pochettino struggled to fight off tears as he held his first press conference since being confirmed as Michael Carrick’s managerial successor at Manchester United.

Mauricio Pochettino is the new head coach of Manchester United having agreed to take over following the sacking of Michael Carrick.

His first game in charge will be at the cauldron of Anfield to take on Liverpool on Sunday 22 November and, not only is it a contest imbued with history and rivalry, but it is also a match that United must show signs of improvement in.

His parting message to the press pack: “Thank you guys. Honour to be with you.”

“Before being a professionals we are people,” he said. “The emotional link between us that we built in these two years is to show the commitment and respect and, and the care and the love even if we took a decision that, for one party, is maybe not the best.

“We were open, you know, because I think we have the respect of the players and the staff, and they understand the situation.

“If Manchester United and the Federation agreed on different terms, I would need to respect the agreement, but this has happened and of course all the games are important.”

“It’s obvious,” he replied. “I never say that I want to fall back.

“They know there’s not too many opportunities that can happen in your career the size of Manchester United, and when that happens, it’s difficult to turn down that situation.”

US Soccer CEO JT Batson said: “The agreement with Manchester United, those funds US Soccer is a non-profit. Those funds are used to hire great coaches, invest in youth development.”

“I will have time next week to talk about the job at Manchester United,” he said. “But now it's much better to talk about us, USA Soccer.

“We now have two games ahead and now is complete focus on this.”

“We were talking with the federation. Yesterday when things were positive in the way that the agreement was, we had a meeting and I communicated to the player and the whole staff.

“The decision is between the federation and Manchester United that we continue with our job in the camp.

“I think we will have time next week to talk about the job at Manchester United. Now I think it is better to talk about USA soccer and the national team. We have two games ahead and the focus is completely on this.”
