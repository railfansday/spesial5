Kane the hero on historic night as England book Nations League QF spot

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

England vs Croatia. UEFA Nations League Group A3.

Match report from Wembley as Harry Kane marks his 126th England cap with the winner from the penalty spot; Morgan Rogers bundled opener but Three Lions needed Kane to convert after Jarell Quansah's error allowed Croatia to level; England into Nations League quarter-finals

Senior Football Journalist @psmithXI

Please use Chrome browser for a more accessible video player

Record-breaker Harry Kane scored from the spot to seal England's place in the Nations League quarter-finals with a 2-1 victory over Croatia, capping a memorable Wembley night as the captain became his country's most-capped player.

There was an inevitability about Kane hitting the net on his 126th appearance. There had been pre-match tributes and accolades as the Ballon d'Or holder passed the mark of legendary goalkeeper Peter Shilton and once again Kane was England's match winner with his second-half spot-kick. That's 92 goals now in England colours for the record scorer.

The win means England will now have the chance to win Nations League Group A3 and be seeded for the quarter-finals - but they will have to be far better than this if they are to beat table-toppers and world champions Spain in Madrid on Sunday.

The Nations League quarter-finals will take place in March, with the top two from each League A group going into the draw.Teams which finish top of their group will be seeded and drawn against teams which have finished second for the two-leg ties which will determine who make it into the Nations League finals in June.

England thumped Croatia 7-0 last month but this was a performance far shy of that previous display, lacking intensity and final-third spark for long periods, and littered with jittery moments at the back.

Thankfully for Thomas Tuchel, he has Kane. The captain orchestrated the opener in his trademark style, dropping deep before clipping a fine pass for Jude Bellingham to cross for Morgan Rogers to bundle in when Josko Gvardiol failed to clear.

But moments after Kane's Bayern Munich team-mate Josip Stansic made a key block on the England skipper, Jarell Quansah's blunder gave Croatia a gift. His header back to Jordan Pickford was too casual and pounced on by Petar Sucic.

Quansah almost immediately made amends, being hauled down from an England corner by Ivan Smolcic for Kane to have his big moment.

But still England couldn't go up through the gears. James Garner's celebrations were cut short by an offside flag but the final stages were nervy for the hosts, with Nikola Vlasic wastefully clipping wide and Ante Budimir heading over.

On Kane's historic night, England were again indebted to their captain fantastic.

"It was an okay performance overall. It took us a while to figure the press out because they played in a back five. It needed a lot of communication because we wanted to stay in our structure.

"Once we figured that out after 15 minutes we were the better team. You felt the confidence coming. We got a bit sloppy in the last five minutes of the first half. Good reaction at the start of the second half. Created more and more.

"We got again sloppy for five minutes and were punished from a set-piece which is uncharacteristic from us. Then we needed to show a reaction because we were desperate for a result.

"Croatia had nothing to lose and you have to overcome these moments."

"It was incredible. Everything was amazing. Thank you to everyone who organised [the build-up], with the tifo and presentations. Thank you to Luka [Modric] and Croatia for their gift.

"I knew there would be something before the game. It's been a crazy few weeks with a lot happening, but it's an important moment in our history as a national team.

"But once the game started it was full focus. These occasions feel much better when you win and now I can take a breath and enjoy this."

Sky Sports News' Rob Dorsett at Wembley Stadium:

"As a game, it was instantly forgettable. As an occasion, it will live long in the memory. But that is all about what happened pre-match, rather than during the 90 minutes.

"Captain Kane being recognised as not only the best player in the world, but also as England's most capped player of all time, was a proper "I was there" moment. Inevitably, he scored to mark the occasion, with a coolly-slotted second-half penalty.

"But beyond that, England were disappointing. Flat. Lifeless, in the main. Lacking energy, spark and invention. The two full-backs, Alexander-Arnold and Hall, came out of it with some credit. And Elliot Anderson was as industrious and effective as ever. But that was about it.

"It was enough to ensure a victory - just about - and to set up a winner-takes-all Nations League group finale against the world champions Spain in Madrid on Sunday. Maybe England's players had that game in the back of their minds, and held back a bit. Let's hope that's what it was, anyway."
