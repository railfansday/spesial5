Russian national accused of stalking San Francisco techie in plot against US defense-tech giant

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

DoJ claims suspect part of 4-month operation to steal secrets, surveil employees

A Russian national is being held in custody after being arrested for allegedly spying on a US defense tech company in Palo Alto, shoulder-surfing employees, and dropping remote surveillance devices at their homes.

Igor D'iu was indicted after FBI agents surveilled his activities between June and October 2026, which they say were issued as "tasks" from what is thought to be Russia’s Federal Security Service (FSB), according to an affidavit [PDF] signed by an FBI special agent.

After FBI agents secured a warrant to raid D'iu's temporary premises in October, they say that Telegram messages harvested from his seized devices confirmed investigators' findings and revealed that he was spying on an unnamed company.

Court documents describe the company in question as a large technology and defense company with offices in Palo Alto, and media-reported links to the Russia-Ukraine war.

While unconfirmed officially, the company has widely been reported as Palantir, which did not respond to our request for confirmation.

D'iu allegedly received instructions from Telegram contacts, whom he only suspected were representatives of the Russian government, weeks after entering the US illegally in July.

The indictment alleges he was ordered to surveil employees' homes, take photographs of their screens while working remotely at coffee shops, and permit his handlers to remotely access his laptop for the purpose of capturing data from nearby devices owned by the company’s employees.

In some cases, the filing alleges, D’iu was asked to leave a laptop outside one employee's apartment complex, all while allowing his Telegram handlers to remotely operate the device.

The first time, he was asked to leave the laptop for one hour while the handlers worked, and another time, he was told to drop it off in the middle of the night and only come back to collect it three hours later, the filing claims.

D'iu is also allegedly to have followed two female employees in his rented car, driving dangerously and violating multiple road laws to do so.

One of the two employees was followed briefly, but D'iu followed another for nearly 30 miles, according to the affidavit signed by FBI special agent Winston Wong.

D'iu was said to have followed the woman as she performed U-turns, illegal left turns, ran red lights, navigated via back and side streets, and through grocery store parking lots.

The car following her only peeled away after the woman drove to the San Francisco Mission police station on the advice of a 911 call handler.

Questioned about the incident, which according to police reports caused the woman significant emotional distress, D'iu confirmed he was the driver but  claimed he was following her to ask her out on a date.

The affidavit did not detail the suspect's background beyond his alleged offenses in the US earlier this year, although it mentioned Russian intelligence's history of recruiting "criminal proxies, organized crime groups, and other disposable agents" to carry out operations.

According to the FBI's account, D'iu entered the US illegally – without relevant visas – in either June or July 2026. Travel records obtained on September 10 showed that his last recorded journey was from Istanbul to Mexico City. There was no record of him entering the US.

Investigators would later come to know that he travelled on that Istanbul-Mexico City flight on June 7, allegedly using a ticket provided to him by one of the three contacts from whom he received orders via Telegram.

A Facebook account, registered using a Mexican +528 number and D'iu's name, posted on June 30 asking for help getting "closer to New York," adding that "I'll pay as much as needed." The account replied to a response from someone who told him to contact a Mexico phone number on WhatsApp.

The filing claims the first mobile phone signals associated with D'iu's devices did not ping US towers until a month later, on July 31, suggesting he did not arrive in the US - specifically McAllen, Texas - until around that date. Telegram messages would also go on to confirm that he was allegedly helped across the border by an "Uzbek contact" in Mexico.

Records obtained from Google showed D'iu created a Google account in April. According to the affidavit, D'iu used it to search for the company's Wikipedia page on July 28, and on August 3, used Google's AI to devise a route around Texas that bypassed border checkpoints.

The FBI believes D'iu's "spying" began in mid-August, after he bought a laptop, a Wi-Fi device, a puck device, and an additional phone. At this point, the agency claims, he had received his first order to visit a coffee shop near the Palo Alto company’s office and snap employees' laptop screens before sending copies back to his handlers.

Three days later, D'iu was ordered to go back to a coffee shop and allow his Telegram contacts to remotely control the laptop and collect data from employee devices that were in range, claims the filing.

D'iu was paid $15,000 on three occasions between August 12 and October 6, according to the court documents. To verify the transaction, he was told to send a picture of a dollar bill's serial number to the Telegram contacts, along with a description of what he was wearing. A courier would then arrive, confirming the serial number and handing D'iu the cash.

The filing alleges he used this money to purchase the tech equipment, accommodation, and travel, including a rented silver 2026 Lexus RX 350 from a private owner in Los Angeles on August 22, which he is said to have driven back to San Francisco.

The same Lexus was captured on security footage outside the unnamed company's office on September 1, and over the following two days was implicated in the two instances of female employees being followed.

Contacted by Palo Alto police on September 4, D'iu confirmed he was the driver who dangerously followed the female staffer for nearly 30 minutes, but claimed he knew nothing about the nearby businesses. D'iu would then claim he followed the woman because he thought she was beautiful and wanted to ask her out on a date, and denied following anyone else.

The affidavit goes on to allege that D'iu then traveled to Reno, Nevada, to lay low after his run-in with the police. He spent the time trying to reroute DHL deliveries from San Francisco to his hideout address, which US Customs would later intercept.

These ended up containing fake IDs with the images the DoJ claims matched D'iu's, including Uzbek documents, a US passport whose number belonged to a New York woman, and a social security card registered to a "Hugo Landry Johns."

He traveled back to the Bay Area in late September, swapping his silver Lexus for a white 2016 Dodge Challenger, and received additional instructions from his handlers, including the order to leave a remote-controlled laptop outside the apartment complex, says the filing.

One Telegram contact named Ian Morales sent photos of at least four employees and their addresses, along with instructions for D'iu to dress as a food delivery worker and visit those locations, asking whoever answered to verify they lived there.

FBI agents executed a search warrant on October 8, seizing a cache of evidence, including what they listed as being:

A notebook with 10 handwritten addresses supplied by Telegram contacts, along with times the suspect was supposed to attend those locations, at least five of which were for company employees’ homes

Profile packages for at least three employees, including: Names, personal information, education and employment histories, names of relatives, narrative histories, and assessments of leadership qualities, strengths, and weaknesses.

Per the affidavit, D'iu was said to have only become suspicious of the tasks he was being ordered to complete in October, when his handlers ordered the laptop to be dropped near the apartment complex.

"D'iu gradually began to understand he was doing something bad and that his actions were illegal," the affidavit stated.

At this point, he allegedly asked the Telegram contacts who he was working for, to which they replied "the Motherland," and the company he was surveilling was involved in providing weapons to Ukraine, resulting in D’iu suspecting the Russian government.

Wong said in the affidavit: "Based on my training, experience, and research, D'iu's communications with his Telegram contacts is consistent with the tactics of [Russian intelligence] and its criminal proxies for spotting, assessing, recruiting, and handling intelligence assets and sources.

"D'iu's targeting of the company and its employees is also consistent with [Russian intelligence] objectives in Russia's war against Ukraine."

The Russian national was indicted and charged with two counts related to spying offences, and one count related to the fake identity documents. He is next due in court on November 24 but remains in federal custody. ®
