Three winners and three losers of Mancini’s new crop of Italy internationals

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Italy’s Nations League journey has come to a premature end after Sunday’s 4-1 defeat away against Belgium, and though the campaign finished in disappointment, the last few weeks have brought opportunities for a number of new, young faces in the Nazionale squad: Here are three who impressed when given the opportunity and three who still have room for improvement.

Italy were defeated 4-1 by Belgium in Brussels on Sunday evening, despite a 10th-minute opener from Inter’s Pio Esposito. Three goals within 11 minutes from Maxim De Cuyper Nathan Ngoy and Jeremy Doku shifted the balance of the game and a late fourth from Diego Moreira put the game beyond any doubt.

Despite starting the night at the top of the group, Sunday’s defeat against Belgium saw Italy overtaken both by their opponents and by France, who claimed a 2-1 victory over Turkey in their final group stage fixture.

Still, many outlets have urged Italy to look for the positive in spite of Sunday’s defeat against Belgium. Roberto Mancini and his players had picked up 10 points from their last five matches in a tough group and showed plenty of promise and character after a miserable year that had seen them miss out on qualification to the 2026 World Cup.

Though Mancini did not get his selections right against Belgium, he has largely been praised for his willingness to put faith in youth: Only three of Italy’s starters on Sunday were born before the year 2000 and five of them were aged 22 or below.

Mancini also gave opportunities to several players who had previously been overlooked, including Brentford right-back Michael Kayode, who now looks a nailed-on first-choice on the right of Italy’s back four for the foreseeable future. Of all the new faces that Mancini has introduced over the last few weeks, his emergence has arguably been the most encouraging.

Sporting CP midfielder Issa Doumbia has also earned praise in the Italian press on Monday: TMW credited his physical qualities but admitted that he still has room for improvement in terms of game management, but assessed his performances as generally positive when given the opportunity.

Napoli’s Antonio Vergara was also impressive on Italy’s right flank during this international break and has handled the step up to international football comfortably. TMW argues that he should be the go-to starter in that position for the time being.

There are, however, a few of the new Italy faces that may still need a bit more time, including two of the front three that started against Belgium on Sunday night. This was perhaps a touch too soon for Luca Koleosho, who remains untested at the highest levels, while Niccolo Pisilli also struggled to impress, albeit out of position on the right wing instead of his usual position in central midfield.

Sebastiano Esposito, who was dropped from the Italy squad on Sunday, will also be hoping for a touch more consistency in his performances, despite some encouraging moments during this Nations League campaign.

Peter Young is a Senior Reporter with Football Italia.
