Zelda: Ocarina Of Time Remake Sold 1 Million Physical Copies In Its First Week, The Most Since Tears Of The Kingdom

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

NintendoPhysical GamesRemakeThe Legend of Zelda: Ocarina Of Time

Physical games are dying, unless your game is called The Legend of Zelda. The Switch 2 remake of Ocarina of Time just sold 1 million physical copies in its first week in the U.S., the most of any release since Tears of the Kingdom released in 2023.

“After just one week in market, The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time is 2026’s best-selling physical video game in the US,” Circana senior director and video game industry advisor Mat Piscatella posted on Bluesky on November 16. “It is the 1st title to surpass 1M physical units in a US launch week since 2023’s The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom.”

Nintendo previously announced that Ocarina of Time sold 5.2 million copies globally across physical and digital in its first four days, which blew the original game’s debut on Nintendo 64 out of the water. It’s a wild number when you consider it’s a Switch 2 exclusive. The newest Nintendo console has only been out for roughly 18 months and only has an install base of around 26 million units, making the attach rate for the game nearly one in five.

There are, of course, two ways to take the Ocarina of Time remake’s physical game sales milestone. The first is to see it as a sign of how strong Switch 2 owners’ attachment to cartridge-based gaming remains even as digital swallows most of the market for game sales. The other is to note that no other game has managed to sell 1 million physical copies in the U.S. within its first week in over three years.

Physical game sales in the U.S. hit a 30-year low in 2025. Back in July, amid the frenzy over Sony’s announcement of the end of PlayStation discs in 2028, Piscatella pointed out that only seven PS5 games released in the first half of 2026 had sold more than 100,000 physical copies at the time. Total spending on physical games actually bounced upward this past August, however, which appears to have been thanks to Switch 2 game key cards.

Physical games might be dying, but on Switch 2 they’re not going without a fight.
