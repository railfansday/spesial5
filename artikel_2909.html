Market's Nvidia obsession obscuring an opportunity in this name

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The market is unable to divert its attention from anything not related to the ongoing saga that is Nvidia and its embattled former CEO Jensen Huang. It's understandable, given both the detail of the story and Nvidia impact on the market. It remains the tech story du jour, and it's getting plenty of ink.

I think investors should remember there are other big names reporting this week outside of Nvidia that deserve time and attention. One such name is The Walt Disney Company (DIS).

After a prolonged period of strategic transitions, the company may finally be turning the corner. Driven by a renewed focus on the bottom line, management's cost discipline now supports a runway for double-digit earnings-per-share (EPS) gains through fiscal 2027, even as it continues to trade at about its 5-year average forward multiple of 14.3x, well below the broad market multiple of more than 18x. Trading back to a market multiple, justified if it establishes above-market EPS growth, suggests ~26% upside, which is also consistent with a sum-of-the-parts analysis that points to an intrinsic valuation of $140 to $160 per share versus the Friday, November 13th closing price of $118.97.

Despite broader macroeconomic anxieties, the theme park and cruise divisions are showing surprisingly resilient demand. Domestically, the metrics are highly encouraging: attendance is climbing, and more importantly, per-capita spending continues to rise.

On the digital front, Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) Disney's streaming margins currently sit in the low teens, respectable given the segment's previous lack of profits. But the real upside scenario requires looking ahead: if Disney can scale these DTC margins to 20% or higher, the stock could at least trade at a multiple comparable to streaming king Netflix (which would imply a share price of ~ $139 or so.

With linear TV stabilizing, the parks firing on all cylinders, and streaming marching toward higher profitability, Disney offers a highly asymmetric risk-reward profile. The magic is returning to Disney's financials, and the current share price heavily discounts a business fundamentally engineered for double-digit growth.

Right now, the options market is pricing a ~6% move by the end of the week (the company reports Tuesday, pre-open), which is below the 8 quarter median move of 7.4%. Anticipating a "vol crush" after earnings, I like selling December premium to help finance the purchase of a longer-dated call - a diagonal call/spread risk reversal.

When selecting an option trade consider the key things that affect its value and how those things differ from simply buying (or selling) the underlying asset. For these option trades, let's consider three sensitivities (Greeks): Delta, the rate at which the option position changes in value for changes in the underlying price. Theta, or decay, which measures how much the value of an option declines as time passes, and Vega, the change in an option's price associated with changes in the implied volatility of an option.

In the case of the diagonal risk reversal each of these factors plays into the structure to our benefit. The delta of the structure is less than 100, meaning that the p&l associated with moves up or down will be less for one spread than they would be for 100 shares of stock.

We like this because the stock has already had a decent run recently, and the option structure will lose less than the stock will if it gives that back. Because nearer dated expiration decay more rapidly as a percentage of the options value than longer dated options do (and out of the money options also exhibit accelerated decay), the shorter dated December options we're selling should decay in value more quickly than the longer dated March options we are buying, even though the March option has a higher premium. Vega can work in our favor as well due to "vol crush". When option premiums are elevated due to a catalyst such as earnings we should expect that much of that premium will be sucked out or crushed once the event has passed.

This will impact both the December and March options to a degree, but remember that the March options also capture another earnings event in February, so some measure of event variance (elevated option premium) will remain in the March expiration to account for that.
