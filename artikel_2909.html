Ducks Wire staff predicts Oregon Ducks vs. Ohio State Buckeyes

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

We have seen some big-time matchups between the Oregon Ducks and Ohio State Buckeyes over the years, and while this one between two teams having relatively underwhelming seasons so far may not rise to the level of Rose Bowls or National Championships, it still is massively important for both schools.

The Ducks and Buckeyes are playing for their College Football Playoff lives, in essence, and both are at less-than-full-strength, bringing backup quarterbacks to the table. Oregon has seen a lot of turmoil so far this year, parting ways with offensive coordinator Drew Mehringer a couple of weeks ago, but the early returns on new play-caller Jay Johnson are positive.

Meanwhile, Ohio State has had one game since starting QB Julian Sayin went out for the year, and it resulted in a frustrating loss to the USC Trojans behind Luke Fahey.

So, what does Saturday have in store? That's a tough question to answer.

To preview the game, we go through our weekly roundtable with the writers from Ducks Wire to offer our opinions and predictions for what's to come. Here is our roundtable conversation.

Question: Well, that was quite the turnaround. Oregon looked like it was headed for another disappointing loss before storming back to beat No. 20 Northwestern, 34-26. What was your biggest takeaway from that win, and did it change your outlook on the Ducks for the rest of the season?

Zachary Neel: I think my biggest takeaway is just what it says about the team. Down 20-6 in the second quarter, it would have been pretty easy for many to just turn it in and let the season come to a close, but this team fought. I loved what the offense showed as far as downfield passing, and Brandon Smith showed a lot. I want to see more consistency, since that's been the main issue this year, but I do think the ceiling is higher this year than I did a week ago.

Don Smalley: I want to say yes, but I don't know. This team is very streaky. One minute they're really good, and the next minute they stink. I don't know what to make of it. I guess it just depends on which Dylan Raiola comes to play. If it's the Raiola against USC and Northwestern, this could be a fun end of the season. But I'm afraid he just can't be counted on to have that many good games in a row. My biggest takeaway is that they have the talent to run the table. But unfortunately, I won't bet money on it.

Bjorn Bergstrom: I saw a team that rallied around each other when their backs were against the wall. Down 20-6, this team could've just rolled over in what looked like a lost season. But they buckled their chinstraps and got to work, digging themselves out of a hole and avoiding dropping to .500. I may be a prisoner of the moment, but this does change my outlook on the Ducks for the rest of the season. What I saw in the second half was playoff-caliber football — if Oregon can harness that, they can make a run to finish the regular season, though those chances remain slim.

Logan Brown: It was just nice to hear that changes were being made and then actually see those changes in action. Oregon was aggressive throwing the football downfield for much of the game, and that opened up opportunities to run on the edges and create space in the middle of the field for in-breaking routes. If Oregon can maintain that aggression the rest of the way, the Ducks could absolutely win out, but I am a bit worried about the Ducks being able to maintain that mentality, even this week heading into a big game.

Question: Oregon's offense looked rejuvenated in the second half against Northwestern, with Jay Johnson calling plays and Dylan Raiola throwing for 336 yards and three touchdowns. How much credit do you give Johnson for the turnaround, and do you believe this offensive success is sustainable going forward?

Zachary Neel: I think Johnson gets credit for setting up certain plays with other plays, and there seemed to be a better flow to the offense, but on the other hand, this is what happens when you let playmakers make plays. Ultimately, I think it's the players who deserve most of the credit.

Don Smalley: The play-calling was definitely better. Sticking with the run to keep the defense off balance will help Raiola. It's just one game, and really just one half, but I'm looking forward to seeing more.

Bjorn Bergstrom: The players and coaches talked about it after the game, that one of the biggest emphasises was on getting the ball to Oregon's playmakers and letting them do their thing. In that case, I'd give Johnson more of the praise but Raiola did his part in giving his guys chances with the ball. As far as sustainability, I'm not so sure this "let it hang" mentality can be kept up for more than a few games — similar to how a backup QB in the NFL can get hot for a couple games before flaming out. The Ducks' offense will need to find some consistency rather than riding the wave.

Logan Brown: As I mentioned, I do worry that the Ducks built up the change so much for it to pay off against Northwestern because there was an urgent need for things to look different. I think we will know pretty quickly if Oregon's game plan against Ohio State is more of the same or taking steps back and if the momentum isn't rolling over. Jay Johnson has a huge impact, and his experience really matters. I think Dan Lanning learned that he can trust Johnson and doesn't necessarily need to micromanage the offense with him.

Question: Let's talk about the stakes of this game. Oregon's College Football Playoff hopes are still a long shot, but a win over Ohio State would be a massive statement and could keep the Ducks alive in the Big Ten Championship race. Just how important is this game for Dan Lanning and Oregon's season?

Zachary Neel: It's the entire season. Lose, and that's the end of things as far as postseason hopes go. Win, and you're still alive. Can't get more important than that.

Don Smalley: I think it's very important. Lanning has set the precedent that a playoff appearance is the goal. If they don't make the playoffs, that's a failed season. Of course, everyone is doing off-year. But with the talent they brought back, not making the playoffs would be a huge disappointment.

Bjorn Bergstrom: It's paramount towards determining whatever kind of success comes out of this year. Even though Oregon has three losses already, there has to be some kind of satisfaction when looking back at 2026 in beating Ohio State. So, all things considered, there is a lot riding on this game, CFP stakes or not.

Logan Brown: It's everything. This is practically a rivalry game at this point, given the state of the two rosters and their annual expectations in the Big Ten. Just because both are a bit down this season doesn't dilute the importance of the matchup itself for the two programs. Add in the do-or-die conference title and playoff hopes on the line for both teams, and it's hard to overstate what's on the line.

Question: Ohio State is coming off a tough loss to USC, and the Buckeyes are dealing with their own quarterback uncertainty after losing Julian Sayin for the season. Looking at this matchup, where do you think Oregon has the biggest advantage, and where do you think the Buckeyes present the biggest problem?

Zachary Neel: I think Oregon has an advantage particularly on the defensive line against Ohio State's offensive line, and it could be important when it comes to making a backup QB uncomfortable. However, the Buckeyes have quite possibly the best player in all of college football with Jeremiah Smith, so slowing him down will be important.

Don Smalley: Even with a backup quarterback, if he's able to get the ball to his five receivers, the Ducks will have a major problem. But Oregon might be able to take advantage of that defense. They didn't look good against USC at all. With a good Dylan Raiola, Oregon might make some noise on the scoreboard.

Bjorn Bergstrom: The lack of a run game for Ohio State sticks out to me, especially with a backup quarterback under center. Oregon has had its moments of struggling against the run, but, for the most part, they've been stout in that area. If they can limit the Buckeyes' ground game under 100 yards, forcing backup Luke Fahey to throw it often, I think the Ducks should like their chances.

Logan Brown: Oregon might have a significant edge in the trenches. Ohio State was bullied last week on both sides of the ball against USC, which Oregon played well against, and the Ducks have found a core group on the offensive line and a pass rush that can close games. On the other side, Jeremiah Smith is a problem, and the Ducks have struggled to contain top wideouts this season. Wyatt Young (Oklahoma State), Kayden Dixon-Wyatt (USC) and Griffin Wilde (Northwestern) all hurt the Ducks, and I think Smith is capable of having a big day once again, even with a freshman backup quarterback throwing the ball.

Question: Oregon has shown flashes of being an elite team this season, but consistency has been a major issue, particularly on the road. If the Ducks are going to walk into the Horseshoe and pull off the upset, what's the ONE thing they absolutely have to do well on Saturday?

Zachary Neel: They have to be able to run the ball well. If they can keep ahead of the chains with Dierre Hill and Brandon Smith, it will allow Raiola to play within the offense, and also set up some play-action deep shots down the field.

Don Smalley: I'll give you two: not turning the ball over and scoring early.

Bjorn Bergstrom: Staying resilient. They showed that in the second half against Northwestern and it's needed against Ohio State. The Buckeyes will throw plenty of punches; the Ducks just need to roll with them and get their own punches in as well. It's not a matchup between the No. 1 and No. 2 teams in the nation as it would've been preseason, but this is still a heavyweight matchup.

Logan Brown: They have to win the line of scrimmage on offense. It hasn't been perfect, but the unit has been much better in recent weeks. Solid protection against a defense that doesn't blitz a ton could help Raiola avoid turnovers and allow the Ducks to run the ball on early downs and play keepaway. If Oregon wants to win this game, it might have to be a low-scoring, field position battle, and holding the ball behind a strong offensive line is important.

Question: Gambling Corner: You get $100 to spread across the point spread (Oregon +7.5), and the Over/Under (45.5 points). How are you allocating your funds?

Zachary Neel: I'm going $50 on Oregon to cover the 7.5, and $50 on the over.

Don Smalley: I bet $75 Oregon covers and with two backup quarterbacks, I'm going with the under.

Bjorn Bergstrom: Unless the Buckeyes have massive amounts of success on the ground, which I don't anticipate, Oregon should cover — give me $50 on that. Then, I'll go $50 on the under. I think this one will be a grind-it-out sort of contest.

Logan Brown: I'll put $75 on the under because of the questions for both offenses, and I'll sprinkle $25 on Oregon to cover the spread and either keep it close or win outright. This game is a stayaway, in my opinion, because of the lack of consistency on both sides.

Question: Who wins this game, and what is the final score?

Bjorn Bergstrom: Ohio State 20, Oregon 17. Moral victories don't mean anything, especially in November, but I think we'll still come away impressed with how the Ducks performed in this one.

Logan Brown: Ohio State 24, Oregon 19. After a low-scoring first half, I think Raiola does make a mistake or two, and the Buckeyes take advantage of short fields and win behind a big day from Smith against the Ducks' secondary.
