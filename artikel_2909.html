Rockets flounder in Miami 128-110

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Sometimes a team has the energy to take a (private, comfortable) flight after a game, and play another game the next day, and play very well. After a big win in Washington, where pretty much everything from the early third quarter onward was garbage time, one might think the team would be more rested ready to compete in its next game. That's a great plan, and morally sound, but probably easier said than done if that post game flight is to Miami. The Rockets announcers called it a long flight, but it really isn't that long, a bit over two hours. Still, given all the gear, and to-ing and fro-ing you'd expect the Rockets to hit their likely downtown, luxury hotel, after midnight. You may be old, and I may be old, but the Rockets? They're not old. They might want to enjoy some nightlife in Miami.

Could they? Yes, absolutely. Why? Because although last call is typically 2AM in the Miami area, venues in the "Entertainment District" can serve until 5am. Bars and restaurants with a seating capacity under 100 people? They can stay open until 5am. And in certain areas liquor sales go on 24/7. Add in the fact that while you and I might not have access to posh, or wild after parties in Miami, (I can't speak for you) very wealthy young basketball stars probably do. The Rockets might discover a lot more entertainment, amusement and diversion on offer late night in Miami than you or I might find. Again, I can't speak for you.

This is possibly why the Rockets were flat, like a fish, a flat fish, a flounder. And flounder they did. At no point did this game look like a win for the Rockets. It started badly, with Miami shooting 3s like the peak Golden State Warrios, and that great shooting did not not only include a less-washed looking Klay Thompson (Heat Culture Injection!) and Andrew Wiggins, but everyone else, too. It didn't help that Miami came out playing like the U of Miami Hurricanes football team, and this was fine, apparently. They love the Hurricanes down there. Of the football variety, anyway.

The Rockets looked slower, less forceful, and generally not ready to do much. The, unique in the NBA Noah Laroche "No Screen" system, seemed to baffle the Rockets, and their switching and produced about 900 open corner 3pt shots for Miami. The novelty of a system they're not likely to see more than once was too much for the Rockets and they were breaded and fried like fish for 128 points tonight. Whereas Miami's coach, Erik Spoelstra just played the hits from last season, and packed the paint, and dared the Rockets to score inside while being, ah, "vigorously defended and possibly knifed" or make outside shots.

Make outside shots, they did not. The Rockets shot 28% from three, on fairly low volume as well. Miami put up 50 3pt shots, and made 18. That's a stark difference from the Rockets 36 3pt attempts. Why would you want to put up more 3s if you're not making them at a good rate? Because the 2pt shots weren't falling either. At 41% on twos, it's better to shoot more threes.

Would more energy have won this one? I'm not sure. They Rockets typically lose at least one game a season to Miami. The Heat bet on tired legs shooting from range, and swarming defense in close. It wasn't some sort of utter shut down, the Rockets scored about NBA average at 110, but the flat, floundering, start for 15pts in the first quarter couldn't be overcome. A better shooting Rockets team might have won this, as they had a 21 to 13 offensive rebound advantage, but if you don't cash those boards with makes it can look like this game. Maybe, though, more energy would have helped. They deep bench, particularly Isaiah Crawford looked decent in their minutes.

Another point to consider is that the Rockets truly do give up a fair number of games available by their better players by not playing various players on a back to back. Adams might have helped control the paint. It turned out he wasn't really needed against the Tall But Bad Wizards. It wasn't a bad decision to play him in DC, but when you're guessing about FVV and Adams (if available) on every back to back, you're going to roll snakeyes sometimes.

Of concern also, Amen Thompson sat out a game in a return to his native land of South Florida with left knee tendonitis. He seemed to land badly on a play in DC, and the seems to be result of that, despite him playing on. Hopefully this is nothing long lasting, or serious, and more of a precaution.

This might have been a better outcome if the Rockets started in Miami and went to The District, but we'll never know. Best to forget it and move on.
