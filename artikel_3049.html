Palantir Raised Guidance 3 Times This Year. 1 Number Explains Why the Stock Fell.

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Palantir's valuation is getting in the way of the business.

Palantir Technologies (PLTR -0.58%) has been a successful long-term investment since its 2020 IPO. That’s largely because it is also a successful business, as evidenced by its artificial intelligence platform, which has been widely adopted in both commercial and government settings. Palantir continues to outperform expectations, both externally and internally, and management has raised its annual guidance at every quarterly report this year.

It is often the case that raised guidance leads to an improved stock price, but this latest report was different. Palantir's stock price actually fell following its Q3 earnings, and the tech giant is now posting negative returns for the year.

Why is that? I think one figure holds the clue.

Every investor loves a good beat-and-raise quarter, but when that becomes the expectation, it loses its effectiveness. The market now expects Palantir to issue soft guidance each quarter and beat those expectations. After a while, this becomes the status quo for investors, and they demand bigger beats and raises because the original beat and raise have become the baseline.

But Palantir has another issue going on with its stock. While its growth rate was an impressive 96% year over year in Q3, the reality is that its stock has already priced in much of that growth. Shares also fell because of investor concerns about slowing growth in total contract value. The report was also released on a generally bad day for the market and got caught up in the sell-off.

Palantir is one of the most expensive stocks on the market, trading at over 100 times forward earnings and 68 times next year's earnings estimate. And that’s even after this latest sell-off.

PLTR PE Ratio (Forward 1y) data by YCharts

You'd be hard-pressed to find another stock trading at that level in the AI realm, which puts Palantir in a class of its own. Sometimes being an outlier like this is a big problem, as it could lead to Palantir's valuation declining to a more reasonable level over the next year.

Most of Palantir's peers are trading at 20 to 40 times next year's earnings, which means that even if Palantir hits next year's expectations squarely, the stock would need to decline by at least 40% to be in a reasonable valuation range with its peers. That's the harsh reality of Palantir's stock, as so much growth is already priced in. This means investors may need to get used to underperformance for a while.

With so many other great AI stocks growing at similar levels yet trading for far cheaper price tags, I think investors should consider pivoting from Palantir to another investment, as Palantir's stock could be headed for a long stretch of underperformance compared to some of its other AI peers based solely on how expensive its stock is right now.
