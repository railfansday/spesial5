Amaero (AMRO) Q2 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Titanium output restarts as argon costs fall and defense demand builds.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

Amaero Inc. (AMRO -13.63%) reported third quarter results that reflected a transition from facility build-out to commercial production scaling. Management stated that the company is currently focused on the simultaneous demand from several United States defense programs, including hypersonic weapon development and nuclear submarine construction. The company reported the successful restart of its titanium production lines following a safety review and the commissioning of specialized throughput equipment. Management indicated that the strategic focus remains on reshoring critical supply chains for high-value metal powders and near-net-shape components.

Hank Holland: Thank you, Shannon. Welcome. Good afternoon, and thank you for joining us. Good morning to those joining from Australia. Welcome to Amaero's third quarter 2026 earnings conference call, and the inaugural earnings call as a Nasdaq-listed company. Our first earnings call as a Nasdaq-listed company also marks our first results reported in U.S. dollars under U.S. GAAP, so comparative figures may differ from those we previously reported on the ASX, where our CDIs continue to be quoted under the symbol 3DA. To our new shareholders, welcome, and thank you for your confidence. To our longstanding shareholders, thank you for your support over the past four and a half years.

Because many of you are new to Amaero, I want to begin with the bigger picture, the opportunity in front of us, why we believe that Amaero has a differentiated market position, and why we believe the company is at an inflection point. Then I will highlight the financial results in the third quarter and revenue guidance for the fourth quarter. Then Brett will take you through the financials in more detail. Amaero sits at the intersection of three enduring themes, rebuilding the U.S. defense industrial base, reshoring and scaling sovereign manufacturing, and the adoption of additive and advanced manufacturing. For the first time in decades, aerospace and defense are simultaneously experiencing peak demand.

The Columbia and Virginia-class submarine programs, the advent of hypersonic weapons, the replacement of the Minuteman ICBM with Sentinel, and the build-out of missile defense are driving generational investment. At the same time, the war in Ukraine exposed how thin our domestic capacity is in large castings and forgings, among the most constrained links in the defense supply chain. I want to emphasize all these themes are structural, not cyclical. The United States allowed its defense industrial base to atrophy over three decades. Rebuilding it and bringing critical supply chains home is both a national security imperative and sound economic policy. Amaero's corporate strategy has been driven by a demand pull that is a direct result of these thematic forces.

Within that backdrop, Amaero addresses two specific bottlenecks. The first in high-value spherical metal powders. Powder is the consumable of metal 3D printing, the ink cartridge to the printer. So as the installed base of metal 3D printers expands, powder demand expands with it. It is a pick-and-shovel business. Titanium alloy powders serve defense, aerospace, medical, and consumer applications. Refractory alloy powders, such as niobium C103, serve high-temperature applications in high-growth verticals that include the defense, space, and nuclear energy sectors. The second is PM-HIP, or powder metallurgy hot isostatic pressing, a mature technology that was developed over seven decades ago and has the benefit of military specifications and mature datasets.

Amaero's technical team has done much of the pioneering work over the past two decades for producing large, complex, near-net-shape parts as an alternative to constrained castings and forgings. We estimate the U.S. addressable market across our three product lines at approximately $737 million in 2026, growing to approximately $1.5 billion by 2030. That is large enough to build a significant business, yet specialized enough to attract limited competition, which supports a specialty margin structure. As the largest shareholder, that combination is the thesis that led to my investment into Amaero, and it continues to guide our forward-leaning strategy.

Amaero has commissioned the largest U.S. domestic production capacity for titanium and refractory alloy spherical powders, co-located with PM-HIP manufacturing in AS9100D-certified facility near Chattanooga, Tennessee. The competitive landscape is narrow. In titanium powder, our two primary competitors operate in Canada. We have no primary competitor in the United States. In refractory powders, we have one primary competitor. In PM-HIP, others make simple parts, but our proprietary know-how and experience with complex near-net-shape components is highly differentiated. Our advantage rests on three things that are difficult to replicate. First, technology and know-how. We operate EIGA Premium atomizers, a crucible-free process that the Air Force Research Laboratory has called the industry standard for reactive and refractory materials.

Equipment alone does not make commercially viable powder. Our technical team, anchored by Eric Bono, our Chief Technology Officer, has made modifications to the equipment and optimized the process parameters to deliver roughly twice the usable yield while consuming about half the argon gas of conventional atomizers. That matters because yield determines how much feedstock and labor go into every kilogram of sellable powder. Our two largest variable costs, and argon is a significant input of its own right. Our argon recycling system targets a further reduction in argon cost of at least 77%. Second, qualifications. The highest barrier to new entries and to production contracts.

Our C103 has been qualified to a manned space flight standard, and we've closely collaborated with the U.S. Navy, BPMI, and the Naval Nuclear Propulsion Program for development, first articles, and qualification of critical submarine components. Third, customer relationships built on demand pull. Shortly after we moved to Tennessee, we committed to dedicating EIGA Premium atomizer to refractory alloys, including niobium, tungsten, tantalum, and molybdenum. This was a direct response to the long lead times from a sole supplier that was slowing down the development of hypersonic programs. Months later, we began close collaboration with leading stakeholders in Naval Nuclear Propulsion Program, developed PM-HIP as a substitute for long lead time forgings.

Following a roundtable convened at our facility last December, the Navy's Maritime Industrial Base Program Office issued a formal letter of support validating our PM-HIP manufacturing as a viable and technically ready alternative casting and forgings. In July, we received our first low-rate initial production contract with BPMI. On the commercial side, we've structured long-term agreements with leaders in high-growth verticals, several naming Amaero as exclusive supplier. In July, we were awarded a $4.5 million Department of War contract to develop alternative refractory alloy powders that reduce cost and reliance on foreign raw materials. As other companies are beginning multi-year capital programs to address these bottlenecks, we have largely completed ours. Amaero is now shifting from build-out to scaling production.

Through September 30th, we had invested $52.2 million in plant and equipment. We operate three EIGA Premium atomizers with approximately 680 metric tons of annual capacity, 480 metric tons of titanium, and 200 metric tons refractory. Our fourth atomizer, expected in June 2027, takes total capacity to 920 metric tons. In August, we commissioned a custom-designed titanium sieving system that, together with our third atomizer, doubled our titanium throughput capacity. In our first full year of commercial production, the 12 months ended June 30th, we generated $12.5 million in revenue. Trailing 12-month revenue through September 30th was $13.5 million, up 191%, and our backlog as of September 30th of $13.4 million is roughly equal to that.

Because our fixed production costs are largely in place, we expect revenue growth to drive operating leverage. I also want to directly address safety. Following safety incidents earlier this year, we committed to lean in. We paused titanium production and completed a comprehensive process, system, and facility safety review with Jensen Hughes, a leader in safety, dust hazard, and risk-based engineering. We engaged Jensen Hughes to identify risk mitigation strategies that were utilized by larger-scale industrial operations with dust hazards, such as the sugar industry, paper mills, and grain operations. We implemented resulting improvements, and titanium production resumed in late July after approximately a six-week pause. No order cancellations and no employee attrition.

As for Q3 highlights and outlook, turning to the quarter. Third quarter revenue grew 35% to $4.1 million, landing near the top of the previously disclosed range, and nine-month revenue grew 170% to $11.5 million. We ended the period with a backlog of $13.4 million, an increase of 180% from September 2025, and approximately 100% of trailing 12-month revenue. Operating cash use for the quarter fell $1 million year-over-year to $6.3 million. Commercially, the Department of War award and our first BPMI contract advanced both of our production lines in defense, refractory powders and PM-HIP. Operationally, we restarted titanium production and doubled titanium throughput capacity with commissioning an expanded custom-designed fabricated sieving system.

In the capital markets, we began trading on Nasdaq on October 15, receiving aggregate net IPO proceeds of $17.0 million, and amended our EXIM Bank credit agreement to release $2.1 million of restricted cash and move the liquidity covenant test to 2028. As of October 31, we had $19.3 million of cash and cash equivalents after October operating cash use and capital expenditures. Today, we are issuing our first formal guidance as a U.S. listed company. We expect Q4 2026 revenue of $4.5 million-$5.0 million, which would result in a quarterly increase of 121%-146% year-over-year. Combined with our results to date, that would result in full year 2026 revenue of $16.0 million-$16.5 million, up 154%-162%.

Our priorities are clear, convert backlog into revenue, expand the breadth and depth of our commercial partnerships, and bring argon recycling and our fourth atomizer online in the first half of 2027. With that, I will turn it over to our CFO, Brett, to provide more detail on our financial results.
