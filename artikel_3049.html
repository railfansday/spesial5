Morningstar vs. Sezzle: Which Financial Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Sezzle is profitable and growing fast, but lending to shoppers carries credit and regulatory risk. Morningstar is a trusted research brand with rising profits, but its sales growth is modest.

By Sara Appino – Updated Nov 22, 2026 at 5:58PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Morningstar ›

Deciding between established financial research and high-growth payment technology requires a choice between steady market influence and rapid expansion. Is Morningstar (MORN -0.30%) or Sezzle (SEZL -6.09%) the better buy today?

Morningstar provides the critical research and data that power modern investment decisions for millions of retail and institutional users. Conversely, Sezzle is a disruptor in the payments space, offering flexible financing options for younger consumers. While both operate in the financial world, they cater to different market segments with unique risk and reward profiles.

Morningstar sells investment insights, data, and technology tools to a massive audience, including roughly 4.2 million individual investors and 425,000 financial advisors. The company serves eight primary customer groups, ranging from retirement plan providers and wealth managers to asset managers and fixed-income security issuers. Recently, it has integrated artificial intelligence through partnerships with major technology firms to automate analytics and improve user experience.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $2.6 billion, representing revenue growth of approximately 9.4%. This expansion helped the company achieve net income of close to $445.5 million during the same period. The steady climb in both top and bottom lines suggests consistent demand for the company's proprietary financial data and research services.

As of its September 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 1.8x (or 1.6x excluding operating lease liabilities). This ratio, which compares total debt to shareholder equity, reflects an increase in financial leverage after the company took on additional debt to fund acquisitions and share repurchases. The current ratio, which measures a company's ability to cover its short-term debts with short-term assets, is approximately 1.05x. In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, free cash flow reached nearly $492.8 million (following $442.6 million in fiscal year 2025).

Sezzle operates a platform that allows consumers to finance purchases at the point of sale. It focuses heavily on Gen Z and Millennial shoppers, connecting them with a vast network of merchants to increase average order values. By integrating with e-commerce platforms, the company captures a growing slice of the fintech stocks landscape.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached approximately $581.6 million, marking significant revenue growth of nearly 38.9%. This surge in activity led to a net income of close to $182.6 million for the period. The company also maintained a strong net margin of roughly 31.4% during this high-growth phase, indicating it is generating significant profit from its revenue.

As of its September 2026 balance sheet, the current ratio stands at approximately 4.2x, suggesting a robust ability to cover immediate liabilities. The debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 0.4x, meaning its total debt is less than half of its shareholder equity. In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, free cash flow reached nearly $297.6 million, providing the company with substantial capital to reinvest in its growth and fund share repurchases.

Morningstar faces risks tied to its reputation, as any perceived conflict of interest in its credit ratings or research could damage its brand. Its business is sensitive to market volatility, which can reduce asset-based fees when total market values decline. The company also competes with major data providers such as S&P Global (NYSE:SPGI) and MSCI (MSCI -2.22%) in the institutional research space.

Sezzle relies heavily on a single originating bank partner, WebBank, for the majority of its loans, which creates a significant concentration risk. It faces intense competition in the payment sector and ongoing regulatory scrutiny regarding consumer lending practices and interest rates. Additionally, antitrust litigation involving partners like Shopify (SHOP -6.25%) could indirectly impact its growth trajectory or existing merchant relationships.

Morningstar offers a more conservative entry point based on its sales, while Sezzle carries a higher premium that reflects its faster growth and superior net margin. But there are some risks that give me pause: Sezzle lends to shoppers, so bad loans matter. Its newest products start with higher losses, and regulators and a lawsuit against Shopify add costs and uncertainty.

Morningstar, a trusted name in investing research, carries less credit risk, and its stock costs less relative to expected earnings. Its operating profit jumped last quarter, but sales growth is modest. Ultimately, I like that Sezzle is already profitable and still early in its expansion, with room to add new services. If you're investing for the long haul and can handle some risk, Sezzle's fast-growing payments platform looks like the better buy for 2027.
