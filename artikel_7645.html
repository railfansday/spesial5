The Common Thread Among Congressional Immigrants That’s Shaping Politics

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Whatever one thinks of the midterms, we know this: we’re about to get a lot more anti-Westernism, socialism, and Islamism thanks in part to several young, immigrant socialists who have run for office. Among them are Ethiopian-born Melat Kiros, who just won a seat in Congress for Colorado; Ugandan-born Zohran Mamdani, mayor of New York; and Aber Kawas, a 34-year-old Palestinian-American who just won a seat in New York’s state senate.

Most immigration to the U.S. after 1965 came from the “developing,” or Third World, so we will see more of them and their children running for office as time goes on. What does that portend? I looked for common threads in the family backgrounds of the present Congress.

A survey of U.S. congressional representatives and senators who were born abroad or to immigrant parents finds a not-surprising pattern. If you ignore those whose parents were abroad on military service or work, most of those with first- or second-hand experience with communist rule or under dictatorships tend to be conservative.

In the Senate, Ted Cruz (R-TX) was born in Canada, but his parents were Cuban. They escaped Castro’s revolution and the subsequent confiscation of property and economic stagnation. To keep the lid on popular discontent as communist rule kept Cuba poor, Castro and his minions like Ernesto “Che” Guevara ruthlessly suppressed political opposition and free speech. Sen. Cruz is a fierce defender of capitalism, free speech, and democracy.

Sen. Bernie Moreno (R-OH) was born in Bogota, Colombia, and came here in 1971, when he was four. At that time, the left-wing British Guardian admits, “leftist, Marxist-inspired insurgencies” including the ELN and FARC “conducted kidnappings…and carried out a campaign of terror that aimed to unseat the central government…” Moreno’s family wasn’t poor, and his father was a cabinet secretary under a conservative president, but they surely saw how communism threatened Colombia—and all of Latin America.

The third senator born abroad is Mazie Hirono (D-HI), born in Japan. Her mother was Japanese-American from Hawaii, but her father fought for Emperor Hirohito in WWII. Hirono is one of the most left-wing members of Congress, according to Heritage Action.

Of the nine senators born in the U.S. with one or more immigrant parents, all are Democrats. Six had parents born in Europe; two in Mexico, one in South Korea.

In the House of Representatives, of the 16 Members I found who were born abroad to foreign parents, only three are Republican. Carlos Gimenez (R-FL) was born in Cuba, and Victoria Spartz (R-IN) was born in Ukraine—both communist countries at the time. The third Republican, Juan Ciscomani (R-AZ), was born in Mexico.

The other 13 Members born abroad to foreign parents were Democrats—born in the Dominican Republic, Guatemala, India, Mexico, Peru, Somalia, South Korea, and Ukraine.

I found 28 Members who were born in the U.S. to two immigrant parents. Of these, 21 are Democrats, and only seven are Republicans. Do the Republicans have anything in common? One had parents from China, three had Cuban parents, one had parents from Hungary and Poland, one had Portuguese parents, and one had Syrian parents.

So, five of the seven Republicans had parents who left communist countries for the U.S. and likely taught their children why. The remaining two had parents who left countries ruled by dictators at the time: Portugal and Syria.

Of the 21 Democrats whose parents were both immigrants, Mexico (5) and India (3) were the most common countries of origin. Both countries have been at least nominal democracies for generations, albeit with a heavy dose of socialist economics. All six Indian-Americans in Congress appear to be Democrats, which is surprising given that they or their parents experienced India’s “License Raj” socialism that lasted from independence in 1947 until the 1990s. Indian Americans are also a reliable Democrat voter bank.

Ilhan Omar (D-MN) won 83.6% of Democratic [Farmer-Labor] primary votes in 2024 in Ward 6 of Minnesota’s Fifth Congressional District. It has “the largest concentration of Somalis in the state, and possibly is the highest concentration of diaspora Somalis in the world.” Despite credible reports that she lied about her age to become an American citizen and later married her brother to get him a green card, Omar was just re-elected to a fifth term in Congress, with 76% of the vote.

Congress is changing as the country’s demographics change. If ethnic-bloc voting does not break down quickly with assimilation, we will see an increasingly Balkanized legislature. And if experience does not teach immigrants the failures of socialism and the superiority of the American way of life, we will be doomed to repeat the mistakes of the places they fled.
