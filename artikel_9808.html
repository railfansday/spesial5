How Long to Study for Security+? Hours by Background

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Posted on Nov 21 • Originally published at seclern.com

This article was first published on the SecLern blog.

How long to study for Security+ depends on your starting point. Most candidates should plan on 100–150 focused hours, which is roughly 7–15 weeks at 10–15 hours a week. Complete beginners usually need 150–200 hours, or 10–20 weeks at the same pace. Those are planning estimates, not CompTIA requirements. For the SY0-701 exam, CompTIA's certification page lists a maximum of 90 questions in 90 minutes and a passing score of 750 on a 100–900 scale.

CompTIA's own baseline is higher than most candidates expect: the Security+ certification page recommends CompTIA Network+ plus two years of IT administration experience with a security focus before you sit the exam. If you do not have that background yet, use the beginner or help-desk row.

Most working adults should study 10–15 focused hours a week; at that load, a 120-hour plan takes 8 to 12 weeks.

The formula is weeks = total focused hours ÷ realistic weekly hours. A 120-hour plan at 12 hours a week is 10 weeks. The same plan at 6 hours a week is 20 weeks, which is why your weekly availability, not the total, should drive the exam date.

Focused hours means active work: answering questions, doing labs and PBQs, reviewing your error log, and sitting timed practice. A video course playing in the background does not count, even though the clock runs the same way.

Split the load across five or six sessions and protect one block for reviewing what you missed. If the exam is already booked, set aside the final 10–14 days for timed practice and weak-domain review, then divide the remaining hours across the domains by weight. With no IT experience, plan on 150–200 focused hours: about 15–20 weeks at 10 hours a week, or 10–14 weeks at 15 hours a week.

"No experience" has a specific meaning here. It means no help-desk or IT support work, no networking foundation, and no recent security coursework. If that describes you, the first 20 to 30 hours go to vocabulary and networking basics, because exam-style questions are not useful until terms like hashing, VLAN and least privilege mean something.

A sequence that works: skim the official objectives for your exam code, learn core security concepts, refresh networking (protocols, ports, subnetting), move into threats and architecture, then operations, and finish with timed practice.

Book after your practice scores are stable, not when you finish a video course. A readiness check that names your weakest domain is a better gate than finishing a playlist.

Three to five months is a normal timeline from zero. A longer calendar only helps when the hours inside it are active.

Allocate study hours in roughly the same proportions as the exam weights, so Security Operations gets the largest block at 28% of SY0-701.

The weights below come from CompTIA's Security+ page. The hour column is arithmetic on a 120-hour plan: an example allocation, not an official formula.

Security Operations is the largest domain, and it is also where monitoring, log analysis and many PBQ-style tasks live, so give it the biggest block and the most lab time. General Security Concepts is the smallest at 12%, but it supplies the vocabulary the other four domains assume, so beginners should not treat it as a warm-up.

The weights are a starting point, not the plan. Once you have practice results by domain, move hours toward whatever is lowest. Equal hours for every domain is usually the wrong split. Verify the weights against the objectives for your exam code, because CompTIA can rename domains and shift percentages between versions.

A 12-week plan at 10–12 focused hours a week lands inside the 120–150 hour range for candidates with some IT background, and it leaves two full weeks for timed practice.

Compress to 8 weeks only if you already have networking or security work behind you: keep the order, give operations two weeks and program management one. Stretch to 16 weeks by adding two weeks to threats and architecture and two to weak-domain review. The final two weeks stay timed practice either way.

One month is enough only if you already have Network+ or CCNA-level networking, or security work experience, and can study 15–20 or more hours a week.

The math is unforgiving for beginners. Four weeks at 10 hours a week is 40 hours. Four weeks at 20 hours a week is 80 hours, which sits at the very bottom of the experienced range and is less than half of the 150–200 hour beginner range.

The risks in a one-month sprint are predictable: memorizing terms without being able to apply them, skipping PBQs because they take longer than multiple-choice questions, and burning out in the final week. The riskiest move is booking on a practice score that is still climbing.

If your exam is already on the calendar and you are short on hours, rescheduling costs less than a retake. If you cannot move it, spend what is left on timed practice and your two weakest domains rather than trying to cover everything once.

You are ready to book when you can explain each official objective in your own words and your timed practice scores have held steady across several attempts.

How long does it take to study for Security+ with no experience?

With no IT experience, plan on 150–200 focused hours for Security+. At 10 hours a week that is 15–20 weeks, or roughly 3.5 to 5 months; at 15 hours a week it drops to 10–14 weeks. The first 20 to 30 hours go to vocabulary and networking basics before exam-style questions pay off. Confirm the objectives for your exam code on CompTIA's page.

How many hours a day should I study for Security+?

CompTIA publishes no official daily number for Security+. Most working adults do well with 10–15 focused hours a week spread over five or six sessions, which is roughly 1.5 to 2.5 hours per session, plus one review block. Short daily sessions beat one long weekend cram because you retain more of what you actively practiced.

You can pass Security+ in two weeks only with a strong head start. Two weeks at 8 hours a day is 112 hours, which fits the range for experienced admins and security staff. A beginner needs 150–200 hours, so two weeks would mean 11 to 14 hours every single day. Take a readiness check before you commit to a sprint.

How long does it take to study for SecurityX?

No Security+ hour estimate transfers to SecurityX. SecurityX (CAS-005) is a separate, expert-level CompTIA certification with its own objectives, so start from CompTIA's SecurityX page, estimate how much of that material is genuinely new to you, and divide by your weekly hours. Plan it from its own objectives, not by scaling Security+ numbers.

Is AWS Security Specialty study time the same as Security+?

No. AWS Certified Security Specialty is an AWS exam with its own domains, and it assumes working knowledge of AWS services, while Security+ is vendor-neutral and covers five broad domains. If you build in AWS daily your hours will be lower; if you do not, budget substantial lab time. Check the objectives on AWS's exam page.

How long does it take to study for cybersecurity overall?

There is no single number for cybersecurity overall, because it is a sequence rather than one exam. Most people build fundamentals with CompTIA A+ or Network+, then move to Security+, and the total depends on your starting point and the hours you can sustain each week. Plan the next exam, not the whole career.
