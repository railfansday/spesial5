Alexey Gubarev: Why Investors Are Finally Taking Women's Health Seriously

The Business Research Company reports that the global femtech market was worth about $46.5 billion in 2025 and could reach nearly $119 billion by 2030, with an annual growth rate of 20.8%. So, how did femtech move from a niche category to a major investment opportunity, and why are investors now taking women's health seriously? Alexey Gubarev, a tech entrepreneur based in Cyprus, offers his view.

He co-founded Palta, the venture company behind Flo Health, which became the first women’s health app valued at over $1 billion in 2024. He also built Servers.com, a global infrastructure company he led for 18 years before it was sold for $200 million in 2023.

For many years, women's health was seen as a small part of healthcare. Topics like menstruation, fertility, and pregnancy were viewed as specialist areas instead of being recognized as the basis for a much larger healthcare market. Because of this, technology investors often provided limited funding, set cautious valuations, and believed that women's health products had limited growth potential.

That perception is finally beginning to change.

It's not just that women's health is now a more visible social issue. Investors are beginning to see how big the unmet need is, how large the potential market could be, and the chance to build lasting global businesses around problems that have been overlooked for generations.

After years of building technology companies and investing in digital health, I see femtech as one of the most exciting opportunities where technology, healthcare, and consumer behavior meet.

The women's health gap is not a new problem. Research by the McKinsey Health Institute, published in collaboration with the World Economic Forum in 2024, found that women spend approximately 25% more of their lives in poor health than men. The report estimated that addressing this gap could add up to $1 trillion to the global economy annually by 2040.

In the past, medical research and healthcare systems often did not fully consider women's specific needs. Because of this, many conditions were under-researched, underdiagnosed, or not properly addressed. Women have frequently faced complex health questions without reliable, personalized information.

When we established Palta in 2016, our ambition was to empower billions of health-conscious people to live healthier and happier lives. Women's health quickly emerged as one of the clearest opportunities to make a meaningful difference. I talked about the early years at Palta and the apps we started with in this interview with CBN.

About half of the world's population experiences menstruation, but for years there was no credible, technology-based product to address this need on a global scale. We noticed a huge gap between the size of the problem and the solutions that existed.

For investors, this gap should have been a clear sign. Instead, the sector was often seen as niche and overlooked.

In recent years, more digital products have been created to meet women's health needs. What started as a small category focused on period-tracking apps has grown into a much wider range of platforms that cover fertility, pregnancy, hormonal conditions, menopause, and access to specialized medical care.

Several companies illustrate how diverse this market has become.

For example, Maven Clinic has created a virtual healthcare platform that covers fertility, family planning, pregnancy, parenting, and menopause. In October 2024, the company raised $125 million in a Series F round, reaching a reported valuation of $1.7 billion and bringing its total funding to over $425 million. The platform partners with employers and health plans, showing that women's health solutions can also become strong business-to-business healthcare models.

Another example is Midi Health, which focuses on perimenopause, menopause, and midlife women's health. In February 2026, it raised $100 million in Series D funding at a valuation of over $1 billion. This is notable because it shows that investment in women's health is growing beyond fertility and reproductive care into areas that have often been overlooked.

At Palta, we think the best businesses in this field bring together three things: technology that can grow, strong medical expertise, and careful financial management.

The success of Flo and other femtech companies is a big milestone, but it's not the end of the industry's story. It shows what can happen when long-ignored healthcare needs are met with the right mix of technology, science, and good business practices.

We are moving toward a future where a smartphone can act as a personal health companion. It can help women understand their bodies, notice changes, find reliable information, and have better conversations with healthcare professionals throughout their lives.

Advanced AI, medical research, and more sophisticated digital platforms will speed up this change. But technology by itself won't close the women's health gap. That will take ongoing investment in research, stronger clinical evidence, better access to healthcare, and products built around what women really need.

For investors, the question is no longer whether women's health is a real market. The rising number of femtech companies, their growing user bases, and the investments they attract show that this is a business category that matters more and more.

The bigger question is how many other major healthcare opportunities are still undiscovered simply because we haven't been paying enough attention.
