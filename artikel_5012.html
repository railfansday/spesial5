Robinhood Chain pays Ethereum 70% more than Base despite collecting 64% less in fees

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

New data reveals a sharp settlement cost disparity between the two layer-2 networks, even as both retain more than 97% of user fees after Ethereum expenses.

Robinhood Chain paid Ethereum 70% more than Coinbase-backed Base to settle transactions, even though it collected 64% fewer user fees over 30 days.

The network spent approximately $25,700 on Ethereum settlement costs between Oct. 23 and Nov. 21, compared with Base's $15,100, according to Castle Labs data compiled from growthepie.

However, Base generated $2.97 million in transaction fees over the same period, nearly three times Robinhood Chain's $1.07 million.

That disparity left Robinhood Chain spending $2.41 on Ethereum settlement for every $100 collected from users, compared with just 51 cents for Base.

The figures expose a significant difference in the networks' settlement costs, even as both retain the overwhelming majority of transaction fees after paying Ethereum.

The gap is particularly striking because Base consumed considerably more Ethereum data capacity.

According to Blobscan data, Base posted approximately 350,000 blobs during the period, nearly twice Robinhood Chain's 182,000. Blobs let Ethereum's layer-2 networks publish transaction data at lower cost.

Base consequently spent about $4,478 on blob fees, compared with Robinhood Chain's $2,315.

However, the remaining settlement costs tell a different story. Excluding blob fees, Robinhood Chain spent approximately $23,379, more than double Base's $10,602.

Those additional expenses include standard Ethereum transaction fees for posting data and updating settlement states.

The comparison highlights a broader imbalance in Ethereum's scaling economy, where layer-2 networks collect transaction fees while paying relatively little to the blockchain securing their operations.

Across all tracked Ethereum layer-2 networks, users paid approximately $5 million in fees during the 30 days ending Nov. 21, while the networks paid Ethereum just $74,500 for settlement and data availability.

That represents roughly 1.5% of user fees, leaving approximately 98.5% after Ethereum settlement expenses, before accounting for other operating and revenue-sharing costs.

Base and Robinhood Chain accounted for about $4.04 million of those combined fees but paid Ethereum only $40,800.

The difference reflects how Ethereum's scaling architecture distributes economic activity. Layer-2 networks handle user transactions and collect their associated fees, while Ethereum provides settlement and data availability for a comparatively small charge.

For Ethereum, the resulting payments contribute to transaction fee burning and validator income, but remain modest relative to the economic activity taking place on its scaling networks.

The November figures raise two questions: whether Robinhood Chain can narrow its settlement-cost disadvantage against Base, and how much additional revenue Ethereum can capture as layer-2 networks continue handling most user activity.
