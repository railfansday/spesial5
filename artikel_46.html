Italy's former police chief rejected mafia inquiry allegations before death

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Gianni De Gennaro worked in the anti-mafia directorate and became police chief before he headed Italian defence company Finmeccanica, later renamed Leonardo

Italian ex-police chief Gianni De Gennaro has denied misleading investigations into high-profile Mafia killings, in a letter written shortly before his death.

His body was found at his Tuscan country home on Saturday and Italian authorities believe he took his own life. A post mortem examination was due to take place on Friday.

In his eight-page note, De Gennaro, 78, vehemently denied obstructing the inquiry into the murder of anti-mafia judge Paolo Borsellino in Sicily in July 1992.

The Cosa Nostra Mafia was found to have ordered Borsellino's killing and that of fellow judge Giovanni Falcone two months earlier.

But several fruitless investigations were later launched into potential cover-ups by high-ranking officials.

In a recent inquiry De Gennaro, who in the 1990s worked in the anti-mafia directorate and later became Italy's police chief, was accused of misdirecting the initial investigation.

The allegations shocked many Italians, as De Gennaro had worked closely with Falcone and was seen as being on the front line of the fight against organised crime.

"In a few minutes I will be gone, so you understand well that a 'man condemned to death' cannot lie," he wrote in the letter, published by Italian daily Corriere della Sera. "I have never betrayed my oath of loyalty to the Fatherland."

Gianni De Gennaro (L) and anti-mafia judge Giovanni Falcone in 1992

"I hope that, amid the avalanche of falsehoods now overwhelming me, the truth will ultimately prevail," he added.

The case, and the media furore that has surrounded it, have re-opened the wound of the Mafia killings of the early 1990s, which remain an emotionally charged issue in Italy.

The story has dominated Italian headlines since De Gennaro's death, with several of the country's most senior political figures publicly commenting on the case.

On Thursday Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni said she had been "deeply shocked" by the case.

"His words struck us deeply, and I think the most important think we can do now is to help prosecutors who are working to clear things up," she added.

The letter was found next to a hard drive which he said contained notes from his 40-year career.

According to Italian media, investigators seized two hard drives, one from the home of De Gennaro's son and one from a former member of his security detail, and two USB sticks held by a close collaborator, who is also under investigation.

The sticks are reportedly labelled "Stato-Mafia", a likely reference to the alleged relationship between the Italian state and the Mafia, and "Massacres".

Forensic specialists are now expected to examine whether any files on the hard drives or USB sticks were altered or deleted, reports say.
