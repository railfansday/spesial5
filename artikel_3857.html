I'm learning a lot about Maye Musk—that's Elon Musk's mum—today. She's a model. She was born in Canada. She's hugely popular in China. That last one probably explains why she made an appearance in a marketing video for the new version of Infold Games' dress-up gacha Infinity Nikki, of all things.

A tweet from the fan-run Infinity Nikki Update account sparked a healthy amount of bewilderment when it shared a screenshot of a China-only presentation with Maye Musk slapped right in the middle. It looks like she's guest-hosting a first look at the game's upcoming Version 3.0, donning a grey suit and a slightly unsettling articulation as she rattles off what players can expect.

It's presumably a good fit from the perspective of Chinese fans thanks to her popularity in the country along with her storied history with fashion as a model. She's frequently papped with Chinese celebrities, appears in all sorts of ads as a brand ambassador, and said back in 2024 that she visited the country nearly every month. Considering Infold Games is a Chinese developer, the collaboration makes sense when you think about it.

Her whole Musk-ness, however, hasn't gone over brilliantly with Infinity Nikki's western audience. Although the presentation has only been marketed over on the game's Chinese accounts, some have argued that Infold Games could have selected a different woman with more global appeal to serve as a role model. Others have threatened to stop giving their money to the game all together, with one Reddit comment calling the game, ahem, Infinity Nazi.

As Musk's popularity in China has grown over the last few years, it's conversely waned over in the English-speaking world. In 2024 she renounced her affiliation with the Democrats and changed to Republican, claiming that the former party was "malicious and dishonest", particularly in regards to her son. A New York Post article from that time noted how she had said on FOX's The Bottom Line: "When I became a US citizen, of course, I was Democratic because they give you that option because they're the good kind people who care about America.

"But then, they'd also say Elon was terrible, and I was thinking, why are they lying about Elon? They really just cut into my mind that they just didn't know what they were doing. Then when I resigned from the Democrats, I just felt such a relief that I'm not part of the party that's now malicious and dishonest."
