Hi, friends! Welcome to Installer No. 151, your guide to the best and Verge-iest stuff in the world. (If you’re new here, welcome, get psyched because it’s officially Bad Holiday Movie Season, and also you can read all the old editions at the Installer homepage.)

This week, I’ve been reading about Andrew Garfield and GTA VI and sexy joke thieves, finally actually watching The Sopranosfor the first time (I know, I know), mourning the end of Salon, reinstalling the new and extremely improved Notion Calendar, testing the new Keurig Alta coffee machine, reviewing the iPad Mini and the touchscreen MacBook Pro, and really thinking about just throwing it all away and buying an Enough Phone.

I also have for you the long-awaited return of a classic Zelda game, a monster movie worth the trek to the theater, a couple of great new phone accessories, awesome retro headphones, and more. Let’s do it.

We talked last week about Readwise Books, the new app that’s a mix of Kindle, Goodreads, and Strava. I’m a longtime fan and user of Readwise Reader, so I wasn’t shocked that I liked the new app a lot, too. And I’ve heard from a bunch of you that you do, too!

I’ve talked to Tristan Homsi, one of the Readwise cofounders, a few times over the years, and have always enjoyed hearing his thoughts about reading, apps, and digital products in general. I asked him to share his homescreen to see if I might be able to get a hint about what he’s reading and the other stuff he thinks is good. (I also asked Daniel Doyon, Readwise’s other cofounder, to share, and he didn’t respond. Daniel is dead to me.)

Here’s Tristan’s homescreen, plus some info on the apps he uses and why:

The phone: iPhone 13 (!).Believe it or not, I chose to buy this relatively outdated phone last year… not because it’s my favorite form factor or anything, but because it’s closer in specs to the median device our Readwise users have and helps me build a bit more user empathy with them. I’m able to catch performance regressions and bugs in Readwise that the developers on our team with Duos and Pro Maxes and such miss.

Let me tell you though, iOS 26 itself does not work amazingly on an iPhone 13. Lots of lagging and janky animations. I am somewhat bought into the conspiracy theory that they make the old phones suck so you buy a new one… not so much intentionally, but rather by virtue of just making each successive iOS use more and more resources, doing fancier and fancier things.​

The wallpaper: My lovely wife and my cat Sophie :)

The apps: Clock, Settings, Google Calendar, Camera, Google Maps, Spotify, Bay Wheels, Oura, Eight Sleep, Auto Eight, BeReal, ChatGPT, Runmeter, Strava, Readwise Highlights, Readwise Reader, Phone, Messages, Safari.

Of course, I have all three Readwise apps front and center. Highlights and Reader as app icons, and Readwise Books in widget form at the top! It’s a great reminder to jump back into your current book (I’m reading Apple in China right now… speaking of Apple!) instead of scrolling twitter (on a later page), and seeing my friends currently reading really makes me want to jump in and join them :)

I also am a huge fan of the somewhat-defunct social app BeReal. It had its moment in 2022, but quite a few of my friends are still on there! You post once a day, can see what your close friends around the world are doing, and most importantly can’t get sucked in for more than two minutes since it limits you to a random photo or two a day! I still post basically every day, and have almost five years of memories in there.

Speaking of getting sucked in, I don’t have it pictured on my home screen, but I also have Clearspace installed, which helps me stay off of all social media.

Some other apps shown on the homescreen: “auto eight” is something I vibecoded to better control my Eight Sleep mattress (lol)… Strava and Runmeter are awesome for tracking my runs… and Bay Wheels is my main mode of commute around San Francisco daily.

I’ve mentioned Kagi a bunch of times in this newsletter — it has been my default search engine for the last few years, and I can’t recommend it highly enough. (What if Google, but good, you know?) The challenge with Kagi is it costs $10 a month, which is a lot to ask when Google is free. But as of this week, you can now at least try it for yourself, since Kagi will give you 50 free searches before even asking you to sign up. You get 100 more when you do, even before you pay.

If you search a lot, and miss what Google used to be, give Kagi a whirl. I am a very happy customer, and suspect I will be for a long time.
