Two Italians killed in strike in Kuwait, as Iran denies responsibility

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

A Rheinmetall facility in Kuwait was targeted by Iran, Italian and Kuwaiti authorities said

Two Italian nationals have died following a strike on a facility in Kuwait which both countries have blamed on Iran.

The pair were technicians working for German arms manufacturing company Rheinmetall, Italy's foreign ministry said.

Iran has denied responsibility for the attack, with Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi saying the incident looked "suspicious" and that Tehran would work with Kuwait and Italy to "determine the cause".

Kuwait's defence ministry said earlier that four people - the two Italians, a Kuwaiti national and Pakistani national - had been injured from falling shrapnel after a number of drones from Iran were detected and intercepted.

It comes just over a week after Kuwait said it had repelled overnight Iranian drone attacks on an airbase which hosts US forces - the first strikes in more than two months on the country.

Recent exchanges of fire between the US and Iran, and a deadlock in negotiations, have raised fears of a major escalation in the war.

The conflict began when US and Israel attacked Iran in late February, with Iran retaliating by launching missiles and drones at Israel and Gulf Arab states hosting US military assets and personnel.

On Thursday, Araghchi said he had expressed condolences over the deaths in calls with his Italian and Kuwaiti counterparts and that the incident looked like a "precooked recipe for war", without expanding further.

Earlier, the country's armed forces spokesperson Abolfazl Shekarchi said "no military or offensive operations have been conducted" by Iran.

Kuwait's Army said the latest attack was on a "vital site in the country" and was the result of "heinous Iranian aggression", while confirming the deaths of the two Italian nationals.

Italy's foreign minister was quoted by Italian media as saying that investigations showed that the drone used in the attack was manufactured in Iran and that Iranian proxies were known to carry out attacks in the region.

"The Rheinmetall Group is in deep mourning," the German firm said in a statement confirming the deaths of two of its staff members.

"Two highly valued employees lost their lives while carrying out their professional duties, as they were providing technical support for air defence systems," it added.

"Protecting people in the country was a matter of profound importance to them.

"Company management and the workforce are devastated that our colleagues have now lost their lives."

Italian media outlets have named the two individuals. One of the men was reported to be 36, while the other was 65 and nearing retirement.

The men had been performing maintenance on systems used by the Kuwait Armed Forces, according to Italian press agency Ansa citing Italy's foreign ministry.

Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni wrote on X: "On my own behalf and on behalf of the government, I express my deepest sympathy and offer my heartfelt condolences to the victims' families.

"My thoughts are with all those affected by the attack and their families."

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen wrote on social media: "We must step up pressure on Iran to end these unacceptable attacks against its neighbours and our partners in the Gulf.

"Today's events are a further reminder that security in the Gulf is a security issue for us all."
