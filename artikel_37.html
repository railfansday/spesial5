Elon Musk says record $446m spending on US midterms was 'pretty foolish'

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Elon Musk said on Wednesday that he felt "pretty foolish" about the record sum he spent on the US midterm elections, following sweeping Republican defeats.

The Tesla and SpaceX chief executive was the election's top individual donor, giving $446m (Â£339m) to support President Donald Trump's party, particularly in Texas where many of Musk's firms are based.

Musk, who had backed Trump's second White House bid, also said he was "not Maga" - the Make America Great Again movement associated with the president.

Democrats won control of both chambers in the US Congress, flipping both swing seats and making historic gains in places that have for decades been controlled by Republicans.

The election was seen as a bruising defeat for Trump, who has faced pressure over the impact his policies have had on the cost of living in the US.

Musk was speaking virtually, external at the New York Times DealBook Summit, an annual conference hosted in the city attended by major business and political leaders.

"I'm not Maga," Musk said, referring to the right-wing political movement.

He added: "I'm not sure what Maga means... if that means sort of blind adherence to a set of policies by the Republican Party, then I certainly don't have that."

This marks a major shift in tone by Musk, who has previously been seen wearing a Maga hat.

"I'm not just Maga, I'm dark, gothic Maga," he joked at a Trump rally in 2024.

Musk has worn a Maga hat in public on several occasions

For several months at the start of Trump's second term in the White House, Musk served as an unpaid "special government employee" and Senior Advisor to the President, leading the Department of Government Efficiency (Doge) to cut government spending.

In October 2026, the Pentagon named Musk as a co-lead of a future of warfare study called Project Meridian, while he continued to travel with and advise Trump.

Musk went on to say he did not see much for the money he had spent on the Republicans' campaign, though he added that it was likely that candidates would have fared worse without the spending.

The large number of Republican losses could have implications for Musk and his businesses.

Democrats coming into power in both the Senate and House of Representatives have pledged greater scrutiny of tech companies, including over the building of controversial data centres.

In Georgia, where SpaceX has data centre operations, the newly elected Democrat governor promised to limit the rollout of the massive buildings, as well as the tax breaks tech companies often get to build them.

Democratic lawmakers have also suggested that SpaceX, which has gained an estimated $10bn in new government contracts since Trump was re-elected, should be investigated.

One of the most striking midterms wins went to Democrat James Talarico, who beat Republican Ken Paxton for a US Senate seat in Texas.

According to BBC analysis, Musk had spent at least $45m in support of Paxton.

Texas is where key operations for Musk's car company Tesla and rocket and technology firm SpaceX are based.

Musk said on Wednesday that he believes Texas' new governor, Democrat Gina Hinojosa, is "going to be quite moderate".

Hinojosa defeated Republican Governor Greg Abbott, ending more than three decades of the party's control of the state.

The Tesla boss added : "I don't think she's a far-left governor. And based on everything I've heard about her, I think things will be fine in Texas with Gina as governor."
