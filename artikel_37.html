Indian woman gang-raped while pregnant in 2002 'heartbroken' after two perpetrators bailed

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The attack on Bilkis Bano and her family was one of the most horrific crimes during the Gujarat riots

The article has details that some readers may find disturbing

A pregnant Muslim woman who was gang-raped and saw 14 family members murdered during the 2002 Gujarat riots has spoken of her "pain, shock and disbelief" after two of her rapists were recently granted bail.

Bilkis Bano said in a statement that she heard about the top court order through press reports and vowed to fight back.

The freed men are from a group of 11 convicts - part of a Hindu mob - serving life terms for the attack on Bano.

All the men were also freed in 2022 after a Gujarat government order. That decision, and the celebrations as they left prison, caused global outrage. In 2024, the Supreme Court overturned the order.

In a short three-page order dated 28 October, the Supreme Court granted bail to Bipinchand Kanaiyalal Joshi and Pradip Ramanlal Modhiya and suspended their sentences while their petition challenging a 2017 Bombay High Court order is being heard.

The judges explained that the early release was because the two convicts "have been under incarceration for a period of more than 17 years" and "that one of the appellants has a severe handicap".

On Tuesday, Bano's lawyer Shobha Gupta told the BBC that the news of Joshi and Modhiya's release had left "Bilkis heartbroken".

"She has to restart her battle for justice. She had no information that this case was coming up in the Supreme Court. What is troubling for us is that every time something like this happens, we worry that it could affect her faith in judiciary."

The riots began after a fire on a passenger train in Godhra town killed 60 Hindu pilgrims

Gupta said it was difficult to understand why the convicts were being released because "it's not a routine criminal case, it's a case that shocks the conscience of the world, of entire humanity".

The attack on Bano and her family was one of the most horrific crimes during the Gujarat riots, which began after 60 Hindu pilgrims were killed in a fire on a passenger train in the town of Godhra.

Hindu mobs attacked Muslim neighbourhoods, blaming them for starting the fire. The riots continued for three days and more than 1,000 people died, most of them Muslims.

When rioters attacked Bano's village and started setting Muslim homes on fire, she and 16 relatives went on the run. She was 19, had a three-year-old daughter and was pregnant with her second child.

They were attacked on the morning of 3 March 2002 by a group of men "with swords and sticks", she told me in 2017. Her three-year-old daughter, she said, was snatched from her lap and killed.

Her attackers were her neighbours, men she had seen almost daily while growing up. They tore off her clothes and several of them raped her, ignoring her pleas for mercy.

Her cousin, who had given birth two days earlier, was raped and murdered and her newborn baby was killed too.

Bano survived only because she lost consciousness and her attackers left, believing she was dead. Two boys - seven and four - were the only other survivors of the massacre.

The riots continued for three days and more than 1,000 people died, most of them Muslims

Her fight for justice has been long and nightmarish. The first arrests were made only in 2004 after the Supreme Court called in federal investigators and transferred her case to Mumbai, saying that courts in Gujarat could not deliver justice to her.

In her statement released on Monday night, Bano spoke of "fresh tears of pain, shock and disbelief".

"I still cannot believe that once again, for the second time, an order has been passed by the Supreme Court without calling me up and without hearing me," she said.

"For 24 long years, I have stood up to fight for justice... again and again. Yet today, I find myself losing the very justice I had fought so hard to win.... And I am scared of losing faith in justice, yet again."

Bano vowed to "fight this order... for my children, who do not deserve to live haunted by fears and scars for the rest of their lives".

"I do this for my three-year-old Saleha who deserved to live and whose memory demands justice. I do this for women everywhere. Because no woman, no survivor, no citizen should have to endure this eternal test of belonging and equality, and fight for justice for the rest of her life."

Prime Minister Narendra Modi was Gujarat chief minister at the time of the riots, and was criticised for not doing enough to prevent the carnage. He has always denied wrongdoing and has not apologised for the riots.

In 2013, a Supreme Court panel also said that there was insufficient evidence to prosecute him. But critics have continued to blame him for the riots happening on his watch.
