The Logic-less Crusade That Just Killed a Data Center

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Do activists believe that the $5 billion that would have been spent on this project is just waiting to be allocated elsewhere?

An aerial view of a 49.5-megawatt, three-level data center under construction in Vernon, Calif., April 14, 2026. Inset: Taylor Frazier McCollum

Mario Tama/Getty Images, Screenshot via Electric Llama/YouTube

Meet Taylor Frazier McCollum. McCollum just led a successful movement to block the construction of a $5 billion data center campus in Prince George’s County, Md. Simultaneously, McCollum is upset that her county’s government will not pay for a new roof for her grandfather’s home. Somehow, McCollum cannot see the connection between these two things. In an interview with NPR, she explained that the data center project prompted her political “awakening” and boasted that “so many people are depending on this fight to be successful.”

Are they? Prince George’s County is not in good shape. It has a budget deficit of more than $130 million. Unemployment is high. Last year, the Six Flags amusement park there closed permanently, and in the next few years, the Washington Commanders NFL team intends to move out. The FBI is also trying to leave. McCollum’s father believes that his daughter is a hero in the mold of Medgar Evers, who was killed trying to integrate Mississippi’s schools, or of Martin Luther King. “People like Taylor,” he says, “jump out here so the people behind them get to make progress.”
