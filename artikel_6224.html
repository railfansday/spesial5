Buy Choice Privileges Points With 40% Bonus (0.74 Cents Each): Worth It?

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Choice Privileges is currently offering a promotion on purchased points, offering up to a 40% bonus, which is an opportunity to buy points for as little as 0.74 cents each. While this isn’t among the best promotions we’ve seen from the program, it’s still better than no bonus, and there are many situations where you could come out ahead.

Let’s take a look at everything you need to know about this deal, and talk about under what circumstances it makes sense to buy Choice Privileges points. I generally recommend buying points only if you have a short term use in mind, and are sure the math checks out.

Link: Buy Choice Privileges points with a bonus

Between 12AM EDT on November 2 and 11:59PM EDT on November 26, 2026, Choice Privileges is offering a sale on purchased points. The accounts I manage show a bonus of up to 40%, which is structured as follows:

Different accounts may very well show different offers, so you’ll want to log into your Choice Privileges account to see what you’re eligible for.

Choice Privileges’ pricing when buying points isn’t linear, and you get a lower cost the more points you purchase, not even factoring in any promotional bonuses or discounts. Before factoring in any bonuses or discounts, the cost to purchase points ranges from 1.28 cents per point (if buying just 1,000 points) to 1.03 cents per point (if buying 180,000 points), and that’s inclusive of taxes and fees.

If you maxed out this promotion, you could purchase a total of 252,000 points (180,000 base points, plus 72,000 bonus points) for $1,854, which is a rate of 0.74 cents per Choice Privileges point.

Note that purchased points will post within 24 hours of completing the transaction, and the points earned don’t count toward Choice Privileges elite status.

Choice Privileges lets you purchase up to 180,000 points per account per calendar year, not factoring in any bonus points.

When Choice offers a promotion on purchased points, it’s typically either a 30-45% discount or a 35-50% bonus. So this 40% bonus is toward the bottom end of that range. Personally, I’d decide on whether to make a purchase more based on if I had an immediate use in mind, rather than the exact cost per point.

Choice Privileges points purchases are processed by points.com, meaning they don’t generally count as a hotel purchase for the purposes of credit card spending. There is one exception — if you use a co-branded Choice Privileges credit card, then these purchases are rewarded in the same way as hotel spending.

Otherwise, I’d recommend using a card with which you’re trying to reach a minimum spending requirement, or a credit card that maximizes your return on everyday spending. When in doubt, review which credit cards are best for buying points and miles.

While it doesn’t get much attention in the miles & points world, Choice is a huge hotel group, with over 7,000 properties in 40 countries, representing roughly 570,000 hotel rooms. Choice hotel brands include (among others) Ascend, Cambria, Comfort Inn, Sleep Inn, Quality Inn, Clarion, Rodeway Inn, and more.

The catch is that Choice doesn’t have that many aspirational properties. In general there’s the most value outright purchasing points if you have a premium redemption in mind, given that this is the best way to get outsized value compared to the cost of a stay if paying cash.

Choice Privileges doesn’t have a formal award chart, but award pricing usually ranges from 6,000 to 45,000 points per night. In Asia-Pacific, some properties cost up to 75,000 points per night.

There are definitely some properties where you’ll get outsized value acquiring Choice Privileges points at a discount. For example, Choice has an impressive footprint in Northern Europe, where hotels can otherwise be expensive when paying cash, especially in summer.

Let me just give some examples, and let’s just keep the math easy, to show redemptions where you’re getting well over one cent of value per point (which is way higher than the acquisition cost here). Generally the key is to travel during peak periods, since that’s when you’ll get the most outsized value from your points.

For example, if you wanted to travel to Tromso, Norway, in December (to see the Northern Lights), I see two properties charging $560-620 per night, when you could book them for 40,000-45,000 points per night, getting you very nicely outsized value.

If you wanted to travel to Stockholm, Sweden, in July (summer is popular!), I see two properties charging $420-580 per night, when you could book them for 25,000-30,000 points per night, getting you excellent value.

For an example closer to home, say you wanted to go to Dunedin, Florida, in March. I see a property charging $730 per night, when you could book it for 40,000 points per night, also getting you hugely outsized value.

Let me be clear — you won’t always find outsized value in this way. For example, say you need to spend a night near LAX, where you’ll see properties charging $102-149 per night, when a points redemption requires 20,000-30,000 points per night. Paying cash is the much better value there.

The key is to travel places during peak periods, where the cash rate would be significantly higher than usual, while the points rate isn’t as elevated.

Here’s a fun redemption option with Choice Privileges points. While Choice doesn’t manage these properties, Choice Privileges has a partnership with Preferred Hotels & Resorts, which is a group of over 500 luxury independent properties around the globe.

You can redeem Choice Privileges points for stays here, and the hotels cost anywhere from 25,000 to 118,000 points per night. You can find participating hotels and award costs here, and nowadays you can even book these awards online. There are some participating hotels where you can get significant value redeeming this way, though the recent devaluation sure doesn’t help.

Choice Privileges doesn’t have blackout dates, and points can be redeemed for stays up to 50 weeks in advance, as long as a standard room is available.

Choice Privileges points don’t expire as long as you have at least some account activity once every 18 months. Virtually any activity that involves earning or redeeming points would be considered qualifying for these purposes.

Everyone will value points differently. Based on my latest miles & points valuations, I consider Choice Privileges points to be worth ~0.6 cents each. That’s a conservative valuation, though if you’re strategic you should be able to get lots more value out of your points than that.
