Lutnick joins Trump’s China trip as Congress moves to tighten chip curbs

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Bessent says new AI chip curbs are unnecessary. A New York Times investigation ties Lutnick’s family firm to a Malaysian data centre that may serve Tencent, as lawmakers ask why Commerce held back.

Howard Lutnick in the Oval Office at his swearing-in as US commerce secretary on 21 February 2025.

Congress is lining up behind bipartisan AI chip curbs aimed at China. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent says it is not needed. And Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, the official many lawmakers blame for weak enforcement, is set to fly to China with Donald Trump this week.

Bloomberg reported on Sunday that the bill, which also covers chipmaking equipment, could pass within weeks. Lutnick is expected on Air Force One for Trump’s meeting with Xi Jinping at the APEC summit, Semafor reported, citing people familiar with the matter.

“I don’t think we need the legislation. We have all the rules we need. We just need them enforced,” Bessent said on Fox News’s Sunday Morning Futures.

Bessent also said Nvidia needs a “dramatic shakeup”. Its board should go outside the company for its next chief executive, he said, as Reuters reported. He wants answers from the board and its outside counsel within 10 days. His question is whether Nvidia ignored warning signs of shell companies funnelling chips to China. Allies of Jensen Huang are meanwhile discussing his return.

Pressure on Lutnick grew on Sunday with a New York Times investigation by Ana Swanson, Aaron Krolik and Paul Mozur. Lutnick ran Cantor Fitzgerald for decades before handing it to his sons. The Times found that Cantor has worked with Southeast Asian data centre companies that serve Chinese clients.

One is Bitdeer, a Singaporean firm building a data centre called A201 in Malaysia’s Johor state. Cantor acted as a sales agent for Bitdeer’s equity offering of up to $1bn this year. A person with knowledge of the arrangement told the Times the site will likely serve Tencent. Renting chips this way is legal, and it sidesteps the ban on selling advanced chips directly to Chinese firms.

Lutnick is writing the rules that would decide whether that route stays open. Commerce has pushed for a narrower version than others in the administration wanted, people familiar with the talks told the Times. The Times found no evidence that Cantor’s investments influenced him. A Commerce spokeswoman said he acts in the best interests of the United States. He does not discuss department matters with his sons, she added.

On Sunday, the Wall Street Journal reported how Huang built this route. It said the Southeast Asian data centres gave Chinese companies computing power and cover for alleged chip smuggling.

In January, Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter told more than a dozen officials what Trump wanted on licence conditions for H200 exports to China, Semafor reported on Friday. People with access to an account of the call gave Semafor his words.

“I was with the president when he and Jensen discussed this so I want to let you know what the president wanted. He wants this to go fast,” Teter said, according to Semafor.

Nvidia called a rule that sellers know their cloud customers a “poison pill”, Semafor reported. It also pushed to raise the threshold for buyer checks to tens of thousands of chips. A Bureau of Industry and Security spokesperson told Semafor such talks with companies are “completely routine”. Nvidia declined to comment. Commerce had already halted its Megaspeed probe after Teter contacted a senior official, the Times reported earlier this month.

“It looks like he has been the barrier inside the government to stopping the flow of these Nvidia chips to China. And the question is why?” Representative Jim Himes said on CBS’s Face the Nation.

Republican John Moolenaar, who chairs the House China committee, said Lutnick and the whole Commerce Department need to answer questions. Democrats, who take control of Congress in January, plan to ask why Lutnick declined to blacklist suspected smugglers, Representative Stephen Lynch told The Washington Post.

Senator Maria Cantwell, the top Democrat on the Senate commerce committee, wrote to Lutnick on Sunday ahead of the Xi meeting, CNBC reported. She urged him to enforce export controls under the 2018 Export Control Reform Act and stop chips reaching barred companies.

“These failures to carry out the law help give away America’s technology advantage in AI,” Cantwell wrote.

Cantwell also pointed to Cantor Fitzgerald, which she said held $1.2bn in Nvidia stock when Lutnick was nominated. She said the administration’s 25% fee on some Nvidia chip sales to China could create a financial incentive to approve exports. Commerce did not immediately respond to CNBC.

The Post also reported, citing a former administration official, that Bessent confronted Lutnick over why he had not acted despite instructions from Trump on three occasions. Bessent has said publicly that Commerce has the pen. Lutnick has agreed to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on 10 December.

The White House says it has complete confidence in Lutnick, according to Semafor. A senior US official told reporters the Nvidia scandal should have no bearing on the China trip.
