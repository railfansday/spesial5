Patrick Stewart’s Most Infamous Flop Officially Returns to Netflix

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Sir Patrick Stewart was a renowned actor who starred in many projects. While he may be known for his science fiction and superhero work in franchises like Star Trek and X-Men, he has also done several surprising projects that showcase his talent as an actor, like voicing Avery Bullock in American Dad!. After the legendary actor passed earlier this year, Avengers: Doomsday is set to be his final role, but one of his most unlikely — and worst-rated — films is officially resurfacing ahead of the holidays.

It's no secret that Hollywood is a business, with studios investing in projects they believe will succeed. However, as the recent shakeup at DC Studios suggests, their definition of success doesn't always align with audiences' expectations. Beyond revisiting established franchises through sequels, spin-offs, and reboots, Hollywood also looks to capitalize on popular trends. One example is the upcoming Labubu movie, based on Pop Mart's sought-after collectible plush, despite their recent decline in popularity. But before this movie was greenlit, another trend-driven film was released in theaters nearly 10 years ago, and even Sir Patrick Stewart had a role in the feature.

In 2017, Sony Pictures released The Emoji Movie, which follows a "meh" Emoji named Gene (T.J. Miller), who's on the run after he can't pull the "meh" face during a text. Hoping to become normal, he sets out to find a "hacker" to fix him so he can be a normal emoji. The Emoji Movie features a star-studded cast, including James Corden as Hi-5, the high-five emoji, Maya Rudolph as Smiler, a Smiley Emoji, Jennifer Coolidge as Mary Meh, Gene's mother, and Stewart voicing the Poop Emoji. The Emoji Movie was heavily criticized, but it generated over $217.8 million at the global box office, surpassing its $50 million budget. Next month, The Emoji Movie will be returning to Netflix, just in time for Stewart fans to binge the actor's entire filmography, including the bad ones, before Avengers: Doomsday comes out.

Since its release, The Emoji Movie has received overwhelmingly negative reviews, earning a 6% critics' score and a 36% audience score on Rotten Tomatoes, alongside a 1.5-star rating on Letterboxd. Critics described The Emoji Movie as "pointless," criticized its excessive product placement, and argued that it struggled to "express" itself despite featuring a wide range of emojis. While many advised audiences to avoid the movie, a handful of critics praised it, with one calling it "one of the boldest mainstream American movies in ages" and another describing it as "inventive." Despite its box office success, The Emoji Movie was widely regarded as one of the worst films of 2017.
