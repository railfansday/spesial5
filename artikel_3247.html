Mike Leigh Reminds The World Why He Doesn’t Work With Hollywood Stars

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Mike Leigh has a few regrets, but declining to work with Hollywood actors isn’t one of them.

In an interview with The Guardian, the decorated British director reminded the world why his unusual methods mean he’s happy sticking with British character actors. He “snorted” at the idea of casting big Hollywood movie stars.

“Well, first of all let’s clear up what’s involved in doing that,” he said. “You – the name actor – would have to commit yourself to the project for months on end. You may have to get out in the real world and do some research. You may not be playing a character that squares with your idea of how you want to be seen; the idealised version of your own personality.” Related Stories Trailers 'Tender Loving Care' New Trailer: Mike Leigh's Latest Is A Meditation On Love & Growing Old News Gotham Awards Film Nominations Led By 'The Invite', 'Tender Loving Care', 'All Of A Sudden' & More

Leigh added: “If someone were to say, ‘The reason you fucked up and failed is because you didn’t get star names’, then in all humility I’d accept it. But that simply isn’t the case.” Watch on Deadline

Leigh’s methods are collaborative and involve developing scripts with actors as filming progresses. His new movie, Tender Loving Care, features his long-time partner Marion Bailey and Widow’s Bay star Kate O’Flynn. The film follows Amy (O’Flynn), who, after a bitter breakup, moves in with her best friend, Rosie (Alice Bailey Johnson). Both work as social workers in London, at opposite ends of the age spectrum. Rosie becomes like a second daughter to Amy’s parents, notably her elderly father, who is starting to show his age.

Having made bigger period films like Peterloo and Mr. Turner in recent years, the 83-year-old Palme d’Or winner did say he regrets never making a big budget modern-day film with a “broad canvas and a contemporary subject.”

He was promoting Tender Loving Care, which he has frequently said will be his last directing gig. This is because he has a debilitating condition, myositis, which has made the physical demands of directing difficult as it has weakened his muscles and restricted his movement.

“I’m now very seasoned in listening to everybody in the world telling me why and how I can keep working. And my answer to that begins with an ‘F’ and ends with an ‘off’,” joked Leigh.

He insisted he isn’t retiring and instead will “sit down and write something.”
