Mortgage application payments reveal slight homebuyer affordability gain in October

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

MBA's Purchase Applications Payment Index fell 0.3% to a reading of 161.6 in October, signaling slightly better affordability. Median payments rose to $2,236, but a lower median loan amount of $315,424 helped offset higher rates.  AI Summary

Homebuyer affordability improved slightly in October as lower purchase loan amounts offset higher mortgage rates, according to the Mortgage Bankers Association (MBA)’s Purchase Applications Payment Index (PAPI), released Tuesday.

The index, which measures mortgage payments relative to borrower income, decreased 0.3% to a reading of 161.6 in October, down from 162.1 in September. A lower index reading indicates improving affordability.

The improvement came despite the national median mortgage payment for purchase applicants rising to $2,236 in October, up $21 (or 0.9%) from $2,215 in September. The median purchase loan application amount fell to $315,424 from $324,022, helping offset a 37-basis-point increase in mortgage rates.

“While the median mortgage payment rose $21 in October, payments as a share of earnings edged lower — slightly improving affordability,” Edward Seiler, MBA’s associate vice president of housing economics and executive director of the Research Institute for Housing America, said in a statement.

Affordability remains weaker than a year ago. The median mortgage payment increased 9.7% from $2,039 in October 2025, while earnings grew 1.6%. As a result, the PAPI was up 8% year over year.

“Looking ahead, lower mortgage rates and stronger earnings growth would give prospective buyers more room in their budgets,” Seiler said.

The median mortgage payment for Federal Housing Administration (FHA) borrowers increased to $1,944 in October, up from $1,906 in September and $1,789 a year earlier. For conventional loan borrowers, the median payment rose to $2,258, up from $2,244 in September and $2,063 in October 2025.

Among borrowers applying for lower-payment mortgages, those in the 25th percentile, the median monthly payment increased to $1,536 in October from $1,525 in September.

The PAPI for Black households decreased to 162.4 in October from 162.9 in September. For Hispanic households, it fell to 149.6 from 150.1, while the index for white households declined to 162.8 from 163.3.

Idaho had the highest PAPI reading in October at 269.8, followed by Nevada at 243.0, Rhode Island at 233.1, Arizona at 215.2 and Utah at 202.7. At the opposite end of the spectrum, Louisiana had the lowest reading at 119.8, followed by Washington, D.C., at 121.5, Alaska at 126.2, Iowa at 127.6 and Connecticut at 131.6.

The MBA’s mortgage payment-to-rent ratio also edged higher, increasing to 1.44 at the end of the third quarter from 1.43 at the end of the second quarter. The increase indicates that mortgage payments for home purchases rose slightly relative to rents.

The U.S. Census Bureau reported that the national median asking rent increased to $1,541 in the third quarter from $1,531 in the second quarter. The ratio of mortgage payments for lower-payment applicants to median asking rents remained at 0.99 in September.

Separately, the MBA’s Builders’ PAPI showed that the median mortgage payment for new-home purchase applicants increased to $2,375 in October, up from $2,307 in September.
