Tech Moves: Microsoft names 2 leaders, loses 1; DoorDash VP departs; BluePill, Glacis get execs

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Tech Moves covers notable hires, promotions and personnel changes in the Pacific NW tech community. Submissions: [email protected]

by Lisa Stiffler on Nov 18, 2026 at 12:47 pmNovember 19, 2026 at 11:47 am

— Weiwei Yang has left Microsoft after more than a decade to co-found a startup that is “building the power grid for the AI age,” according to her LinkedIn profile.

Yang was most recently a senior director with Microsoft Research, working on grid technology. Her team built a grid dataset and released small foundation models to help users operate and expand electrical infrastructure.

Yang thanked her community at the tech giant, adding, “I hope the relationships and collaborations we’ve built continue well beyond Microsoft, and that we find many new ways to work together.”

— Allie Mellen has joined Microsoft as principal director for security research and threat intelligence. Mellen was previously a principal analyst at Forrester. She also wrote “Code War,” a book that explores cyberattacks as a powerful geopolitical weapon.

In her new role, Mellen said she will lead strategy for protection and threat intelligence, “helping shape what intelligence we produce, how AI can improve its speed and quality, and how we turn that knowledge into better protection for customers.”

— Former tech executive Drew Sheriff has been named CEO and president of the Space Needle and Chihuly Garden and Glass. Sheriff was previously a vice president at DoorDash and has held leadership roles at Amazon, Starbucks and Parachute Home. His roles focused on customer experience, product growth, strategy and marketing.

“His skill set is the ideal background for charting how we attract customers and how we delight customers in this new age,” Jeff Seely, board chair for the Space Needle and Chihuly Garden and Glass, told the Seattle Times.

— Microsoft has named Caroline King as director of its Washington state community and philanthropy team, with a focus on advocacy work across housing, affordability and other priority issues.

King is the founder and past CEO of Washington STEM, an organization that coordinates research, advocacy, policy, philanthropy and partnerships to support science, technology, engineering and math education in the state. She most recently served as a leader on the governing body of an international school in Luxembourg.

— Longtime Amazon and Microsoft tech exec Scott Wiltamuth has retired — mostly.

Wiltamuth joined Amazon in 2011 as an AWS vice president overseeing a large organization building developer and management tools. In 2018, he took a seven-month leave for colon cancer treatment. After returning, he sought a director-level position and thanked company leaders for helping him find a better fit. “Their flexibility and creativity enabled me to extend my AWS career,” he said.

Wiltamuth launched his tech career at Microsoft in 1993 and left nearly 19 years later as director of program management for Visual Studio Pro.

He now spends time with his wife and family, traveling, golfing, swimming and reading. “I don’t plan to pursue another full-time operational role,” Wiltamuth said, “but may do some part-time work.”

— Adam Nygate has exited Palo Alto Networks to co-found and lead a stealth startup based in Seattle. He was founder and CEO of Huntr, a platform launched in 2019 where security researchers could submit information on AI and machine learning vulnerabilities. Protect AI acquired Huntr, and Palo Alto Networks later acquired Protect AI.

“Huntr was a bet that open source gets safer when vulnerabilities are visible to the people who can fix them, even when that made life uncomfortable for vendors who’d rather keep things quiet,” Nygate said in a post announcing his career change and impending new baby.

— BluePill, a Seattle-based marketing startup that helps brands predict consumer behavior, has named Mike Weis as chief revenue officer and head of sales.

Weis has 40 years of experience in consumer packaged goods, retail and commerce, beginning his career as a Pacific Northwest leader at Unilever and holding executive positions at Catalina and Quotient Technologies. Recent roles include adviser at Contento2, eGateway Capital and And1 Advisors.

“Beyond the incredible résumé, Mike is one of the kindest, most genuine, and most passionate people I’ve met. Someone who has accomplished so much, yet brings so much humility, curiosity, and energy to every conversation,” said Ankit Dhawan, CEO and founder of BluePill.

— Glacis, a Seattle startup that builds tamper-proof records of AI behavior, has named Caer Mistral Sanders as a technical co-founder. Sanders has launched startups and joins Glacis from Thoughtworks. Before that, she held leadership roles at Wayfair and Onshape. As a teenager, she published a book on mixed-reality control systems and won a world robotics championship.

In a post announcing the new role, Sanders said she has designed medical record systems, researched robot communication, patented cryptographic technology and built widely used AI platforms. “This is the first time in my career I get to bring all of those skills to bear on one problem, and I couldn’t be more excited!” she added.

— The PNW Battery Collaborative has welcomed Jason Demeny as its director of communications. Demeny is a marketing leader whose past employers include Crusoe, Patch, Qualtrics and Apptio. Earlier in his career, he spent more than a decade at Microsoft over two stints, most recently leading teams that served the Azure Partner Experience group.

The nonprofit PNW Battery Collaborative brings together startups, innovators and researchers focused on energy storage.

— Tom Wheeler, former chairman of the Federal Communications Commission, has joined the advisory council for Known, a Bellevue, Wash.-based company building accountability infrastructure for autonomous AI. Wheeler led the FCC from 2013 to 2017.

Known, previously called Identity Digital Innovation Labs, spun out of Identity Digital in September.

— Versium has promoted Matthew Baumgartner to vice president of engineering. The Redmond, Wash.-based company provides data and software that help businesses identify potential customers, fill in missing contact information and target their marketing. Baumgartner has been with Versium since 2018.

— Charlie Trillingham has been named communications manager for the University of Washington AI and Machine Learning for Engineering programs and the UW NSF AI Institute for Dynamic Systems. He previously served as a marketing and communications lead for the Buerk Center for Entrepreneurship within the UW’s Foster School of Business.

Editor’s note: Story updated 11/19 to correct that Charlie Trillingham worked for the Buerk Center for Entrepreneurship within the UW’s Foster School of Business, not the Foster School itself.
