Steam Bails Out Dead by Daylight After Review Bombing Campaign

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Dead by Daylight's recent Steam rating is back to Mostly Positive, but it wasn't a sudden change of heart among players that got it there. Valve has excluded a wave of reviews posted during the fallout over Behaviour Interactive's dealings with streamer TheBurntPeanut.

Chunky joins the ever-growing list of horror icons removed from Dead by Daylight, leading to fans questioning how well Behaviour can manage licenses

Reviews submitted between November 1 and November 6 have been marked as off-topic after the game was hit by a review-bombing campaign. As Dexerto reports, the decision reversed a slide to Mixed, leaving an asterisk beside the game's Steam score.

Visit the Dead by Daylight Steam page and you will see a notice explaining that reviews from an unusual period of activity have been excluded from the displayed score. At the time of writing, 74% of its recent reviews are positive, putting the game back in the Mostly Positive category.

That doesn't mean Valve has deleted thousands of angry reviews. The reviews are still there; they simply don't count toward the score most Steam users see.

This is part of a system Valve introduced in 2019 to deal with review bombs centered on matters it considers unrelated to the experience of playing a game. It identifies a period of unusually heavy review activity, investigates it, and can exclude that period from the score shown to most shoppers by default.

You can still change your Steam Review Score Settings to include those reviews if you want the unfiltered picture. The filter covers the entire date range, not just the negative posts about a particular controversy. Even an ordinary gameplay review written between November 1 and November 6 is left out of the default calculation.

The review bomb was also coming from two directions. Some players objected to Behaviour associating itself with TheBurntPeanut, while others were angered by the developer's decision to distance itself from him. Both groups ended up expressing their frustrations on Steam, even though neither grievance was primarily about how Dead by Daylight plays.

The trouble began on October 30, when TheBurntPeanut played Dead by Daylight on stream and a member of Behaviour's team granted him access to the game's paid DLC, including characters and perks. The studio later explained that staff had spotted an opportunity to introduce the game to a large new audience, but the gift went ahead without the usual internal approvals.

Players questioned Behaviour's willingness to support a creator whose previous content they felt conflicted with the inclusive environment the developer says it wants to maintain.

Things only got worse when TheBurntPeanut offered to return the DLC and an official Dead by Daylight account responded with, "WE CAN TAKE THE HEAT! THEY ARE YOURS TO KEEP." The reply made it appear that the team was dismissing concerns before it had even considered them.

Behaviour issued an initial apology on November 1. In a longer statement on November 3, it acknowledged that staff had moved too quickly, admitted that its response had escalated the dispute, and confirmed that the gifted content had been removed from the streamer's account.

The developer also promised to rewrite its creator guidelines, tighten the process for approving gifts, and provide further training to its staff. It said it was taking internal action over the incident, although it wouldn't disclose details about individual employees.

The apology hasn't pleased everyone. Some players who objected to the DLC gift still question Behaviour's judgment, while some of TheBurntPeanut's supporters believe the studio caved to pressure. The divide is especially noticeable in a game that, as director Mathieu Côté explained in a June interview with DualShockers, has several distinct communities.

Valve's filter has improved the rating prospective buyers see, but it hasn't done anything to settle the dispute behind the reviews.
