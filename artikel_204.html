Hong Kong democracy activist Joshua Wong sentenced to 10 more years

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Joshua Wong, once a teen activist and one of the faces of the "Umbrella Movement," is spending most of his adult life so far in prison.

Prominent Hong Kong pro-democracy activist Joshua Wong was sentenced on Thursday to 10 more years in prison on a charge of collusion with foreign powers, under the city's China-imposed national security law.

The 30-year-old, who is already serving a nearly five-year jail term for "subversion" under the controversial national security law, was expected to be freed in January next year.

Wong will now not be due for release until the end of 2036.

Wong was charged with conspiring with self-exiled activist Nathan Law and others to ask foreign countries, bodies, or individuals outside China to sanction or engage in other activities against the government.

The offense of foreign collusion may carry a punishment of life imprisonment if found to be of a "serious nature."

Wong pleaded guilty to the offense in September —  a move that can typically reduce the sentence —  but he has also stated that he never advocated for independence for Hong Kong or sought regime change in China.

"The punishment must fit the gravity of the crime," High Court Judge William Tam said while delivering the sentence Thursday.

"It is also necessary for the court to express ​the community's abhorrence of the defendant's scheme and acts with others to collude with foreign forces or external elements to endanger national security," Tam added.

Tam said he considered a 13-year prison sentence as a starting point but reduced it after taking Wong's guilty plea into account.

Wong's second conviction comes as he is serving a four-year, eight-month term behind bars for a separate subversion trial over his role in an unofficial primary in 2020.

Wong was among dozens of activists, a group that came to be known as Hong Kong 47, charged with "conspiracy to commit subversion" in what remains city's biggest national security case.

Critics have said that Wong's imprisonment reflects the authorities' determination to keep prominent dissidents imprisoned.

Beijing sees him as an anti-China figure supporting Hong Kong independence.

Wong first rose to prominence in 2012, when he was just 15 years old, as he led protests against the introduction of pro-China national education in Hong Kong schools. His now-dissolved group, Scholarism, rallied thousands of people to blockade the government's headquarters.

Wong then went on to become one of the faces of the 2014 Hong Kong protests, known as the Umbrella Movement.

He was one of the student leaders in Hong Kong's Occupy Movement, during which demonstrators occupied the Chinese territory's streets for 79 days as they called for direct elections for Hong Kong's leader.

Wong co-founded a political party, Demosisto, which was disbanded when the security law took effect on June 30, 2020.

On Thursday, the activist was sentenced in a packed courtroom. Wong wore a blue shirt and dark-framed glasses as he repeatedly nodded to the gallery before his sentencing.

The United States' Consulate in Hong Kong on Thursday "strongly" condemned Wong's additional jail sentence.

"The United States strongly condemns the Hong Kong High Court's sentencing of Joshua Wong to an additional 10 years of imprisonment and calls for his immediate release," news agency AFP quoted a consulate spokesperson as saying.

"Wong has been unjustly imprisoned since December 2020, demonstrating that Beijing will go to extraordinary lengths to silence those who advocate for fundamental freedoms in Hong Kong," the spokesperson said.

Meanwhile, Taiwan denounced "endless political persecution" of Hong Kong democracy activists.

Taiwan's top China policy body accused Beijing and the Hong Kong government of using "authoritarian tools" to "fabricate charges... engage in endless political persecution, and suppress the people's legitimate rights to pursue freedom, democracy, and human rights."

China, for its part, warned against  "irresponsible" remarks on Hong Kong's judicial affairs.

"Relevant parties should earnestly respect China's sovereignty, respect Hong Kong's legal system, stop making irresponsible remarks regarding the trial of HKSAR judicial cases," Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun told a press conference, using Hong Kong's official acronym.
