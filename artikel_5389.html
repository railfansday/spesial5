Texas Closes In on California as a Top Destination for International Home Shoppers

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Silhouette of real estate industry future business woman moving houses around map of the world

Foreign homebuyers retreated from the U.S. home-buying market this quarter, but still view Florida, California and Texas as their favored U.S. destinations, with interest gaining in the Lone Star state.

According to Realtor.com’s Q3 International Demand Report, 1.3% of the mega portal’s online traffic came from international home shoppers, down slightly from 1.5% in 2025 Q3 but similar to the 1.2% in 2019 Q3, the pre-pandemic level.

Florida leads, California fades, Texas rises

In the third quarter, The most popular three states among international home shoppers are Florida, California and Texas, which together account for 43.1% of international traffic, but each has a different story, according to the report.

Florida remains the top destination for international home shoppers, drawing 21.9% of all international traffic to Realtor.com. Miami, Orlando and Tampa are its leading metros, accounting for 8.3%, 2.9% and 2.5% of U.S. international traffic, respectively. Its share peaked at 24.5% in 2022 and has since settled around 21-22%, close to its 2019 level of 20.8%.

The report shows California has been losing ground. After spiking to 16.6% in 2020, its share has slipped to 10.8%, down from 14.2% in 2019. Los Angeles, Riverside and San Diego are its top metros for international shoppers, drawing 4.7%, 1.4% and 1.2% of U.S. international traffic, respectively.

Texas is catching up. The report shows its share has risen every year since 2022, from 8.8% to 10.3%, narrowing the gap with California from 5.7 points in 2019 to just 0.5 today. If the trend holds, it could overtake California as soon as next year. Dallas and Houston are the leading metros that attract international home shoppers in 2026Q3, drawing 3.0% and 2.4% of U.S. international traffic.

A handful of smaller states are gaining share of international traffic from low starting points. Montana doubled its share from 0.6% in 2019Q3 to 1.2% in 2026Q3, and Idaho (0.4% to 0.7%) and Vermont (0.3% to 0.6%) show similar gains, with Alaska (0.3% to 0.5%) and West Virginia (0.2% to 0.4%) also trending up. Tennessee, from a larger base, climbed from 1.5% to 1.9%. These states are small, but their steady increases suggest growing interest in outdoor and rural markets.

Canada’s share rises, with Montana and Tennessee gaining

According to the report, North American shoppers (excluding the U.S.) were the largest source of international demand, accounting for 47.7% of international traffic, up 5.0 percentage points from 42.7% a year earlier. Canada drove most of that gain: Canadian home shoppers were the number one individual source, at 35.9% of international traffic. After Canadian interest in U.S. real estate declined sharply in 2025, with its share falling to 32.3% in 2025 Q3, the share moved back to 35.9% in 2026 Q3, close to its pre-tariff levels of 36.1% in 2023 and 36.9% in 2024.

Florida, Texas and California are the top states for Canadian home shoppers, and Texas has already edged past California (8.8% vs. 8.6% in 2026 Q3), a crossover that hasn’t yet happened in overall international traffic, Realtor.com writes. Canadians also favor northern and mountain states more than other international shoppers do. Montana drew 2.0% of Canadian traffic in 2026 Q3, up from 1.3% in 2019 Q3 and well above its 1.2% share of overall international traffic, while Idaho doubled from 0.5% to 1.0%. Tennessee also saw growing Canadian interest, rising from 1.3% to 2.0%.

Among the most popular metros, Canadian buyers dominated international views in Sun Belt and Southwest metros. Cape Coral led with 65.9% of its international demand coming from Canada, followed by Naples (65.4%), Phoenix (62.6%) and North Port (61.4%). Notably, these same markets also posted the largest year-over-year growth in Canadian interest between 2025 Q3 and 2026 Q3, led by Cape Coral (+8.9 ppt), Riverside (+6.4 ppt),  Naples (+5.4 ppt), and Dallas (+5.0 ppt). However, Canadians’ interest in Chicago (-1.0 ppt), Las Vegas (-1.0 ppt), New York City (-0.4 ppt) and San Francisco, CA (-0.1 ppt) declined year over year.

European interest in U.S. homes is cooling

Europe moved the other way. The report reveals European shoppers’ share of international traffic stood at its lowest Q3 levels since 2019 (24.5%). The U.K. (6.2% of international traffic) and Germany (3.7%) remain among the top sources of interest in U.S. homes, but both are at their lowest Q3 levels since 2019. The decline coincides with weaker European travel to the U.S., suggesting that European interest in U.S. homes may be cooling alongside interest in visiting.

Even so, a growing share of European traffic is going to New York and Texas. In 2026 Q3, New York drew 8.1% of European traffic, up from 6.0% in 2019 Q3, and Texas drew 8.0%, up from 7.1% in 2019 Q3. In addition, smaller states are also gaining ground among European shoppers. Montana’s share among European traffic nearly doubled, from 0.5% in 2019 Q3 to 0.9% in 2026 Q3, and Tennessee’s rose from 1.7% to 2.0%.
