Bitcoin Miners Sold Nearly Half Their BTC for AI: Most of Their Stocks Fell Further

Publicly listed Bitcoin miners have sold nearly half of their Bitcoin since January. Blockchain analytics firm Glassnode published the report today, November 18. The miners are using the coins to pay for a move into AI data centers. Its chart of six miners, led by MARA Holdings, shows combined holdings falling from about 110,000 BTC early this year, with the biggest monthly drop coming in November.

For years, these companies held what they mined, and some bought more. That made them steady holders in the Bitcoin market. Now they are sellers, at a time when the price is already down 28% this year.

We used these data to check whether the switch helped shareholders of these Bitcoin miners. Four of the six stocks fell more than Bitcoin this year, with a median loss of 41%. Only Riot Platforms and Hut 8 are up.

So who is selling, and what does it mean for the Bitcoin price?

Glassnode tracks the Bitcoin that six listed miners report holding: MARA Holdings, Riot Platforms, CleanSpark, Hut 8, American Bitcoin and Cango. From 2021 to late 2025, their combined stack only grew. This year, it has shrunk in most months, and November’s drop is the largest on the chart.

MARA, the largest holder, accounts for most of it. Its latest filing shows it sold 13,597 BTC for about $1.1 billion over six weeks, at an average price of about $81,000. It used $750 million to repay a loan backed by its Bitcoin and said it would keep selling to build cash for AI infrastructure.

The reason is money. Since the April 2024 halving, each block pays half as much Bitcoin, and the price has fallen from its peak. Renting power and buildings to AI companies pays steadier income, and the coins on the balance sheet are the fastest way to fund the switch.

We compared each company’s share price this year with Bitcoin’s 28% drop, using Yahoo Finance daily closes from January 2 to November 17.

MARA’s timing looks better than its stock. It sold at about $81,000 on average, roughly 27% above Bitcoin’s $63,947 today.

Sales by Bitcoin miners add supply, but MARA’s $1.1 billion over six weeks is small next to a market worth about $1.3 trillion. The bigger change is in behavior: a group that used to soak up coins now sells them.

That matters most when demand is weak, as it is now. The Fear & Greed Index, a daily sentiment gauge from 0 to 100, sits at 11, in “extreme fear.” Each miner sale adds to the coins buyers have to absorb.

On the daily chart, Bitcoin trades at $63,947. Its 50-day exponential moving average (EMA), an average that gives recent prices more weight, has just slipped below its 200-day EMA, at about $75,750 each. Traders call that a “death cross,” a sign that the longer downtrend is taking over.

Bitcoin miners won’t go back to hoarding soon. The AI build-out needs cash for years, and their coins are the cheapest source of it. The next sign to watch is December’s holdings data, which will show whether November was a one-off or the new normal.
