Hosting, traveling, or staying in? How to keep pets happy this holiday season

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

SEATTLE, Nov. 19, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- The holidays are full of good things: big meals, visits from loved ones, and cozy nights at home. They're also full of temptations and hazards that pets tend to find before their people do.

In a new Rover survey of U.S. pet parents, 63% say their pet has eaten holiday food it wasn't supposed to, and half have had a pet knock over the tree or chew on lights or decorations. For nearly 1 in 5 (18%), a forbidden snack ended in a trip to the vet.

The good news? Most of these mishaps are preventable. Here are five simple ways to keep the season safe, from the first feast to the last frost.

The season's first misstep often happens at the dinner table. More than 8 in 10 pet parents (82%) share scraps at least some of the time, so if your pet gets a holiday treat, keep it small and plain.

Ask guests not to share extras, and secure the trash after the meal. If your pet eats something toxic, call your vet or an animal poison control hotline right away, even if they aren't showing symptoms.

Cozy winter touches deserve a second look, too. Keep snow globes out of reach, since some are filled with antifreeze, and keep curious pets a safe distance from fireplaces and space heaters.

With parties come guests, and with guests comes an open door. Nearly half of pet parents (48%) say their pet has slipped out during a holiday gathering or come close, which is especially risky when it's cold outside. Before guests arrive, make sure your pet's ID tag and microchip info are current, and keep a recent photo on your phone.

Arrivals can be stressful, too. About 44% of pet parents say their pet hides, gets anxious, or acts protective around guests. To help ease this behavior, set up a quiet room with water, a bed, and a favorite toy before the doorbell starts ringing.

The holidays are one of the busiest times of year to travel, which can make pet care harder to come by. Half of pet parents (50%) have shifted their travel dates or destinations, or canceled a trip altogether, because care wasn't available. When pets do come along, how they travel matters too: more than half of pet parents (55%) have chosen to drive instead of fly to keep their pet safe and comfortable. More than 4 in 10 pet parents (43%) say their pet has needed an emergency vet visit during the holidays. Before the season gets busy, check your vet's holiday hours, save the number for the nearest 24-hour emergency clinic, and keep an animal poison control hotline in your phone.

As temperatures drop, also watch for signs your pet has gotten too cold, like shivering, weakness, sluggishness, or pale or gray skin on the ears, tail, or paws. If you see them, move your pet somewhere warm and call your vet.
