Whats new to streaming this week? (Nov. 13, 2026)

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Don't be overwhelmed or waste an hour scrolling through your services to determine what to watch. We've got your back, whatever your mood. Mashable offers watch guides for all of the above, broken down by genre: comedy, thriller, horror, documentary, and animation, among others. But if you're seeking something brand new (or just new to streaming), we've got you covered there, too.

What if dinosaurs terrorized a suburb? That's the premise of the latest from writer/director David Robert Mitchell (It Follows). And while there's plenty of action and jump scares in this Amblin-inspired adventure, I was not a fan of The End of Oak Street.

Anne Hathaway and Ewan McGregor star as an oft-bickering married couple who need to reunite as a team to keep their teenage children from being eaten by the prehistoric creatures prowling their neighborhood. Grim and gory with a dunderheaded ending, I'm not remotely recommending this movie. But it sure is streaming this weekend. — Kristy Puchko, Entertainment Editor

Starring: Anne Hathaway, Ewan McGregor, Maisy Stella, and Christian Convery

How to watch: The End of Oak Street will debut on HBO Max on Nov. 13.

Is this a safe space to admit I've seen every episode of Deadly Women at least once? I won't deny the Investigation Discovery doc series, which ran for over 190 episodes across 14 seasons, is problematic. The show's experts (and writers) tended to frame its subjects under misogynistic archetypes, like deadly gold diggers, ungrateful daughters, and sex-crazed maniacs, with little more than lip service to motives or the societal conditions or psychological distress that led to murder. But I couldn't get enough of the succinct storytelling, which traversed the globe and centuries, to tell stories of all kinds of women who were admittedly deadly.

Deadly Women 2.0 offers a new cast of expert psychologists, along with re-enactments, evidentiary footage, and disturbing new stories. Will this one be better — or less problematic — than its older sister series? I'll definitely be watching to find out. — K.P.

How to watch: Deadly Women 2.0 premieres Tuesday, November 10 at 9 p.m. ET/PT, with new episodes airing weekly. Episodes will be available to stream on HBO Max.

This docu-series traces the origins of looksmaxxing, which is part of the bizarro maxxing trend that Dictionary.com named word of the year. Get Huge: Looksmaxxing to Death follows the story of Anthony Hughes (aka Tony Huge/Dr. Huge), a fitness influencer and self-described "millionaire lawyer and body-building nerd" whose close friend — and fellow looksmaxxing influencer — Leo Longevity was found dead in Thailand in 2023. "A lot of people in my past have died," Hughes is heard saying in the trailer. "And it's nothing to do with me." — Sam Haysom, General Assignment Editor, UK

How to watch: Get Huge: Looksmaxxing to Death is now streaming on Hulu.

If you like your found footage horror with a side of grief and misery, you may want to check out Tinsman Road. Writer/director Robbie Banfitch also stars in this story about a man on the trail of his missing sister, who vanished while out walking in the woods around their home long ago. Banfitch's own mother plays his mother in the film, with the two of them battling both their grief and the possibility of something supernatural. This sounds like one for fans of Lake Mungo. — S.H.

Starring: Robbie Banfitch, Leslie Ann Banfitch, Salem Belladonna, and Heather Middleton

How to watch: Tinsman Road debuts on Shudder on Nov. 13.

Karin Slaughter's best-selling thriller The Good Daughter gets the TV treatment this week, with Oscar nominee Rose Byrne and Emmy nominee Meghann Fahy at the helm. The pair play sisters Samantha and Charlotte Quinn, who have spent the past 20 years processing a violent attack on their family. When another act of violence shatters their small town, it dredges up complicated memories and painful family truths. If you love thrillers and all-star casts, The Good Daughter might be right up your alley. — Belen Edwards, Entertainment Reporter

Starring: Rose Byrne, Meghann Fahy, Brendan Gleeson, Harper Steele, Paula Malcomson, Jake McDorman, Olivia Williams, Michael Dorman, Jayson Warner Smith, Audrey Grace Marshall, and Drew Cheek

How to watch: The Good Daughter is now streaming on Peacock.

Portrait photography great Richard Avedon is on the other side of the lens in this Netflix documentary from Ron Howard. Through archival footage and interviews with his family, friends, and contemporaries (Avedon himself passed away in 2004 at the age of 81), the film explores the artist who disrupted and defined modern portraiture.

You would have seen at least one of his signature striking photographs — everyone from Martin Luther King Jr. to Charlie Chaplin, Marilyn Monroe, Michael Jordan, James Baldwin, Andy Warhol, Louis Armstrong, the Dalai Lama, Toni Morrison, The Beatles, and so many more. An overarching picture of the pioneer, Howard looks into Avedon's influence on the fashion world, while examining his need to capture the people instrumental in global politics. — Shannon Connellan, Senior Editor

How to watch: Avedon is now streaming on Netflix.

Abbott Elementary fans know five-time Emmy nominee Janelle James as the always unfiltered, always hilarious principal Ava. But before her role on Abbott Elementary, she was primarily known as a stand-up comedian. Now, audiences can get better acquainted with James' stand-up work in her debut stand-up special, Janelle James: How Romantic. Over the course of the special, James touches on American politics, dating, and more, making for a fun, candid hour of comedy.  — B.E.

How to watch: Janelle James: How Romantic is now streaming on Netflix.

Remember how Odyssey audiences freaked out over the trailer for Jessica Chastain as Other Mommy? Well, now's your chance to get your spine tingled at home by the latest from Rob Savage (Host, The Boogeyman).

Based on Josh Malerman's New York Times best-selling novel Incidents Around the House, Other Mommy centers on Bela (Arabella Olivia Clark), a 7-year-old girl who is being stalked by an entity she calls "other mommy," which looks like her real mommy (Jessica Chastain). A haunted house narrative that's thrillingly twisted, gruesome, and stupid, Other Mommy might not be for the whole family. But it's some sick fun. — K.P.

Starring: Jessica Chastain, Jay Duplass, Arabella Olivia Clark, Dichen Lachman, Arian Moayed, Sean Kaufman, and Karen Allen

How to watch: Other Mommy is now available for rental or purchase on digital.

Look, by this point, chances are you've already seen Avengers: Endgame. But if you need to rewatch it as part of your preparation for Avengers: Doomsday, be sure to watch Avengers Endgame: Encore, debuting Friday on Disney+. It's the same tale of the Avengers battling fight Thanos (Josh Brolin) and trying to reverse his catastrophic snap, only this time, there are a few extra scenes tacked on at the end that tease what's to come in Doomsday. — B.E.

Starring: Robert Downey Jr., Chris Evans, Mark Ruffalo, Chris Hemsworth, Scarlett Johansson, Jeremy Renner, Don Cheadle, Paul Rudd, Brie Larson, Karen Gillan, Danai Gurira, Benedict Wong, Jon Favreau, Bradley Cooper, Gwyneth Paltrow, and Josh Brolin

How to watch: Avengers Endgame: Encore hits Disney+ Nov. 13.

If you love your TV shows full of flashbacks and non-linear storytelling, do I have a show for you! Feast your eyes on 12 12 12, which follows a heist across three different timelines: the 12 months of planning leading up to it, the 12 hours of the heist itself, and the 12 days following the heist. Jamie Dornan plays longtime thief Paul Galvin, and Anthony Mackie plays FBI agent Jack Strickland, who's diligently on his tail. Together, the pair face off in a cat-and-mouse game that may sneakily get you in the holiday spirit, as the heist takes place on Christmas Eve. — B.E.

Starring: Anthony Mackie, Jamie Dornan, Jack Kesy, Kali Reis, Ólafur Darri Ólafsson, Agathe Rousselle, Sallie Harmsen, and Patrick Fabian

How to watch: 12 12 12 debuts on Apple TV on Nov. 13.

A Charlie Brown Christmas is a holiday classic, and this week, Apple TV hopes to add another Peanuts special to your winter rotation.

In Snoopy Presents: 12 Days of Snoopy, Charlie Brown's beloved beagle has wound up on Santa Claus's naughty list. Now, with only 12 days left until Christmas, he'll have to prove he's a good dog, and of course, learn some valuable holiday lessons along the way. Get ready for some Christmas sweetness, as well as jazzy music from composer Jeff Morrow and original music from John Legend, Kamasi Washington, Leon Bridges, and Laufey. — B.E.

Starring: John Legend, Kamasi Washington, Leon Bridges, and Laufey

How to watch: Snoopy Presents: 12 Days of Snoopy debuts on Apple TV on Nov. 13.

From Siân Heder, the director behind the Best Picture Oscar winner CODA, comes Being Heumann, a biographical drama about Judith Heumann, an advocate known as the mother of the disability rights movement.

An adaptation of her memoir Being Heumann: An Unrepentant Memoir of a Disability Rights Activist, this movie focuses on the 1977 sit-in that began as an impulsive political action and became the longest occupation of a federal building in American history. Sounds like a stirring drama that could well return Heder to the Oscar campaign trail. While critics have cheered the film's performances, from a compelling cast of actors with disabilities, they've been less impressed with Heder's filmmaking. Essentially, this is a movie about revolution that feels frustratingly traditional.* — K.P.

Starring: Ruth Madeley, Mark Ruffalo, Dylan O'Brien, Rob Delaney, and Ray Fisher

How to watch: Being Heumann debuts on Apple TV on Nov. 13.

Jessica Chastain's country-music-filled thriller comes to Netflix this week, from writer/director Shana Feste (Country Strong). Heartland sees Chastain playing Misty Jones, a country music star who has stepped out of the spotlight — and, unfortunately, into the role of amateur investigator. Returning to her hometown of Nashville 20 years later, she's tracking the disappearance of her 22-year-old niece, Charlie Heart (Carter Faith), a rising country star. Decades since she's been in town, Misty must determine suspects from allies in the city that made her. — S.C.

Starring: Jessica Chastain, Garrett Hedlund, Carter Faith, and John Hawkes

How to watch: Heartland debuts on Netflix on Nov. 13.

Imagine if 20 years after the events of E.T., Elliott hadn't moved on from obsessing over his alien friend. That's essentially the premise of the oddball Aussie comedy Allen.

When Petey (T.J. Power) was a kid, he met a cute extraterrestrial he named Allen. But after playing together and making a pact to be besties for all time, Allen took off to parts unknown. Decades later, Petey hasn't grown up, rejecting building a career, a relationship, or a family while he waits for Allen to return. But when he does, their reunion sparks a bizarre revelation that will force Petey and his human loved ones to come together to save the world.

From Daley Pearson, co-founder of Bluey production company Ludo Studios, Allen offers a family-friendly setup with comedy that's decidedly not! But for those who grew up on the '80s, there's plenty of irreverent humor, heart, and nostalgia to enjoy. — K.P.

Starring: T.J. Power, Charlotte Nicdao, Josh Helman, Emily Barclay, and Benedict Hardie

How to watch: Allen is now streaming on Netflix.

The Proud family is back, but with a Rankin/Bass twist! Leaning into the classic aesthetic of the animators who brought us stop-motion classics like Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer and Santa Claus is Comin' to Town, A Proud Family Wizmas reimagines its 2D clan in a new dimension.

When businessman Wizard Kelly rebrands Christmas as Wizmas, Penny Proud sounds off at a mall Santa, hurting the holiday spirit. Now, it's up to her to Santa Clause it up and save Christmas. We're always game for more Proud family fun. So, the holiday flare plus the stop-motion reimagining is the cherry on top of our whipped cream and hot cocoa. — K.P.

Starring: Kyla Pratt, Tommy Davidson, Paula Jai Parker, Jo Marie Payton, Cedric the Entertainer, Karen Malina White, Soleil Moon Frye, Alisa Reyes, Carlos Mencia, Alvaro Gutierrez, Raquel Lee Bolleau, and Melissa De Sousa

How to watch: A Proud Family Wizmas debuts on Disney+ on Nov. 14.

Rivals enters its darkest days yet, as the lauded Jilly Cooper adaptation returns in uncharacteristically sombre form for Season 2, Part 2. But this is the Cotswolds in the '80s — there's also plenty of drama and debauchery. Reeling from a major character death at the end of Part 1, the residents of Rutshire are grieving, guilt-ridden, and swiftly pivoting to post-funeral sex to the sounds of George Michael’s "Faith." As the feud between Rupert Campbell-Black (Alex Hassell) and Lord Tony Baddingham (David Tennant) reaches a point of no return, secret affairs hide an inch from discovery, unexpected new romances simmer, and the all-important war for commercial television dominance raises the stakes for one of the most superbly salacious shows on TV. — S.C.

Starring: David Tennant, Aidan Turner, Katherine Parkinson, Victoria Smurfit, Alex Hassell, Nafessa Williams, Bella Maclean, Emily Atack, Lisa McGrillis, and Danny Dyer

How to watch: Rivals Season 2, Part 2 debuts on Disney+ on Nov. 11.

All hell breaks loose in Netflix's new series The Body, about a Catholic high school dance team who might have summoned the Devil.

Created by Quinn Shephard (Under the Bridge), The Body is an intoxicating blend of strange visions, sapphic angst, and religious hysteria, led by a formidable young cast. As I wrote in my review, "Mix the teen-girl ferocity of Yellowjackets with the moral panic of The Crucible, dress it up in a blinged-out Juicy Couture tracksuit, and you'll get The Body." Come on, who wouldn't want to give that a whirl? — B.E.

Starring: Kristina Bogic, Sara Boustany, Geena Meszaros, Nnamdi Asomugha, Louisa Krause, Shirley Chen, Jackson Kelly, and Sofia Wylie

How to watch: The Body is now streaming on Netflix.

The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power returns for Season 3, and this time, Sauron (Charlie Vickers) is running the show. He's conquered most of Middle-earth and is working on forging one ring that will force all other rings of power to bend to his will. Can the rebellious Elves, Dwarves, and Men of Middle-earth stop him in time? If you have any passing knowledge of J.R.R. Tolkien's work, you know the answer. But, as is the case with Tolkien, the journey to the end point is where the real fun happens.

While The Rings of Power still struggles with its pacing and unwieldy scale, Season 3 is the show's most fun outing yet. Turns out, it's a total blast watching Sauron be fully evil and corrupt those around him, just as it's a blast to watch the show's spectacular fight sequences. And with the introduction of new characters like Celeborn (Jamie Campbell Bower), Galadriel's (Morfydd Clark) long-lost husband, there's many a new juicy dynamic to explore. — B.E.

Starring: Morfydd Clark, Charlie Vickers, Robert Aramayo, Ismael Cruz Córdova, Cynthia Addai-Robinson, Charles Edwards, Owain Arthur, Maxim Baldry, Jamie Campbell Bower, Trystan Gravelle, Robert Strange, Markella Kavenagh, Daniel Weyman, Eddie Marsan, Andrew Richardson, Zubin Varla, Adam Young, and Simon Pegg

How to watch: The first four episodes of The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power Season 3 are now streaming on Prime Video, with more every Wednesday.

Philomena Cunk (Diane Morgan) has taught audiences about life and earth in outrageously funny satirical mockumentaries. Now, she's tacking the history of movies with Cunk on Cinema.

Reaching back to the earliest days of the moving image, this mini-series, made up of six 15-minute episodes, offers many actual facts about the evolution of cinema, moving from Edison and Kuleshov to Spielberg and AI. Of course, through the lens of this deadpan and daft character, the results are a mix of informative and chaotically comedic. I warn you, it's hard to watch just one. I binged the whole lot in one sitting, cackling so hard and often that my face hurt in the end. Cheers to Cunk, and all the bewildered interviewees who endured her. — K.P.

Starring: Diane Morgan, Michael Bay, and Benedict Cumberbatch

How to watch: Cunk on Cinema is now streaming on Netflix.
