Goo Goo Dolls are bringing their hometown sound to the national stage this Thanksgiving as they headline the Buffalo Bills‘ halftime show against the Kansas City Chiefs on Nov. 26, 2026. The Buffalo-born rock band will perform at Highmark Stadium in Orchard Park, New York, giving local fans a special holiday treat. Presented by M&T Bank, the performance will feature synchronized light-up bracelets throughout the stadium, creating an interactive concert experience.

Led by John Rzeznik and Robby Takac, the Goo Goo Dolls formed in Buffalo, New York, in the mid-1980s. Known for Billboard Hot 100 hits such as “Iris,” “Slide,” “Name” and “Black Balloon,” the band has sold more than 15 million records worldwide. Although an official Thanksgiving setlist has not been announced, fans can reasonably hope to hear popular favorites like “Iris” and “Slide.” Their performance is expected to last approximately six to eight minutes, consistent with recent NFL Thanksgiving halftime shows. This is not their first Thanksgiving NFL appearance either. The band previously performed during the Detroit Lions’ game against the Green Bay Packers 19 years ago in 2007.

The NFL and Hollywood always collide, especially during Thanksgiving games when celebrity spotting becomes almost as much of a sport as football itself. This year, fans may get the chance to spot two famous faces as Taylor Swift and Hailee Steinfeld could attend the Chiefs vs. Bills matchup to support their husbands. Neither attendance has been confirmed, but based on their past appearances at games, a visit is certainly possible. Swift’s husband, Chiefs tight end Travis Kelce, is expected to take the field while Steinfeld’s husband, Bills quarterback Josh Allen, will be starting.

Steinfeld, who’s currently promoting Disney’s Hexed, was last spotted supporting Allen at the Bills’ Oct. 12 game against the Los Angeles Rams, while Swift’s most recent Chiefs appearance was Nov. 22 when Kansas City faced the Arizona Cardinals. If the “Reputation” singer shows up in Buffalo, it could mark her first public appearance since the controversial Nov. 23 Vanity Fair article about her sparked widespread attention. Considering Swift has yet to attend a Chiefs Thanksgiving game (she didn’t attend the Chiefs’ game in 2025), an appearance at the 2026 matchup would mark a first for the singer and add another high-profile moment to the mix of football and celebrity culture.
