Hating Turkey Doesn’t Make You Cool

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

It is fun to be contrarian. I admit to the light thrill it gives me to tell people that I hate oat milk, or that I prefer a wet martini. It’s healthy for all of us to have our preferences. But sometimes what should be a matter of personal taste goes viral, amassing an army of haters who make this preference their entire personality. Think back to the great cake vs. pie wars of the 2010s, when anyone who admitted to liking cake was a tasteless rube. This isn’t even about what you like anymore—it’s about the attention that comes with going against the mainstream.

This is all to say that complaining about turkey on Thanksgiving has got to stop. It is good, and I need people to stop pretending as if it’s not.

You would think from the way people talk about turkey around Thanksgiving that a flock of the birds came into their house and killed their mother. In 2020, Business Insider wrote that turkey isn’t good and you should leave it off your Thanksgiving table. Vox insists the (horrifying) numbers of turkeys wasted every year are in service of “a dry, flavorless bird no one likes,” while Milk Street claims anyone who likes it must be “pretending.” Even this very publication called it “The Big Freaky Chicken.”

Respectfully, what on earth are you people talking about? Every year I cannot wait for the unveiling of the turkey. It is the ultimate reminder that this isn’t a normal meal because we do not eat turkey on the regular.

Over the years, my family has more often entrusted me with the cooking. We tend to do a version of this Martha Stewart en papillote method and I relish in the work. There is care required to ensure the meat is juicy and golden, and the resulting gravy silky and rich. There is great satisfaction in carrying out the gorgeous centerpiece. There is a subtle butteriness to the meat, and something wilder that even the most factory farmed bird can’t erase. Roasting a chicken is for a Tuesday. Roasting a turkey is for when you have the day off work.

Part of the joy of the turkey, of course, is the leftovers. Turkey congee, turkey pot pie, turkey melts, which will only exist because of Thanksgiving. A feast stretched out, reminding you that abundance does not last for just a day. Do not rob yourself of such plenty.

Obviously, the haters have not stopped millions of turkeys from being served every Thanksgiving. The biggest complaint seems to be that the turkey is never cooked well. And I’ll give you that, with a bigger bird, that you only encounter once a year, it takes planning and effort to ensure the middle doesn’t become a powdery mess while the legs are still raw. But I don’t know, try. There are many things that are tricky to cook that are beloved precisely because of the exhilaration of the challenge, so I don’t know why turkey gets all the heat.

If you’re here, it means you like to cook, even love it, and there are no shortage of recipes guiding you toward bird nirvana. So put your hobby to good use, get a meat thermometer, and figure it out. Because as much fun as it is to be a hater, it’s even more fun to prove everyone wrong with a feast.
