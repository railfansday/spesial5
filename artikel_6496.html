I Tried Mashed Potatoes From KFC, Popeyes &amp; Jollibee—There's Only One I'd Order Again

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Turns out, a little gravy can make a very big difference.

As a kid who grew up making weekly trips to Boston Market, I’m no stranger to getting mashed potatoes to go. While the chain is a lot harder to find these days, I can still turn to a handful of others when I don’t feel like peeling potatoes and making a batch myself. I might not be rushing to supply my Thanksgiving dinner table with takeout mashed potatoes, but I’m certainly not averse to ordering some to dip my fried chicken into.

I ordered taters from three local fast-food chains to see which one passes muster for texture, seasoning, and all-around flavor. Here’s how it all went down.

Along with Boston Market, I grew up as a regular at KFC. Sadly, its mashed potatoes didn't hit quite as well as I remember. Here, you can order mashed potatoes with or without gravy (the only chain on this list with a gravy-free option), but for this tasting, I opted for the topped version. While they were super creamy, I'd argue they're almost too creamy. They could benefit from a bit more texture and structure. I also found them a bit bland.

I appreciated the gravy, which kept the potatoes from tasting dry, but the potatoes themselves weren't much to write home about.

Popeyes’ mashed potatoes come topped with Cajun gravy, setting them apart from your standard dish. I really enjoyed the flavor of the gravy—it was almost reminiscent of gumbo—and I thought it added a unique element to the mashed potatoes. There was a tiny hint of spice here that also worked really well.

The potatoes are very creamy but thicker than KFC's, and overall, they were more satisfying in terms of both texture and flavor.

Jollibee's taters were quite good and, hands down, the most classic iteration of the bunch. I liked that you could see actual pieces of potato skin in the mashed potatoes. The gravy also tasted the most traditional, with an almost turkey-like flavor. Notably, these were the least creamy potatoes of the three I tried, but thanks to the gravy, they didn't taste dry.

However, the scoop of actual mashed potatoes was pretty meager, and they were drowned in the gravy. Still, I would choose these over the others when I'm looking for that quintessential gravy-and-mashed combo.
