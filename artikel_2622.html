MVC NOTEBOOK: As Paradise Jam awaits, Graves assesses ISU's 2-2 start

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The cellphone on Matthew Graves' desk in the Indiana State Sycamore basketball coaches office rings.

A visitor awaits nearby. Mark Slessinger, Graves' associate head coach, heads toward the ensuing practice in the adjacent ISU Arena.

It's a busy Tuesday afternoon, especially with the Sycamores preparing to catch a 2:30 a.m. flight — with one stop — to St. Thomas. ISU will play three games in four days at the Paradise Jam, an eight-team tournament at the University of the Virgin Islands, beginning with Friday's 4 p.m. matchup with Stephen F. Austin.

Graves' Sycamores enter the event with a 2-2 record. They lost 78-70 to Charlotte in Terre Haute, won 69-66 at Ball State, lost 82-73 at home to Milwaukee, and then routed overmatched Earlham 109-37 in Hulman Center on Saturday.

In the Missouri Valley Conference nonconference standings, the Sycamores are currently among the bottom four teams, joining Evansville at 2-2, with Northern Iowa 2-3 and Illinois State 1-3 going into Tuesday's games. Murray State tops the league at 5-0, so far, after Tuesday afternoon's 82-66 win over Hofstra at the Savage Brothers Showcase in Mississippi. Five Valley teams are 3-1, including Belmont, Drake (an 83-79 loser to Tarleton State on Monday), Illinois-Chicago and Valparaiso (a 106-35 Monday winner over Calumet College of St. Joseph). Southern Illinois is 2-1.

ISU opens the Virgin Islands tournament against a SFA squad that stands 3-1, with its lone loss coming 81-78 in overtime to Rice on Saturday.

Graves believes the Sycamores also could be 3-1 right now, or even 4-0. The debut setback to an athletic Charlotte team was close throughout, but last week's loss to Milwaukee didn't set well with the third-year ISU head coach.

"You want to try to win every game, obviously. The Milwaukee game is the one you look at that we just did not play up to our standards," Graves said Tuesday. "It wasn't that we [should've] won the game or not — that remains to be seen — but we just didn't play a very good basketball game. In the Charlotte game in the opener and at Ball State, I felt like we competed and obviously won one of them and lost the other."

Against Milwaukee, the Sycamores got outrebounded 43-33 and outshot 51.7% to 42.4% from the field and lost an early lead barely five minutes into the game and never regained it.

ISU emerged from that disappointment to overwhelm Earlham — a NCAA Division III program the Sycamores hadn't played since 1927 — and had 12 Sycamores score points. Standout guard Karmani Gregory sat out that game, resting a leg injury incurred against Ball State.

"The Earlham game served its purpose," Graves said.

He figures Gregory will be OK to play in the Paradise Jam tournament, which will include a second-round game against either Temple or Middle Tennessee at 4 p.m. Saturday (if ISU loses to SFA) or 6:30 p.m. Sunday (if ISU beats SFA). Monday's schedule includes a seventh-place game at 1:30 p.m., fourth-place at 4 p.m., third-place at 6 p.m. and the championship at 9 p.m.

"He's available. I wouldn't say he's 100-percent healthy, but he's healthy enough to play," Graves said. "And barring anything crazy the next couple days, I anticipate him being in the starting lineup on Friday."

Gregory ranks third on the MVC scoring list, averaging 21.3 points per game, right behind Southern Illinois' Jalen Haynes at 25 ppg and Drake's Jon Carroll at 22.3.

Haynes and his SIU teammates could face Indiana State at the Paradise Jam. The Salukis loom in the tournament's other bracket and open the event against Tennessee Martin on Friday. The field also includes East Carolina and Utah Valley.

Other MVC teams will trek to tournaments in warmer climes this weekend. Murray State plays Seton Hall on Friday in the Bahamas, Valpo plays Troy on Saturday at the Pensacola Invitational in Florida and Illinois-Chicago plays Miami (Ohio) on Sunday at the Scout Jacksonville Classic in Florida.
