Expectations for Saints Were Not Based in Reality

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

New Orleans dropped their second divisional matchup of the year in the most Saints fashion as they tied the game in the final minute on Sunday just to have a pass interference call give the Panthers 30 yards and a routine field goal situation to win the contest. The loss moved the Saints to 3-6 on the year (0-2 in the NFC South) with the most difficult three game slate on their schedule on deck and the playoffs can be considered an unattainable dream at this point. While the Saints have gotten off to a bumpy start this year (no denying that the team has underperformed in certain areas), the frustration that many Who Dats feel should be owned by the fanbase as a product, in part, of their own making for believing undue hype. The context of the team entering the year was a second-year head coach, a quarterback with limited (yet promising) experience, and a serious lack of depth resulting from an extensive and still ongoing roster reconstruction; expectations for this year's team were based in hope rather than reality. The Saints were a hot take for a "Surprise Team" that could make noise in the playoffs amongst the national media which inevitably bled into the minds and hearts of many Black and Gold blooded fans, but that's all they ever were – a possible surprise team that could be better than last year (6-11).

The success the Saints found at the end of last year was blinding to many as the team had chosen a young offensive minded coach (which many fans wanted at the time of the hire) and found a likeable quarterback in the draft that was the bright spot of the entire team. Polar opposites from Dennis Allen and Derek Carr. This is also a formula that the national media can and did latch onto as it is easy for anyone that has watched NFL to say "exciting coach and quarterback combination could be… exciting" and most takes when it comes to small market teams, especially the Saints, are usually just that lazy. Nostalgia of Drew Brees and Sean Payton's success came rushing into the souls of fans that remembered the good times of 2006 and 2009 (early years of the era), and how easy they made being good in the NFL look. One must not forget the following facts, which many have, that make comparing the current team and that of the early BP (Brees and Payton) years more like comparing apples to kumquats than apples to apples. By the time Brees arrived in New Orleans he had already led a franchise to 3 – .500 or above seasons, been through two quarterback competitions, and passed for over 3,000 yards 3 times in a run heavy offense. The sights we all witnessed in the 15 years of BP were a once in a lifetime experience and should not be expected again, yet this seems to be the measuring stick that a fanbase hungry for the same level of success is using. This is a catastrophic mistake by those who have chosen that path of thought and will (already is) causing Fleur De Lis fatigue. Simply put, Shough and Moore need more time and a better roster before lofty expectations should be applied as they are still a relatively inexperienced team with a serious lack of depth.

Contrary to popular belief, this season has not been a failure as the team has renewed its ability to face adversity that it lost in the last year under Dennis Allen and has shown signs that they are just a few pieces away from being the team that many expect them to be currently. The rebuild from the years of going for it all has a foundation, but the building part of the process isn't anywhere close to being complete.

4 of the 6 Saints' losses have been 1 score games including the Detroit Debacle in week 1, an outlier occurrence in the Raiders game (4 – 4th quarter turnovers), and two games (Panthers and Steelers) where the Saints found themselves on the wrong side of games where one play could have swung the favorable result to either team. This is less of the Saints being a terrible team with no hope in the future and more of how the NFL works. Given the dynamic of the team going into the year, I knew this (3-6 start) was a possibility – as a fan I hoped it wouldn't come true, but it also does not set the precedent to burn the house down. There is more to how a program is performing than wins and losses, despite what group think will tell you. Other than simple bad luck, the Saints have faced bad injury luck as well. To start the year, the Saints lost a vital piece of their offense that most forget in Dillon Radunz who was handpicked to be the 6th man on the offensive line as a versatile lineman that started 10 games last year and could fill in at guard or tackle if need be. This became a real issue in the 4th quarter of the previously mentioned Raiders game as the Saints were down to their 7th lineman to replace Kelvin Banks Jr. at left tackle and Asim Richards wasn't able to hold up against the Raiders pass rush which alone led to multiple turnovers and the game being flipped on its head. Next came the injury to Kaden Ellis, whom no one seems to want to give the Saints credit for finding or re-signing which exploited the previously mentioned lack of depth as Danny Stutsman was not up for the challenge in the run game, specifically which helped lead to the Falcons having their way on the ground on Monday Night Football in the Superdome. In the Dome that night you could almost feel the tides shift as red jerseys were all that remained in the lower bowl and whispers of firing Moore, Loomis, or both started to emerge. Combine these with Bryan Breese being out the entire season from an already limited interior defensive line group, 3 wins at this point should have been more or less expected. While there are definitely places the Saints need to improve, (starting with the rushing offense) this team was always at least a year away from truly competing for anything more than winning the worst division in the NFL to start this season.

Therefore, any talk of firing Moore, firing Loomis, or having Todd Graves buy the team are a waste of breath, energy and quite frankly are tired takes at this point. If the wheels fall off, there is constant weekly regression, and the team starts playing the way we saw in the last year of Aleen then sure, but that is not what the product on the field each week says.
