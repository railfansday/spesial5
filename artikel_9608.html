The Key To The Perfect Pie Crust Is In These 5 Simple Rules

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Admit it, a pie is only as good as its crust. No matter how good the filling is, a flaky, buttery crust is what separates the good from the great. But how to achieve the pie crust of your (and your guests’) dreams? The master of all things baking, Senior Food Editor Makinze Gore, is here to explain her five golden rules of pie crust—just in time for the holidays.

A buttery, flaky pie crust is the stuff of dreams; to achieve this, Gore says it’s essential to use cold butter. The same goes for many flaky pastries and baked goods, as the steam that cold butter releases as it melts creates those layers inside the crust. If you use butter that isn’t cold, it will melt too quickly—it can even melt just during the process of rolling the dough out if it's warm enough, which won’t produce any steam. Warm butter will leave you with a dense, mealy crust.

The size of your butter chunks will affect the end product. Gore says larger and smaller chunks can both work, but larger chunks leave a flakier crust, while smaller chunks let the crust hold more intricate designs. Typically, you want your chunks to be at least pea-sized to form the steam pockets required to create flaky layers.

Sensing a theme here? Gore says it’s vitally important to chill your dough at several points throughout the process. This is because you need to keep the butter cold, as it will warm up as you work with it. Gore recommends chilling your crust after you first make it, after rolling it out, before crimping it, and once more before baking it. This will help keep the butter cold and prevent the crust from shrinking as it bakes.

One great trick to ensure an even bake is to use a metal pie tin. Metal conducts heat better than glass, so it bakes the crust more evenly, though glass does have some benefits. Baking pie crust in a glass pie dish lets you see the bottom of the crust and know exactly when it’s perfectly cooked.

Another tip Gore says will help bake your crust more evenly is to set a baking sheet in the oven as it preheats, then to place the pie tin on top of the hot baking sheet as it cooks. This extra heat helps the crust cook slightly faster, giving it more time to set before the liquid filling seeps in.
