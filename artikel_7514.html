Florida Doctor and Medical Research Employees Convicted of Falsifying Data in Clinical Trials of New Drug Treatments

A federal jury in the Southern District of Florida convicted a medical doctor and three staff members of Pines Care Research Center LLC (Pines Care), a medical research center in Florida, for their roles in a scheme to falsify data in clinical trials of prospective new drug treatments. A fourth staff member previously pleaded guilty.

“Accurate human testing data is critical to the U.S. Food and Drug Administration’s evaluation of whether to approve new drug treatments for Americans,” said Assistant Attorney General A. Tysen Duva of the Justice Department’s Criminal Division. “These defendants abused their positions to falsify human testing data gathered to evaluate the safety and efficacy of new asthma medicine. The Justice Department and FDA will continue to work together to protect the integrity of the data used to evaluate the safety and effectiveness of new medicines.”

“The cornerstone of FDA’s evaluation of a new drug is reliable and accurate data from clinical trials,” said Assistant Commissioner for Criminal Investigations Justin Green of the Food and Drug Administration (FDA). “Fraudulent clinical trial data could impact the agency’s decisions about the effectiveness and safety of the drug under review. We commend the efforts of the Department of Justice for vigorously pursuing the prosecution of this matter.”

According to court documents and evidence presented at trial, Jaynier Moya, M.D., 50, of Southwest Ranches, Florida, Luis Montano, 56, of Hialeah, Florida, Yuniarka Garcia, 41, of Plantation, Florida, and Luis Cuza, 42, of Cape Coral, Florida, conspired to commit fraud while conducting human clinical research trials at Pines Care in Pembroke Pines, Florida. Alexandra Olivera, 38, of Hialeah, was separately charged in a criminal information with participating in the conspiracy and has pleaded guilty. Moya co-owned Pines Care and served as principal investigator for research studies. Montano, Garcia, Cuza, and Olivera were clinical research coordinators at Pines Care.

Trial evidence established that Pines Care was selected as a trial site to gather human testing data regarding the safety and efficacy of prospective new asthma treatments on behalf of a pharmaceutical company sponsor. However, beginning no later than 2019, the defendants fabricated testing data and falsified records while conducting trials for the sponsor. Evidence at trial established that the defendants used identification documents from people who did not actually participate in the studies to create false records purporting to show that these individuals were participating as research subjects. The defendants then falsified records to make it appear as if these individuals had taken the study medication and undergone testing to evaluate the medications’ effects, as called for in study protocols. In fact, the evidence established that the defendants paid other individuals to undergo tests to generate fabricated data that the defendants could attribute to the purported research subjects. The defendants then submitted the fabricated test data to the sponsor to obtain payment for purportedly completing the research.

The jury convicted Moya, Montano, and Garcia of wire fraud and conspiracy to commit wire fraud and Cuza of conspiracy to commit wire fraud. Sentencing is set for Feb. 9, 2027. Moya, Montano, and Garcia face a maximum penalty of 80 years in prison, and Cuza faces a maximum penalty of 20 years in prison. In August 2026, Olivera pleaded guilty to conspiracy to commit wire fraud, and her sentencing is set for Dec. 15. Olivera faces a maximum penalty of 20 years in prison. A federal district court judge will determine the defendants’ sentences after considering the U.S. Sentencing Guidelines and other statutory factors.

The FDA’s Office of Criminal Investigations Miami Field Office is investigating the case. The Justice Department’s Office of International Affairs provided significant assistance with obtaining the testimony of a witness for trial. The U.S. Marshals Service provided substantial assistance in securing the arrests of the defendants.

Trial Attorneys Andrew Crawford, Brianna Gardner, and Rowan Reid of the Criminal Division’s White Collar and Corporate Enforcement Section, Health and Safety Unit, are prosecuting the case. Trial Attorney Michael Murali, Law Clerk Autumn Young, and Paralegal Janice Park provided substantial assistance.

The Health and Safety Unit within the Department’s Criminal Division works with law enforcement partners to investigate and prosecute violations of federal laws designed to protect public health and safety. The unit focuses on corporations and individuals who make and sell dangerous drugs, food, and other consumer products that could cause significant harm to Americans. For more information, see https://www.justice.gov/criminal/criminal-white-collar/health-safety-unit.
