6 times players came back to haunt their old club ahead of Bruno Guimaraes’ Newcastle return

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Bruno Guimaraes is the latest Premier League player to return to a former club as Arsenal take on Newcastle United, but will the Brazilian follow in the footsteps of others and get a goal?

From apologising to full-length runs of the pitch, players scoring against their old clubs results in a number of different reactions.

Here are six of the best of them from Premier League history.

We’re generally against players not celebrating goals against former clubs but when that player has played 648 times for them, perhaps we can understand it.

Lampard’s move from Chelsea to New York City came with an unexpected stopping-over point of Manchester City where of course he scored against his former club.

It was at the Etihad and City were trailing thanks to a goal from Andre Schurrle when Lampard scored an equaliser five minutes from time.

He looked like a dog that had just eaten something he knows he shouldn’t have as he put a single hand up in the air to apologise.

Rooney’s departure from Everton was a little messy with Everton fans chanting against him even as he was still their player and that bitterness was still there three years later.

In 2007, United trailed Everton 2-0 which gave the home fans the confidence to give their old player a bit of stick.

But it was a case of kicking the bear. United levelled in the 68th minute after Phil Neville put the ball in his own net and 11 minutes from time, Rooney grabbed the winner.

And there was certainly no lack of a celebration.

After a few bounces like a man who could not decide what to do, Rooney looked the Goodison Park faithful in the eye and kissed the United badge.

Would Wayne Rooney have worked in Pep Guardiola’s Barcelona?

It is the absolute gold standard of blood-boiling celebrations.

Adebayor was annoyed at Arsenal for what he felt was Arsene Wenger forcing him out, resulting in a £25.5m move to Manchester City.

Wenger suggested it was more a need to raise money but Adebayor took it to heart and let his emotions come out when the two clubs next met.

He netted in a 4-2 win and sprinted the full length of the pitch to taunt the travelling Arsenal fans. Referee Mark Clattenburg looked like a disappointed parent while stewards struggled to hold the enraged Arsenal fans back.

It was not Adebayor’s only moment of the game either. He stamped on Robin van Persie’s face, landing him a retrospective three-match ban, and even got into arguments with Cesc Fabregas and Alex Song.

Ince had controversially made his way to Liverpool having previously spent six years at United and he rubbed salt into the wound in 1999.

A few weeks before United would go on to lift the treble, Ince scored a late equaliser at Anfield and celebrated by hugging about six different fans in the stands.

The goal and result looked to have swung the title race to Arsenal but United did eventually lift the trophy.

Van Persie was enemy number one around the Emirates in 2012 after he left for Manchester United and he acknowledged that fact when he scored in the first three minutes when they met at Old Trafford in the 2012/13 season.

He gave a double-handed apology and looked genuinely disheartened by the goal. In the away leg, he scored once more but again refused to celebrate.

Six months on though and Van Persie very much did celebrate.

He rose well to meet a Wayne Rooney corner and went full knee slide in his celebration. As he was hugging Rooney, his face did though look like a man who’s just remembered he was supposed to not celebrate.

It’s not one of the more well-known names but we are including it because it led to a rule change.

LuaLua was on loan at Portsmouth from Newcastle at a time when there was no restriction on when loan players could feature.

Newcastle were winning 1-0 away at Fratton Park when guess who scored to equalise it. Bearing in mind he was still an employee at this point, LuaLua celebrated by taking his shirt off in what is possibly the furthest thing away from an apology.

Newcastle manager Bobby Robson said: “He was the last player we wanted to score against us. One of our players has taken a point off us.”

That bizarre situation led to the Premier League tweaking the rules to prevent loaned players from facing their parent club.
