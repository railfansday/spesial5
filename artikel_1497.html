Same old Spurs given reality check of epic proportions

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

The players and the transfer fees might be different, but this was same old Tottenham Hotspur. The same Tottenham that finished each of the last two seasons in 17th. The same Tottenham that folded in adversity. The same Tottenham that opposition fans loved to laugh at.

Of course, it is very early days and the good news for Spurs fans and head coach Roberto De Zerbi is the side that started the opening day humiliation to Brentford may well never play together again this season.

But in a summer in which Tottenham have allowed their fans to dream of good times ahead by breaking the bank in the transfer window, this was a reality check of epic proportions.

The Spurs supporters who have become sick of being made the butt of their rivals’ jokes had to listen to Brentford fans singing “Thomas Frank, he’s laughing at you” in reference to the Dane’s unhappy spell in north London.

The impending arrival of Savinho will take Tottenham’s spending over £300m, but none of their big-money debutants at the Gtech Community Stadium hit the ground running.

There was only one man who looked anything close to a £100m player and it was not Sandro Tonali.

Tottenham’s first-ever £100m signing was completely upstaged by Brentford’s Mamadou Sangaré on his first competitive start for the club, as Spurs were run ragged.

Sangaré might not have scored on his Brentford debut, but he had a major contribution in two of his side’s goals and bossed the middle of the park. He deservedly received a standing ovation when he was substituted with 15 minutes remaining.

“He is a top player, no doubt about it,” said head coach Keith Andrews. “He is the one we were after in the window. The club deserve a lot of credit because a lot of clubs were looking at him.

“He deserves a lot of credit too, because he could have gone elsewhere for a few more bob. He recognised this would be a good place to come and he has settled quite nicely.”

A club-record £39m signing from French club Lens, Sangaré may prove to be a steal for Brentford. He ran the game for long periods and was responsible for Keane Lewis-Potter’s opening goal.

Having played a lovely one-two with Dango Ouattara, Sangaré kept his cool to brilliantly pick out Lewis-Potter and give him the easy task of finding the back of the net.

But if ever there was proof that spending a fortune offers no guarantee of happiness, then this was it.

Roberto De Zerbi has admitted that big-spending Tottenham Hotspur “are not a team yet” after watching his new-look side fall to an opening-day defeat to Brentford.

Tottenham have splashed out over £200m on new players, but head coach De Zerbi has warned it will be impossible to set any targets until they become a team.

Record £100m signing Sandro Tonali made his competitive debut for Tottenham, along with fellow new signings Jan Paul van Hecke, Marcos Senesi, Mateus Fernandes and Andy Robertson.

Tottenham have been tipped to challenge for European qualification, having finished 17th in each of the last two seasons, but De Zerbi acknowledged those optimistic predictions may have come too soon.

His team looked like strangers in their humiliating loss at Brentford and the Italian said: “We are not a team yet for a lot of reasons. We can fix the target [for the season] when you become a team. Before you become a team, you can’t say what you can do.

“You can’t become a team from the transfer market on paper. You become a team when the players find a connection, a spirit, a soul.

“We are not at the right point in our physical condition for a lot of reasons. Late from the World Cup and because in Australia [on pre-season], we don’t work in the right way. But no excuses, it’s a normal situation when you are building a new project. I don’t want time but we have to pass through these problems.

“I am surprised by the result and performance. They [Brentford] deserved to win and we played very bad. I knew we were not in the right condition. We can’t find an excuse. I am sorry for the result and performance.

“Marcos Senesi, Jan Paul van Hecke and Sandro Tonali are not in the best physical condition. We knew that before the game. We have to improve on that and many other things, like organisation and pressing.

“We can improve quickly but first, we have to understand this game better. I know their physical condition and [they are] new players but we have to put something more.

“We had to fight more. Players know I have a lot of confidence in them and we hope to improve quickly. Until we reach these conditions we have to suffer and today is an important defeat. Maybe we can wake up stronger.”

We had some problems with the timing of the pressure, we arrived too often late. We lost too many duels to compete with Brentford. I am sorry for that and sorry for the defeat. We have to improve in the physical condition and the organisation.

It was the first game and part of our journey, we knew we had a lot of new players. To become a team I said before the game we need two things, soul and style of play. We didn’t fight in the right way. Brentford is maybe one of the most important teams in the Premier League in terms of duels.

It was a game, especially in the first half, in one direction. We were not in the right position to make the high pressure, with Mikey Moore on the left centre.

We didn’t play well, we didn’t play in the right way. I think not because we don’t remember last season, because these players are good people but we lost too many duels. One of the best qualities of Brentford is the one-to-one, the man-to-man and the set-pieces.

Really pleased. Felt like there was a performance coming, you never really know after pre-season. The reality is the first game of the season comes and it can quickly come unstuck. I felt good for a number of weeks about how we were coming together. I was pretty pleased.

Our fans were amazing today. There is a genuine connection here that helps. I thought we started the game brilliantly and they were fully engaged. We want to be relentless. We looked like a real team.

That’s what we wanted, we wanted to start sharp, we want to show people what we’re made of and we did that today. We know last season we were one point off, this year, we’ve added quality people to the squad.

Former managers Mauricio Pochettino, José Mourinho, Antonio Conte and Ange Postecoglou will all be scratching their heads about Spurs’ change of strategy in the club’s first post-Levy summer.

It was Postecoglou who publicly shared his belief that Spurs would never spend £100m on a player and yet, just over a year after his departure, the club splashed out exactly that on midfielder Tonali.

Whereas Postecoglou and his predecessors often had to answer questions over Spurs’ perceived profligacy, De Zerbi has been able to tease fans with promises of “bomba” signings.

Good evening and welcome to our live coverage of Brentford vs Tottenham Hotspur in the Premier League, a London derby between the smart spenders and the big spenders.

That is probably an unfair dichotomy, because Spurs’s squad has been crying out for investment, and that has certainly arrived this summer. The club’s new leadership team has overseen more than £300m worth of purchases.

The arrival of Jean Paul van Hecke and Marcos Senesi, two of the league’s best progressive passers from centre-back, makes it clear that improving Spurs’s build up play is a priority for Roberto De Zerbi.

De Zerbi wants his teams to draw pressure from opponents and progress down the middle of the pitch with short passes, so it was imperative that Spurs upgraded in midfield. They have done just that by buying Mateus Fernandes and Sandro Tonali, for a combined £185m.

Spurs’s forward options remain much the same though, which could prove a drag in the season’s early weeks. Compared to Manchester United, Chelsea, and Liverpool, Spurs do not have the same firepower, but they should have more control and solidity this season.

Brentford lost just three league games at home last season, and they were unfortunate not to qualify for Europe. A lighter workload offers Keith Andrews the chance to consolidate on an excellent first season as head coach, and perhaps overtake some of those clubs with continental commitments.

Midfielder Mamadou Sangaré, signed for £39m from Lens, looks the pick of their summer business, tipped to be a £100m sale to one of the big boys (which is an unromantic perspective on the transfer, but very much Brentford’s model).

Asked about the Mali international, Andrews said: “He’s a very talented midfielder who can do lots of different aspects of that role.

“He’s a very focused, very determined and very ambitious young man. He’s very humble but equally as hungry to achieve impressive things. He’s certainly a player on an upward trajectory.”

Forwards Jadon Anthony and Callum Wilson are other arrivals, and Michael Kayode’s long throws will continue to be a serious weapon.
