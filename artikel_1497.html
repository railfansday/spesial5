Bayern Munich’s treatment of Jamal Musiala is grim, depressing and utterly classless

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

There are few things more exciting than watching an academy graduate develop into a superstar. Bayern Munich got to experience exactly that with Jamal Musiala.

He joined the club as a 16-year-old, grew into a world-class talent, has made over 200 first-team appearances and won six Bundesliga titles. For a time, he was the envy of Europe. It was only a couple of years ago that he was widely regarded as one of the finest young talents in the world.

So how did we get here? To a situation where Bayern’s hierarchy seem intent on unsettling one of their most valuable players and making his position at the club increasingly uncomfortable.

“We’re in a dilemma with him,” honorary president Uli Hoeness told BILD a couple of weeks ago.

“He has an illness that cannot simply be cured, but can be managed to some extent with medication. However, whether he will ever regain the level of performance he once had remains uncertain.”

To understand the situation, you have to appreciate just how much Musiala has been through. His momentum was first halted by a devastating ACL injury at the 2025 Club World Cup, which saw him sidelined for half a season. He hasn’t quite ever recaptured his best form since returning to action.

There have been further complications, too. Musiala revealed in August that he was being treated for a neurological dysfunction which causes temporary seizures, following two collapses during Bayern’s pre-season friendlies.

“The experience with such an injury suggests that it will be difficult for him to reach the level he had before,” Bayern legend Karl-Heinz Rummenigge has since stoked the fire.

“His high salary is no longer sustainable. Nobody will buy him under these conditions. The discussion hasn’t taken place yet, but we have to think about the finances.”

It is difficult to escape the feeling that Bayern’s hierarchy are turning the screw. German outlet BILD have subsequently reported that Musiala’s medication continues to cause problems, with recurring side effects proving problematic. Whether coincidental or otherwise, the steady drip-feed of negative headlines is doing little for the player.

Bayern have already made Harry Kane Europe’s highest-paid player and are reportedly keen to reward Michael Olise with a bumper new deal. The prospect of having Musiala on similarly astronomical wages is said to be a headache for the Bayern hierarchy.

That is hardly Musiala’s problem. Contracts work both ways, and Bayern cannot expect players to honour their commitments only when it suits the club financially. They were happy to offer him those wages in the first place.

FIFPRO is the global representative organisation for professional footballers’ unions, advocating for players’ rights, welfare and working conditions around the world. They have struck the perfect tone.

“FIFPRO is deeply concerned by the repeated public statements from FC Bayern Munich’s leadership calling into question the contract of Jamal Musiala as he returns from injury,” they posted in a statement.

“Bayern’s honorary president, chief executive and former chief executive have all publicly questioned Musiala’s contract in quick succession. In FIFPRO’s view, this is a coordinated strategy to put pressure on Musiala and his representatives ahead of any negotiations. A club of Bayern’s standing should know better.

“Bayern has a duty of care to Musiala. Across professional football, uncertainty about a player’s future can take a toll on their mental health, particularly when they are recovering from injury. Making Musiala’s health and future a matter of public debate adds to that pressure. Any concerns about his contract should be raised privately with him and his representatives.

“Contracts are there to be respected. An injury or a dip in form gives a club no grounds to reopen terms it agreed to, just as a run of outstanding performances gives a player no automatic right to a pay rise. Clubs, leagues and player unions have agreed at FIFA level that contractual stability is a core principle of the game. It protects clubs as much as it protects players.

“FIFPRO stands with Jamal Musiala and with all players who honour their commitments. We expect their employers to do the same.”

They’ve absolutely nailed it. We couldn’t have put it better ourselves.

Bayern’s old guard are entitled to their opinions, but we’re equally entitled to call out behaviour that is utterly classless. Musiala is going through a horrible experience, battling a difficult medical condition while trying to get his career back on track – all the while feeling like his club doesn’t have his back.

Borussia Dortmund are top of the Bundesliga. Long may that continue as Bayern continue this sorry spectacle.
