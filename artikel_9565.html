I Tried AMD AI Academy: Free, Hands-On AI Courses from AMD (and What to Watch Out For)

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

When I signed up for the AMD AI Developer Program, I found something I didn't expect: AMD AI Academy, a set of free self-learning AI courses — hands-on Jupyter notebook labs included, GPU environment and all.

I'm currently working through "Introduction To AI Agents" myself. Here's what the Academy offers, how it works, why it's free, and the rough edges I ran into.

AMD AI Academy is a collection of self-paced courses built for AI developers. The official page lists three benefits: free access to cutting-edge knowledge, hands-on labs with real-world use cases, and course completion certificates.

The courses are organized into four learning paths:

Some of the courses I came across include AI on AMD, Introduction to AI Agents, Introduction to Prompting, Introduction to RAG, AI Agents 101: Building AI Agents with MCP, AI Agents 201: Design to Deployment, LLM Serving with vLLM & MI300X GPUs, Fine-tuning Reasoning Models (Unsloth), and Intro to Post-training.

The agent-focused content stood out to me — there's even an "Agentic AI Specialisation" track.

The flow is simple. Pick a course, read the material, and run the hands-on labs in Jupyter notebooks.

The notebooks come with a GPU environment and run entirely in the browser. You don't need a GPU of your own to try running models — that's the biggest draw for me.

When you finish a course, you get a course completion certificate, which you can share on LinkedIn. It's a nice record of what you've learned.

One thing worth clarifying: a completion certificate is not the same as an official certification. The Academy also offers the "ROCm Certified Associate," an official AMD certification that validates your ROCm and AI development skills. The certificate says you finished a course; the certification tests your skills. Use each for what it's meant for.

Let's separate facts from my own take on this.

Fact: the Academy is one of the member benefits of the AMD AI Developer Program. The same program bundles other developer perks, including $100 in Developer Cloud credits, one free month of DeepLearning.AI Pro, and hackathons like the AI League. So the Academy is less a standalone free course and more one piece of a broader developer outreach effort.

My take: winning in GPUs isn't just about hardware performance. It also depends on which environment developers are used to, and how deep the ecosystem runs. With so many developers comfortable with NVIDIA's CUDA, getting more people to try ROCm is itself groundwork for AMD.

Free courses, a free notebook environment, and a shareable certificate all lower the friction of getting started. And the learning naturally leads toward real usage of Developer Cloud and Instinct GPUs down the line. The courses look like an investment in future users.

Three things stood out from my own experience.

First, notebooks sometimes stop mid-run. More often than I'd like, a hands-on notebook has stalled partway through and I've had to start over. It happened in the Module 9 lab (MCP Tools With LangChain) too. I don't know the cause, but the GPU side may be congested. Treat it as the price of a free tier and keep going.

Second, the material is mostly in English. If you're not comfortable reading and writing in English, progress will be slower.

Third, the Academy labs are separate from the $100 Developer Cloud credit. In my case, I was able to keep learning in the Academy before the credit had even been granted. You don't have to wait for the credit to arrive before starting.

AMD AI Academy is official, free learning material for AI developers, with GPU-backed hands-on labs included. Four learning paths take you from the basics to production deployment, and the completion certificate is shareable on LinkedIn.

Watch out for notebooks stalling mid-run and for the English-only material. But if you want to get a feel for AI development on ROCm and AMD GPUs, it's a good place to start.
