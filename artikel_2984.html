Advance Auto Parts vs. Airbnb: Is a Retail Rebound or Travel Giant Stock the Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

One runs thousands of stores with a sizable debt load, the other rents out millions of listings without owning a single room. Their valuation gap is wide, and the risks differ sharply.

Whether you are betting on people fixing older cars or traveling to new destinations, Advance Auto Parts (AAP -0.37%) and Airbnb (ABNB +3.12%) offer very different ways to play the consumer economy.

Advance Auto Parts provides essential components for vehicle maintenance, catering to both professional mechanics and hobbyists. Airbnb operates a digital platform that has changed how travelers book lodging worldwide. While one relies on physical retail and the other on a high-growth tech marketplace, both companies face unique challenges heading into 2027.

Advance Auto Parts operates a vast network of stores across North America, serving both professional mechanics and do-it-yourself customers. The company focuses on the automotive aftermarket, providing everything from engine components to accessories among retail stocks in the automotive niche. It serves over 4,300 company-operated stores and supports hundreds of independently owned locations through its distribution network, with its professional segment accounting for approximately 50% of total sales in 2025.

In the 12 months ended Oct. 10, 2026, revenue reached around $8.6 billion. This was a slight decline of approximately 0.3% compared to the previous 12-month period, which management attributed to a challenging macroeconomic environment for consumers. During this time, the company reported a net income of about $100 million.

As of its January 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 2.4x. This ratio shows that for every dollar of equity, the company has $2.40 in total debt, which indicates that it relies more on borrowing than on shareholder investments. The current ratio, which compares short-term assets to liabilities, is close to 1.7x. In the fiscal year ended Jan. 3, 2026, free cash flow was negative $298.0 million, reflecting a period where cash outflows for operations and equipment exceeded cash inflows.

Airbnb operates a global two-sided marketplace connecting hosts who offer unique stays and experiences with travelers around the world. The company utilizes a co-host network to help hosts manage their listings more effectively and ensure a high quality of service for guests. It currently manages a massive platform that spans more than 220 countries and regions, allowing it to offer over 9 million active listings without owning real estate directly.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached approximately $14.0 billion. This reflects growth of around 16.9% over the prior year. Net income during this same period was close to $3.2 billion.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 0.3x, and the current ratio is approximately 1.4x, suggesting the company maintains a healthy liquidity position. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was nearly $4.6 billion. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 34.3% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since stock-based compensation is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Advance Auto Parts faces significant competition from national chains like AutoZone (AZO +0.23%) and O'Reilly Automotive (ORLY +0.69%), as well as mass merchandisers and online retailers. It also deals with uncertainty regarding its 2024 restructuring plan and the ability to maintain long-term operational efficiencies. Geopolitical risks, inflation, and the shift toward electric vehicles could also disrupt its complex supply chain and traditional business model.

Airbnb is highly dependent on the global travel and hospitality industries, which are sensitive to economic downturns, geopolitical crises, and health emergencies. The company faces ongoing regulatory challenges and short-term rental bans in various cities worldwide that could limit its listing inventory. Additionally, its reliance on third-party providers like Amazon.com Inc. (AMZN -0.29%) for cloud infrastructure adds operational risk, alongside potential cybersecurity threats.

Advance Auto Parts appears cheaper based on its P/S ratio, while Airbnb has a higher Forward P/E relative to its future earnings estimates.

Airbnb reported an excellent third quarter of fiscal 2026. The business beat consensus revenue estimates by more than $100 million, reporting $4.87 billion in sales, up nearly 19% from the same period in 2025, driven by a strong increase in booked nights. Foreign markets like India and Latin America, two markets in which Airbnb is seeking growth, have been strong this year. The seasonally weaker fourth quarter should see sales close to $3.3 billion and net income around $488 million.

The business is finding ways to expand its popular travel business, embracing features like buy now, pay later (BNPL) on reservations, a form of short-term lending. The network advantage of Airbnb's history, over 2 billion guest arrivals since 2008, and the lack of host presence on AI (meaning a competitor using AI has little data to scrape to form a competing product) give it a competitive moat.

For the full year, management expects sales of $14.1 billion with net income of $3.2 billion.

Advance Auto Parts is in the midst of turning around the struggles it has experienced over the past few years, including sales growth, margins, and market share. The aftermarket auto parts business has been in a slump, largely due to a mix of factors: weak do-it-yourself demand, vehicle electrification, intense competition, and general pocketbook pressures on consumers.

Company management has been addressing the business's issues by rolling out a new multi-year turnaround program. That started in late 2024, including closing stores, optimizing store footprints, and overhauling its supply chain--for instance, reducing the number of carriers by 70% through consolidated shipments.

To date, the turnaround program has had mixed success. The first quarter of fiscal 2026 brought results much better than expected: revenue of $2.6 billion and net income of $25 million. The second quarter and third quarter disappointed. In the third quarter, reported in early November, the company fell short of expectations on revenue, with just over $2 billion in sales and net income of $15 million. Revenue was slightly below the year-ago quarter, while the business did swing from a net loss in the same comparable period.

Analysts see full-year 2026 sales slightly lower year-over-year at around $8.5 billion, with net income improving to around $132 million, more than three times 2025.

This is a choice between a brick-and-mortar retailer working to turn its slump around and an asset-light web broker of vacation rooms. Advance's forward P/E feel a little rich for a company still trying to find its legs again, while Airbnb's premium isn't so high that long-term investors would have trouble justifying a purchase. 2027 is probably a 'prove it' year for Advance Auto Parts. Go with Airbnb.
