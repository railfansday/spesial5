Apple Is Now the World’s Largest Company. Time to Buy the Stock?

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Nvidia's fall has opened up Apple's lead.

Nvidia (NVDA -0.86%) was formerly the world's largest company by a fairly wide margin. However, that lead is now gone thanks to the market’s reaction to news of its CEO resigning, and Apple (AAPL +0.64%) is firmly in the lead. As of the time of writing, Apple's market capitalization sits at about $5.2 trillion, while Nvidia's is at $4 trillion.

That's a monster lead, but has Apple reached a ceiling, or is there more room to run? I think there are a few things that investors need to watch out for with Apple, as the future doesn't look very promising.

When you look at the companies that are deeply involved in artificial intelligence (AI), Apple is about the only tech giant that's absent from the list. Nearly every company above a $1 trillion market value is spending big on AI or is a key component provider.

While Apple has integrated some AI feature sets into its products and upgraded its Siri assistant, all of those technologies are using someone else's AI model. Apple realized that just using someone else's product and deploying it in its software was a far more cost-effective option, and that the market has loved that.

Instead of Apple spending hundreds of billions of dollars on an extensive data center network, Apple has kept its spending modest and continued to repurchase shares at an aggressive rate. As of now, this looks like the right choice, as all of the deep AI investments some of Apple's peers are making haven't paid off.

As a result of not going all-in on AI, the stock has become a safe haven for investors who don't want as much exposure to AI. This has caused a lot of investors to buy up the stock, which has inflated its valuation.

Apple is now one of the more expensive stocks on the market, trading at 38 times forward earnings. For reference, the S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.45%) trades at some one-time gain on investments is skewing net profits and is trading at about 19 times forward earnings. That's a major mismatch, but is Apple's stock worth the premium from a business standpoint? I don't think so.

While Apple's big tech peers are spending big on AI data centers and haven't completely paid for themselves yet, the reality is that these companies are still growing far faster than Apple. Of the five largest companies in the world, Apple is growing the slowest (Nvidia is excluded because its growth rate was over 100% last quarter).

AAPL Revenue (Quarterly YoY Growth) data by YCharts

While Apple's growth rate is similar to Amazon's, Amazon's business is also shifting to a heavier reliance on cloud computing, which has better margins. When you look at how quickly operating profits are growing (net profits are being skewed by some one-time gain on investments), this argument goes away.

AAPL Operating Income (Quarterly YoY Growth) data by YCharts

The reality is that Apple is growing far slower than its peers yet trades for a substantial premium over them. That is an odd mismatch, and it's something that likely won't last forever. Apple is also facing its own challenges, such as the rising cost of components because the AI build-out is consuming a large amount of production capacity at chip companies.

At the end of the day, Apple is a very dominant company with a very disciplined approach to what it spends money on and how much stock it buys back. This discipline has gotten it to the status of the world's largest company and is unlikely to change anytime soon.

However, the stock has gotten so expensive that buybacks are less effective. This could dampen future returns on the stock, and while Apple may not crash, I think investors are at serious risk of underperformance over the next few years. As a result, I think there are far better stocks to buy now.
