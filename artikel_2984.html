Airbnb vs. Shopify: Which Consumer Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Airbnb's cash generation is substantial, but Shopify's growth rate and valuation gap raise a tougher question about what a dollar of future earnings is worth.

By Jake Lerch – Updated Nov 18, 2026 at 8:45AM EST

Will the future of growth be driven by global travel experiences or the expansion of independent digital commerce? Deciding between Airbnb (ABNB -0.56%)and Shopify (SHOP -0.50%)involves choosing between two very different platform leaders.

Airbnb connects millions of travelers with unique stays, while Shopify provides the software backbone for modern retail. Both companies benefit from powerful network effects, where more users or merchants make the platform more valuable for everyone involved.

Airbnb operates as a massive marketplace for lodging and experiences, connecting more than 5 million hosts with guests in roughly 220 countries. In its latest annual report, the company highlights its reliance on Amazon (AMZN +1.38%) for platform hosting and its use of third-party providers for community support. As the platform evolves, it is exploring the use of AI in travel to enhance its search capabilities and host support.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $13.9 billion. This represents a revenue growth of approximately 16.7% compared to the prior twelve-month period. During the same time frame, the company reported net income of nearly $3.2 billion.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the current ratio, which measures a company's ability to cover its short-term debts with liquid assets, reached roughly 1.4x. The debt-to-equity ratio, which measures total debt relative to shareholders' equity, is approximately 0.3x. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was close to $4.6 billion, though note that stock-based compensation accounted for roughly 34.3% of operating cash flow, inflating reported cash generation.

Shopify serves as the central operating system for millions of merchants across 175 countries, providing tools for both online and in-store sales. The company supports everyone from small entrepreneurs to large enterprise brands, relying on partners like PayPal and Stripe for payment processing. This ecosystem approach allows it to scale without owning inventory, creating a software-centric business model with high recurring potential.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached close to $14.2 billion. This reflects a revenue growth of roughly 32.5% over the preceding period. Shopify also reported a net income of approximately $2.1 billion for these twelve months.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the current ratio is approximately 6.0x, indicating a strong ability to meet short-term obligations. The debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 0.0x, indicating a very low level of debt relative to equity. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was nearly $2.0 billion, and stock-based compensation accounted for roughly 22.1% of operating cash flow, inflating reported cash generation.

Airbnb faces significant regulatory hurdles, such as the restrictions on short-term rentals in New York City, which could be adopted by other global cities. The company also competes intensely with major travel platforms like Booking Holdings (BKNG -0.14%), Expedia Group (EXPE +1.37%), and Trip.com Group (TCOM -0.72%), as well as search engines like Alphabet. Furthermore, an ongoing IRS tax audit involving a $1.3 billion claim adds a layer of financial uncertainty to the company's long-term outlook.

Airbnb appears to be the more conservatively valued stock, carrying both a lower Forward P/E and a lower P/S ratio compared to Shopify. The Forward P/E compares the stock price to future earnings estimates, while the P/S ratio is the market cap divided by sales over the past twelve months.

Valuation metrics include those sourced from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from those of other data providers.

When comparing Airbnb and Shopify, investors should consider a few key factors. Let's have a look at them and see what that tells us about each stock.

Firstly, we should consider revenue growth. Here, Shopify can claim an advantage. Over the last three years, the company has averaged year-over-year revenue growth of 28.7%. What's more, in recent quarters, that growth has accelerated, with Shopify delivering 31.6% revenue growth in its most recent quarter. Airbnb's three-year average is 13.5%; its growth has also accelerated, with the company reporting 17.7% revenue growth in its most recent quarter.

Another factor to consider is each company's profitability. Both companies have been consistently profitable in recent years. However, Airbnb can claim an edge in the category. Its operating margin is 22.6%, while Shopify's is 18.1%.

Finally, there's valuation. Since both companies are delivering consistent profits, let's use price-to-earnings (P/E) to compare their valuations. Airbnb stock trades at a P/E multiple of 34x, while Shopify trades at around 87x. That makes Airbnb stock significantly more affordable.

To sum up, investors may want to consider both of these consumer stocks, given their impressive growth rates and profits. However, in this particular head-to-head comparison, Airbnb has the overall edge, in my view. The company's profitability, combined with its more reasonable valuation, complements its revenue growth, which exceeds 15%, making it my choice in this battle of consumer stocks.
