A Diwali Menu That’s Full of Joy (and Sticky Chicken Wings)

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Growing up, I remember a steady stream of guests coming in and out of our home in the weeks leading up to Diwali. The season would start with Navratri, a nine-night Hindu festival celebrating the divine feminine, Shakti, through the worship of Goddess Durga. Our celebrations were led by women, my grandmother, mother, and aunts, who would meticulously clean the house and spend weeks preparing snacks and sweets. They taught me the art of hosting and how to share the joy of food with our community.

I came to associate Diwali with that sense of abundance, care, and graciousness. There was always more food than we needed, and always room for more people. When I moved to New York, I started to miss that deeply, so I began a tradition to share Diwali with my chosen family here, many of whom were experiencing the holiday through food for the first time.

I’m the kind of host who likes to enjoy her own party, which means being strategic about the menu. I lean on dishes like my homestyle chicken kari, which can be made ahead and tastes even better the next day. And then there are dishes that bring a little theater to the table, like the creamy rice pudding which gets torched before serving. I want food that feeds a crowd, feels special, and lets me spend less time in the kitchen and more time with the people I love.

Guests always ask me for this crowd-pleasing app. Japanese sweet potatoes have a creamy texture that pairs beautifully with the spicy, crunchy elements of chaat. Best of all, the spuds can be cooked ahead, with the toppings added just before serving. One less thing for you to do on the big day.

Wings reign supreme when it comes to a party appetizer, and these tamarind-glazed ones deliver tangy punch in every bite. The marinade combines tamarind purée, fish sauce, brown sugar, red pepper flakes, and cumin, with extra glaze brushed on for maximum flavor and char. Be sure to finish with fried onions for bonus crunch.

This tastes like you spent hours cooking, but actually it comes together in less than an hour. The best part? It’s even better the next day, making it ideal for hosting. Gently reheat the kari before guests arrive, letting the warm, heady spices fill your home before everyone gathers around the table.

Mushrooms are an underrated ingredient in Indian cooking, and this creamy masala puts the earthy veg front and center. Sautéing them in batches gives the shrooms beautifully caramelized edges, while deglazing the pot captures all those flavorful browned bits. Serve the masala with rice or warm flatbread to sop up all the savory sauce.

No Diwali celebration is complete without something sweet, but dessert doesn’t have to be elaborate to feel special. This recipe transforms a handful of pantry staples into a rich, creamy rice pudding. For a playful twist, finish it with a crackly brûléed sugar crust and serve with a scoop of mango sorbet.
