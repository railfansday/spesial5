Leighton Meester Is Still Having Fun

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Nearly 20 years after Gossip Girl, the Guarding Stars actress is just glad to be here.

Every item on this page was chosen by an ELLE editor. We may earn commission on some of the items you choose to buy.

When Netflix released the trailer for its new holiday movie, Guarding Stars, with Leighton Meester in a lead role opposite Jared Padalecki, better known as Dean from Gilmore Girls, it was catnip for millennial women everywhere. “Blair is back on my screen and I’m seated,” one commenter wrote, referencing Gossip Girl’s Blair Waldorf, the role that made Meester famous. “Put Leighton in anything and I shall watch it,” remarked another. Others chimed in with “Inject this straight into my veins” and “We’re all showing up for Leighton right???”

It’s been nearly 20 years since the September 2007 premiere of Gossip Girl, when Meester won our hearts as the sharp-witted, immaculately coiffed queen of the Upper East Side, and she says now that she’s just as happy as we are that she’s still appearing on our screens. “I’m so lucky. I think about it every day, how so many people don’t get to do what they want to do. It’s so wonderful and rare,” Meester tells me over Zoom from her home office in Los Angeles. “Obviously, there are lots of elements that are really hard. Sometimes it’s negative 20 degrees, it’s muddy or cold or raining, and you’re sitting in a tent, trying to warm your hands. But to me, overall, it’s pretty damn glamorous.”

In Guarding Stars, which is based on the 2022 novel The Bodyguard by Katherine Center and drops on Netflix today, Meester plays a no-nonsense bodyguard charged with protecting one of the most famous actors in the world (Padalecki) while he visits his family in Montana during the holidays. The catch is that, in order to keep the actor’s mom from worrying about him, she must pretend to be his girlfriend. You can see where this is going…but unlike your typical Hallmark-esque tale, Guarding Stars also features action scenes as Meester’s character protects her charge from a stalker. There’s an explosion; there’s a horse race scene. “I’m really proud of it,” she says. “It has all the stuff that I like in a movie. There’s action, there’s romance, there’s definitely comedy, and it feels very cozy, like a place you want to live and stay.”

In a way, Meester manifested the role. “I feel like I somehow summon things,” she says, telling me about a junket where she was asked what sorts of projects she was looking to take on in the future and she mentioned the genre. “I was like, ‘I’d like to do something where there’s action.’ And then, right away this came about.” She did many of her own stunts in the film. “I got to ride a horse—I got to run and jump in-ground explosive thing that blows up—and I also got to flip a stunt person and do some wrestling.” (Near the beginning of the film, her character flips Padalecki’s character onto the ground to showcase her skills when he doubts her ability as a bodyguard.) “I’m pretty sure the stunt person can do a backflip from standing, so it wasn’t really me,” Meester says, “but it still felt super powerful.” (The horse stunt, however, was less of a confidence booster: “Horses don’t respect me, and I learned that, but that’s okay,” she adds.)

They filmed last winter in Calgary, Canada. “I remember the cameras would freeze and they’d be like, ‘We can’t film,’ because it was 25 below Celsius,” she says. “But you get so much cinematic value from shooting in actual snow. A lot of the time, winter scenes are not shot in the cold. You do it in June and there’s pretend snow on the ground and you’re wearing a sweater even though it’s sunny and hot. This was truly cold and I think you can tell the difference.” But the frigid temperatures did require some creative costuming. “The wardrobe department was very prepared,” she adds. “In every scene, you have to understand, I was wearing at least four pairs of pants, all layered on top of one another, and then multiple pairs of socks.”

She’s hoping the film will make its way into the holiday movie lexicon. “We could use a Christmas classic this year,” she says. “And it feels very much like no matter what you’re looking for in a movie to cozy up with, this kind of has it. I would tell people it’s a Christmas cowboy action Hollywood rom-com—it’s kind of just all those things mixed together in a beautiful garland of a film.”

Blissfully, Guarding Stars is just the first project starring Meester on the horizon. She will also soon appear in Basic—her first theatrical release in years—another rom-com written and directed by Chelsea Devantez that premiered at South by Southwest earlier this year and will hit theaters in February. According to Meester, in Basic, she plays “a basic bitch” who does stand-up and fights with her co-star Ashley Park over a man they both dated. “She wears workout gear 24/7, drinks matcha all day, and speaks in hashtags—that’s who she is,” she says. I joke that it’s somewhat of a return for her—Blair wasn’t basic per se, but she could certainly be a bitch. “Somehow it comes easy to me,” Meester says with a laugh. “Don’t know why!” She notes that two of the characters she played in recent guest-starring roles—as a “try-hard” influencer on Nobody Wants This and as a “girl boss” on I Love L.A.—are also quite basic. “I guess I just really like doing it,” she shrugs.

As she did with Blair, Meester manages to give nuance to the women she portrays. “I try to wade through and find common ground and humanity and dignity within a character and relate to it,” she says. “I often start with, how can I bring myself into this so that I can embody this person? And somehow through the course of playing a character, I find her within myself and I’m like, ‘Oh, actually I have this inside of me.’ So we sort of find each other.”

I’m curious what she thinks of the “she’s back,” Leightonaissance narrative that seems to emerge every time she’s cast in a new role. She says the warm reaction from fans is nice and she doesn’t have a negative association with it, but she also doesn’t really feel like she left. “I have done many shows and projects through the years, but I think maybe they just weren’t as visible,” she explains. “They weren’t seen or loved as much.” Even after having kids, she returned to work when her first child was 6 months old. “So, I didn’t take years off or anything,” she notes.

It’s all part of riding the waves of Hollywood. “Anything that I’ve done, I’ve definitely wanted it to be seen,” Meester says. “Like I’ve been on a couple shows that I was like, ‘Oh, I’m really proud of this. I want people to see it.’” She cites Making History, a time-travel comedy that aired on Fox in 2017 for one season, and Good Cop/Bad Cop, another comedy—this one about a family of detectives—that premiered on The CW in February but was canceled after an eight-episode run, as two projects she wishes would have gotten more attention.

I ask if being married to a fellow actor—Adam Brody, her husband of 12 years—helps her weather the ups and downs in the industry. “Yes, I think it does help, but I also feel like he’s the best person in the world, so no matter what job either of us did, I would think the world of him,” she gushes. “But I do think that having that in common is really nice. It gives you a shorthand of being able to talk about it and support each other, for inspiration and input, and just an ongoing dialogue about what it all means.” I tell her that, in a recent interview with ELLE, Brody was asked what he’s learned from being married for over a decade, and I’d like her to answer the same question. She says she’s been most surprised by how it just keeps getting better. “It’s amazing that when you marry someone, you’re so, so, so in love and you think of the future and you fantasize and talk about it and plan, but then you find yourself in the future and somehow you’re even more in love,” Meester says. “The intimacy and the love and the depth of the relationship is that much more, and you’re like, ‘I never thought that I could love you more.’ Life is filled with ups and downs and going through everything together, you get closer and it’s kind of hard to believe that’s possible.”

“I know that I felt this way when I met him, but even more so now, my life is better living it with him,” she continues. “And to be loved and understood by somebody, especially when you think the world of them, it’s kind of surreal and it’s the best thing in the world.” I mention that losing their home in the devastating Los Angeles wildfires in January 2025 was probably one of those hard things that made them stronger. “We are very, very lucky because we have each other and that’s the main thing,” she says. “Actually, that’s the only thing, full stop, that we all have each other. It’s just true that nothing else matters.”

When it comes to Meester’s enduring popularity, it’s not just Gossip Girl that holds lasting appeal. Her debut solo album, Heartstrings, released in 2014, is also beloved online. I ask her if she has plans to release more music. “I have been writing and actually recording music over the years. I took a beat last year, but I’d really like to wrap it up and put it out,” she says. “So, I do have new music to put out, but when that will be, I have no specifics.”

Before we part, I tell her I have to give the people what they want and ask whether she would ever reprise the role of Blair Waldorf. “I’ve been asked this exact question forever, but I never have a good answer because I just am always like, ‘I never say never because I don’t know,’” she says. “Obviously that character meant something to me. And I’m grateful that it meant something to other people. So you never know, but I really just don’t know.” She is aware that Cecily von Ziegesar, who wrote the Gossip Girl book series the show was based on, is currently writing a new standalone sequel novel called Blair; in fact, Meester says she has already read the first seven chapters. I say that perhaps it, too, will be adapted into a show, which would give her the chance to play Blair now. But all Meester does is smile.

Whatever role comes next, she seems content so long as they keep coming. “I’m sure that this is cliché at this point, but every time I finish a job, I don’t know what my next job is,” she says. “There’s nothing that’s guaranteed, so every time you get a role, you’re like, ‘I get to do this again. Here we go.’”

Every part is a chance to put herself in someone else’s shoes and see what happens. “I’m like, would I want to be a detective? No, but I would like to play one. Would I want to be a bodyguard? Probably not, I want to play one. And so it’s just truly playing make-believe all the time,” Meester says. “To be an actor is such a dream, and it’s never lost on me. I’m almost nostalgic about it because I can’t believe I still get to do this. Not to sound like a crazy person, but sometimes before I do a scene, I will tell myself, ‘This is my dream, and my life, in one way or another, has led to me being able to be there when they yell ‘action!’”
