Newcastle scrape past Millwall to extend Jaissle’s unbeaten start

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Joe Willock’s deft finish ensured Newcastle United survived a tough test against Millwall to keep their excellent run in the Carabao Cup intact.

This competition had been fruitful for Newcastle under former manager Eddie Howe and although this match was hard work – as well as hard to watch at times – his successor Matthias Jaissle guided them through a difficult trip to south-east London.

He called it “job done,” and that was pretty much all it was. “We needed patience on one hand, but also to be passionate and tough. We had the chance [to score] and took it,” he said.

Newcastle have reached the semi-finals of this competition three times in the last four years, but struggled to get any sort of tempo going against their Championship opponents.

You could argue the winning goal came against the run of play late in the second half, moments after Millwall substitute Taïryk Arconte had smashed a low shot against the post.

Given they were missing 13 players because of injury, this was a brave display from Alex Neil’s side. They frustrated Newcastle for more than an hour, nullifying their attack, to the point where Jaissle’s team looked as though they had run out of ideas.

That was until Lewis Hall combined with Matias Fernandez-Pardo down the left and his drilled cross found Willock who cleverly used the angle to pass the ball into the net. Willock has been rejuvenated under Jaissle. He looked certain to be sold this summer but is now starting regularly under a new manager in the number 10 role his system demands. It has been a nice redemption arc for the former Arsenal academy graduate.

Moments after Millwall hit the post, Joe Willock strikes to put Newcastle in front! ⚽️ pic.twitter.com/rSB8yV6NRO

The victory is all that matters and extends Jaissle’s unbeaten start to five games. Given he arrived just three weeks before the start of the season, that deserves praise even if it is obvious there is a lot of work to be done to improve their offensive play.

Playing in their lilac, or should that be Parma-violet, third kit for the first time, Newcastle looked off colour in the first half. Even though Jaissle named a strong team packed with experience of English cup ties, they struggled to overpower Millwall.

There were a few half-chances as Willock forced a save and Jacob Murphy fired over. There was a header missed from a corner by Sven Botman, which should have hit the target and Yoane Wissa also mistimed his jump after finding space for another header.

Millwall’s two centre-backs, Jake Cooper and Caleb Taylor were immense, flinging themselves in the way of every shot and clearing every loose ball that came into their vicinity. Given their injuries, this was a performance full of fight and defiance from the home side.

However, there was also an absence of quality in the final third from Newcastle, the same lack of cohesion and incision that was apparent at home to Bournemouth last weekend and in the first half away at Tottenham Hotspur.

It meant Millwall were happy to play for set-pieces close to the Newcastle box and Taylor got in front of his marker, flashing a header wide of the post.

That lacklustre first half meant Jaissle’s side had mustered just five shots on target in five halves of football since the 3-2 win over West Bromwich Albion in the previous round of the Carabao Cup.

Millwall started the second period like a side sensing Newcastle were vulnerable. A thunderbolt from Jenson Metcalfe, after the visitors had struggled to clear two crosses, almost gave them a spectacular lead.

Newcastle saw lots of the ball. Millwall were happy enough to let them have it given how little they were hurting them.

So much of Newcastle’s play was slow and cumbersome. Their set-piece delivery was also largely inept.

Neil’s side retained a threat and came so close to taking the lead when, not for the first time, a long ball forward caught the Newcastle defence out of shape and Tino Livramento’s mistake allowed Arconte to run clear and hit the post from a tight angle.

Well in, @Joewillock! 💪 pic.twitter.com/5a8G7lzD3H

Speaking after the game, Millwall manager Alex Neil said: “I’m very proud, I don’t think we deserved to lose that game.

“We had some good moments, and we got done by a bit of sucker punch at the end. But I’m very proud of the lads.”

As for their injury problems, he added: “I didn’t bring the teenagers on as a token gesture, I need to keep the first-team players fit. We’re really at bare bones.

But we beat Bolton at the weekend, got a couple of defeats before that but we started the season really well and once we get everybody back, we’ll be in good shape.”

With the first of the 16 teams through tonight, the draw won’t take place until most of the teams have played next week.

The draw will be shown on Sky Sports on Wednesday 16th Sept, after Manchester United play Brighton. Man City vs Norwich will be the only game outstanding then.

The third round had to be spread over two weeks as the nine Premier League sides in UEFA competitions entered the tournament in this round, as the same time as their Champions League and Europa League fixtures began. To avoid clubs facing clashes, the EFL split the fixtures across the two gameweeks.

Newcastle have named a strong side – probably the strongest they could field when factoring in senior players needing game time – to take on Millwall in the Carabao Cup.

Jacob Ramsey impressed coming off the bench against Bournemouth last weekend and manager Matthias Jaissle refused to sanction winger Jacob Murphy’s transfer to Nottingham Forest late in the window. Both deserve a chance to impress from the start.

Veteran centre back Fabian Schar comes in for Malick Thiaw, but those are the only three changes from the side that drew with Bournemouth.

Jaissle insisted he would take the competition seriously and the teamsheet proves it, but it also raises questions about some of Newcastle’s summer recruitment.

Bazoumana Toure cost £42m but was withdrawn at half time in the win over West Brom in the previous round, which means Harvey Barnes does not get a rest. That’s a lot of money to spend on a winger who has not looked Premier League ready so far.

Aladji Bamba, a £30m signing from Monaco, has not started a game yet and has been an unused substitute since the opening game against Liverpool. He remains on the bench.

Even Sean Steur, who has looked good since his summer move from Ajax, cannot dislodge another more recent arrival in former Manchester City midfielder Nico González.

Having made a total of eight new signings in a much-vaunted summer rebuild, Jaissle only started three of them against Bournemouth and only two start tonight at The Den.

Early days but this was one of the main reasons Eddie Howe quit – he did not think the likes of Toure, Steur and Bamba were ready to play first team football.

Welcome to tonight’s live coverage of Millwall vs Newcastle United, as the Geordies travel to south London for the third round of the Carabao Cup.

Newcastle held off West Brom in their second-round match, with Harvey Barnes scoring two second-half goals to see his side through. It was a mighty comeback, after Isaac Price scored early to stun the crowd at St James’ Park. Bazoumana Toure equalised for Matthias Jaissle’s side just before half-time, and Barnes, his replacement in the second half, put them ahead minutes after the restart. West Brom pulled it back to 2-2 with a penalty converted by Price after Lewis Hall was deemed to have handled the ball in the box, but Barnes got his second just nine minutes from full-time and the hosts were able to see out the match to progress.

Alex Neil’s Millwall have reached round three via a 0-1 win over Cambridge United, following a penalty shoot-out in the first round to beat QPR. They come into this tie with a squad stretched thin, as they have been hit with an injury crisis a month into the new season. Millwall have confirmed that they will be without Casper De Norre, Josh Coburn, Tristan Crama, Alfie Doughty, Mathis Servais, Ryan Leonard, Mihailo Ivanovic, Lukas Jensen and Luke Cundle.

Both sides sit seventh in their respective leagues, with Millwall on a record of three wins and two losses this season, while Newcastle remain unbeaten (two draws and a win).

Newcastle will hoping to progress smoothly in a tournament that they have recently enjoyed good form in – winners in 2025 and semi-finalists last season – and with Millwall dealing with a bare bones squad, the Championship side are the underdogs on paper going into this fixture.
