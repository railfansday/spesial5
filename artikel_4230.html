Mini Review: Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered (PS5) - Nostalgic Kaiju Brawls in This Fun But Meagre Package

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Godzilla is approaching the generator, the generator is losing power

Version Reviewed: PS5 (Standard) / European

Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee was a grand arena brawler in its day, especially if you consider yourself a fan of the king of the monsters. With a fun roster of characters, an impressive array of arenas, and solid gameplay, the title was a kaiju fan's dream.

That sentiment is still true with this remaster. Outside of a couple of small tweaks, the core gameplay that was such a success nearly 25 years ago is still intact.

Controlling Godzilla or any number of the other creatures from the big green lizard's rogues gallery is a blast, though you only get four characters to choose from at the start.

Gigan is especially fun, with light, nimble attacks. Some of the best kaiju are sadly absent, like Biollante. Hedorah was formerly present, but has been removed for the remaster, though mercifully Mothra remains.

Getting into a flow state with timing your attacks and watching large creatures go flying across a cityscape is immensely satisfying. The sound design is weighty — and authentic to the movies, featuring many iconic sound effects — and the wanton destruction the monsters leave in their wake has never looked better.

In fact, the game looks brilliant on all fronts. All the updates to the game's visuals are most welcome. Textures are crisp, environments pop, and the frame rate is rock solid.

The most notable change from the original probably comes by way of the unlock system, which, while not egregious, has been turned into a bit of a "games as a service" grind, though most everything is easy to get just from playing the game.

Whether or not yo'’ll stick around to unlock everything is another story, however. While the game is great fun, it's not terribly deep. The "story" mode can be run through in about 15 minutes if you take your time, and the other modes offer only fleeting enjoyment. Survival sees you fighting kaiju until you lose, and Destruction simply has you destroy buildings for a high score. There's just not enough substance here to keep you coming back in the long term.

The remaster ends up offering a brief jolt of fun, and while it may not keep you coming back to play it, the little time that you do spend with it will be memorable and enjoyable. And if you consider yourself a Godzilla fan, this is ultimately still a must-play.
