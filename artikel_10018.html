I have a small arts newsletter of about 400 people. Mailchimp was perfect because its free tier allowed me to send messages every couple months easily, and I couldn't justify a paid tier as it was overkill. Then they drastically lowered the max number of recipients and then forced plain text only emails on the free tier. I migrated my mailing list to sender.net and everything works perfectly over there, in fact their service seems much better than MC ever was.

Bending Spoons won't make it better. They're a horrible company to deal with.

"Bending Spoons will take something good and completely fork it up."

Bending Spoons ruins everything it touches. Any recommendations for alternatives?

Loved Evernote many many years ago, then tolerated it, then hated it, then switched to several others before settling on Notion's free tier, Obsidian, and some homebrewed solutions.

Never looked back, but I remember fondly the earliest days of Evernote. A good product.

Can't help but think Mail Chimp is going the same way.

Jacked up the prices, in my case 10 times, per year. Had a Pro subscription with two more people in a team, seeing very little use. We talk of around 100 gigabytes of video, a few dozens public links, mostly for videos on our intranet and sent to some customers to help their pets do physiotherapy home. In practice, very few views on these videos. In September they came out saying that based on their "Plan Usage Policy" we were qualified only to their new Studio Subscription, yada yada ya

My dad has an AOL email account with Bending Spoons. The reason he has it is because Verizon pawned off their email service to AOL years ago. AOL was then acquired by Bending Spoons.

For over a month he lost access to his email. Everything he had ran through that account. The problem occurred when one day, out of the blue, he was being told he needed to verify himself, something he hadn't needed to do in all the years he had the account. The problem was, there was nothing to verify against. He never gave them his phone number or anything else because it wasn't required when the move took place. When the screen came up and said select a verification option, there was nothing. It was a blank screen.

Three times I called support with him standing next to me so he could answer questions and three times all we were told is, "His account looks fine" followed by, "Every so often people need to verify who they are for account securtiy." That was the entirety of the phone calls. The one call was even followed by, "If you pay us X dollars a month we can provide extra support."

I then used their onlne "support" and laid out the problem. His problem was escalated to "Greg" who asked me to provide a screenshot of what we were seeing, which I did. No response. I followed up with two additonal emails and even opened a second ticket. Finally, "Greg" gets to back to me. "I have reviewed your father's account. Everything looks fine. Every so often people need to verify who they are for account security."

I then sent an email to their PR people right before their IPO and told them point blank, this needs to get resolved or my next step would be to notify the underwriters and tell them you were not forthcoming in your IPO documents, followed by every social media account I can lay my hands on. Surprisingly, five days later my dad had access to his account again. No emails, no follow up, he just had access.

Bending Spoons sucks. Dump their stock and if you use anything they own, find something else.

P.S. Mark Levin, a right-wing radio host, was recently locked out of his AOL account for 8 hours without explanation. Also, there are multiple comments on Reddit about people being locked out of their AOL account even though everything is set up correctly and not getting any useful response.

As for Mailchimp, those mass mailing services all need to die. Those services provide zero value to the recipients.
