Forget about Michael Carrick. Don’t dwell on Women’s Bigger Cup. Fret not about Pierre Sage. No, the big football story of the day is Big Website’s Ultimate 1-50, a rundown of the male footballers best associated with each squad number. It’s lighthearted reading designed to entertain, maybe even inform, and perhaps spark a few memories and a touch of debate. Or, if you prefer, a rage-bait waste of time that shames the internet. Where the hell is Roy Keane! How dare you leave out Bobby Moore! And Pelé? A list without Pelé? I don’t want to live in a world where there are lists without Pelé. Such is the world these days, though surely a certain amount of respect is due to an exercise that shuns Lionel Messi and Zinedine Zidane but can welcome in Salvatore Sirigu and Mateusz Klich. And true, it might be a challenging read for those (and, let’s be clear, we’re absolutely definitely not referring to ourselves here, no sirree) old enough to have been brought up in an era of 1; 2, 5, 6, 3; 7, 4, 8, 11; 9 (big man), 10 (little man). The days of two substitutes who wore Nos 12 and 14. The days of a quarter of wine gums in your pocket and misty rain drifting across the floodlights and … oh my god Asamoah Gyan is wearing No 3 and playing up front. This is not a drill. Repeat this is not a drill. Burn it all down, the game’s gone. There is something about it all that does invoke strangely strong reactions, but every day is a school day. So, yes, there remains a hallowed aura around 1-11 but even some of the game’s greats – we’re looking at you Gerd Müller and Johan Cruyff – are best associated with numbers outside of the traditional undectet. And, as Jonathan Wilson brilliantly illustrates here, there’s a risk of being a bit Eurocentric when it comes to numbering – Argentina’s tactical evolution, for example, meant their traditional back four lines up 4, 2, 6, 3, with the No 5 in central midfield. Brazil too has No 5 as the defensive midfielder, with the standard back four as 4, 2, 3, 6. And don’t get us started on Hungary at Wembley in 1953 – Nandor Hidegkuti, you rascal! So do yourself a favour and dig into today’s offering. You might learn something. Or you might just get really, really cross about Curtis Jones. Either way, it’s win-win.
