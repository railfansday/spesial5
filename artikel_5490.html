In Portland, OR, Entry-Level Buyers Suddenly Have More Options

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

A new metro-by-metro analysis from Realtor.com® reveals where buyers can still find a starter home. These options have felt increasingly scarce as rising prices and climbing mortgage rates create challenges for many would-be buyers.

According to research, the national share of starter homes in active inventory fell from 38.1% in August 2019 to 36.2% in August 2026. During that same period, starter home prices rose 30.8%, from about $260,000 to $340,000.

However, some cities are bucking this trend, and Portland, OR, has seen an encouraging uptick in starter homes in recent years.

A starter home is defined as a property priced at roughly 80% of a metro's median list price, capturing the relative affordability of an area's lower-end market. These homes are typically smaller and less expensive, making them accessible to a first-time buyer or a household with an Income at or below the local median.

In the Portland-Vancouver, OR-WA, metro area, the share of starter homes has shown positive growth. The share was 28.0% in 2019 and dipped slightly to 27.8% in 2022 before rising to 32.0% in 2026.

This represents a total increase of +4.0 percentage points since 2019, signaling growing opportunity for buyers in the market.

For those deciding between renting or buying, current data suggests renting is the more affordable path for now. A July Rent Report found that the cost of buying a starter home in the 50 largest metros was $858 higher per month than renting one. This financial advantage to renting holds true across all 50 of the largest metros.

In the Portland-Vancouver, OR-WA, market, the median rent cost was $1,606 as of July 2026.

This figure represents a 1.60% decrease year-over-year, making an apartment an even more financially sound choice while saving for a down payment. Prospective buyers can use a rent vs. buy calculator to weigh their options.
