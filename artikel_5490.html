The 2026 Manhattan Wealth Rush: Ultraluxury Real Estate Is Smashing Sales Records

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The Manhattan luxury market has had a banner year despite the pied-à-terre tax and the recent stock market correction.

Trophy properties—those priced $10 million and above—made up half the contracts signed last week, leading the luxury market into the second biggest year in two decades, according to Olshan Realty.

As of Nov. 22, an impressive 294 contracts were signed at $10 million and above, not far off the peak year of 2021, when 400 were signed. The average asking price for last week was $10.5 million, and properties were moving with a fairly low 5% average discount.

Corcoran's Deborah Grubman, who co-listed last week's most expensive sale—a $28.75 million triplex in the Pritzker Prize–winning Richard Meier building in the West Village—says that the luxury market normally shows signs of slowing down around the Thanksgiving holiday. But not this year.

"New York is still a very desirable destination for people of wealth," she tells Realtor.com®.

The glass triplex at 176 Perry St.—one of the city's most coveted blocks—was owned by Broadway and movie star Hugh Jackman. The condo, built in 2001, had been on and off the market since June 2022, and at one point was priced at $39 million.

Jackman and his ex-wife, Deborra-Lee Furness, purchased the 11,000-square-foot minimalist abode for $21 million in 2008. Spanning the eighth, ninth and 10th floors, it is "one of the most dramatic apartments in Manhattan," the listing states.

The full-service condominium comes with sweeping views of the river and Hudson River Park and is within walking distance of The High Line, Little Island, the Meatpacking District, Bleecker Steet, and the Whitney Museum. Jean-Georges Vongerichten's Perry Street Restaurant is on its ground floor.

The Richard Meier building, one of the first ultraluxe buildings that far west, helped change the face of the once-gritty area.

"It was the first 'star-chitect' building in the West Village on the waterfront," says the broker. "It was groundbreaking in terms of luxury. At the time, people said, 'Who is going to live in the highway?' It predated all that green space."

While it took Jackman about five years to unload the property, Grubman says that the West Village—and Perry Street, which stretches from Greenwich Avenue to the river—remains in high demand.

"It's a street that's got lots of charm and history," she says. "The West Village is out of central casting. You still have the charm of the small townhouses and tree-lined streets.

"The only thing that's really changed a lot is the price point."

Michelle Griffith of Douglas Elliman represented the buyer of one of the year's top sales: a $16.5 million, 3,855-square-foot condo in the Waldorf Astoria Residences at 301 Park Ave. in Midtown.

The luxury broker says her trophy property sales for 2026 are on track to beat her previous best year, which was 2021.

That year, when 400 ultraluxe contracts were signed, those who weren't fleeing lockdowns were shelling out for multimillion-dollar properties, spurred on by historically low interest rates in the 2% to 4% range.

Today, with higher rates hovering around 7%, there are plenty of cash-flush buyers snapping up ultraluxe homes. (Some still prefer 7% mortgage rates to pulling their money out of more lucrative investments.)

"The phrase that sums up 2026 for me is 'bidding wars,'" the broker tells Realtor.com. "This year will go down as my best year."

She attributes the surging high-end market to a couple of factors: tight inventory pushing up prices and sparking intense competition, and the "insanely high and insanely competitive" rental market, with rents double or triple what they were just a few years ago.

According to Realtor.com, a three-bedroom-plus in Manhattan has a median monthly rent of $5,375, an increase of 35.6% over the past seven years, requiring a household to earn $215,000 a year just to stay afloat with rent.

However, those who want a luxury three-bedroom-plus abode in a trendy neighborhood can shell out significantly more. Griffith says that a few years ago, a large luxury dwelling in an area like Tribeca could cost around $19,000 a month. But now?

"It would work itself up close to $30,000," she says. "So those people are saying, 'Do I want to spend $30,000 a month or should I pivot and think about buying something? How much am I going to overspend before I say, 'OK, let me buy'?"

Combine stratospheric rents with "inventory levels down across the board," and prices have skyrocketed.

"If you have something good in the West Village, in Tribeca, on the Upper East Side, it is likely going to trade itself up in a bidding war," says the agent.

When dream turnkey apartments began to disappear from listings, buyers became more willing to take on projects, resulting in a stronger-than-normal luxury co-op market. In general, co-ops are much older—and cheaper—than condos.

While the week ending Nov. 22 saw luxury condos outpace co-ops 18-4, there have been several weeks during 2026 when that gap was considerably narrower—and one week in June when co-ops won out.

"These are places that might require several million dollars to renovate," says Griffith. "And now that market too is getting absorbed."

She says those who didn't leave the city during the COVID-19 pandemic—fleeing to Westchester, Connecticut, or Florida—are the die-hards who are committed to the city long term. And they are the ones out there bidding up and buying.

Especially relevant are families whose children are deep in the school system.

"They're putting down roots for a minimum of 10 to 15 years," she says.

Despite her banner year, Griffith says she isn't planning a celebration just yet. Next year could be a different story as the pied-à-terre surcharge ramps up to replace Department of Finance valuations with significantly higher "fair market" valuations for 2029.

"This year was an emotional journey," she says. "There were many bidding wars we won, but also many we lost. I'm the type who focuses more on, 'What are the deals that we lost?'"

The second-highest contract of the week was a $25 million sponsor duplex penthouse at 133 E. 73rd St. Listing agency Modlin Group did not return a request for more information.
