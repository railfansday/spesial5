Norton Holiday Report 2026: 1 in 6 Americans Fell Victim to Online Shopping Scams

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Survey finds 35% of Americans bought from a suspected scammer because the deal was too good to pass up, while AI shopping assistants steer more shoppers to unfamiliar retailers

TEMPE, Ariz., Nov. 5, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- According to the 2026 Norton Insights Report: Holiday Scams, nearly half of Americans (48%) are more worried about online scams this holiday season than last. And for good reason: 30% of Americans say they have been targeted by a scam while holiday shopping online, and more than half of them (54%) fell for it.

Americans willing to gamble safety for deals

Nearly three in four Americans (72%) say they're swayed by deals during the holiday season, even though 45% suspect those deals might be scams. Of those surveyed, 65% would buy from a website they'd never heard of if the price is right, and 36% would risk money on a purchase from a seller they suspect is a scam.

"Scammers are counting on holiday shoppers being rushed, distracted and afraid of missing out," said Leyla Bilge, Global Head of Scam Research for Norton. "Imagine seeing a designer handbag advertised for 70% off. The website looks real, the deal ends in minutes, and something tells you it might be a scam. But what if it's legitimate? That moment of hesitation is exactly what scammers exploit. They create a sense of urgency so the fear of missing a bargain outweighs the fear of losing money."

Social media is the No. 1 source of holiday shopping scams, Gen Z hit hardest

Among US holiday scam victims, 47% say they were scammed on a social platform with the top holiday scams being online shopping scams (32%).

One in four Gen Z Americans (25%) has been scammed while holiday shopping online, five times more than Baby Boomers (5%). Gen Z is also the most likely to take more risks during the holidays (45%) and to have bought from a seller they suspected was a scam (49%). They know the risk: 57% of Gen Z are more concerned about scams this year than last.

As the 2026 holiday shopping season gets underway, fake online stores are proliferating at an alarming rate. New data from Gen Threat Labs reveals a 129% increase in fake shop activity compared to the start of last year's holiday season, with the Gen family of brands, including Norton, blocking nearly 25 million attempted visits to fraudulent shopping sites in the last month alone. The latest surge builds on an already troubling trend, with fake e-shops doubling during the 2025 holiday season compared to 2024. And with increasingly sophisticated AI tools making it easier than ever for scammers to create convincing storefronts, holiday shoppers face a growing threat.

AI is changing online shopping habits, but not always protecting people from scams

Americans are increasingly using AI for their holiday shopping despite not having full confidence in the tools to help them avoid malicious sites.  While 83% say they would use an AIassistant to shop, only 42% of them are confident it would recognize and avoid a fraudulentwebsite. Sixty-three percent have used an AI shopping assistant or agent, and 42% plan to use a tool like ChatGPT for holiday shopping this year.

Gen Threat Labs is already seeing examples of AI assistants failing to recognize scams. In one case, a shopper asked an AI assistant to find affordable alternatives to a designer leather jacket. Among the recommendations was a $279 jacket from a fraudulent online store, presented alongside legitimate retailers without any warning. The example highlights a growing risk: shoppers may trust a recommendation because it comes from AI, even when the website behind it cannot be trusted.

The full 2026 Norton Insights Report: Holiday Scams is available at us.norton.com/blog/research/holiday-scams-insights-report-2026.

The US survey was conducted online by Dynata on behalf of Gen from Sept. 10 to Sept. 21, 2026, among 1,000 US adults ages 18 and older. Data are weighted by age, gender and region to be nationally representative.
