With Grand Theft Auto VI set to finally launch tomorrow, November 19th, our long-awaited return to Vice City is nearly here. But after all this time, some of the gear you’ve used in San Andreas might feel out of place on the glamorous streets of Leonidas County. Luckily for you, we’ve gathered a few of our favorite Xbox and PlayStation 5 accessories to help your long gaming sessions run as smoothly as possible.

The 8BitDo Ultimate 3E — an upgraded pick for Xbox Series X and Series S consoles — is a very capable, comfortable controller with elite-level features, including a high polling rate and adjustable triggers. It’s on the expensive side at $149.99, but the customization and clean execution justify the cost. Read our review.

PlayStation 5 owners looking for a controller upgrade, meanwhile, might consider the DualSense Edge, Sony’s first-party pro-style controller. In addition to a variety of extra function buttons and paddles, the wireless gamepad sports adjustable trigger heights and swappable joysticks (in case the potentiometers start to drift). It’s a little bit heavier than Sony’s regular DualSense, and the battery life isn’t quite as good, but it offers customization you won’t find on the console’s default controller. Read our review.

Sony’s pro-style controller, the DualSense Edge, might suffer from shorter battery life than the standard DualSense model, but it features great software integration with the PlayStation 5 and plenty of customizable components.

Nothing’s worse than having to pause a heist so you can go hunt down a fresh set of AA batteries. The PowerA Duo Charging Station includes a rechargeable battery and replacement rear doors for two Xbox Series X / S or Xbox One controllers. Simply set them on the dock, and they’ll start charging.

PowerA’s PlayStation equivalent charges two DualSense Wireless Controllers simultaneously, with an included AC adapter, and comes with a two-year warranty.

The 2026 Roku Pro Series is the brand’s first OLED, and the 55-inch version retails for $999.99, which is on the low end of the price spectrum for OLED. Games feel richer and more immersive on OLED panels, thanks to their deeper black levels, which are the result of unlit pixels or sections of the screen. Each Pro Series panel also sports a 120Hz refresh rate, four HDMI 2.1 ports, and both low latency and variable refresh rates for gaming.

For a more premium offering, the Sony Bravia 8 II is one of our favorite screens for movies, with an engaging, immersive picture and excellent image processing. It has great gaming features, too, like a 120Hz refresh rate with variable refresh and an automatic low-latency mode. It’s not cheap, but the OLED makes for an excellent upgrade to your setup. Read our review.

Sony’s latest first-party headset, the $99 PlayStation Pulse Wireless, launches the same day as GTA VI. My colleague Cameron Faulkner recently went hands-on with the wireless headset ahead of its launch on November 19th, and came away impressed with its overall audio quality and comfort. The newest Pulse is also straightforward to set up and use, especially when paired with devices like the PlayStation Portal that support PlayStation Link, Sony’s proprietary low-latency audio connection. It also offers Bluetooth for use with other devices.

For Xbox gamers, it’s hard to beat the SteelSeries Arctis Nova 3X, which retails in multiple colors for $109.99 or less. The wireless headset is lightweight, comfortable, and compatible with a wide variety of other consoles and mobile devices as well, for those rare moments when you aren’t playing GTA VI.

If you’d rather the simplicity of a wired option, Sony’s InZone H6 Air skips the batteries and dongles, instead opting to plug straight into your controller. This open-back gaming headset is lightweight and comfortable, which is great for long hours and late nights. My colleague Cameron Faulkner described its sound as “spectacular,” with several equalizer options available for helping fine-tune the sound to your liking. Read our review.

Ikea’s Poäng chair balances affordability and comfort, with wide, supportive armrests that are just the right height for holding a controller. It comes in a variety of frame materials and cushion colors, too, making it easy to match your other furniture or console. This is my personal chair for console gaming, and I recently added a matching ottoman to the set for maximum relaxation.
