Wrexham should revisit Pavel Sulc situation after latest Lyon revelation: Opinion

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Wrexham have had a very poor start to the season considering their finish to last year. However, they are a couple of strong January additions away from pushing themselves up towards the Championship play-off picture. One target could be a returning bid for Lyon’s Pavel Sulc.

Phil Parkinson’s side reportedly made the highest offer for the Lyon attacker in the summer of 2026, with their bid understood to be worth approximately £35m. However, Sulc rejected the opportunity, admitting he was not attracted by the prospect of playing in the Championship or moving to a small town in Wales.

That rejection appeared to end Wrexham’s hopes, but Lyon’s financial difficulties have created a potential second opportunity. The French club must sell players during the January window to satisfy financial commitments, and Sulc has emerged as one of their leading candidates for departure.

The 25-year-old has established himself as an exciting attacking talent since joining Lyon from Viktoria Plzen for an initial €7.5m in August 2025. Under contract until June 2029, he can operate as an attacking midfielder, striker or left winger, offering considerable versatility.

His underlying numbers also demonstrate why Wrexham were prepared to spend so heavily. He has 11 goals and three assists in 27 Ligue 1 appearances during his first season at Lyon.

He has also made an encouraging start to 2026/27, registering one goal and three assists in five league appearances, alongside another goal in the Europa League.

His creativity is particularly appealing. Sulc recorded eight chances created in just 417 Ligue 1 minutes this season, while his ability to contribute goals from several attacking positions would address one of Wrexham’s biggest weaknesses.

Parkinson’s side have struggled for attacking consistency, with injuries to Sam Smith, Josh Windass, Nathan Broadhead and Callum O’Hare leaving them short of creativity and firepower. Sitting around 15th in the Championship, they need reinforcements if their promotion ambitions are to remain realistic.

There are, however, two significant complications. Sulc underwent surgery on a meniscus injury in October, with his return potentially falling anywhere between January and March. Lyon’s reduced negotiating position could also make a deal more affordable, although his injury represents a genuine availability risk.

His previous comments about the Championship and Wrexham’s location cannot be ignored either. Nevertheless, the club’s continued investment and ambitions could offer a different proposition several months later.

Wrexham should not assume Sulc has changed his mind, but the sporting circumstances and Lyon’s financial position have shifted. Given their need for attacking quality, reopening the conversation would certainly be worthwhile.
