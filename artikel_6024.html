Norse’s cash crisis extends far beyond its own operations, forcing international airline and cruise partners to reassess their flying plans.

Norse Atlantic Airways formally suspended all of its flights Thursday and warned that its board intends to file for bankruptcy unless it can urgently secure fresh funding. The development comes a day after the Norway-based low-cost, long-haul carrier described its financial situation as "critical."

In a stock exchange filing on Thursday, Norse confirmed it was in discussions with investors over a possible takeover that would include a financing solution. The company also held a meeting with holders of its $52 million senior secured bonds on Wednesday. “If a short-term financing solution is not secured urgently, it is the Board's intention to file for bankruptcy,” Norse said.

The company cautioned that there was no guarantee a viable solution would be found. Norse apologized to passengers for the disruption but gave no details on refunds or alternative travel arrangements.

Norse Atlantic Airways suspended all flights Thursday and said its board intends to file for bankruptcy unless it secures urgent funding, a day after describing its financial position as "critical." The Norway-based low-cost, long-haul carrier said it is in discussions with investors over a possible takeover that would include a financing solution, and met Wednesday with holders of its $52 million senior secured bonds. Shares closed down 85% Wednesday, and the airline has lost 99% of its market value since the start of the year. CEO Eivind Roald told Finansavisen that all aircraft had been returned to bases in Norway, Sweden, and Britain. The suspension immediately hit partners: Pakistan International Airlines canceled London–Islamabad and Manchester–Lahore flights, citing an "unexpected operational suspension" by its wet-lease partner, and said it would deploy its own Boeing 777 fleet on UK routes from Thursday. P&O Cruises told Skift it was exploring alternative options but that its Caribbean fly-cruise program is unaffected for now. Norse began a formal strategic process in July that could result in a sale, merger, or partnership, and a June rights issue, the August bond financing, and a cost-cutting program called Project Falcon all proved insufficient. The company said it has been particularly exposed to the surge in jet fuel prices following the outbreak of the U.S.-Iran war in February.
