California subpoenas Nvidia over whether chips or secrets reached China

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The subpoena follows former chief executive Jensen Huang’s affair with an alleged Chinese spy. Rob Bonta’s office will use the answers to decide whether Nvidia broke California law.

Nvidia’s Voyager building at its headquarters in Santa Clara, California

California Attorney General Rob Bonta has served Nvidia with an investigative subpoena. His office wants to know whether the company’s advanced chips or confidential information may have gone to China. It is also asking whether Nvidia misled investors and the public, the California Department of Justice said in a statement on Thursday.

“The conduct of Nvidia and its former CEO raises bright red flags, including about the ways in which the company may have misled investors,” Bonta said. “Nvidia is in a messy, transnational situation but the underlying principle of our corporate securities law is clear: Companies cannot mislead their investors.”

The department said the subpoena relates to former chief executive Jensen Huang’s affair with an alleged Chinese spy. The answers will help it decide whether Nvidia broke California law.

Two days earlier, Bonta told Bloomberg Television the situation was “very possibly” something his office could examine, Bloomberg reported. He said his office first needed a California law to give it jurisdiction.

Huang resigned as chief executive last week after revelations of extramarital affairs, Reuters reported. One was with Chinese businesswoman Alice Huang. US intelligence agencies are trying to establish her ties to China’s Ministry of State Security, according to Reuters. The two are not related, and China rejects the espionage allegations.

Nvidia’s board has ruled out his return and set up a national security committee. Huang has agreed to appear before House committees on 1 December.

Also on Thursday, the House Foreign Affairs Committee said the Trump administration had approved nearly 900,000 advanced Nvidia and AMD AI chips for Chinese companies, Reuters reported. The approvals ran from December 2025 to May, and it is unclear how many chips shipped. Nvidia told Reuters the approvals had strict conditions and limited volumes. It said it had stopped shipping H200 chips to China.

Bonta has taken on other technology companies this year. His office sued OpenAI earlier this month.
