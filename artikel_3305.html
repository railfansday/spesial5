‘Once Upon A Time In The Middle East’ Takes Snow Leopard For Best Film At Asian World Film Festival

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

EXCLUSIVE: Chinese drama Once Upon A Time In The Middle East, directed by Wen Muye, walked away with the top award, the Snow Leopard for Best Film, at this year’s Asian World Film Festival (AWFF) in Culver City, Los Angeles.

The film, which is China’s submission for the Best International Feature category of the Oscars, also scooped Best Actor for Shen Tang’s performance. Set in a fictional Middle East city, it tells the story of a Chinese chef whose restaurant becomes a refuge when war breaks out.

Previous winners of the Best Film Snow Leopard have included Lebanon’s A Sad And Beautiful World in 2025 and Malaysia’s Abang Adik the previous year. Best Actress in the Snow Leopard competition went to Nazifa Tushi for her role in Bangladesh drama Roid, directed by Mejbaur Rahman Sumon, which also won acclaim when it premiered at this year’s Rotterdam film festival. Watch on Deadline

Iraqi action thriller No Paradise If You Are Killed By A Woman, directed by Halkawt Mustafa, took home the Snow Leopard Special Jury Prize.

Israeli writer-director Moshe Rosenthal was presented with the Snow Leopard Jury Discretionary Award for Best Screenplay for Tell Me Everything. The family drama, which premiered at Sundance film festival, revolves around a 12-year-old boy who discovers his father is having a relationship with another man.

U.S. audiences honored Poh Si Teng’s documentary American Doctor, a co-production between the U.S., Palestine, Malaysia and Qatar, about three American physicians who worked out of a Gaza hospital before and after the 2025 ceasefire. Dr. Rizk Abu Shaaira, a Palestinian surgeon featured in the film, was reportedly killed in a Gaza hospital strike last week.

In the festival’s Asia Vision section, the top award was presented to fact-based Kyrgyz drama Kurak, co-directed by Erke Dzhumakmatova and Emil Atageldijev.

Lebanese filmmaker Samah El Kadi’s coming-of-age story Baadarane won the Best Short Film Award, which came with a $3,000 WDA Entertainment studio credit. Two additional shorts – Nava, directed by Dena Rassam, and Touch, from Mohammad Davoodi Kia – both Iranian films, received special mentions.

Armenian-U.S. producer Arthur M. Sarkissian headed the feature film jury, while French-Chinese producer Celine Zen served as short film jury president.

Among the stars and executives who walked the red carpet were breakout actress Mason Reeves (Josephine); Kyrgyz director Artykpai Suyundukov; veteran actor John Savage (The Deer Hunter); actress Irene Tsu (Down & Out in Beverly Hills); writer-director Moshe Rosenthal; actress, stunt performer and filmmaker Diana Lee Inosanto (The Mandalorian); actor and stunt performer Simon Rhee (Mission: Impossible III); actor Mark Irvingsen (Finding Normal); and producer Andre Morgan (Walker, Texas Ranger).

The gala celebration was emceed by Mico Saad, TV host, actor and filmmaker, and Maria Shantanova, actress and model.

AWFF’s closing night gala capped a week of around 60 screenings from more than 33 Asian nations and autonomous regions, along with filmmaker Q&As. The line-up included 27 official submissions for the Best International Feature Film category of the Oscars.

This year, AWFF also presented its first-ever official Focus On Kazakhstan program, featuring the world premiere of The Nomad’s Legacy, an action thriller written and directed by Marina Kunarova, and the U.S. premiere of Elzat Eskendir’s Abel, which is Kazakhstan’s official Oscars submission.

Shannon Lee, chairperson of the Bruce Lee Foundation, presented the Bruce Lee Award to Diana Lee Inosanto. Mason Reeves was presented with the Rising Star Award. Other honorees included Kyrgyz director Artykpai Suyundukov, who received the Lifetime Achievement Award, and Chinese-U.S. actress Irene Tsu who received an award for Outstanding Cinematic Achievement.

“We founded AWFF because we wanted audiences to share in the excitement we felt about films from across Asia and the Asian diaspora,” said AWFF executive director Georges N. Chamchoum.

“Watching this year’s lineup, we felt that excitement all over again. Asian artists continue to fuel the stories, the energy and imagination of world cinema, and it was a joy to close the week by honoring the very best of their work.”

Best Film: Once Upon A Time In The Middle East (China); Wen Muye, director

Best Actress: Nazifa Tushi, Roid (Bangladesh)

Best Actor: Shen Teng, Once Upon A Time In The Middle East (China)

Special Jury Prize: No Paradise If You Are Killed By A Woman (Iraq); Halkawt Mustafa, director

Jury Discretionary Award: Best Screenplay, Tell Me Everything (Israel); Moshe Rosenthal, writer-director

Audience Award: American Doctor (US, Palestine, Malaysia, Qatar); Poh Si Teng, director

Asian Vision Award: Kurak (Kyrgyzstan); Emil Atageldijev & Erke Dzhumakmatova, co-directors

Best Short Film: Baadarane (Lebanon); Samah El Kadi, director

Special Mention: Nava (Iran); Dena Rassam, director

Special Mention: Touch (Iran); Mohammad Davoodi Kia, director

Lifetime Achievement Award: Artykpai Suyundukov, Kyrgyz director; presented by Andre Morgan, producer

Outstanding Cinematic Achievement (Female): Irene Tsu, Chinese-American actress; presented by John Savage, actor

Rising Star: Mason Reeves, actress; presented by Diana Lee Inosanto, Filipino-American actress, director, writer, stunt coordinator & stunt performer

Bruce Lee Award: Diana Lee Inosanto, Filipino-American actress, director, writer, stunt coordinator and stunt performer; presented by Shannon Lee, chairperson, Bruce Lee Foundation
