Blackburn boss Mowbray steps back over health issues

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Tony Mowbray's last game in charge of Blackburn Rovers was an impressive 2-1 win over West Ham United before November's international break

Blackburn Rovers head coach Tony Mowbray has stepped back from his role with the Championship club amid health issues.

The 62-year-old returned for a second spell in June having previously spent five years managing Rovers between 2017 and 2022.

Mowbray had previously taken time out of the game during his time as Birmingham City boss in early 2024 following a health scare, and later revealed he had been undergoing treatment for bowel cancer.

Rovers said Damien Johnson and Mike Dodds would take responsibility for first-team duties "for the time being".

BBC Sport was initially told Mowbray had "stepped down" but we have since been updated that he has "stepped back". No further details have been given although the club are not thought to be looking for a successor.

"Unfortunately, due to health issues, I have to step back from my role as head coach of Blackburn Rovers," Mowbray said in a statement., external

"I'm immensely proud of this group of players. Their dedication and professionalism have given us some memorable performances and wins, and I've loved working with them.

"Thank you to my coaching staff for all your hard work and for the support you've given me. It's been a pleasure working alongside you.

"To the fans, thank you for the way you've supported me and the team this season. I've always tried to give you a team that you can be proud of. Your support has meant a great deal to me."

Mowbray has transformed Blackburn this season, after they hovered around the relegation places for much of last term before eventually finishing 20th in the Championship and five points above the drop zone.

They are currently 13th in the Championship table having won six of their 15 league games and three points outside the play-offs.

The announcement comes before Sunday's Lancashire derby against Burnley at Turf Moor, which was set to be Mowbray's 900th game in management as well as his 63rd birthday.

Mowbray has been in management for over two decades and his first spell at Blackburn ended at the conclusion of the 2021-22 season when he was eventually replaced by Jon Dahl Tomasson.

He subsequently held roles with Sunderland, Birmingham and a second spell with West Bromwich Albion before his return to Ewood Park.

In his first spell at Blackburn, he could not halt the club's relegation from the Championship, yet he led them to promotion back from League One at the first attempt and subsequently re-established them in the second tier.

In February 2024, he had been diagnosed with cancer but was eventually given the all-clear following treatment.

He leaves with Blackburn in better shape than they were during a tumultuous 2025-26 campaign, which finished with Michael O'Neill opting not to take the manager's job on a full-time basis and instead choosing to remain as boss of Northern Ireland.

"Tony's passion for the club and his professionalism are clear to see, but it is his kindness and the care he shows for players, staff and those around him that mean so much," Blackburn chief executive officer Steve Curwood added.

"Our priority now is to make sure he feels that same care and support from all of us. We will support him and his family in every way we can."

Tony Mowbray won seven of his 17 matches (D3 L7) in charge of Blackburn in all competitions in the 2026-27 campaign

Mowbray's decision was communicated to the club's playing and coaching staff at the Brockhall training ground on Tuesday morning by chief executive Curwood and chief operating officer Suhail Shaikh.

Staff based at Ewood Park, as well as those working remotely and at other club sites, were also informed before the news was made public.

The announcement was met with understandable concern from players, who are supportive of Mowbray's decision.

A number have since paid tribute to the 62-year-old on social media, thanking him for his support and guidance, with some having worked under him during two separate spells at the club.

Rovers remain in close contact with Mowbray and his family and continue to offer their full support.
