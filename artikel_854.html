Premier League clubs vote through new rules to increase legal protection for board

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Individual indemnity extended for potential lawsuits

Key figures may be named in action over Manchester City

Clubs rallied behind the Premier League’s leadership in a key vote on Thursday, overwhelmingly backing new rules that would protect the chief executive, Richard Masters, from potentially damaging lawsuits.

A vote to expand the legal indemnity afforded to Premier League directors was won 18-2 at a shareholders’ meeting in central London, granting Masters and other figures greater protection in the event they were subjected to legal action as a consequence of doing their job.

Manchester City are understood to be one of two clubs to vote against the extension of indemnity, and one unnamed side spoke out at length against the measures during the meeting. The other vote against was by Nottingham Forest, who felt insufficient time had been granted to consider the proposal.

The rule change follows reports that the organisation’s leadership may be named in possible legal action by City’s Abu Dhabi sponsors, who have claimed their reputations were damaged as a result of the excoriating verdict of an independent commission regarding City’s breaches of the league’s financial rules. City have appealed against the verdict.

This week the Abu Dhabi telecommunications giant and City sponsor Etisalat released a statement describing the contents of the verdict as “entirely false and defamatory”. Referring to the independent commission’s report as “the published findings of the English Premier League”, Etisalat said it was “actively considering all options relating to its rights and associated legal remedies in this matter”.

The details of any increase of indemnity, including who would cover any potential costs, were not discussed in the meeting.

The chief refereeing officer of Pro Ref, Howard Webb, attended and came under sustained questioning from club executives over recent referee and video assistant referee failures.

A report by the independent key match incidents panel this week found there had been two serious mistakes that were not corrected by the VAR during the most recent round of matches. Webb is understood to have acknowledged that performance in matchday 10 had been “poor” but argued that apart from that weekend, key metrics on decision-making had been trending in the right direction this season.

The Premier League has confirmed next season will begin on Friday 13 August and finish on 21 May. It said players would have 75 “clear days” of break at the end of the current season.
