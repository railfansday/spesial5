Upwind Unveils Upwind Response to Stop AI-Driven Cloud Attacks Within Seconds

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Upwind, the Cloud and AI security platform, has announced Upwind Response, a new capability that the company says stops AI-driven attacks within seconds of detection and keeps attackers from moving deeper into a customer's environment. According to Upwind, the company introduced Response on November 12, 2026, and it is available now to all Upwind customers.

Response is built for speed. When Upwind sees an attacker attempting to exploit a cloud application, Response cuts off that attacker's access to the targeted service and halts the kill chain. It does this without waiting for a person to step in. Security teams still set the rules: they decide which threats Response mitigates on its own and which ones require their approval. Every action Response takes is temporary and can be rolled back.

The timeline for exploitation has collapsed. Nearly one in four known exploited vulnerabilities (KEVs) identified in the first half of 2026 showed evidence of exploitation on or before the day its CVE was published. For defenders, that leaves little or no head start. A flaw can be in active use before there is even a public identifier to track it, and well before a patch reaches production systems.

Defenders, in many cases, are starting the race after it has already begun.

AI is a major factor in that acceleration. AI agents can probe an environment from hundreds of angles, chain dozens of small weaknesses into a breach, and keep working around the clock until they succeed. JPMorgan Chase described the imbalance in a recent post on AI and security, writing that "vulnerability discovery and attack automation are moving at warp speed. Remediation is not."

Upwind's position is that finding risks and detecting attacks remain the foundation of cloud security. The challenge lies in what comes after. When a new flaw can be used within hours, a scan-and-patch cycle cannot close the window quickly enough. Meanwhile, backlogs of risky settings and unused permissions keep growing, and each one represents a possible way in.

That gap between finding a problem and fixing it is where Response is designed to operate.

Automatic remediation has been considered as an answer for some time. Many security teams, however, have resisted it. Their concern has been that false positives or over-reaching responses would lead to ongoing problems, with automated tools disrupting the very operations they are supposed to protect.

That hesitation has left many organizations relying on people to act on every alert, even as attackers have automated more and more of their own work.

Upwind designed Response to address that concern directly. It unifies signals across cloud, runtime, code, and identity to understand the full picture of a threat before it acts. That context is what allows Response to separate an actual attack from activity that only looks suspicious.

When Response spots an attacker carrying out a malicious activity, such as an attempt to exploit a vulnerability or take remote control of a server, it blocks the attacker's access point from reaching the specific service under attack. The block works at the cloud network level and takes effect in seconds. Normal customer traffic keeps flowing, because the action targets the attacker and the affected service rather than the application as a whole.

Response escalates only as far as the situation requires. If an attacker gets further, it can terminate a specific malicious process and keep that process from starting again. This avoids large disruptions to regular operations while removing the attacker's foothold.

Response can also limit what a compromised machine can reach. It does this by trimming that machine's cloud identity so it loses access to object storage and databases, cutting the path to the data an attacker is likely after.

As a last resort, Response isolates the machine completely. The machine is taken off the internet and the rest of the network, while a secure tunnel into it is kept open so the team can still work on it.

Taken together, these steps form a clear escalation path. The lightest action comes first, and heavier measures are held back until an attack has progressed far enough to call for them.

The rollout model is gradual by design. Teams can begin by requiring approvals before Response takes action, then allow it to act on its own as they gain confidence. Every action is temporary, visible, and can be easily rolled back, which gives teams a clear record of what happened and a simple way to reverse it.

Approval mode also lets teams see how Response would act in their own environment before giving it more independence.

Amiram Shachar, Co-Founder and CEO of Upwind, framed the launch around the speed of modern attackers.

"Finding risk will always matter, but against attackers that move at machine speed, defense has to be active," said Amiram Shachar, Co-Founder and CEO of Upwind. "Because Upwind sees what is actually happening on the servers running our customers' applications, we know exactly which address sent the attack and which service it hit. That precision is what makes automatic blocking safe. Every blocked attempt costs the attacker time and money, while our customers' business keeps running."

Response is also meant to make the industry more resilient overall. According to Upwind, each attack it encounters is cataloged, anonymized, and added to the knowledge of the Upwind Platform. The company says this allows customers to benefit from the collective experience of their peers.

This turns individual incidents into shared protection. The more attacks Response handles across the customer base, the more the platform knows about how those attacks unfold.

That shared knowledge grows with every customer that turns Response on.

Upwind Response is part of the Upwind Cloud & AI Security Platform and builds on the detections customers already use. According to the company, admins can enable it today within the console. Because Response uses cloud- and AI-native primitives, there is no extra firewall to deploy and no new infrastructure to manage.

Upwind says Response is available to all Upwind customers. Those who want to learn more or request a demo can visit Upwind Response.
