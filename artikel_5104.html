The latest on-chain signals point to a bearish market, but the next Bitcoin bottom may not be as deep as previous cycles. CryptoQuant data suggests the market could be heading toward its realized price near $53,300 rather than another brutal capitulation.

Short-term holder SOPR has remained below 1 for two weeks, ranging between 0.95 and 0.99. In bearish conditions, the 1 level can act as resistance.

Overhead supply adds another problem. At roughly $61,000, every UTXO age group from one month to three years is underwater, with realized prices between $63,200 and $104,200. That could create selling pressure as prices recover into the $63,000–$82,000 range.

Demand isn’t helping either. The Coinbase Premium has stayed negative since October 7, while exchange stablecoin reserves dropped 20% in a month, from $60.7 billion to $48.8 billion.

Open interest declined 24% in ten days, falling from $25.4 billion to $19.3 billion. Meanwhile, MVRV slipped from 1.50 to 1.15.

Historically, previous cycle bottoms formed below an MVRV reading of 1. But this cycle’s peak was also weaker, reaching around 2.8 compared with 3.9 in 2021 and 4.8 in 2017.

That difference supports the possibility of a shallower correction, not a guaranteed floor.

The analyst expects the Bitcoin bottom to form around the realized price of $53,300. A sustained reversal, however, could still take months rather than weeks.

Stablecoin reserves recovering, positive Coinbase Premium and sustained ETF inflows would offer early bullish signals. Holding SOPR above 1 and reclaiming $80,000 would provide stronger confirmation.

For now, the Bitcoin bottom prediction rests on whether price approaches realized value without triggering deeper capitulation. A decisive MVRV break below 1 would weaken that shallower-bottom thesis.
