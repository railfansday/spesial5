How to Transfer a Domain Name to a New Registrar: .uk and .com Explained

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Posted on Nov 16 • Originally published at transcom.co.uk

Transferring a domain name sounds technical, but it is mostly paperwork. You might want one bill for all your services, better support, or clearer renewal pricing. Whatever the reason, the process depends on the type of domain you own. A .uk domain moves in a completely different way from a .com.

This guide covers both, explains what to check before you start, and shows how to avoid the most common problem: a website or inbox that stops working halfway through.

A transfer moves the management of your domain from one registrar to another. It does not change who owns the domain. You stay the registrant, and the domain name stays exactly the same.

What a transfer does not automatically move is your DNS: the records that point your domain at your website, email and other services. Those live on whichever nameservers your domain uses. If your old registrar also hosts your DNS, you need to plan for that separately. More on this below.

Domains ending in .uk, .co.uk, .org.uk and similar are run by Nominet. Every Nominet registrar has a unique identifier called an IPS tag. A .uk transfer simply means changing the tag on your domain from your current registrar's tag to the new one.

One point that catches people out: a .uk transfer does not normally add time to the registration. The expiry date stays the same, so renewal is a separate step. If your domain is close to expiry, renew it first or confirm with the new registrar how they will handle it.

Unlike .uk, transfers of most global extensions usually include a one-year renewal. In practice, the transfer fee often doubles as your next year's renewal.

Many global domains cannot be transferred for 60 days after they are registered or transferred, and sometimes after certain changes to the registrant details. If your transfer is refused, check whether the domain falls inside this window.
