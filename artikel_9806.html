This week, we spotlight Rise (trade-rise), a trading app built from the ground up for a new generation of traders. It originates from INGOT Group and aims to get a trader from idea to open position in a few steps, with features like first-trade protection and one-tap multi-trade strategies.

Most trading apps still make you do the same chores before every trade. You have to research a trend, hunt down the right assets, calculate position sizes, and place every single order one by one. Rise was built because that repetitive routine does not match how traders follow the markets.

Rise is a new trading app created by INGOT Group, an established global brokerage company with 20 years of experience. To celebrate its 20th anniversary, the company launched Rise in the Middle East, North Africa, and Latin America. To welcome new users, the app offered a safety net by refunding up to $100 if their very first trade lost money. It also gave users access to over 1,000 different financial assets to trade.

The app's newest feature is called Rise Moments, which lets you invest in a big market trend with just a single tap. It starts with a simple question about where the market is heading, instantly groups all the right investments connected to that idea, and lets you open them all at once. You just pick a trend, read the logic behind it, decide how much money you want to put in, and tweak the list of investments if you want to. Once you make the trade, you are fully in charge of managing it, and all the normal risks of investing still apply.

Inauguration is a next-generation compiler that lets developers write in over 40 programming languages while using libraries from any supported ecosystem as if they were one. It compiles itself in under two seconds with a sub-10 MB binary. The idea is to make language choice an implementation detail rather than a wall between developers and the libraries they need.

TransactionLink automates KYB and KYC onboarding for compliance and risk teams at financial institutions, fintechs, and lenders. It orchestrates checks across multiple data providers in a single workflow, with a unified dashboard for investigating exceptions without switching tools. The team says this cuts verification costs by 60%, and it points to its work with Pliant as a case study.

TransactionLink’s Proof of Usefulness Score: 66/1000

FlowMind is an AI project manager that joins live voice meetings, transcribes them, and assigns tasks to the right team member based on their skill profile. It builds a knowledge graph of the team on Neo4j, so it improves with every meeting. It’s in early beta, aimed at remote-first engineering teams tired of status-update meetings and manual task delegation.

FlowMind’s Proof of Usefulness Score: 47/1000
