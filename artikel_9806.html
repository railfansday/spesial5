InfoQ Homepage News Grafana Begins Its Biggest UI Refresh Since Version 7

Nov 27, 2026 3 min read

Grafana Labs has introduced the first phase of its biggest visual refresh since Grafana 7, bringing updated themes, more consistent interface components, and improved visual hierarchy to its observability platform. Introduced as part of Grafana 13.3, the refresh is now available in public preview across Grafana Cloud and self-managed deployments.

The changes address a growing problem as Grafana has expanded: years of rapid feature development have created design inconsistencies, competing visual elements, and different interaction patterns across the platform. Rather than introducing a complete redesign, Grafana is standardising its existing interface while retaining familiar navigation and plugin compatibility.

The longer-term objective is to make the platform easier to use as observability workflows increasingly incorporate AI assistants and agent-driven interactions.

Grafana's extensive growth has brought support for a broad range of observability use cases, but this has also introduced what the company describes as design debt. Different parts of the interface have evolved independently, resulting in inconsistent visual cues, component variations, and interaction patterns.

The design refresh is intended to address these inconsistencies without slowing the platform's continued development. Grafana wants users to learn an interaction pattern once and apply that knowledge consistently across dashboards, panels, drawers, and other parts of the interface.

The work has also been influenced by the development of Grafana Assistant and Assistant Workspace, which introduced new interaction patterns for AI-powered workflows and prompted the design team to reconsider the platform's visual language.

New default light and dark themes use redesigned colour palettes intended to improve accessibility. Grafana has also reduced the number of available component variations, helping standardise how common interface elements behave.

Typography, borders, shadows and visual layering have been updated to create a clearer information hierarchy. The intention is to reduce competing visual elements and make it easier for users to identify important information when investigating incidents or exploring data.

These changes extend across fundamental interface components, including cards, panels, and drawers. Rather than simply applying a new visual style, Grafana is revisiting the underlying design primitives so that the interface remains consistent as the product evolves.

An interesting motivation behind the refresh is Grafana's view that traditional graphical interfaces will increasingly coexist with terminal-based and agentic chat interactions.

As more workflows become accessible through AI assistants, the remaining graphical interactions need to be particularly clear and predictable. Users should be able to understand the information presented, recognise important changes and move between workflows without having to relearn different interface conventions.

This makes visual consistency more than a cosmetic exercise. It becomes part of ensuring that engineers can work effectively across both traditional observability interfaces and newer AI-assisted experiences.

Grafana is deliberately introducing the refresh in phases, allowing users and contributors to adapt while providing feedback before further changes are made.

Importantly, the company describes this as a visual refresh rather than a complete redesign. Existing navigation remains unchanged, and the update introduces no breaking changes for plugins.

Self-managed users can enable the new styling through the grafana.visualDesignRefresh feature toggle and can switch back to the previous appearance if required. Grafana Cloud users can also access the public preview.

The company has encouraged feedback through its community Slack and an in-app survey, using early adoption and user responses to guide subsequent iterations.

Other observability platforms, including Datadog and New Relic, have also invested in simplifying how engineers navigate increasingly complex monitoring environments. Grafana's challenge is particularly interesting given its open-source ecosystem, where plugins and contributions introduce additional interface requirements.

The refresh reflects a broader product engineering principle: as platforms grow, maintaining consistency becomes as important as introducing new functionality.

For observability tools, where engineers often work under pressure during production incidents, reducing cognitive overhead can directly improve how quickly they find and interpret relevant information.

Grafana's refresh is therefore less about changing how the platform works and more about making its growing capabilities easier to understand and use. As AI increasingly changes how engineers interact with observability systems, a clear and consistent graphical experience remains an important part of the overall design.
