The American Hospital Association (AHA) and West Health Institute announced Tuesday a new collaboration with medtech company Becton, Dickinson and Company (BD) aimed at accelerating the adoption of technologies shown to improve patient care.

The collaboration builds on the West Health Accelerator at AHA’s Health Research & Educational Trust (HRET), a three-year initiative to aid healthcare organizations in scaling technology-enabled solutions.

“No single organization can close the gap between innovation and everyday care on its own,” said Zia Agha, M.D., West Health Institute chief medical officer, in a statement. “This collaboration brings hospital reach, clinical technology expertise and performance intelligence together from the start. We’re beginning with a focused group of hospitals, learning what it takes to make changes last, and building a model that can reach hospitals of every size and resource level.”

The new collaboration will begin with an initial cohort of 10 hospitals and health systems. It will incorporate advanced physiologic monitoring with clinical protocols in practice, specifically targeting issues associated with postoperative complications and longer hospital stays. Additionally, Vizient will provide performance benchmarking and analytic capabilities, the organizations said.

Takeaways from the early cohort will help to identify adoption barriers, refine implementation approaches and outline necessary support to expand the model to a broader group.

BD Advanced Patient Monitoring Worldwide President Tim Patz said in a statement that the company believes innovation “has the greatest impact when it translates into meaningful improvements in patient care.”

“Our experience working with healthcare systems has shown us the value of bringing together advanced hemodynamic monitoring, data and the expertise of hospital care teams to support better-informed patient care,” Patz said. “This collaboration gives us an opportunity to build on that experience, further integrate proven solutions into clinical practice and help more patients benefit from them.”
