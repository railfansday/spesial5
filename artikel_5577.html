Housing Week Ahead: Construction Insights, Rental Trends, and Pending Sales

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The coming week holds a packed slate of insights from Realtor.com Economics, as well as vital indicators about the state of the housing market.

On Monday, senior economist Jiayi Xu  published a report on free and clear mortgages, which breaks down trends in where the most homeowners own their home outright.

On Tuesday, senior economist Joel Berner has our third-quarter New-Construction Insights report looking at how new-home prices and homebuilder incentives have trended in the past quarter.

Then on Wednesday, Jiayi is back with a look at October rent trends in our monthly rental report, which will also dive deep into how apartment supply shifts are likely to affect the market.

Finally, on Thursday, senior economist Anthony Smith will analyze our weekly housing data for clues on the latest trends.

Turning to the macro indicators that will be released, both pending home sales and new construction will be released on Wednesday and on Thursday, we’ll see mortgage rates.

October pending home sales, released by the National Association of Realtors®, are likely to be low. In September, the pending data hit a series record low, tumbling more than 7% in the month and 11% from a year ago.

Realtor.com data showed double-digit declines in both pending sales and new contract signings in October. I think we’re likely to see another tough few months for housing activity as we wrap up 2026.

Similar challenges are likely for builders and the high-rate environment is likely to slow October construction.

In September’s construction report from the U.S. Census Bureau, permits, starts, and completions all fell from both the prior month and prior year. Declines were mildest for permits, and single-family fared better than multifamily in starts and completions.

Meanwhile, mortgage rates notched a fourth consecutive week of decline last week, getting all the way down to 7.11%, but I think that streak ends this week.

Last week’s inflation data came in hot, and the bond market reacted, sending the 10-year Treasury yield above 5% for the first time in November.

Home shoppers planning to take out a mortgage will want to prepare, while home shoppers planning to use cash are likely to find a less-competitive housing market to navigate.

Danielle Hale is the chief economist of Realtor.com.
