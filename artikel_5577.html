Resident of Collapsed Brooklyn Building Sues for Negligence After Losing $100K Record Collection

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The first of what is likely to be several lawsuits was filed in Kings County Superior Court on Monday, alleging widespread negligence by a host of landlords, construction companies, and contractors in last week's deadly Brooklyn building collapse.

Dengane Omari Jack lived at 1321 Flatbush Ave., which collapsed last Wednesday, tragically killing eight people and wounding 18 more. Jack was at work at the time of the collapse, but he rushed home to see if his extensive reggae vinyl record collection, valued at $100,000, might be salvaged. It was a total loss.

"He was one of the lucky ones," a friend wrote on a GoFundMe campaign to support Jack. "But surviving came at an unimaginable cost: in a matter of seconds, he lost his home, everything he owned, and a lifetime's worth of memories and work."

Now, Jack is suing his landlord and management company, as well as a half-dozen companies affiliated with 2625 Bedford Ave., the lot adjacent to his Flatbush building. None of those entities has filed a response to the complaint yet in court.

The suit alleges the building's owner, Isolag Realty Group, and the property manager, Olufemi Falade, failed to take action after he pointed out cracks in the facade of 1321 Flatbush Ave. earlier this year.

According to the lawsuit: “During October 2026, Plaintiff observed cracks at or near the Building's entrance, photographed those cracks, and reported the condition to the landlord or management responsible for the Building.”

The suit goes on to allege that Falade “personally received or responded to the report of the cracks on behalf of the Building's ownership or management. Plaintiff was falsely told, in substance, that the condition was not the landlord's fault or could not be fixed.”

Falade has appeared on the city's "Worst Landlord" watch list, put out by the city's Public Advocate office, six times in the past decade.

Jack’s suit also names several entities tied to 2625 Bedford Ave., the adjacent lot where excavation work was taking place at the time of the building collapse.

Those defendants include general contractor Townhouse Builders, d/b/a Promont; New Jersey–based LMW Engineering Group and its president, Jieming Wang; W6 Facility X, an LLC affiliated with Robert Fischman; and LLCs affiliated with lot owners Faigy Fischman and Judy Swimer.

The suit points to a pre-construction survey commissioned by the builders, which documented that the facade of 1321 Flatbush Ave. was in "poor" condition prior to the excavation.

"The Defendants, who were responsible for excavation, design, supervision, inspections and monitoring received, or in the exercise of reasonable care, should have obtained and considered, the Pre-Construction Survey to evaluate all relevant Pre-Construction Site Conditions and, in particular, the Building's condition and its need for support and protection before proceeding with work at the Construction Site," the suit alleges.

Furthermore, the suit says the defendants "had actual notice through the surveys, reports, observations or complaints applicable to their respective work, or constructive notice because the conditions were discoverable through the inspections and monitoring they were required to perform."

The suit also claims that "the excavation, inadequate structural protection, negligent engineering orinspection services, and failure to address the Building's developing dangerous conditionproximately and/or substantially caused the Building to collapse, causing Plaintiff's losses."

The 2625 Bedford Ave. construction project had a history of safety violations noted by the Department of Buildings. The demolition of a building that had previously been on the site was halted in March of this year after demolition contractor N Tech Design of America had failed to take proper safety precautions, including installing netting and putting up sidewalk shields, records show.

A month later, inspectors returned to the site and found that the demolition had gone ahead anyway, in defiance of the stop-work order, according to city records. A $10,000 fine was issued.

In May, a city inspector issued additional citations and another stop-work order against the contractors for allegedly failing to ensure adequate protections of neighboring buildings and for deviating from approved construction plans. Within a week, the city lifted that stop-work order.

Jack is suing for compensatory damages, rent relief, and punitive and exemplary damages.

In an interview with Gothamist, he valued his lost record collection at more than $100,000.

“ Nobody has contacted me from the city for, like, ‘We found some stuff that is still usable.’ I didn't hear anything like that,” Jack told the outlet. “And I don't know who to reach out to either.”

Thankfully, he'd been transferring many of his vinyl treasures into digital files prior to the collapse and had his hard drive with him at work when the building fell.

“ Every single day I was just backing up stuff. I don't know if it's intuition was telling me to do this. But I was doing it every day for the past week,” he added.

The GoFundMe to support Jack, who DJs under the name Roots Outernational, has raised more than $12,000 so far.

Last week, hours after the collapse, Brooklyn District Attorney Eric Gonzalez opened a criminal inquiry into the collapse. No charges have been filed yet.

In a statement late Wednesday, DOB Commissioner Ahmed Tigani said that the department was working with law enforcement in the ongoing investigation.

"The Department of Buildings is doing everything we can to understand exactly how this collapse occurred, so that the responsible parties can be held to account," said Tigani.

Tigani also revealed that neighboring 1323 Flatbush Ave., which was heavily damaged in the collapse, was unsalvageable and would have to be brought down in a controlled demolition.

Realtor.com® has reached out to Townhouse Builders, LMW Engineering, Jack, and Jack's lawfirm, Antin, Ehrlich & Epstein LLP, for comment. Isolag Realty Group and Falade were not immediately reachable for comment.
