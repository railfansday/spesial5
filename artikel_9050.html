Laura Hoptman Wins CCS Bard’s 2027 Audrey Irmas Award for Curatorial Excellence

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Center for Curatorial Studies at Bard College (CCS Bard) has given its 2027 Audrey Irmas Award for Curatorial Excellence to Laura Hoptman, executive director of New York’s Drawing Center. She will receive $25,000 and will be honored at CCS Bard’s spring gala in April.

Hoptman joined the Drawing Center in 2018 following eight years as a curator in the department of painting and sculpture at the Museum of Modern Art (MoMA). While at MoMA, she oversaw major traveling exhibitions devoted to Isa Genzken (2013) and Bruce Conner (2016), as well as the museum’s hotly debated survey of contemporary painting, “The Forever Now” (2014–2015). After beginning her career as an assistant curator at the Bronx Museum, Hoptman worked as an assistant curator in MoMA’s department of drawings from 1996 to 2002. In 2003 she guest-curated “Drawing Now: Eight Propositions,” featuring more than 200 representational works by 26 contemporary artists, at MoMA QNS, and in 2004 she was director of the 54th Carnegie International in Pittsburgh. Between 2006 and 2010 she was senior curator at the New Museum in New York, where she co-organized “Unmonumental: The Object in the 21st Century” (2007–2008) and “The Generational: Younger than Jesus” (2009).

The Audrey Irmas Award has been given annually since 1998 and recognizes “groundbreaking visionaries in the curatorial field,” according to a press release. Past recipients include Adriano Pedrosa, Connie Butler, Carolyn Christov-Bakargiev, Thelma Golden, and Okwui Enwezor.

“Laura’s longstanding curatorial practice has furthered new art historical analysis and international recognition of drawing as a creative medium and social practice,” said CCS Bard executive director Tom Eccles in a statement. “Her international projects have reexamined the legacies of an array of artists through new and creative frameworks. What is often forgotten is how consistently Laura has demonstrated a boldness and bravery in championing artists across many institutional structures.”
