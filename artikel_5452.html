Attached Homes Drive Salt Lake City’s New-Home Sales Growth

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

New home demand in Salt Lake City is increasingly splitting along product lines, with attached housing gaining momentum while detached sales remain comparatively flat.

In September, annualized new-home sales across the metro topped 2,800 units, up roughly 4% from a year ago. Attached housing drove much of the gain, with annualized sales reaching 1,350 units, a 10% increase year over year. Meanwhile, detached sales remained slightly higher at 1,450 units but edged down 0.7% from last year.

Although detached homes still accounted for more sales in absolute terms, virtually all of the market’s recent growth stemmed from attached product. Active project counts showed a similar pattern. As of November, Salt Lake City had 92 active detached new-home projects, down 3.2%, compared with 65 attached projects, up 3.2%.

Pricing helps explain why attached housing may be resonating with buyers, especially given today’s affordability challenges. As of July, the average list price for a new detached home was roughly $738,000, up 11% from a year earlier, while new attached homes averaged approximately $470,000, up about 4%. That puts the difference between the two averages at more than $268,000, although it is important to note that attached and detached homes differ in size, location, design, and community mix.

The distribution of closings reinforces that distinction. In the past 12 months ending August, attached new-home closings were concentrated heavily in lower price bands, particularly between $350,000 and $450,000. Detached closings were distributed much more broadly, with the most substantial shares falling between $450,000 and $650,000.
