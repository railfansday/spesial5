Roth 401(k) employer match is becoming mainstream, new report finds

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Share of employers adding Roth treatment of employer contributions rose to 42.4% from 19.7%, the Plan Sponsor Council of America said

The Plan Sponsor Council of America reported in its 69th annual survey that 42.4% of organizations have already adopted, or will adopt, a Roth option for employer contributions in their 401(k) plans, compared with 19.7% a year earlier.

The survey draws on responses from 753 plan sponsors about how their plans performed during 2025. Another 11% are considering the option, according to CNBC.

“The Roth era is here. SECURE 2.0’s mandatory Roth catch-up made Roth essential for nearly every plan, and sponsors aren’t stopping there,” Will Hansen, the council’s executive director, said in a statement.

Roth use is also growing among employee contributions, the survey found. Among the surveyed plans, 96.9% give participants a Roth option, and 24.5% of participants now use it, compared with 22.1% before. Among plans with Roth deferrals available, 65.0% let participants shift existing balances into Roth money without leaving the plan, compared with 61.0% before.

Workers used to receive their employer's matching dollars solely on a pretax basis, according to CNBC. The SECURE 2.0 law, signed in 2022 by then-President Joe Biden, allows employers to provide a Roth match if they choose. A plan that offers one does not apply it automatically, and workers must opt in.

Hattie Greenan, the council's research director, told CNBC she expects the share of employers offering the option to keep rising. "Roth is becoming mainstream," she said.

Financial advisers told CNBC the choice between Roth and pretax savings varies by person. Roth contributions carry no up-front tax break, but qualified withdrawals in retirement are tax-free. “It’s personal preference,” said Philip Chao, who holds the certified financial planner designation and serves as chief investment officer at Experiential Wealth, in comments to CNBC.
