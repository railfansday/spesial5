Satoshi’s Bitcoin DNA in XRP Ledger Uncovered in Two Cryptographic Mechanisms

XRP Ledger Foundation Director of Community Hussein Zangana (Vet) shared a tidbit of XRPL's early history in a recent X post.

Vet noted that the XRP Ledger (XRPL) still carries traces of Satoshi Nakamoto’s original Bitcoin design, highlighting two features.

In 2011, three engineers—David Schwartz, Jed McCaleb, and Arthur Britto—began developing the XRP Ledger (XRPL). Fascinated by Bitcoin, they set out to create a better version that improved upon its limitations—with the goal of creating a digital asset that was more sustainable and built specifically for payments.

As reported, the first line of code for the core of what would become the XRP Ledger (XRPL) was written by David Schwartz, who is Ripple CTO emeritus, about 15 years ago, on November 11, 2011.

Vet delves into the earliest part of XRP Ledger history, noting that 15 years ago, the XRP Ledger started out using some Bitcoin code, including helper functions from the Bitcoin client that Bitcoin's pseudonymous creator, Satoshi Nakamoto, wrote. Vet added that Jed McCaleb also copied Satoshi’s timekeeping code into the XRPL, even including one of the Bitcoin creator’s comments shared as a screenshot.

Did you know all of us on the XRP Ledger use design choices by Satoshi daily? 15 years ago, the XRP Ledger started out using some Bitcoin code, including helper functions from the Bitcoin client that Satoshi wrote. Jed copied Satoshi's timekeeping code into the XRPL, including one of Satoshi's comments. By the end of 2014, the original Satoshi code was gone. In December 2014, Nik Bougalis removed the last of the timekeeping code, along with Satoshi's comment. Two design choices of Satoshi still remain on the XRPL and all of us use at least one of it. Signature curve secp256k1 (we also support Ed25519 thus not everyone uses this) and how XRPL addresses are generated from the public key with SHA-256 followed by RIPEMD-160, that's what all of us use.

The quote "Never go to sea with two chronometers; take one or three" is a classic maritime adage famously included by Satoshi Nakamoto (the pseudonymous creator of Bitcoin) within the Bitcoin source code.

Vet explained that by the end of 2014, the original Satoshi code was gone from the XRP Ledger. In December 2014, former Ripple engineering lead Nik Bougalis removed the last of the timekeeping code, along with Satoshi's comment.

However, two of Satoshi's design choices still remain on the XRP Ledger: the first is the secp256k1 signature curve, which Bitcoin uses and which the XRP Ledger continues to support. However, the ledger also supports Ed25519, meaning not every XRPL user relies on secp256k1.

"I remember David saying they used secp256k1 so no one would doubt the security because it's the same as Bitcoin's, etc.," Vet stated, citing the insight of the Ripple CTO emeritus on secp256k1, adding, "Lots of interesting things to dig into from the early days."

The second is the method used to generate XRP Ledger addresses from public keys. The process applies SHA-256 hashing followed by RIPEMD-160, a design inherited from Bitcoin that remains common to all XRPL addresses.

Asked why the timekeeping code and Satoshi's comment were removed, Vet explained that this became leftover code as new code was introduced, so it was cleaned up.
