EFL preview: Evans' gripes & unexpected departures

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Steve Evans criticised his Bristol Rovers players last week after a disappointing 2-0 loss to Chesterfield left them 18th in the League Two table

The Championship resumes this weekend - but not all will be as it was.

Blackburn Rovers and Swansea City dip their feet into life without Tony Mowbray and Vitor Matos at their sides, for very different reasons.

Meanwhile, Bristol Rovers boss Steve Evans remains in charge in League Two but, given his comments after their last game, it might not be a relationship built to last.

Here are five things to capture your attention this EFL weekend.

Tony Mowbray (right) has left Blackburn Rovers in 13th place in the table while Vitor Matos (left) departs for France with Swansea City in sixth

It would have been a crushing blow to Blackburn fans to lose boss Mowbray, who stepped away due to ill health on Tuesday.

The veteran was surprising the league with a competitive Rovers side only three points away from the top eight and last registering a shock 2-1 win over big dogs West Ham United.

Rovers are not looking for an immediate successor and Mowbray may yet resume his managerial duties at some point, but the side will want to ensure they keep their fine form going rather than leaving him a salvage job to pull off should he return.

To do so, the team will have to put their concerns aside and focus on the job at hand - a Lancashire derby with struggling Burnley at Turf Moor on Sunday (12:00 GMT).

But they are not the only team to be without their trusty manager.

Matos has spread his wings to fly over to Strasbourg and leaves his Swans in a healthy league position but also in poor form.

The Welsh side have lost four of their past five (W1) to begin falling down the pecking order but still remain inside the play-off places, three points below the league leaders and three above West Ham in ninth.

Matos has taken four members of his coaching staff with him, meaning former Swans players Joe Allen and Ashley Williams have stepped in as a makeshift coaching set-up before a difficult game against fifth-placed West Bromwich Albion on Saturday (15:00 GMT).

"Swansea were doing great but I felt they were performing at their absolute maximum," former Stoke full-back Tommy Smith told the 72+ podcast this week.

"I think it's a good opportunity for him, a good opportunity for Strasbourg but it's a tough one for Swansea to take."

Swansea's summer signings 'let down' by Matos

Millwall have won their past three home fixtures against London rivals QPR

Queens Park Rangers boss Julien Stephan believes their London derby against Millwall on Saturday lunchtime (12:30 GMT) will be the first of five games to truly define his side.

The R's are going strong so far this season. They sit inside the play-off positions, five points off the league leaders but it's not enough yet to convince everyone they have finally become promotion contenders after 12 years mostly in the doldrums.

"The thing that is going to hold us back is consistency," QPR fan Gareth Dixon told BBC Radio London.

"We have far too many players injured too frequently. Getting that first, strongest XI is a challenge for us.

"If we can get our strongest team out, if we can keep the performances up then we are looking at top eight."

Millwall, meanwhile, know what it's like to push for a place in the Premier League in recent times, but things have not panned out so well this season.

Injuries have hampered them and after only two wins in their past 10 league games, both against teams in the bottom three, the Lions' season is yet to really get going.

All three of the Championship clubs based in the West Midlands plus Staffordshire's Stoke City are within one point of each other at the top of the table

There is something in the air around the western side of the Midlands.

Only Middlesbrough disrupt a top five of West Midlands and Staffordshire clubs and the two best of that bunch are league leaders Birmingham City and third-placed Stoke City.

Both are on three-match winning streaks and sit on 29 points, separated merely by Blues' superior goal difference.

Saturday's game at the Bet365 Stadium (15:00 GMT) will see the two go head-to-head for a top-two spot - and possibly top spot itself.

But a draw could open the door for either Wolves or West Bromwich Albion to reach the summit instead.

I mean, just look how tight the division is.

Dave Challinor was announced as new Sheffield Wednesday boss on 1 November

In a game against fellow promotion contenders Plymouth Argyle last weekend, Sheffield Wednesday claimed a convincing 3-0 victory to mark Dave Challinor's return to League One management.

Their next opponent, Barnsley, seem like they will be an easier ride. The Tykes have the joint fewest points in the league with nine, kept off the bottom through goal difference alone.

A derby under the lights can produce something special though.

Barnsley have won both their previous matches against Wednesday and will be seeking a third in a row for the first time since 1946.

No one is expecting the Tykes to claim all three points when they meet on Friday night (20:00 GMT) but that means all the pressure is on the rebuilding Wednesday side.

The Owls did not deal so well with that expectation in the two cup games Challinor began his reign with, losing in the FA Cup to Oldham Athletic and suffering a 4-0 drubbing in the EFL Trophy against Accrington Stanley.

Can they rise to the occasion or is it Barnsley's time to get motoring?

Challinor hoping Argyle win is just 'the start'

Few bosses in League Two have the experience of Steve Cotterill and Steve Evans

Cheltenham Town boss Steve Cotterill isn't much loved by the Bristol Rovers fan base.

The 62-year-old led the Gas' local rivals Bristol City to the League One title and the EFL Trophy in 2015, earning the Robins' eternal admiration and Rovers' unrelenting dislike.

However, back at his boyhood club, Cotterill will be aiming to quieten his critics among the away support as he looks to take Cheltenham into the League Two play-off spots when the two sides face each other on Saturday (15:00 GMT).

His current Robins have lost five of their past seven games (W2) but still remain just a point shy of the top seven.

Rovers, meanwhile, are tumbling down the table at a rapid rate after a seven-game winless run where they have taken just one point - and they are hardly putting up a united front.

Manager Steve Evans has suggested he will depart the club if he feels they no longer want his services and has placed blame on his players for their run of poor form.

"You're going to Steve Cotterill's Cheltenham side, who are very good, particularly at Whaddon Road," Evans told BBC Radio Bristol after their loss to Chesterfield last weekend.

"We have to pick a team first and foremost that works hard and fights.

"I'll still turn up at 6.30 in the morning. I'll still leave at 6 o'clock at night. I still go and watch the opposition, do the analysis. What's changed? I know what's changed â inconsistent players."
