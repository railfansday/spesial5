Pochettino’s new-manager bounce falls flat with De Ligt move a disastrous step | Jamie Jackson

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Decision to pick the Dutch defender over Harry Maguire backfired as Manchester United fell apart in the Anfield bearpit

Chaotic, bewildering, and on this evidence in a relegation dogfight: welcome to the three‑ring circus that is Manchester United, Mauricio Pochettino.

This ended as a 4-0 rout in favour of Liverpool, the new head coach overseeing the poorest opening result of the eight who have followed Sir Alex Ferguson since May 2013. Factor in this result coming against United’s fiercest rivals and Pochettino’s despair is complete.

He is firmly in the dock for this loss, due to a disastrous preference for Matthijs de Ligt over Harry Maguire, the hapless Dutch defender playing a leading part in three Liverpool goals.

Towards the end the Kop chirped: “You’re going down with the City.” There may be truth in the humour unless Pochettino gets hold – fast – of the rabble inherited from Michael Carrick.

Eight short minutes into a nascent tenure and a disgusted shake of his flowing locks was the greeting to the madhouse he has taken over. By then Leny Yoro had been sent off for bringing down Alexander Isak and Anfield was in bearpit mood.

Very far from ideal – and worse was to follow. Far worse. After the Frenchman departed, Dominik Szoboszlai smashed the free-kick through a nonexistent wall and beyond Senne Lammens and Liverpool were 1-0 up.

The visitors’ next act? To enter full meltdown as Diogo Dalot allowed Rio Ngumoha room to cross and Florian Wirtz hooked home, De Ligt losing his man.

Thirteen minutes in and 2–0 behind made Omar Berrada’s talk of the “buzz” of Pochettino’s entrance at United farcical and made the chief executive appear to be as much as a chump as his latest appointee, who was authoring a new head-coach plummet, not bounce.

Yoro had been forced into hauling down Isak after De Ligt lost first the ball, then the Liverpool striker. De Ligt’s inclusion was Pochettino’s headline selection move, the dropping of Maguire handing the centre-back a first league start in a year and it backfired almost immediately.

“I don’t know who played the last game – we assessed the squad from the day we arrived and we believe it’s the best team for today,” Pochettino said beforehand when quizzed about the XI he sent out.

Words to haunt him and, as De Ligt’s only other start was in United’s 4-1 humiliation at Como last month in the Champions League, questions regarding the coach’s judgment were already being posed.

By now “sacked in the morning” was the derisory cry of Kopites as Pochettino’s new project was in the damage-limitation mode of a 5-3-1 with 21 minutes played, as off went Bryan Mbeumo for Maguire.

After spells with Espanyol, Southampton, Tottenham, Paris Saint-Germain, Chelsea and the USA, Pochettino’s honours roll reads one Ligue 1 and one Coupe de France – a modest two trophies in a 17-year managerial career.

Losing a Champions League final (with Spurs) and two EFL Cup finals (Spurs and Chelsea) had characterised him, to some, as a “bridesmaid” and at half‑time his team were definitively second best, fortunate to be only two behind.

In the perpetual soap opera that is United, the buildup featured a drone filming Pochettino’s first training session, Berrada’s interview and Bruno Fernandes saying new contract talks between the captain and club were paused due to the worst league start in 40 years.

Going into the game, United’s dire form contained a solitary win in 10 league matches and eight points. Why? Because they are a rabble, lacking cohesion from goalkeeper to attack, with individuals consistently embarrassing themselves and the shirt.

It cost Carrick, culpable for this too, his job and cleared the path for Pochettino to occupy a role he has long coveted. Each new manager since Ferguson has faced a two-pronged challenge: the state of the squad and the state of the club – each related.

Those who know Pochettino say he will bring charm and warmth – useful, maybe, for man-managing a group of multimillionaires, but at the interval he needed to perform the miracle of convincing them that something, somehow, could be salvaged after a diabolical opening 45 minutes.

They were staring at defeat by an Andoni Iraola side that began 10 points and 15 places better off. Pochettino hoped to avoid this by taking off Matheus Cunha for Joshua Zirkzee for the second half but ended the non-contest ashen‑faced, with Berrada and Jason Wilcox, the director of football, appearing just as lost in the posh seats.

A main takeaway from Berrada’s interview was his conviction that “we are heading in the right direction”. When he spoke United were 18th, and by full time they were 19th, with De Ligt a glaring emblem of their disarray. When again he was marking air not Isak, the Swede saved the centre-back more blushes by spraying wide.

This had Pochettino applauding, unfathomably, when invective seemed an apt response.

Yet when Ngumoha made it 3-0 after 66 minutes there was no more faux touchline enthusiasm from a man who had promised to “create something from scratch”. As with all who have followed Ferguson, he had discovered that United’s shaky foundations make building anything sustainable a sisyphean task.

De Ligt’s loose pass led to Víctor Muñoz’s fourth, meaning the answer to whether Pochettino’s debut could have gone any worse was a resounding no.
