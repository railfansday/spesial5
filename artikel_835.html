How do NI replace 'perfect player' Donley?

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Northern Ireland play Hungary in Budapest needing a point for Nations League promotion

With six of his starting outfield players against Ukraine on Saturday night having gone into the game a yellow card away from suspension, it was always likely that Northern Ireland manager Michael O'Neill would need to reshuffle his team for the Nations League Group B2 finale in Hungary.

That the player who picked up the booking ended up being Jamie Donley, however, has presented the boss with something of a selection conundrum.

The Tottenham Hotspur forward, currently on loan at Luton Town, has started the side's past 10 games at centre-forward, a different role to the deeper position he has more usually occupied in his club career.

With O'Neill having circled through traditional number nine options such as Dion Charles, Jamie Reid and Lee Bonis since returning to the job four years ago, it feels as if Donley has made the shirt his own since being handed the start against Luxembourg 12 months ago.

While the manager has pushed back on the idea that Donley is playing out of position at international level, the one-game ban is certainly an untimely one with the former Leyton Orient and Oxford United loanee's hold-up play in particular against Ukraine showing how much he has grown into the role.

After the 21-year-old had a goal chalked off by the video assistant referee (VAR) on Saturday, he has still scored just once from open play for his country, but there is a growing appreciation that he is asked to bring much more than just goals to the team.

"He's been such a good player for us. Even without the goals, the way he holds the ball up and spreads the play, his movement is just so good. Playing balls in the air, he pins the defender so well," said Fulham midfielder Shea Charles.

"He's got better every game and he's like the perfect player for us."

There is no obvious like-for-like replacement in O'Neill's squad for a game in which they need a draw to secure promotion to Nations League A for the first time.

Arsenal's Ceadach O'Neill pulled out of the double-header through injury last week, with Josh Magennis, Callum Marshall and the uncapped pair of Eoin Kenny and Braiden Graham the other strikers in the travelling party.

Similar to the original ask of Donley, there is also an option to move a wide or deeper player such as Isaac Price or Paddy Kelly into a central striking role.

With only three days between games, however, there has been little time to work on such a plan to replace a player whose four Spurs appearances all came under Ange Postecoglou in the 2023-24 season.

"There are other things we can do. We can change the shape a little bit, play with two front players," said O'Neill, who himself is suspended for the game after two bookings and will watch from the stands.

"There's a number of things that we will look at. Obviously the time between the games makes that somewhat more difficult in terms of preparation.

"We'll find hopefully the right player that can first of all fit into what we want to do, but obviously secondly give the opposition some problems as well."

Nobody in O'Neill's squad has more international caps or goals than Stevenage striker Magennis.

But while the 36-year-old has started 11 games in League One this season, it has been more than three years since he last did so at international level.

"My role has obviously changed since I've come into the squad," said the player with 94 international appearances and 12 goals to his name.

"If Michael wants to play with the target man and with experience, obviously, he's going to go for me. If he wants a like-for-like, he's probably going to go with Marshy [Callum Marshall]. So, me and Marshy will probably play rock, paper, scissors.

"Jamie brings so much to the team, but I don't think there's going to be a drastic downfall if one of us has to step in for us. I think the competition for places in the squad for us is absolutely brilliant and if you get that opportunity, you've just got to take it."
