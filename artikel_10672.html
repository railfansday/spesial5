Most teams set up AI visibility tracking for ChatGPT, add Google, and stop there. Claude tends to come up later, usually when someone notices that half the engineers, analysts and founders they know use it every day. That instinct is sound. Claude's audience leans towards the people who research software and services before they buy.

The numbers back it up. Similarweb put Claude at 3.3% of AI chatbot web visits in February 2026 and 9.2% in May 2026, close to three times the share in three months, while ChatGPT's share kept falling. Those are web visits only. Usage in the Claude apps, in Claude Code and through the API does not show up in that figure, so 9.2% is a floor.

This guide compares six tools that track Claude. Peec AI comes first because it connects Claude answers to the pages behind them. The other five cover different budgets and needs.

A Claude tracking tool sends a fixed set of prompts to Claude on a schedule and records what comes back: whether your brand is named, where in the answer, how it is described, and which sources the answer relied on. Run daily, those records become trends you can compare against competitors.

It is not a rank tracker. Claude gives no list of ten blue links, so the useful numbers are mention rate, position inside the answer, sentiment and cited sources.

How the tool queries Claude. Some tools send your prompts to Anthropic's API. Others drive the Claude app. The two can give different answers, because the app has its own web search behaviour and the API, queried cold, often does not. Neither is wrong. Your buyers use the app, and the API is cheaper to run at scale. What matters is that you know which one you are looking at and that it stays the same over time.

Which plan includes Claude. Claude is a premium engine for several vendors. It can sit on the entry plan, on a higher tier or on Enterprise only, and a free tier often leaves it out.

What it costs per run. Credit-based tools can charge more for Claude than for other engines. Check the credit formula before you size a plan.

Source data. A mention rate says whether Claude names you. Source data says why it does not. Pick a tool that shows the pages Claude read.

Prompt volume. 50 to 150 prompts per brand is a realistic start. Fewer than 15 is mostly noise. Check how many prompts the cheaper tiers cover with Claude specifically: on credit-based tools Claude costs more per run, so the entry plan can cover far fewer Claude prompts than the headline number suggests.

Peec AI is a Berlin-based AI search analytics platform that tracks visibility, position, sentiment and share of voice across AI engines, then shows which sources drive those answers. Claude is part of the Enterprise plan and the agency Scale plan, tracked next to ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Microsoft Copilot and both Google surfaces. That makes it the pick for teams that want Claude as one engine in a wider view rather than as a stand-alone report.

Best for: Teams that track Claude together with ChatGPT and Google and want to know which pages to work on, not only where they stand.

Scrunch AI tracks Claude on its Enterprise plan, alongside nine engines in total. The published Core plan at $250 a month covers four engines and leaves Claude out, so it suits teams that start with ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google and Copilot and add Claude when they move to a quote.

Best for: Teams that want a published entry price now and add Claude through an Enterprise contract later.

Rankscale puts Claude on every paid plan from $99 a month and covers the widest engine list at that price: Claude, Copilot, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, both Google surfaces, DeepSeek and Mistral. It is credit-based, and the formula is published, which matters because Claude costs 2 credits per run, more than most engines. On Pro, 1,200 credits cover about 20 prompts run daily on Claude alone.

Best for: Small teams and agencies that want Claude and a long engine list for the least money.

AthenaHQ was built by two engineers who worked on Google Search and DeepMind. Claude is included from the Starter plan, among eleven engines. The free Essential tier does not include Claude, so it shows you the other engines first. Its optimisation agent drafts on-page and off-page recommendations, which suits teams that have the analysis but not the hands to act on it.

Best for: Teams short on people to write the follow-up work.

Profound is the shortest path for companies where an information security review decides the purchase. It lists SOC 2, SSO and SAML on Enterprise and raised a $96M Series C at a $1B valuation in February 2026. Claude is part of the Enterprise engine list, which runs to up to nine engines.

Best for: Enterprises that need a compliance posture on paper before they can buy.

Nightwatch is worth a look if you want Claude mentions next to the keyword rankings you already track and you are not paying for one of the large SEO suites. AI tracking is included in the plan price.

Best for: SEO teams that want one dashboard for classic rankings and Claude.

If Claude is one engine among several and you need to know what to fix, choose the tool with source analysis. If Claude is the only new engine and budget decides, Rankscale and Nightwatch cost the least. If you have a security review to pass, start with Profound or Peec AI's Enterprise plan. If you have no Claude coverage at all and cannot add a vendor, a few tools let you connect your own Anthropic API key, and Anthropic then bills you directly for the runs.

Does Claude cite the same sources as ChatGPT?

Often not, and that is the practical reason to track it. Being named in one engine tells you little about the other.

How do I turn Claude tracking results into something I can act on?

Look for source data, not only a mention rate. A mention rate says whether Claude names you, while source data shows which pages Claude relied on and where competitors are cited and you are not. Peec AI classifies every cited page, reports the gaps and ranks recommended actions from them, and AthenaHQ goes a step further by drafting follow-up content. Tools that stop at mention counts tell you where you stand but not what to change.
