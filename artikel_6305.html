I’ve held the (see rates and fees) for eight years, and despite carrying more than 25 credit cards, it’s one I’ll continue renewing year after year.

The reason is simple: World of Hyatt is my favorite hotel loyalty program, and this card makes it even more rewarding to stay with the brand. Between an annual free night, automatic elite status and the ability to earn valuable Hyatt points on spending, I consistently receive far more value than the card’s affordable $95 annual fee.

It isn’t the right fit for everyone. Hyatt has a much smaller global footprint than Marriott, Hilton and IHG, so travelers who rarely stay at Hyatt hotels may be better served by a more flexible travel rewards card. However, if Hyatt is already part of your travel plans — or you’d like it to become one — the World of Hyatt card is one of the best mid-tier hotel cards on the market. Card rating*: ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Unlike cards that earn transferable rewards, this is very much a Hyatt-focused product. If you’re loyal to Hyatt — or want an affordable way to accelerate your progress toward higher status — it deserves serious consideration.

If there’s one reason why I continue paying the World of Hyatt card’s annual fee, it’s the anniversary free night certificate.

Each cardholder anniversary, you’ll receive a free night award valid at any Category 1-4 Hyatt hotel or resort. Since Hyatt prices these properties between 3,000 and 25,000 points per night, there’s plenty of opportunity to extract well over $95 in value. Free nights expire 12 months after issuance.

I’ve redeemed my certificates at The Standard, Brussels, Hyatt House Manchester in the U.K. and Andaz Mexico City Condesa. In each case, paying cash would have cost considerably more than the card’s annual fee.

Even better, you can earn a second Category 1-4 certificate after spending $15,000 on the card in a calendar year.

That’s the spending threshold I personally target each year. It unlocks another free night and an additional six elite night credits toward Hyatt status for even more value beyond the points you'll earn from that spending.

Simply holding the card gives you complimentary Discoverist status.

While Discoverist isn’t Hyatt’s most rewarding elite tier, I still appreciate benefits like preferred room upgrades and late checkout (when available) and a 10% bonus on eligible Hyatt spending.

That said, for frequent Hyatt guests, this perk probably won’t be all that enticing since they’re likely to earn Discoverist status organically. The card becomes more valuable when working toward a higher status level.

Every calendar year, you’ll receive five qualifying night credits automatically, and you’ll earn another two qualifying night credits for every $5,000 spent.

I’ve found those elite night credits incredibly valuable. By combining the five annual elite nights from the card with my Hyatt stays and strategic spending to earn an additional six nights, I qualified for top-tier Globalist status in August 2025, which I’ll enjoy through February 2027.

One of my favorite things about the World of Hyatt program is that earning and redeeming points is refreshingly straightforward.

Unlike most major hotel loyalty programs, Hyatt still publishes an award chart, making award pricing more predictable.

When you stay at Hyatt hotels, these earnings stack with the points you earn as a World of Hyatt member. Since cardholders receive complimentary Discoverist status, you’ll also earn a 10% bonus on your base points, meaning you can earn up to 9.5 total points per dollar spent on eligible Hyatt purchases. That represents a solid 16% return on Hyatt purchases, per our November 2026 valuations.

I generally use this card for Hyatt stays and tax payments that help me reach the $15,000 annual threshold for the second free night certificate.

Otherwise, I typically use my  (see rates and fees) for most travel and dining purchases, then transfer those Chase Ultimate Rewards points to my Hyatt account whenever I need to top up my balance for an award stay.

That gives me the best of both worlds: I earn more rewards on everyday travel and dining while keeping my points flexible until I’m ready to book. If I don’t end up needing Hyatt points, I can always transfer them to one of Chase’s 13 other airline or hotel partners instead.

In my opinion, Hyatt points remain among the most valuable hotel points due to their flexibility.

Award nights start at just 3,000 points for a Category 1 property at the Lowest rate and range up to 75,000 points for a top-tier Category 8 property under Hyatt’s five-tier standard award chart.

Despite being one of my favorite hotel credit cards, the won’t be the right fit for every traveler.

The biggest drawback is Hyatt’s relatively small footprint. While Hyatt has more than 1,500 hotels and all-inclusive properties across 80-plus countries, that’s still far fewer than Marriott or Hilton. If you frequently travel to destinations without a Hyatt, you may struggle to maximize the card’s benefits.

Those who prioritize earning the highest possible rewards on everyday spending will likely find better options elsewhere. Beyond Hyatt purchases and spending toward the $15,000 threshold for a second free night certificate, this card isn’t particularly rewarding for dining, travel or everyday purchases.

Finally, travelers looking for premium perks like airport lounge access, annual travel credits and luxury benefits across multiple hotel brands should consider a premium travel rewards card instead.

For most travelers, I think the personal World of Hyatt Credit Card is the better choice than the (see rates and fees).

Both cards help you earn Hyatt points and elite status, but the business version is designed primarily for business owners. It offers bonus categories geared toward business spending and additional benefits for companies, while the personal card provides better long-term value for most travelers, thanks to its annual Category 1-4 free night certificate.

By comparison, I think the World of Hyatt Business card's $199 annual fee is harder to justify year after year because it lacks an anniversary free night. It does offer up to $100 in Hyatt statement credits each anniversary year, but that requires Hyatt spending and there's no way to get outsized value like you might with a free night.

Unless you own a business and can maximize the business-specific earning categories, I’d start with the personal card.

The currently offers new cardholders four free nights (at Category 1-4 Hyatt hotels) after spending $5,000 on purchases in the first three months from account opening.

These are hotels that would ordinarily require 3,000 to 25,000 points per night, depending on the property and stay dates. Someone who uses all four free night certificates at the highest-priced properties would save up to 100,000 World of Hyatt points, making this offer worth up to $1,650 (based on TPG valuations).

This is a very strong bonus compared to other historical welcome offers, though not the absolute all-time highest offer for new cardmembers. The best-ever welcome offer included five free-night awards (offer no longer available). Having said that, if you max out the four certificates, this would be considered the most lucrative offer since February.

Despite being a great offer right now, some travelers may prefer to wait until the introductory bonus is awarded in points instead. Unlike free nights, there are no hotel category restrictions for redeeming points. The expiration date for free nights is also stricter.

I’ve carried the World of Hyatt Credit Card for eight years, and it’s one of the few hotel credit cards I have no intention of canceling.

The annual Category 1-4 free night certificate has consistently offset the card’s $95 annual fee, while the elite night credits helped me qualify for Globalist status last year. Add in Hyatt’s strong redemption value, and it’s easy to see why this remains my favorite mid-tier hotel credit card.

If you regularly stay at Hyatt properties, I think the World of Hyatt card deserves a place in your wallet. If you rarely stay with Hyatt, though, you’ll probably get more long-term value from a flexible travel rewards card.
