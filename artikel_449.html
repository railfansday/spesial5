Whats new to streaming this week? (Nov. 27, 2026)

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Don't be overwhelmed or waste an hour scrolling through your services to determine what to watch. We've got your back, whatever your mood. Mashable offers watch guides for all of the above, broken down by genre: comedy, thriller, horror, documentary, and animation, among others. But if you're seeking something brand new (or just new to streaming), we've got you covered there, too.

Apple TV's Way of the Warrior Kid focuses on struggling middle schooler Marc (Jude Hill), whose classmates bully him relentlessly. When his Navy SEAL uncle Jake (Chris Pratt) comes to spend the summer with him and his mom Sarah (Linda Cardellini), Marc will get a chance to learn how to defend himself.

To help his nephew, Jake puts together "Operation Warrior Kid." Based on Jake's own military training, his program is less about teaching Marc to fight, and more about teaching him to embrace true courage. Along the way, Jake will wrestle with demons of his own, as both he and Marc discover their own bravery.* — Belen Edwards, Entertainment Reporter

Starring: Chris Pratt, Jude Hill, Linda Cardellini, Levi McConaughey, Ava Torres, Darien Sills-Evans, Carl McDowell, Parker Young

How to watch: Way of the Warrior Kid is now streaming on Apple TV.

In 2010, Aaron Sorkin wrote The Social Network and David Fincher directed. For its sort-of sequel, The Social Reckoning, Sorkin writes and directs, while Jeremy Strong takes over the role of Facebook founder Mark Zuckerberg from Jesse Eisenberg. And all of this means a finger-wagging film that's more frustrating than revealing.

Based on investigative reporter Jeff Horwitz's The Facebook Files, the film stars The Bear's Jeremy Allen White and Anora's Oscar-winning Mikey Madison as Horwitz and whistleblower Frances Haugen, respectively. But the title is a bit of a misnomer, because what reckoning?

Sorkin's political idealism is just one of his vexing indulgences, alongside his nostalgia for antiquated gender dynamics. In my review, I denounced The Social Reckoning as "misogynistic" and "unfunny," concluding, "Sorkin's dusty devices, problematic archetypes, and cringeworthy dialogue make the film feel instantly dated. Scroll on." — Kristy Puchko, Entertainment Editor

Starring: Mikey Madison, Jeremy Allen White, Wunmi Mosaku, Betty Gilpin, Billy Magnussen, Bill Burr, and Jeremy Strong

How to watch: The Social Reckoning is now available on digital.

Having been a Hollywood star for decades, Rob Lowe's filmography meets people in different eras, whether you saw his 1980s Brat Pack breakout roles in The Outsiders and St. Elmo's Fire, revered his '90s smarminess in Wayne's World, or followed him down 2000s West Wing and 2010s Parks and Recreation hallways.

Being an 18-year-old breakout star in '80s Tinseltown proved quite the start for Lowe, the focus of Netflix's new documentary directed by Chris Smith (Tiger King, Sr., Fyre). Featuring interviews with Lowe as well as famous friends like Gwyneth Paltrow and Demi Moore, the film focuses on Lowe's chaotic life in Hollywood, through ups, downs, scandal, and stardom. — Shannon Connellan, Senior Editor

How to watch: Rob Lowe: The Outsider is now streaming on Netflix.

'Twas the night before Christmas, when all through the house, demons invaded, so GTFO. That's essentially the set-up for On the Last day of Christmas, Paramount+'s festive horror film written and directed by Kevin Arbouet. Set in an apartment in Baton Rouge, Louisiana, the film sees Queen Sugar's Tanyell Waivers as a mother whose yuletide celebrations with her teen son (Way of the Warrior Kid's Ellis Hobbs IV) are interrupted by sinister entities. — S.C.

Starring: Tanyell Waivers, Ellis Hobbs IV, David Jensen, and Brandon Gilpin

How to watch: On the Last Day of Christmas debuts on Paramount+ on Nov. 27.

The story of Karen Read began on Jan. 29, 2022, when her boyfriend John O'Keefe was found dead in the snow outside of the home of a friend. As a well-respected member of the Boston Police Department, O'Keefe was mourned by friends, family, and Boston as a whole. But when it came to how he died, two camps emerged.

One — including the Boston PD — alleged he was killed in a hit and run, by his girlfriend Karen Read, and left to die in the snow. The other — spurred on by a podcaster known as Turtleboy — alleged that Read was being framed by O'Keefe's colleagues in an elaborate cover-up. This three-part Netflix documentary series from Danielle Johnson Cutts delves into both theories through interviews with O'Keefe's family, friends, and the most notorious investigator attached to the case. However, while Cutts includes plenty of archival interviews, she does not have new interviews with Read or Turtleboy, who both declined to participate in the doc. Cutts' investigation is thoughtful, thorough, and balanced. But don't expect big twists like Netflix's Unknown Number or outrageous interviews like The Crash. — K.P.

How to watch: The Trials of Karen Read is now streaming on Netflix.

Here's a documentary you'll want to watch with the volume loud and proud.

Directed by Douglas Tirola, New Wave is a four-part documentary series delving into the rise of the eponymous music genre from 1977 to 1987 and beyond. Its music was bouncy, bizarre, and massively influential. To explore New Wave's past, present, and future, Tirola brings together a jaw-dropping lineup of musicians for interview. Look at the featuring list below, for all the reason you need to tune in and bliss out. — K.P.

Starring: Andy Bell (Erasure), Andy McCluskey (OMD), Annabella Lwin (Bow Wow Wow), Billy Idol, Bob Geldof (Boomtown Rats), Brian Ritchie (Violent Femmes), Brian Setzer (Stray Cats), Chris Frantz (Talking Heads, Tom Tom Club), Curt Smith (Tears for Fears), Danny Elfman (Oingo Boingo), Dave Wakeling (English Beat), Fred Schneider, Glenn Tilbrook (Squeeze), Jim Kerr (Simple Minds), John Taylor (Duran Duran), Johnny Marr (The Smiths), Kate Pierson (The B-52s), Kathy Valentine (The Go-Go's), Mark Goodman (MTV), Mark Mothersbaugh (Devo), Martha Davis (The Motels), Martha Quinn (MTV), Matt Pinfield (music journalist), Miles Copeland (IRS Records music executive), Richard Blade (DJ), Stewart Copeland (The Police), and Thomas Dolby

How to watch: New Wave is now streaming on MGM+.

With Andrew Tate and his brother Tristan currently in U.S. jail fighting extradition to the UK on charges of rape and sex trafficking, Andrew Tate: Toxic Empire delves into the backstory of the manosphere influencer. Cutting between archive footage of the Tate brothers and interviews with people in his circle and alleged victims, the four-part series from director/producer team Julian Hobbs and Elli Hakami explores how the Tates made their money — and the allegations they're currently facing. — Sam Haysom, Editor, General Assignments

How to watch: Andrew Tate: Toxic Empire is streaming on HBO Max, with new episodes on Wednesdays.

Enemies-to-lovers Christmas rom-com with a job cuts edge, anyone? Michelle Randolph and Chris Briney lead Prime Video's Clashing Through the Snow as publishing editors Hannah and Ryan, who are fighting over one career-saving contract (nothing like redundancies in media to celebrate the holidays, right?) — and they've got to make it to New York in time. So, of course they'll be snowed under at the airport! Of course they'll have to share one rental car to meet deadline! Of course there'll be small towns en route to the big city to make them remember what Christmas is all about! Also, Paris Hilton and Julia Fox are in this, so there's something for everyone. — S.C.

Starring: Christopher Briney, Michelle Randolph, Lukas Gage, Paris Hilton, Julia Fox, and PJ Byrne

How to watch: Clashing Through the Snow is now on Prime Video.

Emma Fogarty and Colin Farrell's longtime friendship fuels Rachel Fleit's lauded documentary, The Slightest Touch, streaming on HBO Max this week. Living with Epidermolysis Bullosa (EB), Fogarty's condition means her skin is extremely fragile and can easily blister. However, she's determined to run the Dublin Marathon for her 40th birthday with her movie star friend, raising awareness for EB together. It's this important cause that sees Farrell inviting cameras into his personal life too, as Fleit explores this powerful, lasting friendship as parallel portraits. — S.C.

How to watch: The Slightest Touch is now streaming on HBO Max.

Sesame Street is back with its 57th season! This timeless series is never to be taken for granted. Each episode brings family-friendly fun in the form of games, animation, original music, celebrity guests, and plenty of laughs with our favorite Sesame Street residents. This new volume (Volume 4 on Netflix) also promises more adventures with Elmo, Abby, and Tango. — K.P.

How to watch: Sesame Street, Season 57 is now playing on Netflix.

Fans of religious horror with a side order of telekinetic power may want to mark this one in their calendars. — S.H.

Starring: Michele Riondino, Giulio Feltri, Paolo Pierobon, and Romana Maggiora Vergano

This is probably the most bonkers title on our list this week, so all hail Jennifer Lopez, a consummate entertainer.

A recording of her Las Vegas residency, The JLo Show features the American pop icon singing her hits, including "Jenny from the Block" and "I'm Real." But there's so much more. Surrounded by back-up dancers and snatched in the tightest, most glittery nude illusion bodysuits, Lopez also covers classics like "Let Me Entertain You" and recreates one of her big numbers from the bold musical bomb Kiss of the Spider-Woman. But that's still not all. Lopez also cracks jokes about her many marriages with an uncrackable face, and generally mythologizes herself through selected anecdotes. And that is still not all.

Performing numbers reminiscent of West Side Story and Chicago, JLo also lets Broadway know what they're missing. Much like her wild autobiographical visual album This is Me...Now, The JLo Show offers Lopez doing too much, and being absolutely dazzling all the while. — K.P.

How to watch: The JLo Show debuts on Paramount+ on Nov. 26.

Remember how Curtis "50 Cent" Jackson produced a doc about Diddy that made the public reconsider the 1996 murder of Tupac Shakur (among other things)? Well, if you binge-watched Netflix's Sean Combs: The Reckoning, we wager you're already seated for The Tupac Tapes with Angie Martinez.

This one focuses on Radio Hall of Famer and New York Times bestselling author Angie Martinez, as she looks back on her taped interview with Shakur, recorded in his Los Angeles home months before his death. In this four-part video podcast, Martinez plays portions of the tapes never heard before by the public. Plus, she discusses Shakur's life, legacy, and death with guests like Charlamagne Tha God, Joe Budden, Big Boy, Dream Hampton, RZA, and Wallo. — K.P.

How to watch: The Tupac Tapes with Angie Martinez debuts on Netflix on Nov. 27.

It can be hard to get scientific research funded. And in writer-director Alex Goyette's horror Breeder, no one knows this better than MIT dropout Russell (Daniel Doheny). But it seems like his luck has changed when he finds an investor in Patti Kolb (Glee's Dot-Marie Jones), an affluent breeder of minature poodles. There's just one catch. She wants to meet him. In person. At her remote ranch. Where she's got a fleet of young women in residence.

As you might guess from this setup (or the trailer above), Kolb isn't only interested in breeding dogs. And her methods for studs that don't meet her expectations are...harsh. It's little wonder that this mean indie has been gathering praise all summer long as it hit the film festival circus. Now you can see what horror lovers have been barking about. — K.P.

Starring: Daniel Doheny, Dot-Marie Jones, Maddie Phillips, and Tanaya Beatty

How to watch: Breeder is now available on digital.

Romanian filmmaker Cristian Mungiu was previously best known for his 2007 drama 4 Months, 3 Weeks and 2 Days. However, after winning the Palme d'Or at this year's Cannes Film Festival for Fjord, he's finding a new international audience, with a little help from the MCU's Sebastian Stan.

With a shaved head and bushy beard, Stan is unrecognizable from his role as the Winter Soldier. Opposite Norwegian film star Renate Reinsve (The Worst Person in the World, Sentimental Value), he plays a soft-spoken Romanian engineer, who's moved his wife and children to Norway to be closer to her family. But cultural differences hit hard, as the family's evangelical Christian devotion is seen as suffocating and even abusive by the children's teachers. When child protective services step in, Fjord becomes part family-drama, part crime-drama, inviting audiences to make their own decisions about right and wrong. — K.P.

Starring: Sebastian Stan and Renate Reinsve

How to watch: Fjord is now available on digital.

Aside from holding titles from the World Boxing Council and the International Boxing Organization, British boxer Tyson Fury might be best known for his ferocity and endurance in the ring. However, his Netflix documentary, Tyson Fury, goes behind his tough exterior to reveal his tenderness.

Interviews with his fighter father John Fury reveal a trying childhood, as well as the shocking revelation that challenged Tyson's idea of his dad. Director Lucy Wilcox also interviews his wife, Paris Fury, who gives insights into the boxer as a supportive partner and loving father. Wilcox's approach to Tyson is compassionate, giving him space to be vulnerable as he talks about his trauma, his self-esteem issues, and his struggles with suicidal ideation. And yet, she doesn't avoid touchy topics, like the public backlash over his comments about homosexuality and women. So, while this is an empathetic portrait of the fighter, it's also as hard-hitting as its subject. — K.P.

Starring: Tyson Fury, Paris Fury, and John Fury

How to watch: Tyson Fury debuts on Netflix on Nov. 27.

If you haven't had an entanglement with Wicker in regular unmoving cinemas or in rumbling 4DX, this is your chance to log it. Based on Ursula Wills-Jones' short story "The Wicker Husband," this raunchy, hilarious, heartwarming fairy tale sees a fisherwoman (Olivia Colman) requesting a basket weaver (Peter Dinklage) to literally make her a husband in order to spite the local townspeople (led by Elizabeth Debicki). And it works, with Alexander Skarsgård brought to life as a dashing wicker man.

"It's a deeply silly, fairy tale concept," I wrote in my review. "But what directors Alex Huston Fischer and Eleanor Wilson have up their sleeve is a hilarious and heartfelt script, an incredibly talented cast, and magical production design...It's an artisanal Frankenstein tale, and one that will melt that brittle basket-woven heart of yours — or simply have you thinking about Taylor Swift's "Wood" differently." — S.C.

Starring: Olivia Colman, Alexander Skarsgård, Peter Dinklage, and Elizabeth Debicki

How to watch: Wicker is now available on digital.

Amy Poehler reunites with Parks and Recreation co-creator Mike Schur for Dig, a charming sitcom based on Kate Myers's novel Excavations. Poehler plays Elise, lead archaeologist on a Grecian archaeological dig. When she makes the discovery of a lifetime, she'll team up with a crew of fellow passionate archaeologists to uncover it in secret, far from the prying eyes of egotistical dig head Neville (Hugh Laurie).

While not always laugh-out-loud funny, Dig is a perfect warm and cozy watch, not to mention a wonderful character study of its wonderful core ensemble, made up of Poehler, Laurie, Geraldine Viswanathan, Antonia Thomas, and Fina Strazza. As I wrote in my review, "despite the flashy location and a new mystery twist, Dig retains the core formula that made Parks and Recreation so irresistible: investing deeply in a lovable new crew of unlikely friends." — B.E.

Starring: Amy Poehler, Hugh Laurie, Geraldine Viswanathan, Antonia Thomas, and Fina Strazza

How to watch: Dig premieres Nov. 23 on Peacock, with new episodes on Mondays.

You've never seen Blade Runner like you will in Blade Runner 2099. The TV series upends the hierarchy of the prior Blade Runner films entirely. Following a brutal uprising, replicants are now in control of humanity. Now, replicant blade runners are in charge of hunting rogue humans — among these is Cora (Hunter Schafer). Tired of running, she undertakes a dangerous mission that forces her to pose as a blade runner. She'll soon cross paths with blade runner Olwen (Michelle Yeoh), a replicant with only days left to live. In those final days, she and Cora will search for a group of runaway replicants on the hunt for a secret that can change the world.

Yeoh and Schafer are a match made in heaven, each handling Blade Runner 2099's action with eternal cool. Their unofficial third co-star is the world around them, brought to brilliant life by the production team. Imposing pyramids and seedy shops shine under neon lighting and threatening dust clouds, while Vangelis-esque synths usher us into a sense of cyberpunk noir. It's a towering technical achievement fronted by two exceptional leads, and a worthy addition to the Blade Runner franchise. — B.E.

Starring: Michelle Yeoh, Hunter Schafer, Dimitri Abold, Lewis Gribben, Katelyn Rose Downey, Daniel Rigby, Johnny Harris, Amy Lennox, Sheila Atim, Matthew Needham, Tom Burke, and Maurizio Lombardi

How to watch: Blade Runner 2099 premieres Nov. 25 on Prime Video.

Arguably the most talked-about movie of the year has landed on digital. And you better believe that's going to mean a fresh round of memes, spoofs, debates, and obsession.

When the teaser for You Can See Everything first hit, it seemed like a joke. Canadian comedian Nathan Fielder — of all people — interviewing notorious Theranos founder Elizabeth Holmes in her home, ahead of her prison sentence? How could that be a real movie? The critics were raving (me included). But audiences did too, taking this A24 doc to record-breaking heights, and turning Holmes and her romantic partner Billy Evans into a national conversation. It's buzz has even reached space.

We're still saying the best way to see You Can See Everything is in theaters. But we're also giddy at the chance to be able to pause, rewind, and geek out with friends on a whole new level now that we have the remote. — K.P.

Starring: Nathan Fielder, Elizabeth Holmes, and Billy Evans

How to watch: You Can See Everything is available for purchase on Prime Video.
