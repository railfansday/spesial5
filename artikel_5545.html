Can the new Congress help lower mortgage rates?

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

After a Democratic midterm win, lawmakers may try to ease drivers of inflation tied to oil, AI investment, tariffs, and jobs. The Fed is likely to respond mainly through how these factors move the 10-year yield and inflation expectations.  AI Summary

After the Democrats’ historic midterm win, the question now is: can the new Congress help get mortgage rates lower? Obviously, the Iran conflict and trade war have taken their toll on President Trump’s approval ratings, but can Congress — both Democrats and Republicans — now work together on getting something done that the President will not veto?

Let’s look at the economic factors influencing rates in this environment, what Congress can do about each, and how the Fed might consider these as they set monetary policy.

When WTI oil was at $112 on April 7 this year, the 10-year yield was at 4.31% — but back then the Fed was still talking about cutting rates two to three times. A lot has changed since then.The Federal Reserve has made the Iran conflict a big talking point, so some type of resolution to this conflict could impact rates. Obviously, the conflict has hurt President Trump and the Republicans; it’s very unpopular, especially with farmers and truckers who have to deal with high diesel prices. Trump himself acknowledged the war played a major part in the midterm results.

Congress made some attempts to try to end this conflict in the past, but may have renewed incentives now to find a solution. We shall see what comes next, as President Trump has said he could make a deal after the midterms.

Now, the AI boom is a little bit more complicated because there are a lot of projects still in the pipeline that can’t be shut down by the Democrats right now, but future projects could definitely be affected. And with some type of regulation and moratorium, some of the growth prospects might be slowed down.

This is a critical factor because the Federal Reserve has really made the AI boom and inflation a hot topic. It’s part of the reason why, when you see AI softness — whether it’s the credit markets or their stock prices getting hit — bond traders tend to want to buy bonds, not sell them, in that environment. For now, the Fed needs to see a material slowdown to lose its hawkish stance on AI, but it would be another positive for rates.

On another note, the labor productivity data was awesome today. This data really affects how the Fed sees AI and will be the best news for mortgage rates if this trend continues. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh himself has made a dovish case around AI as long as it creates more productivity and lowers labor unit costs.

Here is the chart of the 10-year yield for the last six months as of this second, the 10-year yield is down once again to 4.83% as AI stocks are selling off.

This will be very interesting because I am sure President Trump will try every legal angle to continue his tariff policy — he still wants to have tariffs because it’s his favorite tool to cut deals. However, what the midterms have done is limit the power he can have to escalate the trade war into something worse. That can be a very big positive: we might have seen the high of the 10-year yield at 5.35% if the trade war doesn’t get worse amid the conflict with Iran.

For now, we need to wait and see how much farther the President wants to push the trade war. He might just cut a deal with Canada, leave other countries alone and just focus on China — which would be a positive for rates.

Usually, in an economic cycle, when manufacturing jobs and construction jobs are being lost, it’s not a good sign for the labor market: we saw that in 2025. But the labor market has improved this year and that has kept the Fed’s focus fully on inflation.

One thing to think about going forward is that, if we do have some data center moratoriums and rates stay elevated, construction labor might get hit a little bit harder than before the midterms. If the unemployment rate rises as growth slows down, that will be a positive for mortgage rates.

For rates to really make a meaningful move lower and stick, three of the four things above need to get better. You need to get oil prices trading between $67 and $82. You need to get diesel prices down so the Fed won’t talk about energy prices being passed onto the consumer. You’ve got to de-escalate the trade war 2.0 and get some deals in and not make it inflationary.

The AI moratorium and regulations will be a welcome signal to the Federal Reserve; whether that impacts the construction data enough to matter to them is still an open question, but the three things above need to occur to have rates go under 7% and stay there.
