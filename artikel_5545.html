S&P 500 futures are flat as traders brace for the October CPI report

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Fed Governor Christopher Waller said a December rate hike is likely, while Dow futures rose 0.3% and Nasdaq-100 futures slipped 0.4%

S&P 500 futures barely budged Tuesday while market participants waited for October's CPI release, set for 8:30 a.m. ET.

Futures tied to the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 156 points, or 0.3%. Nasdaq $NDAQ-100 futures slipped 0.4%.

Economists polled by Dow Jones expect headline CPI to have risen 0.3% from the prior month and core CPI, which excludes food and energy costs, to have risen 0.2%, according to CNBC. In September, headline CPI climbed 0.6%, while the core reading rose 0.3%.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics will not provide annual figures in the report. The 2025 government shutdown prevented the agency from gathering certain information, so there is nothing to compare against.

Fed Governor Christopher Waller said a rate hike in December looks probable to him, and he did not rule out a bigger half-point move while price pressures remain stubborn. "While the precise timing of the hikes doesn't matter, ensuring that the promised hikes are implemented in a reasonable period of time does," Waller said, according to CNBC.

Traders see a quarter-point hike next month as all but certain, according to The Wall Street Journal. Waller's remarks pushed up Treasury yields.

Brent crude fell after President Donald Trump described renewed strikes on Iran as "quite unlikely."

In Europe, the pan-European Stoxx 600 was 0.6% higher. The U.K.’s FTSE 100 added 0.3%, France’s CAC 40 rose 0.9% and Germany’s DAX gained 0.9%.

In Asia, South Korea’s Kospi surged 4.3% and Japan’s Nikkei 225 added 2.9%. Hong Kong’s Hang Seng Index rose 0.4%, mainland China’s CSI 300 added 0.3% and Australia’s S&P/ASX 200 gained 1.3%.

On Monday, the Dow gained 1,104 points, or 2.2%. The S&P 500 rose about 2.4% and the Nasdaq Composite climbed 2.7%. The rally followed a choppy week when investors fretted that construction of new data centers could slow after the Democrats' strong midterm showing, according to CNBC.
