Trump administration plans to name Nvidia among U.S. AI 'national champions'

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Designated companies can receive expedited chip export licenses, overseas government support and federal financing

Samuel Boivin / NurPhoto via Getty Images

The Trump administration intends to give “national champions” status in artificial intelligence to more than two dozen companies, Nvidia $NVDA among them, according to The Wall Street Journal, citing unnamed sources.

Amazon $AMZN, Microsoft $MSFT, OpenAI and Anthropic are set to receive the designation as well, according to The Wall Street Journal. The program is intended to boost American AI companies' chances of landing foreign customers and to strengthen their position against competitors from China.

Companies given the designation can get faster approval of licenses to export chips and other technology, along with federal financing and government backing for their work abroad, according to The Wall Street Journal. The program is meant to bundle American chips, AI models and related offerings into a single package that foreign buyers can acquire all at once.

Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick is expected to unveil the program, according to The Wall Street Journal. The announcement is due in the coming week, though the timing might shift, The Wall Street Journal said. The program was first announced roughly a year ago, yet no firms had been selected for it.

The Wall Street Journal said the plan signals that the administration is pressing forward on its technology agenda even as Nvidia is in upheaval. Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang’s relationship with a woman suspected of spying for China has renewed demands from hawks that national security take priority over market share, including through tighter chip export controls.

Lutnick has faced criticism that the Commerce Department failed to close loopholes that let Chinese companies reach high-end Nvidia chips, according to The Wall Street Journal. The department is preparing to advance new regulations meant to curb the rerouting of chips to China, The Wall Street Journal said.

Lutnick agreed to appear before the Senate Banking Committee to answer questions about Nvidia's chip exports to China. The committee has asked him to testify on Dec. 10 and Huang on Dec. 3.

Huang stepped down as Nvidia CEO last week, and CFO Colette Kress is serving as interim CEO. Huang has admitted to a relationship with a former executive of Singapore-based Megaspeed who is suspected of spying for China. Nvidia's board has opened an independent investigation.
