7 Home Organizing Trends That Will Be EVERYWHERE in 2027, According to Pros

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Your clutter-free future starts with what you already own.

Although trends prevail in the interior design world, they also can find their way into other areas beyond decor—such as home organization. Creating orderly spaces can make life a lot easier. While tried-and-true solutions are still around, there are always fresh ways to tackle organizing projects.

As we look to the year ahead, four professional organizers are here to share what they think will be the top organizing trends.

While AI has been all over the place, from work to our personal lives—it appears to be tapering off, according to Lenora O’Keith, decluttering and organizing expert and owner of Aronel Home.

“AI and smart home solutions were everywhere at the beginning of the year, but there’s definitely been a lot of AI fatigue and a push for ‘dumb tech’ lately,” she shares. “When working with clients, a lot of them are over AI being added into everything. They just want simple solutions that work.” This can include things like setting up a household command center and labeling storage containers.

Moving has traditionally been a stressful experience that you were expected to figure out on your own—but that may be changing. Allison Flinn, owner of Reclaim Professional Organizing, says that more people are taking advantage of organizing services when they move.

“You can pack and unpack, but you won't truly have a fully functional and an organized new home if you haven't decluttered prior to moving or aren't intentional during the unpacking process by implementing a system that truly works for your lifestyle,” she says.

Instead of doing what a lot of us do—devising storage systems for each individual space—2027 may welcome the idea that you should treat your home as an entire unit to be organized.

Michelle Urban, owner and founder of The Organized House, says that people are increasingly not treating each room as its own one-off organizing project. She says that instead, they’re looking at how belongings move from one space to another and where they need to be throughout the day. For instance, items like backpacks, shoes, and sports gear may land in the entry, get stored in the garage, and need to be back by the door the next morning.

“When those spaces are planned together, the system supports the family’s routine instead of just moving things from one pile to another,” Urban says.

Viewed more holistically, experts are also seeing that organizing isn’t just an occasional task, but something to stay on top of each week. “I think more homeowners will begin to view organization as ongoing home maintenance rather than a one-time project,” says Ashley Bailey, founder and professional organizer at Everything Orderly. “Just like other aspects of maintaining a home, organizing requires occasional attention as our lives and needs change.”

Flinn says that more and more people are outsourcing household tasks to a home manager or home concierge. In addition to handling things like doing laundry, returning packages, and making doctor’s appointments, these professionals can also help you organize your home in just a few hours a week.

Eco-friendly everything is still as popular as ever, and it even applies to how we organize our homes. While O’Keith says that sustainability never really goes out of style, homeowners are increasingly turning to eco-friendly organizing products—specifically, by avoiding plastic and embracing natural materials like bamboo, rattan, and sea grass for baskets and bins.

But she says that there is a exception for basements and attics. “I always recommend heavy-duty plastic storage [for these areas],” O’Keith says. “Since those spaces deal with dampness, temperature changes, and pests, other materials could get damaged easily.”

While it can be lovely to live in a well-designed, beautiful home, if there isn’t function, you might make your daily life more difficult. That’s why people are going beyond Instagram-ready interiors and are wrapping in plenty of function.

Urban believes that progressively more people are bringing functional storage into a room’s design, and it isn’t just an add-on.

“A credenza can hold games in the living room; a dining room buffet can store wrapping paper and party decor between celebrations,” Urban points out. “As rooms take on more jobs, people want storage that looks like it belongs and does a useful job.”

As we head into 2027, people are thinking about their budgets more and more, which is why using items you already have as storage solutions is cost-effective.

“I'm seeing more clients interested in making better use of what they already own rather than automatically purchasing new organizing products,” Bailey says. For instance, she recently took on a project that transformed a playroom, but used items the family already had.

“Sometimes the best solution isn't another organizing product. It's rethinking how you're using the space and what you already have,” she says. “I think we'll see more emphasis on thoughtful editing and repurposing before purchasing something new.”
