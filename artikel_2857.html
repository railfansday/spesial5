The plays that have set the tone for the Bengals’ defensive turnaround this season

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

It's no exaggeration to say Dexter Lawrence's sack against Tennessee Titans quarterback Cam Ward, late in the fourth quarter of Week 8, saved the Bengals' season.

On a day when the Bengals gave up 36 points and 440 yards, both season-highs for opposing offenses against the Bengals, the defense stepped up when they needed to. Lawrence's sack knocked the Titans out of field goal range inside the final minute of regulation with the score tied 36-36, and the Bengals capitalized in overtime with Joe Burrow running in a nine-yard touchdown on the Bengals' drive and the defense forcing a subsequent Titans four-and-out to close out the game.

Since that possession punctuated by Lawrence's sack, the Bengals' defense has been stellar. Against the Atlanta Falcons in Madrid, where the Falcons scored 10 points on their first two drives, the Bengals kept the Falcons off the scoreboard on six of their final seven possessions. Three of those six drives ended in punts, one ended in an interception, and another ended on a missed field goal.

This past Sunday against the Pittsburgh Steelers, the Bengals forced three-and-outs on the Steelers' first three drives, forcing five punts on 10 total Steelers drives. Cincinnati held Pittsburgh to 222 total yards Sunday night, a season-low for the Bengals' defense and just seven weeks after allowing 411 yards to the Steelers in Pittsburgh.

Going into Week 11, the Bengals' defense ranks No. 10 in scoring and No. 19 in yards allowed per game. They're allowing just 21.4 points per game and have just one game in their four games since the bye week where they've allowed more than 20 points.

Have there been some frustrating moments this season? Absolutely. Week 3 at Pittsburgh, Week 8 against Tennessee, and the first half at Baltimore in Week 7 were all frustrating and shades of 2024 and 2025.

But there have been a lot of bright moments so far this season that have set the tone for the Bengals' defensive turnaround. Going all the way back to Week 1, where the Bengals forced and recovered four fumbles and sacked Tampa Bay Buccaneers quarterback Baker Mayfield four times, there have been plays and games that have set the stage for the revived Bengals' defense to completely flip the narrative this season both locally and nationally.

Let's take a look at those key plays and games that have set the stage for the Bengals' defensive turnaround.

With 6:29 left in the first quarter, the Bengals trailed the Buccaneers 3-0. Tampa Bay drove 56 yards in 11 plays for a field goal on their opening drive, and the Bengals answered with a three-and-out. It wasn't a great start, especially with Mayfield and the Buccaneers' offense going right down the field on their opening drive.

But on 3rd&9 from the Buccaneers' own 33-yard line on Tampa Bay's second drive, Myles Murphy and Barrett Carter got to Mayfield with Murphy jarring the ball loose. Demetrius Knight Jr. scooped it up and raced 27 yards for the Bengals' first touchdown of the season, sending The Jungle into a frenzy.

Think about all three of the Bengals' defensive players who were involved in the play. Particularly Carter and Knight, who were much maligned as rookies in 2025. But on just their second possession of this season, Carter got to Mayfield and caused a fumble that Knight recovered and ran back for a touchdown. That set the tone for both Carter and Knight that they were going to play much faster this year and much more in a flow state.

Also, Murphy, who faced criticism over a lack of production for being the Bengals' first-round pick in 2023, finished this play by forcing the fumble. For a player who the Bengals decided not to pick up his fifth-year option, that play showed that Murphy is a vital part of the Bengals' deep wave of pass rushers.

Knight's scoop-and-score was the first of four fumbles the Bengals would force and recover on the day, and it was also their first of four sacks on Mayfield. The Bengals currently have a +3 turnover differential and 26 sacks through nine games this season. Carter has 74 tackles through nine games, and he's on pace for 140 this season, which would easily pass his 106 from 2025. He also has five tackles for loss, compared to just one last year, and he has five passes defensed and two forced fumbles.

Knight has 71 tackles and is on pace for 134 this season, easily surpassing his 106 from a year ago. He also has four tackles for loss this season, already equaling his total as a rookie. Murphy has 2.5 sacks, two tackles for loss, six quarterback hits, and a forced fumble. All three of these guys are having really good seasons. It was worth believing in them.

This game started as an offensive struggle on both sides. Then, though, the Texans' offense started to find some rhythm. On just their previous drive prior to Lawrence's 4th&1 stop, the Texans drove into the red zone. But a botched snap on a 34-yard field goal attempt denied the Texans the game's first score.

On their next drive, though, the Texans made it to the red zone again. This time, they faced a 4th&1 from the Bengals' 18-yard line. There was 6:59 left in the second quarter, and the Bengals had managed just 35 yards on their first four drives. But this is why the Bengals traded for Dexter Lawrence. It wasn't just to spark the defense; it was to spark the entire team.

The Texans ran a handoff to David Montgomery, ironically, a player they traded for this past offseason. Lawrence shot through the middle and stuffed Montgomery for a one-yard loss. After not showing up on the stat sheet a lot in Week 1, but still making a viable impact, Lawrence's stop on this 4th&1 was his first big-play moment with the Bengals. It was that feeling of 'Oh, this is why the Bengals went and got him.'

The Bengals answered by driving 81 yards in seven plays with Ja'Marr Chase hauling in a sensational 32-yard touchdown to put the Bengals up 7-0, swinging all of the momentum over to their side and taking the life out of the Reliant Stadium crowd.

Lawrence finished the game with three tackles and a tackle for loss, as well as a quarterback hit. The Bengals' defense finished with four sacks and eight tackles for loss, holding the Texans to just 60 yards rushing. Montgomery finished with just 10 yards on six carries.

The Bengals currently rank 21st in allowing 123.9 rushing yards per game. However, that's down from ranking dead last in 2025, allowing 147.1 rushing yards per game. There is marked improvement through nine games for the Bengals' run-defense, and Lawrence's 4th&1 stop in Houston showed his value and the Bengals' improvements in stopping the run. Lawrence currently has five sacks and nine tackles for loss through nine games, with 28 tackles, eight quarterback hits, and a fumble recovery.

Every Bengals fan is going to remember the game in Baltimore for Joe Burrow and the Bengals' offense exploding for 35 unanswered points in the second half, erasing a 13-0 halftime deficit for a 35-13 season-changing win. However, there's a defensive play from midway through the third quarter that stands out as another catalyst in the Bengals' defensive turnaround. It's not even the stop on 4th&2 on the first play of the fourth quarter or Demetrius Knight Jr.'s interception as the play I'm talking about.

The Bengals had just scored a touchdown on their first possession of the second half, cutting the deficit to 13-7. Momentum was starting to shift. But after allowing 258 yards to the Ravens in the first half, including going 8-11 on third down in the first 30 minutes, the Bengals now needed to flip the script on defense. On 2nd&7, Derrick Henry took a handoff to the right side. Mind you, when Derrick Henry takes a handoff to the perimeter, good luck. Especially considering the Bengals' run defense had not been good on the perimeter up to this point in the season.

But on this play, Jordan Battle, who is this Bengals' defense's most polarizing player, met Henry, took away his center of gravity, and pile-drove him backward four yards. Cashius Howell sacked Ravens' quarterback Tyler Huntley on the next play to force a three-and-out. The Bengals took a 14-13 lead on their next possession, and they never looked back from there.

Battle has clearly had his ups and downs this season. But his highs are incredible. Battle leads the Bengals with three interceptions and has eight passes defensed. He also has a forced fumble and 58 tackles this season.

Lawrence's crucial sack the next week against Tennessee proved that this defense is different. That game was wild and weird. The Bengals fell behind 13-0, then scored 21 unanswered points to lead 21-13. I thought the Bengals were in business at that point. They had weathered a slow start to take an eight-point lead. But the NFL is wild and weird sometimes.

Suddenly, this game became a shootout. The Bengals prevailed 43-36 in overtime. And even on a day when the Bengals' defense gave up a lot of points and a lot of yards, they made more than enough plays to win the game. It was a complete 180 from 2024 and 2025, when the Bengals were in shootout games. The Bengals of the last four years, the four years since their Super Bowl run in 2021, don't win the wild and weird games. The 2026 Bengals win these games.

Something that may get overlooked in this game is that the Titans only got three points off Joe Burrow's three interceptions. Those three points came off his first interception, when the Titans took a 13-0 lead late in the first quarter. After Burrow's second interception, with the Bengals leading 14-13 early in the third quarter, the Bengals' pass rush finally got to Cam Ward for the first time on the day.

Boye Mafe met him for a strip-sack, which Dexter Lawrence recovered and rumbled to the 17-yard line to set the Bengals up in the red zone. On the Bengals' first play of their ensuing drive, Burrow hit Tee Higgins for a 17-yard touchdown. Disaster turned into extending the Bengals' lead. Both sides picked each other up and complemented each other. Beautiful football. Mafe currently has 6.5 sacks and two forced fumbles, along with 12 quarterback hits. He's worth every dollar the Bengals paid him in free agency.

Burrow's third interception, where he underthrew Ja'Marr Chase, wasn't good. It caused tensions to rise on the Bengals' sideline. But two plays into the Titans' ensuing drive, with the score tied 21-21, Demetrius Knight Jr. shot into the backfield to tackle Titans running back Tony Pollard in the end zone for a safety. It gave the Bengals a 23-21 lead. Two points in that game were crucial, and it was the second time in the third quarter the Bengals' defense picked up the offense after a turnover.

This is what Bengals fans clamored for in 2024 and 2025. They begged for a defense like the one the Bengals have in 2026. Not just an average defense, but a defense that can change games. That's what this Bengals' defense is doing through nine games. Monday night, they'll head into Washington looking to avenge a loss to the Commanders from two years ago on Monday Night Football in Cincinnati. That game was the first sign that season the Bengals' defense may not be good. Two years later, the Bengals' defense is in a much better place. They've turned the corner. Now, they're ready to rev their engine alongside the Bengals' offense, which could potentially take this team to Los Angeles in February.
