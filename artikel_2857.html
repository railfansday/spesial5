49ers' 'Hulk' was a zero-star recruit. Now he's manhandling NFL linemen (paywall)"He didn't play football until sixth grade. He was a no-star recruit out of Monte Vista High School in Southern California, where he also wrestled for three years and won a California Interscholastic Federation state championship. In fact, Valdez said, "there was a point in high school that I enjoyed wrestling more than football."

Despite his success on the mat, he earned no collegiate wrestling offers. He did receive one in football, though, so he informed his parents — both Mexican immigrants — of his intentions to attend Montana State on a scholarship. Jesús and Norah Valdez struggled to grasp the concept.

"My parents never even, like — they didn't even understand how football works," Valdez said. "They didn't understand that I was gonna go away for school and that it was free."

They eventually understood, and Valdez went off to Bozeman for his freshman campaign under a new and unfamiliar coaching staff. He played "pretty solid" as a fill-in defensive tackle as a freshman, he said, yet there was one problem.

In order to play defensive tackle, he needed to add weight. And a lot of it.

Howe instructed Valdez, weighing around 235 pounds at the time, to eat like crazy, but in a controlled manner. "He didn't want me getting, like, fat," Valdez deadpanned. So, what did he eat to remain in an extreme caloric surplus as a college student living in the middle of Montana?

"Oh, man," Valdez said with a chuckle. "It was chicken and rice, chicken and rice. I can't even eat chicken and rice anymore. … It was just constantly that.

"Even my roommates would see me with a little scale to weigh food, and they were kind of like, 'What are you doing?' And I was just like, 'Man, trust. Like, I got to do this.' And it worked out. Gained the weight the right way. My speed, my strength got better. … I think my second year I was like 265."
