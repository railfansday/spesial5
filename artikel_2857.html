Brad Gilbert agrees with Rafael Nadal’s call for major tennis rule change after Jannik Sinner’s injury

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Rafael Nadal has called for tennis to introduce a two-year ranking system.

During a recent interview with 'AP News', Nadal suggested that a two-year ranking system would 'give the players a little bit more freedom to make better decisions', and that 'when you get injured, you [would] have more time to recover'.

Coco Gauff's former coach, Brad Gilbert, agrees with Nadal's proposal and has shared another rule change he'd like to see implemented. Gilbert made these comments while discussing Jannik Sinner's knee injury and potential surgery, which could keep him sidelined for the foreseeable future.

"Reading this morning, if this is reputable, they're talking about the meniscus for [Jannik] Sinner, and potentially having to have knee surgery. That's the trickiness of waiting, resting, rehabbing, because now if he has to have surgery, who knows how long he's out? Maybe he's out nine-ish months," said Gilbert on X.

"It's one of my all-time pet peeves, with all players having the protected ranking when they come back. I feel like, especially the top 10, top five players, even all players, I do feel like the protected ranking, great, it gets you in the tournament. I think it's a joke that Sinner, let's say he misses a full year, comes back and he's unseeded because he's unranked. I feel like you should be seeded with your protected ranking.

"This kind of adds to Rafael Nadal's point about changing the ranking system to two years. Because he felt like maybe he rushed back a few times from injury, it's a fascinating talking point with the rankings. But I definitely feel like with the protected rankings, you should be able to get seeded, so it doesn't screw up the draw."

Sinner may require meniscus surgery that could keep him out for several months next year. If Sinner returns after Wimbledon, he will do so unranked. The Italian would return with a protected ranking that would guarantee him entry into 9-12 singles tournaments, but he wouldn't be seeded.

Gilbert thinks that needs to change, suggesting events would be unbalanced if Sinner entered as an unseeded player. In theory, if Sinner returns after Wimbledon next year, he could play Alexander Zverev or Carlos Alcaraz in the first round of tournaments.

These are all hypotheticals, though, and the reality is we don't know how long Sinner will be out for. The Italian is reportedly against having surgery, but his medical team haven't ruled it out.

Sinner hasn't played a match since winning Wimbledon in July, and has since missed the US Open and the ATP Finals, where he was the two-time defending champion. He will finish 2026 as the world number two, but if Alcaraz wins in Turin, he will be at risk of dropping to third in early 2027.

The details of Sinner's injury and any potential surgery haven't been confirmed, but many believe he will be forced to miss at least the Australian Open if the problem can't be solved without going under the knife.

This year, Sinner played the Qatar Open shortly after the Australian Open, and could skip that event as well if he requires knee surgery this off-season. The Sunshine Double Masters 1000 events in Indian Wells and Miami begin a month later.

If he is fit to do so, he could either return to try and defend both titles, or rest up and start his 2027 campaign on clay, at the Monte Carlo Masters, another event he won this year.

More news will likely break over the coming days as Sinner and his team decide whether to go ahead with surgery or to rehab the injury instead. He is currently scheduled to return to tennis for the Six Kings Slam in Saudi Arabia this December, although it's unlikely that Sinner will be fit to compete for the multi-million-dollar prize this year.
