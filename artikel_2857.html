Raptors fall to defending champs in overtime

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The Toronto Raptors hosted the New York Knicks in the first of a home-and-home series tonight, a hard-fought, at times frustrating, and eventual narrow loss to the tune of 108-112. While this is their fourth straight loss, the seventh to a team over with.a better record, and another game where they gave up a double-digit lead, this wasn't a complete loss. Instead, it was an opportunity to see just how well the Raptors can play, making the defending champions sweat a little.

Scottie Barnes had a great first game back, barely missing a step as he found his shot from all over the floor, leading the team with 30 points, 9 boards, and 4 assists. Jamal Shead was undoubtedly Toronto's biggest difference maker though, with his intensity, speed, and facilitation shifting the tone of the game entirely in Toronto's favour every time he touched the floor. He contributed 19 points, 2 rebounds, and 4 assists. RJ, IQ, and Poeltl all chipped in double digit points as well.

Jalen Brunson played phenomenally for the Knicks, finishing with 28 points and 9 assists. OG Anunoby and Karl Anthony Towns contributed to the game as well to the tune of 21 points and 9 rebounds and 28 points and 17 rebounds, respectively. Landrey Shamet proved to be a solid bench option for New York, connecting well from long range and scoring 23 points including four 3s.

New York is still trying to find their way this year despite carrying over much of their championship roster from last year. Coach Mike Brown doesn't seem fazed in the slightest though, saying he's "happy where we are right now", recognizing the recent growing pains will better prepare them for the future. If they identify and deal with these problems now, they won't crop up as the playoffs. Especially important when you're defending a title.

Of course, for Knicks fans, some of the issues feel worth worrying about. The trifecta of their biggest concerns were things Toronto, at times, tried to capitalize on. Going into this game, New York is the slowest team in the League based on pace, with a far worse transition game than last year. Being able to get back and set up defensively is a huge asset to limit easy possessions, which actually leads to the Knicks' second problem. They are one of the worst shooting teams in the league right now, forcing contested shots in the paint rather than regrouping and making smart plays. This leads them to being the third-most blocked team in the NBA currently. Protecting the paint is crucial, Scottie and CMB the most capable of doing so. Finally, despite having some great individual defenders, collectively as a team they've underperformed, ranking 22nd and 27th in steals and blocks respectively. Taking advantage of their lacklustre defence through ball movement and finding the best shot should make the offence easy.

Scottie "our best player" Barnes was back in the lineup, a welcome sight for everyone. He took absolutely no time announcing his present, rolling off a screen and soaring in to dunk over defenders in the first possession of the game. He had 10 of their first 13 points, making a pair of long range shots in the process. An excellent indicator to his readiness to be back.

Collin Murray-Boyles got the start ahead of Poeltl, in an effort to match up better against Karl Anthony Towns who plays more as a stretch than a traditional centre. He did well, as KAT's early scoring was the result of switches that freed him from CMB.

Toronto certainly capitalized on those weaknesses early, allowing them to stay in the game despite their own turnovers, defensive lapses, and shooting struggles throughout the first quarter. They ended the frame 7-22 from the floor, but remained only two possessions behind the Knicks, 17-23.

Despite a low-scoring first quarter, there was no shortage of bodies flying to secure extra possessions when the ball was loose. Unsurprisingly, this started to translate into chippiness. Scottie Barnes almost dropped Tyler Kolek by ripping the ball from his hands. Alijah Martin thrived in this atmosphere, and was able to draw a flagrant foul from Jordan Clarkson likely from his peskiness.

The Knicks started to take their game outside the three-point line, finding a lot more success than inside the paint. New York gradually started to build a lead, but Toronto countered well, working to run them off the line and forced them to make unfavourable shots. At the same time, their own shots were starting to fall, allowing them to work their way back and eventually tie the game. Jamal Shead made his presence known with a pair of fast-break full-speed layups.

The Raptors continued to probe the paint and found more success than from beyond the arc but New York fought harder in the final minutes of the half, heading to the locker room with a narrow 47-43 lead.

The third quarter wasn't smooth sailing by any means. Unforced turnovers and miscommunications on the offensive cost the Raptors possessions. Toronto's defense kept them in the game through these slumps, forcing the Knicks to take tough shots that weren't falling for them. CMB was in foul trouble before the halfway point of the third quarter, forcing him to head to the bench, but in his absence, Poeltl managed to play solid defence in the middle.

The biggest shift came with the presence of Shead on the floor. He continued to push the pace in his minutes, forcing New York on their heels. Without a set defence, Shead was able to dictate the game, distributing to the open man but also making his own shots. In a departure to his last few games, where he was left open on the 3-point line, struggling to make shots, he feasted from long range, cashing in back-to-back buckets and forcing the Knicks to respect him. This energy gave Toronto their first significant advantage since the first frame, closing the third with a 74-67 lead.

Toronto showed flashes of disorganization again in the final frame, still working on their ability to finish games. New York weren't willing to yield and the Raptors weren't trying hard enough on every possession. Give-away rebounds that allowed the Knicks to get second and third chances were their undoing to an extent, leaving the door open for New York to get back into a game that they previously trailed by double-digits. A series of empty trips seemingly sucked all of the air out of the building until Scottie Barnes took over. The Raptors lead was back, going shot for shot with the Knicks in the final seconds, cashing in a game-tying three-pointer. Then he put his body on the line, tossing himself on the floor to trap the ball and prevent the Knicks from getting a final shot up, forcing OT.

Extra time matched the tone of the first 48 minutes of the game, with fans on their feet from start to finish. Scottie led offensively, CMB had a phenomenal block that sent the ball deep into the stands, and they were all tied up.

Inhuman shot-making by Brunson meant that the Raptors were once again behind. Clutch free-throws from Walter allowed Toronto to try and foul to regain possession, but they weren't able to make the final shot, submitting to the Knicks in overtime.

After the game, Darko attributed the loss to poor decision-making at points throughout the game, and especially in the final minutes. Shead echoed this, pointing to missed rebounds and lapses in judgement, saying "we played 44 minutes of good basketball", remarking that this is a growing experience for them, and all it will do is make them better.

They'll have their chance to make up for tonight's loss and finally break their losing streak versus the Knicks on Tuesday when they host them for a second time. Tune in at 7:30pm ET on Sportsnet.
