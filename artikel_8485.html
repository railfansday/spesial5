Mystery shopping is real side work that usually pays a few dollars to about $20 a visit, plus any purchase the company reimburses. Sign up with several established companies so more jobs show up near you. Never pay to join, and treat any check that arrives before you’ve done a shop as a scam.

That rule matters because scammers pose as mystery shopping companies, mail you a check to cover the shopping, and have you deposit it and wire part of the money back. The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) warns that the check is worthless, so the money you withdraw and send is your own.

Real mystery shopping works the other way around. A company lists a store visit, a phone call, or a task online with its fee and any purchase it will reimburse, you do the job and file a report, and the money arrives after the report is approved. Some honest companies do pay by mailed check, but only for shops you’ve already done.

Joining is free, and the FTC says any company that charges you to sign up is running a scam. Expect part-time money that usually won’t replace a full-time paycheck, and expect the work to come and go, since each company has shops only where its clients need them. Signing up with several is how you see enough jobs near you.

The first six run traditional mystery shops at stores, restaurants, and other businesses. The last two are apps that pay for quick tasks.

In the gift card version, which the FTC flagged in a 2024 alert, the scammer keeps the money loaded onto the cards, and because a bank can take weeks to spot a fake check, you’re left repaying the bank.

Membership in MSPA Americas, the industry’s trade group, is one sign a company is real, and its Service Provider Search lists Intouch Insight, Ipsos, IntelliShop, Market Force, BestMark, and Secret Shopper as members. The association doesn’t hire shoppers itself, so ignore any message offering you shops from MSPA. Field Agent and Premise aren’t members, but both apps are free to download and pay only for work you’ve done.

Sign up with three or four of these companies, and report any check that arrives before your first shop at ReportFraud.ftc.gov instead of depositing it.
