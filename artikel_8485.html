Where to Get Quarters – 13 Places, Starting With Your Own Bank

Get quarters where coins are kept in bulk. Ask a teller at your own bank or credit union for a $10 roll of 40 quarters, then try a store’s service desk or a laundromat or car wash change machine. Before you go looking, check whether the machine you need them for takes a card or an app instead.

Fewer quarters come back in change because fewer people pay with cash. The Federal Reserve’s 2026 Diary of Consumer Payment Choice found that cash made up 14% of consumer payments in 2025, about six cash payments a month per person, down from 14 a month in 2016.

A store’s register can spare only a few dollars’ worth, since it needs its coins to make change. Banks hand quarters out by the roll, and businesses whose machines run on quarters often keep a change machine. The 13 places below run from easiest to hardest.

Ask a teller, not the ATM, for a $10 roll, which holds 40 quarters, and take a few rolls if you’ll need more later. Customers usually pay just the face value, out of their account or in cash, though a few banks charge a small fee per roll. If you don’t bank there, call first, since some banks and credit unions offer coin services only to their own customers or members.

Skip the checkout lanes and ask at the customer service desk, where some stores will trade a $10 bill for a roll. Others make change only with a purchase, and then just a few dollars’ worth. A coin-counting kiosk like Coinstar won’t help, since it takes coins and pays out only a voucher for cash, an eGift card, or a donation receipt.

A big-box store’s service desk works the same way and often stays open after banks close. If the clerk needs a sale to open the register, buy something you’d use anyway, like a bottle of water, and ask for the change in quarters.

A laundromat with coin-operated washers often has a change machine that breaks bills into quarters, though a $10 or $20 bill may come back partly in smaller bills. Check the labels on its front before you feed it, since some take only certain bills and some pay out tokens that work only in that laundromat.

Self-serve car washes that take coins often keep a change machine near the bays. Check it the same way, since some pay out the car wash’s own tokens instead of quarters.

An arcade with coin-operated games usually keeps quarters in a change machine or behind the counter. Many arcades have switched to tokens or reloadable game cards, and neither works anywhere else, so check what you’ll get before you put in a bill.

Convenience stores ring up a lot of small cash sales, so their drawers usually hold quarters, and many are open late. Ask for a few dollars’ worth with a purchase, and expect less late at night, when the drawer has to last until morning.

Ask at the front register, where the line is often shorter than at a grocery store, and expect a few dollars’ worth of quarters at most, usually with a small purchase. Many drugstores stay open late, too.

Ask at the register inside, not at the pump. Buy a drink or a snack with a $5 bill and ask for your change in coins, which usually gets you a few dollars in quarters.

Ask at the counter when the line is short, and order something small if the cashier needs a sale to open the drawer. Expect a couple of dollars’ worth. If there’s a tip jar, ask whether you can trade a bill for its coins.

Pay with a bill and the change comes back in coins, often quarters, so a $1 bill for a 75-cent snack returns one quarter. Expect a quarter or two per purchase, and dimes and nickels when the machine runs low on quarters.

Any cashier has to make change when you pay with cash, so ask for yours in quarters. Pay for a $1.25 item with a $5 bill, and the $3.75 you get back can come as 15 quarters if the drawer can spare them.

Ask a friend, relative, or coworker to trade quarters for your bills, especially one who keeps a change jar or a stash for the office vending machine. Expect only what has piled up, often a few dollars’ worth.

Many washers, dryers, parking meters, and vending machines now take a card or a phone app, so check for a card reader or a sticker naming an app before you go looking for quarters. PayRange lets you pay at laundromats and vending machines from your phone, and ParkMobile does the same for street and garage parking in place of the meter. Download the app the sticker names and add a payment card at home, before you need it.
