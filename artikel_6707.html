30 Classic Photos of John F. Kennedy Jr., in Honor of His 66th Birthday

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

If you weren’t familiar with John F. Kennedy Jr. in the ’90s, chances are that you are now, thanks to the overwhelming popularity of Love Story, this year’s hit FX series about the marriage of President John F. Kennedy’s son to fashion publicist and style icon Carolyn Bessette-Kennedy, and the couple’s tragic death in 1999.

Love Story may have piqued Gen Z’s interest in “John-John,” as John F. Kennedy Jr. was known in the press, but the real man would have turned 66 today. In his honor, we’re taking a look back at some of the images that helped define the presidential scion’s public persona.

President John F. Kennedy and his wife, Jackie, during the christening of their son, John Jr., in the chapel at Georgetown University Hospital. John Jr.—dressed in the same gown his father had worn in 1917—was just 13 days old at the time.

As the New York Times reported: “The child, christened John Fitzgerald Kennedy Jr., gave one subdued cry during the eleven-minute ritual and opened his eyes when a news photographer took his picture. Otherwise the baby appeared relaxed and drowsy.”

A young JFK Jr. playing with his mother’s ropes of pearls at the White House.

President John F. Kennedy walking with his toddler son along the White House’s West Wing colonnade.

John F. Kennedy Jr. was just three days shy of his third birthday when his father was killed in Dallas in November 1963. Here, he’s pictured with his mother and his older sister, Caroline, during President Kennedy’s funeral ceremony.

Awaiting his results at St. Bernard’s 17th Annual Horse Show in New Jersey, where his sister also competed.

Feathery-haired and 10-ish, hitting a forehand return in Central Park.

Posing with Willie Mays at New York’s Shea Stadium.

With a head full of curls—and his first cousin Anthony Radziwill—at the kickoff party for that year’s Robert F. Kennedy Memorial Tennis Tournament in New York.

Celebrating his graduation from Phillips Academy, Andover.

Cutting a lithe figure—and carrying a boombox!—in Hyannis Port.

Kennedy as Bonario in a production of Ben Jonson’s Volpone during his freshman year at Brown.

Wearing cowboy boots with his graduation robes on Commencement Day at Brown. (His bachelor’s degree was in American Studies.)

In a jaunty little red bow tie at a fundraising event.

With his cousin Maria Shriver at Caroline Kennedy’s wedding to Edwn Schlossberg on Cape Cod. (Caroline’s dress was by Carolina Herrera.)

Looking matinee-idol handsome during a portrait session in New York.

Speaking to the press about passing the New York bar examination on his third attempt. (He also passed the bar in Connecticut.)

Enjoying a courtside snack at the US Open.

At the HBO premiere of The Attack of the 50 Foot Woman—starring his then-girlfriend Darryl Hannah—in Los Angeles.

Outside of his mother’s building with his sister, his brother-in-law, and Mrs. Onassis’s diamond dealer—and “private companion”—Maurice Tempelsman following her death. The funeral Mass took place a few blocks away, at the Church of St. Ignatius Loyola on Park Avenue and East 84th Street, before her body was flown to Washington, DC, to be buried beside her first husband.

During a press conference announcing the launch of his political magazine, George.

With his then-fiancée, Carolyn Bessette, at an event. Bessette’s engagement ring—said to be modeled after one worn by Kennedy’s mother—was made of round-cut sapphires and diamonds on a platinum band.

The couple at a fundraising gala for the Whitney Museum.

Turning his camera—a Leica Minilux point-and-shoot—back on the paparazzi outside his Tribeca apartment.

Exiting their building on North Moore Street.

Bessette-Kennedy and Kennedy back home in New York after their wedding on Cumberland Island, Georgia.

Meeting with President Bill Clinton in the Oval Office.

Looking plenty sportif, walking Friday in New YorK.

Attending the Municipal Art Society’s presentation of the Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis Medal—formerly the President’s Medal—to Stephen Swid.
