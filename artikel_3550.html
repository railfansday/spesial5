Susan Smith's Ex David Had 'Dark Days' After She Killed Their 2 Sons

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

More than 30 years after Susan Smith was arrested for drowning her two young sons, her ex-husband, David Smith, is looking back on the moment he learned what happened to their children.

“It was a blur. I didn’t know which way was up and which way was down, left or right. It was a blur. For years afterwards, too,” David tells Us.

David adds that the time following the tragedy is also unclear. “Family get-togethers, or events or something that we did, and I just don’t remember,” he says.

David’s current wife, Tiffany Smith, tells Us that there “were a lot of dark days” for her husband.

“He had a lot of dark times, and I had to be persistent and keep pushing him,” she explains.

David adds that “you don’t ever think about having to bury a child” as a parent.

“We’re supposed to outlive our children. They’re supposed to bury us,” he continues. “They were my reason for living every day. And then she just took that away in a blink of an eye, and I just had to start finding other reasons to keep moving forward.”

He says that things were “really bad for the first five years,” and it was difficult to “not take my life.”

Susan, 55, was sentenced to life in prison in 1995, one year after she rolled her car into John D. Long Lake in South Carolina with her sons, Michael and Alex, strapped inside. The boys were just 3 years old and 14 months old at the time.

She initially made national headlines when she claimed that a man carjacked her and took her sons. Susan and David appeared on TV as she made a heartfelt plea for the carjacker to return her children safely. Nine days after the alleged carjacking took place, the case took a drastic turn when Susan admitted to killing her sons.

Susan’s first parole hearing took place in 2024. At the time, the board unanimously denied her release after David, Tiffany and their daughter, Savannah, pleaded that Susan be kept behind bars.

David also tells Us that he and Tiffany plan to attend Susan’s upcoming parole hearing on Thursday, November 19, and they will urge the board to deny her release.

“I’m not looking forward to speaking again and going through that, but I do plan on being there every time,” David says.

He adds that he will “speak from the heart” when addressing the board, and he plans to keep the focus on his late sons.

“I’m going to tell them, don’t keep her incarcerated because of what she’s done to me. Keep her incarcerated because of what she did to Michael and Alex,” David says. “Her behavior through the years [in prison] has shown it’s still all about Susan. In my heart of hearts, I don’t think she’s remorseful, and I don’t think she really cares what she did.”

David says that “not a day goes by” that he doesn’t think about his sons.

“Maybe I’m looking at it from a moral standpoint, and I know I’m emotionally involved, but I don’t know how you could put a timeline on any child’s death where the mother kills the child,” he says.

He also admits that reliving the crime at Susan’s hearings has been painful, but he “made peace” with the killings years ago.

“I accepted early on when this happened that I wasn’t gonna be mad at the world or bitter,” David says. “I didn’t blame God for what happened. The world didn’t do this to me. Susan did it.”

David says he has struggled to make sense of the crime, saying that “obviously something was wrong” with Susan when she killed their sons.

“I’ve never disputed that for her to do what she did,” he says. “When I put in all the factors that, you know, like she lied for nine days trying to get away with it. She didn’t make a horrible mistake. It wasn’t a tragic car accident or something.”

According to David, Susan “did this deliberately, and she tried to get away with it for nine days.”
