5 Funniest 'Peanuts' Comics About Dogs

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Starring Schroeder! (I kid, they’re all with Snoopy)

Broadly speaking, there are three types of Peanuts comics. There are the funny ones, the sweet ones, and ones that are both funny and sweet. (And, let’s be honest, there are a good number of boring ones too.) But, very generally speaking, those are the three types of good Peanuts comics: funny, sweet, and the ones that do both of those things.

I bring this up because my goal here is to find the five funniest Peanuts comics about dogs. Not like, just Peanuts comics “starring Snoopy,” even though all of them do, but comic strips that are actually about dogs and the nature of dogs in general, if that makes sense. So that means no Flying Ace or Joe Cool stuff. I love those, but I wanted to define this one pretty narrowly about “dogs” (which, I guess, is still pretty broad).

I also wanted to just go for the funny ones. These aren’t the sweet “go hug your dog” Peanuts comics, or even the sweet/funny ones that check all the boxes for Snoopy fans. I was determined to just find funny, dog-themed Peanuts comics which illustrate Charles Schulz’s impressive abilities as a joke-writer.

Dogs do live a pretty enviable life. They have no jobs, people bring them food for free, and simple pleasures like stick-throwing are entirely fulfilling. A lot of people wish life was that simple for them — I know I do. Here, Snoopy addresses those feelings of jealousy many humans have towards dogs and basically says “Yeah, being a dog is pretty great.”

From Jan. 6, 1952, this is the first-ever Sunday strip of Peanuts. The comic had begun in 1950, running six days a week with its regular, four-panel daily strips, but its growing popularity brought it to Sundays.

In these early ones, Snoopy didn't really have all of his more human-like personality traits that would become his signature. Instead, he was just a regular, adorable old dog, which is evidenced here when Snoopy does something that it’s hard to imagine him doing: biting Charlie Brown. It comes during a game of tag where a bunch of kids are running around, tagging each other. One of the kids tags Snoopy, then we see Charlie Brown shout “Ow!”

Then we see Charlie Brown with the back of his shorts missing, with an angry expression as Snoopy looks on innocently. Meanwhile, a kid named Patty — this is the original Patty, not Peppermint Patty — tells Charlie Brown, “Don’t get mad, Charlie Brown…. That’s just his way of tagging!”

What I love about this one is it seems like it could be about something deeper. Snoopy is experiencing some serious malaise, feeling like his life has no meaning at all. And because we all love Snoopy, it makes us all empathize with him and worry about him. Snoopy often acts like more of a human than a dog, and in this comic strip, he seems like he is a super-depressed human. And then, after multiple panels that make Snoopy look like he’s going through a mid-dog-years crisis, Charlie Brown arrives with a full bowl of food. All of a sudden, Snoopy snaps out of it and his life has meaning again. Snoopy may act like a man, but he’s all-dog.

I love how mad Snoopy is in this one. He’s such a happy dog that we rarely see him in a bad mood, which is why this one is so funny and feels like he’s being a real dog here. Dogs hate to see their food bowls empty, and it’s hilarious to see Snoopy play that very dog-like emotion.

Is this one too sweet? It is really funny, but I fear I’m breaking my own stupid rules here. I just couldn’t resist this one. I mean, it is about “dogs” and quite literally the “nature of dogs,” with Lucy talking about the reason for a distinctive dog behavior. Yet the sleepy Snoopy on top of Linus’ head is super cute. I guess it’s impossible to entirely separate the humor of Peanuts from its inherent sweetness.
