Littler stunned by Wattimena at Grand Slam of Darts

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Luke Littler was beaten 10-8 by Jermaine Wattimena in the last 16 at the Grand Slam of Darts; It brings his title defence to an end; you can watch the Grand Slam of Darts in Wolverhampton live on Sky Sports until November 22

Luke Littler's title defence at the Grand Slam of Darts is over after he suffered a 10-8 loss to Jermaine Wattimena in the last 16 in Wolverhampton.

It was simply a stunning performance from Wattimena in which he averaged 101.7, outscoring the world No 1 and outplaying him in every facet, hitting 10 180s and 11 140s on the way.

The loss for Littler means he will not join Phil Taylor and Michael van Gerwen on a short but illustrious list of players who have won the Grand Slam three times in a row.

Littler had picked up the Grand Slam title in 2024 with a win over Martin Lukeman and then backed that up with victory over Luke Humphries in 2025. But his attempt at a hat-trick was shattered with Wattimena's win blowing the competition wide open.

"It feels emotional, absolutely incredible," Wattimena told Sky Sports immediately after the victory.

"I saved my best game for this stage and I'm really proud of it.

"I was 7-2 ahead and missed three darts to go 8-2 up. He then started playing well again.

"But in the last leg, one dart for the match - it feels amazing."

Wattimena will now face Kevin Doets after he came through with a classy 10-5 victory over Cameron Menzies.

The match was close and went with the throw until the 10th leg when Doets landed D16 for a two-leg advantage. From there, he claimed three legs on the spin before Menzies got another on the board and then took the match with his 10th 180 and then match dart at D4.

It was evident from early in the first session that Wattimena was going to give Littler a real game as he broke 'The Nuke's' throw twice to lead 3-2 going into the first break.

It was the second session in which he then found a level that even Littler could not match. Wattimena made it five legs in a row, secured two more breaks of throw, and was averaging as high as 107 before Littler stopped the rot and broke back to bring the score to 7-3.

The question for the Dutchman then was whether he could keep such an incredible level up. He was still scoring well, but Littler's level raised. When the reigning Grand Slam champion picked up three legs in a row to bring the score to 9-8, it looked as though he was going to pull off an almighty comeback.

But Wattimena closed the door on it. In the 18th leg, Wattimena had the darts for the match on 77 and took it out with a match dart on tops.

He then let out the emotions and tears of joy as he took in just what he had achieved.

Jermaine Wattimena is a Dutch darts player who has peaked as high as world No 16 in 2026 and won three titles in the last two years.

In fact, he beat Littler en route to his Players Championship title back in August.

This is his 11th year on the ProTour but the last two years have seen him reach new heights as he has slightly slowed his very fast throw down. He has reached the last 32 at the Worlds and the Masters and made the last 16 of the World Matchplay in 2025.

"I said at the top of the show that Luke could be vulnerable if he didn't have the full respect of what could happen to him from his opponent.

"If you keep letting the same things happen, and let someone get ahead of you like that, you're not always going to catch them.

"Tonight was definitely Jermaine's night. He played superbly. He was the guy in top mode and not Luke."

Gerwyn Price came out on top in a high-class contest with Ryan Searle and made it into the quarter-finals of the Grand Slam with a 10-7 victory to continue his quest for a fourth Grand Slam title.

Price romped into a 4-1 lead in the first session, two breaks of throw and then a brilliant 148 finish bringing out the roar from the 'Iceman'.

But Searle came out in the second session and was scoring with ease, allowing him to take three of the five legs on offer but Price was still finding stunning moments, a 147 checkout then a 90 finish via T17 and tops moving him 6-4 ahead.

In the final session it was a real grudge match but Price's magnificent finishing kept him ahead despite Searle matching every break of throw.

A match dart came and went for Price and Searle took advantage to bring the game to 9-7 ahead. It did not take long though for Price's reply, as, in the 17th leg, Price landed his fourth attempt at a match dart in D10.

Next up, he will take on Niko Springer who is through to his first-ever TV quarter-final after a tough 10-8 victory over Rob Cross.

There was nothing separating the two until Springer went on a blistering burst that saw him race from 5-4 down to one leg from the quarters at 9-5 up.

That was when the nerves started to creep in, the trebling fell away, and Cross looked like he was coming back. 'Voltage' rattled off three legs in a row and was the man with the momentum.

However, Springer found the big darts at the key moment to take out 111 on tops and seal his spot in the last eight.
