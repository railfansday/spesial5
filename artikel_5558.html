78% of Workers Have Done Only the Minimum Required to Keep Their Job

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

92% say employees are not consistently rewarded for going above and beyond, while 86% have been asked to work outside their role without extra pay

GUAYNABO, Puerto Rico, Nov. 20, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- More than three-quarters of U.S. workers (78%) say they have intentionally done only the minimum required to keep their job, according to the new Act Your Wage Report. The survey from the MyPerfectResumeÂ® research center, a premium resume-building service, examines how pay, workload, and perceptions of unfair rewards shape the effort employees are willing to give at work.

The national survey of 1,000 U.S. workers found that 69% have intentionally limited their effort specifically because they felt their pay did not match the expectations or responsibilities of their role. Meanwhile, 92% say employees are not consistently rewarded fairly for going above and beyond, and 86% have been asked to take on responsibilities outside their job description without additional compensation.

"Doing more work without more pay doesn't always mean you're getting ahead," said Dr. Jasmine Escalera, Career Expert at MyPerfectResume. "Workers should be thoughtful about where they invest their time and energy. Setting boundaries matters, but so do staying engaged, building valuable skills, and looking for opportunities to grow. The goal shouldn't be to do the bare minimum, but to make sure the effort you're putting in is helping you move forward in your career."

Most Workers Have Taken a Minimum-Effort Approach

More than three-quarters of workers (78%) say they have intentionally done only the minimum required to keep their job at least once.

Nearly half (49%) say they have taken this approach frequently or occasionally.

Pay Mismatches Are Causing Workers to Pull Back

Forty-three percent have pulled back frequently or occasionally, while fewer than one-third have never reduced their effort because of a perceived pay mismatch.

Only 9% Say Extra Effort Is Consistently Rewarded

Just 9% believe employees are consistently rewarded fairly for going above and beyond, while 52% say extra effort is rarely or almost never rewarded fairly.

Workers identify feeling underpaid and burnout as the top reasons employees stop going above and beyond, at 22% each.

Combined, 58% point to feeling underpaid, lacking recognition, or seeing hard work go unrewarded.

86% Have Been Asked to Work Outside Their Role Without Extra Pay

86% of workers have been asked to take on responsibilities outside their job descriptions without additional compensation.

Altogether, 74% say employees commonly take on responsibilities beyond their official roles without additional compensation.

44% Spend at Least Two Hours a Week Outside Their Formal Role

Nearly three-quarters of workers (73%) spend at least some time each week doing work outside their formal job responsibilities, while 44% spend at least two hours.

16% spend 6 or more hours per week on work beyond their formal responsibilities.

Underpaid and Overworked Employees Are Changing Their Behavior

Eighty-three percent of workers have taken at least one of the listed actions or considered leaving their job because they felt underpaid or overworked.
