Graceful Finance’s Anna Frankowska pitches home reversions for seniors’ toolbox

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Graceful Finance founder and CEO Anna Frankowska is introducing a home reversion product in the U.S., starting in Florida, as an alternative to reverse mortgages and other home equity lending tools. Frankowska told HousingWire’s Reverse Mortgage Daily that financial advisers and reverse mortgage lenders are receptive, seeing it as another tool amid high rates and the lock-in effect, although regulatory treatment and capital markets appetite will shape its long-term growth.  AI Summary

Anna Frankowska, founder and CEO of Graceful Finance, is pitching home reversion products to U.S. seniors. Her company’s Lifestyle Agreement product aims to be an alternative to reverse mortgages and other home equity lending tools amid high interest rates and the lock-in effect.

“There is no loan, so no interest builds up against the home,” Frankowska said in written comments given to HousingWire’s Reverse Mortgage Daily (RMD). “Pricing is driven mostly by the home’s value, how the housing market performs and life expectancy.”

Under a home reversion structure, homeowners essentially sell a share of their future ownership rights in exchange for a lump sum payment, regular income or a combination of both. They retain the right to live in the home for life without mortgage or rent payments.

The minimum age for the product is 75, higher than the 62-year minimum for Federal Housing Administration-insured Home Equity Conversion Mortgages (HECMs) and the typical 55-plus eligibility for many proprietary reverse mortgage products.

Frankowska launched Graceful about four years ago with the goal of adapting a product more common in Europe to the U.S. market, starting in Florida. She added that reverse mortgage lenders and financial advisers have been “receptive” so far, viewing home reversion as another tool they can offer clients.

In an interview with RMD, Frankowska discussed the regulatory treatment of home reversions in the U.S., the product’s growth potential and capital markets appetite.

Editor’s note: This conversation has been edited for length and clarity.

Flávia Nunes: How competitive is the home equity solutions market today, given high interest rates and the mortgage rate lock-in effect?

Anna Frankowska: Many older homeowners have most of their wealth tied up in their homes, while their everyday costs keep rising. Home equity is the natural place for them to turn to supplement their income and enhance their lifestyles. However, high interest rates are putting pressure on the value of reverse mortgages, especially HECMs.

Diving a little deeper, we see it in the numbers. When rates rise, a reverse mortgage borrower can access less of their home’s value. Not surprisingly, HECM endorsements fell 14.2% year over year, from 28,172 in fiscal year 2025 to 24,182 in FY2026, reaching their lowest level since FY2003.

This creates an opportunity for products like home reversions, which are less sensitive to interest rates. There is no loan, so no interest builds up against the home. Pricing is driven mostly by the home’s value, how the housing market performs and life expectancy.

FN: How receptive have traditional reverse mortgage lenders and financial advisers been to partnering with Graceful as a referral option?

AF: Very receptive. Every person I’ve spoken with in the industry wants to help people age better. They want more tools in their toolbox, and they know there are gaps in the existing product suite. There are barriers in the reverse mortgage that are hard to overcome for some homeowners.

For example, at today’s rates, the amount a homeowner can access may simply not be enough. And income solutions are top of mind for the financial advisers I talk to. Professionals in this industry are busy with their immediate priorities and the products they know. That creates a place for us to support them with an additional option.

Our conversations with reverse mortgage lenders helped us think through what’s working and what isn’t, and who helped share what Graceful does. So we really see ourselves as an integral part of the ecosystem, working alongside reverse mortgage lenders.

FN: How has Graceful adapted its product to the U.S. market compared with the home reversion models available in other countries?

AF: We took the concepts of the European markets and adapted them to American preferences. I see it through three lenses: the legal framework, the existing products and how capital markets work.On the first two, we heard directly from customers on their needs for access to their home equity to generate income and that helped us understand what we should be solving for. Looking internationally, we imported best practices from the U.K. market, such as mandatory independent counseling, a suitability assessment, a vulnerable customer policy that classifies all elderly participants as vulnerable by default, plain-language disclosures, a trusted contact requirement, and a comparison process. The result is an attractive product design with strong consumer protections, structured within the U.S. legal framework.

To find capital to fund our home equity solutions, we draw on the U.S. capital markets, which offer deeper securitization markets than Europe’s and a broader range of funding opportunities. Investor interest in these products is high. Deep U.S. markets give us access to more capital, a wider range of investors, and more choice in how we structure our funding. Together, we expect this to support significant volume in the U.S.

FN: As Graceful looks to expand into California and Texas, what specific regulatory challenges are you navigating compared with Florida?

AF: The fundamental homeowner need remains the same across these states. Let me first mention that helping seniors age better is a bipartisan policy priority. I think this will become even more of a priority as the senior support system comes under increasing pressure.

Of course, each state has its own legal and regulatory framework, and we are approaching the expansion thoughtfully. We know that a structure developed in Florida cannot simply be replicated elsewhere. This includes, but is not limited to, state-specific real estate and consumer protection requirements, property and title law, property taxes, and ongoing ownership obligations.

However, I wouldn’t call them challenges, but rather different regulatory frameworks that we need to build into our model and supplement with our strong consumer protection mindset.

FN: How are you approaching funding and capital markets opportunities?

AF: We’re seeing a strong appetite, for a couple of reasons. Investors are looking for long-duration assets with competitive returns and cash flows they can match against long-dated liabilities, which is exactly what many insurance companies need. In addition, they like asset-backed investments with a real asset behind them. Notably, this includes the U.S. housing market, which is very large with decades of extensive and well-documented price history.

Our approach to funding is step by step. We’re starting with anchor partners who can fund the portfolio while it’s young, and building the track record and scale that larger institutions need. From there, more financing channels open up.

FN: Critics argue that selling a portion or all of a home’s future ownership can limit a homeowner’s ability to pass the property on to heirs. How are your clients and their families navigating that trade-off in practice?

AF: As we did our consumer research, we heard that a top priority for older Americans is not to be a burden on their children. Finding sources of income to balance their current lifestyles and future needs will often be more important than passing on their property.

Our focus is on helping older homeowners enjoy the wealth they’ve accumulated, which includes remaining in their houses. Except for the wealthiest, many older Americans struggle to make ends meet and have to choose between having a fulfilling retirement or leaving a legacy for their children. We are providing them that option. Certainly, if a legacy is the most important objective for someone, we would not expect them to try to access their home equity.

The practical reality is that most people have to prioritize either current lifestyle or legacy. It is our mission to make finance available to empower people to live their elder years on their own terms. It is common for something unexpected, especially with health, to occur as we age.

By setting up their home equity release early before that event, people can access some of the wealth they have built in their homes before a situation becomes a crisis, they have more options to invest in their health, home, care, transportation and overall quality of life.

FN: What do you see as the home reversion product’s long-term growth potential in the U.S.?

AF: We believe in building a foundation for the long term. As we build growth, our early focus will be on client experience, ease of doing business, product refinement and deep relationships. Over the last couple of months, we have been focused on product iterations and listening to the market. To give you an early preview, that work has led us to expand our offering, which isn’t live yet.

The time for rapid growth will come because demand is growing. But getting this right is extremely important, so right now we are focused on our home market in Florida, with California and Texas next. Long term, I see a future where people have multiple options that work for them, with reverse mortgages sitting side by side with home reversions, among others.

To put it simply, it’s a multibillion-dollar annual volume opportunity. For scale, homeowners 62 and older hold $15.34 trillion in home equity, and the entire reverse mortgage industry originated about $7.3 billion last year. That’s less than a tenth of one percent of that equity converted each year.

When people are young, they dream of owning a home as a place to raise a family, enjoy time with friends and grow old. It is also one of the best ways to accumulate wealth. In order to enjoy that wealth as they grow old and live the life they desire, home equity release serves an important role.
