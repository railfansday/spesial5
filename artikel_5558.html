IMF chief warned AI circular financing tops $1 trillion as Nvidia fallout rattles markets

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The IMF estimates two-thirds of the financing is debt-funded, while Kristalina Georgieva called recent market swings orderly

International Monetary Fund Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva warned on Monday that the world economy's dependence on AI poses a risk to financial stability, she told CNBC. She spoke on the sidelines of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations summit in the Philippines.

"We have a world economy that is standing on one leg on the AI leg. If this leg wobbles, that can lead to financial stability troubles," Georgieva said. She said a "sudden drop in investment confidence and withdrawal from the AI field" could weaken economic growth, according to CNBC.

Circular financing in the AI industry, where an investor's money returns to it via orders placed by the company it backed, now totals just over $1 trillion, with debt covering two-thirds, the IMF estimates. "It's not a huge size, but it is a rapid increase," Georgieva said, according to CNBC.

Nvidia $NVDA, whose shares have shed over $1 trillion in value across the last 30 days, is a central player in those deals, according to CNBC. Nvidia founder Jensen Huang resigned as CEO and board member last week after reports of two extramarital affairs. One involved a businesswoman whom U.S. intelligence officials suspect of working for, China's intelligence services.

AI-linked stocks have traded lower around the world since Huang’s departure. Nvidia is scheduled to report fiscal third-quarter earnings on Tuesday, Nov. 17.

Georgieva called the latest market moves "orderly" and said they were not likely to escalate into a threat to the broader global economy. "We have to be watchful," she said.

She said investors' excitement about AI mirrors hopes that have outrun the technology's track record so far. "We tend to rush ahead" when valuations rise, she said, a tendency that makes "some corrections" unavoidable, according to CNBC.

The IMF warned in October that a crash in AI-related stocks could threaten global financial stability. The fund pointed to 14 publicly disclosed deals since 2023 in which hyperscalers put roughly $300 billion in equity and $650 billion in debt into financially weaker AI companies. Those partners committed $1.4 trillion in future revenue in return.

The IMF's worst-case scenario from October assumes AI fails to meet market expectations. Under it, U.S. stock prices could drop 20% during 2026, while global output might finish 1.1% under the baseline projection for 2028, according to CNBC.
