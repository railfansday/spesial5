Add This Packet Of Seasoning To Pot Roast For A Delicious Southern-Style Sandwich

Pot roast is one of those simple homemade meals that may take a long time to cook but is pretty hands-off in the Crock Pot. Just brown the meat, add your ingredients, and let the cooker do its job. There are plenty of ways to upgrade a basic pot roast and some interesting regional pot roast dishes, and this simple trick is a bit of both. To give your pot roast a hint of Southern twang, add a packet of dry ranch seasoning. Similar to a set-it-and-forget-it easy French dip sandwich but Southern, a bit of ranch in your pot roast will give you some delightfully intense flavor and get you darn near a classic Mississippi pot roast.

Ranch seasoning is basically an off-the-shelf, ready-to-go spice packet containing multiple seasonings — usually salt, onion powder, garlic powder, and other dried herbs and spices, like parsley and black pepper. Most brands also contain buttermilk and whey, which will add a bit of a creaminess factor, but you don't have to worry about the roast tasting like a bottle of ranch dressing because the mix will cook down with added beef stock and natural juices to form a nice, thick gravy. It will come out rich, bold, and savory with just a hint of tanginess from the buttermilk. Once your roast is nice and tender, you can simply slice it (or shred it) up and serve it Italian-beef or French-dip style, preferably with some melted cheese on top. The first bite through the bread into the perfectly tender and umami-rich, seasoned beef will make you a believer.

If you want to go for a more or less standard pot roast route, you can simply rub your roast with a packet of Hidden Valley Ranch Seasoning, available online and in stores, or something similar, and cook it how you normally would with beef stock, potatoes, onions, carrots, and whatever other veg you like to use. To go full-on Mississippi roast, you can forgo the veggies and slow cook it with the ranch coating, beef stock or au jus gravy mix, and a half dozen or more whole pepperoncini. Up the spice level with extra hot peppers or use the whole jar. You can even add a bit of the brine for some supplementary heat, plus a zingy kick of vinegar. This will get you closer to an actual Mississippi pot roast, which typically features said peppers.

For the bread, something that will hold up to the moisture of the roast is ideal. Toasted French bread adds a crunchy texture, but a toasted hoagie roll works great, and a thick sourdough will add a little extra tang. You'll want a cheese that melts easily, so you can quickly broil it without overcooking the bread. A simple mozzarella is an easy, mild choice, but a nice Gruyère will bring a richer and nuttier flavor with some subtle sweetness.

Ranch-seasoned pot roast or the full Mississippi mix is also great on egg noodles, mashed potatoes, or rice, or you can make a simple open-faced sando with a thick Texas toast. For an even saltier, more savory roast, you can try a three-packet blend with ranch, brown gravy, and Italian-style salad dressing mixes. However, to get that good Southern flavor, don't veer too far off the ranch.
