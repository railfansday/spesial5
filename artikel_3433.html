The Rings Of Power Resolves Tolkien's Longstanding Ringwraith Mystery After 72 Years

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Warning! Spoilers for Rings of Power season 3, episodes 1-4 ahead!It's a testament to J.R.R. Tolkien's creative mastery that all these decades later we're still obsessed with the mysteries of his fantasy world. It's been 72 years since the legendary author published The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring, a work that has had a significant impact on media today. Prime Video's The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power season 3 is an example. While the story it covers is set many thousands of years before the Fellowship left Rivendell, the narrative finds its foundation in this first of the trilogy.

It isn't long in Rings of Power season 3 before the heroes find themselves confronted by Sauron's Nazgnagôl—Ring servants. These are the early versions of the Nazgûl that were first introduced to readers in The Fellowship of the Ring. According to Tolkien, the Nazgûl were originally ordinary men who were granted the Nine Rings by the Dark Lord Sauron. These small weapons provided long life and wealth, as well as the ability to perceive the Unseen World.

This power allowed the Nazgûl to become invisible as they slipped into the parallel realm, but the power ultimately ate away at their physical forms. As they became phantoms, their minds were also tethered to the Unseen World. Tolkien explained that this process took centuries, but he never said precisely what happened from the very first moment these Men put on their Nine. This left Rings of Power with some room for creative liberties in season 3.

Rings of Power season 3 picks up five years after the end of season 2, and we see that Sauron already selected a few of his Nazgnagôl. Lucky for us, however, he isn't quite done, and we get to observe the moment that Khamûl the Easterling (one of the few canonically named members of the Nine) puts on his ring for the first time.

In Rings of Power, Khamûl suffers from a physical affliction resulting from the Dark Wizard's curses in Rhûn. When Sauron puts the Ring on his finger, we see the bloody sores covering Khamûl's body begin to fade. However, the process seems to cause the man great pain. Sauron explains that he is simply adjusting to his body's new ability to exist simultaneously in both the physical and Unseen worlds. Once this settles, Khamûl seems completely intoxicated by his new strength and power. Little does he know that over time he will lose himself entirely to a life of torment.

Tolkien's works made it seem as if the Nine had a similar impact on their wearers as the Dwarves' Seven. While the power was instantaneous, the corruption was far more gradual. This is only somewhat true for Rings of Power. We see through Theo's story that, though he wears one of the Nine, he maintains some autonomy. When he saw Arondir, he removed his mask and urged his dear friend to flee. It was clear Theo had no desire or intention of harming Arondir. However, the moment that Sauron arrived and gave the order, Theo acted.

Theo's sword went through Arondir's body so quickly that it's clear there was no option to hesitate. Theo was in control of himself, but the Dark Lord's word took that away entirely in only a moment. It's an interesting concept, and a sign that, regardless of how long someone wears one of these Nine Rings, they can't resist Sauron's direct orders while it's on their fingers. This likely has to do with the fact that these specific Rings, unlike the others, were made using Sauron's own blood. Even without the full corruption that comes over time, the Nazgnagôl are bound to their Lord.
