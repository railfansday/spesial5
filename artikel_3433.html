Director Ridley Scott has had a rather rough run at the box office in the last decade, with several major movies falling short of box-office expectations. Most recently, he returned to the sci-fi genre after a nearly decade-long gap with The Dog Stars, which grossed less than $40 million worldwide against a reported budget of around $100 million. The relative success of Scott's biggest hit of the decade, Gladiator II, was dampened by its high production cost of around $300 million.

However, Scott has always had a love/hate relationship with box-office success. While his sci-fi classic Blade Runner famously underperformed commercially, a fellow neo-noir thriller emerged as a major hit despite having received mixed reviews. The movie in question isn't regarded as one of Scott's finest, although it has developed quite a strong cult following. It was recently added to the library of a free streaming platform, giving a new generation an opportunity to check it out.

Released in 1989, the film starred Michael Douglas in the lead role of an NYPD detective who is sucked into the Japanese underworld while repatriating a Yakuza member. The movie grossed more than $130 million worldwide against a reported budget of $30 million, emerging as a rather solid hit.

However, it received mixed reviews, and is now sitting at a 56% score on the aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes. Despite this, Scott's film emphasized the elegant atmospheric style he brought to Blade Runner, this time highlighting the dangerous underbelly of Japan's organized crime scene. It's an entertaining watch in Scott's filmography, especially for fans of old-school action thrillers.
