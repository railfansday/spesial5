5 Yoga Poses That Help You Embody Gratitude

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Calin Van Paris is a YJ editor and certified yoga instructor. Here, she shares five yoga poses that can help you access gratitude throughout your body—just in case you’re having a tough time feeling it in your heart.

The practice of gratitude can be a sticky subject. Certain events and times of year—season shifts, rites of passage, the holidays—prompt us to ponder the things in our lives that elicit gratitude. Life feels a little richer when experienced through the lens of gratitude.

But at times, accessing that gratitude can be easier said than done. Global happenings and situations in our individual lives aren’t always sunny or sparkly, and we don’t always feel grateful. Sometimes we’re just mad, sad, frustrated, or over it—even in November and December.

Rather than convincing your head (or heart) to be thankful, try feeling it in your body first. The following yoga poses can help transform the sometimes elusive concept of gratitude into something more physical.

Spend a few minutes in one of these poses contemplating  gratitude. The rest of you might follow.

There’s something worshipful about bowing on your knees, which makes Child’s Pose (Balasana) an easy choice for this list. When you’re in a grateful state, thanking the Universe can become akin to a prayer or mantra, one worth saying over and over again. Spend some time here, with your forehead and palms pressed to the earth, and imagine roots sprouting from all points of contact.

Another shape that finds you close to the earth, Squat (Malasana) is a pose of both humility and strength. Your feet planted on the ground offer stability, while the deep squat requires the cooperation of your entire lower body. Add the usual prayer hands, and Malasana becomes a demonstration of gratitude for your body, spirit, and anything else you choose to be grateful for.

When you imagine a full-body expression of joy, chances are the pose in your mind looks a lot like Upward Salute (Urdhva Hastasana). The act of standing strong and lifting your arms in celebration mirrors the instinctive expression of wholehearted gratitude. When you’re in an authentically grateful place, expressing it doesn’t feel like a chore or an obligation—it feels like a party.

Opening your heart can also begin in your body. Bending back in Camel Pose requires a certain amount of trust in your body as well as in gravity. Once you’ve found stillness in the pose, your heart and throat face upward in a way that’s both vulnerable and liberating. Embracing this shape is a physical prompt for you to revel in—and perhaps exude gratitude for—the human experience.

Lying prone on your back is perhaps the ultimate shape of surrender. Savasana finds your front body fully exposed to the Universe and all it has to offer while your back body roots you to the earth. Stilling your mind and body here demands an acceptance of this present moment, just as it is. And if that isn’t an expression of gratitude, then perhaps nothing is.
