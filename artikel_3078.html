Starwood: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Starwood's $800 million 2026 resolution target would clear over 40% of its nonaccrual real estate, while the board weighs whether the dividend can stay.

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 6, 2026, Starwood (STWD -0.75%) reported distributable earnings, a non-GAAP metric measuring cash available for dividend payouts, of $0.41 per share excluding realized losses. Shareholders can't ignore how effectively the commercial mortgage lender recycles capital from older loans into higher-yielding investments. Three things management said stand out.

Starwood resolved $254 million of nonaccrual loans, which are nonperforming loans where interest collection is in doubt, and real estate owned, which refers to foreclosed properties on the balance sheet. Cleaning up those legacy positions allows management to recycle capital into fresh loans with higher interest rates.

Starwood increased its Current Expected Credit Losses (CECL) reserve, an accounting buffer for expected bad loans, by $369 million because of rate-driven macroeconomic models. Management noted that the stock's current discount to undepreciated book value, or net asset value, implies severe loss rates that don't match real-world asset sales.

Third-quarter distributable earnings came in at $0.41 per share, falling short of the quarterly dividend payout of $0.48 per share. Management plans to review the payout ratio, which measures dividend payments against earnings, with the board to preserve liquidity until loan resolutions restore earning power.

Commercial real estate conditions are healing as construction slows and property supply declines across major markets. Starwood relies on its massive scale, energy infrastructure lending platform, and non-real-estate earnings streams to cushion the impact of higher interest rates. Executive focus centers on optimizing borrowing costs and executing steady asset resolutions.

A deep liquidity pool of $1.1 billion lets the business manage pre-2023 loans for long-term recovery rather than forced short-term sales. Reinvesting repaid capital into high-yielding new loans positions the portfolio to rebuild distributable earnings as credit market friction eases.
