XRP Is Back Under $1. Is It Finally a Buy?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Ripple's success is only loosely tied to the token.

By Johnny Rice – Nov 22, 2026 at 5:30AM EST

XRP (XRP -0.57%) is trading for less than $1 again, down more than 55% during the past year. Meanwhile, the privately held company behind the token, Ripple, looks as strong as ever. Why?

A fall like that tends to bring out the bargain hunters, especially if you believe Ripple and its products are on their way to becoming an essential part of international finance. A token that has lost more than half its value looks like a cheap way to get in on a compelling story.

But I think some investors are missing something pretty important.

At its Swell conference last month, the company said it expects to end 2026 with an annualized product revenue run rate approaching $900 million, roughly triple what it was a year ago. It says it has partnered with more than 1,700 institutions, corporations, and enterprises that are now using Ripple.

But investors need to realize that Ripple's success is only loosely connected to XRP's.

The case for the token has always been that greater demand for Ripple's products means greater demand for XRP. XRP acts as the bridge asset in a payment, typically across international borders. On one side of a transaction, dollars are swapped for XRP, then swapped for, say, euros on the other side.

It stands to reason, then, that as more customers use Ripple's payment systems, demand for XRP would increase.

Unfortunately, that job can now be done by RLUSD (RLUSD +0.00%), Ripple's own stablecoin. RLUSD offers most of the speed and cost benefits without anyone having to hold a volatile token along the way -- even for a few moments. That is attractive for banks and financial institutions who don't want any more volatility than is absolutely necessary.

While customers can still use either one, Ripple is clearly pushing RLUSD hard. Its website reads: "Integrate stablecoin payments into your business." That doesn't bode well for XRP over time as RLUSD becomes the bridge asset of choice.

What's more, Ripple still unlocks 1 billion XRP from escrow every month, and while the company relocks 70% to 80% of it, that still leaves 200 million to 300 million new tokens entering circulation each month. There's creates constant pricing pressure from Ripple itself.

At present, about 60% of XRP investors hold it at an average cost of $1.44 -- about 50% more than XRP's current price. That will make it hard for XRP to break out, as investors who have been sitting on losses sell whenever the token nears their break-even point.

I had expected XRP to be back below $1 by 2031, and obviously it got there a whole lot faster. XRP could certainly go on another run, but with the structural forces the way that they are, I don't see why this would change in the long run.

Crypto is notoriously hard to value because there are no earnings or cash flows to analyze. There aren't any hard, objective figures to hold on to. Most of crypto seems to be valued based on perception alone, and the expectation that other investors will pay more for it than current holders. And the truth is, XRP has had some serious catalysts in the last two years, and yet it is still back under $1.

I think investors have woken up to the fact that Ripple can keep growing rapidly while XRP continues to struggle. That's why I'm going to pass on XRP under $1.
