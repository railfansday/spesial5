Tired of Wi-Fi drops throughout your home? The best mesh Wi-Fi systems can eliminate internet dead zones and turn your house into a powerhouse for connectivity, no matter how far away devices are from a router. To prove it, we've done the testing to help you find the right mesh system.

At Tom's Guide, we put each mesh Wi-Fi system through real-world scenarios, from multi-story homes to outdoor use. This allows us to see how fast internet speeds are at multiple ranges, especially through objects and walls. So, if you've been struggling to get a strong Wi-Fi signal across your home, the mesh Wi-Fi systems we recommend will help you sort that problem out.

For the best mesh Wi-Fi system you can buy today, we recommend the MSI Roami BE Pro for reliable speeds and long-distance coverage. This Wi-Fi 7 mesh system will take you far for its price, but there are more options we've tested for homes.

Soon, you'll see more of the next standard of mesh Wi-Fi systems arrive with Wi-Fi 8. In the meantime, we have even more mesh systems worth considering!

The MSI Roamii BE Pro isn't the fastest around, nor does it deliver the longest range, but it excels at speeds, distances and setup for half the price of other tri-band WiFi 7 routers. That's why it's the best mesh Wi-Fi system we recommend for most, especially if you're looking for an affordable way to upgrade to Wi-Fi 7.

The Roamii BE Pro immediately stands out for its real-world speed across a whole house, reaching 1.885 Gbps at 15 feet and still delivering 66.3 Mbps at a distant 90 feet. That kind of consistent performance means you won’t be left hunting for a signal in the farthest room.

In our testing, the Roamii BE Pro also excelled at mid-range distances, recording 815.2 Mbps at 50 feet — a result that puts it squarely among the fastest mesh systems we’ve seen.

We found it outpaces the TP-Link Deco BE23 at close range (1.885 Gbps vs. 1.449 Gbps), but its real advantage is balancing those speeds with broader coverage, a feat some rivals like the Netgear Orbi 870 only match at longer distances. That makes the Roamii BE Pro a better pick for anyone who wants both fast speeds and reliable whole-home coverage, not just one or the other.

During two weeks of everyday use, the Roamii BE Pro kept up without breaking a sweat. That includes streaming 4K video, downloading large files, gaming and emailing.

Plus, wired device fans get a trio of 2.5 Gbps LAN ports and a USB 3.0 port. It's just a shame that this isn't available in single or three-packs, meaning you'd have to buy another two-pack if you're looking to add more nodes. At least it isn't too pricey!

Setup is a breeze: after scanning the QR code, entering a network name and updating the firmware, I had both nodes running in just 15 minutes. If you want the quickest path to reliable, top-tier Wi-Fi 7 speeds without fuss, the Roamii BE Pro is the system to beat.

The TP-Link Deco BE23 shows that high-speed, Wi-Fi 7 connectivity in a mesh system doesn't have to cost you a premium. For as low as $180, you can get a set of three powerful mesh networking devices that offer up far-reaching connectivity with some absolutely amazing speeds.

Each of the BE23 units comes in a neat, discreet design that can be placed anywhere while hiding in plain sight, and you can set up a connection for the whole house easily with TP-Link’s Deco app. Plus, any of the units can be used as a host router. Once you're set up, you can take control of your connectivity and devices via the app, like integrating and controlling Tapo, Kasa, Philips Hue and Matter home automation devices.

Speed is the star of the show, especially for this mesh system's affordable price. In our testing, we saw highs of 1.449 Gbps at 15 feet, and still 150.1 Mbps as far away as 90 feet. Those numbers beat many routers we've tested, even though it understandably falls behind the likes of a tri-band system (like the TP-Link Deco BE63 with 1.821 Gbps at 15 feet away).

With a maximum range of 115 feet for each unit, this mesh system will take you far (quite literally, connectivity-wise). Now that's an excellent balance between price and performance.

What you won't get here are more advanced features, like the 6 GHz band (just 2.4GHz and 5GHz here) or a super-wide 320 MHz data channel, and there's only a pair of 2.5 Gbps networking ports without a USB port, which makes wired connections limited.

For an affordable way to keep your whole home connected with fantastic internet speeds, the price is right with the TP-Link Deco BE23. It may not be the fastest or the most advanced, but for value, it's hard to go wrong with this mesh setup.

The Netgear Orbi 870 is a powerful mesh Wi-Fi system that really excels at medium and long ranges, even outperforming its larger, more expensive older sibling.

Despite its more premium price, I really like the more cylindrical design with a cleaner overall look of the Orbi 870 and the rest of the company’s Wi-Fi 7-powered mesh kits. While you’ll often see it in white at online retailers, you can actually get an all-black version too, but only on Netgear’s site.

The Orbi 870 is a very reliable and feature-packed mesh Wi-Fi system that’s ideal for futureproofing your home network thanks to its slew of multi-gig ports, including a 10 Gbps input. In his testing, Brian saw speeds of 1.82 Gbps at close range (15 feet).

However, it was the Orbi 870’s mid- and long-range performance that impressed him the most. For instance, at 50 and 75 feet, this mesh Wi-Fi system had download speeds of over 600 Mbps, beating both the entry-level Orbi 770 and even the Orbi 970 at the top of Netgear’s mesh lineup.

Even at a distance of 90 feet from Brian’s test laptop, it was at just over 100 Mbps, while the Orbi 970 and even TP-Link’s Deco BE85 were under 50 Mbps. At launch, a two-pack cost $1,000 (though you can often find it cheaper now), so the Orbi 870 is certainly an investment, just like the rest of the devices in Netgear’s Orbi line.

However, if you have a large home you want to fill with a strong Wi-Fi signal and a top-tier multi-gig internet plan, it could definitely be worth it. Just keep in mind that you will need to set aside some extra cash if you want to secure your home network with it using Netgear’s Armor security package or want extended technical support, as only 90 days are included.

The Acer Predator Connect T7 is a mesh Wi-Fi system with a heavy focus on gamers. However, unlike my previous mesh router pick for gaming, the Asus ROG Rapture GT6, it’s a Wi-Fi 7-powered device with a much more subtle look.

Instead of RGB and lots of logos, the Predator Connect T7 features a taller, rectangular design in white or black, and at first glance, it looks quite similar to an Xbox Series X. There’s a splash of RGB lighting on the top, but this mesh kit has a sleeker and more restrained look that I think definitely works in its favor.

Just like with the best gaming routers, the Predator Connect T7 has a low-latency design that uses Intel’s Killer Prioritization engine for peak gaming performance. It also comes with free built-in security software and makes full use of all of Wi-Fi 7’s best features.

During testing, Brian saw download speeds of 1.9 Gbps at close range (15 feet) with good mid-range performance. While the Predator Connect T7 has a multi-gig input port, its wired Ethernet ports are limited to gigabit speeds, so plugging in one of the best gaming laptops won’t give you a significant speed boost over connecting via Wi-Fi.

The Acer Predator Connect T7 is one of the few mesh Wi-Fi systems specifically designed for gamers and if you want a mesh router that looks as slick as your gaming PC, it’s a big step up from the Asus ROG Rapture GT7 (which superseded the GT6 we used to recommend).

However, it is on the expensive side at $330 per device, and unlike other mesh kits, you won’t see any discounts at all when buying a multi-pack. Still, if you want gaming-level network performance with the added benefits of mesh and want something less flashy, the Predator Connect T7 is an excellent mesh Wi-Fi system and the only one for gamers with Wi-Fi 7 support so far.

The Eero Pro 7 is a smaller and sleeker mesh Wi-Fi system that’s incredibly simple to set up and use. Unlike Eero’s past mesh kits, which had a puck-shaped design, this one has a rounded rectangular shape and, at 7.1 x 5.8 x 2.6 inches, you won’t have any trouble finding a place for it and its satellites around your home.

After testing the larger, more powerful Eero Max 7 myself, I really like the Pro 7’s more compact design and the fact that it's significantly cheaper, too. The biggest selling point of the Pro 7 and Eero’s other mesh Wi-Fi systems is that even a home networking novice will have no trouble at all getting them up and running.

Setup and configuration are handled entirely through the very user-friendly Eero app. Besides how it looks, I really like the way that updates are downloaded and installed automatically, so your mesh Wi-Fi system is always running the latest software.

In his testing, Brian saw download speeds of 1.9 Gbps at close distances (15 feet), but the Pro 7 was equally impressive at mid-range distances with speeds just under a gigabit.

The Eero Pro 7 does cost a bit more than the competition, but nowhere close to what you’d pay for an Orbi mesh kit. As an Amazon company, Eero works great with your Alexa-powered and other smart home devices, and you can even use an Echo Dot as a mesh extender to add some extra range to your home network.

If you want a fast and capable Wi-Fi 7-powered mesh system that’s simple to set up and just works, the Eero Pro 7 is very easy to recommend, especially since it's a lot cheaper than the Max 7. The one downside is that you’re limited to just two Ethernet ports, but they are the faster 5 Gbps variety, which is great if you have a multi-gig internet plan.

The TP-Link Deco BE85 is a top-of-the-line mesh Wi-Fi system that was actually the first mesh router with Wi-Fi 7 support. It builds on the cylindrical design of the Deco XE75 with a more refined and polished look, but it packs in a ton of multi-gig and even a USB port.

As someone who upgraded to fiber internet, I love how it has its own SFP+ port, which lets you plug your fiber line directly in at the back instead of into a separate ONT box first. Even if you’re still using one of the best cable modems, though, the Deco BE85 is a powerful mesh Wi-Fi system that won’t disappoint when it comes to performance.

One thing that’s really convenient about this mesh kit and TP-Link’s other Deco devices is that they’re all identical out of the box and you can use any of them as your main router. The company’s Deco app also makes setup and configuration very easy, and in my experience, it’s on par with Eero’s app in terms of usability.

Yes, a mesh Wi-Fi system can go through walls. Because Wi-Fi uses radio waves, these waves can pass through solid materials like walls to connect to a device.

However, when Wi-Fi meets a wall, some of it goes through, some bounces off and some is absorbed. Because of this, the signal can be weaker, especially when more walls are in the way.

A mesh Wi-Fi system has an advantage in getting past this. Instead of using one router to get a signal, a mesh system uses multiple nodes placed throughout a home to bypass hard objects or walls in order to get an unobstructed signal. This means there are fewer barriers to getting a clear Wi-Fi signal.

The main downside of using a mesh Wi-Fi system is that it can be very expensive, especially compared to traditional Wi-Fi routers. These are only needed for those living in large homes or for anyone who needs Wi-Fi coverage over a long distance. For those in smaller spaces, this is overkill.

Since mesh systems come in a multi-pack of nodes, this bumps up the price, as these extra devices deliver a better range of coverage throughout a space compared to other routers.

Nodes also must be placed close enough to catch a strong signal, which can be tricky in a home with many rooms separated by concrete or brick walls. Plus, with this signal hopping between multiple nodes, there may be an increase in latency, which isn't ideal for competitive gameplay.

Otherwise, the best mesh Wi-Fi systems try to overcome these downsides, and these are still the best option to upgrade your home network wirelessly.

Mesh Wi-Fi systems come with nodes and are usually available in two-packs or three-packs, but deciding which one to get depends on the size of your home. If you live in a small or medium house, then a two-pack will usually do the trick. For large homes with more than three bedrooms or multiple stories, a three-pack is the better choice, as this will ensure that your Wi-Fi signal reaches every corner and even upstairs.

The great thing about mesh Wi-Fi systems, though, is that you can always start small and work up to something bigger. For instance, if you buy a two-pack and find that you’re still dealing with Wi-Fi dead zones in certain parts of your home, you can always add another node later to improve your coverage.

Plug-in Wi-Fi extenders aren’t nearly as advanced or as powerful as the nodes in a mesh Wi-Fi system. The other big problem with Wi-Fi extenders is that most of them create a separate network you have to manually switch to each time you want to use them. For instance, let’s say you put a Wi-Fi extender in your bedroom. Whenever you want to get online in that particular room, you’ll have to switch from your main Wi-Fi network to the extender, which gets annoying really fast.

On the other hand, with a mesh Wi-Fi system, the nodes are all part of your main network and no switching is required. As you move from one side of your house to the other, your mesh system automatically hands off your connection to whatever satellite you’re closest to, which ensures you have a strong signal and a good connection wherever you are in your home.
