10 Shows Like 'Yellowjackets' You Should Watch Next

Yellowjackets, the time-hopping survival drama about a group of teenage girls who are stranded in the wilderness in 1996 and do terrible things to survive, is coming to an end. Whether or not it sticks the landing, it's been one helluva ride, and we'll soon have room in the streaming schedule for other other shows about young people having a pretty rough go of it while just trying to survive.

Situated between flashbacks and flash-forwards (not entirely unlike Yellowjackets), mystery/thriller The Wilds sees an airplane full of teenage girls crash on the way to an empowerment program in Hawaii. It quickly becomes clear that the accident was engineered and that the whole thing is some sort of social experiment, and the survivors are forced to compete against each other if they want to live. The show understands the ways in which young women are exploited and expected to compete against each other in the real world, not just a show about girlfights. Stream The Wilds on Prime Video.

A bit more grounded...but only at first glance, The Stranded takes place on a Thai island in the midst of a tsunami. The island was formerly home to a prestigious high school, and a bunch of students have snuck back for an ill-timed party on the last day of class. A bad idea, clearly, after the storm hits. Kraam (Papangkorn Lerkchaleampote), one of the few students who actually lives on the island, is trapped there along with a couple dozen classmates. Kraam knows the island, but Anan (Chutavuth Pattarakampol) assumes leadership while other students either take on necessary roles or fall in line. As the miniseries progresses, the new social order breaks down, even as the island's mysteries reveal themselves and things get weirder and more complex. Stream The Stranded on Netflix.

A handful of influencers get invited to a remote island, which is obviously a very good thing to agree to that will not lead to dramatic complications in any way. Behind the neon-lit party vibes, there's a cult at work and some ritualistic shenanigans with deadly consequences. Eden is more concerned with the survival horror than with some of the deeper themes and mysteries of Yellowjackets, but we're still playing with shifting power dynamics in this Spanish import. Stream Welcome to Eden on Netflix.

The premise of The Leftovers is brilliantly subdued: Around two percent of the world's population disappears without explanation, and it's enough to upend just about everything. Politics have adapted to the new normal, religions have collapsed and reformed, and families have had to make peace with the inexplicable loss of loved ones. The first season revolves around the Garvey family, led by Kevin (Justin Theroux), a sheriff whose wife (Amy Brenneman) left him to join a cult, while subsequent seasons broaden the scope to bring in other characters in other locations. It's more philosophical than Yellowjackets, and quieter, but the shows share an interest in the lingering effects of trauma, and in the stuff we get up to under threat and in the dark. Stream The Leftovers on HBO Max.

The 100 tells the story of the descendants of refugees of nuclear devastation who return to Earth from their habitat in space to encounter the remnants of humanity who have survived on Earth. Naturally, the first people sent to scope things out are the juvenile delinquents (better them than me), and they discover that three civilizations have risen up in the aftermath of the apocalypse, and they are all pretty scary (including one populated by cannibals, naturally). More sci-fi than horror, but the survivalist themes and mysteries here make it more than a decent match. The show builds an impressive mythology over the course of its run, leading to a conclusion that’s borderline metaphysical. Stream The 100 on Pluto TV or buy it from Prime Video.

When pretty, popular girl Kate Wallis (Olivia Holt) goes missing without a trace in 1993, awkward Jeanette Turner (Chiara Aurelia) steps in to fill the gap, modeling herself after Kate by working her way into the missing girl's social circle and getting close with her boyfriend. That's all going great until Kate reappears two years later, accusing Jeanette of having witnessed the events around her disappearance and keeping quiet. Whoops. There's even more going on, as the show shifts back and forth between 1993, 1994, and 1995 to slowly reveal the missing pieces. Season two takes on a different story set in 1999 and 2000, and the forthcoming (surprise) third season will shake things up again. The emphasis here is on mystery, but it's a show about the aftereffects of trauma and of the dark secrets that can tear families and communities apart. Stream Cruel Summer on Hulu.

A massive apocalyptic tidal wave strikes during the 10-year reunion of the graduating class of an all-girls high school. The event leaves them all trapped together (a nightmare in any event), and forced to survive while reliving old struggles and dramas. It's Yellowjackets-esque, absolutely, but also Australian and a comedy, which doesn't feel like as much of a stretch as you'd think given the over-the-top nature of that show. Stream Class of '07 on Prime Video.

A remote, charming town with some deep, dark secrets (the biggest of which, and one that it shares with Yellowjackets, is rather strongly hinted in the title), Gannibal stars Yuya Yagira as Daigo Agawa, a police officer from the big city who takes a job in the super cute (and, of course, remote) village of Kuge following some personal trouble. The perfectly charming Goto family runs the town, more or less, and welcomes Daigo and his family. It's all looking pretty great—until a mysterious man gives Daigo's daughter a severed finger as a gift, and his wife Yuki finds the words "Run Away" scrawled over a door in their new home. Just a couple of reasons why I'm more of a city person. Stream Gannibal on Disney+ and Hulu.

This latter-day plane crash narrative goes all-in on mystery and philosophy, virtually pioneering the timey-wimey reveal format that makes Yellowjackets so juicy. We kick off with flashbacks that peel back the layers of the various crash survivors before diving headfirst into warm-weather polar bears and mysterious underground bunkers and monsters made of smoke. Whether it's an uncommonly smart network show that rewards careful viewing or a load of nonsense is in the eye of the beholder—but it remains one of the most discussed and debated shows in TV history. Stream Lost on Disney+ and Hulu.

Kathryn Newton heads this surprisingly smart Lord of the Flies riff about a group of teenagers forced to create their own community after all the adults disappear and a dense new forest crops up around their small enclave, cutting them off from everything and everyone else. This one's about coming of age in a world where the usual rules are no longer in effect, and about how we're quick to reestablish an oppressive social order even when outside forces are no longer at work. Stream The Society on Netflix.
