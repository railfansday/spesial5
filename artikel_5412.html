Akash’s Solana Migration Clears November 2 Vote at 97%, With Four More On-Chain Decisions Ahead

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

AI-assisted summary, reviewed by NullTX editors.

AKT stakers are close to approving Akash's move from Cosmos to Solana, with Mintscan projecting a pass at 97.2% yes as voting enters its final hours.

But a yes on Proposal 346 opens a door rather than completing a move. Before a single AKT reaches Solana, two independent audits and at least two more on-chain votes still have to happen.

The summary going around is simple: Akash is leaving Cosmos for Solana. The proposal itself is narrower than that, and the details matter for anyone holding AKT.

Proposal 346 on akashnet-2, titled "Migrate the Akash Protocol to Solana," closes at 15:02 UTC on November 2. A yes vote approves the migration plan and authorises AKT to launch as an SPL token through a one-way portal that stays open for two years. It does not set a cutover date, it does not move a single lease, and on its own it does not open the portal.

The technical spec for that portal was published the day after voting opened. It shows how many further steps lie between this vote and a working SPL AKT.

It also says plainly what it doesn't do: "it does not set a cutover date and does not move the marketplace." Live leases, staking and governance on akashnet-2 continue as normal. The marketplace moves only after audits and rehearsals pass and "a separate cutover proposal passes on this chain."

A no vote would stop the whole plan. In the proposal's words: "Nothing moves; the token does not launch and the program returns to option analysis."

At 08:43 UTC on November 2, about six hours before voting closed, Mintscan's page for Proposal 346 showed an expected result of "Will PASS":

Turnout stood at 68.80% of bonded stake, against a quorum of 33.40%. The same figures can be read from Akash's own chain API, through the proposal object and its tally endpoint.

Mintscan's 97.2% is yes as a share of all votes cast. Akash's pass threshold leaves abstentions out, and on that basis yes stands at about 98.7%, far above the 50% needed. The veto share is negligible.

The tally has barely changed since early morning. A read of the chain at 07:29 UTC showed almost exactly the same totals, with only a handful of AKT added to the yes side. With turnout and margin that large, the outcome is not in doubt. The more useful question is what happens after it passes.

The portal's design is in AEP-95, "One-Way AKT Sink to Solana,", a draft published on October 27 by core developers Artur Troian and Joseph Chalabi. It describes a "deliberately one-way" bridge with no return path. The authors say this removes "the largest class of bridge attacks."

Why supply doesn't change: every SPL AKT is backed by AKT locked on Akash, and nothing is minted independently on Solana. The token is a legacy SPL mint with six decimals and no freeze authority.

The spec's own risk table names the biggest trust point. The upgrade authority over the Solana programs could "mint without limit." That power is meant to sit with a time-locked multisig appointed by Akash governance, with monitors set to alert on every admin action.

At launch, SPL AKT has no protocol role. It will support "transfers and DEX liquidity only, with no staking or governance rights." Inflation stays on Akash. To seed trading, Overclock Labs, Akash's core developer, will send 1 million AKT through the bridge to a Solana treasury vault. That deposit follows the same rules as everyone else's.

Proposal 346 requires "two independent audits of the token and portal code before launch," with reports published within 30 days. AEP-95's launch sequence shows what that requirement means on-chain.

So Proposal 346 is the first of at least five on-chain decisions. The "2 to 3 weeks" launch estimate depends on the audits finishing and the upgrade and parameter votes passing on schedule.
