Paris Hilton and AOC are pressing the House to pass a deepfake porn bill

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The bipartisan Defiance Act has passed the Senate twice but has not received a House vote, with 70 cosponsors split evenly by party

Paris Hilton and Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, a New York Democrat, pressed House leaders on Tuesday to bring the Defiance Act to a vote, according to CBS News. The bipartisan bill would give people targeted by explicit AI fakes the right to take legal action against anyone who makes or shares such images without permission.

Hilton and Ocasio-Cortez spoke at a news conference outside the Capitol with Rep. Laurel Lee, a Florida Republican and fellow cosponsor. The Senate approved the bill in January without any objection, and it has sat in the House since, according to CBS News. The bill would also entitle victims to reclaim any earnings generated from the images, and it would give them 10 years to file suit.

"For 10 months, this critical legislation has sat untouched in the House, just waiting to be put on the floor," Ocasio-Cortez said, according to CBS News. She said advocates have been talking with the White House and feel "very optimistic" that President Donald Trump is receptive to the bill.

Hilton directed a plea at Speaker Mike Johnson, Majority Leader Steve Scalise and House Judiciary Chairman Jim Jordan. "You have the power to move this legislation forward," she said. Hilton said that among the 119th Congress's 156 women, 83 are the subject of sexual deepfakes found online.

Johnson's office did not answer CBS News when asked what is holding the bill up. Johnson told reporters in September that the bill was "good policy" but that House members had not yet united behind it, according to CNBC. CNBC reported that lawmakers in the House have not disclosed what exactly they object to.

Ocasio-Cortez introduced the House version in May 2025, and the House Judiciary Committee received it for review, according to CNBC. The Senate also passed a version in 2024. The House bill has 70 cosponsors, with Democrats and Republicans each accounting for 35.

The Sexual Violence Prevention Association, which contributed to drafting the bill, sent letters to Johnson and Scalise on Tuesday requesting that they schedule it for a House floor vote, according to CNBC. "Now it's time for House leadership to say yes," said Omny Miranda Martone, the group's founder and CEO, in a statement.

Should the House let the current Congress wrap up without taking action, supporters would need to introduce the bill again in the next Congress, according to CNBC.
