Masked ICE Agents Arrest Father And Daughter Boarding Alaska Airlines Flight, But “Visa Overstay” Is Not The Whole Story

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

ICE arrested a father and daughter as they boarded an Alaska Airlines flight in Seattle, and the government’s explanation is that they overstayed their visas. But their attorney says they entered legally, have pending immigration applications and valid work permits, and have no criminal history. I support secure borders…but I think this operation deserves a better explanation than “they broke the law.”

By now, you may have seen the disturbing passenger recordings from the jet bridge for Alaska Airlines flight 1306 from Seattle to Los Angeles on November 21, 2026.

Masked federal officers detained Ricardo Larrondo Mendoza and his adult daughter, Sofia Hernandez Mejia, during boarding. The encounter involved a struggle in front of other passengers and delayed the flight.

The crew was so shaken by the incident that replacements had to be called in, delaying the flight for three hours.

Yesterday, the family’s attorney, Olia Catala, claimed they were traveling to California for a family baptism.

ICE says Mendoza overstayed his visa. It also alleges that Hernandez Mejia threw hot coffee at officers during the encounter and says she could face an assault charge.

On November 21, 2026, at approximately 8:05 a.m. PST, ICE officers conducted a targeted enforcement operation to arrest Ricardo Larrondo Mendoza, an illegal alien from Mexico who overstayed his visa in violation of our nation’s laws.

The individual who threw the hot coffee was identified as Sofia Hernandez Mejia, an illegal alien from Mexico and visa overstay. She was subsequently arrested as well. She will be charged with assaulting a federal officer.

The family’s lawyer disputes that account, saying the coffee spilled during the confrontation. An eyewitness has also challenged the government’s description. If Mejia did deliberately throw coffee on officers, she should be held accountable.

The family’s account adds information that is critical to this discussion.

Catala says the father and daughter entered the United States legally in 2015 and subsequently sought asylum. The father’s application remains pending. The daughter has since married a U.S. citizen and has a separate application to adjust her immigration status. According to their attorney, both have work authorization and neither has a criminal record.

Yes, a work permit is not a Green Card and a pending immigration application does not automatically prevent detention: I am not declaring that ICE lacked legal authority to make these arrests.

Even so, if the government permits someone to work while it considers an immigration application, the expiration of the original tourist admission does not tell us everything we need to know about that person’s circumstances.

We constantly hear that people should come legally and follow the process. Here, the family’s lawyer says they entered legally and have been pursuing that process for years. I’m not seeing any information to justify the raid in hte DHS statement above…I struggle to understand the urgency of arresting these particular travelers on the way to a family baptism.

And sorry, but I don’t think federal agents should be allowed to wear masks. Here it may have violated Washington State law, but it is more fundamental: the masks betray fear…not reasonable fear of crazy people attacking them for doing their jobs…but subconscious fear that they pursuing injustice, not justice, and that there is often a difference between the spirit of the law and the letter of the law.

I hope that President Trump and the Democratic-controlled Congress can reach a grand bargain on immigration reform. Give Trump his wall if it means a pathway to citizenship for those otherwise law-abiding citizens who may have entered illegally but are now part of the American fabric. Let’s make a deal.

Alaska Airlines published its statement on Saturday afternoon, complaining it was not given notice of the immigration raid:

“In this instance, Alaska Airlines was not provided appropriate notice of the enforcement action.”

The airline says it must comply with applicable law, but does not direct or coordinate immigration enforcement. It expressed concern for affected passengers and employees and said it would work with the Port of Seattle and government authorities to determine whether proper protocols were followed.

Alaska too is right to demand answers about an operation that disrupted boarding and left its employees managing the consequences.

The Port of Seattle’s initial statement opened:

“We are outraged about this morning’s incident involving ICE and two passengers as travelers boarded AS 1306 to Los Angeles.”

The Port said it did not participate in the enforcement action and was examining with DHS whether previously agreed airport protocols had been breached. On Monday, commissioners went further, calling for a review of airport access and security credentials. Commissioner Sam Cho said:

“The traveling public and our airport employees should not be left wondering whether they are witnessing a lawful arrest or an abduction. Period”

That concern strikes me as reasonable. Passengers should be able to identify law enforcement and understand what is happening around them, especially in a confined space immediately outside an aircraft.

Again and again I return to this: whether an agency has authority to act and whether it exercised that authority wisely are separate questions.

I know that interdicting illegal aliens make a lot sense in airports because it is easy to verify where someone is and relatively easy to detain them if you know what flight they will be boarding or arriving on. But on a crowded jetbridge?

A jet bridge is a narrow passageway with passengers carrying bags, sometimes holding children, and often standing in a line that barely moves. There is little room to step away when a confrontation begins.

I see abosutley no need to for these (yes, I see no need for these raids at all for visa overstays without criminal records). I also reject the idea that concern about these tactics means supporting open borders or opposing immigration enforcement. We can enforce immigration laws and still expect the government to exercise restraint and judgment.

For me, the unresolved coffee allegation reinforces the need for a careful review. Deliberately throwing hot coffee at officers would be serious (an accidental spill during a sudden struggle would be a very different matter). The available accounts conflict, and the consequences for the daughter could be substantial.

There should be an accounting of what occurred before the passenger recordings began, how officers identified themselves, and why this operation unfolded during boarding. I hope more recordings emerge.

The government says it arrested visa overstayers. Their lawyer says they have pending immigration applications, work permits, and no criminal history. Both seem to be true, which is precisely why the victory lap by DHS is an inadequate explanation.

I support enforcing immigration laws, but I want to understand why these travelers were targeted in this manner and whether the force used was justified. The GOP lost big in the Midterms because reasonable Americans who do support secure borders did not vote for this sort of nefarious tomfoolery. Alaska Airlines and airport officials deserve answers. So do the family and the passengers who found themselves caught in the middle. As I often ask, where are the adults in the room?
