Southwest Airlines Plus Card Review: Modest Benefits For A Low Annual Fee

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Southwest Airlines and Chase have a great portfolio of co-branded credit cards at a variety of “price points” (and by that I mean annual fees). In this post, I’d like to take a closer look at the Southwest Rapid Rewards® Plus Credit Card, which is Southwest Airlines’ lowest annual fee credit card.

In my Southwest Plus Card review, I’ll cover the benefits and explain why now is a good time to consider this card if you want to use credit card spending to earn the Companion Pass. I’ll also explain why other Southwest cards can be better, and share a great alternative to earning Southwest Rapid Rewards points altogether.

Let’s take a look at the Southwest Plus Card‘s welcome bonus, annual fee, and eligibility rules, as these are some of the most important factors to take into account if you’re considering applying.

The Southwest Plus Card is offering a limited time welcome bonus: Earn 80,000 bonus points after spending $1,000 on purchases in the first 3 months from account opening. This is one of the higher bonuses I’ve seen on this card recently, making now a particularly good time to apply.

Rapid Rewards is a revenue-based frequent flyer program, and based on my latest miles & points valuations, I consider Rapid Rewards points to be worth 1.2 cents each. So to me, the points from this welcome offer are worth $960.

In terms of Southwest credit card eligibility, the card isn’t available to anyone who currently has a Southwest Rapid Rewards personal card, or anyone who has received a welcome bonus on a Southwest Rapid Rewards personal card in the past 24 months. However, you are eligible if you have a Southwest business credit card.

Beyond the restrictions related to which Southwest cards you have, keep in mind the general restrictions for Chase card approvals, including the 5/24 rule. However, there are increasing data points of this no longer being consistently enforced.

Another rule worth mentioning here involves how Southwest credit card welcome bonuses help you earn the Companion Pass, which lets you bring a companion with you on flights (both cash and award tickets) and pay just taxes and fees.

For a long time now, the points earned as part of Southwest credit card welcome bonuses have counted towards the 135,000 qualifying points needed to earn the Companion Pass. But starting soon, credit card welcome bonuses will no longer count toward qualification.

You’ll need to apply for a card by December 2, 2026, for your welcome bonus to count toward the Companion Pass for a final time. So apply for this card now if you’re hoping to earn that perk with the help of credit card spending.

Now is also your last chance to get an annual boost of 10,000 Companion Pass qualifying points as a Southwest credit card holder. Starting next year, instead of a boost of 10,000 TQPs for having a Southwest card, you’ll get 10,000 TQPs if your TQP balance is 130,000 by December 31, 2027. That would be enough to get you to Platinum status, which will include unlimited Companion Pass trips as a benefit.

This change eliminates the easiest way to earn this valuable benefit, and it’s not the only new rule: referral bonuses, anniversary points, bonus points from spending, and partner spending won’t count toward the Companion Pass either. In fact, Southwest’s rolling the Companion Pass into its highest elite tier, Platinum, and requiring either 140,000 TQPs or 100 qualifying flights to earn it.

The Southwest Plus Card has a $99 annual fee, and you can add authorized users to the card at no extra cost.

$99 is the lowest annual fee of any Southwest Airlines credit card. Looking at the high end, Southwest’s personal cards have annual fees of up to $229, and Southwest’s business cards have annual fees of up to $299.

The Southwest Plus Card has no foreign transaction fees, so it’s a solid card to use for purchases abroad.

Since I value Southwest Rapid Rewards points at 1.2 cents each, I think you get an effective return of 2.4% on purchases that qualify for 2x points, and a 1.2% return on everything else.

It’s nice that the card has some bonus categories, though there are ultimately more rewarding cards out there for everyday spending. Plus, a few of the non-Southwest bonus categories have an annual cap, which limits how useful this card is for maximizing your return on gas station and grocery store purchases.

I have a dedicated post that covers how to redeem Southwest Rapid Rewards points for maximum value, but let’s briefly look at how you can use the points you earn from the Southwest Plus Card.

Rapid Rewards is a revenue-based frequent flyer program, meaning that the value you get from your points is tied to the cash cost of Southwest tickets. There are a few different levels, ranging from Basic tickets to Choice Extra fares, so the exact value can vary a bit.

Basic award fares tend to offer the best redemption value. You’ll also usually get the best value on international Southwest routes to destinations like Costa Rica and Aruba. I’ve been able to get about 2.0 cents per point on these redemptions.

Starting in spring 2027, Southwest will add points earning and redemption opportunities with select airline partners, which should make your Rapid Rewards points more valuable for international travel. The planned partners include Icelandair, China Airlines, and Etihad Airways — I look forward to seeing how these add value to the program.

One great thing about the Southwest Rapid Rewards program is that there are no change or cancellation fees, and rebooking a fare when the price drops gets you a refund equal to any difference in points. Just note that Basic fares can only be changed by upgrading to a higher fare bundle, so for a cheaper Basic award you’d cancel and rebook rather than change.

The Southwest Plus Card offers some benefits that could make the card worth keeping long term. However, this is also Southwest’s lowest annual fee card, so it doesn’t have the most benefits. What are the ongoing perks of having this card?

The Southwest Plus Card gets the primary cardholder and up to eight additional passengers on the same reservation a first checked bag at no additional cost. Checked bags on Southwest cost $45 each way, so this can save you up to $90 roundtrip on checked bag fees per person. So with just one roundtrip flight per year, this benefit can almost offset the annual fee.

Southwest now charges for seat selection, so it’s helpful that the Southwest Plus Card includes complimentary standard seat selection starting 48 hours out from departure. This perk is based on availability, and it could save you around $20 to $40 per flight.

Another Southwest benefit is early boarding. You’ll be able to board with Group 5, or earlier if you have elite status or a fare or seat type that qualifies you for an earlier boarding group. Southwest has eight total boarding groups, so while there are several ahead of Group 5, it’s better than boarding last.

Each year on your Southwest Plus Card cardmember anniversary, you’ll get a 10% promo code that applies to Southwest flights. Just note that this discount excludes Basic fares.

On your Southwest Plus Card cardmember anniversary every year, you’ll receive 3,000 bonus points. Southwest Rapid Rewards is a revenue-based frequent flyer program, and you’ll typically need 83 Rapid Rewards points for every dollar of airfare you book. In other words, those 3,000 Rapid Rewards points should get you around $36 worth of airfare on Southwest annually.

As part of an overhaul of the Rapid Rewards program, Southwest is simplifying TQP earning on credit cards. Starting in 2027, every dollar spent on a co-branded Southwest credit card will earn you one TQP. In other words, $35K in spending would earn you Silver, $65K in spending would earn you Gold, and $140K in spending would earn you Platinum.

This earning rate is the same across cards, so this isn’t specific to the Southwest Plus Card.

The Southwest Plus Card offers 25% savings on inflight purchases of Wi-Fi and drinks. Simply pay with your card, and you’ll automatically receive a statement credit for that amount.

One of the great features of Chase cards is access to Chase Offers, which provides savings on purchases with all kinds of retailers. This program has saved me a significant amount of money.

The Southwest Plus Card is a solid entry-level airline credit card. If you want a credit card from Southwest and want an annual fee as low as possible, then this card could make sense to you.

You’ll receive a 3,000-point bonus on your cardmember anniversary every year, and 25% savings on inflight purchases. The free checked bag benefit can save you $90 on a roundtrip and the complimentary standard seat selection benefit can add up quickly after a few flights. These perks can more than justify the annual fee, and that’s not even accounting for the massive welcome bonus.

However, there are other options that I think can make more sense.

If your main reason for considering the Southwest Rapid Rewards Plus Card is to earn points for Southwest award flights, you might be better off skipping a Southwest co-branded credit card altogether and just earning Chase Ultimate Rewards points. You can transfer Chase points to Southwest to book flights, and cards like the Chase Sapphire Preferred® Card (learn more) have stronger earning categories and benefits overall.

You can also redeem Ultimate Rewards points with other travel partners, not just Southwest, so this option gives you more flexibility.
