Star Alliance member Air New Zealand is gearing up to commence its newest long-haul service, as the airline adds Christchurch (CHC) to Perth (PER) from November 30. This will be the first time the airline has operated the city pair since it terminated the nonstop service from New Zealand's South Island in 2019.

The flights are set to run seasonally, three times a week, providing another alternative for travelers looking to experience Australia's West Coast. Air New Zealand will face competition on the route, as Jetstar also operates a seasonal service.

The airline's decision to resume services to Perth came in the same announcement ​​​​​ as the carrier added Singapore (SIN) and Tokyo Narita (NRT) from the Garden City. Christchurch is the second largest city in the South Pacific nation, and its airport is the only one in the South Island equipped for widebody aircraft.

Air New Zealand will resume the service to Perth on November 30, and operate the flight up to three times a week through until May 7. These flights will complement the airline's existing year-round service from Auckland (AKL).

These new flights will add up to an additional 37,000 seats across the Southern Hemisphere summer season, and are a welcome addition to the Southern airports expansion, off the back of a successful season of short-haul seasonal flights to Rarotonga (RAR). The resumption of services to Perth from Christchurch will cater to strong travel demand that otherwise has seen travelers forced to connect via other airports, including Auckland, Brisbane, Melbourne or Sydney. As already mentioned, Jetstar, the low-cost subsidiary of the Qantas Group, commenced its own seasonal service from Perth to Christchurch with its Airbus A321LR narrowbody twinjets.

These services are expected to run through until late March. Air New Zealand's return to the city pair marks the only widebody and aircraft with premium cabins serving the route. Jetstar's schedule is detailed in the table below.

Air New Zealand has an extensive domestic and trans-Tasman network from Christchurch, and the new service will be popular with both leisure and business travelers. Those visiting the South Island can now transit via Christchurch Airport from other airports across the island, without the need to travel via another major hub.

Low-cost carrier Jetstar prepares to launch its first-ever flights between Western Australia and New Zealand's South Island.

With Air New Zealand having commenced its nonstop services from Christchurch to Singapore on October 28, this marked the airline's resumption of the route since it was suspended in February 2020. On the day of the first flight this year, the carrier announced that it would actually expand its schedule to year-round.

This complements the existing daily Singapore Airlines service to Christchurch, on which it partners through a joint venture. Services from Christchurch to Tokyo Narita also take flight on November 28, providing nonstop services to the Japanese capital through until late May. Air New Zealand previously operated nonstop flights between the two cities up until 2015.

While not operated with long-haul aircraft, Air New Zealand also quietly resumed nonstop services from Christchurch to Adelaide (ADL) in October. This route is served twice weekly onboard the carrier's Airbus A320 or A321neo, and will continue through to the end of March.
