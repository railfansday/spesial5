European powers rallied behind Ukraine at a meeting of the "Coalition of the Willing" in Berlin earlier today.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz emphasized that he wanted European powers to play a role in bringing the war in Ukraine to an end.

"We know that this war will only end if there is a diplomatic solution," Merz said after the meeting in the German capital on Thursday.

"But this war must end with a Ukraine that preserves its territorial integrity, has strong security guarantees on its side and is above all embedded in European security," he added.

IG Metall, Germany's largest union, railed against automaker Volkswagen's latest demands on its workforce days before both sides are set to begin collective bargaining talks next Tuesday.   The union described VW's vision for the future of work at the Wolfsburg-based giant as a "catalog of horrors."

VW, the world's second-largest car company by both sales and production, has seen profits nosedive — it announced quarterly losses of €5.8 billion ($6.5 billion) in October — for a number of reasons, ranging from increased competition to tariffs and soft sales. Now the company plans to slash 120,000 jobs at 10 brands by 2030.

Arguing that its financial outlook has "deteriorated," VW management says it has no choice but to make deeper cuts faster than expected.

VW wants to scrap the current contract's 35-hour week and is demanding employees work overtime without pay if needed and went so far as to announce that pay would be "adjusted" if workers were forced to work fewer than 35 hours. Moreover, it is calling for a pay freeze for 2027-2028, as well as cuts to benefits for long-term employees.

IG Metall says workers haven't seen a raise since May 2024 and is calling for a 5% hike now, citing ever-rising living costs.

Regional IG representative Thorsten Gröger said, "VW management is stabbing its employees the back." He added that negotiations will be "tenacious, bitter and exhausting" and that they "will leave deep scars at the company."

Workers are primed for walkouts, with the union warning that the battle may be far larger than anything VW has ever seen.

VW works council chairwoman Daniela Cavallo, who represents employees within the company, said, "Management's promises are obviously worthless." Cavallo said workers felt "betrayed" after they agreed to major job cuts for the health of the company two years ago.

The current contract applies to some 130,000 employees at German production sites in Braunschweig, Chemnitz, Dresden, Emden, Hanover, Kassel, Salzgitter, Wolfsburg and Zwickau.

German Defense Minister Boris Pistorius on Thursday called on Europeans to be vigilant against far-right extremism and warned of divisions and labels in society.

Speaking during a visit to Auschwitz-Birkenau, a former concentration camp, Pistorius said the site bore witness to the worst of Nazi Germany's crimes, calling it an absolute low point.

"It all started with defining what made a life unworthy; by dividing according to race, origin, faith, sexual orientation," Pistorius warned, saying that such marginalization was experiencing a "renaissance" and "gaining ground with far-right extremist parties across Europe."

The defense minister said Auschwitz provides all people, but particularly Germans, with a "symbol warning that we all bear responsibility for what happens and what must not be allowed to happen."

During his tour of the camp, Pistorius laid a wreath at its death wall, where Nazi soldiers shot roughly 5,000 prisoners dead, and lit a candle at the victims' memorial.

Before he departed the site, Pistorius expressed his wish that more German students and travel groups would visit the camp "than appears to be the case at present."

The deputy chairman of Germany's right-wing populist Alternative for Germany (AfD) has accused French presidential hopeful Marine Le Pen of deserting her core values in her bid to achieve wider popularity and improve her chance of victory in French presidential elections.

For years, Le Pen claimed to be embarking on a policy of what's often called "de-demonization," trying to shed the extreme-right image of her father and predecessor, Jean-Marie Le Pen, and soften the stance of the party now called the National Rally or RN.

"Mrs. Le Pen, whom we all once admired greatly, now seems to have one fundamental principle: I say whatever the polls demand of me," Sven Tristschler told the right-leaning website The Pioneer.

France is headed into the last months of a presidential election campaign, with incumbent Emmanuel Macron's second and final term coming to an end and no clear candidate emerging as a main challenger to Le Pen as yet.

"At the end of the day, her victory would likely be even more catastrophic for the right than her defeat," Tritscher asserted. "The citizens would think they were voting to the right, only to get another Macron in the end."

He said that in hindsight, he was glad the AfD and RN parties had fallen out in European politics in recent years, and said AfD supporters should not anticipate a similar move toward moderation.

"A hollowing-out will not take place with us," he said, using the German scientific term for enucleation, the removal of the internal nucleus or core of a cell.

The AfD has made similar complaints about Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni, accusing her party of a process of "Melonization" involving a softening of its core policy stances in a bid to retain power.

A similar power struggle is thought to be brewing within the AfD itself as it remains far adrift of majority support despite regularly polling as the largest single party in Germany at present.

Germany's central bank, the Bundesbank, said in its monthly report that inflation in Germany will rise "sharply" at the beginning of 2027.

Inflation could remain stable through the end of the year, given that a fuel tax cut is still keeping a lid on prices.

Inflation had fallen to 2.9% from 3.3% in September after the German government introduced the fuel discount.

But the central bank said the expiration of that fuel tax cut, along with other government measures, would drive inflation higher.

The report also highlighted that natural gas prices are expected to rise.

"On top of this, higher energy and input costs are expected to gradually feed through into the prices of other goods and certain services," the Bundesbank said.

German telecommunications firm, Deutsche Telekom, announced that it will invest €100 million ($109.75 million) in French start-up Mistral AI, Europe's largest artificial intelligence company.

The investment will pour into Mistral from Telekom's subsidiary T.Capital Tech Fund.

Telekom said the move is a first step toward a strategic partnership, where the two firms plan to jointly support companies and the public sector in Germany in introducing tailored AI.

"Europe’s competitiveness depends on our ability to develop and use powerful AI," Telekom board member Ferri Abolhassan said in a statement announcing the deal. "Businesses must be able to trust this technology while retaining control over their data."

The move is the latest investment by Telekom in the AI space. In February, Telekom built an AI factory in Munich, which has since been expanded due to high demand.

Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelenskyy arrived in Berlin for a summit of the "Coalition of the Willing." The group, which represents Kyiv's allies, is expected to push for a deeper European role in any efforts to end the war.

The process has so far been dominated by the US, Russia and Ukraine.

Zelenskyy wrote on X that "we are doing everything we can to ensure that Europe's presence in the negotiation process is fully represented."

US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff flew to Germany to attend the summit at the Chancellery.

An 18-year-old in western Germany has been held in pre-trial detention in Germany since August over the alleged plot to carry out a terrorist attack on a mosque, prosecutors have said.

The teen, who is from the town of Eslohe in North Rhine-Westphalia, allegedly held far-right extremist views and was planning the attack for late 2026 or early 2027, senior public prosecutor Alexandra Wiese said.

"He also directed his racist ideology against people of the Muslim faith," Wiese told public broadcaster WDR.

A search of the suspect's home led investigators to find numerous weapons, weapon parts and ammunition, including items manufactured using a 3D printer.

Wiese did not provide details of potential targets in order to protect possible victims.

No charges have yet been filed against the teen and Wiese stressed that the presumption of innocence applies as the investigation continues.

Italian commercial bank UniCredit and the German government reached agreement on the big sticking points of its takeover of Commerzbank.

The EU greenlit UniCredit's takeover of Germany's Commerzbank earlier this week.

The merging of ​UniCredit and Commerzbank would create a bank with more than €1.3 trillion ($1.4 trillion) in assets across two of the eurozone's largest economies.

It would be one of the most significant cross-border ​European banking deals in years.

In a statement to Reuters news agency, Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil said Germany "supports a swift process and an orderly transition of control."

"We have reached common ground and UniCredit has ‌made clear commitments to Germany as a business location, to the rights of Commerzbank employees and to a continued constructive dialogue with ‌the works ​council," Klingbeil said, referring to a body of elected employee representatives.

Among the concessions, Germany is said to have given up its insistence on naming two supervisory board members to Commerzbank.

Meanwhile, UniCredit agreed to the key German demand to keep UniCredit's enlarged German business headquartered and listed in Frankfurt.

UniCredit also reportedly pledged to put German nationals on UniCredit's board of directors.

German Defense Minister Boris Pistorius will travel to Poland for a two-day visit.

Pistorius is scheduled to meet his Polish counterpart, Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz, in the capital, Warsaw, for talks on defense policy.

Poland is a member of both the EU and NATO and is one of the most important political and military allies of Ukraine.

The trip to Germany's neighbor to the east will begin with a stop at the memorial site of the former Nazi extermination camp Auschwitz-Birkenau, which was built during the German occupation of Poland during World War II.

Auschwitz-Birkenau is one of the biggest symbols of the Holocaust and Germany's Nazi regime, with some 1.1 million people having died in the camp between 1940 and 1945, most of them Jews.

The camp's victims were shot, murdered in gas chambers or died of starvation and disease.

The German Federal Office for Information Security (BSI) said in a report that cheap gadgets such as streaming boxes are increasingly being hijacked by cybercriminals.

According to the report, the number of hacked systems has nearly doubled within a year, with BSI reporting some 75,400 infected systems in Germany to internet providers every day, an 89% increase. Some of the gadgets were found to be already infected at the factory, the report says.

The vast majority of cases involved Android devices, particularly consumer electronics connected to the so-called "Internet of Things."

BSI said criminals rent the hijacked devices as proxy botnets on the dark web to hide their own attacks, which would allow for consumers themselves to unknowingly come under investigators' scrutiny.

BSI President Claudia Plattner has also warned of AI-driven attacks.

"The disruption coming in particular from high-performance, agentic AI has set new benchmarks," she said.
