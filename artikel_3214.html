Asbury: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Software integration headwinds are fading, freeing management to focus on store productivity and debt reduction.

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Oct. 27, 2026, Asbury (ABG -2.12%) announced that it completed converting all of its dealerships to a single software system. Long-term investors should care because operational integration drag is ending while productivity gains start showing up in store results.

Asbury finished converting all dealerships to Tekion, a dealer management software platform that handles daily operations. Productivity typically dips during a six-month learning period before staff master the tool, but long-tenured stores are already delivering higher service profits.

Management shifted strategy to expand used car volume while preserving margins. Used retail volume rose 4.5% from the previous quarter, supported by disciplined store appraisal processes.

Management's directing cash flow toward paying down debt rather than buying back stock or acquiring dealerships. Asbury closed the quarter with a net leverage ratio of 3.5 times operating earnings and wants to lower that ratio before repurchasing shares.

Operational focus is shifting back to core dealership execution now that software integration headwinds are fading. Management expects selling, general, and administrative expenses as a percentage of gross profit to reach the low-60% range by late 2027 as store productivity builds.

The company plans to maintain capital discipline while working down balance sheet debt over the next two years. Lowering debt builds strategic flexibility, paving the way for eventual stock repurchases and targeted dealership acquisitions once leverage targets are met.
