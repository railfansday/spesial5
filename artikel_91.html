She Is Rewriting Mexico’s Ballads, One Woman’s Story at a Time

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Grammy-nominated songwriter Vivir Quintana is known throughout Latin America for giving voice to women who fought back against domestic violence.

Grammy-nominated songwriter Vivir Quintana is known throughout Latin America for giving voice to women who fought back against domestic violence.

Against the backdrop of a centuries-old stone church in the Mexican city of Oaxaca, the singer Vivir Quintana raised her deep, husky voice above the blare of trumpets, trombones and the booming pulse of a tuba.

Behind her stood a brass band of young Indigenous girls in embroidered white blouses and brightly colored skirts. Hat in hand and fist raised, Ms. Quintana sang a corrido, a traditional Mexican folk ballad usually used to recount tales of revolutionaries, outlaws, folk heroes, and more recently, drug traffickers.

Corridos are typically sung by men, about men. Ms. Quintana sang a corrido she wrote based on the true story of a woman who fought back when her abusive partner tried to gouge out her eyes with a knife.

“I used to think that what united us women was our pain. Now I have realized it is love and resistance,” Ms. Quintana told the crowd in October during a women’s conference in Oaxaca called Decididas, drawing a roar of applause. “We must keep defending ourselves!”

On Thursday, Ms. Quintana will take the stage at the Latin Grammy Awards in Las Vegas alongside the Colombian pop superstar Juanes, with whom she is nominated for song of the year for “Humano.” It is her second Latin Grammy nomination.
