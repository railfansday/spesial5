Kellie Sloane says she’s ‘pretty sure’ NSW Liberals exodus is over after Mark Speakman resigns

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Former opposition leader warns party against being ‘One Nation lite’ as he becomes eighth out of 24 Liberal MPs not to recontest seat in March

The New South Wales opposition leader, Kellie Sloane, says she is “pretty sure” the exodus of Liberals before the March state election has finished, after Mark Speakman became the eighth MP to decide not to recontest their lower house seats.

In a late-night speech on Thursday, the final sitting day of NSW’s legislative assembly before the vote, Speakman said he would not run again in his south Sydney seat of Cronulla, which he has held since 2011.

A third of the 24 current Liberal MPs have announced they will not recontest their seat in March, including Jordan Lane, Mark Coure, Tanya Davies, Ray Williams, James Griffin, Alister Henskens and Mark Taylor.

In a press conference on Friday, Sloane insisted the departures were not an indictment of the party’s election prospects.

“No, that’s not the truth. What we are seeing is an impressive cross section of abilities and talent and tenure in our party,” she told reporters in the same room where, exactly a year ago, Speakman announced he would resign as Liberal leader in her favour.

“We have people who have been in government before. We have people who have been in for a medium amount of time, and we’re going to have people with fresh ideas.”

Sloane said she had tried to convince Speakman to stay and said many of the MPs leaving had been in parliament for more than 10 years, although Lane is a first-term MP.

Sloane said a significant number of Liberals had retired before the 2023 election, in which the Coalition lost government and the party dropped nine seats.

Asked if she could guarantee that further MPs would not step down ahead of the March vote, Sloane said: “Look, I certainly hope not. I certainly hope not. I’m pretty sure that’s done. I’m pretty sure that’s done.”

Since last year’s leadership spill, the Coalition has gone from commanding 28% of the primary vote in NSW to 23%, Sydney Morning Herald Resolve polling suggests.

Labor has gone from 37% to 30%, while One Nation, which Resolve did not start polling separately until March, is now at 26%.

Last week, Sloane said her party would not form government with One Nation, but she did not rule out a confidence and supply arrangement. The Australian Financial Review has reported many of her MPs privately expect the Liberals to go into a coalition with One Nation.

On Friday, Sloane rejected the claims from “anonymous names in a Financial Review article”, saying the Coalition only needed to flip six Labor seats to form minority government.

In his speech in the dying minutes of the parliamentary term, Speakman, a member of the party’s moderate faction, said the Liberals were “said to face an existential threat” following a wipeout at the 2025 federal election and an “orange wave that seems to be coming across the country”.

He warned the Liberals against becoming “Labor lite” or “One Nation lite”, saying they should stick to the values of “Menzian Liberalism”.

Referencing Pauline Hanson’s notion of “monoculture”, Speakman said Australia was “the most successful multicultural society on the planet” and was “so much better for it”.

“You know, we hear certain people say we want a monoculture, and that people who don’t support that monoculture somehow are enemies of Australia, hate Australia,” he said.

“I love Australia for its multiculturalism. Diversity is our strength. It is our strength, and we should build on that.”

Speakman, a barrister, served as environment minister between 2015 and 2017, and was attorney general between 2017 and the Perrottet government’s defeat in the 2023 election.

He was chosen as the Liberal and opposition leader after Dominic Perrottet’s resignation, a position he held until November last year.

On Thursday, he said being opposition leader “is often said to be the toughest job in politics” and, while it had “ultimately unravelled” for him, he had mostly led an opposition “which was competitive with, or even ahead of, the government in the polls”.

He said his record as attorney general included legislating for NSW to be the first Australian jurisdiction to criminalise coercive control, introducing affirmative consent tests in criminal sexual assault trials and reforming criminal and civil child sexual abuse cases.

Earlier on Thursday, the NSW premier, Chris Minns, announced the former Liberal councillor and parliamentary staffer Tanya Raffoul would contest the Liberal-held seat of Epping as a Labor candidate.

On Friday, Sloane said Raffoul had been talking to her about Liberal party roles “only two months ago”.

“She’s decided to chase personal ambition. She’s a very ambitious person, but she’s changed sides, and that’s a decision Chris Minns has made.”

Earlier, the NSW treasurer, Daniel Mookhey, rejected the idea that One Nation was pushing Labor to become “Liberal lite”, saying Raffoul’s candidacy reflected “a journey that I think many Liberal people are going through as well”.
