Wales look to World Cup when taking on Japan

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Wales will have one eye on the World Cup when they take on Japan at finals weekend of the Nations Championship.

Steve Tandy's side finish the tournament against the Brave Blossoms at Twickenham's Allianz Stadium on Saturday (13:10 GMT).

Wales name their team on Thursday morning and are poised to freshen up the XV after successive Tests against Japan, New Zealand and Australia in Cardiff.

"There will be some changes," said forwards coach Danny Wilson. "In terms of growing the squad and competition, there will be some players getting an opportunity and freshening up.

"There were some boys that played really well against New Zealand that didn't play against Australia.

"The aim is to really try and grow the squad, to improve the squad because there is a big year ahead of us."

The Japan fixture presents a final opportunity to look at the wider group before the first World Cup training squad is named next summer, with experimentation unlikely during the Six Nations.

Eleven players have started all three November fixtures - Blair Murray, Louis Rees-Zammit, Eddie James, Max Llewellyn, Sam Costelow, Tomos Williams, Nicky Smith, Dewi Lake, Archie Griffin, Dafydd Jenkins and Jac Morgan.

Full-back Murray, centre James and lock Jenkins have played every minute this month, and Murray was also on the pitch throughout the July away fixtures.

"You need to have one eye on the longer-term plan and what we are trying to piece together for the World Cup and where we think we need to be with the squad," admitted Wilson.

There are four uncapped players in the squad – Scarlets wing Macs Page, Dragons centre Aneurin Owen, Dragons scrum-half Che Hope and Cardiff loose-head prop Rhys Barratt.

Seven others - full-back Tom Rogers, wing Ellis Mee, centre or fly-half Joe Hawkins, fly-half Jarrod Evans, hooker Liam Belcher, tight-head Keiron Assiratti and lock Seb Davies - are yet to feature in November.

Wales enjoyed a historic win against New Zealand in the second November fixture but made four changes to the starting line-up that lost to Australia.

The decision to leave man of the match James Botham out against the Wallabies, replaced at blind-side flanker Alex Mann, has been under the spotlight.

The forward's partner, Izzy, was expecting the birth of their first child - and they announced the birth of a daughter at the weekend - but Tandy said that was not a factor in the selection.

James Botham's father Liam criticised the decision on social media after Wales' defeat but Wilson defended his boss Tandy.

"Jim [Botham] was outstanding against the All Blacks but there was a plan to freshen up the squad," said the forwards coach.

"You think about Aaron Wainwright and Alex Mann and what they have done in a Welsh jersey recently as well, we are really fortunate to have depth in the back row.

"With Jim it was nothing to do with his performance, it was very much about freshening the squad going into that game and the ability to grow our squad.

"Taine Basham played against New Zealand and has had a really good campaign. Growing that depth is important."

Botham, Basham and wing Gabriel Hamer-Webb were left out after facing the All Blacks, along with injured lock Adam Beard.

Wales and Japan meet for the fifth time in 16 months and three of the past fixtures, including Wales' 28-21 win at the start of the month, have been settled by a single score.

The biggest margin of victory was Wales' 31-22 win in 2025 to end an 18-Test losing streak.

The expectation will be that Tandy's side enjoy a more comfortable success against a Brave Blossoms side beaten 78-15 by England and 67-5 by Scotland, conceding 24 tries in the process.

"They'll be frustrated and want to finish a long block of games with a better performance against us," said Wilson. "We are fully aware of what will probably be coming."
