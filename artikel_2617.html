Lakers biggest test is coming, but it’s not Spurs, per JJ Redick

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The Lakers' biggest test of the season is coming up.

It's not Friday, when they'll face Victor Wembanyama and the Spurs.

It's Saturday, when the world will be reacting to whatever transpired.

"Whether we lose the game, whether we win the game, we're either going to be contenders on [ESPN's] 'First Take' or we're not going to be contenders and we're fake," Lakers coach JJ Redick said. "That stuff is just noise."

For the Lakers, their upcoming game against the Spurs is a temperature check.

The teams are tied for the best record in the NBA at 15-3. And their stars, Luka Doncic and Victor Wembanyama, are in a two-man race for the MVP Award.

In other words, this is prime theater. And we all know what that means.

San Antonio Spurs center Victor Wembanyama dunks against the Portland Trail Blazers in Portland, Ore., Wednesday, Nov. 25, 2026. AP Photo/Craig Mitchelldyer

If the Lakers win, they'll be the front-runners for the title. Doncic will officially be ahead of Wembanyama in the MVP race. The darlings of the NBA will be the crown jewel of the basketball world.

But if they lose, well, the opposite will happen. There will be some version of sound the alarm. Press the panic button. The Lakers aren't elite. Doncic isn't the real deal. Walker Kessler can't compete against the league's top centers.

So, the Lakers' biggest test isn't going to be whether they win a game in November.

It's going to be whether they can ignore the inevitable chatter when it becomes deafening. And it's about to be dialed up to a feverish pitch.

Luka Doncic (77) of the Los Angeles Lakers handles the ball against the Utah Jazz on Monday, Nov. 23, 2026, at Crypto.com Arena in Los Angeles. NBAE via Getty Images

That's what happens when the two top teams in the league meet, especially when one of those teams is the Lakers, who are arguably under a more powerful magnifying glass than any other franchise in the league.

It's fun to talk about the Lakers. They've won 17 championships. They've had some of the biggest stars in NBA history, including Kareem Abdul-Jabbar, Magic Johnson, Kobe Bryant, LeBron James and Doncic. They're a globally recognized brand.

When they win, they're the talk of the town. When they lose, the sky is falling. They inspire overreactions. They're the dream fodder for pundits.

Los Angeles Lakers head coach JJ Redick watches his players during an NBA basketball game against the Utah Jazz in Los Angeles, Monday, Nov. 23, 2026. AP Photo/Kyusung Gong

There are nine new players on the Lakers. They haven't experienced the weight of that attention. It can be paralyzing. Even Doncic, who has been in the spotlight for most of his life, has never been the face of a franchise as big as the Lakers.

If the Lakers play great, they can't let it get to their heads. And if they fall apart, they need to dust their shoulders and move forward.

Championship teams lose games. But they don't allow themselves to be defined by them.

So, this game doesn't matter and yet it means everything.

It's the ultimate test for a young group. And it has nothing to do with the score.
