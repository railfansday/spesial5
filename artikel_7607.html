Over $157M in pro bono legal services donated by law class of 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

The class of 2026 has donated more than $157 million in pro bono work, or more than 4.3 million hours in legal services, according to an Association of American Law Schools survey released Friday.

That’s an average of 197 of donated hours per student, according to this fall’s survey of 110 law schools, which reported on 22,249 students, or about 58% of the class of 2026 in ABA-accredited schools. With a value of $36.14 an hour, according to nonprofit coalition Independent Sector, the donated time has an estimated value of $157 million.

And these numbers might be low, according to the report, since the hours are difficult to track and did not include law students in master’s degree programs. Total donated hours could be more than $250 million if average hours per student were applied to the total enrollment of all AALS member law schools, the study states.

In the decade since the survey was established, it has reported more than $1 billion in legal services contributions from law students.

Many law schools now emphasize serving clients in legal deserts. Some offer remote pro bono opportunities and others offer clinics, externships and fellowships. For instance, University of Nebraska College of Law Rural Estate Planning Clinic students provide free estate‑planning services to senior citizens in rural communities, preparing documents like wills, powers of attorney, living wills and transfer‑on‑death deeds.

“Access to justice is a first principle for legal education and the legal profession,” Danielle M. Conway, AALS president, said in the release. “The pro bono and clinical legal work done by today’s law students help to bridge access-to-justice gaps and reinforce the profession’s obligation to serve every community.”
