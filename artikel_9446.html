Closing the Skills Gap for Autistic Employees

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The unemployment rate for workers with autism is staggering. One Australian company is aiming to change that.

In Australia, the unemployment rate for neurodivergent adults hovers around 34%, nearly eight times the national average. Clearly, there is a stigma around hiring neurodivergent people and it stems from a fear of the unknown. One not-for-profit company, Australian Spatial Analytics (ASA), is working to fix that.

ASA was established in 2020 to increase workforce participation among young neurodivergent adults by training them in big data for the geospatial and digital engineering fields, then placing them in corporate and government sectors. At ASA, we leverage these employees’ cognitive strengths — attention to detail, pattern recognition, laser-sharp focus, and innovative problem-solving skills, all of which are invaluable in these fields.

About 80% of ASA’s staff identify as neurodivergent, which includes ADHD, autism, dyslexia, dyscalculia, and dyspraxia. ASA’s recruitment process goes beyond conventional practices; it assesses potential candidates based on transferable skills, hobbies, and interests, rather than relying on formal qualifications or previous experience. Many of the new data analysts we have successfully placed in jobs are in paid positions for the first time.

Yet the analysts’ value to their employers is without questions. Executives have praised the ASA-trained analysts who have joined their companies, citing their work ethic, dedication, and problem-solving skills.

During the job-seeking process, ASA provides candidates with sample interview questions 48 hours in advance to help minimize anxiety. Once hired, employees receive comprehensive and paid on-the-job training that includes soft skills like communication and self-advocacy coaching.

[Read: Nail Your Job Interview – 3 Strategies for Neurodivergent Applicants]

Based on our experience, creating a supportive and productive environment requires only minor accommodations. Flexible start and finish times, predictable routines, the option for hybrid work to reduce commuting stress, adjustable lighting, noise-canceling headphones, and direct communication make a profound difference, not only for neurodivergent people, but for everyone in the workplace.

To help change paradigms, ASA offers a Neurodiversity in the Workplace Training Program for other organizations. It covers neurodiversity awareness, plus effective strategies for communication, managing anxiety in the work environment, leading neurodiverse teams, and promoting neurodivergent inclusion.

Ultimately, our goal is to put ourselves out of business.

Karly Ischenko is a Human Resources Business Partner at Australian Spatial Analytics.
