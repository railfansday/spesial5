AFC postpones Asian Cup in Saudi Arabia to late 2027 due to conflict in Middle East

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Tournament pushed back 11 months with proposed start on 23 December

New dates with 21 January 2028 final must be approved by Fifa

The 2027 Asian Cup in Saudi Arabia has been postponed as a result of the conflict in the Middle East.

After a month of speculation following the airport attack on Riyadh’s international airport, the Asian Football Confederation (AFC) confirmed on Tuesday that the tournament had been pushed back 11 months.

The rescheduled tournament is expected to kick off in the 2027 Christmas break with a 23 December start date leading up to a 21 January 2028 final.

“The organising committee for AFC Asian Cup approved to reschedule the flagship national team tournament in light of the current exceptional circumstances arising from developments in the region, including substantial logistical complexities and travel disruptions,” the AFC said in a statement.

“Exceptional circumstances have been created for the organisation and delivery of the competition as originally scheduled, making the postponement necessary in the interest of all participating teams, officials, spectators and other stakeholders.”

The proposed new dates – which fall in one of the busiest periods in European club football – must be approved by Fifa.

Saudi Arabia’s suitability to host the tournament has been a hot topic since the Riyadh airport attack by Iran-backed Houthi rebels in Yemen.

Other nations, who were scheduled to travel to Saudi Arabia in the November international window, have been forced to relocate their friendlies.

There was little appetite for another host nation to step in at such short notice – the tournament was slated to start on 7 January 2027 – but going ahead as planned in the current climate was fraught with risks.

In Australia, the delayed tournament could have ramifications for coach Tony Popovic, who signed a short-term extension on the eve of the World Cup.

Popovic’s contract is due to expire after the previously scheduled January tournament, where Australia were due to face Tajikistan, Singapore and Iraq, who are coached by ex-Socceroos boss Graham Arnold.

Arnold was handed a job reprieve by the Iraqi FA last month after a form slump following the country’s first World Cup qualification in 40 years.
