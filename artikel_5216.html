Bitcoin Price Crashes to $72,700 as Leveraged Traders Buy the Dip: Is the Bottom In?

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

The crypto market crash deepens as $1B+ in longs get liquidated. Bitcoin sits mid-range at $72,969; fear at 28 isn’t a bottom yet.

Bitcoin fell below $73,000 on Thursday, November 12, extending a week-long crypto market crash in which traders who bet on a rebound were forced out of their positions again and again.

Bitcoin touched $72,735 on Binance, a hair above Tuesday’s $72,727 low and its weakest price since August 20. It trades at $72,969, down 9.4% from the November 4 close.

The altcoins are doing worse. Over seven days, Ethereum has lost 16.4%, Solana 14.7% and XRP 21.3%. CoinGecko values the whole crypto market at $2.42 trillion on Thursday, and Bitcoin makes up 60.5% of it.

Leverage made every drop bigger. More than $1 billion of bets were liquidated on Monday alone, almost all of them longs, the positions that profit only if prices rise. Liquidation means the exchange closes a trade by force once the trader’s collateral runs out, and those forced sales push the price down further.

So is this the flush that ends the crypto market slide, or is there more to come? The data has an answer, and it is not a comforting one.

The sell-off had no single cause. Four pieces of bad news landed one after another:

Each hit forced out more leveraged longs across the crypto market. Thursday’s slide continued the pattern on a smaller scale. We added up every forced closure in Bitcoin, Ether, Solana, XRP and Dogecoin futures on OKX, one of the largest exchanges, over the past 24 hours. Longs lost $27.6 million, against $12.3 million for shorts.

Big liquidations usually clear out the crowd. This time, the crowd came back.

In plain terms, traders keep buying every dip with borrowed money. That leaves fuel for another round of forced selling if Bitcoin drops through the next support. Bottoms tend to come when funding turns negative and open interest falls hard, a sign that leveraged buyers have given up.

The Crypto Fear & Greed Index, which scores market mood from 0 (extreme fear) to 100 (extreme greed), reads 28 today. Fear is usually read as a buy signal. The history says it is not enough on its own.

We checked every day since February 2018 when the index sat at 28 or lower, 943 days in all, and where Bitcoin stood later.

This year’s two bottoms came with real panic. When Bitcoin hit $60,000 on February 6, the index read 9. When it bottomed at $57,800 on July 1, it read 11. Today’s 28 is fear, but it is not capitulation, the moment sellers give up all at once.

On the weekly chart, where each candle covers one week, Bitcoin has spent 2026 in a wide range. The low is $57,800 from July 1 and the high is $87,396 from late September. The midpoint is $72,598, almost exactly where Bitcoin trades now.

The price lost its 50-week exponential moving average (EMA), an average that gives recent prices more weight, at $78,219. Its 200-week EMA sits at $69,597. Bitcoin closed below that line for 16 weeks this year, in February and March and again from June to mid-August, and both of the year’s bottoms formed during those stretches.

For the crypto market, three signs would say the bottom is close: the fear index dropping toward the teens, funding turning negative, and open interest falling as longs give up. None has shown up yet. Until they do, the 200-week EMA at $69,597 is the line that separates a pullback inside the 2026 range from a retest of the summer lows.
