The Best Fancy Food Gifts (That We Actually Want for Ourselves)

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

These Japanese toaster ovens, sleek barware sets, and design-forward coffee grinders will bring a lifetime of (tasty) memories

Forgive the Daddy Warbucks-ness of what I’m about to say, but I think it’s important to remember that a splurge and an extravagance aren’t quite the same thing when it comes to gifts. On one end of the spectrum, there are vapid luxury items; on the other, there are the gifts whose steeper price point is tied directly to their artistry, quality, and/or durability. While the former can be fun to window-shop, our hard-earned clams are best spent on the latter.

As a seasoned shopping writer, I ask myself two big questions when mulling over a pricey purchase: What is the story behind this item? Can I picture it in my life for years to come?

Consider the iconic, mid-century modern Moccamaster coffee machine, which first debuted in the 1960s and has been trending on social media lately for its array of colorful, modular designs. I took the cult-fave machine for an extended test run (it’s still my daily driver), and the yellow pepper iteration made me fall in love with drip coffee all over again. The Dutch company behind the appliance, Technivorm, really exercises what it calls its core tenets of “form and function,” and will service any repairs on the machines for up to five years. That is exactly the kind of lore I crave from the splurge-level items on my holiday shopping list (and the kind that will make your own design-savvy, coffee-loving giftee feel special).

If you’re reaching into your budget for gifts that last, impress, and — if you’re giving them to a family member or partner — you might be able to use yourself for years to come, the fancy gifts for food lovers start getting good. There are the big-ticket kitchen titans: a high-tech Mill food recycler, sleek Miele vacuums (post-kitchen-prep cleanup doesn’t stand a chance), and electric pizza ovens that can reach 850 degrees Fahrenheit in just 90 seconds. Sometimes “splurges” are just wise investments, especially with appliances and cookware; your giftee is going to use a well-made cutting board or top-tier kitchen knife for years or decades to come. There are also the gifts with spectacular whimsy: a stationary exercise bike that makes smoothies, a martini-lover’s menorah, a tabletop lighter that’s also an incredible conversation piece.

These gifts may be at higher price points than a classic stocking stuffer, but they’re also extra-special — we’d say, so worth it if you have the dough. If you’re looking to roll out the red carpet with splurge-worthy food and kitchen gifts, look no further.

The casual gift for someone who loves food and loves to host might be nice set of chip and dip bowls, cocktail napkins, or perhaps some beeswax candlesticks. The gift of a whole dry-cured Spanish ham leg, however, is a next-level present for the most passionate entertainer in your family or friend group.

No better way to pull focus at the dining table than with this copper and brass pepper mill, which East Fork says can literally last for “hundreds of years” when cared for properly. The 10-inch tall grinder is made in collaboration with a small, family-run company in Greece, and is actually modeled after the same mills 20th-century Greek soldiers would carry to grind coffee, a fact that finds homage in the Greek lettering inscribed on the mill.

The Eater staff has tested its fare share of toaster ovens, and crowned the Balmuda as one of the best for its minimalist design and ability to not just warm but rehydrate breads and pastries; it uses a steam process to reinvigorate that day-old croissant like a wizard. This Balmuda Pro has an even more powerful heat source, and a setting called Salamander Grill Mode for giving your salmon the restaurant-grade finishing treatment.

The cultural martini discourse has never been stronger, or brinier. Help your giftee celebrate Hanukkah with Susan Alexandra’s dirty martini menorah. As the designer’s site proclaims, “The miracle of Hanukkah was that the olive oil that was enough only for one day of light lasted for eight days. Our favorite way of consuming olives? In a bath of vodka/ gin and a touch of vermouth!”

Behold, a splurge that is also a cooking investment! We’ve all experienced the frustration of chopping special ingredients on a sub-par cutting board, and the moment you level up to a thick, handsome slab is one that can help give you newfound confidence and efficiency as a passionate home cook. Boardsmith’s walnut end grain cutting boards are all made to order out of a sustainably harvested black walnut, resulting in beautiful, checkerboard-like and ombré color variations. Cheese boards have never looked better, folks.

The Chicago-based luxury chocolatier Vosges is known for its intricate, decadent holiday gifts (don’t miss its lodge-inspired advent calendar), and it has curated the perfect assortment of its truffles to go with a bottle of Ceretto Barolo wine. “This is a classic Barolo,” the site explains, “[it’s] light garnet in color with Barolo’s quintessential aromas of roses, tart cherries, and hints of truffles.”

Made in Victor, Idaho, the boutique blades of New West KniveWorks are a departure from the recent fad of making knives extra wide, favoring instead the graceful docility of a light, 8-inch steel blade and a handle made out of a durable resin composite material that will last a lifetime. In the fighting words of New West, “this precision instrument isn’t a club like Mom’s old Wüsthof Trident.” If you’re shopping for a partner who tends to their knife collection like the Crown Jewels, it’s the kind of gift that feels equal parts lifetime cooking investment and personality-forward essential tool.

Gohar World’s elevated kitchen essentials walk the line between Dalí-esque absurdity (remember when Julia Fox wore its kitchen gloves to the Grammys?) and functionality, and this metallic apron is no exception: it lends you a lacy hand (two, actually) while you’re cooking.

I really do think the Moccamaster coffee machine is the Platonic ideal of drip coffee machines; my own yellow pepper model swept me off my feet with its talent for quick, smooth brewing and its modular, chic mid-century modern design. If you love someone who loves coffee — and perhaps already owns the GOATed machine — why not invest in the brand’s powerful burr grinder? It’s certified by the European Coffee Brewing Centre (ECBC), which means it has passed a rigorous testing process for uniform grinding and quality construction.

Imagine casually inviting your beloved over for dinner, and you serve this gorgeous Japanese wagyu ribeye steak along with an Old-Fashioned? Imperia Caviar is best known for its royal osetra, but it’s also where you can find a variety of A5 Japanese wagyu (the highest designation for beef in the country); this grade of wagyu has up to five times more intramuscular fat, which makes for an even richer, buttery flavor.

I always have a few lighters floating around my house for candles and incense, but there’s something so retro-chic about a coffee table lighter. Edie Parker, master of eccentric and surreal lifestyle [puffs chamomile pre-roll] accessories has a line of jelly-inspired tabletop lighters with all kinds of “fillings,” and the olive version is one of the most charming.

Unsurprisingly, we’re picky when it comes to choosing cookware at Eater — that’s why we collaborated with Heritage Steel to make the kind of cookware collection that will last a lifetime. Every frypan, lid, and pot is made out of 5-ply stainless steel, and this robust bundle includes everything you need to embark on all kinds of culinary adventures: two fry pans (8.5- and 10.5-inch), a 2-quart saucier, a 3-quart saucepan, a 4-quart sauté pan, and an 8-quart stock pot ready to simmer your most Strega Nona-worthy stews.

Here’s an idea: Order this massive wheel of Manchego cheese from Flamingo Estate, and ride it to victory at the Cooper’s Hill cheese rolling competition. That, or gift it to someone who can appreciate the fact that it’s sourced, per Flamingo Estate, from one of only ten cheese mongers in Spain that are still making farmstead PDO Manchego Artesano, which results in an even richer and more complex flavor.

You know the big (or rather, full-size) Rocco: a $1,500 high-tech drink fridge, endorsed by Eric Wareheim and seemingly designed by someone who loves Eames chairs. This year, Rocco has launched a miniature version of its posh drink refrigerator, dubbed the Mini Mini Fridge, this 14-inch tall personal fridge fits up to 12 cans of your preferred beverages (or sheet masks; choose your journey). Unlike most mini fridges, the Mini cools down to 39 degrees Fahrenheit (the majority of countertop fridges hit 48 degrees Fahrenheit at their coldest). Kind of the dream set up for your — sorry, your giftee’s — office.

Atelier Sebastião Lobo crafts otherworldly flatware that’s almost too beautiful to use. This set of Calder-esque cutlery has spawned many imitations, because it’s one of the most original designs to come out of the flatware world in recent years, and a prime example of how cutlery and tableware in general got really squiggly and freaky this year.

There is nothing like devoting counter space to yet another coffee machine that requires highly specific capsules, but hey! This one is different — it brews only cold coffee. (Nitro cold brew, cold brew, and cold espresso to be specific.) Sure, it’s definitely a bit of a luxury, but then again, so is spending $7 on an iced cortado from the coffee shop every morning.

N/A drinks were created to be a more inclusive product. Only a rare few can order bottles of bubbly for over $100. The Venn diagram where these two groups overlap is small, but it exists, along with this $119 bottle of booze-free bubbly made with French Chardonnay grapes.

In a way that I cannot quite explain, this feels like it’s targeted at the 1990s kids who grew up with Smart House and Richie Rich. This stationary exercise bike, complete with an orange slice wheel, whips up one of your favorite smoothies courtesy of your own pedaling power. As one reviewer writes, “I am 67 years old, widowed twice, and I have seen enough in this life to know when somebody has finally invented something useful. This bicycle-blender contraption has changed the direction of my spirit. Every morning I put in one banana, some frozen peaches, yogurt, a little honey, and whatever green thing is closest to expiration in my refrigerator. Then I pedal until either the smoothie is done or I start seeing little sparkles. Whichever comes first.”
