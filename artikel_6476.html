Space Outposts Beyond the Moon May Rely on Planetary AI

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

NASA plans to return astronauts to the lunar surface in 2028 for the first time since Apollo 17 in 1972. But the famed agency has no intention of stopping there, as they plan to establish a permanent human presence on the Moon through a slow and meticulous process involving power, resource extraction, and habitat technologies. However, with the soaring use of artificial intelligence (AI) in our everyday lives, how could this emerging technology be used to identify new landing sites for future outposts on the Moon and possibly Mars?

Now, a team of researchers from The Ohio State University might have contributed to how AI can be used for future space outposts. As discussed in a recent study presented at the 58th Annual Meeting of the Division for Planetary Sciences (DPS 58) of the American Astronomical Society, the researchers introduced how a novel AI model could be used to both identify safe landing sites and develop a human outpost strategy at those landing sites. The model is called LUMINA, which is an agentic AI framework designed to help mission planners evaluate safe landing sites on the lunar surface, specifically focusing on key resources like water ice, along with potential scientific value.

It incorporates a myriad of lunar surface data, including topography and terrain hazards, scientific value, geology, surface properties, and even comparing the candidate landing sites to each other. As a result, LUMINA demonstrates a keen ability to identify landing sites across the lunar surface that could maximize mission success and effectiveness for both human habitation and science. But the researchers donât want to limit LUMINA to the Moon, as they envision LUMINA contributing to other planetary bodies, specifically Mars.

LUMINA is built upon previous research by this same group called the Large Language-and-Vision Assistant for Lunar Exploration (LLaVA-LE), which demonstrated its ability to analyze and describe orbital images of the lunar surface. The LLaVA-LE model is currently available on arXiv, was recently presented at the CVPR2026 AI4Space Workshop in June 2026 and can be found on GitHub.
