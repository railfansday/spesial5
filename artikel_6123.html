Expedia’s 2026 deals are a fraction of the nearly $6 billion it spent in 2015. But three acquisitions in nine months, plus CEO Ariane Gorin’s pledge to keep investing, signal that Expedia is a buyer again. And B2B is where the money is going.

Expedia Group spent $676 million on three acquisitions in the first nine months of 2026, according to a third-quarter financial filing. It is the company’s most active stretch of dealmaking since its 2015 buying binge, when it acquired HomeAway (Vrbo), Orbitz Worldwide, and Travelocity.

The filing doesn’t name the companies Expedia bought in 2026. It said Expedia bought two travel-related companies in its B2B segment and one AI trip-planning company in B2C. Those are Amsterdam-based attractions platform Tiqets, Dublin-based car rental and ground transportation platform CarTrawler, and Berlin-based AI trip planner Layla.

Of the $676 million, $4 million was accrued and the rest paid in cash. The acquired companies brought $124 million of cash with them. Expedia Group spent $676 million on three acquisitions in the first nine months of 2026, its most active stretch of dealmaking since the 2015 run that brought in HomeAway, Orbitz, and Travelocity. The third-quarter filing doesn't name the targets, but they are Tiqets, the Amsterdam attractions platform; CarTrawler, the Dublin car rental and ground transportation platform; and Layla, the Berlin AI trip planner. Two of the three sit in the B2B segment, and the targets differ from 2015: Expedia is mostly buying supply and technology for partners rather than consumer brands and market share. Skift's reporting and the filings narrow the prices — about $279 million of Q1 cash spending on "acquisitions and other" when Tiqets was the only deal, at least $350 million for CarTrawler, and less than $100 million for Layla, according to two industry sources. CEO Ariane Gorin said on the third-quarter earnings call that the B2B investment will continue, tying the deals to making Expedia a one-stop shop for airlines, banks, loyalty programs, and travel agencies building their own travel offerings. Buybacks still come first: Expedia repurchased $1.5 billion of shares through September, more than twice what it paid for the three companies.
