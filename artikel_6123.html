Although the holiday season can be joyous, it can be an expensive time of year, with gifts, travel and gatherings putting extra pressure on your budget.

But with a little planning, you can make the most of your credit card rewards and benefits without overspending.

Here are five tips to help you save money and earn rewards like a pro this holiday season.

First, map out your holiday shopping plans and set a realistic budget for spending. Who are you shopping for? How much are you planning to spend on gifts? What other expenses should you factor into your holiday budget?

These are all good questions to help you set expectations and prevent impulse buys that can lead to overspending.

A spreadsheet is a great way to keep track of spending and budgeting. You can simply insert your budget into a premade template or write it down on paper. If you already know what you plan to buy, insert links to the sales pages so you know how much it will cost.

While adjusting and finalizing your budget, remember to factor in additional costs due to extra gatherings, like larger grocery and dining expenses.

One of the best things about rewards cards is that they often come with perks to help you save money.

Before making your holiday purchases, check whether your credit cards offer statement credits or other benefits that could help you save money.

For example, comes with an up to $75 quarterly statement credit (up to $300 annually) Lululemon credit that can be used at U.S. stores (excluding outlets) or online after enrollment. This is a great way to maximize purchases on clothing, yoga gear or other accessories.

Or, if you have a frequent jet-setter in your life, you can use a credit card’s Global Entry/TSA PreCheck credit to cover their application fee of up to $120.

Statement credits aren't the only card benefits worth checking before you shop. For an expensive electronic device or other high-value gift, purchase protection or extended warranty coverage are other useful perks.

We talk a lot about shopping portals during this time of year. That's because skipping them is essentially leaving money on the table.

By doing your online holiday shopping through a portal, you can earn bonus points or cash back at a massive number of online retailers. Most of the major U.S. airlines have shopping portals to help you earn additional miles on every eligible purchase, or you can use third-party portals such as Rakuten to earn cash back or Amex Membership Rewards or Bilt Points.

We suggest using a shopping portal aggregator like Cashback Monitor to find the best portal for each online purchase. That way, you don’t have to sift through each portal’s offers individually.

No matter where you shop, review any Amex Offers, Chase Offers or Citi Merchant Offers available on your cards. They vary by cardmember, and often stack with your card's standard rewards plus a shopping portal. Some require activation or enrollment before making a purchase.

We’re big fans of points and miles at The Points Guy — and for good reason. Points and miles can reduce the cost of future travel, but cash-back cards may be more useful if you want rewards to offset your holiday expenses directly.

A flat-rate cash-back card such as the (see rates and fees) is an easy way to earn rewards no matter where you shop since it offers 1% cash back when you make a purchase and another 1% cash back when you pay your bill. Redeeming those rewards as a statement credit helps reduce the size of January's bill.

The (see rates and fees) is another great option that earns at least 1.5% back on every purchase.

Paying your statement balance in full is generally best, but some rewards credit cards offer a 0% introductory annual percentage rate period on new purchases. That can give you time to pay for a planned expense without interest as long as you have a plan to pay it off completely before the introductory period ends.

Another option is to use one of the financing options some credit card issuers offer. Amex Plan It® and Chase Pay Over Time after purchase may allow eligible cardmembers to divide qualifying purchases of $100 or more into fixed monthly payments, generally with a fixed monthly fee instead of interest.

You’ll almost always save money using these programs compared to paying interest over time.

Holiday shopping doesn't have to derail your budget. By planning ahead, using the right rewards cards and taking advantage of statement credits and shopping portals, you can make your spending more rewarding without buying more than you can afford.

The key is to prioritize your budget over earning points, miles or cash back. After all, the value of those rewards can quickly disappear if you're carrying a balance and paying interest.
