Stuck In The Mud: Lagos-Bound British Airways Boeing 787-10 Veers Off Taxiway In London

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

A British Airways Boeing 787-10 Dreamliner has suffered a taxiway excursion at London Heathrow Airport (LHR). The widebody twinjet airliner had pushed back from its gate at the West London facility's satellite terminal 5C, and it was on its way to runway 27L ahead of its planned departure to Murtala Muhammed International Airport (LOS) in Lagos, Nigeria, as BA75. However, it missed a turn and got stuck.

Several hours later at the time of writing, the aircraft remains beached in the grass at the western end of runway 27L between the satellite terminals 5B and 5C, where it is visible on a popular planespotting livestream. British Airways has confirmed that it is working to get the passengers of flight BA75 on their way again, but, as a result of the Boeing 787-10's taxiway excursion, the return leg has been canceled.

As seen in the Reddit post embedded below, this morning's incident involved British Airways flight BA75, which was operated by G-ZBLF. Heathrow's website shows that it pushed back at around 10:30 AM local time ahead of what should have been a six to seven-hour flight to Nigeria. According to flight tracking data, however, at 10:45 AM local time, it failed to make its planned turn as it headed to the runway.

After emerging at the southern end of the taxiway that bisects the satellite terminals 5B and 5C, G-ZBLF attempted to make a left turn to head east to the far end of runway 27L. However, while taxiing at a speed of around five knots (9.3 km/h), it only partially managed to make the turn before its nose wheel got stuck in the mud, beaching the aircraft. At present, the cause of the taxiway excursion remains unknown, but a spokesperson for British Airways did tell Simple Flying that:

"We're very sorry to our customers for the disruption to their flight. Our teams are working to get them on their way as quickly as possible and are in touch with them to put things right."

According to fleet data from ch-aviation, G-ZBLF is one of 14 examples of the Boeing 787-10 Dreamliner that British Airways currently has at its disposal. These aircraft are just 3.8 years old on average, compared to a fleet-wide mean figure of 13.2 years old. Going forward, BA has another 36 units of the 787-10 on order, meaning it will eventually fly 50 examples. It also operates 12 Boeing 787-8s and 18 787-9s.

G-ZBLF itself is one of BA's older examples of Boeing's longest 787 Dreamliner variant, clocking in at 5.56 years old. Having been ordered in December 2013, it first flew in April 2021, but was only delivered to the UK flag carrier and oneworld founding member in September 2022. This made it just the third 787-10 to reach BA, following the arrivals of G-ZBLA and G-ZBLB, both of which came in June of 2020.

BA configures its 787-10s in a relatively low-density layout given their size, with a grand total of just 256 seats on board. This is made up of eight First suites, 48 'Club World' business class flatbeds, 35 'World Traveller Plus' premium economy recliners, and 165 'World Traveller' economy class seats. At the time of writing, it remains stuck in the mud, with the return flight from Lagos having already been canceled.

The higher-capacity jet has some impressive capabilities.

British Airways flight BA75 is a daily scheduled service from London Heathrow Airport to Lagos. With its scheduled departure time being 10:10 AM, its 10:30 AM pushback would not normally have been the end of the world as far as delays are concerned. Now, of course, the taxiway excursion has changed that. The flight has a scheduled block time of six hours and 45 minutes, with its arrival timed for 5:55 PM.

According to Cirium, an aviation analytics company, British Airways serves this route on a daily basis in both the summer and winter using the Boeing 787-10. Competition comes from Virgin Atlantic and Air Peace, although the latter flies from Gatwick (LGW) instead. Elsewhere in Nigeria, BA also flies daily from Heathrow to Nnamdi Azikiwe International Airport (ABV) in Abuja using older Boeing 777 widebodies.
