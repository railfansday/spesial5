Netflix’s Brutal 6-Part Western Beats 'Yellowstone' at Its Own Game

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Yellowstone might have made Westerns cool again, but it’s not the only series that depicts the atrocities committed in the name of claiming American soil. Like Taylor Sheridan's landmark Western, Netflix’s American Primeval revolves around the fight for Native land. But instead of greedy corporations, the conflict involves Mormon militias, Indigenous communities, and white settlers all caught in the middle.

While men are often the ones framing these narratives, American Primeval shows a different side of these conflicts, particularly through the women pulled into a world that disregards their value. Yellowstone might have shocked viewers with its relentless violence, but it doesn’t come close to American Primeval. The best part is that the miniseries only needs six episodes to expose the truth of America's colonial past, giving its female characters room to tell stories that Westerns have often left on the sidelines.

Set during the 1857 Utah War, American Primeval reimagines the Mormon Church’s expansion under Brigham Young (Kim Coates), rooted in deep religious fanaticism and historical violence committed in the name of faith. Young’s campaign to take control of Fort Bridger puts him in conflict with the U.S. Army officer Captain Edmund Dellinger (Lucas Neff), the fort’s owner Jim Bridger (Shea Whigham), and Red Feather (Derek Hinkey), the leader of a renegade group of Crow warriors known as the Wolf Clan.

At the center of the miniseries are mother and son Sara Rowell (Betty Gilpin) and Devin Rowell (Preston Mota), who have come a long way from Missouri to Fort Bridger, Wyoming, with the intention of reaching Devin’s father in Crooks Springs, Utah. However, getting there is easier said than done. With the treacherous terrain, bounty hunters scattered across the region, and wildlife that could kill them in an instant, their chances of making it are slim to none — and that's before they have to contend with the conflict brewing around Fort Bridger. Fortunately, the reluctant mountain man Isaac Reed (Taylor Kitsch) is willing to join Sara and Devin on their journey, but even that doesn't guarantee survival.

There’s no doubt that Yellowstone doesn’t shy away from violence. The series already has plenty of stomach-churning moments: a charging bull let loose inside a bar, Beth Dutton (Kelly Reilly) assaulted in her own office, and the controversial Monica (Kelsey Asbille) storyline, in which she puts herself in danger while trying to lure a predator. However, American Primeval arguably takes things much further, largely because of its historical setting. Unlike the more modern world of Yellowstone, where the Duttons still have to contend with law enforcement, government officials, and legal bureaucracy, the Utah Territory depicted in American Primeval is a largely lawless frontier where formal authority has little control over the men fighting for power and land.

In American Primeval, violence is normalized, with little regard for human life or legal consequences. Public hangings, the abduction of women, and the slaughter of entire groups of settlers are all part of the brutal reality of the frontier. The series takes things even further with its bloody depictions of clashes between opposing groups, including a fictionalized portrayal of the Mountain Meadows Massacre of September 1857. In this world, there’s no telling when someone might fire the first arrow or when a seemingly ordinary verbal confrontation could end with someone getting stabbed. Whether they're battling for control, to settle scores, or simply to defend themselves, nearly everyone resorts to violence to survive.

American Primeval focuses on three women whose lives are upended by violence. From different societal, cultural, and religious backgrounds, each faces the limited choices available to women on the frontier. Sara is a mother fleeing her past to protect her disabled son; Abish (Saura Lightfoot-Leon) is a Mormon wife questioning religious expectations after falling into the hands of the Shoshone; and Two Moons (Shawnee Pourier) is a mute Shoshone woman escaping her village after killing her rapist.

Yellowstone depicts its female characters in fairly rigid roles throughout its five seasons. In contrast, American Primeval gives its women more room to evolve in just six tight episodes. Initially appearing helpless, they're forced out of their comfort zones to protect themselves and others, adapting to circumstances they never imagined facing. With its complex history, religious conflicts, and women doing whatever it takes, American Primeval shows there's more to these stories than bloodbaths and conquest, offering a more complex and haunting portrait of the country's violent past than Yellowstone ever explores.

American Primeval is a Netflix limited series that follows the lives of several men and women in the middle of the expansion of the American West. Social dynamics clash as men and women battle for a piece of the new world against rivals and each other.
