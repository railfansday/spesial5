6 Upcoming Sci-Fi Movies, Ranked by Hype

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The next wave of sci-fi TV is looking out of this world

Science fiction has always been one of the most exhilarating genres to experience on the big screen, and today we’re entering another massive era for theatrical spectacle. Studios like Warner Bros., Disney, and Legendary have given filmmakers, VFX artists, and visionary directors the canvas to explore everything from humanity's distant future to planets in galaxies far, far away. Fortunately, the next few years of sci-fi cinema look just as — if not more — promising.

As studios double down on high-stakes bets, pouring absurd budgets into sprawling worlds and complex narratives like Dune: Part Three and Avengers: Doomsday, speculative fiction remains Hollywood’s ultimate proving ground for event-sized storytelling. To see what’s most exciting on the near horizon, here are the biggest upcoming sci-fi movies ranked by hype.

It’s always a gamble betting on a mid-budget sci-fi concept in a release calendar dominated by legacy IP, even with Keanu Reeves at the center of it. Directed by Deadpool’s Tim Miller, Shiver has been pitched as Edge of Tomorrow meets The Shallows. Reeves plays an as-yet unnamed smuggler who gets trapped in a deadly time loop in the middle of the shark-infested Caribbean Sea after a job goes sideways.

Even in the age of original sci-fi spectacles like Everything Everywhere All at Once and Severance, the lack of any built-in franchise awareness or even a massive marketing blitz behind it makes Shiver sit outside almost everyone’s hype level at the moment, especially in the face of such massive juggernauts on the horizon. Still, watching Miller pair R-rated action choreography with a high-concept time-loop premise in the same vein as Looper, all while letting Reeves carry the story, makes Shiver one of the more fascinating wildcards on the slate, especially if you’re a John Wick fan.

At this point, Legendary's MonsterVerse has settled comfortably into its wild identity, built primarily on ridiculous kaiju matchups. Following the events of Godzilla x Kong: The New Empire, the next installment looks ready to take things past the stratosphere, both literally and figuratively. Director Grant Sputore, who takes over for Adam Wingard this go-around, is gearing up to steer the franchise into cosmic territory. Even with the limited amount of story details, it’s clear SpaceGodzilla will emerge as the franchise's next big bad, which would mark a significant escalation for the sixth film in the series.

The MonsterVerse isn’t exactly the most cerebral science fiction franchise around, and nowhere close as jaw-dropping as Minus Zero, but it has become one of Hollywood's most dependable sources of blockbuster entertainment. You can’t deny there’s something undeniably exciting about seeing kaiju go toe-to-toe, wreaking havoc across some of the world’s most beautiful cities. The idea of a threat from the stars crashing into the MonsterVerse makes Godzilla x Kong: Supernova particularly intriguing, though not quite Star Wars-level intriguing.

It’s not an overstatement to say Star Wars is in rough shape right now. Following the poor reception of The Mandalorian and Grogu, the franchise is in dire need of structural reinvention before one of cinema’s most beloved sci-fi properties falls into full-blown creative obsolescence. At least Star Wars: Starfighter looks like a promising breath of fresh air, bypassing the Skywalker Saga to jump five years past The Rise of Skywalker.

Set to hit theaters on the 50th anniversary weekend of the original 1977 film, Shawn Levy’s standalone entry stars Ryan Gosling as a cynical rogue pilot alongside Mia Goth, Amy Adams, and Matt Smith. The story sees Gosling's character taking a young Jedi child under his wing, giving the film a chance to explore a more personal mentor-student dynamic set against a galactic adventure. In the wake of countless Disney Plus spin-offs that never quite stuck the landing, Levy has his work cut out for him, especially with the film carrying the weight of the much-maligned sequel trilogy with it.

After spending nearly three decades stuck in development hell, The Incredibles director is finally bringing his long-gestating passion project to the silver screen. Set in an alternate retro-futuristic megacity as imagined from 1939, the animated sci-fi noir follows Raymond Gunn (Sam Rockwell), the last human private investigator as he is pulled into a murder conspiracy involving extraterrestrial visitors and multimedia superstar Venus Nova (Scarlett Johansson).

Director Brad Bird has tapped a laundry list of additional stars to back the voice ensemble, from Alan Tudyk to Michelle Rodriguez, and it looks like the exact kind of stylized swing mainstream animation rarely takes anymore. It already reminds me of Satoshi Kon’s unfinished final project, Dreaming Machine, which was also set in a retro-futuristic society like Ray Gunn’s Metropia. Watching Bird blend hard-boiled detective fiction with mid-century pulp sci-fi is an exciting prospect, and you won’t have to wait long to see it. Ray Gunn releases in theaters on December 4 ahead of its debut on Netflix later that same month.

Denis Villeneuve has already pulled off what filmmakers thought was impossible by turning Frank Herbert’s dense prose into twin modern sci-fi classics. Dune: Part Three is poised to ring in even more hype and acclaim as it pulls in new players and villains into the overarching conflict. Adapting Dune Messiah, the third installment picks up with Paul Atreides (Timothée Chalamet) sitting on the Golden Lion Throne, forced to reckon with the fanatical fallout of his holy war across the imperium.

Jason Momoa returns as the ghola Hayt, a resurrected clone of fallen Atreides swordmaster Duncan Idaho, and is joined by Robert Pattinson as the shape-shifting conspirator Scytale. Villeneuve's closing chapter on Dune is undoubtedly one of the most ambitious and exciting sci-fi events of the year, but it lands on the same day as Marvel’s Avengers suit up to take on one of their toughest foes yet.

Opening on the same "Dunesday" weekend, the Russo brothers’ return to Marvel isn’t pure hard sci-fi in the traditional sense, yet its multiversal stakes make it the loudest pop-culture collision on the calendar. Built around reality-bending incursions and a crossover that pulls the Avengers, The Fantastic Four, and the Fox-era X-Men into one universe-shattering event, Avengers: Doomsday is easily one of the most anticipated comic book blockbusters in years. Bringing Robert Downey Jr. back into the fold as Victor Von Doom is a swerve unlike any other, but one that will prove the most exciting to witness as he collides with Marvel’s pantheon of heroes on-screen later this year.

The multiversal saga has had more than its fair share of missteps, leaving plenty of skeptics wondering if Marvel can actually recapture its Infinity War and Endgame momentum. Love it or hate it, nothing else on the release schedule commands a bigger mainstream footprint than watching Marvel’s architects try to pull off the biggest crossover event Hollywood has seen yet. And, in the wake of that surprise VisionQuest post-credit scene, the hype is at a fever pitch.
