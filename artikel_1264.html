Johnny Giles Named English Legend as ‘Best Player I Ever Played Against’

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Active from the late 1950s until the mid-1980s, Johnny Giles, whose real name is Michael Jones Giles, enjoyed an outstanding career. His achievements extended beyond being an exceptional football player, as he also made his mark in management.

The midfielder began his professional journey at Manchester United, where he lifted the 1963 FA Cup, before making the switch to Leeds United, where he spent 12 hugely successful years. At Elland Road, the Dublin-born star became an integral part of the Whites' team, collecting several major honours, including two league titles. He also earned a reputation as one of the toughest players in Leeds United's history.

A hugely talented footballer, Giles also represented the Republic of Ireland, taking on the role of player-manager in 1973. He scored 59 times (five goals) between 1959 and 1979. It was an unusual responsibility, but one that suited the Boys in Green icon, who also enjoyed spells with West Bromwich Albion and Shamrock Rovers.

Throughout such an illustrious career, Giles naturally shared the pitch with many of the finest footballers of his generation. However, one particular English legend stood out above the rest and left a lasting impression on him.

Speaking to Off The Ball, Giles revealed that he regarded Sir Bobby Charlton as the finest opponent he ever encountered on the pitch. It would hardly be surprising if the former midfielder also considered him among the best players he shared a team with. Giles described Charlton's abilities:

"He was the best player I ever played against, he was quick, had a great shot on him on either foot. I never really spoke to him about football, I think he played instinctively and - I don't mean to be disrespectful - I don't think he knew much about the game. Bobby didn't need anybody, he could dribble, he was quick and good with both feet."

Before facing one another as opponents, Giles and Charlton enjoyed considerable success together at Manchester United. However, Giles' move to Leeds United changed their relationship on the pitch, with the pair subsequently meeting as rivals. Their respective international careers also brought them into direct competition. Interestingly, the Republic of Ireland midfielder enjoyed the better record in their encounters, winning eight of their 20 meetings, drawing eight and losing just four. Nevertheless, Charlton remained a formidable opponent on every occasion.

It is difficult to argue against Giles' assessment when considering the extraordinary ability of Charlton, one of the greatest footballers ever. The Irishman clearly admired his former colleague, having also included him in his all-time England XI. It was a fitting recognition of a player whose quality was impossible to overlook.

Ask any Manchester United supporter to name the greatest footballer in the club's history, and Bobby Charlton will almost certainly feature in the conversation. One thing that cannot be disputed is his status as the greatest English player in Manchester United's history. The legendary forward, one of the best English footballers of all time, earned that distinction through years of outstanding performances. Having turned professional in 1956, the Ashington-born star spent 17 seasons representing the Red Devils.

Across that period, Charlton registered 758 appearances and scored 249 goals​​​​​​, while also collecting an impressive range of individual and team honours. Among his greatest achievements were winning the World Cup and claiming the Ballon d'Or in 1966, making him one of the seven British Ballon d'Or winners. His playing career might easily have ended in tragedy following the Munich Air Disaster, but he survived the devastating incident with relatively minor injuries. He subsequently dedicated much of his life to remembering and honouring the Manchester United teammates who lost their lives that day.
