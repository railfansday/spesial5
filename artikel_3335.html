Edward Burns Lines Up Awards Push For Black-And-White Indie Movie ‘Beneath The Blue Suburban Skies’ & Star Jennifer Ehle Seven Years After It Debuted At Toronto

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

EXCLUSIVE: Edward Burns’ micro-budget black-and-white family drama Beneath the Blue Suburban Skies debuted at the Toronto Film Festival in 2019.

Seven years later, the film is finally getting a release with a one-week awards-qualifying theatrical run in New York from December 25, ahead of a rollout in major cities in January.

The film stars Burns alongside Tony and BAFTA winner Jennifer Ehle with Hannah Dunne and Brian Wiles also among cast. Multi-hyphenate Burns is getting behind the film’s release via his production company Marlboro Road Gang Productions. The filmmakers are also getting behind Ehle who they view as giving an awards-worthy turn. Twenty five years after The Brothers McMullen made waves for Burns by winning the Grand Jury Prize at Sundance, the filmmaker returned in 2019 with another portrait of a working-class family. At the center of the film is Tina Collins (Ehle), who struggles with shame and anger toward her two adult children still living at home, and growing resentment toward her husband, Jim (Burns), whom she fears has held her back. As financial pressures mount, addiction and alcoholism further threaten the family’s fragile stability. Watch on Deadline

Pic is produced by Burns (who also scripts and co-edited), Aaron Lubin and William Rexer (who also served as director of photography) for Marlboro Road Gang Productions.

There aren’t many notices for the film online. The few that can be found are positive. Variety’s review of the movie from 2019 praised the performances and Ehle in particular.

According to sources, the film did get distribution offers out of TIFF seven years ago but those didn’t chime with the producers in a tough releasing landscape for indie pictures. Then COVID hit. After finally being taken off the shelf by the team this year, the movie played at the Montclair, Austin, Ojai, Charleston and Seattle film festivals, and won Best Picture at the Boston and Richmond festivals. It screens next at the Key West Film Festival, where Burns will be awarded the Golden Key Award.

The filmmakers are now gunning for Indie Spirit, Gotham, SAG, PGA, DGA, and even Academy consideration. Zero Dark Thirty and Lioness actress Ehle will be supporting Burns with press efforts in coming weeks.

Hollywood loves a comeback and it also loves a David vs Goliath story (even if awards season is tilted in favour of Goliath). Andrea Riseborough proved with her surprise nomination for To Leslie four years ago that it wasn’t impossible for very low-budget movies to break through.

Burns said of the film: “Having grown up a working-class kid on Long Island, I wanted to explore what I saw as a growing crisis facing working families today. With fewer well-paying entry-level jobs, a shortage of affordable housing, and rising drug and alcohol addiction, the promise of a better tomorrow seems to be slipping away. The American Dream—that your children would do better than you, and that you could take pride in their success as you reached middle age—was becoming increasingly elusive.”

Burns is known for starring in movies including Steven Spielberg’s Saving Private Ryan, The Holiday and Man On A Ledge, while TV turns include TNT series Mob City and Public Morals. He has directed 16 movies, including The Brothers McMullen, She’s The One, and most recently Finnegan’s Foursome.

Ehle is a two-time Tony Award winner, for The Real Thing and The Coast of Utopia, and won a BAFTA for her role as Elizabeth Bennet in Pride and Prejudice. Her film work includes The King’s Speech, Zero Dark Thirty, Contagion and A Quiet Passion.
