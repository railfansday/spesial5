Issuers aim most of the hardest credit cards to get at credit scores of 720 or higher, and they want an income that supports a high credit limit. The most exclusive have no public application. Check for preapproval offers before you apply, and pay a big annual fee only for perks you’ll use.

Income counts because federal rules require it. A card issuer can’t open a card or raise its limit until it considers whether you can make the minimum payments, based on your income or assets and the debts you already have.

Issuers can meet that rule by estimating your payments as if you’d already charged the whole credit limit they’re considering. So the bigger the credit limit an issuer has in mind, the more income it takes to qualify.

The score is the other test. Card issuers report to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau which credit score range each card mainly targets, and for seven of the ten cards below, it’s 720 or higher.

Each of these takes an online application.

None of these has a public application, so the bank decides who gets one.

If an issuer turns you down anyway, call its reconsideration line for a second look.

Before you pay a big annual fee, total the credits, perks, and rewards you’d use in a normal year. A card whose fee is bigger than that total loses you money, however hard it was to get.

Compare the best credit cards for excellent credit by the perks you’d use, or the best high-limit credit cards for the biggest credit limits.
