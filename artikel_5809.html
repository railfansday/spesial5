Sen. Elizabeth Warren, D-Mass., grilled acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao over the cost of the Iran war during his confirmation hearing on Tuesday.

Warren repeatedly pressed Cao on how much U.S. taxpayers were spending to support the U.S. Navy's blockade on Iranian ports as well as recovering from damage sustained in the conflict. Cao dodged multiple questions, stating that assessments were ongoing or he simply didn't have access to the data.

"Acting Secretary Cao what is the daily cost of the U.S. naval blockade on Iranian ports?" Warren asked.

"Senator, I do not know the daily cost, ma'am," Cao responded.

"You don't have any idea. You've been running this thing since April and you don't have any idea what that costs," Warren pressed.

"Well, senator, I know the rough cost of how much it's costing us so far. But again, the cost of losing the strait–," Cao said.

Warren cut him off, "I'm not asking you that. I just want to have the dollars here. Independent estimates are about $32.5 million a day. Does that sound about right to you?"

"Senator, let me do some quick Asian math in my head. But yes, it's about $30 billion so far," he confirmed.

"So you you confirm that number, and that means the American taxpayers are spending about $1 billion a month just on this blockade," she said.

Warren then moved on to the Iranian attack on the U.S. Navy's base in Bahrain, but Cao once again said he did not know the cost, saying the assessment is still ongoing.

The Senator referenced an estimate from the Wall Street Journal placing the cost at roughly $400 million. When asked if it was accurate, Cao said simply, "I'll find out."

Warren went on to say that the U.S. should not "spend another cent" on the war in Iran, noting the total estimated cost of the war provided by the Pentagon is over $47 billion.

Cao urged lawmakers to approve further funding for the Navy throughout Tuesday's hearing. He argued that the U.S. is in a "1939 moment," and must "be able to build like we did back in World War II."

He said the U.S. Navy currently has roughly 280 active vessels, but he said the country needs to surge up to 355.

U.S. Central Command released this photo on Feb. 24, saying, "Sailors and Marines aboard USS Abraham Lincoln (CVN 72) regularly perform maintenance to keep aircraft mission-ready." (CENTCOM)

Several lawmakers shared concerns about reports of poor morale and mental health among sailors due to long deployments for the Iran war. Sen. Kirsten Gillibrand, D-N.Y., highlighted eight suicide attempts aboard vessels in the USS Abraham Lincoln's carrier strike group, blasting Cao's characterization that there had been eight "mitigated" suicides.

Cao argued that the long deployments seen in the Iran war are due to a lack of available vessels, once again urging lawmakers to approve funding to bolster U.S. fleets.
