Here's How Much a $10,000 Investment in McDonald's Pays You Every Single Year

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

For McDonald's stock, steady, consistently growing dividends are just one part of the recipe.

By Thomas Niel – Nov 25, 2026 at 2:53PM EST

10 stocks we like better than McDonald's ›

After recently implementing its 50th consecutive annual dividend increase, McDonald's (MCD +1.00%) has finally reached Dividend King status. This milestone has arrived as shares in the fast-food giant attempt to emerge from a multi-month slump.

Yet while the stock's 2026 performance has proven disappointing for longtime investors, for those entering or adding to a position today, this is a highly favorable setup. Since it's still sporting a higher-than-usual forward dividend yield, while at the same time positioned to implement further moderate dividend increases, this remains a top blue chip stock to buy and hold for dividend growth.

Following the recent dividend hike, which raised the quarterly payout by 4% to $1.93 per share, McDonald's now pays $7.72 in cash dividends annually. The stock has recently traded for around $275 per share, so a $10,000 investment gets you around 36 shares. Multiply 36 by $7.72, and you'll have the total annual dividend income generated from this investment: $277.92.

That's a forward annual dividend yield of around 2.8%. However, this is far from the only long-term economic return you can receive from investing in McDonald's. Beyond the prospect of further dividend growth, much suggests that McDonald's will keep climbing out of its mid-2026 slump, toward past price levels, and on to new all-time highs.

In recent years, dividend growth for McDonald's shares has averaged in the mid-single-digit range. The latest dividend increase came in below this average, but if the company's NEXT-branded turnaround efforts prove successful, greater dividend growth could follow.

Success with a turnaround, including a growth resurgence, could drive a market rerating. McDonald’s shares trade for around 20 times forward earnings, while faster-growing consumer staples blue chips, like Coca-Cola, trade for over 25 times forward earnings. McDonald's still has its work cut out, but it has successfully overcome headwinds before. If it resolves this latest crop, it could serve investors the perfect recipe for strong total returns.
