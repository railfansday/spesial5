2 Reasons to Buy SpaceX (SPCX) Stock -- and 1 Reason to Wait

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The aerospace and AI company remains a polarizing investment.

SpaceX (SPCX -3.53%) has been a divisive stock since its IPO on June 12. The aerospace and AI company went public at $135 per share, closed at an all-time high of $201.80 four days later, but now trades at about $138 per share. Investors were initially impressed by its explosive growth, but its luster faded as rising interest rates and Treasury yields chilled the market.

Should investors buy SpaceX's stock as the bulls look the other way? Let's review two reasons to buy SpaceX -- as well as one reason to sell it -- to see if it's a contrarian investment.

Starlink also acquired Grain Management's nationwide 800 MHz low-band spectrum, which previously belonged to T-Mobile (TMUS -0.91%), for $8 billion in October. That acquisition, which adds terrestrial cellular towers to its networks, could make it a formidable challenger to AT&T (T -0.46%), Verizon (T -0.46%), and other telco giants in the wireless carrier market.

SpaceX's Connectivity segment, which houses Starlink, is the company's only profitable business by generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP). Its ongoing growth should subsidize the expansion of its lower-margin space and SI ("super intelligence") businesses.

SpaceX has launched Starship, its largest rocket ever built, in 15 test flights since April 2023. It isn't a major revenue source for the Space segment yet, but it plans to launch 50-100 Starship rockets in 2027. That acceleration would likely widen its moat, dilute its expenses, and boost its adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) -- which remained negative in the first nine months of 2026 -- into positive territory.

Elon Musk believes the combined growth of Starlink, Starship, and its SI business will boost SpaceX's revenue from an estimated $49 billion this year to $1 trillion by 2030. With a market cap of $1.95 trillion, it trades at 40 times this year's sales but less than two times its 2030 target.

SpaceX's Starlink and space segments have clear long-term catalysts, but the SI segment -- which houses X, xAI, Grok, Cursor, and its other AI assets -- faces a murkier future. The SI business generated $10.9 billion in revenue (40% of SpaceX's total revenue of $27.1 billion) in the first nine months of 2026, but it racked up an operating loss of $3.8 billion.

That loss -- along with the Space segment's $1.2 billion operating loss -- nearly erased the Connectivity segment's operating profit of $5.21 billion. SpaceX still squeezed out a total operating profit of $705 million in the first nine months of the year, but that balancing act could falter as SpaceX ramps up its AI investments over the next few quarters.

SpaceX already plans to roughly double its capex sequentially, from $19.6 billion in the third quarter to $35-$40 billion in the fourth quarter, with most of that spending earmarked for Nvidia's (NVDA -3.30%) data center GPUs. That spending spree will continue to weigh down SpaceX's stock, especially if interest rates remain elevated.

With Anthropic's (ANT +17.07%) IPO sucking the oxygen out of the market and Nvidia dominating the headlines, it seems like fewer investors are paying attention to SpaceX. So if you can look past its near-term challenges and ride out the volatility, it might be a great time to accumulate the stock as it hovers near its IPO price. Over the long term, its three core growth engines -- space, Starlink, and SI -- could catapult its stock to fresh all-time highs.
