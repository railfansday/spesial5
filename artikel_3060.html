Anthropic Stock Fell Below Its IPO Price. Is It a Buy, or Is More Downside Just Ahead?

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The stock is disappointing investors after its explosive IPO.

By Adria Cimino – Nov 26, 2026 at 4:02AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Anthropic ›

Anthropic (ANT +1.08%) soared into the spotlight last week, completing the biggest initial public offering ever. The artificial intelligence (AI) company raised $81 billion and immediately entered the market with a trillion-dollar valuation, placing it among long-established trillion-dollar companies such as Microsoft and Apple. And given the high demand for the stock, underwriters exercised an overallotment option that brought the total amount raised to more than $93 billion.

The stock, which priced at $83, opened at $111, and soared 17% on its first day of trading. The following days brought a sharp turnaround, however, with progressive declines. And on Nov. 24, Anthropic fell below its IPO price, closing at about $81.

At this level, is the tech giant a buy? Or is more downside just ahead? Let's find out.

We'll start by considering the Anthropic story so far. Anthropic is the developer of the AI model Claude, a product it rolled out only three years ago. Since that time, updated versions of Claude have launched, and demand and revenue growth have been explosive. Prior to the initial release, the company delivered zero revenue, and in the third quarter of this year, revenue came in at $17 billion.

Considering we're in the early days of AI's application in the real world and usage by companies -- Anthropic's focus is on enterprise -- it's reasonable to be optimistic about growth ahead. Anthropic says that 50% of Fortune 500 companies are generating at least $100,000 in revenue on a trailing 12-month basis, and this represents a very small part of these companies' budgets. So there's much room for growth, particularly considering that Claude updates bring new use capabilities.

Anthropic drew attention because it represented the first pure-play AI company to launch an IPO, and it offered investors a new AI investing opportunity. The company's soaring revenue over a relatively short period of time surely attracted the eye of those focused on growth. Investors may also like the management team for its experience in building frontier models and its commitment to safety. Chief Dario Amodei and a small team of researchers left OpenAI several years ago to create Anthropic.

With such a bright picture, why is the stock falling so soon after the IPO? One main reason is that this market launch happened at a difficult time for AI stocks. Investors have worried this year about the depth of spending on the AI build-out and its sustainability. Meanwhile, Democrats won U.S. midterm elections, and they've expressed caution regarding the pace of the build-out -- their leadership could result in regulation. These elements have put pressure on AI stocks, making the environment a difficult one for a newly launched player, particularly when this stock comes with a certain amount of risk.

It's important to remember that Anthropic has reported net losses in each of its years of operation, and this may continue as the company builds and trains updated models. On top of this, Anthropic listed many other risks in its prospectus, from the potential for a serious AI-driven event to the possibility that insurance may not cover such disasters.

In an already jittery market, investors may have shifted their focus away from the growth story and to the risks. If, for instance, the pace of AI development slows and strict regulation is put into place, AI stocks across the board may feel the impact. But younger companies that aren't yet delivering sustainable profitability may suffer the most.

Now, let's consider our question: At today's level, below its IPO price, is Anthropic a buy, or is more downside ahead? I think it's still too early to get in on this AI growth story. While revenue may continue to climb and there's reason to be optimistic about demand from corporate clients, the general environment has become more difficult for AI stocks. These players have seen significant volatility, with gains one day and declines the next, and the uncertainties above are weighing on sentiment. While well-established tech players may offer investors considerable visibility, a newcomer like Anthropic doesn't.

More downside could be ahead, so right now, Anthropic stays on my watch list -- and doesn't move to my buy list.
