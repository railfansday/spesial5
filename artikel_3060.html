The Stock Market Just Got Bad News on Inflation.

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

October inflation cooled, but not as much as Wall Street expected, and now investors have to take a half-point Fed hike in December more seriously. That could be a problem for an AI trade that already looks wobbly.

The latest inflation numbers were released this morning. The Consumer Price Index (CPI) showed prices rose less in October than the month before. Stocks fell anyway, with the S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.38%) finishing down 0.4% and the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC -0.74%) down 0.7%.

The problem isn't that inflation didn't slow; it's that it didn't slow as much as Wall Street expected, and now the market is bracing for a potentially larger-than-expected rate hike in December.

A quarter-point hike was already widely expected. Now investors have to take the possibility of a half-point hike a little more seriously. That could be a problem for an artificial intelligence (AI) trade that already looks quite wobbly.

The Consumer Price Index (CPI) rose 0.4% from September to October. That's actually down quite a bit from the 0.6% seen the month before. However, economists were expecting 0.3%. Interestingly, for this round there's no year-over-year figure because last year's government shutdown kept the Bureau of Labor Statistics from collecting data in October of 2025.

After jumping 9.1% in September, gasoline prices rose just 1% in October, a welcome change and a major factor in the overall month-over-month decline. Brent crude -- an international benchmark for global oil prices -- dipped below $90 a barrel on Tuesday, which hopefully will help that trend in the months to come.

However, housing costs accounted for more than 30% of October's increase, and medical care and car insurance got more expensive, too. Lower oil prices may be too little too late.

When the Fed held rates steady in October, the most recent jobs report showed the economy had added just 29,000 jobs in September, which helped drive their decision. But that figure has since been revised up to 60,000, and the latest numbers showed employers added another 165,000 jobs in October while the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%. The strong jobs data gives less reason to avoid raising rates.

Image source: Official Federal Reserve Photo.

Before today's inflation reading was released, Fed Governor Christopher Waller said he was leaning toward a quarter-point hike. But if core CPI came in around 0.3% again, he said the Fed should consider going a half point. It did just that.

While the market is still betting a quarter-point hike is what the Fed will go with in December, the odds of a half-point raise spiked 11.1% today according to FedWatch.

With further rate hikes likely and sticky inflation keeping longer-term bond yields elevated -- the 10-year U.S. Treasury was up more than 13 basis points today -- borrowing costs for companies are rising.

Lenders have already been asking for a bigger premium on debt from companies involved in the AI data center build-out. That's not likely to change after today's numbers. Expensive borrowing puts a strain on the capital flowing through the system, and if yields get too high, companies may rein in spending. That wouldn't be good for a stock market historically concentrated in a single theme -- AI -- that needs historic levels of investment to thrive.
