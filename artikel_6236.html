Lufthansa Announced Columbus, Ohio Flight—Then City Council Blocked Part Of The Subsidy

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Lufthansa announced Columbus’ first scheduled nonstop flight to Europe last month. Now the city council has refused to approve the first $300,000 of the mayor’s promised support for it. The Frankfurt flights are still scheduled to start May 5, 2027. But the fight exposes something that tends to get lost in the celebration of a new international route: local officials helped make the numbers work by offering up taxpayer money to protect the airline if route revenue falls short, and the city’s portion still needed council approval. Lufthansa plans to fly Wednesdays, Fridays and Sundays using a Boeing 787-9 with Allegris business class. There’ll be 28 business class seats, 28 premium economy seats and 231 coach seats. The region has 38 German companies, Bayer is building a pharmaceutical plant in New Albany, and Frankfurt offers connections throughout Europe and beyond. With the city using residents’ money to guarantee it, Lufthansa’s downside is protected trying it out. Lufthansa would collect under the guarantee if revenue falls below the agreed target, subject to caps and a three year limit. The airport’s current incentive program also offers Frankfurt service $1 million in marketing support over two years, plus two years of landing fee, terminal rent and international inspection fee waivers. That’s available as long as they operate at least 100 departures annually over at least six months. The city’s $300,000 was included in a proposed $1 million grant to One Columbus, the regional economic development organization. The city ordinance was seeking authority to enter into a grant agreement with One Columbus. The council wasn’t technically voting to cancel a Lufthansa contract, just on whether to appropriate the funds to the entity that had promised Lufthansa a fixed guarantee. The Columbus Partnership says it still expects the flight to happen. Council could reconsider, or other backers could fill the funding gap.
