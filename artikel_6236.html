The Airlines Offering Premium Economy On Airbus A380 Flights

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Six of the 10 airlines still flying the Airbus A380 offer a premium economy cabin on the type. British Airways will shortly have the largest at 84 seats, following the retrofit announced on October 5, 2026, which also gives its aircraft the highest premium ratio of any A380 at roughly 48%. All Nippon Airways (ANA) follows at 73 seats, then Qantas at 60, Emirates at 56, Lufthansa at 52, and Singapore Airlines at 44.

The four without it are Qatar Airways, Etihad, Korean Air and Asiana. Qatar's layout of 8 first, 48 business and 461 economy produces a premium ratio of around 11%, the lowest among A380 operators. Where the cabin exists, the experience varies considerably. Emirates won the Skytrax award for best premium economy seat in 2026 and is adding the cabin to 68 A380 routes. ANA fits 73 premium economy seats against only 56 in business and flies them on a single route between Tokyo Narita International Airport (NRT) and Honolulu (HNL).

British Airways announced a full retrofit of its Airbus A380 fleet on October 5, 2026, and the new layout puts World Traveller Plus at 84 seats. That is the largest premium economy cabin on any A380 operating anywhere. The refurbished configuration runs 12 First, 106 Club Suites, 84 World Traveller Plus and 215 World Traveller for a total of 417 seats, down from 469 in the current layout. Economy absorbs the entire reduction, falling by 88 seats, while premium economy grows by more than half.

The proportions are what make the aircraft unusual. Head for Points calculated that the new layout gives British Airways 202 premium seats, roughly 48% of the aircraft, which is the highest premium ratio of any A380 operator by a wide margin. Emirates and Qantas are the next closest at around 30%, and both carry at least 56 fewer premium seats than the refurbished BA aircraft will. Since the A380 is the largest passenger aircraft ever built, that also gives British Airways the highest absolute number of premium seats on any single aircraft in service.

The timing matters for anyone trying to book. The first refurbished aircraft is expected in service within months, with the program running into 2027 and 2028, which means the 84-seat cabin will exist alongside the older 55-seat World Traveller Plus layout across the fleet for an extended period. British Airways operates 12 A380s to a small number of destinations from London Heathrow Airport (LHR), so the chance of drawing a refurbished aircraft on any given booking depends on how far along the program the fleet has progressed.

Emirates won the Skytrax award for the best premium economy seat in 2026, finishing ahead of Cathay Pacific and EVA Air, and The Points Guy named it Best International Premium Economy for a second consecutive year. AirlineRatings.com's passenger-voted Flyers' Choice Awards also named Emirates the preferred premium economy. The airline ranks second overall for premium economy with Skytrax and first for both the seats and the catering.

The A380 cabin seats 56 passengers within a four-class layout of 14 first, 76 business, 56 premium economy and 341 economy, giving the aircraft 146 premium seats and a premium ratio of around 30%. The seats are leather with adjustable headrests, calf rests, and footrests, and Emirates serves multicourse meals on china tableware with stainless steel cutlery, which is closer to a business class service pattern than most premium economy products attempt.

Scale is the other factor. Emirates operates roughly half the world's A380 fleet, with 116 aircraft, about 100 of which are active, and premium economy is arriving through the carrier's retrofit program rather than on new deliveries. The cabin joined Dubai to Milan on A380 services from November 10, 2025, alongside New York routes, and Emirates confirmed plans to offer premium economy on 68 routes by March 2026. Given the size of the fleet receiving it, a passenger searching for premium economy on a superjumbo will find more Emirates departures than from any other operator.

The cost of the world's longest A380 premium economy fares typically range within the $2,000-2,500, although this varies by discounts and season.

Qantas carries 60 premium economy seats on its A380s within a four-class layout of 14 first, 70 business, 60 premium economy and 341 economy, totaling 485 seats. That gives the aircraft 144 premium seats and a premium ratio of around 30%, effectively matching Emirates. The seats offer more width and legroom than economy, with adjustable headrests, footrests, storage and charging ports, and the fare includes priority check-in, priority boarding, upgraded meals, noise-canceling headphones and an amenity kit. Qantas operates 10 A380s, with around eight active, on routes from Sydney and Melbourne to London, Los Angeles, Dallas and Singapore.

Lufthansa fits 52 premium economy seats into a 509-seat A380 alongside 8 first, 78 business and 371 economy. The carrier operates eight aircraft and has committed to the type while retiring other quadjets, with summer 2026 routes including Los Angeles, Boston, Denver, Washington and Delhi, plus a new Munich to Mumbai service from July 2026. Lufthansa's premium economy ranks seventh overall in Skytrax's 2026 assessment and fifth for the seat specifically, though its highest-rated version is the Allegris cabin on the A350 and 787-9 rather than the A380.

Singapore Airlines has the smallest premium economy cabin on the type at 44 seats, in a 471-seat aircraft carrying 6 suites, 78 business and 343 economy. That is a premium ratio of around 27%, the lowest of the three. The product itself rates highly, placing third overall with Skytrax for premium economy and fourth for the seat, with calf and footrests, personal storage, charging ports and large entertainment screens. Premium economy passengers also get the Book the Cook service, allowing a main course to be reserved before departure, alongside priority check-in, boarding and baggage handling. The carrier operates 12 A380s across eight major routes from Changi for summer 2026.

All Nippon Airways carries 73 premium economy seats on its A380s, which ANA's own fleet material confirms in a 520-seat layout of 8 first, 56 business, 73 premium economy and 383 economy. That makes it the second-largest premium economy cabin on any A380, and Simple Flying reported it as the largest premium economy cabin across ANA's entire fleet. The seats sit at the rear of the upper deck behind first and business, arranged 2-3-2.

The aircraft itself is unlike any other A380 operation. ANA has three, each painted in a different sea turtle livery, and every scheduled A380 flight operates between Narita and Daniel K. Inouye International Airport (HNL) in Honolulu. No other operator confines the type to a single route. The aircraft entered service in May 2019 and continues to fly Narita to Honolulu year-round, with ANA adjusting frequencies seasonally. The third airframe only entered commercial service in late 2023 after an extended period in storage during the pandemic.

The configuration reflects who the aircraft carries. ANA built it around the Hawaii leisure market, and the cabin mix shows it, with 73 premium economy seats against 56 in business. The economy deck includes ANA COUCHii, where groups of three or four seats convert into a sofa-style space with raisable leg rests for an additional fee, which Airport Spotting described as aimed at families traveling with young children. For a passenger wanting premium economy on an A380, ANA offers one of the largest cabins available and the only route where you can find it.

Four airlines fly the A380 with no premium economy cabin. Qatar Airways configures its aircraft with 8 first, 48 business and 461 economy seats across 517 total, giving it 56 premium seats and a premium ratio of around 11%, the lowest of any A380 operator by a substantial margin. Simple Flying excluded Qatar entirely from an earlier comparison of premium economy fares on the world's longest A380 routes, noting the airline does not offer the cabin on the type.

Etihad, Korean Air and Asiana complete the group. Etihad operates nine A380s and Asiana six, with Asiana running a three-class layout that includes 104 upper deck economy seats arranged 2-4-2. Those Asiana aircraft are also on borrowed time. Simple Flying reported that following the Korean Air merger, both carriers were expected to retire the type as part of fleet optimization.

The division is not a straightforward premium versus budget split. Qatar Airways has one of the most highly regarded business class products in the industry in Qsuite, yet its A380 carries the smallest proportion of premium seating of any operator of the type. A passenger wanting more than economy on a Qatar A380 has to buy business outright, with no intermediate option. Across the roughly 10 airlines still operating the A380, six offer premium economy and four do not, meaning the cabin is available on most superjumbo flights but not all of them.
