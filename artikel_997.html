Why Raya is the Premier League's best goalkeeper

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

David Raya is the focus of The Radar column as he nears a new Arsenal contract. Plus, the numbers showing Newcastle's pressing problems under new head coach Matthias Jaissle. Watch Newcastle vs Arsenal live on Sky Sports on Saturday; kick-off 5.30pm

Football Features Writer @nicholaspwright

Welcome to The Radar, a Sky Sports column in which Nick Wright uses a blend of data and opinion to shed light on need-to-know Premier League stories. This week:

Unai Simon was named goalkeeper of the year at the Ballon d'Or ceremony but the 20,000 professional players who vote on the FIFPRO World XI went for David Raya. In fact, his total of 10,278 votes was the highest of any player - goalkeeper or otherwise.

Raya had to settle for a supporting role during Spain's World Cup campaign. Simon started every game and remains Luis de la Fuente's first choice in the position. But Raya's FIFPRO vote share reflected his status in the eyes of his fellow players.

Arsenal see him in the same way. Their new contract for Raya will reflect his standing as one of the world's best; recognition of his vital role in last season's Premier League title win and reward for how he has flourished since joining from Brentford in 2023.

Raya is now a three-time Golden Glove winner who has kept clean sheets in nearly half of his 117 Premier League appearances for Arsenal. He has an even better success rate in the Champions League, with 21 shutouts in 40 games in the same timeframe.

The numbers are extraordinary. But clean sheets are of course a collective endeavour. A deeper look into the data is required to build a true picture of Raya's individual excellence.

Gradient Sports, who grade players out of 100 based on the quality of their execution, rather than just the volume of their actions, have Raya as the Premier League's best or second-best performing 'keeper for a third straight season, reflecting his consistency.

Their goalkeeper grading system factors a broad range of performance elements, including, of course, the art of shot-stopping, one of many areas in which Raya continues to shine.

According to Gradient, who also grade shot quality, Raya is the only goalkeeper in the Premier League this season yet to concede a 'saveable' goal. For context, the data shows Emiliano Martinez, Alisson Becker and Gianluigi Donnarumma have conceded three, four and five respectively.

This is not just a statistical quirk, either. Since the start of last season, Raya's total of only six saveable goals conceded equates to just 0.13 per 90 minutes, the lowest rate of any goalkeeper to have played at least 20 per cent of available Premier League minutes.

Raya's reflexes are key to his shot-stopping. The quality of his positioning is another factor. According to Gradient's analysts, he hasn't made a single positional error when facing a shot this season.

Raya is of course well protected. Arsenal have the best defence in the Premier League and maybe Europe. But those defenders benefit from Raya's presence as much as he benefits from theirs.

When interviewing him for Sky Sports in 2024, Raya highlighted the importance of another of his best attributes: his ability to claim crosses, which he credited to Iñaki Caña, the goalkeeping coach who followed him from Brentford to Arsenal.

"If you watch highlights from me when I was back at Blackburn, you will see a 'keeper that just stood on the line, not coming for anything, rarely going for a cross, rarely going outside the box, rarely anticipating, just waiting to make that save," he said.

"When I signed for Brentford, Iñaki just completely changed my style of being a goalkeeper, to be more proactive and anticipate stuff that has not happened yet. So, if you can go for a cross, just go for it. Don't wait for the save. Instead, avoid the chance."

Brentford fans will recall him being caught off his line by Joe Byram's long-range free-kick in the 2020 Championship play-off final. Arsenal fans will remember him failing to reach a high ball before Brian Brobbey's 94th-minute equaliser away to Sunderland.

But the rewards comfortably outweigh the risks overall.

This season, Gradient's data for crosses caught, factoring crosses caught at waist height or above, shows him top among goalkeepers, with 13. And that despite facing roughly 20 per cent fewer crosses than the two goalkeepers closest to his total.

Raya's dominance from crosses acts as a pressure release valve for Arsenal when defending their box. His efforts this season help to explain why the Gunners have one of the lowest opponent cross completion rates among Premier League sides.

Raya is shorter than the average goalkeeper at 6ft 1in, instead relying on his spring and athleticism when commanding his box. In possession, meanwhile, he benefits from vision that once prompted Jurgen Klopp to say he "should have number 10 on his back".

According to Gradient, Raya has completed the joint-most line-breaking passes of Premier League goalkeepers with this season. His total of 33 puts him level with Aston Villa's Zion Suzuki but with a higher success rate at 87 per cent to Suzuki's 79 per cent.

Suzuki stands out in the data as another goalkeeper who offers proactivity on crosses as well as progressive passing ability. But the Villa goalkeeper has conceded five saveable goals already this season, nearly as many as Raya in the last two combined.

It is that completeness, in shot-stopping, cross-claiming and distribution, that separates Raya from the rest in the Premier League. And while the Ballon d'Or voting went another way, it gives him a strong case to be considered one of the world's best too.

Arsenal next test comes at St James' Park, live on Sky Sports on Saturday, where Newcastle boss Matthias Jaissle could really do with a result. He has only won one out of five five Premier League home games since arriving from Saudi Arabian club Al Ahli.

Speaking to him while at Red Bull Salzburg, where he won back-to-back Austrian titles between 2021 and 2023, provided an insight into his philosophy. Jaissle is an advocate of quick, vertical attacks. But what came across most strongly was the overarching importance he places on out-of-possession work.

"The mentality is important, to see the nature of the player," Jaissle told Sky Sports. "Does he really want to chase the ball, to win it back? We also have a saying that mentality eats talent."

Jaissle likes to his teams to channel their intensity into Jurgen Klopp-like counter-pressing, which is defined as attempting to win the ball back within a few seconds of losing it. The idea is to capitalise on an opponent's disorganisation in the moments after regaining the ball, ideally high up the pitch.

Jaissle's Red Bull Salzburg excelled on this front. In their 2021/22 and 2022/23 Champions League campaigns, they ranked ninth and seventh respectively among the 32 competing sides for possessions won in the final third per game. But his Newcastle players have so far struggled to make the same tactics work.

According to Opta, Newcastle sit 17th among Premier League sides for high-intensity counter pressures this season.

Brighton, Arsenal and Manchester City, the Premier League's top three, also feature low on the list. But that is largely because they dominate the ball, averaging roughly 60 per cent possession, meaning there are fewer opportunities to counter-press. Newcastle, by contrast, only average 50 per cent possession.

Counter-pressure locations provides further evidence of Newcastle's struggles. While Brighton. City and Arsenal rank among the sides with the highest percentages of counter-pressures in the final third, Newcastle sit among the lowest.

Their share of just 36 per cent in the final third, compared 56 per cent in the middle third and eight per cent in the defensive third, means they are counter-pressing in less dangerous areas as well as less than most of their rivals overall.

For Jaissle, the issues are compounded by structural problems which have given opponents space to attack when Newcastle's attempts to press are unsuccessful, as shown by their Premier League-high total of 20 shots faced from fast breaks this term.

Expect Arsenal to be set up to capitalise.

After a difficult season in front of goal last term, Evanilson is becoming yet another Bournemouth success story. He faces Nottingham Forest on Saturday having scored, assisted, or done both, in seven of his last eight games in all competitions..
