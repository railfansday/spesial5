Red Dead Redemption Movie And TV Rights Could Reportedly Cost $100 Million

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Grand Theft Auto VIGTA 6MoviesRed Dead Redemption 2Rockstar Games

With many legacy franchises in decline and few new ones taking off, Hollywood has turned to video games for big box office blockbusters. There have been huge success stories like Minecraft and big bombs like Borderlands. But some of the biggest video game series have yet to make the jump to the silvers screen. Chief among them is Grand Theft Auto. Could that be about to change?

According to a report in The Ankler this week, the answer is no. Multiple sources told the outlet that Rockstar Games isn’t interested in doing a movie adaptation of GTA. It makes sense, in a way, since GTA has increasingly become a series about bringing prestige, cinematic storytelling to the messy, systems-driven gameplay of open-world gaming. Putting that engine in reverse would be a bit like turning the video game version of Indiana Jones back into a movie. Who would ever do that?

But it sounds like Rockstar, owned by Take-Two, is interested in shopping around the rights for a movie or TV adaptation of its Western open-world series, Red Dead Redemption. The Ankler adds that a number of $100 million was at one point thrown around for the price of licensing Red Dead. It points out that Amazon paid $250 million for the TV rights to Lord of the Rings.

Red Dead is in a similar lane to The Last of Us, which HBO managed to thread the needle on making into a prestige television show that still hooked fans of the game. It’s easy to imagine a similarly placed streamer doing the same for Rockstar’s IP, with internal executives pointing to mood boards that include the critical success of things like Deadwood and the commercial appeal of things like Yellowstone.

The race to adapt other popular game franchises is already well underway. According to The Ankler, Amazon, Netflix, and FX are paying between $15 million and $25 million per episode for adaptations of Tomb Raider, Assassin’s Creed, and Far Cry. Those are essentially mid-range Hollywood blockbuster budgets.

But Take-Two hasn’t rushed to turn its IP into streaming adaptations, and following the disaster that was the Borderlands movie, which was in production prior to it acquiring series maker Gearbox Entertainment, it has good reason to be. It’s games are already breaking sales and revenue records that Hollywood can’t compete with (GTA 6‘s day-one revenue eclipsed Avengers: Endgame). Why jeopardize that for a small side deal? A good Red Dead show could help the game sell ten million more copies, but a bad one could severely hamper its growing popularity beyond traditional gaming audiences.

A 2008 adaptation of Max Payne, which Remedy Entertainment developed but Rockstar publishes, starred Mark Wahlberg and was moderately successful at the box office but critically panned with a 15 percent on Rotten Tomatoes. The misstep didn’t doom the series, which eventually got Max Payne 3 and is currently slated for remakes of the first two games, but it’s clearly not the sort of thing Rockstar or Take-Two are interested in repeating, especially when legacy movie studios are in the decline and they’re the entertainment industry’s new big shots.
