This 500 kWh energy storage system runs on retired EV batteries

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

A retired EV battery can still have years of use left in it when it’s taken off the road. Moment Energy’s new Nova battery storage system puts those packs to work on commercial sites. And when a pack wears out, it can be swapped without pulling out the whole system.

Nova was unveiled at RE+ in Las Vegas this week, and it’s already in production at Megafactory 1, the battery-repurposing plant that Moment Energy opened in the Vancouver area in June.

Each Nova unit delivers 500 kilowatt-hours (kWh) of energy and 200 kilowatts (kW) of power. It has a 2.94 m by 1.80 m (9.7 x 5.9 ft) footprint, and units can be linked together for projects in the tens of megawatt-hours. A site could charge the batteries when electricity is cheaper or when its solar panels are producing more than it needs, then draw on that power when demand spikes or the grid goes down.

Nova can use packs from different EV models, even when those packs have aged at different rates. Moment’s battery management software tracks how each pack is doing. As packs wear out, the company can replace them while leaving the enclosure and concrete pad in place. Moment says its pack replacement service could keep a system in service for more than 20 years.

“We see a future where reengineered EV batteries become essential infrastructure for powering businesses and onshoring manufacturing and supply chains. Nova brings that vision to life, combining safety, engineering, and North American manufacturing to deploy the safest and highest-performance batteries ever made: EV batteries to supercharge our aging grid,” said Edward Chiang, co-founder and CEO of Moment Energy.

Moment Energy says Nova is the first storage system built from retired EV batteries to earn both UL 1973 certification for the batteries and UL 9540 certification for the complete system. It was also tested under UL 9540A, which looks at how a battery fire can spread. The factory itself is certified under UL 1974, which covers how used batteries are assessed and graded for reuse.

That matters for businesses with limited space. Nova is designed to sit outdoors alongside the buildings it serves. It uses liquid cooling, detects smoke, heat, and flammable gases, and is rated to operate from −13F to 122F (−25C to 50C).

Moment Energy also says an independent review found that Nova meets US federal sourcing restrictions tied to the clean electricity investment tax credit. Those rules are one part of determining whether a project qualifies for the credit.

Orders are open, and Moment Energy expects the first units to ship in Q1 2027 for deployments across North America.
