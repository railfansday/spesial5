E-quipment highlight: Mantsinen 200 material handler has tank treads

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Are you a port or rail terminal operator looking for a new electric material handler that can move serious cargo without sacrificing maneuverability? Do you wish it had tank treads? If that’s you, the Mantsinen 200 might be just the thing.

Designed to handle demanding port and rail terminal operations, the Finnish-built Matsinen 200 material handler combines impressive reach and lifting capacity with the ability to move around a worksite on its own tracks. And, yes: I said tracks.

By far the most fun feature of the Mantsinen 200 is the fact that it looks like a rejected concept for a ROBOTIX dinosaur set and/or something He-Man would use to run over Trap Jaw. And while, yes – most big material handlers have similar form factors, there’s something about the 200 that really sells the whole Dragon Walker vibe.

The Mantsinen 200 is powered by a 380 kW (~510 hp) grid-connected electric motor running at 1,800 rpm. Power comes from a 460V electrical connection as standard, with other voltage options available to suit local site requirements.

Mantsinen says its electrically powered machines deliver the same performance as their diesel counterparts, while eliminating exhaust emissions at the machine and reducing operating noise, energy consumption and maintenance costs. Power is delivered through a load-sensing hydraulic system featuring four variable-displacement piston pumps and a separate closed-loop swing circuit.

Mantsinen also fits the 200 with its proprietary HybriLift energy-recovery system. HybriLift works a lot like regenerative braking by capturing potential energy as the main boom is lowered, but instead of storing that energy as electricity in a battery, the material handler stores it in hydraulic accumulators for reuse during the next lift. The company claims HybriLift improves the big machine’s energy efficiency by up to 50%, effectively giving the machine an additional 300 (ish) hp of lifting assistance at average lifting speed.

And, while the machine normally draws power from the grid, operators aren’t necessarily tethered to an outlet. Mantsinen offers an optional PowerPack that allows the machine to disconnect from its main power supply and still travel around the worksite, with some basic hydraulic systems still functional. The PowerPack is currently diesel-powered, but a battery-backed version isn’t out of the question. It’s a useful bit of flexibility for terminal operators who want the benefits of electric material handling without giving up the ability to reposition the machine when a fixed electrical connection isn’t available.
