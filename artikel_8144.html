Appalachian Trail Threatened by ‘Uncommonly’ Large Wildfire in White Mountains

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

The Wild River Fire burns in the White Mountain National Forest (Photo: Courtesy USFS)

A wildfire that may be the largest in White Mountains history is currently burning just miles from the Appalachian Trail, and officials say a human may have caused it.

The Wild River Fire started on Tuesday, November 3, in Coos County, New Hampshire, and has grown to 865 acres as of this writing, with just 11 percent contained. While the Appalachian Trail (AT), roughly 10 miles from the blaze, currently remains open, the U.S. Forest Service (USFS) has closed numerous other trails and roads in the area.

Small brush fires and wildfires occur in White Mountains National Forest (WMNF) almost every year, but according to Gorham Fire Chief Philip Cloutier, who spoke with NHPR on Wednesday, a wildfire of this size is unusual. Both extended drought and extremely dry fall conditions have fuelled its speedy growth.

“That’s very uncommon in our area,” Cloutier said. “The largest wildfire that we can remember was just under 100 acres, and that was over 20 years ago.”

Officially, USFS is still investigating the wildfire’s source. Authorities have indicated that they believe it was human-caused, though they have released no further details about the origin.

In addition to these closures, Wild River Road is also closed in its entirety.

According to the USFS, the response to the Wild River Fire has been swift, and additional resources, including thje Cherokee Hotshot Crew and a smoke suppression module, have been sent to aid in the fight. The area’s steep, rocky terrain, remote location, and wet conditions have made it particularly challenging for firefighters. A USFS spokesperson told NHPR that firefighters had to cross the Wild River on foot to mount a direct attack.

However, the efforts seem to be paying off. On November 4, the fire swelled to 982 acres. That evening, it was reduced to 865 acres, where it remains currently.

“I am proud of the firefighters who responded quickly and continue to work tirelessly to contain the Wild River Fire,” noted Josh Sjostrom, District Ranger for the White Mountain National Forest’s Androscoggin Ranger District. “We will continue to work with our neighbors and local and state partners to contain this fire.”
