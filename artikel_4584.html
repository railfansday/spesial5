New Jersey is putting 10 MW of community solar on warehouse roofs

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Warehouse roofs are prime real estate for solar, and Faropoint is putting more of that underused space to work in New Jersey. The industrial property owner has started construction on the first of 16 community solar projects totaling more than 10 megawatts (MW).

First up is a 1.6 MW rooftop array at 19-05 Nevins Road in Fair Lawn. Another 15 projects, totaling about 8.5 MW, have been accepted into New Jersey’s Community Solar Energy Program, and nine are already cleared to start construction.

That’s more solar going onto buildings that are already there, with participating households set to get savings on their electricity bills.

Large warehouse roofs offer something solar developers need: lots of flat space. Putting panels up there gives the same building the job of generating electricity without taking up additional land.

Faropoint specializes in industrial properties used for local deliveries and distribution. “Utilizing previously unused portions of our buildings allows us to support the communities where we invest,” said Brian Nave, Faropoint’s vice president of asset management and renewable energy.

Faropoint and its independent solar adviser, SolarKal, expect the 16 projects to generate around 12.7 million kilowatt-hours (kWh) of electricity annually once they’re operating. They estimate the portfolio will avoid about 8,500 metric tons of carbon dioxide emissions a year.

Faropoint says at least half of each project’s capacity will be reserved for low- and moderate-income households. New Jersey’s program requires a minimum of 51%.
