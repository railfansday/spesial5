I watched Rangers down Motherwell and spotted Ibrox’s new leading man, injury blow plus eyebrow-raising stat

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Rangers maintained their strong run of domestic form by defeating Motherwell 2-1 at Fir Park.

Emmanuel Fernandez had his headed opener for the away side cancelled out by a Jake Girdwood-Reich effort before Ryan Naderi’s deflected winner. It means Rangers are second and four points behind Premiership leaders Celtic with a game in hand while Motherwell sit 10th after their latest defeat.

I was at the game played in bitterly cold North Lanarkshire conditions. Here are some of the things I spotted.

Whisper it in hushed tones... but this is the type of game that teams who go on to clinch silverware usually grind out. The expected goals tally of both sides show the tale of the tape, where not too dissimilar to Rangers’ 1-0 at Ibrox last time out, Derek McInnes’ side battered on the door without getting through.

Motherwell goalkeeper Alex Paulsen was doing his best ‘you shall not pass’ Gandalf impression, but much like The Balrog dragging the grey wizard from Lord of the Rings into the abyss, eventually his resistance was broken. It was messy but Rangers cared not as they got their winner just before a minimal amount of stoppage time was added on. It’s a clash teams of the recent past in Govan coughed points up in and making it 13 unbeaten domestically will be of great satisfaction to boss McInnes.

Motherwell under Alfred Johansson have set their stall out as a side who want to dominate the ball but that hasn’t happened in either home game against Rangers or Celtic. Both sides of the Old Firm have come to Fir Park and been able to dictate play, Martin O’Neill’s side winning 2-0 last month, having controlled proceedings with well over 60% possession. This was much the same albeit the Fir Park defence stood valiantly until they were eventually overwhelmed, Rangers enjoying just under that 60% mark in terms of time on the ball.

Motherwell fans, and supporters of other provincial clubs, have been used to these types of games against Glasgow’s big two over the years. It’s a defensive stand that more often than not ends in defeat, but it’s more of a shock to the system for Motherwell, given what they have built over the last 18 months. Rangers’ winner was perhaps harsh with the effort put in but the amount of chances coughed up proved there could be little complaints over their visitors claiming all three points. Key playmaker Ibrahim Said looked visibly emotional after being subbed in the first half with injury and that will be of major concern to boss Johansson, with much of his side’s good attacking play usually involving the Nigerian.

Since replacing an injured Lawrence Shankland last time out versus Motherwell, Ryan Naderi has been a man transformed. He bundled home the winner off defender Martin Moormann here to make it 11 goal contributions in as many games. Questions over quality were abundant last term upon his arrival from Hansa Rostock but he has been vital for McInnes this term.

Amid long-term blows to Shankland and Youssef Chermiti, Naderi has firmly made himself Rangers’ main man in attack. Any striker coming into the team would need to play alongside him as it’s hard to see how they function without him. It might be unconventional at times but Naderi’s pressing and knack of being in the right place at the right time is proving invaluable.

This clash between Motherwell and Rangers was the first time since Opta records began in season 2005/06 that there was not one Scottish player in the starting line-up of a top-flight match. As conversations continue over development of young talent in our country, it’s perhaps inevitable it became a talking point across analysis of the match.

Let’s get off our high horses for a minute though as the conversation with this particular game needs more nuance. Alex Lowry and Stephen O’Donnell are regular starters for Motherwell but both were unavailable, while club captain Paul McGinn is only recently back from long-term injury. James Penrice would likely have been a starter for Rangers had he been fully fit while Findlay Curtis did get on to make a key impact in the winner. Callum Hendry offered more bite to the home attack at the other end. All of that is not to say a lack of development Scottish talent isn’t an issue, it is, but perhaps this stat isn’t the platform to build that argument from.
