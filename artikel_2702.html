Jazz's Center Crisis Is Distorting Their Rebuild Attempt

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

The Utah Jazz are supposed to use this season to evaluate their future, but Friday's 125-118 loss to the Houston Rockets exposed a problem with that plan. Utah's depleted center rotation is no longer merely a weakness. It is beginning to distort what the organization can learn about everybody else.

Houston collected 18 offensive rebounds and received 43 points from Alperen Sengun. Jaxson Hayes played only 13 minutes before leaving with a knee injury, adding another concern to a frontcourt missing Lauri Markkanen, Jaren Jackson Jr. and Jusuf Nurkic. Mo Bamba competed hard, but eventually fouled out in a larger role.

Those absences affect Utah's defense and rebounding. However, their impact reaches beyond the box score. A guard cannot be evaluated as a pick-and-roll creator when screens lack consistency, vertical spacing disappears and every missed shot threatens to become an opponent's transition opportunity.

The same problem affects Utah's wings. Ace Bailey should be learning when to cut, relocate and attack a rotating defense. Instead, an unstable interior can force him to create against a set defense because possessions never develop as designed.

Will Hardy cannot manufacture healthy players, but he can prevent the circumstances from corrupting Utah's developmental goals. The Jazz should simplify the responsibilities of their available centers, demand reliable screening and prioritize securing the first rebound. They do not need spectacular production from that position right now.

They need functional basketball. That means setting contact, protecting the restricted area without chasing every block and giving the guards a dependable outlet when pressure arrives. Those details would create a cleaner environment for Darryn Peterson, Keyonte George, Bailey and Isaiah Collier to reveal what they can become.

Utah's 1-12 record makes every problem feel like evidence that the rebuild is failing. That conclusion would be premature, especially when several foundational players are unavailable. Still, the Jazz cannot spend months collecting misleading information and call it development simply because young players received minutes.

Development requires context, structure and responsibilities that resemble winning basketball. Otherwise, Utah may reach the spring knowing who scored points without understanding which combinations can survive together. That is why stabilizing the center position matters beyond finding someone capable of grabbing rebounds.

The Jazz do not need their next franchise center to walk through the door tomorrow. They need enough order inside to evaluate the players who are already here. Until that happens, every judgment about Utah's young core must carry an asterisk.
