New retro electric motorcycle to debut at EICMA with a novel feature: a second gear

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Most electric motorcycles have something in common: a single-speed transmission. But a British company is preparing to shake things up at this year’s EICMA motorcycle show in Milan, where it will unveil a new electric motorcycle demonstrator featuring an unusual two-speed transmission designed to shift gears without interrupting power delivery.

The company behind the technology, E-Motiv Shift Systems, has developed what it calls a seamless powershift transmission, promising improved acceleration, better efficiency, and a more engaging riding experience.

Unlike conventional motorcycle transmissions, which generally require a brief interruption in power during gear changes, E-Motiv’s system is designed to maintain torque throughout the shift.

That could offer some interesting advantages for electric motorcycles, where a lower first gear could provide stronger acceleration while a taller second gear could help optimize motor efficiency at higher speeds.

Of course, most electric motorcycles don’t actually need multiple gears, and very few have ever offered multi-speed transmissions. Electric motors produce strong torque across a wide operating range, which is why manufacturers generally prefer the simplicity, lower weight, and reduced maintenance of single-speed drivetrains.

But that doesn’t mean a second gear couldn’t offer some benefits.

E-Motiv’s technology actually traces its roots back nearly 25 years to the Zeroshift transmission system, which eventually found its way into Aprilia’s MotoGP racing motorcycles in 2015.

It's possible, from my perspective, that running a motor at lower rpm under reduced load like flat terrain cruising, could improve range, and lower temperatures, and might be beneficial for cars and trucks as well.

The latest electric version was developed through a £1 million UK government-funded project involving motorcycle manufacturer BSA, the University of Bath, and the Institute of Advanced Automotive Propulsion Systems (IAAPS).

The demonstrator motorcycle, known as Demo 1, was conceived by British design consultancy Envisage and is being built by IAAPS. Real-world testing is scheduled to begin in early 2027.

E-Motiv will showcase the technology at EICMA from November 3-8, though the demonstrator remains a development project rather than a production-ready motorcycle.

Whether two-speed transmissions eventually become common on electric motorcycles remains to be seen. But if E-Motiv can deliver meaningful efficiency and performance improvements without adding too much weight, cost, or complexity, it could give future electric motorcycles another interesting trick up their sleeves.
