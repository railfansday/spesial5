Why Is Tim Cook Being Mentioned as Nvidia’s Next CEO?

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Home » Crypto Market News » Why Is Tim Cook Being Mentioned as Nvidia’s Next CEO?

As prediction markets price in a Jensen Huang exit, Yale’s Jeffrey Sonnenfeld names an outside successor.

Former Apple CEO Tim Cook has entered the conversation as a potential successor to Jensen Huang after Yale professor Jeffrey Sonnenfeld floated his name in Time magazine, citing Cook’s unique track record following a major tech founder.

Now serving as Apple’s executive chairman after stepping down in September, Cook is drawing speculative interest on prediction markets as reports surrounding Huang’s personal life and links with alleged Chinese spy spark boardroom scrutiny at the world’s largest chipmaker.

Still, there is no indication that Nvidia has approached Cook or that he is actively seeking the job. And to understand why succession is even being discussed, it helps to look at the controversy unfolding inside Nvidia’s boardroom.

Since November 8, media reports have alleged that Huang had an affair with a woman suspected of ties to Chinese intelligence—a relationship he reportedly acknowledged in a letter to staff.

The revelations have raised immediate questions around national security risks and executive succession. In response, Nvidia’s board launched an independent investigation, sending Nvidia shares down more than 8% in after-hours trading.

While the board investigates, prediction market traders are already betting aggressively on how soon Huang could step down—and who might replace him.

On Polymarket, traders are giving an 88% chance that Huang leaves his position by December 31, with the year-end contract drawing more than $4.7 million in volume.

Bettors also place 59% odds (across $2.43 million in volume) on an announcement happening as soon as today, November 12. On Kalshi, 63% of bettors expect Huang to depart by November 15.

Trading is equally active in the successor markets, though insiders currently lead the pack.

On Kalshi current Nvidia CFO Colette Kress leads at 30%, closely followed by Ian Buck, who runs Nvidia’s hyperscale and high-performance computing business, at 29%. Tim Cook sits at 5%.

On Polymarket, Buck holds the top spot at 25%, just ahead of Kress at 22%.

Because Nvidia sits at the center of the global AI trade, sudden leadership uncertainty ripples far beyond a single stock. Alongside Nvidia’s after-hours drop, broader tech equities and AI-linked crypto assets, including Nvidia-linked crypto derivatives and tokenized stocks, have already posted losses as markets brace for what comes next.
