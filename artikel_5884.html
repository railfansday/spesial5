Strobe Talbott, former deputy secretary of state and Brookings Institution president, dies at 80

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Strobe Talbott, a former deputy secretary of state who once worked at Time magazine, died at age 80.

Strobe Talbott, a former deputy secretary of state whose deep expertise on the Soviet Union shaped U.S. foreign policy before he went on to lead the Brookings Institution, died Thursday at the age of 80.

Talbott’s son, Devin Talbott, said his father died peacefully after a long illness at his home in East Hampton, New York, The Associated Press reported.

An Ohio native, Talbott began his journalism career as an intern at Time magazine while studying at Yale and Oxford, where he was roommates with future President Bill Clinton. Clinton later joked that his job in their shared apartment was to make Talbott breakfast every morning.

While at Time, Talbott was tapped to translate the leaked memoirs of Nikita Khrushchev, the former Soviet leader ousted in 1964, which earned him a full-time role at the magazine.

Over a 21-year career there, he focused on U.S.-Soviet relations, led the Washington bureau, and wrote as a foreign affairs columnist.

In 1994, Talbott joined the Clinton administration as deputy secretary of state, helping guide U.S. relations with Russia and other post-Soviet states.

In a statement, Bill Clinton said no one was better suited to advise him on Russian affairs than Talbott, noting that former Secretary of State Hillary Clinton also relied on his counsel.

Strobe Talbott, president of the Brookings Institution, speaks during a news conference in Berlin on July 16, 2008. (AP Photo/Franka Bruns)

"He was analytical, completely devoted to diplomacy, and on the level," Bill Clinton said. "With his expertise, we made real progress building trust between two former adversaries — moving beyond decades of confrontation to reduce the nuclear dangers future generations would inherit and build a Europe where more people could live in freedom and peace, including through the expansion of NATO and resolving ethnic conflicts in the Balkans. Together we laid the groundwork for greater cooperation while putting Russia on a path to democracy."

"Sadly, its current leader has taken the country in a different direction," Clinton added, referring to Russian President Vladimir Putin.

President-elect Bill Clinton and Strobe Talbott of Time magazine talk privately as they walk along the beach on Hilton Head Island, S.C., Jan. 2, 1993. (AP Photo/James Finley)

He described Talbott as a "good and brilliant man who made our world more peaceful and secure," and said, "Hillary and I will miss him very much."

After leaving government, Talbott served as president of the Brookings Institution from 2002 to 2017 and chaired the State Department’s Foreign Affairs Policy Board.

He is survived by two sons, Devin and Adrian, and six grandchildren. His first wife, Brooke Shearer, died in 2009; he later married author Barbara Lazear Ascher.

The Associated Press contributed to this report.
