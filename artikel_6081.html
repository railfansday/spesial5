Hotel groups have spent years buying lifestyle brands. Accor just committed $1.8 billion to buyout the minority investors in its Ennismore joint venture. A comparison with past deals adds some enlightening context.

Accor's buyout of its partners in its Ennismore joint venture, announced Tuesday, is the largest amount a hotel group has paid for a lifestyle brand company to date.

The Paris-based group will pay about €1.6 billion (nearly $1.8 billion) in a multi-stage deal that values the lifestyle venture's equity at about €3.7 billion ($4.1 billion).

It's the latest in a string of lifestyle-brand purchases by major hotel groups. Marriott bought the citizenM brand for $355 million. Hilton got Graduate for $210 million. Hyatt paid $150 million upfront for The Standard, in a deal that eventually totaled about $335 million. IHG picked up Ruby for about $116 million.

Accor's buyout will be more than the four deals above, combined. A look at the deal and recent comparisons helps cast some light on the lifestyle trend and what Accor is betting on.

Accor will pay about €1.6 billion (nearly $1.8 billion) to buy out the minority holders of its Ennismore lifestyle joint venture, a multi-stage deal that values Ennismore's equity at about €3.7 billion ($4.1 billion) — the largest sum a hotel group has paid for a lifestyle brand company to date, and more than Marriott's citizenM, Hilton's Graduate, Hyatt's Standard International, and IHG's Ruby deals combined. What Accor is buying differs from those transactions: where Marriott and Hilton acquired trademarks and a fee stream, Ennismore brings brand teams, management contracts for nearly 200 hotels, a design studio, and more than 800 restaurants and bars, including Paris Society's venue collection. Ennismore accounts for about 20% of Accor's recurring EBITDA and generated €205 million in EBITDA in 2025, putting the deal at roughly 18 times earnings on both Accor's 2027 projection and Skift's calculation against 2025 results. That's the same multiple Accor used in 2022 when it sold a 10.8% stake to Qatari investors, and Ennismore's annual EBITDA has roughly doubled since. By comparison, Hyatt paid 12 to 13 times projected EBITDA for Two Roads Hospitality in 2018, and the recent brand-only deals ranged from about 9 to 15 times expected fees.
