The cheats prosper: Man City thrash 10-man Liverpool

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Just when the Premier League and the world outside of east Manchester and Abu Dhabi needed Liverpool to step up, to strike back, to make a point, they faltered. Maybe it was unfair to expect them to do so, given the circumstances. But unfairness, lack of clarity and uncertainty are all prevailing moods in English football at present.

How we had rubbed our hands in anticipation of Manchester City having to play their first league fixture here at Anfield after being found guilty of industrial-scale fraud with 114 of the 115 financial charges being proven against them.

It felt apt that it was at the home of Liverpool, who, along with Arsenal, possess the strongest sense of grievance at having been denied trophies and glory by City’s cheating. As expected, there were banners everywhere at Anfield. “A generation of supporters cheated,” one read and it summed up the depth of the anger.

Their fans would surely create the kind of bear-pit atmosphere that would leave City in no doubt at the vitriol that will rain down on them from now on. At every away ground. Liverpool could set the tone. The defiance.

This is a team who can stir the blood, surf the emotion and triumph against the odds more than any other; who have a proud history of doing so. Who feel the injustice at its most acute and could feed off that.

In the end Liverpool suffered their first defeat of the season. Their first under Andoni Iraola. And comprehensively so. It could not have come at a worse time for them and for the Premier League, and for the fans of every other club who wanted to see City humbled. On the pitch.

There was another sense of injustice. Up until the dismissal of Ryan Gravenberch, Liverpool were the better side. Especially as they attacked the Kop in the second half, drawing saves from Gianluigi Donnarumma, and began to build the kind of momentum that has, over the years, appeared to suck the ball into the net.

The midfielder’s first yellow card looked harsh. His second, for a foul on substitute Jérémy Doku was less so (but still not clear-cut) and it meant Liverpool had to play the final third of the game with 10 men. Instead of weaponising that; they wilted and the fans directed their frustration at referee Chris Kavanagh, believing he was lenient towards City.

This is not a slight on Iraola, but it is hard to imagine Jürgen Klopp not riding that wave of emotion more effectively and wringing more out of the available players. Turning to the fans; imploring them; cajoling them.

Instead it was City making the statement with an extraordinary power play as they scored three times in 13 minutes, inspired by Doku, whose cameo made him man of the match. That City could hold the winger back, on the bench, only to introduce him with such devastating effect again pointed to their phenomenal strength in depth.

They are again three points clear at the top, with six wins from six games, ahead of champions Arsenal and there is a frightening awesomeness about them at present that will make the Premier League shudder. For several reasons. What if they run away with it this season before being sanctioned, probably sometime in the spring? Where will that leave the competition?

In the face of City’s power and the adversity of losing Gravenberch, Liverpool simply could not cope. They were already depleted – without Dominik Szoboszlai, Alexander Isak, Hugo Ekitike and Cody Gakpo – and needed some of their other big signings to respond.

But this was not a good day for Bradley Barcola or Florian Wirtz, in particular, and it felt telling that the latter was withdrawn soon after Erling Haaland opened the scoring. He could, in fact, have also been sent off after catching Elliot Anderson late. The German’s contribution was once more woefully negligible; worryingly marginal. He was lightweight and so, ultimately, were Liverpool.

Enzo Maresca hailed Manchester City’s ability to “make our own noise” after their emphatic 3-0 win at Liverpool.

In the first fixture since being found guilty of all but one of the 115 charges laid against them for serious breaches of the Premier League’s financial rules, City made their statement on the pitch as they re-established a three-point lead at the top of the table.

“It was not easy for the players after the last two, three weeks, but they dealt with it very well,” Maresca, the head coach, said. “I said during the week we need to learn to deal with the outside noise because of the situation, because that will be there. But we have to make our own noise.

“Zero-three at Anfield away is a great result, for sure. I think all the organisation needs a win like this. It’s good for all [of] the club.”

Maresca said City will have to get used to such hostile occasions, with Liverpool fans making clear their anger over the judgment, and added: “All the away games are going to be with noise.”

Maresca confirmed that chief executive Ferran Soriano and director of football Hugo Viana had travelled on the team bus to Anfield in a sign of unity.

“Ferran was there, Hugo was there. We were all together,” Maresca said. “For the importance of the game, Liverpool away, Anfield away. It’s good to be together.”

The Italian claimed that despite the verdict, and the attention and uncertainty as to what will happen next, it had not affected his squad.

“The players just focus on the football. They are not thinking about the future,” he said. “It’s how we learn to deal with the noise. It’s our duty to make noise and to do that we win games. You cannot imagine how happy I am. Hopefully I am going to speak about this situation when we are going to win the appeal.”

Since City last kicked a football in anger, they have been found guilty of 114 of the 115 charges levelled at them by the Premier League in a verdict that has shaken professional football to its very core. The club have appealed and the extent of their punishment now rests with an independent panel. Until then, though, the show goes on and City’s players and staff must compete knowing that their every move now comes with a caveat.

City have put up a united front and on Thursday Maresca and Haaland duly delivered the party lines, the City head coach revealing that the feeling among his charges was “fantastic”. “[My players] know what we can control is the football side – they are not worried at all about different things,” Maresca said, before adding that he trusts the club “completely”. The Italian doubled down on his remarks in his Friday press conference.

City maintain their innocence and many of Maresca’s peers have remained tight-lipped on the case, though Manchester United head coach Michael Carrick admitted this week he has questions, while Régis Le Bris urged football’s governing bodies to be “relentless” in safeguarding competitive integrity.

Given all that has happened, it would be easy to forget that this is, for now, a top-of-the-table clash and has been one of the Premier League’s marquee rivalries since the days of Guardiola-Jürgen Klopp. Liverpool fans have never been ones to roll out the red carpet when City are in town, but the ill feeling and belligerence will only intensify at Anfield this time around owing to the events of the past weeks.
