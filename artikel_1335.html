Steely Chelsea triumph despite losing Martinez to head injury

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

This was the afternoon when Xabi Alonso’s Chelsea went from swashbuckling to stoic, thrilling to tenacious.

For all Alonso’s attacking vigour at the start of his reign, there can have been no more symbolically satisfying victory under the new coach than this resolute win at Everton – the most vivid example of an increasingly impressive transformation.

This was what Alonso described as “winning in a different way”. A triumph amid the adversity of losing goalkeeper Emiliano Martínez to what appears to be a serious injury after a head collision with Jarrad Branthwaite is just one facet of why the joyous visiting supporters might consider this an early turning point.

Where recently they wilted, on Saturday they thrived in securing a first Premier League clean sheet for nine months.

Much more of this and the adjectives which were splattered across Chelsea during the golden years of José Mourinho – solid, robust and dogged – will be part of the Stamford Bridge lexicon once more.

“Just one day,” Alonso warned, both acclaiming the importance of the win while wisely preaching caution. “It will be key when we manage 10 clean sheets, even though this one feels good. We need to repeat it again and again, but the mentality was very good. Sometimes it is not to play nice, it is to play good – to win second balls, and to be efficient. We needed one of these: a clean sheet and a win.”

Asked about Martinez’s condition, Alonso said: “It was a concussion, but beyond that I do not know. I have only seen one clip of the incident, so we need to wait.

“What we know is that he left the stadium conscious and is now in hospital undergoing precautionary tests.

“We need to wait. Hopefully it will not be too serious, but at the moment we just have to wait.”

Alonso is speedily shifting perceptions of this previously underperforming bunch, the Stamford Bridge culture on and off the pitch already unrecognisable from a few months ago.

It was Chelsea’s trip to Everton last season which facilitated the endgame of Liam Rosenior’s short, but generally hideous reign.

For those who tried to banish memories, it was a darkly comical period when Chelsea’s players were paying their respect to stationary footballs, seemingly finding new ways to stand in front of travelling fans to apologise for meek and rancid performances.

How different the scenes after Pedro Neto’s double on Merseyside – a success built on those old-fashioned and too often underestimated qualities of character and application before the artists had the final say. These are the wins that build connections and trust between players and supporters, especially during the getting-to-know-you period of a managerial tenure. While neutrals mutter about a generally disappointing spectacle, the players and supporters return home clutching three points believing they have fought together in the trenches.

The home team’s argument that this was a potential game changer was supported when the England midfielder was inevitably involved in the move which led to Neto breaking through 44 seconds into the second half – a lead Chelsea never looked like surrendering as Everton’s attacking limitations were exposed.

Welcome to Telegraph Sport’s live coverage of Everton vs Chelsea.

Chelsea return to Premier League action this weekend looking to harness the momentum of their emphatic 5-1 victory over Bournemouth before the international break. That Stamford Bridge masterclass ended a frustrating three-match winless streak and was built upon braces from Joao Pedro and Jordan Henderson, alongside another strike from summer signing Morgan Rogers.

Yet the true catalyst for the resurgence was the return of Moises Caicedo. Having shaken off the calf problem that hindered his early-season involvement, the midfielder anchored the side’s win. While defensive vulnerabilities persist - extending an unwanted run of league matches without a clean sheet back to January - the victory offered clear signs of progress under their head coach, Xabi Alonso.

Next up is a notoriously difficult trip to Merseyside to face Everton in Saturday’s lunchtime fixture. Historically, Everton have proven to be a formidable stumbling block for the capital club, particularly on home turf. Everton enter the weekend unbeaten in eight of their last nine home encounters against Chelsea, a stretch that has seen the Londoners fail to score on seven visits and suffer six defeats, including a chastening 3-0 reverse last term that spelt the end for Liam Rosenior.

David Moyes will demand a sharper defensive showing from his side this time around, following a frustrating 2-2 draw with Hull City where a 2-1 lead slipped away. Even so, Everton’s broader defensive record at home offers encouragement, having kept clean sheets in two of their opening three league fixtures at their base.

With both teams eager to build consistency, Saturday’s clash pits Chelsea’s newly found attacking spark against an opponent that traditionally relishes this fixture.
