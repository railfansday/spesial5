The Best Rainbow Six Game is Getting an Unexpected Sequel

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

For nearly two decades, Rainbow Six: Vegas has been left in the past while Siege has become the face of the franchise. The two games offer very different experiences, though. Vegas was built around a proper campaign, room-clearing, and a squad that followed your orders. It's been a long time since Rainbow Six offered anything quite like it.

It was a huge disappointment when Ubisoft cancelled each of these games, and they deserve to be revisited at some point in the future.

Now, Insider Gaming reports that Ubisoft has greenlit a new Rainbow Six: Vegas project at Ubisoft Montreal. It's reportedly codenamed Spades, with a third entry considered more likely than a remake. Ubisoft hasn't announced anything yet, but a new Vegas campaign would be a welcome change of pace.

According to the report, Spades is one of three projects recently started at Ubisoft Montreal, including another game said to be linked to Rainbow Six. There's no word on what that other project might be. Spades itself is believed to be in early development, so its setting, features, platforms, and release window remain unknown.

The original Rainbow Six: Vegas arrived in 2006, followed by Vegas 2 in 2008. Both games streamlined some of the more demanding elements of the early Rainbow Six formula, but they didn't abandon squad tactics. Teammates could be ordered into position, stacked at doors, and sent in with different breach commands. The cover-based firefights could get hectic, but there was usually time to think about how to approach the next room.

The squad AI wasn't always up to the task, either. A badly timed move could turn a straightforward room-clearing attempt into a scramble to keep everyone alive. The teammates weren't terrible, but SWAT 4 generally did a better job with its AI officers. That's one area a sequel could improve without losing the appeal of the older games.

I used to watch Vegas gameplay on a TV show about video games as a kid. I only got around to playing both games years later, and they were well worth it.

I've played most of the Tom Clancy games  over the years, completing and even replaying almost all Ghost Recon and Splinter Cell titles multiple times. Vegas was more linear than some of them, but its mix of tactical gameplay and straightforward action worked particularly well.

The snake cam let you check behind a door before breaching, and different entry points kept encounters interesting. Cover was useful, but the squad commands were what made the firefights memorable. Vegas also placed second in our ranking of the best Tom Clancy games, behind Splinter Cell: Chaos Theory.

Siege has given Rainbow Six a strong multiplayer identity, but there's room for something different alongside it. The older games had campaigns and squad-focused missions that offered a change from competitive multiplayer. A new Vegas wouldn't need to follow the Siege formula. It could bring back a side of Rainbow Six that hasn't had much attention in years.

Rainbow Six Tactics is another welcome development. Turn-based strategy is a good fit for the squad-focused side of the series, and it doesn't need to resemble Vegas to be interesting. A new first-person campaign and a separate tactical strategy game could happily coexist.

As our coverage of Rainbow Six Tactics pointed out, Ubisoft is making it a fully single-player game without an in-game shop or microtransactions. It's currently scheduled for 2027, with Paul Bishop from Vegas 2 returning as the head of Rainbow. That connection is a nice touch, but the turn-based gameplay is interesting in its own right.

If Spades does turn out to be Vegas 3, it has a solid foundation to build on. Keep the squad commands, the room-clearing, and the single-player campaign; make the teammates a little smarter. There isn't much reason to turn it into something else.
