Christie’s Adds UK Auction House Dreweatts to Its Portfolio

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Christie’s has added the UK auction house Dreweatts to its portfolio.

In a statement on Wednesday, Christie’s chief executive Bonnie Brennan said that the auction house has long admired Dreweatts. “Most importantly, our clients collect across categories and at every price point, and together we have the opportunity to provide them with an even broader and more complete level of service. Both businesses will continue to operate much as they do today, while building on their respective strengths and the significant opportunities we see for future growth,” she said. Dreweatts specializes in single-owner collections and country house sales, ranging from furniture and works of art, old masters, modern and contemporary art, Asian and European ceramics, modern design, jewelry, silver, luxury accessories, clocks and wine. The auction house was founded in 1759 by Thomas Davis, a cabinetmaker, upholsterer and land agent from Abingdon, Oxfordshire. Dreweatts operates its main salesroom from its country house, Donnington Priory, in Newbury, Berkshire, and also has a showroom in London on Pall Mall.

Henry House, chief executive of Dreweatts, said in a statement that under this new arrangement “we can use the additional advantage of one another’s expertise to better serve our buyers and sellers. Dreweatts has had a parent company before, which enabled us to grow the business and prosper, and this move is an extension of that model.” He added that the Christie’s acquisition provides certainty for the auction house and that Dreweatts will continue, under his and chairman Will Richards’ stewardship.

Christie’s president of EMEA, Anthea Peers, said that the François Pinault-owned auction house recognized Dreweatts as a “healthy, compatible business to our own and the value it can add to what we do for all of our clients.”

On November 19, Dreweatts presented its Fine Watches & Luxury Accessories auction that totaled £529,934 with an 89% sell-through rate. The sale included fine watches, designer handbags, writing instruments and luxury objects from Patek Philippe, Cartier, Hermès, Chanel and Montblanc. The top lots were a Lange & Söhne that sold for £49,780 and a Montblanc black porcelain fountain pen that sold for £8,320.
