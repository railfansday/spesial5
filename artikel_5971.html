‘Political malpractice’: Calls grow to delay Senate GOP leadership elections

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

After last week’s midterm elections saw them lose at least seven Senate seats and the majority, some Republicans are pushing for a reckoning, saying the conference should stop and think about its future.

“After the stunning defeat [we] just can’t continue on business as usual. That kind of defeat demands change,” Sen. Ron Johnson, R-Wis., said on Monday evening.

Asked if he himself would want to run for Senate minority leader, the Wisconsin Republican responded with a question of his own: “That’s part of our problem. Who’d want the job? Would you want it?”

Though no hopeful had emerged as of Monday afternoon — at least publicly — to challenge current Majority Leader John Thune, R-S.D., for the top GOP post, the frustration was palpable as Republican senators returned to D.C. and grappled with the election results. Calls grew throughout the day to delay the conference’s leadership elections planned for Tuesday morning.

“Well, I mean, I’m open to hearing what the arguments are for that,” Thune told reporters Monday when asked about the possibility of postponing the leadership votes. “I don’t know if that solves anything, but like I said, it’s going to be a decision that’s ultimately made by Republican senators. We’ll hear them out.”

Johnson told reporters on Monday that if the election is held, he would “leave the room” and walk out. Either way, he seemed certain to at least get a “fulsome” discussion of the topic, as he requested in a letter he sent to Senate Conference Chair Tom Cotton, R-Ark. Four other Republican senators signed on to the letter, hitting the threshold to call a conference meeting under internal GOP rules.

Also signed by Sens. Mike Lee of Utah, Ted Budd of North Carolina, Rick Scott of Florida, and John Kennedy of Louisiana, the letter obtained by CQ Roll Call cites last week’s “stunning defeat” and reads in part: “Holding leadership elections without engaging in a robust discussion and evaluation of how those running for leadership intend to lead would be political malpractice.”

Kennedy said Monday evening that he was asking to postpone the leadership elections because “we need time to talk to each other.”

“We’re like a shop owner who’s losing his customers. It’s not the customer’s job to fix that. It’s the shop owner’s job, and I’m not looking for blame-shifting. I’m looking for self-examination,” he said, adding that he wouldn’t consider running for minority leader himself.

In the letter, the group asked that at the time the leadership elections were set to begin, 9 a.m. on Tuesday, they instead “have a fulsome conference discussion and delay holding leadership elections until we all have a chance to hear from leadership candidates as to what type of collaborative conference governing model we should adopt and what our strategy will be to counter the emboldened Democrats.”

Thune echoed that wording Monday as he spoke to reporters, saying, “I do think that we need to have a very fulsome, robust conversation about where we go from here, and then we’ll see what the consensus of the conference is.”

But Sen. Shelley Moore Capito, R-W.Va., who serves as chair of the Senate Republican Policy Committee, said she has seen such requests launched in the past and didn’t think it would do much to affect the ultimate outcome. “I think Sen. Thune will be reelected,” she said.

And several Senate GOP aides said that they suspected a “robust” conversation wouldn’t do much to delay the plans and that leadership elections were still expected to take place Tuesday. They noted similar efforts from some of those same senators in previous years to sow discord in the ranks.

That includes Scott, who unsuccessfully challenged Thune in 2024 and then-GOP leader Mitch McConnell, R-Ky., in 2022. But Scott told Fox News over the weekend, “I don’t want to run again. I’ve run twice.”

Republicans “failed. We had a horrible night,” Scott said. “Instead of having an [leadership] election, the first thing you do is you say, ‘OK, what are we going to change? We have to change something.’”

Lee floated the idea of waiting until after the uncalled Senate races in Mississippi and Alaska are officially resolved.

An online pressure campaign was building in some of the same MAGA corners of the internet that have pushed to pass a sweeping voter ID bill, known as the SAVE America Act, that has stalled in the Senate. On Monday morning, influencer Scott Presler in an X post urged constituents to call their senators while praising the lawmakers who have already pushed for the leadership elections to be postponed. “Where is everyone else?” he wrote.

Sens. Jim Banks of Indiana and Dave McCormick of Pennsylvania also called for a delay, though they did not appear on Johnson’s letter.

“A week ago, we had the worst election results in decades,” McCormick said in a post on X, calling Tuesday “too soon” to vote for leaders. “I know it’s been practice to go straight to leadership elections in recent years, but we should not rush it.”

Banks similarly said Tuesday was “too soon,” posting on social media, “Our conference needs to sit down and have a frank conversation about where we fell short, what we stand for, and what we’re going to do for the working families who sent us here.”

One senator speaking out in Thune’s defense was Sen. John Cornyn, R-Texas, who competed with Thune for the top spot in 2024 but on Monday said the leader isn’t to blame for the midterm results.

“I think the president needs to look in the mirror and look at the flawed candidates that he endorsed, and the kind of environment that we’re in,” said Cornyn, who is still smarting from losing his primary to a challenger backed by President Donald Trump.

Cornyn’s loss means he has no say in the coming conference leadership elections. But he said, “I think Thune’s a good man, and did as well as he could against a president and an environment that was pretty hostile.”
