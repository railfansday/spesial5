People are using Meta’s Muse to purge who they’re following on Instagram

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Since the days of MySpace’s Top 8, social media has made it easy to hurt your friends’ feelings. Some platforms make it easy to judge relative friendship, like Snapchat’s publicly viewable “Best Friends” feature.

Instagram, however, keeps users’ social dynamics under wraps from one another, with accounts unable to see when someone unfollows them. Instagram head Adam Mosseri has previously said the platform hasn’t introduced a “who unfollowed me” feature so that users feel free to unfollow whomever they please without fear of judgment or tension.

But a new tool from Instagram’s parent company Meta is shaking things up. Its AI assistant Muse has an apparently petty personality under its cute and cuddly exterior—and it’s happy to help users spot who isn’t following them back and then unfollow them en masse.

How Muse can purge your Instagram

Social media users have discovered that Muse can sort through the people they follow on Instagram and point out any non-mutual connections. Though third-party apps have offered similar features in the past, they’re notorious for getting accounts flagged and taken down from Instagram. Muse, as an in-house app, doesn’t pose the same risk.

One Instagram user who tried out the feature, Khalil Roberts, told Wired about his experience using Muse. Roberts gave Muse the prompt, “Can you show me a list of people (non celebrity) that [don’t] follow me back on Instagram, and can you unfollow them for me.” It quickly returned an Excel spreadsheet with 151 accounts that fit his criteria, then asked if he wanted to follow through on unfollowing them.

Roberts was among the chorus on social media singing Muse’s praises for letting them purge such accounts so easily. “I just cleared 80+ chopped hos who thought I was a fan,” one user posted.

“Just unfollowed 1000+ people on IG,” another user boasted, crediting Muse in a follow-up post.

Meta’s secret menu

Though Muse’s potential for pettiness is a much-requested feature, it’s only being spread through word of mouth. Meta itself isn’t advertising the capability—or at least not anymore.

In October, Los Angeles-based social media consultant Rachel Karten noticed a Meta ad on Instagram touting that Muse could let users “see who unfollowed you each week.”

“I get that this is technically a requested feature but wouldn’t it lead to more resentment towards your own platform?” Karten asked in a post sharing a screenshot of the ad.

Indeed, Instagram has since taken down the ad, as a Meta representative confirmed to Business Insider. Still, the feature remains active in Muse—as long as users know to ask for it.
