Thelma Golden To Step Down From Role as Director of Studio Museum in Harlem

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Thelma Golden, the director and chief curator of the Studio Museum in Harlem, will step down from her position in late 2027, the museum announced Tuesday.

The announcement coincides with Golden’s 20th anniversary in the role, which she assumed in 2006, and comes just one year after the museum reopened in a new, purpose-built building after a seven-year closure. The new $125 million building was designed by architect David Adjaye and provides 82,000 square feet across seven floors, including a 139-seat public lobby, a rooftop terrace, education spaces, a library, and a cafe. It received rave reviews upon its reopening. Kathryn C. Chenault, chair of the museum’s board of trustees, said in a statement that the board has formed a search committee to identify Golden’s successor and has retained an international executive search firm to assist. Golden will remain in her post until a replacement is hired.

While Golden’s departure was not entirely expected, it is not unusual for museum directors to exit their positions after a major renovation or expansion. Lisa Phillips announced in September 2025 that she would leave her role as director of the New Museum after its reopening, and Janne Sirén, director of the Buffalo AKG Art Museum, said in April that he would step down following the museum’s 2023 expansion.

“I know this is the right moment to begin this transition—and as I look ahead to new projects and opportunities, I also look back with gratitude toward the many people whose achievements have been my bedrock,” Golden said in a statement. “I think of my teachers, mentors, and professional colleagues; the long line of Directors, Trustees, and donors who established the Studio Museum and made it thrive; half a century’s worth of endlessly dedicated staff members, audiences, and beloved Harlem neighbors; and above all the generations of artists who have enlightened us. Together, they are an expansive community I cherish that will continue to inspire and enable the next leader of the Studio Museum.”

Before becoming director of the Studio Museum, Golden worked there as a curatorial fellow. She then joined the Whitney Museum of American Art in 1988 as a curatorial assistant and held several curatorial positions there, co-organizing the 1993 Whitney Biennial and organizing the landmark 1994 exhibition “Black Male: Representations of Masculinity in American Art.” In 2000, she returned to the Studio Museum as deputy director for exhibitions and programs under then-director Lowery Stokes Sims. When Sims stepped down in 2005, Golden was named as her successor.

Golden has been credited with raising the profile of the Studio Museum through numerous groundbreaking exhibitions, including 2001’s “Freestyle”. Organized with curator Christine Y. Kim, the show featured 28 emerging Black artists whose work the curators described as “post-Black.” Among the artists championed in that show were Mark Bradford, Rashid Johnson, and Julie Mehretu.

In 2023, Golden won the Dorothy and Lillian Gish Prize, which carries a $250,000 award and recognizes a person who has made a significant contribution to the arts.

Speaking to ARTnews in 2020, Golden described what the Studio Museum meant to her and other young Black creatives:

[At the Studio Museum], they begin their careers and are able to not only get real professional experience but to form a mission and get a vision of real museum work. I say this as someone whose career is the result of those early experiences at the Studio Museum. It cannot be overstated how significant it was for me, as a 19-year-old art history/African-American studies double major from Smith College, to encounter Dr. Mary Schmidt Campbell, art historian and director of the Studio Museum. I can’t tell you what it meant for me, after over a decade as a curator at the Whitney, to become chief curator at the Studio Museum and to work for Dr. Lowery Stokes Sims, a pioneer in the museum field.
