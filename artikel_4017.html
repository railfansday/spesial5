Feature: Don't Have Your Wii Set Up? Here Are 20 Wii Games You Can Play On Switch

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Many people reading this will have a Wii in the house. Even if it's collecting dust in the cupboard after launching 20 years ago, there's over 100 million of the things out in the wild, so the chances are that you or your family have access to one somewhere.

However, on the off-chance you don't have a Wii to hand — or you sold it years ago to save three DVDs' worth of space — don't fret. While the system has a selection of exclusives you can't easily play elsewhere, many Wii games have come to Switch in HD remastered form over the years.

So if you're jonesing for some Wii waggle but would rather use the Nintendo console already plugged into the telly, take a look at some of the highlights below, in alphabetical order.

A Switch remaster of WayForward's remake of the NES original, A Boy and His Blob hasn’t changed since its release back in 2009, but it’s well worth checking out if you’re after a decent puzzle platformer. The visuals still look incredible after all these years, with spectacular animation to boot.

Some aspects feel a bit outdated by comparison to more modern alternatives, with the inability to map certain abilities being a key culprit. Nevertheless, it remains a fun, breezy experience, and one that the younger generation of gamers in particular will adore.

This Nintendo-published remake includes not just DS' Another Code: Two Memories, or Trace Memory in North America, but also the Wii sequel Another Code R, A Journey Into Lost Memories (which never came out in North America).

This is a fine reworking of a couple of point-and-click cult classics from now-defunct studio Cing (who also did the excellent Little King's Story), with a touching narrative, weighty themes, and light puzzling.

Available separately or bundled in the BIT.TRIP COLLECTION, this was the first entry in Commander Video's Runner series, and both Runner 2 and 3 are on Switch eShop if you want the trilogy.

The WiiWare original garnered a 10/10 on this very website back in 2010. Worth a look, then.

In the great platformer pantheon, we're not talking all-timers here, but these are slick, solid efforts with plenty of personality. Both de Blob and its sequel are on Switch in HD form, so if you're jonesing to see how devs approached 3D platformers in the late 2000s, get this inked in your diary.

Epic Mickey was a bold and very fine piece of work, although one where you could still make out the work-in-progress ideas that didn't quite come to fruition with the level of polish Nintendo fans might expect. It reimagined the squeaky-clean mascot and put him in a much darker world than we had come to expect, adding a touching narrative and putting a spotlight on his 'forgotten' predecessor, Oswald the Rabbit.

The Rebrushed remaster makes some tweaks and adds mouse (heh) controls. One for fans of vintage animation and anyone who likes to see creators take exciting chances with the most established of properties.

Donkey Kong Country Returns HD is a fine way to play the 2010 original, but it’s also lacking any real reason for fans to double-dip beyond waggle-free controls. It’s less of a remaster and more of a port, which would be all well and good if it wasn’t being sold at such a premium. Maybe wait for a sale.

There's plenty of fun to be had, but grab the superior Donkey Kong Country: Tropical Freeze instead - or at least first.

A 2023 Deluxe remaster of 2010's original Return to Dream Land, the HD Switch version threw in various tweaks, a couple of new copy abilities, an epilogue with Magalor, and an assortment of minigames mostly cherry-picked from past Kirby games.

Worth a look even if you've played the original to death, and the choice pick if you never had the pleasure.

Klonoa started out on PlayStation, of course, with Door to Phantomile back in 1997. Lunatea's Veil followed on PS2 in 2001, and Klonoa Phantasy Reverie Series bundles remasters of them both into a single Switch package.

So why this here at all!? yells the person at the back who's been grumbling ever since they saw the lead image on the article.

Well, although developer Monkey Craft tweaked some elements to push it closer to Namco's PS1 original in look and feel, the first remaster here uses Paon's Wii remake as its foundation. So if you played the Wii version in 2008, you'll be right at home.

The 2011 WiiWare release was a remake of the PC original, but fortunately La-Mulana found its way to Switch in 2020 (alongside its sequel).

Though brilliant, La-Mulana is definitely one of those games that will only appeal to a dedicated few. This is not something you can casually play every once in a while and expect a great experience; it demands much more of you through its peerless puzzle design.

If you do give it the time of day, however, it proves itself to be one of the most intricately crafted Metroidvanias ever made. La-Mulana gets a strong recommendation, but with the caveat that you should expect to be frustrated and stuck often. Don’t be scared off by the daunting difficulty, though; this is one for the ages.

A sneaky early addition here, as this remaster of Vanillaware's Wii (and Vita) game isn't out until 4th February 2027.

A gorgeous side-on action RPG, 2009's Muramasa: The Demon Blade was ahead of the wave of side-scrolling platformers that would arrive from indie studios over the next few years, and its eye-catching adventure through Japanese folklore is still a treat many years later.

Definitely one to look out for in the new year.

Probably cult developer Suda51's most well-known work, No More Heroes came to Switch in 2020 along with its sequel ahead of No More Heroes III's launch the following year.

Crammed with okatu-fuelled pop culture references (but hailing from nearly 20 years when those references weren't quite so tired), this was an adrenaline shot for Wii owners in 2007 looking for something a bit different after a year of genial waggle.

Wii scored not one, but two Touchdowns (and Switch has so far doubled that). Grasshopper returned with a punchy sequel in 2010, which once again helped put some personality on the Wii shelves alongside all the sanitised, motion-based sports and party fare.

Ironing out some of the wrinkles of the original and throwing plenty more irreverence and off-the-wall antics from the mind of Goichi Suda, No More Heroes 2: Desperate Struggle is still the peak of the trilogy for many.

It may have debuted on the PS2, but Ready at Dawn's Wii port felt like a minor miracle back in 2008. It's a great game that came to Wii, and you can play it on Switch, so here it is!

Okami HD is an utterly fantastic piece of software; a 40-hour adventure that emulates Zelda wonderfully, adds in plenty of memorable mechanics, features one of the most beautiful art styles in gaming, and is completely playable on the go, to boot.

It may be showing its age a bit visually and its combat is sometimes a little on the easy side, but Okami is an important, fun, and notable landmark in gaming history — and one of the easiest recommendations for your Switch library.

Stretching things a little here, both Pikmin and Pikmin 2: This Time It's Pikminable* were both GameCube games that got Wii-specific releases in the New Play Control series which added pointer controls and other minor tweaks.

Both games are available on Switch, though, so I'm including them. In fact, all four mainline Pikmin games are playable on Switch, something which irritates Metroid Prime fans no end.

*I enjoy the idea that this could turn up in somebody's AI summary. Ah, it's the little pleasures that get us through the day.

Perhaps the best thing about Sonic Colors Ultimate is the menu option to change the logo and UI from the American English 'Colors' to the British English 'Colours', mirroring the regional variants of the original 2010 Wii release. Great attention to detail, and an example of the care and respect that went into this solid remaster.*

Regardless of which you think is better, these are two of the finest platformers ever made, both on Switch and looking better than ever.

Nursing the gyro pointers is a minor irritation the originals didn't suffer from, but if you somehow missed either of these on the Wii, you've now got no excuse.

Bringing the Wii launch game back with a lick of HD 13 years later, Banana Blitz HD featured more refined controls — or more specifically let you control the mazes with the analogue stick — to help you enjoy this batch of 100 levels.

The wider game was still let down by weird leaderboard decisions and some lacklustre minigames, though. Hardly a return to the monkey-in-a-ball heyday of Super Monkey Ball 2, but not bad.

The Legend of Zelda: Skyward Sword HD introduced a raft of technical improvements and quality-of-life updates that revitalised the Wii game.

The alternate button control scheme totally worked if you still couldn't get on with the motion controls, the graphics got a sensitively handled HD overhaul, and a once-bothersome sidekick was streamlined into something altogether more useful. The best way to play.

World of Goo is a bona fide indie classic and 10/10 video game in its original incarnation. Its Wii pointer controls married perfectly with the gooey puzzling, and the social satire provided a subtle narrative framework that elevated the entire game.

The Switch version comes with joys and cons. Touchscreen is a brilliant addition in handheld mode, but you'll need to recentre the gyro pointer controls a lot. A lot.

If you've played it previously, the Switch incarnation might be worth a second go for its portability and on-the-go co-op, but if it's your first time in the World of Goo, it's absolutely a must-play. And then there's the sequel to consider. There's goo in them there hills.

The third iteration of Monolith Soft's classic following the Wii original and the New 3DS version in 2015, Xenoblade Chronicles: Definitive Edition presented an already excellent JRPG improved in nearly every way, short of a complete reimagining.

With the Nintendo Switch 2 Edition polishing this gem even further, it's hard to recommend the original release these days unless you're a purist wanting the exact experience everyone else got back in 2011. This is a great update, and a worthy game to finish on.

A pleasant little selection, no? Before heading off, let's address a handful of questions...

Yes, I know there are technically 22+ here. The Pikmins were borderline and bundled, though, and there are two Mario Galaxies in a single entry, a couple of other fuzzy collections, and Muramasa isn't even out yet. It all comes out in the wash.

Not impossible! Presumably it would be Switch 2-only (as with the GameCube library). It would be simple enough for Nintendo to produce NSO Wii Remotes and Nunchuk controllers, as they have for previous retro consoles.

There are potential flies in the ointment, though. For one thing, the precision of the infrared Wii pointer would either need to be emulated with gyro or reproduced (meaning you'd need some sort of sensor bar equivalent to function with the new controllers). Either or both are possibilities, but it does introduce another layer of complexity.

Then again, we're talking about a company that requires an expensive Virtual Boy facsimile to access that system's library on Switch, so (much) stranger things have happened.

As for the library itself, as we've seen above, several first-party Wii games have already received the HD treatment on Switch, so there may be less incentive on Nintendo's side to duplicate those on NSO. Likewise, Switch Sports and Switch Sports Resort update many of the games from the Wii originals, so it's not like you can't play Wii Sports-style tennis already.

But bolstered with a good selection of third-party titles, a Wii NSO library could be a winner.

Along with our list of Wii exclusives (or, in a couple of cases, games originally released on Wii that only appeared elsewhere years later), I'd do bad things for a 4K remaster of the beautiful Kirby's Epic Yarn.

It would be great to have access to other exclusives like New Super Mario Bros. Wii - not everyone's favourite 2D Mario, but an important one. Likewise, it's a shame to have Super Paper Mario and other entries from big Nintendo series (hello, Radiant Dawn) locked to a 20-year-old platform.
