Hello again from STAT’s London outpost, with reporter Andrew Joseph here for Mr. Pharmalot. This newsletter will be off tomorrow and Friday, and while Thanksgiving is a U.S. holiday, it never hurts to take a moment to feel some gratitude, wherever you might be. In the meantime, a few tidbits for your day. …

The cofounders of BioNTech are in talks to raise about $1 billion for a new oncology company, as they prepare to step down from the Covid-19 vaccine maker, the Financial Times reports. Uğur Şahin and Özlem Türeci, the husband-and-wife team behind the German company, are speaking with potential investors about the funding round, which is being led by European venture capital firm Plural. Their new company will be called Arife and will focus on using messenger RNA to treat cancer.

A long-acting drug from GSK performed at least as well as an existing treatment for patients with eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis, potentially setting up the medicine to be a less burdensome option for patients, Fierce Pharma shares. The non-inferiority study tested Exdensur, which was dosed just twice over a year, versus GSK’s other drug Nucala, which required monthly treatment. Exdensur resulted in remission rates at least on par with those from Nucala, and the drugs had similar adverse event rates. GSK says the results give it confidence that Exdensur now has a shot at becoming the first ultra-long-acting option for EGPA.
