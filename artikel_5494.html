Exclusive: Cogent Security’s DefenseOS adds policies and automations for AI agents

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Vulnerability management startup Cogent Security Inc. today launched DefenseOS, an operating system designed for artificial intelligence agents that carry out defensive cybersecurity work inside the enterprise.

Arriving with DefenseOS are two new capabilities, Agent Policies and Agent Automations. The first is designed to give Cogent’s AI agents a deep understanding of the enterprise environment, while the second puts recurring security work on an automated schedule.

Cogent is pitching DefenseOS at security teams that are struggling to keep up as AI speeds up the discovery of vulnerabilities. In October, Microsoft Corp. fixed more than 1,400 security flaws in a single Patch Tuesday release, more than it fixed in all of 2025. The window between disclosure and exploitation has also shrunk, by Cogent’s count, from months in 2024 to only hours this year.

Prioritizing at that pace requires context such as which assets are business-critical, which are exposed to the internet and who owns them. According to the company, that information is spread across documents, teams and tribal knowledge, and most organizations see only a fraction of it.

In its role as the context engine for Cogent, DefenseOS ingests data from scanners, business systems and other security tools. Once the data has been normalized and deduplicated, DefenseOS encodes how the enterprise functions to deliver accurate risk scoring, prioritization and remediation. The platform serves as the AI harness that sets the working environment and limits for Cogent’s agents.

The first of the two new capabilities, Agent Policies, is described by Cogent as creating a context layer that builds and grows via agents, turning how an enterprise works into rules. Those rules then shape how the Cogent platform handles asset context, risk scoring, prioritization and remediation. The company claims the feature strips out much of the operational overhead that enterprises formerly had to put into running a vulnerability management program.

The second, Agent Automations, acts as the execution layer within DefenseOS. Using it, security teams can build agentic workflows that run automatically to generate alerts and reports, investigate vulnerabilities and deliver remediation guidance on a consistent basis.

Co-founder and Chief Technology Officer Geng Sng said recurring reports, known investigations and routine alerts take up a large share of a security team’s week. With Agent Automations, he said, teams can “define that work once” and have Cogent’s agents run it on their schedule.

“Every enterprise runs its security program differently, and moving at machine speed means grasping those differences,” said co-founder and Chief Executive Vineet Edupuganti. “DefenseOS unifies the context of an enterprise environment, surfacing rules into Agent Policies that a customer can codify, and creating the runtime environment for continuous cyber defense led by AI agents.”

The launch follows the release earlier this month of VR-1.1, a new version of Cogent’s frontier reasoning model that writes fixes for newly disclosed software vulnerabilities.

DefenseOS and Agent Automations are available now to all Cogent customers. Agent Policies has launched in beta for new Cogent deployments ahead of general availability at a later date.

Founded in 2025 and staffed by former researchers and operators from Google DeepMind, Abnormal AI Inc. and Coinbase Global Inc., Cogent says Fortune 500 security teams using its platform have cut the exposure window for critical vulnerabilities by 97%. The company has raised $53 million to date, including a $42 million Series A led by Bain Capital Ventures in February.
