Tyson Foods sees its beef losses easing as plant closures and cattle imports help

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Tyson projects a fiscal 2027 beef result of a $300 million loss to a $100 million profit, versus a $654 million adjusted loss in fiscal 2026

Tyson Foods projected a smaller beef loss for fiscal 2027 on Monday, forecasting adjusted segment operating results between a $300 million loss and a $100 million profit. The segment reported an adjusted operating loss of $654 million in fiscal 2026.

An annual profit would mark the segment's first positive year since 2023, according to Bloomberg. Tyson projected companywide adjusted operating profit between $1.7 billion and $2.15 billion for fiscal 2027, versus the $1.98 billion it posted in fiscal 2026.

Beef remained a drag in the fourth quarter ended Oct. 3. The segment lost $514 million on an operating basis, against $277 million a year earlier, as volume fell 6.0%.

Tyson expects fiscal 2027 sales to drop 3.5% to 5.5%, mainly reflecting reduced beef volumes as it reshapes its plant network and contends with scarce cattle. The company cited U.S. Department of Agriculture projections that domestic beef production will decrease about 2% in fiscal 2027.

Margins are improving because the shuttered plants need fewer cattle and because foreign shipments of live animals and ground beef are adding supply, according to Bloomberg. Live cattle shipments from Mexico have resumed, with more than 80,000 head entering the U.S. since late August.

Tyson cut its fiscal 2026 profit forecast in September for the second time in about a month as beef losses widened. The company said at the time that the restructuring should begin reducing operating cost pressures as it enters fiscal 2027.

In August, Tyson announced plans to close its Joslin, Illinois, beef plant and its Eagle Mountain, Utah, case-ready facility and to pursue the sale of its Pasco, Washington, plant. The moves followed the earlier closure of its Lexington, Nebraska, plant and a cutback to one shift at Amarillo, Texas.

Last week, Tyson began restoring second-shift operations at its Amarillo plant ahead of a planned long-term partnership with Allied Beef Company, a consortium of ranchers. The company expects the shift to reach full operation by summer 2027.

The industry outlook remains uncertain. Typical retail prices for ground beef have stopped climbing near $7 a pound, and drought could force ranchers to send more animals to slaughter, according to Bloomberg.
