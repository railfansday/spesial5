Leeds United will be on high-alert after big West Ham news

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

West Ham United have sacked Nuno Espírito Santo after consecutive home defeats to fellow promotion hopefuls, Southampton and West Brom.

Now, all eyes will be on who shines under the new boss, who is set to be Scott Parker, and Leeds United loanee Joel Piroe may be one of them.

There were plenty of flaws with Nuno in the opening stages of the Championship, even if the Hammers had scored the most goals in the division and were among the early pace setters until this run of two straight defeats.

One of the main issues was his struggles coaching a side expected to have the ball, given the sheer quality of the West Ham squad compared to their second-tier counterparts.

Of their six league wins, five of them saw them keep their lowest possession numbers of the season. The other, against Lincoln City, serves as a clear outlier as they played against nine men for the majority of the game.

But what was arguably more concerning was that, barring Jarrod Bowen on the right and Arne Engels, who is the most expensive signing in Championship history, in the middle, there was hardly any consistency in Nuno's sides in terms of attacking personnel.

The striker position was the most egregious case of this. West Ham fans would head to games not knowing which two of Taty Castellanos, Pablo and Leeds loanee Joel Piroe would get the nod.

That's led to some disconnection up there, and is arguably the reason why, despite the strong goalscoring numbers, hardly any of them had come from those whose job it is to put the ball in the net.

Piroe leads the trio in scoring this season with three. But he's hardly been first choice. He's failed to start any more than two straight games, and he's come off the bench in each of the last three.

West Ham's loan capture of Piroe was as strong a sign as any that they'd be one of the strongest attacking sides in the Championship this season. The Surinamese international has been one of the best goalscorers in the second tier's recent history, both at Swansea City and Elland Road.

He's scored at least 19 times in three of his four full seasons at the level, the latter of which saw him win the Golden Boot en route to lifting the league title with his parent club.

Unfortunately, Piroe's style couldn't translate to the intense levels of the Premier League, which led him back to the top end of the Championship in a loan move which will become permanent for £12 million if West Ham get promoted.

With just two league starts in the 2025/26 campaign at Leeds, the move would have been facilitated to get two things over the line. A return to regular minutes and goalscoring form, and an avenue to start afresh with a new side if all went well at the London Stadium this year.

That's not happened under Nuno at all, and as it stands, the Whites will be set to bring Piroe back with his stock value even lower than it was this past summer. They'll be hoping that can change under Scott Parker, as and when he's given the job.

Parker's arrival at West Ham should get some Hammers fans who were dissatisfied with Nuno's reign back onside. Not only did he spend parts of his playing career at Upton Park, but he's got a perfect track record of securing promotion from this level.

The 46-year-old has taken Fulham, Bournemouth and, most recently, Burnley to the Premier League, the latter with record-breaking defensive numbers.

All three squads are more well-remembered for being difficult to score against, rather than outscoring opponents, and that's partially true. Burnley conceded 16 goals in 2024/25, Bournemouth had the best defensive record in 2021/22, and only the top three conceded fewer than his Fulham side in 2019/20.

However, it's not just defending that Parker sides are well-renowned for. Arguably, the fact he's been competing with some of the best attacking sides this division has ever seen in those two seasons has skewed some opinions.

Burnley scored 26 fewer than Leeds two years ago, but had the third-best goalscoring numbers. At Bournemouth, he finished second to Marco Silva's Fulham, who netted 106 times. In comparison, the Cherries' 74 goals aren't as good, but it was the second-best tally in the division.

Parker's sides have also benefitted from having a potent goalscorer, too. Whether that be Aleksandar Mitrovic's 26 in 2019/20 or Dominic Solanke's 29 a couple of years later.

Even at Burnley, while Zian Flemming only scored 12, he played at least 1,000 minutes fewer than Mitrovic and Solanke in the aforementioned campaigns. When Flemming was the recognised number nine in 2026, he scored nine in 19.

So, with Parker's arrival to succeed Nuno seemingly imminent, it shouldn't be long until he starts to implement some of what has made his side so effective in this division, and that includes having one set striker to play through.

Piroe has been more of a link man in recent years at Leeds, but the 27-year-old has been at his best when leading the line. It was his performances as a striker which led the Whites to spend over £10 million on him in the summer of 2023.

If Scott Parker chooses the Surinamese international to be that focal point, rather than Taty Castellanos, he could well have a near-20-goal campaign once more at the level and contribute to another promotion.

That, ultimately, is what Leeds will want out of this managerial switch, as they'll be hoping they can rid themselves of Piroe permanently in the summer, as it's clear he won't have a Premier League future at Elland Road.

Perhaps both he and Scott Parker could prove some people wrong in 2027/28, though, if they both help the Hammers back to the top flight.
