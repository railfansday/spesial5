Bitcoin’s plunge below $60,000 triggers $20 million buying spree at Twenty One Capital

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Two purchases four days apart marked the company's return to Bitcoin accumulation despite steep losses in its publicly traded shares.

Twenty One Capital spent $20 million on its first Bitcoin purchases since going public, even as its shares plunged.

The company acquired 326.57 BTC in two transactions last week, lifting its holdings to approximately 43,841 BTC as Bitcoin fell more than 6% and briefly dropped below $60,000 for the first time since July.

The purchases marked Twenty One's return to Bitcoin accumulation since its December 2025 stock market debut, coming as its shares fell nearly 19% last week.

Twenty One bought 154.32 BTC for $10 million on Nov. 16 at an average price of $64,801 per coin, then purchased another 172.25 BTC for the same amount on Nov. 20 at $58,054 per coin.

The second purchase came at roughly a 10% lower price, allowing the company to acquire nearly 18 additional BTC without increasing spending.

CEO Rapha Zagury described the purchases as an exercise in patience amid the market downturn. He said:

“Disciplined capital allocation requires patience. We don't deploy cash simply because it's available.”

Meanwhile, Twenty One shares closed Friday at $4.03, extending their year-to-date decline to 54%. The stock recovered about 3% to $4.16 in overnight trading ahead of Nov. 23, per Yahoo Finance data.

Meanwhile, the latest purchases cement Twenty One Capital's position as the world's third-largest publicly traded Bitcoin treasury company, behind Michael Saylor's Strategy and Japan's Metaplanet.

Its holdings put it just 159 BTC behind Metaplanet's 44,000 BTC and 437 BTC ahead of fourth-ranked Strive, which holds 43,404 BTC. The narrow gap leaves the three companies competing for position behind Strategy's much larger Bitcoin treasury.

The $20 million Bitcoin expenditure represents approximately 19.4% of the $102.85 million in cash and cash equivalents Twenty One reported at the end of September.

Its third-quarter filing also showed $13.25 million in operating cash outflows during the first nine months of 2026, placing the latest Bitcoin purchases alongside competing demands on its liquidity.

Those demands include operating expenses, financing obligations, and plans to acquire cash-generating businesses.
