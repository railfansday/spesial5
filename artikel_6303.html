The 4 Amex cards I’d use to earn Membership Rewards points faster

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Here's the fun part about American Express Membership Rewards: You don't necessarily need to spend more to earn more points. You need the right card in your hand at the right time.

The best Amex setup shouldn't require a flowchart. It should feel almost effortless, with your points balance growing in the background while every card earns its keep.

These are the four Amex cards I'd choose to maximize your Membership Rewards balance, whether you're looking for one great earner or a multifaceted points strategy.

These cards made my list because each has a distinct job. Most people won't need all four, but picking the right one (or complementary pair) can help your existing spending earn 2, 4 or even 5 points per dollar instead of just 1 point per dollar.

Want a single card to handle your personal expenses? For that, the American Express Gold Card is the workhorse of an Amex points strategy.

It earns 4 points per dollar at U.S. supermarkets on up to $25,000 in purchases each calendar year and at restaurants worldwide on up to $50,000, then 1 point per dollar. If groceries and dining are a large share of your budget, these bonus categories can build your points balance quickly.

That range makes it my strongest one-card recommendation for someone who wants to earn Membership Rewards points across both everyday spending and travel.

The card has a $325 annual fee (see rates and fees), but includes up to $10 monthly Uber Cash for eligible U.S. Uber and Uber Eats orders†, up to $10 monthly dining statement credit with select partners and up to $50 biannual statement credits at U.S. Resy restaurants. (Enrollment is required for select benefits.)

That makes the fee easiest to justify for someone who will use the statement credits naturally and put the earning categories to work. Still, the credits are supporting actors here: The strong bonus categories are why the Gold deserves top billing.

If, like me, you spend about $800 per month at the grocery store, that alone will earn you 38,400 points annually. If you also spend $500 per month on dining, $3,000 per year in flights and $1,000 in prepaid hotels booked through Amex Travel, your annual haul climbs to 76,400 points.

That's worth over $1,500 according to TPG's October 2026 valuations.

Personally, I'd be tempted to transfer 60,000 of those points to Air France-KLM Flying Blue for a one-way business-class saver award between the U.S. and Europe. Those seats can be difficult to find, but that's the kind of redemption that makes my grocery runs feel more rewarding.

†Uber Cash will only be deposited into one Uber account when you add the Amex Gold as a payment method and redeem with any Amex card.

Airfare is where the American Express Platinum Card gets its chance to flex.

The card earns 5 points per dollar on flights purchased directly from airlines or through American Express Travel®, including through AmexTravel.com and the Amex Travel App, on up to $500,000 in these purchases each calendar year (then 1 point per dollar). It also earns 5 points per dollar on prepaid lodging booked through Amex Travel and 1 point per dollar on all other eligible purchases.

Spending $4,000 on airfare each year would earn 20,000 Membership Rewards points on the Platinum card. That's worth about $400 according to our valuations, compared to just $40 earned on a 1% cash-back credit card. That level of spending isn't outlandish: A family of four paying $500 per ticket for two trips a year would reach it. I usually travel more than twice a year, so that difference can add up even faster.

Don't overlook the fact that these rewards come from booking flights directly with the airline, either. Buying straight from the source instead of through a travel portal can make it easier to change or cancel your reservation if your plans change, or when you have to make last-minute adjustments due to delays or operational cancellations.

The Platinum carries an $895 annual fee (see rates and fees), so it makes the most sense for frequent flyers who can pair the airfare earnings with benefits they'll also use. My favorites are its broad airport lounge access through the American Express Global Lounge Collection, up to $200 in annual airline fee statement credits for incidental charges with one selected qualifying airline and complimentary Hilton Honors Gold status. Enrollment is required for select benefits.

Every business has expenses that refuse to cooperate with bonus categories. The Blue Business Plus Credit Card from American Express makes sure those purchases still pull their weight.

The card earns 2 points per dollar spent on the first $50,000 in eligible purchases each calendar year, then 1 point per dollar. It also earns 2 points per dollar spent on prepaid hotels and other eligible travel purchases booked through amextravel.com (and those purchases don't count toward the $50,000 annual threshold). There's also no annual fee (see rates and fees).

That makes the Blue Business Plus an excellent catch-all card for eligible business owners. Rather than worrying about whether each purchase falls into the right merchant category, you can use it for general business spending and know you're earning twice the standard rate until you hit the annual cap.

Maxing out the threshold with $50,000 in business expenses would earn 100,000 Membership Rewards points. If your business values simplicity more than squeezing every last penny out of a redemption, one of the easiest options is to use those points to cover $1,000 in airfare booked through Amex Travel. Transferring your points to travel partners might be worth even more.

It isn't the flashiest card in the Amex portfolio, but quietly doubling your points on broad business spending is a skill worth having.

Some businesses spend a little everywhere. Others have two or three expense categories that swallow the budget whole.

The American Express Business Gold Card is built for the latter. It automatically earns 4 points per dollar in the two eligible categories where your business spends the most each billing cycle, on up to $150,000 in combined purchases each calendar year, then 1 point per dollar.

My favorite part: Amex automatically identifies your two highest eligible spending categories each billing cycle, so you don't need a crystal ball to make a selection in advance, and you don't have to analyze your expenses line by line to choose a category after the fact. The strongest categories can even change from month to month, allowing the card to adapt to your business rather than forcing your business to follow the card.

A business putting $50,000 of qualifying expenses on the card would earn 200,000 Membership Rewards points, even without maxing out the full annual bonus threshold. One way I'd put those points to work is by transferring them to Aeroplan. That balance would cover two round-trip tickets from Miami to Rio de Janeiro — in business class.

The card has a $375 annual fee (see rates and fees), so its sweet spot is a business with enough qualifying spending to put the 4-point-per-dollar rate to serious use. For smaller or more varied expenses, the Blue Business Plus may be the easier fit.

For most customers, holding all four cards is overkill, but pairing two together can supercharge your earnings compared to carrying a single card.

For personal spending, the Gold does most of the earning work on its own with its generous earning rates. Adding the Platinum improves your rewards on flights, but the combination is better evaluated as using the Gold as your primary earner and the Platinum as a way to enhance your experience through its many benefits and statement credits.

The business pairing is much easier to defend. Use the Business Gold for purchases in your two highest eligible bonus categories and the Blue Business Plus for everything else. One handles the obvious expenses; the other cleans up everything left behind.

If you're juggling both personal and business expenses, my pick is for the personal Gold and Blue Business Plus. Between the two, you'll earn 2 to 4 points per dollar on an impressive number of charges while paying only one annual fee.

Welcome offers are exciting, but short-lived. Long-term point maximization comes from paying with the right card.

And the best Amex card for earning Membership Rewards points depends on where you spend the most.

I may dream of an infinite points balance, but the goal isn't to collect every shiny Amex card; it's to make more of my spending earn 2, 4 or 5 points per dollar.

For personal spending, the Gold is a strong choice for groceries and dining, while the Platinum can provide additional value for frequent flyers who use its premium travel benefits. Business owners may prefer the Blue Business Plus for everyday purchases or the Business Gold for concentrated spending in its bonus categories.

Once your biggest expenses earn more than 1 point per dollar, all that's left is remembering which card to use when it's time to pay and letting the points pile up from spending you were already doing.
