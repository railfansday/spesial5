The Sky Today on Friday, November 27: Catch Comet Hartley-IRAS

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Looking for a sky event this week? Check out our full Sky This Week column.

November 26: Thanksgiving turkey in the sky

The Moon-free window for observing Comet 161P/Hartley-IRAS reopens tonight, the same day the comet reaches perihelion, the closest point to the Sun in its 21½-year orbit. It has been a fairly stable 10th to 11th magnitude, and although it has no appreciable tail, the nucleus’ green glow has been notable in recent astrophotos.

You’ll find Hartley-IRAS in the west a few hours after sunset. Around 7 P.M. local time it is still 30° high, well above the turbulent air near the ground that can muddy astronomical views. Tonight the comet is in western Sagitta, about 6.3° north-northeast of 3rd-magnitude Zeta (ζ) Aquilae. Even a small (4-inch) scope will net you a view, but larger apertures will show more detail around the nucleus, allowing you to look for features such as a sharpened southern flank as it flies into the solar headwind.

While you’re in this region, make sure to sweep just 2.1° northeast of the comet’s location to spot the famous Coathanger asterism in Vulpecula. This 1°-long grouping of stars looks just like its namesake, with the hook on the southern side of the hanger’s arms.
