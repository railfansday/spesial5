Air Canada is a major player in the transatlantic aviation market. The airline maintains an extensive network of flights from Canada to Europe, and earlier this year the airline had three Boeing 737 MAX 8 flights across the pond that were canceled.

As part of Air Canada's network transformation, it is transitioning its fleet of MAX planes to its leisure-focused subsidiary, Air Canada Rouge. This will modernize Rouge's leisure network across North America, the Caribbean, and Central America. This has led the airline to pull back from three transatlantic markets with the type.

With the MAX 8 having the range and capabilities to make nonstop flights from Canada's East Coast to Europe, the airline operated a number of routes with the type to destinations. These included Halifax / Stanfield International Airport (YHZ) to London Heathrow Airport (LHR) and Montréal-Trudeau International Airport (YUL) to both Edinburgh Airport (EDI) and Nantes (NTE).

With the planes now being allocated elsewhere, these routes will move to the Airbus A321XLR instead, another narrowbody jet operated by the Star Alliance carrier, which is renowned for its long-range capabilities.

Air Canada will continue to operate the 737 MAX on select transatlantic routes next summer, with the airline planning to operate flights from Halifax to Brussels Airport (BRU), Montreal and Toronto to Keflavik International Airport (KEF), and Toronto to Ponta Delgada (PDL).

These changes indicate that a total of just 3.5% of all transatlantic flying for Air Canada will be operated by the MAX 8, down from 6.8% for the third quarter. Overall, this represents a 43% decrease in transatlantic flights with the MAX between 2026 and 2027.

As Air Canada Rouge prepares to take flight on five new nonstop routes to Europe, passengers can expect a refreshed onboard experience.

Air Canada will continue to transition the MAX 8 to Air Canada Rouge through the end of this year. The first phase of the transition began in March, with the service debuting on routes domestically from Vancouver International Airport (YVR) to Edmonton International Airport (YEG) and Kelowna (YLW).

These planes are configured with 177 seats: 12 Business Class, 18 Preferred Extra Legroom, and 147 standard Economy. Unlike older Rouge planes, these aircraft retain the mainline features, including seatback entertainment, free WiFi for the airline's Aeroplan frequent flyer members, and complimentary beer, wine, and snacks on select routes.

Improvements to the Rouge fleet will cater to customers now enjoying upgraded cabins on shorter trips, with improved technology and enhanced smaller details that make the journey easier and more comfortable.
