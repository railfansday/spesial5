United Airlines Adds Five Routes From San Francisco, Including Four Surprising Transcons

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

United Airlines is adding five routes from San Francisco next spring, including four transcontinental flights to smaller East Coast markets. The latest additions, Richmond and Norfolk, are particularly interesting because both will operate just twice weekly. United is also taking on Breeze Airways directly in Richmond.

Across two rounds of announcements in late October, United unveiled new or returning service from San Francisco International Airport (SFO) to Charleston, Hartford, Oklahoma City, Richmond, and Norfolk.

I had not yet covered the earlier additions, so let’s look at all five together:

The new routes were flagged by Ishrion Aviation.

The first round of additions, from SFO to CHS, BDL, and OKC were loaded on October 22–23.

Charleston International Airport announced its seasonal San Francisco route on October 22, listing Boeing 737-800/MAX 8 aircraft. The airport emphasized both travel to the Bay Area and connections beyond United’s West Coast hub.

Bradley International Airport followed with its announcement of daily, year-round Hartford service. Unlike Charleston’s limited weekly schedule, this represents a daily commitment to the Connecticut market.

A Boeing 737 MAX 9 will be used for Hartford and a SkyWest-operated Embraer E175 for Oklahoma City. The OKC flight will operate daily.

The newer announcements extend that expansion into Virginia. Interestingly, none of these five new routes were formally announced by United.

Richmond International Airport announced its twice-weekly service on October 30. Meanwhile, Norfolk International Airport confirms its new San Francisco flights begin March 31.

The twist on the RIC and ORF flights are the frequencies. Twice-weekly service is familiar territory for leisure-focused airlines but rather atypical of the daily-or-better schedule many passengers associate with a United hub route or that of another network carrier. That certainly suggests United is approaching these markets differently from Hartford. A Richmond traveler can choose a Thursday departure and Sunday return for a long weekend. Someone needing to travel on other days will still need a connection or another option.

The Richmond addition also creates a direct competitive overlap with Breeze Airways, which already offers nonstop service between Richmond and San Francisco.

Breeze is an annoyance to United at SFO and while Breeze is smart enough not to full Independence Air (ramping up at a major United hub too much, too fast, then going bust), I would be incredulous if Breeze did not influence United’s decision to launch this route, particularly given the twice-weekly schedule.

The bigger question is whether both airlines can make the route work over time. More competition is useful when it lasts.

Both Hartford and Charleston’s airport announcements explicitly promote those onward connections…that’s true for all five routes. Ultimately, this is just United trying to grow its network and dominance at its primary West Coast hub.

United Airlines will add or restore five San Francisco routes in March 2027, with daily service to Hartford and Oklahoma City, four weekly flights to Charleston, and twice-weekly flights to Richmond and Norfolk. Richmond brings direct competition with Breeze, while the broader expansion strengthens United’s reach into smaller domestic markets. These are less glamorous additions than another international route announcement, but quite useful as United also continues to rebuild its domestic network.
