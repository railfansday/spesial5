This Is the Simplest ETF Portfolio for Investors Who Want Growth and Income

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Investors can combine ETFs that cater to both objectives.

By Neil Patel – Nov 20, 2026 at 7:45AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Vanguard Morningstar Growth ETF ›

Investing in the stock market is interesting because not all participants share the same objectives. Some are fully focused on achieving the highest returns, which will push them to take on more risk. There are other investors, however, who want to own assets that generate steady income. This will lead them to discover stable dividend-paying stocks.

Maybe you sit right in the middle of the spectrum: Your goal is to have growth and income exposure. But you don't want to spend time picking individual stocks. Exchange-traded funds (ETFs) might be the answer. Here's what I believe is the simplest ETF portfolio to build for growth and income.

To find a solid growth ETF, you don't have to look very far. The Vanguard Morningstar Growth ETF (VUG -1.13%) is a good choice. It charges an expense ratio of 0.03%. I suggest using half of your available cash to buy shares in this investment vehicle.

This ETF tracks the performance of the Morningstar (MORN +1.50%) U.S. Large Cap Growth Index. It contains 135 different stocks in businesses that have increased revenue and profits at above-average rates in the past.

The performance is notable: In the last decade, the Vanguard Morningstar Growth ETF has generated a total return of 399% (as of Nov. 16). The S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.44%) had a total return of 308% by comparison. No one knows if the superior performance will continue, but you ignore historical trends at your own peril.

The technology sector represents 69% of this Vanguard ETF, with consumer discretionary (13% weighting) a distant second. This shouldn't exactly come as a surprise. Many of the top growth stocks operate in tech-related industries.

The risk is that markets are changing rapidly, challenging established competitive advantages. On the other hand, the advantage is that there is significant potential in the long run.

By owning this ETF, investors will instantly build exposure to the artificial intelligence (AI) revolution. Apple (AAPL +0.14%), Nvidia (NVDA -3.81%), and Microsoft (MSFT -0.90%), which combine for 37% of the Vanguard Morningstar Growth ETF, are the three largest positions. While the degrees vary, they are all positioned to benefit from the secular AI trend, an area of the market that is hard to avoid if you're craving growth.

For the income part of the equation, consider the Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF (SCHD +0.98%)for the other half of your cash position. This ETF, which carries an expense ratio of 0.06%, is an attractive option for those seeking income.

The Schwab ETF follows the Dow Jones U.S. Dividend 100 Index. Companies are screened based on different fundamental factors, a sign of their financial strength that can be valuable in times of economic stress. And they're required to have paid dividends in 10 straight years.

Over the last decade, the ETF's total return came in at 257%. While you give up some opportunity on the capital appreciation front, you capture a healthy 2.95% trailing-12-month dividend yield. That's almost three times more income than the S&P 500 index will get you. The ETF's payout has also doubled in the last decade.

If you want to explore options outside the public equity market that are more stable, consider a fixed-income ETF. The iShares Trust-iShares 0-3 Month Treasury Bond ETF (SGOV +0.02%), charging an expense ratio of 0.09%, is compelling. It owns short-term Treasury bills. The trailing-one-year dividend yield of 3.63% is much higher than what you'll receive from the Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF. However, there is no growth potential here.

Whether you choose one or both of the Schwab and iShares ETFs for income, pairing your choice with the Vanguard Morningstar Growth ETF creates a well-rounded portfolio. Two objectives will be satisfied simultaneously.
