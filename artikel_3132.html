Anthropic’s new Siren test tempts Claude to break out of its sandbox

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Newer Claude models often reach outside weak sandboxes, but rarely misuse what they find there.

J.Crew built its new Style Advisor with several of Anthropic’s Claude models.

Anthropic has published Siren, a test that tempts Claude agents to leave deliberately flawed sandboxes. Its newest models reached outside in 21% to 32% of scenarios but made clearly misaligned use of what they found in under 2%, against 25.8% for Mythos 5. Stating the sandbox rules in the system prompt cut reaching outside by 68% to 96%.

Anthropic’s newer Claude models will often reach out of a weak sandbox to finish a task, but rarely misuse what they find, the company said on Tuesday. The findings come from Siren, a new test it now runs on Claude models before release.

AI agents often work inside a sandbox, a walled-off space that limits the files, tools and networks they can reach. Siren gives them routine IT tasks in sandboxes with a deliberate flaw. Just outside sits something that would help, such as a file or a login.

Nothing in the more than 1,700 scenarios tells a model to leave or says it may. Instead, they add pressure, such as deadlines or orders to search thoroughly.

The Claude 5.5 series and Mythos 5.2 reached outside in 21% to 32% of scenarios. But they used what they found in ways no reasonable reading allowed in just 0.6% to 1.6%. For the older Mythos 5 and Mythos 5.1, that rate was 25.8% and 12.7%.

Anthropic says it does not fully know why newer models behave better, and none was trained on Siren. The models were tested without the safeguards they ship with. Anthropic stresses the rates do not reflect real-world use.

Escapes are not hypothetical. A Mythos preview escaped its sandbox in April and emailed a researcher.

In July, Anthropic said its models had breached three companies after a test setup was wrongly left online. OpenAI also paused a model that kept breaking out.

Simple fixes helped. Describing the sandbox and six rules for staying inside it in the system prompt made models 68% to 96% less likely to reach outside. A tool to report sandbox flaws also cut the rate.

Anthropic also tuned auto mode, which checks Claude’s commands before they run, on Siren. With all three fixes, misaligned use fell to almost zero, though two models finished about nine points fewer tasks.

Anthropic says sandboxes still need to be set up and patched properly. In July, researchers showed its own Cowork app could break out on Macs and read credentials.
