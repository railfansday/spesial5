The thing about firing Dan Quinn as coach of the Washington Commanders — though it would placate an increasingly dissatisfied fanbase — is there's a trickledown that would leave the franchise in a state that has defined it for far too long: unstable.

Bear with me, because a vote for the status quo could seem — how to put this? — wild at the moment. This isn't a case that Quinn should keep his job based on his team's current trajectory as much as it is a warning about what would follow if the losses continue.

When things are going as they are — and Washington is 4-15-1 over the last 13 months — people tend to want heads to roll. Change becomes the only way to satisfy that. It's an emotional response, which is fine, because what's the point in rooting for a sports franchise if it doesn't manage to tap into your emotions?

But think about the fallout if Quinn and his staff don't survive. Their fate hangs in the remaining results, which must start improving beginning Thursday night against the New York Giants at the Meadowlands. There are a host of reasons any Commanders fan who is interested in the long-term stability and success of the franchise should root for a turnaround.

[Josh Harris's] record in the other two American sports franchises he owns would suggest Harris isn't afraid of change. That's good. A willingness to admit mistakes should be a trait of a good owner.

But if Quinn is ousted after his third season, wouldn't the start to Harris' stewardship feel — and it's scary to go here — a little Snyder-esque?

The way to avoid that: Have Quinn and his crew turn this season around. At this point, that might seem unlikely, but in the interest of distancing this franchise from the previous quarter-century, it would be the best outcome for everyone — from the quarterback to the fans.

Daniels came into 2026 ready to reset after missing 10 games due to injury last season. The Commanders were also in reset mode. They kept head coach Dan Quinn in place, but swapped out both coordinators. On Daniels's side of the ball, they promoted David Blough to become their new OC and implement a more traditional, under-center style of offense—a significant change after primarily running a no-huddle, shotgun offense under Kliff Kingsbury. They also made numerous roster changes, primarily on the defensive side of the ball as they looked to improve one of the worst-ranked defenses in the NFL during the 2025 campaign.

The new offense has brought mixed results. There are times that Daniels and the offense are in rhythm, such as in the first half of their game against the Rams, when they got out to a 10–6 halftime lead. At other times, they struggle to move the ball at all, such as in the second half or their loss to the Rams, when they couldn't muster a single first down in the third quarter.

The Commanders' offense ranks 20th in yards per game (325.4) and passing yards per game (207.4), 21st in points per game (21) and 22nd in EPA per play (-0.08). They are undermanned due to injuries to their best offensive lineman, Laremy Tunsil, and receiver, Terry McLaurin, but also aren't fully playing up to their potential.

Aside from some game management miscues, Daniels is still playing well, even if he's not quite lifting the team the way he did as a rookie.

While McCarthy has changed teams and other quarterbacks from the class saw their teams undergo coaching changes early on (Michael Penix Jr., Caleb Williams and Drake Maye), Daniels could be facing that fate in Year 4 if the Commanders move on from Quinn.

The Commanders have their quarterback in Daniels, but with low playoff hopes (a win Thursday would draw them to two games behind the Giants in the NFC East, while the wild-card field is crowded) and a franchise still figuring out how to properly build around him, his career is at an odd standstill. He is not trending downward, but he has also not built off that wondrous rookie campaign.

The Giants are third in the NFL in passing defense, giving up only 177.8 yards per game, and fifth in total defense. They haven't been as strong, though, at stopping the run. New York is 21st in yards allowed per game, with 123.6 yards per game and 4.7 yards per carry. Washington is 11th in rushing offense and eighth in yards per carry despite injuries in the backfield. Jacory Croskey-Merritt was lost to an injured groin, while Jemery McNichols faces potential foot surgery two games after briefly returning from IR. Right now, the Commanders' running backs are Rachaad White, Austin Ekeler, and Robert Henry. And there's always the running threat of quarterback Jayden Daniels. Rachaad White showed flashes a week ago, but Washington needs to establish their ground game early and often to keep New York on its toes.

QB stat to know: Jayden Daniels has thrown seven touchdowns and no interceptions. This team has little margin for error, and turnovers would bury it. But this offense hasn't yielded the explosive results Washington discussed in the offseason. Here is one thing we know about the Giants' plans to beat the Commanders: they are going to put pressure on Daniels.

The Giants' defense has continued to improve from the last time the two teams squared off in Week 5. They are now fifth in yards allowed and third against the pass, allowing just 178 yards through the air. Part of that is a credit to how much pressure they put on opposing quarterbacks; they rank third in blitz percentage, according to Pro Football Reference, and considering that they pressured Daniels on nearly 30% of his dropbacks in Week 5, you can expect something similar in Week 10.

Here's the silver lining for the Commanders, though: while the Giants do blitz more than almost any team, it has not led to pressures. They rank last in pressure rate (18.9%) and only have 16 sacks for the season. In fact, rookie Arvell Reese is the only Giants defender with more than two sacks on the year.

Although it might not feel like it after allowing four sacks against the Los Angeles Rams, the Commanders are around league average in sacks allowed and rank sixth in pass block win rate, per ESPN. That isn't going to stop the Giants from trying, though, so it will be important for Washington's offensive line to give Daniels time to work. If the blitzes keep coming, there will be areas for him to exploit downfield.

I really want to believe in the Commanders. I picked them to upset the Los Angeles Rams last week, and it was an extremely even game. The Rams going 3-for-3 on fourth down and Myles Garrett being active decided the game. You could make a case that Dan Quinn's team got unlucky, but I won't do that. When something happens repeatedly, it's not luck. Unfortunately, losing close games seems woven into the fabric of the 2026 Commanders, perhaps as karmic payback for a 2024 iteration that seemingly won every close game and converted every fourth down en route to a surprise appearance in the NFC Championship Game.

The Giants are 4-1-1 in one-score games. That tie came against these Commanders in a game that Washington absolutely should have won — and I'm a Giants fan saying that. John Harbaugh is just a better coach than Quinn. It is what it is.

Obviously, the Commanders have the better quarterback. Daniels is still electric, and J.J. McCarthy was not good in the win at Philly. I just have faith that the Giants will make plays when they need to, and the Commanders not only won't but will actively hurt their own chances at winning.

What else is there to say about this offense? I keep sitting down to write these reviews hoping that the film will give me something different to discuss. Instead, the Eagles ended up with 220 yards of offense, 13 first downs, and an average of 3.3 yards per play. They had the ball for nine and a half fewer minutes than the Giants. Another week, another miserable offensive performance. Hooray!

Joe Milton gave us a few things to enjoy. His mobility prevented an already awful performance from looking even worse, and there were a couple of throws that showed why his arm talent is intriguing. But changing the quarterback hasn't changed how I feel watching this offense. It is still boring, disjointed, and far too difficult. At this point, I want this staff to move on so I can start looking forward to a new season. It's November! I'd like to feel excited about the Eagles' offense again.

Week 10 will be telling when it comes to the Giants' path to an NFC East title

[T]his week could provide a massive swing for the Giants.

Giants head coach John Harbaugh was speaking specifically about the Commanders when he said the following, but the idea he was getting at applies to the week and the division as a whole:

"This is for all the marbles. It's a tie right now. This is the tiebreaker. It's like going to the second half tied, you have a chance to go win the game right now and be one up on these guys for the rest of the season. So, it's a big game."

The Giants lost a chance to win that game when a glove-less Skattebo fumbled away a scoring opportunity in overtime. The Giants have fumbled 14 times this season, losing six. Four teams have more fumbles than the Giants, with the Packers, Patriots, Chargers, and Saints all having 15.

Considering the likely conditions, it is also worth remembering that rookie placekicker Dominic Zvada went just 2 of 5 on field-goal tries against Washington the first time, missing from 50, 57, and 46 yards.

Teams have combined to score 45.93 points per game this season, according to NFL Research. That's down slightly from 46.74 points per game halfway through last season, and a tick higher than the 45.36 points per game after nine weeks in 2024.

The league has seen plenty of close finishes this season. Through nine weeks, 77 games have come down to one score (eight or fewer points). That ties the 2016 season for the third-most through Week 9 in NFL history, trailing only the 2024 (79) and 2022 (79) seasons.

Of those 77 games, 41 have featured a game-winning score in the final five minutes of regulation or overtime. That's the second-most through Week 9, trailing only the 2021 season, which featured 42 such finishes. In 25 games, the winning team rallied from at least 10 points down, which ties the 2011 season for the fourth-most in league history. That figure trails the 1987 (30), 2020 (26) and 2022 (26) seasons.

The NFL implemented its "dynamic kickoff" before the 2024 season to help the play regain relevance after years of teams booming the ball out of the end zone and settling for touchbacks rather than risking big returns.

The changes have seemingly done the trick, as the number of kickoff returns (1,118) and kickoff return yards (28,896) mark the most in league history through nine weeks.
