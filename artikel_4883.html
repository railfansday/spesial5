Former Robinhood Engineer Pleads Guilty to Crypto Insider Trading Scheme

Former Robinhood software engineer Huaisong “Jerry” Xiang pleaded guilty Monday to wire fraud for using confidential plans for upcoming cryptocurrency listings to trade on Hyperliquid. The 30-year-old agreed to forfeit $125,523 in profits from the scheme, according to the Justice Department.

Xiang entered his plea before U.S. District Judge Colleen McMahon on November 16, two months after prosecutors accused him and another Robinhood engineer of trading ahead of the brokerage's token announcements. He pleaded guilty to one count of wire fraud; the DOJ announcement did not report a guilty plea to the commodities-fraud charge initially brought against him.

As a software engineer working on new listings for Robinhood Crypto, Xiang had access to a private Slack channel reserved for employees classified as “Coin Aware Individuals.” It contained information about which tokens were coming to the platform and when, according to the FBI complaint filed in September.

Robinhood's internal policy prohibited that group from trading around upcoming listings on any platform, not just Robinhood. The restriction began before a listing was announced and continued for 24 hours after it became public. A November 2024 email warned Xiang to “refrain from abusing access to or knowledge of this information,” the complaint says.

Investigators described one trade involving POPCAT, a Solana-based meme coin. After receiving internal messages about a planned March 13, 2025 listing, Xiang moved approximately 18 Ethereum, then worth about $34,000, into a Hyperliquid wallet and opened a bullish position tied to POPCAT, according to the complaint.

He closed that position at a profit after the token became available on Robinhood but before the company published its listing announcement, the FBI alleged. The complaint identified at least 10 other occasions through February 2026 when the same wallet traded ahead of announcements, including positions involving MEW, MOODENG, ONDO and RENDER.

Hyperliquid's perpetual futures let traders speculate on a cryptocurrency's price without owning the token itself. Unlike conventional futures, the contracts have no expiration date, and a “long” position gains value if the underlying token's price rises.

New listings can increase a token's exposure to buyers and put upward pressure on its price. Prosecutors said Xiang used his advance knowledge to position himself for those moves, sometimes closing his trades before the public knew Robinhood had added the asset.

“No matter the trading platform, corporate insiders will be held accountable when they trade based on misappropriated information,” U.S. Attorney Jamie McDonald said in Monday's announcement.

Robinhood said in September that it had investigated the matter, reported it to authorities and maintained a zero-tolerance policy on insider trading.

Xiang faces a statutory maximum of 20 years in prison, although the judge will decide his sentence. He is scheduled to appear before McMahon for sentencing on February 25, 2027.
