How Does a 74-Year-Old in Argentina Escape House Arrest on the Fifth Floor?

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

It involved a heating pad, animal fat and a bomb scare. The police are still looking for an Italian man facing extradition on 1970s-era terrorism charges.

By Zane Irwin and Lucía Cholakian Herrera

Zane Irwin reported from New York. Lucia Cholakian Herrerra reported from Buenos Aires.

Time was running out for Leonardo Bertulazzi. After decades of safe harbor in Argentina, Mr. Bertulazzi, a former member of a far-left militant group that kidnapped and murdered Italy’s prime minister in 1978, faced imminent extradition.

Back in Italy, a 27-year prison sentence for terrorism-related crimes awaited Mr. Bertulazzi, according to Argentina’s defense ministry.

Mr. Bertulazzi, 74, was placed under house arrest. The police were stationed across the street from the fifth-floor apartment he shared with his wife in Buenos Aires, Argentina’s capital. He’d had three strokes in the past two years, his lawyer said, and he was required to wear an ankle monitor.

None of that seemed to stop Mr. Bertulazzi from planning his escape.

On Tuesday, while much of the law enforcement apparatus of Buenos Aires was focused on the visit by Pope Leo, two police officers checked on Mr. Bertulazzi during a routine shift change, according to an official with knowledge of an investigation into Mr. Bertulazzi’s flight who was not authorized to speak publicly.

But this time, when the officers rang the apartment’s doorbell, nobody answered. Investigators believe Mr. Bertulazzi had escaped between 6 a.m. and 8 a.m. that morning.

Mr. Bertulazzi’s ankle monitor still showed he was at the apartment, the official said. A search revealed a metal box holding a mess of wires plugged into an outlet, prompting the officers to call an explosives disposal unit, according to the official.
