Accor’s Largest Shareholder Moves to Oust CEO After Ennismore Buyout

Parvus, an activist investor, has criticized Accor's Ennismore buyout and 13 years of alleged stock price underperformance under CEO Sébastien Bazin. Accor's management has its own side to the story.

Accor’s largest shareholder, Parvus Asset Management, said Thursday it will ask shareholders to remove Sébastien Bazin as chairman and CEO at the hotel group’s next annual meeting, citing Accor’s €1.6 billion (about $1.8 billion) buyout of its partners in its Ennismore joint venture and years of lagging returns.

Parvus, which holds about 14.1% of Accor’s share capital, worth about €1.6 billion, laid out its case in a letter published Thursday. Accor’s annual meetings are typically held in May, but the next date hasn’t been set.

Accor didn’t respond to Skift’s request for comment.

Parvus argues Accor has long underperformed its peers. “Accor shares have returned 99% from December 2013 to November 2026, against 593% for IHG, 863% for Hilton, and 950% for Marriott,” it wrote. “In our view, this sustained gap reflects an unfocused strategy, inconsistent capital allocation, and inadequate governance oversight.”

Parvus Asset Management, Accor's largest shareholder with about 14.1% of share capital, said Thursday it will ask shareholders to remove Sébastien Bazin as chairman and CEO at the group's next annual meeting, typically held in May. In a published letter, Parvus cited Accor's €1.6 billion ($1.8 billion) buyout of its minority partners in the Ennismore lifestyle joint venture and a long stretch of lagging returns, noting Accor shares returned 99% from December 2013 to November 2026 against 593% for IHG, 863% for Hilton, and 950% for Marriott. Bazin countered on Accor's analyst call that the €3.7 billion equity value for Ennismore came from a formula written into the 2022 shareholders' agreement, not from negotiation this week, and that the 2022 sale proceeds funded a buyback and special dividend that amounted to "basically a 2x return" for shareholders. Accor will buy the 44.3% minority stake in three tranches through January 2028, funded in rough thirds from non-core asset sales, new debt, and cash or shares. Bazin said he struggles to see Accor holding 100% of Ennismore over the medium to long term and that an IPO is still possible. Accor didn't respond to Skift's request for comment.
