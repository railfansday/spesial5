Air France Economy Class: Impressions From A Flight Across The Atlantic

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

In the spirit of trying out new ideas and topics here at OMAAT, and having learned that many of you are interested in reading about economy, here are some thoughts about how well Air France does the job.

I’ll focus on the daytime return flight from Dublin (DUB) back to Rio (GIG) via Paris (CDG), as I slept through as much of the outbound overnight as I could. This is not a full review — rather, it’s some general comments on the seats, food, and tech, in Air France’s economy offering.

After completing this trip, I’ve flown back and forth between Brazil and France in all four of Air France’s cabin classes. Yes, including La Première – I do fly in the pointy end sometimes. 😉

Having transited through Paris Charles de Gaulle so many times now, I’m actually starting to learn my way around the crazy maze of terminals.

On this occasion, the transfer was on the simpler side, as both flights used gates in Terminal 2E. That said, I still had to catch the light rail transit from Hall K to Hall L and pass through a full security screening before entering the departures area. Not all airports in Europe require this, and I hope it’s taken into account when CDG simplifies its terminal numbering, which is expected in March 2027.

Thanks to my Flying Blue Gold status, I had access to Air France’s Hall L Lounge, which is currently undergoing some refurbishments.

Getting a post- or pre-flight facial would have been amazing, but, like my five previous visits to Air France’s lounges in Paris, I’ve had no luck at getting a slot.

Either there were simply no slots left, or, as on this visit, the remaining slot(s) were after my departure time.

Speaking of lounges, I took this photo from Air France’s lounge in Terminal 2E, Hall L. According to a staffer I spoke with, the construction outside will eventually be a huge new flagship Air France lounge for Terminal 2E.

I sent this photo to Ben before I boarded, and he was surprised to see that the board specifically referenced pre-boarding for Flying Blue Ultimate members. In case you didn’t know either, now you do.

Here’s one final pic for the AvGeeks: even though Air France’s version of the A380 wasn’t particularly loved, the elevated jetbridge above the Boeing 777-300ER in this pic was a nostalgic reminder of the airline’s former superjumbos.

If you’re locking in for 10+ hour flight in economy, there’s only a limited level of comfort that any airline would be able to offer. I wasn’t envious at all walking through the premium economy cabin during boarding…

Thanks to my Flying Blue Gold status, I was able to choose any seat in the cabin from 72 hours before departure. Contrary to my avoidance of bulkhead/exit row seats and their fixed armrests on night flights, I picked bulkhead window seat 21L in the front row of the economy cabin.

The legroom here was more than I needed for this day flight. My plan was to spend most of the flight working, so this seat also avoided anyone reclining into my laptop.

Of Air France’s three Airbus A350-900 configurations, this was the newest 292-seater version that has been rolling out since 2023. The economy cabin has 212 seats in a 3-3-3 layout, which I know isn’t usually a favorite of families of four. Non-bulkhead/exit row seats have a pitch of around 31 inches.

I didn’t move the four-way adjustable headrest at all during the flight, and the recline is listed as being around 118 degrees.

No matter where I’m sitting, I can’t help but marvel at that wingtip.

Of the 29 flights I’ve taken with Air France, I can only remember one meal that wasn’t good. In fact, it was terrible, and was actually when I was flying premium economy from Paris to Zanzibar (ZNZ) in 2024.

Of the two meal choices, I went for the chicken pasta (I didn’t note down the second option as I only decided to write about this after the flight).

I thoroughly enjoyed the main dish. It had lots of protein, the sauce was flavorful, and the little mini pasta pieces weren’t dry at all. Given the several accompaniments, I consider this a generous helping of food. The Président camembert cheese was a nice premium touch, and the bread roll was quite the opposite of stale. I can’t tell you how good the mini apple tart was, as I’m intolerant to apples.

I wish there had been a menu so I could know for sure what was in this really tasty salad. I’m pretty sure it was a mix of mainly carrots, potato, and barley, in what I guess was a honey mustard sauce.

For those who don’t know, Air France differentiates itself by serving Champagne in economy. Partial though I can be, I stuck to Coke Zero for the caffeine.

I like the intention of using wood instead of plastic for cutlery; I just wish the knife could actually cut!

Despite the hefty lunch serving, I got very hungry midway through the flight as I didn’t eat a proper breakfast. I went to raid the snack baskets at the rear-most galley. The area very busy with lots of passengers lingering, and there was some kerfuffle over a medical issue, so I didn’t take any pictures.

I did, however, get a snap of my snack haul. A mini ice cream, a couple of chocolate truffles, a packet of savory biscuits, and half a cheese sandwich. While the snacks might have lacked in nutrition, it’s nice to know that you won’t go hungry on Air France’s economy flights.

I was so tired after the 3:30 a.m. alarm that I skipped the pre-landing light meal on the return flight in favor of a nap.

Neither did I touch the box containing a very beige selection of a bread roll and a pastry on the flight to Paris from Rio. Craving caffeine after a patchy night of sleep, I attempted the drip-style coffee, but a sip was enough to turn my nose up. Yes, I am a coffee snob, and I’m proud of it.

Air France made a great choice to mount the IFE screens on the bulkhead. Only the screen in the aisle seat in my row of three was in that annoying underseat position that needs to be folded away for takeoff and landing.

I could just about reach the very tactile screen when leaning forward. Otherwise, there was a mousepad-style remote.

The 13.3-inch screens are among the largest in the industry, and are the same size as those in the premium economy cabin on this plane.

I counted four charging outlets: a USB-C and a USB-A port in the IFE screen, and, under the seat, a regular power outlet and a second USB-C port.

As for Wi-Fi connectivity, there were no disruptions to the complimentary Starlink for the duration of the flight. However, the whole IFE system had to be reset shortly after we took off. Then, a few hours into the flight, the vast majority of the movies disappeared from the system, including the one I was halfway through. I was able to connect my AirPods to the screen with no issue.

Other amenities, so to speak, were a rather coarse pillow, a blanket, and over-the-head headphones.

One of my favorite things about flying and traveling is meeting new people, including cabin crew, though due to the busyness of economy cabins, that can be tough.

If I have time, I also try to bring a sweet treat on board for the crew to enjoy during the flight, no matter which cabin I’m flying in, as it is usually a great way to build rapport. It worked on this flight, as several crew members came to thank me for the mince pies. Mattieu, to whom I handed them during boarding, brought me a little note to say thanks for both the pies and my Flying Blue loyalty.

Over the course of the flight, I noticed crew in the economy cabin engaging enthusiastically with passengers. At the start of interactions during meal service, I even heard crew asking passengers whether their preferred language was English or French. On at least three occasions, I noticed crew walking through the cabin offering water.

After being spoiled with lots of lie-flat beds over the years, I’ll admit the pre-flight excitement isn’t quite the same when boarding a long-haul flight in economy. This return trip in Air France economy was perfectly fine, with the pros certainly outweighing the cons.

Whether you only fly business class, or a frequent economy class flier, I hope you gained some insights into the Air France economy experience.
