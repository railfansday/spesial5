Dell vs. Nvidia: Chip Stocks Just Suffered Their Worst Sell-Off Since 2020. Which Is the Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Dell's cash flow and NVIDIA's margins tell very different stories, and a sizable valuation gap may matter more than either name suggests.

The artificial intelligence (AI) boom has fueled extraordinary growth across the technology sector, but a recent sell-off has Wall Street questioning whether massive infrastructure investments can continue to justify elevated valuations. For investors looking toward 2027, the pullback presents an opportunity to reassess two major beneficiaries of the AI boom: Dell Technologies(DELL +3.67%) and Nvidia (NVDA +2.77%).

Dell provides the servers and infrastructure needed to support AI workloads, while Nvidia designs the advanced chips powering them. Both have benefited from surging demand, but their business models, profitability, and valuations differ substantially. As investors become more selective about AI exposure, those differences could determine which stock offers the more compelling long-term opportunity.

Dell provides servers, storage systems, and personal computers to enterprises, governments, and cloud providers. Its Infrastructure Solutions Group has become an increasingly important driver of growth as demand for AI-optimized servers accelerates.

In fiscal 2026, Dell generated $113.5 billion in revenue, an increase of approximately 19% year over year. Net income reached $5.9 billion, representing a profit margin of 5.2%. More recent results highlight the momentum behind its AI business. In the second quarter of fiscal 2027, revenue surged 58% to $47 billion, with AI-optimized server revenue doubling to $16.4 billion. Dell also reported a $95 billion AI server backlog, providing considerable visibility into future demand, although orders remain subject to customer spending decisions.

At the end of fiscal 2026, its debt-to-equity ratio was negative due to a shareholder equity deficit, while its current ratio stood near 0.9x. Nevertheless, the company generated substantial operating cash flow, providing resources to fund operations and return capital to shareholders.

NVIDIA is a leader in semiconductor stocks, supplying the GPUs, networking equipment, and software infrastructure powering much of the industry's expansion.

In fiscal 2026, Nvidia generated $215.9 billion in revenue, an increase of approximately 65% year over year. Net income reached $120.1 billion, representing an exceptional profit margin of 55.6%. More recent results highlight continued momentum. In the second quarter of fiscal 2027, revenue surged 106% to $96.2 billion, driven by a 117% increase in data center revenue to $89 billion.

Nvidia also maintains an exceptionally strong financial position. At the end of fiscal 2026, its debt-to-equity ratio stood near 0.1x, while its current ratio was approximately 3.9x. The company generated roughly $96.6 billion in free cash flow during the year, providing substantial financial flexibility to invest in new technologies.

Dell faces intense competition from rivals like Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE +1.38%), particularly in the rapidly expanding AI server market. Its relatively thin profit margins leave less room for rising costs or pricing pressure, while reliance on a concentrated group of large AI customers creates additional risk if infrastructure spending slows.

Nvidia faces a different set of challenges. Its dominant position in AI computing is increasingly being challenged by AMD (AMD +11.04%) and custom chips developed by major cloud providers. Heavy reliance on a relatively small number of customers exposes the company to changes in their capital spending plans, while dependence on Asian manufacturers introduces geopolitical and supply chain risks.

Both companies remain vulnerable to a slowdown in AI infrastructure investment. However, Dell faces greater margin and balance-sheet pressures, while Nvidia must defend its market leadership and justify the substantial growth expectations surrounding its business.

Dell appears more attractively priced for value-conscious investors based on its lower Forward P/E and P/S ratio than its rival.

The recent sell-off across AI and technology stocks has raised legitimate questions about the sustainability of massive infrastructure spending. However, the latest earnings from Dell and Nvidia suggest that demand remains strong, even as investors grow increasingly cautious about valuations and future growth expectations.From a valuation standpoint, Dell appears significantly cheaper. It trades at 14.2 times forward earnings and 1.5 times sales, compared with Nvidia at 21.7 times forward earnings and 16.1 times sales. Dell's lower valuation could offer substantial upside if AI infrastructure spending remains elevated, although its relatively thin profit margins and balance-sheet constraints introduce additional risks.Nvidia's premium valuation, however, reflects a fundamentally different business. Its dominant position in AI computing, exceptional profitability, and substantial free cash flow provide financial strength that Dell cannot match. With a net profit margin exceeding 55%, Nvidia captures considerably more value from the AI boom, while its software ecosystem and technological leadership provide competitive advantages that could support continued growth.For investors looking toward 2027, Nvidia is the better buy. The recent correction has made valuations increasingly important, but Nvidia's combination of earnings growth, financial strength, and market leadership offers a more compelling investment case.
