IYH and XBI: Two Distinct Paths to Investing in Healthcare

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

XBI delivered stronger 1-year returns at 45%, but IYH's diversified portfolio weathered volatility far better, with a maximum drawdown of just 17.9% versus 53.2%.

iShares U.S. Healthcare ETF (IYH -0.19%) provides broad-based exposure across pharmaceuticals and medical equipment, whereas the State Street SPDR S&P Biotech ETF (XBI +2.57%) focuses narrowly on the more volatile biotechnology segment.

Investors choosing between these two funds are deciding between a total-sector approach and a sub-industry tilt. While both reside in the healthcare space, their risk profiles and underlying drivers differ significantly. One offers the stability of established cash flows, while the other tracks an equal-weighted index of companies often in the clinical trial phase.

The iShares fund is slightly more expensive, with a 0.37% expense ratio compared to 0.35% for the SPDR fund. However, IYH offers a higher yield of 1.1%, representing a 0.75 percentage-point advantage over XBI. For long-term investors, this higher payout could provide a meaningful cushion during periods of sectorwide volatility, even though the annual management fees are nominally higher for the iShares product.

iShares U.S. Healthcare ETF holds 100 stocks and spans multiple sub-sectors, including pharmaceuticals at 37%, biotechnology at 24%, and healthcare equipment at 14%. Its largest positions include Eli Lilly (LLY -0.79%) at 14.70%, Johnson & Johnson (JNJ +1.43%) at 9.84%, and Abbvie (ABBV +1.06%) at 7.64%. It was launched in 2000. iShares U.S. Healthcare ETF has paid $0.80 per share over the trailing 12 months, which, on its recent ~$72.14 share price, works out to a 1.1% yield.

State Street SPDR S&P Biotech ETF is more concentrated, with 165 holdings, primarily in the healthcare sector (99%). Its largest positions include Moderna (MRNA +5.27%) at 2.20%, Twist Bioscience (TWST +0.15%) at 1.84%, and Natera (NTRA -14.59%) at 1.79%. It was launched in 2006. State Street SPDR S&P Biotech ETF has paid $0.54 per share over the trailing 12 months, which, on its recent ~$153.77 share price, works out to a 0.4% yield.

When comparing the iShares U.S. Healthcare ETF (IYH) and the State Street SPDR S&P Biotech ETF (XBI), investors should consider several key factors. Let's see what they tell us about each fund.

First, we should examine each fund's strategy. IYH spans most of the healthcare sector, including pharmaceutical and medical device stocks, as well as health insurers. It holds around 100 stocks, and it is heavily concentrated, with its top 10 holdings accounting for about 58% of its total allocation. XBI focuses solely on the biotech sector. It holds about 160 stocks and has a relatively low top 10 concentration given its equal-weighting methodology.

Two other important factors are historical performance and income potential. Since 2021, IYH has generated a total return of 43%, equating to a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 7.4%. XBI, on the other hand, has delivered a total return of 14%, with a 2.6% CAGR. As for income, IYH has an edge. It has a dividend yield of 1.1%, while XBI's is only 0.4%.

One final factor to weigh is cost. Both funds have similar expense ratios. IYH's annual expense ratio is 0.37%, while XBI's is modestly lower at 0.35%.In summary, IYH and XBI are both healthcare ETFs, but they are quite different funds. IYH will be favored by those investors seeking broad healthcare exposure. IYH also has a leg up on XBI given its better historical performance and higher dividend yield. That said, XBI may still appeal to those investors seeking targeted exposure to the biotech sector alone.
