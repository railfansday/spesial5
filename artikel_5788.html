Storms disrupt Thanksgiving travel. And, FCC violated ABC's free speech, judge says

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Severe storms sweeping across the nation threaten to cause widespread travel delays today, as millions of people gather with their loved ones. New York City officials announced that the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade has been rescheduled to Saturday due to the threat of powerful wind gusts that could reach up to 60 mph. This year marked the 100th procession, and the first time the iconic event is being postponed.

The Trump administration has proposed new tariffs targeting goods from 14 major U.S. trading partners, including China, Japan and the European Union. Administration officials argue the measures counter unfair foreign trade practices, though critics question their ultimate effectiveness. Proposed rates may reach 40% on Chinese imports, 35% on Vietnamese goods, and 20-25% on products from other targeted nations. Additional tariffs on Brazilian sugar and aircraft are also being suggested.

Federal scientists are testing the air, soil and water in Eastern Iowa for lead contamination after the U.S. Army detonated thousands of pounds of unstable lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown yesterday. Officials said the controlled explosion was the only safe way to dispose of the bomb-making compound.

A federal judge has blocked Trump from challenging the ABC television network's broadcast licenses. U.S. District Court Judge Loren AliKhan ruled that the president retaliating against satire and commentary he dislikes is unconstitutional. AliKhan issued the preliminary injunction yesterday and dismissed the administration's request to throw out ABC and Disney's lawsuit challenging the review of the local license. Licenses typically last eight years and are rarely revoked. AliKhan's decision drew heavily on Trump's public statements and social media posts against ABC and Disney, as well as comments from Brendan Carr, the president's pick for chair of the Federal Communications Commission.

Holiday shopping season is in full swing. Before you spend, consider if you actually need what's in your cart. Sales are exciting because our brains are wired to seek newness. Retailers capitalize on that urge. To escape the cycle of constantly wanting more, shop intentionally and appreciate what you already own, says Eric Athas, author of Saying No to New. He shares his insights on breaking this cycle and finding joy in your current possessions with Life Kit.

Apple farmers across the Pacific Northwest are ripping out thousands of acres of orchards and grinding trees into mulch because growing the fruit no longer pays. Many say they're being crushed by soaring labor and equipment costs and international tariffs. Even multigenerational growers are leaving land unused rather than hemorrhaging money. Thousands of acres of fruit have been left to rot unpicked on branches, pushing some well-established farms into bankruptcy. Federal farm relief has also largely failed to reach specialty-crop growers. Take a look at photos and videos of affected farms, and read more about how the region that supplies the majority of the U.S.' fresh apples is handling the challenge.
