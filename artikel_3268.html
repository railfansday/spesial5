10 Hacks Every 'GTA 6' Player Should Know

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

I've been playing Grand Theft Auto VI since launch, and I'm having a blast so far. The world feels even more detailed and immersive than previous GTA games, which means there's a lot to learn beyond the basic tutorials and prompts the game shares at the beginning. These are the 10 GTA 6 hacks I used to improve the game's quality of life. If you do the same, you'll spend more time immersed in the world, and less time fighting the game's default settings.

I'm not going to share any spoilers here, but you should know that the side quests in Chapter 1 are important. If you ignore all the optional quests or small asks by other characters, you won't be able to return to those later. These are small things like fixing up the house for Lucia, meditating with her, and buying her a necklace when she asks you to. All of these have an impact on your progress. Take your time in this chapter, and remember to save before starting the mission called "Recalibration." That mission leads to Tourist Trap, which is the point of no return for Chapter 1.

If you're used to playing racing games, you'll find the driving experience in GTA VI to be quite different. It's hard to keep an eye on minimap directions while you're also getting used to vehicles in the game, and I used voice navigation to ensure that I don't keep getting lost, particularly for time-sensitive missions. You can enable this by going to GTA VI settings > Audio > GPS Voice > Voice. If you're playing the game on PS5, your DualSense controller's speaker plays GPS voice prompts. This is great as those alerts can be hard to hear over all other in-game sounds from your TV's speaker.

Using GTA 6's default controls, making Jason and Lucia run is quite annoying. The game makes you hold L3 to run, which hurts my thumbs. If you alter movement speed in settings, you can make the characters run just by using the left stick. You can walk by easing off the left stick, or switch to a fast walk, jog, or run when you push the stick all the way. Tweak this by going to GTA VI settings > Gameplay > Movement Speed > Jog. This lets you move faster without draining your stamina. You can also change from Jog to Run if you want the fastest possible movement speed with the left stick. Having said that, my pro tip is to go to Control Scheme in settings, and switch to Classic. This lets you run by holding the X button, as opposed to L3 with default controls.

Haptic strength is one area where GTA 6 takes the immersion a bit too far, as it's dialed up to the maximum level by default. This makes the controller's vibration strength a bit too intense for my liking, and also has a negative impact on battery life. You can fix that by reducing haptic strength a little. It's under GTA VI settings > Controller > Vibration & Haptics Strength.

Many modern controllers develop "stick drift," an issue where small movements of the analog sticks register as large inputs. This causes a big problem in games that require precise inputs, such as GTA 6. To counter this potential problem, the game allows you to adjust the deadzone for each analog stick in your controller. That said, this setting can be used to your advantage, even if you don't have stick drift issues in your controller. I recommend dialing up the deadzone to 7 to improve the driving experience. I find most cars in GTA 6 to be a bit floaty to drive, and adjusting the deadzone fixes this issue to a large extent.

Committing a heist attracts heat in GTA 6, and there are many different ways to escape the attention of in-game cops. The usual tricks include stocking up on outfits to change into, parking your vehicle a block or two away, and being so stealthy that you leave no witnesses to your crimes. However, an underrated trick involves using the pickup truck you get at the start of the game. Get a bike in the game, and stash it in your pickup truck. Then, use the truck to drive to the crime scene, and hide the truck a short distance away. Unload the bike, and use it to get to the crime scene. Once the heist is complete, ditch the bike, and run to your truck. Witnesses won't have seen you using the truck, which allows you to escape the area quickly.

If you are using a car as a getaway vehicle, you can use two tricks to make a smooth escape. First, double-tap the Triangle button to exit your vehicle without switching off its engine. When you return to the vehicle in a hurry, you can escape a lot faster. If you have quick fingers, you can double-tap Triangle first, and just as your character is about to exit the vehicle, press L3 (or X, if you use classic controls). This leaves the car's door open, and saves you a little more time when you're fleeing.

I suffer from chronic migraines, and I am sensitive to bright lights. Fortunately, GTA 6 has a bunch of accessibility settings that help me play the game for longer. Even if you don't face the same issues, these settings will help reduce eye strain in longer play sessions. Go to GTA VI settings > Display > Flashbang, and switch to Dark. This stops flashbangs from showing a bright white light when they go off. On the same settings page, you can tweak Player Status Effects Intensity to reduce screen effects when your character is low on health or stamina. You can also enable Brightness Reduction to reduce the intensity of bright lights in the game, and try Focus Trail Colorblind Mode if you find it hard to distinguish between different colors.

I haven't been able to play first-person shooters for decades because the camera movement makes me nauseous and gives me a headache. Of late, I've noticed that it's getting harder to play third-person games too. My first two hours in GTA 6 had me quite worried because I was feeling quite sick, and I had to turn off my PS5. Luckily for me, I found a few tweaks to mitigate this problem. You can do that in GTA VI settings > UI > Persistent Center Dot. This permanently shows a white dot in the center of the screen, and it reduced my motion sickness enough to help me play the game. You can also go to GTA VI settings > Camera, reduce Camera Bob, and turn off Camera Shake, and Weapon Sway. I also recommend going to the Controller tab in settings, and tweaking all the options under Aim & Look Sensitivity until you find a comfortable level.

GTA 6 includes realistic ringing sounds that linger after explosions in the game. This may be fine for some people, but it can negatively affect some players' gaming sessions. Those who suffer from tinnitus or chronic migraines may not enjoy those ringing sounds, and, luckily, the game allows you to disable them. Open GTA 6's in-game settings, and enable the feature from Audio > Tinnitus Reduction.
