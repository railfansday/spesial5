Marvel Officially Confirms Another A-List Hero Struggles With Alcohol Addiction

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

This article discusses alcoholism and problematic drinking.

Marvel has given one of its most recognizable Guardians of the Galaxy a surprisingly revealing character moment. In the most recent issue of The Amazing Venom #3, Rocket Raccoon openly acknowledges being drunk during a dangerous situation. His explanation for why he is still managing to function, however, makes the exchange considerably more concerning than an ordinary joke.

Rocket describes himself as “high-functioning” when Venom questions his drinking, suggesting that alcohol consumption is something he has learned to accommodate rather than confront. Although Marvel does not explicitly diagnose Rocket with alcohol use disorder, the admission places him alongside Tony Stark and Carol Danvers, two prominent heroes whose comic book histories have explored alcoholism.

The moment appears in The Amazing Venom #3, written by Jordan Morris, with art by Luke Ross and colors by David Curiel. The issue follows Fred Myers, formerly Boomerang, and the symbiote Passenger as the unlikely duo Comeback travel toward Klyntar. Their journey brings them to Knowhere, where they stop to refuel before continuing through space.

While there, Fred and Passenger cross paths with Spider-Man, who is bonded with the Venom symbiote. The group visits a local bar, where Rocket is among the patrons. The establishment draws a strange crowd whose members worship a deity and embrace an unruly lifestyle. Fred and Passenger encounter the gathering while looking for fuel, giving the stop an oddball atmosphere.

That changes when violence breaks out and the bar begins to collapse. Venom and Rocket move to get people out, and Venom points out that Rocket is drunk too. Rocket does not deny it or claim he has only had a drink; he answers that he is “high-functioning.” The exchange is brief, but its meaning is hard to overlook.

Rocket’s wording frames his drinking as something he believes he can manage while continuing to act, fight, and make decisions. That is the implication behind the label, because a person can keep up appearances or meet some responsibilities while alcohol use is still causing harm. The phrase is conversational, however, not a clinical diagnosis or a reliable measure of safety.

The comic does not explain how often Rocket drinks, whether he has tried to stop, or what consequences alcohol has had for him outside this scene. It therefore leaves important questions unanswered. What it does establish is that Rocket is intoxicated during an emergency and appears comfortable treating that condition as compatible with doing his job.

That contradiction gives the gag a sharper edge. Rocket’s confidence does not make him sober, and being able to function in one dangerous moment does not mean alcohol is harmless or under control. The scene also avoids turning the admission into a lengthy speech, allowing a short exchange to reveal a potentially serious pattern without resolving it.

Marvel has explored alcohol addiction through Tony Stark, whose struggle became important to the celebrated “Demon in a Bottle” storyline in Iron Man #120–128, published from 1978 to 1979. The arc showed drinking undermining Stark’s judgment and responsibilities, and the character’s later stories revisited recovery and relapse. His wealth, intelligence, and armor could not simply erase the problem.

Carol Danvers also confronted alcoholism during her time as Warbird with the Avengers. Her drinking escalated as she struggled with personal trauma and diminished powers, damaging her relationships with teammates. In Iron Man vol. 3 #25, published in 2000, Carol acknowledges her addiction at an Alcoholics Anonymous meeting with Tony’s support, placing recovery at the center of her story.

Rocket’s moment is much less developed than either of those arcs, and readers should not mistake one line for confirmation of a full addiction storyline. Even so, the comparison is meaningful, because Marvel is showing another powerful hero treating drunkenness as ordinary and manageable. Whether future issues explore the consequences or leave it as dark humor remains to be seen.
