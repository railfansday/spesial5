10 Shows Like 'MobLand' You Should Watch Next

This is Streamalikes, where you can find a curated list of recommendations to stream based on what you already like.

Crime dramas often attract big stars, and MobLand is no exception. The Paramount+ series stars Tom Hardy as mob fixer Harry Da Souza, with Pierce Brosnan and Paddy Considine in supporting roles and Helen Mirren having a ball as the thoroughly evil matriarch of the Harrigan crime family.

While families are complicated in even the best of circumstances, that's especially true of the Family. After you've watched the recently released second season of MobLand, try one of these other shows mixing crime and family (or Family) drama.

Theo James plays army officer Eddie Horniman (a name mentioned as often as possible), heir to the Horniman estate (there it is again) who, upon the death of his father, is named the Duke of Halstead. He learns that his dad was tied to various criminal enterprises, and that his scouse brother is millions of pounds in debt to a drug dealer. What else is the dapper, military-trained Duke to do but learn to navigate the violent underworld while looking cool? You won't be surprised to learn that this British crime drama comes from creator/writer/producer Guy Ritchie, who also has a hand in MobLand. Stream The Gentlemen on Netflix.

Another series from Taylor Sheridan (the Yellowstone guy), this pandemic-era hit stars Jeremy Renner as Mike McLusky, head of a family that’s been keeping the peace, through means fair and foul, in the title's company town for decades. The "business" of the corrupt burg happens to be incarceration, and the McLuskys thrive when business is up, even if Mike has slightly different ideas about how to run things. The show deals broadly with systemic racism and inequality in the prison system, but mostly it's a modern-day western about bringing justice to a corrupt town. It will conclude with a fifth season, streaming in 2027. Stream Mayor of Kingstown on Paramount+.

A consistently compelling combination of smart plotting and lurid sex and violence, Banshee stars Antony Starr as Sheriff Lucas Hood...kinda: He's actually a man recently released from prison who takes over that identity when the new sheriff in the small town of Banshee is killed before anyone really has a chance to get to know him. It's all part of a complicated plan to reunite with his lover Ana (Ivana Miličević), and to get back the $15 million in diamonds he lost when he went to jail—but the town is ruled over by various criminal factions, and being sheriff doesn't guarantee any kind of safety; rather, the opposite. Stream Banshee on HBO Max.

MobLand creator Ronan Bennett previously made this acclaimed series that succeeds by maintaining a narrow scope—in this case, a single London council estate. Dushane (Ashley Walters) and Sully (Kane Robinson) are residents and drug dealers at Summerhouse, maintaining an increasingly precarious grip on the criminal element in the community. Most compellingly, the series maintains an extensive supporting cast of locals both in the drug trade and impacted by it, building a disturbingly compelling world. A little confusingly, the first couple of seasons were renamed Top Boy: Summerhouse when the show moved to Netflix, so you'll want to start there before moving on to plain ol' Top Boy. Stream Top Boy: Summerhouse on Netflix.

Based loosely on a nonfiction work investigating the Mafia-like Camorra in Campania, this show focuses on the head of the Savastano clan, Pietro Savastano (Fortunato Cerlino), and his ambitious lieutenant Ciro Di Marzio (Marco D'Amore). When Pietro is arrested at the series' outset, a civil war erupts between family members and among the factions vying for control. Gone are the suits and poker games of many a mob drama—this is a brutal, street-level take on organized crime that forsakes the typical trappings of the genre. Stream Gomorrah on HBO Max.

I'm here for the Pierce Brosnan of it all, but there's also a very strong vibe match in this series that casts the one-time James Bond actor as the head of a literal crime family, even if this one's set in the American West. Adapting Philipp Meyer's 2013 Pulitzer Prize-nominated novel of the same name, the series follows Eli McCullough, a ruthless cattle baron making moves to get in on the burgeoning oil industry in the Rio Grande Valley of 1915. A parallel narrative sees Eli as a young man, kidnapped and raised among a Nʉmʉnʉʉ family. While his backstory lends the character welcome complexity, in the present, he's as determined to build his empire as he is to prepare his son and grandson to take over when he's gone. Stream The Son on Prime Video.

Tom Hardy playing a tough guy isn't terribly unexpected, but here the setting is London, just after the end of the War of 1812. Hardy plays James Delaney, who returns from Africa after ten years away (he says he'd been taken prisoner, but his story doesn't entirely add up) for the funeral of his father. Dad left nothing but a little hunk of land off of Vancouver, not worth much until the war's conclusion creates new maritime trade opportunities. Suddenly, the United States, Great Britain, and the East India Company all have eyes on the real estate, and all are willing to do whatever it takes to acquire it—and the stakes become clear once James realizes that his father's cause of death wasn't natural. Stream Taboo on Peacock and Tubi.

Best not ponder too much on the fact that this one's based on a video game, as its pedigree is only hinted at via the levels of action and violence (certainly compared to some of the other shows on this list). Colm Meaney plays Finn Wallace, the absolutely indispensable leader of London's criminal underworld, a man who's been holding everything together for decades, until his shocking murder at the outset of the series (that's not a spoiler, and we spend more time with him via flashbacks). The completely random killing by someone who had no idea what he was doing begets chaos and open violence. Finn's son Sean (Joe Cole) has been raised to take his father's place, but local, Pakistani, and Albanian gangs all move in to take control from the Finn family in the power vacuum. Stream Gangs of London on AMC+.

The great Forest Whitaker stars as the real-life Bumpy Johnson, who returned from a prison sentence to find his neighborhood falling apart and in the grip of Italian mobsters. A more than capable crime boss in his own right, Johnson resolves to take on the Genovese crime family, eventually forming a tentative alliance with Malcolm X (Nigél Thatch and, later, Jason Alan Carvell). Johnson is the lesser evil as far as Malcolm and others are concerned, and a complicated figure as both crime lord and family man with aspirations of saving his neighborhood. The show will conclude with a two-hour finale, likely airing in 2027. Stream Godfather of Harlem on Hulu.

Adopted into an Italian organized crime family as a baby, Park Joo-hyung took on the name Vincenzo Cassano, eventually becoming a mafia consigliere. After his adoptive father Don Fabio dies, Fabio’s biological son comes gunning for his brother, whom he now sees as competition. Cassano flees to Seoul, on the hunt for a secret stash of money, and in the process discovers a love interest and a new adversary in a giant business conglomerate that definitely deserves to be taken down. An incredibly popular K-drama, this one definitely puts a unique spin on the tropes of the mob genre. Stream Vincenzo on Netflix.
