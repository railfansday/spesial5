After a stellar six-week run in domestic theaters, A24's latest word-of-mouth hit of the year has officially debuted on digital, taking the number-one spot on the domestic iTunes chart. The movie comes on the heels of The Drama, the psychological dark comedy film starring Zendaya and Robert Pattinson, which rode a wave of positive buzz and grossed more than $130 million worldwide. During the summer, A24 released the horror sensation Backrooms, which emerged as its biggest-ever hit with a $400 million global box office haul. The latest in this streak of A24 hits is an even more unlikely success story than a tonally ambiguous tale about one of America's greatest ills and a lo-fi horror movie about inner demons.

It's a three-hour documentary that toys with the genre in ways that have clearly struck a chord with audiences. The film's existence was kept a secret for the duration of its production. It was revealed to the world at this year's Telluride Film Festival, after which it was given a limited release in eight locations nationwide. By this time, the movie had built intense buzz, thanks to positive reviews, an intriguing marketing campaign, and some old-fashioned supply and demand.

In its second week, the movie expanded to more than 2,650 theaters nationwide. The count rose to more than 3,250 theatersin week three. In these three weeks, the film grossed a stupendous $65 million — an almost unheard-of gross for a documentary feature​​​​​​. Subsequently, the film's theater count began to decrease, as is the market norm. Now in its sixth week, the film is playing in roughly 1,200 locations nationwide.

We're talking, of course, about You Can See Everything, the three-hour epic in which Nathan Fielder embeds himself alongside disgraced tech innovator Elizabeth Holmes as she prepares to be incarcerated for fraud. Fielder, best known for the Comedy Central series Nathan for You and the HBO series The Rehearsal, co-directed You Can See Everything with Lance Oppenheim, who broke out this year with the A24 psychological thriller Primetime.

You Can See Everything will begin its international rollout this week, after having debuted on the domestic PVOD market on November 24. The movie has so far grossed $93 million at the domestic box office, but since it's still playing in hundreds of theaters nationwide, it has a solid chance of becoming only the second documentary feature to surpass the $100 million domestic milestone, after Fahrenheit 9/11​​​​​​. You Can See Everything will no doubt be aided in this mission by awards season success; it's a candidate for a Best Picture nod at the Oscars, armed with a "Certified Fresh" 97% score on Rotten Tomatoes.
