If You Loved 'Yellowjackets,' There's One Movie You Need to Watch Next

Yellowjackets is a vibe. The story of a high school girls’ soccer team that crashes into the wilderness and spends years fending off madness, starvation, and despair (and not always doing a very good job of any of those things) is equal parts funny and terrifying. The dual timelines build tension around the story’s many mysteries, and the complex characters (no one’s completely good or evil on this show) make for a lot of surprising plot swerves that feel grounded despite the supernatural implications all around them. If you’re looking for more of that specific flavor of horror, you should probably watch Alive.

Based on the true story of a rugby team whose plane crashed in the Andes Mountains in 1972, Alive will satisfy anyone whose favorite part of Yellowjackets is the core premise: Survivors of a disaster are stranded in the wilderness without supplies, and have to figure out how—and, er, who—they’re going to eat.

The survivors are initially as calm and resourceful as someone could be under those circumstances. They use the wrecked plane as shelter in the withering cold, they scavenge for anything that could be useful, and they ration out the only food they have: Some chocolates and a case of wine. But an early mistake leaves them without any food at all, and slowly they come to the sickening realization that their best chance at survival is to treat the corpses of their friends and families scattered around the crash site as a food source.

There’s no supernatural aspect, but the story has a similar tone of desperation and despair as Yellowjackets. These are people determined to survive in an impossible situation, and they do things that no one outside of their shared experience could possibly understand. The real-life origins of the story add a sense of horror that more than matches the fictional “It” that haunts the girls of Yellowjackets; putting yourself into that very real experience makes it clear just how terrified everyone was. Rent Alive on Prime Video.

If you’re craving more cannibals, cults, and crazies at the mercy of nature itself, here are a few more movies you should check out.

If you want to watch a movie that is essentially an adaptation of Yellowjackets, check out Eden. Even though it was released years before the show, the stories are so similar (albeit largely gender-flipped) that the producers of Eden actually sued, claiming plagiarism (they lost). In the film, a men’s soccer team (and two women on the same plane) crash-land on a deserted island, and as they fight for survival, things descend into, yes, madness and cannibalism as the survivors suffer dwindling food and water supplies and the rise of two violently opposed factions. It’s not nearly as deeply imagined as Yellowjackets, and it doesn’t have the supernatural hints of the show, but if you want a truly similar story, it’s a good choice. Stream Eden on The Roku Channel, Hoopla, or Tubi, or rent it on Prime Video.

You obviously cannot discuss Yellowjackets without discussing Lord of the Flies, William Golding’s 1954 novel about a group of schoolboys who descend into brutality and savagery when they’re stranded on a deserted island. The book has been adapted many times, but the 1963 version directed by Peter Brook is still the best version. Brook managed to elicit remarkably naturalistic performances from his young actors, giving the story a realistic tone that is somehow more terrifying than any amount of horror vibes. Stream Lord of the Flies on Kanopy or rent it on Prime Video.

A slower and more haunting story, Picnic at Hanging Rock explores some of the same themes as Yellowjackets in an entirely different way. On Valentine’s Day in the year 1900, several students and teachers from an all-girls private school in Victoria, Australia, head to Hanging Rock for a picnic. Four students head off to explore and fall inexplicably asleep, and then three vanish, along with one of the teachers. Dreamy and drenched in dread, the story never offers any easy answers, and it explores themes of invading natural areas where you’re not wanted, burgeoning (and confusing) adolescent sexuality, and how our fear of the unknowable warps our perspective and often diminishes our humanity. Stream Picnic at Hanging Rock on HBO Max or rent it on Prime Video.

You can never really go wrong with a Hitchcock film, and if you’re looking for another story about a disparate group of people forced to make terrifying decisions in order to survive, watch Lifeboat (based on a story by John Steinbeck). One of the director’s most underrated films, it’s the story of a group of survivors in a lifeboat after a German U-boat sinks their ship during World War II (including one survivor from the German boat). It’s a masterful suspense engine that’s not afraid to have its characters discuss the moral and ethical quandaries facing them as they struggle to survive and maintain their humanity under a constant threat of death. Stream Lifeboat on Hoopla or rent it on Prime Video.

If you love the supernatural aspects of Yellowjackets as the girls come to believe a force they call “It” is out in the woods, check out The Ritual. Four friends embark on a hike in the wilds of northern Sweden to honor their deceased friend. When one of them is injured, they leave the marked trail to take a shortcut through the forest—where they are soon forced to shelter in an abandoned cabin, just like the Yellowjackets—and things begin to slide into the terrifying when they encounter a cult worshipping an ancient deity that grants them immortality in exchange for human sacrifices. It’s as if the show was condensed to an hour-and-a-half and the terror quotient was turned way, way up. Stream The Ritual on Netflix.
