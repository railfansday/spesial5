MSCI Is Cutting Strategy and Metaplanet From Its Indexes. Here's What It Means for Bitcoin Treasury Company Stocks.

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

MSCI is booting Strategy from its indexes at the end of November. What does that mean for Bitcoin treasury stocks?

By Anders Bylund – Nov 21, 2026 at 2:17PM EST

MSCI (MSCI -2.22%) made it official on Nov. 11. Strategy (MSTR -9.67%), the software company turned Bitcoin (BTC +1.74%) treasury, will be deleted from the MSCI USA Index at the close of Nov. 30. Japan-based Bitcoin treasury peer Metaplanet (MTPLF -10.78%) is also on its way out, and Ethereum (ETH +3.75%) treasury company SharpLink (SBET -9.13%) lands on a new public watch list.

The decision caps a fight that has run for more than a year. In October 2025, the market index manager proposed excluding any company whose digital-asset holdings made up 50% or more of total assets. More than 250 organizations objected, and MSCI dropped the idea in January. In August, it came back with a broader screen aimed at "non-operating companies," built on five financial-ratio flags. Trip four of them, and you're out.

I went back to Strategy's formal response, and wow. The letter calls the proposal "pretextual," suggesting that MSCI may be inventing reasons to exclude Strategy. The letter points out that this company alone makes up about 87% of the market value affected across all six names on the list of exclusions. Strategy insists that its Bitcoin business is an operating segment of an established software company, a treatment it says followed discussions with SEC staff.

Fair points, some of them. When a supposedly neutral rule lands that heavily on one name, it's tough to call it a coincidence. It's like announcing a new dress code that happens to ban exactly one guest's unusual hat.

Then again, the same 10-page letter says the exclusion would have "no meaningful impact" on Strategy's business. If it really doesn't matter, that's a lot of footnotes for a shrug.

Strategy held a special investor call on Nov. 19, eight days after the announcement. Executive Chairman Michael Saylor and CEO Phong Le spent the hour on preferred stock, credit markets, and Bitcoin-per-share metrics. The index removal never came up.

Instead, the focus was the Strategy Stretch (STRC -1.01%) preferred stock. Le said Strategy has bought back about $3 billion of Stretch at $95 to $99 per share, after selling it on the open market at $100 per share. Saylor estimated that organic demand for the 12% instrument has shrunk from about $10.5 billion six months ago to roughly $7 billion today. The buybacks are meant to find that floor.

But no analysts asked questions on that call, and management never uttered the word "MSCI" on this seemingly convenient stage. The silence spoke volumes.

Strategy's homepage is now essentially a Bitcoin cockpit, full of data on MSTR stock and the company's Bitcoin holdings. That's a strange look for a company that still sells analytics software, but the numbers are useful:

That last one is the real gut punch. The market values Strategy at about 63% of its Bitcoin holdings. Even after subtracting $19.4 billion of debt and preferred stock, the stock trades at a slight discount to what's left.

The premium shimmer that let Strategy print stock and buy coins hand over fist has faded. Saylor himself says the stock-for-Bitcoin trade needs roughly a 1.29x multiple to make sense. There's a canyon between the existing mNAV and the required one.

Index funds tracking MSCI benchmarks will have to sell their Strategy and Metaplanet shares by the end of November. Some of that selling was likely priced in after the August proposal, but forced selling is never handled with velvet gloves.

The bigger issue is precedent. Other index providers, such as the S&P Dow Jones Indices group, have not adopted MSCI's screen. Strategy could still qualify for the S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%) index someday (if it meets many other requirements). But now there's a template that the others could follow. Any company that builds its balance sheet around a single digital asset has to consider that the passive-investing bid may not be a given.

Metaplanet goes out the door with Strategy on Nov. 30, and SharpLink is standing in the hallway. The copycats that built their businesses on Saylor's playbook are learning that the operating plan didn't come with an index guarantee.

Strategy isn't in trouble tomorrow. It has $4.7 billion in U.S. dollar reserves, which covers about 3.4 years of interest and dividends. That's a meaningful cash cushion.

But a cushion isn't a catalyst. Without the index bid, and trading at a discount to its own Bitcoin reserves, the old flywheel has stalled. The interesting question isn't whether Strategy survives. It's what it takes to bring back the lost mNAV premium.
