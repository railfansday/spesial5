Tested: The V-12 Mercedes-Maybach S680 Is Too Much, Which Is Just Right

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

The Maybach is a throwback rich with modern comforts, and for those with the dough, it’s a deal.

Some things are meant for showing off: engagement rings, watches, fancy manicures, face tattoos. If nobody notices, what was the point of spending all that money? Other purchases are more subtle, like a well-cut jacket or a youthful neck. They make you look rich, but nobody can quite pinpoint why. The 2027 Mercedes-Maybach S680 is the first kind. There’s no modesty to the Maybach, but as a symbol of prosperity, it won’t be ignored.

Maybach models are for buyers who think the S-class is nice but could make do with more. More bespoke badging, more unique colorways—and more cylinders, at least for lucky shoppers in Asia, the Middle East, and here in the United States. We get the option of a twin-turbocharged 6.0-liter V-12, a gem rarer than a diamond in these mini-mill times. Maybach also offers the S580 with a twin-turbo 4.0-liter V-8, which is far more sensible, quicker, and more fuel efficient . . . but nobody should shop a Maybach with any of those descriptors in mind.

The Maybach is nearly 18 feet of two-tone paint and illuminated callouts. The front grille is a waterfall of chrome flanked by lacy insets of repeated Maybach logos. The LED headlights are three-pointed stars surrounded by rose gold. Even the Maybach badges on the C-pillars glow to ensure that at no time, day or night, will anyone fail to notice what you’re driving.

And people do notice. At a gas station stop, a man in a Honda called over from the opposite pump, “Hey, that’s some hard work right there.” If I had purchased the car myself, I would have felt proud to have my effort recognized. As it was, I admitted to stolen valor, but I still drove off floating on air. That wasn’t from the compliment, though; the Maybach uses a new air-spring suspension that can anticipate bumps and potholes using cloud-based data from other Mercedes cars on the same road for real-time predictive damping.

With its long, 133.7-inch wheelbase and outrageously kitted-out back seat (we’ll get to that), it’s obvious that the S680 was designed with rear passengers in mind, but it’s so fun to drive that I’m tempted to take up a chauffeur job. The V-12 is as smooth as a beurre blanc and as meaty as the filet beneath it. It doesn’t move out with the instant response of a V-8 or an electric setup, but such haste is unseemly. Instead, it steps out with authority and builds up steam like a locomotive pulling a private railcar. The only stumble it makes is when the stop-start system kicks the V-12 back into action. Unlike with the V-8’s 48-volt assist, a noticeable shudder accompanies the return of the 12-pot’s combustion. Thankfully, it’s a problem easily solved by deactivating the system with a single push of a button. It’s not like you’re getting good fuel economy with this monster anyway.

The S680 is a thirsty machine, with an EPA combined fuel-economy estimate of 14 mpg. Our test car was a mossy silver and white two-tone with a sage leather interior, and that’s the only thing green about it. At least you’ll get lots of compliments at every single gas station.

The steering and its optional rear-axle assist makes the long car turn in a relatively small circle, and the brakes (15.4-inch ventilated and cross-drilled rotors in the front, 14.2-inch ventilated units in the back) do a heroic job of stopping all 5336 pounds. At the test track—where technical editor Dan Edmunds would not let me ride in the back during testing despite my assertion that it would be more accurate to the car’s use case—the Maybach accelerated to 60 mph in 4.5 seconds. At the world’s fanciest drag strip, it would cross the quarter-mile mark in 12.6 seconds at 118 mph. Edmunds brought it back down to zero from 70 mph in 177 feet without a hint of brake fade. Most impressive, the S680 whipped up 0.90 g of grip around the skidpad. Not bad for something my neighbor Shirley described as “bigger and nicer than my whole house.”

The Maybach is not actually bigger than Shirley’s house, but she doesn’t have a leather-covered massaging recliner. The S680 has four. Our test car was equipped with the $6000 Executive Rear Seat Package Plus, among many other extras, and be forewarned that if you invite neighbors, gym buddies, or your hair stylist to check out the leather-covered footrests, Maybach-branded pillows, yacht-striped wooden console, folding lunch trays, and back-seat wine cooler with champagne-flute-specific cup holders, they might be unwilling to vacate the premises. I thought I might have to give the car back with a full crew inside.

The Maybach is excessive. It’s not about providing a quiet space or restrained elegance. Every surface that isn’t covered in leather or wood is an interactive screen. The Maybach gets Benz’s theater-wide Superscreen with a 12.3-inch driver display that merges with a 14.4-inch center screen and a 12.3-inch passenger display. Everything glows, or is gold, or is both golden and glowing. In some of Mercedes’s other cars, the Superscreen feels tacky, like a Times Square billboard, but among the lights and quilted details of the Maybach, it’s just one more sparkly surface.

The screen is the main control center for all the electronic luxuries, which Maybach is equally generous about providing. Driver-assistance technologies include all the usual crash-avoidance systems and multi-view cameras, plus parking assist with wheel rim protection (something you’ll want if, like our car, your example has $1300 worth of 21-inch wheels). While the assists can feel overbearing, like a too-attentive waiter hovering over your table with the crumb scraper, the cameras and sensors make parking and maneuvering this massive car much less stressful.

At $291,600 as tested, the V-12 Maybach is not going to be on everyone’s shopping list, but in the elite scene of ultra-high-end taxicabs, it’s a bargain compared to offerings from marques like Rolls-Royce or Bentley. As a bold statement, the Maybach announces your arrival loud and clear, from all 12 cylinders.
