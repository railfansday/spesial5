Palworld Dev Gives Employees an Official GTA6 Vacation Week

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Daniel is a lifelong gamer with a love for various genres and gameplay styles who enjoys exploring games and playing from various angles.

We are officially… let me check my calendar here… one week out from the release of Grand Theft Auto 6. The hatches are being battened down, evacuations are underway, toilet paper is sold out across the world. We’re looking at a Category 5-hype video game release here, and everyone waits with bated breath for the moment to arrive.

For about as long as the game’s official release date has been known, prospective players have been planning their time off, whether they get it formally approved by their employers, or they just plan to spontaneously develop a virulent cold and call in sick. Offices, schools, and just about every other public place will be virtually empty as the world screeches to a halt on November 19.

Grand Theft Auto 6 is releasing exactly one day before the official nomination cutoff date for the 2026 Game Awards.

With all that in mind, if everyone is going to take time off or sick days anyway, the sporting thing to do would be to just make it official. This is the avenue that the developer of popular creature-collect and crafting survival game Palworld, Pocketpair, is taking with its employees; as the company announced in a tweet on the official Palworld X, formerly Twitter account, everyone working for it will be getting five days off from work for the release of Grand Theft Auto 6.

As the tweet on the Palworld account reads, “All Pocketpair employees have been granted special leave to attend to an urgent five-day business trip in Vice City when GTA6 launches.”

This was followed by an image showing a statement on what I’m assuming is official Pocketpair company stationary, which reads, “on November 19, millions of gamers around the world will find themselves sitting at school or work, gazing longingly out the window, knowing that a new era of gaming is about to begin.”

“At Pocketpair, we believe that such a historic occasion deserves our full attention.”

All Pocketpair employees have been granted special leave to attend to an urgent five-day business trip in Vice City when GTA6 launches.

The note then announces that all employees who intend to play Grand Theft Auto 6 will be granted a special five-day holiday running from Thursday, November 19 to Monday, November 23, which also provides enough time to fly to Miami for the game's big release festival. The note adds that only those who intend to actually play GTA6 are eligible for this time off, though considering the nature of this break, I kind of doubt that’ll actually be enforced.

The note also adds, presumably for the sake of end users, that update work for Palworld and Craftopia is currently on schedule and will be carried out as normal.

Logitech wants employees to reap the personal and professional benefits of gaming.

The note concludes with a well-wish for employees, reading, “we hope everyone enjoys their time away from the office and returns safely from their travels.”

This is a very sporting thing for Pocketpair to do, not to mention a pretty amusing way to go about it. Honestly, though, it would probably behoove many companies in the game industry, and indeed, the entire United States business sector, to follow suit.

When I said before that the world will grind to a halt on November 19, that wasn’t an exaggeration. Last month, an economics professor at Pompeu Fabra University in Barcelona, José García Montalvo, spoke to the International Business Times, providing an estimate of how much money the U.S. economy will lose due to people taking vacation, calling in sick, or otherwise skipping work on GTA6 release day. With a rough estimate of 1.5 million absences, based on an average annual income of $70,000, Montalvo ballparks that this single day will cost the U.S. economy roughly $1 billion in lost productivity.

There’s even a precedent for this within the greater GTA legacy, as when Grand Theft Auto 5 released in 2013, approximately 6% of all working adults aged 18 to 35 in major metropolitan U.S. cities took the day off.

Grand Theft Auto 6 is set to be one of the biggest drops in gaming history, and Take-Two is looking to bag some seriously stacked cash.
