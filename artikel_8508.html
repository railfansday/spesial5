Short on Rent – Offer Your Landlord a Plan Before It’s Due, Then Call 211

If you’re short on rent this month, call your landlord before it’s due and offer part of it now with a firm date for the rest, then get the deal in writing. The same day, call 211 or visit 211.org to reach local emergency rental assistance and charities that can cover the gap.

A week before rent is due, you’re offering your landlord a plan. A week after, you’re explaining a missed payment that may already carry a late fee.

An eviction costs the landlord too, in court fees and in rent that goes unpaid while the case runs. Offering part of the rent now and the rest by a set date gives your landlord a cheaper way to get paid than going to court.

Say your $1,000 rent is due on the first and you have $400. Call on the 25th and offer to pay the $400 on the first and the other $600 by the 20th. Ask whether your landlord will waive the late fee as long as you keep to the plan, then send a text or email that restates the amounts and dates so you both have the deal in writing.

Then call 211 to find the $600. The federal rental assistance created during the pandemic ended in September 2025, so the money now comes from state and local programs, charities such as the Salvation Army and Catholic Charities, churches, and one-time emergency grants. A 211 specialist can tell you which of these serve your area. If you’re behind on other bills too, ask about emergency help with those bills on the same call.

Skip payday loans and cash advance apps. With both, you borrow against your next paycheck, which only moves the shortfall to next month’s rent. A common payday fee of $15 per $100 borrowed works out to an annual rate of almost 400% on a two-week loan, according to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau.

Apply for help the same day you make the deal with your landlord. Rental assistance can take several weeks to arrive after you apply, and the rest of your rent is due on the date you promised.
