Nvidia Shares Sink to 52-Week Low After Report SEC Is Probing Accounting

Shares of the AI chipmaker fell about 13% to $127.16 in afternoon trading after touching a fresh 52-week low of $126.64.

The drop followed a Reuters report that SEC enforcement staff are examining potential accounting issues at the company. Nvidia’s market value slipped to about $3.05 trillion.

The Wall Street Journal reported Tuesday that the SEC was investigating Nvidia, including whether its disclosures misled investors. Reuters, citing two people briefed on the matter, said the inquiry centers on potential accounting issues, though it couldn’t confirm when the probe began or exactly what the agency is examining.

The SEC declined to comment, and Nvidia didn’t immediately respond to Reuters. Investigations aren’t evidence of wrongdoing, and many close without enforcement action.

Renewed attention has fallen on a change Nvidia made last year to how it reports revenue by geography. It switched from customers’ billing addresses to their headquarters, which removed Singapore from its regional breakdown.

The Financial Times reported this week that Nvidia made the switch as Singapore sales climbed to nearly half its global revenue. Reuters didn’t say whether the change is part of the SEC’s inquiry.

Investors have also grown wary of circular AI financing deals, in which chipmakers help customers fund chip purchases and data center construction. On Tuesday’s earnings call, interim CEO Colette Kress said Nvidia would be far more selective about such financing going forward.

Jensen Huang resigned as CEO last week following reports of extramarital relationships, including one with Alice Huang.

Alice Huang previously led Singapore-based Megaspeed, a major Nvidia customer, and U.S. authorities suspect her of being a Chinese spy; China has denied she is a spy.

U.S. authorities have been investigating Megaspeed since 2024 over possible export control violations tied to alleged shipments of Nvidia chips to China, according to the Justice and Commerce departments.

Former CEO Huang said Tuesday he would be honored to return if the board believed it would benefit shareholders and employees. Hours later, the board shut that down.

“The Nvidia board does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO,” board chair Harvey Jones said on the earnings call, adding that the company had hired executive search firm Heidrick & Struggles to find a permanent replacement.

The crisis has erased more than a third of Nvidia’s market value, even after the company beat expectations Tuesday with $115.8 billion in quarterly revenue, up 103% from a year earlier.
