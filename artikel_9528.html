8 Graza Olive Oil Alternatives Worth Buying

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

We may receive a commission on purchases made from links.

Walk into the closest grocery store near you, ask an employee where that cool fun olive oil is, and they'll take you to a (metaphorically) glowing row of funky green squeeze bottles. These are, of course, Graza bottles. It's the sensational olive oil brand that rode to fame on the slick, green waves of cute packaging and a quality product. Graza's oil comes primarily from Jaén, Spain. The company commits to a single origin and single varietal mission, so the oil in your newly-acquired bottle comes from a single place and a single type of olive.

You'll undoubtedly notice that Graza has some acolytes waiting on the shelves next to its empire of squeeze bottles. Whether they existed before or after Graza debuted on the scene, each of these unique oils takes after Graza in some way; some are also from Andalusia, while others sport a youthful aesthetic. If you're interested in what's beyond Graza olive oil, we think these alternatives are totally worth trying out.

Hopping onto the green squeeze-bottle trend is O Olive Oil and Vinegar's organic California Extra Virgin Olive Oil. Though do note that the olive oil also comes in glass bottle form, so there's a version for everyone's packaging preferences. Olive oil-heads will enjoy the fruity notes in this oil that evoke Graza's Sizzle product. Squeeze it into a hot pan for a delicately sweet cooking oil, or combine with any of O's extensive line of vinegars in a dressing or marinade so good you'll want to drink it.

These bottles proudly wear that tantalizing "first cold pressed" label that tingles our wallets, which is the first signal that you're looking at a quality olive oil. Though all extra virgin olive oils are cold pressed and kept below 80 F, first cold pressed means that the olives were only crushed (or processed) once, making for a richer oil with more nutrients. Besides the label, O California Extra Virgin Olive Oil has a really pleasant flavor that'll be a talented supporting player in your culinary ensemble.

A Greek olive oil whose name screams professional, The Chef's Way oil by Iliada is in a squeeze bottle to provide convenient flavor whenever you need it. The Chef's Way is single-variety and pulsing with Mediterranean flavor like almonds and artichokes, which breaks up your regularly scheduled olive oil programming with a nicely acidic interlude.

It may be scary for the olive oil purists to be accosted by an aisle of squeeze bottles when you walk into the store nowadays. But one easy, squeezy afternoon of cooking will change their minds completely with the undeniable convenience. The Chef's Way olive oil comes in several different flavors, including Kalamata PDO Extra Virgin and Premium Greek Extra Virgin, so you can choose whichever variation calls to you the most. Put on your chef whites, nonslip shoes, and backwards hat, because The Chef's Way is coming to transform your home kitchen into one of those casually elegant eateries.

Costco lovers, you're going to appreciate this find. The Kirkland-branded olive oil warrants its own inclusion in this article because of its robust flavor and versatility. Like Graza, Kirkland's best olive oil comes from Spain, the country that beats Italy as the top olive oil producer in the world.

The Kirkland Signature Spanish Extra Virgin Olive Oil comes in an absurdly large jug, like many other items at Costco, so you'll get an incredible value for your money. It's the perfect olive oil to use in baking to add a strong, but not abrasive, creaminess. For instance, an olive oil cakes would be the perfect vehicle for the Kirkland Signature Spanish Extra Virgin Olive Oil. Olive oil cake batters tend to eat up a cup or more of oil, which can get really expensive really fast. You can save your fancy olive oil for other pursuits. But don't worry — this olive oil isn't only suited for baking. It can accommodate your shallow frying and vinaigrette needs, as well.

Another Spanish olive oil from Jaén, Oro Bailén has that slick aesthetic appeal of Graza, along with the high-quality taste. While it can be a little pricier than Graza, it offers something Graza does not: A slightly more acidic olive oil for those times when you want a punchier flavor. The brand offers a few different intensities, so you can choose how much punch you want in your olive oil. Along with the punch, you'll find notes of banana that add a ripeness you don't encounter so often with run-of-the-mill olive oils.

Oro Bailén oils comes from Picual olives, harvested very early and milled very quickly to obtain what it instead calls "olive juice," bringing to mind a flavorful and nourishing elixir you'll want to have in your kitchen. Oro Bailén has a forward quality in its flavor: outgoing, fruity, and spicy. Like Graza, which also sells potato chips, Oro Bailén has some intriguing offshoot products, like EVOO pearls and an olive oil jam for when a simple oil isn't enough to put on bread.

Featuring sleek labeling that tells you everything you need to know about the brand, Brightland Extra Virgin Olive Oil belongs to the same cool kids club that Graza is currently the ringleader of. Brightland's product line also closely resembles Graza's, offering several different types of oils for different purposes, including a popular pizza oil (which is different from olive oil). Brightland is the kind of olive oil you'll want to casually leave out on your dinner table to impress your guests.

High in polyphenols, which have healthy antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties, Brightland feels as attractively carefree as it looks. The brand has also branched out into different varieties and flavors of oil, as mentioned above, in a manner more abstract and artsy than Graza. "Lucid," "Rosette," and "Arise" are some of the olive oil options when it comes to Brightland, signifying lemon, garlic, and basil. These options give you the same carefree star power you can feel when putting down a bottle of Graza on your table.

If Martha Stewart tells you to get some olive oil, you can expect two things: It'll be wicked expensive, and likely delicious. Flamingo Estate's olive oil line is perhaps a more luxurious option than Graza's, but it has a peppery bite thanks to the quick processing that will be reminiscent of Graza's "drizzle" finishing oil. Martha Stewart swears by this $50 bottle, the olives of which come from Ojai, California.

This oil is green, sumptuous, and glossy — you'll want to dip a finger into it. Though it is cosmically destined for a hunk of crusty bread, Flamingo Estate's oil also has a high smoke point, so you can use this verdant gold in the oven or on the stovetop. The look of it is one of its best qualities, so a fresh drizzle will be a beautiful finish to your dishes. It isn't unlike Martha, when you think about it: It's vibrant, tasteful, and one of the fanciest things you could come across in your kitchen.

Kosterina is an award-winning Greek olive oil, and right away you can tell: Its shiny white bottle looks like it was plucked right from the Mediterranean. The olives for this brand are harvested early, so the resulting olive oil is a little more assertive than Graza's Sizzle, but it's just as versatile. This is a single varietal olive oil, made from only Koroneiki olives that are nutrient- and oil-rich. Single varietal oils can have a crisper flavor compared to blends, and Kosterina is as refined as they come.

Buying a metal bottle of Kosterina olive oil — metal, as it helps prevent oxidation and harmful chemicals leaching into the oil from plastic — feels intentional in a similar way to getting one from Graza. It feels a little better for the environment, and it even tastes like it's doing you good. Another way that Graza's changed the home-cooking game: No longer can a home kitchen function with one singular olive oil. Now, you need one for cooking and another one for drizzling. Kosterina follows suit with this idea, offering even more choices for your olive oil-purchasing escapades.

A wildly popular olive oil, California Olive Ranch feels a little more homey than some of the other brands on this list. But it's just as good as them: The brand is is backed up by glowing reviews from both customers and publications alike. It offers its olive oil in regular pour bottles, squeeze bottles, oil sachets (for all your emergency olive oil purposes), and even spray cans for your baking pan needs. The grassy, cushiony flavor of this oil makes it one of Giada De Laurentiis' top favorite olive oil brands. In terms of an everyday, affordable olive oil that's still comparable to Graza, California Olive Ranch is where it's at.

With California Olive Ranch, you'll find some top-tier qualities you may be looking for in an olive oil: a balanced taste along with an all-purpose use. Its affordability is just the cherry on top for this great olive oil brand you should try if you want a Graza alternative.
