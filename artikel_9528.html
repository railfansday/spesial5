When Your Body’s Operating System Glitches

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

The autonomic nervous system regulates everything from heart rate to digestion. Abnormalities may contribute to ADHD and POTS symptoms.

You probably don’t give much thought to how your heart speeds up during exercise or how blood continues flowing to your brain when you stand up. These essential functions are managed by the autonomic nervous system, the body’s complex operating system, which runs quietly in the background.

The autonomic nervous system controls heart rate, blood pressure, the digestive process, body temperature, and continuous blood circulation in the presence of gravity — squeezing blood out of the legs and torso and redistributing it to the head and heart when you move from lying down or sitting to standing.

Some studies suggest that autonomic nervous system abnormalities may contribute to ADHD symptoms like inattention and impulsiveness. Clinicians may notice and use these symptoms to diagnose ADHD, but the underlying cause might reflect atypical autonomic nervous system activity.

Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome (POTS) is an atypical or malfunctioning state of the autonomic nervous system. People with ADHD and/or hypermobility appear to have a higher prevalence of POTS than do members of the general population. Symptoms include dizziness or fainting when standing up from a sitting or lying position due to sudden changes in blood pressure and heart rate.

In individuals with hypermobility, autonomic dysregulation may be caused by irritation of the nerves that are intermittently stretched due to lax ligaments at the craniocervical junction, where the brain and upper spine meet.

A tilt table test, which measures your heart rate and blood pressure as you change position, is the main way POTS is diagnosed. Other assessments include blood and urine tests and a measure of the autonomic nerves that control sweating.
