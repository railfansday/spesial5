Binance Says Its $6.3 Billion Hack Began With a Fake Job Interview, but Traders Aren’t Convinced

Binance said on Tuesday, November 3, that the Binance hack started with a fake job interview, in which North Korean hackers gave one of its engineers a coding test laced with malware, but many traders still doubt that is the whole story.

The October 29 theft took $6.34 billion from Binance’s hot wallets, the largest crypto heist on record. Binance’s incident report describes a campaign that ran for months: a tampered download in June, the fake interview in September and an internal AI tool that led the attackers to the engineers who ran its wallets.

Not everyone is buying it. Since the hack, traders on X have called it an inside job. They point to how fast the money left, across ten blockchains in about 20 minutes, and to how often Binance’s account has changed.

BREAKING: Binance says hackers used a fake job interview to infect an employee's computer.The malware let them hijack Binance's internal AI tool, take over more engineers' accounts, and drain $6.34 billion from its hot wallets.

No one has offered proof. The FBI has tied the theft to North Korea’s TraderTraitor group, and blockchain analytics firm TRM Labs agrees with high confidence.

BNB hasn’t recovered either. It trades at $703.33, up 0.7% today but still 7.4% below its $759.85 close the day before the hack.

So what exactly does Binance say happened, and why are traders still unconvinced?

Slowly, through three engineers. Binance co-CEO Richard Teng told CNBC the attackers “spent months building their way in” and rejected the idea that a single laptop was the weak point.

“This wasn’t one mistake or one breach – it was a months-long campaign,” Teng wrote on X.

Binance says users won’t lose anything. It has replaced 98% of the stolen assets with its own funds, and about $2.85 billion has been frozen or intercepted with the help of Tether, Circle and others.

Some of the louder claims have already fallen apart. A viral post blaming a North Korean “honey trap” passed 800,000 views before co-founder Yi He called it untrue and said ZachXBT, the investigator it quoted, never said it.

An employee’s laptop being the way in doesn’t make it an inside job. Fake job offers with booby-trapped coding tests are TraderTraitor’s best-known trick, the same group behind the $1.5 billion Bybit hack in February 2025.

Less than half of it has been stopped. Of the $6.34 billion taken, Binance says $2.85 billion has been frozen or intercepted, much of it stablecoins that Tether and Circle could lock.

The rest is on the move. Blockchain analytics firm Elliptic counts about $3.24 billion still in addresses linked to the attackers, and Chainalysis has found the stolen funds mixed with money from September’s Bitget exploit.

One pile hasn’t moved at all. The 233.46 million XRP taken from Binance, worth about $299 million, has sat in six wallets since November 2, untouched as of Wednesday morning.
