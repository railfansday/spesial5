Ariane 6 successfully launched 36 Amazon Leo satellites after Vulcan loss

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The launch was Amazon's first since a United Launch Alliance Vulcan rocket lost 40 of its satellites on Nov. 15

Arianespace launched 36 satellites for Amazon $AMZN.com's Amazon Leo internet network on Tuesday, sending an Ariane 6 rocket up from the European Space Agency's spaceport in French Guiana, according to Bloomberg.

The flight was Amazon's first since Nov. 15, when a malfunction struck a United Launch Alliance Vulcan rocket 10 minutes after liftoff and cost the company the 40 satellites it was hauling, according to Bloomberg. The Ariane 6 liftoff proceeded without problems, according to Space.com.

Vulcan is grounded while the Federal Aviation Administration leads an investigation, according to Bloomberg. The Nov. 15 mission opened a series of 38 flights that Amazon has reserved on the rocket.

The Ariane 6 launch had been planned for Monday, but a technical issue with the rocket's ground support systems led to a scrub, according to Space.com.

Tuesday's flight was the 10th for Ariane 6 and the fourth for Amazon, according to Bloomberg. Space.com reported earlier Amazon Leo launches on the rocket in February, April and June.

Arianespace holds contracts for 24 Amazon launches after Amazon added six in September, according to Bloomberg. Tuesday's mission brought Arianespace's running count of Amazon spacecraft placed in orbit to 136.

Amazon has not started providing internet coverage, according to Bloomberg. Space.com said about 400 Amazon satellites have reached orbit, against a planned network of more than 3,200.

The Federal Communications Commission granted Amazon a conditional waiver in June from a rule that required the company to get 50% of its 3,232 first-generation satellites into orbit by July 30. Satellites launched after that date lose the spectrum priority status Amazon received in the 2020 and 2021 FCC processing rounds.

That priority can be recovered once Amazon passes the 50% deployment threshold, or automatically in March 2028. The waiver left in place Amazon's obligation to have all 3,232 satellites deployed by July 30, 2029.
