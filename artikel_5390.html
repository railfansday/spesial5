Embraer stock sinks as U.S. proposes 10% tariff on Brazilian aircraft

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The proposal would end the aircraft exemption from the 25% Section 301 tariff on Brazilian goods; comments are due Dec. 30

Embraer stock fell as much as 9.6% in São Paulo trading on Wednesday after U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer proposed an additional 10% tariff on finished aircraft exported from Brazil. That marked the company's deepest drop during a trading session since May, and no other stock on Brazil's main equity gauge fell as far, according to Bloomberg. The stock remains up 14% year to date.

The U.S. Trade Representative's office is considering ending the exemption for finished aircraft in its Section 301 action against Brazil and imposing a 10% ad valorem duty on those imports, the agency said. Finished aircraft are Brazil's fourth-largest export to the United States, according to USTR.

The proposal also covers sugar. USTR is considering higher out-of-quota tariffs on sugar imports from Brazil, applied on a nondiscriminatory basis so that Brazilian exporters cannot backfill the markets of other sugar-producing countries.

"Imposing an additional, specific tariff on sugar imports from Brazil and an additional 10% tariff on finished aircraft exports from Brazil is intended to help bring Brazil to the table," Greer said in a statement. He said more than a year of talks has produced no substantive headway from Brazil, and that the United States remains open to continuing negotiations.

USTR is taking public comment on the proposal. Requests to appear at the hearing are due by Dec. 7, written comments are due by Dec. 30, and a hearing is set for Jan. 14, 2027.

Embraer's E175 regional jet holds roughly 80% of the U.S. market for regional aircraft, according to Bloomberg. The company counts American carriers among its big customers, and the U.S. ranks among its key markets. Brazil's presidency, Embraer and Brazil's trade ministry had not responded to Bloomberg's requests for comment.

The U.S. imposed 25% tariffs on most Brazilian imports in July, with carve-outs for goods including aircraft and aircraft parts. Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva rejected the decision and vowed to pursue countermeasures under Brazil's Reciprocity Law and to take the matter to the World Trade Organization's dispute settlement process.

Since the July action, Brazil has taken legal steps to retaliate against the United States, USTR said.
