Pantera-backed Codex raises $30 million to link Asia to global markets with stablecoins

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Money might make the world go round—but that doesn’t mean getting it around the world is easy. And while stablecoins, or cryptocurrencies pegged to fiat money, can simplify the process considerably, many businesses still lack the infrastructure to use them for cross-border payments. That’s where Codex sees an opportunity.

The Delaware-based startup announced Tuesday it raised $30 million in a Series A round to help businesses send money across borders and exchange currencies, without the need to deposit money in advance to use the service. Codex aims to carve out a niche in Asia and connect the region to markets in Latin America and sub-Saharan Africa.

“We help people convert money into whatever form they want and give it to whoever they want, wherever they want,” Haonan Li, cofounder and CEO of Codex, told Fortune. “Turns out, in lots of parts of the world, this is actually super hard to do.”

The 35-person company plans to use part of the capital to further automate its foreign-exchange platform, with AI agents central to its vision for how the business will operate, and keep costs down as transaction volumes grow.

Pantera Capital led the round, with participation from Dragonfly and Coinbase Ventures, at a $130 million post-money valuation. Both participated in Codex’s earlier seed round, which Dragonfly led alongside backers Circle Ventures, Cumberland, and Wintermute.

Stablecoins have been one of cryptocurrency’s longest-lasting trends, gaining traction as a lifeline in countries with high inflation or capital controls, and as a low-cost payment tool for businesses. Payments made with stablecoins reached at least $401 billion in the first eight months of 2026, up 42% year over year, with businesses on the receiving end of an estimated 58% to 64% of that volume, according to a September report by global consulting firm Alvarez & Marsal.

“[The team] is building Codex to meet the challenge of what happens after this massive explosion of stablecoin issuance, which is… a really fragmented system,” Franklin Bi general partner at Pantera Capital, said.

An economist by training, Li began his career in geopolitical forecasting research for political psychologist and writer Philip Tetlock, followed by a stint in quantitative finance. He later caught the “crypto bug” and became an early employee at Optimism, where he worked on Ethereum scaling and protocol economics.

There, he began to explore crypto’s most practical applications and landed on stablecoins, which some in Ethereum circles dismissed as boring. Li saw a way to help people excluded from traditional financial networks save, transfer, and exchange money.

To build that business, he teamed up with an old friend from his military days. Li had met Victor Yaw, now a Codex cofounder, about a decade earlier in infantry officer candidate school in Singapore’s military. Fellow cofounder Momo Ong, whom Li met through San Francisco’s founder community, initially advised the team before he joined full time. Together, they founded Codex in 2024.

After a few iterations, the company settled on its current product about a year ago. It now has offices in Singapore, Hong Kong and London, as well as New York and San Francisco. As Li put it: “the journey stretched from the jungles of Brunei to the skyscrapers of New York.”
