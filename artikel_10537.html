Kids are experiencing more extreme weather than any generation has before. Sesame Workshop is helping them prepare

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

As weather events increase in frequency and impact, Sesame Workshop is positioning itself as an up-to-date resource for helping children and parents handle an emergency.

For more than a year—through the Texas floods and a historic fire season—Sesame Workshop, the global nonprofit behind the 57-year-old Sesame Street, has been producing videos and guides about preparing for hurricanes, floods, and wildfires that it has run across social media and on its site, with information about packing a “go bag” in case evacuation is necessary. It has also produced physical care packages—go bags with storybooks—that have been distributed to local partners via the American Red Cross and PBS affiliates.

Sesame’s preparedness push culminated in August with Storm on Sesame Street, a half-hour special in which Elmo and friends get ready for a coming weather event.

After premiering on Netflix and PBS Kids, in late September it hit YouTube—where views across the organization’s channels had grown by 130% in 2025 to total 5 billion. The special racked up more than 250,000 views in four days.

The initiative epitomizes Sesame’s mission: “Someone has to give voice to what children are experiencing,” says Rocio Galarza, Sesame Workshop’s vice president of U.S. educational programs, while also making it so “the parent is empowered to help.”
