Booking Holdings’ newest AI agent can now transact to book hotels, flights, and other trip arrangements. But Vayo's ability to complete reservations depends on supplier sites.

Booking Holdings' month-old AI startup Vayo is moving from planning trips to booking them. That includes reservations with travel companies outside its parent's portfolio.

Vayo said Tuesday it can book flights, hotels, and activities through direct supplier connections or by navigating brands' websites. But according to a spokesperson, where suppliers don't permit agent bookings, Vayo hands the checkout back for the traveler to finish.

For hotels, airlines, and rival booking platforms, that could mean travelers purchasing their products through a Booking-owned agent, without necessarily visiting their websites.

Vayo, the AI travel startup Booking Holdings launched October 14, can now book trips as well as plan them. The company said Tuesday it books flights, hotels, and activities either through direct supplier connections or by navigating brands' websites, and hands the checkout back to the traveler where suppliers don't permit agent bookings. Direct API connections cover Booking.com for hotels, rental cars, and activities, OpenTable for restaurants, and SeatGeek for event tickets; flights, trains, buses, ferries, airport transfers, and parking run through supplier websites where permitted. The booking technology is Vayo's own rather than Booking Holdings' existing infrastructure, and the company says it has no exclusive integrations. Every Vayo user gets Booking.com's Genius Level 3 pricing, the highest loyalty tier, with discounts of up to 20% on select stays — preferential pricing that could make its parent's inventory more attractive in some comparisons, though Vayo says it shops wherever the best option is available. Traveler appetite remains the open question: an October Skift Research report found 27% of U.S. travelers surveyed were comfortable using AI to book, and AI accounted for 21% of touchpoints at inspiration but 6% at booking. The features are in private beta, Vayo hasn't disclosed booking volumes or how often it hands a transaction back, and Booking Holdings plans to invest $100 million in the startup contingent on progress and market conditions.
