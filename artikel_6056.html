Why JetBlue Is My Favorite Way To Fly Economy Across The Atlantic

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

I’ve flown back and forth across the Atlantic dozens of times in all cabins, including many times in economy. JetBlue definitely stands out as being my favorite, and dare I say it, the most premium of all the transatlantic economy flights I’ve experienced.

My most recent trip was from Europe to Boston (BOS) for the opening of JetBlue’s newest BlueHouse location. The airline covered my flights for the event, but had no input in this article. I’d like to share some of the reasons that JetBlue’s transatlantic experience impressed me so much.

Economy class meals can seem like quite the downgrade should one be accustomed to the elevated fare at the pointy end of the plane.

JetBlue currently partners with NYC-based Dig Inn for its transatlantic economy-class meals, and they’re far better than average. They also offer products from a range of interesting brands, rather than just your classic soda labels.

Here’s an example of the drinks menu on my flight from Amsterdam to Boston.

Meals are served in these stylish, reusable, matte-black sealed containers. I’d love to see more airlines do more to reduce their waste like this.

I was very happy with my choice of chicken pesto pasta salad as the main course, accompanied by sides of beet and carrot slaw and snap peas with feta. The fresh, flavorful, yet cold dishes were far better than many of the reheated slop I’ve been served in economy before.

I was equally impressed by the breakfast I had on the morning departure back from Boston to London Heathrow (LHR). I went for the Bubly sparkling water again, as I avoid coffee on a plane unless it’s served as espresso. The stuff they pour out of those vats is pretty much just glorified dirty water.

As far as pre-landing light meals go, this pizza thing was pretty good, too.

In addition to the main meal and pre-landing light bite, JetBlue keeps a well-stocked pantry at the back of the Airbus A321LR.

JetBlue has been offering fast, free, in-flight Wi-Fi for as long as I can remember. The same cannot be said for some full-service carriers, which still can’t guarantee a reliable, fast, and free connection on their transatlantic flights.

They’ve also added some fun tech features, like the ability to choose the elements for the main meal service via the IFE screen, even in economy. I think it has a more premium feel than being asked “chicken or beef?”

I’m not a frequent JetBlue flyer, so I couldn’t really make the most of the fun personalization features, such as the option to continue watching the movie where you left off on your last flight.

Being given anything more than the standard pillow and blanket in economy is a rare bonus. It had been so long since my last JetBlue flight in economy that I’d forgotten about the complimentary Snooze Kit.

I prefer to use my AirPods to earplugs, but I did make good use of the eye mask for a mid-flight nap.

I’ve flown over my fair share of narrow bodies on long-haul flights, and I genuinely don’t know why such a fuss is made about them being terrible.

JetBlue’s version of the aircraft has comfy seats, with standard Core (economy) seats having 32 inches of pitch. Another common complaint I hear is that there are fewer toilets. For a plane with far fewer passengers (only 114 in economy), fewer toilets kind of makes sense. I didn’t once find myself waiting in line to use the bathroom on either flight.

For what it’s worth, there are two lavatories for 114 economy passengers, so that’s one lavatory per 57 passengers. That’s a little lower than the industry standard, but not by much. For example, American’s standard 787-9s have five lavatories for 255 economy seats, so that’s a ratio of one lavatory per 51 passengers.

If you’ve only got the budget or the points for economy for your next trip to Europe, I couldn’t recommend JetBlue more. They now fly to eight cities in Europe, the planes are comfortable and modern, and the in-flight food and beverage is top of the game for economy.
