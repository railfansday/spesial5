Record pipelines aren't translating into the net room growth that major hotel groups had forecast, and 2027 isn't looking much better, as a whole.

The largest hotel groups have record development pipelines. But several have been struggling to turn these promised hotels into open projects, while some others have had more rooms leaving their systems than expected.

Marriott, Hyatt, Wyndham, and Accor have trimmed their 2026 net rooms growth forecasts since late July. Hilton held its target, but its third-quarter growth sat near the bottom of its range.Net rooms growth is one of the two main drivers of fee revenue at asset-light hotel companies, along with revenue per available room.

The U.S. hotel industry saw supply grow only 0.4% in the third quarter, year over year, CBRE said. The shortfall reflects construction delays, a slow pace of converting hotels into new brand networks, rising interest rates, and higher-than-planned exits of hotels.

The biggest hotel groups are sitting on record development pipelines, but fewer of those promised rooms are opening on schedule — and more existing rooms are leaving their systems than planned. Marriott, Hyatt, Wyndham, and Accor have all trimmed their 2026 net rooms growth forecasts since late July; Hilton held its 6% to 7% target but needs a surge of fourth-quarter openings after third-quarter growth of 6% missed analyst expectations. The causes differ by company: Marriott cited construction delays in the Middle East, Wyndham pointed to terminations of substandard rooms and a more than 30% drop in economy and midscale hotel transactions that normally trigger conversions, and Accor's CFO said its cut "is all China," where franchisees are closing economy and midscale hotels faster than expected. The backdrop is an industry-wide supply slowdown: U.S. supply grew just 0.4% in the third quarter, CBRE said, and CoStar forecasts 0.6% growth in 2027 against a 1.6% historical average since 1989. Construction economics are a drag — MCR's Tyler Morse said costs are up about 50% in five years, putting a wood-frame Home2 Suites in Nevada at $250,000 a key. IHG is the exception, backing consensus of about 4.8% net system growth in what it called its fifth straight year of acceleration. Hyatt is using AI to draft property improvement plans, cutting turnaround from several weeks to a day, and says it expects a more even growth pace in 2027.
