Charalampos Kostoulas identifies Brighton turning point under Fabian Hurzeler after James Milner trust

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Brighton striker Charalampos Kostoulas believes earning the trust of his teammates has been just as important as his goals this season.

The 19-year-old has made an encouraging start to the 2026-27 campaign, with five goals and three assists in 14 appearances across all competitions as he establishes himself as an increasingly important figure in Fabian Hurzeler's side.

It marks a significant step forward from his first season at the Amex Stadium, when he scored twice in 21 Premier League appearances, making just two starts.

Brighton head coach Hurzeler - who last season questioned Kostoulas’ commitment to training - is now impressed by the progress, particularly the forward's growing physical strength, work rate and understanding of the demands of English football.

But Kostoulas says there has been no dramatic change to his game. Instead, he has gradually learned what the team requires from him and become more comfortable in his role.

"Mainly, it's about understanding what the team wants," he said to Sussex World.

"For me, as a striker, with time and after some months, I started to understand that. With the minutes I've got and things like that, I gained the trust of my teammates and I want to keep going like that."

Hurzeler has previously highlighted the difference in the youngster's ability to compete physically, saying: "When you see him now and how resilient he is in personal duels, it's something completely different."

The Brighton boss has also praised his pressing and attitude towards improvement, describing a player who is becoming increasingly professional.

Kostoulas credits the manager's clarity and attention to detail, while revealing he has enjoyed the greater freedom given to him in the attacking role.

"He's clear with the instructions he gives on the pitch. He's always clear," he said.

"But I think I didn't change the way I was playing. Last season I could also do these types of things on the pitch, but I have more of a free role, especially in my position. I like it."

The transition from Olympiacos to Brighton was not without its challenges, however.

Kostoulas admits the biggest adjustment was the daily routine, with longer hours at the training ground, different training methods and a greater emphasis on meetings and preparation.

"The daily routine, I think, was the hardest," he told Sussex World. "We spend many hours at the training ground, more than I was used to in Greece.

"There are different types of training, a different approach to the game, more meetings and things like that."

He was helped by some of Brighton's more experienced players, with former midfielder James Milner playing a particularly important role during his first season.

"Most of the older players would come and say something to me as advice," Kostoulas explained. "Last season, James Milner was speaking a lot to me and trying to help me."

He still occasionally speaks to Milner, while Pascal Gross has also become an important figure in his development.

For Kostoulas, having someone to turn to is vital when moving to a different country and footballing culture.

"It's important to have someone to help you understand the way the team works here," he explained to Sussex World.

"It's totally different. If someone from here moves to Greece, they will also need time. It will be hard for them.

"You have to find someone you can trust, have a good relationship with, speak to and who can give you an understanding of most of the things here and what's expected."

There is also the prospect of sharing the pitch with fellow Greek forward Stefanos Tsitsimas, whose return from a lengthy spell out has delighted Kostoulas.

"I'm really happy for him," he said. "He has a really good mindset and I have big trust in him. I think he will do great."

Kostoulas was keen to avoid looking too far ahead when asked about Brighton's ambitions after their impressive start to the season.

Rather than focus on the possibility of challenging at the top of the table or making a deep run in Europe, he insists the squad are concentrating on the next match.

"We don't think about what will happen after six months when the season will be finished," he said to Sussex World.

"We're just enjoying every day, trying to be happy and win as many games as we can."
