What 1 GB and 16 GB of managed Postgres really cost: notes from 11 providers

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

I wanted a plain answer to a plain question: what does it cost to keep one Postgres database running all month on a managed service? Not the "from" price, but the bill once storage and any plan fee you must pay first are added.

So we priced the same setup on 11 providers at two sizes:

The rules: one database, always on (730 hours), single node with no standby, in London where offered (Railway in Amsterdam, Render in Frankfurt, Heroku in its EU region). Storage and any compulsory plan fee are included. Prices are in US dollars, before VAT, and were checked in November 2026. They change, so check the vendor's page before you commit.

Heroku is cheapest on paper, but it doesn't publish Essential-1's memory, so it may not match 1 GB. The cheapest with a stated 1 GB are DigitalOcean at $15.15 and AWS RDS at $15.80.

Neon's figure keeps the compute on all month. A Neon database that scales to zero when idle costs less: Neon's own "typical" Launch figure is $15 a month.

Railway's figure is an estimate. It bills the RAM and CPU a service actually uses, and we assumed the database holds all 16 GB and averages 10% of its CPU; each extra 10% adds $8. Heroku's Standard-3 ($400) has only 2 vCPU and 15.25 GB, so Standard-4 is its first plan that fits.

Almost everyone bills storage separately, per GB a month: Neon $0.35, Render $0.30, Google Cloud SQL $0.204 in London, Railway $0.15, AWS RDS $0.133, and Supabase $0.125 after the 8 GB it includes.

At 200 GB that's a real line on the bill: $70.00 at Neon, $60 at Render, $40.80 at Google Cloud SQL, $30 at Railway and $26.60 at AWS RDS.

Minimums matter at the small end. AWS's gp3 storage starts at 20 GiB, so a 10 GB database pays for 20. Azure's Burstable servers can't go below a 32 GiB disk. Aiven's cheaper plans are fixed at 8 GB of storage, so its cheapest plan that holds 10 GB is Startup-4 at $75, the same plan and price as a 2 vCPU / 4 GB database.

Neon, AWS, Google Cloud, DigitalOcean, Aiven and Heroku charge no platform fee.

On 15 October 2026 DigitalOcean changed its database plans. Accounts that have never created a database, and every account from 30 November, can only create Standard Edition clusters up to 4 GiB, with no standby nodes.

So a 16 GB database, or any high availability, now means Advanced Edition, from $135.75 a month. The 16 GB setup above is $283 there. The 16 GiB Standard plans still shown on the pricing page ($198.55 and $244.35) can't be bought by new customers. If you priced DigitalOcean before mid-October, price it again.

At 1 GB, platform fees and storage minimums move the order as much as the compute price does. At 16 GB the range runs from $198 to $750, and the shape of the plan (burstable or not, more RAM than you asked for, which edition a new account may buy) matters more than the hourly rate.
