Spurs finally score... but still lose

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

They scored. Would you believe it? Tottenham Hotspur, the team with more zeroes than an England Ashes batting collapse, found the back of the net but still frustration persists for Roberto De Zerbi.

It could have been something different. The sight of the Italian dashing down the touchline in hailstones, grabbing his head in disbelief in the final seconds, showed this was not a straightforward tale of misery but, ultimately, the misery endures.

“I think this is five or six games we have been for sure not lucky,” De Zerbi lamented, but sympathy did not extend too far. Liverpool deservedly moved into the next round of the Carabao Cup, the gloss on Andoni Iraola’s most enjoyable night provided by Dominik Szoboszlai’s goal-of-the-season contender.

THAT'S MAGNIFICENT! 💥🔴Outrageous strike from Dominik Szoboszlai 👏👏👏 pic.twitter.com/meQ7nilsz6

Liverpool were abject against Fulham on Saturday but with 10 changes to the starting line-up, infused with the exuberance of novices Rio Ngumoha and the terrific Lewis Koumas, Iraola’s team were invigorated and only a lapse of concentration enabled Tottenham to make a fist of it.

Yes, they went down swinging, as their noisy fans demanded. But carelessness cannot be excused and while De Zerbi complained about bad luck, a bad miss from Lucas Bergvall in the 10th minute was the fork in the road for the tie. Tottenham stalled and Liverpool never looked back.

“We have to shoot more times and we have to shoot better,” De Zerbi harrumphed. “We have to finish better the clear situations. We are sorry for the result, disappointed and frustrated. I think it was unfair but we have to be stronger in these unlucky moments.”

Rather than fizzing his drive into the net, Bergvall succeeded only in firing straight at Giorgi Mamardashvili, when he had the whole target in front of him. It was, in all fairness, a brilliant save but he should never have been allowed to make it.

Big save from Giorgi Mamardashvili! 💪Lucas Bergvall is denied by the Liverpool goalkeeper. pic.twitter.com/fKUbfGLMku

The miss changed the atmosphere, as suddenly Liverpool grasped the need to be competitive. At the heart of it all were Ngumoha, James McConnell, Trey Nyoni and Koumas, the last-named a nuisance to Tottenham’s defenders in the way a wasp is to picnickers on a summer’s afternoon.

For all the money that has been lavished by this club over the past 14 months, the one thing that the locals buy into more than anything is the basics done properly: hard work, tackling, a refusal to take second best. Iraola did not want Koumas to go anywhere this summer and it was obvious why.

“I see him training every day,” Iraola said. “He knows very clearly what he needs to do, what the team needs. And also the energy he shows is contagious for the rest. When you see the runs up front, giving everything, you are kind of forced to go behind and use the same intensity levels.”

Simple tenacity enabled Liverpool to get back on an even keel and laid the foundation for them to take the lead. It was a terrific goal, started with a raking pass from Joe Gomez that Cody Gakpo controlled instantly on his chest before scuttling inside and inviting Ngumoha to charge forward.

Whether it was the winger or Destiny Udogie, who was sliding to make a challenge, that ended up playing the ball to Alexis Mac Allister is immaterial, all that mattered is that the Argentine effortlessly placed his shot into the top corner, leaving Martin Dubravka planted and helpless.

Pick that out!! 🇦🇷Alexis Mac Allister hits a beauty into the roof of the net 🔴 pic.twitter.com/G7OT27CA8P

Is Mac Allister playing with a point to prove? Possibly, given his comments about not being offered a new contract last week. Will Iraola be bothered? Not in the slightest. All he wants is top players at the top of their game and Mac Allister is climbing towards the levels he showed two years ago.

What De Zerbi would do to have a player in such form, but Tottenham are a skittish bunch, not the type of team who respond well in the face of adversity and their head coach, dressed in black with his oversized white trainers, regularly shook his head in exasperation.

His mood was not helped just before the interval, when Mohammed Kudus blasted over from eight yards following a zipped-in cross from Udogie, but he would not have called it truthfully if he had suggested Tottenham deserved parity at that stage. Soon Liverpool’s advantage would be doubled.

The buffer they sought arrived in the 54th minute and, again, it was another genuinely attractive goal, with possession being kept until it sped up when Ngumoha scurried forward, found Milos Kerkez, who played it to Mac Allister, who in turn gave Gakpo the opening he craved.

His touch was excellent, his finish was even better and away in the distance Iraola rattled his fists in delight. That is two goals and four assists to start the campaign for the Dutchman and yet Liverpool were ready to sell him to Manchester City two weeks ago. What on earth were they thinking?

From that point, it seemed inconceivable that Liverpool would finish living on their nerves, but De Zerbi’s decision to introduce Andy Robertson – given a hero’s reception on his first return to Anfield – and Mateus Fernandes wrestled back some impetus. There was also a significant helping hand.

Unsurprisingly, Andy Robertson gets a great reception from the Liverpool fans on his return to Anfield 👏 pic.twitter.com/uNM6A00k5F

Mamardashvili had made some important saves, including one from Kudus that he celebrated on his own, but he is vulnerable at set-pieces and Fernandes’s looping corner gave Conor Gallagher the easiest headed opportunity in three years to change the dynamic.

“We were under pressure at the end, but I don’t think we were nervous,” Gakpo said.

He was right. Try as they might, Spurs could not quite get the opening they needed and as they chased in vain, Szoboszlai administered the final blow in mesmerising fashion, thrashing a bouncing ball into the Kop net from 30 yards. And as De Zerbi realised in the sodden conditions, with Tottenham it never rains. It only pours.

A place in the League Cup fourth round is at stake at Anfield as Liverpool host Tottenham in an all-Premier League tie.

Liverpool were frustrated on the weekend, being held to a 0-0 draw at home against Fulham, meaning they have one win and three draws from their opening four league games.

“I am very happy with the squad,” Liverpool head coach Andoni Iraola said. “I value a lot my players, if we win games, the squad will look amazing and fantastic, if we do not there is going to be a problem with the depth. I think we will see also tomorrow [tonight] players we have not seen still and I am really looking forward to seeing. I feel they are going to play well and help us and they still have not had their chance. You can decide and put the same players in every game. The problem is there will be a moment when they are really tired. And there is more risk of injury.

“But that is why we cannot say it does not work for anyone. There are players who play every three days with no issues. They are very robust, their performances do not change too much. There are other players where the risk is bigger and the performances are different. I have to learn who is ready to play games every three days and who needs a rest so their performance level is not affected. I thought I had it quite clear in my mind with some, but with others, it is new, even for them, because they have not played as many games. We are learning.”

Liverpool were knocked out in the fourth round of this competition last season, losing 3-0 at home against Crystal Palace. Joe Gomez returns to the squad for the hosts tonight after an injury early in pre-season but Giovanni Leoni, Hugo Ekitike, Conor Bradley and Federico Chiesa remain out.

Tottenham are still without a league win or goal this season. Their 0-0 draw at home against Everton Saturday means they sit just above the relegation zone. Tottenham overcame Charlton in the second round, convincingly winning 5-1 at the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium last month.

“I think we are working well,” Tottenham boss Roberto De Zerbi said. “We played 30 minutes, 25 minutes very well [against Everton]. In that moment we could score two goals. Then we lost maybe the confidence because we did not score. For sure we have to improve. For sure I have big confidence in my players, they are very good players.

“We have to improve, yes. We can say the coach has responsibility if we do not score, yes, I accept, no problem. We did not win any game so far, yes. We are disappointed, sorry for that. But we have other positive things, like I said we did not [concede goals] in the last two games and the things we have to improve, we start our work. Nothing changed in my project and my idea.”

Tottenham’s League Cup journey last season came to an end in the fourth round last season, losing 2-0 at Newcastle. Sandro Tonali and Pedro Porro will not be involved tonight for the visitors and neither will Richarlison, who has been left out. Andrew Robertson returns to Anfield for the first time after leaving Liverpool in the summer following nine years on Merseyside.
