Bright turns on Ocusell integration so agents enter listings once

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Bright MLS launched a live integration with Ocusell’s network, letting agents enter and edit listings once and syndicate to Bright and other MLSs. Ocusell says it can cut listing creation time up to 75% and prevent over 85% of compliance errors.  AI Summary

Bright MLS has turned on its live integration with Ocusell’s listing network, allowing brokers and agents who work across multiple MLSs in the Mid-Atlantic to enter and edit listings once and syndicate them into Bright and other participating MLSs without double data entry, the companies announced Tuesday.

The operational launch marks the first major product milestone from the joint venture Bright and Ocusell formed in 2025 and introduces OneList, a co-developed listing input platform built on the Ocusell Network that is designed to give brokerages and MLSs a single, connected listing interface nationwide.

“We entered into this joint venture to remove friction from the daily work of our brokers and agents,” said Bright MLS President and CEO Brian Donnellan, who is also a HousingWire Vanguard award winner. “Our role as an MLS is to adapt to how real estate pros operate and to help them succeed. This partnership with Ocusell is a clear example of that.”

The launch comes as the Open Real Estate (ORE) initiative is running a national request for proposal search for a vendor that can deliver “input once, distribute everywhere” listing entry across markets. Ocusell says it is already live across 30 MLSs, and with OneList now operating at Bright, one of the nation’s largest MLSs, the company is positioning its network as a scalable solution for multi-MLS entry.

For brokers, the integration is aimed at reducing staff time and compliance risk tied to re-keying listing data into separate MLS systems. Agents can enter listing specifics, photos and disclosures once; the platform then auto-validates those fields against each local MLS’s rules while maintaining existing access and display policies. According to Ocusell, its infrastructure can cut listing creation time by up to 75% and prevent more than 85% of compliance errors.

On top of cross-market sync, according to the announcement, the forthcoming OneList interface layers in real-time AI-driven compliance and Fair Housing screening, intelligent media management with automated photo tagging and organization, and integrated listing promotion that lets agents launch targeted marketing campaigns from the listing input workflow.

“This is a total game changer for agents, brokers, and home buyers and sellers,” said Steve Mapes, executive in charge of growth strategy at Bright MLS. “When agents aren’t stuck doing double data entry because they belong to multiple MLSs, they have more time to serve their clients and do what they do best. Technology should make things better, faster and more efficient, and we’re excited to get this up and running.”

“Now is the time for MLSs to help shape what comes next for their brokers,” Hayden Rieveschl, the CEO of Ocusell and a 2026 HousingWire Insider, said in a statement. “Bright and Ocusell are demonstrating what’s possible when you listen to brokers and turn their needs into action. As more brokerages operate across markets, MLSs have an opportunity to remove barriers and make doing business easier.”

The move reflects a broader shift in organized real estate as large regional brokerages, teams and franchises increasingly span multiple MLS territories while regulators and plaintiffs’ attorneys scrutinize how listing data is shared and displayed.

For brokers and agents, multi-MLS input has historically meant duplicate work, inconsistent data, higher error rates and delays getting listings in front of buyers across markets. A centralized entry layer like OneList, if widely adopted, could streamline operations for multi-market firms, tighten compliance controls and create more standardized listing data for downstream tools, portals and valuation models.

For MLS organizations, the Bright-Ocusell rollout is a signal that listing input may increasingly be handled through connected, vendor-operated platforms rather than bespoke MLS-only interfaces, potentially reshaping how MLSs think about technology procurement, interoperability and data licensing.

Ocusell said the integration is live now for active Bright MLS brokerages that manage listings across multiple markets, according to the announcement. Bright said rollout timelines and early-access details for the broader OneList platform will be shared in future communications.
