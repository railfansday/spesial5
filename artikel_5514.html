How Bedroom Counts Vary Across Rental Products in Growth Markets

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Rental housing encompasses a range of product types, each offering different configurations and levels of space. Across major growth markets, bedroom counts provide one way to understand how these products differ and where they may compete for renters.

Zonda’s third-quarter 2026 Rental Housing Outlook examined bedroom counts across cottages, townhomes, higher-density detached homes, and single-family rentals in major housing markets.

Single-family rentals generally offered the most bedrooms, while cottages tended to offer the fewest. Beyond that pattern, individual markets revealed notable differences in how rental products were configured, providing additional context for builders and developers assessing their competitive landscape.

Among the markets analyzed, Salt Lake City had the highest average bedroom count for single-family rentals, at 4.8 bedrooms. That is nearly two bedrooms more than the market’s average townhome rental, highlighting the differences in space offered across rental products within the same market.

Charlotte also ranked near the top for average bedrooms in single-family rentals, while San Antonio sat at the other end of the spectrum.

These differences can also influence how rental properties compete with for-sale housing. Larger single-family rentals may appeal to households that might otherwise consider purchasing a home, while smaller cottages and townhomes may attract renters with different space needs.

Single-family rentals, however, are only part of the story, with market rankings shifting across other rental product types.

Tampa led the analyzed markets for bedroom counts in higher-density detached rentals, while Orlando stood out for townhomes. San Antonio, despite having the lowest average bedroom count for single-family rentals among the markets studied, had the highest cottage average. Nashville sat at the opposite end of the cottage comparison.

These variations highlight the importance of looking beyond overall rental inventory when evaluating a market. Understanding the mix of rental products and the space they offer can provide additional context for builders and developers assessing local competition.

The insights in this article were taken from more in-depth research reports published in Zonda’s Rental Housing Outlook subscription.
