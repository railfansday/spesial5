Lenders increasingly pull with new credit score model, but few use it for pricing

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Bank of America Global Research, using Recursion data, found that more than 170 lenders now pull VantageScore 4.0, but only a small share of conventional loans are actually priced with VS4. In October, 20% of issuance reported a VS4 score, yet roughly 8% of total issuance used VS4 for pricing.  AI Summary

Mortgage lenders are frequently pulling credit scores with VantageScore 4.0, but most of them still price with Classic FICO, according to a new Bank of America Global Research report based on Recursion data. The report, released on Friday, shows that Rocket Mortgage and United Wholesale Mortgage (UWM) are driving nearly all of the production actually priced with the new scoring model in the agency mortgage-backed securities (MBS) market.

Since October, Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac have required that agency MBS pools disclose both Classic FICO and VantageScore 4.0 whenever both are pulled, and to identify which score was used for pricing.

According to Bank of America, 148 sellers in October delivered agency MBS loans for which a VS4 score had been pulled. That figure rose to 173 sellers during the first 10 days of November. But the number of lenders actually pricing loans with VS4 remains much smaller. Only eight lenders sold loans priced with VS4 in September, which rose to 22 in October and 37 to date in November.

In October, 20% of conventional issuance reported a VS4 score, BofA said. Of that subset, 39% used VS4 as the pricing score, which works out to roughly 8% of total conventional issuance. Most of these loans had both scores: 18% of issuance reported both FICO and VS4, while only 2% reported VS4 alone.

Thus far in November, 21% of issuance had VS4 reported and 32% of these loans used VS4 for pricing, or about 7% of total issuance.

Rocket and UWM are the main early adopters on the pricing side. In October, BofA found that Rocket and UWM together accounted for 65% of all loans with a VS4 score pulled. They also were the sources for 95% of loans that used VS4 as their pricing score in the agency MBS market.

Among other lenders that have shown they can pull VS4, just 13% of their production had a VS4 score reported and only 1% of loans actually used VS4 to set prices.

“We think this disconnect largely reflects a gradual ramp-up in operational and infrastructure readiness,” BofA analysts said. “Pulling and reporting an additional score is relatively straightforward, while actually incorporating VS4 into loan pricing likely requires more extensive changes to lenders’ pricing systems and operational infrastructure.”

BofA’s report also notes that loans priced with VS4 have distinctive collateral characteristics. VS4-priced loans tend to have higher average loan balances, a lower share of first-time homebuyers, and higher shares of cash-out and rate-and-term refinance loans.

Among VS4-priced loans where both scores were reported, the VS4 score was on average about 43 points higher than FICO. For FICO-priced loans with both scores present, the two models are broadly comparable, the analysts said.

Because lenders can choose between scores when both are present, the emerging “best-of-two” selection framework is affecting the distribution of published scores. BofA found that among October loans with both scores reported, 24% had FICO scores below 700 and 21% had VS4 scores below 700, but only 17% had a pricing score below 700.

BofA also addressed investor concerns that borrowers in legacy low-FICO pools who have materially higher VS4 scores could see a one-time chance to refinance as markets and pricing grids adjust to VS4.

“We acknowledge that legacy low-FICO pools are exposed to this risk,” the analysts said. “However, newly issued low credit score pools may offer a way to mitigate this risk and potentially access even better convexity collateral.”

According to the report, low credit score pool collateral today generally consists of loans that either have both FICO and VS4 reported with both scores below 700, or are VS4-only loans. Loans with both FICO and VS4 below 700 have already been screened against the risk that a significantly higher VS4 score would give borrowers a one-time refinancing opportunity.

Additionally, a historical analysis indicates that loans with both scores below 700 exhibit better convexity than loans defined only by FICOs below 700.
