Compass pushes back on antitrust claims, says it owes StreetEasy nothing

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Compass filed a motion to dismiss an amended antitrust complaint in SDNY tied to pulling rental listings from StreetEasy. Compass argues the suit conflates sales and rentals, lacks causation, and cites the FARE Act as a driver of inventory visibility.  AI Summary

Compass International Holdings is seeking to dismiss an antitrust lawsuit alleging that it created a monopoly through its acquisitions, which it used to harm consumers as well as competitors like Zillow when it decided to pull its rental listings from StreetEasy in early August.

Filed in late August, the lawsuit claims that Compass’s removal of listings from StreetEasy reduced consumer choice, while also driving up rents and brokerage fees.

In a filing on Tuesday, Compass told the court it was seeking to dismiss the amended complaint with prejudice, which would prevent the plaintiff from refilling the lawsuit.

In the filing, Compass claims that the plaintiffs have confused the residential sales market with the rental market, noting that the information cited in the complaint pertains to for-sale homes and not rental listings. The filing also notes that Compass does not own rental properties or set rental prices, claiming instead that Zillow and StreetEasy dominate the online listing-search market. The defendant also claims that the plaintiff cannot demonstrate that Compass’s alleged conduct caused his higher rent.

The filing also notes that rental inventory in New York City began to decline seven months prior to its acquisition of Anywhere and more than a year before Compass began instructing agents to pull rental listings from Street Easy. Instead, Compass points to the June 2025 implementation of New York City’s Fairness in Apartment Rental Expenses (FARE) Act, which prohibits brokers hired by landlords from charging broker fees to tenants and requires landlords to disclose applicable fees before a lease is signed, as the reason behind the drop in visible inventory.

Compass also claims that it does not have monopoly power in the rental listings market and that it has no legal obligation to share its listings with platforms like Street Easy.

According to a notice on the court docket, the plaintiff has until December 8, 2026, to respond to Compass’s motion to dismiss the suit. While the parties wait for the court to rule on the motion, Compass is also asking the court to stay discovery. The court has not yet ruled on that motion.
