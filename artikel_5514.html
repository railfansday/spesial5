NAR membership proves more resilient than expected, topping 1.46 million

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

NAR told directors membership totaled 1,469,127 at the end of September, 22% above budget and 1.5% below a year earlier. Dues revenue was about $220 million of more than $250 million, while expenses ran about $7 million under budget.  AI Summary

It is no secret that the National Association of Realtors (NAR) had a challenging few years. From facing a sexual harassment scandal, navigating the commission lawsuits and the implementation of the resulting settlement, to looking to create a more accountable and responsible version of the trade group, NAR has experienced its fair share of growing pains. But, when it comes to the association’s financials, 2026 was far better than anticipated.

In an update at NAR’s 2026 NXT Board of Directors meeting by NAR’s 2026 Treasurer Craig Sanford, it was revealed that at the end of September, the association had 1,469,127 members, 22% better than the 1.2 million members the association had budgeted for and just 1.5% lower than membership a year ago. In addition, Sanford said membership renewals for 2027 are tracking in line with where they were in 2026.

Revenue from membership dues accounted for roughly $220 million of the more than $250 million in revenue NAR had reported as of the end of September. Sanford also noted that NAR’s expenses as of late September had come in roughly $7 million better than budgeted, but that he expected some of those savings to be diminished by fourth quarter advocacy spending due to the recent midterm elections. Despite this, Sanford said that NAR’s cash and investments remain strong, allowing the trade group to meet its upcoming settlement payments while still investing in new products and services for its members.

“In my two years as treasurer, I have seen the start of one of the greatest financial modernization in transformation in NAR’s history,” Sanford said. “We have implemented a new accounting system along with accounting and budgeting tools, moved to monthly accrual accounting, selected and onboarded the new auditor, managed through massive personnel changes, implemented zero-based budgeting and now we are minutes away from voting to send our third consecutive balanced budget to the Board for approval.”

Despite this progress, Sanford said that the work is not done, instead it’s just getting started.

The next stage of this work kicked off with the approval of three budgetary members by the board of directors. Sanford noted that this budget was created after stringent review of each department, resulting in a budget “that is more intentional, more strategically aligned and better positioned to support [NAR’s] core mission while maintaining financial discipline.”

During the meeting, the board overwhelmingly approved a 2027 operating and advocacy budget of $280,169,801 in gross revenue, and $246,952,390 in gross expenses. Over 90% of the directors also approved a 2027 consumer advertising campaign budget of $12 million in gross revenue and $13.696 million in program expenses. Additionally, over 95% of the directors approved a recommendation that found that the capital expenditure needs identified for 2027 technology total $2.42 million and that the capital expenditure needs identified for 2027 building needs total $3.06 million.

In addition to approving budgetary measures, the board also voted on proposed amendments to the bylaws of the institutes, societies and councils. During the meeting, Brown noted that the amendments were reviewed by NAR’s general counsel and were deemed to be “not inconsistent with the policies and constitution” of NAR.

The proposed amendments were approved by 864 directors voting in favor and six opposed.

Beyond voting on these measures, the meeting also saw the installation of the 2027 leadership team, including 2027 NAR President Christine Hansen, a HousingWire Woman of Influence, President-elect Colin Mullane, First Vice President Pete Kopf and Treasurer Patti Fitzgerald. The leaders were approved by the board of directors back in May at NAR’s Realtors Legislative Meetings.
