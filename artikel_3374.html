Sony Fans Debate Major Game Awards Snub

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Sony titles and The Game Awards often go hand-in-hand, as plenty of PlayStation exclusives feel tailor-made for the kind of mainstream critical appeal that makes winning awards a cinch. From The Last of Us to the modern God of War games, the company's emphasis on cinematic storytelling has resulted in plenty of nominations and wins over the years.

Of course, that's no guarantee of victory. Sony's 2024 win for Astro Bot was bookended by Marvel's Spider-Man 2 losing to Baldur's Gate 3 in 2023 and Death Stranding 2: On the Beach falling before the unstoppable Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 wave in 2025. 2026 has shaken things up to a significantly greater degree, though, leaving plenty of PlayStation fans conflicted about the nominations.

As Andy Robinson of VGC pointed out on X, 2026 marks the "first time in 11 years" that a title published by Sony hasn't landed among the Game of the Year nominations at The Game Awards. While Marvel's Wolverine followed the critically successful Marvel's Spider-Man games from developer Insomniac Games, it failed to make the same splash, instead earning nominations for Best Performance and Innovations in Accessibility.

Plenty of players seem to be perfectly fine with this snub, at least if the responses to Robinson's post are anything to go by. Jakeinvades argues that Marvel's Wolverine "absolutely did not deserve to be nominated," and FOX suggests that the publisher simply "hasn't produced a GOTY quality IP this year."

There's still some room for debate, however. Some Marvel's Wolverine fans would certainly argue for its inclusion, but the more prevelant argument seems to be in favor of SAROS, which was also released under the Sony umbrella. While X user simon agrees that the Marvel's Wolverine snub is correct, they suggest that SAROS "could've been in the conversation."

I have less affection for games like Marvel's Spider-Man and the 2018 God of War than most, so Sony breaking its nomination streak is no particular skin off my back. ScreenRant's Austin King, who provided our review of Marvel's Wolverine, also wasn't an advocate of any Game of the Year potential, awarding it a respectable but unexceptional 7/10 score.

Sadly, the absence of Marvel's Wolverine in the Game of the Year pool didn't seem to open the way for any particularly intriguing dark horse candidates, as this is a year of fairly predictable AAA inclusions. Games like Control Resonant and Phantom Blade Zero are certainly less omnipresent than Grand Theft Auto 6, but there's no upstart indie darling like 2024's Balatro in the mix.

As to whether Sony can still take home any awards, the company may not go home empty-handed. Marvel's Wolverine might not take either of its categories — Wolverine actor Liam McIntyre could have an uphill battle to take Best Performance over standouts like Resident Evil Requiem's Angela Sant'Albano, and its laudable accessibility options might be too heavily associated with its heavy hand-holding to secure a win for Innovations in Accessibility. MARVEL Tōkon: Fighting Souls in the Fighting Game category at The Game Awards 2026, though? That one could work out.
