NYC Book Publishing Mainstay Farrar, Straus & Giroux Marks 80th Anniversary

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

As NBC marks its 100th anniversary this year, another mainstay of the media landscape is turning 80: book publisher Farrar, Straus & Giroux.

The literary division of Macmillan Publishers has grown from a small boutique into a home for Nobel laureates, Pulitzer Prize winners, and National Book Award recipients. On Monday night, as NBCUniversal CEO Mike Cavanagh hosted a star-studded centennial party at the Rainbow Room atop 30 Rockefeller Plaza, FSG President and Publisher Mitzi Angel presided over her own bash.

The celebration, held at the historic event venue Storied in Chelsea, drew its own share of distinguished guests. While late-night host Jimmy Fallon was among the A-listers at NBC, his Tonight Show drummer, Roots frontman Ahmir “Questlove” Thompson, made a surprise appearance at the FSG gathering, delivering a DJ set. Thompson is publisher of FSG imprint AUWA Books. Countertenor Anthony Roth Costanzo, star of the Metropolitan Opera’s Lincoln in the Bardo, also performed. Other guests included National Book Award winners Jonathan Franzen, George Packer, Alice McDermott, and Justin Torres; and Pulitzer Prize winners Paul Muldoon, Amanda Vaill, and The Virgin Suicides’ Jeffrey Eugenides; film and TV director Rebecca Miller; Broadway playwright, director and librettist James Lapine; and filmmaker John Waters. Angel addressed the crowd in attendance to salute the company’s durability and make an impassioned case for its mission during a time of cultural transformation.

“We live in an age of algorithms, and algorithms are very good at what can be measured now. Literature works on another timescale, in a place measurement can’t reach. A machine can now produce the most probable next sentence. Our job is to find, and to keep, the improbable one: the distinctive, idiosyncratic voice with a music of its own,” she said.

Reflecting on the milestone, she added, “Eighty years is a long time in publishing, but not so long in the history of literature. A publishing house, finally, is people standing behind books, for as long as it takes.”
