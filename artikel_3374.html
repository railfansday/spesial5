Netflix’s New Jane Austen Adaptation Officially Debuts in 1 Week

Bringing one of literature's greatest works to life is no easy task, but Netflix is rising to the challenge with Pride and Prejudice. Written by Jane Austen, Pride and Prejudice was her third novel and the second to be published, released in 1813. At the center is the dynamic between Elizabeth Bennet (whose personal growth drives the story) and the proud, wealthy Mr. Darcy. Through their time together, Elizabeth recognizes the mistakes of judging people too quickly and learns how to distinguish fake charm from true kindness.

In the century since Pride and Prejudice was published, it has been adapted for the screen several times, beginning with a 1938 BBC television broadcast written and directed by Michael Barry. Unfortunately, that version is permanently lost because it aired live before television recording technology existed. Memorable adaptations include the Academy Award-winning movie starring Greer Garson and Laurence Olivier, partly based on Helen Jerome's 1935 stage adaptation; Joe Wright's acclaimed 2005 feature starring Keira Knightley and Matthew Macfadyen; andthe 1995 BBC miniseries starring Colin Firth and Jennifer Ehle. Now, the upcoming Netflix version joins the bunch, premiering in just one week.

Starring Emma Corrin and Jack Lowden as Elizabeth and Darcy, Netflix's Pride and Prejudice series was first reported to be in development in October 2024 and officially announced in April 2025. Filming began in the UK in the summer of 2025, with first-look behind-the-scenes images released in July. Bestselling author and screenwriter Dolly Alderton wrote the series, and Euros Lyn directed. The six-part series premieres on December 3, 2026, as Alderton announced at the Jane Austen Festival in September.

Four love stories, four completely different ideas of what it means to be somebody's person. This is the one that matches how you actually fall, and what it would ask of you.

You do not fall for strangers. You fall for the person you have already been talking to for years, which is the slowest and by some distance the most durable way anybody has found to do this.

You want a partner, not an audience. Somebody who wants their own life as badly as you want yours, and who would never ask you to be smaller to make the relationship easier.

The intensity was never the problem. The secrecy is. You are drawn to a love that lives entirely in private, and to the particular ache of wanting somebody you are not supposed to want.

You do not do this cautiously. When it happens for you it is immediate and total and it rearranges everything, and you would not accept a smaller version of it.

In Netflix's Pride and Prejudice, viewers are treated to a fresh yet faithful adaptation. Elizabeth, the second of five daughters, faces pressure to marry well or risk losing the comfortable life she knows. As she steps into society, she meets the prickly Mr. Darcy, and their love story unfolds alongside that of her older sister Jane, who falls for the charming Mr. Bingley.

Other stars in the Regency romance adaptation include Olivia Colman as the Bennet family matriarch, Mrs. Bennet; Rufus Sewell as her husband, Mr. Bennet; Freya Mavor as the eldest Bennet daughter, Jane; and Rhea Norwood as Lydia Bennet. Newcomers Hollie Avery and Hopey Parish make their debut roles as Kitty and Mary Bennet, respectively. Daryl McCormack will portray Jane's love interest, Mr. Bingley, while Siena Kelly will play his sister, Caroline Bingley.

Netflix's Pride and Prejudice premieres in one week.
