How Arsenal will change tactics to suit Kepa in goalkeeper’s last chance to impress

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Your weekly briefing on Arsenal, featuring team news and expert analysis from Matt Verri

Any and all updates on David Raya were welcome if you were an Arsenal fan.

There were the attempts to lip read what head physio Simon Murphy said as he walked off the St James’ Park pitch with Raya.

Then the sighting of Raya the following day in Oxford Circus with his arm in a sling. And the report that he had been able to drive himself home after the club returned to London from Newcastle.

The sense of panic was understandable. Raya is Arsenal’s most irreplaceable player, pivotal to the team not just because of his shot-stopping, but the way he commands his area and how central he is to the build-up in possession as well.

It was good news, then, that he has avoided a serious injury. Raya has not yet been ruled out of facing Manchester City this weekend.

Even with the outlook more positive than it might have been, Arsenal must still find a way to cope without the Spaniard for at least tonight’s match against Borussia Dortmund.

This will be a big night for Kepa Arrizabalaga. He started against Kairat Almaty last season, but that dead rubber aside has not played in the Champions League since making four appearances for Real Madrid in the competition in 2023.

This is a chance to prove he still belongs at this level. Kepa will surely still have an eye on becoming a first-choice goalkeeper again at a top club elsewhere in the seasons ahead. However, that kind of evidence has been in short supply since he arrived at Arsenal.

Mikel Arteta put his faith in Kepa last season in the Carabao Cup final and it was not repaid. Kepa was booked for hauling down Jeremy Doku after misjudging a bouncing ball, before gifting City the lead when spilling a cross.

His distribution, too, was poor. Throughout the match, Arsenal’s build-up was almost entirely nullified by City and that started with the goalkeeper.

A key facet of Arsenal’s build-up is Raya’s ability to not just go short, but to ping flat, long passes up the pitch to play over the press when required.

“David Raya can play six, I swear,” Noni Madueke said in a recent interview.

Kepa cannot play those same passes with the same trajectory or accuracy. Nor is he as convincing in inspiring the same confidence when going short under pressure.

Asked on Monday whether Arsenal would have to change how they play without Raya, Arteta said: “We adapt to players’ qualities, and they all have different ones.

“There are certain things that, in our game or in the way that we understand the game and we want to play the game, everybody has to be 100% committed to, but then we make adjustments in relation to the qualities of the players.”

One of those adjustments will have to be how Arsenal build up. Illan Meslier would be more comfortable in that respect, with the ball at his feet, but he is liable to make big mistakes and can be exposed when the ball is crossed on top of him.

There has never been any real consideration from Arteta or his coaching staff that it would be Meslier getting the nod over Kepa to stand in for Raya.

When he signed in the summer, Meslier was aware of the pecking order. He arrived primarily to allow Tommy Setford to go out on loan, rather than to replace Kepa as No2.

Meslier started in the Carabao Cup defeat to Fleetwood, even with Kepa fit and in the squad. However, that was due to a desire to reward Meslier for his consistency in training and impact on the squad, not a rejigging of the pecking order.

It is easy to forget just how highly rated Kepa once was. He remains the most expensive goalkeeper signing of all time, joining Chelsea for £72million in 2018.

That price tag has followed him around ever since. So too Carabao Cup finals, both last season’s horror show and the 2019 edition when he refused to be substituted.

The temptation is to say a quiet night, one in which he is not noticed against Dortmund, would be ideal for Kepa, but Raya does not have many of those.

Even when he is not required to pull off saves, he is still making line-breaking passes or pushing up close to the halfway line to snuff out attacks.

Kepa failed on all those counts in the Carabao Cup final last season. Another poor performance this time and it is hard to see how he can retain Arteta’s trust.

In his biggest test since that Wembley occasion, Arsenal need Kepa to minimise the disruption of Raya’s absence.
