10 royals with vast fortunes and the assets behind them

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Free daily briefing on global business news.

A look at 10 wealthy royals around the world and the crown estates, oil economies, banks and holding companies tied to their ruling families

Royal wealth is rarely audited and almost never disclosed in full. That makes any single dollar figure attached to a king, sultan or emir an estimate. Published estimates for the same person also differ from one outlet to the next and from one year to the next. A more useful question is what each family controls, and through which structures.

The answer changes sharply by country. In some Gulf monarchies, the ruling family sits at the head of a state built on oil and gas revenue. There, the line between the treasury and the palace is often unclear or undocumented. Large sovereign wealth funds in the region belong to governments, even when members of the ruling family run or oversee them. In constitutional monarchies such as the U.K., the separation is spelled out in law. Some assets belong to the crown as an institution. Others produce private income for the monarch and the heir.

Other royal fortunes run through named institutions and companies. Thailand's royal holdings are managed by the Crown Property Bureau. A 2018 law put those assets under the king's personal control. Morocco's royal family is linked to Al Mada, a holding company with interests across several sectors of the national economy. Liechtenstein's princely family owns LGT Group, which provides private banking and asset management. Brunei's sultan presides over an oil and gas economy and lives in a palace with 1,788 rooms.

This article presents a selection of 10, not a ranking. Each slide covers a different monarch or ruling family, from Bangkok to Monaco. The mechanisms run from crown property and royal duchies to state oil companies, sovereign funds and private banking. Together they show how wealthy royals around the world hold and manage assets, and where those assets end and the state's begin.

Where reliable numbers do not exist, this list leaves them out. The order of the slides carries no meaning about relative wealth. The focus stays on what can be verified: who rules, since when and which institutions sit behind the throne.

King Maha Vajiralongkorn of Thailand personally controls the royal holdings managed by the Crown Property Bureau. Under legislation passed in 2018, those assets became his own rather than property of the monarchy as an institution.

He took the throne in 2016 after the death of his father, King Bhumibol Adulyadej, who had reigned since 1946. His coronation was held in May 2019 in Bangkok. The ceremonies ran over several days and drew on Buddhist and Brahmin ritual.

Vajiralongkorn is the 10th king of the Chakri dynasty and is also known as Rama X $TWTR. King Rama I founded the dynasty in 1782 and moved the capital to Bangkok. Siam, as Thailand was then called, became a constitutional monarchy after a 1932 revolution ended absolute royal rule.

The Crown Property Bureau was created in the 1930s to manage assets linked to the throne. Thai law at the time sorted royal-related wealth into three categories. These were the king's private property, state property used by the monarchy, and crown property, which belonged to the monarchy as an institution.

For decades, the finance minister chaired the bureau's board. That arrangement tied the royal portfolio to the government, even though the assets were not state property.

The bureau's holdings include large areas of land in central Bangkok. Much of that land is leased to businesses, government agencies and residents. The portfolio has also long included major stakes in Siam Cement Group, an industrial conglomerate, and Siam Commercial Bank, one of the country's oldest lenders.

The 2018 change erased the old line between crown property and the king's personal wealth for these holdings. Shareholdings once listed under the bureau were then registered in the king's name.

The shift made Vajiralongkorn the direct owner of assets that earlier monarchs did not hold personally. No audited valuation of the portfolio is published. The bureau does not release full accounts to the public.

King Salman bin Abdulaziz Al Saud has led Saudi Arabia since January 2015. His family, the House of Saud, rules a kingdom whose economy rests on oil. How much of that wealth belongs personally to the royals, rather than to the state, is not publicly documented.

Salman became king after the death of his half-brother, King Abdullah. He is one of the sons of King Abdulaziz, known as Ibn Saud, who founded the modern Saudi state in 1932. Since Abdulaziz died in 1953, the crown has passed among his sons.

Before taking the throne, Salman spent decades as governor of Riyadh province. He became defense minister in 2011 and crown prince in 2012. That long administrative career made him one of the most senior figures in the family well before his accession.

Saudi Arabia is an absolute monarchy. The king is head of state, and the country has no elected national legislature. In 2017, Salman named his son Mohammed bin Salman crown prince. In 2022, the crown prince also became prime minister, a post the king had previously held.

Oil defines the kingdom's finances. Commercial quantities were discovered in the late 1930s, and the industry grew under American oil companies. The Saudi government took full ownership of that operation over the following decades, and it became Saudi Aramco. The state remains its majority owner. In December 2019, the government listed a small portion of its shares on the Riyadh stock exchange.

That structure explains why estimates of Saudi royal wealth are hard to pin down. Oil revenue flows to the state, and the royal family governs the state. No public accounting separates the family's private holdings from national assets. Any single figure for the House of Saud's fortune rests on that unresolved boundary.

The family is also large. It includes thousands of members descended from Abdulaziz and his relatives. Many hold government posts, business interests or both. Those individual fortunes vary widely and are not disclosed.

The kingdom's economic planning now looks beyond crude. The Vision 2030 program, announced in 2016 under Mohammed bin Salman, aims to diversify the economy away from oil. The Public Investment Fund, the state's sovereign wealth fund, is a central vehicle for that effort.

Sultan Hassanal Bolkiah has ruled Brunei since 1967, and his wealth comes from the small state's oil and gas. He is also Brunei's prime minister. That makes him both head of state and head of government.

He became sultan in October 1967, when his father, Sultan Omar Ali Saifuddien III, abdicated. His coronation took place the following year. He is the 29th sultan in a royal line that traces its origins back more than six centuries. In 2017, Brunei marked his Golden Jubilee, 50 years on the throne.

The premiership is only one of his cabinet posts. He also holds the defense and finance portfolios. Brunei has no elected legislature. Its Legislative Council is appointed. The state's official ideology, Melayu Islam Beraja, or Malay Islamic Monarchy, places the throne at the center of national life.

Hydrocarbons underpin the economy. Oil was discovered at Seria in 1929. Crude oil and liquefied natural gas remain the country's main exports. Brunei Shell $SHEL Petroleum, a joint venture between the government and Shell, runs much of the oil and gas production. The Brunei Investment Agency manages the state's overseas reserves. Brunei does not publish an accounting that separates the sultan's personal assets from those state holdings.

The Istana Nurul Iman is his official residence. It stands on the bank of the Brunei River in Bandar Seri Begawan, the capital. Guinness World Records lists it as the world's largest residential palace. The Filipino architect Leandro Locsin designed it. Its throne hall is used for state ceremonies.

The palace was completed in 1984, the same year Brunei became independent from the U.K. Independence ended nearly a century of Brunei's status as a British protectorate.

The palace also opens to the public once a year. At the end of Ramadan, it holds an open house for Hari Raya Aidilfitri. During those days, the sultan and members of the royal family receive visitors in person.

Sheikh Mohammed bin Zayed Al Nahyan rules Abu Dhabi, the emirate that holds most of the UAE's oil. He became president of the UAE in May 2022, after the death of his half-brother Sheikh Khalifa bin Zayed.

He is a son of Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan. His father led the founding of the UAE federation in 1971 and served as its first president. The Al Nahyan family has ruled Abu Dhabi for generations.

The UAE is a federation of seven emirates, and each has its own ruler. The Federal Supreme Council, made up of those seven rulers, elects the president. The ruler of Abu Dhabi has held the presidency since the federation began.

Sheikh Mohammed spent nearly two decades as crown prince of Abu Dhabi before taking the top post. He was named crown prince in 2004. He trained at the Royal Military Academy Sandhurst in the U.K. and built his early career in the UAE armed forces.

Abu Dhabi is the largest of the seven emirates by area. It covers most of the country's land. The state-owned Abu Dhabi National Oil Company, known as ADNOC, runs the emirate's oil production.

Much of the emirate's surplus revenue goes into sovereign investment vehicles. The Abu Dhabi Investment Authority, set up in 1976, invests government money across global markets. Mubadala, established in 2002, holds stakes in a range of industries. Its holdings include the chipmaker GlobalFoundries.

Neither fund is Sheikh Mohammed's private fortune. Both are government entities that invest on behalf of the emirate. As ruler, he heads the state that owns them. The assets themselves belong to Abu Dhabi, not to him personally.

His personal wealth has never been publicly disclosed. No public accounting separates the family's private holdings from the emirate's state assets.

Other members of the family hold major private assets of their own. His brother Sheikh Mansour bin Zayed bought the English football club Manchester City in 2008. He made the purchase through his investment company, Abu Dhabi United Group.

Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum has ruled Dubai since January 2006. He is also vice president and prime minister of the UAE. The emirate's best-known assets, including the airline Emirates, sit with government-owned entities rather than in a disclosed personal fortune.

He became ruler after the death of his elder brother, Sheikh Maktoum bin Rashid Al Maktoum. Their father, Sheikh Rashid bin Saeed Al Maktoum, led Dubai before both of them. The Al Maktoum family has governed Dubai since 1833.

Dubai is one of seven emirates that make up the UAE, a federation formed in 1971. By long-standing practice, the ruler of Abu Dhabi holds the federal presidency and the ruler of Dubai holds the vice presidency. Sheikh Mohammed pairs that role with the post of prime minister. That puts him at the head of the federal cabinet.

Emirates shows how Dubai's largest businesses are structured. The airline is owned by the Investment Corporation of Dubai, the emirate's main investment arm. That body belongs to the Dubai government. Emirates is therefore a state asset, not the ruler's private property.

Dubai's economy has developed along different lines from Abu Dhabi's. Its own oil output is small. The emirate has leaned instead on trade, aviation, tourism, real estate and financial services. Emirates began flying in 1985. Its growth helped turn Dubai International Airport into a major hub for long-haul connecting traffic.

The ruler's personal holdings are far less documented than the state's. His best-known private venture is Godolphin, the thoroughbred racing and breeding operation he founded in 1992. Its horses race in Europe, the Middle East, Australia and other markets. No official figure for his personal net worth has been published.

His son, Sheikh Hamdan bin Mohammed Al Maktoum, was named crown prince of Dubai in 2008. He is next in line to rule the emirate. In July 2024, he was also appointed the UAE's deputy prime minister and minister of defense.

Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani has been emir of Qatar since June 2013, when his father abdicated in his favor. His country's wealth comes mainly from natural gas. The largest pools of that wealth belong to the state, not to the ruler personally.

His father, Sheikh Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani, had ruled since 1995. Voluntary handovers of power are rare among Gulf monarchies. Sheikh Tamim was born in 1980 and trained at the Royal Military Academy Sandhurst in the U.K. He served as deputy commander of the armed forces and headed the Qatar Olympic Committee before taking the throne.

The Al Thani family has governed the peninsula since the 19th century. Qatar gained independence from the U.K. in 1971. A constitution approved by referendum in 2003 makes the emir head of state. He appoints the prime minister and the cabinet. Qatar held its first elections to the Shura Council, its legislative body, in 2021.

The economic base is the North Field, an offshore gas reservoir in the Persian Gulf and one of the largest in the world. Iran shares the same formation and calls its portion South Pars. State-owned QatarEnergy, known as Qatar Petroleum until 2021, develops the field. It exports liquefied natural gas to buyers across Asia and Europe.

Gas revenue flows into the Qatar Investment Authority, the state's sovereign wealth fund, which was established in 2005. The fund is a state asset, not part of the emir's private estate. Its investments reach well beyond the Gulf. Qatar Holding, a subsidiary of the fund, bought the London department store Harrods in 2010.

That overseas activity has made Qatar a visible investor in property, finance and sport. The country hosted the 2022 FIFA World Cup, the first held in the Arab world.

The emir's personal fortune is a separate matter, and it is not publicly disclosed. Qatar does not publish an accounting that divides the ruling family's private holdings from national assets. Any figure attached to Sheikh Tamim alone is an outside estimate, not an official number.

King Mohammed VI of Morocco has reigned since July 1999. His family is linked to Al Mada, a Casablanca-based holding company formerly known as SNI, with stakes in banking, mining, energy and telecoms.

Mohammed VI was born in Rabat in 1963. He came to the throne after the death of his father, King Hassan II, who had reigned since 1961. Both belong to the Alaouite dynasty, which has ruled Morocco since the 17th century. The king's constitutional titles include Commander of the Faithful, which gives him a religious role alongside a political one.

Morocco is a constitutional monarchy, but the king retains wide executive power. A new constitution approved by referendum in 2011 gave the elected prime minister and parliament more authority. The king kept control over the military, religious affairs and major strategic appointments.

Al Mada took shape through a series of mergers and name changes. SNI, short for Société Nationale d'Investissement, merged with the conglomerate Omnium Nord-Africain, known as ONA, in 2010. The combined group adopted the Al Mada name in 2018. Casablanca, where the group is based, is Morocco's commercial capital.

Banking is the best-known part of the portfolio. Al Mada holds a stake in Attijariwafa bank, one of Morocco's largest lenders. The bank also operates in a number of other African countries. The mining company Managem, which produces metals including cobalt and silver, belongs to the same group. So does Nareva, an energy company that develops wind farms. In telecoms, Al Mada holds a stake in the operator Inwi. Its retail interests have included the hypermarket chain Marjane. Attijariwafa bank and Managem both trade on the Casablanca Stock Exchange.

Al Mada is a privately held company, not a state investment fund.

The value of the royal family's holdings has not been officially published. The family's role in major companies has been a recurring subject of debate in Moroccan politics and media.

Prince Hans-Adam II of Liechtenstein heads a princely family that owns LGT Group, a private banking and asset management firm. He has been the reigning prince of the small Alpine state since 1989.

LGT is based in Vaduz, the capital of Liechtenstein. It manages wealth for private clients and institutional investors. The firm ties the princely house directly to financial services, a sector that plays a large role in the country's economy.

Hans-Adam was born in 1945. He studied business economics at the University of St. Gallen in Switzerland. He succeeded his father, Prince Franz Joseph II, who died in November 1989.

In 2004, he handed day-to-day government duties to his son, Hereditary Prince Alois. Hans-Adam remains head of state. Alois exercises the sovereign's powers on his father's behalf.

Liechtenstein's monarch holds more political power than most of Europe's reigning royals. In a 2003 referendum, voters approved constitutional changes proposed by Hans-Adam that confirmed the prince's veto over legislation. The changes also gave the prince the power to dismiss the government.

Voters returned to the question in 2012. They rejected a proposal that would have stripped the prince of his power to veto the results of referendums.

The principality sits between Switzerland and Austria. It is one of only two doubly landlocked countries in the world, along with Uzbekistan. Liechtenstein uses the Swiss franc as its currency.

The family's history predates the country. Its name comes from Liechtenstein Castle in Lower Austria. The principality was created in 1719, when Holy Roman Emperor Charles VI combined the lordship of Schellenberg and the county of Vaduz.

Beyond banking, the princely family holds the Princely Collections, a private art collection that includes works by Peter Paul Rubens. Pieces from the collection are displayed at the Liechtenstein Garden Palace in Vienna.

The family lives at Vaduz Castle, which overlooks the capital. It does not publish a total value for its holdings.

King Charles III receives his private income from the Duchy of Lancaster, an estate held by the reigning British monarch since 1399. The Duchy of Cornwall is the other main income-producing estate tied to the monarchy. It now provides income to his heir.

Charles acceded in September 2022 after the death of his mother, Queen Elizabeth II. His coronation took place at Westminster Abbey in May 2023. Before that, he was the longest-serving heir apparent in British history.

The Duchy of Lancaster is a portfolio of land, property and financial investments, mostly in England and Wales. Its net income goes to the monarch and is known as the Privy Purse. The sovereign holds the title Duke $DUK of Lancaster, whether a king or a queen. A government minister, the Chancellor of the Duchy of Lancaster, answers for the estate in Parliament.

The Duchy of Cornwall is older. Edward III created it in 1337 for his eldest son, Edward, the Black Prince. It passes to the monarch's eldest son when he is heir to the throne. Charles held it from 1952 until his accession. It then passed to Prince William.

Most of the Duchy of Cornwall's land lies in southwest England, including large parts of Dartmoor. It also owns property in Kennington, in south London. Under Charles, the duchy built Poundbury, an urban extension of Dorchester in Dorset.

The Crown Estate is a separate matter. It belongs to the monarch by virtue of the office, not as private property. Its revenue goes to the Treasury. A share of its profits funds the Sovereign Grant, which pays for the monarch's official duties. The Sovereign Grant Act 2011 replaced the older Civil List system with this arrangement.

Some royal property is personal. Sandringham in Norfolk and Balmoral in Scotland are privately owned by the monarch and pass by inheritance. Prince Albert bought Balmoral in 1852. Sandringham was bought in 1862 for the future Edward VII.

The monarch has paid income tax voluntarily since 1993, including on income from the Duchy of Lancaster. Charles's total personal fortune has not been publicly disclosed.

Prince Albert II has reigned over Monaco since 2005 and heads the House of Grimaldi, one of Europe's oldest ruling dynasties. The family's assets are not publicly itemized, and no verified breakdown of its holdings is available.

Albert became sovereign prince in 2005 after the death of his father, Prince Rainier III. Rainier had been on the throne since 1949. The handover was the principality's first change of ruler in more than half a century.

Rainier married the American actress Grace Kelly in 1956. Albert, born in 1958, is their only son.

The Grimaldi claim to Monaco dates to 1297. That year, François Grimaldi seized the fortress on the Rock, the outcrop that still holds the princely palace. The family has ruled the territory for most of the centuries since.

Monaco sits on the Mediterranean coast and is bordered on land by France. It is the world's second-smallest independent state, after Vatican City. Monaco is not a member of the European Union, but it uses the euro under an agreement with the EU.

The principality levies no personal income tax on most residents. French nationals living there are an exception. They are taxed by France under a 1963 agreement between the two countries.

The Monégasque state holds a majority stake in Société des Bains de Mer, the company founded in 1863 that runs the Monte-Carlo Casino. That stake belongs to the government, not to the Grimaldi family as private owners.

The prince's role is not purely ceremonial. Under Monaco's 1962 constitution, the sovereign holds executive authority. He also shares legislative power with the National Council, the principality's elected parliament.

In 2006, Albert set up the Prince Albert II of Monaco Foundation, which funds environmental projects.

The line of succession runs through Albert's children. He married Charlene Wittstock in 2011. Their son, Hereditary Prince Jacques, was born in 2014 alongside his twin sister, Princess Gabriella. Male-preference succession places Jacques first in line to the throne.
