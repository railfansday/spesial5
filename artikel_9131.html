GRRIZ’s moiré sculpture set atop paris parking entrance moves only when people walk past it

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

installed on the parvis de la grande arche, grriz’s moiré sculpture relies is activated by the movement of passersby and heightened at a commuter’s walking speed.

GRRIZ edited by Iñaki Laguardia November 19, 2026 4 min

Installed on the parvis de la Grande Arche at Paris La Défense, CROMAWALTZ is a permanent, unpowered sculpture by Italian-French studio GRRIZ. Commissioned by Q-Park for Paris La Défense and curated by Archibald Verney-Carron (Art Entreprise), the project marks the studio’s first permanent installation in France. Crowned atop the cylindrical granite entrance volume of the Les 4 Temps P2 parking garage, the facade wraps the infrastructure in an undulating profile without motors or electricity. Instead, two superimposed grids of colored vertical slats generate an optical moiré effect, producing shifting waves and figures activated solely by the changing viewpoint of passing pedestrians, calibrated to peak at the brisk walking pace of daily commuters.

Designed by GRRIZ studio in response to the concrete, glass, and long horizontal sightlines of the La Défense esplanade, the installation shifts visually according to viewing distance. From afar, the structure reads as a unified colored mass against the skyline; at closer range, the overlapping grids separate and the chromatic interference becomes visually dynamic. Its three-tone palette draws directly from the site: dark gray echoes the surrounding concrete architecture, blue reflects the open sky, and yellow introduces a high-contrast accent that sharpens the optical resonance between the layers. As passersby alter their pace, step backward, or shift their angle of view to track the shifting patterns, the static installation prompts subtle, unscripted movements across an otherwise linear commuter plaza.
