Synology C3RB3R Ransomware: SA-26:13 vs SA-24:20 Fixes

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Posted on Nov 7 • Originally published at aitecharchive.com

Synology C3RB3R ransomware is encrypting internet-exposed Synology NAS devices that run outdated DSM, and the verdict is simple: install a DSM build that satisfies the newer advisory, SA-26:13, because it also supersedes the older SA-24:20 fix. On October 29, 2026, Synology publicly warned that attackers are exploiting a flaw patched in December 2024, and BleepingComputer reported the wave on November 2, 2026. Two September 2026 flaws, CVE-2026-13639 and CVE-2026-13684, both carry a CVSS score of 9.8 and are the entry points named by one security vendor, so patching to the newest build closes both possibilities at once.

Synology C3RB3R ransomware is a file-encrypting threat that Qihoo 360's anti-ransomware team classifies as a Cerber variant. The team says it began fielding help requests from NAS owners in early October 2026; all malware-analysis claims here come from its sampling, and Synology has not confirmed them.

According to Qihoo 360, each file gets its own 256-bit ChaCha8 key wrapped with an embedded RSA-2048 key and appended to the file. It fully encrypts the 187 priority extensions, partially encrypts others, and skips 27 system directories, keeping the NAS running so the victim can read the note.

The note tells victims to buy a "C3rb3r Decryptor" from a dark-web portal, and each note carries a six-digit device ID. Qihoo 360 lists the analysed sample's MD5 as a1375e0f82ae86df528876c049b5a23c.

Both matter, but SA-26:13 is the one to aim for. Synology's own warning refers to a vulnerability patched in December 2024, which maps to advisory Synology-SA-24:20. Qihoo 360 instead attributes the intrusions to flaws fixed in Synology-SA-26:13, published September 18, 2026. Synology has not said which flaw the current wave uses, so a cautious admin treats both as live.

The practical difference is build numbers: on shared branches the SA-26:13 build is later, so reaching it satisfies both advisories. But DSM 7.2, 7.1.1 and 6.2.4 appear only in the SA-24:20 list, so those units cannot reach the September fix without a newer branch. If a unit is stuck there, patching still helps, but taking it off the internet is the stronger control.

Work through these steps in order; build numbers come from Synology's two advisories.

Patching does not repair a unit that was already breached. An attacker with arbitrary file write may have left persistence, so a patched box with the indicators present is still an incident. Edge appliances fail the same way, as in our Ivanti Connect Secure zero-days and CISA patch deadline coverage.

Isolate the device from the network first, by unplugging the network cable rather than powering it off. ThaiCERT's advisory of November 3, 2026 recommends preserving evidence, keeping the ransom note and logs, and assessing scope before any recovery or rebuild. Wiping first destroys the information you need to learn how the intruder got in.

Do not pay: no free decryptor existed as of November 2, 2026, and payment guarantees nothing. Restore from a backup the NAS cannot write to, then rebuild on a patched build with credentials changed, and treat every account used with the device as exposed. Nobody has published a victim count. The figure in circulation, more than 380,000 internet-exposed Synology NAS units, comes from the Shadowserver Foundation dashboard. That measures exposure, not infections.

As of catalogue version 2026.11.04, released November 4, 2026, none of CVE-2026-13639, CVE-2026-13684 or CVE-2024-10441 appears in CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. There has also been no law-enforcement announcement and no attribution to a named group or country. Absence from KEV is not a reason to wait, since entries often follow confirmed exploitation.

Q: Is there a free decryptor for C3RB3R ransomware? A: No public decryptor was available as of November 2, 2026. Restore from an offline or unmounted backup instead.

Q: Which DSM version is safe from C3RB3R? A: Builds at or above 7.4-90075, 7.3.2-86009-4, 7.2.2-72806-9 or 7.2.1-69057-12 carry the SA-26:13 fixes. Synology has not confirmed which flaw the attackers use, so these builds are the safest available target.

Q: Which flaws does C3RB3R exploit? A: Synology points to a flaw patched in December 2024 under SA-24:20, while Qihoo 360 names CVE-2026-13639 and CVE-2026-13684. Neither claim has been reconciled publicly.

Q: How can I tell if my NAS has been hit? A: Look for files ending in .L0CK3D and a read-me3.txt note in folders, and search for the MD5 a1375e0f82ae86df528876c049b5a23c. These indicators come from Qihoo 360.

Q: Is turning off port forwarding enough to protect my Synology? A: It removes the most direct route in, and Synology and BleepingComputer both advise blocking internet access to the management interface. It does not fix the underlying flaws, so you still need to update DSM.

For most owners the decision is not close. Update to a SA-26:13 build, turn on automatic updates, and take the management interface off the internet today. Keep one backup the NAS cannot write to; it is the only thing that turns this incident into an inconvenience.
