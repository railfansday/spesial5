Grand Slam permutations: How Littler can be knocked out on Monday

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Luke Littler, Luke Humphries, Nathan Aspinall and Rusty-Jake Rodriguez can all qualify for the last 16 with high-scoring defeats in the final group games; watch the Grand Slam of Darts in Wolverhampton live on Sky Sports with the final on Sunday November 22

The Grand Slam of Darts has a brutal new format for 2026, which means every match on Monday and Tuesday night has something riding on it.

The 48-player field is split into 16 groups of three with only one going through, meaning each player has only two pool matches to try and earn a place in the second round.

Monday sees the conclusion of Groups A-H with Groups I-P to be concluded on Tuesday.

Read on to see how Luke Littler, Luke Humphries and Nathan Aspinall can all lose their final matches and still qualify for the last 16...

Luke Littler can afford to lose to Niels Zonneveld on Monday night and still have a chance of progressing.

The Nuke simply needs to win three legs to reach the last 16 with Zonneveld needing to win 5-2 or better to reach the knockout stages.

Beau Greaves is one of the few players to be eliminated despite winning one of her matches.

Lisa Ashton has been eliminated after losing both of her first two matches, which means whoever wins Monday's match between Jermaine Wattimena and Alan Soutar will reach the last 16.

Eighth seed Josh Rock has been eliminated after two defeats, which means whoever wins the Kevin Doets v Tommy Morris clash on Monday will qualify for the knockout round.

Two last-leg defeats mean that Raymond Smith has been eliminated from the competition. Cameron Menzies will face Stephen Bunting in a winner-goes-through decider on Monday.

Joe Hunt crashed out after losing both of his match, so Gerwyn Price and Sebastian Bialecki will clash on Monday night with the winner reaching the knockout rounds.

Charlie Manby has been eliminated from the competition after suffering two last-leg defeats, meaning the victor in Monday's clash between Ryan Searle and Andrew Gilding will secure a spot in the last 16.

Jonny Clayton was the highest-ranked player to be knocked out of the tournament on Sunday, meaning Rob Cross and Anton Ostlund will face off on Tuesday with the winner qualifying.

Adam Sevada has been eliminated after two 5-1 losses, meaning the winner of Wessel Nijman vs Niko Springer on Monday night will reach the last 16.

In Group I, all three players are still in the tournament.

Luke Humphries needs to win four or five legs against Joe Cullen on Monday night to reach the last 16. A 5-4 defeat would still be enough for him to qualify.

Cullen must win that match 5-0 to get through - while Paolo Nebrida will reach the last 16 if Cullen wins by a scoreline of 5-1, 5-2 or 5-3.

Luke Woodhouse became the first seed to be eliminated from the competition as he lost both of his group matches. Whoever wins Tuesday night's match between Krzysztof Ratajski and Tom Sykes will therefore qualify for the knockout rounds.

Cam Crabtree was beaten in both of his Group K matches, which means this is another pool where the final match will decide the winner. James Wade will take on Ricky Evans on Tuesday with the winner going through.

This is the group where things could get very interesting with all three players still in contention to top the group.

Any sort of win for Michael van Gerwen on Tuesday will see him qualify for the last 16. However, a victory of 5-3 or better would see Jeffrey de Graaf take top spot.

If De Graaf was to beat Van Gerwen 5-4 then Henry Coates would still be in the mix as all three players would be tied.

The group would then be decided on legs won against throw and then the highest total tournament average (to two decimal places).

Group M is another group where the final match is a straight shoot-out for a place in the last 16. That match will take place between Gian van Veen and Bradley Brooks after Carl Sneyd was eliminated following two defeats in his first two matches.

Jan Schmidt lost both of his matches to drop out of the tournament, which means it's a straight shootout between Chris Dobey and Martin Schindler on Tuesday night to see who tops the group.

Gabriel Clemens is one of the few players to have lost a match but can still qualify for the last 16. He must beat Rusty-Jake Rodriguez 5-3 or better to top Group O.

Rusty-Jake Rodriguez simply needs to win four legs to qualify. A victory of a 5-4 defeat would be enough for him.

Group O favourite Ross Smith has already been eliminated, but can still finish second in the group if Rodriguez wins Tuesday's final match.

Nathan Aspinall doesn't even need to win on Tuesday night. He will qualify if he wins just three legs against Karel Sedlacek.

After throwing away a 4-1 advantage against Maik Kuivenhoven, Karel Sedlacek knows he must beat Aspinall 5-2 or better if he wants to reach the next round.

Kuivenhoven has been eliminated from the tournament but will finish second in the group if Aspinall wins.

Instead of eight groups of four, the expanded 48-player field is now split into 16 groups of three.

Rather than the top two in each group progressing like in previous editions, only the top player from each group will progress.

In the group stage, two points will be awarded for a win, and no points are awarded for a defeat.
