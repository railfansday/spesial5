AI is already in real estate, and its next opportunity is connection

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Surveys show broad AI use across real estate and title. The next opportunity is connecting data across systems so teams can answer file status, reduce re-entry and route tasks with clear review and permissions.  AI Summary

In real estate, a simple customer question can send a professional searching across several systems: “Where is my file?” AI’s next opportunity is to help bring the answer into view.

Imagine being able to answer that question with the relevant information already in view. The order, the outstanding requirements and the next steps would be available to the person helping the customer. That is an exciting prospect for an industry where getting an answer can still mean checking several systems and making a few calls.

For real estate and title, AI becomes more useful when it can reach the information people need to do their jobs.

We already have momentum. In a Realtors Property Resource survey of 225 real estate professionals, 82% of respondents said they use AI in their business, commonly for listing descriptions and emails. Qualia’s 2025 survey of 631 title and escrow professionals found that nearly 90% of respondents use at least one AI tool.

People are putting AI to work. Now we have an opportunity to connect that work across the transaction.

A connected workflow could draw on information from across the transaction. Think about what that could mean for a title professional answering a customer, an underwriter reviewing a file or a manager deciding where a team needs help.

Of course, real estate has its own complications. A title file depends on agents, lenders, underwriters, public records and production systems. Requirements vary locally. Acquisitions bring together companies with different technology and processes. Connecting that environment takes work, but the benefits show up in very practical places.

Start with a field conversation. Relationships drive this business, and representatives learn valuable information while talking with agents and referral partners. Capturing activity from a phone while it is fresh could help teams schedule follow-ups and give colleagues a shared view.

Picture that information reaching the appropriate team while the conversation is still recent. The follow-up begins with context. Colleagues know what was discussed and can help move it forward. A customer benefits from the relationship without having to start the conversation over with each new person.

Then look at intake. Purchase agreement terms arrive in a document, but they need to become information the production team can use. A connected workflow could extract those terms for a person to verify against the source agreement before transferring the approved information into production. That could reduce repeated entry and help carry consistent information into the next stage of the file.

For the employee, that could mean less time retyping details. For the customer asking about progress, connecting customer and order information could make it easier to provide a dependable answer.

Title review offers another useful example. An examiner identifies a commitment requirement. Connecting that finding to a trackable task in production, with examiner sign-off, could give the work a clear next step. The team could see what needs attention and track it through resolution.

These are the kinds of improvements worth getting excited about. They put better information within reach and help professionals spend more time serving customers and moving transactions forward.

Title professionals know that an answer is only useful if they can trust it. Connecting information should make its source easier to see and professional review easier to perform.

Legal description and survey checks are also areas to explore, including flagging potential discrepancies in property boundary measurements. Automation could help direct a professional’s attention to an issue that needs investigation, with qualified review determining its significance.

The same principle applies when an output affects a document or recorded instrument. Keep the source visible, establish the appropriate review and record the final decision. AI can help organize information and flag issues, while qualified professionals remain responsible for title-related judgments and approvals. In title, accuracy is the product.

Permissions belong in the workflow from the beginning, too. Decide what a tool can access, which actions it can take and when human review or approval is required. Log its actions so the team can understand how a result was reached. Those decisions help make connected workflows dependable enough for people to use with confidence.

For leaders deciding where to begin, I would start by following a single file through its handoffs. Where do people re-enter information? Where does a finding wait for someone to assign the next action? Which customer questions take several conversations to answer?

Choose one connection to improve, then measure the difference. Look at repeated entry, response times and whether customers receive clearer answers. Let those results guide the next step.

We have experienced professionals, valuable information and people already willing to work with AI. Bringing those strengths together can help make each handoff more useful and each customer conversation more informed. That is progress customers will feel. And that is worth building.
