Sen. Cotton urges Commerce to add Chinese memory maker CXMT to export ban list

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Letter to Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick says the Chinese DRAM maker poses a growing risk to U.S. prosperity and military superiority

Sen. Tom Cotton, an Arkansas Republican, is urging Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to add Chinese memory chipmaker ChangXin Memory Technologies, known as CXMT, to the Entity List, according to a letter first obtained by CNBC. Cotton sent the letter Thursday and wants the department’s Bureau of Industry and Security to place the company on the list.

"CXMT, one of Communist China's national memory chip champions, poses a growing risk to our economic prosperity and military superiority," Cotton wrote, according to CNBC. "The U.S. must take action to prevent CXMT from gaining a stronger foothold in the global market."

Entity List status would bar U.S. companies from doing business with CXMT and cut the company off from U.S. technology, Kyle Chan, a Brookings Institution fellow who studies China's technology development, told CNBC.

CXMT appears on the Pentagon's "1260H" list, a roster of Chinese firms that the U.S. government links to the country's armed forces. That list carries few restrictions for U.S. businesses working with the companies on it, Chan said.

CXMT ranks fourth worldwide among DRAM manufacturers, and that dynamic random access memory handles core tasks in computers and smartphones. Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron $MU control most of the market. CXMT's share of DRAM revenue rose from 4% to 10% between the second quarter of 2025 and the same quarter of 2026, according to CNBC, citing Counterpoint data.

Cotton also referred in the letter to CXMT's push into high-bandwidth memory, or HBM, a component used in the AI processors that Nvidia $NVDA, Google $GOOGL and Amazon $AMZN Web Services build. Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron are the three main makers of HBM.

CXMT's decision to move ahead "in less than ideal conditions" shows China's "desperation for chips and manufacturing struggles without advanced equipment and knowledge," Cotton wrote, according to CNBC. "More can be done to ensure that CXMT is cut off from the financial and technological resources that aid its supply chain and undercut American growth," he wrote.

CXMT went public in July, and its stock rose 466% in its first trading session in Shanghai. The debut lifted its market capitalization to 3.3 trillion yuan ($488 billion), making it China's most valuable onshore-listed company.

In October, global chip stocks sold off after CXMT announced progress on a new memory chip design. SK Hynix stock fell more than 10% in Seoul and Samsung Electronics stock dropped 7.4%, while Micron stock was down around 3% in U.S. premarket trading.
