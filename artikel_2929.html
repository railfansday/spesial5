Heat Should Not Chase Dejounte Murray Amid Trade Chaos

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The Miami Heat are hearing whispers about acquiring a new guard, but the target might be a trap. New Orleans Pelicans guard Dejounte Murray wants out of his current team, and his agents are already talking to other franchises. ESPN's Shams Charania wrote that "New Orleans Pelicans guard Dejounte Murray wants a trade from the franchise, and his agent Klutch Sports CEO Rich Paul and team officials have started working on a resolution for the one-time All-Star." This news has fans wondering if Miami should make a move. The answer is a hard no.

Murray's time in New Orleans has been a disaster from the start. He fractured his hand in his very first game and missed 17 contests. Later, he tore his Achilles tendon, which kept him out for most of the following season. He only played 14 games last year before returning to the court this February. His current stats are weak, showing an average of 13.4 points and 4.4 assists in just nine games. The Pelicans are struggling to find a buyer because Murray is seen as a negative asset right now.

Any team thinking about trading for Murray needs to look at the injury report first. He is two years removed from a major Achilles tear, which is a scary thought for a team needing reliability. Yahoo Sports noted that "Murray is two years removed from an Achilles tear. He played 14 games last season and is not a three-point threat." This lack of shooting is a huge problem for Miami. The Heat need players who can space the floor, especially when they are playing alongside big men like Giannis Antetokounmpo and Bam Adebayo who do not shoot well from deep.

Murray has shot below 31 percent from beyond the arc for each of his last three seasons. Even in a small sample of 54 games, his mark was just 34.4 percent on 3.6 attempts. This is a mediocre number for a guard who uses the ball so much. Adding a player who struggles to hit open shots next to non-shooters creates a lineup that defenses can easily pack into the paint. The Heat cannot afford to clog the lane with more players who cannot shoot.

The money involved makes this trade even less attractive for Miami. Murray is due $32.8 million this season, and he has a $30.8 million player option for the next year. To get his contract, the Heat would likely have to give up valuable pieces like Andrew Wiggins or Bobby Portis. Sports Illustrated's Chris Mannix wrote that "Two team officials I talked to said they would ask for a first-round pick to absorb Murray's contract." Why would Miami send out a first-round pick and good players for a guard with limited upside?

Even if the Heat could make the numbers work, the risk is too high. Murray was once an All-Star, but his recent form suggests he has lost his edge. He has regressed as a playmaker and a defender since his early years. The Pelicans front office has not helped his situation, but Murray has not helped himself either. He has looked disinterested on the court and has made cryptic comments about the team's direction. Trading for a disgruntled player who might not want to be there is a recipe for failure.

The Heat have other options to fix their guard play without taking on this much baggage. They should focus on players who fit their system and have a clean health bill. Dejounte Murray is not that player. The Pelicans may need to find a taker, but Miami is not the right home for him right now.
