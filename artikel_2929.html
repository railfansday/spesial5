Wolves 104, Grizzlies 95: Defense Downs the Bear

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Basketball is a funny thing. One night, the world is obsessed with your offense-capping defensive stalwart and the next you're holding the number one offense in all of basketball to 95 points while that aforementioned rim protector has an incredible game (and still missed some layups).

In a game in which they looked dominant throughout, the Minnesota Timberwolves taught the Memphis Grizzlies that there is joy in being a young, upstart team, but it is still incredibly hard to face off with a capable team on a random Saturday with some of your best players absent.

No, luckily, the Wolves have seemingly learned this lesson.

Minnesota's nine-point margin is significantly smaller than it likely should be after the Grizzlies outscored them by 12 in the fourth quarter. Don't be fooled, this was functionally a wire to wire victory.

The Wolves employed a bit of a platoon plan on offense tonight, spreading usage to each of the starters and allowing Jonathan Kuminga, Jaden McDaniels, and Ayo Dosunmu to all turn in impressive point totals. This Wolves team just keeps showing up and playing well in a way that could not be further from what Wolves fans are used to. Memphis leaned on Cedric Coward and Most Improved Player frontrunner Ty Jerome, but struggled to do much of anything until a garbage time run.

This was a game that put the floor of the Wolves on display more than its ceiling. This was a close win against an admittedly great team with clear weaknesses. If we're going to start there, with the two-in-one ceiling capper and floor raiser, there's only one man to talk about.

Look, the discourse surrounding Rudy Gobert does make sense. He may be the single greatest floor-raising defensive presence since Tim Duncan or Hakeem Olajuwon, but he also makes those two look like Moses combined with prime Escalade on offense.

(And yes, folks, going from being a bad catcher of the basketball to a stone-handed anomaly who is putting up 40ish% on layups is, in fact, a major regression).

Tonight was one of those nights where everything looked decent for Gobert, even if he only scored seven points. Considering the state of the timeline over the past few days – if you are lobbying for Myles Turner, you live in a 2K fanfiction – it was nice to have a reminder of what a *good* Gobert game can look like.

Anytime Minnesota has a great defensive game, everyone will place the flowers at Gobert's feet. This is, after all, exactly what he was supposed to do here. However, the hand-wave of "Gobert was great" doesn't give him his full deserved credit. Memphis did not look at the rim all game. That meant that by the time they got hot, they were in two deep of a hole to get out of.

The man may be an absolute deadbeat, have the health/food knowledge of a yule log (maybe one that's worm-eaten too), but he is an absurdly game-altering talent.

It may be impossible to replace him, and no one seems willing to give up anything of value for him. At that point, it seems the future holds a lot of floor-raising. After tonight, that sounds pretty perfect.

Five different players hit double digits for Minnesota tonight. Jaden McDaniels (25 points on 11/17 from the field), Ayo Dosunmu (19 points), Jonathan Kuminga (16 points on 6/9 shooting), LaMelo Ball (on 50% from the field after a bad stretch), and TJ Shannon Jr. each scored over 10 points, and together they provided all but 17 points in this game.

The headliner is a hyper-efficient Jaden McDaniels game, but it feels like he is destined to never truly empty the clip, instead opting to be a capable third option no matter who he is sharing the court with. Some days, he's the number three guy to Anthony Edwards and LaMelo Ball; other days, he's letting Greg Monroe initiate the offense while on a 10-day. Tonight, however, he deferred to Kuminga and Ayo as the two turned defense into offense.

However, the real storyline is TJ Shannon, who continues to rise up the depth chart (much to my personal surprise) as another change-of-pace option in attacking the rim. His 15 points in 26 minutes comes after he looked to have found some confidence and comfort in his last few games.

There was a tweet from a few weeks ago that sticks with me about this team. It said the Wolves have on of the best 1-9s in the league, but have the most garbage 10- 15s as well.

Depth is so hard to really pull off, but the Wolves have the right kind of options. Instead of playing around waiting for a loophole, Minnesota just straight up outperformed and out delivered what the Grizzlies could do off the bench.

The Wolves will face off against Julius Randle and the Brooklyn Nets Monday evening, 6:30pm CT on Peacock, as the two former allies will fight over who deserves their low ppg elbow touches. Considering all of this is about raising the floor, the 3-12 Nets probably wish they pursued Gobert instead of Randle.
