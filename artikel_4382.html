Japanese Military Unleashes Beautiful Ace Combat 8 Recruitment Video

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Bandai Namco has been basking in the glory of Ace Combat 8: Wings of Theve since it came out to rave reviews on October 1, but the gaming world isn’t the only one to take advantage of the stunning tunes put together by composer Keiki Kobayashi for Project Aces’ latest game.

To celebrate the popularity of the game and the aviators that inspired it, the Japan Air Self-Defense Force (JASDF) put together a full music video scored to the Ace Combat 8 main theme, Everflow:

The video combines footage from the Japanese military’s air arm in all of its facets, including the JASDF Music Corps, which contributed to the song’s recording.

The liberal application of sick footage of fighter jets and stylish crews for propaganda and military recruitment purposes is nothing new, and nobody did it better than director Tony Scott, Tom Cruise and co. in the homoerotic naval aviation hit, Top Gun (1986).

Although the JASDF’s own attempt to leverage Ace Combat 8 into a recruitment drive is a lot more subtle, it does a brilliant job showcasing the different specialties and career paths available to prospective airmen

Seven years after Ace Combat 7, Wings of Theve is already number one on Steam and the second highest rated entry in the franchise's history.

Ace Combat 8 fans will recognize the F-15J and F-2A fighters from the game’s tech tree.

The F-15J is a domestically produced variant of the McDonnell Douglas (now Boeing) F-15C Eagle air superiority fighter. Built by Mitsubishi Heavy Industries from 1980 to 1999, the fleet has been heavily upgraded throughout its life, effectively becoming a multirole fighter.

In Ace Combat 8, the F-15J is the only aircraft of Western design to carry the High-Power Air-to-Air Missile (HPAA) special weapon. It’s a poor weapon against maneuvering targets like fighters, but you can send a whole generation of Sotoan bomber pilots to an early grave with these.

Also built by Mitsubishi Heavy Industries is the F-2A, nicknamed ‘Viper Zero’ as a tribute to the F-16 it is derived from and Mitsubishi’s A6M Zero of World War 2 fame. The F-2A is a heavier, larger evolution of the F-16 design, and holds the distinction of being the first-ever fighter to have an AESA radar. As in real life, its Ace Combat 8 counterpart differs from the F-16C through the ability to fire long-range anti-ship missiles (LASM).

Although the F-35A and F-35B feature in the video, Ace Combat 8 only has the F-35C naval version, which makes sense given the FCU Endurance is factually a protagonist in the game. The aircraft represents the late-game pick in the multirole tech tree on the Western side, with the F-22A holding up the air-to-air side.

Another nice touch from the JASDF is the presentation of certain squadrons depicted in Ace Combat 8 style, with badges and an aura-farming shot of the aircraft before all hell breaks loose.

Ace Combat is no stranger to collaborating with the Japanese military. In 2021, Bandai Namco released a Japan Air Self-Defense Force DLC for Ace Combat 7: Skies Unknown that added real JASDF liveries to the aircraft in-game and brought back a series of bangers from the franchise’s previous soundtrack.

Since the turn of the century, Japanese military recruitment has stood out against many of its Western peers for how it tries to leverage pop culture, including video games and anime (a whole lot of it), to engage with its target audience, instead of testosterone-infused montages or melodramatic beach volleyball shots.

The Japanese military made headlines back in 2015 with its collaboration with the GATE series, an Isekai manga (later anime) based on the Japan Self-Defense Forces fighting in a fantasy world.

Perhaps the most famous crossover of anime and aircraft in the land is the ‘Kisarazu Sisters’. Between 2011 and 2013, four AH-1S Cobra attack helicopters based in Kisarazu, Chiba Prefecture, got commemorative paint jobs with original anime-style characters designed at the 4th Anti-Tank Helicopter Unit. They were immortalized in the game world through War Thunder, with the AH-1S Kisarazu premium vehicle.

The success of these JSDF recruitment drives is outside the scope of video game coverage, but as an Ace Combat fan, I can only hope they keep making great videos like this.
