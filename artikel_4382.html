Logitech Gives Staff a Gaming Holiday to Celebrate the Benefits of Playing

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

News Editor at DualShockers, 10+ years writing experience, OpenCritic-listed reviewer, Master’s Degree in Developmental Psychology

This article was produced in collaboration with GamingWire

Grand Theft Auto 6 is going to come out on November 19, and while that has led to enough time-off requests and questionable sick leaves, Rockstar is not the only company triggering a big gaming holiday this month.

To celebrate gaming and the benefits of healthy play, peripheral giant Logitech has declared 'Logi Game Day' an official holiday for its employees. According to Logitech, the goal of Logi Game Day is to allow its staff to enjoy the cognitive and professional benefits of gaming.

Logi Game Day is set to take place on November 19. The company's gaming division, Logitech G, will be streaming live from its San Jose headquarters. The event will be hosted by general manager Robin Piispannen.

The PlayStation 5 has officially exceeded 100 million units sold worldwide in its lifetime.

In a LinkedIn post, Logitech G chief executive Hanneke Faber joked that this means employees will no longer need to catch the elusive 'GTA Flu' to dive into Vice City on day one.

The Game Day time off extends to around 7,000 employees, including interns, and also allows rescheduling to a future day. This will be especially useful for PC-only players at Logitech, given that Rockstar has not announced a Windows release date for GTA 6.

In the first-ever global Logitech Ctrl Tomorrow Study, researchers explored the relationship between technology and humanity, specifically how the two parties affect each other.

An earlier study centered on over 600 IT decision makers in the US, UK, and Australia showed that 82.7% of those surveyed played games daily, and a staggering 98% consider gaming an essential catalyst for workplace skills.

For prospective employees, 82% of IT hiring managers interviewed admitted that candidates who list gaming experience in their resumes are likely to be seen more favorably during the application process.

Internal evaluations at Logitech mirror these findings. According to Robin Piispannen, a former competitive gamer, the pursuit of perfection in games helps the team at Logitech G work better. The manager explains that he sees "how Logitech employees who sharpen their skills in-game translate directly back to work and problem solving."

Chief People Officer Elain Laird echoes the sentiment and highlights the importance of events like Logi Game Day: "When people have dedicated time to log off, decompress, and immerse themselves in play, they return with clearer focus, renewed energy, and fresh approaches to complex challenges."
