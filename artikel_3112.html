3 Dividend Kings to Buy in November and Never Sell

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Recent developments could drive strong total returns for these three Dividend Kings.

By Thomas Niel – Nov 19, 2026 at 3:45PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Abbott Laboratories ›

Dividend Kings are dividend stocks that have raised their payouts for at least 50 consecutive years. Currently, there are around 59 stocks that meet this criterion. These stocks have strong records in total returns, the returns generated by both price appreciation and dividend payments.

Yet while these high-quality stocks rarely become bargains, Abbott Laboratories (ABT -0.58%), McDonald's (MCD -0.61%), and Tennant (TNC +0.32%) have recently pulled back from their 52-week highs, creating an opportunity to capitalize on recent weakness to start accumulating a long-term position.

Abbott Laboratories has paid a quarterly cash dividend for 102 years and has raised it for 54 consecutive years. The healthcare company's shares currently have a forward dividend yield of around 2.3%, and over the last five years, dividend growth has averaged around 9.4% annually.

However, it's not just Abbott's strengths as a dividend stock that make it a strong buy this month. Consider other recent news, which may pave the way for higher prices.

I'm talking about last month's announcement that the company plans to spin off its nutrition business. This segment, which makes products like Ensure nutritional supplements and Similac infant formula, has contended with both weakening sales and litigation headwinds, and spinning it off could bode very well for what remains of Abbott.

Why? By divesting this business, Abbott can focus on a more focused medical technology (medtech) play, centered on its faster-growing equipment and diagnostics segments.

This stock currently trades for 18 times forward earnings, while comparable medical device stocks trade at price-to-earnings (P/E) ratios in the 20s or higher. If shares re-rate to a higher valuation and its overall earnings and dividend growth rate improve once the nutrition business is spun off, this could potentially lead to strong total returns in the years ahead.

McDonald's shares were hammered hard earlier this year. Mostly because high prices and the financial squeeze facing consumer households due to high inflation raised worries about long-term growth for the Golden Arches.

More recently, however, investors have flocked back to the fast-food stock. This comes on the heels of the company's Q3 2026 earnings release earlier this month. Alongside an earnings beat, management provided upbeat comments on improving performance at its U.S. locations and offered further details on its ongoing turnaround.

Some may view McDonald's recent rally as premature. Still, if further moves to improve fiscal performance prove successful, this Dividend King may have more room to run, given its current valuation relative to its historical valuation. Trading at around 20 times forward earnings today, McDonald's has in the recent past traded at an earnings multiple of 25 to 30.

In the meantime, investors collect the stock's dividend, which it has raised for 50 consecutive years. Backed by a steady stream of franchisee royalty and rent payments, the stock has a forward dividend yield of 2.8%, with annual dividend growth averaging 7.3% over the past five years.

Tennant, a manufacturer of floor maintenance and cleaning equipment, is hardly a household name. But just as its products are quietly used to keep commercial and institutional flooring looking its best, this stock has been quietly rewarding investors with annual dividend increases over the last 55 years.

In fact, this month, Tennant just implemented its latest dividend increase, with its quarterly cash payout rising 5% to 32.5 cents per share. Following this increase, Tennant now has a forward dividend yield of around 1.75%. Over the last five years, annual dividend growth has averaged around 6.1%.

Tennant may be the epitome of an "old economy" company, but make no mistake. This is one of the industrial stocks with a strong catalyst in robotics and automation. Its pivot toward selling automated floor-cleaning equipment is already helping drive a resurgence in growth.

Last quarter, revenue increased 6%, while orders increased by 9.5%. While ERP-related technology costs and increased investment in its robotics business are affecting earnings growth, these issues could prove short-lived. In the coming quarters, Tennant's earnings could improve, potentially leading to a rebound in shares.

Trading in the mid-$70s today, the stock stood at north of $100 per share as recently as mid-2024.
