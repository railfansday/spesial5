Is KKR a Buy After Its Latest Earnings Report?

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

The financial company posted an impressive jump in adjusted earnings.

One of the most seasoned and distinctive financial companies on the scene, KKR (KKR +2.81%) won some positive attention from investors early in the week. That's because the company started it off by publishing a quarterly earnings report that easily beat analyst estimates. KKR has always been a prominent company in its niche, and in the wake of earnings, it was a popular one. That doesn't necessarily mean it's a buy, however, so let's try to evaluate its performance on that basis.

While KKR topped consensus estimates -- more on that in a moment -- it actually saw a year-over-year decline in total revenue under generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) during the third quarter. This fell to $4.56 billion from $5.53 billion in the same frame of 2025. The drop was due to a slide in the business that comprises much of the company's take, insurance; this declined to slightly over $2.8 billion from the year-ago tally of $3.78 billion. KKR's total assets under management (AUM), meanwhile, rose by 11% to $804 billion.

The other arm of the company's operations, asset management and strategic holdings, rose during the period. The increase was modest, however, with only incremental growth to $1.76 billion.

Non-GAAP (adjusted) net income rose despite the revenue slide. It improved by a very sturdy 29% to $1.6 billion, or $1.78 per share. Much of this derived from a 27% jump in fee-related earnings to $1.3 billion.

With the caveat that the financials of asset managers and (to a lesser extent) insurers can be tricky to estimate, KKR's fundamentals were well above the pundit consensus numbers. On average, analysts tracking the company's stock were modeling only $2.69 billion for revenue, and $1.59 per share for adjusted net profit.

Essentially, KKR is a business anchored by steady revenue earners, namely the insurance operations and the fees and related revenue derived from its considerable asset management activities. The company balances this with its far more up-and-down investment activity, as it directly invests in a variety of assets to exit them at a profit for itself and its limited partners. When these exits are successful, the revenue generated can be substantial.

KKR has also been deeply involved in the capital markets for decades, and because it operates in both equity and debt verticals, there's a built-in hedge in this business.

The company is a financial sector powerhouse, and despite the ultimately up-and-down nature of its business, it usually manages to net a relatively high-margin profit. But all in all, it thrives when markets and companies thrive, and the economy may be heading into more challenging times. Since inflation persists, there's scope for fresh Federal Reserve interest rate hikes. Higher rates make the financing that powers KKR's deals more expensive and less available (therefore not as lucrative in the aggregate).

To me, all this means that KKR is a sharp operator, but one whose business model has a higher-than-average level of inherent volatility. I'd flag it as a buy only for investors with a long-term horizon who don't mind an occasionally bumpy ride along the way.
