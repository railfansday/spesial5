How to animate the history of U.S. statehood

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Posted on Nov 7 • Originally published at map-animation.com • AI-assisted

Originally published in the Map Animation Studio guides. I work on Map Animation Studio. AI assistance was used to prepare this illustrated cross-post.

Build a 44-second map timeline from the original thirteen states to Hawaii. Use timed state fills, chapter labels and three camera moves to explain how the U.S. reached 50 states.

Table 1 of the Congressional Research Service statehood guide supplies the dates. For the original thirteen, those dates record ratification of the Constitution. For the other states, they record when admission took effect. We joined the 50 names to the Census Bureau’s 2024 cartographic state boundaries at 1:20 million scale, then counted the states after each chapter. The outlines are modern and the colors describe statehood, rather than historical territorial control.

The film was assembled as an editable Studio project and exported through the Studio engine. Open the U.S. statehood example in the homepage gallery and choose its template to inspect the same actions and timings. The frames and Studio captures below are from that English project.

How the U.S. became 50 states · Play the finished example. The steps below teach a similar workflow, not an exact reconstruction of its settings. This map example has no narration.

The English Studio project with the statehood timeline at 21.50 s.

Use the dark data visualization basemap, hide base labels, and start with a north-up view centered at 96.5° W, 39.6° N (zoom 3.45, tilt 0°). Set a single scene to 44 seconds. Place the title from 0–2.6 s, then introduce the original thirteen from 2.6–7.6 s, in ratification order. Use one Area highlight action for each outline. This opening holds the same camera across the continental chapters so viewers can compare the map directly.

The first thirteen states during the 1787–1790 chapter.

**Check: **At the end of the first chapter, the thirteen outlines are filled and the counter reads 13.

Put the period, state count and event description in separate Screen text actions. At each boundary, target the previous chapter’s text actions with Hide layers; in this film the exit starts 0.22 s before the boundary and lasts 0.20 s. Chapters begin at 7.6 s (1791–1812, 18 states), 11.8 s (1816–1821, 24), 15.9 s (1836–1848, 30), 20.1 s (1850–1876, 38), 24.8 s (1889–1890, 44) and 28.9 s (1896–1912, 48). Keep one persistent legend at the bottom right.

The 1850–1876 chapter adds eight states, including California and Oregon.

**Check: **Scrub across 20.1 s. The previous labels disappear, the counter becomes 38, and California’s cohort enters.

Set the first new state’s gold Area highlight (#F4CE65) to start 0.12 s into the chapter and last 0.40 s. Add 0.055 s to each following start. At chapter start +1.95 s, add a blue Area highlight (#3878ED) for each new state and use Hide layers to fade out that state’s gold fill and border over 0.45 s. Leave the blue actions visible for the rest of the film. In the 1850–1876 chapter, California’s gold action starts at 20.22 s and its blue action starts at 22.05 s.

California’s Area highlight action. The gold fill starts at 20.22 s and lasts 0.40 s.

**Check: **After the transition, the newly admitted states are blue and the earlier states remain visible.

At 33.0 s, add a 0.80 s camera move to Alaska (151° W, 63° N, zoom 3.65) and show state 49. At 37.4 s, move to Hawaii (157.4° W, 20.85° N, zoom 6.25) and show state 50. For these two chapters, delay the gold entrance until +0.77 s and the transition to blue until +2.40 s. At 41.0 s, pull back to a view centered at 127° W, 49° N (zoom 2.52) to show all 50 states together. Preview the full 44 seconds and export a 1920 × 1080 MP4 at 24 fps.

Alaska is highlighted as state 49 in January 1959.

Hawaii is highlighted as state 50 in August 1959.

The closing view shows all 50 states in blue.

**Check: **The final view includes the contiguous states, Alaska and Hawaii, and the counter remains at 50.

Keep statehood and territorial expansion separate. A territory could be part of the United States for decades before it became a state. The film uses each state’s modern outline to make its admission easy to see.

Replace the example with a city you know, finish a short sequence, and review it end to end. Make one clear story before adding more effects.
