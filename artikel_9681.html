Melbourne, Florida, November 5th, 2026/CyberNewswire/--OpenMatter Network, a pioneer in decentralized infrastructure for agentic artificial intelligence, is positioning its decentralized computing platform as an alternative to massive, centralized data center development amid growing public opposition to new facilities.

The company says its approach can support continued AI adoption by enabling computing workloads to run across distributed resources rather than relying primarily on large-scale data center infrastructure.

The results of Tuesday's midterm elections have put growing public opposition to massive data center development in the national spotlight. However, according to OpenMatter Network, resistance to new facilities need not signal a slowdown in AI adoption. Instead, it could accelerate the transition to a computing model that reduces dependence on massive, centralized data centers.

“Voters have made it clear that they are increasingly concerned about the enormous energy demands, environmental impact and rising utility costs associated with massive data center development,” said Mike Anderson, CEO of OpenMatter Network. “But the need to deploy agentic AI isn’t going away. Businesses don’t have to choose between advancing AI and addressing these very legitimate concerns. There is another path forward, and OpenMatter is helping make it possible.”

Across the country, communities have expressed concerns about the impact of large-scale facilities on electricity rates, water supplies, local infrastructure and the environment. The election has brought renewed attention to those concerns and the policy choices surrounding new construction.

“The assumption that AI must be built around increasingly massive concentrations of computing power deserves to be challenged,” Anderson continued. “Our decentralized architecture allows computing resources to be distributed across many different locations. That means organizations have another way to move forward with agentic AI as communities and policymakers reconsider the costs and consequences of building more enormous data centers.”

The OpenMatter Network platform is designed to transform widely available computing hardware into usable network resources through its distributed architecture, algorithms and verifiable computing capabilities. By enabling independently owned computing resources to participate in a shared network, OpenMatter is working to make decentralized infrastructure a practical alternative for enterprise AI deployment.

“There’s an enormous amount of computing capacity outside the walls of traditional data centers,” said Renee Davis, co-founder and chief business officer of OpenMatter Network. “Our technology makes it possible to bring those resources together in a verifiable network capable of supporting real-world AI workloads. Rather than concentrating computing power in a relatively small number of massive facilities, we can distribute workloads across existing resources and smaller installations, creating opportunities for a more flexible and potentially more energy-efficient approach to AI infrastructure.”

The decentralized model also offers organizations greater flexibility in where computing resources are located and how they are deployed. This can be particularly valuable for businesses and government institutions seeking greater control over computing infrastructure, including those with requirements for data sovereignty and locally managed resources.

“The question is no longer simply how many more massive data centers we need to build,” Anderson concluded. “It’s how we can deploy the AI capabilities businesses need without imposing unnecessary burdens on the communities where computing resources are located. Tuesday’s election results make that question more urgent than ever. OpenMatter offers a new way forward.”

Headquartered in Florida’s Space Coast, OpenMatter Network is building the Verifiable Trust Layer for Secure Collaboration and AI Agents. Guided by the principle “Don’t Trust Data. Prove It.,” the company’s cryptographically verifiable architecture enables secure collaboration, governed AI behavior and mathematically verifiable execution across untrusted environments.
