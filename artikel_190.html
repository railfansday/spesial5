US midterm elections 2026 live: all eyes on last two razor-thin Senate races as Democrats vie to expand blue wave

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Tight races in Mississippi and Alaska could further tip scale for Democrats; blue wave has already given the party more than 40 new House seats

Live results: see results from races in all 50 states

Welcome to our live coverage of the US midterm elections – here are some of the latest key developments.

A number of closely watched contests in the midterms are still to be called, including the final two US Senate races – in Mississippi and Alaska. The Democratic candidates are hoping to oust Republican incumbents to bolster their new majority in Congress. Right now, Democrats have a 106-seat majority in Congress as we await final results.

Democrats flipped another eight House seats, strengthening their majority in the lower chamber. Democrats are now leading Republicans 253 to 157, with 25 races left to call, as a number of GOP representatives felt the weight of the blue wave that has swept their party out of power. Republicans held on to two House seats in the west as congressman Mike Kennedy won re-election in Utah and Amanda McKinney won in Washington.

Democratic socialist Nithya Raman was elected to serve as the mayor of Los Angeles, eking out a narrow win over incumbent Karen Bass, in a major shake-up in local politics that reflects the growing dissatisfaction with the direction of the city. The progressive urban planner had served on LA’s city council since 2020.

Harmeet Dhillon, a key Trump ally, resigned from her position leading the justice department’s Civil Rights division. Attorney general Todd Blanche said he accepted her notice. Dhillon is the first high-ranking justice department official to leave after the midterm elections. Democrats are expected to use their power in taking control of Congress to aggressively investigate executive agencies, including the justice department.

Abdul El-Sayed, the Democratic senator-elect in Michigan, said the new majority in the upper chamber needed “action” from their leader. On the campaign trail El-Sayed refused to back Senator Chuck Schumer, who currently serves as the minority leader. Speaking to CNN, El-Sayed reiterated that Senator Chris Van Hollen, the Maryland Democrat, would be an “amazing Senate majority leader”, but didn’t completely rule out supporting Schumer.

Joining the chorus of Republicans who blasted their party’s midterms performance is Senator Ted Cruz of Texas, who said the GOP were handed an “ass-kicking” in losing control of Congress. “It was not simply a bad 2006 midterm election, it was a Watergate-level repudiation,” he said on his podcast.

The Nasdaq share index had its worst day in 19 months after slumping 5.5% on Thursday amid growing signs the artificial intelligence industry will face tougher regulation because of the midterm results.

Donald Trump signed a proclamation that preserves the Oval Office and Cabinet Room as national monuments in his first official action after the Republican wipeout.

The US jobs report shows that employers added 165,000 jobs in October – a significant increase, defying expectations. Meanwhile, the unemployment rate saw a slight drop to 4.1%.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics is set to release the latest US jobs report at 8.30am ET today. Analysts have predicted a slight increase in October job numbers, while predicting that unemployment will stay around 4.2%.

A reminder that the last report before the midterm elections saw US employers add only 29,000 jobs in September, a sharp drop from August’s gains, and unemployment rose slightly.

We’ll bring you the latest as we learn more.

European stock markets have fallen sharply as a rout in US tech stocks spread across the Atlantic, after the Democrats’ midterms victory sparked fears of a slowdown in the AI industry.

A sell-off on Wall Street on Thursday was followed by sharp falls in Asian stocks markets on Friday morning. The investor sentiment then reached London, where the market is poised for its worst day since March.

The FTSE 100 index, which tracks blue-chip shares listed in London, was down 207 points, or 1.9%, at 10,575 points in mid-morning trading.

Germany’s Dax index fell by 1.9% in morning trading, France’s Cac 40 dropped by 1.5% and Spain’s Ibex lost 1.1%.

Susannah Streeter, the chief investment strategist at Wealth Club, said: “The tech sell-off has intensified, with the pain of the brutal sell-off on Wall Street carried straight through to Asia and knocking sentiment in Europe.

“Big worries are colliding – the build-out of datacentres looks set to slow, which risks shredding the spending spree underpinning the boom, right at the time that investors have become super nervous about returns on this massive investment.”

Meanwhile, Donald Trump has issued a statement on his Truth Social app calling on US states to embrace artificial intelligence (AI), or “superintelligence” as he prefers to call it, arguing that those which attract AI companies, data centres and investment stand to become enormously wealthy. As my colleague Graeme Wearden writes, global markets have been rattled by a Wall Street selloff in AI-related stocks, amid concerns that the prospect of tighter regulation following the US midterm elections could add to worries over the sector’s mounting debt and spending.

The post-midterms market rout has been particularly painful for Elon Musk.

Musk (who put $446m of support into Republican campaigns), has lost around $113bn of wealth in the last two days.

This is because his two largest companies have both been badly hit in the sell-off.

Tesla fell 3.34% on Wednesday, as investors reacted to the Democrats’ electoral success, then 10.7% on Thursday. Musk owns 17.7% of the carmaker.

SpaceX lost 3.8% on Wednesday, then 9% yesterday – which also hurt Musk as he owns 10.3% of it.

In total, Musk’s paper wealth fell by $33.5bn on Wednesday, and then by another $79.7bn yesterday, according to Bloomberg’s Billionaires Index.

Despite this, Musk is still the world’s richest person with a paper wealth of $785bn, more than double Jeff Bezos in second place with $370bn.

Tuesday night’s explosive rout of the Republican party was supercharged by the fury of Hispanic Americans who turned out in record numbers in key battlegrounds to protest the Trump administration’s anti-immigrant assault on their communities.

“Trump went against us,” said Benny Melendez from Weslaco, Texas. “He made us the target of ICE operations, so we decided to punish him by voting against his party. That’s what happened.”

Melendez, the owner of a small housebuilding company in south Texas, voted for Donald Trump in the presidential election two years ago, attracted by his promise to secure the Mexican border and improve the economy. But on Tuesday, he cast his vote for the Democrats in a display of pure anger.

Two of his cousins and up to 15 of his building workers have been picked up and deported to Mexico by ICE. When Melendez voted for Trump in 2024 he expected the immigration raids would focus on those with criminal records, he said, not his relatives and employees.

“They’re taking our neighbors. They’re taking our friends. They’re they’re taking our workers, and they’re taking our family members. This is an attack against our community.”

Melendez’s rage, replicated in the millions across Texas, Florida and other critical swing states, itself tells a story. It speaks to how Trump first wooed, and then alienated, the backing of many Latino voters, effectively shooting himself in the foot.

Following a difficult set of midterm results for the Republican party, Donald Trump has spent much of this week talking up his renovation projects in Washington. Thursday brought the first official action of his post-midterms presidency – a proclamation designating the gold-adorned Oval Office and Cabinet Room as a national monument.

With the dust still settling on the midterms, attention is already turning to the Republicans who might seek to lead the party into the next presidential race in 2028. JD Vance, Ted Cruz and Ron DeSantis are among those in the mix, as the Republican party begins to reckon with the fallout from its midterms performance.
