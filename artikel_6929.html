Patek Philippe's Golden Ellipse Reimagines a Design Classic

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

A watch that celebrates a mathematical ratio for natural harmony, now in verdant green.

Above: Patek Philippe Golden Ellipse watch in white gold, $43,011, patek.com

Few watches make the case for pure design quite as convincingly as the Patek Philippe Golden Ellipse. Introduced in 1968, its distinctive shape was conceived around the golden ratio, the mathematical proportion found throughout the natural world and long associated with harmony in art and architecture. Neither round nor angular, the curves of the Ellipse have an elemental quality that elevates its simplicity. Despite origins rooted in Renaissance mathematics, the watch also has an unmistakable groovy ’60s elegance.

For 2026 Patek Philippe returns to that formula with the Golden Ellipse (Reference 5738G-001). A larger case gives the watch more presence on the wrist, and at just a hair under 6mm thick, it retains the sleek profile that has always been central to its appeal. Inside is Patek’s ultra-thin self-winding caliber 240, equipped with a 22K gold mini-rotor.

The Golden Ellipse’s ode to nature continues through its use of color. The olive-green sunburst dial—and coordinating calfskin strap—recalls a shade found almost everywhere in the landscape, from leaves and moss to deep forest tones, shifting from an earthy green to brighter grassy flashes as it catches the light. Against the cool white gold case, matching baton markers and slender cheveux-type hands keep the dial remarkably clean.

Nearly six decades after its debut, the Golden Ellipse remains a design classic: mathematics, nature, color, and restraint distilled in one resonant shape.
