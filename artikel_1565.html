GdS: Milan ‘conquer Athens’ – how Hutchinson and Amorim’s changes inspired a comeback

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

When it looked like they had been knocked to the floor for good in the Europa League, AC Milan got up and produced a stunning response in Greece.

La Gazzetta dello Sport (seen below) begin their analysis this morning by writing that Milan showed their ‘true Italian colours in Europe at last’ in a ‘magnificent, incredible performance’. It took a while to get going, with two goals from the home side needed as a wake-up call, but it worked.

Ruben Amorim reshaped his side and they produced epic comeback, turning a 0–2 deficit into a 4–2 victory, with the Rossoneri suddenly appearing courageous, aggressive, and dominant. For one night, Omari Hutchinson was the god of Athens, with his Messi-like goal and two assists.

In stoppage time, the tireless Diego Moreira also got on the scoresheet. It’s a massive boost to both energy and morale, putting the team back into the play-off places. There is more work to be done, but it feels like a start.

Also worth examining is the Milan side seen in the first half and the beginning of the second, a period when Joao Silva’s quick goal to make it 2-0 threatened to turn into yet another cursed night.

The Rossoneri were off the pace again, completely at the mercy of Olympiacos’s frenetic pace and 4-2-3-1 formation, which hemmed them into the final thirty metres and stifled any chance of a counter-attack.

It was a case of no Luke Modric, no party. In truth, one counter did materialise, coming down the left flank. Despite being off-balance, the ever-present Cissé managed to get a shot on target from the ground, only for Pirola – an Italian playing in Greece – to clear it off the line.

Milan need to be built around Cissé, but in the first half it was Goncalo Ramos who occupied the centre of the 3-4-2-1, functioning less as a ‘false nine’ and more as a centre-forward who simply wasn’t there, much like the team-mates who should have been supporting him.

Milan got off to a poor start. Entrusting the protection of the midfield to the Jashari-Rabiot duo – neither of whom are a true defensive specialist – proved to be a questionable strategy against Freuler and Gustavo Sà.

In truth, the whole side struggled. Hutchinson was ineffective as a right-sided attacking midfielder; Cissé was never brought into play on the opposite flank; and Estupiñán – tasked with a hybrid role as both full-back and wing-back – looked completely flat on the left.

Amorim’s tactical experiments backfired, not least because De Winter was far from flawless at the back, while Diawara appeared somewhat naive. At the other end, Pirola looked every bit the Achilles among the Greeks.

He scored the opening goal with a header (officially recorded by UEFA as an own goal, as the ball ricocheted off the crossbar and then Maignan), and later made a crucial intervention to deny Cissé.

One could have held endless debates over why Milan wins five out of six matches in Italy, yet was on the verge of a fourth defeat in Europe. Arguments about the ‘pace of European competition’ feel a bit reductive: fair enough regarding Benfica, but we are talking about Ferencváros and Olympiacos.

Milan simply needed to restore their balance. Amorim took drastic measures: he brought on Yunus Musah to bolster the midfield and Pulisic to inject energy into the attack, while taking off Estupiñán and Ramos (the latter due to a knock), effectively reshaping the entire side.

In defence, Mario Gila and Koni De Winter remained (though Fikayo Tomori, who soon replaced the latter, performed much better), joined by Sankhoun Diawara. In the middle, Hutchinson and Moreira took the flanks, while the well-balanced duo of Jashari and Musah took control of the centre.

Up front, Rabiot and Pulisic flanked Cissé. When Jota Silva made it 2–0 just four minutes in, it seemed as though there was no effective remedy, but it was merely a matter of regrouping.

Moving away from his hybrid role as an attacking midfielder/central midfielder, Hutchinson transformed into a devastating wide player. In the 11th minute of the second half he won the ball in midfield, embarks on a brilliant slalom run and found the bottom corner.

From that moment on, the Greek side effectively disappeared from the match. They ran around aimlessly, lacking any tactical cohesion or ideas, while their manager Alguacil faltered with his substitutions.

The equaliser came from Cissé, who headed home a pinpoint assist from Hutchinson after more good work on the right. It was his tenth goal of the season (four in the Europa League, six in Serie A): when Cissé plays he scores, simple as that.

The 3-2 goal arrived courtesy of Pulisic, who executed a stunning mid-air half-volley, once again set up by the Englishman. Pulisic himself squanders a chance to make it 4-2 on the counter-attack, but Moreira eventually sealed the deal in the closing seconds.

It can’t be said that ‘we’ve found the real Milan’ even after the comeback, not with a Milan that possesses so many different identities. It’s clear that Ramos needs to reflect, but the urgent need for points suggests it’s time for Cissé and Pulisic.

A defensive-minded player is a must in a two-man midfield. If Hutchison is such a dynamo on the right, then Moreira could perhaps be ‘sacrificed’ on the left. And at the back, maybe Tomori was cast aside a bit too soon.

Half the squad is sidelined with injuries, yet there are more resources available than one might imagine. Six points is not enough: the team need another six from their last three games to make the knockouts. The night in Piraeus, however, is a start.
