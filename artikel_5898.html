US Army to proceed with Iowa detonation of degraded bomb materials: ‘A consensus decision’

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

American Military Project director Will Thibeau reacts to the federal approval of a controlled detonation of expired explosives at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant. Thibeau also breaks down a Wall Street Journal report indicating the U.S. suspects Iran is behind dozens of recent cyberattacks targeting commercial ships in the Middle East.

The U.S. Army will detonate 68 barrels of unstable, degraded bomb-making chemicals in Iowa on Wednesday morning after a federal appeals court rejected a legal challenge and allowed the controlled blast to move forward.

In a statement late Monday, Army officials confirmed they are proceeding with destroying the crystallized lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant near Middletown in coordination with state, local and federal authorities.

"This is a consensus decision," Acting Army Secretary Adam Telle posted on social media. "Every expert agency agrees, and both Iowa’s current and incoming Governors say the call belongs to the experts."

Hours earlier, a three-judge panel of the 8th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals lifted a temporary pause and denied an emergency request from the Iowa Environmental Council and the Sierra Club to block the operation. The judges found that the Environmental Protection Agency built adequate safeguards into its emergency permit to limit lead dust dispersal.

"Here we are faced with a situation in which the risk is between a controlled detonation and an unplanned explosion, not between the risk of a controlled detonation and no explosion at all," the court wrote.

The ruling followed a Truth Social post from President Donald Trump declaring he had ordered the military to hold off unless both the appeals court and Democratic Gov.-elect Rob Sand signed off. Trump warned he would hold Sand "responsible for any and all death or destruction" if the operation were blocked.

Sand, who takes office in January, declined to approve or reject the blast on Monday evening. He posted on X that the Trump administration should make the call based on the experts it brought in months ago and urged federal officials to release their briefing details publicly.

Meanwhile, a flight carrying Army officials departed Joint Base Andrews for Iowa just before Trump's post went live.

The Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown, Iowa. (U.S. Army via AP)

The operation has already been postponed three times amid public concern over toxic lead dust. Munitions specialists warned in court filings that dropping temperatures increase the risk that crystallized material inside the barrels could trigger an accidental, fatal explosion if left untreated.

In a public advisory, the Army said Wednesday's blast will produce two rapid detonations, ground vibrations and a dust plume expected to linger for no more than an hour.

An aerial view shows homes on Nov. 20, 2026 in Middletown, Iowa. (Scott Olson/Getty Images)

People living near Middletown, some of whom have already sealed their windows and doors, have been advised to stay indoors and wash outdoor surfaces with water afterward.

Fox News Digital’s Alex Nitzberg and Sophia Compton, along with The Associated Press, contributed to this report.
