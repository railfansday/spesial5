Why Midwest buyer demand is holding up in the face of higher mortgage rates

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Midwest metros, including Columbus and Cincinnati, are keeping pending sales near or above new listings as mortgage activity stays mixed. Lower list prices and smaller loan amounts appear to support demand, even with rates above 7% pressuring affordability.  AI Summary

Several Midwest housing markets are showing signs of resilient buyer demand compared to other parts of the country, with pending home sales keeping pace with new listings even as purchase mortgage activity remains largely flat or declines.

Columbus, Ohio, had 127 pending sales for every 100 new listings as of Nov. 6, according to HousingWire Data. Cincinnati followed at 125. These markets were well above the national average of 95 pending sales per 100 new listings, while Cleveland (91) and St. Louis (90) were running slightly below the U.S. average.

The same markets also had median list prices that were considerably lower than the national average of $429,000. Cleveland led the way at $174,000, followed by St. Louis ($225,000), Columbus ($255,000) and Cincinnati ($300,000).

In St. Louis, buyers remain active despite elevated mortgage rates, although they are being more deliberate about their decisions, said Greg Iverson, a divisional sales manager for Neighborhood Loans.

“I wouldn’t say it’s necessarily a buyer’s market, but it’s starting to become that way in certain areas,” Iverson said in an interview with HousingWire.

Like most buyers nationwide, affordability remains the biggest obstacle for his clients, Iverson said. Mortgage rates above 7% have added to the rising costs of property taxes, homeowners insurance, building materials and home repairs. Inventory has also increased in some price ranges, he said, but not across the board.

But Iverson said St. Louis continues to see activity from first-time and move-up buyers alike since the market’s lower home prices have helped keep these buyers from being priced out entirely.

A similar pattern is playing out in smaller Ohio markets. Scott Snapp, a Findlay, Ohio-based branch manager at loanDepot, said his business has remained steady, with loan applications coming in at about the same pace as last year.

When comparing his October 2026 production with the same point a year earlier, Snapp said he had closed 57 loans totaling $12 million last year, versus 51 loans totaling $11 million at the time of the interview. Snapp said his average loan size is about $215,000.

“I’m not seeing a drop, realistically,” he said. “Applications coming in are still at the same pace as they were last year around here.”

Mortgage data platform Modex shows that purchase lending has been relatively steady in several Midwest markets, but it has not uniformly increased.

Cleveland was the only one of four Midwest metros in HousingWire’s comparison to record year-over-year growth in purchase loans from January through July, with 11,809 loans, up 0.9%. Cincinnati recorded 14,048 loans, down 2.2%, while Columbus had 12,820, down 2.7%. St. Louis recorded 9,811 loans, a 6.8% decline. The St. Louis figure excludes St. Louis County since 2026 county records were still being updated.

Modex data indicated that median purchase loan amounts in these Midwest markets ranged from $217,075 in Cleveland to $294,566 in Columbus. Lower loan amounts and home prices could attest to some of the Midwest’s relative resilience. More than 20% of purchase loans in Cleveland and St. Louis were for less than $150,000.

But the resilience is still being tested. Jason Harris, a Columbus-based branch manager at Rate, described September as a slow month for contracts that would have closed in October. And he expected October to be one of the slower months the market had seen in years due to higher mortgage rates.

But he added that contract activity improved over the course of October as mortgage rates eased.

“I feel blessed to be in Columbus because it’s still an area where you can purchase an affordable home,” Harris said. “Affordability is something that is so desperately needed by buyers right now. … Even three-eighths of a percent is having a meaningful impact on demand.”

Harris said first-time buyers, as well as borrowers using down payment assistance and state bond programs, typically make up a larger share of his business late in the year. These buyers, coupled with lower-priced homes, typically bring down his average loan size in the fourth quarter, while also driving more Federal Housing Administration loans and government-backed adjustable-rate mortgages, he said.

Back in St. Louis, Iverson said the metro area has continued to attract interest from real estate investors, including those who are becoming more selective about where they purchase as costs rise in other markets.

Snapp also pointed to local employment as a source of housing demand in his part of Ohio, citing new distribution centers and business activity, including facilities opened by Amazon and planned by Sheetz.

“We’re seeing a lot of businesses coming into this area and opening up, and that’s helped us,” he said.

The market has also benefited from buyers relocating from other states, Snapp said. Some have moved for work, while others are returning to the area to be near family.

St. Louis and smaller Ohio markets contrast with areas where mortgage professionals have reported weaker activity. Iverson said he has heard of homes sitting longer in markets such as San Diego, while Snapp said colleagues in Las Vegas and Seattle had described slower conditions.

Harris said he expects demand in central Ohio to pick up again as the market moves into 2027, even if some buyers are postponing purchases until the spring. He said the region’s underlying need for housing remains strong.

“The number of people that need homes and want homes in central Ohio over the next five years is going to be good,” he said.
