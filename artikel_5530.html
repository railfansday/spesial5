Trump says Iran war could end "fairly soon" after Qatar talks, plays down new strikes

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Oil prices slid on Tuesday as the president called new U.S. strikes on Iran "quite unlikely," though Iran disputes that talks are under way

President Donald Trump said on Monday the conflict with Iran might end "fairly soon," following his White House meeting with Qatar's prime minister, Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani.

"We're seeing momentum toward the end of this conflict. I think it's going to be ending fairly soon," Trump told reporters. He said Tehran was "talking very politely" with Washington.

Trump described new U.S. strikes on Iran as "quite unlikely," though he said the U.S. could resume them if circumstances change. He repeated that Tehran will never be permitted to obtain nuclear arms.

In a Truth Social post, Trump said Qatar, Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Bahrain and Kuwait "prefer the status quo," which includes the naval blockade of Iran, according to CNBC.

Al Thani said he was "grateful for the positive discussions on reaching a deal and bringing peace to the region," according to Bloomberg. Bloomberg, citing an unnamed official, said negotiations are expected to continue and military escalation is unlikely while they do.

Large gaps remain. Secretary of State Marco Rubio told reporters on Sunday that the talks "hadn't been very productive" because of Iranian demands, according to Bloomberg. Tehran is demanding that the U.S. end its blockade of Iranian Persian Gulf ports and drop sanctions before any negotiations begin. Washington wants Iran to halt its strikes on vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz and to give ground on its nuclear work.

Iran has sent the U.S. a proposal through Qatari intermediaries under which the Strait of Hormuz could reopen within two days, in exchange for sanctions relief and a different shipping lane through the strait, according to CNBC. Washington was reviewing it, CNBC said. Iranian officials dispute that talks are under way. Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council, said Washington should stop “manufacturing fake news about imaginary talks,” according to CNBC.

Oil prices fell after Trump suggested the conflict might wrap up soon. Brent crude for January delivery was down 2.7% at $90.14 at 5:33 a.m. ET on Tuesday, a level not seen since August. U.S. West Texas Intermediate was down 2.4% at $84.78.

The U.S. and Israel began the war with strikes on Iran. A truce followed in early April, and the two sides agreed in mid-June to a memorandum of understanding that included a ceasefire. It collapsed about two weeks later, according to Bloomberg.

Republicans lost both chambers of Congress in the Nov. 3 midterm elections, according to Bloomberg, and Vice President JD Vance named the war as the main reason.
