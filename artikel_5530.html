Mortgage applications slipped again even as rates fell for a third straight week

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Purchase applications fell 2% and ran 19% below a year earlier as the 30-year rate eased to 7.30%, MBA data showed

The Mortgage Bankers Association said total mortgage application volume fell 1.2% last week on a seasonally adjusted basis as the average 30-year fixed rate declined for a third straight week, according to CNBC.

Applications to buy a home slid 2% compared with the week before, leaving them 19% under where they stood 12 months ago, according to CNBC. The MBA's purchase index has fallen in all but one of the past nine weeks and is hovering near its weakest reading since January 2025, according to Bloomberg. Refinance applications rose 0.1% for the week and stood 60% below their year-earlier level, according to CNBC.

The contract rate for 30-year fixed mortgages eased to 7.30% from 7.37% for the week ending Oct. 30, covering conforming loans up to $832,750, according to CNBC. Points slipped to 0.75 from 0.76 for borrowers putting 20% down, a figure that counts the origination fee. The rate remains nearly a point above where it stood a year ago, so borrowers have scant motivation to refinance.

"Mortgage rates drifted down to their lowest level since September 2026 last week in the wake of the Federal Reserve's decision to hold on additional rate hikes at its October meeting," said Mike Fratantoni, MBA senior vice president and chief economist, according to CNBC.

The average rate for 5/1 adjustable-rate loans eased to 6.29%, down from 6.41%, according to CNBC. Fratantoni said more borrowers are turning to adjustable-rate loans because they carry smaller starting payments, and ARMs now account for the largest share of applications since September 2025.

A separate survey by Mortgage News Daily showed rates moving higher at the start of this week after a report on job openings came in above expectations, according to CNBC.

Applications had dropped 5.3% in an earlier October reading, when the 30-year rate slipped to 7.41% from 7.50%. That 7.50% level, reached in the week ended Oct. 9, was the highest since November 2023.

The housing market has weakened since rates returned to 7% in mid-September, according to Bloomberg. According to Bloomberg, an index tracking contract signings for previously owned homes hit a record low in September.
