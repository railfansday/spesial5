Raising your auto and homeowners deductibles cuts your premiums every year, but it only works if you could pay the higher amount tomorrow without borrowing. Get a quote at a $1,000 deductible on each policy, and make the switch only if your emergency fund could cover both deductibles at once.

The Insurance Information Institute estimates that raising a car’s collision and comprehensive deductible from $200 to $500 can cut the cost of that coverage by 15% to 30%. Going from $200 to $1,000 can cut that cost by 40% or more. On a homeowners policy, raising the deductible from $500 to $1,000 can cut the premium by as much as 25%.

The catch is timing. You collect the savings a little at a time, every year you don’t file a claim, and you pay the higher deductible all at once on every claim you do file. Say moving from $500 to $1,000 saves $125 a year. It takes four claim-free years for the savings to cover the extra $500 out of pocket.

That’s why your emergency fund decides it. One hailstorm can dent your car and your roof on the same afternoon, and that’s two deductibles due at once. Some homeowners policies also carry a separate wind or hail deductible, usually 1% to 5% of the home’s insured value, that stays in place whatever you pick for the standard one.

If you couldn’t pay the higher deductible this week without a credit card, keep the lower one until your savings catch up.
