IEA warns that data center power use could double by 2030, straining grids

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The agency said at least 25 million kilometers of new power lines are needed over the next 10 years to keep up with demand

The International Energy Agency warned in its World Energy Outlook 2026 on Thursday that data centers around the globe could use twice as much power by 2030, putting pressure on power grids.

Data centers could use roughly 1,000 terawatt-hours of power annually by 2030 under the IEA’s Stated Policies Scenario. That scenario reflects policies governments have already put in place or announced, the IEA said. Constrained supply chains, resistance from nearby communities and insufficient grid capacity could all hold up construction.

Planned data center projects in several fast-growing markets would collectively draw more electricity than those markets use at their present peak if every one were built, according to Bloomberg. Fewer than 100 large AI-focused projects would require a combined 50 gigawatts, a load comparable to Great Britain's peak power consumption.

If grids are not upgraded, networks risk growing increasingly congested and less dependable, with new projects waiting longer to get connected and generators ordered to curtail production when the grid is unable to keep up with demand, according to CNBC.

Over the next 10 years, at least 25 million kilometers (15.5 million miles) of new power lines are required to meet growing demand, while 15 million kilometers (9.3 million miles) of current lines must be replaced or upgraded, the IEA said. Supplies of copper, transformers, cables and other key electrical equipment are tight, the agency said. It added that surging demand from data centers is amplifying strains in some markets and supply chains.

Data center investment is projected to hit $4 trillion worldwide by 2030, up from $565 billion in 2025, according to Bloomberg. About three-quarters of the projected increase comes from facilities built specifically for AI. Data centers' carbon emissions are set to climb 70% by 2030 as a growing share of their electricity comes from gas- and coal-fired plants.

Researchers led by the Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health estimated that hyperscale data centers in the U.S. produced about 25.7 million metric tons of carbon dioxide between May 2024 and April 2025. The facilities drew between 68 and 99 terawatt-hours of power, about 1.8% of total U.S. electricity consumption, the researchers said.

Data centers have also become a political issue in the U.S. New York froze most new large-scale data center construction in July, and Texas directed regulators to pause approvals of pending projects in August. Governors who ran on pausing new projects won in Texas, Ohio and Georgia in midterm elections earlier this month.
