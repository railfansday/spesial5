Media Mogul Omri Hurwitz Launches 'Live from Tel Aviv Stock Exchange' TV Show for Bay Area Audiences

The Tel Aviv Stock Exchange is serving as the filming location for a new television program focused on Israeli technology and its connections to Silicon Valley. Media entrepreneur Omri Hurwitz has launched Live from Tel Aviv Stock Exchange, an independently produced show filmed at the exchange with permission and intended for Bay Area audiences.

Announced in November 2026, the program will feature interviews with Israeli technology founders, introducing their companies, innovations and perspectives to audiences in Silicon Valley and the wider San Francisco Bay Area.

The show is presented by Omri Hurwitz Media, the media company Hurwitz leads. The Tel Aviv Stock Exchange has granted permission for filming at its premises. Hurwitz says the series is being carried on KRON-TV, Channel 4 in San Francisco, with additional coverage planned across Bay Area media outlets, including The San Francisco Tribune. Hurwitz introduced the project to his professional network with obvious enthusiasm: "I'm beyond excited to announce my new TV show."

Filming at the Tel Aviv Stock Exchange (TASE) gives the show a recognizable backdrop for conversations about business and technology. The exchange, known in Hebrew as הבורסה לניירות ערך, is an important institution in Israel's public securities market, making it a relevant setting for interviews with technology founders.

For Bay Area viewers, the location provides visual context for conversations about Israeli innovation, entrepreneurship and access to international markets. However, the choice of filming location does not indicate any official involvement by the exchange in the program's production, editorial direction or distribution. The arrangement is limited to permission to film at the premises.

The editorial mission of the series is direct. In his announcement, Hurwitz described it this way: "The show will focus on interviewing leading Israeli tech founders and introducing the Silicon Valley/Bay Area market to the amazing innovations coming from Israel." That focus places entrepreneurs at the center of every episode and treats the Bay Area audience as the market those entrepreneurs want to reach.

For investors and business leaders in Northern California, the format offers a window into Israel's technology sector without leaving home. Each conversation is built to introduce founders and their innovations to the people who build, fund and buy technology in Silicon Valley. The result is a show that works as both business programming and a practical introduction between two technology communities, delivered through long-form interviews on local television.

The distribution plan gives the show a presence across several formats. The CW Network is a U.S. broadcast television network, and its affiliate KRON-TV serves the San Francisco market on Channel 4, providing a local television outlet for the program. Coverage across Bay Area media outlets, including The San Francisco Tribune, is also part of the show's announced distribution strategy.

This combination suits the audience Hurwitz wants to reach. Decision makers in Silicon Valley take in information across many channels, and a show that appears on broadcast television while also being featured in regional publications has more opportunities to reach founders, executives and investors in the places where they already spend their attention.

Hurwitz also placed the launch within a broader view of the relationship between the two countries. "I believe this show is another strong testament to the amazing Judeo Christian values and love that are being shared between Israel and the United States of America," he said.

For Hurwitz, then, the show is about more than business introductions. It is a visible, recurring expression of the partnership between Israel and the United States, carried out through conversations with the founders whose companies connect the two economies. Bringing Israeli innovators onto American screens gives that partnership a practical and public form that viewers can watch for themselves.

Hurwitz says the first episode of Live from Tel Aviv Stock Exchange is already out, and he has invited viewers to check it out and follow him for more. The independently produced program uses the Tel Aviv Stock Exchange as its filming location with permission, while Hurwitz and Omri Hurwitz Media remain responsible for the show's content and distribution. News of upcoming episodes will come from Hurwitz and his media company as the series continues.
