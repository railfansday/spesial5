'As I understand it...' - Stefan Borson explains what Tom Brady will bring to Chelsea

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Tom Brady is unlikely to invest "meaningful money" when coming aboard as a minority shareholder at Chelsea.

That is according to former Manchester City financial adviser Stefan Borson, who exclusively told Football Insider that Chelsea fans should not be getting too excited over the new developments.

After cutting ties with Birmingham City, the seven-time Super Bowl winner has now opened talks with those behind the scenes at Stamford Bridge.

It is understood that Clearlake Capital would remain in full control, still owning 86.5 per cent of the Blues, with Brady given no voting rights.

This deal appears to have been in the works for quite some time. Brady was spotted celebrating with the Chelsea players after winning the Club World Cup in the summer of 2025.

There is excitement building amongst the fans about what his introduction could mean for the club, but it may not be as thrilling as many think.

Speaking exclusively to Football Insider, Borson expects Brady to make very little impact when coming aboard as a minority owner at Chelsea.

"The way these deals work, I think Tom Brady was given the shares in Birmingham previously. They were non-voting shares," he said.

"So I think it might be slightly different at Chelsea, but Chelsea is a much bigger organisation. He's not going to put in meaningful money.

"It's much more about being a figurehead and about seeing the upside of that. As far as I understand it, Tom Brady is not somebody who effectively fronts a fund.

"If you look at someone like Ashton Kutcher, he's effectively a major technology investor through his personality, but also he's built a fund.

"I don't think Tom Brady is a supporting equivalent of that. So if he would just be a figurehead associated with Chelsea, I think it would have very little impact to be honest, either way."

Speaking to Football Insider, Keith Wyness has already predicted Brady's deal to be worth as little as £50million should it be completed.

That would likely see the iconic NFL figure own a tiny share at Stamford Bridge, possibly as small as one per cent to come aboard.

This kind of money is simply a drop in the ocean for the Blues' current owners, but they would never say no to further investment.

Changes are still taking shape after Todd Boehly's exit, and it has been reiterated that the two stories have no correlation in West London.
