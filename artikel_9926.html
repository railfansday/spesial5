Simply put, there needs to be regulations to verify that the jobs listed actually exist and are clearly identified:

The company pays at token $20 fee to the state government for a job listing ID which must include

- The job city and state,- Physical work address- Employer's full name, physical address, phone number, corporate tax id- Parent company of the employer, recursing upwards to the ultimate parent company, with names, addresses, tax id numbers, stock ticker symbols- Date the job was registered- Expiration date of the job listing which must be within 90 days of registration- Full title of the position- BLS job category, job title, pay range- Number of hours per week- If salaried or per-hour- Duration of the position if it is only for a fixed term- Internal job number for the company- Employer internet address, employer careers page- Whether or not this job is related to a H1B or other immigration process.- A third-party recruiter advertising a job must also include their name, address, tax id, etc. and pay an additional $20 registration fee per job listing

The company would have to put up a $1000 bond for any errors.

Job listings must be advertised with the job listing registration number, and the full name, address, tax id, etc. of the company listing the job. Third party listings must be clearly called out.

That should be publicly listed on a state-level and federal level jobs listing database within the same day it is registered.

Each job listing would then require a filing by the original requesting company within 7 days of the job listing expiration as to the status of the job 1) Not filled, 2) Job listing cancelled, 2) Internal employee hired, 3) External person hired (US Citizen), 4) External person hired non-US citizen (including country of citizenship), 5) Visa status, type, issue date, expiration date, etc. if person hired is not a US Citizen, 6) Date the person applied for the job, 7) Date the person will/did start work, 8) If the person is a new hire or a re-hire of someone who worked for the company before

This will allow for:- No more ghost jobs, each job listing by law has to identify the actual employer, be for a real job, identify where the work is to be, and include a token $20 listing fee- A way for candidates to examine the job before applying- A way for candidates to see past jobs listed by the company, how/if they get filled- A way for the BLS and state governments to track jobs,- For employees to have a stated work location, number of hours, etc. for cases of bait and switch listings such as a job listed in St Louis which is actually 250 miles away 3 days a week.

A lot of this is already required by the Federal Personal Responsibility and Work Opportunity Reconciliation Act of 1996 (PRWORA) - 42 U.S.C. 653a.

And indeed, linked-in, etc. must verify that the job listing number actually exists in the federal/state government database before accepting a listing for the job. Included in this is verifying the name, employer address, dates, etc..

And, importantly, a way for individual US citizens to report fake job advertisements, incorrect information on a job listing to collect $500 fee for identifying the fake / factually incorrect job.
