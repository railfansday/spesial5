From one of Ajax's future stars to quitting football for music

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Elikia Mbinga made his Eredivisie debut for PEC Zwolle in September 2021

Elikia Mbinga dreamed of becoming a professional footballer and playing in sold-out arenas after captaining an Ajax side brimming with future stars.

But at 25, his life has taken an unexpected turn. Rather than showcasing his creativity on the pitch, Mbinga is making a name for himself through music.

He has become one of the most successful emerging afrobeat artists in the Netherlands, with more than 30 million Spotify streams.

It is a world away from Ajax's famed De Toekomst academy, where he trained alongside a host of Dutch talents who have since made their mark in the Premier League - from the Timber brothers to Ryan Gravenberch and Brian Brobbey.

It is clear the lessons Mbinga learned during his esteemed footballing education have helped shape his identity as an artist.

"As a footballer, I played in midfield and was a very creative player. That was my style on the pitch, and I try to bring that same creativity to my music," he tells BBC Sport.

"I want to experiment with songs, be as creative as possible and develop a sound of my own."

Mbinga (second from right) during his time at Ajax

By the age of 17, Mbinga was living the dream of many aspiring footballers in the Netherlands.

After impressing at boyhood club PEC Zwolle, he earned a move to Ajax and began a new life in Amsterdam away from his mother and seven siblings.

During his four seasons at the club, he shared the dressing room with brothers Quinten and Jurrien Timber, now of Crystal Palace and Arsenal, who joined Ajax from Feyenoord around the same time Mbinga arrived from Zwolle.

"We had a feeling that we had joined this journey together," Mbinga recalls. "We stepped in together. Already then, you could see that Quinten and Jurrien were like machines. They were built like machines from a young age."

He remembers the technical elegance of Liverpool midfielder Gravenberch and the physical prowess of striker Brobbey, who went on to score 56 goals in 163 appearances for Ajax's first team before moving to Sunderland last September.

Mbinga also had the opportunity to test himself against some of Europe's biggest clubs, including Real Madrid, Manchester United and Manchester City.

At one point, he even captained the group and saw little to separate them in terms of ability. However, their careers would take very different paths.

"In terms of talent, there wasn't really a big difference at the time," he says.

"Then, maybe two or three years later, Ryan Gravenberch had already made his first-team debut for Ajax. So it's all about development, right? And the same goes for the other guys."

Looking back, Mbinga now deeply treasures those years and the opportunity to have played alongside such talented footballers.

"I played with some amazing, talented players, and at the time, you already knew that they were really talented, right? It's the highest level," he says.

"But you don't know that they're going to become Premier League stars in the upcoming years. So that's amazing to look back at."

After struggling to break through, Mbinga returned to PEC Zwolle in 2021 at the age of 20, where the familiar, less pressurised surroundings prompted him to consider life beyond football and his ambition to build something of his own.

"I think it was also part of the personal growth. I really liked the music. I also liked looking up to the other artists at the time who were popping," Mbinga says.

"For me, it was also a dream to do something in music, create my own songs. But at that time, I was really talented with football, but I couldn't make music yet."

Mbinga made his professional debut for his local side in September 2021, coming off the bench against Ajax, of all teams, in the Eredivisie.

But the youngster would make just five further first-team appearances as Zwolle were relegated, before dropping down the Dutch football pyramid.

He ended up playing semi-professionally in the fourth tier to fund his music career.

"When I made my professional debut, everything went really well. I signed the contract and everything," he says.

"But the purpose of creating something for myself in terms of music, being very creative, was getting stronger."

Five years on, that gamble is paying off. He has almost 500,000 monthly Spotify listeners, while his 2024 track Mentality has surpassed 16 million streams.

Every now and again, making music offers Mbinga a thrill similar to that he once chased on the football pitch.

"For me, all the achievements in music are like I'm scoring a goal or I'm giving an assist or playing a good match," he laughs.

"And of course, if one of my songs really takes off, then it feels like I'm scoring the winning goal in a very important game - like a final!"

AS Monaco and Spain forward Ansu Fati (left) with Elikia Mbinga

Although Mbinga remains content with his decision not to pursue a career in professional football, he remains closely connected to the sport that shaped his life.

Earlier this month, he travelled to the United Kingdom to promote his new album, First of a Kind, allowing him to reunite with former Ajax team-mates Jurrien and Quinten Timber.

Mbinga attended an Arsenal Champions League match at Emirates Stadium and a Premier League Crystal Palace game at Selhurst Park.

"I'm really focused on creating this world for the people, for the fans," says Mbinga. "Creating a world that can unite football and music - like a creative bridge."

Another footballer supporting his musical journey is Spain forward Ansu Fati, who plays for AS Monaco.

The pair met as teenagers at a youth tournament in Europe when Fati was at Barcelona and have remained close friends ever since.

In February, Mbinga released a song dedicated to the forward and the challenges he has faced since breaking through at Barcelona in 2019.

Fati was seen as one of the new generation of La Masia stars at Camp Nou, making 51 appearances for the first team in 2022-23.

But he was loaned to Brighton the following season and struggled for minutes on his return to the Catalan giants, eventually joining Monaco on loan last season in a deal that became permanent in the summer.

Mbinga wrote the track in 2024 but agreed with Fati to release it only when the "time was right".

"You know what's crazy? Seeing him again and seeing everything that he's gone through," he recalls. "We went out to eat, went out for dinner, and then he was playing my song in the car, singing it lyric for lyric.

"I was like, 'wow, this life is crazy, man'."
