I've only dipped my toe into Grand Theft Auto 6 thus far, but I'm already impressed by the detail Rockstar has baked into its world. One thing I'm always curious about with games like this, however, is how much of that detail is on the surface versus what's actually happening under the hood.

I'm still too early in the game to judge this for myself, but according to Rockstar, there is way more going on in terms of how NPCs behave than players will ever likely see. One striking example of this is how GTA 6's gang compounds conduct their drug operations. In an interview with IGN, Rockstar's senior design director for its open world Steven Messinger explained that GTA 6 fully simulates drug distribution from top to bottom.

"A big part of [the open world's design] was establishing deep backstories for almost everything, even if they were never meant to be fully explained to the player, and then really sticking to those backstories," Messinger pointed out. "For example, our gang compounds have a complex drug network we've fully mapped out: where the drugs come from, who brings them in and how, how they're distributed to each compound, what happens at each stop, and how they eventually reach the street-level buyer."

Messinger notes that "players will only pick up bits and pieces of this". But representing this process wholesale means that, when players do notice it, it always feels authentic and believable. "Having that structure in the background as something we consistently refer to helps everything feel more real, like this big machine is always running even when players aren't there."

While this may only be designed for players to notice passively, I'm now keen to follow this system and see just how consistently it holds up. But it's such commitment to representing its world that has led GTA 6 to receive near-universal praise from critics, and it probably didn't hurt when it earned $3.79 billion on launch day, either.
