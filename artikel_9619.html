Telemarketing Caller Numbers: How Countries Mark Sales Calls, and What 140 Means

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Countries take two broad approaches to telemarketing calls. Some give sales calls their own number range, so the digits on your screen tell you a call is marketing before you answer. Others leave the number alone and rely on caller ID rules, calling hours and do-not-call registries. India's 140 series is the clearest example of the first approach, so this guide uses it as its worked example, alongside the rules in the US, UK and France.

The approaches differ more than the goal. Each one tries to let people avoid sales calls they don't want, and to make the caller traceable.

India's system is the most explicit of these, because the prefix tells you the call type for every business in every sector. The rest of this guide walks through it in detail, and notes where other countries differ.

It tells you the type of call, and nothing about the company. Think of the prefix as a label on an envelope that says "advertising", with no return address.

You may also see the series written as 140xx or 140-XXXX. Those are the same thing. The "x" stands for the digits that follow.

The short answer is that no one has told the system you object. India uses an opt-out model for promotional calls. In its September 2026 notification, TRAI says a customer's consent is treated as existing until that customer registers a preference to stop the calls.

So your number doesn't need to be leaked or sold for a 140 call to be lawful. Being reachable and not having opted out is enough for a registered sender to try.

The US and UK work the same way. A telemarketer may call until you register: the FTC says US registrations don't expire, and in the UK a marketer has up to 28 days to stop after a number joins the TPS.

Other countries set fixed hours instead of bands you can change. In the US, the Telemarketing Sales Rule bars calls to a person's home before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. local time, unless that person agreed.

Outside India, the registry is the same idea. In the US, register your number on the National Do Not Call Registry. In the UK, register with the TPS, which the ICO describes as the list marketers must screen against before live calls. Neither removes calls you agreed to, or calls from organisations the rules exempt.

India's "fully block" setting is changing. The same notification moves everyone on Fully Block to Block Promo within 15 days of commencement. Block Promo stops promotions, and it doesn't stop service calls, transaction calls or government calls. If you relied on Fully Block to silence everything, expect these to keep coming.

Some use cases don't fit neatly. Debt collection is the best known. The concern is that a 140 call can't reach anyone who has blocked the category. We didn't find a TRAI document that settles the question for every lender, so take this one to your compliance team.

One honest trade-off: a 140 number is a visible label. Your calls will be recognised as marketing, and some people won't answer. That's the design. Good targeting and a clear opening line matter more than the digits, and consent you can prove protects you when a complaint arrives.
