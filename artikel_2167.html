‘A club I like’: Andy Woodman quizzed on Crawley Town managerial post as search for new boss continues

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Andy Woodman was sacked by Bromley last month following a 4-0 defeat to Leicester City at home. The dismissal came as a surprise for many at Hayes Lane and beyond, given the remarkable job Woodman had done in his five years in charge.

Crawley Town are currently without a manager after they sacked Colin Kazim-Richards in October. And, Woodman has been quizzed on the vacancy and whether it is one he would be interested in.

Speaking on BBC Sport’s 72+ EFL podcast, Woodman said: “Crawley’s a club that is very close to me. My son [Freddie Woodman] went on loan. I’ve grown up around the area. It’s a football club I’ve got a lot of time for.

“They have got some big games coming up against teams around them, and if they can climb out of the bottom, they can start getting that club back to where they should be. I do hope they do, because it’s a club I do like.”

Former Bromley manager Woodman will rightly feel hard done by when it comes to his sacking. With this, he will want to prove the decision-makers at the Ravens that they were incorrect to fire him.

Woodman could have the opportunity to return to management with Crawley Town at the bottom of the table, and a big task would await him if he did take the job. But, if the 55-year-old returns to the dugout and gets it right with the Red Devils, it would be another feather in his cap.

Crawley only narrowly survived last season, finishing in 22nd. Their slide from League One straight to the bottom end of League Two means the next boss has a big task on their hands.

Woodman’s time at Bromley was nothing short of incredible. He helped guide the Ravens to the Football League for the first time in their history, then went one step further in earning promotion to League One.

The 55-year-old was a big hit in just his second managerial role, and he would be backed to bring better fortunes to Broadfield Stadium if he was to be hired. If the Red Devils could convince Woodman to be their new manager, they can quickly start looking back up the League Two table.

League Two’s bottom side needs an injection of life, and Woodman has not written off a move to the club.

The big question is whether Wodman would be willing to drop down to League Two and take charge of the side sitting in a perilous position. His work with Bromley means he arguably should be aiming a little higher, but the connection between him and Crawley could sway him into taking charge of their rescue mission.

There is plenty of work to be done at Broadfield Stadium between now and the end of the season, but the former Bromley boss may see the challenge as one he wants to take on.
