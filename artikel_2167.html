Blackburn Rovers legend David Dunn leaps to Tony Mowbray’s defence amid ‘dinosaur’ criticism

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Blackburn Rovers brought Tony Mowbray back for a second stint in the summer, and it’s been so far so good for the 62-year-old. Rovers sit in a respectable 13th place in the Championship table with 21 points to their name, also defeating West Ham 2-1 in the last game before the break.

Mowbray has earned wide praise for his implementation of a more easy-on-the-eye style of play, helping the likes of Andri Gudjohnsen, Dapo Afolayan and Todd Cantwell thrive. And, among his admirers is Ewood Park legend David Dunn.

During a debate over the most desirable manager between Mowbray and Bolton Wanderers’ Steven Schumacher, the Rovers boss was labelled a “dinosaur”. Dunn issued a stern defence, stating: “Far from it. Miles off. He’s older, but his methods aren’t dying.

“Tony Mowbray is one of the best guys you’ll ever meet in football. I think the way he wants to play the game is brilliant; I think he’s great with young players and giving them a chance. The style of football – Blackburn from last season compared to this season are playing some really good stuff.”

Blackburn Rovers were in a tough position in the summer. Michael O’Neill had come in and saved the club from relegation to League One, but he rebuffed offers of a long-term contract and returned to his duties with Northern Ireland’s national team.

Experience in the Championship was sought by the club, but supporters desperately wanted a style of football they could truly get behind after Valerien Ismael‘s managerial stint to forget. Mowbray ticked both boxes for the Rovers hierarchy and supporters, and his return has certainly improved the mood around Ewood Park.

Of course, unrest towards the ownership and club chiefs remains, but Mowbray has given supporters a team and play style that the Ewood Park faithful can get behind. He wants attacking players to be expressive and creative, and he encourages his experienced and strong defensive players to be aggressive.

Not only has this brought results, but it has lifted the mood in Lancashire. Mowbray is a popular boss who the supporters truly want to succeed, and he’s shown he knows how to deliver results while pleasing the masses in the process.

At 62, Mowbray is the Championship’s oldest manager. He retired from playing in 2000 and took up his first permanent managerial role in 2004 at Hibernian.

Over the 22 years since his maiden job, he has led 899 matches in all competitions, and 513 of those have been in the Championship. Blackburn Rovers have one of the most experienced bosses still in the game, but his age should not be mistaken for dinosaur-esque management.

As Dunn said, Mowbray is always looking to the future by offering opportunities to young players. And although his style of play is less risk-averse than that of most modern managers, it puts the players at the heart of the game rather than his own tactical intricacies, which arguably makes his methods more timeless than most, given how quickly the game evolves.
