West Ham’s home problem summed up in one sentence – by a Preston fan

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Preston North End, the bottom side in the Championship, arrived in east London yesterday unaffected by the occasion and kept West Ham at bay for an hour until Dinos Mavropanos headed equaliser restored parity and saved Scott Parker’s blushes.

Two weeks’ work during the international break had accounted for very little as Preston withstood everything the Hammers and their almost 60,000 fans had to throw at their lowly opponents.

And therein lies the problem. Having surrendered the small, intimidating, sometimes downright hostile atmosphere of Upton Park back in 2016, the distance from the pitch and the wide open spaces of the Olympic Stadium are about as intimidating as a day out at London Zoo for our opponents and their fans who continue to pour scorn on the venue.

It just doesn’t strike fear into opponents as it should. Even when we score.

Isolated tiers in the stadium and poor pitch proximity do little to help the atmosphere at the London Stadium – as our opponents are finding out

Preston fan Tm Mercer, writing in the Lancashire Evening Post, makes it abundantly clear that there was no noticeable crowd reaction to lift the home side, as there should have been once Mavropanos restored parity:

“Aside from play-off finals, I’m pretty certain it is the first time I’ve been in a stadium for a league match with 60,000-plus through the gate.

Yet, when they equalised, it did not feel like the roar of that many people in such a cavernous ground. Give me somewhere like Gigg Lane, stood in their paddock pitch side, any day!”

For the uninitiated, Gigg Lane is the home of Bury FC with a capacity of around…..12,000.

Nobody is suggesting the Hammers fans were silent. The cursèd accoustics from the soulless bowl and sheer distance from the pitch play right into opponents’ hands.  And, ten years after moving in, it’s going to take a lot more than some safe standing and a couple of family zones to fix it.

Those who say it is a supporter issue seem to think a button can be pressed and we all suddenly start singing in unison like a choir on BGT! At UP, to a degree, this could happen in the sense that a few dozen start singing and in a compact stadium it can easily spread around quicker than an Alvarez Mexican wave. In our new home, you can have a few dozen fans starting different songs and you spend half the time trying to work out where it has started, what is being sung and where are they in the song as you wonder what song you may take up. The space between any group of fans and the rest of the stadium is massive…..not just because it is 60,000 people but because the fact the stands cover an enormous area as they border an athletics arena (being track as well as the jumping sports), not just a football pitch. At UP we were in a relatively packed environment. where you could pick up on any song/chant started in any part of the stadium and virtually look into the eyes of whoever started the chant (clearly an exaggeration but hopefully point made). All this doesn’t make it impossible to create an atmosphere at the LS (we have a good few times so far) but it makes it much harder. We need some help from the team as a whole and players as individuals…..show heart, determination, hard work, a desire. A bit of flare. It would mean everything. The supporters…….well, there is no doubt the fans attending the games are changing. We have lost too many disillusioned long term fans. We have had too many tourist fans(less so this season) More families attending – a positive but need their own defined area Increasingly more Arsenal type fans replacing traditional fans à la Arsenal (Highbury is a Library). The latter point shouldn’t be derided or discouraged. We want to maximise the potential of the LS but equally recognise the impact such fans have on the atmosphere. They need more encouragement, something to cheer about rather than start the cheering. Other than a number of games under Moyse, we have had few and far between games a moments to get excited about. I truly hope Parker changes this for us. Finally, to those questioning the atmosphere at UP……they are correct, some of the time. If the team weren’t up for it, the fans could go asleep. The big difference is how easy it could change. A piece of Sir Trev or Devo piece of skill, a crunching Bonzo/Dicksie tackle, a Sir Geoff/TC/PDC goal could see an immediate reaction sweep around the ground. It’s this immediacy that the configuration of UP enabled and the LS makes difficult. If the players can deliver consistent performances, the fans will react. Sadly it is far more likely to be in that order but for the moaners and groaners, OldTrafford is no different, Anfield is no different, the Emirates is definitely no different. And yes, I have been to these stadiums in recent times.
