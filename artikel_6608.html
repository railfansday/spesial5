Cynthia Erivo Brought the House Down at Lincoln Center’s Arts for All Benefit Concert

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

If there was ever a reason to get dressed up on a chilly Monday night, Cynthia Erivo made the case. The award-winning actress and singer brought her powerhouse vocals to Lincoln Center’s annual Benefit Concert, drawing a glitzy crowd to David Geffen Hall for an evening where culture, couture, and cocktails took center stage—all in support of the institution’s Arts for All initiative.

The festivities began with a lively reception along the Grand Promenade, where guests mingled over sparkling pours, a generous sushi spread, and food stations dishing out hanger steak, chicken roulade, and wild mushroom farro. Among those in attendance were Zac Posen, Tony Award winner Caissie Levy, and stylist June Ambrose, alongside a fashionable mix of New Yorkers, all gathered to toast the power of the arts.

For Posen, the evening was also something of a homecoming. “I grew up here in New York City. I first came to Lincoln Center as a young child for its concerts for kids, and my grandparents would also come here for the symphony. So I have a long history with this place. Music is a great connector and an emotional unlock,” he told Vogue on the red carpet.

The designer also has a special connection to the woman of the hour. “Cynthia is a very close friend. We’ve been working together since she first started doing press for The Color Purple,” Posen said. “She came to the studio and we lent her clothing for her whole press tour. We’ve had amazing collaborations, and she’s one of the world’s greatest performers and artists.”

Beyond the glamour, a meaningful mission permeated the occasion. The benefit spotlighted Lincoln Center’s commitment to making the arts accessible to all through its free and Choose-What-You-Pay programming. Since launching in 2022, the initiative has welcomed more than three million people to its campus for performances, educational events, and festivals. At its heart is a belief that the arts should be an essential part of everyday life—not a privilege reserved for a select few.

“Every dollar we raise tonight makes everything else possible,” Dr. Mariko Silver, president and CEO of Lincoln Center for the Performing Arts, told Vogue ahead of the performance. “We want to ensure that cultural experiences remain available not only to New Yorkers, but to audiences from around the world, because the arts are the beating heart of humanity. The arts tell us how to see one another, help us to feel, help us to build community.” In an increasingly digital world, Silver believes there’s no substitute for experiencing live performance together. “Those in-person moments are essential to our humanity,” she said. “AI can’t replicate that. Nor should we want it to.”

Then came the main event. Barefoot in a burgundy floral-motif gown, Erivo commanded the stage with a 10-song set that put her extraordinary vocal range in the spotlight. Her interpretation of Whitney Houston’s “I Wanna Dance with Somebody (Who Loves Me)” had the crowd on its feet, while her soulful rendition of Sting’s “Fields of Gold” offered a more tender moment. An exuberant performance of Phil Collins’s “In the Air Tonight” brought another surge of energy, prompting a standing ovation.

“I just sing the songs that I like, and you’re just here along for the ride,” she shared to the audience at the top of the show. “So I always start by saying thank you for indulging me. And along this journey, you might notice some songs—join me and sing along.”

Erivo also performed Bryan Adams’s power ballad “(Everything I Do) I Do It for You.” Before launching into the number, she told the crowd how she sees it as “the most beautiful love song ever written.” Fittingly, on the day that the new James Bond was announced, she also launched into Gladys Knight’s “Licence to Kill,” the title track from the 1989 film of the same name. “I really want to do a James Bond theme song. It’s a dream,” she revealed. “Since I haven’t been able to do it yet, hopefully it’s something I can do. Is anyone listening?”

For the grand finale, Erivo turned to “I Will Always Love You,” the beloved classic written by the late Dolly Parton and immortalized by Whitney Houston. As she unleashed its soaring final notes, the hall erupted, bringing the evening to a spectacular close.
