Ionna Slashes EV Charging Prices For Thanksgiving

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Ionna, the EV charging network that ranked first in J.D. Power’s latest public charging satisfaction survey, has slashed prices at its stations across the country for Thanksgiving weekend.

The company, which usually charges an average of $0.40/kilowatt-hour, is now asking for $0.20/kWh plus tax at all its DC fast charging stations in the United States. That’s a 50% discount, and drivers don’t have to do anything special to get it—just plug in and pay, either through the automaker’s official smartphone app, the on-site payment terminals, or the Presto app for Tesla owners who want to enjoy the Autocharge function.

For long-distance travel, the slashed charging rates can make a big difference. A single 50-kilowatt-hour session that used to cost $20 will now be just $10 plus tax, but seeing how many people are traveling across the country these days, multiple sessions will be necessary, driving the cost savings even further.

And it’s not like the cost reduction comes with any strings attached. Ionna uses 400-kilowatt chargers from Alpitronic exclusively, so there’s plenty of wiggle room for even the most power-hungry EVs out there. What’s more, many stalls have dual cords for each port, with a CCS2 connector and a NACS connector, so charging adapters aren’t needed at most locations.

Separately, Ionna has discounts for several automakers, including Mercedes-Benz, BMW, General Motors, and Stellantis.

The charging operator installed its first stalls in 2024, and it has wasted no time expanding its footprint quickly. On its second anniversary, Ionna celebrated by opening its 100th site, and the company now has 229 sites and nearly 1,900 ports nationwide, according to the Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center (AFDC). Still, the charging network is not slowing down, with dozens of locations under construction and plans to introduce 500-kW chargers in the near future.

Most EV brands support Plug & Charge at Ionna, with Tesla recently getting Autocharge functionality through a third-party smartphone application. Ionna itself doesn’t have a smartphone app, instead relying on automakers and third-party apps to integrate its locations. So far, this approach has turned out to be quite successful, with J.D. Power’s latest public charging survey putting the operator first, ahead of Tesla and the rest of the competition.
