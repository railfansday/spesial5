10 Kohl's Black Friday deal categories where the sticker price is only part of the cost

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Ten Kohl's Black Friday categories, from towels to Shark vacuums, and how codes and Kohl's Cash change what shoppers pay.

Shoppers tend to picture Black Friday as the day stores sell their goods cheaply, and the eye-catching price on a doorbuster feels like the whole story. This year shoppers may find fewer such deals. "We won't see as wide a selection of doorbusters, like we have in the past," Andrea Woroch, a consumer saving and spending expert, told PBS News. A store gives up money on the doorbuster and earns it back on the rest of the cart, so the shopper who comes out ahead buys the cheap item and questions everything placed around it.

Kohl's $KSS heads into the holiday season knowing its customers are short on money, and the company has flagged pressure on consumer spending ahead of the holidays, according to Reuters. Kohl's responds by offering its discounts in layers. Some of that discount comes back as Kohl's Cash, store credit that has value only when a shopper spends it at Kohl's again, so a deal that looks complete at checkout may be complete only after a second trip.

Passionate Penny Pincher lists Kohl's top Black Friday doorbuster prices and the savings shoppers can get by stacking coupons, on goods that range from Shark vacuums and half-price toys to luggage and bath towels. Shoppers who enter TAKE20 get 20% off, and members of Kohl's rewards program who are buying floor care, kitchen goods or furniture can add a second code, 10HOME. Kohl's also hands back $15 in Kohl's Cash for every $50 spent from Nov. 22 through Nov. 30.

Bath towels are the cheapest thing in this aisle. Kohl's $KSS sells a towel that regularly costs $9.99 for $2.99 as a doorbuster, and the TAKE20 code brings it to $2.39. Many doorbuster towels are excluded from the extra 10HOME code, so plan on $2.39. The towel is where a shopping trip begins, not where a shopper saves much money.

The coupon codes save more on the pricier pieces in the same aisle. Kohl's sells the Big One bowl set for $11.49, down from its usual $23, and both codes bring it to about $8. If the bowl set was already on the shopping list, this is the week to buy it.

Kohl's puts the doorbusters online on Nov. 26, a day before it offers them in stores on Nov. 27, so a shopper who orders from home gets the towel without a walk through aisles that Kohl's arranges to sell more.

The towel is worth taking. Anything else belongs in the cart only if it was on the list before the sale.

Kohl's $KSS sells cold-weather boots for $9.99 to $13.99 as a doorbuster, against a regular price of $35. Few items in the store serve as bait as openly, so the question is whether the saving behind the low price is real.

Kohl's sells the pair simply as "Cozy Boots," with no brand or style named, so a shopper can't check the $35 regular price at another store. The fair test is whether you would want an unbranded pair of boots at $9.99 to $13.99. Shoppers who enter the store-wide TAKE20 code at checkout pay $7.99 to $11.19 for the boots.

Buy the boots if you'd want them at that price, and stop there.

The ZeroXposur puffer coat is a markdown a shopper can check, because Kohl's $KSS lists its regular price right beside the sale price. Kohl's prices the puffer at $19.99 to $29.99 against a regular price of $80 to $85, and shoppers who enter the TAKE20 code pay $15.99 to $23.99.

The sweaters carry a low number too, but nothing to judge it by. Kohl's advertises them at $11.99 and up with no regular price listed, so a shopper can't tell whether that marks a real saving, and the sweater they like may cost more than the starting price.

In this aisle, buy the coat on the numbers, and check what a similar sweater costs at other stores before treating its price as a deal.

Kohl's $KSS has set one $29.99 sale price for its Big One down-alternative comforter, no matter which size a shopper picks. Its regular price runs from $50 to $70 depending on size, so the size that normally costs the most gets the deepest cut. Shoppers who apply the TAKE20 code pay $23.99 for any size.

The Big One plush throw is the cheapest piece in this category. With TAKE20, a throw costs $7.99, an easy small holiday gift, but not a reason to buy a stack of them because each one feels cheap.

In bedding the code cuts prices that are already low, so the one discipline a shopper needs is to buy the size and the number of pieces they would have bought anyway.

A Cuisinart coffeemaker or air fryer is halved to $99.99, down from a regular price of $199.99. Woroch names small kitchen appliances among the best Black Friday deals and treats 30 percent off as a discount worth buying, so a Cuisinart at half price clears that bar with room to spare.

Kohl's $KSS also marks down its cheaper machines, selling Toastmaster appliances for $14.99 as a doorbuster against a regular price of $25. On the Cuisinart, a shopper who applies both the TAKE20 and 10HOME codes pays $72 out of pocket.

A half-off sign is only as honest as the regular price behind it, so the $199.99 figure is the one to verify before paying. "See what things cost before the deal drops," Woroch said.

In this aisle the sticker can be a true deal, but only the shopper who checked the old price knows it.

A Pyrex Simply Store food storage set costs $14.99 during the sale, down from a regular price of $30. Kohl's $KSS is also selling a Farberware Build-a-Board for $19.99, an easy host gift.

These low prices make kitchen basics the easiest things to add to an order built around something else. If the storage set or serving board was on the list, it's one of the cleaner bargains of the weekend, because the price is plain and nothing else is required to get it.

Buy the piece only if you'd want it without the sale sign.

Shark vacuums are priced at roughly half off, with Kohl's $KSS Cash back on top. The complication is the lowest price attached to the deal, which is not the price on the tag.

The Shark Rotator's "as low as $97.50" is a net cost after rebates. A buyer actually spends about $150 at the register, and the largest part of the gap is $45 in Kohl's Cash, credit that comes back only for later spending.

Shoppers should judge the Shark at about $150, and count the store credit only if they already plan another trip to Kohl's.

Half off is real here. The rest of the discount is a reason to come back.

Kohl's $KSS is taking 60% or more off luggage, and shoppers also earn Kohl's Cash when they buy. Kohl's doesn't say which brands or sizes get the full cut, so a shopper still has to check the price of the bag they actually want.

The one named suitcase, a Kensie hardside spinner, sells for $48 in any size. It also comes with $15 in Kohl's Cash, but because Kohl's issues that credit for every $50 spent, a shopper who buys only the bag would need to add a small item to get it.

Price the suitcase at what you pay at checkout. Count the credit as a bonus only if you already planned to shop at Kohl's again.

Kohl's $KSS promises half off toys, but the LEGO Lilo & Stitch set sells for $38.99 against a regular price of $64.99, about 40% off. The Potato Head set carries a sale price with no regular price beside it, so "50% off" works as a headline for the section, not a promise about any one box.

A percentage off is only as useful as the price it is taken from, and that starting price is higher this year. Toys in the U.S. cost 6.6% more than they did a year earlier, and Chinese factories produce the majority of the toys American stores sell. "Tariffs add a noticeable cost to toys and other goods," Jay Foreman, CEO of toymaker Basic Fun, said.

In a year of higher toy prices, a half-off sign is Kohl's description of the deal, not a measure of it, and the measuring is up to the shopper.

Kohl's $KSS marked the Sol de Janeiro 3-Spray Set down to $14.50 from a regular price of $27.50, a named product with two prices a shopper can check at another store. No coupon-adjusted price is given, so plan around $14.50.

Select Sephora items are 50% off, but which products qualify isn't spelled out, so the better approach is to choose the product first and then check whether it is marked down.

Kohl's limits a REWARDS10 code to members of its rewards program from Nov. 25 to Nov. 30, but no one has said whether it applies to the Sol de Janeiro set or the Sephora items, so leave it out of the math until it comes off the total.

In beauty, trust the price printed on a named product, and treat every other promise as unconfirmed until the cashier rings it up.
