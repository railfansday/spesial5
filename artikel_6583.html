This Is the Jarred Pasta Sauce Ina Garten Always Keeps in Her Pantry

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

"Store-bought is fine" carries a little more weight when Ina tells you exactly which pasta sauce to buy.

I can happily spend an entire afternoon making dinner. Give me a little music, a glass of wine and something to braise, and I’ll settle in without much concern for when it all reaches the table. It’s a lovely way to cook when I have the time, but leisurely cooking isn’t always practical for busy parents. On most days, dinner needs to fit into my family’s schedule, and my kids don’t want to hear that dinner is still achieving important levels of depth and complexity as it enters its sixth hour on the stove.

But having less time to cook shouldn’t lower our expectations for dinner. A simple plate of pasta can be every bit as satisfying—provided the ingredients involved are worth eating. It’s why I’m particular about store-bought sauce. I’m happy to buy it, but I want to enjoy it as much as anything I spent time making myself.

Ina Garten seems to have arrived at a similar conclusion. Her signature “store-bought is fine” reassurance comes with some excellent recommendations, including the marinara from Rao’s Homemade, a brand I’ve been loyal to for years. It’s a small thing to have in common with Ina, but until I acquire a Parisian apartment of my own, I’ll gladly take it.

“Why make your own marinara when Rao’s marinara sauce tastes homemade?” Ina has said. It’s a reasonable question, particularly coming from someone with very little left to prove in the kitchen.

She’s equally decisive on her website. When a reader asked her favorite jarred marinara, Ina answered, “That’s easy! Rao’s Marinara Sauce.” It also appears among her recommended ingredients in the site’s shopping section.

Her advice to an incoming college student gives the recommendation some practical context. When asked which staples to have on hand, Ina suggested keeping pasta and Rao’s marinara around for a ten-minute dinner. That’s useful advice at any age, particularly once you discover that graduation doesn’t necessarily leave you with more time to cook.

Ina’s comment about homemade flavor gets at why I’ve continued to buy it, too: Rao’s has the richness and depth I want in a sauce, even when I’m serving it with nothing more elaborate than bucatini and Parmesan. I actually look forward to that dinner. The fact that it comes together quickly is just a bonus.

Lately, I’ve been especially impressed with the brand’s new creamy roasted garlic sauce (so much so that I gave it a Taste of Home Award). The mellow roasted garlic and the richness of mascarpone have made it a favorite in my house.

Ina’s explanation of “good ingredients” offers a useful starting point. In Modern Comfort Food, she writes, “They don’t have to be expensive, but they need to be chosen thoughtfully.” Her emphasis is on flavor and choosing ingredients because of what they contribute to the finished dish.

When it comes to Rao’s, she points to slow simmering and good tomatoes, basil, garlic and oregano. That makes the ingredient label a sensible place to begin when comparing jars. The marinara label lists whole peeled Italian tomatoes, followed by olive oil, onions, salt, garlic, basil, black pepper and oregano—and no added sugar.

For a classic marinara, look for that familiar foundation of tomatoes, oil, aromatics and herbs. If you prefer a more savory sauce, keep your eye on the added sugar, too. These are useful details to compare across brands, although the label can only tell you so much about how well the ingredients have been cooked and balanced. In this case, I’m happy to let Ina’s recommendation settle the matter.
