This Clever Fork Trick Makes Slicing Cheese So Much Easier

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

A little ingenuity with an ordinary piece of silverware makes for a beautiful holiday cheese board.

As much as I love a big, elaborate holiday dinner, I have a particular fondness for the hours leading up to it, when the wine has been opened and everyone is grazing around the kitchen. A good cheese board is almost always part of the festivities in my house. My affection for charcuterie boards is, admittedly, not doing much to challenge the millennial stereotype.

I love choosing cheeses and spreads that complement one another and bringing out some of my favorite serving pieces for the occasion. It’s one of the more enjoyable bits of prepping holiday party appetizers, in part because so much of the work can be done well before anyone arrives. And while I’m happy to devote a little extra time to the presentation, I’m always equally happy to discover a little shortcut that leaves me more time for the people I’m feeding.

One of my favorite recent discoveries is a clever trick for slicing cheese into neat, even pieces using the tines of an ordinary dinner fork. It’s the sort of kitchen trick that seems almost embarrassingly obvious once you’ve seen it.

Rather than eyeballing the thickness of each slice, you can use the evenly spaced tines of a fork to mark the cheese beforehand, giving yourself a cutting guide.

Start with a wedge of cheese and gently press the tips of the fork’s tines into the face you’ll be cutting into, leaving a row of small, evenly spaced indentations. Repeat as needed across the back of the wedge, then use a sharp knife to slice between the marks. Having those little reference points makes it much easier to maintain a consistent thickness from one slice to the next.

This trick is particularly well suited to cheeses like Gouda, cheddar and Gruyere, which are generally firm enough to slice cleanly but pliable enough to take the impression of a fork. I wouldn’t bother with a ripe Brie or a crumbly blue cheese, both of which lend themselves to a less structured presentation anyway.

I love this trick for fanning slices of cheese around the edge of a charcuterie board or arranging them in pretty, overlapping rows. And given the number of things competing for my attention when I’m entertaining, I’m delighted to delegate the finer points of cheese geometry to a fork.
