Packers-Rams Thanksgiving Eve betting odds, picks and predictions: Best bets, player props

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

It's the holiday we didn't know existed until the NFL told us it did!

What was once an all-time night for those returning home from college (and still might be), has been commandeered for our viewing purposes, with the first ever "Thanksgiving Eve" NFL game, but you know what?

The season hasn't gone as expected for Green Bay nor Los Angeles, but the result is two 6-4 teams that are battling for the final playoff spot in the NFC, and a higher-leverage matchup than we could have ever expected. Like that one time your high school crush seemed happy to see you at the bar during the holiday break in freshman year.

Ahead of each Monday Night Football game this season, we do our best here to explain the spread, but there's nothing complicated about this betting line: The Rams are perceived slightly better than the Packers, and are playing at home.

How we got to that perception is the interesting — and informative — part.

With stars at every position, the Rams opened the season at the top of the betting market's NFL power ratings, while the Packers, without arguably their biggest star — Micah Parsons — were considered a merely mediocre team just a couple weeks into the season. A month ago, this line would have been well over a field goal.

As we start Week 12, the Rams have lost Puka Nacua and Davante Adams, going from the top-rated team to No. 8, just ahead of where the Packers were before they faced the Vikings in Week 9 (having won five of six).

It's the rare instance where, strictly speaking, the Rams exist in a buy-low area relative to their range of expectations, especially after four straight spread covers.

Whether you found us on Mondays buying low (Chargers in Week 10) or selling high (Bengals in Week 11), sometimes that goes out the window when there's a bad matchup on the field.

For Wednesday, coming out of their bye week, Aaron Donald (first Rams sack in Week 10) and Myles Garrett (three sacks in his Week 9 return) are well-rested, having barely played in a blowout win against the Cardinals, and, unlike Parsons, are in midseason form. Given what we saw from the Packers' offensive line when it allowed eight sacks to the Vikings, the last thing you want to feel is the regret from expecting that unit to pass-protect.

Add in that concerns about the Rams offense without their star receivers should be assuaged, knowing the Sean McVay has had another week of prep for the players who are available, and you should able to trust L.A. still has enough to win what should be considered their regular season Super Bowl … at least, for now.

Jordan Love under 36.5 pass attempts (-114, FanDuel)

It might seem strange, but this is a bet on the Rams offense.

Forced to be more methodical and conservative, but still efficient, there just hasn't been enough time for the other team to accumulate plays.

Blake Corum over 9.5 receiving yards (-115, Bet365)

With one hand tied behind the Rams' collective backs, McVay's latest chance to cook produced a two-running back set.

Kyren Williams and Blake Corum went from being on the field 0% of the time, to playing together around one-third of the offensive snaps at Arizona.

On the first two drives against the Cardinals, Matthew Stafford targeted each running back three times.

Having both out there allows for Stafford to pick which side he wants to throw to when he doesn't trust the receivers downfield, and which tailback he needs to stay in to help protect. Williams' added value is in his ability to pass-block, which means Corum might get more opportunities. In what should be a closer game than the Rams' Week 10 blowout, that could be mean even more than their nine total RB targets.

Tyler Higbee over 28.5 receiving yards (-115, DraftKings)

Down to a shallow group of wide receivers, when the Rams go to their patented 13-personnel, each tight end seems to have his own specialty role.

When the Rams are running the ball on repeat, and the defense is ready to bite on play-action, they sneak Davis Allen out of line for a wide open play. Against man-to-man coverage, the 6'7 Colby Parkinson is a matchup nightmare. However, veteran tight end Tyler Higbee is the one charged with finding the gaps in opposing zones.

Green Bay plays zone at the 8th-highest rate (over 75%) in the NFL.

The Packers' best chance to keep this game close is to take advantage of the key defensive injury on the Rams' side: 6-foot-2 cornerback Jaylen Watson being out.

That seemingly would have been the Rams' biggest hope for guarding the 6-4 Christian Watson, with Trent McDuffie the better size matchup for Matthew Golden. Instead, Josh Wallace or Emmanuel Forbes (both six feet tall), might get the task of trying to hold Watson down, which is a mismatch.

It's not a full changing of the guard in the Rams' backfield, but:

Corum: 19 red zone touches, 5 TD (one in each game)

At plus-money odds, either tailback could be a valuable bet, but Corum is just as likely to score late as early. Williams, as the nominal starter, needs to get in the paint in the first quarter.

Back to Higbee and the Packers' zone defense.

Higbee's number was called to score against Arizona, which plays the sixth-most zone in the NFL, in the wake of their former head coach, Jonathan Gannon, now the defensive coordinator at Green Bay.

Last season, in his one chance to face Gannon's defense with the Cardinals, Higbee had five catches for 91 yards and a touchdown — the first time he'd played Gannon's defense since a two-touchdown game in Arizona in 2023.

Without Nacua and Adams, what Rams receivers were on the field the most when last they played?

Regular No. 3 receiver Konata Mumpfield played 48 snaps, but CJ Daniels played just two fewer. The rookie out of Miami caught his first career pass for 22 yards against Arizona, and had an end zone target that Stafford just missed.
