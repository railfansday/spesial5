Why even Trent Williams envies the high point of new 49ers starter Vederian Lowe's career

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Vederian Lowe has picked Trent Williams' brain and studied the generational left tackle's film since signing with the San Francisco 49ers in March, but the onetime sixth-round pick and part-time NFL starter understands there are limits to what he can replicate.

"A lot of the things that he does, I can't do," Lowe said. "Because Trent is one of one."

However, there is something Lowe has done that Williams has spent his illustrious 16-season career wishing he could do. On Nov. 17, 2024, Lowe, then with the Patriots, realized every 300-pound man's dream when he caught a 4-yard touchdown from quarterback Drake Maye in a loss to the Chargers.

Asked where his lone catch ranks among his career highlights, Lowe smiled and invoked Williams, who is catch-less and has long lobbied head coach Kyle Shanahan for a goal-line pass.

"That (catch) might be number one because they don't come by - I was talking to Trent and even Trent doesn't have one," Lowe said. "So I'm like, 'OK.' That's up there for sure."

The lofty ranking Lowe has assigned his reception speaks to the rarity of such a snag. But it also speaks to the modest number of highlights he's collected in a five-season career that has included 25 starts, just four since Jan. 5, 2025.

But that could be changing. Last Sunday, in a 35-10 win over the Vikings in Mexico City, Lowe was unexpectedly thrust into the role of a key starter on a Super Bowl contender when right tackle Colton McKivitz suffered a broken and dislocated ankle in the second quarter.

McKivitz's season is over. Lowe's chance to show why the 49ers signed him to a two-year contract with a maximum value of $12 million to serve as their swing tackle is just beginning.

"That's why we brought him here," Shanahan said. "Anytime you have a swing tackle - that means you better be capable of starting. He's started a lot of games in this league and has played like a starter. And that's why we targeted him."

Lowe is assuming some sizable cleats. McKivitz, 30, was earning every bit of the three-year, $45 million extension he signed last year by not allowing a sack in 327 pass-blocking snaps this season before his injury.

The very early returns, however, are promising. On Lowe's first snap after replacing McKivitz, he pancaked defensive tackle Tyrion Ingram-Dawkins on a 10-yard run by Christian McCaffrey. It was the start of a 38-snap performance in which Lowe committed a false start, but he didn't allow a QB pressure on 19 pass-blocking snaps against Minnesota's chaotic, blitz-heavy defense, a unit which leads the NFL in sacks.

Lowe did so despite logging most of his snaps at left tackle throughout his career. His only four starts at right tackle were in 2023 with the Patriots and he'd spent most of his practice time on the left side since joining the 49ers.

"Swinging from both sides," Lowe said, "it's not an easy task at all."

Center Jake Brendel wasn't surprised Lowe managed to make it look seamless. Brendel, 34, was struck by the questions Lowe was asking about the 49ers' intricate blocking schemes not long after his arrival. Lowe peppered Brendel with queries about "chokes," "blunts" and "seals," all backside blocks from tight ends or other players lined up outside the 49ers' offensive tackles.

"He asked me questions that guys don't ask me until year three or four in the system," Brendel said. "So the guy knows football … A lot of backup tackles kind of just float around and then they go in and they're like, 'All right, I'll get it done physically, but I'm not expecting to be at the level of a starter.' This guy's definitely starting (material)."

The Patriots weren't convinced about Lowe's first-string ability. After Lowe made a season-high 13 starts in 2024, New England drafted left tackle Will Campbell with the No. 4 pick to replace him last year. Lowe made four starts as a fill-in in 2025 after making 21 starts the previous two seasons. After his step down in status, he joined the 49ers knowing he'd have no shot to unseat Williams or McKivitz.

Now, however, he's a starter on a 9-1 team with legitimate Super Bowl aspirations. Lowe has a chance to help his new team earn a ring while showing he's more than a well-compensated backup.

If last Sunday's performance was any indication, it's an opportunity he's ready to seize, much like that pass he caught in 2024, a moment even Williams can only hope to replicate.

"The opportunity doesn't come by too often," Lowe said, "and I was not dropping that."
