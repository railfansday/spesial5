Live service titles are a double-edged sword, both for the developers working on them and the greater industry as a whole. While the promise of constant support and almost endless content sounds incredibly promising, the strain and repetition lead to many titles that attempt the feat crashing and burning, oftentimes before they really get off the ground.

After a nearly 4-year wait, macOS fans of Relic's Company of Heroes franchise will finally get to play the most recent entry.

Back in 2023, Sony announced a myriad of live service titles aimed to help the studio earn a spot in the active multiplayer space, many of which have petered out pre- and post-launch. Fairgames joins the list of canceled games, officially canceled after lackluster numbers in player tests. Despite this decision, Sony claims no employees will be laid off, offering a silver lining amid another live service disaster.

Usually, failed live service titles lead to major consequences, as their nature requires mountains of work and advertising to really get off the ground. Not only do they need substantial content in the initial launch, but plenty ready to go in order to tide players over as weeks and months pass by. Concord, Redfall, even successes like Destiny 2 tend to stumble before getting snuffed out with a whimper.

Usually this means jobs lost and talented developers ousted, but Sony is making the great call to keep everyone working at Haven Studios on, instead shifting them to support other active development projects across PlayStation’s catalog before letting them pursue their own creative output again.

While the promise of constant support and almost endless content sounds incredibly promising, the strain and repetition lead to many titles that attempt the feat crashing and burning, oftentimes before they really get off the ground.

Sony made it clear through an internal email release that the issue wasn’t the quality of Haven Studios’ work, but the incredibly saturated online live-service shooter market. Titans like Overwatch, Call of Duty, and Marvel Rivals make it incredibly difficult to get a foot in the door, despite the “strong operational excellence, live ops readiness and community engagement” Haven Studios managed to put together.

With the failure of most of Sony’s live service initiatives, it’s a bit comforting to see them not lash out at the devs who tried to make a bad plan work, and instead pivot to projects that hopefully have a bit sturdier legs to stand on. Their single-player titles have easily been the platform’s strongest selling point, and focusing on what they know works will do wonders to earn back public support.

After the announcement of their redoubled efforts to keep Sony games on PlayStation, it feels like Sony is aiming to tighten up their ship a bit, forgoing some of their more haphazard programs to hone in on what people come to PlayStation for: strong, single-player titles with truly unique gameplay. Spider-Man, Horizon, and Ghost of Yotei are recent successes that are the real reason Sony still has a place.

With the rise and constant fear of studio closures and layoffs, Sony needs to keep their ship tight, rewarding longtime devs while inviting new blood in for opportunities to show their stuff. Otherwise, they could continue to stagnate in the console space, allowing Nintendo to pull even further ahead. Sony’s real rival, Xbox, is in a similar rut, and digging their way out would be a great way to surge ahead.

While the odd live-service title may find its niche audience, they often require numbers that are nigh impossible to maintain over any extended period. Titles like Marvel Rivals and Call of Duty have ridiculous numbers of constantly paying customers, a situation that is much harder to replicate than many would have you believe.

With Fairgames gone, Haven Studios has another chance to define itself, even if that only starts as support for bigger teams. A debut title can make or break a studio, and if Fairgames crashed and burned, I don’t think Haven would have lasted much longer.
