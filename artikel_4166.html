Petit Planet Preview: Living the Cozy Life

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

The latest major entry in HoYoverse's catalog sends us to distant planets. We explore the multiplayer mini-games and friendly neighbors and planet customizations at a recent event!

HoYoverse is well known for their free-to-play gacha experiences, telling surprisingly intricate stories all while compelling their player base to get their favorite characters. That looks to change in their latest entry, Petit Planet.

This is a big deviation from what we’ve become accustomed to when it comes to HoYoverse, as while aspects to the overall formula remain, a lot of it has been modified to accommodate a cozier experience. This is all about building a life on a small planet with all your friends, be them NPCs or those online.

We were invited out to talk to the developers a couple of weeks ago and play a newly updated build of Petit Planet, and we couldn’t have had a cozier experience.

Before getting into anything, it should be said how much more of a multiplayer game this is compared to any of the other HoYoverse games we’ve had in the past. Genshin Impact has multiplayer functionality, which somewhat carried into Honkai: Star Rail (which uses its events well for cooperative and competitive play), but Petit Planet looks to up it to another level.

This is supposed to resemble something like Animal Crossing, having players not only be able to visit each other, but interact with them to a great degree. You can send friend requests and then easily access their world, although the number of things to do there is contained. It’s going to the Grand Bazaar that really turns things up.

This is a social hub-like area where players can go and play games with one another. For example, there's a rhythm game four players compete in, along with an Uno type game. Each of these games have a maximum number of four players to utilize, although there are possibilities for more. Being that the game isn’t out, this likely will be the groundwork for even more mini-games to come.

Participating in this mini-games seem to give you a Jelly currency, which can then be exchanged from various vendors for emotes, some of which can be interacted with other players, and other articles of clothing.

Because this is a life sim, there will be several things to do in the world on a daily basis, especially given this is a free-to-play HoYoverse game. There are daily and weekly challenges that can be accomplished, from chopping down trees to interacting with the Neighbors, and each generate different types of currency.

Because this is a free-to-play experience, there are quite a few different curries you will be able to obtain through daily activities but of course spend with your hard-earned money. While this wasn’t in the build we had, there’s no denying that’s how the game will generate its income from. It will still allow players to do their daily and weekly activities to generate some of these currencies, ensuring you don’t have to swipe, but remember this is still a HoYoverse game at the end of the day.

As for what you’ll be able to participate in, there are various energy-spending activities, from gathering materials from around your world to catching bugs and sea life. All of this contributes to the overall progression, although we aren't entirely sure what the endgame will truly be.

This feels like a game where you can sit back and chill with friends, slowly building upon a world without much pressure or FOMO. Regardless, there are still things that can be done in the world to ensure for some sort of progression, but whether there's a true endgame is still up for debate.

This wouldn’t be a cozy game without NPCs that roam around the worlds, and Petit Planet has a bevy to see. We’ve already been introduced to a number of the cute and cuddly cast in the past, but there’s even more we got to see in the latest demo. Each neighbor has their own function for why they are in your world, but mainly it’s for you to build and expand upon the relationships.

The Neighbors come with their own progress tree, going up three levels and seemingly able to go down if you neglect them. You'll be able to bring them onto your planet and set up little houses for them, and that’s not all. They'll have their own stories and journeys to go through from meeting them, talking them over time and giving them gifts, unlocking various moments with them.

By obtaining Footprints and the other currencies in Petit Planet, you'll be able to exchange them for various in-game cosmetics, such as clothing, which include facial wear and full outfits, and various placeable items.

Making sure your home is homey is key to immersing yourself into the Petit Planet system, as there are numerous options to choose from when building your perfect little abode. Whether it’s the furniture, the appliances or simply the wallpaper, there’s a staggering number of customizable options.

On top of that, building out your planet itself is just as important, as setting up roads to your neighbors and modifying the different accessories are put around things such as gardens lets you do so much more. It’s your personal little place you want to call home, even though the chilly planet we got to play on is relatively small. Here’s hoping there’s ways to expand upon it.

Petit Planet will be out by the end of the year on December 4 for PC and Mobile devices, with a Nintendo Switch 2 launch sometime in 2027.
