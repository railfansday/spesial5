Packers' offensive rollercoaster explained by one key stat

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Green Bay Packers offense has been a rollercoaster ride in 2026, with some lofty peaks and unfathomably low valleys. They have looked unstoppable for stretches and completely broken at other times.

At this stage, the numerous issues contributing to this inconsistency are well established, but there is one key stat which perhaps does the best job of explaining what is wrong with Green Bay's offense, relating to how they perform once they fall behind in a game.

Green Bay's offense ranks 27th in the NFL in expected points added (EPA) per play when trailing, compared to 14th when tied and ninth when leading. That 27th place is arrived at via a ranking of 21st in EPA per pass and 32nd in EPA per run.

Why that matters is that if you look at some of the best and most efficient offenses in the league year on year, most of them are right near the top of the standings in EPA when trailing.

In 2026, the top ranked teams in that metric are the Bills, Lions, Bengals, Chiefs, Cowboys, Jaguars, Rams. On the flipside, the only teams ranked below the Packers are the Dolphins, Chargers, Ravens, Colts and Falcons.

It is not difficult to conclude that being effective even when the odds are against you, and often when the defense knows what is coming, is the hallmark of an elite offense.

The low ranking is a stark change from previous Packers seasons. They were fourth in EPA per play when trailing in 2025 behind only the Rams, Broncos and Bears. Every team ranked in the top eight, including the Patriots, Bills, Seahawks and Jaguars were playoff teams.

Green Bay also ranked seventh in EPA per play when trailing in 2024 and eighth in 2023.

Going back through each game this season, the Packers completely crumbled once they fell behind late in Week 1, with their three subsequent drives ending in a loss of 22 yards and a fumble, an interception, and a turnover on downs.

They came back from a 10-point deficit to beat the Jets in overtime, but needed a lot of chances. Green Bay had four regulation drives after falling behind in the game, and three of them went for seven yards or less, including a two-yard field goal 'drive' after a New York turnover.

Against the Falcons, they first trailed with around five minutes left in the second quarter. From there the Packers went: missed field goal, punt, turnover on downs, punt, before finally scoring a touchdown once they were down 20 points, and then ending with an interception.

The Packers did not trail in any of the next three games versus the Buccaneers, Bears and Cowboys. Then against the Lions they fell behind early and ended up only scoring 17 points. Multiple missed field goals were partially to blame, but there were also also two punts after drives that combined for minus two yards, a turnover on downs and an interception against a subpar defense

Against Carolina they trailed early and ultimately grabbed control pretty quickly, but Green Bay did have a seven-yard drive end with a punt as well as a fumble while they were behind.

They never trailed the Patriots, and then after falling behind to the Vikings early, the Packers failed to score a point until around five minutes left in the third quarter.

So what is the exact reason for this problem? It would be easy to point at the woeful run game, but it is not like Green Bay was great at running the ball when trailing last year, and they still ranked near the top of the league overall.

Maybe the lack of run game has made them predictable, or perhaps more patience is needed by Matt LaFleur and Jordan Love to take what the defense gives them, rather than taking deep shots against a defense that knows what's coming in an effort to get all the points back at once.

Whatever the root cause of the issue, it has become a serious one for the Packers and speaks to the volatility of their offense based on how the circumstances they face.
