Dan Evans to compete in controversial Gazprom-backed exhibition tournament

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Retired British tennis player Dan Evans is in the line-up for a highly controversial exhibition tournament which is sponsored by Russian energy giant Gazprom.

Gazprom is majority state-owned and has been sanctioned by the UK government for its role in funding Russia's war in Ukraine, with a 2025 statement saying its profits were "lining Putin's war chest and facilitating the war".

The company sponsors the annual Northern Palmyra Trophies exhibition, which is held this year from 27-29 November in St Petersburg and features 12 current or former tennis players.

A spokesperson for the Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport told The Independent: "Our position remains that we strongly advise British athletes not to travel to Russia to participate in sports events while the illegal invasion of Ukraine continues."

Russia has not held any official ATP or WTA Tour events since the invasion of Ukraine in 2022 and Russian and Belarusian players cannot represent their country, instead competing as neutral athletes.

However, exhibitions are not covered by the rules governing Tour events and players are free to play in such tournaments on Russian soil. Evans, who retired at Wimbledon this year, has not commented on his decision to take part in the event.

The Northern Palmyra Trophies sees two teams face off in a mix of singles and doubles matches, with Evans drawn against world No 11, Russia's Diana Shnaider, in a Battle of the Sexes-style clash.

Frenchmen Adrian Mannarino and Corentin Moutet are also in the line-up, which primarily consists of Russian or Russian-born players.

Top-100 players Anastasia Potapova, Kamilla Rakhimova, and Yulia Putintseva, who were all born in Russia, are competing, as is the retired former world No 13 Elena Vesnina. Potapova and Rakhimova have both switched nationality since the war began, with the former now representing Austria and the latter Uzbekistan. Putintseva has represented Kazakhstan since 2012.

Russian 17-year-old Anna Pushkareva, compatriot Karen Khachanov, retired Serbian player Jakno Tipsarevic and Kazakhstan's Alexander Bublik complete the line-up.

Former US Open champion Daniil Medvedev competed last year but is absent for 2026.

The Netherlands' Tallon Griekspoor, who is in a relationship with Potapova, sparked a diplomatic incident when he decided to play in last year's tournament.

Dutch Foreign Minister David van Weel issued a public statement urging him to withdraw, to no avail: "It is not forbidden to participate in tournaments in Russia, but I appeal to him not to do so where possible and to look at the moral aspects." The Russian embassy said it was "deeply concerned" by the "pressure" put on Griekspoor.
