Keenan Allen’s head is no longer spinning after big 49ers debut

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

ARLINGTON, Texas — Earlier this week, Keenan Allen said his head was spinning. Acquired at the trade deadline from the Colts, it was his job to get up to speed on Kyle Shanahan's complex and extensive playbook in time to make an impact in just a few days' time.

After making his debut in the 49ers' 46-40 win over the Cowboys on Sunday?

"It wasn't too bad," Allen smiled. "Wasn't too bad. Not too bad."

Sounds like a guy who's head is firmly on straight.

San Francisco 49ers wide receiver Keenan Allen reacts after a catch against the Dallas Cowboys at AT&T Stadium in Arlington, Texas, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect

Allen looked the part for the better part of the preceding three hours. Five days after he touched down in Santa Clara, Allen caught more from Brock Purdy than anybody besides George Kittle.

"We were just playing ball," the 34-year-old veteran said after his first game with his fourth team.

The final tally: Seven targets, five catches, 61 yards.

Kittle hauled in one more pass for an additional 13 yards, and Christian McCaffrey outgained both of them with touchdown scampers of 66 and 74 yards, respectively. Not bad for a guy who didn't know the route trees last week.

"Not shocked," McCaffrey said. "Everybody's seen him play throughout his entire career. When you have a vet like that, it's just learning terminology. Style of play's there. Obviously he's had so much success, so he's going to fit right in and only get better."

San Francisco 49ers wide receiver Keenan Allen (3) reacts after making a catch against the Dallas Cowboys, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026, in Arlington, Texas. IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect

Allen wasn't on the field for every snap, but it wasn't for a lack of preparation or any limitations. Shanahan threw the entire game plan at him. Well, everything except the 2-minute offense.

"Obviously," Allen laughed. "I didn't want to kill myself."

By Saturday, he told his coach he was good to go. A hard few days with receivers coach Leonard Hankerson and three practices under his belt had paid off.

"He said his biggest worry was [that] I was going to call something that wasn't in the game plan,"  Shanahan said. "Fortunately, we avoided that."

Still, Shanahan said there was only a "chance" that he would wind up playing such a large role.

"You never know how the coverages are going to go," he said. "But I think he did a hell of a job. He made all the plays that we went to him on and a couple more we just missed him on."

Allen's biggest gain of the day was also his most crucial catch — an unscripted scramble drill on third-and-12 that resulted in a 23-yard gain down the sideline. Allen's catch extended a drive that ended in three points and at the same time showed instant chemistry with Purdy.

San Francisco 49ers wide receiver Keenan Allen (3) catches a pass over Dallas Cowboys cornerback Cobie Durant (2) in Arlington, Texas, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. AP Photo/Jeffrey McWhorter

Purdy finished with a career-high 389 yards through the air on 22-of-28 passing.

Allen's initial review of his new quarterback? "Fire." He described his mindset as a receiver as "just trying to stay quarterback friendly" and to "be an outlet."

Maybe the biggest takeaway from Allen's debut was how easy it was to forget that Mike Evans wasn't on the field. The 49ers put up 580 yards of offense without Purdy's favorite target.

Evans missed his third straight game with Achilles tendinitis but is due back any week.

"Oh my gosh, when you add Mike Evans to this," tight end George Kittle said. "I mean, when Mike Evans is on the field and the threat that he poses, I don't get covered by corners anymore – and that's all Mike Evans. It makes everybody's lives easier."

Keenan Allen looks on after defeating the Dallas Cowboys 46-40 at AT&T Stadium in Arlington, Texas, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. Getty Images

Going from the 3-7 Colts to the 8-1 49ers, Allen instantly noticed the difference in the stakes.

He played the Cowboys last week, too, in Indianapolis.

Deja vu? "A little bit," he said. "Not really."

"This game felt more playoff vibe-ish. It just felt good to be out there. Just because the intensity was up a little bit, the energy was high in the building. Obviously [when] the fans come out like that, feels a little bit better."
