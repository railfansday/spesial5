2026-27 NBA News, Injury Updates Nov. 23: Dylan Harper, CJ McCollum out, but Brandon Ingram returning

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Welcome to our weekly Monday Morning NBA notebook, a quick hit of NBA news and injuries from around the league to catch you up on what happened in the past week. While a couple more big names go on the injury list, we get at least one All-Star off that list.

While nobody is saying this is a good thing, it's also a lot better outcome than was feared when it happened. Spurs second-year guard Dylan Harper suffered a left hip external rotator strain when he slipped on a wet spot on the floor. Officially, he is out 1-3 weeks, but the smart bet is on a couple of weeks.

Harper has become the Spurs' second-best player, already averaging 18.4 points on 50.5% shooting, plus 5.4 rebounds and 4.5 assists per game, while playing high-level defense. His absence means a lot more Stephon Castle with the ball in his hands — which is not a bad thing, but his handle is a little more loose, and he had seven turnovers in a sloppy win against the Clippers.

• Guard CJ McCollum suffered a strained left calf and will be reevaluated in four weeks, the team announced.

• Big man Asa Newell has surgery to repair a left great toe hyperextension, and while the Hawks' announcement says he will be re-evaluated in four months, that will be mid-March, weeks away from the end of the regular season (April 11). It's unlikely we see Newell again this season.

McCollum had moved to the bench already to make way for more minutes for rookie point guard Kingston Flemings, and the offense plays faster and looks better to the eye test with him in there.

There is one NBA All-Star getting healthy and returning to play.

When Ingram underwent heel surgery last May, doctors discovered a partially torn Achilles tendon. To be clear, this wasn't a full-blown rupture, but he was going to miss time, and the Clippers were aware of it when they traded for him as part of the Kawhi Leonard deal.

Ingram is coming off a bounce-back season in Toronto, averaging 21.5 points a game, shooting 38.2% from 3, grabbing 5.6 rebounds a night, and earning an All-Star selection. The Clippers could use that kind of production to lift their 23rd-ranked offense.

• Tyrese Haliburton is still at least a couple of weeks away from a return from a hamstring injury. He said on social media his goal is to be back before the end of November. The Pacers are 3-8 without him.

• The Grizzlies GG Jackson underwent surgery Tuesday to repair a broken metatarsal in his left foot, and while the team says he will be re-evaluated in three months, expect him to miss most of the rest of the season. Which sucks. Jackson broke the same bone in his right foot a couple of years ago.

• Sacramento signed veteran point guard Gabe Vincent to back up Darius Acuff Jr. It's a homecoming for Vincent, who was born and raised in central California.

• Kawhi Leonard remains out in Toronto but should return in the "next few weeks," reports ESPN's Shams Charania. History suggests taking any news of a Leonard return with a grain of salt.

• Chicago's Norman Powell tweaked his hamstring and will miss a game or two, he will be re-evaluated later in the week.

• There is a lot of manufactured buzz about the Dejounte Murray trade, because that's good for traffic, but the reality is there is no traction right now around a deal. Not to the Suns, not to anyone. Murray missed the majority of a couple of seasons with a torn Achilles, hasn't looked the same, he's not shooting well from 3 or playing like the All-Star player he was five years ago, and has a player option for $30 million next season. Teams view him as a negative contract and want a first-round pick or a pick swap attached to have a discussion. New Orleans isn't going there, at least not yet. Expect this to drag out until the new year and likely close to the February trade deadline.

• The sense in league circles is that Washington coach Brian Keefe's seat is getting hot. However, Doug Christie's seat in Sacramento is not.
