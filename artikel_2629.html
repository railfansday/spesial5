New York Jets: A Glass-Half Full Approach

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

On Sunday, the Jets traveled across the country and beat the Los Angeles Chargers on the road, snapping a five-game losing streak in the process. Ordinarily, a win like that would be met with plenty of positivity, especially considering the offense produced its highest EPA per drive performance in nearly 23 years.

Instead, the result was met with a surprising amount of mixed emotions. Some of the reaction was downright negative.

So what does this win actually mean? And more importantly, where do the Jets stand through 11 games?

Let me start with the obvious. This team is not good. At 3-8, we've seen enough to establish that. I'm not going to pretend one win against the Chargers fundamentally changes anything.

The defense is very bad from a talent standpoint and continues to perform poorly. Even against a relatively mediocre Chargers offense, there were plenty of disappointing moments. The coaching has also continued to raise legitimate concerns, particularly when it comes to clock management, situational decisions, and execution late in halves.

For Aaron Glenn, I do see some encouraging development from the younger players on this roster. The offensive line as a whole, David Bailey, Adonai Mitchell, and Braelon Allen are among the players who have shown signs of progress. But the continued issues with clock management and situational football are major red flags. Unless something fundamentally changes down the stretch and the Jets start convincingly beating the majority of their remaining opponents, I think Glenn should be fired.

That being said, I'm considerably more optimistic about this team than its 3-8 record might suggest. And it largely comes down to one thing: the offense is finally starting to look like something.

Now, to be clear, this isn't some elite offense. The numbers don't suggest that, and anyone who has watched the Jets this season knows they've struggled mightily to execute in high-leverage situations.

But as more games pass, it's becoming increasingly difficult to argue that this offense isn't improving.

If you've followed the Jets over the last 10-15 years, you know just how consistently terrible their offenses have been. That's what makes this season somewhat different and frustrating in a different way. For the first time in a long time, we're seeing an offense that can actually move the football with some consistency, particularly against weaker defenses.

And that's not an insignificant development.

Look at Sunday's game against the Chargers. I understand the argument that the Chargers aren't particularly good. They aren't. But coming into the game, their defense ranked 14th in EPA per play allowed. By most measures, they were a relatively average defensive unit.

They scored on every drive, consistently moved the ball, and made it look relatively easy while doing so. The young offensive line was dominant, creating opportunities throughout the afternoon and allowing the offense to operate at a level we aren't particularly used to as Jets fans.

For additional context, Sunday's performance was the sixth-best offensive performance by EPA per drive of any team in the NFL this season. For the Jets, it was their best performance by that metric in nearly 23 years. You have to go back to 2004, when a Chad Pennington and Curtis Martin-led team took on the Seahawks, to find a better performance.

And this isn't necessarily an isolated occurrence.

We've seen this offense dominate bad teams throughout the season. They beat down the Titans earlier in the year. They put up 33 points against the Raiders, producing the second-highest EPA per play of any offense that week. They also averaged 7.1 yards per play against Miami.

I'll go back to even just looking at numbers since the Jets last won. Since Week 7, the Jets rank seventh in offensive Success Rate and 12th in EPA per play. Those are genuinely encouraging numbers, especially considering the age and experience level of this group.

Of course, some of that production can be attributed to the competition. I won't try to argue or ignore that. Playing bad defenses makes a difference. But part of becoming a legitimate NFL offense is consistently taking advantage of those opportunities. That's what the Bills do. That's what the Chiefs do. That's what the Bengals do. Personally, I can't count how many times over the last decade-plus we've watched Jets offenses struggle to do even that against statistically poor defenses.

That's an average offense. Nothing more, nothing less.

But considering where this franchise has been offensively for most of the last 15 years, being average is a pretty significant step forward sadly. And as I will continue to repeat, I'm particularly encouraged by the fact that this is a young group that should have plenty of room to improve.

The offensive line has shown legitimate development. The tackles have set in very nicely after a rocky start. Dylan Parham at guard looks to be more or less like a positive signing. Joe Tippmann continues to be volatile in pass-protection, but it's hard to ignore how well he's performed as a run blocker by pretty much any offensive line metric.

Virtually everyone of note should be back next season, with the obvious exception of the quarterback situation which will remain very much in flux. Outside of that, the Jets should have an opportunity to build on what they've already established rather than completely reconstructing the unit. It's reasonable to expect some consistency and stabilization here. This isn't a unit that has been driven by players in their 30s this season.

I'm not suggesting this offense is suddenly going to become one of the league's best. There are still plenty of issues, particularly in critical situations. But for the first time in a long time, I can look at this group and see a foundation worth building around.

I understand why some fans are frustrated with the Jets winning games at this point in the season.

At 3-8, draft positioning matters. Seeing the team win a game that potentially hurts its draft position, particularly when the roster still has so many holes, it can be frustrating. I'm not going to argue that reaction isn't fair or warranted.

I get it. I remember watching the Jets win seemingly meaningless games during Adam Gase's final season, ultimately costing themselves the opportunity to draft Trevor Lawrence. To be honest, them beating the Rams was one of the worst moments of my Jets fandom. I said it at the time and fast-forward eight-years later and I still believe it. I was right there with everyone else.

But I do think there's an important distinction between those teams and the current group.

This isn't a team that won a game yesterday because of fluky special teams production, like we saw last season (that performance ultimately proved difficult to replicate this year). Nor is this a team with an older roster stumbling its way to a respectable finish despite having a historically inept offense for most of the season like we saw during the 2019 Gase year.

This is a relatively young team whose offense is showing legitimate signs of progress and could return virtually intact next season.

And the underlying numbers suggest the Jets aren't nearly as bad as their record indicates, which is a huge difference as well compared to past Jets teams that won games at the end of the year by arguably lucking into wins despite miserable underlying metrics.

They're currently 23rd in DVOA and 23rd in net EPA per play. Again, obviously not good, but it also puts them in a different category than some of the truly dreadful teams we've seen throughout the league (we just watched one in Los Angeles). There's a legitimate argument that this team is closer to 16th in the league than it is to being one of the three worst teams in football.

That's an important distinction when evaluating where this franchise stands moving forward. I guess I'm not as upset because I never really viewed the Jets draft pick as a realistic top 3 pick option. Their offense has shown too much life and their underlying metrics never had them in that conversation.

But there's a team that might help them get there. The Colts. That pick might very well lead to a top five pick. Right now it's more likely than not that it does.

The other factor? To me this isn't necessarily a quarterback class with one obvious prospect who stands above everyone else. Given the depth of unproven and volatile quarterback prospects, I actually think there's a reasonable argument that the Jets would be better off using their second or third first-round selection on a quarterback rather than surrendering additional assets to move all the way up to No. 1.

That's a discussion for another day, though.

Ultimately, I'm not trying to convince anyone that the Jets are a good football team. They're not. There are legitimate reasons to be frustrated with this season, and the coaching situation remains a major concern.

But I also don't think it's unreasonable to come away from Sunday's win feeling encouraged.

For years, the Jets have been stuck in a cycle of terrible offensive football with an inability to develop young players into legitimate contributors. This season hasn't solved all of those problems, but there are finally some signs that things could be changing.

The offense is average by virtually every meaningful metric, it's trending in the right direction, and much of its production is coming from young players who should be around for the foreseeable future.

That's something worth being excited about.

A win against the Chargers doesn't change the fact that the Jets have a long way to go. But it does provide another data point suggesting that this team might be closer to having a legitimate offensive foundation than its record would lead you to believe.

And after watching some truly dreadful Jets offenses over the last decade-plus, I'll take that as a meaningful step forward.
