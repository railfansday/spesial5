Aaron Rodgers officially a free agent with NFL future in jeopardy after unprecedented Steelers exit

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Aaron Rodgers is officially a free agent, with the final chapter of his NFL career shrouded in uncertainty.

The legendary quarterback officially cleared waivers on Thursday, a day after an explosive series of events that saw him cut by the Steelers on Wednesday following Rodgers' stunning takedown of coach Mike McCarthy on "The Pat McAfee Show."

Rodgers is now free to sign with whichever NFL team he chooses, but his "options appear limited" per a report by NFL insider Dianna Russini. The Patriots, after Drake Maye's injury, have been floated as a potential landing spot, but ESPN insider Adam Schefter believes this may be the last we see of Rodgers in an NFL uniform.

Pittsburgh Steelers quarterback Aaron Rodgers (8) prepares to throw against the Cincinnati Bengals at Paycor Stadium in Cincinnati, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. USA TODAY Network via Reuters Connect

"Texted a couple GMs today," Schefter said during a WEEI radio appearance on Wednesday. "I think that he knew his career was over and he tried to end Mike McCarthy's career as well. Two guys going down together today. That was my sense of it. Now, could somebody give him a shot? Sure. I'm not expecting that."

Rodgers, who has said this will be his final season in the NFL, was benched on Tuesday night by the Steelers, who said backup Mason Rudolph would start Friday against the Broncos. Rodgers then revealed his side of the story during McAfee's show, saying the breakup partially stemmed from him asking McCarthy to relinquish play-calling duties as frustrations mounted over the team's back-to-back losses.

Rodgers claimed McCarthy was behind on film study, missing or falling asleep in meetings and broke down the halftime huddle in their loss to the Eagles by saying, "1, 2, 3, Packers."

"I just said [to McCarthy], 'Listen, I think there's got to be a change,'" Rodgers said. "'You don't want me to call it because you don't think I'm going to call enough runs? Fine, let somebody else call it, that's what's in the best interest of the team.'"

McCarthy owned up to the Packers mistake and denied there is any grudge between him and the QB but didn't take kindly to what he felt was an "ultimatum" from Rodgers, while not addressing the play-calling request directly.

Pittsburgh Steelers quarterback Aaron Rodgers gestures at a news conference after an NFL football game against the Philadelphia Eagles in Philadelphia, Sunday, Nov. 22, 2026. AP Photo/Chris Szagola

"It was a process that went on throughout this week and we got to a point, we talked about some things — communication and things like that," McCarthy told reporters Wednesday evening. "There was an ultimatum made clearly outside of his job description, and so it was my decision to bench him."

Steelers owner Art Rooney broke his silence on the situation Thursday, throwing his full support behind McCarthy while admonishing Rodgers for going public with his grievances so quickly.

"There's maybe a time and place after the season if he wanted to kind of unload," Rooney said. "He talks about wanting to be a good teammate, but you don't do that to your teammates a couple days before the game, that's for sure.
