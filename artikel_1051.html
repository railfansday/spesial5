Mauricio Pochettino faces the media in his first press conference as Manchester United’s head coach having replaced Michael Carrick last week.

The former USMNT boss concluded his time in the United States on Wednesday and flew straight to Manchester to meet the players and get to grips with the squad ahead of a crucial clash with Liverpool at Anfield on Sunday.

Pochettino spoke to United’s in-house media on Thursday and has already expressed his main priority which is to help take the pressure off his players and allow them to thrive.

“The players need to express their talent,” Pochettino said. “We need to create the environment and the platform for the players to perform. That is the most important thing and that is our priority now – it’s to work with them and try to take the pressure.

“That is what we need to create all together.... then settle all the principles and the ideas and to create our own philosophy and start from zero.”

Pochettino has taken several of his staff with him to United, with goalkeeper coach Toni Jimenez, assistant Jesus Perez and fitness coach Sebastian Pochettino departing for England after the US’ 6-0 victory over Haiti in the first leg of their Nations League quarter-final on Saturday.

Assistant coach Miguel D’Agostino, fitness coach Sylvia Tuya Vinas and analyst Alex Scott, along with Pochettino himself, headed off after Tuesday’s second leg.

As Michael Jones points out, Pochettino’s long-term plan will be to improve the fitness of the team and get his players to outrun other teams in the league - but for now his goal should be midfield security.

But with the January transfer window fast approaching, we could also see some major changes in personnel - including an influx from the USMNT.

Juventus and US midfielder Weston McKennie said as much before the boss departed: “USMNT players at Manchester United? I think that, obviously, the time Pochettino spent here allowed him to appreciate this squad and see the quality it has to offer.

“And yes, I think it's a possibility. We have a lot of guys on this team who will probably make a move soon or within the next year. So I think the links are somewhat inevitable.”

Quite aside from the footballing problems, of which there are many, Pochettino is also walking into quite a toxic atmosphere.

Man United’s later games of the Carrick era were marked by widespreading booing from fans, although the manager was always loudly backed, and there were protest banners held up against the Glazers and Jim Ratcliffe’s oversight.

The Manchester United Supporters’ Trust (MUST) branded Michael Carrick’s sacking “another depressing moment” and argued the club’s problems rest primarily with its ownership, in a now-familiar plea to end the ongoing feeling of crisis at Old Trafford.

It said: “The Glazers’ debt-funded takeover continues to burden the club. When Ineos came in they promised sporting excellence, but yet with repeated changes in football leadership we have seen anything but.

“It is time for the owners to explain themselves and show some accountability.”

Now in the role of head coach, Pochettino will inherit a bruised and battered Man Utd squad lacking in confidence and in dire need of inspiration in order to turn around their fortunes.

There are plenty of problems facing Pochettino when he arrives at Old Trafford, both on and off the pitch, but he must tackle these five in the short term to rebuild confidence in his squad.

His first game in charge will be at the cauldron of Anfield to take on Liverpool on Sunday 22 November and, not only is it a contest imbued with history and rivalry, but it is also a match that United must show signs of improvement in.

Mauricio Pochettino said he received contact from Manchester United on Sunday about possibly taking over as manager.

Michael Carrick was not officially sacked until Thursday.

"All was so quick,” he said. “Sunday afternoon, there started to be some contact. We were very transparent [with US Soccer].

"We’ll have time next week to talk about the job at Manchester United. It’s much better to talk now about US Soccer."

Pochettino struggled to fight off tears as he held a press conference to explain his decision to swap the USMNT - which he penned a four-year extension with in the summer, to keep him until 2030 - for Man United.

He said: “It’s obvious. I never said I wanted to go back but they know there aren’t too many opportunities that happen in your career like Manchester United. When it arrives, it’s difficult to turn down.”

He described it as “the most difficult decision of my career”.

United will pay US Soccer compensation worth an initial $7m (£5.3m).
