Nike to replace Adidas as Champions League and Uefa ball supplier from 2027

Nike is set to replace Adidas as the official match ball provider for the Champions League and all other Uefa men’s club competitions from 2027.

The deal, which runs until 2031, was announced on Monday, bringing an end to Adidas’ long-running association with the Champions League.

Nike will provide the official match balls for the Champions League, Europa League and Conference League - marking the first time all three tournaments will have the same supplier - as well as ⁠the Super Cup, Uefa Youth League and ​Uefa ⁠Futsal Champions League.

The deal was announced by UC3, the commercial joint venture between UEFA and European Football Clubs (EFC), which launched the tender process in March, and its agency Relevent Football Partners.

The Athletic reported that the deal is worth around $45m per year.

Nike Football vice president and general manager said: “We are excited and honoured to return to the Uefa Champions ‌League, one of football's biggest stages, after more ‌than two decades, and to bring Nike match balls across Uefa’s men's club competitions.

“Football is the ⁠world's game, and the ball is at the centre of it, the one thing every player, in every competition, has in common. This is a long-term commitment for Nike.

“We will listen to the athlete's voice, push the edges of innovation, honour the prestige and tradition of these competitions, and make sure the ball represents the instinct, creativity and joy football brings ‌to billions of fans.”

Analysts had said the deal would boost Nike's ​visibility but was unlikely to address the company's underlying challenges, ‌including slowing sales and a lag ⁠in product innovation that have stymied the sportswear giant in recent ⁠years.

“In terms of getting its groove back, high-visibility corporate partnerships are not the silver bullet,” ‌Zacks Investment Research analyst ​David Bartosiak had said.

“Whether you're reinventing ‌the wheel or reinventing the ball, ​you're still not bringing any sort of meaningful innovation.”
