Warhammer 40K Gets A Studio Ghibli Twist In Epic New Sci-Fi Action Series From Acclaimed Creator

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

As diametrically opposite to each other as they may sound, Warhammer and Studio Ghibli find common ground in a new sci-fi series that arrived this month. What once was a niche British tabletop miniature wargame from Games Workshop has evolved into a multi-billion-dollar global entertainment behemoth rivaling sci-fi titans like Star Wars and Dune. Today, the grimdark 41st Millennium spans hundreds of bestselling Black Library novels and blockbuster video games like Warhammer 40,000: Space Marine 2, Rogue Trader, and Darktide, as well as Amazon Prime Video's acclaimed Secret Level episode, and a live-action cinematic universe currently in the works, spearheaded by Henry Cavill.

On the opposite side of the spectrum, Studio Ghibli’s signature hand-drawn animation style is known for its gorgeous watercolor landscapes and atmospheric moments that allow quiet moments to steal the show. Ghibli movies prioritize organic movement, retro-futuristic flying machines, and famously, mouthwatering realistic feasts. Such a beautifully calm aesthetic has inspired a rich wave of non-Ghibli tributes across animation and other media, from acclaimed anime features like Mamoru Hosoda’s The Girl Who Leapt Through Time and Makoto Shinkai’s Children Who Chase Lost Voices to Western animated gems such as Song of the Sea and Luca, as well as video games like Spiritfarer and Baldo.

Warhammer 40K and Studio Ghibli couldn't be more different, but there's always a bold idea out there bringing two clashing concepts together.

Eisner-winning creator Daniel Warren Johnson returns to creator-owned sci-fi with Image Comics’ explosive new ongoing series Chainkata, which recently made its debut on November 18, 2026, and immediately sold out its 250,000-copy initial print run ahead of release. Developed over nine years since Johnson first sketched the protagonist in 2017, the forty-page premiere introduces Luma, the exiled warrior queen of a fallen kingdom who travels across deep space aboard her starship armed with a roaring chainsaw-sword. Although Luma longs for peace, bloodshed and interstellar terror pursue her through every black hole she crosses.

Chainkata shines brightest through Johnson’s fearless visual experimentation, with bold black-and-white ink work and brutal melee combat against painterly watercolor backdrops of swirling purple and blue nebulae, punctuated by sudden splashes of blood. This contrast captures the spirit of its famous pitch: "Princess Mononoke meets Warhammer 40K." Like Warhammer 40,000, the series revels in heavy-metal grimdark spectacle and visceral chainsword carnage, but it balances its furious action with the melancholic heart of a Studio Ghibli classic. Luma’s grief for her lost family and the quiet tragedy of her nature as a warrior forced to shed blood are patent in its art style.

Chainkata creator Daniel Warren Johnson has built a signature style across several comic series. In Extremity, he delivered a post-apocalyptic revenge tale heavily inspired by Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind and Akira, mixing Hayao Miyazaki’s floating sky-islands and organic machinery with brutal war drama about the hollow cycle of vengeance. Johnson followed it with Murder Falcon, an exuberant heavy-metal adventure where a grieving guitarist summons a musclebound cyborg falcon through pure rock riffs to battle emotional monsters. Later, Do a Powerbomb! presents a necromantic tournament that infused professional wrestling with the heightened choreography of classic Dragon Ball fights and Japanese puroresu.

For fans of Guillermo Del Toro's Pacific Rim, there is a new Dark Horse Comics series for them, Dawnrunner, now available as a hardcover book.

Daniel Warren Johnson brings the same synthesis of heart and spectacle to major licensed icons as well. In Marvel's Beta Ray Bill: Argent Star, Johnson sends Thor’s noble Korbinite ally on a cosmic quest to reclaim his warrior dignity. In DC's Wonder Woman: Dead Earth, he reimagined Diana Prince as a gauntleted wasteland survivor in a Mad Max-meets-Fist of the North Star apocalypse. More recently, his Eisner-winning run on Skybound’s Transformers revitalized the Autobots and Decepticons with its emotional rendition of the shapeshifting robots, while his poignant graphic novel The Moon is Following Us, co-created with Riley Rossmo, opted for a heartfelt take on bedtime-story magic.
