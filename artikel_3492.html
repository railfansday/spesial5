‘The Expanse’ Meets ‘True Detective’ in New Sci-Fi Thriller Officially Out Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Hannah covers horror, sci-fi, and games at Collider, where she writes news and features, reviews new releases, and interviews the people who make them. She’s helping build out Collider’s gaming coverage, with Pokémon as one of her specialties. If she isn’t writing about a monster, she’s probably watching one on a tape from her VHS collection.

The last thing Yoshi Graham remembers is being outside a bar. Twenty-four hours later, the junior officer is back in his quarters aboard the Archimedes, telling security that somebody took him. Faisal Vasconcelos suspects Yoshi had too much to drink. It’s a plausible explanation, even a welcome one for the officer who has to file the report, but then somebody finds a body in one of the ship’s artificial wombs. Faisal knows those tanks well, because he was born in one.

The Archimedes is 109 years into a 133-year trip to Tau Ceti, which makes for a hell of a time to propose turning around. The ship has sustained enough damage, and supplies are running low enough, that some passengers want to take their chances going home. Nobody back there has answered a transmission in decades. Yoshi works in Life Sciences and is rumored to sympathize with the people who want to turn back. Depending on whom Faisal believes, that could give someone a reason to grab him or a reason to discredit him afterward. It's a book premise that sounds immediately like True Detective and The Expanse combined, and it's available to read now.

Adam Oyebanji’s Line of Descent is out now from DAW Books. The 368-page sci-fi thriller arrived November 17 in hardcover and ebook. Oyebanji, the author of Esperance, follows Faisal through the two investigations as senior officers fight over who gets to command the ship. His jurisdiction is a city in deep space, and whoever killed someone in those tanks is presumably still living in it.

The first lead in Yoshi Graham’s disappearance comes from his own department. Yoshi is a sublieutenant in Life Sciences and a rumored supporter of the movement to turn the Archimedes around. He says someone abducted him outside a bar and returned him to his quarters a day later. Before Faisal can establish what happened during those missing hours, he picks up a murder case: Someone has drowned in one of the ship’s reproductive incubators.

Faisal works both cases while senior officers compete for command of the damaged vessel. At launch, the Archimedes carried 30,000 people. Five generations later, its residents face a supply shortage with another 24 years to go before their scheduled arrival at Tau Ceti. The faction calling for a return trip has yet to hear from anyone who could receive them, and messages to the Home System have gone unanswered for decades. Oyebanji described the murder as “the lens through which the rest of the story is told.”

Line of Descent is available now in hardcover and ebook from DAW Books.
