6 Upcoming Fantasy TV Series, Ranked by Hype

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Fantasy has been one of the most prolific and beloved genres on television since the medium's infancy, delivering several of the greatest genre series that the small screen has ever seen. The last few years have offered masterpieces like Arcane and House of the Dragon, and thankfully for people who love the genre, there are plenty of upcoming fantasy shows to look forward to in the coming months and years. These are series that are worth the utmost hype, surrounded by such buzz and sky-high excitement that it's difficult to imagine them not being absolute hits by the time they come out.

Ranked here in order of how much hype they're worthy of, the most exciting upcoming fantasy shows are proof that the genre is still more than live and well in the current day. Whether they're charming low fantasy series bound to become gargantuan pop-cultural sensations in the coming years, or historic high fantasy series based on legendary books, these are shows which pretty much anyone and everyone who loves fantasy storytelling should add to the very top of their watchlist. For the purposes of this list, only shows that have been officially confirmed and green-lit (in other words, shows with an official IMDb page) will be counted.

Back in 1997, Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone (published in the United States as Harry Potter and the Sorcerer's Stone) changed not only J. K. Rowling's life and writing career, but the entire fantasy genre. It's still one of the best-selling works of fiction in history, and the influence that the whole Harry Potter book series has had on children's and YA fantasy over the decades cannot be overstated. Fifteen years after the conclusion of the film series that further enshrined this as one of the most pop-culturally relevant stories in modern history, the world will be treated to an HBO show adaptation that will reportedly dedicate one season to each book in the series.

There are few fantasy books better than Harry Potter, and as such, it's hard to imagine how HBO's Harry Potter could be anything less than a generational smash hit. Even though Rowling's reproachable views of and actions toward the trans community can never be omitted when talking about anything Harry Potter-related, and even though the show has already made itself subject to some other controversies before its premiere, fans' hype for the series is nevertheless off the charts. Will the show be consistently successful enough to make it all the way to The Deathly Hallows? Only time will tell. But for now, fantasy lovers have plenty to be excited about when it comes to Harry Potter's first season.

Back in 1993, businessman Peter Adkison asked mathematician Richard Garfield to create a portable, fast-playing card game that could be easily brought to game conventions and was relatively cheap to produce. Inspired by Cosmic Encounter, Dungeons & Dragons, and other unpublished game ideas that he had designed before, Garfield game up with Magic: The Gathering, a collectible card game where players take the role of powerful wizards trying to reduce their opponents' life totals to zero. It has remained one of the most iconic and successful game franchises on the planet since the '90s; so, if anything, it's surprising that it has taken this long for a television adaptation to materialize.

Rebecca Yarros' Fourth Wing is the first volume in her Empyrean series, and one of the best new adult romantasy books of the last decade. The story follows Violet, a physically fragile young scribe who's forced by her cold military general mother to enter a deadly dragon-riding war college. There, she has to endure ruthless classmates, bond with powerful dragons, and uncover the kingdom's dark secrets while falling for a dangerous rival. Though people who don't typically like romantasy are unlikely to be converted by Fourth Wing, those who love the subgenre (or at least don't much mind it) are in for the literary ride of a lifetime, full of high-octane action and engrossing character development.

It's one of the most essential fantasy books of the 2020s, and mere months after its publication, Amazon MGM Studios confirmed that a TV adaptation was in production. Executive-produced by Yarros herself, as well as people like Michael B. Jordan, Lisa Joy, and Jonathan Nolan, the series counts with Locke & Key's Meredith Averill as its showrunner and Esther McGregor and Emilio Sakraya in its cast. Bringing a BookTok phenomenon of this caliber to life on the small screen is never a small task, but with such an unprecedentedly high budget and such a high-profile creative team behind it, it's hard to imagine how Fourth Wing could be anything less than a delight for fans.

Four worlds and four completely different ideas of what a good life looks like. This is where you belong, what it would ask of you, and what you would get back in return.

You belong somewhere old enough that the mountains have names and somebody can still tell you who gave them. Middle-earth is for people who think the right thing is worth doing even when it will not be finished in their lifetime.

You want the sea, a crew, and absolutely nobody telling you where you are allowed to go. The Grand Line is the world for people whose main ambition in life is finding out what is over there.

You would rather be feared and useful than liked and useless. The Continent is a world where the monsters are the straightforward part of the job and the people are where it all gets complicated.

You want the myths to be real and loose in the actual world, and you want to find out at about thirteen that you were never anywhere near as ordinary as everybody kept telling you.

Brandon Sanderson has dominated the fantasy literature space throughout the 21st century, confidently cementing himself as one of the most important, prolific, and influential fantasy authors currently writing in English. Part of his overarching Cosmere multiverse, The Stormlight Archive has become the writer's flagship book series, starting with 2010's The Way of Kings and still ongoing. We're talking about five (soon to be more) of the most noteworthy books that are a masterclass in high fantasy writing, which made this year's news that Apple TV had secured the rights for Sanderson's entire Cosmere universe all the more hype-worthy.

This is, after all, a book series so inherently grand and cinematic that it lends itself perfectly to any sort of adaptation.

A Stormlight Archive TV series was the first Cosmere televisual project that Apple announced, and it's rather unsurprising. This is, after all, a book series so inherently grand and cinematic that it lends itself perfectly to any sort of adaptation—and certainly one for television, which is the only medium vast enough to capture the sheer grandeur and epicness of Way of Kings and its sequels. Sanderson will reportedly be heavily involved with the project, expecting to write most of the first season himself, so fans of the series are confident that things will turn out just fine. And as for people who haven't read the books yet, this show's production should be more than enough time for them to dive into one of the best epic fantasy book series of all time.

Starting with 2005's God of War, the video game franchise of the same name follows a haunted Spartan warrior named Kratos, who wages a bloody war of vengeance against the Greek pantheon and later flees northward to bury his past and learn how to move on. Composed exclusively of some of the most epic video games in history, the God of War franchise has greatly evolved over the years, and soon enough, that evolution will culminate in a TV series created for Amazon Prime Video. The show will be based on the Norse mythology era of the original franchise, which is already more than enough reason for fans to be hyped about seeing how the creative team will handle the narrative.

Best-known for his award-winning work on the Battlestar Galactica 2004 remake, Ronald D. Moore will be the series' showrunner. As for the cast, it includes Callum Vinson as Atreus and Dave Bautista as Kratos himself (following Ryan Hurst's departure from the role after an injury). This is already more than enough of a pedigree for fans to put their whole confidence in the team, and should also be a perfect excuse for fantasy gamers who somehow haven't dipped their toes into the God of War franchise to finally play some of the greatest action games in history.

The 1999 anime series One Piece is one of the best anime shows of the '90s, an absolutely legendary cornerstone of the medium based on the equally iconic manga series written and illustrated by Eiichiro Oda. In it, a rubber-bodied young pirate named Monkey D. Luffy sails across the seas with a loyal crew to find the ultimate treasure, the eponymous One Piece, and claim the title of King of the Pirates. But as legendary as it undeniably is, if there's one thing about the One Piece show that has been widely criticized for years, it's that its slowly-paced 1,100+ episode run makes it a little too daunting and inaccessible for those not looking for such a massive commitment.

That is precisely why the show is being remade for Netflix next year, seeking to fix the pacing issues of the original, remove fluff, and make the story much more highly accessible for newcomers—whether they're already familiar with the franchise or not at all. Fans can be hyped about a much-needed update to the somewhat-dated original, a show that will offer a fresh way to experience this epic story right from the get-go again. And as for newcomers who might have been intimidated by the original show's length before, The One Piece will be the ideal entry point.
