Singapore Airlines Cancels This 14-Hour A380 Nonstop Route: See Map Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Singapore Airlines has scrapped plans to bring the Airbus A380 back to Frankfurt Airport (FRA) next summer, meaning the superjumbo will remain absent from Germany's busiest airport until at least late October 2027. The aircraft has not been scheduled to Frankfurt since the winter season began on October 25, but was due to return on March 28, 2027, with a daily service from Singapore Changi Airport (SIN).

Instead, the Boeing 777-300ER is now set to operate the route throughout the summer. The service, operated as SQ326/325, is scheduled to take up to 13 hours and 40 minutes on the 6,386-mile (10,278 km) route. The equipment change means Emirates will be the only airline flying the A380 to Frankfurt next summer.

With the A380 not returning to Frankfurt next summer, the carrier will operate both of its daily flights with the 777-300ER, rather than splitting the services between the 777 and A380 as previously planned. SQ326/325 will be a midday departure from Changi and a late-evening return flight from Germany. Meanwhile, SQ26/25 will depart Singapore just before midnight and continue to New York JFK Airport (JFK) after Frankfurt.

Interestingly, Singapore Airlines has fifth-freedom rights on this service, allowing it to sell tickets between Frankfurt and New York. The carrier has previously operated three daily flights to Frankfurt on some summer dates, including in 2023 and 2024, but won't be offering a third flight next summer.

The airline's four-class 777-300ER has 264 seats, including four in First Class, 48 in business class, 28 in premium economy and 184 in economy. The A380, by comparison, offers 471 seats: six Suites, 78 in business class, 44 in premium economy and 343 in economy. This will result in a significant 28% reduction (207 fewer daily seats each way) in capacity next summer, with weekly seat capacity dropping from 5,145 in summer 2026 to 3,696 next summer.

Singapore Airlines first introduced the A380 to Frankfurt in January 2012, replacing the Boeing 747-400 on its daily service between Singapore and New York via Frankfurt. At the time, Frankfurt and New York became the airline's ninth and tenth A380 destinations, with Frankfurt its fourth in Europe after London, Paris and Zurich.

In May, the airline removed the A380 from Frankfurt for the entire 2026/2027 winter season, having previously planned to keep it on the route until mid-January. The final A380 service departed Singapore on October 24. Speaking to Simple Flying last year, Singapore Airlines’ Regional Vice President for Europe, Joey Seow, pointed to Frankfurt as a heavily slot-constrained airport, praising the A380 for enabling the airline to "put in more passengers without having to get additional slots."

The Superjumbo is still a regular feature at several airports across the world.

Without Frankfurt, the carrier is scheduled to operate the A380 to seven destinations next summer. London Heathrow Airport (LHR) and Sydney Kingsford Smith Airport (SYD) will each receive two daily flights. The other five will each receive one daily flight: Auckland Airport (AKL), Melbourne Airport (MEL), Mumbai Airport (BOM), Delhi Indira Gandhi International Airport (DEL) and Shanghai Pudong International Airport (PVG).

However, Shanghai will only have the A380 between June 20 and July 24, when it temporarily replaces Delhi, while Mumbai will lose the superjumbo between July 25 and August 28. London will consequently be the airline's only European A380 destination and, at 6,761 miles (10,881 km), its only A380 route longer than 6,000 miles (9,656 km).

Overall, Singapore Airlines' A380 flights next summer will be down by around 10% compared with summer 2026. This is despite the airline retaining all 12 superjumbos that remained after its pandemic-era retirements.
