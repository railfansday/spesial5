The 50 Best Restaurants For 2026 Have Been Announced—With a Remote European Spot in First Place

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

The 50 Best Restaurants for 2026 have just been announced as part of a prestigious awards ceremony in Lima on Wednesday, November 5, 2026. As with every year, we wait for the release—an indicator of which restaurants will be topping travelers' lists in the year ahead, and the trends that will shape the experiences they offer. Below, we cover which restaurant snagged the top spot, which restaurant led on every continent, and the industry stars celebrated with individual awards like Best Pastry Chef, Best Sommelier, and more.

Read on for the full list, and notable takeaways from the winners. (For more on the trends revealed at the Lima event, read our reporter's conversation with winning chefs here.)

The Best Restaurant in the World for 2026 is hidden away in sleepy Basque village, Axpe.

Climbing up from its number two spot in last year's list, this year’s winner is Asador Etxebarri in Atxondo, in Spain's Basque Country.

Basque wood-fired cooking is at the heart of this rustic fine-dining restaurant. Chef-owner Víctor Arguinzoniz (also known as Bittor Arginzoniz) is considered a pioneer of grilling and live-fire cooking. At Asador Etxebarri, every course of Bittor Arginzoniz's €300 euro (about $337 USD) tasting menu touches the wood and charcoal grills—from Palamós red prawns to smoked milk ice cream.

Hidden away in the sleepy mountain village of Axpe, about 45 minutes by car from Bilbao, foodies have been making the pilgrimage to try Arguinzoniz's signature grilled dishes since the restaurant opened in 1990. It's been an 18-year journey to the top, as Asador Etxebarri has been a mainstay on The World's 50 Best Restaurants list since 2008, starting at number 44.

Once a restaurant gets the coveted number-one trophy, it is ineligible for nomination in forthcoming years—with the intention of allowing other restaurants a chance to earn top billing. Top-placing restaurants are then inducted into a new list, called the Best of the Best. This list includes such legendary eateries as Chef Thomas Keller's The French Laundry in Yountville, California, and Disfrutar in Barcelona, Spain. Last year’s winner, Maido in Lima, Peru, is the most recent inductee to this prestigious list.

It’s tempting to imagine the enviable job held by a panel of judges that sample every restaurant in the world. But that’s not quite how the 50 Best works. Rather, the 50 is an international media company that taps the expertise of over a thousand hospitality experts from around the world to weigh in on the industry that they know best. Chefs, restaurateurs, food journalists, and well-traveled gourmets all anonymously nominate restaurants that they have dined at within the last 18 months. (Members can’t vote for a restaurant they have a financial interest in.) Professional services consultancy Deloitte has access to the voting data to ensure the validity and fairness of the process.

Because of the voting-style nature of the awards, a restaurant might fall off the list one year only to return later. This year, previous entries to the list such as The Ledbury—a fine-dining beauty in London, United Kingdom where chef Brett Graham marries British produce, Japanese flavours, and French culinary techniques—returns to the list in the number 83 spot. Similarly, Luxe Mosu in Seoul, South Korea—home to chef Sung Anh's elevated interpretations of traditional Korean fare—returns to the list at number 78.

“Every year The 50 Best Restaurants list gives us a snapshot of where more than 1,100 individuals who live and breathe this industry have had their most memorable restaurant experiences over the previous 18 months," explains Rikki Tidball, managing director of awards and rankings for The 50. “And I think that’s what makes it so special. Each Academy member votes independently, so nobody at The 50 or any of our partners has a say in who makes the list, and restaurants can’t nominate themselves. This means every restaurant on the list has genuinely earned it.”

Gaggan in Bangkok climbed up to Number 2 after placing at Number 6 the year prior.

As always, the highest-ranking restaurants on each continent have been given a special mention during tonight's awards—and there are a few repeats from last year. Gaggan in Bangkok (this year's number 2) won the best restaurant in Asia again. Mexico City's Quintonil (4) was the best restaurant in Latin America again, while New York City's Atomix (11) was the best restaurant in North America. Dubai's Trèsind Studio (28) was named the best restaurant in the Middle East for another year. The best restaurant in Africa was Cape Town's La Colombe (35). Sydney's Saint Peter (50) was the best restaurant in Oceania, while top spot Asador Etxebarri took the best restaurant in Europe award.

Every year, the panel awards special prizes to chefs, front-of-house staff, and activists who are leading the charge in hospitality. In 2026, the Best Female Chef Award went to Jessica Rosval, head chef of Al Gatto Verde. Bangkok’s Dej Kewkacha won Best Pastry Chef Award, and Lesley Liu of Odette in Singapore won the Best Sommelier Award while the Estrella Damm Chefs' Choice Award went to Junghyun ‘JP’ Park from Atomix in New York City. Doris Lam won The 50 Best Restaurants Scholarship. Spanish chef Ferran Adrià of El Bulli won the SevenRooms Icon Award.

Restaurants given a special mention during the 2026 ceremony included Tuju in São Paulo, which earned the Highest New Entry Award. Copenhagen's Kadeau won the Highest Climber Award after soaring 33 places this year, and Naar in Kasauli, India, won the Sustainable Restaurant Award.

This year, only one US restaurant made the top 50: Atomix, in New York. It came in 11th place, rising one place from 2025, when it ranked 12.

Prior to the awards ceremony when the top 50 restaurants were announced, The 50 released its extended list of restaurants ranked 51 to 100. A number of new restaurants made the list, including Khufu’s in Giza, where diners savor modern and classic Egyptian dishes crafted by executive chef Mostafa Seif, paired with staggering views of the Great Pyramid. Newcomer Lamdre in Beijing is where chef Dai Jun dreams up plant-based dishes that dance between art and gastronomy, with a focus on native Chinese ingredients.
