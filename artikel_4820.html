Fitbit Edge vs. Fitbit Air: Which New Fitbit Should You Buy?

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Do you want a less distracting tracker or one with a screen and built-in GPS?

Our product picks are editor-tested, expert-approved. We may earn a commission through links on our site. Why Trust Us?

GOOGLE DIDN’T RELEASE a major new Fitbit fitness tracker for two and a half years after the Charge 6 came out in October 2023. Then it released two new models in five months. The first, the $99 Fitbit Air, came out in May and works a lot like a Whoop. You wear it day and night, and it sends your heart rate, sleep, and workout data to the Google Health app on your phone.Second is the $179 Fitbit Edge, which followed in October as the Charge 6’s successor. It offers the same features as the Air and adds a 1.34-inch screen, built-in GPS, and an ECG app that checks your heart rhythm for signs of atrial fibrillation, an irregular heartbeat that raises your stroke risk. Both trackers pair with iPhones and Android phones, so neither is exclusive to Google’s platform.For most people, deciding between them comes down to one question: do you want to see your pace and heart rate on your wrist during a workout, or are you fine checking everything on your phone afterward?

With no screen and no buttons, the Fitbit Air is as simple as they come to use. Google rates its battery at up to seven days, so you won’t have to charge every night. It’s also the cheaper way to get Google’s Health Coach. The $99 price includes three free months of Google’s Health Premium, which costs $9.99 a month after that.The Fitbit Edge shows your pace and heart rate on its screen during a workout, and its built-in GPS records your route without your phone. If you run or ride outdoors, you can leave your phone at home and still come back with a map and split times. The Edge also has two sensors the Air doesn’t: electrical sensors for the ECG app, and an altimeter that measures elevation gain on a hike and counts the floors you climb. It costs $80 more than the Air, and turning on its always-on display cuts the battery from about seven days to about two.

The Air’s tracker module is a 1.37 x 0.66-inch pod, and it’s about 25 percent smaller than the discontinued Fitbit Luxe. The pod pops out of its band and snaps into another one in a few seconds, so you can wear the fabric Performance Loop to sleep and switch to the silicone Active Band for a sweaty workout. If you want something dressier, the polyurethane Elevated Modern Band and a new Metal Mesh band are also available.The Edge measures 1.54 x 1.01 inches and is almost half an inch thick, with a pill-shaped body that’s noticeably larger than the Air. A side button opens the app list and takes you back to the clock face. It comes in Obsidian, Fog, Olive, and Canyon, with six band styles that run from the sweat-resistant Active Sport Band to a Metal Links bracelet. Both trackers are rated IP68 and 5ATM, which means they have dust and water protection.

The Fitbit Air and Fitbit Edge record the same health data. Both measure your heart rate around the clock, plus blood oxygen, overnight skin temperature changes, and heart rate variability (HRV), the variation in time between heartbeats. HRV usually drops when you’re stressed, sick, or haven’t recovered from hard training.The Google Health app uses those readings to set a Readiness score each morning. It also tracks Cardio Load, which estimates how much cardiovascular strain your workouts create, while your personalized weekly target helps you balance activity and recovery. It can help you spot changes in your training load, but it can’t tell you exactly what your body can handle. Both trackers can alert you to an irregular rhythm that could be atrial fibrillation. They can also notify you when your heart rate stays unusually high or low while you’re inactive. Both also have Smart Wake, which vibrates to wake you during light sleep inside a time window you set.The Edge adds a few health tools the Air doesn’t have. Its ECG app records your heart’s electrical activity whenever you start a reading. If your heart feels like it’s racing or skipping, you can take a reading in the moment and save it to show your doctor. The Edge has a dedicated skin-temperature sensor, while older Fitbits such as the Charge 6 use a device-temperature sensor to track overnight skin-temperature variation.With Google Health Premium, both trackers get Google’s advanced Health Guardian features, which track trends in your blood pressure and insulin resistance. Google says they aren’t meant to screen or diagnose any condition.Both trackers start logging common workouts on their own. In my testing, the Air logged walks and lifting sessions without my starting them. Google says an update expands automatic detection on both the Air and Edge from 10 workout types to more than 100, including strength training, swimming, and racket sports. A workout only gets logged automatically if it lasts at least 15 minutes and keeps your heart rate up, so you may have to log short or stop-and-start sessions yourself.

The Fitbit Edge has built-in GPS, so it can map your route and measure your pace and distance without your phone. Google says the Edge’s GPS is up to 40 percent more accurate than its earlier trackers, thanks to a new GPS chip and case design. The Edge also has an altimeter, which reads air pressure to measure how much elevation you gain on a hilly run or how many floors you climb on a stair workout.The Edge’s screen also shows your stats during a workout, giving you instant feedback. You can set a pace target before a run and check on the screen whether you’re ahead of pace or lagging behind. During HIIT intervals, you can see which heart rate zone you’re in. On the track, it shows your split for each lap.The Fitbit Air has no GPS and no screen. To map a run, you have to carry your phone, and you can’t check your pace or heart rate until you look at the Google Health app afterward. If you mostly lift or walk and don’t want instant feedback, the Air is a great option. But if you care about your splits, the Edge is the better tracker.

The Fitbit Edge comes with nine apps, including Google Maps, Weather, and Timer. You can’t download more, and unlike the Pixel Watch 5, it doesn’t let you talk to Gemini from your wrist. Google Maps shows turn-by-turn directions on the Edge only after you start navigation on your phone, so you can’t look up a destination on the tracker itself.Calls and texts from your phone show up on the Edge’s screen. On Android, you can answer a text with a preset reply like “on my way.” Those quick replies don’t work with an iPhone, so iPhone owners can read texts on the Edge but have to answer them on the phone. Swiping down on the Edge opens a control panel where you can turn on Do Not Disturb, use the screen as a flashlight, or make your phone ring when you can’t find it.The Fitbit Air has none of this. Its only feature you’ll notice day to day is the Smart Wake alarm, which vibrates to wake you up. Everything else, including the Health Coach, lives in the Google Health app, which also syncs with Apple Health if you want your data in one place on an iPhone.Neither tracker has NFC, so you can’t use either one to pay with Google Wallet.

You don’t need a subscription to use either tracker. Without one, the Google Health app still shows your steps, heart rate, HRV, blood oxygen, sleep score and stages, Readiness, Cardio Load, and workouts. It also alerts you to an irregular heart rhythm. Google Health Premium costs $9.99 a month, and the Air and Edge each come with three months free.Premium adds the Gemini-powered Health Coach, which you can message anytime with questions about your data, plus weekly workout plans that adjust to your schedule. When I told it my shoulder was sore, it reworked that week’s plan around it. It also adds more detailed sleep summaries, meal logging from a photo, and plain-language summaries of your lab results.

The Charge 6 came out in 2023 and retails for $159.95. It has an ECG app and built-in GPS, and it can track more than 40 types of exercise. The Fitbit Edge improves on almost all of that. Its screen is 46 percent bigger, it’s rated IP68 for dust resistance, and it charges in about 90 minutes instead of two hours. And when the Edge’s battery wears out, you can replace it yourself instead of buying a new tracker.The Edge does drop two features the Charge 6 offers. The first is NFC, the chip that lets you pay with Google Wallet. The second is the EDA Scan app, which measures small changes in the sweat on your skin to estimate your stress level. If you’re buying a new tracker, the Charge 6 makes sense only if you pay with your wrist or find it well below the Edge’s $179 price (it’s down to $129.95 on Google’s site at the time of publication).The Fitbit Inspire 3 is the cheapest Fitbit with a screen, at about $100. It’s smaller than the Charge 6, lasts longer between charges, and starts tracking walks and runs automatically. It has no GPS or ECG, but it remains a solid choice if you want to see the time and your step count on your wrist without paying for the Edge.

Buy the Fitbit Air if you lift, walk, or take classes and mostly want to know how well you slept and how hard to train each day. At $99, it gets you the same heart rate, HRV, and sleep data as the Edge, and the same Health Coach if you pay for Premium. Because it has no screen, nothing on your wrist lights up or buzzes during dinner or in the middle of the night.For $80 more than the Air, the Edge gets you built-in GPS that maps your route and measures your pace without your phone. The screen shows your splits and heart rate zones while you’re moving, and the altimeter counts the elevation you climb. The ECG app also lets you check your heart rhythm the moment something feels off.If you already have a Charge 6, you don’t have to upgrade to get Google’s newest software. The Health Coach and Premium features come through the Google Health app, so they work on the tracker you already own. Upgrade to the Edge if the Charge 6’s battery no longer gets you through the week. But it’s worth keeping if you pay with your wrist. Neither new Fitbit has NFC, so the Charge 6 is the only one of the three that works with Google Wallet.
