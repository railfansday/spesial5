Chelsea: Mykhailo Mudryk points finger for drugs ban as Tottenham loan star prepares for comeback

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Your weekly briefing on Chelsea, featuring team news and expert analysis from Dom Smith

Mykhailo Mudryk has blamed the Ukrainian football association for his doping ban.

The Chelsea winger, currently on loan at Tottenham, sat out the entirety of the 2025-26 season due to a positive drugs test for meldonium, a banned substance, back in October 2024.

The substance is believed to enhance stamina and respiratory capacity.

The forward was allowed to play once more in the summer, when his four-year ban, that he appealed through the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS), was lifted.

Chelsea manager Xabi Alonso pushed for the winger to go out on loan to earn regular game time, and he reunited with his former boss at Shakhtar Donetsk, Roberto De Zerbi, at Spurs.

Now, Mudryk has pointed the finger at the Ukrainian FA for the ‘adverse finding’ in his out-of-competition sample that was supplied two years ago.

"It was a bolt from the blue,” he said of his positive drugs test on Ukrainian podcast ‘Guest of Gordon’.

“I couldn’t believe it was true. It was like falling off a skyscraper. In a dream you would hit the ground. In life, I just kept on failing. It hit me very hard, mentally.”

The 25-year-old claims that he had never heard of meldonium, insisting that there was “no security” on international duty.

“It is a drug you can only get in Eastern Europe,” he explained. “It could only have got into my body in Ukraine. There is no such drug in England.”

Asked how it had got into his system, he replied: "The Ukrainian national team has absolutely no security system. At clubs, when you travel to an away game, there are always people responsible for what happens on the hotel floors and in the team.

“There are people who keep an eye on it. In the Ukrainian national team we have a nutritionist who hands us pills every couple of days before games.

“He brings us a little cup. Everyone has to take the pills that are in that cup.”

The winger, who moved to Stamford Bridge in 2023 for an initial £62million - that could rise to £89m - was asked point-blank if he blamed his national setup.
