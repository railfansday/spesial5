James Gunn’s DC Studios Exit Came at the Worst Time

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

The news that James Gunn and Peter Safran are stepping down as co-heads of DC Studios was a major shock, whether you're a longtime comic book fan or someone who's been enjoying the current slate of DC-themed projects. It also felt out of nowhere: while DC Studios had a bit of a road bump due to Supergirl's less-than-stellar reception, it more than made up for it with two critically acclaimed projects in Lanterns and Clayface. This decision will loom over future DC projects, and it comes at the wrong time.

While Gunn has taken to social media to stress that stepping down as co-CEO was his decision, eyebrows are being raised at the man chosen to replace him and Safran. Skydance has tapped Jeremy Latcham as DC Studios' new president and chief creative officer, and Latcham isn't lacking in terms of box office credits; he produced some of Marvel Studios' biggest movies, including The Avengers and Guardians of the Galaxy (which Gunn directed) alongside the highly underrated Dungeons and Dragons: Honor Among Thieves. Latcham's Marvel work likely led to his top spot at DC Studios, but it also highlights a recurring trend in DC-based movies.

Jeremy Latcham's hiring is far from the first time that Warner Bros. has hired a creative best known for working at Marvel Studios. Back in 2017, Joss Whedon was hired to complete principal photography on Justice League after Zack Snyder departed the project following the loss of his daughter, Autumn. The result was a disaster; Snyder's more operatic filmmaking style clashed with Whedon's zippy, quippy approach to writing superheroes. Add in accusations of "gross misconduct" by star Ray Fisher, and Whedon's time on Justice League ended rather poorly. It also meant that DC's bid to reclaim some of the Marvel magic was dead in the water.

Though it's never been said out loud, some fans have speculated that a large part of the reason Gunn was chosen as DC Studios' CEO, beyond his work on The Suicide Squad and Peacemaker, is his previous work on the Guardians of the Galaxy trilogy. However, Gunn proved that he could make DC Studios its own thing by delivering character-driven stories, whether it's the blood-soaked chaos of Creature Commandos or the sci-fi spectacle of Superman. Latcham needs to keep that in mind, rather than chasing Marvel Studios' success.

Four teams, four completely different reasons to put yourself in danger for strangers. This is the roster you actually belong on, what they would want from you, and what it would cost you to stay.

You belong where the stakes are largest and the personalities are loudest. This is a team of people who are all used to being the most important person in the room, which is exactly why it works and exactly why it keeps nearly falling apart.

You have never had the luxury of being thanked for this. The X-Men protect a world that has never once been comfortable with them existing, and something in you understands exactly why they keep doing it anyway.

You do not want colleagues, you want your people. The Titans are the team where the friendship came first and the world-saving turned up afterwards as an inconvenient side effect.

You are not asking who to trust. You worked out the answer a while ago and it was nobody in a cape. This is where you go once you have decided the people in charge need watching, and that nobody else is going to do it.

James Gunn and Peter Safran's departures come as DC Studios prepares to release its biggest movie yet, Man of Tomorrow. Written and directed by Gunn, Man of Tomorrow will feature Clark Kent (David Corenswet) and Lex Luthor (Nicholas Hoult) joining forces to battle the malevolent artificial intelligence, Brainiac (Lars Eidinger). It's also shaping up to be the massive event movie fans have been waiting for, with the cast now including Milly Alcock as Supergirl, Aaron Pierre as John Stewart, and even Xolo Maridueña as Blue Beetle. Next are Season 2 of Creature Commandos and The People vs. Gorilla Grodd, with future projects stillup in the air.

It raises the question: What direction will DC Studios take next? Both Gunn and Safran are shifting back into creative roles, with Gunn saying that he's written another movie post-Man of Tomorrow:

"That's not to say Peter and I are immediately done with DC or superheroes. We have Man of Tomorrow out this summer. And we've just been engaged on another DC project which I'm writing, and we'll see where it goes after that. And, of course, the characters we've set up will live on."

The opening credits for Superman mentioned that it was ushering in "a new era of Gods and Monsters," hinting at James Gunn and Peter Safran's plans for the DC Universe. Time will tell if those plans shake out under Jeremy Latcham's leadership.

Man of Tomorrow premieres in theaters on July 9, 2027.
