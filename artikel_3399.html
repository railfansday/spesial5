Use this beginner-friendly plan to cut your risk of all-cause mortality by 45%

It’s an age-old conundrum faced by beginners and experienced exercisers alike, assuming you can’t improve one without undermining the other.

But hybrid training, the increasingly popular practice of developing strength and fitness side-by-side, flips this principle on its head.

New research suggests doing both could even add years to your life.

According to an observational study following 147,000 health professionals over three decades, mortality risk was lowest among participants who trained cardio and strength.

Compared with doing neither, combining one to two hours of resistance training with a hearty dose of aerobic activity (30-45 MET-hours per week) reduced mortality risk by 45%.

For context, 30 Metabolic Equivalent of Task (MET) hours per week is roughly three times the minimum physical activity guidelines for Americans, equating to several hours of brisk walking, running or other moderate-to-vigorous activity.

Strength training alone was also associated with a strong reduction in all-cause mortality (13%). As was aerobic exercise. But combining both led to the greatest results.

That’s no surprise to Charlie Marchon, Co-Founder and Head of MARCHON Training, who specializes in the field of hybrid training and devised this accessible seven-day plan.

“At MARCHON, hybrid training means developing multiple performance qualities side-by-side. Put simply: strength and conditioning,” he tells Tom’s Guide.

“The science lies in managing the interference effect, so that strength and endurance training don't get in each other's way.”

That, he explains, comes down to how sessions are ordered, how hard each one is, and how much recovery sits between them. “At beginner level the interference effect is small and sensible amounts of each will improve both.”

Here, you’ll find Marchon’s seven-day hybrid training plan, plus advice on how to measure improvement and progress the plan over the course of six weeks.

Charlie Marchon is Co-Founder and Head of MARCHON Training, the training app built around structured, coach-designed programmes for strength, conditioning and hybrid racing. A Strength and Conditioning coach with 10 years of experience, he has programmed for everyone from complete beginners to competitive athletes.

How to use this hybrid training plan to build strength and cardio

To complete this plan, you’ll need two pairs of dumbbells—one lighter for pressing movements and one heavier for squats, hinges and rows.

The week is divided up into three types of sessions:

While suitable for beginners, exercising six days per week will be a big jump if coming from a standing start.

If you’re short on time or would rather ease in slowly, Marchon recommends stripping it back to just the Monday strength workout, Thursday intervals and Saturday hybrid circuit.

“That three-day version still covers strength, harder running and the hybrid link,” he says.

2B Dumbbell floor press (or bench press if available)

Take five to 10 minutes to warm up. Do a three-minute brisk walk or jog to raise your temperature, three minutes of bodyweight lunges, inchworms and glute bridges, then two lighter sets of your first superset before your first working set. For each superset, perform exercise A, rest for 30 seconds, then exercise B, rest for 60-90 seconds.

Marchon says: “A squat and a pull, then a hinge and a push. Every major muscle group gets worked without two exercises fighting for the same muscles back to back.”

20-40min run at an easy pace (3-4 out of 10 RPE)

Warm up with a brisk three-minute walk or easy jog. Swing your legs forward and back then side to side for 10 reps. Do 10 walking lunges, 20 small pogo hops on the balls of your feet, 20m of high knees and 20m of heel flicks. Then you’re good to go. Once complete, walk for five minutes then gently stretch your calves and hips.

Marchon says: “Easy running builds your aerobic base and lets you recover from Monday. The pace will feel slow. Don't try to chase intensity. Hold a conversational pace throughout, meaning you can speak in full sentences without pausing for breath. If you can’t run that long yet, alternate two minutes of running with one minute of walking.”

Warm up and cool down as per Monday’s strength workout. For each superset, perform exercise A, rest for 30 seconds, then perform exercise B, and rest for 60-90 seconds.

Marchon says: “This session swaps in single-leg and overhead work, so you're challenged in different positions to Monday.”

Warm up as per Tuesday’s run, finishing the 10min easy jog with three 10-second sprints to get ready for faster running. The intervals should feel comfortably hard, meaning you can utter a few words at a time, no more. Let your breathing settle during the two-minute recoveries before each next effort.

Marchon says: “The one harder run of the week. Short efforts improve your speed and your heart's capacity without the strain of a long hard run. Aim to run every effort at the same pace. If the last one is much slower than the first, you started too fast.”

Walking, mobility, or a gentle yoga class is fine, but keep it light.

Marchon says: “Recovery is where the adaptation happens, so take this day seriously.”

Warm up as per Tuesday’s run, then perform one easy practice round of the stations above with light dumbbells before you start. Aim to complete four rounds, resting one minute between rounds. Use moderate weights. This is about moving continuously, not lifting heavy.

Marchon says: “This hybrid session ties the week together. You run, then lift, then run again while you're still a little tired, which builds the ability to keep moving well under fatigue. Treadmill users can do the runs indoors. Outdoors, run an out and back route of 90 seconds each way and leave the dumbbells at your starting point.”

30-40min run at an easy pace (3-4/10 RPE)

Warm up and cool down as per Tuesday’s easy run. Set a pace that means you finish feeling like you could have gone further.

Marchon says: “This long easy run is optional in week one; it becomes a key session as the plan progresses. You can use run-walk intervals if you need. If your legs feel heavy from the week, swap in a brisk walk for 45 minutes.”

This week-long hybrid training plan sets the template to build both strength and cardio in parallel. Marchon recommends sticking with it for a minimum of six weeks.

“Think long term,” he says. “The real aim is a lasting training habit that keeps improving your strength, fitness, health and longevity.”

That means challenging yourself with slightly heavier weights, running for slightly longer, or repeating an extra few reps, sets, or intervals as your strength and fitness improves.

If your sleep starts to suffer, you still feel sore 48 hours after a session, or something hurts rather than just feels worked, Marchon suggests repeating the week rather than moving on.

“Progress that holds beats progress that breaks,” he says.

After six weeks, Marchon suggests repeating the six-week plan starting with your new weights and run times.

“Minimal kit will take you a long way, but in time you'll need heavier weights and more structured training to keep challenging yourself.”
