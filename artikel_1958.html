Rangers pay tribute following death of club great Ralph Brand

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Rangers have paid tribute to club great Ralph Brand, who has died at the age of 89.

One of the most prolific strikers in the club's history, Brand contributed 222 goals in 347 appearances for Rangers over an 11-year spell at Ibrox between 1954 and 1965. He later played for Manchester City, Sunderland, Raith Rovers and Hamilton Academical.

Brand won 11 major honours during his time with the Light Blues and played in the Treble winning side of 1963-64. As well as lifting four Scottish titles, he also won four League Cup and three Scottish Cup winner's medals, the latter set achieved in consecutive years in which he was a scorer in every final, the only player to have achieved that feat.

He won eight Scotland caps and scored eight goals - no mean feat during a time when Denis Law was also in his prime.

Born and raised in Edinburgh, Brand was signed for Rangers by the late, great Bill Struth in 1952 after he impressed for Scotland Schoolboys in a match against their English counterparts at Wembley. He formed a famed strike partnership with fellow Edinburgh native Jimmy Millar in what was considered a golden period for the club.

“When Bill Struth signed me, Rangers were the club to play for,” Brand once remarked. “There were a number of clubs in for me from south of the border – big clubs too – but the only team I ever wanted to play for was Glasgow Rangers, and that’s a fact.”

Brand was also part of the Rangers side that reached the Cup Winners Cup final in 1961, becoming the first British team to contest a European final, before losing 4-1 to Fiorentina over two legs, home and away.

The club announced his passing in a statement which read: "The Rangers family is today mourning the loss of Hall of Fame forward, Ralph Brand, at the age of 89.

"A penalty box forward who would scarcely look out of place in the modern game, Brand’s strike partnership with Jimmy Millar stands alone in Rangers folklore. In many ways, it is impossible to think about one without thinking about the other; a double act which supercharged a glorious era for Gers supporters in the late 1950s and early 1960s. Where Millar was the steel; Brand was the silk.

"Ralph Brand’s contribution to Rangers was vast and, through his Hall of Fame recognition, he will forever be remembered as a great of the club. The thoughts of everyone at Rangers are with his loved ones at this sad time."
