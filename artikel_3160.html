They prioritize driving performance over everything

I’ve said it countless times this year: If there’s one type of vehicle I’ve driven more than anything else, it’s the 3-row family hauler. When you’re looking for maximum passenger capacity without skimping on comfort, full-size SUVs certainly fit the bill — and the Nissan Armada and Dodge Durango are no exception.

Unlike mainstream models like the Chevrolet Tahoe or GMC Yukon Denali that prioritize plush comfort over driving dynamics — often making them feel like lumbering giants — these two are far from it. Instead, these heavyweights prove that family practicality and raw enthusiasm can genuinely coexist.

The $55,225 as-tested 2026 Dodge Durango R/T 392 AWD Launch Edition delivers pure old-school muscle with a 475-hp 6.4-liter HEMI V8 engine that sounds genuinely imposing. That looks like a steal next to the $83,735 as-tested price of the 2026 Nissan Armada Nismo, but the Nismo counters with a twin-turbo V6 pushing 460 horsepower, sharper styling, and enough high-tech amenities to make a case for its steeper sticker.

After testing each one for a week, only one earns my recommendation.

2026 Dodge Durango R/T 392 AWD Launch Edition

NISMO-Tuned 3.5L Twin-Turbo V6 Engine (4WD)

13 mpg city / 19 mpg highway / 15 mpg combined

Frankly, neither design captured my attention at first — but one of them eventually grew on me. Even though they compete in the full-size, 3-row segment, they take entirely different styling routes.

The 2026 Dodge Durango R/T 392 Launch Edition leans hard into classic muscle swagger — reminiscent of the Dodge Charger — with its scooped performance hood, functional air intakes, bold Brembo brake calipers, and a rounded profile that still looks surprisingly menacing. It catches plenty of glances thanks to that aggressive stance, but it lacks the visual flair to truly sell me on its familiar look.

Between the two, the 2026 Nissan Armada Nismo gets my nod. Despite its noticeably more upright, boxy proportions, it delivers far more drama and curb appeal. Nissan gave it the full NISMO treatment, outfitting the SUV with massive 22-inch machined alloy wheels, an aggressive front fascia and grille, sculpted side steps with flared fenders, and full-width LED taillights. The sharp two-tone premium paint finish seals the deal, transforming this traditionally square full-size SUV into an imposing showstopper worthy of a cameo in a Fast & Furious flick.

2026 Dodge Durango R/T 392 AWD Launch Edition

Nobody shops for performance-tuned, three-row SUVs with the intent of saving on fuel. However, one of these gas-guzzlers still proved friendlier at the pump over the long haul.

The 2026 Dodge Durango R/T 392 Launch Edition surprised me most on dedicated highway runs, achieving a best mark of 24.2 mpg and handily overdelivering on its 19-mpg highway rating. On short-distance trips, I averaged 21.6 mpg — equally impressive given its 13-mpg city EPA estimate. Overall, it managed 18.1 mpg across 237.9 total miles of mixed driving.

In comparison, the 2026 Nissan Armada Nismo made a much stronger case for real-world consistency. While its best recorded long-distance run of 23.9 mpg fell just shy of the Dodge, its twin-turbo V6 and 9-speed automatic worked far more efficiently across everyday traffic. Over the course of 302 miles of mixed driving, the Armada Nismo delivered an average of 19.9 mpg — not only beating its official 17-mpg combined EPA rating, but also comfortably edging out the Durango by nearly two full miles per gallon overall. When you’re feeding a sport-tuned hauler week in and week out, that spread makes a noticeable difference.

When SUVs are slapped with performance badges, driving dynamics are what make them special over conventional haulers. While the Armada managed to surprise me in a big way, the Durango takes the crown here.

Nissan deserves immense credit for defying expectations given the Armada's boxy proportions, which initially made me apprehensive about driving it on crowded highways. It packs a genuinely strong 3.5-liter twin-turbo V6 pushing 460 horsepower and 516 lb-ft of torque, delivering instant thrust and effortless passing power.

It's also surprisingly easy to drive for its size, thanks to an ultra-responsive steering wheel that feels very light and loose. If that’s not enough, its adaptive electronic air suspension and great shocks make cruising over bumpy roads a breeze.

Yet for all the Armada’s composure, it can’t match the raw power and exhilarating feel of the 2026 Dodge Durango R/T 392 Launch Edition. Backed by a 475-horsepower 6.4-liter HEMI V8 under the hood, it completely transforms the driving experience.

Between the thunderous exhaust roar, immediate throttle response, and quick Brembo stopping power, the Durango delivers a far more thrilling ride every time I put the pedal to the metal. Honestly, it handles like a muscle car — giving enthusiasts the genuine excitement that almost every other three-row hauler lacks.

Both SUVs deliver great accommodations when it comes to passenger capacity, but one takes interior luxury and space to another level. What I love about both full-size haulers is how they offer second-row captain's chairs paired with a convenient center console for cupholders and armrest storage.

In the Dodge Durango R/T 392, second-row passengers have 38.6 inches of legroom to stretch out comfortably, while the third-row bench yields 33.5 inches. Even though adults can fit back there, my knees practically touch the seatbacks in front of me. Finally, there’s a cavernous 17.2 cubic feet of cargo space behind the third row that easily swallowed my electric scooter without needing to wedge it at an angle.

The one big downside for the Durango is that it lacks lane-centering assist when adaptive cruise control is engaged — meaning, it won't actively keep you centered between the lines.

The 2026 Nissan Armada Nismo simply offers more cabin volume to comfortably seat adults while pampering them with genuine luxury. Thanks to its taller cabin, legroom feels far more generous in the second row with 39.2 inches of space, while the third row provides 32.9 inches. The Armada also delivers significantly more cargo capacity behind the rear seats with 20.4 cubic feet of trunk space.

Between its plush, red-and-black trimmed NISMO suede seats, whisper-quiet cabin insulation, and ProPILOT Assist for hands-free highway cruising, the Armada makes long distance drices effortless no matter where you sit. That said, its lane centering isn't always spot-on when activated, but the overall luxury leap is impossible to ignore.

On paper, the pricing advantage leans heavily toward the Durango, but it's important to look at what each model actually delivers before jumping to conclusions.

Dodge’s value proposition is centered squarely on performance, yet this bargain full-size SUV still includes a respectable set of amenities. For example, you get a single-pane sunroof, a Harman Kardon sound system, and a standard backup camera. Still, I’m not as impressed with the dashboard layout, which leans far too conventional and dated.

By contrast, the 2026 Nissan Armada Nismo commands a steep $83,735 as-tested price that’s nearly $28,500 higher than its rival. That’s a significant stretch, but it buys a modern suite of luxury and tech features: an expansive panoramic moonroof, a high-fidelity 12-speaker Klipsch premium audio system, heated front seats, and a sophisticated 3D Enhanced Intelligent Around View Monitor that makes navigating tight parking spaces a breeze.

While the Armada undoubtedly delivers the more modern, tech-rich experience, the Durango's $55,225 as-tested price makes it an untouchable performance bargain in the three-row space.

I can’t overstate the price disparity between these two sport-tuned SUVs, with one costing nearly $28,500 more than the other. At their core, however, both deliver large family-hauling utility without forcing you to completely give up your passion for driving.

Without question, the 2026 Nissan Armada Nismo is the superior all-around modern SUV for everyday living. Between its sharp two-tone finish, plush suede seats, massive passenger volume, whisper-quiet cabin, and cutting-edge tech suite, it feels every bit like an $84,000 flagship that justifies its price tag. If your budget allows, the Armada won't disappoint with its effortless acceleration and smooth, composed ride — making it tough to beat.

2026 Dodge Durango R/T 392 AWD Launch Edition

Yet when it comes to the vehicle I’d actually spend my own money on, the 2026 Dodge Durango R/T 392 AWD Launch Edition gets my vote, despite coming up short on paper once all the category scores are tallied. Sure, the Durango's interior aesthetics lack the modern flair of its rival, but it unapologetically prioritizes genuine muscle car performance with three rows of seats.

With an as-tested price of $55,225, it isn’t just competitive — it's an untouchable performance bargain in a segment otherwise dominated by $60K+ land yachts.
