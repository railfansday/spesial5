AOC campaign memo frames growth from progressive firebrand to national Democratic Party force

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

‘The Five’ co-hosts discuss reports that Democrats running in 2026 midterm races in swing states are asking Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, D-N.Y., to stay away from their campaigns.

An internal strategy memo released by the campaign committee for Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-N. Y.) and distributed to key allies makes the case that the lawmaker has evolved from a progressive firebrand into a formidable national political force.

The five-page document makes no mention of a potential 2028 presidential or Senate run. Instead, it urges stakeholders to view her record as that of one of the Democratic Party's most effective messengers for working-class voters.

Following President Donald Trump’s 2024 election victory, the memo states, Ocasio-Cortez launched an aggressive nationwide push to rebuild the party around economic populism and establish a broad movement for working families.

"Over the past two years, Rep. Ocasio-Cortez has built a record of success that extends beyond her own electoral victories," the memo reads. "The Congresswoman has emerged as one of the most powerful endorsements in the United States and New York State specifically. Candidates from rural districts and battleground seats see her as an asset for their general election campaigns."

Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez at the New York Is Not For Sale rally on Oct. 26, 2025. A memo circulated among key allies highlighted the progressive lawmaker's ability to fundraise and deliver race-changing endorsements. (Neil Constantine/NurPhoto)

The document highlights her fundraising reach, citing more than 2 million contributions totaling $38 million, an average donation of $17. 56, and over $3 million funneled to other Democratic candidates and state parties.

Her small fundraising operation is unmatched in the House and rivals that of Sen. Bernie Sanders, I-Vt., the memo claims.

According to the document, Ocasio-Cortez’s strategy was shaped by post-2024 conversations with "Trump-AOC" split-ticket voters in her New York district, who expressed deep, cross-partisan frustration with a political establishment perceived as favoring corporate billionaires over ordinary workers.

"As Democrats tried to understand why Trump had won again, Rep. Ocasio-Cortez argued that the path for the party to win back working class voters should be centered on economic populism, confronting concentrated wealth and power, and revitalizing American democracy," the memo states.

The memo points to race-altering endorsements in key primary and general election races across California, Michigan, Pennsylvania, and Montana. It highlights Abdul El-Sayed’s Senate campaign in Michigan and Sam Forstag’s Congressional primary victory in Montana as central case studies of her influence in rural and conservative-leaning areas.

Forstag trailed significantly in the polls before joining Ocasio-Cortez and Sanders on stage at their "Fighting Oligarchy" rally in Missoula, Montana. Following her endorsement and appearance, he was able to erase the deficit and win the Democratic primary.

Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez Addresses A Crowd Of 36,000 People At The Fighting Oligarchy Rally At Gloria Molina Grand Park In Downtown Los Angeles On April 12, 2025. (Sam Ghazi / Middle East Images / Middle East Images via AFP)

The document outlines a turning point following her unsuccessful bid for ranking member of the House Oversight Committee, alongside a March 2025 budget standoff in which Senate Democratic leadership accepted concessions during federal shutdown negotiations.

In response, Ocasio-Cortez shifted focus to taking her message directly to voters.

Speaking at a rally in Idaho, Ocasio-Cortez laid out the central thesis of her national tour:

"I want us all to understand that this constant pressure we feel in our lives—of the water rising up to our throats, the impossibility to afford anything easily, the fear of speaking up or being who we are in public, the bitter divisions driven more by online algorithms than the truth, the crumbling of our rights and protections— understand that this, right now, is what it feels like to be governed by billionaires," she said.

In the two years since, the memo states, Ocasio-Cortez has held large-scale rallies in purple, red, and blue places alike.

"She made race-changing endorsements in swing districts and deep-blue party primaries, as well as in her home base of New York City," the memo added.
