Why Mikel Arteta must eradicate familiar Arsenal habit after Champions League frustration

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Your weekly briefing on Arsenal, featuring team news and expert analysis from Matt Verri

Here was Arsenal’s very own Groundhog Day.

Mikel Arteta would have feared what was coming as the misses piled up and his team found increasingly remarkable ways to not score.

It was rinse and repeat from an Arsenal perspective. Completely dominate the opening 45 minutes with some sublime football. Take the lead but then fail to kick on. Drop the intensity in the second half and regret those missed opportunities.

That happened against Slavia Prague, when the Gunners just about held on in the second half to win. It happened on Saturday, when they had to come from behind just to draw a match they should have been three or four goals up in.

And it happened here, in perhaps the most ridiculous way yet. With some better decision-making in the final third, the Premier League champions might have put six past Dortmund.

Instead, from their 30 shots, Arsenal managed just four shots on target and one goal. The visitors rode their luck but they stayed within touch and when their moment came, Maximilian Beier took it with the kind of ruthlessness Arteta can only dream of.

It is unrealistic to expect the north Londoners to dominate a match for the full 90 minutes, even more so against Dortmund than the likes of Slavia Prague and Newcastle.

The problem is Arsenal’s lack of efficiency when they are on top means they cannot afford even the slightest drop in standards or intensity.

Score the two or three goals that the dominance deserves in the first half and a dip after the break will not be so decisive. As it is, with Arsenal leaving the margins so thin, one moment is all it takes for the hard work to be undone.

At St James’ Park, that moment was Odegaard mis-kicking a clearance. Here, it was Joane Gadou charging away from Christos Tzolis and nobody in the home midfield coming across to cover.

The match should have been done by then. Again, Arsenal were sensational in the first 45 minutes.

Noni Madueke, a constant threat, could not sort his feet out quickly enough when a rebound fell to him. The winger was then completely open, screaming for the ball shortly after when Declan Rice instead went by himself and had his shot deflected wide.

Counters were wasted. Viktor Gyokeres, who opened the scoring with a superb strike from distance, suddenly could not hit the target. When Arsenal went in only 1-0 up, what followed next was as predictable as it was galling for the Gunners.

The level dropped just slightly and the Bundesliga leaders came back into the game, equalising ten minutes after the restart.

Even then, though, Arsenal should have been able to recover. Unlike against Slavia Prague or Newcastle, Arsenal did not fall away for an extended period. This match was there for them to win and the chances came thick and fast.

Gyokeres headed wide from a few yards out. The Arsenal fans spent almost the entire final half an hour screaming at the players to shoot as extra touches were taken around the area.

When Serhou Guirassy then bounced the ball off his body onto his own post not once but twice, Arteta collapsed to the ground in disbelief.

The winner would not come. A top-two finish, which was so important in last season’s run to the final, has now become much harder.

But that is what this side keep doing. They seem determined to make things hard for himself. Another match that should have been won inside an hour turned into a 90-minute slugfest.

With Arsenal hoping this is another long season domestically and in Europe, it is a habit they must shake fast.
