Shopping for a tween? These gifts won’t get an eye roll

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Buying a gift for a tween is a risky endeavor. Nail it, and you’re a legend. Miss, and you’ll get an eyeroll.

Tweens live in a tricky in-between zone. They’ve outgrown the kids’ section, but they’re not ready for the adult aisle. For the last decade, brands weren’t much help, as magazines and clothing labels targeted to this age group went out of business.

But this holiday season, tweens are back in the spotlight. A wave of startups is making clothes and skincare for kids ages 7 to 14. Claire’s is reinventing itself. Print magazines are making a comeback. Even the phone is being reimagined for them.

For this year’s guide, I went looking for gifts that hit the sweet spot. These products are mature enough to feel like a step forward without pushing kids to grow up before they’re ready. Many of them also lure kids off their screens and back into the real world.

Each of these gifts had to win over both the tween who unwraps it and the parent who pays for it.

Print is back

Anyway, Annual Magazine Subscription, $40

If you grew up taking quizzes in YM to find out which Spice Girl you were, you already understand the magic of the tween magazine. Anyway is a new print magazine for kids ages 9 to 15. It covers the awkward issues that can feel mortifying to ask about (crushes, body odor), along with style and culture. Every issue comes with a sticker sheet, a comic, and other screen-free activities.

A suitcase for jet setters in training

Away, The Carry-On in Misty Purple, $278; Featherlight Daily Backpack in Misty Purple, $98

Away built its suitcases for millennial travelers. Now those millennials have tween children, and those tweens want luggage of their own. This summer, Away launched In Between, a collection designed for this age group. The Carry-On is the brand’s classic hard-shell suitcase but sized slightly smaller than its adult version, and slightly bigger than its kids’ product. It even has room for a monogram of up to three letters. You can also get a matching backpack that slides right over the suitcase handle; it has a laptop sleeve, a water bottle pocket, and a D-ring for bag charms.

Her first fragrance

Evereden, Hair and Body Fragrance Mist Trio, $72

The first signature scent is a rite of passage. (I admit, I doused myself in Anna Sui’s Sui Dreams before the school dance.) Evereden’s trio of hair and body mists is formulated for kids and teens ages 6 and up, without phthalates, parabens, or common fragrance allergens. The scents have great names—Main Character, Darling, and Supernova—or whoever your tween wants to be that day.

GRWM: Holiday edition

Bubble, Main Attraction Hydrating Super Set, $70

After hordes of tweens took over Sephora, parents became wary of their kids’ slathering on harsh anti-aging serums meant for women decades older. So it’s comforting that Bubble designs skincare products specifically for the needs of young skin, namely moisture. The Main Attraction set packs six of the brand’s favorites into a festive travel bag, including Soft Launch cream cleanser, Cosmic Silk milky toner, Water Slide serum, Power Wave moisturizer, and Talk Back lip serum. The formulas use gentle, hydrating ingredients like hyaluronic acid and shea butter.

The hallway status symbol, upgraded

Lululemon, Everywhere Belt Bag with Long Strap 1.3L, $44

If middle school has an official uniform, the Lululemon belt bag is part of it. This year, the brand gave the original a makeover. The strap now detaches with hidden snaps, and the main zipper clips into a little paracord loop as an extra safety feature. It’s big enough for a phone, lip balm, and emergency snacks, and comes in an array of colors—like lavender and fuchsia—that tweens will love.

Screen-free relaxation

Lifelines, FlowArt Dot-By-Letter, Landscapes, $12.99

Between homework, after-school activities, and friend drama, tween life can be overwhelming. With FlowArt, kids can decompress without a screen. It works by allowing them to create landscapes using tiny dots. Each pad has 20 scenes and comes with four double-sided markers in eight colors, along with a tray to keep them in, so they don’t get lost in a bag. At under $15, it’s a perfect stocking stuffer.

A first phone parents will approve of

Freckle, $199, plus $25 per month for service

If you’re looking to give your kid independence but are wary of giving them a smartphone, you’re in luck. Freckle, which I wrote about this fall, is a phone for kids 7 to 13 that skips the browser, the app store, social media, and YouTube. Instead, it has a camera that identifies bugs, birds, and plants and saves them to a digital sticker book. Kids can call and text approved contacts, and two Freckles tapped together turn into walkie-talkies. There’s even a tap-to-pay wallet for pocket money. The first batch ships in December, so preorder soon if you want one under the tree.

A Monthly Build Box

CrunchLabs, Build Box Subscription, $329.40 per year

There’s a good chance your tween already worships Mark Rober, the former NASA engineer whose YouTube experiments include glitter bombs for package thieves and obstacle courses for squirrels. He’s launched a subscription box called CrunchLabs with a new build-it-yourself toy that arrives every month. Past builds include a coin-snatching machine, a disc launcher, and a drawing robot, and each is linked to a 15- to 30-minute video of Rober explaining the engineering behind it. The kits are designed so kids 8 to 13 can finish them on their own in an afternoon, and then spend hours playing with what they made. It’s also available month to month for $29.95 a box. Kids 14 and up can graduate to the Hack Pack, which adds coding and robotics.

Make Your Legos Move

LEGO Education, Computer Science & AI Kit, $429.95 (grades 3–5) or $529.95 (grades 6–8)

LEGO’s new kit allows kids to build machines, and in doing so, teach themselves the basics of robotics and AI. The kits come with the LEGO bricks your child has played with their whole life, but thanks to motors and sensors, they can create machines and robots that can move. The system is designed to teach coding basics in a fun way. LEGO is careful about keeping data private. No data is transferred to the company, kids don’t have log-ins of their own, and all projects are saved locally. The grades 3–5 kit suits younger tweens, and the grades 6–8 kit adds an extra motor and a controller for kids 11 and up.

The Claire’s comeback collection

Claire’s, Disney Villains Earring Stack, $29.99, and Faux Gel Nails, $19.99

Claire’s, the last survivor of the ’90s mall, is staging a comeback, with new owners and redesigned stores. Its exclusive Disney Villains collection of accessories and cosmetics features Ursula, Maleficent, the Evil Queen, or Cruella De Vil in matching earring stacks, fragrances, and faux gel nails. Tweens gravitate towards villains because they are still part of the Disney world, but they are too cool to feel babyish. If you want to make it an experience, you can pair the earrings with a trip to Claire’s for a new piercing.

Beautiful books

Juniper Books, Harry Potter Mashup Book Set, $424.99

The bookshelf is the new status symbol, and this set is a showstopper. Juniper Books wraps all seven Harry Potter hardcover editions in custom jackets that serve as decor in a tween’s room. On a shelf, the spines form a single design that blends the colors and symbols of each of the four Hogwarts houses. It’s definitely a splurge, but it is likely to be on the shelf long after they’ve outgrown many of the items on this list.

A party dress

Pleat, Ruby Dress, $72

Sara Fowler, founder of Pleat, started the brand because she couldn’t find anything for tween girls between the smocked dress and the crop top. The Ruby Dress is one of Pleat’s most popular styles. Soft pleats gather at the neckline above a bloused top, and a fitted waist gives just a hint of shape without hugging the body. It comes in a silky fabric in teal, dark green, red, and a Forget Me Nots print, in sizes 7 to 16. It’s festive enough for a holiday concert, family dinner, and New Year’s Eve.

The sleepover flex

Buttah London, The Button Me Up Lounge Set, £99 (about $130)

It’s sleepover season, and tweens are often stressed about what to pack when they’re invited to a friend’s house. London-based Buttah has created a just-right lounge set for the occasion. It features a long-sleeve top with nice details like ruching and heart embroidery. It’s cropped, but it lands right at the waistband, so there’s no midriff showing, which parents will appreciate. It pairs with wide-leg pants that have a contrasting bow.
