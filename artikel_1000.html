Ten-man England hold on to claim famous win over Spain

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Spain vs England. UEFA Nations League Group A3.

Match report as England secure a stunning 3-2 win over Spain to end the world champions' 43-game unbeaten run and finish top of Nations League Group A3; Three Lions raced into a three-goal lead then clung on after Elliot Anderson's red card

Football Features Writer @nicholaspwright

England withstood a Spain fightback after Elliot Anderson's red card to secure a famous 3-2 victory and finish above the world champions at the top of their Nations League group.

Thomas Tuchel's side were sensational for an hour in Madrid, taking the lead when Harry Kane scored a penalty at the end of the first half and then adding quickfire goals from Ezri Konsa and Ben White.

But a one-sided game was turned on its head when Elliot Anderson was sent off for a last-man foul on Lamine Yamal following Jarell Quansah's error, allowing Mikel Oyarzabal to score from the spot.

Spain reduced the deficit to one goal when substitute Nico Williams converted Lamine Yamal's cross after Anthony Gordon had been dispossessed, but England clung on thanks to James Trafford.

The goalkeeper, selected ahead of Jordan Pickford, made a string of saves as Spain peppered the England goal, including a stunning double stop when he denied Fermin Lopez then somehow pushed Oyarzabal's follow-up around the post at full stretch.

His heroics, in addition to a vital last-man challenge by White on Williams, allowed the Three Lions to end Spain's 43-game unbeaten run with a historic victory which ensures they will be seeded in the Nations League quarter-final draw.

It appeared certain to be a more emphatic victory when White drilled the third goal through the legs of Unai Simon after Konsa had headed in Declan Rice's corner for the second. Spain had not even registered a shot on target at that point.

But Anderson's red card, after Quansah was caught on the ball by Lamine Yamal, reinvigorated Spain and meant England had to show the qualities demonstrated in their World Cup last-16 win over Mexico at the Azteca Stadium in July.

Their steel, and the contribution of their goalkeeper, got them over the line - and showed evidence of progress from their World Cup semi-final exit to Argentina, when they lost their lead late on.

"We are fully aware of the performance we put out there for 60-65 minutes. To be 3-0 up and deservedly 3-0 up.

"Of course the red card and penalty changed the momentum and the characteristic of the game. Thirty minutes against Spain, in Spain, feels like three hours.

England captain Harry Kane on Spain's pre-match World Cup trophy parade:

"If anything, that made us more hungry. It gave us the bit between the teeth to say 'okay, it's four months later but we're the team that's going to go on and be the next European and world champions.

"We still have to prove that, of course. We still have a long way to go. Nights like tonight will only help the belief in the team. It shows what we are doing. When we talk about it, it's one thing. When we show it against the best team in the world, then of course that's a rewarding factor.

"We're not a team that wants to retreat. We want to sit back and go again. Show the type of team we are when we are at our best. Full tempo, high press, winning balls back, running in behind, counter-attacking.

"We were hard to stop in abundance in the first 60 minutes. Then in the last 30 you lean on experiences in the Azteca and those types of games. I was speaking to Dec [Rice] out there saying that we have been through worse than this, and it shows.

"There were no big big chances. [James Trafford] made some amazing saves, the double saves towards the end of course, but when you can hold on to a lead against Spain with 10 men away, it shows the mental aspect."

"We knew we could beat them. We knew we were going to beat them today.

"The message from the manager after the Croatia game was so positive. The message from the boys, everyone pushing together, knowing that we're going to come here and not sit back and hit them on the counter.

"We knew from the first minute - our first 10 minutes was probably the best we've played in a 10-minute spell. Pressure, pressure, pressure, we could have had two or three.

"The red card changes the course of the game as it could have been a couple more goals if I'm being honest."

England's football DNA has been in focus this autumn. In Madrid, we saw the English style at its best. From the first whistle, England were boldly on the front foot, overwhelming Spain with pace, power and directness.

They snapped into tackles and gave short shrift to their opponents when they called for fouls. It was physical, it was aggressive. It was certainly not England trying to replicate the Spanish style.

The set-piece routine for the second goal was a reflection of how they have risen in importance in the Premier League and fittingly saw two Arsenal players combine to score it. Then, when down to 10 men, there was fight, heart and commitment.

Roy Keane called England's performance "perfect" at half-time. The errors from Quansah, Anderson and Gordon meant it certainly couldn't be called that in the end. But England's attitude and approach outweighed their mistakes.

This is football England fans will get behind.

England find out who they will face in the Nations League quarter-finals when the draw takes place on Friday November 20 at 11am.

The two-legged ties are scheduled for the March international break, with the first legs to be played between March 25 and 27 and the second legs between March 28 and 30.
