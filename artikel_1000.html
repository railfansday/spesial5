Khawaja branded 'biggest traitor ever' in Sandpapergate fallout

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Ex-South Africa bowler Fanie de Villiers says Usman Khawaja "broke every single code of conduct" with allegations in autobiography; Khawaja's claims since backed up by David Warner, Chadd Sayers and Cameron Bancroft; Dr Simon Longstaff feels Pat Cummins' denial of knowledge is "sincere"

Former South Africa bowler Fanie de Villiers has branded Usman Khawaja "the biggest traitor he's ever seen" after the ex-Australia batter's allegations about the Sandpapergate affair.

Khawaja said in his autobiography that every member of the Australia team was aware of the plot to manipulate the ball with sandpaper on the 2018 South Africa tour, a claim backed up by David Warner, Chadd Sayers and, most explosively, Cameron Bancroft.

Bancroft - who, along with Warner and then-captain Steve Smith, served a ban following the rumbling of the ball-tampering plot - wrote in a 4,000-word-plus article in the West Australian newspaper that Pat Cummins' vehement denials made him break his silence.

Current Australia Test skipper Cummins stated in a 68-minute press conference last week that Khawaja's claims were "utter nonsense", which Bancroft said left him feeling "angry, betrayed and alone".

De Villiers, who played 18 Tests and 83 ODIs for South Africa between 1992 and 1998 told Perth's 91.3 SportFM, that Khawaja had "broken every single code of conduct".

The 62-year-old said: "I want to go on the record and say I think Khawaja is probably the biggest traitor that I've ever seen ... over my last 40, 50 years of watching the game.

"He's sold out his fellow players. This is not about right or wrong. The wrong has already been handled. It's already been part of Australian cricket, and it's sad that it happened.

"But this guy has brought everything to the fore again.

"He's brought Australian cricket in disrepute all over again, breaking down of friendships, the code of conduct that one has within the team, he's done everything wrong one can think of."

Dr Simon Longstaff, part of the cultural review into Australian cricket that Cricket Australia sanctioned after Sandpapergate, says he did not think Cummins was lying in his fiery press conference but also believes Bancroft's account of the saga.

"I think both (Cummins and Bancroft) have been sincere in what they're saying," Longstaff told Australian broadcaster Nine.

"It's possible, that as a very young, inexperienced bowler at the time, (Cummins) was having conversations about the ball without understanding the conditions under which it was being altered. That has got to be allowed still as a possibility.

"I've been around long enough and have investigated various problems of this kind, where people have sworn black and blue that they were telling the truth, and you eventually find out that they both were being sincere.

"It's possible to utter what is objectively false but not do so with any kind of inclination to lie, so I think it's something still in the middle of that that needs to be explored.

"What is clear now is that the whispers that I was hearing at the time line up pretty much with what Bancroft has said, including the timing for the first use of sandpaper.

"What's distressing is that when people were given the opportunity to tell the truth, including by me, they chose not to do so. And that's a stain that carries across to the team to this day."

Former England captain Michael Atherton said on the Sky Sports Cricket Podcast that he does not expect Cummins to resign from his role as Test skipper.

Atherton said of the 33-year-old: "He's obviously got a lot of credit in the bank in terms of performance as captain, having transformed the culture and kept Australia at the top of the tree, if you like.

"So I don't think there's an immediate threat there."
