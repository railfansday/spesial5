We Tested How Long It Takes To Run Out Of Gas In GTA 6

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Grand Theft Auto 6 is a massive game! It takes about an hour and a half to sprint across the map. But most players will be driving around Vice City, and this time around, GTA 6 demands that you monitor your gas tank while cruising the streets and highways of Leonida. So how long before you need to fill up in GTA 6, and does it get annoying?

To test this out, I booted up Grand Theft Auto 6 and hopped into two different cars. First, I tested a basic and somewhat older pickup truck. I found a loop of highway near the southern part of Vice City and filled up my gas tank at a local station. Then, the journey began. For this test, I drove around like I normally do in GTA 6 when not being shot at or racing someone. I also tried really hard not to stop, but traffic is traffic, and sometimes I had to slow down or completely brake. I wanted to avoid damaging the engines of any vehicles during testing so as not to toss in yet another variable.

It took 29 minutes and 8 seconds for the pickup to run out of gas completely.

To provide some context: You can drive across the entire map of GTA 6 via its highways and with a lead foot in under 15 minutes. So, in theory, I think I could drive across the map twice before running out of gas.

For my next test, I swapped out the older pickup truck for a Banshee, a fast-as-heck sports car that has been a part of the franchise since at least GTA 3. I loaded up the gas tank and hit the same highway loop as before. I went faster in the Banshee, of course, but still avoided crashing and stopping as much as possible. I may have had to restart the test at one point when I slammed into a minivan and basically blew up my engine. Science is dangerous.

In GTA 6, while driving a Banshee sports car, it took 18 minutes and 31 seconds to drain my tank entirely.

So, assuming I don’t hit a minivan again, that’s enough time to drive across the entire map via highways and still have some fuel left over to get to a nearby station and fill up.

Finally, I grabbed an electric car modeled after a Tesla 3, found a nearby charging station, got the battery juiced up, and hit that same highway loop one more time. Luckily, I didn’t crash at all during this test, and after following the same rules as before, it took 20 minutes and 23 seconds.

Personally, across over 130 hours of Grand Theft Auto 6, I only ran out of gas once, not counting these tests. I ran out of battery once as well. In both cases they were stolen cars that didn’t have full tanks and I was too busy dodging cops to notice I was running out of go-go juice or zoom liquid. I also had a few moments where I got low on gas during getaways and had to change plans or vehicles to accommodate.

But for the most part, I forgot vehicles needed fuel when playing GTA 6. And the game does a good job of letting you know when you hit halfway and when you’re nearing empty, even marking the nearest station on the compass to help you get fueled up ASAP. There are also a lot of gas stations around, so I was rarely too far away to reach one. Also, if I was driving a personal vehicle that I owned, I could just use the in-game Scooter Bros app to request another one of my vehicles. And when, a bit later, I summoned back the car in which I’d run low on gas via that same app, it would have some extra fuel in the tank. Nifty.

So I never found the fuel tank (or battery management) annoying at all. In the early hours, when money is tight and car options are limited, it proved a bit more of a hassle. But later on, it stopped being a problem. If anything, it added a fun variable that could derail a robbery if I wasn’t being careful or cause me to ditch a stolen car for another option. I doubt it will cause most players too much annoyance.
