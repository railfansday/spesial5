Microsoft hit with Canadian class actions over massive outage

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

According to consumers in one lawsuit, Microsoft should have anticipated and prevented the outage, given previous blackouts that also disrupted services.

MONTREAL (CN) â The Microsoft outage that ground the world to a stop last week triggered a pair of class actions in Canada, claiming the blackout disrupted business and violated consumer contracts.

In a lawsuit filed in Quebec Superior Court, Montreal resident Maryse Dinelle says individual consumers like herself were blocked on Nov. 12 and 13 from accessing Microsoft services that they paid for.

Dinelle, represented in the suit by Consumer Law Group, says users who were inconvenienced â even by simply being unable to send an email â by the blackout that started last Thursday and was not fully restored until Saturday, deserve compensation from the trillion-dollar company.

“You would have paid a yearly fee, and that’s presumably to use it 365 days a year. And so, if you didn’t have the ability to use it, there’s a certain amount of money that was collected where services were not offered,” said attorney Jeff Orenstein of Consumer Law Group in a Wednesday interview.

Dinelle also claims Microsoft should have anticipated the outage â given two other major outages in 2025 â and made efforts to prevent the issue.

“Defendants committed faults in failing to prevent the service interruption to the Microsoft 365 services in light of previous recent service interruptions to Microsoft 365,” she writes in the suit filed Monday.

According to Microsoft in a report released Monday, the source of the outage was a combination of a latent defect from July and a reduced server capacity overwhelmed its systems. The American tech giant assured the public the blackout was not the result of a cyberattack.

In the class action made available Wednesday, Dinelle is seeking to represent affected individual consumers in Canada. Orenstein explained that businesses that use those platforms are not included in the proceedings because they have arbitration clauses that do not apply to individual users in Quebec.

But in another class action filed Monday and made available Tuesday, Metus Lykos Debt Law looks to represent all Canadians, including businesses, affected by the outage.

“A 24-hour email blackout isn’t a minor inconvenience when your business runs on it. There are lost customers, missed deadlines and employees you still have to pay,” said attorney Darryl Singer of Diamond & Diamond Lawyers representing the law firm. “Microsoft sold reliability. This lawsuit asks who pays when that reliability fails.”

Both class actions name the Washington state-based Microsoft Corporation and Toronto-based Microsoft Canada Inc. as defendants.

In the lawsuit filed in Ontario Superior Court, Metus Lykos claims Microsoft was negligent and breached the contract it held with its users by failing to prevent the outage.

“Before the outage, the defendants represented that the affected services were reliable and highly available, and that they had designed and operated them to prevent and contain outages,” the law firm claims in the suit.

Diamond & Diamond estimated economic losses from business disruption, lost productivity and other expenses could amount up to CA$100 million, or $67 million.

Courthouse News reporter Tatiana Mulowayi-Pelletier is based in Montreal, Canada.
