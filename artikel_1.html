Russian warship sinks in Benghazi after suspected Ukrainian drone attack

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

No one has claimed responsibility for the strike on the vessel, which had docked on its way back to Russia

A Russian warship docked in the Libyan port city of Benghazi was struck in the early hours of Sunday in a suspected Ukrainian sea drone attack, causing a dramatic night-time explosion and the sinking of the vessel.

The strike severely damaged the Merkury, a Russian navy corvette that had stopped to refuel and take on supplies, according to Libyan officials from the country’s eastern government.

Initial reports said several Ukrainian sea drones were launched at the ship. At least four drones were intercepted and destroyed, while two hit the warship, triggering the explosion, the officials told the Associated Press.

Satellite imagery showed the Merkury had sunk after the attack, which took place in the Mediterranean, more than 1,000 miles from south Ukraine, marking a notable geographic expansion of the now near five-year war.

Though Ukraine began the war with no navy, it has developed a sea drone capability that threatened Russian warships in the Black Sea. The Russian fleet has been forced to retreat to the eastern port of Novorossiysk because Sevastopol in occupied Crimea had become unsafe.

Hours later, a Russia-linked cargo ship said to be carrying military supplies, the Ascalon, was hit by a sea drone off the coast of Benghazi. Video showed the drone heading directly towards one end of the vessel, again causing a huge explosion.

Libyan naval forces ‌rescued the vessel’s crew, the ‌sources added. The health ministry said some crew members were in stable condition with minor injuries. The number of injured and their nationalities are not known.

Benghazi is controlled by the self-styled Libyan National Army, led by Gen Khalifa Haftar, which controls eastern and southern Libya and has been aligned with Russia.

There was no immediate reaction from Russian authorities, and no immediate claim of responsibility for the strike, but it comes as Ukrainian forces have stepped up their long-range attacks against Russia far beyond the frontlines.

The UN mission in Libya (Unsmil) expressed “serious concern” over the two drone attacks off Benghazi and called for “an immediate, independent and transparent investigation”, in a post on X.

The ‌eastern-based Libyan authority said port and naval base facilities, including warehouses, were safe and that ​normal operations would resume once the fire was brought under control and the site secured.

It condemned the suspected attack as a violation of Libya’s sovereignty and ⁠a threat to maritime safety, saying it would take ​legal and ​diplomatic measures against ​anyone found responsible.

“The Libyan government will not ​allow Libyan ‌ports, territory or territorial ​waters ​to be turned into an arena for settling scores or exporting international conflicts,” it said.

The specialist website Naval News earlier identified the ship attacked as the Merkury, a Russian corvette – the smallest class of warship, smaller than a frigate – and said it had almost certainly been destroyed.

HI Sutton, a naval analyst, said the attack was probably conducted by sea drones, after a photograph of one emerged apparently taken off the Libyan coast. The drone, he said, “is also likely the type used in the subsequent attack”. Similar-looking drones were involved in the subsequent attack on the Ascalon cargo ship.

If it is confirmed that Ukraine has been able to blow up or seriously damage a Russian warship in Benghazi, it would represent one of its most successful naval strikes during the almost five-year war.

But it also represents a risky extension of the fighting far beyond eastern Europe, in an incident that could affect civilians on another continent and the working of the port.

A small unit of Ukrainian special forces operatives has been based at Misrata, western Libya, and is believed to have coordinated drone attacks on Russian tankers and transport ships in the past year.

Russian ships regularly make use of the port, which has a growing military importance to the Kremlin, to supply Russian mercenary forces in Africa and as a base in the Mediterranean since the country lost its bases in Syria.

The Finnish military said the strike on the Sparta II ⁠on Saturday was probably carried out by Ukraine and that the vessel later drifted into Finnish waters, where it remained at anchor. The 16 crew members were evacuated on to a nearby Russian ship. One crew member was slightly ⁠injured.

On Sunday, visible flames had been put out, with operations focusing on cooling down the containers and stabilising the vessel, Finnish authorities said.

The ship, bound for the Russian exclave of Kaliningrad, had been damaged in international waters west of Russia’s Gogland Island, Finland’s defence forces said, corroborating an account by Russian officials. Kyiv has sent waves of military drones towards the Leningrad region on Russia’s north-west coast this year, including some that strayed into the airspace of nearby Baltic countries, leading to fears that the war was spilling on to Nato territory.

The risk of ⁠further attacks in the Gulf of Finland, a narrow arm of the Baltic Sea, could not be ruled ​out, Ivo Värk, the Estonian navy commander, told the public ​broadcaster ERR on Sunday.

Finland’s military said last ​week it had detected a strike on a Russian warship in the same area during Ukrainian drone attacks.
