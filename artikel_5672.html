RFK Jr.'s 'Lavish' Gifts; Next Fauci Named; Staff Cuts Tied to Outbreak?

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

— A weekly roundup of how public health policy is shaping practice

Two Democratic senators are investigating "lavish" gifts, such as flights to Fiji and Greece, given to HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. by a former employee.

Sen. Rand Paul, MD, (R-Ky.) said he is throwing his hat in the ring to become the ranking Republican on the senate's key healthcare committee. (The Hill)

The FDA mothballed a fast-track drug review program instituted under former FDA commissioner Marty Makary, MD, MPH. (Reuters via AOL)

The Trump administration appointed Natalie Dodson, a Project 2025 contributor who has criticized mifepristone, to counselor in the Immediate Office of the Secretary at HHS. (Mother Jones)

John Powers III, MD, will become the next director of the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases, a role that Anthony Fauci, MD, served in for almost four decades.

Meanwhile, Rear Adm. Brian Monahan, MD, the attending physician for Congress, resigned in the wake of investigations into allegations of lax oversight of controlled substances. (Wall Street Journal)

NIH said it will relocate its remaining chimpanzees at one Texas facility to a federal sanctuary in Louisiana.

House Democrats are investigating the "woefully inadequate" response to ongoing measles outbreaks in the U.S. by CDC director Erica Schwartz, MD, MPH, JD. (Inside Medicine)

Democratic senators are asking the HHS Inspector General to look into whether personnel changes at FDA and CDC worsened the response to this summer's Cyclospora outbreak.

And two House Democratic leaders asked the Trump administration for more information about recent actions to disenroll 750,000 people from their Affordable Care Act health insurance plans.

The American Medical Association has asked the Trump administration to abandon its plan to make certain peptides more readily available.

A group of physician organizations asked House and Senate leaders to delay the Medicare payment cut for modifier -25 and extend this year's 2.5% pay increase for another year.

The doctors and scientists elected in this month's midterms are hoping to bring back trust in science, but it won't be easy. (STAT)
