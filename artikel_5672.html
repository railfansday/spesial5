Texas finalizes ERCOT CEO contract extension, pay raise tied to performance

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Let Utility Dive's free newsletter keep you informed, straight from your inbox.

CEO Pablo Vegas could take home more than $4 million next year. The contract extension and pay raise were put on hold in September following public outcry.

The Public Utility Commission of Texas on Thursday filed an order finalizing a roughly 7% base pay raise and potential performance-based bonuses worth millions more for Pablo Vegas, the CEO of the Electric Reliability Council of Texas.

PUCT officials said the contract is “in line with professional peers nationwide.”

The contract was signed in September but put on hold following public outcry, including objections from Texas Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick, who said on social media, “during a time of increasing utility costs, this is not the time to give the CEO of ERCOT a multi-million-dollar pay raise.”

Neither Patrick nor Texas Gov. Greg Abbott responded to a request for comment on the contract’s finalization. Both lost elections in November and will leave office in January.

ERCOT filings with the IRS show Vegas earned a total of about $3.6 million in 2024 and 2025, with a base salary above $1 million for each year.

His new contract could see him earn $6.47 million this year, according to a September presentation to the ERCOT board, though only about $4 million would be paid in 2027 with some potentially due later based on long-term incentives. The new contract runs from 2027 through 2032 and includes base pay of $1.16 million, plus an annual short-term performance incentive of up to 100% of his base salary and an annual long-term incentive up to 180% of his base pay. There is also a $1.38 million “make-whole” payment due in 2027, related to agreements in his previous contract.

The performance incentives are set by the ERCOT board. The compensation agreement “ensures that pay follows performance, where incentive compensation is earned each year only by meeting key grid reliability and performance targets and unpaid incentives are forfeited if the CEO is terminated for cause or resigns without good reason,” PUCT Chairman Thomas Gleeson said in a statement. “This is not a guaranteed payout.”

Vegas has served as president and CEO of the Texas grid operator since August 2022.
