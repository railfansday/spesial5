MLB rumors: Mets' Tarik Skubal follow-up, where Blue Jays turn now, plus Yankees, Phillies, Red Sox, more

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

The offseason's biggest domino fell late Sunday evening: Tarik Skubal agreed to a nine-year, $450 million contract with the Mets. It is far and away the richest pitching contract in history, crushing Yoshinobu Yamamoto's $325 million deal with the Dodgers. The Mets still have other things to address, including adding another starter, but they landed the winter's best free agent.

It should be a busy week on the hot stove leading up to the expiration of the collective bargaining agreement at 11:59 p.m. ET on Dec. 1. Here are our top 50 free agents and top 25 trade candidates, and the important offseason dates and deadlines you need to know. Here now are Monday's hot stove happenings.

The Yankees and left-hander Brent Suter have agreed to a one-year contract worth $4 million, reports the New York Post. Suter will make $3.75 million in 2026, and there's a $3.5 million club option for 2027 with a $250,000 buyout. He is the fourth reliever the Yankees have signed this offseason, joining David Bednar (accepted qualifying offer), Tim Hill, and Jonathan Loáisiga.

With the bullpen mostly assembled, the Yankees figure to pivot to offense the rest of the offseason. They need a third base upgrade over Ryan McMahon and a right-handed hitting catcher to pair with the lefty-swinging Austin Wells. It seems likely the Yankees will go with Anthony Volpe at second base and Spencer Jones in center field, but those positions can be improved as well.

The Mets have agreed to a deal with free agent first baseman Paul Goldschmidt, reports the New York Post. It's a one-year deal worth $8 million, according to The Athletic. He will presumably pair with Jared Young in a first base platoon. We did not rank Goldschmidt, 39, among our top 50 free agents. He accepted a reduced role for the Yankees over the last two seasons and was a valuable clubhouse presence.

At this point in his career, Goldschmidt is no longer an everyday player, but he remains a solid platoon bat. He hit .317/.402/.572 against lefties over the last two years, compared to .235/.271/.337 against righties. Goldschmidt can still play a nifty first base as well. The Mets received the worst first base production in the sport in 2027. Even late-career Goldschmidt should be an upgrade.

After missing out on Tarik Skubal, the Blue Jays are now believed to be prioritizing Freddy Peralta, according to MLB Network. Toronto has a very good one-two punch in Dylan Cease and José Soriano. Behind them are Spencer Arrighetti, Cody Ponce (returning from a torn ACL), Trey Yesavage, and maybe Jake Bloss and Ricky Tiedemann. There's room for another starter, for sure.

Peralta, Michael King, and Trevor Rogers are the best available free agent starters now that Skubal has signed. Rogers declined the qualifying offer and is attached to draft pick compensation, which perhaps makes King or Peralta more desirable. Any of the three would boost a Blue Jays rotation that has upside but not much in the way of reliability behind Cease and Soriano.

The Dodgers are not expected to aggressively pursue pitching help after losing out on Tarik Skubal, reports The Athletic. They're comfortable letting things play out and seeing what shakes loose among second- and third-tier free agents. As much as the Dodgers would have liked to retain Skubal, they're in a spot where they didn't absolutely need him.

Despite this year's biceps and knee injuries, Shohei Ohtani is expected to pitch next season. He'll join Tyler Glasnow, Roki Sasaki, Blake Snell, and Yoshinobu Yamamoto in the rotation. Emmet Sheehan, Gavin Stone, and Justin Wrobleski are next in line, with prospects Jackson Ferris and Christian Zazueta not far away. The Dodgers could use another starter but aren't desperate for one.

The Mariners, Padres, and Phillies are among the teams interested in Red Sox outfielder Jarred Duran, according to MassLive. The Red Sox tendered Duran a contract at Friday's deadline and will now look to trade him. The Jake Bauers signing pushed Duran, who had a .266 on-base percentage in 2026, out of the regular lineup. A trade is the logical next step.

Philadelphia and San Diego badly need outfield help and are obvious fits for Duran. Even if the Mariners manage to re-sign Randy Arozarena, they could use another outfielder, though Duran would be a less-than-stellar fit for a lineup that is already heavily left-handed. Regardless, it seems unlikely Duran will be with the Red Sox by the time Opening Day arrives.

The Blue Jays, Phillies, and Yankees are considered the favorites for Japanese slugger Teruaki Sato, reports MLB.com. The Cubs and Mets are believed to have interest as well. Sato primarily played third base for the Hanshin Tigers but is not regarded as a strong defender, and it is likely that he will play first base or a corner outfield spot in MLB.

This past season, Sato, 28 in March, hit .306/.383/.609 with 39 home runs and won Japan's Triple Crown. He is similar to Munetaka Murakami in that he has big left-handed power, swing-and-miss concerns, and defensive issues. Murakami is a much more patient hitter, however. Sato is an aggressive swinger and is unlikely to walk much or post a strong on-base percentage.

The Blue Jays offered Tarik Skubal a nine-year contract in the $400 million range before he signed with the Mets, according to The Athletic. Meanwhile, the Dodgers never reached the point of making Skubal an offer, reports ESPN. Los Angeles was known to prefer a short-term, high-dollar contract to a long-term arrangement. Getting Skubal on such a deal always seemed unlikely.

As for the Padres, they put forth an offer in the $400 million range but with deferrals that would have lowered the present day value, reports the New York Post. Skubal's contract with the Mets includes $30 million in deferred money, which will lower the present day value some, but not significantly. The exact deferrals in San Diego's offer are unknown.

The Royals and left-handed reliever Caleb Theilbar are in agreement on a one-year deal, per ESPN. Theilbar, 39, was excellent for the Cubs in 2025, but took a step back in 2026, pitching to a 4.86 ERA (87 ERA+) and 1.30 WHIP with 60 strikeouts in 50 innings pitched. He did strand 18 of the 23 runners he inherited, which is an adequate clip for a middle-relief type.

The Royals recently acquired late-inning lefty José Ferrer in a trade and Daniel Lynch remains in the bullpen, so Thielbar could well be their third option as a lefty in relief.
