perforated corten steel curbs reimagine street boundaries letting urban vegetation grow through

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

introducing openings into corten steel curbs, this intervention plays with the idea of letting nature through, turning an otherwise rigid boundary into an opportunity for growth.

Sara Fernandes edited by christina vergopoulou November 9, 2026 3 min

Conceived by designer Sara Fernandes, Giving Nature a Voice: Let Them Through is an urban intervention that reevaluates the standard street curb as an active threshold rather than a rigid divider. Drawing observations from spontaneous plant growth emerging within fractured concrete infrastructure, the project translates structural decay into intentional landscape architecture, questioning how standard municipal boundaries segregate soil and pedestrian corridors.

The project by designer Sara Fernandes substitutes conventional cast-concrete curbs with weathered Corten steel profiles punctured by an irregular series of circular apertures in varied diameters. These perforations allow adjacent soil and low-lying vegetation to permeate the boundary, transitioning the linear barrier into a porous edge condition. The weathered patina of the Corten steel establishes a tonal bridge between raw earth and paved pedestrian surfaces, while the non-uniform distribution of openings introduces intentional irregularities that accommodate natural succession. Rather than proposing a mass-manufactured civic standard, the prototype functions as a conceptual critique of hard urban thresholds, testing how small-scale physical modifications can accommodate biological agency within civic infrastructure.
