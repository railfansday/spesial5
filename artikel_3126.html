If AI Chips Become Commoditized, Nvidia Has the Most to Lose, While These 3 Stocks Are Set to Gain

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Nvidia has a lot to lose as AI chips become fungible, while these stocks could gain.

By Geoffrey Seiler – Nov 21, 2026 at 6:15AM EST

The AI chip market is starting to shift, and with it, Nvidia's (NVDA -14.01%) grip. Anthropic recently revealed that its Claude Mythos model could start running part of its AI models on alternative AI chips to Nvidia's graphics processing units (GPUs) in just a few days with basically no human intervention, a process that used to take six to 12 months and dozens of engineers. This greatly reduces Nvidia's CUDA moat at a time when competitors are starting to catch up.

Alphabet's (GOOGL -2.70%) (GOOG -2.59%) Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) have started to outperform Nvidia's GPUs on a performance-per-dollar basis, according to Semianalysis (though some dispute the results). Meanwhile, Advanced Micro Devices' (AMD -13.25%) GPUs continue to gain traction. The company has improved its software stack, and its chips' larger memory capacity makes them ideal for inference.

If the gap between AI chips continues to shrink and AI models can be quickly shifted to run on any AI accelerator, this is very bad news for Nvidia. Not only will the company continue to lose market share, but, more importantly, its pricing power will erode, and its margins will shrink. The Nvidia premium can be seen in its gross margins, which are in the mid-70% range, versus AMD’s in the mid-50% range. Meanwhile, all of Nvidia's largest customers, outside of SpaceX (SPCX -7.69%), have been developing their own custom AI chips to try to break free of Nvidia's high prices.

All is not lost for Nvidia, as the company still holds some advantages. It has created a cohesive hardware, software, and networking ecosystem that should still be able to command a premium. It also has major current supply chain advantages, having locked up more access to constrained high bandwidth memory (HBM) supply and having more manufacturing and packaging capacity with Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing (TSM -9.48%). That will help it maintain its leadership position, even as it will likely face continued market-share and pricing pressure.

While Nvidia may struggle, these three AI stocks look set to benefit.

Lower AI accelerator prices and more Nvidia GPU alternatives could boost overall AI chip demand, which would benefit Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing. It would also lower TSMC's customer concentration and could actually help with pricing power and gross margins.

Whether a hyperscaler, neocloud, enterprise, or government buys Nvidia GPUs, AMD accelerators, or ASICs through a company like Broadcom (AVGO -12.19%) or Marvell Technology (MRVL -11.69%), TSMC still wins at the end of the day. That makes the stock a buy.

As both a cloud computing giant and a developer of TPUs, Alphabet is one of the best-positioned companies to benefit from the commoditization of AI chips. Alphabet has more than a decade's head start with custom chips and is creating an ecosystem to rival chipmakers like Nvidia and AMD. This lets it get better utilization out of its data centers and gives it an advantage over other companies using their own custom AI chips.

Meanwhile, because its offerings cost much less than Nvidia's, it should be able to gain share with external customers without sacrificing margins. This ultimately could make Alphabet one of the biggest winners in the AI space during this transition.

Starting from a much smaller market-share base with an operating model already built around slimmer gross margins, AMD should benefit as AI chips become more commoditized. Between its lower prices, improving cost performance, and the recent introduction of a complete AI rack system (Helios), AI chip pricing normalization and chip fungibility actually become a tailwind rather than a headwind for a smaller Nvidia competitor like AMD.

Meanwhile, the market is heading in AMD's direction with inference and agentic AI, with customers caring far more about memory capacity, central processing unit (CPU) integrations, and cost-per-action, which is where its chips shine. With its new Helios system, which combines its GPUs and CPUs, AMD has a big opportunity to establish itself as a meaningful alternative to Nvidia in the coming years. This makes the stock a buy.
