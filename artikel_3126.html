Google’s Gemini 4 Neon ranks third in AI tests, replacing Argon in Gemini

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Gemini 4 Neon costs $3 per million input tokens and sits between Hydrogen and Carbon. Independent tests put it third, but its savings come from price, not shorter answers.

Google has released Gemini 4 Neon, a model that sits between Gemini 4 Hydrogen and Gemini 4 Carbon in its line-up. It is rolling out now in the Gemini API, Gemini for business, Google Antigravity and the Gemini app. Koray Kavukcuoglu, SVP of Google DeepMind and Google’s chief AI architect, announced it in a blog post on Wednesday.

Neon is Google’s fourth Gemini 4 model since Argon arrived on 30 September. It follows Gemini 4 Carbon on 10 November and Gemini 4 Hydrogen two days later.

“Neon delivers frontier-level reasoning for complex, high-frequency workflows faster and more cost efficiently,” Kavukcuoglu wrote.

Neon costs $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens, with cached input at $0.15. It has a 1 million-token context window and four thinking levels: low, medium, high (the default) and xhigh.

Google says Neon scores 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at xhigh, third overall. Artificial Analysis, an independent benchmarking firm, put it behind OpenAI’s GPT-6.2 Astra and Anthropic’s Claude Fable 5.2. It sits one point ahead of Carbon.

The firm also showed where the savings come from. Neon used about 15% more output tokens per task than Carbon. Its lower cost per task, $6.44 against $7.76, comes from cheaper tokens, not shorter answers. It also ran at about 110 output tokens per second, against about 57 for Carbon.

On coding agents, the firm scored Neon at 59. Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Opus 5.5 both scored 58. Neon cost $6.86 per task, against $12.61 for Opus and $15.47 for Fable.

Those cost figures use Carbon’s standard price of $4 and $20. Until 30 November, Carbon sells at an introductory $2 and $10, which leaves Neon 50% dearer for now, Artificial Analysis noted.

In Antigravity, Neon can also direct parallel Hydrogen subagents as an orchestrator, Google said.

A cyber version of Neon will reach eligible defenders over the next few days. It goes through Google’s Fairwind Program, the route that gave cyber defenders Argon first.

Google says Neon scored 90.5% on SEC-bench Pro, a test of finding code flaws. It did so at about half the cost per challenge of rivals. It also says Neon keeps Carbon’s safeguards against cyber and biosecurity misuse, while turning down far fewer legitimate tasks in those areas.
