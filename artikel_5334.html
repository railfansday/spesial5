Seattle police unleash dog-like robot whose best trick is bomb dispoal

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

by Kurt Schlosser on Nov 17, 2026 at 11:43 amNovember 17, 2026 at 11:43 am

Seattle police already have a dog that sniffs out tech. Now they’ve got a piece of tech that looks like a dog.

It’s a big upgrade for the squad, which has handled more than 600 calls so far this year. SPD’s main bomb robot is nearly 20 years old, weighs more than 100 pounds and rolls on tracks, and it can only be hauled around in the squad’s mobile workshop. Spot weighs under 100 pounds, climbs stairs and rides in a pickup truck that bomb techs drive daily.

The robot will only be used for explosive threats in the city and at the port, and it won’t carry a weapon. SPD said a weapon would go against Boston Dynamics’ rules for its robots. Spot’s 360-degree camera sends an encrypted live feed to the operator but doesn’t record or use facial recognition.

The only thing the robot does on its own is open a door, after an operator lines it up. As one bomb technician put it in SPD’s post, Spot will “be as dumb in 10 years as it is today.”

It’s not the first time SPD has turned to a four-legged partner for tech-related work.

Nala, an electronic storage detection K9 — profiled by GeekWire in 2024 — is trained to smell a chemical compound used in making electronics. The Labrador retriever helps SPD’s Internet Crimes Against Children task force find hidden phones, hard drives and micro SD cards during warrant searches. Her handler, Det. Daljit Gill, told GeekWire at the time that Nala turns up a device human searchers missed about 30% of the time.

Boston Dynamics, maker of backflipping Atlas humanoids and other viral-video robots, first unveiled the robodog in 2015. A smaller, all-electric version followed a year later.

The machines have long stirred dystopian unease. A 2017 “Black Mirror” episode featured killer robot dogs inspired by the company’s designs. Still, Spot has turned up in friendlier settings: then-Amazon CEO Jeff Bezos walked one at the company’s MARS conference in 2018 — a scene described by GeekWire’s Alan Boyle as featuring the “anxiety-inducing” robot.

Police departments have embraced the robots, too. More than 60 bomb squads and SWAT teams in the U.S. and Canada use Spot, Bloomberg reported last year, including a regional bomb unit in Portland. New York police returned one in 2021 after public backlash, then brought the robots back two years later.

Back in Seattle, SPD’s bomb technicians see more potential uses down the road, according to the department’s post, including communicating and observing from a distance as part of de-escalation. For now, Spot’s job is limited to explosives.

And the robot won’t be getting a new name, SPD said, “because it is not a toy, or a dog, even though it kind of looks like both.”
