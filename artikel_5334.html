U.S. Treasury has an unusual national debt headache: An $18 billion hole from GLP-1s, with families spending $500 less a year on groceries

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent—and whoever comes after him at the end of Trump’s term—has an unusual new headache to consider when it comes to budget deficits. Investors are usually concerned with long-dated Treasuries pushing up interest rates, or international lenders choosing to put their cash into other assets. But in the era of GLP-1s, weight-loss medications are a new concern for the budget bottom line.Nearly 60 million people will be using GLP-1s such as Ozempic and Mounjaro by 2030, Oxford Economics suggested in a note shared with Fortune this week. This is well above other estimates published earlier this year. JP Morgan predicted in February that 25 million Americans would be using the medication by the end of the decade, for instance.

But demand has surprised in 2026, so much so that Denmark’s national bank increased its economic growth estimates in September, largely due to U.S. demand for Novo Nordisk products Ozempic and Wegovy.

But with the drugs now covered by government health programs and contributing to potentially longer lifespans, Oxford Economics’ Grace Zwemmer writes that GLP-1s “are an additional factor driving a deterioration in the fiscal situation over the next decade.”

Budget watchdogs like the Congressional Budget Office (CBO) have already said the fiscal impact of approving GLP-1s is going to be a problem for Medicare. In 2024, the CBO wrote that coverage of drugs for specific use cases would, on net, increase federal spending by about $35 billion between 2026 and 2034.

Zwemmer suggests the costs will be higher due to above-expected demand. More than 1.1 million beneficiaries in the U.S. have signed up for discounted GLP-1s under a pilot introduced this year, allowing patients to access the drugs for reasons other than Type-2 diabetes.

“On net, we estimate Medicare coverage of GLP-1s for weight loss will add close to $18 billion to the deficit by 2034. This is roughly 0.9% of the annual deficit, but a much smaller share of GDP, only about 5bps. The cumulative cost over 2026-2034 comes to around $107bn, about three times greater than the CBO’s estimate,” Zwemmer wrote

From a macroeconomic perspective, there are also benefits to the government in supporting access to these medications. Zwemmer says that up to 480,000 more Americans could be working by 2030 thanks to improved health. This would contribute a meaningful lift to payrolls and taxable incomes in a labor force shrinking due to aging and lower immigration.

Individual households that use GLP-1s could also see changes in their personal finances.

A 2024 study by Cornell and Numerator found that households with at least one GLP-1 user reduced their grocery spending by 5.3% within six months of adoption. Most categories saw spending declines, but the largest reductions were concentrated in calorie-dense, processed categories, including a 10.1% decline in savory snacks.

The change in consumer behavior is so marked that GLP-1s are helping “keep a lid on food inflation,” Oxford Economics added. Zwemmer writes: “We estimate that GLP-1-using households will reduce their annual spending on food both at home and away from home by a combined $500.”

Zwemmer also notes that some of those funds may be diverted into buying new wardrobes, highlighting that new starters on GLP-1s are likely to spend $200 on refreshing their clothes. The U.S. economist added: “Scaled up to our estimate of nearly 40 million users entering 2027, this would translate to an $8bn uplift in apparel spending next year.”
