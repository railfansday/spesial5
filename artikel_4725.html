The GFG Style Peralta D Is a Maserati-Based Grand Tourer Rebodied in Actual Copper

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

The Fabrizio Giugiaro-penned one-off is inspired by his father Giorgetto’s iconic work in the late 1960s and 1970s.

Legendary car designer Giorgetto Giugiaro founded GFG Style with his son Fabrizio after leaving Italdesign Giugiaro over a decade ago. The studio has kept busy since then with work on concepts, design studies, and even one-offs, the latest of which blends elements of some of Giorgetto’s most famous designs with an unusual material: unpainted copper.

This one-off is the GFG Style Peralta D, a Fabrizio Giugiaro-penned 2+2 grand tourer that plays the hits with a wedge shape and a glassy canopy that can be opened either as exceptionally small gullwing doors or as one massive single piece. GFG Style calls this an evolution of the “dome” design used on the previous Peralta S one-off, and it gets even more unusual at the back of the car. Here, two more gullwing sections open up like the engine cover on the elder Giugiaro’s DeTomaso Mangusta design to allow rear passengers to enter and exit ... provided they can contort themselves through the diagonal opening created by the combined moving roof and rear panels.

The lower section of that roof, like the rest of what sits under the beltline, is made of bare copper. This creates a rich reddish surface now, although it is one that would presumably be susceptible to the same surface oxidation that develops over the metal that turned the Statue of Liberty green over time. Carbon fiber elements do exist along the front and rear bumpers, but these are relatively hidden. That leaves just the extensive copper brightwork, the reflective glass, and areas of more muted black trim to define the look of the car.

All that copper conceals what GFG Style calls “an aluminum chassis and top-of-the-line mechanical components based on the Maserati GranTurismo.” As both the current V-6 GranTurismo and its V-8 predecessor are themselves built around extensive uses of aluminum, this point suggests that the Peralta D could be built around a production Maserati. Whether or not that is the case, the mechanical details of the Peralta D are relatively trivial in comparison to the Cu exterior.
