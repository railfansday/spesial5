America’s rental market just hit a surprising turning point. Here’s what it means for your rent

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Depending on where you live, this might seem unbelievable, but rents have actually come down in many places in the past year.

Across the 50 largest U.S. metro areas, the median asking rent increased only $1 from last year, to $1,692 in October for studio, one- and two-bedroom properties, according to a new report from Realtor.com. This marks only the first annual increase in median rents in more than three years, as 26 major metro areas have actually seen rents fall in the past year, according to data from the Austin-based real estate site.

Of course, location matters. The story of today’s rent prices depends largely on how robust the construction boom was earlier this decade—and how many projects are in the pipeline. That’s why many renters may not feel like they’re seeing much relief.

“Renters still face a very different affordability picture than they did before the pandemic,” Jiayi Xu, senior economist at Realtor.com, said in a statement. “Elevated borrowing costs may keep more households renting, just as new deliveries slow. That makes the local construction pipeline increasingly important to the outlook for rent relief.”

WHERE RENTS ARE FALLING

Amid a broader affordability crisis, rents have been somewhat contained in recent years. Median asking rents nationwide fell for three straight years thanks largely to a boom in multi-family construction projects between 2021 and 2022 that added supply to the rental market.

Nationwide, it’s been a good run for people who don’t mind small living, as the median rent for a studio fell 1.2% in the past year to $1,411 as of October, according to Realtor.com. That marked the 39th consecutive month of annual declines in studio rents.

Renters in the South have experienced the most rent relief, as the metro areas that saw the biggest declines in median rent in the past year were:

  • San Antonio, Texas: Down 3.7% to $1,117
  • Tampa, Florida: Down 2.3% to $1,604
  • Nashville, Tennessee: Down 2.1% to $1,504
  • Houston, Texas: Down 1.9% to $1,377
  • Detroit, Michigan: Down 1.8% to $1,146
  • Sacramento, California: Down 1.8% to $1,794

Though rents edged up in Oklahoma City, this metro area holds the distinction for being the only in the latest report where the median rent is below $1,000, at $956.

WHERE RENTS ARE RISING

As has been true with the broader housing market, the current rental situation and outlook is a tale of four regions. And yet, renters nationwide may need to brace for potential rent increases ahead.

The Northeast has recently entered a rebuilding phase of the construction cycle, but given the lag when those projects will be delivered, rents could continue to rise. Meanwhile, the Midwest has the largest pipeline of projects relative to pre-pandemic levels but still saw rent increases in the past year. Even in the South and West, which have rent prices continuing to come down in recent years, those declines are narrowing in some metro areas in the South and weaker construction activity in the West could limit the relief to renters in that region.

Renters who want more space to stretch their legs or because of their familial needs are seeing the biggest increases in monthly rent. For the first time in more than three years, the median rent for a two-bedroom increased on an annual basis, up 0.3% to $1,889 nationwide. These renters have seen prices surge by nearly 19% in the past seven years, compared with a median increase of 15% in that time across all unit sizes.

Renters in the Midwest and California’s greater Bay area are getting squeezed most, as the metro areas that saw the biggest increases in median rent in the past year were:

  • San Francisco, California: Up 6.3% to $3,094
  • San Jose, California: Up 6.2% to $3,540
  • Milwaukee, WIsconsin: Up 4.1% to $1,767
  • St. Louis, Missouri: Up 3.2% to $1,298
  • Chicago, Illinois: Up 3.2% to $1,907
