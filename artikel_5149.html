Kenya Directs Crypto Providers to Apply for VASP Licences

Kenya’s central bank and capital-markets regulator have told businesses providing virtual-asset services to apply for licences under the country’s VASP framework. Their November 13 notice brings attention to the practical application process: firms now have to explain what they do, who controls them and how customer transactions will work.

The joint CBK and CMA notice says the implementing regulations were gazetted on July 22, 2026. The underlying Act dates to 2025. November’s development is therefore a regulatory direction about putting the framework into operation, rather than a law created this week.

Providers are assigned to the Central Bank of Kenya or the Capital Markets Authority according to their services. That structure makes the business model central to the application. Calling an app a wallet or an exchange is not enough to explain whether it also processes payments, holds assets or issues a token.

The distinction has a regional parallel in Nigeria’s separate stablecoin licensing rules. African markets are developing their own frameworks, with different activities and responsible agencies. An operator cannot treat a licence in one country as a general permission to serve the continent.

The CBK’s licensing checklist covers stablecoin issuers, virtual-asset payment processors and wallet providers. Preliminary submissions include a business-model presentation, three-year projections, an organisational structure, customer journeys and fund flows. Applicants must disclose cross-border operations and their regulatory status elsewhere.

Those requests reach beyond whether a platform’s software works. A regulator examining a customer journey wants to understand where funds enter, which entity controls them and what the user receives at each step. A company relying on several overseas partners would need to describe those handoffs clearly.

For a prospective customer or banking partner, the same map can reveal practical dependencies. If a wallet provider relies on a separate issuer for redemption, a successful token transfer is not the same as receipt of spendable local currency. Each stage has an accountable party, even when the interface presents one seamless service.

The implementing regulations set out application and licensing requirements. The published checklist then turns that framework into a staged process, covering preliminary engagement, names and formal company documentation. It specifies a non-refundable application fee of 100,000 Kenyan shillings for each activity sought.

The fee should not be confused with the total resources needed to operate. Preparing reliable records, managing access to customer assets and maintaining the people and systems behind a service can be much more consequential than submitting an application. A firm offering several activities also has to ensure that its documentation describes how they interact.

Ghana’s crypto sandbox programme offers another regional example of regulators moving from broad policy statements to defined business assessments. Kenya’s notice concerns its own licensing process, so the conditions of that separate sandbox do not transfer across borders.

The regulators say they will publish lists of licensed providers. The notice itself names no approved firm and gives no application deadline. A company announcing that it has applied should therefore be described as an applicant until the responsible authority confirms a licence.

That distinction gives customers a useful way to evaluate future announcements. They can compare the legal entity and permitted activity against the official record, rather than rely solely on a platform’s brand or a claim that it is regulated somewhere.

The commercial consequences will emerge as applications are assessed. Clearer responsibilities may help credible businesses build relationships, while compliance costs could change which services smaller operators can sustain. Kenya has made the application route more concrete; actual authorisations will show how the market develops under it.
