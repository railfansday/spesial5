Lido Earn’s new safety net still leaves depositors on the hook for losses

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Lido’s illustration puts conditional loss cushions at 7.2% for EarnETH and 12.1% for EarnUSD, not guaranteed repayment.

Lido Earn’s DeFi vaults gained a new layer of loss protection on Nov. 5, 2026, with different potential cushions for ETH and dollar depositors. Lido’s published illustration puts combined protection near 7.2% of EarnETH assets and 12.1% of EarnUSD assets, subject to eligibility, approval and available capital.

Firelight, an onchain protection protocol, now absorbs eligible approved losses before the existing Lido DAO buffer. Each layer has its own conditions and capital limits.

Lido Earn’s vaults put deposits into DeFi strategies. EarnETH allocates ETH and stETH, while EarnUSD offers dollar-denominated strategies. Users receive tokens representing their vault shares, whose value reflects performance. Protection attaches specifically to Earn vault deposits, with stETH used as an input to EarnETH’s strategies.

For the first 30-day period beginning Nov. 5, Lido says Firelight can absorb eligible, approved losses up to 5.7% of EarnETH’s total value locked, or TVL, and 8% of EarnUSD’s, within limits for each protected market.

Its November illustration puts EarnETH’s assets at $212.9 million, with up to $12.1 million from Firelight and $3.2 million from the DAO position. Lido states a combined ceiling of $15.4 million. For EarnUSD, it lists $50.3 million in assets, up to $4 million from Firelight and $2.1 million from the DAO, totaling $6.1 million.

Dividing Lido’s stated combined totals by those vault assets gives approximately 7.2% for EarnETH and 12.1% for EarnUSD. Its rounded ETH components add to $15.3 million rather than $15.4 million; both produce 7.2% to one decimal place.

This snapshot combines Nov. 5 vault sizes and first-period Firelight limits with DAO positions referenced to its Sept. 30 quarterly reporting. The dollar estimates come from Lido’s November illustration and should not be treated as Nov. 13 balances.

The totals also assume Firelight can fund a payout in full. Lido says settlement capital may leave payment below the validated loss. A larger illustrated percentage therefore does not, by itself, establish that EarnUSD has lower overall investment risk.

Firelight’s integration announcement identifies six failure categories: smart contract exploits, oracle failures, governance exploits, and bad debt, depegs or redemption failures caused by a protocol mechanism failing. A falling asset price or a depeg alone does not establish eligibility.

Lido says ordinary price moves and liquidations using accurate prices are excluded, alongside stolen keys, user error, offchain issues and flaws known before a protection period began. Protected markets and limits remain fixed for each 30-day period. Capital moved into a new market midway through a period becomes protected only when a later period includes it.

An independent consortium of five security and risk firms must approve Firelight’s loss assessment. Approved payments draw first on its reserve, then on staked capital.

Lido said on Nov. 5 that Current Meta Treasury pays for this protection, so the premium does not come out of vault returns. That funding is separate from the DAO’s invested loss-absorbing position.

The DAO layer operates under its own mandate and is not restricted to specific causes. Once a loss of at least 1% of a vault’s TVL is confirmed, the Growth Committee can burn up to all DAO-held shares in that affected vault. The mandate can be changed by DAO vote.

Lido’s original DAO mechanism was backed by an allocation of $3 million worth of wstETH to EarnETH and $2 million in USDC to EarnUSD. Those deposits hold shares on ordinary depositor terms, earning returns and carrying risk. Burning them reduces the share supply to offset losses for other holders. The capital available through this mechanism is limited to the DAO’s vault position.

Both layers operate at vault level, so depositors do not need to submit individual compensation requests. Incident approval, the DAO’s activation threshold and each layer’s capital limits still apply. Lido also says neither layer is insurance or a guarantee, and that Firelight protection gives depositors no right of action against a Firelight party.

A loss outside Firelight’s scope may still qualify under the DAO’s separate mandate. Any loss left after applicable protection, including amounts above market limits or beyond available capital and DAO shares, can remain with depositors.
