Bitcoin’s crash fears fade ahead of $7.5 billion options expiry, but December tells a different story

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Traders are paying less for immediate downside protection even as persistent December put premiums and ETF outflows threaten Bitcoin's recovery.

The premium for protection against a Bitcoin drop nearly vanished for Friday’s options, ahead of a Deribit expiry covering about $7.5 billion in outstanding Bitcoin and Ethereum contracts.

December options still priced protection against a fall, though at a discount to comparable bets on a rise, and traders may still move prices as they adjust hedges around settlement. Whether the rally lasts depends on demand for Bitcoin after those contracts expire.

A bounce as Friday’s contracts roll off would leave the question of spot demand returning for Bitcoin unanswered.

As of Nov. 26, GEX Monitor found that the premium for comparable Friday Bitcoin puts over calls had shrunk to 0.18 volatility points from 13.71 in its previous daily reading.

Puts offer protection against a fall, while calls give traders exposure to a rise. By Thursday morning, the two sides were priced almost evenly on that measure for Friday’s expiry.

Comparable puts carried premiums of 5.82 volatility points for Dec. 4, 8.10 for Dec. 11 and 6.62 for Dec. 25. All three premiums narrowed from the prior snapshot, yet each remained far wider than Friday’s.

An OptimistFi snapshot showed the same pattern, with Friday’s put premium near 0.2 points and the Dec. 11 and Dec. 25 premiums at 6.4 and 6.9 points.

December puts kept a larger premium over comparable calls in the Nov. 26 options snapshot.

Annualized implied volatility for Friday's options with strikes near Bitcoin’s price fell from 59.04% to 43.03% for Nov. 27, a drop of 16.01 volatility points. For Dec. 25, it fell from 51.17% to 48.21%, just 2.96 points.

PerpFinder’s expiry calendar showed over $6.8 billion of Bitcoin inverse options and $629.2 million of Ethereum inverse options due to settle at 08:00 UTC on Nov. 27.

The payout depends on where the settlement price lands relative to each contract’s strike.

Deribit’s inverse options settle financially in Bitcoin or Ethereum, depending on the contract. An option that finishes out of the money expires worthless, while an in-the-money contract pays out its intrinsic value, converted into the settlement coin.

As an option near the current price approaches expiry, even a small move can change how much a dealer needs to buy or sell to offset risk. After settlement, participants can unwind those hedges or replace them if they roll into a later expiry.

A study by Dustin Weiss and co-authors found intraday Bitcoin price reversals around past Deribit expiries. The reversal was most pronounced when open interest was concentrated near the prevailing price, and estimated dealer positioning was consistent with buying into rises and selling into declines to stay hedged.

Trading activity also rose in Deribit perpetual futures and the spot venues used for settlement.

The Dec. 11 maturity was carrying $717.1 million in Bitcoin inverse-options open interest in PerpFinder’s Thursday collection, and falls after the Federal Reserve’s Dec. 8-9 policy meeting.

Further out, PerpFinder’s Thursday tally showed $10 billion in Bitcoin inverse-options open interest for Dec. 25, about 47% more than Friday’s Bitcoin-only expiry. It gives December its own large settlement to watch alongside the higher relative price of its puts.

Farside Investors recorded over $3 billion of net outflows from US spot Bitcoin ETFs across seven consecutive trading sessions through Nov. 25, including $453 million in the last session.

Daily net flows for US spot Bitcoin ETFs tracked by Farside Investors, Nov. 17-25.

Bitfinex’s Nov. 25 analysis described how quickly an earlier rebound faded. After a Nov. 21 low near $55,500, Bitcoin reached $64,673 on Nov. 23 and gave back its intraday gain, finishing near $61,300.

A recovery that holds beyond the settlement window, a turn toward ETF inflows and a continued narrowing of December’s put premiums would give buyers a fuller picture.

ETF inflows would show demand returning through the funds, and cheaper December puts would show the change in options pricing had spread beyond Friday.
