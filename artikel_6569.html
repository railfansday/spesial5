I Tried the New AirDoctor 1500 Tower—And It’s the Perfect Size for Eliminating Kitchen Odors

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

It happens to the best of us. You’re minding your own business as dessert bubbles in the oven or dinner sears on the stove, then—bam! The smell of burning reaches your nose, and before you know it, you’re dragging out a stepladder to silence the blaring smoke alarm.

I hate to say that this is a fairly frequent scenario in my kitchen. While I do my best to prevent pans of lasagna from overflowing and exercise caution when searing steak, accidents still occur. The only difference is that I now have a solution to clear the lingering smell from my space.

I’ve been testing the AirDoctor 1500 air purifier for two months, and it’s a portable yet powerful option for eliminating pesky kitchen odors. Here’s everything you need to know.

Banish bad odors from your kitchen with this mini-yet-mighty air purifier.

The AirDoctor 1500 is a high-performance air purifier packed into a sleek, compact package. Unlike other air purifiers that can take up as much space as a dehumidifier, the 1500 measures less than 10 inches wide and 21 inches high. It features three filters: a pre-filter for trapping larger particles like pet hair, a carbon and VOC filter for filtering odors and an UltraHEPA for capturing everything else. It’s best suited for small-to-medium spaces up to 320 square feet and comes in black or white.

I’ve been testing the AirDoctor 1500 air purifier since its launch in September 2026. I’ve used it in my kitchen, entryway, office and family room.

The AirDoctor arrived on my doorstep in a sturdy brown box. It was well-packaged to prevent damage during shipping, and setup couldn’t be easier. All you have to do is lift the unit out of the box, remove the plastic bag, peel off a few stickers and, most importantly, set up the filter.

The filter housing snaps off easily, and installation is as simple as removing the filter from its packaging and popping it back in. In all the air purifiers I’ve tested, AirDoctor has the most seamless filter access, thanks to built-in magnets in the housing. This is important not only for changing the filter but also for cleaning it.

After completing setup, I plugged in the AirDoctor and turned it on. It immediately whirred to life, starting at high speed with a red light and working its way down to a low-speed setting. The control panel is reactive to a simple touch, with options for speed (auto, one, two, three or four), ion, dim and timer. The interface also includes a light that signals when the filter needs to be checked or replaced.

As the air purifier got to work, I was impressed with how little sound it produced. On level one, you can barely hear it. It’s a slightly audible white noise that blends easily into the background. Level four is obviously the loudest, similar to a fan on high speed. If you’re very sensitive to noise, you might find any air purifier, including the AirDoctor, too loud to sleep with. But in a kitchen, the whir blends right into the din of cooking, a running dishwasher and other ambient sounds.

Within a week of the AirDoctor being in my space, it faced its first real challenge. I had a potpie galette in a 425-degree oven, and some of the filling started to leak onto the pizza steel it was baking on and burn. Within minutes, my oven filled with smoke. I managed to get the galette out without setting off the alarms, but my space was filled with an unpleasant, burning-vegetable odor.

I turned on the AirDoctor, set it to auto, and it immediately kicked into high gear. The light turned red—a sign that it senses poor air quality—and the fan worked overtime to cleanse the air. After about 15 minutes, the light turned yellow—moderate air quality—and I could already sense a difference. Within an hour, it was back to a blue light, which signifies good air quality, and it’s like the accident never happened.

Over the next few weeks, the AirDoctor helped clear the air after I burnt some toast in the toaster and cooked cruciferous vegetables. It also helps combat day-to-day odors, like a full trash can or a garbage disposal ready for a scrubdown.

What I like best is that the AirDoctor isn’t just masking these scents; it’s actually removing them from the air. As an allergy sufferer, I’ve also noticed fewer symptoms (especially sneezing) when the air purifier is running. Thanks to the 1500’s portable size, it’s easy to tote it around my downstairs living area, too.

The AirDoctor’s filters are designed to last—but the exact lifespan depends on many factors. How long you run the air purifier each day, for starters. Along with what kind of baseline air quality is in your home. Pets, for example, are an obvious culprit for added dander and hair. Luckily, the 1500 includes a built-in sensor light that tells you when it’s time for a refresh.

You can also pop off the housing and give the filter a good vacuum. I like to do this once a month or so, but again, buildup may be more or less severe depending on your home’s air quality conditions.

I’ve had the opportunity to also test the AirDoctor 1500’s larger cousin, the AirDoctor 3500. But how do these models compare?

Size is the most obvious difference. The 1500 is six inches slimmer than the 3500 and weighs roughly half as much. I find that the 1500 blends more seamlessly into my space than the 3500, taking up less footprint. The round tower design is also very easy to slip into a corner.

That said, the 1500 covers rooms up to 320 sq. ft., while the 3500 covers up to 630 sq. ft. This makes the 1500 a great option for kitchens, offices, laundry rooms and small bedrooms, while the 3500 is a better choice for large living rooms and open-concept homes.

Beyond that, performance is very similar. Both models are quiet and easy to operate. They use a 3-stage filtration system, including an UltraHEPA. They’re both high-quality machines that are built to last. And that quality does come at a premium. The 1500 retails for $399, while the 3500 costs $659.

My spouse likes to joke that I’m like a basset hound given how sensitive I am to smells. That’s why the AirDoctor 1500 is an obvious choice in our kitchen. It efficiently rids our space of odors from cooking, pungent foods and even the occasional smoke-induced accident. Not only is this machine reducing odors that would otherwise linger for days, but it’s also cleansing our air of other contaminants.
