Here’s How the Celebrities Finished at the 2026 New York City Marathon

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

The stars were out Sunday morning in New York—and more than a few had some serious miles behind them.

The New York City Marathon is always good for some celebrity spotting, and this year, there was plenty happening between Staten Island and Central Park. On November 1, roughly 60,000 runners took the course as New York marked 50 years since the marathon first expanded to all five boroughs, with actors, musicians, former professional athletes, reality stars, and a small army of Peloton instructors mixed into the field.

Here’s how some of the noteworthy people finished this year.

The former New York Giants running back has become much more than an occasional celebrity entrant since retiring from football. Barber first ran New York in 2014, and has completed more than 15 marathons since taking up the distance. He ran 1:51:34 at the United Airlines NYC Half this March.

Connelly already knows this course pretty well. The Academy Award winner ran 3:45:47 at the 2024 NYC Marathon while raising money for Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center, and she returned this year in support of Fred’s Team.

S. Holden Jaffe, the singer-songwriter better known as Del Water Gap, ran the Los Angeles Marathon in 2025 and later said the four or five months he spent training were some of his best in years. After spending much of 2026 on his Chasing the Chimera world tour, he put another New York date on the calendar—only this one didn’t involve a microphone.

The comedian, actor, and New York Times bestselling author is took on her first marathon with Team Maybelline in partnership with the National Alliance on Mental Illness (NAMI). Garg is also featured in the brand's "Miles of Maybes" campaign and documented her training on Instagram, finding plenty to joke about as she prepared to tackle 26.2 miles for the first time.

Holmes returned for his fourth NYC Marathon. The journalist and media personality made his debut in 2022 with a 4:25:31 finish, improved to 4:14:39 alongside Amy Robach in 2023, and came back the following year to finish in 4:41:41.

The Bachelor alum and ABC Food Tours co-founder is practically an NYC Marathon regular, with five finishes already under his belt. His fastest came in 2024, when he clocked an impressive 3:00:20, just shy of sub-3. He returned Sunday for his sixth trip through the five boroughs.

Litz’s 2025 marathon Sunday was ridiculous, even by Broadway standards: He ran 3:40:53, then made it back to the Gershwin Theatre to perform as Fiyero in Wicked twice that same day. Now starring in the show on London’s West End, he returned to New York with a sub-three-hour goal—and, mercifully, no two-show day waiting at the finish.

The Tony-winning Broadway star is another repeat New York runner. Miller finished in 4:00:36 in 2023—the same year she had an extra assignment of singing the national anthem before the race—and improved to 3:52:44 last November.

Eight Peloton instructors ran New York this year: Robin Arzón (4:13:37), Mariana Fernández (3:34:00), Hannah Frankson (3:51:31), Alex Karwoski (2:51:17), Zacharias Niedzwiecki (4:22:17), Ash Pryor (8:29:13), Camila Ramón (3:58:33), and Matt Wilpers (2:54:37). Arzón ran marathon No. 28 and finished her ninth NYC. Wilpers and Fernández also have some serious speed, clocking 1:25:27 and 1:46:22, respectively, at this year's NYC Half, while Frankson completed the London Marathon in 2024.

The Filipino American rapper and producer isn’t jumping straight from the recording studio into his first 26.2. P-Lo has already completed the San Francisco and Oakland marathons, and he told Men’s Journal that New York has been on his bucket list for years.

Roll’s endurance resumé stretches well beyond traditional road racing. The plant-based ultra-endurance athlete and podcaster has posted top finishes at the Ultraman World Championships and, in 2010, completed the EPIC5 challenge—five Ironman-distance triathlons on five Hawaiian islands in under a week.
