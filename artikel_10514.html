What we've been playing - "It's slow, it's awkward, it's weird, and it's hard"

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

A few of the things that have us hooked this week.

Did anything notable happen this week? Not that I can remember. The weather was quite rainy - will that do? I'm just being silly, of course. We've had - and I suppose are still having - an historic week in video games, as Grand Theft Auto 6 arrives. It's dominated everything and I fear it's going to dominate this article too. But a reminder: next week is our "Not-GTA Week", so we'll be making a concerted effort to talk about games, probably smaller games, that aren't Godzilla blockbusters.

As you know, this is our regular feature where we say a bit about the games we've been playing this week. This week, rather a lot of us have been playing GTA 6, as you can imagine, and discovering various things about it. But not all of us. Connor, ever the contrarian, has been continuing his race to level 60 in World of Warcraft: Forever, and he's agonisingly close to getting there.

But enough about us, what about you? What have you been playing this week? Do tell all! And don't forget we have an entire archive of previous What We've Been Playings for your leisurely perusal, just in case you forgot what you were playing a year or a month or a week ago.

It probably comes as no surprise that I'm - much like the rest of the world - playing GTA 6 right now. While I found Chapter 1 to be quite a slow start, and where I felt somewhat restricted, I've now started Chapter 2 and found myself enjoying stealing cars, jamming along to the radio, and simply exploring Leonida - and all the strange happenings that come with it. There's so much to see and do!

I'm looking forward to deviating from the main missions for a while to do a spot of bird-watching (I like birds a lot, alright) and seeing what other side activities Leonida has to offer outside of the criminal lifestyle at the core of things. I'm also trying to be the best partner possible to both Jason and Lucia, but I'm itching to cause a bit of trouble soon... Hurting NPCs comes with so much guilt this time around though!

It's slow, it's awkward, it's weird, and it's hard. It's a perfect Eurogamer game, right? Right? GTA 6's in-game lampoon of Jonathan Blow, Hideo Kojima, Dan Pinchbeck and - weirdly - Mark Z. Danielewski is a wonderful bit of top-shelf pastiche. It goes to show just how much fun Rockstar had making GTA 6, and the freedom its dev team had to install as much depth and satire in just about every nook and cranny conceivable. Don't sleep on Tenebrae Lux, it's genuinely satisfying to complete.

I'm not, historically, the biggest GTA fan - although I've absolutely got a soft spot for Vice City's sublime mood - so I wasn't quite as excited to play GTA 6 as some. But it took, oh I dunno, about 30 seconds before I was sold. This thing has style. And everything else a billion dollars and some incredible development talent gets you. And it's those because-we-can details that have, in my first few hours, left the biggest impression. I spent a good five minutes just marveling at Jason dance - specifically the way his little stationary wiggle (perfectly timed to the radio beat!) shifts seamlessly into a wiggle-strut when you hit the forward button.

I love the way the setting sun seems to shine just right through the clouds when the moment calls, and those perfect needle drops. And careening through the night - amid the driving rain and smear of taillights, Kylie classic Spinning Around booming on the radio and Jason rocking a recently acquired, slightly improbable hat - this extravagant, ridiculous thing had me completely transfixed. I'm genuinely, unexpectedly excited to see where it takes me next.

Assuming there's lots of Grand Theft Auto 6 in this article, I'll save you from my own early impressions of this week's blockbuster release. Instead, you get more tales from an MMO. It is what it is!

I'm level 58 right now, pushing my way into the endgame dungeons. I've almost maxed all my professions, and am gradually getting gear that should serve me well when the raids are added next month.

But forget all that. I got a salt shaker! As a leatherworker, you can buy a salt shaker from engineers for a little bit of gold on the auction house. These are more important than you'd assume. With them you can make one refined deeprock salt every three days - a high-end crafting material. Rather than hoard these for fancy gear, I just sold 'em back to the auction house. They're current going for 50 gold a pop on my server, which is fantastic passive income.

This is important because I'm currently saving for an epic mount: a process that hit a major roadbump after I unlocked my secondary class specialisation for 50 gold, and then blew way more than that on unlocking all the available skills (that I likely won't use) from my druid trainer (for no good reason). I'm currently sitting on 232 gold as of writing. There's a long way to go to 1000, but hey, that's the game.

I can't imagine anyone changing the radio station when Drive by The Cars begins during that first moment of proper uninterrupted freedom. The buttons to do that might as well not even be there. If you did, we're completely different people.

I'm not very far in but my major impressions of GTA 6 so far are: the performances and their presentation are wonderful, the game controls a bit sludgily and feels like it's not quite in focus on PS5, and I really like DIY.

Let's take that middle one, that criticism: I'm sure this will lift as I get used to the game and how it moves, and how the characters work. It's also just a fact of life, so to speak, that it's going to struggle a bit on PS5 rather than PS5 Pro. And I'm nitpicking; on the whole, this thing is staggeringly technically impressive.

The latter point, DIY: it's absurd how much detail Rockstar has put into you doing up your shack. I especially like how it's not boring. A deeper thought related to this, and to the rest of the game, is the fantasy of self-improvement GTA 6 delivers. In GTA 6, as in all GTA games I think, you can drastically alter your life's fortunes in mere moments. I set about doing up my shack in a way I could never do in real life given a week or a month, but here, I did it in moments. That appeals to me greatly.

Honourable mention to Star Wars: Galactic Racer which I finally played this week. Love the speed, love Biddy Blas, and also really gel with the Roguelike approach, whereby you take the game in 'runs' to see how far you can progress each time. Cracking game.
