Bird Flu Cases May Be Underreported in Washington Outbreak

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

— Farm workers union says official numbers are a "vast undercount"

Human cases of H5N1 avian influenza are likely going unreported amid an ongoing bird flu outbreak in Washington state, experts said.

There have been 3 avian flu cases and one death in a patient with underlying health issues, according to Yakima County health officials. But a union representing farm workers said it believes the "official case numbers are a vast undercount."

"We have now connected with a number of workers and have confirmed that more workers are sick with avian flu symptoms who have not been tested," United Farm Workers said in a post on Bluesky. "Their symptoms range from very mild to very severe."

Amesh Adalja, MD, senior scholar at the Johns Hopkins Center for Health Security in Baltimore, told MedPage Today that if workers with symptoms aren't getting tested, mild infections are likely going unreported.

"The case count is a floor," Adalja said, adding that the recent death "is a reason to look harder."

He noted that farm workers "have every reason not to come forward," as many have "no paid sick leave, and some fear immigration consequences."

"Given the current and ongoing ICE [U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement] surge in the Yakima Valley, many or most of the workers are understandably reluctant to proactively engage with public agencies," the union posted on Bluesky.

The union also raised concerns that workers may not be receiving adequate training on how to safely handle infected cattle or milk. Adalja said that simply handing out personal protective equipment (PPE) without adequate training on handling infected milk and animals does little to help stop disease spread.

A spokesperson for the Washington State Department of Health told MedPage Today that the agency is doing site visits and educating both owners and workers on how to use PPE.

The agency also is "reaching out to producers and workers to encourage them to report symptoms and to get tested," the spokesperson added.

"We are emphasizing in our messaging that health information is private, and that we are not sharing identifying information with federal agencies," the spokesperson said.

Yakima Health District said it is running radio advertisements in Spanish encouraging dairy workers to protect themselves and to visit a clinic if they develop symptoms. It is also distributing printed materials in English and Spanish on how to prevent avian influenza and where to seek care and vaccines.

The state health department said it's also working with Yakima health officials and local clinics to provide dairy workers with vaccines and antiviral medications.

Since the outbreak began in October, the only cases in humans have been among those exposed to infected dairy cattle, officials said, and the general public remains at low risk for developing avian influenza. Currently, 43 dairy herds have cases of H5N1, and they are concentrated in Yakima and Benton counties.

Adalja noted that what's particularly concerning about the timing is the intersection with seasonal influenza. If a patient was infected with both seasonal flu and H5N1 at the same time, that would be a "real concern," he said.

Such a case would call for aggressive case-finding among workers and their households, rapid sequencing every positive, giving oseltamivir to exposed or symptomatic workers, and seasonal flu shots for dairy workers, Adalja said.

He added that every human infection gives bird flu another chance to adapt and potentially become more transmissible to and between people.
