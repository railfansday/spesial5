10 Most Extravagant Video Games of 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Ronald is a video game journalist with more than 10 years of experience in the industry, working on several of the most important Spanish websites.

As someone who's been playing and analyzing games for practically half my life, I've been getting increasingly hard to impress within the interactive entertainment industry after having experienced all sorts of things.

However, this insensitivity to the regular attempts to offer a unique campaign isn't impenetrable, as every year there are developers who manage to break through my shell of apathy and leave a truly strong impression.

Whether they're good or bad games—since the ability to surprise isn't always linked to quality—I always enjoy a campaign that evokes new sensations, given it's become increasingly rare.

Therefore, if you're looking for the kind of adventure that generates feelings you can't even describe, I recommend you try any of these ten most extravagant video games of 2026.

Games that follow in Pokémon's footsteps have left me indifferent for a very long time, but I didn't expect that one of the titles that would hook me the most this year would be Grombo Card Shop Tycoon, the early access version of a game that emulates buying and selling booster packs from the famous franchise.

With simply phenomenal humor and magnificent creature design, brimming with pop culture references, this title strikes a tremendously satisfying balance between incremental mechanics and shop management, becoming exhilarating very quickly.

Dealing with customers looking for specific cards and others who want to bet on whether they'll sell you one for double the price or give it to you for free, deciding what to sell and at what price, decorating to your liking, filling the enormous skill tree... It's a satire that's as conscious and funny as it is addictive.

Never in a million years would I have imagined that opening dozens of booster packs from my own business to find golden feces with a face or a Charizard made of sausage would be so satisfying, but Grombo Card Shop Tycoon made it a reality.

Remember the LCD aesthetic of the Game & Watch? Me neither, until I stumbled across Future Knight by accident and fell completely in love with its take on a rather forgotten variant of retro gaming.

Based solely on its glorious and unique visual style, the title's presence on this list is practically essential, but if we add the plot, a homunculus tumor inside a robot who travels to the past to defeat the tyranny of a cultist who erased humanity's memory and set up a pyramid scheme as a cult, it becomes even more compelling.

Regardless, the gameplay is equally impressive, as you control both the android, based on a 90s soap opera protagonist, and the tiny companion, resulting in an extremely fun 2D action-platformer with challenging bosses and spectacular animations.

Given its arcade-style focus, Future Knight lasts three hours yet will win you over for life, especially since, unless you're approaching old age, I'm sure you've never played anything remotely similar.

Ever since I played Ghostrunner at launch, I've been forever linked to One More Level and everything bearing its name, though even with that affection, I couldn't have imagined what Valor Mortis would bring.

The Soulslike genre is usually quite traditional and rigidly adheres to its conventions, but the studio decided to focus more on its own expertise than on FromSoftware's legacy, resulting in the only game of its kind where movement is a delight.

William inherits Jack's motion abilities in a first-person action title that's an ARPG, an FPS, and a parkour experience simultaneously, blending all sorts of gameplay concepts and philosophies that one wouldn't think should work together, yet they do so magnificently.

Add to that magnificent worldbuilding in an apocalyptic France of Napoleon Bonaparte, some of the best level design the genre has ever seen, and particularly high production values compared to its peers, and Valor Mortis becomes a rather particular specimen.

If you want to spend the autumn in the safest yet most violent way, I invite you to play the ten best action games that you need to play in fall 2026.

Faith: The Unholy Trinity is among the best horror video games I've ever played, and Tenebris Somnia became the finest spiritual successor I could have imagined for such an outstanding production.

Instead of rotoscoping, the game uses cinematic cutscenes with live actors and real sets, complemented by spectacular makeup and editing work that blends seamlessly with the title's refined pixel art to create otherworldly, bizarre, and terrifying creatures.

From its themes and levels to its creative puzzles and fascinating story and mythology, Tenebris Somnia is one of the most distinctive references the genre has seen in the entire decade, as easy to spot among hundreds of titles as finding an anvil in a haystack.

Because of its fundamental ideas and the perfect pacing with which it moves between video game and cinematic experience, you'll never expect the kind of story that awaits you, much less the fidelity with which that story is translated into gameplay.

Inside the AAA spectrum, few developers are as functionally eccentric as Capcom, which, as Pragmata demonstrates, doesn't need to sacrifice experimentation to deliver a truly enjoyable title.

The campaign unfolds through the dichotomies of hacking and shooting, Hugh's brutishness and Diana's innocence, the slow pace and dynamic levels, and so on with every element that constitutes it, because it doesn't run away from the tension of conflicting concepts.

It's an emotional and charismatic story, set against its backdrop so that, along with the spirited gameplay, it never stops moving forward, always feeling like a fresh and captivating experience that you never want to stop playing.

Most big-budget products avoid contradictions and risks, and therein lies Pragmata's extravagance: going against the grain of its class and prioritizing its creative vision above all else.

Kyle Thompson's craftsmanship is becoming increasingly refined with each new Metroidvania he releases, but he has truly outdone himself in every conceivable way with Well Dweller.

Speaking of contrasts, his latest adventure leaves you feeling both profoundly unsettled and captivated by its environments and inhabitants, as the juxtaposition of gorgeous biomes and atmospheric music with gothic and unsettling designs is mesmerizing.

Fighting bosses whose behaviors and aesthetics seem lifted from sleep paralysis is magnificent, even when it involves giant hands chasing you through the pipes of a kingdom, a dollmaker trapping your sister's soul in a plush toy, or a guardian decapitated by the moon to possess his body.

Well Dweller is a collection of fables and legends seen through the lens of its action-adventure gameplay, which, needless to say, is extraordinary and offers some of the best structures in the entire genre in terms of combat, movement, exploration, and progression.

The strategy genre displays extraordinary depth, and offers some truly unique and underrated titles.

"Extravagant" means unusual, striking, and peculiar, so I can't think of better adjectives to describe Phantom Blade Zero, which continues Black Myth: Wukong's work in breaking down cultural barriers related to China.

The video game industry of this Asian giant had only previously showcased its talent on a large scale in the mobile ecosystem, but projects like S-Game's open our minds to the world of possibilities that comes with seeing the interactive medium through their eyes.

The result? A glorious action game that can't be reduced to being inspired by one or another benchmark of the genre, but rather driven by Chinese idiosyncrasies in terms of functionality, visual identity, narrative, mechanics, and sound, offering a unique journey both on paper and in execution.

Among everything released this year, Phantom Blade Zero is by far one of the most exciting and culturally significant products, something we can't say about half of the creations that boast its production values.

If Remedy Entertainment ever releases a video game that isn't an enlightening, mind-blowing experience, I'll be the first to speak out, but Control Resonant shows us that we'll still have to wait to see if that day ever comes.

The sheer audacity of making such drastic changes from its predecessor, especially after Alan Wake 2's financial hardships, is the kind of gallantry we must praise, support, and cherish to keep the art of video games alive, because it transcends mere commercial success.

Consequently, we got a title whose story, setting, characters, enemies, and even gameplay systems transcend the ordinary, becoming part of those special titles you can only sum up as "I can't tell you about it; you have to experience it".

Even though Control Resonant isn't among my top titles from the studio, it's still magnificent and light-years ahead of what the average developer can achieve, proving once again that Remedy simply operates in a league of its own.

The name Edmund McMillen has become synonymous with irreverence, weirdness, and iconic status, as you can see games like Mewgenics from miles away and still have every fiber of your being know who created it and what to expect.

However, even with full awareness of its creator's unsettling talent, mere mortals aren't prepared to anticipate the absurd display of depth, inventiveness, and ability to cohere oddities into one of the most addictive products in video game history.

I don't know what it says about our industry that such adjectives can be applied to a work that consists of breeding cats with autism, ADHD, cancer, bipolar disorder, and other conditions to battle in turn-based duels against "fetus-like enemies", feces, or angel pigeons, but I'm glad nonetheless.

If you have the willpower to dedicate less than hundreds of hours to Mewgenics, I applaud you, but I'm not that strong, because I'm afraid I have a weakness for roguelites that have more quirkiness than pixels.

Now, the 2026 game that most often left me speechless, simply because I couldn't believe what I was seeing, is Romeo is a Dead Man, which is completely to be expected if you know who Suda51 is.

Author-driven video games aren't as common a phenomenon as they are in film, but Goichi Suda is an exception to the rule precisely because of how he can take a story so often repeated throughout the centuries across every imaginable artistic medium and still make it original.

You think you know the Romeo and Juliet fable, though Romeo is a Dead Man thoroughly subverts it to create a title with mechanics from hack and slash, visual novel, on-rails shooter, third-person shooter, creature management, and other genres I can't even name.

An intergalactic, interdimensional, and intertemporal twisted love story that plays with the rules of the universe as much as with our expectations couldn't help but be extravagant, yet that's just how the great Suda51 works.

I'd venture to say it's far from being the best game on the list, but that hardly matters because, when it comes to being unique, everyone would have to ask for its blessing just to even enter the conversation.
