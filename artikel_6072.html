Japan Is Closing in on the U.S. as High Costs and Politics Deter Travelers, Brand USA Says

Research shows the U.S. losing ground to Japan as costs and politics turn travelers away. Brand USA is betting the lost demand is deferred, not gone, but its budget to win it back is shrinking.

The U.S.’ place as international travelers’ top destination is slipping as more key markets look to Japan and other destinations, according to new research from Brand USA shared at its board meeting Tuesday.

While the U.S. once held a comfortable lead over competing destinations, that gap has narrowed as Japan gains ground and interest in the U.S. wanes. In January of last year, the U.S. led Japan by more than eight percentage points as the top destination. In August and October 2026, Japan finished ahead of the U.S.

“We still lead year to date, but Japan is now our most direct competitor for the long-haul International traveler,” said Chelsea Benitez, senior director of research and analytics at Brand USA.

The survey polls roughly 11,000 people each month from 11 key markets for the U.S.: Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Canada, France, Germany, India, Japan, Mexico, South Korea, and the United Kingdom.

New Brand USA research presented at its board meeting Tuesday shows the U.S. losing its long-held lead as the destination international travelers are most likely to visit. U.S. consideration fell from 30% in 2019 to 24% year to date in 2026, while Japan climbed to 23% and finished ahead of the U.S. in both August and October 2026. The monthly survey of roughly 11,000 people across 11 key markets — Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Canada, France, Germany, India, Japan, Mexico, South Korea, and the UK — points to cost and the political climate as the joint top barriers, each cited by 24%, with 23% saying they want to visit other countries first. Demand for international travel overall is steady, with 57% of October respondents planning an international trip in the next 12 months, in line with 2024 and 2025, so the U.S. is losing share to competitors rather than to travelers staying home. Net advocacy has weakened: the share who would recommend the U.S. fell from 64% in 2024 to 53% year to date, and those who would discourage visitation rose from 7% to 16%. Brand USA framed much of the decline as deferred rather than lost, with nearly 60% of travelers choosing other destinations this year saying they expect to visit the U.S. within five years. The data lands alongside overseas visitation of 24.1 million through September, down nearly 6% year over year, and a Tourism Economics forecast of 67.8 million international visitors in 2026, more than 2 million below its June projection.
