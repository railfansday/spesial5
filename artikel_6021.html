Dems, GOP rebels fail to rein in Trump's Iran war in Senate, but bigger battle looms

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Senate Majority Leader John Thune warns Democrats that attempting to shut down the government over military funding will backfire on them. He emphasizes that national security remains a top priority for the American people.

Senate Democrats failed to peel off enough Republicans to rebuke President Donald Trump and his war against Iran as another battle looms on the horizon.

In the first attempt after the midterm elections, Democrats and the squadron of rebel Republicans, including Sens. Susan Collins, R-Maine, Lisa Murkowski, R-Alaska, and Rand Paul, R-Ky., were not enough to pass a non-binding resolution that originally passed the House, pushed by Rep. Seth Moulton, D-Mass., in September.

Like the previous war powers resolutions, this effort would have required the removal of troops engaged in hostilities not authorized by Congress in the region. Even if it had passed, it was a more symbolic move given that it was non-binding and would not have been sent to Trump’s desk.

Sen. Chuck Schumer said President Donald Trump, seen on Oct. 28, 2026, would be the subject of Senate investigations if he violates the law. (Kevin Dietsch/Getty Images)

But in the background, Senate Democrats are eyeing another battleground to be more forceful with their desire to rein in the war: government funding.

The deadline to either fully fund or extend current funding is Dec. 11. Senate Republicans have warned that their counterparts intend to use the process as a cudgel to stymie tax dollars meant for the war and are willing to shut down the government to do so.

"I'm increasingly concerned that the Democrats may try to initiate another, a third government shutdown this year," Senate Majority Leader John Thune, R-S.D., said. "And that would be a terrible outcome for the American people and something I hope that they will think better of."

Sen. Tim Kaine, D-Va., countered to Fox News Digital that Senate Republicans were the ones who wanted to "shut the government down to maintain an illegal war that has just been massively repudiated by the American public."

"We're gonna fund government in a robust way. We're gonna fund the defense in a robust way, but if they say, ‘Yeah, that's not enough, we have to be able to continue a war that's illegal, and the American public hates,’ that'll be on them, not on us," Kaine said. "We're not gonna shut government down."

Thune’s comments came as Republicans were, until Wednesday afternoon, considering taking defense funding off the table and ramming a $350 billion request from the administration through with the budget reconciliation process.

But after a marathon closed-door meeting with Deputy Secretary of Defense Steve Feinberg, Republicans opted to give a shot at bipartisanship.

SENATE REPUBLICANS PLAN 54-DAY BLITZ TO PUSH TRUMP'S AGENDA BEFORE DEMOCRATS SEIZE POWER

Sen. Tim Kaine, D-Va., plans to force a vote on his war powers resolution to rein in President Donald Trump's military action in Iran. (Graeme Sloan/Bloomberg via Getty Images)

"I think the consensus of the conference is, you know, there are downsides to reconciliation," Sen. Ron Johnson, R-Wis., said. "We're not giving up by any means, but we would like to give Democrats a chance to work through an appropriations process, defense supplemental, that kind of stuff."

Senate Democrats acknowledged that they’re in a hard spot when it comes to the funding back and forth, with some recognizing that they need to agree to funding the Pentagon and restocking munitions while struggling to find the balancing act on guardrails against taxpayer dollars flowing to the war effort.

"We're in a really tough place," Sen. Chris Coons, D-Del., said. "I've been saying for months that the war with Iran has no support in my caucus. We are searching for responsible ways to bring it to an end, and a supplemental is a nonstarter, a reconciliation bill is a nonstarter, but I recognize we also need to find ways to invest in and support our troops and our security."

Republicans are also cautiously optimistic that Democrats will play ball, given the recent history with two record-shattering government shutdowns.

Sen. Kevin Cramer, R-N.D., was more optimistic than some of his colleagues, and told Fox News Digital that "I've got a lot of Democrat friends, both on the Armed Services Committee, who are, who are very supportive of our military. [They] understand the threat in the world."

"You know, they may have to, they may have to swallow their ‘I hate Trump pride’ a little bit, but, at the end of the day, there are a lot of people that are convincible," Cramer said. "But it does require talking to them. You know?"

Alex Miller is a writer for Fox News Digital covering the U.S. Senate.
