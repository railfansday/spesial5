It has been a messy few weeks for air travel in the U.S. Unfortunately, it appears the Thanksgiving rush could bring another big wave of flight delays.

Storm systems developing along both coasts threaten to disrupt holiday plans for travelers catching last-minute flights.

On the West Coast, United Airlines is letting flyers passing through its San Francisco International Airport (SFO) hub change their tickets as heavy rain and wind move into the Bay Area, coinciding with the travel rush.

Meanwhile, forecasters are warning of a “monster” storm that could disrupt flights at several of the nation’s busiest East Coast hubs on Wednesday and Thursday.

“The timing couldn’t be worse,” Fox Weather meteorologist Bob Van Dillen told TPG.

It’s looking like the latest in a series of frustrations for flyers. In the past two weeks alone, travelers dealt with a nationwide ground stop that led to 18,000 flight cancellations as well as technology outages at United and Delta Air Lines.

Now, it appears Mother Nature will be the biggest obstacle during one of the busiest weeks of the year at airports.

The West Coast storm is expected to impact the Thanksgiving getaway.

Late Monday, United issued a travel advisory for its San Francisco hub — a sign the carrier expects disruptions on the eve of Thanksgiving.

Fortunately, Bay Area leaders appear less worried about flooding at the airport itself.

In recent days, forecasters had grown concerned that heavy rain and high tides could lead to water covering SFO’s taxiways, which are right next to San Francisco Bay.

“At this moment, we are not expecting flood water to reach onto the tarmac at SFO, which is a positive development,” San Francisco Mayor Daniel Lurie said in a social media post — while warning that ground stops because of weather conditions were “still a possibility.”

In a statement to TPG, SFO officials said the airport was keeping pump stations and drainage areas clear, and staging sandbags ahead of the forecasted bad weather.

The worst travel disruptions could come from a powerful coastal storm developing in the east, meteorologists warn.

That East Coast system could bring strong winds, heavy rain and flooding to a large swath of the coast — including many of the nation’s busiest airports, according to Van Dillen.

“My advice would be, the earlier you can get to your destination, the better,” Van Dillen advised East Coast travelers.

We've often seen how significant East Coast storms can snarl air traffic. With so many key airline hubs, a major weather system can cause cascading flight woes across the country.

While Thursday could be a messy weather day, Thanksgiving Day itself is typically one of the quietest days of the year at airports.

All eyes will be on how Mother Nature cooperates on Sunday, as travelers return from the holiday. The Sunday after Thanksgiving is typically the single busiest day of the year to fly.

Last year, the post-Thanksgiving rush set the record as the busiest day ever at U.S. airports for passenger traffic.
