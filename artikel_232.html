Dozens missing as deadly fire hits Indonesian candy factory

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

At least three people were killed in a devastating fire at a confectionery factory on Java. Officials said many more people remain unaccounted for, prompting fears of a much larger tragedy.

Indonesian rescue teams are searching for 37 workers who remained unaccounted for after a fire at a candy-making factory killed at least three people, officials said on Sunday.

The fire occurred at the Fonusa Agung Mulia factory on Java, around 50 kilometers (31 miles) west of the capital, Jakarta.

Footage from the scene showed workers' family members waiting for news.

The factory caught fire on Saturday afternoon, local search and rescue agency official Rizky Dwianto said.

"All elements of the joint SAR (search-and-rescue) team are making maximum effort to locate the victims while prioritizing the safety of personnel in the field," he added.

Rizky said four people injured in the blaze had been taken to a nearby hospital and health clinic. Eleven more were said to have been rescued alive, with dozens of others still missing.

The authorities deployed 15 fire trucks to the scene, along with a rescue team including the police, the military, the Red Cross and volunteers.

Authorities said the blaze had largely been contained as of Sunday. However, firefighters were still carrying out intensive cooling operations to allow access all parts of the factory, according to Tubagus Mahfud, the operations chief of the district fire department.

He noted there were still hot spots in some parts of the building.

"We are conducting sweeps in the areas that have already been brought under control," Mahfud said. "Parts of the structure had collapsed, while poor lighting conditions required firefighters to proceed cautiously."

Drone footage shows the fire destroyed large sections of the three-story building, bringing down the roof in some places.

Survivors said the fire started in a cooling unit on the third floor.

The exact death toll was not immediately clear. While three people were confirmed to be dead, a report by the Reuters news agency indicated police removed 24 body bags from the scene.

Separately, the company's CEO said around 300 people were on site on Saturday, with 65 workers believed to be in the affected area when the fire broke out.

Senior police official in charge of victims' identification, Wahyu Hidayati Dwi Palupi, urged family members to provide DNA samples and other information to identify the victims, warning that the victims' condition made visual identification impossible.

The factory, located in the Serang district of Banten province, produces Milkita, an Indonesian brand of milk-based candies and snack products exported to over 40 countries worldwide.

The blaze marks only the latest in Indonesia's series of deadly industrial fires in recent years, raising questions about the country's workplace safety standards.

Among other disasters, at least 22 people were killed in a fire last December that broke out in a seven-story building in Jakarta after a suspected drone battery explosion, police said.

An explosion in a nickel-processing plant in the east of the country killed at least 13 people in 2023.
