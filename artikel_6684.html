A Brief Guide to Grand Theft Auto VI’s Nike Sneaker Dupes

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

GTA 6 is finally out, which means we’ve got some in-game sneaker shopping to do. We’d already spotted Air Force 1 dupes in the trailers, but now that we can finally play the long-awaited masterpiece itself, we’re taking a closer look at what else is on the shelves. There are tons of different sneaker stores sprinkled throughout the game, and Eris—Rockstar’s fictional answer to Nike—has given us a lot to work through.

Even the name is a joke. Nike takes its name from the Greek goddess of victory, while Eris is the goddess of strife and discord—an appropriate choice for kicks you might buy with money from an armed robbery. The Swoosh has been replaced with a pointier, upside-down logo, and plenty of the sneakers baring the mark are instantly recognizable.

The Eris Rise Low is the obvious starting point. It’s the game’s AF1 equivalent, complete with the familiar paneled upper, perforated toebox, and a big, beefy sole underfoot. The Eris Strife 360, meanwhile, looks straight out of the Air Max 97 catalog. There’s the rippling upper, rounded toe, and long cushioning window underneath. Designed by Christian Tresser and released in 1997, the IRL sneaker’s silhouette took inspiration from water ripples, while the OG “Silver Bullet” finish drew on mountain bike components. Eris keeps enough of that shape for you to be able to spot it from across the shop.

Some versions get even more specific. There’s a white-and-orange Strife 360, for example, with oversized branding, contrasting lace loops, and printed side text that recalls Off-White’s own Air Max 97s. It isn’t a straight one-to-one recreation, but the reference feels pretty clear. An apparent take on the Off-White Air Force 1 Mid also appeared in the teasers. Someone at Rockstar is clearly a fan of Virgil Abloh’s work.

Then there’s the Eris Strife 90, with pairs that borrow heavily from the Air Max 90 “Infrared.” The bright red accents and sharp, aggressive lines are all there, with one colorway even adding black scribbles across the overlays.

Arguably the most iconic signature shoe of all time, the Air Jordan 1, gets a look in the game as well. The Eris Rook 1 High comes in leather and suede variations, with a tall ankle collar and laces running almost all the way up. There’s a black-and-white pair that swaps Jordan branding for an Eris basketball graphic on the tongue. You’ve probably got something quite similar in your closet at home.

The Eris Cross Trainers are, well, harder to pin down. Available in several colorways, they have a mesh base and leather overlays. One white-and-gray version gets bright green hits that nod to Nike’s signature “Volt” hue. The simpler construction puts it closer to an older Nike runner, somewhere around MD Runner 2 territory, than a direct Zoom Vomero 5 or V5 Rnr copy. And for those times that tying laces feels like too much work, there are the very appropriately named Eris Basic Slides. The broad branded strap and flat footbed are very Nike Benassi.

There are different versions to consider, too. “Origins” go all-white, while “Elite” options are matte and all-black. So if you‘ve found a silhouette you like, you can still spend another 10 minutes deciding which version works with your trousers. A problem we were hoping to leave outside the game.

It gives you an idea of just how much there is in GTA 6. The older games let you change your clothes, but here you’re choosing between different colorways, materials, and versions of individual shoes. And Eris is only one part of it: There are clothes and accessories that look like takes on Stüssy, Loewe, Chrome Hearts, and Louis Vuitton, too. You could spend a fair bit of time getting dressed before you get anything else done.

We’ve waited this long to play the game. A few more minutes in the sneaker shop before you really get going on missions won’t hurt.
