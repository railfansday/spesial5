Sherrill commits $20.2M to deportation defense while condemning Rutgers over Border Patrol speaker

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Chief Border Patrol Agent Greg Bovino addresses the status of Kilmar Abrego Garcia and of operations in New Orleans on ‘Jesse Watters Primetime.’

New Jersey Gov. Mikie Sherrill put her money where her mouth was this week as the Democrat criticized Rutgers University’s College Republican chapter for hosting a top former Border Patrol leader around the same time her administration allocated millions to a program meant to help illegal immigrants and others in the state fight deportation.

Sherrill’s condemnation of the Rutgers students’ event drew criticism on X from observers who said it fueled protests outside the New Brunswick building where retired Border Patrol Cmdr. Gregory Bovino spoke. Protests erupted inside the room as well, with video from inside showing Bovino responding to shouting protesters before he was briefly led out of the forum.

"Everyone in this country is entitled to due process and a fair hearing under the law — regardless of where they were born," Sherrill said Friday in announcing a total of $20.2 million to fund the Detention Deportation Defense Initiative within Trenton’s Office of New Americans.

Some of the funds will help migrants held at Delaney Hall in Essex County, where nightly protests have erupted since May. The governor previously visited the facility in solidarity with the detainees inside, while Sherrill ally Sen. Andy Kim, D-N. J., drew widespread attention online after being caught in a cloud of pepper spray dispersed by ICE toward surrounding protesters.

"With the Trump Administration ramping up ICE activity and creating impossible obstacles for people facing detention and deportation to navigate alone, New Jersey is stepping up to connect residents with qualified legal counsel and equip more attorneys to take on this vital work," Sherrill said Friday, adding that the state’s immigrant community should not have to face a "complex" legal system alone.

Commissioner Stephen Cha, head of Sherrill’s Department of Human Services, where the Office of New Americans sits, added in a statement that access to legal representation upholds due process and promotes a more "efficient" immigration system.

The $20.2 million will be spread among 10 service providers and continue the Murphy-era initiative’s mission to offer legal expertise and representation for "low-income residents facing detention and deportation."

Sherrill’s new cash infusion nearly tripled the initiative’s most recent budget allocation.

After Sherrill announced plans in June to increase the deportation defense initiative’s funding, Republicans in the state warned that the money could be better spent elsewhere, including on New Jersey residents.

"New Jersey families are being crushed by some of the highest electric bills, property taxes, and costs of living in the entire country, and Governor Sherrill's answer is to spend more than $20 million defending illegal immigrants," said outgoing Rep. Jeff Van Drew, R-N.J., who was unseated by Cape May Mayor Zachary Mullock in this month’s blue wave.

"It is a complete insult to every hardworking taxpayer in this state. New Jersey citizens should come first. If they can find $20 million for immigration lawyers, they can find money to lower utility delivery charges or provide relief to the people who actually live, work and pay taxes here."

Van Drew said in June that every dollar Trenton spends on an illegal immigrant is a dollar that could have helped keep a New Jersey family from leaving the state.

A DHS spokesperson said Sherrill has "consistently shown more concern for criminal illegal aliens who've broken our laws and destroyed any possibility of ever remaining in our country than she ever has for Rachel Morin, Laken Riley, Jocelyn Nungaray and the true victims of illegal alien crimes in America," while adding it does not comment specifically on matters involving former personnel like Bovino.

Fox News Digital reached out to Sherrill for comment.

Sherrill had publicly condemned Bovino and Rutgers’ College Republicans for holding the event.

Rutgers is New Jersey’s state university, and the governor appoints some of its board members. Delaware Valley radio host Rich Zeoli said the state gives the New Brunswick school about $1.4 billion annually, adding the argument can be made she is "using a classic intimidation tactic to silence speech she doesn't like."

A file photo shows a Rutgers University campus in New Jersey. Democratic Gov. Mikie Sherrill criticized the university’s College Republican chapter for hosting retired Border Patrol Cmdr. Gregory Bovino in New Brunswick. (Eduardo Munoz/Reuters)

Sherrill urged attendees to press Bovino on why he chooses "cruelty over humanity" and what he thinks of the November election results and the argument that they represent a collective rebuke of his enforcement approach.

The North Carolina native shot back at Sherrill, saying her statement was "spoken like a true lib who puts illegal aliens before her own citizens."

"Gubner, I am deeply disappointed in your penchant for using the Governor’s Office to limit Constitutional rights of Americans," Bovino said in a statement.

"This coming from someone who cheated at the Naval Academy (you weren’t allowed to walk), and enriched yourself via insider trading in defense stocks as a Congressgirl — priceless." Sherrill has said she was barred from walking at the graduation not because she was involved in cheating but because she declined to identify classmates involved.

New Jersey State Assemblyman Paul Kanitra, R-Point Pleasant Beach, said Sherrill’s criticism of Bovino contrasts with her silence over Democrats electing a candidate in the state who was once acquaintances with the mastermind behind the 1993 World Trade Center bombing.

"The thought-police strike again," Kanitra said.

"I didn’t see you weighing in when any Adam Hamawy said some pretty heinous things recently? That didn’t seem to warrant a tweet," he said, referring to the Mercer County doctor and former defense trial witness for the Blind Sheikh who won the race to succeed retiring Rep. Bonnie Watson Coleman, D-N.J.

Hamawy was never charged with any wrongdoing in connection with Abdel-Rahman, and his campaign previously told Fox News Digital related criticisms amount to "guilt-by-association" shaming and that Republicans and right-wing press are simply concerned that Hamawy will be "Donald Trump's worst nightmare."

Bovino later challenged Sherrill to attend his event, which the governor did not attend.

"Tell you what, come on down to Rutgers and put your insider-trading money where your mouth is. You won’t be able to cheat your way through this one."

Sherrill’s 2025 gubernatorial campaign previously denied the stock-trading allegation raised by Bovino, which stemmed from Republican challenger Jack Ciattarelli’s claim that she "cash[ed] in to the tune of $7 million" by "flipping stocks" while in Congress.
