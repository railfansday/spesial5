It’s not necessarily too late to plant amaryllis bulbs for Christmas, but timing does matter. Amaryllis typically blooms six to eight weeks after planting, so early to mid-November is your last chance to get bulbs in the pot if you want striking holiday flowers in time for the big day.

If you’re growing amaryllis for holiday flowers, choose the right type. Amaryllis usually refers to Hippeastrum, a South American native, not true Amaryllis, also known as belladonna lilies from South Africa. Most Hippeastrum bulbs sold this time of year are grown in the Southern Hemisphere and will bloom in six to eight weeks. Dutch-grown bulbs take longer, so avoid these.

Flowering also varies by the plant’s growing conditions, which means you have some control. While early or mid-November is the ideal time to plant a holiday amaryllis, you can do a few things to speed up the process if you’ve just missed the window.

Getting healthy holiday blooms isn’t just luck. How you choose and plant your bulbs is important. Start by selecting the best bulbs. Look for large, firm, and healthy bulbs without soft spots, damage, or mold. If you see a bulb that has the tip of a green stem emerging, grab it. This bulb has already begun to break dormancy and should flower sooner.

While unusual varieties can be expensive, you don't need to spend a lot of money on bulbs – Walmart sells preplanted bulbs for under $6.

Next, choose a good-quality potting mix, such as Miracle-Gro Indoor Potting Mix, available at Amazon, and a container with drainage holes that is only one or two inches (2.5 to 5 cm) wider than the bulb. Position the bulb in the pot so that the pointy end is up and one-third to one-half of the bulb is above the surface of the potting mix.

Water your planted bulb thoroughly, letting it all drain through, before setting it in a warm, bright spot. Keep the potting mix just slightly damp, never soggy. Amaryllis doesn’t need much water and can rot in soggy conditions.

You can’t do a lot to force it, but these conditions provide everything your plant needs for a healthy growth rate. Just don’t overdo it. Fertilizer, too much heat, and too much water can do more harm than good.

Be realistic about timing with amaryllis bulbs. If it’s late November, you might still have time for a Christmas flower. Choose the largest, healthiest bulb, with a bit of green growth, if possible, for the best start. Provide the best conditions and cross your fingers.

If it’s already December, you won’t get a flower by Christmas from a dormant bulb. However, you can probably find a potted amaryllis that has already broken dormancy and has a stem. This plant will likely bloom by Christmas.

You got some gorgeous blooms in time for Christmas, but now you want them to last. It’s time to switch your care strategy from encouraging growth to keeping flowers looking their best.

When the flowers do fade, snip them off to keep the plant looking tidy. Once all the flowers are done blooming, cut the stem back close to the bulb. With the right care, you should enjoy healthy, attractive flowers for two to three weeks.
