Microsoft outage cause revealed: It was a perfect storm of problems

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

You're probably aware that Microsoft 365, the popular suite of products that includes Outlook, suffered a massive global outage last week. In fact, it was the worst outage of the year, by a sizable margin. The problem took the computer giant roughly 24 hours, over the course of two business days, to get everything back up and running.

Aside from wondering when their email would be working again, the number one question on everyone's mind was what in the world happened to keep Microsoft 365 down for so long?

Now, we know. In a statement shared with customers and on X, Microsoft explained there were actually three causes, which created a perfect storm.

First, a bug caused servers to become unresponsive in specific conditions; second, an issue with Microsoft Teams increased server traffic and created those conditions; and third, many additional servers were already offline from an unrelated issue.

As Microsoft explained, on their own, neither issue would have caused such a large-scale problem. However, together, well, they kept one of the world's biggest tech companies scrambling for two days to fix their service.

In a social media post published on X, Microsoft's EVP of Copilot, Agents, and Platform Charles Lamanna explained the situation in detail.

According to Lamanna, a "latent defect" resulted in the company's servers becoming "overwhelmed and unresponsive under certain load conditions." The defect that caused the problem was introduced to Microsoft's systems during a July 2026 update as part of a fix for Microsoft 365 networking gateway servers.

For months, as Lamanna described it, the issue went undetected, and it didn't cause any problems under normal load during Microsoft's testing process.

The second problem occurred when a surge in server load set off the defect. Lamanna says that Microsoft maintains two independent network gateway systems for Microsoft 365 to handle outages and disruptions. However, 15 hours before the global Microsoft 365 outage, Teams "failed over" from the alternative networking gateway to the primary one, which supports Exchange Online. This wouldn't usually cause a problem, except for the new defect introduced by the July update.

Finally, Microsoft's servers weren't operating at 100 percent at the time. According to Lamanna, it just so happened that nearly 14 percent of the server capacity for the networking gateway was out of service "due to an unrelated issue from the previous day."

Again, normally, this would have been routine maintenance. The service would still have sufficient capacity to work as normal.

All three issues occurring at once led to disaster.

"Taken together, the increased load and the reduced server capacity, caused the network gateway to encounter the latent bug from July," Lamanna writes. "This caused requests to build up until servers ultimately stopped responding. This occurred during the peak window of traffic in the United States."

Recovery from the outage also took much longer than usual because servers were "immediately overwhelmed by waiting traffic and became unresponsive again." In addition, Microsoft was unable to roll back the code because the bug dated back to July. So, Microsoft essentially had to develop a patch within that short timeframe, which returned the code to its previous "good state."

The entire explanation from Microsoft is actually quite an interesting read and shows that no matter how big the company is, massive tech issues can still occur. Readers can check out Lamanna's entire post on X; Microsoft enterprise customers can also learn more in the Microsoft 365 Admin Center.

Microsoft has since apologized for the outage. Businesses affected by the downtime can also file a claim with the company for credits due to the downtime.
