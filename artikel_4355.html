Driven: 2028 Mercedes-Benz GLA EV Is Both Familiar and a First

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The electric Benz hatchback shares much with its CLA EV sibling.

At the debut of the redesigned Mercedes-Benz GLA, company chairman Ola Källenius said, “The ICE [internal-combustion engine] world and the BEV [battery-electric vehicle] world have come together.” And that’s true: Apart from their grilles, the gas-engine GLA and the first-ever GLA EV are virtually indistinguishable from each other. Källenius described the design of the new GLA as “clearly electric-first,” so it’s perhaps appropriate that the EV version is the first one we’re driving.

Electric-first is also the go-to-market strategy—at least in Europe. It was to be in the U.S., too, but there’s been a reversal; we’re now slated to get the GLA hybrid first. Really, though, we won’t be getting either one for a while. We’re told to expect the gas-engine GLA sometime late next year, while the EV won’t arrive until the first half of 2028. Our recent drive of the electric GLA in Spain, therefore, was an extra-long-lead preview.

We’d say that gives buyers plenty of time to warm up to the new styling, except that the look is not all that different from the current GLA. More substantial, maybe: The body is bigger, sitting astride a wheelbase that is stretched 2.4 inches. The new GLA is also longer, lower, and wider.

The GLA EV is also adorned with more three-pointed stars, though not to the extent of some other recent Mercedes products. Besides the illuminated logo on the flat-panel “grille,” there’s a large badge on the rear liftgate, and the motif also appears in the headlights and taillights. Opt for Sky Control for the standard glass roof panel, and 172 light-up stars appear on the glass, which is electronically dimmable in sections.

The dash is lifted straight from the GLA’s sedan sibling, the CLA. As in other Benz models, there’s an available Superscreen that adds a 14.0-inch passenger display to the standard 14.0-inch center touchscreen and 10.3-inch instrument screen. With it, virtually the entire dashboard is given over to digital real estate. Sadly, almost none of the space contains any physical switchgear, although at least we still get air vents that can be aimed by hand. Wireless smartphone mirroring is standard, but a wireless charging pad costs extra. The navigation uses Google Maps and optionally offers Benz’s augmented reality, an inset showing a forward-camera view with overlaid arrows indicating direction. Unfortunately, that inset appears on the left side of the touchscreen, where it’s obscured by the driver’s right hand on the wheel; you have to peer around to see it.

The on-screen climate controls are another ergonomic fail, as the touch points to change the temperature are tiny, and you have to open the climate menu to adjust the fan speed. You could ask for help from Benz’s digital assistant, which lurks within the screen, always listening. And there’s a camera trained on the driver, which can use facial recognition to automatically call up the driver’s profile and saved preferences. It can also take selfies! In a more worthwhile use of technology, buyers can purchase dash-cam functionality for the forward-facing camera.

Beyond the digital, the interior features high-back front seats that are firmly padded and well shaped. The high center console makes for a somewhat confining feel up front, but its floating design creates a large open bay underneath for storage. The rear-seat area boasts a flat floor and is much roomier than in the CLA sedan. The cargo hold is 15 cubic feet, expandable to 49 cubes with the rear seatbacks folded. There’s also a frunk that’s about the size of a carry-on suitcase.

The powertrains mirror the CLA EV’s. There’s a single-motor GLA250+ that sends 268 horsepower to the rear wheels. The dual-motor GLA350 4Matic has all-wheel drive and 349 horses. Both employ a two-speed transmission on the rear axle for improved efficiency at highway speeds.

The standard suspension has passive dampers, but the cars we drove had the optional adaptive dampers and rode on 20-inch wheels (18s are standard; 19s are also available). The smooth pavement of Barcelona and sunny coastal environs gave the dampers little to adapt to—any real assessment of the GLA’s ride quality will have to wait until we drive one on the meaner streets back home. However, the suspension acquitted itself well on our drive route’s long series of hillside curves. The GLA is composed but would be more confidence-inspiring if the steering weren’t so light and remote.

The GLA isn’t a hot hatch, though; it’s more of an urban runabout. In that context, the 268-hp GLA250+ is adequately peppy, even though Mercedes posits a rather languid 60-mph time of 7.2 seconds. We expect it to do better than that, as the CLA250+ we tested managed a 5.8-second result. The dual-motor GLA350 felt much more energetic, particularly outside of urban environs, where it quickly passed dawdlers on two-lane roads and merged easily onto the freeway. For the GLA350, Mercedes claims a 5.3-second 60-mph time, but again that’s likely an undersell; testing a mechanically identical CLA350, we got 4.3 seconds. Even so, there are quicker EVs in this class, but one positive here is that even the higher-horsepower dual-motor version doesn’t feel like its output overwhelms its chassis—often an issue with high-horsepower EVs. Besides the standard drive modes of Eco, Comfort, Sport, and Individual, the dual-motor GLA adds a Terrain mode that optimizes the powertrain for loose surfaces; its camera system is able to stitch together a “transparent hood” view of what’s immediately in front of the car.

When it comes time for slowing, there are three levels of liftoff regen (D, D+, and D-), as well as an Auto setting that varies the regen level based on info from the navigation system and the forward-facing cameras. Our preference is D-, which is essentially coasting, but D+ will please fans of one-pedal driving. We also tried the automated parking function, which was able to back into and out of a parallel-parking spot without trying our patience. It’s a digital add-on that’s purchased for a set time period; the same goes for the MBUX Entertainment package of multimedia streaming and app downloads.

Like the CLA EV sedan, the GLA is built on Benz’s MMA platform, with an 800-volt architecture and an 85-kWh battery. That architecture allows for speedy DC recharging, up to 320 kilowatts. Should you find a power source robust enough to reach that figure, Mercedes says the GLA battery can go from 10 percent to 80 percent in 22 minutes. (In our testing of the CLA250+ with the same hardware, it went from 10 to 80 percent in just 21 minutes.) EPA range estimates for the GLA aren’t yet available, but the CLA offers a clue, and its impressive range figures run from 312 miles on the low end to as high as 374 miles.

If the GLA ends up with numbers like those, it’ll look pretty good against the electric Mini Countryman, Genesis GV60, and Cadillac Optiq, and similar-ish to a Rivian R2. Those look to be the GLA EV’s prime competitors at a base-price guesstimate of around $50K. But the EV landscape could shift significantly by the time the GLA gets here. After all, a lot can happen between now and 2028.
