BYD Just Put a Fold-Away Steering Wheel in a Car People Can Buy

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

One touch of a button and you instantly get extra cabin space to relax.

Anyone who has ever tried to eat lunch sitting in the driver’s seat while parked—or worse yet, flip open a laptop to catch up on emails—knows that a steering wheel takes up a surprisingly large amount of real estate. Thus, manufacturers of all kinds have come up with various steering solutions that have largely remained conceptual, cool show-off pieces for the auto show circuit but little more. Chinese manufacturer BYD just stuck one in a mainstream SUV.

The Fang Cheng Bao Tai 9 is a large six-seater SUV that’s about the size of an Infiniti QX80, with prices starting at the equivalent of $41K in the Chinese market. It’s not a pure EV, but rather a series plug-in hybrid with a 150-hp 1.5-liter turbo four-cylinder acting as a generator to power the 66-kWh battery pack; the dual electric motors combine for 536 hp.Fang Cheng Bao is one of BYD’s subbrands, aimed at delivering an off-road-ready flair. The Tai 9 is certainly a chunky-looking thing, but what’s perhaps most interesting about the top trim level is how it behaves when parked.

Spend the equivalent of $49K for the highest-grade Tai 9, and you get a folding steering wheel that disappears into the dash. Because the vehicle is entirely steer-by-wire, a technology that only became legal in China less than six months ago, the Tai 9 has no steering column geometry to worry about. The rim splits in half, folds back over the column, and the whole thing retracts, airbag still facing forward.Paired with a driver’s seat that reclines almost completely flat, the appeal here is to provide a more spacious place to relax or work while you charge. Again, this feature is only available when parked, so you can’t retract the wheel while using the Tai 9’s driver-assistance functions.

However, this folding steering wheel does show how car interiors might look for vehicles with Level 4-or-higher autonomy, ones that can operate completely without human intervention in certain cases. On a geofenced section of highway, with automated cruise control activated, you could simply pack your steering wheel away for extra room.

Obviously, driving enthusiasts who obsess over steering feel aren’t going to be too happy about the proliferation of steering-by-wire, never mind having the steering wheel fold away entirely. However, it’s not like a three-ton, three-row PHEV SUV is going to feel like piloting a Caterham anyway.

What’s perhaps most impressive of all is the speed at which BYD managed to get its folding steering wheel technology into the hands of the public, considering the regulatory requirements only came into being months ago. Combined with the company’s production-car speed record under its Yangwang performance brand, and the sub-$10K price of its Seagull entry-level EV, BYD is certainly active in all segments of the market.
