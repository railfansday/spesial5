Short Circuit: An inexhaustive weekly compendium of rulings from the federal courts of appeal

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Mostly law professors | Sometimes contrarian | Often libertarian | Always independent

Tear gas in your home! Pepper balls in your face! But no carte blanche for your LinkedIn.

Please enjoy the latest edition of Short Circuit, a weekly feature written by a bunch of people at the Institute for Justice.

New news! This month, IJ client Libby Souder asked the South Carolina Supreme Court to let her resume teaching private swim lessons in her backyard pool, which she'd done for years with city approval and without incident. But after a nosy neighbor (whose life is in no way affected by the lessons) complained, officials reinterpreted an ambiguous provision of the zoning code to shut down Libby's business. Click here to learn more.

New on the Short Circuit podcast: Baltimore police can use a "Hailstorm" device to stimulate your cell phone and figure out where you are. Do they need a warrant for that?

Phooey! Over four years after Meridian officials evicted IJ client Chasidy Decker from her home, the Idaho Supreme Court ruled this week that her challenge to the city's irrational ban on tiny mobile homes is not ripe. That's because, the court said, she and her landlord failed to apply for a permit that officials repeatedly testified they would not and could not grant. The silver lining? The city still can't show the ban serves a legitimate purpose, and one day they're going to have to. (Or they could, you know, repeal the thing.) Click here to learn more.
