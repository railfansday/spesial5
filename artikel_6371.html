The 8 Best Outdoor Pizza Ovens That Deliver Delicious Homemade Pies

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

We tested the Ooni Karu 2, Ninja Artisan, Gozney Tread, and others to find out which models are worth the dough.

Just like tending to the grill and gathering around a firepit are inherently enjoyable, enjoying a fresh, piping-hot pizza is similarly rewarding, and it’s just a bit more special when it’s homemade. Baking a pie in the oven can yield good results, but there’s nothing like the crunchy crust and smoky flavor of a pizza made in a proper hearth. If you’re hoping to replicate your favorite restaurant’s pie, an outdoor pizza oven can take your homemade slices from decent to genuinely artisan.

Stand-alone and countertop outdoor pizza ovens reach much higher temperatures than regular ovens, and in less time. That high heat is exactly what you need for a perfectly cooked pizza crust—and several of the models we tested can cook up a pie in as little as 60 seconds.

Plus, many of the best outdoor pizza ovens we write about are suited for cooking other tasty foods, just like a real hearth. You can make fish, meat, roasted vegetables, and even bread in these wood-fired and gas-fueled models.

To find the best pizza ovens, we looked at models with good features and designs and called in a handful to try out for ourselves. We selected pizza ovens that use a variety of fuels, including gas, charcoal, and wood. Outdoor pizza ovens all pretty much come with a learning curve, but we identified models best suited to beginners and pizza aficionados alike to offer a range that caters to different skill levels.

For testing, we heated each oven for 30 minutes to season them and burn off any oils or contaminants from manufacturing, then let them cool completely. Fifteen minutes after firing up each oven on high using propane burners, we measured the temperatures on the pizza stones with an infrared thermometer.

Then, we made pizza, using the same dough each time, with propane and oak firewood as our fuel. During cooking, we noted how well each oven performed, different features, and its ease of use. Finally—and most enjoyably—we assessed (a.k.a. ate) the pizza. We recommend the following pizza ovens based on our hands-on experience, their specs, and quality.

Over a decade ago, Ooni built its first portable pizza oven with a design that many have mimicked. Our previous pick for the best pizza oven, the Karu 16, has been replaced by the Karu 2 Pro. It’s the exact same as the Karu 16 with three new features: an air vent, Bluetooth-connected thermometer, and an even larger window on the oven door.

Like the Karu 16, the Karu 2 Pro makes 16-inch pizzas fired either with propane or wood. In testing, we liked the locking door with a glass window, which helped maintain a consistent temperature and allowed us to keep an eye on our pizza while it cooked.

Ooni also added a digital thermometer to this model that monitors air temperature in the oven via a wired probe. That info is sent to your phone so you can be in the kitchen prepping more pies or enjoying company instead of babysitting the oven.

Using the propane burner is easy and gets the job done, but we appreciate the large firebox for wood, which provides a lot more flame across the top of the oven. The large capacity also had us refilling it less frequently than smaller wood-fired ovens.

The Karu 2 Pro also doubles as a capable charcoal grill if you want to cook meat or veggies. While we like a dedicated grill for burgers and steaks, the Karu 2 is a do-it-all outdoor kitchen if you have limited outdoor space. The addition of the new air vent even gets it down to a consistent 200 degrees Fahrenheit for low-and-slow cooking.

The Ninja Artisan is the only electric outdoor pizza oven we've come across that's worth a double take. It needs to be covered if left outside because of the electrical components, but the body is made of weather-resistant steel.

Our tester, Danny Perez, didn’t have to hassle with pellets or propane to get things started—he simply plugged it in, preheated, and started cooking. It has a max temp of 700 degrees Fahrenheit, which is less than most gas and wood-fired ovens, but it's not necessarily a bad thing.

While a 1,000-degree oven that makes pizza in 90 seconds sounds easy, there's a lot more to it. Shredded mozzarella starts melting in seconds, the dough crust needs a minute to bake, and toppings may require a different cooking time. Electric temperature controls and a lower max heat offer more runway before taking off on your pizza-making journey. Another plus of an electric outdoor pizza oven is even cooking because there's no need to turn the pizza when it's in the oven.

The Ninja has built-in preset pizza modes to cook different styles of 'za: Neapolitan, New York, thin crust, and pan. It preheats to respective target temps for each and then prompts you to add the pizza. Hit start and it starts counting down for a perfectly timed cook.

He tried the thin crust setting, which cooked the pizza at 550 degrees Fahrenheit for four minutes. When the timer went off, out came an evenly cooked pepperoni pizza.

The Neapolitan setting worked great as well. It cooked the pizza at 700 degrees F for two and a half minutes yielding an impressively crisped crust with some leopard spotting.

Typically, rotating pizza stones are a gimmick you find on budget pizza ovens to make up for a low temperature range, especially for an electric model. GreenPan’s Stanley Tucci pizza oven, however, has both firepower and a surprisingly effective rotating stone for indoor and outdoor use.

The controls on this model go from 350 to 850 degrees Fahrenheit, so you can use it for more than just cooking pizza. The oven also has a temperature boost feature that allows you to “boost” the top, bottom, or both heating elements. During testing, Ford used this to crisp up the top crust and brown the edges, and this feature offered more flexibility for cooking his pizza exactly how he liked it.

The Stanley Tucci pizza oven uses a round stone, which sometimes can make it harder to accurately pitch your creation onto the stone. With this model, the oven door has a mechanism attached so that when you open it, the stone is lifted and pulled out, making it easier to toss in your pizza and avoid accidental spills and messes.

Overall, this is the best electric rotating pizza oven that Ford has tried yet, though he noted that this design is limited to 12-inch pizzas only.

Outdoor pizza ovens use gas or wood to create flames across the top that radiate intense heat downward, warming the pizza stone—like preheating an oven in your house. However, unlike your home oven, outdoor pizza ovens can reach up to 950 degrees Fahrenheit.

Once the outdoor pizza oven hits cooking temperature and your pizza is placed inside, it only takes a couple of minutes to cook. The pizza stone or oven floor gets to the same temperature as the roof so the pizza cooks fast and evenly. While it cooks, those flames up top produce a golden crust that’s crispy on the outside and soft and airy on the inside. Because the pizza only takes a minute or so to cook, you can make a few with different toppings one after another.

Wood is the traditional fuel of choice for pizza ovens, favored for the smoky flavor it imparts in the crust and toppings. However, wood (or charcoal) fires take longer to heat up and require more attention to maintain the right temperature. That may not be an issue if you’re only making a couple of pies, but it can become a hassle if you’re cooking multiple pies for a large group, or entertaining a party while you cook.

Propane fuel can heat a pizza oven up rather quickly. Since it only takes around 15 to 20 minutes to preheat, it's hard to deny the convenience of being able to cook a pie in a relatively short time frame. Plus, cleanup is easy when there’s no ashy firebox to empty. Just keep in mind that a propane oven won't give you that unique, wood-fired flavor.

Luckily, you don't have to sacrifice one for the other: A few of the brands we recommend offer extra accessories you can buy to accommodate a different fuel type. There are even some options with combined fuel, so you can heat up your oven fast with the propane and imbue the pizza with wood-fired flavor, too.

The oven’s size determines the maximum temperature output the unit can reach, as well as the size of pie you can cook. Most outdoor pizza ovens are designed for 12- or 14-inch pies. Our Best Overall pick, though, can accommodate pizzas up to 16 inches for oversized, restaurant-like slices.

Pizzas are typically cooked between 450 and 600 degrees Fahrenheit. Classic Neapolitan pizza requires temperatures around 900 degrees—these are the ones with golden crusts, mottled with dark leopard spots. High-end models top out at around 950 degrees Fahrenheit.

The pizza ovens we recommend have max temperatures that far exceed 500 degrees, but note that an oven with a relatively low maximum temperature still gives you a delicious, cooked-through pie—it just may not look quite as picture-perfect.

In short, yes. Outdoor pizza ovens are incredibly versatile. Beyond pies, you can cook meats like steak and chicken, roast veggies, bake bread (like sourdough or focaccia), prep seafood, make cast-iron dishes, and bake desserts. Since it's essentially a super-hot wood-fired oven, you can use plenty of different methods to whip up your favorite foods.

Accessories aren't necessarily a requirement for operating your pizza oven, but they can make the process a lot smoother and safer. We recommend using tools such as a pizza peel to move and adjust your pie, an infrared thermometer to check stone temp accurately, heat-resistant gloves, and a brush or scraper for cleaning the stone.

Outdoor pizza ovens are generally easy to clean and don't require a ton of maintenance since their high temperatures naturally burn off a majority of food residue. Usually, you can just brush off ash and debris after the oven has cooled down. You can also occasionally run it at high heat to get rid of any extra stubborn bits, and wipe down the exterior as needed. Avoid using water or soap on the pizza stone to prevent cracking.
