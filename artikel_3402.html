‘Rivals’ Season 2 Boss Reveals the Meaning Behind That Rupert and Taggie Scene in Episode 9 [Exclusive]

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Editor's Note: The following contains spoilers for Episodes 7-9 of Rivals Season 2.Fans of Rupert (Alex Hassell) and Taggie (Bella Maclean) were dealt another blow at the end of Rivals Season 2, Episode 9 last Wednesday, when Rupert pulled away just as the pair seemed poised to kiss. With Episode 10 and the much-anticipated Hunt Ball on the horizon, a little more yearning seems like just what the doctor ordered for these two. Speaking with Collider, Rivals executive producer Alex Lamb explained why these pivotal Rupert and Taggie scenes needed to bookend the episode and what they mean for the pair’s burgeoning relationship.

Episode 8 ended with a shocking reveal as Maud (Victoria Smurfit) and Tony’s (David Tennant) affair finally came to light. The episode, already full of fireworks and explosions thanks to Bonfire Night, culminated in a violent and very public fight between Maud and Declan (Aidan Turner) as Declan revealed to his Venturer pals that Maud has been the one leaking secrets to Tony the entire time.

Four love stories, four completely different ideas of what it means to be somebody's person. This is the one that matches how you actually fall, and what it would ask of you.

You do not fall for strangers. You fall for the person you have already been talking to for years, which is the slowest and by some distance the most durable way anybody has found to do this.

You want a partner, not an audience. Somebody who wants their own life as badly as you want yours, and who would never ask you to be smaller to make the relationship easier.

The intensity was never the problem. The secrecy is. You are drawn to a love that lives entirely in private, and to the particular ache of wanting somebody you are not supposed to want.

You do not do this cautiously. When it happens for you it is immediate and total and it rearranges everything, and you would not accept a smaller version of it.

Of course, we learn later from Tony that the mole is, in fact, male and therefore can't be Maud. But before that big twist, Venturer lies in shambles at the beginning of Episode 9. Maud is staying above Bar Sinister, while Declan wakes up in a pile of trash after a night of binge drinking. When Bas (Luca Pasqualino) brings him back to the O’Haras home, it feels like all hope is lost. Caitlin (Catriona Chandler) is going back to school, devastated by the idea that her parents might actually split up. Not only that, but the franchise seems to be Corinium's to win as Tony uses Monica's (Claire Rushbrook) death as an emotional crutch to explain his publicized dalliance with Maud.

Episode 9 then sees Rupert and Declan come together to try and fix their problems and get Venturer back on track. They fly to New York to win Cameron (Nafessa Williams) back after her breakup with Rupert, and Declan brings the IBA board out to Penscombe to show off their greatest strengths as they present the board with their brand-new TV ideas complete with a musical performance, a delicious lunch, and a robot-made martini to boot!

The impetus for all of this comes when Taggie decides to bring the application straight to Rupert's door, pushing him to fix the mess that her parents have made. Set to an instrumental version of Bonnie Tyler’s “Holding Out for a Hero," she shows up at Penscombe not to cry about the state of her home life or in search of a knight in shining armor, but to push Rupert to do what he does best: win.

While the scene at the end of the episode might leave "Rutag" shippers a bit disappointed that even after all of that, the two haven't reconciled, Lamb still calls the scenes "the spine of the episode." In both this first scene between Rupert and Taggie at Penscombe and the last one, we not only get a development of their current rocky relationship, but are once again reminded about Taggie's impact on Rupert. Lamb explains, "We felt we needed to up Rupert and Taggie's story and have that be the bookend around that push of the Venturer plot and how we are going to pull Declan out of the doldrums."

There are worse things in store than laxatives and escaping naked out of a bathroom window.

Rupert and Taggie have been avoiding each other since Monica’s funeral, when he called her "childish" while still reeling from having witnessed Monica’s death firsthand. But Lamb explains that the Episode 9 scenes are vital for their relationship. "It felt very important to us that we had a really strong, inciting incident for Rupert and Taggie to go, 'Right, we have been apart from each other. There's been unpleasantness between us,' but now the chips are down, Taggie knows the only person that can help her dad is [Rupert]."

It's because of Taggie's plea that Rupert goes on his journey across town with her to find Declan, leading them to an ominous encounter with Tony and Beattie (Annabel Scholey), where he once again tries to tear the team apart — though this time without much success. After that, both men are pushed to get their vengeance against Tony, delivering a standout performance for the IBA and proving that Venturer is still a horse worth betting on in this race.
