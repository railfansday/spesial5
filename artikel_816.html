Belief and bench steer Northern Ireland into Nations League top tier

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Highlights: Northern Ireland secure to promotion to League A

After going 500 minutes without conceding a goal, Northern Ireland could be forgiven for having to make adjustments to play from behind.

Having travelled to Budapest knowing a point against Hungary would be enough to top Nations League B2, Barnabas Varga's strike after 35 minutes for the hosts at the Puskas Arena altered the picture and, for the first time in the campaign, left Michael O'Neill's side having to chase a game.

Their response to the deficit - on the pitch, from the bench and high in the stands where their suspended manager was watching the game - displayed the best characteristics of a side who had not won at this level before this unbeaten campaign.

While the point from a 1-1 draw in Budapest will be long remembered for Justin Devenny's brilliant equaliser and the scenes of celebration that greeted promotion to the competition's top tier for the first time, it was beyond the highlights and headlines that O'Neill saw the emerging qualities of his side.

"We went behind tonight and it was difficult, you know? Big stadium, big atmosphere, but we came out and played well in the second half and got what we needed from the game," said the manager.

"Those are things that I'm very pleased that are in this squad at such a young age. The technical side of the game we'll always look to improve on, but in terms of the physical, the tactical and the mental side of a game, we're very far ahead of where I thought we would be at this time."

Hungary enjoyed more than 70% possession in the first half, and they could have been further than a goal ahead with Liverpool star Dominik Szoboszlai striking the base of the post after only five minutes.

O'Neill made his first change at half-time, bringing on veteran striker Josh Magennis, while Kieran Morrison, Brodie Spencer and Paul Smyth all followed in the second half.

Magennis and Morrison made a real impact before the equaliser, but it was the belief shown by all involved that O'Neill felt was key to the turnaround.

"I felt in the first half from where I was seeing the game, we just looked a little bit leggy which is natural," added O'Neill.

"I just felt we needed that freshness at the right points in the game that helped us, but it was the belief that we could get back into the game, and the belief that we could see the game out.

"I think we showed a lot of attributes that are in this squad at this moment in time and certainly in the second half that was that was the case."

'I genuinely believe it will only get better' - O'Neill

For all the plaudits earned by this team who conceded just one goal in six games while picking up 12 points, among the most impressive aspects of the campaign has been who they have had to do without.

Talismanic Liverpool defender Conor Bradley has not played for this side in over 12 months through injury.

Ali McCann is another who missed both 2026 Nations League windows, while influential defender Daniel Ballard featured only in the first game in Georgia before a hamstring injury.

Arsenal's Ceadach O'Neill was forced to pull out of the November squad through injury at a time when he would perhaps have been the most natural replacement for Jamie Donley after the striker picked up a second booking against Ukraine last week and had to miss the finale through suspension.

Having taken over an ageing squad four years ago, O'Neill has rebuilt his playing panel with a group that feels as if it is getting deeper with each international window.

"The encouraging thing is we have players on the outside - we've Bradley to come back in, Ballard to come back in, Ali McCann, young Ceadach, Eoin Toal," added the former Stoke City and Blackburn Rovers boss.

"We have good competition for places now. We're going to need that competition, and some players are going to miss out, but we are building depth to the squad that will help us going forward."

Come the next Nations League, that will be all the way into the uncharted territory of the top tier.
