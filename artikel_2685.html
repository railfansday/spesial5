Thunder are NBA's deepest team, but not even they can weather all these injuries

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

MIAMI — It became immediately evident as soon as the layup lines started.

On one side, the Miami Heat were mostly at full strength, the lone exception being their new star and Most Valuable Player candidate Giannis Antetokounmpo missing his first game of the season.

On the other: the Oklahoma City Thunder, who dressed only nine players.

Of the five players who opened the season as starters, four — Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, Jalen Williams, Chet Holmgren and Cason Wallace — missed the game.

It was no surprise, therefore, that the Thunder could not keep up with Miami, which blitzed past Oklahoma City with relative ease Sunday, Nov. 22, 123-113.

It was never close. This was the Thunder's sixth loss in their last eight games, in what has become their least productive stretch in more than three seasons — basically since they became legitimate and ubiquitous championship contenders.

Entering the 2026-27 season, the Thunder were the deepest team in the NBA. But as Oklahoma City keeps sliding down the standings – the loss dropped the Thunder to 10-7 and ninth in the Western Conference, their lowest position since Nov. 4, 2023 – it's becoming clear that even they cannot withstand this many injuries.

The latest player to go down was the one Oklahoma City can least afford to miss. Gilgeous-Alexander, the reigning two-time consecutive Most Valuable Player, suffered a back contusion two days earlier in a win over the Pelicans.

The good news is that Gilgeous-Alexander did participate in his pregame shootaround, in which he put up dozens of shots from different spots on the floor. He didn't appear to have any long-term lingering effects, and all indications are he won't miss extended time.

The bad news is that there really is no way to replace his impact.

"I think the first thing is having a set of principles that transcend circumstances," Thunder coach Mark Daigneault said Sunday when asked how his team will need to weather this stretch. "I think  that's important when you're in unpredictable circumstances. That's what makes you feel at home and makes you feel comfortable. It makes you feel secure; it makes you feel confident – is when you have a way of operating, a way of playing, a way of thinking."

While Daigneault is right, an established identity does go a long way, at some point, identity and culture will never be able to make up for a deficiency in talent. And with this many injuries to both stars and key contributors, this is precisely the disadvantage with which the Thunder are operating.

Against Miami, the Thunder did have six different players reach double figures in scoring. What they lacked, however, was someone who could control the game and get to his spots (like Gilgeous-Alexander so smoothly does), or a player who created opportunities for his teammates. The Thunder did not have a player to surpass 5 assists in the game.

Heat center Bam Adebayo, by comparison, dished out 12 on his way to a 24-point, 15-rebound triple-double.

And, yes, it is November, so players will presumably get healthy. There's plenty of time to claw back up the standings. But in order for that to happen, the team simply will need its abysmal injury luck to reverse. Because it's not just about getting players back, they'll need to work their way back into game shape and they'll need to rediscover their chemistry on the floor.

Oklahoma City was so depleted that by the time the game was out of reach late in the fourth, Daigneault opted to empty his bench with more than six minutes to play. But since there were only four reserves available, forward Micah Peavy, who started the game, had to stay on the floor.

"It's also an acceptance of what the circumstance are," Daigneault said. "And we have to reframe the challenge to what the circumstances are: you've got to be a team that can play the hand that you're dealt. And this is the hand we were dealt. I think not asking for sympathy, not making excuses and just figuring out the best way to operate gives us the best chance to continue to be competitive."

That's all fine and good. But these Thunder won't compete in the West against the Spurs or the Lakers unless they get healthy.
