Week in Review: Signature win remains elusive, but that could change soon

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Welcome to Week in Review: a Monday feature that looks back at the week that was for the San Antonio Spurs, takes a look at the week ahead, and more. Enjoy!

Week 4: Another undefeated week stretched the Spurs' winning streak out to 12, with the young core spreading the wealth. Victor Wembanyama put on a show for his second Western Conference Player of the Week award this season, beginning with finishing off their road trip with a blowout win in Phoenix and come-from-behind win against the Bulls. They then briefly returned home for an absolute beatdown of the Steph Curry-less Warriors, led by a season-high performance from Stephon Castle to move to the top of their Cup group, followed by a career-high 31 points from Dylan Harper to beat the Jazz and complete the season sweep (yes, already).

The Spurs seemed overdue for a challenge, and they finally got one in a Pistons squad that was shorthanded but still featured Cade Cunningham, who has been making his own early case for MVP. After getting out to an early 14-point lead, the Spurs offense fell off as the Pistons found ways to keep Wemby out of the paint, and no one else could get anything going. They also greatly suffered in the non-Wemby minutes, and Detroit rallied for the win. However, the biggest worry was Harper having to leave the game late after an awkward fall. While he is expected to miss 2-3 weeks with a hip strain, it could have been much worse had it been the groin, so they got lucky there.

It wasn't impressive, but the Spurs found a way to get back on track. Overcoming a season-high 21 turnovers and hot three-point shooting from the Clippers, they used strong defense and timely shot-making in the fourth quarter to pull away. Castle put up a classic "PG Castle" stat line of 21 efficient points, 12 assists, 8 turnovers and 4 steals (it was very much a case of taking the good with the bad), and the bench unit had one of its better outings of the season, with everyone posting a positive plus/minus, including Luke Kornet!

With Zion Williamson a late scratch, depleting the Pelicans even further, Jeremiah Fears showing no fear (get it?) by getting chippy with the Spurs and baiting them into a couple of technicals in an up and down first quarter. But talent eventually won out, and the Spurs rode another 50-point game from Wembanyama to comfortably put New Orleans away in the second half.

OffRtg: 118.1 (4) DefRtg: 106.0 (3) NetRtg: +12.0 (1)

The Spurs' 12-game winning streak ended against the Pistons on Tuesday, when they also lost Dylan Harper to a hip strain. But they've since taken care of business against the Clippers and Pelicans; Harper's absence allows them to go back to last season's starting lineup, and they continue to have the league's best point differential.

1. The loss to Detroit was the Spurs' worst offensive game of the season, and included them scoring just 23 points on 29 possessions (79 per 100) in Victor Wembanyama's 14 minutes off the floor. Wembanyama has registered a positive plus-minus in every game since the season opener vs. the Thunder (minus-2), but the Spurs have been outscored with him on the bench in 12 of the 17. So he just went and scored 50 points (adding 18 rebounds, four steals and five blocks) in less than 32 minutes against the Pelicans on Saturday.

2. The Spurs have seen a drop in 3-point rate from last season, but that rate goes up with Julian Champagnie (the league leader in corner 3s) replacing Harper in the starting lineup. They took 56 (63%) of their 89 shots from beyond the arc, the highest rate for any team this season, in the win over New Orleans.

Coming up: The Spurs have played the league's fourth easiest schedule (cumulative opponent winning percentage of .412) thus far and will visit the Kings on Monday. After that, they'll play three games against teams — the Blazers, Lakers and Celtics — with a cumulative record of 35-15 (.700). They're 6-3 in games played between the 18 teams currently over .500, with a 22-point win in Portland included.

Kurt Helin, NBC Sports — 1 (last week: 1)

Something to be thankful for: Victor Wembanyama's loyalty. As much as San Antonio should be thankful for Wembanyama's MVP-level start to the season — 29.9 points, 12.3 rebounds and 3.9 blocks per game — what matters even more is his Duncanesque loyalty to the Spurs. "I think this is really the ideal environment because even in other cities, where we're not the home team, people aren't as respectful. People can be very persistent or intrusive sometimes, but never here…" Wemby said postgame recently. "I'd say it's the ideal environment to spend 15 or 20 years." One other Wembanyama note: He has 72 dunks this season, which not only leads the league but is more dunks than 20 NBA teams have. He made so many it was hurting his hand, so he decided to jump-shoot it from directly above the rim (which is officially registered as a dunk).

Brett Siegel, Clutch Points — 1 (last week: 1)

Victor Wembanyama continues to prove why the San Antonio Spurs are championship favorites. At the start of November, Wemby joined Wilt Chamberlain (5x), Anthony Davis, and Bob McAdoo as the only players in NBA history with 50+ points, 15+ rebounds, and 5+ blocks in a single game. On Saturday, Wemby did it again with 50 points, 18 rebounds, and five blocks in a 114-91 win over the New Orleans Pelicans.

Only Wemby and Chamberlain have done this more than once, and with him already doing it twice this year, it seems destined that the Spurs' young star will do it again.

Even with Dylan Harper out and expected to miss at least one more week with his hip injury, the Spurs are still rolling and are the team to beat in the West. We will learn a lot about them and the Los Angeles Lakers when the two teams meet on Friday in the final game of NBA Cup group play, with the winner set to advance to the knockout round.

Mon. 11/23 at Sacramento Kings (9:00 PM CT)

Wed. 11/25 at Portland Trail Blazers (Emirates Cup, 9:00 PM CT)

Fri. 11/27 vs. Los Angeles Lakers (Emirates Cup, 8:30 PM CT)

Sun. 11/29 vs. Boston Celtics (3:00 PM CT)

Prediction: 3-1 — This is tough. The Kings game is a win (or it better be, even if Wemby and De'Aaron Fox are questionable), and they seem to have the Blazers' number lately. What worries me are those two home games, altough this is their chance for that elusive signature win. The Spurs seem to be much more focused on the road than at home this season, where they are 0-3 against contenders. Healthy Luka Doncic always seems to give them trouble, and Sunday matinees can be unpredictable, even if the Celtics are 5-5 on the road and currently on a 4-game losing streak. The Spurs especially need to win the Lakers game since it could have MVP implications (odd as that may sound this early in the season), and obviously it could be the decider of who advances to next round of the Cup Tournament.
