Mike Tomlin on current Steelers situation: "It's a dumpster fire"

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Many folks in and around football have had opinions about the recent brouhaha between former Steelers quarterback Aaron Rodgers and Steelers coach Mike McCarthy. Few have the insight and perspective that former Steelers coach Mike Tomlin brings to the table.

Appearing on NBC’s Chiefs-Bills halftime show on Thanksgiving night, Tomlin shared his thoughts on the situation in a conversation with Mike Tirico. (A portion of it was on the live broadcast; the full interview is here.)

“I had a range of emotions watching those course of events yesterday,” Tomlin told Tirico. “I think the prevailing one is just general sadness. Sad on a micro level, sad on a macro level. And by that, I mean, you know, just from an individual perspective, sad that we might be looking at the end of a legendary career of the quarterback, Aaron Rodgers, and just on the macro level, whether it’s organizationally or for the game of football at large, to see laundry aired out that way in a public forum.

“Those are things that kind of run counterculture to professional football. So, just a lot of emotions, but probably more than anything, man, general sadness on a lot of levels.”

Tirico asked Tomlin if he was shocked by Rodgers’s decision to air dirty laundry in a public forum.

“It certainly did,” Tomlin said, “but, you know, and particularly because I know both men involved, certainly had a lot of intimate work and development of relationship with Aaron over the last couple of years, and have known and competed against Mike, you know, respected and appreciated him as a coach for a long time. But I don’t — what I acknowledge is I don’t know them as a collective, the nature of their relationships, and so certainly I was surprised by that.”

Regardless of Rodgers’s conduct on the way out the door, Tomlin explained that Rodgers was viewed favorably by his teammates in 2025.

“He was beloved, and I’m not, you know, over-exaggerating that,” Tomlin said. “He worked at it, and people appreciated his openness to getting to know and understand the Steeler culture, to be engaged with teammates, and particularly from my perspective, I worked with a lot of veteran players over the years and at times they lose a little patience for the process, or they lose patience for relationships with younger guys, et cetera. Man, he seemed to embrace all of that and really embrace it in a real, natural way. He was a tireless worker regarding the process.

“I called early-morning quarterback meetings on Wednesday and Thursday. He was always early and engaged. Did a heck of a job in terms of engaging and developing, assisting in the development of young players. Specifically, Will Howard was always in that meeting, and, shoot, man, [Rodgers] and I were shoulder-to-shoulder in terms of the development of Will and challenging Will to develop professional habits. And so, you know, it was early and extremely consistent during my time with him, in terms of his willingness to work in those areas, and I think that’s why he’s so beloved in that locker room, as been reported by multiple sources in light of this incident. That is real. The guys in that locker room love and appreciate Aaron Rodgers.”

Had Tomlin gotten any sense that things were so close to breaking down?

“Absolutely none, but it’s a really specific reason why, to be honest with you, man,” Tomlin said. “One of my cultural mantras, when I worked there for the 19 years, is don’t talk to people about your problems unless they can be a component of the solution. Because if you do, it’s counterproductive, and people know where I stand there. And by that I mean if you have a problem with your playing time, don’t talk to your teammate about it. Talk to somebody that can do something about it. That’s problem solving.

“And so everybody understands my posture on some of that stuff, and so although I have really good, fluid communication with many of those guys, particularly, the more veteran guys, they know me and know me well enough to know that I didn’t want to hear about any potential issues, because I’m not a component of the solution. And so although, particularly, like, Cam Heyward, he and I spent a little time together doing his bye week. Man, we had no mention of anything bubbling under the surface, if you will.”

Can McCarthy effectively lead the team, given what Rodgers said?

“You know, it certainly created some challenges and challenges is probably an understatement if all of those things are true,” Tomlin told Tirico. “And certainly the ‘1-2-3 Packers’ is, because he acknowledged that. But his ability to overcome that or inability to overcome that in my opinion is based on relationships and the depth of those relationships and how those guys feel about him, or how those guys buy into what he’s selling. And so, certainly, he’s been there a short period of time. And so it makes you wonder, the level of that challenge. But it’ll be relationship-based, his ability to overcome the things that you mentioned.”

How does Tomlin think the Steelers will fare against the Broncos on Friday?

“Based on my knowledge of the men, particularly the leaders, the longstanding guys, they’ll do a great job of getting hyper-focused on what really matters, and that’s that football game,” Tomlin said. “They’ll use it as a mantra to bring them together. I expect those guys to fight. Now, fight and quality play are two different things. I’m certainly not gonna expect those guys not to fight. I think they’re gonna fight and fight extremely hard. What I’m interested in is quality of play, quality of execution. And those are things that could be impacted by the level of distraction that they had on a short week in particular.”

Tirico then asked Tomlin how he feels, overall, about what has transpired this week.

“I’m upset about it, you know, it’s a dumpster fire,” Tomlin said. “And I don’t say that in a judgmental way. I presided over a few dumpster fires in my 19 years. But it certainly is upsetting, and I’m sure Steeler Nation is upset.”

If owner Art Rooney II calls Tomlin for advice, what would Tomlin say?

“Hey, Mike, that’s a hypothetical, because I know he wouldn’t and, and because I know how he’s trained,” Tomlin said. “You know, Art is a guy that’s not a hedge fund guy that bought a football team. He’s not a, you know, artificial intelligence guy that bought a football team. This is a family business. It’s generational. Any advice or thought process in that way, I imagine he just says, ‘What would my dad do?’ Or, ‘What would my grandfather do?’ Their mode of operation is time tested, it’s generational. And so, I doubt, very, very seriously, that he called anyone for outside advice.”

Finally, Tirico asked whether Tomlin believes Rodgers will get another NFL opportunity this season, if he wants one.

“There’s certainly a possibility for that,” Tomlin said. “And I just say that based on the 19 years that I held the position that I held. It’s a 32-team league. All it takes is one. Over time, I’ve laughed and watched that people talk about, you know, ‘No, that could never happen.’ In a 32-team league, when everybody is highly competitive and there’s some real challenges, particularly as you get to this portion of the journey from a player availability standpoint, there’s certainly a possibility if he desires that he could play again, because all it takes is one suitor.”

Having cleared waivers, Rodgers is available to that one suitor, if/when that one suitor calls. Still, that one suitor will need to be willing to accept the risk that, if Rodgers doesn’t have things go the way he wants, he’ll choose to do again the same thing he did this week.
