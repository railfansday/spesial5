SHT Day 12: Castle Danger

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Today started out pleasant enough! Who can complain about waking up dry and warm, with a toilet, and even a microwave to warm up one of my butter brioche waffles? These are something new I’m trying on trail and they haven’t disappointed. I even brought along maple syrup. It provides just the sugary kick I need sometimes in the afternoon. Having breakfast washed down with an energy drink, and I’m rearing to go!

The motel owner called me at about 8:40 to see if I was ready, which I was, so we got about a 10 minute head start out of town. She insisted on taking me on a little tour around town first. I wasn’t about to say no after all the kindness she’s shown me, and besides, I’m always willing to see and learn something new. She drove me down to the water’s edge to show off where all the giant cargo ships dock, and around to some of the local businesses, including the brewery. I’ll have to try to fit in a visit to that one at a later date.

She got me back to the trailhead shortly after nine, and I expressed my gratitude to her again. She was incredibly sweet and helpful. The trail started off as a .5 mile road walk on Highway 2 before turning off into the woods along snowmobile track. The first four miles or so were very smooth, at times winding along Silver Creek, where I stopped for an early lunch at its namesake campsite.

The trail follows picturesque Silver Creek

The first four miles were easy. The end of the day was easy and had me winding along the Gooseberry River mostly downhill. The middle portion of the day? Well, that’s a different story. Those miles were easily the most difficult of the trip so far.

This trail register looked brand spanking new

I came across something earlier this morning known in the guidebook as “rock cliff.” I should have taken that as a warning of what was to come later. I was nearing a section of trail known as Castle Danger. The middle 10 miles of trail for my day consisted of very steep ups and downs, at times a legit rock scramble, and hella rocks and roots.

Hard to tell here, but the trail drops off this “rock cliff”. Sign of things to come!

My pace slowed to a crawl. To make matters worse, I shorted myself on water a bit, and it was a hot day. I was getting frustrated and moody, so I had to firmly remind myself to just sit and have a snack. I have a funny mindset on this trail that I want to take breaks only at the official campsites. This mentality sometimes keeps me from doing things that I would otherwise do at any appropriate time that I actually need to do them, i.e., filter water, sit down for a minute, have a snack. This time, I just found the next fallen tree that I could sit on and forced down some food.

This trail marker cracked me up. The arrow is showing that I really do just go “UP”, not straight ahead.

Mood turned! Also, a few miles later I ran into a hiker who shared some water with me and gave me positive news about my next few miles. That was just the motivation I needed.

I ended up hiking 17 miles today despite the late start and tough terrain. I hope I didn’t wear out the ol’ knees and achilles! All told, the Castle Danger section was actually really interesting and would be a really cool day hike. Having a light or no pack would make the scrambles so much fun. I also saw my first significant wildlife today, a beautiful, big-bodied buck, and a cute little snake. As I look back through my pictures, I’m reminded too, that this section afforded me absolutely beautiful views of the landscape and changing colors.

No zoom needed here. I almost stepped on the little fella.

Crossing an unexpected dry river bed in this cool canyon.

Tonight I made camp at Middle Gooseberry Campsite. There are four Gooseberry campsites clustered close together here (west, east, middle, and just Gooseberry). The first one (west) was unappealing and east had a giant group already there. The next one, middle, was small and had two tents and three people staying, but I couldn’t go another step. The next campsite was another mile away and off trail. I was able to find a suitable spot to pitch my tent and make dinner as the sun set on my challenging day.
