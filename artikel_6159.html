American Airlines Expands Block On Last-Minute Partner Award Bookings

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

American Airlines appears to have expanded a frustrating restriction on last-minute award travel. Domestic connections are now subject to the same six-day cutoff that already blocked nonstop saver awards through partner programs. For travelers using Alaska Atmos Rewards points or British Airways Avios, another useful way to book American flights seems to have vanished.

As first flagged by One Mile At A Time, American is now withholding saver award inventory on wholly domestic connecting itineraries within 144 hours of departure. The restriction affects both economy and premium cabins.

American began blocking domestic nonstop saver awards within that window in May. Connections initially remained available, but that appears to have changed. International itineraries, including those with domestic connections, reportedly remain outside this restriction.

When using AA miles, there appears to be no change. AAdvantage members can still book flights through American itself, where award prices vary. The restriction concerns the inventory that other frequent flyer programs need to sell an award on American.

First, a short primer…each day this blog has folks who have read it for almost 20 years and those who are reading it for the first time.

A seat available for miles on AA.com is not necessarily available to Alaska or British Airways. American controls what it releases to those programs, and its own award pricing varies by flight and date.

When the original restriction emerged, flights disappeared from partner searches as they crossed the six-day threshold, even while remaining available through AAdvantage.

So an empty search result on a partner website does not necessarily mean the flight is full or that American has stopped accepting miles. It may simply mean you have the wrong kind of miles…

I understand why American might prefer that someone book through AAdvantage rather than redeem another airline’s currency. Giving travelers a reason to accumulate its own miles has commercial value and is part of AA’s plan to return to profitability.

But understanding the incentive does not make this a welcome development.

One of the most useful things about miles is being able to arrange a trip when cash fares are high. Think family emergency or holiday travel.

In those circumstances, having access to American through another program can be very valuable. Removing that option makes the points already sitting in those accounts less useful, even if the published award chart does not change.

We spend plenty of time discussing award price increases, but availability deserves just as much attention. An attractive mileage price is of little practical value when the program cannot book the flight you need, which is frequent criticism of Alaska Airlines Atmos Rewards, now even less valuable than due to AA blocking space.

No one is asking American to release every unsold seat to every partner….AA has every right to monetize as many seats as it can, just like it doing with first class upgrades.

Still, I would rather see American manage availability flight by flight than impose a broad cutoff based on how soon someone wants to travel. A flight with weak demand and a flight that is nearly sold out do not present the same commercial circumstances.

American does not comment on program changes like this, but has apparently concluded that the additional control is worth the lost partner bookings. Without knowing the reimbursement arrangements, I am not going to pretend I can calculate that trade-off.

From the passenger perspective, however, this is another reason to temper expectations about airline partnerships. The ability to earn points in one program and redeem them across a network is a major selling point. Restrictions like this make that promise less useful…and this is hardly a unique trend: we see the same thing with Delta and United.

If you need an American flight within six days of departure, check AAdvantage directly, for now that is your only option. Always compare the mileage price with the cash fare too…using miles is not automatically the better deal and is often not.

For travel farther out, I would still compare partner options before booking. But I would be certainly not transfer points speculatively while hoping that domestic American availability will open shortly before departure.

The expansion of partner award blocking removes another option for travelers hoping to use miles for last-minute domestic American flights. AAdvantage remains an option, but it’s a big blow to Alaska Airlines Atmos members.

I understand why American wants to favor its own program. Even so, I cannot celebrate this news.
