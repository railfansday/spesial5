When I was working for a startup in the early 2000s, we had our own servers. We were growing, so were adding servers at a pretty good clip. After one particular expansion, all of a sudden, Saturday night at 3 am, we blew breaker. No one had a good explanation, but the sysadmins were not happy about needing to go to the office in the middle of the night to fix things up.

A week later, the same thing happened. I suggested that it was the RAID check kickoff that might be doing it. And, indeed, it was. A couple of dozen servers all seeking their drives at once was too much for the circuits they were on. Staggering the startup time fixed it.

Maximum amperage draw for 1/10th of a second is not usually taken into account, breakers don't usually blow that fast because of a current spike. (Those numbers are usually not on the specification sheet either.)

The maximum load is calculated over a period of time, not a very short period of time. It is in this fuzzy gap that issues like this arrive.
