Tanggal: 2026-11-17

For myself and the other professionals I know who were there, it's not really about marketing. We mostly have client wait lists already, due to the overall situation where demand for skilled advice-only planning wildly exceeds the supply.

More often, it's because most advisor conferences frankly feel kind of gross. There's a bunch of talk about sales strategies, when that's not really of interest. And the whole time you're being solicited by sales reps from fund companies, software providers, etc. So it's nice to attend a Bogleheads Conference where marketing is prohibited and everybody is just there to talk about actual financial planning topics, rather than "how to build your business."
I thought about that too when I heard about how many advisors were attending (and met a few). It would make sense for an hourly or advice-only advisor to attend the conference, since they'd be interested in many of the same topics as us DIY investors, rather than envisioning attendees as potential clients. Do advisors need professional development hours and does the conference count?
