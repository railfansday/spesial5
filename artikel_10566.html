AI stock sell-off accelerates as markets head for steep weekly losses

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Wall Street’s losses mounted Friday, pulling the S&P 500 10% below its record high set last month, as the sell-off for AI stocks accelerated and the market headed for a sharp weekly loss.

The S&P 500 index was down about 2%. If it closes down more than 10% from its October 6 high, it will have entered what professional investors call a “correction.” The benchmark index is on track for a seventh straight day of losses, which would mark the longest such streak since the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020.

The Nasdaq composite, which is more greatly influenced by tech stocks, slumped 3.5%. The Dow Jones Industrial Average, which is much less exposed to big technology stocks, fell 289 points, or 0.6%, as of 12:20 p.m. ET.

More stocks were gaining ground within the S&P 500, but AI stocks with hefty market values have a much bigger impact on the market’s direction. They were responsible for Wall Street’s big gains this year and are now behind the big slide for the market.

AI stocks have been losing ground partly over worries that their big market values and heavy spending are unjustified. Fears of a bubble within the sector have also run up against the recent midterm elections, where there has been a shift in power after the GOP, seen as more friendly to the industry, suffered an electoral sweep.

The big market debut for Anthropic, maker of the Claude AI tool, fizzled on Friday with the rest of the tech sector. The stock fell 12.4% to $85.16. It is still above its initial public offering (IPO) price of $83 per share. It was the biggest public stock offering ever, at one point surpassing $2 trillion in value.

Market debuts for tech companies are often followed by a volatile period. SpaceX, which previously held the title for the biggest IPO, rose nearly 50% in its first three days, but fell below its IPO price a month later.

Chip stocks, big beneficiaries of the buildout of AI data centers, led the decline. Nvidia is falling for the ninth day out of the last 10, down 7.4%. The chipmaker has been under more pressure over worries about its future following the sudden downfall of Jensen Huang, its former CEO and cofounder.

Advanced Micro Devices dropped 11.7%, and Broadcom fell 11.5%. The PHLX Semiconductor Index sank 9%.

Outside of tech, though, most stocks were gaining ground. Cheerios maker General Mills jumped 4.8%, and homebuilder Lennar rose 3.4%.

Treasury yields fell in the bond market. The yield on the 10-year Treasury fell to 4.93%, from 5.04% late Thursday.

Brent crude oil, the international standard, fell 0.3% to $91.69 per barrel, despite continued tension near the Strait of Hormuz as the U.S. war with Iran continues. Saudi Arabia said on Friday that Iran-backed Houthi rebels had fired more missiles and drones at the kingdom days after it launched a major air campaign across Houthi-controlled parts of Yemen. Iraq apparently reached a compromise with a powerful Iran-backed militia it accused of smuggling oil.

Markets were lower in Europe and Asia.

—By Damian J. Troise and ALex Veiga, AP business writers

AP Business Writers Yuri Kageyama and Michelle Chapman contributed to this report.
