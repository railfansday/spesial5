Two specs matter before you buy a travel battery. The FAA caps carry-on lithium batteries at 100 watt-hours without airline approval, which works out to roughly 27,000mAh, and power banks always belong in your carry-on bag instead of checked luggage. If you want to charge a modern laptop, look for at least 65W of USB-C Power Delivery output, and 100W to 140W for larger machines. For snap-on phone packs, Qi2 certification means 15W wireless charging (25W on newer Qi2.2 packs).

This is the pack to start with if you want laptop-grade output without laptop-grade pricing. Amazon has it down to $44.33 from its $85.99 list price, which is 48 percent off. EcoFlow lists two 140W USB-C ports, a 25,000mAh capacity, and a display that shows what the pack is doing. The trade-off is the 170W total: one laptop can pull full speed, but two power-hungry machines will split the output. If you need more headroom, the EcoFlow Rapid Pro is rated for 300W and costs $89.99.

UGREEN’s 130W Nexode is $54.99 at Amazon, down from a $89.99 list price (39 percent off). The listing describes three ports, PD 3.0 charging, and a TFT display for checking charge status at a glance. UGREEN rates it at 72Wh, so it sits comfortably below the 100Wh carry-on limit. You give up some capacity compared with the 25,000mAh packs further down. The 25,000mAh, 145W Nexode is only $5 more at $59.98 if you’d rather have the extra capacity.
