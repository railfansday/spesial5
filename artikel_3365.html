Dragon Ball’s Best Episode In 8 Years Proves Fans Were Wrong About The Series’ New Anime

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The Dragon Ball Super: Beerus anime has been doing a great job of removing unnecessary filler and improving on the original Dragon Ball Super series, but changes in the latest episode were so good that they've justified the entire project. While long-time Dragon Ball fans have been anxiously awaiting the continuation of the story in the Galactic Patrol arc, the news that the latest anime project would be devoted to improving Dragon Ball Super's first three arcs felt like a waste of Toei's talents, but the latest episode proves those fears were unfounded.

When the original Dragon Ball Super anime first aired, its extremely rushed and poorly planned production cycle created episodes that were lacking in many ways. With the franchise being one of the world's most beloved, the anime's static battles and mind-numbing filler material deflated the years of hype that had been building for Dragon Ball's anime return.

In 2015, episode #5 of Dragon Ball Super featured Goku's first sparring match against Beerus, the God of Destruction. The battle's flat presentation and underwhelming choreography felt like a mistake that nearly destroyed the hype the Dragon Ball franchise had built for its return. The goal of Dragon Ball Super: Beerus was to fix those mistakes and also make the Destroyer God more intimidating, and after six episodes, it's clear that goal has been accomplished.

It didn't take long for Dragon Ball Super: Beerus to prove that it would be different from the original anime and even the Battle of Gods movie. The first arc of the franchise's big revival introduced the God of Destruction, Beerus, and his Angel attendant Whis. It was an arc that took the first anime series 14 episodes to tell, but the new series was able to condense that all into only six episodes, with the sixth episode being the perfect example of the series' improvements.

Now that the production team was given time and the opportunity to revise previously released content, far more detail, upgraded coloring, and even entirely new animations help fix the series' stalest moments. Those improvements can be noticed in several of episode #6's upgraded scenes. This is especially true during Goku's first battle as a Super Saiyan God, which now includes higher levels of drama and intensity, especially with the changes to Beerus.

After watching from the sidelines for weeks, English dub viewers finally have an official streaming schedule for Dragon Ball Super.

One particular part had Beerus holding Goku underwater, and in the original series, the Saiyan broke out of the hold, violently pushing the God of Destruction back while Beerus made a silly expression of surprise. It was one of the many moments with the Destroyer that was played for comedy. However, the new Dragon Ball anime has removed a lot of those moments, with episode six even replacing Beerus's expression with one of competent curiosity, one that reminds viewers that Beerus was always in control.

Besides the improved artwork, pacing, and music, brand-new sequences have also helped Dragon Ball Super: Beerus feel more than a simple remaster cash grab. Near the end of Goku and Beerus's match at the highest levels of Earth's atmosphere, an added monologue from the Destroyer God to an unconscious Goku saw Beerus explicitly express his respect for the Saiyan. It was an extremely rare peek into an important character's honest thoughts, and it was a moment that added to Beerus's developing humility and Goku's accomplishment of impressing him.

When it was announced that Dragon Ball Super: Beerus would be condensing the original series' first 41 episodes into only 19, it was inevitable that some good material would land on the cutting room floor. Fortunately, the remastered series removed most of the scenes that failed to move the main plot forward, including the Pilaf gang's annoying side-quest to steal the Dragon Balls. Still, there were a few moments that were drastically shortened and some that were completely removed that I wish had stayed.

Dragon Ball Super: Beerus has premiered on Crunchyroll, and the first episode of the anime is so popular that it crashed the streaming service.

Moments that were specifically missing from the part of the story that episode #6 covered were great character moments for Piccolo, Whis, and even Goku. The part where Piccolo grabs Whis's arm, and the Angel's demeanor suddenly shifts into a cold and threatening tone, is gone. Notably, a fan-favorite moment where Goku breaks through several layers of Earth's bedrock while screaming "I won't let you destroy my world!" at the God of Destruction also did not make it into Dragon Ball Super: Beerus. Those scenes add moments of hype and character depth that new audiences will miss if this is all they watch.

One of the most divisive alterations relates to Vegeta', and the depths he was willing to go to protect the new life he's worked so hard to create. In both the Battle of Gods movie and the original Dragon Ball Super anime, Vegeta is willing to embarrass himself in an attempt to entertain Beerus. It showed that the safety of his family now means more to him than his Saiyan pride. That change removed a moment that speaks to Vegeta's character development and ultimately helped improve the overall narrative.

Now that the first Dragon Ball Super arc has concluded in the remastered series, it's safe to say that it's an improvement. Large sections of the original series that focused on unfunny and uninteresting scenes involving characters who have no narrative impact on future events severely dragged the pacing. And when battles did finally happen, the flat animation and repeated sequences felt like another insult that ultimately repelled viewers who'd grown up idolizing Dragon Ball Z. Thankfully, Dragon Ball Super: Beerus has addressed those glaring problems and become the best way to enjoy the beginning of the sequel series.

Even though Goku & Vegeta's strength has become godly, there are still a handful of Dragon Ball characters they can't & may never be able to defeat.

The first arc has been an improvement in almost every way, but there are still scenes that Toei left in that could have been removed. The shapeshifting pig, Oolong, hasn’t provided anything important to the narrative in decades, which is why it was baffling to see his prolonged game of rock-paper-scissors against Beerus remain in the new series. Still, even if a few moments like that stay and reduce the time for beloved scenes, Dragon Ball Super: Beerus has made enough smart and exciting changes to change my mind about it and power up my anticipation for more.
