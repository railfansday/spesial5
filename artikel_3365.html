Jeff Hays’ Least Favorite Dungeon Crawler Carl Character Voice Isn’t Who You’d Expect

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

While Dungeon Crawler Carl fans wait for news on Book 9 and the TV adaptation, audiobook narrator Jeff Hays and series author Matt Dinniman have offered plenty of fun behind-the-scenes tidbits. The author has even shared some early details on Book 9 via his Patreon page for those who just can't wait. And earlier this year, the dynamic duo got together to create a definitive tier-ranking list of every audiobook voice in the series so far.

The Dungeon Crawler Carl audiobooks have played a huge part in the series' massive popularity. Though the brightly colored hardcover editions and shiny new Deluxe Edition have no doubt graced many shelves, readers swear by Jeff Hays' narration of the series. And for good reason. Hays does such an impressive job with his character voice work that you can easily forget Dungeon Crawler Carl isn't a full-cast production. But that doesn't mean he, or author Matt Dinniman, love every character voice equally.

In fact, Hays and Dinniman have previously shared their "official" tier list of every character voice in the series as of A Parade of Horribles, which came out in May 2026. This video from Audible serves as an impressive reminder of just how many unique characters Hays has had to embody for the series. But while some rankings, including Princess Donut's, aren't surprising, a few characters certainly don't fall where you'd expect.

Talented though Jeff Hays clearly is as a narrator and voice actor, that doesn't mean every voice in the series is easy for him to step into. One of the factors Hays took into consideration for his tier ranking was how difficult each performance was for him, and that gives readers some fun insight into the audiobook process. Fan-favorite Zev is one of the trickier voices, due to the high pitch Hays uses for the character. But his least favorite will probably surprise you.

Despite being the titular character, Carl is, "the most difficult voice out of all these characters," according to Hays. In the early audiobooks, Carl has a heavy Patrick Warburton influence that brings The Emperor's New Groove's Kronk to mind. Hays has toned that down in more recent Dungeon Crawler Carl installments and is ultimately happy with where Carl's voice ended up. But the legacy of those early Patrick Warburton impressions lives on, leaving Hays unable to even listen to the first three audiobooks.

Author Matt Dinniman is quite happy with how Carl's voice turned out, suggesting it belongs in A or even S-Tier. Though it took some evolution for the main character's narration and speaking voice to hit its stride, Hays and Dinniman agree it's still one of the best voices in the series, giving it an A-Tier ranking alongside the likes of Prepotente, Mordecai, and Odette. Given that Carl is the main character, and Dungeon Crawler Carl is written in first-person, however, it's pretty surprising to learn that Hays considers it one of the toughest voices even compared with the likes of high-pitched, excitable Zev or Icelandic Valkyrie Hekla.

Alongside the tier ranking for voices, Dinniman and Hays shared some more insights into their relationship to the story. For instance, Dinniman confirmed that fan-favorite Samantha is one of his two favorite side characters to write. Hays is also a fan because, "the worst things to come out of anyone's mouth comes out of Samantha." But despite their mutual fondness for her, Samantha landed in the A-tier.

What does it take to hit S-Tier? Well, quite a few fan-favorite characters managed to snag the spot, including one that will surprise zero people who've heard Hays as Princess Donut. Along with Her Royal Highness, Hays ranks the System AI as one of his all-time favorites to voice. Given the ever-increasing chaos of that character, that's hardly a surprise. But of course, the tier ranking video saved Princess Donut for last for a reason.

Hays confirmed that Donut isn't just his favorite character in Dungeon Crawler Carl. She is, "one of [his] favorite characters to voice ever." Given that, it's hardly a surprise he agreed to reprise the role for the upcoming adaptation, and that Donut claimed a spot among the S-Tier voices for the entire series.

What is interesting, however, is that we almost had an entirely different Donut. The original plan was to hire a female voice actor to star alongside Hays as the royal cat character. The version of Donut we know and love was initially just a placeholder until that actor stepped in, but everyone wound up loving it so much that no other version of Donut would do.
