Bradford City takeover: Everything we know right now

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Bradford City are being linked to a potential takeover by a Thai-based consortium, so here's a round-up of the latest on whether this move might come off.

The international break couldn't have come at a better time for Bradford City.

Following an exceptional start to the season, the departure of manager Graham Alexander to Preston North End at the start of October hit the club hard, and they went into the break with just one win from their last six league matches and having slipped from the top of the League One table to eighth place, having replaced Alexander with the former Wycombe Wanderers manager Michael Duff.

But losing Alexander may not even be the biggest change to be coming to Bradford City this season. It's been rumoured that a Thai-based consortium are considering launching a bid to buy the club from current owner Stefan Rupp, with the group having been attracted by the club's recent upward trajectory and potential, given the size of attendances they were attracting, even while the team was misfiring in League Two.

This isn't the first time since Rupp purchased the club in 2016 that there has been an attempt to wrest Bradford City from his control, so here's a round-up of what's been said about ongoing reported interest in buying them, and what it could mean for a club who previously slipped from the Premier League to League Two in just six years, between 2001 and 2007.

A report from journalist Alan Nixon on Sunday morning has reopened conjecture that the Bradford City owner Stefan Rupp could be set to sell the club after ten years of ownership. Nixon confirmed on his Patreon that "Bradford City are a surprise target for a Thai-based group" who have been considering buying into the EFL.

Nixon confirmed that the prospective bidders have shown interest in buying a club before, and that, "Bradford could be for sale at the right price and the Thais, who have an intermediary with inside knowledge, are considering an official offer."

But before anything else, any new owners will have to get a takeover past Rupp, and that might not be completely straightforward. The German has been reported to be more open to selling since the club were promoted from League Two in 2025, with The Athletic's Matt Slater reporting in March 2026 that, "Rupp has been listening to offers for years but has showing a bit more leg ever since last season’s promotion."

Rupp has, however, said in the past that he won't sell up to just anybody. In 2021, he rejected an offer from the US cryptocurrency investors WAGMI United. They instead turned their attention to Crawley Town but, since completing their takeover of the Sussex-based club in April 2022, Crawley's position has waned considerably, with the Red Devils currently propping up the rest of the EFL and six points from safety.

Their recent slump in League One notwithstanding, the direction of travel that Bradford City have undertaken over the last couple of seasons makes them a prime target for those seeking to buy into the English game.

For years, it felt as though the Bantams had everything bar the team. They had a stadium which is fit for Premier League football, and attendances were extremely healthy, even when the team wasn't performing especially well in League Two. On top of this, being the only professional football club in a city of half a million people gives the opportunity for further growth, should they continue to rise through the divisions.

Under Alexander, the team was finally successful, winning promotion back to League One in 2025 following an absence of six years, and their first season back hinted that the Bantams weren't done either, with the team finishing in a highly creditable fourth place before losing 2-0 on aggregate to Bolton Wanderers in the semi-finals of the play-offs.

But there is one word in Alan Nixon's report which may cause Bradford fans to worry a little about the nature of the group who are now being linked with the club. Nixon described this consortium as "speculators", and that will raise concerns about the motives of this group among some.

There isn't anywhere near enough detail about this group to be able to draw too many inferences about their intentions for the club, though it's previously been reported that they are connected to the former owners of Oxford United. But "speculators" is a loaded word, and there may be concern over whether they will have their own interests at heart or those of the club should they take over.

And when speaking to the Bradford Telegraph & Argus at the end of June, Rupp confirmed that, "You get requests from time to time but they are tyre kickers, people without money, wannabe football club owners, or people you simply don't like," and that, "I'm the keeper of the keys and I would never hand it over to somebody where I have the impression he can't do any better."

The sale price, should it be the same as it was reported to be earlier in 2026, would be unlikely to be too much of a sticking point. The Athletic's report in March suggested that £10 million could be enough to persuade him to sell, with Bradford owning neither their own ground nor their training ground, but such a sum could still only be considered a drop in the ocean, were the club to ascend to the Championship or beyond.

Still in the FA Cup, and with an EFL Cup quarter-final at Manchester City to look forward to in the middle of December, Bradford City remain in a better condition on the pitch than they have been for years, and the big irony of their current position is that this very success may push up their sale price. Stefan Rupp has said before that he will only sell the club to the right buyer, but the true test of his principles may come when the rumours cease and concrete offers start to land on his desk.
