GTA 6: Complete Beginner's Guide

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Pretty sure we’ve all had an eventful week with Grand Theft Auto 6 so far because it is undoubtedly the most ambitious video game release, shattering an entertainment-product record on release and going way beyond people’s expectations with the amount of detail and what it has in store. And undeniably, anyone jumping in for the first time may feel overwhelmed by the mechanics and additions.

Grand Theft Auto 6 delivers an enormous open world, a gripping story, a vibrant Vice City and an astonishing range of activities.

But hey, whether you’re a seasoned veteran of the series or totally new to video games who’s playing this blindly for the first time, you don’t have to worry because I've put together an extensive Beginner’s Guide to GTA 6 that compiles the most helpful tips and explanations of the game’s most complex aspects. Strap in, because this will be worth your time, trust.

I’m getting the formalities out of the way first because I’m quickly going to suggest tuning some settings right from the main menu or even in-game, actually. These are all up to you, but I’m mostly here to show you how each one can be helpful.

First, if you’re used to the OG controls of the GTA series, quickly set the control scheme to Classic, as this brings back some familiar GTA 5-style inputs. By default, running and sprinting are mapped to clicking L3, while jumping is assigned to X (A on Xbox). Switching to Classic moves running and sprinting back to X (A), puts crouching on L3, and swaps a few other actions around. I genuinely find it much more comfortable, especially if you've spent years playing the older games.

Next, I'd recommend setting your Minimap to Always On under UI > Navigation. GTA 6's default Dynamic HUD can hide certain information when you're exploring, which looks great for immersion but isn't always practical when you're still learning your way around Leonida. And while you’re at it, reduce Motion Blur Strength if you don’t want to get sick.

And beyond whichever aiming sensitivity suits you, be sure to experiment with the Targeting settings under Gameplay > Combat. Partial is a good middle ground for newcomers, as it helps move your aim towards nearby targets without completely locking onto them. Finally, in UI > Units of Measurement, you can choose your preferred unit system, like the 12-hour clock over the 24-hour one.

Grand Theft Auto 6 puts a large emphasis on keeping your relationship sound, so you need to know what ways are the best to raise the romance between Jason and Lucia.

Briefly going over this as well, since you can easily handle this, contrary to what other players might have you believe. Jason and Lucia are your own, like the little make-believe babies for a class project. Besides their relationship, keep in mind that these characters now have individual stats, like hunger, muscle mass, weight, and sleep management. These can actively influence gameplay in a few ways, such as sleep affecting your total stamina. Meanwhile, things like weight affect how fast you can sprint.

We already have a list of the best ways to boost the couple’s relationship, but the basic gist that you need to remember is not to let them drop to Partners in Crime or below. There are plenty of things that can help you easily boost their relationship, so it’s honestly impossible to not maintain it unless you’re purposely not using both characters or sabotaging it yourself.

And since we’re here, make sure to actively invest in the couple’s BuckMe Family Savings account later on until you max it out at the highest tier, since both that and their relationship are among the primary factors influencing the ending of GTA 6.

Again, you’re free to check our dedicated guide on the mechanic as well, but with the Criminal Profile system, just think of it as a more approachable karma system, like the Honor one from Red Dead 2. This is also one of the other parameters that can influence GTA 6’s ending. A lot of people will have you believe that it strips away your maniacal “GTA” playstyle, but trust me, with everything in your power, this is the most forgiving and manipulable mechanic in the game.

The only thing to watch out for is taking things to the “Irredeemable” state because by that point you can’t revert it back, and you’ll be stuck with that profile unless you have a save file. You can achieve and maintain a Professional Criminal Profile by simply being a good citizen or trying to have as few casualties as possible during side activities, or even successfully making clean getaways and stealth takedowns.

Don’t be afraid to take headshots when necessary because even if you reach a state like Psycho, you can easily change that back by petting a couple of domestic animals, spending some casual time with each other, or just by avoiding headshots altogether for the next few kills by shooting in the limbs instead.

GTA 6 has a noticeably more complex Wanted System than its predecessors. For starters, we’re back to six Wanted Stars, and the police are now much more observant of our actions. Witnesses can report your crimes, security cameras can capture your current identity, and even certain high-end vehicles have trackers that forbid you from just stealing them. In other words, no more crazy Grand Theft Auto-ing.

The biggest change, however, is the introduction of the Heat system. Even after you've managed to lose the cops, they will still remember certain identifying details about you, including your appearance, clothing, getaway vehicle, visible weapons, and whether Jason and Lucia were together. These are represented by small red icons underneath your Wanted Stars, so pay attention to them whenever you're trying to escape.

It’s either how fast you can clear the heat markers off of you in the natural way or some creative tactics that I can suggest, like using water vehicles or boats for escaping higher-level wanted stars, since the rivers and sea tend to be open and less swamped with traffic than VC’s urban infrastructure.

Money plays a much bigger role in GTA 6 than you might initially expect, and you'll quickly find yourself needing extra cash to purchase weapons, clothing for both characters, vehicle upgrades, and other necessities. Thankfully, Leonida is practically overflowing with opportunities to make a quick buck. But the progression works steadily throughout each chapter here.

You’ll start off small by robbing convenience stores and other local businesses in chapter 2, but by chapters 3 and 4, the Robberies tab on your map will be almost flooded with bigger opportunities, including pawn shops, jewelry stores, and banks. Even the occasional gang hideout has different valuables like unique guns to own, vehicles to sell, or safes to bust open and loot.

However, be mindful that robbing a place blind isn't always the smartest idea since many of these sites have security cameras and alarms. Try to use Focus Mode as much as possible to identify enemies, valuable items, and locate safe combinations before making your escape.

Of course, not everything in Leonida requires you to point a gun at someone. There are several side activities that can help you earn some extra money, including stealing vehicles for buyers like Moses and Randy, hunting wildlife for Bret, and competing in Demolition Derbies at Ambrosia Raceway.

One final thing worth remembering is that GTA 6 has three forms of allowance: pocket cash, bank savings, and the valuables you carry in your stash bag. While your bank balance is safe, getting Wasted or Busted can cost you the cash you're carrying, along with any stolen goods you haven't managed to sell, so make a habit of visiting ATMs to deposit your earnings into BuckMe whenever possible.

Many of us are already feeling nostalgic about GTA 6’s first chapter; I mean, seriously, the vibe in Key Lento is unparalleled, especially when playing the game for the first time. But as all things come and go, the vibe doesn't last for long here.

There are a handful of missables you can easily overlook if you rush through the story, so I'd strongly recommend taking your time and exploring everything the Keys have to offer before moving on. Without spoiling, The Tourist Trap mission marks the point of no return for Chapter 1's free roam, so make sure you’re fully ready.

For starters, make sure you complete Shirley's optional jobs, Get Shirley's Rum and Steal the Scooter, as these are exclusive to the first chapter. You might also want to check the loose floorboards for any money you've stashed away, and deposit that into your BuckMe Family Savings.

Lastly, it doesn’t hurt to have a separate manual save for this chapter specifically since GTA 6 doesn't currently let you replay completed story missions, so having a backup gives you the opportunity to revisit Key Lento without having to start another playthrough.

You don't necessarily need to complete every single activity before moving on, but I'd hate for you to miss out on some of the more interesting encounters and neat discoveries Rockstar’s tucked away in this opening chapter. Trust me, you'll appreciate this thing the most here.

Grand Theft Auto 6 makes you lose everything at the end of the first chapter, so make sure that you complete these necessary tasks before taking the plunge into chapter two.
