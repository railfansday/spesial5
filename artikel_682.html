‘Dang Me’! Roger Miller Posthumously Inducted Into Songwriters Hall of Fame

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

His "King of the Road" received Grammy nods for both record and song of the year in 1966.

Roger Miller, the witty wordsmith who delivered hits as “Dang Me” and “King of the Road,” was posthumously inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame, it was announced on Monday (Nov. 23). Miller, who died in 1992 at age 56, was honored during an intimate ceremony held on Nov. 17 at BMI headquarters in Nashville, prior to the BMI Country Awards.

SHOF board members Fletcher Foster (chairman of SHOF’s Nashville Committee), Mike O’Neill and Jon Platt made the induction to Miller’s widow, Mary Miller.

“We were ecstatic to learn that Roger was being inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame,” Mary Miller said in a statement. “Of all his many talents, he was most proud of his songwriting. This lifetime achievement would have meant a great deal to him, and we are honored to celebrate the recognition of his extraordinary body of work.”

The annual SHOF Induction & Awards Gala in New York City each June does not usually include posthumous inductions, but the organization has taken to hosting posthumous inductions at special events to recognize songwriters they missed during their lifetimes. In the past two years, Prince, Cindy Walker, Hugo Peretti & Luigi Creatore, Bert Berns, Jerry Ragovoy and Donna Summer have been inducted into the SHOF posthumously in this way.

Miller had 11 top 10 hits on Billboard’s Hot Country Songs chart in the 1960s, all but one of which he wrote or co-wrote. He had long runs at No. 1 with “Dang Me” in 1964 and “King of the Road” in 1965. Both songs won Grammys for best country and western song, a term that was phased out in favor of country in 1969. Both of these songs also made the top 10 on the all-genre Billboard Hot 100, as did three other Miller hits: “Chug-a-Lug,” “Engine Engine #9” and “England Swings.”

“King of the Road,” which received Grammy nominations for both record and song of the year, is one of the most famous country songs from the 1960s.

Miller won 11 Grammys in just two years (1965 and 1966). In 1966, he became the first artist from any genre to win six Grammys in one year. “Dang Me” and “King of the Road” have both been voted into the Grammy Hall of Fame.

Miller received five awards from the Academy of Country Music Awards, including single record of the year in 1969 for his country version of Bobby Russell’s song “Little Green Apples” (which O.C. Smith popularized in the pop market). Miller’s other ACM Awards are songwriter of the year and man of the year in 1966, the pioneer award in 1988 and the ACM Poet’s award in 2012.

Miller was voted into the Nashville Songwriters Hall of Fame (no relationship to the SHOF) in 1973 and the Country Music Hall of Fame in 1995.

Miller was also a popular TV personality, frequently appearing on variety and talk shows and starring in his own NBC series, The Roger Miller Show, in 1966.

Miller wrote the music and lyrics for Big River, a musical adaptation of Mark Twain’s The Adventures of Huckleberry Finn. Big River won seven Tonys in 1985, including best musical. Miller received both a Tony for best original score and the Drama Desk Award for outstanding lyrics.

Subsequent generations of artists have continued to record Miller’s songs and achieve top rankings on Hot Country Songs. Ricky Van Shelton reached No. 1 in 1988 with a remake of Miller’s 1970 song “Don’t We All Have the Right.” Dwight Yoakam reached No. 7 in 1992 with “It Only Hurts When I Cry,” which Yoakam cowrote with Miller. Alan Jackson took “Tall, Tall Trees,” which Miller cowrote with George Jones, to No. 1 in 1995. Brooks & Dunn’s reached No. 1 in 1988 with a new version of Miller’s 1966 hit “Husbands and Wives.” Though it was decades old at that point, Miller’s song was nominated for song of the year at both the CMA Awards and the ACM Awards in 1999.
