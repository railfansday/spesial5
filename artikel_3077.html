Anthropic IPO: Here's What Its Spending Commitments Mean to Microsoft, SpaceX, Amazon, and Alphabet

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

One of these four companies has significantly more important exposure to Anthropic's compute needs than the others, and that could be good news for it.

By Lee Samaha – Nov 20, 2026 at 9:20AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Anthropic ›

It's no secret that the AI market has many interlinked relationships among companies that are both competitors and customers, and Anthropic's (ANT -8.45%) initial public offering certainly highlights this. The company's $900.9 billion spending commitments include massive infrastructure service agreements with Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -5.18%), Microsoft (MSFT -0.59%), Alphabet (GOOG +1.78%) (GOOGL +1.85%), and Amazon (AMZN -0.63%). Here's what those commitments mean to each company.

The company is spending staggering amounts on compute and infrastructure, but the good news for potential investors is it's spreading its purchases across various providers and time frames. About $451 billion of that $900.9 billion is expected to be spent by the end of 2030, with the rest spread between 2031 and 2045.

The table below shows a breakdown of its major service deals, their time frames, and a rough estimate of each company's revenue share over the deal period. Two points stick out from this data.

First, Anthropic’s deal with SpaceX will allow either party to cancel with just 90 days’ notice in the near future. Second, the share of SpaceX’s estimated revenue that will derive from its deal with the AI lab is highly significant for it.

Data sources: Anthropic SEC filings, *Visible Alpha Wall Street Consensus, author's analysis.

SpaceX's exposure to Anthropic is arguably more important than the 10.1% figure implies because it represents a larger share (about 14.7%) of the expected revenue for its “super intelligence” (SI) segment over the period.

While Anthropic could conceivably cancel the deal early, SpaceX CEO Elon Musk recently said on X that he thinks "the opposite is going to happen." And in an interview at the DealBook summit this month, he said Anthropic "told me that their sort of highest probability outcome is that they would like an incremental 10 gigawatts of compute from SpaceX."

If Musk is right, the risk is arguably that SpaceX could be the party that cancels the deal or attempts to negotiate better terms for itself down the road, not least because Musk believes "the value of compute per watt will increase over time, not decrease." If that assumption proves correct, then SpaceX will be better positioned than Amazon, Microsoft, and Alphabet to profit from the Anthropic IPO.
