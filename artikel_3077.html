Tower Records Japan restarts shipping after leak of 11 million records

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Login details, birth dates and home addresses were taken from its members scheme. The music retailer is the latest in Japan to report a leak, after Zozo.

A Tower Records shop in the RyuBo department store in Naha, Okinawa

Tower Records Japan will start shipping online orders again from Monday. A breach last week exposed about 11 million customer records, it said. The music retailer gave the update on Sunday evening in its notice on the incident. Its online store, Tower Records Online, stays shut for now. Its marketplace has no date to reopen.

Tower Records spotted the unauthorised access at about 5:10pm on Thursday, 19 November, and took the store offline. Its investigation then found that customer data had leaked.

The leak covers members of its Tower Records Members scheme, including people who left within the past three years. The company broke it down into three groups:

A hashed password cannot be read as it stands, the company said. No credit card data leaked. As of Saturday it had found no misuse of the data. It warned customers about emails pretending to come from Tower Records. It is contacting affected customers by email.

Tower Records has not said who was behind the attack or how they got in.

Tower Records is the latest Japanese retailer to disclose a breach, Bloomberg reported. A day earlier, the online fashion retailer Zozo reported a separate leak. It said attackers got into KARTE, a marketing platform run by the Japanese firm Plaid, on 13 November.

The Zozo leak covers about 15.2 million ZOZOTOWN shoppers and 13.9 million WEAR app users. Most records hold member IDs, ages and genders. For about 47,000 shoppers they also hold emails and phone numbers. No passwords leaked, Zozo said. Marui Group, which uses the same platform, reported a similar incident, Bloomberg reported.

Japan has already stepped up cyber warnings after a run of leaks. Bloomberg named older IT systems as a weakness and said the PHP2Shell flaws hit Japan hard. In the US, Chase, Visa and Mastercard confirmed vendor hacks last week.
