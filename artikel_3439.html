The ‘Godzilla’ Movie That Divided Fans Is Officially Getting a New Streaming Release

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Hannah covers horror, sci-fi, and games at Collider, where she writes news and features, reviews new releases, and interviews the people who make them. She’s helping build out Collider’s gaming coverage, with Pokémon as one of her specialties. If she isn’t writing about a monster, she’s probably watching one on a tape from her VHS collection.

By the time Madison Square Garden becomes the most dangerous building in New York in Roland Emmerich’s Godzilla, the military has spent much of the movie trying to find one enormous animal. Then the eggs hatch, and Matthew Broderick has to get out of an arena full of mini-Godzillas. If this was your first Godzilla movie, there’s a decent chance you remember the scramble to get out of the Garden better than you remember what the big guy himself looked like.

Twenty-eight years later, Takashi Yamazaki’s Godzilla Minus Zero is heading into its second North American weekend after opening to an estimated $31.6 million. The sequel picks up two years after Godzilla Minus One, with Japan still rebuilding from the last attack. Yamazaki is asking what it takes for a country to get back on its feet after Godzilla comes through, while Emmerich had a more immediate question for New York: how do you get everybody out before the rest of the eggs hatch?

Godzilla (1998) is now streaming free with ads in the U.S. on Pluto TV. Fandango at Home also added it to its free lineup on November 1, timed to Minus Zero’s theatrical release. Broderick plays Niko Tatopoulos, the scientist who figures out what brought the creature to Manhattan, while Jean Reno, Maria Pitillo, and Hank Azaria round out the cast.

Emmerich makes Godzilla fast enough to lose in traffic and gives his human characters a problem that gets worse the longer they spend chasing him. Niko realizes the creature came to New York to reproduce, which means the military has been looking for one animal while an entire nest is about to hatch. It’s a fun setup for a creature feature, and the design sells it. The long snout and hunched posture make him look like he could outrun a cab, so it’s at least fun to watch right up until you realize you’re watching Godzilla run from the army, when a lot of fans showed up to watch the army run from him.

Of course, plenty of people met Godzilla in the middle of that chase. The movie made $376 million worldwide, and for some viewers it was their first Godzilla movie. That’s part of what makes it fun to revisit alongside Minus Zero. If you came in expecting a monster who could walk through everything an army threw at him, watching him duck around a corner was a hard sell. If you were eight years old hoping nobody would open the wrong door at Madison Square Garden, you’ve probably been defending this movie ever since.

Godzilla is available to stream now on Pluto TV, while Godzilla Minus Zero is still in its theatrical run.
