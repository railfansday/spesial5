Could $1,000 in Oklo Stock Set You Up for Life by 2035?

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Oklo stock is as jittery as an electron. Can this volatile nuclear stock deliver explosive gains by 2035?

By Steven Porrello – Nov 19, 2026 at 3:30PM EST

Oklo (OKLO -4.40%) is a nuclear tech company with a radical vision for how companies could get electricity in the future. Instead of relying on the power grid (which is old, aging, and not always reliable), companies, in Oklo's best-case future, would buy electricity directly from small nuclear reactors built and operated by Oklo. It's a bold proposition, one that could not only reshape the utilities sector but could also reward early Oklo investors handsomely.

The question, though, remains: Can Oklo really turn this idea into a profitable business? And, if so, can it make early investors rich?

First, the exciting part: Oklo already has a potential customer with fairly deep pockets. Yes, I'm talking about Meta Platforms (META -3.27%). Earlier this year, Meta agreed to prepay and fund the development of a 1.2 gigawatt nuclear power campus in Ohio (using power from Oklo's Aurora powerhouse), with the first phase targeted to begin generating electricity as early as 2030.

Oklo investors greeted that news with just about every celebratory emoji in the book. Since then, however, the stock has plunged almost 80%.

The long and the short of it: Nothing. I mean, nothing happened to Oklo's business. Technically speaking, nothing has ever happened to Oklo's business: It's still a (mostly) pre-revenue company. A commercial reactor? Nope. It doesn't even have regulatory approval to operate one yet.

And, yes, though it boasts a massive potential order book of roughly 14 gigawatts, much of that is backed by preliminary agreements rather than firm commitments to buy electricity.

So, in a nutshell, Oklo stock simply ran out of steam. Or rather, to be more precise, it ran out of hype. Oklo soared in 2025 as investors were excited about nuclear's potential to meet soaring electricity demands, not just from AI data centers but also from factories, electric vehicles, and an increasingly electrified economy.

To be sure, it still has potential to do that. But investors appear to be waking up to the harsh reality of nuclear energy: The industry is slow, expensive, and encumbered by regulatory red tape. If Oklo is going to deliver spectacular returns, it's going to take a while. But can it do it by 2035?

Consider, for a second, that this is 2035: Do you think AI data centers will be powered by nuclear reactors? Indeed, do you think communities will welcome nuclear reactors in their backyard? If you hesitate to answer either question in the affirmative, I don't blame you: It's pretty difficult to imagine that future today, especially with resistance to data center projects achieving rare bipartisan agreement.

To be sure, I think Oklo will have a place in the future economy, which is why I hold the stock today. But I don't think it will necessarily live up to the sky-high expectations that gave its stock so much juice in 2025.

The math is even more sobering: Even if Oklo were to grow tenfold, which isn't impossible but would also require near-perfect execution, a $1,000 investment would be worth $10,000 by 2035. That's an excellent return for an energy stock, but hardly enough to retire on.

So, could $1,000 in Oklo stock help you build wealth by 2035? I think so. But could it set you up for life? I wouldn't count on it.
